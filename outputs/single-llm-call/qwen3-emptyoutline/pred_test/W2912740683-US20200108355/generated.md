# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The persistent challenge of biofouling in membrane-based water treatment systems has long impeded the efficiency, longevity, and economic viability of critical industrial processes such as reverse osmosis, ultrafiltration, microfiltration, and membrane bioreactors. Biofouling arises from the uncontrolled adhesion, proliferation, and metabolic activity of microorganisms on membrane surfaces, culminating in the formation of complex, resilient biofilms composed of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS). These biofilms act as physical and biochemical barriers that drastically reduce membrane permeability, necessitate elevated operational pressures to maintain throughput, and accelerate the frequency of membrane cleaning and replacement. The cumulative impact of these effects translates into substantial increases in energy consumption, chemical usage, maintenance labor, and capital expenditure across municipal water supply networks, industrial cooling circuits, desalination plants, and wastewater reclamation facilities. Unlike mineral scaling or colloidal fouling, which can often be mitigated through pretreatment with coagulants, antiscalants, or physical backwashing, biofouling remains uniquely recalcitrant due to the self-replicating, adaptive, and synergistic nature of microbial communities. Conventional strategies to combat biofouling have included chemical disinfection via chlorination, ozonation, or UV irradiation; however, these methods frequently generate harmful disinfection byproducts—such as trihalomethanes, haloacetic acids, and bromate—that can permeate membranes and compromise the safety of treated water. Moreover, ozonation has been shown to inadvertently generate assimilable organic carbon, which serves as a nutrient substrate that promotes rather than suppresses microbial growth. Alternative biological interventions, such as quorum sensing inhibition or bacteriophage therapy, have demonstrated promise in laboratory settings but face insurmountable hurdles in scalability, regulatory approval, and long-term stability under real-world operating conditions. Membrane surface modification through polymer blending, surface grafting, or coating with antimicrobial agents has emerged as a more sustainable and proactive approach. Yet, many of these modifications suffer from limited durability, leaching of active components, or narrow-spectrum efficacy against diverse microbial populations. Furthermore, the majority of existing modified membranes have been evaluated using only a single bacterial strain, typically Escherichia coli, thereby failing to account for the heterogeneity of microbial communities encountered in natural and industrial feedwaters. There exists, therefore, a critical and unmet need for a membrane material that is inherently resistant to biofouling across a broad spectrum of clinically and environmentally relevant bacterial species, including both Gram-positive and Gram-negative pathogens, without relying on toxic leachables, chemical additives, or external energy inputs. Such a material must be compatible with standard phase inversion fabrication techniques, scalable to industrial production, and capable of maintaining its antifouling properties over extended operational lifetimes under fluctuating pH, temperature, and ionic strength conditions.

The development of polymeric membranes with intrinsic antifouling properties has been the subject of extensive research, particularly through the incorporation of hydrophilic additives that alter surface energetics and electrostatic potential. Polymers such as polyethersulfone (PES) and polysulfone (PS) are widely used in membrane fabrication due to their excellent mechanical strength, thermal stability, and chemical resistance. However, their inherent hydrophobicity renders them highly susceptible to protein adsorption and microbial colonization. To counteract this, numerous studies have explored the blending of hydrophilic polymers, surfactants, or nanoparticles into the casting solution to enhance surface wettability and reduce adhesion forces. Among these, natural polysaccharides have garnered increasing attention due to their biocompatibility, low toxicity, and abundance. Arabic gum (AG), a complex heteropolysaccharide exuded by Acacia trees, has been previously investigated as a membrane modifier in PES systems, where it was shown to improve flux and reduce fouling by E. coli. However, these investigations were limited in scope, lacking comprehensive characterization of surface charge, pore architecture, and, critically, evaluation against a diverse panel of bacterial strains representative of real-world biofouling challenges. Moreover, the underlying mechanism by which AG exerts its antifouling effect—whether through direct antimicrobial activity, physical barrier formation, or electrostatic repulsion—remained ambiguously attributed and inadequately validated. The absence of systematic studies correlating AG concentration with membrane morphology, hydrophilicity, zeta potential, and microbial adhesion across multiple bacterial species has left a significant knowledge gap in the field. Additionally, the potential for AG to induce structural changes in the membrane matrix—such as increased porosity, enhanced macrovoid formation, and improved interconnectivity—has not been fully leveraged to simultaneously optimize both permeability and fouling resistance. Previous attempts to incorporate high loadings of hydrophilic additives have often resulted in compromised mechanical integrity, reduced flux due to pore blockage, or phase separation anomalies during casting. Consequently, the optimal concentration of AG that maximizes antifouling performance without sacrificing structural or hydraulic properties has not been established. There is a clear and urgent need for a membrane system that integrates the multifunctional properties of AG in a precisely controlled manner, enabling the synergistic enhancement of hydrophilicity, surface charge, and pore architecture to achieve unprecedented resistance to biofouling across a wide range of microbial contaminants, thereby offering a durable, non-leaching, and chemically inert solution to one of the most persistent problems in membrane technology.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a novel class of polysulfone-based composite membranes that exhibit exceptional resistance to biofouling by a broad spectrum of bacterial species, including both Gram-positive and Gram-negative pathogens, through the strategic incorporation of arabic gum into the membrane matrix during phase inversion casting. These membranes are fabricated by dissolving polysulfone in a suitable organic solvent, introducing arabic gum at specific weight percentages ranging from 0.1% to 7%, subjecting the resulting dope solution to sonication and degassing to ensure homogenous dispersion, and subsequently casting the solution onto a flat surface followed by immersion in a non-solvent coagulation bath to induce phase separation and membrane solidification. The resulting composite membranes demonstrate a marked increase in surface hydrophilicity, as evidenced by a reduction in water contact angle of up to 45% compared to unmodified polysulfone membranes, and a substantial enhancement in negative surface charge, with zeta potential values increasing by more than 100% at neutral pH in membranes containing 7% arabic gum. These surface modifications are directly correlated with a significant reduction in bacterial adhesion across multiple clinically and environmentally relevant strains, including Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa, with the most pronounced inhibition observed at arabic gum loadings of 3% and 7% by weight. Importantly, the antifouling mechanism is not attributable to bactericidal activity, as scanning electron microscopy reveals intact, morphologically unaltered bacterial cells on the membrane surface, indicating that the primary mode of action is anti-adhesive rather than antimicrobial. The enhanced hydrophilicity promotes the formation of a tightly bound hydration layer at the membrane-water interface, which acts as a physical and energetic barrier to bacterial attachment, while the increased negative surface charge generates strong electrostatic repulsion against the negatively charged cell walls of both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. Concurrently, the incorporation of arabic gum induces structural modifications within the membrane matrix, including increased total porosity, enlarged average pore size, and the formation of interconnected macrovoids, which collectively enhance water permeability by over 100% relative to unmodified polysulfone membranes. This dual functionality—simultaneous improvement in flux and fouling resistance—represents a paradigm shift in membrane design, as traditional approaches typically involve trade-offs between permeability and fouling resistance. The membranes are fabricated using standard industrial phase inversion techniques, require no post-treatment, and exhibit no leaching of arabic gum under prolonged aqueous exposure, ensuring long-term stability and compliance with regulatory standards for potable and industrial water systems. The invention further encompasses a range of membrane configurations, including microfiltration, ultrafiltration, and nanofiltration membranes, each tailored to specific applications in desalination, wastewater reuse, pharmaceutical processing, and food and beverage production. The invention provides a durable, cost-effective, and environmentally benign solution to biofouling that eliminates the need for chemical biocides, reduces energy consumption, extends membrane service life, and enhances the safety and reliability of membrane-based water treatment technologies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a polysulfone-based composite membrane that exhibits superior resistance to biofouling through the synergistic integration of arabic gum as a multifunctional modifier within the polymeric matrix. The membrane is fabricated using a phase inversion process, a well-established industrial technique for producing asymmetric porous membranes, wherein a homogeneous dope solution is cast onto a flat substrate and subsequently immersed in a non-solvent bath to induce phase separation and solidification. The novelty of this invention lies not in the fabrication method itself, which remains consistent with conventional practices, but in the precise selection, concentration, and interaction of arabic gum within the polysulfone matrix to achieve unprecedented and simultaneous improvements in hydrophilicity, surface charge, porosity, and antibiofouling performance. The arabic gum employed in this invention is a naturally derived, water-soluble polysaccharide obtained from the exudate of Acacia senegal and Acacia seyal trees, composed of a complex mixture of arabinose, galactose, rhamnose, glucuronic acid, and 4-O-methylglucuronic acid residues linked through glycosidic bonds. Its amphiphilic character, stemming from the presence of both hydrophilic hydroxyl groups and negatively charged carboxylic acid moieties, enables it to interact favorably with both the polysulfone polymer and the aqueous coagulation medium during membrane formation. This dual affinity facilitates its preferential migration toward the membrane-water interface during phase inversion, resulting in a surface-enriched distribution that maximizes its antifouling efficacy without compromising the structural integrity of the bulk membrane. The concentration of arabic gum in the casting solution is a critical parameter, with optimal performance observed at loadings between 3% and 7% by weight relative to polysulfone. At concentrations below 0.1%, the modification is insufficient to impart measurable changes in surface properties or fouling resistance. At concentrations above 7%, the increased viscosity of the dope solution impedes solvent-nonsolvent exchange kinetics, leading to denser surface layers, reduced porosity, and diminished flux, thereby negating the benefits of the modification. The invention encompasses a range of membrane compositions, each defined by a specific arabic gum loading, with the most preferred embodiments being those containing 3% and 7% arabic gum, as these formulations consistently demonstrate the highest degree of bacterial adhesion inhibition across all tested microbial strains.

The fabrication process begins with the dissolution of polysulfone in N,N-dimethylacetamide (DMA), a polar aprotic solvent with high solubility for polysulfone and compatibility with arabic gum. The polysulfone used in this invention has a molecular weight of approximately 35 kDa, selected for its balance of mechanical strength, thermal stability, and processability. The concentration of polysulfone in the solvent is maintained at 16% by weight to ensure sufficient viscosity for casting while allowing for adequate solvent-nonsolvent exchange during phase inversion. Arabic gum, with an approximate molecular weight of 250 kDa, is then introduced into the solution at predetermined weight percentages—specifically 0.1%, 1%, 3%, 5%, and 7%—relative to the polysulfone. The resulting mixture is subjected to ultrasonic agitation for a period of one hour using a probe sonicator operating at controlled amplitude and frequency to ensure complete dispersion of the arabic gum and prevent agglomeration. This step is essential, as any residual clustering of arabic gum particles would lead to heterogeneous surface properties and localized regions of reduced antifouling performance. Following sonication, the solution is degassed under vacuum for two hours to eliminate entrapped air bubbles, which, if not removed, would result in defects such as pinholes or voids in the final membrane structure. The degassed solution is then cast onto a clean glass substrate using a precision knife-gap casting system, with a knife gap height of 200 micrometers and a casting speed of 3 meters per minute. The casting is performed at ambient temperature to avoid thermal gradients that could induce premature phase separation. Immediately after casting, the substrate is immersed in a coagulation bath composed of deionized water, which acts as a non-solvent for both polysulfone and DMA. The rapid diffusion of DMA into the water bath and the concomitant ingress of water into the polymer solution trigger a non-solvent-induced phase separation, causing the polysulfone to precipitate and form a solid, porous structure. The presence of arabic gum accelerates this demixing process due to its hydrophilic nature, which increases the thermodynamic instability of the casting solution and promotes faster solvent extraction. This results in the formation of a more open, interconnected pore architecture, as evidenced by scanning electron microscopy, with finger-like macrovoids extending from the dense surface layer into the substructure. The newly formed membrane is then rinsed thoroughly with deionized water to remove residual solvent and stored in water until further characterization or use.

The resulting composite membranes exhibit a suite of physical and chemical properties that collectively confer exceptional antifouling characteristics. Scanning electron microscopy reveals a distinct morphological evolution with increasing arabic gum loading. The surface of the unmodified polysulfone membrane is relatively smooth and featureless, with minimal visible porosity. In contrast, membranes containing arabic gum display a marked increase in surface porosity, with the density and size of surface pores increasing progressively with arabic gum concentration up to 7%. The cross-sectional images further confirm the development of larger and more numerous macrovoids in the substructure, indicating enhanced phase separation kinetics. This structural modification is directly correlated with an increase in total membrane porosity, which rises by 24% at 3% arabic gum loading compared to the unmodified membrane, and remains elevated at 5% and 7% loadings despite a slight decline at the highest concentration due to increased solution viscosity. The average pore size, calculated using the Guerout-Elford-Ferry equation, increases from 24 nanometers in the unmodified membrane to 39 nanometers at 3% arabic gum, before decreasing slightly to 28 nanometers at 7% loading, consistent with the observed porosity trend. This pore architecture is not merely a structural byproduct but a functional advantage, as the enlarged and interconnected pores facilitate higher water transport rates while minimizing internal concentration polarization. The water flux of the membranes, measured under standardized dead-end filtration conditions at 1 bar pressure, increases by over 100% in the 3% arabic gum membrane compared to the unmodified control, demonstrating that the modification enhances permeability without sacrificing structural integrity. This is a critical advancement, as most antifouling strategies that improve surface hydrophilicity do so at the expense of flux, either by forming dense surface layers or by clogging pores with additives. The present invention overcomes this limitation by leveraging arabic gum’s dual role as both a surface modifier and a pore-forming agent.

The hydrophilicity of the membrane surface is quantified through contact angle measurements, which provide a direct indication of wettability and, by extension, resistance to hydrophobic interactions that drive microbial adhesion. The water contact angle of the unmodified polysulfone membrane is approximately 82 degrees, indicative of a moderately hydrophobic surface. With the addition of arabic gum, this angle decreases progressively, reaching a minimum of 45 degrees at the highest loading of 7% arabic gum—a reduction of 45% relative to the control. This dramatic increase in hydrophilicity is attributed to the preferential orientation of arabic gum’s hydroxyl and carboxyl groups toward the membrane-water interface during phase inversion, where they form a dense, hydrated layer that resists the adsorption of hydrophobic biomolecules and microbial cells. The hydration layer functions as a physical barrier, preventing direct contact between bacterial cells and the underlying polymer matrix. This mechanism is fundamentally different from antimicrobial coatings that kill or inhibit microbial growth; instead, it operates by denying the initial attachment event, which is the critical first step in biofilm formation. The formation of this hydration layer is further stabilized by the presence of hydrogen bonding between water molecules and the abundant hydroxyl groups of arabic gum, creating a dynamic, energy-intensive interface that bacterial cells cannot easily penetrate or displace.

In parallel with enhanced hydrophilicity, the membrane surface exhibits a profound increase in negative surface charge, as measured by zeta potential analysis across a pH range of 2 to 12. The unmodified polysulfone membrane displays a modest negative zeta potential of approximately -15 mV at pH 7, primarily due to the presence of trace acidic groups in the polymer backbone. In contrast, the membrane containing 7% arabic gum exhibits a zeta potential of -30.8 mV at the same pH—an increase of over 100%. This substantial enhancement is directly attributable to the ionization of carboxylic acid groups present in the glucuronic acid and 4-O-methylglucuronic acid residues of arabic gum, which dissociate over a broad pH range, particularly above pH 2. The resulting negative surface charge generates strong electrostatic repulsion against bacterial cells, which themselves possess net negative surface charges due to the presence of teichoic acids in Gram-positive species and lipopolysaccharides and phospholipids in Gram-negative species. This electrostatic repulsion acts synergistically with the hydration layer to prevent bacterial adhesion. The magnitude of the repulsive force is sufficient to overcome the weak van der Waals and hydrophobic attraction forces that typically facilitate initial bacterial attachment. Importantly, this electrostatic effect is not limited to a single bacterial strain but is effective across a diverse panel of microorganisms, including both Gram-positive and Gram-negative species, demonstrating the broad-spectrum nature of the antifouling mechanism. The zeta potential measurements also reveal that the surface charge remains stable over time and under varying pH conditions, indicating that the arabic gum is firmly integrated into the membrane matrix and does not leach out during operation. This durability is a key advantage over surface-coated membranes, which often lose their antifouling properties after repeated cleaning cycles or prolonged exposure to aqueous environments.

The antifouling performance of the membranes was evaluated through direct microbial adhesion assays using four clinically and environmentally significant bacterial strains: Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. These strains represent a comprehensive spectrum of biofouling agents, with S. aureus serving as a model for Gram-positive pathogens commonly found in healthcare and food processing environments, and E. coli, K. pneumoniae, and P. aeruginosa representing Gram-negative species prevalent in wastewater, drinking water systems, and industrial effluents. The membranes were incubated with bacterial suspensions adjusted to a concentration of 2.4 × 10⁷ colony-forming units per milliliter for 10 minutes under controlled conditions, followed by fixation and imaging via field emission scanning electron microscopy. The results revealed a stark contrast between the unmodified polysulfone membrane and the arabic gum-modified variants. The control membrane was densely colonized by bacterial cells, with evidence of cell aggregation and early biofilm formation. In contrast, the membranes containing 3% and 7% arabic gum exhibited near-complete absence of adhered bacteria, with only isolated, sporadic colonies observed. Notably, even at the lowest arabic gum loading of 0.1%, a measurable reduction in bacterial adhesion was detected, confirming that even trace amounts of arabic gum exert a beneficial effect. The most dramatic inhibition was observed at 3% and 7% loadings, where bacterial colonization was reduced by more than 90% compared to the control. Importantly, the morphology of the bacterial cells observed on the arabic gum-modified membranes was intact and unaltered, with spherical cocci clearly visible for S. aureus and rod-shaped forms for the Gram-negative species. This observation is critical, as it rules out any bactericidal or lytic mechanism of action. If the arabic gum were acting as a biocide, the cells would exhibit signs of lysis, membrane disruption, or cytoplasmic leakage—none of which were observed. Instead, the cells appeared viable and undamaged, suggesting that they were simply unable to adhere to the surface. This conclusion is further supported by experiments in which membranes were incubated with E. coli for an extended period of 24 hours. While the unmodified membrane became densely colonized after overnight incubation, the arabic gum-modified membranes showed no significant increase in bacterial adhesion compared to the 10-minute incubation, indicating that the antifouling effect is persistent and not merely a transient delay in attachment. This resistance to prolonged exposure is a hallmark of true antifouling performance, as opposed to delayed fouling, which merely postpones the inevitable.

The mechanism underlying this exceptional antifouling performance is therefore a synergistic combination of three interrelated phenomena: enhanced hydrophilicity, increased negative surface charge, and optimized membrane porosity. The hydrophilic surface promotes the formation of a tightly bound hydration layer that acts as a physical barrier to bacterial adhesion. The negative surface charge generates electrostatic repulsion that actively repels negatively charged bacterial cells. The porous, macrovoid-rich structure enhances water flux, reducing the likelihood of concentration polarization and minimizing the residence time of nutrients and cells near the membrane surface. Together, these factors create a surface environment that is fundamentally inhospitable to microbial colonization. This is in stark contrast to conventional antifouling strategies that rely on the release of antimicrobial agents such as silver nanoparticles, quaternary ammonium compounds, or halogenated biocides, which are subject to leaching, environmental toxicity, regulatory restrictions, and the eventual development of microbial resistance. The arabic gum in this invention is not released; it is chemically and physically entrapped within the polysulfone matrix during phase inversion, forming a permanent, non-leaching modification. This ensures long-term stability, eliminates the risk of contaminating treated water with toxic compounds, and satisfies stringent regulatory requirements for potable water applications. Furthermore, arabic gum is a naturally derived, biodegradable, and non-toxic substance, making the membranes environmentally sustainable and compatible with green manufacturing principles.

The invention further encompasses the scalability and industrial applicability of the membranes. The fabrication process requires no specialized equipment beyond standard membrane casting systems, ultrasonic mixers, and coagulation baths—all of which are routinely employed in commercial membrane manufacturing facilities. The arabic gum is readily available in bulk quantities at low cost, and its incorporation into the casting solution adds negligible expense to the overall production process. The membranes can be fabricated in any geometry, including flat sheets, hollow fibers, or spiral-wound modules, and are compatible with existing membrane module designs used in reverse osmosis, ultrafiltration, and microfiltration systems. The membranes exhibit excellent mechanical strength, thermal stability, and chemical resistance, with no degradation observed after prolonged exposure to common cleaning agents such as sodium hypochlorite, citric acid, or sodium hydroxide. This durability ensures that the antifouling properties are maintained even after repeated cleaning cycles, unlike surface-coated membranes that lose their functionality after the first cleaning. The invention thus provides a commercially viable, scalable, and sustainable solution to biofouling that can be implemented immediately across a wide range of water treatment applications, from municipal drinking water plants to industrial wastewater reclamation systems.

The invention also extends to the use of these membranes in novel applications beyond conventional water treatment. For example, in pharmaceutical manufacturing, where membrane filtration is used for sterile filtration of biologics and parenteral solutions, the reduction in biofouling minimizes the risk of microbial contamination and product loss. In food and beverage processing, where membrane systems are used for juice clarification, dairy ultrafiltration, and beer stabilization, the antifouling properties reduce downtime and cleaning frequency, improving operational efficiency. In desalination plants, where biofouling is a primary cause of performance decline in reverse osmosis membranes, the invention offers a pathway to significantly extend membrane life and reduce energy consumption. In membrane bioreactors used for wastewater treatment, the membranes can reduce the need for chemical dosing and improve sludge settleability by minimizing biofilm accumulation on the membrane surface. The invention further enables the development of point-of-use water filters for residential and emergency applications, where the absence of chemical additives ensures safe, reliable, and maintenance-free operation. The membranes may also be integrated into hybrid systems combining ultrafiltration with advanced oxidation processes, where their resistance to fouling allows for more efficient operation under variable feedwater conditions.

The invention is further distinguished by its comprehensive characterization and validation across multiple metrics. Unlike prior studies that rely on a single bacterial strain or a single performance indicator, this invention provides a holistic evaluation of membrane performance, including morphological analysis via scanning electron microscopy, quantitative assessment of porosity and pore size, measurement of hydrophilicity through contact angle goniometry, determination of surface charge via zeta potential analysis, and direct microbial adhesion testing across four distinct bacterial species. This multi-faceted approach ensures that the antifouling effect is not an artifact of a single measurement technique but a robust, reproducible, and mechanistically understood phenomenon. The consistency of results across all analytical methods confirms that the arabic gum modification induces a fundamental change in the surface properties of the membrane that is both physically and biologically significant. The invention thus represents not merely an incremental improvement in membrane technology but a transformative advancement that redefines the paradigm for biofouling resistance.

In summary, the present invention provides a polysulfone-based composite membrane that achieves unprecedented resistance to biofouling through the synergistic incorporation of arabic gum. The membrane exhibits enhanced hydrophilicity, increased negative surface charge, and optimized porosity, which collectively prevent bacterial adhesion without the use of leachable biocides or external energy inputs. The fabrication process is industrially scalable, the materials are cost-effective and environmentally benign, and the performance is durable and broad-spectrum. The invention eliminates the need for chemical disinfection, reduces energy consumption, extends membrane service life, and improves the safety and reliability of membrane-based water treatment systems. It represents a significant leap forward in the field of membrane science and offers a practical, sustainable, and commercially viable solution to one of the most persistent challenges in industrial water processing.
