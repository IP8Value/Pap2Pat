# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccine development, specifically to synthetic glycomimetic antigens designed for use in conjugate vaccines against pathogenic bacteria, with particular application to Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A. The invention provides novel, chemically synthesized carbohydrate-based analogs of the native capsular polysaccharide of Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A, wherein the natural ring oxygen atom within the pyranose ring of the repeating unit is replaced by a methylene group, thereby generating a non-hydrolyzable carba-sugar scaffold. These synthetic analogs are further modified through controlled acetylation at specific hydroxyl positions to mimic the natural acetylation pattern observed on the native polysaccharide. The resulting glycomimetics are covalently conjugated to a carrier protein, such as CRM197, to generate immunogenic glycoconjugates capable of eliciting high-titer, functional, and long-lasting antibody responses in mammals, including humans. The invention further encompasses methods for the chemical synthesis of these stabilized oligomeric glycomimetics, their conjugation to carrier proteins, and their use in prophylactic vaccination regimens to prevent invasive meningococcal disease caused by Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A. The disclosed compositions and methods represent a significant advancement over conventional polysaccharide-based conjugate vaccines by offering superior chemical stability, batch-to-batch consistency, and the ability to precisely tailor structural features to optimize immunogenicity and functional antibody induction, thereby overcoming the inherent limitations of naturally derived polysaccharides, including their susceptibility to hydrolytic degradation, structural heterogeneity, and poor immunogenicity in vulnerable populations such as infants and the elderly.

## BACKGROUND ART

Vaccination remains one of the most effective public health interventions for the prevention of infectious diseases, with conjugate vaccines representing a cornerstone in the protection against bacterial pathogens that express polysaccharide capsules. Historically, vaccines composed of purified capsular polysaccharides from encapsulated bacteria such as Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae type b, and Neisseria meningitidis have demonstrated efficacy in adult populations. However, these polysaccharide antigens are inherently T-cell independent, meaning they fail to engage the adaptive immune system in a manner that generates immunological memory, class switching to IgG, or booster responses upon re-exposure. This limitation renders them ineffective in infants and young children, who constitute the most vulnerable populations for many invasive bacterial infections. To overcome this, the field developed glycoconjugate vaccines, in which the polysaccharide is covalently linked to a protein carrier, such as tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, or the non-toxic mutant CRM197. This conjugation converts the immune response to a T-cell dependent mechanism, enabling robust IgG production, memory B cell formation, and long-term protection. These conjugate vaccines have been instrumental in drastically reducing the incidence of meningococcal disease in many parts of the world.

Despite their success, conventional glycoconjugate vaccines face significant challenges related to the physicochemical properties of the polysaccharide component. The capsular polysaccharide of Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A (MenA CPS) consists of a linear polymer of (1→6)-linked 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-α-D-mannopyranosyl phosphate repeating units. This structure is characterized by a phosphodiester backbone and variable O-acetylation at the C3 and C4 hydroxyl positions, with acetylation levels reported to range between 50–60% at C3 and 25–30% at C4. The phosphodiester linkage, particularly in the context of the axial orientation of the C2 acetamido group, renders the polysaccharide highly susceptible to hydrolytic cleavage under physiological conditions, including elevated temperatures and neutral to slightly acidic pH environments. This intrinsic instability leads to chain degradation during storage, transportation, and even after administration, resulting in loss of antigenic integrity, reduced immunogenicity, and unpredictable vaccine performance. To mitigate this, manufacturers have relied on lyophilization and strict cold-chain maintenance, which are costly, logistically complex, and often infeasible in low-resource settings where meningococcal epidemics are most prevalent.

Attempts to synthesize MenA CPS fragments using traditional organic chemistry have yielded short oligosaccharides that can bind to anti-MenA antibodies but fail to elicit robust or functional immune responses in vivo. These synthetic fragments, while structurally similar to the native polysaccharide, do not fully recapitulate the conformational and epitopic features required for high-affinity antibody recognition, particularly when acetylation is absent or improperly positioned. Moreover, the chemical synthesis of longer oligomers has been hampered by the difficulty of forming stable phosphodiester linkages with high efficiency and purity, especially when the molecule contains multiple hydroxyl groups requiring orthogonal protection strategies. Previous efforts to stabilize the phosphodiester linkage by replacing the anomeric oxygen with a methylene group to generate C-phosphono analogs have shown promise in improving hydrolytic resistance, but these analogs retain the natural pyranose ring oxygen and thus do not address the conformational instability arising from the ring structure itself. Other approaches have focused on enzymatic synthesis or semi-synthetic derivatization of natural polysaccharides, but these methods suffer from batch variability, contamination risks, and limited scalability.

The need for a synthetic, stable, and immunologically faithful mimic of the MenA CPS has therefore remained unmet. While some studies have explored the use of synthetic oligosaccharides as antigens, none have successfully combined the dual advantages of structural stabilization through ring modification and precise control over acetylation patterns to achieve immunogenicity equivalent to that of the natural polysaccharide conjugate. Furthermore, the role of acetylation in determining the functional quality of the antibody response—particularly its capacity to mediate complement-dependent bactericidal activity—has not been systematically exploited in synthetic vaccine design. Conventional conjugate vaccines based on MenA CPS have demonstrated efficacy but are limited by their instability, leading to suboptimal shelf life and inconsistent immunogenicity. The development of a synthetic glycomimetic antigen that overcomes these limitations would represent a transformative advance in vaccine technology, enabling the creation of a truly stable, scalable, and universally effective meningococcal vaccine that does not rely on the cold chain and can be deployed globally without compromise.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of synthetic glycomimetic antigens for use in conjugate vaccines against Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A, comprising oligomeric structures that mimic the native capsular polysaccharide but are chemically stabilized through the replacement of the ring oxygen atom in the pyranose moiety with a methylene group, thereby forming a carba-sugar scaffold. These synthetic antigens are composed of repeating units of 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-α-D-manno-pyranose analogs linked by phosphodiester bonds, wherein the pyranose ring lacks the oxygen atom at the 5-position and instead contains a methylene group, rendering the entire structure resistant to hydrolytic cleavage at the anomeric center. The oligomers are synthesized to a defined degree of polymerization ranging from four to eight repeating units, with the octamer (eight repeating units) identified as the optimal length for eliciting high-affinity antibody binding and functional immune responses. Each oligomer is equipped with a chemoselective linker at the reducing end, enabling controlled and reproducible conjugation to a carrier protein such as CRM197, tetanus toxoid, or diphtheria toxoid. The invention further encompasses the critical discovery that the immunogenic potency of these carbaMenA oligomers is dramatically enhanced by the introduction of acetyl esters at the C3 and/or C4 hydroxyl positions of the sugar rings, with a total acetylation level of approximately 50–75% mimicking the natural distribution observed in the wild-type polysaccharide. The acetylated analogs, termed Ac-carbaMenA, exhibit binding affinity to protective monoclonal antibodies that is nearly indistinguishable from that of the native MenA CPS, and when conjugated to CRM197, elicit serum bactericidal antibody titers in mice that are statistically non-inferior to those induced by the benchmark MenA CPS-CRM197 conjugate. This represents the first demonstration that a fully synthetic, non-natural carbohydrate structure, when engineered with precise structural and chemical modifications, can functionally replicate the immunological properties of a complex natural polysaccharide antigen, thereby overcoming the historical paradigm that synthetic antigens must be exact structural copies to be effective.

The invention further provides a method for the chemical synthesis of these glycomimetic antigens using phosphoramidite-based solid-phase oligomerization, a technique adapted from nucleic acid chemistry and optimized for the unique steric and electronic demands of carba-sugar building blocks. The synthesis begins with a suitably protected carba-mannose monomer, which is sequentially elongated through iterative cycles of phosphoramidite coupling, oxidation, and deprotection, using dimethoxytrityl (DMTr) as a temporary protecting group for the primary hydroxyl and cyanoethyl groups for phosphite protection. The use of dicyanoimidazole as a coupling activator and (1S)-(+)-(10-camphorsulfonyl)-oxaziridine as an oxidizing agent enables high-yielding, high-fidelity chain elongation while preserving acid-labile protecting groups. The resulting oligomers are deprotected under mild conditions using aqueous ammonia followed by catalytic hydrogenolysis to remove benzyl and carboxybenzyl carbamate groups, yielding the fully deprotected carbaMenA oligomers with a free primary hydroxyl for linker attachment. The linker, typically a di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate spacer, is introduced via amine-reactive chemistry to enable stable amide bond formation with lysine residues on the carrier protein. The invention further discloses a novel method for the site-selective and controlled acetylation of the carbaMenA oligomer at the C3 and C4 hydroxyl positions using acetic anhydride and imidazole under carefully optimized conditions, with the acetylation level quantified and confirmed by nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy to be within the range of 50–75%, with a significant proportion of disubstituted (C3/C4) acetylation events occurring. This acetylation step is performed prior to conjugation to ensure homogeneity and reproducibility, and the resulting Ac-carbaMenA DP8 conjugate is shown to induce IgG antibodies capable of recognizing the native MenA CPS and mediating complement-dependent bacterial killing in vitro, with titers matching those of the licensed MenA vaccine. The invention thus provides a complete, scalable, and chemically defined platform for the production of a next-generation meningococcal conjugate vaccine that is stable at ambient temperatures, free from biological contaminants, and capable of eliciting protective immunity in all age groups, including infants and immunocompromised individuals who respond poorly to conventional polysaccharide-based vaccines.

### Particular Embodiments of the Invention

In a first particular embodiment, the invention provides a synthetic glycomimetic antigen for use in a vaccine against Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A, comprising an oligomer of eight repeating units of a carba-mannose phosphate backbone, wherein each repeating unit is represented by a 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-α-D-manno-pyranose analog in which the ring oxygen atom is replaced by a methylene group, and wherein the phosphodiester linkages between the repeating units are non-hydrolyzable due to the absence of the anomeric oxygen, and wherein the oligomer is functionalized at the reducing end with a chemoselective linker selected from the group consisting of di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate, succinimidyl esters of dicarboxylic acids, and aminoalkyl spacers, and wherein the oligomer is further modified by the presence of acetyl esters at the C3 and/or C4 hydroxyl positions of the sugar rings, with a total acetylation level of 50% to 75% as determined by nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and wherein the acetyl groups are randomly distributed across the oligomer with a preference for monosubstitution at C3 and disubstitution at both C3 and C4.

In a second particular embodiment, the invention provides a glycoconjugate comprising the synthetic glycomimetic antigen of the first embodiment covalently linked to a carrier protein selected from the group consisting of CRM197, tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, outer membrane protein complex, and recombinant protein carriers derived from Corynebacterium diphtheriae or Clostridium tetani, wherein the glycomimetic antigen is conjugated to the carrier protein via an amide bond formed between the chemoselective linker and a lysine residue on the carrier protein, and wherein the molar ratio of glycomimetic antigen to carrier protein ranges from 8:1 to 20:1, and wherein the glycoconjugate is formulated as a sterile, pyrogen-free, aqueous solution with a pH between 6.5 and 7.8, containing a stabilizing agent selected from the group consisting of sucrose, trehalose, sodium chloride, and phosphate buffer, and optionally further comprising an adjuvant selected from the group consisting of aluminum phosphate, aluminum hydroxide, squalene-based emulsions, and toll-like receptor agonists.

In a third particular embodiment, the invention provides a method for producing the synthetic glycomimetic antigen of the first embodiment, comprising the steps of: (a) synthesizing a protected carba-mannose phosphoramidite building block from a commercially available glucal derivative through a sequence of deprotection, regioselective dimethoxytritylation, and phosphoramidite installation; (b) assembling the oligomer by iterative cycles of phosphoramidite coupling using dicyanoimidazole as an activator, oxidation with (1S)-(+)-(10-camphorsulfonyl)-oxaziridine, and detritylation with trichloroacetic acid in dichloromethane; (c) cleaving the oligomer from the solid support and removing the cyanoethyl protecting groups using aqueous ammonia; (d) performing catalytic hydrogenolysis over palladium black in the presence of acetic acid to remove benzyl and carboxybenzyl carbamate protecting groups; (e) purifying the resulting deprotected carbaMenA oligomer by size-exclusion chromatography; (f) introducing a chemoselective linker by reacting the primary hydroxyl group of the oligomer with di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate in dimethyl sulfoxide in the presence of triethylamine; (g) purifying the activated oligomer by precipitation with ethyl acetate; and (h) acetylating the oligomer by treatment with acetic anhydride and imidazole in anhydrous pyridine at 40°C for a duration sufficient to achieve a total acetylation level of 50–75%, as confirmed by ¹H and ¹³C NMR spectroscopy.

In a fourth particular embodiment, the invention provides a method for producing the glycoconjugate of the second embodiment, comprising the steps of: (a) dissolving the acetylated, linker-functionalized glycomimetic antigen in dimethyl sulfoxide; (b) mixing the activated glycomimetic with CRM197 at a molar ratio of 10:1 to 15:1 active ester to protein in a 50 mM sodium phosphate buffer at pH 7.2; (c) incubating the mixture at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours with gentle agitation; (d) purifying the resulting glycoconjugate by tangential flow filtration using a 30 kDa molecular weight cutoff membrane; (e) quantifying the saccharide-to-protein ratio by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry; (f) formulating the purified glycoconjugate in a sterile, isotonic buffer containing a stabilizing agent and, optionally, an adjuvant; and (g) sterilizing the final formulation by filtration through a 0.22 μm membrane.

In a fifth particular embodiment, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising the glycoconjugate of the second embodiment in a therapeutically effective amount, formulated for parenteral administration, wherein the composition is stable for at least 12 months when stored at temperatures between 2°C and 40°C, and wherein the composition induces a serum bactericidal antibody titer of at least 1:8 in 90% of vaccinated individuals after two doses, and wherein the composition elicits a geometric mean IgG titer against native MenA CPS of at least 1:10,000 in mice after three immunizations.

In a sixth particular embodiment, the invention provides a method for inducing an immune response against Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A in a mammal, comprising administering to the mammal a therapeutically effective amount of the glycoconjugate of the second embodiment, wherein the mammal is selected from the group consisting of a human infant, a child, an adolescent, an adult, an elderly individual, and an immunocompromised patient, and wherein the administration is performed via subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intradermal injection, and wherein the mammal receives two to four doses administered at intervals of two to six weeks, and wherein the immune response is characterized by the production of IgG antibodies that bind to the native MenA capsular polysaccharide and mediate complement-dependent bactericidal activity against live Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A bacteria in vitro.

In a seventh particular embodiment, the invention provides a kit for vaccination against Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A, comprising a vial containing the glycoconjugate of the second embodiment in a lyophilized or liquid formulation, and a separate vial containing a diluent for reconstitution, wherein the glycoconjugate is stable in the liquid formulation for at least 12 months at 25°C, and wherein the kit further comprises instructions for administration, including dosage, route, and schedule, and wherein the kit is labeled with a statement indicating that the vaccine does not require refrigeration for storage or transport.

In an eighth particular embodiment, the invention provides a method for evaluating the immunogenicity of a synthetic glycomimetic antigen, comprising: (a) conjugating the antigen to CRM197; (b) immunizing a group of BALB/c mice with the glycoconjugate; (c) collecting sera from the mice after the second and third immunizations; (d) measuring the IgG antibody titer against native MenA CPS by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay; (e) measuring the IgG antibody titer against de-O-acetylated MenA CPS; (f) determining the serum bactericidal activity using human or rabbit complement and a live Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A strain; and (g) comparing the bactericidal titer to that induced by a licensed MenA CPS-CRM197 conjugate, wherein a glycomimetic antigen is deemed immunogenic if the bactericidal titer is within a two-fold range of the benchmark conjugate.

In a ninth particular embodiment, the invention provides a synthetic glycomimetic antigen wherein the degree of polymerization of the carbaMenA oligomer is between four and eight repeating units, with the octamer being the preferred embodiment, wherein the phosphodiester linkages are non-hydrolyzable, wherein the acetylation pattern is randomized but maintains a total acetylation level of 50–75%, wherein the linker is di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate, and wherein the glycomimetic antigen is capable of inhibiting the binding of a bactericidal monoclonal antibody JW-A1 to native MenA CPS with an IC50 value less than 1 × 10⁻⁵ M, as determined by competitive surface plasmon resonance.

In a tenth particular embodiment, the invention provides a synthetic glycomimetic antigen wherein the acetyl groups are distributed such that at least 30% of the sugar residues are acetylated at both C3 and C4 hydroxyl positions, at least 40% are acetylated at C3 only, and less than 20% are unacetylated, as determined by quantitative ¹H NMR analysis, and wherein the glycomimetic antigen exhibits a conformational profile in solution that is substantially similar to that of the native MenA CPS, as determined by molecular dynamics simulations and circular dichroism spectroscopy.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The invention is carried out through a series of integrated chemical, biochemical, and immunological processes that enable the design, synthesis, characterization, and application of a novel class of synthetic glycomimetic antigens for use in conjugate vaccines against Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A. The mode of carrying out the invention begins with the preparation of the fundamental building block, a protected carba-mannose monomer derived from a commercially available glucal precursor. This monomer is subjected to a sequence of chemical transformations to install the necessary functional groups for oligomerization, including the removal of acetyl and silyl protecting groups, regioselective introduction of a dimethoxytrityl ether at the primary hydroxyl, and conversion to a phosphoramidite derivative. The phosphoramidite is then employed in a solid-phase or solution-phase iterative coupling strategy, wherein each elongation cycle involves the activation of the phosphoramidite with dicyanoimidazole, coupling to the growing chain, oxidation with (1S)-(+)-(10-camphorsulfonyl)-oxaziridine to form a stable phosphotriester linkage, and removal of the dimethoxytrityl group with trichloroacetic acid. This process is repeated for each successive monomer unit, with the amount of phosphoramidite and coupling time adjusted to ensure complete conversion, particularly for longer oligomers. The use of dicyanoimidazole, rather than conventional tetrazole, is critical to preserve the acid-labile dimethoxytrityl group throughout the synthesis, while the choice of oxaziridine as the oxidant ensures high solubility in acetonitrile and minimizes side reactions. The resulting protected oligomers are purified by size-exclusion chromatography after each cycle to ensure homogeneity and remove failure sequences.

Once the desired degree of polymerization is achieved, the oligomer is subjected to global deprotection. First, the cyanoethyl groups on the phosphate backbone are cleaved using aqueous ammonia, followed by catalytic hydrogenolysis over palladium black in the presence of acetic acid to remove benzyl ethers and carboxybenzyl carbamate groups. The resulting deprotected carbaMenA oligomer is purified by size-exclusion chromatography to isolate the target species, typically the octamer, from shorter or longer fragments. The purified oligomer is then functionalized with a chemoselective linker, di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate, which is reacted with the primary hydroxyl group at the reducing end under mild conditions in dimethyl sulfoxide in the presence of triethylamine. The activated oligomer is precipitated with ethyl acetate and washed to remove excess reagents, yielding a stable, amine-reactive intermediate. This intermediate is then subjected to a controlled acetylation reaction using acetic anhydride and imidazole in anhydrous pyridine at 40°C for approximately nine days, a duration determined empirically to achieve a total acetylation level of 50–75% without over-acetylation or degradation. The extent and pattern of acetylation are rigorously characterized by ¹H and ¹³C nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, which confirm the presence of acetyl groups at C3 and C4, with a significant proportion of disubstituted residues. The acetylated glycomimetic is then conjugated to CRM197 by mixing the activated oligomer with CRM197 in a 10:1 to 15:1 molar ratio of active ester to protein in a 50 mM sodium phosphate buffer at pH 7.2. The reaction is allowed to proceed overnight at room temperature with gentle agitation to ensure complete conjugation without denaturation of the carrier protein. The resulting glycoconjugate is purified by tangential flow filtration using a 30 kDa molecular weight cutoff membrane to remove unreacted oligomer and free protein. The saccharide-to-protein ratio is quantified by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry, and the final product is formulated in a sterile, isotonic buffer containing sucrose or trehalose as a stabilizer, with or without an adjuvant such as aluminum phosphate.

The immunogenicity of the resulting glycoconjugate is evaluated in a murine model, wherein groups of BALB/c mice are immunized subcutaneously with three doses of 2 μg of saccharide administered at two-week intervals. Sera are collected before immunization and after each boost, and antibody titers are measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay using native MenA CPS and de-O-acetylated MenA CPS as coating antigens. The functional quality of the antibody response is assessed by serum bactericidal assay using human or rabbit complement and a live Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A strain. The bactericidal titer is defined as the serum dilution that results in a 50% reduction in colony-forming units after one hour of incubation. The results demonstrate that the Ac-carbaMenA DP8-CRM197 conjugate induces IgG titers against native MenA CPS that are significantly higher than those induced by the nonacetylated analog and comparable to those induced by the licensed MenA CPS-CRM197 conjugate. More importantly, the serum bactericidal titers elicited by the Ac-carbaMenA DP8 conjugate are statistically non-inferior to those of the benchmark vaccine, with human complement-mediated killing reaching titers of 1:128 or higher in pooled sera. This level of functional activity is unprecedented for a synthetic glycomimetic and confirms that the structural modifications introduced—namely, the methylene substitution for the ring oxygen and the controlled acetylation pattern—are sufficient to generate an antigen that is not only stable but also immunologically indistinguishable from the natural polysaccharide.

The stability of the glycomimetic antigen is demonstrated through accelerated aging studies conducted in 5 mM sodium acetate buffer at pH 7 and 37°C. The Ac-carbaMenA DP8 oligomer shows no detectable degradation over a period of 56 days, as monitored by ¹H and ³¹P nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, whereas the native MenA CPS fragment of similar length undergoes significant chain shortening within 28 days, with the de-O-acetylated form showing even greater instability. This confirms that the carba modification confers exceptional resistance to hydrolytic cleavage, independent of acetylation status, and that the acetylated form retains its structural integrity under conditions that would rapidly degrade natural polysaccharides. The ability to store the glycoconjugate in liquid form at ambient temperatures for extended periods eliminates the need for cold-chain logistics, a critical advantage for global vaccine distribution, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, where meningococcal epidemics are most common and infrastructure is most limited.

The invention further encompasses the use of the synthetic glycomimetic antigen in combination with other meningococcal serogroup antigens to form multivalent conjugate vaccines. For example, the Ac-carbaMenA DP8-CRM197 conjugate can be co-formulated with analogous synthetic antigens derived from serogroups C, W, Y, and X, each engineered with the same carba stabilization and controlled acetylation strategy, to produce a single-dose, pentavalent meningococcal vaccine that provides broad protection against all major disease-causing serogroups. The chemical homogeneity and scalability of the synthetic approach make such multivalent formulations feasible and reproducible, unlike traditional polysaccharide-based vaccines, which suffer from batch variability and compatibility issues when combined. The invention also enables the development of pediatric vaccines, as the synthetic antigens are not subject to the age-related immune unresponsiveness that plagues polysaccharide vaccines, and can be administered to infants as young as six weeks of age without the need for adjuvants or booster doses.

The manufacturing process is designed for industrial scale-up, with each step amenable to automation and process optimization. The phosphoramidite chemistry employed is analogous to that used in commercial oligonucleotide synthesis, allowing for the adaptation of existing high-throughput synthesizers. The deprotection and purification steps are scalable using standard chromatographic and filtration technologies. The final glycoconjugate formulation can be produced under current good manufacturing practices, with rigorous quality control measures including endotoxin testing, sterility testing, and potency assays based on ELISA and serum bactericidal activity. The resulting vaccine is not only safer than traditional conjugate vaccines—since it contains no biological material from bacterial cultures—but also more consistent, with each batch exhibiting identical chemical structure, acetylation pattern, and immunological profile. This level of control ensures predictable efficacy across diverse populations and geographies, fulfilling the global health imperative for a truly universal meningococcal vaccine.

The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in carbohydrate vaccine design, moving beyond the notion that synthetic antigens must be exact replicas of natural structures to embrace the concept that intelligently engineered glycomimetics, with tailored modifications for stability and immunogenicity, can outperform their natural counterparts. The Ac-carbaMenA DP8-CRM197 conjugate is not merely a substitute for the native polysaccharide; it is a superior immunogen, offering enhanced stability, reproducibility, and functional potency. Its development opens the door to a new generation of synthetic vaccines against other bacterial pathogens whose polysaccharide antigens are similarly unstable or heterogeneous, including Streptococcus pneumoniae, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Acinetobacter baumannii. The methods and principles disclosed herein provide a blueprint for the rational design of next-generation vaccines that are chemically defined, thermally stable, and universally effective, transforming the landscape of global infectious disease prevention.
