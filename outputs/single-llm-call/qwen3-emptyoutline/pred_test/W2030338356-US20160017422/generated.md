# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for predicting the likelihood of successful pregnancy following assisted reproductive technology (ART), particularly in the context of single embryo transfer (SET) procedures. More specifically, the invention provides a diagnostic method based on the quantitative measurement of gene expression levels in cumulus cells surrounding human oocytes, wherein the expression profile of a defined panel of genes serves as a biomarker signature capable of distinguishing between oocytes with high and low potential for resulting in live birth. The method enables the selection of the most viable oocyte for transfer in a given cycle, thereby improving clinical outcomes while minimizing the risk of multiple gestations. The invention further encompasses predictive models derived from multivariable statistical analysis of gene expression data, optionally integrated with patient-specific cycle parameters, to generate a quantitative probability score for pregnancy success. The invention is applicable in clinical settings involving in vitro fertilization (IVF), intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), and frozen embryo transfer (FET) protocols, and is particularly suited for laboratories seeking to enhance embryo selection beyond traditional morphological grading by incorporating molecular biomarkers derived from the oocyte’s microenvironment.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Assisted reproductive technologies have revolutionized the treatment of infertility, enabling millions of individuals worldwide to achieve parenthood. Among the most significant advances in this field has been the widespread adoption of single embryo transfer (SET) as the standard of care, driven by the imperative to reduce the incidence of multiple pregnancies, which are associated with elevated maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality. While SET has markedly improved perinatal outcomes, it has also intensified the clinical challenge of selecting the single embryo most likely to result in a successful pregnancy. Traditional methods of embryo selection—primarily based on morphological assessment of cell number, symmetry, fragmentation, and blastocyst expansion—remain inherently subjective and limited in predictive accuracy, particularly when multiple embryos of comparable morphology are available. As a consequence, many patients undergo multiple cycles of IVF before achieving a live birth, resulting in increased emotional, physical, and financial burdens.

To address this limitation, researchers have sought to identify molecular biomarkers that reflect oocyte quality and developmental potential. Among the most promising candidates are cumulus cells (CC), which form a protective and communicative layer around the oocyte throughout folliculogenesis and ovulation. These somatic cells are not merely passive supporters of oocyte maturation; they actively participate in bidirectional signaling with the oocyte, regulating metabolic supply, redox balance, hormonal responsiveness, and extracellular matrix remodeling. Consequently, the transcriptomic profile of cumulus cells is believed to mirror the functional state of the enclosed oocyte, making them an ideal, non-invasive source of predictive biomarkers. Unlike the oocyte itself, which cannot be sampled without compromising viability, cumulus cells can be readily collected during standard denudation procedures immediately prior to intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), without affecting subsequent embryo development.

Numerous studies have attempted to correlate gene expression patterns in cumulus cells with various endpoints of reproductive success, including embryo morphology, fertilization rates, blastocyst formation, chromosomal aneuploidy, and, most critically, clinical pregnancy and live birth. However, the field has been plagued by a lack of reproducibility across laboratories. While certain genes, such as hyaluronan synthase 2 (HAS2), have been reported as differentially expressed in association with embryo quality in some cohorts, other studies have failed to replicate these findings, suggesting that observed differences may be confounded by variations in patient populations, ovarian stimulation protocols, culture media formulations, and analytical methodologies. Furthermore, many prior studies have focused on embryo development as the primary endpoint, despite the fact that the ultimate goal of ART is not merely the formation of a morphologically normal embryo, but rather the establishment of a viable, ongoing pregnancy leading to a healthy live birth. This disconnect between intermediate phenotypes and clinically relevant outcomes has hindered the translation of gene expression signatures into routine clinical practice.

Moreover, the majority of published studies have employed inter-patient analyses, comparing cumulus cells from women who achieved pregnancy with those who did not, without accounting for intra-patient variability. This approach inherently overlooks the critical fact that, in a single ovarian stimulation cycle, multiple oocytes are retrieved, each surrounded by its own cumulus complex, and each has the potential to yield an embryo that may be transferred either in a fresh or frozen cycle. The existence of sibling oocytes within the same patient—some yielding a pregnancy and others failing to do so—provides a powerful internal control that eliminates confounding variables such as age, ovarian reserve, hormonal milieu, and endometrial receptivity, which vary substantially between patients. To date, no study has systematically exploited this intra-patient comparative design to validate the predictive capacity of cumulus cell gene expression signatures in a setting that mirrors real-world clinical decision-making.

Additionally, while some prior studies have proposed multivariable models incorporating both gene expression and clinical parameters, these models have typically been derived from small, homogenous cohorts and have not been rigorously validated in independent datasets. Furthermore, the statistical approaches employed have often lacked robustness, with insufficient correction for multiple hypothesis testing, inadequate handling of non-normal data distributions, and failure to account for potential collinearity among variables. As a result, many proposed gene signatures have not demonstrated sufficient predictive power, sensitivity, or specificity to warrant clinical adoption.

There remains, therefore, a substantial unmet need in the field of reproductive medicine for a reliable, objective, and reproducible molecular method capable of predicting the pregnancy potential of individual oocytes prior to embryo transfer. Such a method must be grounded in a well-defined panel of genes whose expression is consistently associated with live birth outcomes across diverse patient populations, must be validated using a rigorous intra-patient comparative design, and must be translatable into a clinically feasible diagnostic assay that can be implemented in routine IVF laboratories without requiring extensive infrastructure or prolonged turnaround times. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing a novel, statistically validated, gene expression-based predictive model for pregnancy outcome in single embryo transfer cycles, derived from a comprehensive analysis of cumulus cell transcriptomes and validated through both inter-patient and intra-patient comparisons.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for predicting the likelihood of pregnancy following the transfer of a single embryo derived from a human oocyte, wherein the method comprises the steps of: (a) isolating cumulus cells from the oocyte prior to or during denudation; (b) extracting total RNA from the isolated cumulus cells; (c) quantifying the expression levels of a defined panel of genes comprising EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3; (d) calculating a composite score based on the relative expression levels of these genes, optionally adjusted for patient-specific cycle parameters; and (e) determining the probability of pregnancy based on the composite score, wherein a higher score correlates with a higher likelihood of live birth. The invention further provides a diagnostic kit for performing the method, comprising reagents for RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) of the specified gene panel, along with a reference database of expression thresholds and predictive algorithms for interpreting the results.

The invention is based on the discovery that the combined expression profile of five specific genes—ephrin-B2 (EFNB2), glutathione S-transferase alpha 3 (GSTA3), glutathione S-transferase alpha 4 (GSTA4), glutathione peroxidase 3 (GPX3), and progesterone receptor (PGR)—in cumulus cells is strongly and reproducibly associated with the potential of an oocyte to result in a live birth following single embryo transfer. This gene panel was identified through a systematic, multi-stage analysis of cumulus cell transcriptomes from 47 patients undergoing ICSI with single embryo transfer, utilizing both inter-patient and intra-patient comparative approaches. The predictive power of the gene panel was validated in an independent cohort and demonstrated superior accuracy, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve compared to previously reported gene signatures.

Crucially, the invention demonstrates that the expression levels of these five genes are sufficient to predict pregnancy outcome with high fidelity even in the absence of clinical covariates, and that the addition of patient-specific parameters such as age, duration of ovarian stimulation, and relative estradiol levels further refines the predictive accuracy to a level suitable for clinical decision-making. The invention further establishes that the same gene panel can be used to rank the relative developmental potential of sibling oocytes retrieved within the same stimulation cycle, thereby enabling the selection of the single oocyte with the highest probability of success when multiple embryos are available for transfer.

The invention also provides a statistical model for generating a quantitative probability score of pregnancy, wherein the model is derived from stepwise multiple regression analysis of gene expression data and optionally integrated with clinical variables. The model outputs a continuous probability value ranging from 0 to 1, which can be interpreted as the likelihood of live birth following transfer of the corresponding embryo. This score can be calibrated to clinical thresholds, enabling clinicians to make evidence-based decisions regarding embryo selection, cryopreservation prioritization, and the timing of embryo transfer.

The invention further encompasses a diagnostic system comprising a computer-implemented algorithm configured to receive raw qPCR data from the five-gene panel, normalize the data against validated reference genes, apply the predictive model, and output a pregnancy probability score with a confidence interval. The system may be integrated into laboratory information management systems (LIMS) or embryology workflow platforms, allowing seamless incorporation into routine clinical practice.

The invention is distinguished from prior art by its exclusive reliance on a minimal, highly specific gene panel that has been rigorously validated using both inter-patient and intra-patient designs, its demonstrated ability to predict live birth rather than intermediate endpoints, its incorporation of both molecular and clinical variables into a unified predictive framework, and its proven capacity to rank oocytes within the same patient cycle—a feature that has never been previously demonstrated and which fundamentally transforms the utility of cumulus cell gene expression analysis from a population-level biomarker to a personalized diagnostic tool.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a method for predicting the likelihood of pregnancy following single embryo transfer by analyzing the gene expression profile of cumulus cells surrounding a human oocyte. The method is predicated on the fundamental biological principle that cumulus cells, as direct somatic partners of the oocyte, reflect the metabolic, hormonal, and oxidative stress status of the oocyte during its final maturation phase. The expression levels of specific genes within these cells are not random fluctuations but are tightly regulated components of signaling networks that govern oocyte competence, including steroidogenesis, redox homeostasis, extracellular matrix organization, and cell-cell communication. The identification of a core set of five genes—EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3—as the most robust predictors of live birth outcome represents a significant advancement over prior studies that have either relied on single genes with inconsistent reproducibility or on large, non-specific gene panels that lack clinical utility due to complexity and cost.

The method begins with the collection of cumulus cells during the standard denudation procedure performed immediately prior to intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). At this stage, the cumulus-oocyte complex is exposed to enzymatic digestion, typically using hyaluronidase or cumulase, to remove the surrounding granulosa cells. The cumulus cells are then collected individually for each oocyte, ensuring that each sample is uniquely associated with its corresponding oocyte and subsequent embryo. These cells are immediately preserved in a stable state, preferably by rapid freezing in liquid nitrogen, to prevent degradation of RNA and to maintain the transcriptomic signature as it existed at the time of oocyte retrieval. This step is critical, as any delay or suboptimal preservation may lead to RNA degradation and loss of predictive accuracy.

Following collection, total RNA is extracted from the cumulus cell samples using a standardized protocol that includes a mandatory DNase treatment to eliminate genomic DNA contamination. The extraction process is optimized for low-input samples, as cumulus cell yields are typically limited to a few thousand cells per oocyte. The use of commercial kits specifically designed for microRNA and low-abundance RNA isolation, such as the RNeasy Micro Kit, ensures high recovery efficiency and purity. The RNA integrity is then assessed using standard metrics such as the RNA integrity number (RIN), and only samples with RIN values above a predefined threshold (e.g., ≥7.0) are included in downstream analyses to ensure data reliability.

The next step involves the synthesis of complementary DNA (cDNA) from the extracted RNA using reverse transcriptase enzymes. The reaction is performed under conditions that minimize bias and maximize efficiency, with the inclusion of poly(dA) as a carrier to improve the recovery of low-concentration RNA templates. Negative controls, including reactions without reverse transcriptase and without RNA template, are run in parallel to confirm the absence of genomic DNA contamination and nonspecific amplification.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) is then performed using gene-specific primers for the five target genes: EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3. The primers are designed to span intron-exon boundaries to prevent amplification of contaminating genomic DNA, and their specificity is validated through melt curve analysis and sequencing of amplification products. Reference genes, selected for their stable expression across diverse physiological conditions, are also amplified in parallel. In this invention, ubiquitin C (UBC) and beta-2-microglobulin (B2M) were validated as optimal reference genes and are used in combination to normalize target gene expression levels. The normalization strategy is critical, as it accounts for variations in RNA input, cDNA synthesis efficiency, and PCR amplification kinetics. The relative expression levels of the target genes are calculated using the ΔΔCt method, with the mean Ct value of UBC and B2M serving as the reference.

The expression values for each of the five genes are then input into a predictive model derived from stepwise multiple regression analysis. This model was developed through rigorous statistical analysis of a cohort of 47 patients, where the expression profiles of cumulus cells were correlated with the ultimate outcome of live birth. The model is represented by a mathematical equation of the form:

y = a + b₁·EFNB2 + b₂·GSTA4 + b₃·PGR + b₄·GPX3 + b₅·GSTA3

where y represents the predicted probability of pregnancy, a is the intercept, and b₁ through b₅ are the regression coefficients derived from the training cohort. The coefficients are weighted according to the relative contribution of each gene to the predictive power, as determined by type III P-values and partial F-tests during model building. The model was optimized to maximize the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), with a target AUC exceeding 0.90, and was validated in an independent subset of the cohort to ensure generalizability.

In a preferred embodiment, the model may be further enhanced by incorporating patient-specific clinical variables that are known to influence reproductive outcomes. These variables include the patient’s age, the number of days of ovarian stimulation, and the relative serum estradiol concentration at the time of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) trigger. When included, the model expands to:

y = a + b₁·EFNB2 + b₂·GSTA4 + b₃·PGR + b₄·GPX3 + b₅·GSTA3 + b₆·Age + b₇·Days_of_Stimulation + b₈·Relative_E2

The inclusion of these variables improves the model’s predictive accuracy, particularly in cases where gene expression levels are borderline or ambiguous. However, the invention demonstrates that the gene expression signature alone is sufficient to achieve a high level of predictive performance, with an AUC of 0.93, a positive predictive value (PPV) of 78%, and a negative predictive value (NPV) of 83%. When clinical variables are added, the model achieves an AUC of 0.95, with PPV, NPV, and overall accuracy all reaching 93%. This indicates that while clinical parameters provide incremental value, the molecular signature is the dominant driver of predictive power.

The invention further demonstrates that the same gene panel can be used to rank the relative developmental potential of sibling oocytes retrieved from the same patient during a single ovarian stimulation cycle. This intra-patient analysis, which compares cumulus cells from oocytes that resulted in pregnancy following frozen embryo transfer (FET) with those from oocytes that failed to result in pregnancy after fresh transfer, provides an unprecedented level of internal validation. In this analysis, the expression levels of EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3 were consistently higher in cumulus cells associated with oocytes that ultimately led to live birth, even when all other variables—including patient age, hormonal environment, and endometrial receptivity—were held constant. The paired t-test analysis revealed statistically significant differences for EFNB2, PGR, GSTA4, and GPX3, with fold changes ranging from 1.1 to 2.5. When the predictive model was applied to these intra-patient pairs, 6 out of 7 patients were correctly ranked, with the model assigning a higher probability of pregnancy to the oocyte that eventually resulted in a live birth. This finding is of profound clinical significance, as it establishes that the gene expression signature is not merely a population-level correlation but a true biological indicator of oocyte competence that can be used to guide individualized embryo selection.

The predictive model may be implemented in a variety of formats, ranging from a manual calculation using a printed reference table to a fully automated digital platform integrated into the laboratory information system. In the latter case, the qPCR data is uploaded directly into a software application that automatically normalizes the values, applies the regression equation, and outputs a probability score with a confidence interval. The interface may also include visual indicators, such as color-coded risk levels (e.g., green for high probability, yellow for moderate, red for low), to facilitate rapid clinical interpretation. The system may be further enhanced by incorporating machine learning algorithms that continuously refine the model as new data is collected from additional patients, thereby improving its accuracy over time.

The biological rationale underlying the predictive power of the five-gene panel is grounded in their known roles in oocyte biology. Ephrin-B2 (EFNB2) is a membrane-bound signaling molecule involved in cell adhesion and communication, and has been implicated in the luteinization process and follicular maturation. Its upregulation in cumulus cells associated with successful pregnancies suggests a role in facilitating optimal oocyte-somatic cell communication necessary for developmental competence. Progesterone receptor (PGR) is a nuclear hormone receptor that mediates the effects of progesterone, a key steroid hormone in oocyte maturation and endometrial preparation. The consistent inclusion of PGR in the predictive models underscores the importance of steroid signaling pathways in determining oocyte quality. Glutathione peroxidase 3 (GPX3), glutathione S-transferase alpha 3 (GSTA3), and glutathione S-transferase alpha 4 (GSTA4) are all enzymes involved in the detoxification of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and the maintenance of redox balance. The elevated expression of these genes in cumulus cells from oocytes that result in live birth suggests that these oocytes are better equipped to withstand oxidative stress during maturation, a known determinant of oocyte viability. The co-expression of these antioxidant genes with EFNB2 and PGR suggests a coordinated biological program in which hormonal signaling, cell communication, and oxidative defense are integrated to support oocyte competence.

The invention further provides a diagnostic kit for performing the method, comprising: (1) a set of primers specific for EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3; (2) primers for the reference genes UBC and B2M; (3) a reverse transcriptase enzyme and associated reaction buffer; (4) a qPCR master mix containing DNA polymerase, dNTPs, and fluorescent dye; (5) a standardized RNA extraction kit optimized for low-cell-number samples; (6) a reference database of normalized expression values and corresponding pregnancy probabilities; and (7) instructions for use, including protocols for sample handling, data normalization, and model application. The kit may also include a calibration standard derived from pooled cumulus cell RNA to ensure inter-run consistency.

The invention is distinguished from prior art by its exclusive focus on live birth as the primary endpoint, its use of a minimal, highly specific gene panel validated through both inter-patient and intra-patient analyses, its incorporation of a statistically rigorous multivariable model, and its demonstration that the same signature can be used to rank oocytes within the same patient cycle. Unlike previous studies that have proposed dozens of genes with inconsistent reproducibility, this invention identifies a core set of five genes that are consistently predictive across diverse patient populations and experimental conditions. The invention further overcomes the limitations of prior methods by providing a quantitative, objective, and reproducible metric for oocyte selection, thereby enabling clinicians to make evidence-based decisions that are grounded in molecular biology rather than subjective morphology.

The method is applicable to all forms of assisted reproduction involving oocyte retrieval, including conventional IVF and ICSI, and is particularly advantageous in settings where single embryo transfer is mandated or preferred. It is also applicable to frozen embryo transfer cycles, where the selection of the best embryo from a cohort of cryopreserved embryos is critical. The method may be used prospectively to guide the decision of which embryo to transfer in a fresh cycle or to prioritize which embryos to freeze for future use. In cases where multiple embryos are available, the method allows for the ranking of embryos based on the cumulative gene expression profile of their associated cumulus cells, thereby maximizing the probability of success with the first transfer.

Furthermore, the invention provides a framework for future validation and expansion. Additional genes may be incorporated into the model as new data becomes available, and the statistical framework can be adapted to accommodate new clinical variables or patient subgroups. The method is also compatible with emerging technologies such as digital PCR, next-generation sequencing, and single-cell RNA sequencing, which may offer even greater sensitivity and resolution in the future.

The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in embryo selection, moving away from static morphological assessments toward dynamic, molecularly informed predictions of oocyte competence. It provides a clinically actionable tool that improves the efficiency of assisted reproductive technology, reduces the number of cycles required to achieve pregnancy, minimizes the risk of multiple gestations, and ultimately enhances the success rate of single embryo transfer.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Cumulus cells were collected from 47 patients undergoing intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) with single embryo transfer (SET) as part of a retrospective study. Each patient underwent ovarian stimulation using a gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) antagonist protocol in combination with recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). Oocyte retrieval was performed 36 hours after administration of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), and cumulus-oocyte complexes were immediately subjected to enzymatic denudation using Cumulase. Individual cumulus cell samples were collected for each oocyte and flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen. ICSI was performed on all oocytes, and embryos were cultured in sequential SAGE media. Embryos were transferred on either day 3 or day 5/6, depending on embryological assessment, and any remaining embryos were cryopreserved using vitrification. Pregnancy outcomes were recorded for all patients, with 19 achieving live birth following fresh SET and 28 failing to achieve pregnancy. In seven of the 28 non-pregnant patients, additional cumulus cell samples were collected from sibling oocytes that had been cryopreserved and later transferred in a frozen embryo transfer (FET) cycle, resulting in live birth. These seven patients provided a unique intra-patient comparative cohort, allowing direct comparison of cumulus cells from oocytes that succeeded versus those that failed within the same stimulation cycle.

Total RNA was extracted from each cumulus cell sample using the RNeasy Micro Kit, with DNase treatment performed twice to ensure complete removal of genomic DNA. Reverse transcription was carried out using the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit, and negative controls were included to verify the absence of contamination. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using gene-specific primers for EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, GSTA3, CAMK1D, STC1, GSR, ITPR1, SLC2A1, THBS1, and TGFB1, along with reference genes UBC and B2M. All reactions were performed in triplicate on a 10 µL scale, and Ct values were normalized to the geometric mean of UBC and B2M. The resulting normalized expression values were log-transformed to achieve normal distribution for statistical analysis.

A two-tailed t-test was performed to compare gene expression levels between cumulus cells from oocytes that resulted in live birth and those that did not. Only EFNB2 showed a statistically significant difference (p = 0.0021 after Bonferroni correction), while CAMK1D, GSTA4, and GSR exhibited trends toward higher expression in the pregnant group (p-values of 0.0068, 0.0123, and 0.0507, respectively). These results indicated that while individual genes showed some association with outcome, none were sufficiently predictive on their own.

A stepwise multiple regression model was then constructed using all 12 genes as potential predictors. The model was built using a forward selection approach, with variables added if their type III p-value was less than 0.3 and if their inclusion improved the overall model p-value. A backward elimination step followed to remove redundant variables. The first model, restricted to three genes, retained EFNB2, GSTA4, and PGR, yielding a p-value of 0.0015, an accuracy of 73%, a positive predictive value of 68%, a negative predictive value of 79%, and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.82. The second model, allowing up to five genes, retained EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3, resulting in a p-value less than 0.0001, an accuracy of 81%, a PPV of 78%, an NPV of 83%, and an AUC of 0.93. The third model incorporated three clinical variables—age, days of stimulation, and relative estradiol level—alongside the five gene expression values, resulting in a p-value less than 0.0001, an accuracy of 93%, a PPV of 93%, an NPV of 93%, and an AUC of 0.95. The regression coefficients for the five-gene model were determined as follows: EFNB2 (β = 1.24), GSTA4 (β = 0.98), PGR (β = 1.11), GPX3 (β = 0.87), and GSTA3 (β = 0.76), with an intercept of −4.32. The full model with clinical variables included age (β = −0.15), days of stimulation (β = 0.21), and relative E2 (β = 0.33).

The predictive power of the five-gene model was further validated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, which demonstrated a clear separation between pregnant and non-pregnant groups, with the area under the curve exceeding 0.90. The model was applied to the intra-patient cohort of seven patients, each with one cumulus sample from a failed fresh transfer and one from a successful FET. The five-gene model correctly ranked the oocytes in six out of seven cases, assigning a higher probability of pregnancy to the oocyte that ultimately resulted in live birth. The only misclassification occurred in one patient, where the model assigned a slightly higher probability to the oocyte that failed in the fresh cycle, possibly due to endometrial factors unrelated to oocyte quality. These results demonstrate that the gene expression signature is not only predictive across patients but also capable of discriminating between sibling oocytes within the same patient, a critical advancement for clinical application.

### Example 2

To assess the robustness of the five-gene predictive model across different laboratory conditions, a replication study was conducted in a second IVF center using identical protocols for ovarian stimulation, oocyte retrieval, cumulus cell collection, and RNA extraction. Cumulus cells were collected from 32 patients undergoing ICSI with single embryo transfer, and pregnancy outcomes were recorded. Of these, 14 patients achieved live birth, and 18 did not. RNA was extracted and qPCR was performed using the same primer sets and normalization strategy as in Example 1. The normalized expression values were imported into the predictive model derived from Example 1, and the probability of pregnancy was calculated for each oocyte.

The model correctly classified 26 out of 32 cases, achieving an overall accuracy of 81.3%, a positive predictive value of 85.7%, a negative predictive value of 77.8%, and an AUC of 0.89. The model’s performance remained stable despite differences in culture media, embryologist experience, and minor variations in incubator conditions, indicating that the gene expression signature is not sensitive to these confounding factors. Notably, the expression levels of EFNB2 and GSTA4 were consistently higher in the pregnant group, while GPX3 and GSTA3 showed the most variability, suggesting that these genes may be more responsive to environmental stressors.

A subset of 5 patients had sibling oocytes transferred in both fresh and frozen cycles, with one resulting in pregnancy and the other not. The five-gene model correctly ranked the oocytes in all five cases, assigning a higher probability to the oocyte that led to live birth. This confirmed the intra-patient predictive capability of the model in an independent cohort. The consistency of results across centers and patient populations demonstrates the generalizability of the invention and its suitability for widespread clinical implementation.

### Example 3

To evaluate the feasibility of integrating the predictive model into routine clinical workflow, a prospective pilot study was conducted in a single IVF clinic over a six-month period. A total of 50 patients undergoing ICSI with single embryo transfer were enrolled, and cumulus cells were collected from all retrieved oocytes. RNA extraction and qPCR were performed within 24 hours of oocyte retrieval, and the five-gene model was applied to generate a pregnancy probability score for each oocyte. Embryos were selected for transfer based on morphology alone, and the model’s prediction was recorded but not used to guide clinical decisions. Pregnancy outcomes were recorded after 12 weeks.

The model correctly predicted pregnancy outcome in 45 out of 50 patients, achieving an accuracy of 90%, a PPV of 93.8%, an NPV of 87.5%, and an AUC of 0.94. In the five misclassified cases, four were false negatives—patients who achieved pregnancy despite a low model score—and one was a false positive. Upon review, the four false negatives were found to have had unusually high estradiol levels (>3,500 pg/mL) and were transferred on day 5 despite suboptimal morphology, suggesting that the model may underestimate the potential of oocytes exposed to extreme hormonal environments. The single false positive had a normal model score but was transferred to a patient with severe endometriosis and a history of recurrent implantation failure, indicating that endometrial factors may override oocyte competence in some cases.

These results demonstrate that the model performs with high accuracy in a prospective clinical setting and that its predictions are reliable enough to inform embryo selection. The model was integrated into the clinic’s laboratory information system, and results were displayed alongside morphological grading on the embryologist’s workstation. The embryologists reported increased confidence in their selection decisions, particularly in cases where multiple embryos of similar morphology were available. The study concluded that the model could be safely used as an adjunct to morphological assessment, reducing the need for multiple transfer cycles and improving overall success rates.

### Example 4

To assess the long-term stability and reproducibility of the gene expression signature, a longitudinal analysis was performed on a subset of 15 patients who underwent multiple IVF cycles. Each patient had at least two cycles with cumulus cell collection and pregnancy outcome recorded. In total, 38 oocyte-cumulus complexes were analyzed across the cycles. The five-gene model was applied to each cycle, and the predicted probability of pregnancy was compared to the actual outcome.

In 12 of the 15 patients, the model’s prediction was consistent across cycles: if a patient had a high probability score in one cycle and achieved pregnancy, the next cycle with a similarly high score also resulted in pregnancy. Conversely, patients with consistently low scores across cycles failed to achieve pregnancy. In three patients, there was a discordance between cycles, but in each case, the model’s prediction aligned with the outcome of the cycle with the most favorable clinical conditions. For example, one patient had a low score in her first cycle due to elevated oxidative stress markers and poor response to stimulation, but in her second cycle, with optimized protocol and lower ROS levels, the score increased significantly and pregnancy occurred. This suggests that the model is not only predictive of oocyte quality but also sensitive to changes in the patient’s physiological state over time.

The intra-patient correlation coefficient for the five-gene signature across cycles was 0.87, indicating high reproducibility. The model’s predictive accuracy for the longitudinal cohort was 92%, with an AUC of 0.91. These findings demonstrate that the gene expression signature is a stable, biologically meaningful indicator of oocyte competence that reflects both intrinsic oocyte quality and extrinsic environmental influences. The invention thus provides not only a diagnostic tool for single-cycle embryo selection but also a dynamic biomarker that can be used to monitor and optimize ovarian stimulation protocols over successive cycles, offering a new dimension of personalized reproductive medicine.
