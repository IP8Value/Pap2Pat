# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

The global demand for sustainable materials has intensified in recent decades as environmental concerns, regulatory pressures, and consumer preferences increasingly favor products derived from renewable resources over those dependent on fossil fuels. Polymers, which constitute a cornerstone of modern industry and daily life, are overwhelmingly produced from petroleum-derived monomers such as styrene, ethylene, propylene, and acrylates. These materials account for more than seven percent of global fossil fuel consumption annually, a figure projected to rise as emerging economies expand their manufacturing and consumer sectors. The environmental burden associated with the extraction, refining, and polymerization of these feedstocks—including greenhouse gas emissions, energy-intensive processing, and persistent waste streams—has prompted urgent calls for alternatives that reduce carbon intensity and reliance on non-renewable resources.

Despite decades of research and development, bio-based polymers continue to occupy a marginal position in the global polymer market, representing less than five percent of total production. This limited adoption stems from a combination of economic, technical, and perceptual barriers. Economically, bio-based monomers and polymers are often perceived as cost-prohibitive due to higher raw material prices, less mature supply chains, and lower economies of scale compared to their petrochemical counterparts. Technically, many bio-derived materials exhibit inferior mechanical properties, thermal stability, or processability relative to conventional plastics, limiting their applicability in high-performance or structural roles. Perceptually, end-users and manufacturers remain skeptical about the durability, consistency, and long-term performance of renewable alternatives, often associating them with niche or low-end applications.

One promising class of renewable feedstocks is terpenes—naturally occurring hydrocarbons derived primarily from citrus peels, coniferous trees, and other plant sources. These compounds are produced on multi-tonne scales through distillation of essential oils or as byproducts of the pulp and paper industry, and their availability is expected to grow substantially with advances in biotechnological production methods. Terpenes such as α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene, and carvone possess molecular architectures rich in alkene functionalities, making them theoretically attractive candidates for polymerization via free radical mechanisms, which are among the most widely used and industrially scalable techniques in polymer manufacturing. However, despite their structural potential, terpenes have historically proven recalcitrant to homopolymerization under conventional free radical conditions. Extensive prior efforts to polymerize these molecules directly have yielded only low molecular weight oligomers, often accompanied by poor conversion rates, uncontrolled branching, or gelation due to unintended side reactions.

Several alternative strategies have been pursued to overcome these limitations. Cationic polymerization methods have succeeded with certain terpenes like β-pinene and α-phellandrene, but require cryogenic temperatures ranging from -80 °C to -40 °C, rendering them impractical for industrial-scale operations. Ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) using ruthenium catalysts has enabled the polymerization of sesquiterpenes such as caryophyllene and humulene, producing elastomeric materials with low glass transition temperatures; however, this approach is incompatible with the most abundant and commercially accessible monoterpenes. Other approaches have focused on chemical modification of terpenes to enhance their reactivity. For instance, Baeyer-Villiger oxidation of ketone-containing terpenoids like menthone and carvone yields lactone monomers suitable for ring-opening polymerization using zinc catalysts, resulting in polymers with moderate glass transition temperatures. Similarly, epoxidation of limonene followed by copolymerization with carbon dioxide using zinc-based catalysts has produced polycarbonates with high thermal stability and controlled molecular weights. While these methods represent significant scientific advances, they rely on multi-step syntheses, specialized catalysts, stringent reaction conditions (e.g., anhydrous, oxygen-free environments), and expensive reagents, all of which hinder scalability and economic viability.

Another line of inquiry has involved functionalizing terpenes bearing carboxylic acid or hydroxyl groups—such as abietic acid from rosin—by esterifying them with acrylic or methacrylic acid to generate acrylate-type monomers. These derivatives have found niche applications in photoresists and specialty coatings, but their utility is inherently restricted to terpenes that already contain these polar functional groups. The vast majority of commercially available terpenes, including α-pinene, β-pinene, and limonene, lack such groups and are therefore inaccessible through this route. Consequently, the field has been confined to a narrow subset of terpenoid structures, leaving the broader chemical diversity of terpenes largely untapped for polymer applications.

Moreover, existing synthetic routes to terpene-based acrylates typically involve the use of acryloyl or methacryloyl chlorides—highly reactive, toxic, and moisture-sensitive reagents that generate corrosive hydrogen chloride as a byproduct. The disposal of chloride-containing waste streams poses significant environmental and regulatory challenges, particularly in large-scale manufacturing. Alternative esterification methods employing carboxylic acids directly, rather than acid chlorides, have been explored in other contexts using coupling agents such as dicyclohexylcarbodiimide (DCC) or 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC); however, these reagents are costly, generate difficult-to-remove urea byproducts, and are incompatible with the sensitive alkene functionalities present in terpenes, often leading to side reactions or decomposition.

There remains, therefore, a critical and unmet need for a novel, scalable, and environmentally benign methodology to convert abundant, non-functionalized terpenes into polymerizable acrylate and methacrylate monomers that can be processed using standard industrial free radical polymerization techniques. Such a methodology must enable the production of polymers with tunable thermal, mechanical, and rheological properties, avoid the use of hazardous reagents, minimize waste generation, and be compatible with existing polymer manufacturing infrastructure. Furthermore, the resulting polymers should exhibit performance characteristics that match or exceed those of conventional petroleum-based acrylics, thereby enabling seamless integration into current supply chains without requiring costly retooling or process modifications.

The invention disclosed herein addresses this need by introducing a new class of terpene-derived acrylate and methacrylate monomers, synthesized through a streamlined, two-step protocol that replaces toxic acid chlorides with renewable carboxylic acids using a non-toxic, water-soluble coupling agent. This approach enables the efficient, high-yield conversion of α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene, and carvone into polymerizable monomers that undergo controlled free radical polymerization to yield materials with exceptional thermal stability, mechanical strength, and processability. Notably, the invention further reveals that certain terpene methacrylates—particularly those derived from carvone—exhibit a unique combination of reactivity and stability that prevents crosslinking during polymerization, despite the presence of multiple unsaturated moieties, thereby enabling the formation of high molecular weight linear polymers. This unexpected behavior opens new avenues for the design of functional coatings, adhesives, and thermoplastics with inherent residual unsaturation suitable for post-polymerization modification.

Additionally, the invention introduces a novel one-pot catalytic method for the direct allylic oxidation and esterification of β-pinene using palladium acetate and benzoquinone in methacrylic acid as both solvent and reactant. This catalytic system achieves unprecedented yields and regioselectivity, producing two distinct methacrylate isomers in a 10:1 ratio without the need for intermediate alcohol formation or purification steps. The process operates under mild conditions, utilizes air as the terminal oxidant, and generates only benign byproducts, offering a transformative pathway for the sustainable, large-scale production of terpene-based monomers.

The polymers derived from these monomers demonstrate a wide range of physical properties, including glass transition temperatures spanning from 12 °C to 142 °C, making them suitable for diverse applications—from flexible films and elastomers to rigid thermoplastics and high-performance powder coatings. In particular, the carvone-derived methacrylate polymer has been successfully formulated into a commercially viable, UV-curable acrylic powder coating that meets or exceeds industry standards for gloss, hardness, chemical resistance, and adhesion. This coating, produced using conventional industrial equipment and procedures, represents the first demonstration of a fully bio-based acrylic powder coating with performance characteristics comparable to those of petroleum-derived equivalents.

Thus, the invention provides not only a set of novel monomers and polymers, but also a comprehensive, industrially viable platform for converting abundant, renewable terpenes into high-value polymer materials that can be seamlessly integrated into existing manufacturing systems. This platform overcomes the longstanding limitations of prior art by combining chemical innovation with practical scalability, environmental sustainability, and performance parity—thereby enabling a meaningful transition away from fossil-fuel-derived polymers without compromising on cost, quality, or process efficiency.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention relates to a novel class of terpene-derived acrylate and methacrylate monomers, methods for their synthesis, and the polymers formed therefrom, which exhibit properties suitable for a broad range of industrial applications. These monomers are synthesized from naturally occurring, commercially abundant terpenes such as α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene, and carvone through a two-step process that first converts the terpene into an alcohol derivative and subsequently esterifies the alcohol with acrylic acid or methacrylic acid using propyl phosphonic anhydride (T3P®) as a coupling agent. This method eliminates the need for toxic acryloyl or methacryloyl chlorides, avoids the generation of corrosive byproducts, and produces water-soluble, environmentally benign triphosphate waste, thereby enabling a sustainable and scalable route to polymerizable monomers.

The resulting monomers are readily polymerized using standard free radical initiators such as azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) under mild conditions, yielding polymers with controlled molecular weights and predictable thermal properties. The molecular weight of the polymers can be precisely regulated by the addition of chain transfer agents, including dodecanethiol, which allows for the fine-tuning of mechanical and rheological characteristics to meet specific application requirements. The polymers derived from α-pinene and β-pinene acrylates exhibit glass transition temperatures ranging from 12 °C to 71 °C, while their methacrylate analogues display significantly higher glass transition temperatures between 85 °C and 142 °C, attributable to the increased steric hindrance imparted by the α-methyl group in the methacrylate backbone. This substantial elevation in Tg, particularly in the case of the α-pinene methacrylate polymer, which reaches 142 °C, is unprecedented for bio-based acrylic polymers and approaches the thermal stability of conventional polycarbonates, thereby expanding the potential application space for renewable polymers into high-temperature engineering domains.

A particularly surprising and novel finding of the invention is the behavior of the carvone-derived methacrylate monomer. Despite the presence of two distinct olefinic functionalities—the methacrylate double bond and an exocyclic alkene—the polymerization of this monomer proceeds cleanly and quantitatively without crosslinking or gelation, even at elevated temperatures. In contrast, the corresponding carvone acrylate monomer undergoes rapid and uncontrolled crosslinking under identical conditions due to the preferential initiation of polymerization at the exocyclic alkene, leading to insoluble networks. Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy confirms that only the methacrylate double bond is consumed during polymerization of the methacrylate monomer, while the exocyclic alkene remains intact. This selective reactivity is attributed to the electron-withdrawing nature of the methacrylate ester group, which renders the adjacent double bond more electrophilic and thus more susceptible to radical attack, while the exocyclic alkene remains inert under the reaction conditions. This unexpected selectivity enables the production of linear, high molecular weight polymers containing pendant unsaturated groups, which can be exploited for post-polymerization functionalization, such as crosslinking via thiol-ene chemistry, surface modification, or UV curing.

The invention further demonstrates the practical utility of these polymers through the formulation and application of a bio-based acrylic powder coating derived from the carvone methacrylate polymer. The polymer was blended with a commercial multifunctional thiol crosslinker, trimethylolpropane tris(3-mercaptopropionate), and other standard processing additives, melt-mixed, extruded, and ground into a fine powder. This powder was electrostatically applied to aluminum substrates and cured under controlled thermal and UV conditions, resulting in a transparent, high-gloss, durable coating with excellent chemical resistance, pencil hardness of 2B, König hardness of 185 seconds, and adhesion properties consistent with industry-standard petroleum-based acrylic coatings. The coating exhibited no delamination in lattice cutter tests and withstood acetone rub testing without degradation, confirming full crosslinking and robust film formation.

In addition to the two-step synthetic route, the invention introduces a novel one-pot catalytic method for the direct synthesis of β-pinene methacrylate monomers via palladium-catalyzed allylic oxidation in the presence of methacrylic acid. This method employs a catalytic system consisting of palladium acetate and benzoquinone, operating under ambient air at 50 °C, with methacrylic acid serving simultaneously as the solvent and reactant. The process yields two regioisomeric methacrylate monomers in an 82% overall yield and a 10:1 regioselectivity ratio, without the need for isolation of intermediate alcohols or the use of stoichiometric reagents. Benzoquinone plays a triple role in this system, acting as an oxidant, a ligand for palladium, and a polymerization inhibitor to prevent premature polymerization of the product. The method is highly efficient, scalable, and environmentally superior to prior art, as it eliminates multiple synthetic steps, reduces solvent consumption, and avoids the generation of halogenated waste.

The polymers of the invention are distinguished by their renewable origin, tunable thermal properties, and compatibility with conventional polymer processing techniques. They are suitable for use in coatings, adhesives, sealants, thermoplastics, 3D printing filaments, and encapsulants. The ability to control molecular weight via chain transfer agents, the absence of crosslinking in methacrylate variants despite multiple unsaturations, and the potential for post-polymerization modification via residual double bonds provide unprecedented flexibility in material design. The invention thus represents a transformative advance in the field of sustainable polymers, enabling the replacement of fossil-derived acrylics with bio-based alternatives that match or exceed performance while reducing environmental impact.

The invention further encompasses the use of the terpene-derived polymers as components in composite materials, where their inherent rigidity, thermal stability, and compatibility with other polymers allow for the creation of hybrid systems with enhanced mechanical properties. The polymers may also be blended with conventional plastics to improve their bio-content without compromising performance, thereby facilitating regulatory compliance and consumer acceptance. The invention further includes methods for the preparation of films, fibers, molded articles, and extruded profiles from the polymers, utilizing standard industrial equipment such as extruders, injection molders, and calenders.

In summary, the invention provides a complete and integrated technological platform for the production of high-performance, renewable polymers from abundant terpenes. It overcomes the historical limitations of terpene polymerization by introducing novel monomers with controlled reactivity, a sustainable synthetic pathway, and demonstrable industrial applicability. The invention enables the commercial-scale production of polymers that are chemically indistinguishable in performance from petroleum-based acrylics, yet derived entirely from renewable biomass, thereby offering a viable and economically attractive pathway to decarbonize the polymer industry.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Terpinolene and Gamma-Terpinene as Chain Transfer Agents

The invention further extends beyond the previously disclosed monomers and polymers to encompass the novel and unexpected use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as chain transfer agents in free radical polymerization processes. While chain transfer agents have long been employed in polymer chemistry to regulate molecular weight, control branching, and modify end-group functionality, the use of terpenoid compounds—particularly those with multiple unsaturated bonds—as effective chain transfer agents has not been previously reported or anticipated in the art. Terpinolene and gamma-terpinene, both naturally occurring monoterpene isomers with conjugated diene and isolated alkene functionalities, were discovered to exhibit exceptional chain transfer efficiency when introduced into polymerization systems containing acrylate or methacrylate monomers derived from terpenes such as α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene, and carvone.

Terpinolene, a cyclic monoterpene with a conjugated diene system and a terminal alkene, and gamma-terpinene, a structural isomer featuring a non-conjugated diene arrangement, were selected for investigation due to their structural similarity to the monomers being polymerized and their commercial availability as byproducts of citrus oil distillation. Both compounds contain allylic hydrogens that are susceptible to abstraction by propagating polymer radicals, a key mechanistic requirement for effective chain transfer. However, unlike traditional chain transfer agents such as dodecanethiol or carbon tetrachloride, which introduce sulfur or halogen end-groups that may compromise polymer stability or compatibility, terpinolene and gamma-terpinene transfer polymer chains via hydrogen abstraction without introducing heteroatoms or polar functionalities. This results in polymers terminated with aliphatic hydrocarbon end-groups, preserving the hydrophobic, non-polar character of the polymer backbone and enhancing compatibility with other hydrocarbon-based materials.

Experimental investigations revealed that the incorporation of terpinolene at concentrations ranging from 0.1 mol% to 5.0 mol% relative to the total monomer feed significantly reduced the number-average molecular weight (Mn) of the resulting polymers in a predictable and reproducible manner. For instance, in the polymerization of α-pinene methacrylate using AIBN as initiator, the addition of 1.0 mol% terpinolene resulted in a reduction of Mn from approximately 23,600 g/mol to 8,900 g/mol, while maintaining a narrow molecular weight distribution (Đ = 1.32), indicative of controlled chain transfer rather than uncontrolled termination. Similar reductions were observed in the polymerization of β-pinene methacrylate and carvone methacrylate, with molecular weights decreasing proportionally with increasing terpinolene concentration. Importantly, the glass transition temperature (Tg) of the resulting polymers decreased in a linear fashion with decreasing molecular weight, consistent with established polymer physics, demonstrating that the chain transfer mechanism did not alter the fundamental microstructure or tacticity of the polymer chains.

Gamma-terpinene exhibited comparable chain transfer efficiency to terpinolene, though with slightly lower effectiveness due to differences in the bond dissociation energies of its allylic hydrogens. Nevertheless, at 2.0 mol% loading, gamma-terpinene achieved a molecular weight reduction of approximately 65% in β-pinene methacrylate polymerizations, confirming its utility as a viable alternative. The use of these terpenes as chain transfer agents offers a compelling advantage: they are derived from the same renewable feedstock as the monomers themselves, thereby enhancing the overall bio-content of the final polymer product. This creates a closed-loop, bio-based system in which the chain transfer agent, monomer, and initiator (where applicable) are all sourced from plant-derived materials, resulting in polymers with up to 100% renewable carbon content.

Furthermore, the use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as chain transfer agents leads to the incorporation of residual unsaturation within the polymer chain ends. Unlike dodecanethiol, which terminates chains with a saturated alkyl group, the chain transfer process involving terpinolene or gamma-terpinene leaves behind a terpene-derived moiety at the chain terminus, which retains one or more double bonds. This feature is of profound significance, as it enables post-polymerization functionalization of the polymer without the need for additional chemical modification steps. For example, the terminal unsaturation can be exploited for thiol-ene click chemistry, UV-induced crosslinking, or grafting with other functional molecules, thereby creating polymers with tailored surface properties, enhanced adhesion, or stimuli-responsive behavior.

The mechanism of chain transfer is believed to proceed via hydrogen atom abstraction from the allylic position of terpinolene or gamma-terpinene by the propagating polymer radical, generating a carbon-centered radical on the terpene molecule. This terpene radical then initiates a new polymer chain by adding to a monomer molecule, effectively transferring the radical site from the growing chain to the chain transfer agent. The efficiency of this process is enhanced by the structural similarity between the chain transfer agent and the monomer, which minimizes steric and electronic barriers to radical addition. The rate constants for chain transfer were determined to be on the order of 10^4 M⁻¹s⁻¹, comparable to those of conventional thiols, confirming their efficacy as transfer agents.

Importantly, the use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene does not induce gelation or crosslinking, even at elevated concentrations. This is in stark contrast to the behavior of other unsaturated compounds such as divinylbenzene or diacrylates, which act as crosslinkers rather than chain transfer agents. The absence of crosslinking is attributed to the monofunctional nature of the chain transfer agents—each molecule contains only one abstractable allylic hydrogen per molecule under the reaction conditions, preventing the formation of multi-arm radical species. This distinguishes them from dienes or polyenes, which can act as crosslinkers by reacting with multiple propagating chains.

The invention further encompasses the use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene in combination with other chain transfer agents to achieve synergistic effects. For example, blending terpinolene with dodecanethiol allows for the simultaneous control of molecular weight and the introduction of sulfur-containing end-groups, which may be desirable for certain applications such as adhesive formulations or rubber compounding. The ratio of terpene-based to conventional chain transfer agents can be adjusted to fine-tune the balance between bio-content and functional performance.

In industrial applications, the use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as chain transfer agents offers significant economic and environmental advantages. These compounds are readily available as low-cost byproducts of the citrus and essential oil industries, eliminating the need for expensive synthetic reagents. Their use reduces reliance on petroleum-derived thiols, which are subject to volatile pricing and supply chain disruptions. Moreover, their non-toxic, non-corrosive nature simplifies handling, storage, and waste management, reducing regulatory burdens and operational costs. The fact that they are volatile and can be recovered via distillation further enhances their sustainability profile, enabling potential recycling and reuse within the manufacturing process.

The polymers produced using terpinolene or gamma-terpinene as chain transfer agents exhibit improved processability due to their lower molecular weights and reduced melt viscosity. This is particularly advantageous in applications such as extrusion, injection molding, and coating, where high melt flow is required for uniform film formation or complex part replication. The lower molecular weight also enhances solubility in organic solvents, facilitating solution-based processing techniques such as spin coating, dip coating, and inkjet printing.

In summary, the invention discloses for the first time the use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as highly effective, renewable, and non-toxic chain transfer agents in free radical polymerization systems. Their ability to reduce molecular weight without inducing crosslinking, preserve polymer hydrophobicity, introduce terminal unsaturation for further functionalization, and enhance the overall bio-content of the polymer system represents a significant advancement in sustainable polymer chemistry. This innovation not only expands the toolkit available to polymer scientists but also provides a practical and scalable solution for the production of bio-based polymers with precisely controlled architectures, opening new possibilities for the design of next-generation materials derived entirely from renewable resources.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In a representative synthesis of the terpene-derived methacrylate monomer, 50.0 grams of (+)-α-pinene was dissolved in 150 mL of anhydrous tetrahydrofuran under a nitrogen atmosphere. To this solution, 1.2 equivalents of borane-tetrahydrofuran complex (1.0 M in THF) were added dropwise over a period of 30 minutes at 0 °C. The reaction mixture was then allowed to warm to room temperature and stirred for an additional 12 hours. Subsequently, 30 mL of 30% aqueous hydrogen peroxide and 15 mL of 10% sodium hydroxide were added sequentially to oxidize the borane adduct, and the mixture was stirred for another 2 hours at room temperature. The aqueous layer was separated, and the organic phase was washed with brine, dried over anhydrous magnesium sulfate, and concentrated under reduced pressure to yield 42.3 grams (89% yield) of the corresponding alcohol, 2-(4-methylcyclohex-3-en-1-yl)propan-2-ol, as a colorless liquid. The product was purified by short-path distillation under reduced pressure (bp 110–112 °C at 5 mmHg) and characterized by ¹H and ¹³C NMR spectroscopy, confirming its structure and diastereomeric purity.

To this alcohol, 55.0 grams (0.52 mol) of methacrylic acid and 12.5 grams (0.052 mol) of propyl phosphonic anhydride (T3P® 50% solution in ethyl acetate) were added in a 250 mL round-bottom flask equipped with a magnetic stirrer and condenser. The mixture was heated to 60 °C under nitrogen and stirred for 6 hours. The reaction progress was monitored by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) using ethyl acetate/hexane (1:4) as the eluent. Upon completion, the reaction mixture was cooled to room temperature and diluted with 100 mL of ethyl acetate. The organic layer was washed three times with saturated aqueous sodium bicarbonate solution to remove phosphonic acid byproducts, followed by a final wash with brine. The organic phase was dried over anhydrous magnesium sulfate, filtered, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by column chromatography on silica gel using a gradient elution of hexane to 20% ethyl acetate in hexane, yielding 51.8 grams (86% yield) of the desired methacrylate ester, 2-(4-methylcyclohex-3-en-1-yl)propan-2-yl methacrylate, as a clear, colorless liquid. The product was characterized by ¹H NMR, ¹³C NMR, FTIR, and high-resolution mass spectrometry, confirming its structure and purity (purity >98%).

For polymerization, 5.0 grams of the purified methacrylate monomer was dissolved in 10 mL of toluene in a 50 mL Schlenk flask equipped with a magnetic stir bar and sealed with a rubber septum. The flask was purged with nitrogen for 15 minutes, and 0.05 grams (0.3 mmol) of azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) was added as initiator. The reaction mixture was heated to 70 °C in an oil bath under continuous nitrogen purge. After 4 hours, the reaction was quenched by cooling the flask in an ice bath and exposing it to air. The polymer was precipitated by adding the reaction mixture dropwise into 200 mL of cold methanol, collected by filtration, washed with methanol, and dried under vacuum at 40 °C for 24 hours. The resulting polymer was analyzed by gel permeation chromatography (GPC) in tetrahydrofuran using polystyrene standards, yielding a number-average molecular weight (Mn) of 23,600 g/mol and a weight-average molecular weight (Mw) of 31,200 g/mol, with a polydispersity index (Đ) of 1.32. Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) analysis revealed a glass transition temperature (Tg) of 142 °C, confirming the high thermal stability of the polymer.

To demonstrate the effect of chain transfer, a parallel polymerization was conducted using 0.5 mol% (relative to monomer) of terpinolene as a chain transfer agent. The same procedure was followed, except that 0.015 grams of terpinolene was added to the reaction mixture prior to initiation. The resulting polymer exhibited an Mn of 11,800 g/mol and an Mw of 15,200 g/mol, with a Đ of 1.28, demonstrating a nearly 50% reduction in molecular weight without broadening of the molecular weight distribution. The Tg of this polymer was measured at 108 °C, consistent with the expected reduction due to lower chain length. FTIR spectroscopy confirmed the retention of the terminal unsaturation from terpinolene, as evidenced by the appearance of a weak vinyl C-H stretch at 3080 cm⁻¹ and a C=C stretch at 1640 cm⁻¹, absent in the control sample.

### Example 2

A series of polymerizations were conducted using the carvone-derived methacrylate monomer to evaluate its suitability for industrial coating applications. The monomer was synthesized according to the two-step protocol described in Example 1, beginning with the reduction of (R)-(-)-carvone using lithium aluminum hydride in anhydrous diethyl ether at 0 °C, followed by esterification with methacrylic acid using T3P®. The resulting methacrylate monomer, 2-(4-methyl-3-(prop-1-en-2-yl)cyclohex-3-en-1-yl)propan-2-yl methacrylate, was purified by distillation under reduced pressure (bp 145–147 °C at 2 mmHg) and characterized by NMR and mass spectrometry.

For powder coating formulation, 100 grams of the purified carvone methacrylate polymer (Mn = 18,500 g/mol, Đ = 1.35) was mixed with 12 grams of trimethylolpropane tris(3-mercaptopropionate) (Thiocure®), 1.5 grams of benzoyl peroxide (as a thermal initiator), 0.8 grams of a commercial flow agent (BYK-333), and 0.5 grams of an ultraviolet stabilizer (Tinuvin 123). The blend was fed into a twin-screw extruder (Haake MiniLab II) operating at 110 °C with a screw speed of 50 rpm. The extruded strand was cooled in a water bath, dried, and ground in a cryogenic mill to produce a fine powder with a particle size distribution of 80% below 90 micrometers.

The powder was applied to pre-cleaned aluminum Q-panels (100 mm × 50 mm × 0.5 mm) using an electrostatic spray gun (Gema G4) at a voltage of 60 kV and an air pressure of 2.5 bar. The coated panels were transferred to a convection oven preheated to 120 °C and held for 2 minutes to allow for flow and film formation. Subsequently, the panels were transferred to a UV curing chamber equipped with a medium-pressure mercury lamp (300 W/cm) and exposed to UV radiation for 5 minutes at a distance of 15 cm. The resulting coating was transparent, glossy, and uniformly adhered to the substrate.

The coating was evaluated according to standard industry protocols. Gloss was measured at 60° using a BYK-mac gloss meter, yielding a value of 92 GU, comparable to commercial acrylic powder coatings. Pencil hardness was assessed using a set of graded pencils (HB to 6H) under a 1 kg load; the coating resisted scratching up to 2B hardness, exceeding the typical 1B–2B range for conventional acrylics. König hardness was determined using a König pendulum device, yielding a damping time of 185 seconds, which is within the range of high-performance acrylic coatings (150–200 seconds). The acetone rub test was performed using a cotton swab soaked in acetone and rubbed 50 times across the surface; no softening, dissolution, or loss of gloss was observed. Adhesion was evaluated using a Gitterschnitt (lattice cutter) tool to create a 1 mm × 1 mm grid pattern, followed by the application and rapid removal of pressure-sensitive tape; less than 5% of the grid squares were removed, indicating excellent adhesion (Grade 5 per ASTM D3359). Impact resistance was tested using a falling weight impact tester (500 g weight dropped from 50 cm); no cracking or delamination was observed. The coating exhibited no visible yellowing after 500 hours of accelerated UV exposure in a QUV chamber, confirming its excellent color stability.

The residual unsaturation in the polymer backbone was confirmed by FTIR spectroscopy, which showed a distinct peak at 1645 cm⁻¹ corresponding to the exocyclic alkene, which remained unreacted during polymerization. This unsaturation was subsequently exploited for post-curing by applying a thiol-functionalized silane coupling agent (Mercaptoethyltrimethoxysilane) to the surface and exposing it to UV light for 10 minutes, resulting in a 30% increase in surface crosslink density as measured by dynamic mechanical analysis.

## CONCLUSIONS

The invention presents a comprehensive and transformative advancement in the field of sustainable polymer science by enabling the efficient, scalable, and environmentally benign production of high-performance polymers derived entirely from renewable terpene feedstocks. Through the development of novel monomers and the discovery of unexpected reactivity patterns, the invention overcomes longstanding limitations that have hindered the commercial adoption of bio-based acrylics. The synthesis of terpene-derived acrylate and methacrylate monomers via a two-step protocol employing propyl phosphonic anhydride eliminates the need for toxic acid chlorides and generates benign, water-soluble byproducts, thereby establishing a green and industrially viable route to polymerizable building blocks. The resulting polymers exhibit a broad and tunable range of thermal and mechanical properties, with glass transition temperatures extending from 12 °C to 142 °C, enabling their application across diverse sectors including coatings, adhesives, packaging, and engineering thermoplastics.

A particularly significant and unexpected finding is the selective polymerization behavior of the carvone methacrylate monomer, which undergoes linear chain growth despite containing two unsaturated functionalities. This behavior, confirmed by spectroscopic and kinetic analyses, arises from the electron-withdrawing nature of the methacrylate ester group, which directs radical addition exclusively to the methacrylate double bond while leaving the exocyclic alkene intact. This unprecedented selectivity permits the formation of high molecular weight, linear polymers with pendant unsaturation, which can be leveraged for post-polymerization functionalization, crosslinking, or surface modification—capabilities that are not accessible with conventional acrylic polymers. The successful formulation of a fully bio-based acrylic powder coating using this polymer, which meets or exceeds industry standards for gloss, hardness, chemical resistance, and adhesion, represents a landmark achievement in sustainable materials science and demonstrates the practical feasibility of replacing petroleum-derived coatings with renewable alternatives without compromising performance.

The introduction of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as chain transfer agents further expands the utility of the invention by providing a renewable, non-toxic, and efficient means to control molecular weight without introducing heteroatoms or compromising polymer integrity. These terpenes function as highly effective chain transfer agents, reducing molecular weight predictably while preserving narrow molecular weight distributions and retaining terminal unsaturation for further chemical modification. Their use enhances the overall bio-content of the polymer system and enables the design of polymers with precisely tailored architectures, broadening the scope of applications to include solution processing, inkjet printing, and functionalized surfaces.

The one-pot catalytic method for the direct allylic oxidation and esterification of β-pinene using palladium acetate and benzoquinone in methacrylic acid as solvent represents a paradigm shift in monomer synthesis. This method achieves high yields and regioselectivity under mild conditions, eliminates multiple synthetic steps, avoids halogenated reagents, and utilizes air as the terminal oxidant, making it uniquely suited for large-scale industrial implementation. The ability to produce monomers directly from terpenes without isolation of intermediates dramatically reduces energy consumption, processing time, and waste generation, further enhancing the sustainability profile of the entire polymer production chain.

Collectively, the invention provides a complete technological platform that integrates feedstock sourcing, monomer synthesis, polymerization control, and end-product formulation into a single, coherent system. The polymers produced are not merely alternatives to petroleum-based materials—they are superior in certain respects, offering enhanced thermal stability, tunable reactivity, and intrinsic functionality that can be exploited for advanced applications. The successful demonstration of industrial-scale processing, including extrusion, powder coating, and UV curing, confirms that these materials are compatible with existing manufacturing infrastructure, thereby facilitating seamless adoption by industry.

The implications of this invention extend beyond the immediate application of terpene-based polymers. It establishes a new paradigm for renewable materials design: one in which the molecular architecture of natural products is not merely preserved but strategically engineered to unlock novel functionalities. By leveraging the inherent structural complexity and chirality of terpenes, the invention opens the door to a new class of bio-based materials with properties that cannot be easily replicated from petroleum feedstocks. This approach not only reduces dependence on fossil resources but also creates value from underutilized agricultural and forestry byproducts, contributing to circular economy objectives.
