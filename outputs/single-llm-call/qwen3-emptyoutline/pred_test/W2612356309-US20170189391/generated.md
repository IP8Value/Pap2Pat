# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and targeted cancer therapeutics, specifically to the use of protein kinase C inhibitors for the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer. More particularly, the invention encompasses the selective inhibition of protein kinase C isoforms, including but not limited to PKN2 and PRKCA, through the administration of chelerythrine or structurally and functionally analogous compounds, for the purpose of inducing apoptosis and suppressing proliferation in triple-negative breast cancer cells. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising such inhibitors, methods of administration, and therapeutic regimens tailored to patients diagnosed with triple-negative breast cancer, particularly those who have demonstrated resistance to conventional chemotherapy or who exhibit elevated expression levels of PKN2 and/or PRKCA. The invention also extends to diagnostic methods for identifying patients likely to respond to such therapy based on the molecular profiling of tumor tissue, thereby enabling personalized treatment strategies. The utility of the invention lies in its ability to address a critical unmet medical need in oncology, as triple-negative breast cancer currently lacks approved targeted therapies and remains associated with poor prognosis, high rates of recurrence, and limited survival outcomes following disease progression.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women globally, with an estimated incidence exceeding one and a half million new cases annually and a mortality rate that continues to pose a significant public health burden. Despite advances in early detection and systemic treatment modalities, a distinct subset of breast cancers—termed triple-negative breast cancer—continues to present formidable clinical challenges due to the absence of expression of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2. This molecular phenotype renders these tumors unresponsive to endocrine therapies and anti-HER2 agents, which constitute the cornerstone of treatment for the majority of breast cancer subtypes. As a consequence, chemotherapy remains the primary systemic intervention for triple-negative breast cancer, despite its limited efficacy, substantial toxicity, and transient nature of response. The majority of patients initially respond to neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapeutic regimens, yet a significant proportion experience rapid disease recurrence, often with metastatic dissemination to distant organs such as the lungs and brain, where therapeutic options are even more constrained. The median survival for patients with metastatic triple-negative breast cancer remains disappointingly low, and no targeted agent has yet received regulatory approval specifically for this indication, underscoring the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies grounded in a deeper understanding of the molecular drivers of this aggressive disease.

Protein kinase C represents a family of serine/threonine kinases that regulate a broad spectrum of cellular processes, including proliferation, survival, migration, and apoptosis. Comprising at least twelve distinct isoforms grouped into conventional, novel, and atypical subfamilies based on their structural and regulatory characteristics, protein kinase C enzymes have long been implicated in the pathogenesis of numerous malignancies. In breast cancer, the role of specific isoforms has been the subject of extensive investigation, with particular emphasis on PRKCA, the alpha isoform, which has been shown to be overexpressed in malignant breast tissue compared to normal epithelium and to correlate with resistance to anti-estrogen therapies and increased tumor aggressiveness. However, prior to the present invention, no comprehensive analysis had established a consistent and pan-triple-negative breast cancer-specific dependency on any single protein kinase C isoform. While some studies had suggested a link between PRKCA expression and triple-negative breast cancer in select cell lines and patient cohorts, the heterogeneity of the disease and the lack of uniform overexpression across all triple-negative breast cancer models left open the question of whether inhibition of PRKCA alone would be sufficient to elicit a broad therapeutic effect. Moreover, the pharmacological tools available at the time were either insufficiently selective, lacked adequate bioavailability, or demonstrated off-target effects that precluded their reliable use in vivo.

The discovery that chelerythrine, a naturally occurring benzophenanthridine alkaloid derived from Chelidonium majus, exhibits potent and selective anti-proliferative activity against triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, while sparing non-triple-negative breast cancer cells, represented a pivotal observation. Chelerythrine had previously been characterized as a potent inhibitor of protein kinase C activity due to its ability to bind competitively at the catalytic domain shared across multiple isoforms. However, its potential utility in breast cancer had not been systematically explored, and its mechanism of selectivity for triple-negative breast cancer remained entirely uncharacterized. Prior investigations into chelerythrine had focused primarily on its cytotoxic effects in hematological malignancies and other solid tumors, without establishing a clear correlation between its activity and the molecular subtype of breast cancer. The prevailing assumption in the field had been that any anti-cancer effect observed with broad-spectrum kinase inhibitors would be attributable to general cytotoxicity rather than target-specific modulation. Consequently, there was no prior art suggesting that chelerythrine could serve as a precision therapeutic agent for triple-negative breast cancer based on the differential expression of specific protein kinase C isoforms.

Furthermore, the absence of validated molecular biomarkers for predicting response to protein kinase C inhibition in breast cancer had hindered the development of targeted approaches. Although gene expression databases such as the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia had been available for years, they had not been interrogated in a hypothesis-driven manner to identify protein kinase C isoforms that were consistently and significantly overexpressed in triple-negative breast cancer relative to other subtypes. No prior study had demonstrated that PKN2, a member of the atypical protein kinase C subfamily, is ubiquitously elevated across all tested triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, nor had any prior work established that its knockdown is sufficient to recapitulate the anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic effects of chelerythrine. The notion that PKN2, rather than PRKCA or other isoforms, serves as the primary mediator of chelerythrine’s selectivity in triple-negative breast cancer was entirely unforeseen. Similarly, the observation that chelerythrine induces apoptosis in triple-negative breast cancer cells through mechanisms involving caspase-mediated cleavage of PKN2 and downstream modulation of Akt signaling had not been previously described or even hypothesized. The failure of prior efforts to identify a targeted therapy for triple-negative breast cancer had led many in the field to conclude that the disease was driven by a complex, heterogeneous network of oncogenic signals that could not be effectively suppressed by inhibition of a single node. The present invention overturns this paradigm by demonstrating that the simultaneous inhibition of a small number of highly expressed protein kinase C isoforms—particularly PKN2 and PRKCA—is sufficient to trigger a robust and selective apoptotic response in triple-negative breast cancer cells, both in vitro and in vivo.

In addition, the clinical context of triple-negative breast cancer presents unique therapeutic opportunities that had been overlooked. Patients with this subtype are more likely to exhibit metabolic comorbidities such as obesity and insulin resistance, conditions associated with chronic activation of protein kinase C signaling in vascular and adipose tissues. Given that chelerythrine has been shown in preclinical models to ameliorate endothelial dysfunction induced by metabolic stress, the present invention not only proposes a novel anti-tumor strategy but also identifies a potential dual benefit: the suppression of tumor growth concurrent with the mitigation of cardiovascular complications that contribute significantly to non-cancer-related mortality in this patient population. No prior therapeutic approach for triple-negative breast cancer has attempted to leverage this intersection between oncogenic signaling and metabolic pathology. The absence of any approved targeted therapy for triple-negative breast cancer, coupled with the high rate of chemoresistance and the lack of predictive biomarkers, creates a critical gap in clinical oncology that the present invention is uniquely positioned to fill. The invention therefore represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer, moving beyond empirical cytotoxic chemotherapy toward a mechanism-based, biomarker-driven therapeutic strategy grounded in the selective inhibition of protein kinase C isoforms essential for tumor cell survival.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious therapeutic approach for the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer through the selective inhibition of protein kinase C isoforms, particularly PKN2 and PRKCA, using chelerythrine or pharmacologically equivalent compounds. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that triple-negative breast cancer cells exhibit a consistent and pronounced dependence on the expression and activity of PKN2, a member of the atypical protein kinase C family, and that this dependence is not shared by non-triple-negative breast cancer cells. Furthermore, the invention demonstrates that chelerythrine, a naturally derived pan-protein kinase C inhibitor, selectively induces apoptosis and suppresses proliferation in triple-negative breast cancer cell lines at concentrations that have minimal effect on non-triple-negative breast cancer cells, thereby establishing a therapeutic window that is both clinically relevant and mechanistically grounded. The invention further reveals that the anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic effects of chelerythrine are mediated primarily through the inhibition of PKN2, as confirmed by genetic knockdown studies that phenocopy the effects of pharmacological inhibition, and that PRKCA, while not universally overexpressed, contributes to the sensitivity of a subset of triple-negative breast cancer cells, suggesting that dual inhibition of these isoforms may yield enhanced therapeutic efficacy.

The invention encompasses a method of treating triple-negative breast cancer in a subject in need thereof by administering an effective amount of chelerythrine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, ester, prodrug, or analog thereof, wherein the subject has been identified as having elevated expression levels of PKN2 and/or PRKCA in tumor tissue, as determined by immunohistochemistry, quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, or next-generation sequencing. The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising chelerythrine or a derivative thereof in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent, formulated for oral, intravenous, intraperitoneal, or subcutaneous administration, with dosing regimens optimized to achieve sustained inhibition of PKN2 and PRKCA activity while minimizing systemic toxicity. The invention also includes a method of enhancing the efficacy of conventional chemotherapy in triple-negative breast cancer by co-administering chelerythrine with taxane-based agents such as paclitaxel or docetaxel, wherein the combination produces synergistic or additive anti-proliferative effects, as demonstrated by combination index values below 1.1, thereby enabling dose reduction of cytotoxic agents and mitigating their associated adverse effects.

The invention further provides a diagnostic method for identifying patients with triple-negative breast cancer who are likely to respond to chelerythrine-based therapy, comprising the steps of obtaining a tumor biopsy or liquid biopsy sample from the patient, quantifying the mRNA or protein expression levels of PKN2 and PRKCA in the sample, and determining that the expression levels of one or both isoforms are significantly elevated relative to non-triple-negative breast cancer controls, thereby indicating eligibility for treatment with chelerythrine or a related compound. The invention further includes a kit for performing such diagnostic testing, comprising reagents for RNA extraction, reverse transcription, quantitative PCR, or immunoblotting, along with instructions for interpreting expression thresholds predictive of therapeutic response. The invention also encompasses a method of reducing tumor burden in a subject with triple-negative breast cancer by administering chelerythrine in vivo, wherein the reduction in tumor volume is accompanied by a measurable increase in apoptotic markers such as cleaved poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase and a decrease in proliferative markers such as Ki-67, as assessed by histopathological analysis of tumor tissue.

The invention further provides evidence that chelerythrine inhibits the growth of triple-negative breast cancer xenografts in immunocompromised mice at a dose of 5 mg/kg administered intraperitoneally every three to four days, with significant suppression of tumor growth observed without evidence of systemic toxicity, weight loss, or organ damage. The invention further demonstrates that chelerythrine enhances the anti-tumor activity of paclitaxel in vivo, resulting in greater tumor regression than either agent alone, thereby supporting the clinical development of combination regimens. The invention further includes the discovery that chelerythrine induces apoptosis in triple-negative breast cancer cells through caspase-3-mediated cleavage of PKN2, leading to the generation of a C-terminal fragment that binds to and inhibits Akt kinase activity, thereby amplifying pro-apoptotic signaling pathways. This mechanism is absent in non-triple-negative breast cancer cells, which do not exhibit sufficient PKN2 expression to support this cascade, thereby explaining the selectivity of chelerythrine’s action. The invention further encompasses the use of chelerythrine to treat triple-negative breast cancer patients who have failed prior chemotherapy, including those with metastatic disease, recurrent disease, or disease resistant to platinum-based regimens. The invention further provides a method of improving survival outcomes in triple-negative breast cancer patients by initiating chelerythrine therapy in the neoadjuvant, adjuvant, or metastatic setting, with the goal of achieving durable remission or prolonged disease stabilization.

The invention further discloses that chelerythrine may confer additional clinical benefits beyond direct tumor suppression, including the amelioration of metabolic dysfunction and endothelial impairment associated with obesity and insulin resistance, conditions prevalent in triple-negative breast cancer patients and known to contribute to cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. By inhibiting protein kinase C isoforms implicated in both tumorigenesis and vascular pathology, chelerythrine offers a dual therapeutic advantage that is unprecedented in the current treatment landscape for triple-negative breast cancer. The invention thus represents a comprehensive, multi-faceted approach to targeting triple-negative breast cancer that integrates molecular diagnostics, targeted pharmacology, combination therapy, and the mitigation of treatment-related comorbidities, thereby addressing the full spectrum of clinical challenges associated with this aggressive disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer through the selective inhibition of protein kinase C isoforms, with a particular emphasis on the isoforms PKN2 and PRKCA. The invention is grounded in the unexpected and non-obvious discovery that triple-negative breast cancer cells exhibit a unique and consistent dependency on the expression and activity of these isoforms, which is not shared by other molecular subtypes of breast cancer, and that pharmacological inhibition of these kinases using chelerythrine results in a potent, selective, and reproducible induction of apoptosis and suppression of proliferation in triple-negative breast cancer cells both in vitro and in vivo. The invention further encompasses the development of pharmaceutical compositions, diagnostic assays, and combination regimens that leverage this mechanistic insight to provide a clinically viable and targeted approach to a disease that has historically resisted all attempts at precision therapy. The following description details the preferred embodiments of the invention, including the composition of matter, methods of administration, diagnostic criteria, combination therapies, and preclinical validation studies that support the utility and novelty of the invention.

The active agent of the invention is chelerythrine, a naturally occurring benzophenanthridine alkaloid isolated from the plant Chelidonium majus. Chelerythrine is a potent, cell-permeable, and reversible inhibitor of protein kinase C enzymes, acting by competitively binding to the catalytic domain shared among multiple isoforms, thereby preventing ATP binding and subsequent phosphorylation of downstream substrates. While chelerythrine has been previously characterized as a broad-spectrum protein kinase C inhibitor, its application in oncology has been limited to studies in hematological malignancies and non-breast solid tumors, with no prior investigation into its effects on breast cancer subtypes. The present invention demonstrates, for the first time, that chelerythrine exerts a highly selective anti-proliferative effect on triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, with IC50 values ranging from 2.6 to 4.2 micromolar, while non-triple-negative breast cancer cell lines remain largely unaffected even at concentrations exceeding 10 micromolar. This selectivity is not attributable to differences in drug uptake or efflux, as confirmed by intracellular concentration measurements, nor is it due to general cytotoxicity, as evidenced by the lack of effect on non-malignant mammary epithelial cells and a wide range of non-breast cell lines. The selectivity is instead linked to the differential expression of specific protein kinase C isoforms, particularly PKN2, which is uniformly overexpressed at both the mRNA and protein levels across all tested triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, including MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, and MDA-MB-468, whereas its expression is significantly lower or absent in non-triple-negative breast cancer cell lines such as MCF7, ZR-75-1, SK-BR-3, and MDA-MB-453. This finding was corroborated through analysis of the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia database, which revealed that PKN2 mRNA expression is significantly elevated in triple-negative breast cancer cell lines compared to non-triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, with a mean log2 fold change exceeding 1.5 and statistical significance of p < 0.001. The same analysis identified PRKCA as another isoform with elevated expression in a subset of triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, particularly MDA-MB-231 and BT-549, suggesting that while PKN2 serves as a universal marker of sensitivity, PRKCA may act as a co-determinant of response in a subset of tumors.

The mechanism by which chelerythrine induces cell death in triple-negative breast cancer cells is not through cell cycle arrest, as previously observed with many chemotherapeutic agents, but rather through the rapid and robust induction of apoptosis. Treatment of triple-negative breast cancer cells with chelerythrine at concentrations of 5 micromolar results in the characteristic morphological features of apoptosis, including chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, and membrane blebbing, as visualized by Hoechst 33258 staining and fluorescence microscopy. These morphological changes are absent in non-triple-negative breast cancer cells treated under identical conditions. Biochemical confirmation of apoptosis is provided by the detection of cleaved nuclear poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase, a well-established marker of caspase-mediated cell death, which is consistently upregulated in triple-negative breast cancer cells following chelerythrine exposure but remains undetectable in non-triple-negative breast cancer cells. Flow cytometric analysis using annexin V and propidium iodide staining quantifies the proportion of apoptotic cells, revealing that 50 to 70 percent of triple-negative breast cancer cells undergo apoptosis within 24 hours of treatment, whereas less than 5 percent of non-triple-negative breast cancer cells exhibit similar levels of cell death. This difference is both statistically significant and biologically relevant, as it translates into a profound reduction in clonogenic potential, as demonstrated by colony formation assays in which chelerythrine treatment abolishes the ability of triple-negative breast cancer cells to form macroscopic colonies after only six hours of exposure, whereas non-triple-negative breast cancer cells retain nearly full colony-forming capacity even after 48 hours of treatment. The induction of apoptosis is dose- and time-dependent, with increasing concentrations of chelerythrine and prolonged exposure leading to a progressive increase in the percentage of apoptotic cells, further supporting a direct causal relationship between protein kinase C inhibition and cell death.

The critical role of PKN2 in mediating the sensitivity of triple-negative breast cancer cells to chelerythrine is established through genetic loss-of-function studies. Stable knockdown of PKN2 using lentiviral-delivered short hairpin RNA results in a dramatic reduction in colony formation in triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, mirroring the effect of chelerythrine treatment, while having no significant impact on the clonogenic potential of non-triple-negative breast cancer cell lines. This genetic ablation phenocopies the pharmacological inhibition of protein kinase C, confirming that PKN2 is not merely correlated with sensitivity but is functionally required for the survival of triple-negative breast cancer cells. Western blot analysis confirms efficient knockdown of PKN2 protein expression, with reductions exceeding 80 percent in all tested triple-negative breast cancer cell lines. The specificity of this effect is further validated by the observation that knockdown of other protein kinase C isoforms, including PRKCA, does not recapitulate the same degree of growth suppression in all triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, as PRKCA knockdown only affects those cell lines in which it is highly expressed. This indicates that PKN2 is the primary mediator of chelerythrine’s activity across the broad spectrum of triple-negative breast cancer, whereas PRKCA contributes to sensitivity in a subset of tumors. The functional importance of PKN2 is further confirmed in vivo, where xenograft models of triple-negative breast cancer derived from MDA-MB-231 cells transduced with PKN2-targeting shRNA exhibit significantly reduced tumor growth compared to control tumors derived from cells expressing a non-targeting shRNA, whereas xenografts derived from non-triple-negative breast cancer cells (ZR-75-1) are unaffected by PKN2 knockdown. This demonstrates that PKN2 is not only necessary for the in vitro survival of triple-negative breast cancer cells but is also essential for their in vivo tumorigenic potential.

The mechanism by which PKN2 inhibition leads to apoptosis is elucidated through biochemical analysis of signaling pathways. Chelerythrine treatment triggers the activation of caspase-3, which in turn cleaves PKN2 at a specific site within its regulatory domain, generating a C-terminal fragment that retains the kinase domain but loses its autoinhibitory region. This truncated form of PKN2 translocates to the cytoplasm and binds directly to Akt, a key pro-survival kinase, thereby inhibiting its phosphorylation and activation. The suppression of Akt signaling leads to the dephosphorylation and activation of downstream pro-apoptotic effectors, including Bad, FoxO transcription factors, and GSK3β, while simultaneously reducing the expression of anti-apoptotic proteins such as Bcl-2 and Mcl-1. This cascade results in mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization, cytochrome c release, and amplification of the caspase cascade, culminating in irreversible cell death. This mechanism is specific to triple-negative breast cancer cells, as non-triple-negative breast cancer cells, which express negligible levels of PKN2, do not generate the cleaved fragment upon chelerythrine exposure and therefore fail to activate this apoptotic pathway. The absence of this mechanism in non-triple-negative breast cancer cells explains the remarkable selectivity of chelerythrine and provides a molecular rationale for its therapeutic window. Importantly, this mechanism is distinct from the known effects of chelerythrine in other cell types, such as the induction of reactive oxygen species or inhibition of heat shock proteins, which are not observed in the triple-negative breast cancer models studied herein, further supporting the uniqueness of the identified pathway.

The therapeutic potential of chelerythrine is further demonstrated in vivo through xenograft studies in immunocompromised nude mice. Subcutaneous injection of MDA-MB-231 triple-negative breast cancer cells into both hind limbs of mice results in the formation of palpable tumors within seven to ten days. Administration of chelerythrine via intraperitoneal injection at a dose of 5 mg/kg every three to four days leads to a significant suppression of tumor growth, with tumor volumes reduced by more than 60 percent compared to vehicle-treated controls after 79 days of treatment. In contrast, tumors derived from non-triple-negative breast cancer cells (ZR-75-1) show no significant response to chelerythrine treatment, confirming the selectivity observed in vitro. Histological analysis of excised tumors reveals a marked increase in apoptotic cells, as evidenced by TUNEL staining and cleaved caspase-3 immunoreactivity, and a corresponding decrease in proliferative cells, as indicated by Ki-67 staining, in chelerythrine-treated triple-negative breast cancer xenografts. Importantly, no significant weight loss, organ toxicity, or behavioral abnormalities are observed in treated mice, indicating that the dose regimen is well tolerated and that systemic inhibition of protein kinase C does not result in unacceptable toxicity. These findings provide compelling preclinical evidence that chelerythrine can be safely administered in vivo to achieve therapeutic efficacy against triple-negative breast cancer.

The invention further encompasses the use of chelerythrine in combination with standard chemotherapy agents, particularly taxanes such as paclitaxel and docetaxel, to enhance anti-tumor activity and overcome chemoresistance. Combination studies conducted in vitro demonstrate that co-treatment of triple-negative breast cancer cell lines with chelerythrine at 2 micromolar and paclitaxel at 10 nanomolar results in a synergistic or additive reduction in cell viability, as quantified by combination index values ranging from 0.75 to 1.14, with values below 0.9 indicating synergy and values between 0.9 and 1.1 indicating additive effects. This effect is observed across all four tested triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, suggesting that the combination is broadly applicable. The combination is more effective than either agent alone, with tumor cell viability reduced by 80 to 90 percent in combination-treated cultures compared to 40 to 60 percent with single-agent treatment. In vivo xenograft studies confirm this finding, as mice bearing MDA-MB-231 tumors treated with a combination of chelerythrine and paclitaxel exhibit significantly greater tumor regression than those treated with either agent alone, with some animals achieving near-complete tumor disappearance. The combination allows for dose reduction of paclitaxel by up to 50 percent while maintaining equivalent efficacy, thereby reducing the risk of neurotoxicity, myelosuppression, and hypersensitivity reactions associated with taxane therapy. This combination strategy represents a major advancement in the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer, as it leverages the targeted action of chelerythrine to sensitize tumors to conventional chemotherapy, thereby extending the utility of existing drugs and improving their therapeutic index.

The invention further provides a method for identifying patients who are most likely to benefit from chelerythrine-based therapy through molecular diagnostics. The preferred diagnostic approach involves the assessment of PKN2 and PRKCA expression levels in tumor tissue obtained via core needle biopsy or surgical resection. Expression levels are quantified using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction for mRNA or immunohistochemistry for protein, with thresholds established based on the expression profiles observed in the cell line panel. A tumor is classified as eligible for chelerythrine therapy if PKN2 expression exceeds a log2 fold change of 1.2 relative to the median expression in non-triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, or if PRKCA expression exceeds a log2 fold change of 0.8. Alternatively, a composite score may be generated by combining the expression levels of both isoforms, with a score above 2.0 indicating high likelihood of response. The invention further includes a diagnostic kit comprising primers and probes for PKN2 and PRKCA, reverse transcriptase, PCR master mix, positive and negative controls, and a standardized interpretation guide for clinicians. The kit may be used in conjunction with formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue or fresh-frozen samples, and may be adapted for use in clinical laboratories with standard molecular pathology infrastructure. Liquid biopsy approaches using circulating tumor DNA or RNA may also be employed, with specific methylation or expression signatures of PKN2 and PRKCA serving as surrogate markers of tumor burden and target expression.

The invention further encompasses the formulation of chelerythrine into pharmaceutically acceptable compositions suitable for human administration. Chelerythrine is a lipophilic compound with limited aqueous solubility, and therefore requires formulation optimization for systemic delivery. Preferred formulations include liposomal encapsulation, nanoparticle dispersion, or conjugation with polyethylene glycol to enhance bioavailability and prolong plasma half-life. Oral formulations may be prepared using solid dispersion techniques with hydrophilic polymers such as hydroxypropyl methylcellulose or polyvinylpyrrolidone to improve dissolution and absorption. Intravenous formulations may be prepared in sterile saline or dextrose solutions with co-solvents such as cremophor EL or polysorbate 80, or alternatively, in cyclodextrin-based complexes to enhance solubility without inducing hypersensitivity. The preferred dosage range for intravenous administration is 1 to 10 mg/kg per dose, administered every 72 to 96 hours, with a total cumulative dose not exceeding 100 mg/kg over a 12-week treatment cycle. For oral administration, the preferred dose range is 5 to 20 mg/kg daily, with treatment cycles of 21 days followed by a 7-day rest period. The invention further includes sustained-release formulations, such as biodegradable polymer implants or microspheres, for localized delivery to the tumor site or for prolonged systemic exposure. The invention further encompasses the use of chelerythrine in combination with other targeted agents, such as PARP inhibitors, PI3K inhibitors, or immune checkpoint inhibitors, to further enhance anti-tumor efficacy and overcome potential resistance mechanisms.

The invention further provides evidence that chelerythrine may confer additional clinical benefits beyond direct tumor suppression. Triple-negative breast cancer patients have a higher prevalence of metabolic syndrome, obesity, and insulin resistance, conditions associated with chronic activation of protein kinase C isoforms in adipose tissue and vascular endothelium. Chelerythrine has been shown in rodent models to improve endothelial function in the context of diabetes-induced vascular dysfunction by inhibiting protein kinase C-mediated oxidative stress and nitric oxide depletion. By simultaneously targeting tumor cells and the metabolic microenvironment, chelerythrine may reduce the risk of cardiovascular complications, which are a leading cause of non-cancer-related mortality in breast cancer survivors. This dual benefit—anti-tumor efficacy and cardiovascular protection—represents a unique therapeutic advantage not offered by any existing treatment for triple-negative breast cancer. The invention therefore encompasses the use of chelerythrine not only as an anti-cancer agent but also as a cardiometabolic modulator in patients with triple-negative breast cancer and coexisting metabolic disorders.

The invention further includes methods for monitoring therapeutic response and resistance. Serial imaging studies, such as magnetic resonance imaging or positron emission tomography, may be used to assess tumor volume and metabolic activity. Circulating tumor DNA may be analyzed for mutations or copy number alterations in PKN2 or PRKCA to detect the emergence of resistance mutations. Changes in serum biomarkers, such as cleaved PKN2 fragments or apoptotic markers, may be measured to assess target engagement and biological activity. The invention further provides for adaptive dosing regimens based on pharmacodynamic monitoring, with dose escalation or de-escalation guided by biomarker levels, tumor response, and toxicity profile.

In summary, the invention provides a comprehensive, mechanistically grounded, and clinically translatable approach to the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer through the selective inhibition of protein kinase C isoforms, particularly PKN2 and PRKCA, using chelerythrine. The invention overcomes the historical failure to identify a targeted therapy for this disease by uncovering a previously unrecognized dependency on these kinases, elucidating the molecular mechanism of selective apoptosis, validating the therapeutic effect in vivo, and establishing diagnostic criteria for patient selection. The invention further enhances the utility of existing chemotherapy agents through synergistic combination, reduces systemic toxicity through targeted action, and offers the potential for improved long-term survival by mitigating treatment-related comorbidities. The invention therefore represents a major advance in oncology and provides a new standard of care for patients with triple-negative breast cancer.
