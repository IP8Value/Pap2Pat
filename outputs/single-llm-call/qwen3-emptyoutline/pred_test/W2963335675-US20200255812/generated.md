# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and enzyme engineering, specifically to novel variants of lipase enzymes derived from Rhizomucor miehei and their use in industrial applications requiring controlled enzymatic activity. More particularly, the invention encompasses polynucleotides encoding prolipase constructs, expression vectors containing such polynucleotides, host cells transformed with such vectors, and methods for the production of prolipase variants that exhibit a stable, inhibited conformation due to the presence of an intact propeptide domain. The invention further includes purified prolipase proteins, methods for their isolation and characterization, and the use of these proteins as templates for the rational design of lipase variants with enhanced stability, altered substrate specificity, or delayed activation kinetics. These inventions are particularly useful in detergent formulations, lipid processing, biodiesel production, food processing, and other industrial contexts where uncontrolled lipase activity during production, storage, or transport is undesirable. The disclosed prolipase variants provide a structural and functional basis for the development of enzyme systems that remain inert until activated under specific environmental conditions, thereby enabling precise temporal and spatial control over lipolytic activity.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Lipases are a class of hydrolase enzymes that catalyze the cleavage of ester bonds in triglycerides and other lipid substrates. Among the most industrially significant lipases are those belonging to class 3, which are characterized by an α/β-hydrolase fold and a conserved catalytic triad composed of serine, histidine, and aspartic acid residues. These enzymes are widely used in detergent formulations, food processing, and biofuel production due to their ability to function at interfaces and their broad substrate specificity. However, one persistent challenge in the industrial application of such enzymes is their inherent instability during production and storage. Many lipases are synthesized as precursor proteins containing an N-terminal propeptide domain that is proteolytically removed to yield the mature, active enzyme. This propeptide has been implicated in facilitating proper folding, but its role in modulating enzymatic activity has remained poorly understood.

Prior art has described the mature form of Rhizomucor miehei lipase (RmL) in both open and closed conformations, with the closed form representing an inactive state in which the active site is occluded by a mobile α-helical lid domain. However, no structural or functional characterization of the full-length prolipase form—comprising the mature enzyme domain covalently linked to its N-terminal propeptide—had been achieved prior to the present invention. Earlier studies had identified the presence of a propeptide in RmL through cDNA sequencing and suggested its involvement in intracellular folding, but no experimental evidence demonstrated its direct inhibitory effect on enzymatic activity or its structural mechanism of action. Moreover, attempts to purify and isolate the intact prolipase had been unsuccessful due to spontaneous proteolytic cleavage during expression and purification, leading to the predominant isolation of the mature enzyme and free propeptide fragments.

The absence of structural data on the prolipase form precluded any rational engineering of the propeptide to modulate enzyme behavior. Conventional mutagenesis approaches focused on the mature enzyme domain, often resulting in unpredictable changes in activity, stability, or expression yield. Furthermore, the lack of a stable, non-cleaved prolipase variant meant that the inhibitory function of the propeptide could not be exploited for controlled-release applications. While some studies had reported mutations in the propeptide region that altered lipase activity, these were interpreted without structural context, and no correlation between specific propeptide residues and their role in active site occlusion had been established. Consequently, the field lacked a mechanistic understanding of how the propeptide physically interacts with the mature enzyme to suppress activity, and there was no precedent for designing prolipase variants that retain the propeptide in a stable, non-covalent complex with the mature domain.

The present invention overcomes these longstanding limitations by providing the first high-resolution crystal structures of the full-length RmL prolipase and a stabilized deletion variant, revealing the precise molecular interactions by which the propeptide binds to and inhibits the mature enzyme. These structures demonstrate that the propeptide wraps around the surface of the mature enzyme, directly contacting the lid domain and burying key hydrophobic residues essential for lipid substrate binding. The propeptide stabilizes the closed conformation of the lid, preventing its transition to the open, active state. This inhibition is not merely a consequence of steric hindrance but involves a network of hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and hydrophobic interactions that collectively lock the enzyme in an inactive form. The invention further provides a method for generating and isolating a stable prolipase variant, ProRmL-del, in which deletion of two residues at the cleavage site prevents spontaneous proteolysis, allowing the intact complex to be purified and characterized without degradation.

This breakthrough enables the development of a new class of engineered lipases that remain inactive during production, storage, and formulation, and are only activated upon exposure to specific environmental triggers such as pH shift, temperature change, or proteolytic cleavage. Such controlled activation systems are particularly valuable in detergent formulations, where premature lipase activity can lead to degradation of fabric components or formulation instability. The invention also provides a structural blueprint for the design of analogous prolipase systems in other class 3 lipases, thereby extending its utility beyond RmL to a broad range of industrially relevant enzymes. The ability to engineer lipases with programmable activation kinetics represents a significant advancement over prior art, which relied on external inhibitors, encapsulation technologies, or post-production activation steps that were inefficient, costly, or lacked precision.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of lipase variants derived from Rhizomucor miehei that maintain an inhibitory propeptide domain in a stable, non-covalent complex with the mature enzyme, resulting in a conformationally locked, inactive state. These prolipase variants are engineered to resist spontaneous proteolytic cleavage, enabling their isolation in intact form and their use as precisely controlled enzyme precursors. The invention encompasses polynucleotides encoding these variants, expression vectors containing such polynucleotides, host cells transformed with such vectors, and methods for their production and purification. The prolipase variants exhibit a unique structural architecture in which the propeptide domain binds to the mature enzyme in a manner that buries the catalytic triad and stabilizes the closed conformation of the lid domain, thereby preventing substrate access and enzymatic activity. This inhibition is reversible and can be triggered by specific environmental conditions, including proteolytic cleavage, pH change, or thermal denaturation.

The invention further provides purified prolipase proteins, including a stabilized variant designated ProRmL-del, which lacks two residues at the cleavage site between the propeptide and mature enzyme domains, thereby preventing endogenous proteolysis and allowing the intact complex to be isolated in high yield. The structure of ProRmL-del, determined by X-ray crystallography at high resolution, reveals extensive molecular interactions between the propeptide and the mature enzyme, including hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and hydrophobic contacts that collectively stabilize the inactive conformation. Key residues involved in this interaction include Leu81 of the propeptide, which forms a critical hydrophobic interaction with Ile183 of the lid domain and a hydrogen bond with Val348 of the mature enzyme, and Lys76 and Tyr77, which form additional hydrogen bonds with backbone atoms of the lid. The propeptide also covers hydrophobic residues on the surface of the mature enzyme, including Ile298, Phe345, and Val348, thereby masking the lipid-binding interface and reducing the enzyme’s affinity for lipid substrates.

The invention further provides methods for the production of these prolipase variants through recombinant expression in fungal hosts such as Aspergillus oryzae, followed by purification using a combination of hydrophobic interaction chromatography and ion-exchange chromatography. The purified prolipase variants are shown to exhibit no detectable lipase activity in standard activity assays, including zymography and fluorogenic substrate assays, until the propeptide is removed by proteolytic cleavage or denaturation. The invention further demonstrates that the isolated propeptide domain, when added exogenously to the mature enzyme, acts as a potent competitive inhibitor with a dissociation constant of approximately 0.2 μM, confirming that the propeptide alone is sufficient to inhibit enzymatic activity.

The invention further extends to the use of these prolipase variants as templates for the rational design of lipase variants with tailored activation profiles. By introducing mutations into the propeptide domain, the strength and kinetics of inhibition can be modulated, allowing for the development of enzymes that activate only under specific industrial conditions. For example, mutations that weaken the propeptide-mature enzyme interface can result in variants that activate more readily at elevated temperatures, while mutations that strengthen the interface can produce variants that require proteolytic cleavage for activation. The invention further includes methods for identifying and selecting such variants through high-throughput screening, structural modeling, and functional assays.

The invention also provides compositions comprising the prolipase variants in combination with detergents, surfactants, stabilizers, or other formulation components, wherein the lipase remains inactive until activated by the intended application environment. Such compositions offer improved shelf life, reduced formulation instability, and enhanced performance upon activation. The invention further encompasses diagnostic and analytical methods for detecting the presence of the prolipase complex in biological or industrial samples, based on its unique structural signature, binding affinity, or resistance to conventional purification methods.

In summary, the invention provides a fundamentally new approach to lipase engineering by exploiting the natural inhibitory function of the propeptide domain to create enzyme systems with programmable activation kinetics. This represents a significant departure from prior art, which treated the propeptide as a folding aid to be removed, and instead leverages it as a molecular switch for precise control over enzymatic activity. The invention enables the development of next-generation lipase products with enhanced stability, reduced off-target activity, and improved performance in industrial applications.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definition

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “prolipase” refers to a polypeptide construct comprising a mature lipase domain derived from Rhizomucor miehei covalently linked to its native N-terminal propeptide domain, wherein the propeptide domain is retained in a non-cleaved, non-covalent complex with the mature domain, resulting in a conformationally inhibited state. The mature lipase domain corresponds to residues 95 to 363 of the precursor polypeptide, while the propeptide domain corresponds to residues 30 to 94, with the numbering based on the full-length precursor sequence as defined by UniProt entry P19515. The term “ProRmL-del” refers to a specific variant of the prolipase in which residues Ser95 and Ile96 of the mature enzyme domain are deleted, thereby eliminating the natural proteolytic cleavage site and preventing spontaneous separation of the propeptide from the mature enzyme. The term “inhibited state” refers to the conformational configuration of the prolipase in which the catalytic triad is buried beneath the propeptide domain and the lid domain is stabilized in a closed position, thereby preventing substrate access and enzymatic activity. The term “activation” refers to the process by which the prolipase transitions from the inhibited state to an active state, either through proteolytic cleavage of the propeptide, denaturation of the propeptide-mature enzyme interface, or exposure to specific environmental conditions such as elevated temperature or altered pH. The term “propeptide domain” encompasses the entire N-terminal segment of the prolipase that is responsible for the inhibition of enzymatic activity, including residues that directly contact the mature enzyme as well as those that contribute to structural stability. The term “mature enzyme domain” refers to the catalytically competent portion of the lipase that, when liberated from the propeptide, exhibits full lipolytic activity. The term “non-covalent complex” refers to the association between the propeptide and the mature enzyme that is mediated by hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, hydrophobic interactions, and van der Waals forces, without the formation of a covalent bond between the two domains. The term “stabilized prolipase variant” refers to any prolipase construct in which the propeptide remains bound to the mature enzyme under conditions that would normally cause proteolytic cleavage, thereby enabling purification and storage of the intact complex. The term “industrial application” encompasses any process in which lipase activity is required, including but not limited to detergent formulations, lipid hydrolysis, biodiesel production, food processing, paper manufacturing, and textile treatment.

### Conventions for Designation of Variants

Variants of the prolipase are designated according to a standardized nomenclature that reflects the origin of the sequence, the nature of the modification, and the position of the alteration. The base sequence is derived from the full-length precursor of Rhizomucor miehei lipase as defined by UniProt entry P19515, with residue numbering beginning at the first amino acid of the signal peptide. The mature enzyme domain is defined as residues 95 to 363, and the propeptide domain as residues 30 to 94. Deletions are denoted by the prefix “del” followed by the residue numbers removed, as in ProRmL-del, which indicates the deletion of Ser95 and Ile96. Substitutions are denoted by the single-letter amino acid code of the original residue, followed by the residue number, and then the single-letter code of the substituted residue, as in L81V, indicating the substitution of leucine at position 81 with valine. Insertions are denoted by the prefix “ins” followed by the residue number at which the insertion occurs and the sequence inserted, as in ins37-40-GlyGlySer, indicating the insertion of the tripeptide Gly-Gly-Ser between residues 37 and 38. All variants are expressed in the context of the full-length prolipase construct unless otherwise specified. Mutations that occur outside the propeptide or mature enzyme domains, such as in the signal peptide or linker regions, are not considered part of the invention unless they directly affect the stability or inhibitory function of the propeptide-mature enzyme interface. The term “wild-type prolipase” refers to the unmodified sequence of the prolipase as naturally produced by Rhizomucor miehei, with the propeptide intact and capable of spontaneous cleavage. The term “engineered prolipase” refers to any variant of the prolipase that has been modified through recombinant DNA technology to alter its stability, activation kinetics, or binding affinity. The term “functional equivalent” refers to a variant that retains the core structural and inhibitory properties of the prolipase, even if it contains substitutions, deletions, or insertions that do not disrupt the propeptide’s ability to bind and inhibit the mature enzyme. Such equivalents may be identified by sequence alignment, structural modeling, or functional assays demonstrating comparable inhibition kinetics and binding affinity.

### Muscle v3.8.31 Basic Usage

The term “Muscle v3.8.31” refers to a specific version of the Multiple Sequence Comparison by Log-Expectation algorithm, a widely accepted bioinformatics tool used for the alignment of protein and nucleotide sequences. In the context of this invention, Muscle v3.8.31 is employed to perform pairwise and multiple sequence alignments of the propeptide and mature enzyme domains of Rhizomucor miehei lipase with homologous sequences from other fungal lipases, including Thermomyces lanuginosus lipase, Gibberella zeae lipase, and Rhizopus microsporus var. chinensis lipase. The purpose of these alignments is to identify conserved residues within the propeptide domain that are likely to be critical for inhibitory function, as well as to determine the degree of structural similarity between the propeptide-mature enzyme interface across different species. The alignment parameters used in this invention include default gap opening and extension penalties, a transition weight of 0.5, and iterative refinement to achieve maximum alignment accuracy. The resulting alignments are used to guide the design of engineered prolipase variants by highlighting regions of high conservation that are amenable to mutagenesis without loss of inhibitory function. The alignments also serve to validate the structural observations made in the crystallographic studies by demonstrating that the key residues involved in propeptide-mature enzyme interactions are conserved across evolutionarily distant lipases, suggesting a common mechanistic principle underlying propeptide-mediated inhibition. The output of Muscle v3.8.31 is used to generate sequence logos and conservation scores that inform the selection of target sites for mutagenesis and the prediction of functional equivalents.

The invention utilizes a range of standard molecular biology and biochemical techniques that are well established in the art, including but not limited to polymerase chain reaction, site-directed mutagenesis, recombinant expression in fungal hosts, protein purification by chromatography, mass spectrometry, native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, zymography, fluorescence polarization, and X-ray crystallography. The use of fluorescence polarization to measure binding affinity between the isolated propeptide and the mature enzyme allows for the quantification of inhibitory strength under physiological conditions. The invention further employs computational tools such as PISA for the analysis of protein-protein interfaces, MolProbity for validation of structural models, and Coot for manual refinement of electron density maps. All of these techniques are applied in a manner consistent with best practices in structural biology and enzyme engineering to ensure the reliability and reproducibility of the results.

### Polynucleotides

The invention encompasses isolated polynucleotides encoding the prolipase variants of Rhizomucor miehei lipase, including the wild-type prolipase and the stabilized ProRmL-del variant. These polynucleotides are designed to be expressed in recombinant host systems and are optimized for codon usage in fungal hosts such as Aspergillus oryzae to ensure high-level expression. The polynucleotide encoding the wild-type prolipase comprises a nucleotide sequence corresponding to residues 30 to 363 of the precursor polypeptide, including the signal peptide, propeptide, and mature enzyme domains, with the coding sequence flanked by appropriate promoter and terminator elements for expression in the host. The polynucleotide encoding the ProRmL-del variant is identical to the wild-type sequence except for the deletion of six nucleotides corresponding to the codons for Ser95 and Ile96, resulting in a seamless fusion between the propeptide and mature enzyme domains without the introduction of any linker residues. The polynucleotides may be synthesized chemically, amplified by PCR from genomic or cDNA templates, or assembled from overlapping oligonucleotides using synthetic biology techniques. The polynucleotides may further include silent mutations to introduce or remove restriction enzyme sites for cloning purposes, or to enhance mRNA stability and translation efficiency. The polynucleotides may also be engineered to contain epitope tags, such as His6 or FLAG, at the N- or C-terminus to facilitate purification, although such tags are not required for the function of the prolipase and are typically removed prior to structural or functional analysis. The polynucleotides are capable of being expressed in a variety of host systems, including yeast, filamentous fungi, and bacteria, although expression in Aspergillus oryzae is preferred due to its native ability to process the signal peptide and produce the prolipase in a form that retains the propeptide in a stable complex. The polynucleotides may be modified to include introns, untranslated regions, or enhancer elements to further optimize expression levels. The polynucleotides may also be engineered to encode fusion proteins in which the prolipase is linked to a second functional domain, such as a cellulose-binding module or a surfactant-binding peptide, to confer additional properties to the final enzyme product.

The invention encompasses host cells transformed with the expression vectors described above, capable of expressing the prolipase variants in a functional form. ### Methods of Production

The invention provides methods for the production of the prolipase variants described herein, comprising the steps of transforming a host cell with an expression vector encoding the prolipase, culturing the transformed host cell under conditions conducive to protein expression, and recovering the prolipase from the culture medium or cell lysate. The method begins with the preparation of the expression vector containing the polynucleotide encoding the prolipase, which is introduced into the host cell by transformation, transfection, or conjugation. The transformed host cells are then selected using appropriate markers and grown in a suitable culture medium, such as M400 medium for Aspergillus oryzae, under controlled conditions of temperature, pH, and aeration. The culture is typically maintained for 3 to 7 days to allow for sufficient expression and secretion of the prolipase. The culture supernatant is then harvested and clarified by filtration to remove cellular debris. The prolipase is recovered from the clarified supernatant by a series of chromatographic steps, beginning with hydrophobic interaction chromatography using a decylamine resin to separate the prolipase from the mature enzyme, followed by ion-exchange chromatography to remove impurities and concentrate the sample. The prolipase may be further purified by gel filtration to remove aggregates or degraded fragments. The purified prolipase is then dialyzed into a storage buffer, typically containing 50 mM HEPES, pH 7.0, and 100 mM NaCl, and may be lyophilized for long-term storage. The method further includes the step of verifying the integrity of the prolipase complex by mass spectrometry, native PAGE, or zymography to confirm the absence of cleavage. The method may be scaled to industrial levels using bioreactors with capacities ranging from liters to thousands of liters. The method may further include the step of formulating the prolipase with stabilizers, surfactants, or other additives to enhance its shelf life and performance in industrial applications. The method is reproducible, scalable, and suitable for commercial production of the prolipase variants for use in detergents, food processing, and other industrial applications.

### Example 1: Variant Generation, Transformation and Expression

The wild-type prolipase gene encoding residues 30 to 363 of Rhizomucor miehei lipase was synthesized de novo with codon optimization for expression in Aspergillus oryzae. The gene was cloned into the pAN52 expression vector under the control of the glucoamylase promoter and the trpC terminator. The ProRmL-del variant was generated by site-directed mutagenesis using two overlapping primers designed to delete the codons for Ser95 and Ile96, resulting in a seamless fusion between the propeptide and mature enzyme domains. The PCR product was digested with DpnI to remove the methylated parental template and transformed into competent Escherichia coli cells. Plasmid DNA was isolated from individual colonies and sequenced to confirm the presence of the desired mutation. The verified plasmids were then used to transform Aspergillus oryzae ToC1512 protoplasts by polyethylene glycol-mediated transformation. Transformants were selected on minimal medium lacking uracil and screened for protein expression by SDS-PAGE of culture supernatants. Positive transformants were inoculated into 1 L baffled shake flasks containing M400 medium and incubated at 34°C for 4 days with shaking at 180 rpm. The culture supernatants were harvested by centrifugation and filtered through a 0.2 μm membrane. The expression levels of the wild-type and ProRmL-del variants were comparable, with both producing visible bands at approximately 42 kDa on SDS-PAGE under reducing conditions. The ProRmL-del variant showed a single band, whereas the wild-type variant showed a minor band at approximately 27 kDa, corresponding to the mature enzyme, indicating partial cleavage. The culture supernatants were used directly for purification without further concentration.

### Purification of RmL WT Controls: RmL WT and RmL WT Pur

The mature wild-type RmL enzyme, lacking the propeptide, was purified from the same culture supernatant used for the prolipase variants. The clarified supernatant was applied to a decylamine affinity column pre-equilibrated with 50 mM HEPES, pH 7.0, containing 1 M NaCl. The flow-through fraction, containing the prolipase, was collected and subjected to ion-exchange chromatography. The mature RmL, which bound to the decylamine resin, was eluted with a step gradient of 100% Milli-Q water. The eluted mature RmL was dialyzed against 50 mM HEPES, pH 7.0, and concentrated to 10 mg/mL. The purified mature RmL was further subjected to gel filtration on a Superdex 200 column to remove aggregates and ensure homogeneity. The final product, designated RmL WT Pur, was confirmed to be homogeneous by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry, with a molecular weight of 27 kDa and no detectable propeptide contamination. The RmL WT Pur was used as a control in all activity assays and binding studies.

### Propeptide Variant Generation

Additional variants of the prolipase were generated by introducing point mutations into the propeptide domain using site-directed mutagenesis. The mutations targeted residues Leu81, Lys76, Tyr77, and Val348, which were identified from the crystal structure as key mediators of the propeptide-mature enzyme interface. The mutations included L81V, L81A, K76A, Y77F, and V348A, as well as combinations thereof. The mutant constructs were cloned into the pAN52 vector and transformed into Aspergillus oryzae as described above. The expression levels of the variants were comparable to the wild-type prolipase, and the proteins were purified using the same chromatographic protocol. The purified variants were analyzed by native PAGE and zymography to assess their inhibitory properties. The L81V variant showed reduced inhibition compared to the wild-type, while the L81A variant showed complete loss of inhibition. The K76A and Y77F variants showed intermediate levels of inhibition, indicating that these residues contribute to the stability of the complex but are not essential for binding. The V348A variant showed a significant reduction in binding affinity, as measured by fluorescence polarization, confirming the importance of this residue in the interface. The variants were used to establish a structure-function relationship between the propeptide and the mature enzyme.

### Example 2: Native PAGE

Native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis was performed to assess the oligomeric state and integrity of the prolipase variants. The samples were prepared in loading buffer containing 0.06 M Tris, pH 6.8, 10% glycerol, and 0.01% bromophenol blue, without the addition of SDS or reducing agents. The resolving gel consisted of 12% acrylamide-bis-acrylamide in 0.4 M Tris, pH 8.8, and the stacking gel consisted of 5.1% acrylamide-bis-acrylamide in 0.25 M Tris, pH 6.8. The gels were run at 100 V for 2 hours in running buffer containing 3 g/L Tris base and 14.4 g/L glycine. The gel was stained with Coomassie Brilliant Blue R-250. The wild-type prolipase migrated as a single band at approximately 42 kDa, consistent with the expected molecular weight of the intact complex. The ProRmL-del variant migrated at the same position, confirming that the deletion of Ser95 and Ile96 did not alter the overall conformation or charge of the complex. The mature RmL WT Pur migrated at approximately 27 kDa, as expected. The isolated propeptide, when run under the same conditions, migrated as a diffuse band at approximately 10 kDa, indicating its unstructured nature in the absence of the mature enzyme. No higher molecular weight aggregates were observed, indicating that the prolipase variants were monomeric and stable under native conditions.

### Example 3: Temperature Stability Assay (TSA)

The thermal stability of the prolipase variants was assessed using a temperature shift assay. The samples were diluted to 0.5 mg/mL in 50 mM HEPES, pH 7.0, and loaded into a 96-well plate. The plate was heated from 25°C to 95°C in 1°C increments using a real-time PCR instrument equipped with a fluorescence detector. The intrinsic fluorescence of tryptophan residues was monitored at an excitation wavelength of 280 nm and an emission wavelength of 340 nm. The melting temperature (Tm) was determined as the temperature at which 50% of the protein had unfolded. The wild-type prolipase exhibited a Tm of 58.2°C, while the ProRmL-del variant exhibited a Tm of 61.5°C, indicating that the deletion of Ser95 and Ile96 enhanced the thermal stability of the complex. The L81V variant exhibited a Tm of 54.8°C, while the L81A variant exhibited a Tm of 49.1°C, confirming that the hydrophobic interaction mediated by Leu81 is critical for stability. The K76A and Y77F variants exhibited Tm values of 56.3°C and 57.1°C, respectively, indicating that these residues contribute to stability but are less critical than Leu81. The mature RmL WT Pur exhibited a Tm of 52.4°C, indicating that the presence of the propeptide significantly enhances the thermal stability of the enzyme. The results demonstrate that the propeptide not only inhibits enzymatic activity but also acts as a molecular chaperone that stabilizes the mature enzyme domain.

### Example 4: Zymogram Analysis

Zymogram analysis was performed to assess the lipolytic activity of the prolipase variants. After native PAGE, the gels were incubated in a solution containing 4% Litex HSB agarose, 50 mM HEPES, pH 7.0, 0.625% polyvinyl alcohol, 1.9% (v/v) olive oil, and 0.05% brilliant green. The gels were then incubated at 37°C for 1 hour to allow for enzymatic hydrolysis of the olive oil. The gels were washed with distilled water and visualized under UV light. The mature RmL WT Pur produced a clear zone of hydrolysis, indicating high lipolytic activity. The wild-type prolipase and the ProRmL-del variant showed no detectable activity, confirming that the propeptide effectively inhibits enzymatic function. The L81V variant showed faint activity, while the L81A variant showed no activity, consistent with the thermal stability data. The K76A and Y77F variants showed no activity, indicating that the inhibition was maintained despite the mutations. The isolated propeptide showed no activity when incubated alone, confirming that it does not possess intrinsic lipolytic function. The zymogram results confirm that the propeptide acts as a potent inhibitor of lipase activity and that the inhibition is reversible only upon removal of the propeptide.

### Example 5: Decylamine Agarose Chromatography

Decylamine agarose chromatography was performed to assess the binding affinity of the prolipase variants to a lipid-mimetic resin. The resin was equilibrated with 50 mM HEPES, pH 7.0, containing 1 M NaCl. The samples were loaded onto the column at a flow rate of 8 mL/min, and the flow-through and wash fractions were collected. The column was then washed with 3 column volumes of the equilibration buffer, followed by elution with a step gradient of 100% Milli-Q water. The wild-type prolipase and the ProRmL-del variant both flowed through the column, indicating that they do not bind to the decylamine resin due to the occlusion of the hydrophobic substrate-binding site by the propeptide. In contrast, the mature RmL WT Pur bound strongly to the resin and was eluted with water, confirming its ability to interact with lipid-like surfaces. The L81V variant showed partial binding, with a small fraction eluting with the wash buffer, indicating that the mutation partially exposes the hydrophobic interface. The L81A variant showed complete binding, suggesting that the loss of the hydrophobic side chain destabilizes the propeptide-mature enzyme interface and allows the mature enzyme to adopt a more open conformation. The results demonstrate that the propeptide effectively masks the lipid-binding site of the mature enzyme and that mutations in key interface residues can modulate this masking effect.

### Example 6: Relative Wash Performance

The relative wash performance of the prolipase variants was assessed in a simulated detergent wash system. The model B detergent formulation was prepared as described and adjusted to pH 8.5. The formulations were supplemented with either the wild-type prolipase, the ProRmL-del variant, the mature RmL WT Pur, or no enzyme as a control. The formulations were applied to cotton fabric stained with olive oil and incubated at 30°C for 30 minutes. The fabrics were then rinsed with water and dried. The residual oil was quantified by reflectance spectrophotometry at 550 nm. The mature RmL WT Pur showed the highest cleaning efficiency, with a 92% reduction in oil residue. The wild-type prolipase and the ProRmL-del variant showed no cleaning efficiency, confirming that the propeptide effectively inhibits activity under wash conditions. The L81V variant showed a 15% reduction in oil residue, indicating partial activation under the wash conditions, while the L81A variant showed no activity. The results demonstrate that the prolipase variants can be used in detergent formulations without causing premature degradation of fabric components or formulation instability. The ProRmL-del variant, in particular, offers the advantage of being stable during storage and only becoming active upon exposure to the intended washing conditions, thereby providing superior control over enzyme activity.
