# DESCRIPTION

## PRIOR ART

Prior to the present invention, the molecular underpinnings of endometrial carcinoma progression remained incompletely understood, particularly with respect to the functional consequences of recurrent genomic amplifications observed in aggressive disease. The chromosomal region 8q24 had long been recognized as one of the most frequently amplified loci across a broad spectrum of human malignancies, including breast, prostate, ovarian, and colorectal cancers. In these contexts, the MYC proto-oncogene, located centrally within this region, was widely presumed to be the principal driver of oncogenic signaling due to its well-documented role as a master transcriptional regulator of cell proliferation, metabolism, and apoptosis evasion. Consequently, therapeutic and diagnostic efforts in oncology had historically focused on MYC as the primary target of 8q24 amplification, despite persistent challenges in directly inhibiting MYC protein function due to its intrinsically disordered structure and lack of conventional druggable pockets.

In endometrial cancer specifically, although 8q24 amplification had been documented in a subset of tumors, its clinical significance and molecular consequences had not been systematically evaluated. Prior studies had established that endometrial carcinomas with high-grade histology, non-endometrioid subtypes, and advanced FIGO stages exhibited higher frequencies of genomic instability, yet no consistent molecular biomarker had been validated to reliably predict recurrence or metastatic potential beyond established clinicopathological parameters such as tumor grade, depth of myometrial invasion, and lymphovascular space invasion. The absence of robust molecular classifiers left clinicians without tools to stratify patients for adjuvant therapy or to identify candidates for novel targeted interventions. Furthermore, while MYC overexpression had been occasionally reported in endometrial tumors, its correlation with patient outcomes remained inconsistent across studies, and no clear mechanistic link had been established between MYC expression levels and downstream transcriptional programs known to drive aggressive disease phenotypes.

Simultaneously, other genes located within the 8q24 amplicon had received minimal attention in the context of endometrial cancer. Among these, ATAD2—encoding an ATPase family AAA domain-containing protein 2—had been identified in prior studies as a bromodomain-containing chromatin reader capable of interacting with MYC and facilitating its recruitment to E-box-containing promoters. In breast cancer models, ATAD2 had been shown to enhance MYC-dependent transcriptional activation by stabilizing MYC binding to chromatin and recruiting histone acetyltransferases. These findings suggested a potential co-operative role for ATAD2 in MYC-driven oncogenesis, yet no comprehensive analysis had been conducted to determine whether ATAD2 was co-amplified with MYC in endometrial tumors, whether its expression was driven by copy number gain, or whether its overexpression conferred a prognostic advantage independent of MYC. Moreover, the functional dependency of endometrial cancer cells on ATAD2 had never been tested, nor had any therapeutic strategy targeting ATAD2 been explored in this malignancy.

In the broader oncology literature, the concept of amplicon-driven oncogene cooperation had been described in other cancers, such as the co-amplification of BIRC2 and YAP1 in hepatocellular carcinoma or MMP13, BIRCH2, and BIRCH3 in osteosarcoma, where multiple genes within a single amplified region contributed synergistically to tumorigenesis. However, such multi-gene amplification models had not been rigorously applied to endometrial cancer, and the prevailing paradigm continued to treat MYC as the singular effector of 8q24 amplification. This assumption was further reinforced by the lack of high-resolution, integrative analyses that simultaneously assessed copy number, gene expression, and clinical outcomes across large, well-annotated patient cohorts. Consequently, the possibility that other genes within the 8q24 amplicon—not merely MYC—might serve as critical drivers of tumor aggressiveness, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance remained unexplored and unvalidated.

Additionally, while histone deacetylase inhibitors had been investigated in various cancers for their ability to modulate chromatin structure and indirectly disrupt transcriptional programs driven by bromodomain proteins, no study had specifically linked their efficacy to ATAD2 dependency or MYC signaling strength in endometrial or other gynecologic malignancies. The Connectivity Map and similar pharmacogenomic databases had identified HDAC inhibitors as compounds whose transcriptional signatures were inversely correlated with MYC activation, yet these associations had not been functionally validated in patient-derived models or translated into predictive biomarkers for drug response. Thus, while the theoretical rationale for targeting bromodomain-containing proteins in MYC-driven cancers existed, the specific identification of ATAD2 as a critical node in this network, its validation as a prognostic biomarker across multiple independent cohorts, and its demonstration as a therapeutically actionable target in endometrial cancer had never been achieved.

In summary, the prior art was characterized by a narrow focus on MYC as the sole oncogenic target of 8q24 amplification, a lack of comprehensive molecular profiling of endometrial cancer specimens with matched clinical outcomes, and an absence of functional validation demonstrating that ATAD2—not MYC—was the most strongly amplified and expressed gene within the 8q24 locus in this disease, and that its overexpression independently predicted poor prognosis and conferred cellular dependency. Furthermore, no prior work had established a direct link between ATAD2 copy number gain and its transcriptional upregulation in endometrial tumors, nor had any therapeutic strategy been proposed that specifically targeted ATAD2 to disrupt MYC-driven oncogenic signaling. The field therefore lacked a molecularly grounded, clinically translatable framework for identifying high-risk endometrial cancer patients and for developing targeted therapies aimed at the true functional effectors of 8q24 amplification.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention represents a fundamental advance in the understanding, diagnosis, and treatment of endometrial carcinoma by identifying ATAD2 as a primary driver of tumor aggressiveness and a critical therapeutic target downstream of the 8q24 amplification, thereby challenging the longstanding assumption that MYC is the principal oncogenic effector in this context. This invention discloses a novel molecular paradigm in which ATAD2, rather than MYC, is the most consistently amplified and overexpressed gene within the 8q24 amplicon in endometrial cancer, and in which ATAD2 expression serves as a robust, independent predictor of disease progression, metastasis, and cancer-specific mortality. The invention further demonstrates that ATAD2 overexpression is causally linked to enhanced MYC signaling activity, even in the absence of MYC amplification, and that endometrial cancer cells exhibiting high MYC signaling dependency are exquisitely sensitive to ATAD2 knockdown, establishing ATAD2 as a non-redundant, functionally essential component of the oncogenic transcriptional network.

The invention provides a diagnostic method for identifying patients with endometrial carcinoma who are at high risk of recurrence or death by measuring the copy number or expression level of ATAD2 in tumor tissue, either alone or in combination with estrogen receptor status, to stratify patients into clinically actionable risk groups. This method enables clinicians to distinguish between patients who may benefit from intensified adjuvant therapy and those who may be spared unnecessary treatment, thereby improving therapeutic precision and reducing overtreatment. The invention further provides a therapeutic method for treating endometrial carcinoma by inhibiting ATAD2 function, either through direct pharmacological inhibition of its bromodomain or through indirect modulation of its activity using histone deacetylase inhibitors, thereby disrupting the MYC transcriptional program and inducing tumor cell death. This therapeutic approach is particularly effective in tumors exhibiting high ATAD2 expression, MYC signaling activation, or 8q24 amplification, and is applicable not only to endometrial cancer but also to other MYC-driven malignancies such as breast, ovarian, and glioblastoma cancers.

The invention is grounded in a comprehensive, multi-platform analysis of primary and metastatic endometrial tumors, validated across multiple independent cohorts, including a large FISH-based series of nearly four hundred patients, a qPCR-validated cohort of over one hundred sixty tumors, and external datasets from The Cancer Genome Atlas. These analyses reveal that ATAD2 copy number gain is the strongest predictor of ATAD2 mRNA overexpression, that ATAD2 expression correlates more strongly with MYC signaling strength than MYC expression itself, and that ATAD2 overexpression predicts poor prognosis independently of established clinical variables such as tumor grade, histological subtype, and estrogen receptor status. The invention further demonstrates that ATAD2 expression is elevated in metastatic lesions compared to matched primary tumors, indicating its role in disease progression, and that its expression is modulated additively by copy number, E2F transcription factor activity, and estrogen receptor signaling, providing a mechanistic framework for its regulation.

Crucially, the invention provides functional validation in patient-derived cell lines showing that genetic knockdown of ATAD2 results in profound loss of viability in MYC-dependent endometrial and breast cancer cells, with effects nearly identical to those observed upon MYC knockdown. This dependency is not explained by baseline expression levels of ATAD2 or MYC, nor by 8q24 copy number, suggesting that ATAD2 functions as a critical enabler of MYC-driven oncogenesis regardless of its absolute expression level. Furthermore, the invention identifies histone deacetylase inhibitors, particularly Trichostatin-A, as compounds whose transcriptional signatures are inversely correlated with MYC signaling and whose cytotoxic effects are strongly correlated with ATAD2 dependency, thereby offering a clinically available, albeit indirect, strategy for targeting ATAD2. The invention thus bridges the gap between genomic discovery and therapeutic application by providing a biomarker-driven, mechanism-based approach to treating a previously intractable subset of endometrial cancers.

The invention further extends beyond endometrial cancer to demonstrate that the ATAD2–MYC axis is conserved across multiple cancer types, including breast, ovarian, and glioblastoma, where ATAD2 amplification and overexpression similarly correlate with MYC signaling activation and poor clinical outcomes. This cross-cancer applicability establishes ATAD2 as a broadly relevant oncogenic dependency and positions it as a pan-cancer therapeutic target. The invention therefore provides a unified molecular framework for identifying and treating MYC-driven cancers based on ATAD2 status, circumventing the historical challenges associated with direct MYC inhibition. By shifting the therapeutic focus from MYC to ATAD2, the invention opens a new avenue for drug development, enabling the design of small molecule inhibitors targeting the bromodomain of ATAD2, the development of RNA-based therapeutics to silence its expression, and the implementation of combination therapies that exploit synthetic lethality with HDAC inhibition. The invention thus transforms the clinical management of endometrial carcinoma and other aggressive cancers by introducing a novel, validated, and actionable molecular target that was previously unrecognized in the context of oncogenic 8q24 amplification.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

### Patient Series

The present invention is based on a meticulously curated series of endometrial carcinoma specimens collected from patients treated at Haukeland University Hospital in Norway, encompassing both primary tumors and their corresponding metastatic lesions, all of which were prospectively collected with comprehensive clinical follow-up data extending through October 2010. The patient cohort was assembled under strict ethical oversight, with all procedures approved by the Norwegian Data Inspectorate, the Norwegian Social Sciences Data Services, and the Regional Research Ethics Committee in Medicine, Western Norway, and all participants provided written informed consent prior to specimen collection. Tumors were immediately frozen upon surgical resection for molecular analyses, while a subset was fixed in formalin and embedded in paraffin for fluorescence in situ hybridization studies to preserve tissue architecture and enable spatial assessment of genomic alterations. The clinical characteristics of the patients, including age at diagnosis, FIGO stage, histological subtype, tumor grade, lymph node status, estrogen receptor expression, and survival outcomes, were recorded prospectively and integrated with molecular data to enable robust correlation analyses.

The primary investigation series consisted of 82 endometrial carcinomas for which genome-wide expression profiles were available, with corresponding copy number data derived from single nucleotide polymorphism arrays for 70 of these tumors. An internal validation series was subsequently assembled, comprising an additional 40 primary tumors and 19 metastatic lesions from the same geographic region, all processed under identical conditions to ensure data consistency. An external validation series was obtained from previously published datasets encompassing 111 endometrial tumors, allowing for cross-cohort reproducibility. To further validate the findings at the level of gene expression, a quantitative PCR series was established, including 162 endometrial carcinoma samples for which ATAD2 and MYC mRNA levels were measured using TaqMan assays with GAPDH as an endogenous control. Additionally, a fluorescence in situ hybridization series was constructed, comprising 399 endometrial carcinoma specimens, including 49 paired primary and metastatic tumors, enabling direct visualization of 8q24 amplification at the single-cell level and assessment of spatial heterogeneity within individual tumors. All specimens were reviewed by board-certified pathologists to confirm histological diagnosis and assign tumor grade and subtype according to standardized criteria, ensuring that molecular findings were contextualized within well-characterized clinical and pathological parameters.

The metastatic specimens included in the study were obtained from sites of distant recurrence, including peritoneal implants, lymph nodes, and distant organ metastases, and were matched to their corresponding primary tumors whenever possible. This paired design allowed for the direct comparison of genomic and transcriptomic profiles between primary and metastatic lesions, revealing a consistent pattern of increased 8q24 amplification and ATAD2 overexpression in metastatic tissue. The follow-up period for all patients was defined from the date of primary surgical resection until the date of disease recurrence, death from endometrial cancer, or last known follow-up, whichever occurred first. Survival analyses were conducted using Kaplan-Meier methods and Cox proportional hazards models, adjusting for known prognostic factors such as age, stage, histological subtype, and estrogen receptor status. The inclusion of both primary and metastatic tumors, the use of multiple validation cohorts, and the integration of clinical outcomes with molecular data ensured that the findings were not artifacts of a single dataset but represented a generalizable biological phenomenon with direct clinical relevance.

The patient series was further enriched by the inclusion of normal endometrial tissue controls obtained from patients undergoing hysterectomy for benign conditions, which were used to establish baseline expression levels of ATAD2 and MYC in non-neoplastic tissue. These controls confirmed that ATAD2 expression was significantly elevated in endometrial carcinomas compared to normal endometrium, and that this elevation was most pronounced in tumors exhibiting 8q24 amplification. The demographic and clinical characteristics of the entire cohort were summarized to ensure representation across age groups, histological subtypes, and disease stages, and statistical tests confirmed that the distribution of key variables was consistent across the different analytical subsets. The integrity of the specimen collection process, the uniformity of sample processing, and the completeness of clinical annotation collectively ensured that the patient series provided a high-fidelity platform for the discovery and validation of ATAD2 as a central driver of endometrial cancer progression.

### RNA Analysis

The transcriptional landscape of endometrial carcinoma was systematically interrogated using high-density oligonucleotide microarrays to assess gene expression patterns across the entire cohort, with particular emphasis on identifying genes whose expression was tightly coupled to the 8q24 amplification event. RNA was extracted from frozen tumor specimens using standardized protocols that preserved RNA integrity, and samples were hybridized to Agilent 44K expression arrays according to the manufacturer’s specifications. Each array was processed in batch to minimize technical variability, and signal intensities were normalized using BRB-ArrayTools, a widely accepted bioinformatics platform developed by the National Cancer Institute, which employs median normalization across all arrays to correct for systematic biases in hybridization efficiency and background fluorescence. This approach ensured that gene expression values were comparable across samples and that differential expression analyses were statistically robust.

The analysis focused on the 26 genes located within the peak region of 8q24 amplification, including MYC, ATAD2, NDUFB9, DERL1, FAM91A1, and WDR67, among others, to determine which gene exhibited the strongest correlation between copy number gain and transcriptional upregulation. Expression levels were quantified as log-transformed intensity values and subjected to correlation analyses with copy number data derived from SNP arrays. The results revealed that ATAD2 expression exhibited the most significant association with 8q24 amplification, with a p-value of 2.77 × 10⁻⁶, far exceeding the association observed for MYC or any other gene in the region. This finding was corroborated across all validation cohorts, including the internal series of 59 tumors and the external series of 111 tumors, where ATAD2 expression remained the most strongly amplified and overexpressed gene within the 8q24 locus.

To assess the broader transcriptional consequences of 8q24 amplification, a previously published gene set of 68 genes known to be transcriptionally activated by MYC across multiple cancer types was employed to calculate a composite “MYC signaling strength” score using Gene Set Enrichment Analysis. This score, derived from the collective overexpression of MYC target genes, provided a more sensitive and biologically relevant measure of MYC pathway activation than MYC expression alone. Strikingly, ATAD2 expression demonstrated a stronger correlation with MYC signaling strength (R² = 0.48, p < 0.001) than MYC expression itself (R² = 0.05, p = 0.002), indicating that ATAD2 was a more reliable indicator of MYC pathway activity than the MYC gene product. This relationship persisted even among tumors lacking 8q24 amplification, suggesting that ATAD2 overexpression could drive MYC signaling through mechanisms independent of genomic amplification, such as transcriptional activation by E2F factors or epigenetic modulation.

The analysis was extended to five additional MYC activation signatures derived from the Gene Set Enrichment Database, representing MYC-driven transcriptional programs in diverse cellular contexts. In each case, ATAD2 expression showed a stronger association with these signatures than MYC expression, further reinforcing its role as a central mediator of MYC-dependent transcription. The correlation between ATAD2 and MYC signaling was also validated in the internal and external validation series, with R² values of 0.54 and 0.45, respectively, confirming the robustness of the association across independent datasets. Importantly, the absence of a strong correlation between MYC expression and MYC signaling strength across all datasets suggested that MYC protein levels alone were insufficient to predict pathway activity, and that other factors—most notably ATAD2—were required to translate MYC presence into functional transcriptional output.

To further refine the analysis, the expression of all genes in the 8q24 amplicon was ranked by their correlation with MYC signaling strength, and ATAD2 consistently ranked first, followed by NDUFB9, DERL1, FAM91A1, and WDR67, none of which demonstrated a correlation as strong as ATAD2. The specificity of this association was further confirmed by examining the expression of genes outside the 8q24 region, which showed no significant correlation with either 8q24 amplification or MYC signaling strength. These findings collectively demonstrated that ATAD2 was not merely a bystander gene within the 8q24 amplicon but was functionally and transcriptionally central to the oncogenic phenotype driven by this amplification. The RNA analysis thus established ATAD2 as the most reliable molecular marker of MYC pathway activation in endometrial carcinoma, providing the foundation for its use as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker and as a therapeutic target.

### DNA Analysis

The genomic architecture of endometrial carcinoma was comprehensively analyzed to determine the structural basis for ATAD2 overexpression and its relationship to the 8q24 amplification event. Copy number alterations were assessed using high-resolution single nucleotide polymorphism arrays, which enabled the detection of focal amplifications and deletions across the entire genome with single-gene resolution. The 8q24 region was identified as the most frequently amplified locus in the primary investigation series, occurring in 16 of 70 tumors (23%), with amplification levels ranging from low-level gains (copy number 3.0–4.0) to moderate amplifications (copy number up to 4.7). The boundaries of the amplification were precisely defined, revealing that the amplicon consistently encompassed the entire ATAD2 locus and extended beyond the MYC gene, encompassing multiple neighboring genes, including NDUFB9, DERL1, FAM91A1, and WDR67.

To determine whether ATAD2 copy number directly influenced its expression, a linear regression model was constructed using copy number as the independent variable and ATAD2 mRNA expression as the dependent variable. The analysis revealed a highly significant association (p < 0.001), with copy number gain accounting for a substantial proportion of the variance in ATAD2 expression. This relationship was confirmed across all validation cohorts, including the internal and external expression datasets, as well as the qPCR validation series, where ATAD2 mRNA levels were directly proportional to copy number status. In contrast, MYC copy number showed a weaker association with MYC expression, with a much lower R² value, indicating that MYC expression was less dependent on its genomic dosage and more susceptible to post-transcriptional regulation.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization was employed to validate these findings at the single-cell level in a large cohort of 399 endometrial carcinoma specimens. Using a MYC Spectrum Orange probe and a chromosome 8 centromere (CEP8) control probe, the ratio of MYC signals to CEP8 signals was calculated in 40 to 60 nuclei per tumor, with amplification defined as a MYC/CEP8 ratio greater than 1.0. The FISH analysis confirmed that 20 of 399 tumors (5%) exhibited 8q24 amplification, and in every case, this amplification was accompanied by increased ATAD2 copy number, as determined by co-hybridization with an ATAD2-specific probe. The spatial distribution of amplified signals was consistent with focal amplification rather than polysomy, and the amplification was rarely observed in normal adjacent tissue, confirming its tumor-specific nature.

Importantly, the FISH analysis revealed that in five of 49 paired primary-metastatic cases, the primary tumor lacked 8q24 amplification, while the corresponding metastasis acquired it, indicating that 8q24 amplification is a late event in tumor evolution that is selected for during disease progression. Conversely, only one metastasis lost the amplification present in its primary tumor, demonstrating a strong directional selection for amplification during metastatic spread. This clonal evolution pattern was corroborated by the observation that metastatic lesions exhibited significantly higher ATAD2 expression than their matched primary tumors, further supporting the notion that ATAD2 amplification and overexpression confer a selective advantage in advanced disease.

To determine whether ATAD2 copy number was the primary driver of its expression, a multivariate linear regression model was constructed incorporating ATAD2 copy number, ESR1 expression, and E2F1 expression as predictor variables. The model explained 77% of the variance in ATAD2 expression in endometrial cancer, with ATAD2 copy number contributing the largest proportion of explained variance, followed by E2F1 and ESR1. This additive model was validated in breast, ovarian, and glioblastoma cancers from The Cancer Genome Atlas, where ATAD2 copy number remained the most significant predictor of expression, even in the presence of other regulatory inputs. These findings established that ATAD2 expression is predominantly governed by genomic amplification, with transcriptional regulators such as E2F and estrogen receptor acting as secondary modulators rather than primary drivers.

The DNA analysis thus provided conclusive evidence that ATAD2 is a direct target of the 8q24 amplification in endometrial carcinoma, that its overexpression is primarily driven by copy number gain, and that this amplification is selected for during tumor progression and metastasis. These findings fundamentally reframe the understanding of 8q24 amplification in endometrial cancer, shifting the focus from MYC to ATAD2 as the principal oncogenic effector and establishing ATAD2 copy number as a reliable biomarker for identifying high-risk patients.

### Statistics

The statistical framework underpinning the present invention was designed to rigorously establish the clinical and biological significance of ATAD2 amplification and overexpression in endometrial carcinoma, using a combination of parametric and non-parametric methods appropriate for the nature and distribution of the data. All analyses were conducted using standard statistical software packages, with significance thresholds set at p < 0.05, and corrections for multiple comparisons applied where appropriate. The primary objective was to determine whether ATAD2 expression and 8q24 amplification were independently associated with disease progression and cancer-specific mortality, after accounting for established clinicopathological variables.

To assess the association between categorical variables—such as tumor grade, histological subtype, estrogen receptor status, and 8q24 amplification status—Pearson’s chi-square tests were employed. These analyses confirmed that 8q24 amplification and high ATAD2 expression were significantly enriched in non-endometrioid histologies (p = 2.98 × 10⁻⁵), high-grade tumors (p = 2.90 × 10⁻⁸), and estrogen receptor-negative tumors (p = 0.02). The association between ATAD2 expression and ER negativity was further quantified using Pearson’s correlation coefficient, revealing a modest but statistically significant inverse relationship (R² = 0.10, p = 0.005), consistent with prior findings in breast cancer.

To evaluate the continuous relationship between gene expression and molecular phenotypes, linear regression models were constructed to predict ATAD2 expression based on copy number, E2F1 expression, and ESR1 expression. The adjusted R² values indicated that these three variables collectively explained 77% of the variation in ATAD2 expression, with copy number contributing the largest effect size. Multivariate linear regression was also used to assess the independent contribution of each variable while controlling for the others, and all three remained statistically significant predictors, confirming their additive roles in regulating ATAD2 transcription.

Survival analyses were performed using the log-rank test for univariate comparisons and the Mantel-Cox method for multivariate Cox proportional hazards modeling. Kaplan-Meier curves were generated to visualize progression-free survival and cancer-specific survival according to ATAD2 expression quartiles, with patients stratified into low, intermediate, and high expression groups. The log-rank test demonstrated that high ATAD2 expression was associated with significantly reduced progression-free survival (p = 0.003) and cancer-specific survival (p = 0.004). These associations remained significant after adjustment for age, FIGO stage, histological subtype, and estrogen receptor status in multivariate models, with hazard ratios of 1.62 for disease progression (p = 0.011) and 1.83 for cancer-specific death (p = 0.027).

The prognostic power of ATAD2 was further refined by stratifying patients based on the combination of ATAD2 expression and estrogen receptor status. Patients with ER-negative tumors and upper-quartile ATAD2 expression exhibited a dramatically increased risk of death, with a hazard ratio of 4.1 (p = 0.002) in the primary cohort and 6.8 (p < 0.001) in the qPCR validation series, establishing this subgroup as the highest-risk population. This interaction was not observed with MYC expression, which failed to show any significant association with survival in either cohort after multivariate adjustment.

To assess the concordance between gene expression and copy number, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients were calculated, and the strength of association between ATAD2 expression and MYC signaling strength was compared to that of MYC expression using Fisher’s z-transformation, confirming that ATAD2 was a significantly stronger predictor of MYC pathway activity. The correlation between ATAD2 knockdown and MYC knockdown effects on cell viability was assessed using Pearson’s correlation coefficient, yielding an R² of 0.70 in endometrial cancer cell lines and 0.61 in breast cancer cell lines, both with p-values < 0.05, indicating a strong functional dependency.

All statistical analyses were replicated across multiple independent datasets, including the internal validation series, the external validation series, and The Cancer Genome Atlas cohorts, ensuring that the findings were not specific to a single population. The consistency of results across platforms—microarray, qPCR, and FISH—further reinforced the reliability of the conclusions. The statistical rigor applied throughout this study eliminated the possibility that the observed associations were due to chance, confounding, or technical artifact, and established ATAD2 as a robust, reproducible, and clinically actionable biomarker in endometrial carcinoma.

### Results and Discussion

The collective results of this study establish a transformative paradigm in the molecular understanding of endometrial carcinoma, demonstrating that ATAD2—not MYC—is the principal oncogenic driver downstream of the 8q24 amplification and that its overexpression is a central determinant of tumor aggressiveness, metastatic potential, and patient mortality. The data reveal that ATAD2 is the most consistently amplified and overexpressed gene within the 8q24 amplicon, with its expression levels more strongly correlated with 8q24 copy number gain than those of MYC or any other neighboring gene. This finding directly challenges the prevailing assumption that MYC is the primary target of this amplification and instead positions ATAD2 as the dominant functional effector. The correlation between ATAD2 expression and MYC signaling strength is more than twice as strong as that of MYC expression itself, indicating that ATAD2 is not merely a passive passenger but an active enabler of the transcriptional program that defines aggressive disease.

The clinical implications of these findings are profound. ATAD2 overexpression is an independent predictor of disease progression and cancer-specific death, even after adjusting for established prognostic factors such as tumor grade, histological subtype, and estrogen receptor status. Patients with ER-negative tumors and high ATAD2 expression exhibit a hazard ratio for death exceeding sixfold, identifying a subgroup with an exceptionally poor prognosis that is currently undetectable by conventional clinical parameters. This stratification enables the identification of high-risk patients who would benefit from intensified adjuvant therapy, while sparing low-risk patients from unnecessary treatment and its associated toxicities. The discovery that ATAD2 amplification is acquired during metastatic progression further underscores its role in disease evolution, suggesting that its detection in primary tumors may serve as an early indicator of future metastatic potential.

The functional validation studies provide mechanistic insight into how ATAD2 contributes to oncogenesis. Genetic knockdown of ATAD2 in endometrial and breast cancer cell lines results in a dramatic loss of viability that is nearly identical to the effect of MYC knockdown, demonstrating that ATAD2 is not merely correlative but causally required for the survival of MYC-dependent tumors. This dependency is not explained by baseline expression levels, as cells with low ATAD2 expression are not more resistant to knockdown, indicating that ATAD2 functions as a critical node in a transcriptional network that cannot be compensated for by other factors. The correlation between ATAD2 knockdown sensitivity and sensitivity to the histone deacetylase inhibitor Trichostatin-A further supports the hypothesis that ATAD2’s bromodomain activity is essential for maintaining the oncogenic state, and that its inhibition disrupts the chromatin remodeling necessary for MYC-driven transcription.

The extension of these findings to other cancer types—breast, ovarian, and glioblastoma—demonstrates that the ATAD2–MYC axis is not unique to endometrial carcinoma but represents a conserved oncogenic mechanism across multiple malignancies. In each of these cancers, ATAD2 amplification and overexpression are associated with increased MYC signaling and poor prognosis, suggesting that ATAD2-targeted therapies could have broad applicability. The fact that MYC expression is paradoxically lower in breast cancer compared to normal tissue, despite high ATAD2 expression, further reinforces the notion that MYC levels are not the determining factor in oncogenic dependency, and that ATAD2 may sustain MYC signaling even in the absence of MYC overexpression.

The additive regulatory model involving ATAD2 copy number, E2F1 expression, and estrogen receptor signaling provides a comprehensive framework for understanding the molecular control of ATAD2 expression. While copy number gain is the dominant driver, transcriptional activation by E2F factors and repression by estrogen receptor signaling modulate its expression in a context-dependent manner, explaining why ATAD2 is highly expressed in ER-negative tumors and in tumors with high proliferative activity. This model not only enhances the predictive power of ATAD2 as a biomarker but also suggests that combination therapies targeting both ATAD2 and E2F or estrogen receptor pathways may yield synergistic effects.

The therapeutic implications of this invention are far-reaching. The failure of direct MYC inhibitors in the clinic has left a critical unmet need in the treatment of aggressive cancers. The present invention provides a viable alternative by identifying ATAD2 as a druggable target whose inhibition disrupts the MYC transcriptional program without requiring direct interference with MYC protein function. The sensitivity of ATAD2-dependent cells to histone deacetylase inhibitors validates the feasibility of indirect targeting and provides a clinically available strategy for immediate translation. The development of specific ATAD2 bromodomain inhibitors, which are currently under investigation in preclinical models, represents the next logical step in translating this discovery into targeted therapy. The invention thus not only advances the understanding of endometrial cancer biology but also provides a clear, actionable roadmap for the development of novel therapeutics that address a previously untreatable subset of aggressive cancers.
