# DESCRIPTION

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The present invention relates to the use of versican isoforms V0 and V1 as predictive biomarkers for the progression of chronic kidney disease in patients suffering from proteinuric nephropathies. The invention is based on the discovery that elevated expression levels of versican isoforms V0 and V1 in renal tissue, as measured by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, are strongly and independently associated with the future decline of renal function, as evidenced by rising serum creatinine levels and the development of end-stage renal disease, even after accounting for conventional clinical and histological risk factors such as glomerulosclerosis and baseline renal function. The invention provides a method for identifying patients at high risk of progressive renal deterioration by measuring the expression levels of versican isoforms V0 and V1 in a renal tissue sample obtained via biopsy, and comparing those levels to established reference thresholds derived from longitudinal clinical cohorts. The method enables clinicians to stratify patients into high-risk and low-risk categories for disease progression, thereby facilitating early intervention, personalized therapeutic planning, and more accurate prognostic counseling. The invention further encompasses the use of versican isoforms V0 and V1 as components of a diagnostic panel for monitoring disease trajectory over time, allowing for the detection of subclinical worsening prior to the emergence of overt functional impairment. The expression of versican isoforms V0 and V1 is not significantly influenced by acute renal injury, proteinuria, or age, and remains predictive even in patients with preserved or moderately impaired renal function at the time of sampling, thereby offering a unique advantage over traditional biomarkers that are confounded by transient fluctuations in glomerular filtration rate or tubular function. The invention is validated across multiple independent human cohorts, including patients with focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, IgA nephropathy, lupus nephritis, membranous nephropathy, and other proteinuric kidney diseases, demonstrating consistent and reproducible associations between elevated versican isoform expression and adverse clinical outcomes. Furthermore, the biological plausibility of this biomarker is supported by in vitro and in vivo data demonstrating that versican is predominantly expressed in renal tubular epithelial cells and interstitial fibroblasts, and that its expression is upregulated by transforming growth factor-beta 1, a key mediator of renal fibrosis. The absence of versican expression in endothelial cells and the minimal presence in healthy renal tissue further support its specificity as a marker of pathological remodeling rather than normal physiological variation. The invention therefore represents a novel, non-invasive, and biologically grounded approach to predicting the natural history of chronic kidney disease, enabling earlier and more targeted therapeutic interventions that may alter the course of disease progression.

### Example 2

The invention further encompasses a diagnostic kit for the detection and quantification of versican isoforms V0 and V1 in renal tissue samples, comprising a set of primers and probes specifically designed to distinguish between the splice variants V0 and V1, a reference gene control for normalization, and instructions for use in quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction assays. The primers and probes are selected to hybridize exclusively to exon junctions unique to each isoform, thereby avoiding cross-reactivity with other versican splice variants such as V2 and V3, which are not associated with renal disease progression. The kit includes lyophilized TaqMan® Gene Expression Assays for V0 (Hs01007944_m1) and V1 (Hs01007937_m1), as well as an internal reference gene assay for cyclophilin A (PPIA; Hs99999904_m1), which has been validated for stability across diverse renal pathologies and patient demographics. The kit also contains a calibration curve generated from serial dilutions of synthetic cDNA standards corresponding to known concentrations of V0 and V1 transcripts, enabling absolute quantification when required. The instructions for use specify the optimal RNA extraction protocol using silica-membrane-based microcolumns, the pre-amplification conditions for low-input samples, and the thermal cycling parameters for the 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System, ensuring reproducibility across clinical laboratories. The kit is designed for use with formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded or frozen renal biopsy tissue, with protocols optimized for degraded or low-yield RNA samples commonly encountered in clinical practice. The diagnostic kit further includes a reference database of expression thresholds derived from a cohort of 74 patients with proteinuric nephropathies, where patients were stratified into stable and progressive groups based on longitudinal serum creatinine measurements and the occurrence of end-stage renal disease. The threshold values for elevated V0 and V1 expression are defined as the 75th percentile of expression levels observed in the stable cohort, with values exceeding this threshold indicating a statistically significant increased risk of progression. The kit may be used in conjunction with standard histopathological scoring systems for tubular atrophy, interstitial fibrosis, and glomerulosclerosis, providing a synergistic enhancement of prognostic accuracy beyond either modality alone. The invention thus provides a commercially viable, standardized, and clinically actionable tool for the early identification of patients with proteinuric kidney disease who are likely to experience rapid functional decline, thereby enabling timely initiation of renoprotective therapies such as angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors, or novel antifibrotic agents under investigation. The kit is suitable for use in both academic medical centers and community nephrology practices, and its integration into routine diagnostic workflows requires no specialized equipment beyond standard real-time PCR platforms widely available in clinical molecular diagnostics laboratories.

### Example 3

The invention further includes a method for determining the likelihood of progression of chronic kidney disease in a patient by integrating the expression levels of versican isoforms V0 and V1 with conventional clinical parameters to generate a composite prognostic score. The method involves obtaining a renal biopsy specimen from a patient with proteinuric nephropathy, extracting total RNA, and quantifying the transcript levels of versican isoforms V0 and V1 using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, with normalization to a housekeeping gene such as cyclophilin A. Concurrently, the patient’s serum creatinine level at the time of biopsy and the degree of glomerulosclerosis assessed by histopathological examination are recorded. These parameters are then input into a multivariate regression model that has been trained on a longitudinal cohort of patients with proteinuric kidney disease, wherein the outcome variable is serum creatinine level at follow-up, typically measured at 12, 24, or 36 months after biopsy. The model assigns weighted coefficients to each variable based on their independent contribution to prediction, as determined by stepwise linear regression analysis, and generates a continuous prognostic index ranging from low to high risk of progression. The model has been validated to achieve an adjusted R² value of 0.2438 when incorporating versican V1, serum creatinine at biopsy, and glomerulosclerosis, which represents a statistically significant improvement over models based on clinical parameters alone, which achieve an adjusted R² of 0.1619. The inclusion of versican V0 further improves the model’s predictive capacity, although the incremental gain is not statistically significant when V1 is already included, indicating that V1 is the dominant isoform in prognostic utility. The composite score may be presented to clinicians as a probability percentage of progression to end-stage renal disease within three years, or as a risk category such as low, intermediate, or high, based on predefined cutoffs derived from receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. The method may be implemented as a software algorithm integrated into electronic health record systems, allowing for automated calculation and display of the prognostic score upon entry of the relevant clinical and molecular data. The invention further provides for the periodic re-assessment of versican isoform expression in follow-up biopsies, enabling dynamic monitoring of disease activity and response to therapy. A rising trend in versican V1 expression over time, even in the absence of significant changes in serum creatinine or proteinuria, may indicate subclinical progression and warrant escalation of treatment. Conversely, a decline in versican expression following initiation of renoprotective therapy may serve as an early biomarker of treatment efficacy, potentially serving as a surrogate endpoint in clinical trials of novel antifibrotic agents. The method is applicable to adult and pediatric patients with proteinuric nephropathies, including those with diabetic nephropathy, IgA nephropathy, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, membranous nephropathy, and lupus nephritis, and is not limited by etiology, age, or comorbid conditions such as hypertension or diabetes. The invention thus provides a comprehensive, data-driven framework for personalized risk assessment in chronic kidney disease, transforming the management of proteinuric renal diseases from a reactive to a proactive paradigm.

### Example 4

The invention further comprises a method for identifying novel therapeutic targets for the treatment of progressive chronic kidney disease by exploiting the biological role of versican isoforms V0 and V1 in renal fibrosis and inflammation. The method involves the use of in vitro and in vivo models to demonstrate that versican expression is induced by transforming growth factor-beta 1 in renal fibroblasts but not in proximal tubular epithelial cells, suggesting a cell-type-specific regulatory mechanism that may be exploited for targeted intervention. The invention further demonstrates that versican isoforms V0 and V1 are expressed in the tubulointerstitial compartment of diseased kidneys, particularly in areas of active fibrosis, where they colocalize with interstitial fibroblasts and extracellular matrix components, but are absent from glomerular structures and endothelial cells. This spatial localization supports the hypothesis that versican contributes to the formation of a pro-fibrotic microenvironment by modulating cell-matrix interactions, promoting fibroblast activation, and facilitating the retention of inflammatory mediators such as chemokines and adhesion molecules. The invention further reveals that versican binds to P-selectin, L-selectin, and CD44 on leukocyte surfaces, thereby enhancing the recruitment and retention of mononuclear cells within the interstitial space, which perpetuates chronic inflammation and accelerates fibrogenesis. Based on these findings, the invention proposes a therapeutic strategy involving the inhibition of versican synthesis or function through the use of antisense oligonucleotides, small interfering RNA molecules, or monoclonal antibodies directed against the GAG-α and GAG-β domains of versican isoforms V0 and V1. The invention further encompasses the use of pharmacological agents that suppress the transforming growth factor-beta 1 signaling pathway, such as pirfenidone, nintedanib, or novel TGF-β receptor kinase inhibitors, as indirect means of reducing versican expression and thereby mitigating renal fibrosis. In preclinical models, the administration of such agents results in a significant reduction in renal versican mRNA and protein levels, concomitant with decreased interstitial fibrosis, reduced inflammatory cell infiltration, and improved renal function. The invention further provides for the use of versican isoforms V0 and V1 as pharmacodynamic biomarkers in clinical trials of antifibrotic therapies, allowing for the early detection of biological response prior to the emergence of changes in serum creatinine or estimated glomerular filtration rate. The measurement of versican expression in serial renal biopsies can serve as an objective, quantitative endpoint for assessing drug efficacy, thereby reducing the duration and cost of clinical trials. The invention further includes the development of transgenic animal models in which versican isoforms V0 and V1 are conditionally overexpressed in renal fibroblasts or tubular epithelial cells, enabling the direct assessment of their causal role in disease progression and the evaluation of targeted interventions. The invention thus bridges the gap between biomarker discovery and therapeutic development, transforming versican from a passive indicator of disease severity into an active participant in the pathophysiology of chronic kidney disease and a viable target for novel therapeutic modalities. The method described herein provides a roadmap for the translation of molecular insights into clinical applications, offering a dual benefit of improved prognostication and the development of mechanism-based therapies for patients with progressive proteinuric nephropathies.
