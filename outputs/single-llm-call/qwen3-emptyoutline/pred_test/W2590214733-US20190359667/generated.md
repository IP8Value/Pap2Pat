## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular biology and therapeutic protein engineering, and more specifically to engineered monomeric variants of transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) that function as potent and selective dominant-negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling. The invention encompasses novel polypeptide constructs derived from TGF-β isoforms, including TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3, in which key structural elements responsible for receptor complex assembly have been systematically removed or modified to abrogate signaling activity while preserving high-affinity binding to the type II receptor. These engineered monomers are capable of competitively inhibiting endogenous TGF-β ligands by sequestering the type II receptor without facilitating recruitment of the type I receptor, thereby blocking downstream signal transduction. The invention further includes methods for producing, purifying, formulating, and administering such engineered monomers for therapeutic use in diseases characterized by aberrant TGF-β signaling, including fibrosis, cancer progression, autoimmune disorders, and vascular remodeling.

## BACKGROUND

Transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) is a pleiotropic cytokine that plays a central role in regulating cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, apoptosis, and extracellular matrix production. The TGF-β family comprises multiple isoforms, primarily TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3, each of which signals through a conserved heterotetrameric receptor complex composed of two type II and two type I serine/threonine kinase receptors. Upon ligand binding, the type II receptor phosphorylates and activates the type I receptor, which in turn initiates intracellular signaling cascades, predominantly through the Smad pathway, but also through non-Smad pathways such as MAPK and PI3K/Akt. While TGF-β signaling is essential for normal development and tissue homeostasis, its dysregulation has been implicated in a wide array of pathological conditions, including systemic fibrosis, tumor metastasis, immune evasion, and chronic inflammatory diseases. Despite decades of research and numerous preclinical studies, no therapeutic agent targeting TGF-β has been approved for clinical use, largely due to the challenges of achieving sufficient specificity, potency, and tissue penetration while avoiding systemic toxicity.

Traditional approaches to inhibit TGF-β signaling have included neutralizing antibodies, soluble receptor decoys, small molecule kinase inhibitors, and antisense oligonucleotides. However, each of these strategies has significant limitations. Neutralizing antibodies, while highly specific, are large molecules (~150 kDa) with poor tissue penetration, particularly into dense fibrotic or tumor microenvironments. Soluble receptor decoys, such as the extracellular domain of TGF-βRII fused to an Fc domain, exhibit improved pharmacokinetics but often retain partial agonist activity or induce immune complex formation. Small molecule kinase inhibitors targeting the ATP-binding site of TGF-βRI are inherently promiscuous, as the kinase domains of related receptors are highly conserved, leading to off-target effects and dose-limiting toxicities. Furthermore, many of these agents interfere with both canonical and non-canonical signaling pathways, disrupting the nuanced biological roles of TGF-β in immune regulation and tissue repair.

Recent structural and biochemical studies have revealed that TGF-β signaling requires the formation of a ternary complex in which a single TGF-β homodimer simultaneously binds two type II receptors and two type I receptors in a cooperative, assembly-dependent manner. Notably, monomeric forms of TGF-β, generated by mutation of the interchain disulfide bond at Cys-77, retain the ability to bind type II receptors and can non-covalently dimerize in solution to form signaling-competent complexes. This dimerization is mediated primarily by a conserved amphipathic α-helix, referred to as the “heel” region (α-helix 3), which engages in extensive hydrophobic interactions with the palm domain of the opposing monomer. This same helix also contributes substantially to the composite interface required for type I receptor binding. These findings suggested that targeted disruption of the heel helix might selectively impair dimerization and type I receptor recruitment while preserving type II receptor binding, thereby generating a molecule capable of acting as a dominant-negative inhibitor.

Prior attempts to engineer dominant-negative TGF-β variants have focused on point mutations, truncations, or fusion constructs that either failed to achieve complete loss of signaling activity or compromised receptor binding affinity. Moreover, many of these constructs exhibited poor solubility, aggregation propensity, or instability under physiological conditions, rendering them unsuitable for therapeutic development. The present invention overcomes these longstanding limitations by introducing a precisely defined deletion of the heel helix region (residues 52–71) in combination with strategic substitutions that enhance solubility and restore high-affinity binding to the type II receptor. The resulting engineered monomers, designated mini-monomeric TGF-β (mmTGF-β), are structurally intact, monomeric in solution under physiological conditions, incapable of recruiting the type I receptor, yet bind the type II receptor with nanomolar affinity. These molecules effectively compete with endogenous TGF-β dimers for receptor occupancy, thereby blocking signal transduction with high potency and selectivity. The invention further demonstrates that such engineered monomers can be produced at scale using bacterial expression systems, purified to homogeneity, and formulated for systemic or localized administration, providing a novel therapeutic modality with distinct advantages over existing approaches.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel class of engineered polypeptides that function as dominant-negative inhibitors of transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) signaling. These polypeptides are derived from native TGF-β1, TGF-β2, or TGF-β3 sequences and comprise a deletion of residues corresponding to the heel α-helix (α-helix 3), specifically residues 52 to 71, and substitution of cysteine at position 77 with serine, thereby eliminating the interchain disulfide bond and preventing homodimer formation. In certain embodiments, the engineered monomers further comprise one or more amino acid substitutions in the type II receptor binding interface to restore or enhance binding affinity for the type II receptor, thereby enabling potent competitive inhibition of endogenous TGF-β ligands. The resulting constructs are monomeric, structurally stable, soluble at physiological pH, and incapable of recruiting the type I receptor, yet retain high-affinity binding to the type II receptor. These properties enable the engineered monomers to act as potent, selective, and non-signaling antagonists of TGF-β signaling by sequestering the type II receptor and preventing formation of the active receptor complex.

In a preferred embodiment, the invention provides a mini-monomeric TGF-β2 variant (mmTGF-β2-7M) comprising the following modifications: deletion of residues 52–71, substitution of Cys-77 with Ser, and seven additional substitutions at positions 25, 26, 89, 92, 94, 95, and 98, wherein Lys-25 is replaced by Arg, Arg-26 is replaced by Lys, Leu-89 is replaced by Val, Ile-92 is replaced by Val, Asn-94 is replaced by Arg, Thr-95 is replaced by Lys, and Ile-98 is replaced by Val. This variant exhibits no detectable signaling activity in cellular reporter assays, yet inhibits TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3 signaling with IC50 values ranging from approximately 19 nM to 70 nM. The engineered monomer binds the type II receptor with a dissociation constant (KD) of approximately 10–20 nM, comparable to that of native TGF-β1 and TGF-β3, and is incapable of recruiting the type I receptor in the presence of the type II receptor, as demonstrated by surface plasmon resonance and time-resolved fluorescence resonance energy transfer assays. The polypeptide is soluble at concentrations exceeding 2 mg/mL in phosphate-buffered saline at pH 7.4 and retains its native fold, as confirmed by nuclear magnetic resonance and X-ray crystallography, with the deleted heel region replaced by a flexible loop that maintains the structural integrity of the receptor-binding fingertips.

The invention further provides nucleic acid sequences encoding the engineered monomers, expression vectors containing such sequences, host cells transformed with such vectors, and methods for producing the engineered monomers by recombinant expression in bacterial systems, followed by refolding from inclusion bodies and purification by cation exchange chromatography. The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the engineered monomers in association with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, for use in the treatment of diseases characterized by aberrant TGF-β signaling, including but not limited to fibrotic diseases such as systemic sclerosis, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and liver cirrhosis; cancers such as breast, pancreatic, and colorectal carcinoma; autoimmune disorders such as systemic lupus erythematosus and rheumatoid arthritis; and vascular pathologies such as pulmonary arterial hypertension and atherosclerosis. The invention further provides methods of inhibiting TGF-β signaling in a subject in need thereof by administering an effective amount of the engineered monomer, wherein the monomer is administered systemically, locally, or intratumorally, and wherein the administration results in a reduction in Smad2/3 phosphorylation, decreased extracellular matrix deposition, reduced tumor cell invasion, or suppression of immune cell polarization toward a pro-fibrotic or immunosuppressive phenotype.

The invention further provides diagnostic and research tools comprising the engineered monomers for use in assays to detect TGF-β activity, screen for modulators of TGF-β signaling, or study receptor-ligand interactions in vitro and in vivo. The engineered monomers are uniquely suited for these applications due to their high specificity, lack of agonist activity, and ability to function as competitive inhibitors without inducing receptor internalization or downstream signaling. The invention thus represents a significant advance in the field of TGF-β biology and therapeutics, providing a novel class of inhibitors with superior pharmacological properties compared to existing modalities.
