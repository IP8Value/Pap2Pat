# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of immunotherapy and cellular medicine, specifically to the generation, characterization, and therapeutic application of allogeneic invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells engineered from hematopoietic stem cells for the treatment of cancer. More particularly, the invention provides a novel in vitro method for the scalable, high-yield, and high-purity production of human allogeneic HSC-engineered iNKT cells, designated AlloHSC-iNKT cells, which exhibit potent antitumor activity, intrinsic resistance to graft-versus-host disease, low immunogenicity, and compatibility with cryopreservation and genetic modification. The invention further encompasses derivatives of these cells, including chimeric antigen receptor-engineered AlloHSC-iNKT cells and HLA-ablated universal HSC-iNKT cells, which are suitable for off-the-shelf administration to multiple patients without the need for patient-specific manufacturing or immunosuppressive conditioning. These cells are particularly suited for the treatment of hematologic malignancies and solid tumors, including but not limited to melanoma, multiple myeloma, leukemia, lung cancer, and prostate cancer, and represent a transformative advancement in the development of universally applicable, non-autologous cellular therapeutics.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of death worldwide, and despite significant advances in conventional therapies such as chemotherapy, radiation, and surgery, many patients with advanced or refractory disease continue to face poor prognoses. The emergence of immunotherapy has revolutionized cancer treatment by harnessing the body’s own immune system to recognize and eliminate malignant cells. Among the most promising approaches are adoptive cell therapies that involve the ex vivo expansion and reinfusion of autologous T cells engineered to target tumor-associated antigens. Chimeric antigen receptor T cell (CAR-T) therapy, for example, has demonstrated remarkable efficacy in treating certain B-cell malignancies, leading to regulatory approvals for products targeting CD19. Similarly, T cell receptor (TCR)-engineered T cells and tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) have shown clinical benefit in melanoma and other solid tumors.

However, the widespread clinical adoption of autologous cell therapies is severely limited by several inherent challenges. The process of collecting, engineering, expanding, and reinfusing a patient’s own T cells is time-consuming, often requiring several weeks, during which patients with rapidly progressing disease may deteriorate. The manufacturing process is logistically complex, requiring specialized facilities, highly trained personnel, and stringent quality controls. Furthermore, the cost of autologous therapies is prohibitively high, rendering them inaccessible to many patients and healthcare systems. Critically, patients with advanced disease, prior chemotherapy exposure, or significant lymphopenia often cannot yield sufficient numbers of functional T cells for manufacturing, thereby excluding them from treatment entirely.

To overcome these limitations, allogeneic cell therapies derived from healthy donors have been pursued as a potential solution. These “off-the-shelf” products are manufactured in large batches from a single donor source and can be cryopreserved, stored, and administered to multiple patients on demand. While this approach offers the promise of scalability, standardization, and reduced cost, it introduces a new set of challenges, primarily centered on immune rejection and safety. Conventional allogeneic T cells, even when engineered to express a tumor-targeting CAR or TCR, retain their endogenous T cell receptor (TCR), which recognizes mismatched human leukocyte antigens (HLA) on recipient tissues and can trigger graft-versus-host disease (GvHD), a potentially fatal condition characterized by immune-mediated destruction of the host’s skin, liver, gastrointestinal tract, and other organs. To mitigate this risk, gene-editing technologies such as CRISPR-Cas9 have been employed to disrupt the endogenous TCR loci, but this adds further complexity to manufacturing, increases the risk of off-target effects, and may impair T cell fitness and persistence.

Natural killer (NK) cells have emerged as an alternative allogeneic platform due to their lack of HLA-restricted recognition and inherently low risk of inducing GvHD. However, NK cells exhibit limited in vivo expansion, poor persistence, and variable antitumor efficacy across patients. Moreover, NK cells are highly sensitive to cryopreservation, often losing viability and cytotoxic function following freeze-thaw cycles, which severely compromises their utility as a standardized, commercially viable product. Additionally, NK cells lack the capacity to respond to specific antigenic stimulation via TCR engagement, limiting their ability to be directed toward defined tumor antigens through genetic engineering in the same manner as T cells.

Invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells represent a unique subset of T lymphocytes that bridge innate and adaptive immunity. Unlike conventional T cells, iNKT cells express a semi-invariant TCR that recognizes lipid antigens presented by the non-polymorphic CD1d molecule, which is widely expressed on tumor cells and antigen-presenting cells. This recognition allows iNKT cells to respond rapidly and robustly to a broad spectrum of cancers without requiring HLA matching. Importantly, iNKT cells do not induce GvHD in allogeneic hosts, as they lack the capacity to recognize mismatched HLA molecules or protein alloantigens. Furthermore, iNKT cells possess potent antitumor effector functions, including the secretion of Th1 cytokines such as interferon-gamma and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, direct cytotoxicity mediated by perforin and granzyme B, and the ability to modulate the tumor microenvironment by recruiting and activating dendritic cells, NK cells, and conventional T cells. They also exhibit superior tissue homing capabilities, enabling them to infiltrate solid tumors more effectively than conventional T cells or NK cells.

Despite these compelling attributes, the clinical translation of iNKT cell therapy has been hindered by their extreme rarity in human peripheral blood, typically comprising less than 0.01% of circulating lymphocytes. This scarcity makes it impractical to isolate sufficient numbers of autologous iNKT cells for therapeutic use, and attempts to expand allogeneic iNKT cells from donor blood are fraught with contamination by conventional αβ T cells, which reintroduce the risk of GvHD. Consequently, there has been no clinically viable method to generate large-scale, pure, allogeneic iNKT cell products suitable for off-the-shelf deployment.

Previous attempts to generate iNKT cells from hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) have relied on in vivo reconstitution strategies, wherein genetically modified HSCs are transplanted into immunodeficient mice to allow differentiation into iNKT cells within a physiological thymic environment. While this approach has demonstrated proof-of-concept, it is not translatable to human clinical use due to the impracticality, ethical constraints, and biological variability associated with in vivo models. There remains a critical unmet need for a robust, scalable, and clinically applicable in vitro method to produce pure, functional, allogeneic iNKT cells that are free from contaminating conventional T cells, resistant to host immune rejection, amenable to cryopreservation, and compatible with further genetic engineering for enhanced tumor targeting.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by providing a novel in vitro culture system that enables the efficient, reproducible, and high-yield generation of allogeneic HSC-engineered iNKT cells from both cord blood and mobilized peripheral blood stem cell sources. This method eliminates the need for in vivo reconstitution, avoids contamination by bystander T cells, and produces cells with a phenotype and functionality superior to both endogenous iNKT cells and conventional allogeneic T or NK cell products. The invention further demonstrates that these cells can be further engineered to express chimeric antigen receptors or to ablate HLA expression, thereby creating next-generation universal cellular therapeutics capable of targeting a wide array of cancers with unprecedented safety, efficacy, and scalability.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for the in vitro generation of allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell-engineered invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells, designated AlloHSC-iNKT cells, which are suitable for use as off-the-shelf cellular therapeutics for the treatment of cancer. The method comprises the steps of transducing human hematopoietic stem cells with a lentiviral vector encoding a human invariant iNKT T cell receptor (TCR) sequence and a suicide gene, followed by culturing the transduced cells in a two-stage artificial thymic organoid (ATO) system supplemented with α-galactosylceramide (αGC) to drive differentiation and expansion into a pure population of iNKT cells. The resulting AlloHSC-iNKT cells exhibit a CD4−CD8+ or CD4−CD8− phenotype, express high levels of memory and NK cell markers, secrete potent Th1 cytokines and cytotoxic molecules, and demonstrate robust antitumor activity against a broad spectrum of hematologic and solid tumors. Crucially, these cells are free of endogenous TCR expression and do not induce graft-versus-host disease, even upon exposure to mismatched allogeneic human cells. Furthermore, the cells retain full functionality following cryopreservation, a property not shared by conventional NK cells, and can be genetically modified to express chimeric antigen receptors without compromising yield or phenotype. The invention further encompasses a method for generating HLA-ablated universal HSC-iNKT cells by combining the above transduction with CRISPR-Cas9-mediated ablation of the B2M and CIITA genes, resulting in cells that are resistant to both T cell- and NK cell-mediated allorejection. These universal cells, including their CAR-engineered derivatives, can be administered to any patient without the need for HLA matching or immunosuppressive preconditioning, thereby enabling true off-the-shelf availability. The invention further provides compositions comprising these cells, pharmaceutical formulations thereof, and methods of using them to treat cancer in human subjects.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for producing allogeneic HSC-engineered iNKT cells comprising: (a) obtaining human hematopoietic stem cells from a donor source selected from the group consisting of cord blood and granulocyte-colony-stimulating factor-mobilized peripheral blood stem cells; (b) transducing the hematopoietic stem cells with a lentiviral vector encoding a human invariant iNKT TCR α chain, a human invariant iNKT TCR β chain, and a suicide gene under the control of a constitutive promoter; (c) culturing the transduced hematopoietic stem cells in a first stage of an artificial thymic organoid culture system for a period of at least six weeks to induce differentiation into iNKT lineage cells; (d) dissociating the differentiated cells from the artificial thymic organoid and transferring them to a second stage of culture in the presence of α-galactosylceramide-loaded irradiated peripheral blood mononuclear cells and interleukin-7 and interleukin-15 to promote expansion; (e) harvesting the resulting population of iNKT cells, wherein the harvested population comprises at least 95% cells expressing the transgenic iNKT TCR and less than 0.1% cells expressing endogenous αβ TCRs; and (f) cryopreserving the harvested iNKT cells for storage and subsequent administration. In a preferred embodiment, the suicide gene is sr39TK, and the lentiviral vector is constructed from a pMNDW backbone. In another preferred embodiment, the artificial thymic organoid is generated using MS5-DLL4 stromal cells cultured in a medium supplemented with B27, ascorbic acid phosphate, and cytokines including FLT3 ligand and IL-7.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a population of allogeneic HSC-engineered iNKT cells produced by the method described above, wherein the cells are characterized by: (i) expression of the transgenic invariant iNKT TCR as detected by Vβ11 and TCRαβ staining; (ii) absence of endogenous αβ TCR expression as confirmed by single-cell TCR sequencing; (iii) a CD4−CD8+ or CD4−CD8− surface phenotype; (iv) high expression of CD45RO, CD161, CCR4, CCR5, and CXCR3; (v) high intracellular expression of interferon-gamma, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, perforin, and granzyme B upon stimulation; (vi) resistance to graft-versus-host disease in mixed lymphocyte reaction assays and in vivo xenograft models; (vii) resistance to cryopreservation-induced loss of viability and function; and (viii) expression of the sr39TK suicide gene. In a preferred embodiment, the population comprises at least 1 × 10^10 cells per donor, and in a more preferred embodiment, the population comprises at least 1 × 10^11 cells per cord blood donor or at least 1 × 10^13 cells per mobilized peripheral blood stem cell donor.

In a further embodiment, the invention provides a method for producing a chimeric antigen receptor-engineered allogeneic HSC-iNKT cell product, comprising: (a) producing allogeneic HSC-engineered iNKT cells according to the method described above; (b) stimulating the iNKT cells with CD3/CD28 activator beads in the presence of interleukin-7 and interleukin-15; (c) transducing the stimulated iNKT cells with a retroviral vector encoding a chimeric antigen receptor specific for a tumor-associated antigen and a truncated epidermal growth factor receptor reporter; (d) expanding the transduced cells in culture for an additional one to two weeks; and (e) harvesting the resulting chimeric antigen receptor-engineered iNKT cells, wherein the cells express the chimeric antigen receptor at a level of at least 70% and maintain expression of the transgenic iNKT TCR and the sr39TK suicide gene. In a preferred embodiment, the chimeric antigen receptor is specific for B cell maturation antigen (BCMA), and the retroviral vector is constructed from a pMP71 backbone. In another preferred embodiment, the chimeric antigen receptor comprises a single-chain variable fragment derived from a humanized anti-BCMA antibody, a 4-1BB costimulatory domain, and a CD3ζ signaling domain.

In yet another embodiment, the invention provides a method for producing a universal allogeneic HSC-iNKT cell product resistant to host immune rejection, comprising: (a) obtaining human hematopoietic stem cells from a donor source; (b) transducing the hematopoietic stem cells with a lentiviral vector encoding a human invariant iNKT TCR α chain, a human invariant iNKT TCR β chain, and a suicide gene; (c) electroporating the transduced cells with a CRISPR-Cas9 ribonucleoprotein complex comprising guide RNAs targeting the B2M and CIITA genes; (d) culturing the edited cells in the two-stage artificial thymic organoid system as described above to generate HLA-ablated iNKT cells; and (e) optionally further transducing the resulting iNKT cells with a retroviral vector encoding a chimeric antigen receptor to produce a universal CAR-engineered iNKT cell product. In a preferred embodiment, the cancer is selected from the group consisting of acute lymphoblastic leukemia, chronic lymphocytic leukemia, multiple myeloma, melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer, prostate cancer, ovarian cancer, and pancreatic cancer. In a more preferred embodiment, the subject has previously failed to respond to standard therapies, including chemotherapy, radiation, or autologous CAR-T cell therapy. In another more preferred embodiment, the subject is not preconditioned with lymphodepleting chemotherapy prior to administration. In yet another embodiment, the method further comprises administering ganciclovir to the subject to induce suicide of the administered cells in the event of adverse events.

In a further embodiment, the invention provides a kit for the production of allogeneic HSC-engineered iNKT cells, comprising: (a) a lentiviral vector encoding a human invariant iNKT TCR α chain, a human invariant iNKT TCR β chain, and a suicide gene; (b) a CRISPR-Cas9 ribonucleoprotein complex comprising guide RNAs targeting the B2M and CIITA genes; (c) a retroviral vector encoding a chimeric antigen receptor and a truncated epidermal growth factor receptor reporter; (d) a culture medium formulation for artificial thymic organoid generation; (e) α-galactosylceramide; (f) recombinant human interleukin-7 and interleukin-15; (g) CD3/CD28 activator beads; and (h) instructions for use. In a preferred embodiment, the kit further comprises magnetic beads for isolation of CD34+ hematopoietic stem cells and for enrichment of HLA-negative iNKT cells.

The invention further provides a method of manufacturing a clinical-grade allogeneic HSC-iNKT cell product in a closed, automated system, wherein the entire process from stem cell transduction to final cell harvest is performed under current good manufacturing practice (cGMP) conditions without the use of animal-derived components, thereby enabling scalable, reproducible, and regulatory-compliant production of off-the-shelf cellular therapeutics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive and transformative platform for the generation of allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell-engineered invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells, designated AlloHSC-iNKT cells, which are uniquely suited for use as universal, off-the-shelf cellular therapeutics in the treatment of cancer. The invention overcomes the longstanding limitations of autologous cell therapies and the safety and logistical challenges of conventional allogeneic T and NK cell products by leveraging the intrinsic biological properties of iNKT cells and combining them with advanced genetic engineering and in vitro differentiation technologies. The resulting cell product is not merely an improved version of existing cellular therapies but represents a fundamentally new class of immunotherapeutic agent that is scalable, safe, broadly applicable, and manufacturable at commercial scale.

The foundation of the invention lies in the discovery that human hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs), when transduced with a lentiviral vector encoding the invariant human iNKT TCR and cultured in a precisely controlled artificial thymic organoid (ATO) system, undergo a highly efficient and faithful differentiation program that recapitulates the developmental trajectory of endogenous iNKT cells but without the contamination of conventional αβ T cells. This is a critical advancement, as previous attempts to generate iNKT cells in vitro from HSCs had yielded heterogeneous populations containing significant numbers of contaminating conventional T cells, which carry the risk of inducing graft-versus-host disease. The inventors have demonstrated that the transgenic iNKT TCR induces allelic exclusion, effectively silencing endogenous TCR rearrangement and ensuring that every differentiated cell expresses only the engineered, invariant TCR. This results in a product that is not only pure but also functionally uniform, a prerequisite for regulatory approval and clinical predictability.

The ATO culture system employed in the invention is a three-dimensional, stromal cell-based platform that mimics the thymic microenvironment necessary for T cell development. The system utilizes MS5-DLL4 murine bone marrow-derived stromal cells engineered to express the Notch ligand Delta-like 4, which is essential for T lineage commitment. These stromal cells are seeded into transwell inserts and co-cultured with transduced HSCs in a serum-free medium supplemented with B27, ascorbic acid phosphate, and key cytokines including FLT3 ligand and IL-7. This formulation supports the survival, proliferation, and stepwise differentiation of HSCs through the CD4−CD8− double-negative stage, to the CD4+CD8+ double-positive stage, and ultimately to the mature CD4−CD8+ or CD4−CD8− single-positive iNKT phenotype. The entire differentiation phase lasts approximately eight weeks, during which the transduced HSCs undergo more than a 100-fold expansion, yielding a population enriched in iNKT lineage cells.

Following differentiation, the cells are transferred to a second stage of culture in which they are expanded in the presence of α-galactosylceramide (αGC), a synthetic glycolipid ligand that binds to CD1d and activates iNKT cells via their invariant TCR. To provide antigen-presenting support without introducing contaminating T cells, irradiated peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) loaded with αGC are used as feeder cells. These irradiated cells serve as a source of CD1d and cytokines but are incapable of proliferation or immune activation. The addition of recombinant IL-7 and IL-15 further enhances the survival and expansion of the iNKT cells. This stage lasts two to three weeks and results in an additional 100- to 1,000-fold expansion, yielding a final product with cell numbers sufficient to provide hundreds to hundreds of thousands of therapeutic doses from a single donor. For example, a single cord blood unit containing approximately five million CD34+ HSCs yields over five hundred billion AlloHSC-iNKT cells, while a single mobilized peripheral blood stem cell unit yields over thirty trillion cells. This level of scalability is unprecedented in the field of cellular therapy and enables the commercial production of a single batch to treat thousands of patients.

The resulting AlloHSC-iNKT cells are characterized by a phenotype that closely mirrors that of endogenous human iNKT cells but with enhanced functional properties. Flow cytometry analysis confirms that over 99% of the cells express the transgenic iNKT TCR, identified by Vβ11 and TCRαβ staining, and that endogenous TCR expression is undetectable at the single-cell level, as confirmed by deep TCR sequencing. The cells predominantly exhibit a CD4−CD8+ or CD4−CD8− phenotype, which is associated with heightened cytotoxicity and proinflammatory function, in contrast to the CD4+ subset found in endogenous iNKT cells, which is more regulatory in nature. The cells express high levels of memory markers such as CD45RO, NK cell markers such as CD161, and tissue homing receptors including CCR4, CCR5, and CXCR3, which facilitate their migration to inflamed tissues and solid tumors. Upon stimulation with αGC, the cells rapidly proliferate and secrete high levels of interferon-gamma, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and interleukin-2, while producing minimal levels of Th2 or Th17 cytokines, indicating a strong Th1 bias. Intracellular staining reveals that individual cells produce abundant perforin and granzyme B, confirming their direct cytotoxic potential.

A critical and unexpected feature of the AlloHSC-iNKT cells is their resistance to cryopreservation. Unlike conventional NK cells, which suffer severe loss of viability and function following freeze-thaw cycles, AlloHSC-iNKT cells retain their phenotype, cytokine secretion capacity, and tumor-killing ability after cryopreservation in standard cryoprotectants such as CryoStor CS10. This property is essential for commercial distribution, as it allows for centralized manufacturing, long-term storage, and just-in-time delivery to clinical sites worldwide. The ability to cryopreserve without functional degradation transforms these cells from a laboratory curiosity into a viable, globally distributable therapeutic product.

The invention further demonstrates that AlloHSC-iNKT cells can be efficiently engineered to express chimeric antigen receptors (CARs) without compromising their purity, phenotype, or expansion potential. A second-generation CAR targeting B cell maturation antigen (BCMA), a validated target in multiple myeloma, was introduced using a retroviral vector encoding the CAR and a truncated epidermal growth factor receptor (tEGFR) as a surface marker for tracking. The transduction efficiency exceeded 80%, and the resulting AlloBCAR-iNKT cells retained high expression of the transgenic iNKT TCR and the sr39TK suicide gene. Importantly, these dual-engineered cells exhibited a triple mechanism of tumor targeting: recognition of CD1d via the invariant TCR, recognition of BCMA via the CAR, and recognition of stress ligands such as ULBP and MICA/B via endogenous NK receptors such as NKG2D and DNAM-1. This multi-targeting capability confers a significant advantage over conventional CAR-T cells, which are vulnerable to antigen escape, a common mechanism of relapse in CAR-T therapy. In preclinical models of multiple myeloma, AlloBCAR-iNKT cells achieved complete tumor eradication and long-term survival, whereas conventional BCAR-T cells, despite initial efficacy, induced fatal graft-versus-host disease and failed to provide durable protection.

The most profound innovation of the invention is the development of HLA-ablated universal HSC-iNKT cells (UHSC-iNKT cells). While AlloHSC-iNKT cells already exhibit low immunogenicity due to reduced expression of HLA class I and II molecules, residual HLA expression may still render them susceptible to host immune rejection in certain clinical contexts. To eliminate this risk entirely, the inventors employed CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing to simultaneously ablate the B2M gene, which encodes the β2-microglobulin subunit required for all HLA class I molecules, and the CIITA gene, which encodes the master transcriptional regulator of HLA class II expression. This dual ablation was performed on the transduced HSCs prior to differentiation, ensuring that the HLA-null phenotype is inherited by all progeny cells. The editing efficiency exceeded 50%, and the resulting UHSC-iNKT cells showed less than 5% HLA-I and less than 1% HLA-II expression. These cells were resistant to both T cell- and NK cell-mediated allorejection in vitro and in vivo, demonstrating no response in mixed lymphocyte reactions and surviving prolonged exposure to mismatched NK cells. When further engineered to express a BCMA CAR, the resulting universal CAR-engineered iNKT cells (UBCAR-iNKT cells) achieved complete and durable tumor eradication in a high tumor burden model of multiple myeloma, while conventional BCAR-T cells induced GvHD and succumbed to tumor relapse. The UBCAR-iNKT cells represent the first truly universal cellular therapy product that can be administered to any patient, regardless of HLA type, without the need for immunosuppression, conditioning, or donor matching.

The safety profile of the AlloHSC-iNKT platform is exceptional. In vitro mixed lymphocyte reaction assays demonstrated that AlloHSC-iNKT cells did not produce interferon-gamma in response to mismatched PBMCs from multiple healthy donors, whereas conventional T cells triggered robust alloreactivity. In vivo, transplantation of AlloHSC-iNKT cells into NSG mice bearing human tumors did not induce GvHD, as confirmed by histological examination of tissues, which showed no lymphocytic infiltration, whereas conventional T cells caused severe multi-organ damage and rapid mortality. The same safety profile was maintained in CAR-engineered and HLA-ablated derivatives. The inclusion of the sr39TK suicide gene provides an additional layer of safety, as administration of ganciclovir leads to selective elimination of the engineered cells within hours, allowing for rapid intervention in the event of cytokine release syndrome or other adverse events. This suicide switch is clinically validated, as sr39TK has been used in prior cellular therapies with an established safety record.

The invention further demonstrates that AlloHSC-iNKT cells exhibit superior antitumor efficacy compared to both conventional CAR-T cells and NK cells in multiple preclinical models. In a human melanoma xenograft model, AlloHSC-iNKT cells suppressed tumor growth more effectively than PBMC-derived NK cells and achieved complete tumor regression in a subset of animals. In a multiple myeloma model, AlloBCAR-iNKT cells and UBCAR-iNKT cells outperformed conventional BCAR-T cells in both low and high tumor burden settings, achieving complete responses and long-term survival without GvHD. The mechanism of action involves direct cytotoxicity, cytokine-mediated immune activation, and modulation of the tumor microenvironment, including the recruitment of endogenous immune cells. The cells also exhibit enhanced persistence in vivo, as evidenced by their detection in the liver, spleen, and lungs of treated mice for extended periods, suggesting a capacity for long-term surveillance.

The manufacturing process described herein is robust, reproducible, and scalable. The invention has been successfully replicated across twelve independent donors, including both cord blood and mobilized peripheral blood stem cell sources, with consistent yields and purity. The process is amenable to automation and closed-system processing, and the use of serum-free, xeno-free media and defined reagents ensures compliance with current good manufacturing practice (cGMP) standards. The final product is cryopreserved, allowing for batch release testing, quality control, and global distribution. The invention eliminates the need for patient-specific manufacturing, reduces costs by orders of magnitude, and enables the treatment of patients who are ineligible for autologous therapies due to disease burden or prior treatment.

The invention thus provides a complete solution to the central challenges of cellular immunotherapy: safety, scalability, efficacy, and accessibility. It transforms iNKT cells from a rare and inaccessible cell type into a universal, manufacturable, and clinically deployable therapeutic platform. The resulting products are not merely cell therapies but represent a new paradigm in cancer medicine: off-the-shelf, universally compatible, multi-mechanistic, and self-regulating cellular agents capable of eradicating tumors across a wide spectrum of malignancies with minimal risk to the patient.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Generation of Allogeneic HSC-Engineered iNKT Cells from Cord Blood and Mobilized Peripheral Blood Stem Cells

Human hematopoietic stem cells were isolated from cryopreserved cord blood units purchased from HemaCare and from granulocyte-colony-stimulating factor-mobilized peripheral blood stem cell units obtained from HemaCare and Cincinnati Children’s Hospital Medical Center. CD34+ cells were enriched using magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS) with ClinMACS CD34+ microbeads, yielding a purity of greater than 95% as confirmed by flow cytometry. Cells were revived in X-VIVO 15 serum-free medium supplemented with stem cell factor (50 ng/mL), FLT3 ligand (50 ng/mL), thrombopoietin (50 ng/mL), and interleukin-3 (10 ng/mL) for 24 hours. The cells were then transduced with a lentiviral vector encoding a synthetic tricistronic gene comprising the human invariant iNKT TCR α chain (Vα24-Jα18), the human invariant iNKT TCR β chain (Vβ11), and the sr39TK suicide gene, separated by P2A and F2A ribosomal skip sequences, under the control of the EF1α promoter. Transduction was performed using a multiplicity of infection of 5, with polybrene (8 μg/mL) added to enhance viral entry. Transduction efficiency, measured by intracellular staining for Vβ11 at 72 hours post-transduction, consistently exceeded 50% across all donors. Transduced cells were then seeded into artificial thymic organoids (ATOs) generated by co-culturing MS5-DLL4 stromal cells with transduced HSCs in a serum-free RB27 medium composed of RPMI 1640, 4% B27 supplement, 30 mM L-ascorbic acid 2-phosphate, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, 1% Glutamax, and 5 ng/mL each of FLT3 ligand and IL-7. ATOs were formed by centrifuging 1.5–6 × 10^5 MS5-DLL4 cells with 0.3–10 × 10^4 transduced HSCs in 1.5 mL Eppendorf tubes, followed by plating on transwell inserts. Medium was changed every three to four days. After eight weeks of culture, ATOs were dissociated by gentle pipetting and filtered through a 50 μm nylon strainer. The resulting cells were then transferred to a second-stage expansion culture in which they were co-cultured with irradiated (6,000 rads) αGC-loaded PBMCs at a 1:1 ratio in C10 medium supplemented with IL-7 (10 ng/mL) and IL-15 (10 ng/mL). The culture was maintained for an additional two to three weeks, with fresh αGC-PBMCs added at week two if expansion was suboptimal. At the end of the culture period, the cells were harvested, counted, and analyzed by flow cytometry. The final product consistently contained over 99% cells expressing the transgenic iNKT TCR (Vβ11+TCRαβ+) and less than 0.1% cells expressing endogenous TCRs. The average yield was 5 × 10^11 cells per cord blood donor and 3 × 10^13 cells per mobilized peripheral blood stem cell donor. The cells exhibited a CD4−CD8+ or CD4−CD8− phenotype, with no detectable CD4+CD8− population. Cryopreservation in CryoStor CS10 followed by thawing did not affect viability, phenotype, or function, as confirmed by flow cytometry and cytokine secretion assays.

### Example 2: Phenotypic and Functional Characterization of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

AlloHSC-iNKT cells generated from cord blood and mobilized peripheral blood stem cells were analyzed in parallel with endogenous human iNKT cells (PBMC-iNKT), conventional αβ T cells (PBMC-Tc), γδ T cells, and NK cells isolated from healthy donor PBMCs. Flow cytometry analysis revealed that AlloHSC-iNKT cells expressed high levels of CD45RO (memory marker), CD161 (NK marker), CCR4, CCR5, and CXCR3 (tissue homing receptors), with expression levels comparable to or exceeding those of endogenous iNKT cells. Intracellular staining for cytokines and cytotoxic molecules following stimulation with αGC showed that AlloHSC-iNKT cells produced significantly higher levels of interferon-gamma, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, perforin, and granzyme B than PBMC-iNKT or PBMC-Tc cells. ELISA analysis of culture supernatants confirmed a Th1-skewed cytokine profile, with high levels of IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-2 and minimal IL-4 or IL-17 production. Deep RNA sequencing of AlloHSC-iNKT cells revealed a gene expression signature highly similar to that of endogenous iNKT cells, with high expression of ZBTB16 (PLZF), TBX21 (T-bet), NFKB1, and JUN, and low expression of GATA3 and RORC. Notably, AlloHSC-iNKT cells expressed exceptionally high levels of NK activating receptors such as NCR1, NCR2, KLR2, and KLR3, and low levels of NK inhibitory receptors such as KIR3DL1 and KIR2DL1. This NK-activating signature was confirmed at the protein level by flow cytometry, which showed high surface expression of NKG2D and DNAM-1 and near absence of killer immunoglobulin-like receptors (KIRs). These findings indicate that AlloHSC-iNKT cells possess a hybrid innate-adaptive phenotype with enhanced cytotoxic potential.

### Example 3: In Vitro and In Vivo Antitumor Activity of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

The antitumor capacity of AlloHSC-iNKT cells was evaluated in vitro against five human tumor cell lines: A375 (melanoma), K562 (leukemia), H292 (lung cancer), PC3 (prostate cancer), and MM.1S (multiple myeloma), all engineered to express firefly luciferase and enhanced green fluorescence protein (Fluc-EGFP) for quantification. In co-culture assays at effector-to-target ratios of 1:1 to 5:1, AlloHSC-iNKT cells demonstrated superior tumor killing compared to PBMC-NK cells across all five lines, with up to 90% lysis of A375 and K562 cells at 24 hours. This killing was mediated by NKG2D and DNAM-1, as blockade with neutralizing antibodies reduced cytotoxicity by 60–80%. Cryopreserved AlloHSC-iNKT cells retained full killing capacity, whereas cryopreserved PBMC-NK cells lost over 70% of their function. In vivo, NSG mice were injected subcutaneously with A375-FG cells and received intravenous infusions of AlloHSC-iNKT cells, PBMC-NK cells, or vehicle control. Bioluminescence imaging showed that AlloHSC-iNKT cells suppressed tumor growth more effectively than PBMC-NK cells, with a 90% reduction in tumor burden by day 21. Terminal tumor weights were significantly lower in the AlloHSC-iNKT group. These results demonstrate that AlloHSC-iNKT cells possess potent, NK-mediated antitumor activity that is superior to conventional NK cells and is preserved after cryopreservation.

### Example 4: Generation and Characterization of BCMA-CAR-Engineered AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

AlloHSC-iNKT cells were stimulated with CD3/CD28 activator beads in the presence of IL-7 and IL-15 for two days, then transduced with a retroviral vector encoding a second-generation BCMA CAR (scFV-4-1BB-CD3ζ) and a truncated EGFR reporter. Transduction efficiency, assessed by tEGFR expression, exceeded 80%. The resulting AlloBCAR-iNKT cells retained 98% expression of the transgenic iNKT TCR and 99% expression of the sr39TK suicide gene. In vitro, AlloBCAR-iNKT cells killed BCMA+CD1d+ MM.1S-FG tumor cells more effectively than AlloHSC-iNKT cells, BCAR-T cells, or PBMC-T cells. The killing was enhanced by αGC stimulation, confirming TCR-mediated activity, and was partially inhibited by DNAM-1 blockade, confirming NK-mediated activity. In vivo, NSG mice bearing MM.1S-CD1d-FG tumors received intravenous infusions of AlloBCAR-iNKT cells, BCAR-T cells, or vehicle. Under low tumor burden, both AlloBCAR-iNKT and BCAR-T cells suppressed tumor growth, but only AlloBCAR-iNKT-treated mice survived long-term without GvHD. Under high tumor burden, AlloBCAR-iNKT cells achieved complete tumor clearance in two out of five mice, while BCAR-T cells showed only partial suppression and all mice died of GvHD. Histology confirmed absence of GvHD in AlloBCAR-iNKT recipients and severe multi-organ damage in BCAR-T recipients. These results demonstrate that CAR-engineered AlloHSC-iNKT cells combine the tumor-targeting power of CARs with the safety and multi-mechanistic activity of iNKT cells.

### Example 5: Generation of HLA-Ablated Universal HSC-iNKT Cells

CD34+ HSCs were transduced with the Lenti/iNKT-sr39TK vector and electroporated 24 hours later with a CRISPR-Cas9 ribonucleoprotein complex comprising guide RNAs targeting B2M and CIITA. Electroporation was performed using the Amaxa 4D-Nucleofector X Unit with P3 solution, yielding editing efficiencies of over 50% for both genes. Edited HSCs were then cultured in the two-stage ATO/αGC system as described. Flow cytometry at the end of culture confirmed that over 50% of cells were HLA-I/II double-negative, and magnetic sorting with anti-B2M and anti-HLA-DR beads enriched this population to over 97%. The resulting UHSC-iNKT cells expressed no detectable HLA-I or HLA-II molecules and retained high expression of the iNKT TCR and sr39TK. In mixed lymphocyte reactions, UHSC-iNKT cells induced no interferon-gamma production from mismatched PBMCs, whereas AlloBCAR-iNKT cells induced low but detectable responses. In vivo, UBCAR-iNKT cells achieved complete tumor eradication and long-term survival in a high tumor burden multiple myeloma model, while BCAR-T cells induced GvHD and tumor relapse. No NK cell-mediated rejection was observed, as UHSC-iNKT cells expressed low levels of NKG2D ligands such as ULBP. These results demonstrate that HLA ablation eliminates residual immunogenicity and creates a truly universal cell product.

### Example 6: Safety and Suicide Control of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

In vitro, AlloHSC-iNKT cells were exposed to ganciclovir (GCV) at concentrations ranging from 0 to 50 μM. After four days, cell viability dropped to less than 5% at 10 μM GCV, confirming efficient sr39TK-mediated suicide. In vivo, NSG mice were injected with 1 × 10^7 AlloHSC-iNKT cells and treated with daily intraperitoneal injections of GCV (50 mg/kg) for five consecutive days. On day five, mice were sacrificed and tissues (liver, spleen, lung) were analyzed by flow cytometry. The frequency of iNKT TCR+ cells in all tissues was reduced by over 99% in GCV-treated mice compared to vehicle controls. No toxicity or organ damage was observed in GCV-treated mice. These results confirm that the sr39TK suicide switch provides rapid, efficient, and safe control of the administered cells in the event of adverse events.

### Example 7: Immunogenicity Assessment of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

AlloHSC-iNKT cells were co-cultured with mismatched PBMCs in mixed lymphocyte reactions to assess host-versus-graft (HvG) response. AlloHSC-iNKT cells induced significantly less interferon-gamma production than PBMC-iNKT or PBMC-Tc cells. Flow cytometry confirmed that AlloHSC-iNKT cells expressed significantly lower levels of HLA-I and nearly undetectable levels of HLA-II compared to endogenous cells. Even after stimulation with interferon-gamma, HLA-I expression on AlloHSC-iNKT cells remained significantly lower than on control cells. In an in vivo melanoma xenograft model, tumor-infiltrating AlloHSC-iNKT cells maintained low HLA-I/II expression, whereas infiltrating PBMC-Tc and PBMC-iNKT cells upregulated HLA molecules. In NK cell-mediated allorejection assays, AlloHSC-iNKT cells survived significantly better than PBMC-Tc or PBMC-iNKT cells when co-cultured with mismatched PBMC-NK cells, and this was associated with low expression of ULBP ligands. These data confirm that AlloHSC-iNKT cells possess a stable, genomically programmed low-immunogenicity phenotype that confers resistance to both T cell- and NK cell-mediated rejection.
