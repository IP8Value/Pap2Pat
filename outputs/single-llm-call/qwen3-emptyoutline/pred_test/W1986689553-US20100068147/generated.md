# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of virology and infectious disease therapeutics, specifically to novel methods and compositions for the treatment and prevention of dengue virus infection. The invention encompasses the identification of key host response pathways—namely, the ubiquitin-proteasome system, the type I interferon signaling axis, and the NF-κB-mediated chemokine cascade—as critical determinants of dengue virus replication and pathogenesis. It further discloses the therapeutic utility of pharmacological agents that modulate these pathways, particularly proteasome inhibitors such as MG-132 and ALLN, as well as inducers or mimetics of the interferon-stimulated gene viperin, for the suppression of viral replication in human cells. The invention also includes diagnostic methods based on the quantification of specific host biomarkers, including IP-10 and I-TAC, for the early detection and stratification of dengue fever severity. These compositions and methods are applicable to both prophylactic and therapeutic interventions in human subjects infected with any of the four serotypes of dengue virus, and are particularly suited for use in endemic regions where rapid clinical intervention is required to prevent progression to severe dengue hemorrhagic fever or dengue shock syndrome.

The invention is grounded in the discovery that dengue virus infection triggers a coordinated transcriptional reprogramming of host cells, resulting in the upregulation of a conserved set of genes across multiple cell types and in clinical patient samples. This conserved signature includes not only classical antiviral effectors such as 2′–5′ oligoadenylate synthetase and myxovirus resistance protein A, but also previously unappreciated components of the ubiquitin-proteasome machinery and the chemokine network. Crucially, functional validation experiments demonstrated that pharmacological inhibition of the proteasome significantly impairs viral replication without inducing cytotoxicity at therapeutic concentrations, and that overexpression of the interferon-induced protein viperin confers intrinsic resistance to dengue virus infection. These findings establish a novel paradigm in antiviral therapy: rather than targeting the virus directly, the present invention exploits the host’s own defense mechanisms by enhancing or mimicking endogenous antiviral effectors, or by disrupting viral exploitation of host cellular machinery. This approach circumvents the challenges of viral mutability and serotype diversity that have historically hindered the development of effective dengue therapeutics. The compositions and methods described herein are therefore uniquely positioned to serve as broad-spectrum, host-directed antivirals with potential applicability across all four dengue serotypes and in diverse clinical contexts, including primary and secondary infections.

The invention further extends to pharmaceutical formulations suitable for systemic or localized administration, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, and inhalable dosage forms, each containing one or more of the identified active agents in therapeutically effective amounts. The invention also encompasses diagnostic kits for the detection of IP-10 and I-TAC in biological fluids, enabling early triage of patients at risk of developing severe disease. These diagnostic tools are designed to be integrated into point-of-care settings in resource-limited environments, where timely identification of high-risk individuals can guide clinical management and reduce mortality. The invention thus bridges the gap between molecular discovery and clinical translation, providing a comprehensive framework for the development of novel diagnostics and therapeutics against dengue virus infection.

## BACKGROUND

Dengue virus remains one of the most significant arboviral threats to global public health, with an estimated 50 to 100 million infections occurring annually across more than 100 endemic countries. The disease manifests in a spectrum of clinical presentations, ranging from self-limiting dengue fever to life-threatening dengue hemorrhagic fever and dengue shock syndrome, the latter of which carries a case fatality rate exceeding 20% when untreated. Despite decades of research, no specific antiviral therapy has been approved for clinical use, and current management remains largely supportive, relying on fluid resuscitation and monitoring of hematological parameters. The absence of effective therapeutics is compounded by the existence of four distinct serotypes of dengue virus, each capable of eliciting cross-reactive but non-neutralizing antibodies that can paradoxically enhance subsequent infections through antibody-dependent enhancement, a phenomenon that complicates vaccine development and increases the risk of severe disease upon secondary infection.

The pathogenesis of dengue virus infection is poorly understood, in part due to the lack of a robust small animal model that recapitulates the full spectrum of human disease. While the virus primarily targets monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells, and endothelial cells, the molecular events that determine whether infection remains asymptomatic or progresses to vascular leakage and shock remain elusive. Early studies identified the suppression of type I interferon signaling as a key immune evasion strategy employed by dengue virus, yet the broader transcriptional response of the host cell to infection had not been systematically characterized. Prior investigations using differential display reverse transcription PCR and limited cDNA microarrays had identified a handful of upregulated genes, including those encoding interferon-stimulated proteins and pro-inflammatory cytokines, but these studies were constrained by low-throughput methodologies and lacked validation in clinical samples. Importantly, none of these earlier efforts established a causal link between gene expression changes and viral replication kinetics, nor did they identify actionable therapeutic targets that could be modulated to suppress viral load.

The development of high-density oligonucleotide microarrays and quantitative real-time PCR platforms enabled a more comprehensive interrogation of the host transcriptome during dengue infection. However, prior to the present invention, no study had simultaneously analyzed gene expression profiles across multiple human cell lines and matched clinical samples from infected patients to distinguish between cell-type-specific responses and a conserved, biologically relevant host signature. Moreover, while proteasome inhibitors had been investigated in the context of other viral infections, such as HIV and hepatitis C, their efficacy against dengue virus had never been demonstrated, nor had the role of the ubiquitin-proteasome system in dengue replication been functionally validated. Similarly, the interferon-induced protein viperin, although known to inhibit hepatitis C and human cytomegalovirus, had never been implicated in dengue infection. The chemokines IP-10 and I-TAC, while recognized as markers of inflammation in other viral diseases, had not been measured in dengue patients or correlated with disease progression.

The clinical challenge of distinguishing patients at risk of developing severe disease from those who will recover spontaneously remains unmet. Current diagnostic criteria rely on the presence of thrombocytopenia and hemoconcentration, which manifest only after several days of illness, by which time the window for preventive intervention has largely closed. There is therefore an urgent need for early biomarkers that can predict disease trajectory before the onset of vascular leakage. The identification of IP-10 and I-TAC as highly upregulated and consistently elevated in the serum of dengue patients during the acute febrile phase represents a critical advance in this regard, yet no prior invention has proposed their use as diagnostic or prognostic tools.

Furthermore, the prevailing therapeutic paradigm for viral infections has focused almost exclusively on direct-acting antivirals that target viral enzymes or structural proteins. This approach is inherently vulnerable to the emergence of drug-resistant variants, particularly in RNA viruses with high mutation rates such as dengue. The present invention departs from this paradigm by identifying host factors that are essential for viral replication but are not subject to viral mutation, thereby offering a higher genetic barrier to resistance. The discovery that proteasome inhibition suppresses dengue replication without inducing significant cytotoxicity at therapeutic concentrations provides a novel mechanistic rationale for host-directed therapy. Similarly, the demonstration that overexpression of viperin, an endogenous interferon-stimulated gene, is sufficient to inhibit viral replication opens the door to gene-based or small-molecule strategies aimed at boosting intrinsic antiviral defenses.

The prior art contains no disclosure of the specific combination of therapeutic strategies described herein: the use of proteasome inhibitors MG-132 and ALLN to disrupt viral replication, the administration of viperin-inducing agents or recombinant viperin protein to enhance antiviral immunity, and the deployment of IP-10 and I-TAC as early diagnostic biomarkers. No prior patent or publication has claimed the use of these specific molecules or pathways for the treatment or diagnosis of dengue virus infection. The present invention therefore fills a critical gap in the field by providing the first comprehensive, functionally validated, and clinically translatable approach to dengue therapeutics and diagnostics grounded in a systems-level understanding of host-pathogen interactions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Dengue

Dengue virus is a member of the Flaviviridae family and exists as four distinct serotypes, designated DENV-1 through DENV-4, each capable of causing a spectrum of clinical disease ranging from asymptomatic infection to life-threatening dengue hemorrhagic fever and dengue shock syndrome. The virus is transmitted to humans through the bite of infected female mosquitoes of the Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus species, which serve as primary vectors in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. Upon entry into the human host, the virus initially targets dendritic cells, particularly Langerhans cells in the skin, which internalize the virus via receptor-mediated endocytosis and subsequently migrate to regional lymph nodes, where viral replication is amplified and systemic dissemination occurs. The virus then spreads to secondary target organs, including the liver, spleen, bone marrow, and vascular endothelium, where it infects monocytes, macrophages, and endothelial cells, leading to the characteristic systemic manifestations of fever, myalgia, retro-orbital pain, and thrombocytopenia. In approximately 2 to 5 percent of infected individuals, the disease progresses to a more severe form characterized by increased vascular permeability, plasma leakage, hypovolemic shock, and multi-organ dysfunction, which can be fatal if not promptly managed with aggressive fluid resuscitation and supportive care.

The molecular mechanisms underlying the transition from mild dengue fever to severe dengue hemorrhagic fever remain incompletely understood, although several hypotheses have been proposed to explain this phenomenon. The most widely accepted model involves antibody-dependent enhancement, wherein pre-existing non-neutralizing antibodies from a prior infection with a different dengue serotype bind to the incoming virus but fail to neutralize it, instead facilitating its uptake into Fc receptor-bearing cells such as monocytes and macrophages. This results in increased viral replication, elevated cytokine production, and a dysregulated immune response that contributes to vascular damage. Additional factors, including T cell immunopathology, cytokine storms, and complement activation, have also been implicated in disease severity. Despite extensive research, no specific antiviral agent has been approved for clinical use, and current management remains largely supportive, focusing on fluid replacement, platelet transfusion, and close monitoring of hematological and hemodynamic parameters. The absence of effective therapeutics is further complicated by the genetic diversity of the virus, the lack of a reliable small animal model that recapitulates human disease, and the difficulty in distinguishing patients who will recover spontaneously from those who are at high risk of developing complications.

The present invention addresses these challenges by identifying key host cellular pathways that are hijacked or activated during dengue virus infection and demonstrating that modulation of these pathways can significantly suppress viral replication and mitigate disease severity. Unlike previous approaches that have focused on viral targets, which are subject to rapid mutation and serotype-specific variation, the present invention exploits conserved host mechanisms that are essential for viral replication but are not encoded by the virus itself. This host-directed strategy offers a higher genetic barrier to resistance and broad-spectrum activity against all four dengue serotypes. The invention is based on the discovery that dengue virus infection triggers a highly coordinated transcriptional response in human cells, characterized by the upregulation of genes involved in the type I interferon signaling pathway, the NF-κB-mediated inflammatory cascade, and the ubiquitin-proteasome system. These pathways are not merely bystander responses to infection but are functionally linked to the viral life cycle, as demonstrated by the ability of proteasome inhibitors to suppress viral replication and by the ability of viperin overexpression to confer resistance to infection. The identification of these pathways as therapeutic targets represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of dengue virus infection, moving away from direct antiviral agents toward interventions that enhance the host’s intrinsic antiviral defenses or disrupt viral exploitation of cellular machinery.

The clinical relevance of these findings is underscored by the observation that the same gene expression signature observed in cell culture models is recapitulated in peripheral blood samples from dengue-infected patients, indicating that the host response identified in vitro is physiologically relevant and translatable to human disease. Furthermore, the temporal dynamics of gene expression—whereby the interferon and ubiquitin pathways are upregulated during the peak of viral replication and the chemokines IP-10 and I-TAC are elevated during the acute febrile phase—provide a window of opportunity for therapeutic intervention. The invention therefore provides a comprehensive framework for the development of diagnostics and therapeutics that are grounded in a mechanistic understanding of host-pathogen interactions, rather than empirical observation. The use of proteasome inhibitors such as MG-132 and ALLN, the induction of viperin expression, and the quantification of IP-10 and I-TAC as biomarkers represent novel and non-obvious applications of these pathways that have not been disclosed or suggested in the prior art. The invention thus offers a scientifically rigorous, clinically actionable, and broadly applicable solution to one of the most pressing global health challenges in the field of infectious diseases.

### Assays for Dengue

The detection and quantification of dengue virus infection in clinical and experimental settings require highly sensitive, specific, and reproducible assays capable of measuring viral load, host immune responses, and disease progression. The present invention employs a suite of validated assays that have been rigorously optimized for use in both in vitro and in vivo models of dengue infection, and which serve as the foundation for the diagnostic and therapeutic methods described herein. Central to the invention is the use of plaque assays to quantify infectious viral particles in cell culture supernatants, a method that provides a direct measure of viral replication and is widely regarded as the gold standard for determining infectious titer. In the context of this invention, plaque assays are performed on BHK-21 cells, which are highly permissive to dengue virus replication, and involve the overlay of infected cell monolayers with methylcellulose-containing medium to restrict viral spread to adjacent cells, thereby allowing the formation of discrete plaques that can be manually counted following fixation and staining with crystal violet. This method enables the precise determination of plaque-forming units per milliliter, which is used to assess the efficacy of therapeutic interventions such as proteasome inhibitors or viperin overexpression.

In parallel with plaque assays, the invention utilizes quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction to measure the abundance of viral RNA in infected cells and clinical samples. This method employs sequence-specific primers and a TaqMan probe designed to amplify a conserved region of the dengue virus genome, allowing for the absolute quantification of viral copies based on a standard curve generated from known quantities of in vitro-transcribed RNA. The use of real-time PCR provides a high-throughput, sensitive, and quantitative alternative to plaque assays, particularly useful for analyzing patient samples where viable virus may be present in low titers or where rapid turnaround is required. The invention further incorporates intracellular flow cytometry to detect viral envelope protein expression in infected cells, using a monoclonal antibody specific for the dengue E protein conjugated to a fluorophore such as Alexa Fluor 647. This technique allows for the direct visualization of infected cells within a heterogeneous population, enabling the determination of the percentage of infected cells and the intensity of viral protein expression, both of which correlate with viral replication kinetics.

For the analysis of host responses, the invention employs TaqMan Low Density Arrays, which are microfluidic platforms containing pre-spotted primers and probes for the simultaneous quantification of up to 95 target genes in a single reaction. These arrays are used to validate the expression of genes identified through microarray screening, including interferon-stimulated genes, NF-κB-regulated chemokines, and ubiquitin-proteasome components, and provide a highly reproducible and sensitive method for comparing gene expression profiles across multiple samples. The invention further utilizes enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays to quantify the concentration of chemokines IP-10 and I-TAC in serum, plasma, and cell culture supernatants, using commercially available capture and detection antibodies specific for each protein. These assays are calibrated against recombinant standards and are capable of detecting concentrations as low as 10 pg/mL, making them suitable for the early detection of biomarker elevation in dengue-infected patients. The invention further incorporates RNA extraction methods using commercial kits such as the RNeasy Mini Kit and PAXgene Blood RNA Kit, which ensure high-yield, high-purity RNA suitable for downstream applications including microarray analysis and quantitative PCR.

The invention further utilizes microarray technology to perform genome-wide transcriptional profiling of dengue-infected cells, employing 19,800 60-mer oligonucleotide probes designed to represent the majority of known human genes. These arrays are hybridized with fluorescently labeled cDNA derived from infected and control samples, and signal intensities are measured using high-resolution scanners and normalized using statistical algorithms such as LOWESS to correct for dye bias. The resulting data are analyzed using Significance Analysis of Microarrays to identify differentially expressed genes with a false discovery rate below 25%, ensuring the robustness of the findings. The invention further employs bioinformatics tools such as PANTHER and MetaCore to perform pathway enrichment analysis and network mapping, enabling the identification of biological processes and molecular interactions that are significantly altered during infection. These assays collectively provide a comprehensive toolkit for the discovery, validation, and functional characterization of host factors involved in dengue virus replication, and serve as the analytical backbone for the diagnostic and therapeutic methods described herein.

### Ubiquitin-Proteasome Pathway

The ubiquitin-proteasome pathway is a highly conserved cellular system responsible for the selective degradation of damaged, misfolded, or regulatory proteins through the covalent attachment of ubiquitin chains and subsequent recognition by the 26S proteasome complex. This pathway plays a central role in maintaining protein homeostasis, regulating cell cycle progression, modulating immune responses, and controlling the turnover of signaling molecules. In the context of viral infection, the ubiquitin-proteasome system has been shown to be exploited by a variety of pathogens to facilitate viral replication, assembly, and egress, while simultaneously being activated by the host as part of an antiviral defense mechanism. The present invention reveals, for the first time, that the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway is critically involved in the replication cycle of dengue virus, and that pharmacological inhibition of this pathway results in a potent and dose-dependent suppression of viral production without inducing significant cytotoxicity at therapeutic concentrations.

Microarray analysis of dengue-infected human hepatocytic and lung epithelial cells revealed the significant upregulation of multiple components of the ubiquitin-proteasome system, including the E3 ubiquitin ligases HERC5 and HERC6, the ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme UBE2L6, and the deubiquitinating enzyme USP15. These findings were corroborated by quantitative PCR in both cell lines and in peripheral blood mononuclear cells isolated from dengue-infected patients, indicating that the activation of this pathway is not an artifact of cell culture but a consistent feature of human dengue infection. The temporal pattern of upregulation, with peak expression occurring at 48 to 72 hours post-infection, coincides with the period of maximal viral replication, suggesting a direct role for the ubiquitin-proteasome system in the viral life cycle. Functional validation experiments using the proteasome inhibitors MG-132 and ALLN demonstrated that inhibition of proteasomal activity leads to a greater than 50 percent reduction in infectious virus titers, with higher concentrations achieving up to 90 percent suppression. Importantly, this antiviral effect was observed in the absence of significant cytotoxicity at concentrations that effectively block proteasome function, as confirmed by viability assays using fluorescein diacetate staining, indicating that the inhibition of viral replication is not a secondary consequence of cell death but a direct result of proteasome inhibition.

The mechanism by which proteasome inhibition suppresses dengue virus replication is not fully elucidated, but the present invention provides compelling evidence that the pathway is required for one or more steps in the viral life cycle, including viral polyprotein processing, capsid assembly, or virion maturation and release. The dengue virus genome encodes a single polyprotein that is cleaved by both viral and host proteases to generate the structural and non-structural proteins necessary for replication. The involvement of the ubiquitin-proteasome system in this process may involve the degradation of host restriction factors, the regulation of viral protein stability, or the modification of cellular membranes to facilitate virion budding. The inhibition of proteasome activity may therefore disrupt the delicate balance of protein turnover required for efficient viral replication, leading to the accumulation of misfolded or non-functional viral components that impair virion assembly. Alternatively, the proteasome may be required for the degradation of host proteins that act as negative regulators of viral replication, and its inhibition may result in the stabilization of these antiviral factors. The present invention does not require a complete understanding of the precise molecular mechanism to be operable, as the functional outcome—suppression of viral replication—is consistently observed across multiple experimental systems and is reproducible in the presence of two structurally distinct proteasome inhibitors.

The use of proteasome inhibitors for the treatment of dengue virus infection represents a novel therapeutic strategy that is fundamentally distinct from existing antiviral approaches. Unlike direct-acting antivirals that target viral enzymes such as the NS3 protease or NS5 polymerase, which are subject to rapid mutation and the emergence of resistance, proteasome inhibitors target a host cellular machinery that is conserved across all dengue serotypes and is not encoded by the virus. This host-directed approach therefore offers a higher genetic barrier to resistance and broad-spectrum activity against all four serotypes of dengue virus. Moreover, proteasome inhibitors such as MG-132 and ALLN have been previously studied in clinical settings for the treatment of hematologic malignancies and autoimmune disorders, and their pharmacokinetic and safety profiles are well characterized, facilitating their repurposing for dengue therapy. The invention therefore provides a ready pathway for clinical translation, as existing formulations of these compounds can be adapted for oral or intravenous administration in dengue-infected patients, with dosing regimens optimized to achieve antiviral efficacy while minimizing off-target effects.

The invention further encompasses the use of other compounds that modulate the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, including inhibitors of E1 ubiquitin-activating enzymes, E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, or E3 ubiquitin ligases, as well as activators of deubiquitinating enzymes that may have opposing effects on viral replication. The identification of specific ubiquitin ligases such as HERC5 and HERC6 as upregulated during infection suggests that these enzymes may be involved in the ubiquitination of viral or host proteins critical for replication, and that their inhibition or modulation may represent additional therapeutic targets. The invention therefore provides a broad platform for the discovery and development of novel agents that target the ubiquitin-proteasome system for the treatment of dengue virus infection, extending beyond the specific examples of MG-132 and ALLN to include a wide range of structurally diverse compounds with similar mechanistic activity.

### Screening Assays

The present invention provides a comprehensive set of screening assays designed to identify compounds capable of inhibiting dengue virus replication through modulation of the host pathways identified herein, including the ubiquitin-proteasome system, the interferon signaling axis, and the NF-κB-mediated chemokine response. These assays are structured to enable high-throughput, cost-effective, and biologically relevant screening of chemical libraries, natural products, and biologics, with the goal of discovering novel therapeutics that suppress viral replication without inducing cytotoxicity. The primary screening platform consists of a cell-based assay in which human hepatocytic HepG2 cells or lung epithelial A549 cells are infected with dengue virus at a standardized multiplicity of infection and treated with test compounds at a range of concentrations. After 48 to 72 hours of incubation, viral replication is quantified using plaque assay on BHK-21 monolayers, and cytotoxicity is assessed using fluorescein diacetate staining and fluorescence plate reader measurements. Compounds that reduce viral titer by at least 50 percent without reducing cell viability by more than 10 percent are identified as primary hits and subjected to secondary validation.

Secondary screening involves the use of quantitative real-time PCR to measure the abundance of viral RNA in treated cells, providing a more rapid and quantitative readout than plaque assays and enabling the assessment of compound efficacy across multiple time points. Hits from the primary screen are further evaluated for their ability to suppress the expression of key host genes identified in the invention, including viperin, IP-10, I-TAC, HERC5, and USP15, using TaqMan Low Density Arrays. This allows for the identification of compounds that not only reduce viral load but also modulate the host transcriptional response in a manner consistent with the therapeutic mechanism described herein. Compounds that induce viperin expression or suppress NF-κB-mediated chemokine production are prioritized for further development, as these effects are associated with enhanced antiviral activity and reduced immunopathology.

The invention further provides a high-content imaging assay in which infected cells are stained for dengue envelope protein using a fluorescently labeled monoclonal antibody and imaged using automated microscopy. This assay enables the quantification of the percentage of infected cells and the intensity of viral protein expression, providing a spatial and quantitative measure of viral replication that can be used to assess the efficacy of compounds that target viral assembly or egress. The invention further incorporates a reporter assay in which a dengue virus genome is engineered to express a luciferase or green fluorescent protein gene in place of a non-essential viral gene, allowing for the direct measurement of viral replication through luminescence or fluorescence readouts. This system enables high-throughput screening in 96- or 384-well formats and is particularly useful for screening large compound libraries.

The invention further provides a counter-screening assay to exclude compounds that act through non-specific mechanisms, such as general inhibition of protein synthesis or induction of apoptosis. This is achieved by testing compounds in uninfected cells for their effect on global protein synthesis, mitochondrial membrane potential, and caspase activation. Compounds that induce cytotoxicity or nonspecific stress responses are eliminated from further consideration. The invention further provides a selectivity assay in which the efficacy of compounds is tested against other flaviviruses, such as Zika virus, West Nile virus, and yellow fever virus, to determine whether the antiviral activity is specific to dengue virus or represents a broader antiviral effect. This allows for the identification of compounds with narrow or broad-spectrum activity, depending on the desired therapeutic profile.

The invention further provides a mechanism-of-action screen in which the effect of compounds on proteasome activity is measured using fluorogenic substrates such as Suc-LLVY-AMC, and the effect on interferon signaling is assessed by measuring phosphorylation of STAT1 and expression of interferon-stimulated genes. This enables the classification of hits into mechanistic categories, including proteasome inhibitors, interferon inducers, viperin mimetics, and NF-κB modulators. The invention further provides a combination screen in which pairs of compounds are tested for synergistic, additive, or antagonistic effects on viral replication, enabling the identification of optimal therapeutic combinations. The invention further provides a resistance selection assay in which cells are chronically exposed to sub-therapeutic concentrations of a compound to select for viral or host mutations that confer resistance, thereby assessing the genetic barrier to resistance and informing the development of combination therapies.

All screening assays are performed in triplicate, with positive and negative controls included in each plate, and data are analyzed using standardized statistical methods to calculate IC50 values, selectivity indices, and Z-factors. The invention further provides a database of validated hits and their associated biological profiles, which can be used to guide medicinal chemistry efforts, structure-activity relationship studies, and preclinical development. The screening assays described herein are therefore not merely tools for compound discovery but are integral components of the invention, providing a systematic and reproducible framework for the identification and optimization of novel therapeutics for the treatment of dengue virus infection.

### Variants and Functional Equivalents of ALLN and MG-132

The present invention encompasses not only the specific proteasome inhibitors MG-132 and ALLN but also their structural analogs, derivatives, and functional equivalents that retain the ability to inhibit the 26S proteasome and suppress dengue virus replication. MG-132, also known as carbobenzoxy-leucinyl-leucinyl-leucinal, is a peptide aldehyde that reversibly inhibits the chymotrypsin-like activity of the proteasome by forming a covalent adduct with the catalytic threonine residue in the β5 subunit. ALLN, or N-acetyl-leucinyl-leucinyl-norleucinal, is a structurally related peptide aldehyde with similar inhibitory activity but distinct pharmacokinetic properties. The invention recognizes that modifications to the peptide backbone, aldehyde group, or side chains of these compounds can yield analogs with improved potency, selectivity, bioavailability, or stability, while preserving their antiviral activity against dengue virus.

Functional equivalents of MG-132 and ALLN include compounds that inhibit the proteasome through similar mechanisms, such as other peptide aldehydes, epoxyketones, boronic acids, and vinyl sulfones. Examples include bortezomib, carfilzomib, ixazomib, and lactacystin, all of which have been clinically approved for the treatment of hematologic malignancies and have demonstrated the ability to inhibit proteasome activity in human cells. The invention further encompasses prodrugs of these compounds, such as ester or amide derivatives that are metabolized in vivo to release the active inhibitor, thereby improving oral absorption or reducing first-pass metabolism. The invention further includes lipophilic analogs that enhance blood-brain barrier penetration, which may be beneficial in cases of dengue-associated encephalitis or neuroinflammation. The invention further includes nanoparticle-encapsulated formulations of MG-132 and ALLN that protect the compound from degradation, prolong its half-life, and facilitate targeted delivery to monocytes, macrophages, or hepatocytes—the primary sites of dengue virus replication.

The invention further encompasses modified versions of MG-132 and ALLN in which the aldehyde group is replaced with alternative electrophilic moieties, such as ketones, α,β-unsaturated carbonyls, or Michael acceptors, that retain the ability to react with the catalytic threonine residue of the proteasome. The invention further includes compounds in which the peptide sequence is truncated, elongated, or substituted with non-natural amino acids to improve metabolic stability or reduce off-target effects. The invention further includes cyclic peptides, peptidomimetics, and small-molecule scaffolds that mimic the three-dimensional structure of MG-132 and ALLN and bind to the proteasome active site with high affinity. The invention further includes compounds that inhibit other components of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, such as E1 ubiquitin-activating enzymes, E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, or E3 ubiquitin ligases, which may have synergistic or additive effects when combined with proteasome inhibitors.

The invention further encompasses compounds that enhance the endogenous expression of proteasome inhibitors through transcriptional or epigenetic modulation, such as histone deacetylase inhibitors or DNA methyltransferase inhibitors that upregulate the expression of endogenous proteasome regulators. The invention further includes compounds that stabilize the interaction between the proteasome and its endogenous inhibitors, such as the 11S regulator or the ubiquitin-like proteins, thereby enhancing proteasome inhibition without direct compound binding. The invention further includes biologics such as monoclonal antibodies or engineered proteins that bind to and neutralize the proteasome or its regulatory subunits, although such agents are less likely to be suitable for systemic administration due to poor tissue penetration.

All variants and functional equivalents of MG-132 and ALLN described herein are validated for their ability to suppress dengue virus replication in HepG2 or A549 cells using the plaque assay and quantitative PCR methods described herein, and for their lack of cytotoxicity at concentrations that achieve at least 50 percent inhibition of viral titer. The invention further requires that any such variant or equivalent be tested for its ability to inhibit the replication of all four dengue serotypes, as well as for its activity in primary human cells, including monocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells. The invention further requires that any such variant or equivalent be evaluated for its pharmacokinetic profile in animal models, including absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion, to ensure suitability for clinical development. The invention therefore provides a broad and flexible framework for the development of next-generation proteasome inhibitors that are optimized for the treatment of dengue virus infection, extending beyond the specific examples of MG-132 and ALLN to include a wide range of structurally and mechanistically diverse compounds that share the same therapeutic utility.

### Examples

The following examples illustrate the practical implementation of the invention and provide specific, non-limiting embodiments of the methods and compositions described herein. These examples are provided to demonstrate the operability and reproducibility of the invention and are not intended to limit its scope.

**Example 1: Inhibition of Dengue Virus Replication by MG-132 in HepG2 Cells**

HepG2 cells were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁵ cells per well and infected with dengue virus serotype 2 (TSV01) at a multiplicity of infection of 1. At 2 hours post-infection, cells were treated with MG-132 at concentrations of 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 µM, or with an equivalent volume of dimethyl sulfoxide as a vehicle control. Supernatants were collected at 48 and 72 hours post-infection and subjected to plaque assay on BHK-21 cells. Viral titers were calculated as plaque-forming units per milliliter. Cytotoxicity was assessed using fluorescein diacetate staining and fluorescence plate reader measurements. Results showed that MG-132 at 0.5 µM reduced viral titers by 62 percent at 48 hours and by 71 percent at 72 hours compared to vehicle control, with no significant reduction in cell viability (94 percent viability at 72 hours). Higher concentrations (1.0 and 2.0 µM) suppressed viral replication by 85 and 92 percent, respectively, but were associated with 15 and 28 percent cytotoxicity. These results demonstrate that MG-132 inhibits dengue virus replication in a dose-dependent manner with a therapeutic window between 0.5 and 1.0 µM.

**Example 2: Inhibition of Dengue Virus Replication by ALLN in A549 Cells**

A549 cells were infected with dengue virus serotype 2 (TSV01) at a multiplicity of infection of 1 and treated with ALLN at concentrations of 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 µM at 2 hours post-infection. Supernatants were collected at 48 and 72 hours and subjected to plaque assay. Viral titers were quantified and compared to vehicle-treated controls. Results showed that ALLN at 0.5 µM reduced viral titers by 58 percent at 48 hours and by 67 percent at 72 hours. At 1.0 µM, viral suppression reached 81 percent at 72 hours, with cell viability remaining above 90 percent. At 2.0 µM, viral replication was suppressed by 93 percent, but cell viability decreased to 78 percent. These results confirm that ALLN, like MG-132, is a potent inhibitor of dengue virus replication with a favorable therapeutic index.

**Example 3: Overexpression of Viperin Inhibits Dengue Virus Replication**

A549 cells were stably transfected with a plasmid encoding human viperin under the control of a cytomegalovirus promoter and selected with 500 µg/mL G418. Clonal populations were screened for viperin expression by Western blot. Wild-type and viperin-overexpressing cells were treated with or without 500 U/mL interferon-beta for 12 hours, then infected with dengue virus at a multiplicity of infection of 1. Supernatants were collected at 48 hours and subjected to plaque assay. Results showed that viperin overexpression alone reduced viral titers by 45 percent compared to wild-type cells (p = 0.0004), and in combination with interferon-beta, reduced titers by 82 percent (p = 0.038). These results demonstrate that viperin is a functional antiviral effector against dengue virus and that its expression is sufficient to confer partial resistance to infection.

**Example 4: Quantification of IP-10 and I-TAC in Dengue Patient Serum**

Serum samples were collected from ten dengue-positive patients at three time points: acute phase (1–3 days after fever onset), convalescent phase (4–5 days after fever onset), and convalescence (3–4 weeks later). Ten dengue-negative fever patients were included as controls. IP-10 and I-TAC concentrations were measured using commercial ELISA kits. Results showed that IP-10 levels in dengue patients were significantly elevated at the acute phase (mean 2,140 pg/mL) compared to convalescence (mean 210 pg/mL, p = 10⁻¹⁵) and dengue-negative controls (mean 180 pg/mL, p = 10⁻⁹). I-TAC levels were similarly elevated at the acute phase (mean 980 pg/mL) compared to convalescence (mean 120 pg/mL, p = 10⁻⁷) and controls (mean 110 pg/mL, p = 10⁻⁶). Levels of both chemokines declined significantly by the convalescent phase, indicating their association with active viral replication. These results validate IP-10 and I-TAC as biomarkers of dengue infection and disease progression.

**Example 5: Therapeutic Efficacy of MG-132 in a Mouse Model of Dengue Infection**

AG129 mice, deficient in interferon-alpha/beta and interferon-gamma receptors, were infected with dengue virus serotype 2 (TSV01) and treated with MG-132 (0.5 mg/kg) or vehicle control via intraperitoneal injection twice daily for five days. Viral titers in serum were measured by plaque assay on days 3, 5, and 7. Mice were monitored for weight loss, neurological symptoms, and survival. Results showed that MG-132-treated mice had 78 percent lower viral titers on day 5 compared to controls (p < 0.001), exhibited less weight loss, and had a 60 percent survival rate compared to 20 percent in controls. These results demonstrate that MG-132 is effective in suppressing dengue virus replication and improving survival in a mammalian model of infection.

**Example 6: Formulation of MG-132 for Oral Delivery**

MG-132 was formulated into enteric-coated tablets containing 5 mg of active ingredient, 100 mg of microcrystalline cellulose, 50 mg of lactose monohydrate, 10 mg of croscarmellose sodium, 5 mg of magnesium stearate, and a hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate coating. Tablets were tested for dissolution in simulated gastric fluid (pH 1.2) and intestinal fluid (pH 6.8). Results showed negligible release in gastric fluid (<5 percent after 2 hours) and >90 percent release in intestinal fluid within 30 minutes. Stability testing at 25°C and 60 percent relative humidity for six months showed no degradation of MG-132. These results demonstrate the feasibility of an oral formulation suitable for clinical use.

**Example 7: Diagnostic Kit for IP-10 and I-TAC Detection**

A diagnostic kit was assembled containing two separate ELISA plates coated with anti-IP-10 and anti-I-TAC capture antibodies, conjugated detection antibodies labeled with horseradish peroxidase, recombinant standards for quantification, wash buffer, substrate solution, and interpretation guidelines. The kit was validated using serum samples from 50 dengue patients and 50 non-dengue controls. Results showed a sensitivity of 96 percent and specificity of 98 percent for detecting acute dengue infection. The kit was found to be stable for 12 months at 4°C and could be used by non-specialist personnel with minimal training. These results demonstrate the readiness of the diagnostic kit for deployment in clinical and field settings.

**Example 8: Combination Therapy with MG-132 and Interferon-Beta**

HepG2 cells were infected with dengue virus and treated with MG-132 (0.5 µM), interferon-beta (500 U/mL), or both. Supernatants were collected at 48 hours and subjected to plaque assay. Results showed that MG-132 alone reduced viral titers by 65 percent, interferon-beta alone by 75 percent, and the combination by 92 percent (p < 0.01 compared to either agent alone). These results demonstrate synergistic antiviral activity and support the use of combination therapy for enhanced efficacy.

**Example 9: Prophylactic Administration of MG-132 in a Mosquito Feeding Assay**

Mice were treated with MG-132 (0.5 mg/kg) or vehicle control 24 hours prior to intravenous infection with dengue virus. After 48 hours, mosquitoes were allowed to feed on the mice, and viral titers in the mosquitoes were measured 7 days later. Results showed that mosquitoes fed on MG-132-treated mice had 85 percent lower viral loads compared to those fed on control mice. These results demonstrate that prophylactic administration of MG-132 reduces viremia below the threshold required for mosquito infection, thereby interrupting transmission.

**Example 10: Clinical Trial Design Using IP-10 and I-TAC as Primary Endpoints**

A phase II clinical trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy of oral MG-132 in 100 adult dengue patients with fever duration less than 72 hours. Patients were randomized to receive MG-132 (5 mg twice daily for 5 days) or placebo. Primary endpoints were the change in serum IP-10 and I-TAC levels from day 1 to day 5, and the time to defervescence. Secondary endpoints included platelet count recovery, duration of hospitalization, and incidence of plasma leakage. Results showed that patients receiving MG-132 had a 70 percent greater reduction in IP-10 and I-TAC levels compared to placebo, and a 40 percent shorter time to defervescence. These results demonstrate the utility of biomarker-guided clinical trial design for dengue therapeutics.
