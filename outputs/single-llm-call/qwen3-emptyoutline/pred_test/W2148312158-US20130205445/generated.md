# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of plant molecular biology and agricultural biotechnology, specifically to methods and compositions for enhancing the storage and translocation of monosaccharides in higher plants through the genetic modulation of tonoplast-localized sugar transporters. More particularly, the invention provides genetically modified plant lines exhibiting increased expression of the TMT1 gene, which encodes a proton-coupled monosaccharide transporter localized to the vacuolar membrane. These modified plants demonstrate enhanced vacuolar sequestration of glucose and fructose, altered subcellular sugar partitioning, reduced cytosolic sugar concentrations, diminished respiratory consumption of imported sugars, increased phloem loading capacity, elevated seed biomass, and improved overall plant growth under conditions of elevated sugar availability. The invention further encompasses the use of such genetically modified plants to improve crop yield, seed quality, and metabolic efficiency in agriculturally important species. The technical solution disclosed herein addresses a long-standing limitation in plant metabolism—namely, that the capacity of the vacuolar membrane to import and store monosaccharides acts as a physiological bottleneck that restricts carbon allocation, sugar signaling, and sink development. By overcoming this constraint through targeted overexpression of TMT1, the invention enables a fundamental reprogramming of carbohydrate dynamics within plant cells, resulting in measurable and commercially significant improvements in biomass accumulation, seed production, and stress resilience.

The invention is particularly applicable to dicotyledonous crop species such as Arabidopsis thaliana, potato, tomato, soybean, canola, and other oilseed and starch-producing plants, where vacuolar storage of sugars plays a critical role in source-sink relationships, carbon partitioning, and developmental regulation. The methods described herein are also adaptable to monocotyledonous species including cereals such as wheat, rice, maize, and barley, where enhanced sugar transport into storage organs could significantly improve grain filling and nutritional content. The invention further provides a novel mechanism for decoupling sugar signaling from cytosolic sugar concentration, thereby mitigating the growth-inhibitory effects of high external sugar environments—a phenomenon commonly observed in tissue culture and under conditions of elevated atmospheric CO₂ or sucrose supplementation. This capability has broad implications for plant propagation, in vitro propagation systems, and the engineering of crops for high-yield production under controlled environments. The technical advancement lies not merely in the overexpression of a single transporter gene, but in the cascade of systemic physiological and metabolic consequences that follow from the reconfiguration of subcellular sugar compartmentation, including altered gene expression profiles, reduced nocturnal respiration, enhanced phloem loading, and increased seed protein and lipid content—all of which are directly attributable to the increased activity of the TMT1 transporter at the tonoplast.

## BACKGROUND ART

Prior to the present invention, the understanding of monosaccharide transport across the tonoplast in higher plants was limited to indirect physiological observations and the identification of candidate transporter genes based on sequence homology. Several families of sugar transporters had been characterized in plants, including the SUT family of sucrose transporters responsible for phloem loading and unloading, and the STP family of hexose transporters localized to the plasma membrane. However, the molecular identity of the primary transporter responsible for the uptake of glucose and fructose into the vacuole remained elusive for many years. Early electrophysiological studies on isolated vacuoles from various plant species, including sugar beet and potato, had demonstrated the existence of an electrogenic, proton-coupled sugar uptake system capable of accumulating monosaccharides against steep concentration gradients. These studies indicated that the transport process was energized by the proton motive force generated by the vacuolar H⁺-ATPase and H⁺-pyrophosphatase, and that the transport mechanism likely operated as a sugar/proton antiporter. Despite these insights, the specific gene encoding this transporter activity in Arabidopsis and other model plants had not been definitively identified until the discovery of the TMT (Tonoplast Monosaccharide Transporter) gene family.

The TMT family, comprising three members in Arabidopsis—TMT1, TMT2, and TMT3—was initially identified through transcriptomic analyses and functional characterization in knockout mutants. Previous work had established that TMT1 and TMT2 are the predominant isoforms expressed in leaf mesophyll cells, with TMT3 showing minimal expression under standard growth conditions. Mutant lines lacking both TMT1 and TMT2 exhibited severely impaired accumulation of glucose and fructose in the vacuole under cold stress conditions, indicating that these isoforms are essential for vacuolar sugar storage during environmental perturbations. However, the functional significance of these transporters under normal growth conditions, and whether their activity could be manipulated to enhance plant performance, had not been explored. Moreover, while it was known that excessive cytosolic sugar concentrations could trigger growth inhibition through sugar-sensing pathways involving hexokinase and downstream transcriptional regulators, no genetic strategy had been devised to alleviate this inhibition by redirecting sugar flux away from the cytosol and into the vacuole.

Attempts to manipulate sugar metabolism in plants had previously focused on altering the expression of enzymes involved in sugar synthesis or degradation, such as invertases, sucrose synthases, and hexokinases. Overexpression of vacuolar invertase, for example, had been shown to increase cytosolic hexose levels, which in turn led to enhanced sink strength and increased biomass in some cases. However, these approaches invariably resulted in elevated cytosolic sugar concentrations, which triggered feedback repression of photosynthetic genes, suppressed growth, and induced premature senescence. Other strategies involved the expression of fructan synthases to convert sucrose into fructan polymers within the vacuole, thereby increasing storage capacity without altering cytosolic sugar levels. While successful in certain species such as chicory and onion, this approach was not universally applicable, as many economically important crops lack the enzymatic machinery to synthesize fructans. Furthermore, fructan accumulation does not address the fundamental issue of sugar signaling, which is primarily mediated by cytosolic glucose and its perception by hexokinase.

The prevailing paradigm in plant physiology held that vacuolar sugar storage was a passive consequence of cytosolic sugar abundance and that the tonoplast was merely a barrier rather than a regulated gateway for sugar flux. Consequently, efforts to improve plant productivity through metabolic engineering had largely ignored the tonoplast as a potential target for intervention. The absence of a clear molecular target for enhancing vacuolar sugar import meant that attempts to manipulate sugar partitioning were largely empirical and often yielded unpredictable results. The discovery that TMT1 functions as the primary electrogenic glucose/proton antiporter at the tonoplast represented a paradigm shift, but prior to the present invention, there was no demonstration that overexpression of TMT1 could be used to reprogram plant metabolism in a beneficial and predictable manner. No prior art had shown that increasing the expression of TMT1 would lead to a reduction in cytosolic sugar concentration, suppression of respiratory carbon loss, enhanced phloem loading, or increased seed biomass. Nor had any study demonstrated that such changes would occur without compromising plant growth or triggering unintended metabolic imbalances.

Furthermore, the molecular mechanisms linking vacuolar sugar sequestration to gene expression regulation had not been elucidated. While it was known that sugars such as glucose act as signaling molecules that repress photosynthesis-related genes like CAB1 and induce nitrogen metabolism genes like NR1, the spatial context of sugar perception—whether in the cytosol, nucleus, or vacuole—remained ambiguous. The prevailing assumption was that cytosolic sugar concentration directly determined gene expression patterns. The present invention challenges this assumption by demonstrating that it is not the total cellular sugar content, but the subcellular distribution of sugars, particularly the exclusion of glucose from the cytosol, that governs transcriptional responses. This insight represents a fundamental advance in plant signaling biology and provides a new conceptual framework for metabolic engineering that prioritizes compartmentalization over bulk concentration.

The prior art also lacked any evidence that enhanced vacuolar sugar import could lead to increased seed yield or improved seed composition. While numerous studies had attempted to increase seed oil or protein content through overexpression of storage protein genes or lipid biosynthetic enzymes, none had approached the problem from the perspective of source strength and carbon allocation. The observation that TMT1 overexpression leads to increased seed weight, higher protein content, and elevated lipid accumulation—without altering seed number—was entirely unforeseen and represents a novel pathway for improving crop nutritional quality. Similarly, the discovery that TMT1 overexpression enhances phloem loading capacity through upregulation of the SUC2 sucrose transporter was unprecedented. Prior to this invention, SUC2 expression was understood to be regulated by developmental cues and phloem-specific transcription factors, but not by cytosolic sugar levels or vacuolar transport activity. The linkage between tonoplast function and phloem loading represents a previously unrecognized axis of metabolic coordination that the present invention exploits for the first time.

In summary, the prior art provided no teaching, suggestion, or motivation to overexpress TMT1 as a means of enhancing plant growth, improving seed yield, or reprogramming sugar signaling. The functional characterization of TMT1 as a tonoplast-localized electrogenic glucose/proton antiporter was insufficient to predict the broad physiological consequences of its overexpression. The present invention is the first to demonstrate that targeted enhancement of vacuolar sugar import through TMT1 overexpression can simultaneously improve multiple agronomic traits—including growth rate, carbon use efficiency, phloem transport, and seed composition—thereby transforming the vacuole from a passive storage compartment into an active regulatory hub for plant metabolism.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

The present invention discloses a novel method for enhancing plant growth, increasing seed biomass, and improving metabolic efficiency in higher plants through the genetic overexpression of the TMT1 gene, which encodes a tonoplast-localized proton-coupled monosaccharide transporter. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that constitutive overexpression of TMT1 in plant cells leads to a profound reorganization of intracellular sugar partitioning, resulting in the sequestration of glucose and fructose into the vacuolar lumen while simultaneously reducing their concentration in the cytosol. This redistribution of sugars triggers a cascade of downstream physiological and transcriptional responses that collectively enhance carbon use efficiency, reduce respiratory losses, increase phloem loading capacity, and promote the accumulation of storage compounds in seeds. The invention provides genetically modified plant lines in which the TMT1 gene is expressed under the control of a constitutive promoter, resulting in sustained and elevated levels of TMT1 protein at the tonoplast membrane. These plants exhibit significantly increased rates of monosaccharide uptake into the vacuole, as demonstrated by electrophysiological measurements showing enhanced electrogenic currents in response to glucose application. The transport activity is dependent on the proton gradient across the tonoplast, with maximal activity observed under physiological pH conditions where the vacuolar lumen is acidic relative to the cytosol.

The invention further demonstrates that the overexpression of TMT1 leads to a marked improvement in plant performance under conditions of high external sugar availability, where wild-type plants exhibit severe growth inhibition. In contrast, TMT1-overexpressing plants maintain robust growth, increased biomass, and normal developmental progression, despite the presence of elevated glucose concentrations in the growth medium. This resistance to sugar-mediated growth repression is directly attributable to the ability of TMT1 to prevent the accumulation of cytosolic glucose, thereby decoupling sugar signaling from bulk sugar concentration. The invention shows that the expression of sugar-responsive genes such as CAB1 and NR1 is regulated not by total cellular sugar content, but by the spatial distribution of sugars within the cell. In TMT1-overexpressing plants, the repression of CAB1 and induction of NR1 normally triggered by high glucose are attenuated, indicating that the cytosolic glucose concentration is the primary determinant of sugar signaling. This finding represents a fundamental revision of the prevailing model of sugar sensing in plants and provides a molecular basis for engineering crops that are resilient to high-sugar environments, such as those encountered in tissue culture, elevated CO₂ conditions, or during periods of high photosynthetic activity.

The invention further discloses that TMT1 overexpression leads to a significant reduction in the respiratory consumption of newly imported glucose, as evidenced by decreased release of radiolabeled CO₂ from [¹⁴C]glucose in both liquid-cultured and soil-grown plants. This reduction in respiratory loss is accompanied by decreased nocturnal CO₂ efflux from aerial tissues, indicating a more efficient conservation of fixed carbon for growth and storage. The invention further demonstrates that TMT1-overexpressing plants exhibit enhanced sugar export capacity from source leaves, as measured by phloem exudate analysis, which correlates with increased expression of the phloem-specific sucrose transporter SUC2. This upregulation of SUC2 is not a direct effect of TMT1 overexpression, but rather an indirect consequence of lowered cytosolic glucose levels in mesophyll cells, which relieves the transcriptional repression of SUC2 normally exerted by high sugar concentrations. The result is a more efficient transfer of photosynthates from source to sink tissues, which directly contributes to increased seed biomass.

The invention further reveals that TMT1 overexpression leads to a substantial increase in seed weight, seed protein content, and seed lipid content, without altering the number of seeds produced per plant. This increase in seed quality is not due to enhanced sink strength alone, but rather to improved source capacity and more efficient carbon allocation throughout the plant. The seeds of TMT1-overexpressing plants contain significantly higher levels of both lipids and storage proteins, suggesting that the enhanced sugar export from leaves not only provides more carbon for energy metabolism but also supplies precursors for amino acid and fatty acid biosynthesis. This dual enhancement of seed composition represents a unique and valuable trait for agricultural applications, as it simultaneously improves both caloric density and nutritional value.

The invention further provides a method for generating transgenic plants with enhanced TMT1 expression by introducing a recombinant nucleic acid construct comprising a coding sequence for TMT1 operably linked to a constitutive promoter, such as the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter, into the genome of a plant cell. The construct may be delivered via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation, biolistic methods, or other standard transformation techniques. The resulting transgenic plants exhibit stable inheritance of the TMT1 transgene and consistent phenotypic traits across multiple generations. The invention further encompasses the use of tissue-specific or inducible promoters to drive TMT1 expression in particular organs, such as leaves, stems, or developing seeds, allowing for spatial and temporal control of sugar partitioning. The invention further includes the use of TMT1 overexpression in combination with other genetic modifications, such as the suppression of invertase activity or the overexpression of sucrose synthase, to further optimize carbon flux and storage.

The invention further provides a method for improving crop yield and seed quality in agriculturally important species by introducing the TMT1 overexpression construct into the genome of a crop plant, such as potato, tomato, soybean, canola, wheat, rice, or maize. The resulting transgenic plants exhibit increased biomass, enhanced resistance to sugar-mediated growth inhibition, improved carbon use efficiency, and elevated seed protein and lipid content. The invention further provides a method for selecting transgenic plants with superior phenotypic traits based on the measurement of vacuolar sugar accumulation, cytosolic sugar concentration, SUC2 expression levels, or seed weight. The invention further encompasses the use of TMT1 overexpression in controlled environment agriculture, including hydroponic systems, vertical farms, and tissue culture propagation, where high sugar concentrations are commonly used to support growth. By mitigating the growth-inhibitory effects of sugar, TMT1 overexpression enables higher plant densities, faster growth cycles, and improved survival rates in vitro.

The invention further provides a method for identifying plants with enhanced TMT1 activity by measuring the electrogenic transport activity of isolated vacuoles using patch-clamp electrophysiology. The presence of increased glucose-induced currents in the presence of a proton gradient is indicative of enhanced TMT1 function. The invention further provides a method for detecting TMT1 overexpression by quantifying TMT1 mRNA levels using quantitative reverse transcription PCR or by measuring TMT1 protein levels using immunoblotting with antibodies specific to the TMT1 polypeptide. The invention further encompasses the use of reporter genes linked to the TMT1 promoter to monitor vacuolar sugar transport activity in real time. The invention further provides a method for screening chemical compounds or environmental conditions that modulate TMT1 activity by assessing their effect on glucose-induced currents or on the expression of downstream genes such as SUC2, CAB1, or NR1.

The invention further provides a method for producing seeds with enhanced nutritional value by growing TMT1-overexpressing plants under standard agricultural conditions and harvesting the resulting seeds, which contain elevated levels of protein and lipids. The invention further provides a method for producing plant biomass with increased energy content by harvesting the vegetative tissues of TMT1-overexpressing plants, which exhibit higher total carbon content and reduced respiratory losses. The invention further encompasses the use of TMT1-overexpressing plants as a source of high-quality feedstock for biofuel production, as the increased carbon retention and reduced respiration lead to higher net carbon accumulation in biomass. The invention further provides a method for improving the efficiency of plant propagation by using TMT1-overexpressing explants in tissue culture systems, where the resistance to sugar inhibition allows for higher sucrose concentrations in the medium without compromising growth or regeneration.

The invention further provides a method for engineering plants with improved stress tolerance by exploiting the link between vacuolar sugar storage and cold acclimation. Since TMT1 is naturally induced by low temperature, its overexpression enhances the plant’s ability to sequester sugars during cold stress, thereby stabilizing cellular metabolism and protecting against freezing damage. The invention further encompasses the use of TMT1 overexpression in combination with other abiotic stress tolerance traits, such as osmoprotectant accumulation or antioxidant enzyme expression, to generate multi-stress-resilient crops. The invention further provides a method for increasing the shelf life of harvested plant products by reducing post-harvest respiration and sugar degradation through the maintenance of high vacuolar sugar stores and low cytosolic sugar concentrations.

In summary, the invention provides a comprehensive and novel approach to plant metabolic engineering by targeting the tonoplast as a central regulator of carbon allocation and signaling. The overexpression of TMT1 fundamentally alters the dynamics of sugar partitioning within plant cells, leading to a suite of interconnected physiological improvements that collectively enhance plant productivity, nutritional quality, and environmental resilience. The invention represents a paradigm shift in the field of plant biotechnology, demonstrating that the regulation of subcellular compartmentation can be as powerful a tool for improving crop performance as the manipulation of biosynthetic pathways themselves.

## BEST MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The best mode for carrying out the invention involves the generation of transgenic plant lines in which the TMT1 gene is constitutively overexpressed under the control of the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter, resulting in sustained and elevated levels of TMT1 protein at the tonoplast membrane. The TMT1 coding sequence, derived from Arabidopsis thaliana (GenBank accession Z50752, At1g20840), is cloned into a binary vector under the transcriptional control of the 35S promoter, ensuring high-level expression in all plant tissues, particularly in mesophyll cells where vacuolar sugar storage is most critical. The construct further includes a terminator sequence derived from the nopaline synthase gene to ensure proper polyadenylation and mRNA stability. The plasmid is introduced into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101, which is then used to transform Arabidopsis thaliana plants via the floral dip method. The transformation is performed on the homozygous tmt1-2::tDNA mutant background, which lacks functional TMT1 and TMT2 isoforms, thereby eliminating potential gene silencing or redundancy effects that might occur in a wild-type background. This genetic context ensures that any observed phenotypic changes are directly attributable to the overexpression of the introduced TMT1 transgene.

Following transformation, T1 plants are selected on media containing kanamycin or another appropriate selective agent, and T2 progeny are screened for homozygosity and transgene integration using PCR and Southern blot analysis. Independent transgenic lines exhibiting high levels of TMT1 mRNA are identified by quantitative reverse transcription PCR, with lines 1, 4, and 10 selected for further characterization due to their consistently elevated transcript levels. These lines are propagated under standard growth conditions in a controlled environment chamber maintained at 21°C with a 10-hour light/14-hour dark photoperiod and a light intensity of 150 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. To confirm the functional activity of the overexpressed TMT1 protein, isolated vacuoles from mesophyll cells of these lines are subjected to whole-vacuole patch-clamp electrophysiology. The vacuolar membrane is clamped at 0 mV, and the bath solution is maintained at pH 7.5 to mimic cytosolic conditions, while the pipette solution is adjusted to pH 5.5 to simulate the acidic vacuolar lumen. Upon application of 50 mM glucose to the bath solution, a significant increase in inward current is observed in vacuoles from TMT1-overexpressing lines, particularly line 4, which exhibits a two-fold greater response compared to wild-type vacuoles. This current is abolished when the pH gradient is eliminated by equilibrating both sides of the membrane to pH 7.5, confirming that the transport is electrogenic and strictly dependent on the proton motive force.

To assess the physiological consequences of TMT1 overexpression, plants are grown in liquid culture medium containing 5% glucose, a condition known to severely inhibit the growth of wild-type Arabidopsis. Under these conditions, wild-type plants accumulate less than 25 mg of fresh weight after 12 days, while the tmt1-2::tDNA double mutant exhibits even greater growth suppression, reaching only 9 mg of fresh weight. In stark contrast, all three TMT1-overexpressing lines exhibit significantly enhanced growth, with line 4 achieving a fresh weight of 78 mg—nearly four times that of the wild type. This dramatic improvement in biomass is not accompanied by an increase in total cellular sugar content, as quantified by HPLC analysis of total glucose, fructose, and sucrose, which remain nearly identical across all genotypes. However, nonaqueous fractionation of leaf tissue reveals a profound shift in subcellular sugar partitioning: in wild-type plants, approximately 40% of glucose is stored in the vacuole, 43% in plastids, and 17% in the cytosol, whereas in TMT1-overexpressing line 1, over 80% of glucose is sequestered in the vacuole, with less than 1% remaining in the cytosol. Similar patterns are observed for fructose, with over 90% of the total pool localized to the vacuole in the overexpressing lines. This redistribution of sugars from the cytosol to the vacuole directly correlates with the attenuation of sugar-mediated gene repression, as evidenced by higher levels of CAB1 mRNA and lower levels of NR1 mRNA in TMT1-overexpressing plants compared to wild-type plants under high-glucose conditions.

To determine whether these metabolic changes translate into improved carbon use efficiency, seedlings are incubated in 100 mM [¹⁴C]glucose, and the release of radiolabeled CO₂ is measured over a six-hour period. Wild-type plants respire 18% of the imported glucose, while the tmt1-2::tDNA mutant exhibits an even higher rate of 26%, indicating excessive carbon loss due to cytosolic sugar accumulation. In contrast, TMT1-overexpressing lines respire only 13–14% of the imported glucose, demonstrating a significant reduction in respiratory consumption. This effect is further confirmed in soil-grown plants, where nocturnal CO₂ release from aerial tissues is significantly lower in TMT1-overexpressing lines 4 and 10 compared to wild-type controls, indicating a more efficient conservation of fixed carbon during the night. These reductions in respiratory loss are accompanied by increased seed biomass, with TMT1-overexpressing line 4 producing 178 mg of seed per plant—42% more than the wild-type average of 125 mg. The increase in seed yield is not due to an increase in seed number, as all lines produce approximately 42 seeds per silique, but rather to an increase in individual seed weight. Analysis of seed composition reveals that TMT1-overexpressing lines contain significantly higher levels of both lipids and storage proteins, with line 4 exhibiting a 12% increase in lipid content and a 14% increase in protein content compared to wild-type seeds.

To investigate the mechanism underlying enhanced carbon export from source leaves, phloem exudate analysis is performed on detached leaves from 6-week-old plants. Wild-type leaves export approximately 12.5 mmol C₆ units per gram of fresh weight over a 16-hour dark period, while TMT1-overexpressing lines export between 16.6 and 17.9 mmol C₆ units per gram, representing a 30–40% increase in phloem loading capacity. This enhanced export correlates with a 150% increase in SUC2 mRNA levels in the leaves of line 4, confirming that the reduction in cytosolic glucose relieves transcriptional repression of the sucrose transporter. The increased sugar export is not due to higher sucrose synthesis, as total sucrose levels in leaves remain unchanged, but rather to more efficient loading of sucrose into the phloem, which enhances sink strength and promotes the translocation of carbon to developing seeds. The invention further demonstrates that these phenotypic improvements are heritable and stable across multiple generations, with T2 and T3 progeny maintaining the same patterns of vacuolar sugar sequestration, reduced respiration, and increased seed biomass.

The invention further encompasses the use of alternative promoters to drive TMT1 expression in a tissue-specific or inducible manner. For example, the use of a phloem-specific promoter such as SUC2 or a seed-specific promoter such as napin or cruciferin allows for spatial control of sugar partitioning, enabling enhanced phloem loading without altering leaf metabolism, or increased seed storage without affecting vegetative growth. The invention further provides for the use of inducible promoters responsive to environmental cues such as light, temperature, or sugar concentration, allowing for dynamic regulation of TMT1 activity in response to changing physiological conditions. The invention further includes the use of CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing to enhance endogenous TMT1 expression by modifying its promoter region, thereby avoiding the need for transgene integration and facilitating regulatory approval in commercial applications.

The invention further provides a method for selecting superior transgenic lines based on measurable phenotypic traits, including vacuolar glucose concentration, cytosolic glucose concentration, SUC2 mRNA levels, nocturnal CO₂ release, and seed weight. High-performing lines are identified by a combination of these parameters, with the most desirable lines exhibiting low cytosolic glucose, high vacuolar glucose, elevated SUC2 expression, reduced respiration, and increased seed biomass. The invention further provides a method for screening chemical compounds or environmental treatments that modulate TMT1 activity by measuring their effect on glucose-induced currents in isolated vacuoles or on the expression of downstream genes such as CAB1 and NR1. The invention further encompasses the use of TMT1-overexpressing plants in tissue culture systems, where high sucrose concentrations are typically used to support growth and regeneration. By mitigating sugar inhibition, TMT1 overexpression allows for the use of higher sucrose concentrations without compromising growth, thereby improving transformation efficiency, callus induction, and shoot regeneration rates.

The invention further provides a method for producing high-value plant products by cultivating TMT1-overexpressing crops under standard agricultural conditions and harvesting the resulting biomass, seeds, or tubers. In oilseed crops such as canola and soybean, the increased lipid and protein content of seeds directly enhances nutritional and economic value. In tuber crops such as potato, the enhanced vacuolar sugar storage leads to improved yield and reduced post-harvest losses due to lower respiration rates. In cereals such as wheat and rice, the increased phloem loading capacity improves grain filling and increases kernel weight. The invention further provides a method for improving the efficiency of biofuel production by using TMT1-overexpressing biomass crops, where the reduced respiratory loss leads to higher net carbon accumulation in stems and leaves, thereby increasing the yield of fermentable sugars per unit of land area.

In summary, the best mode for carrying out the invention involves the constitutive overexpression of TMT1 under the control of the 35S promoter in the tmt1-2::tDNA background, resulting in a plant with enhanced vacuolar sugar import, reduced cytosolic sugar concentration, decreased respiratory carbon loss, increased phloem loading, and elevated seed biomass and composition. The invention is scalable, heritable, and broadly applicable to a wide range of plant species, providing a transformative tool for improving agricultural productivity, nutritional quality, and metabolic efficiency.

### Examples

Example 1: Generation and Molecular Characterization of TMT1-Overexpressing Arabidopsis Lines  
Arabidopsis thaliana plants of the homozygous tmt1-2::tDNA mutant background, which lacks functional TMT1 and TMT2 isoforms, were transformed using Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101 harboring a binary vector containing the full-length TMT1 coding sequence under the transcriptional control of the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter. The TMT1 sequence was amplified from wild-type Arabidopsis cDNA using gene-specific primers and cloned into the pART27 vector downstream of the 35S promoter and upstream of the nopaline synthase terminator. The construct was verified by sequencing and introduced into Arabidopsis plants via the floral dip method. T1 transformants were selected on Murashige and Skoog medium supplemented with 50 µg/mL kanamycin. T2 progeny were screened for homozygosity by segregation analysis, and independent lines were selected based on the level of TMT1 mRNA expression as determined by quantitative reverse transcription PCR. Three lines—designated 1, 4, and 10—exhibited the highest and most consistent levels of TMT1 transcript accumulation, with line 4 showing a 12-fold increase compared to wild-type plants under standard growth conditions. These lines were propagated for further phenotypic analysis. Northern blot analysis confirmed the absence of TMT1 mRNA in wild-type and tmt1-2::tDNA plants under normal conditions, while TMT1 mRNA was readily detectable in all three overexpressor lines. The expression of TMT2 and TMT3 remained unchanged, confirming that the observed phenotypes were specifically due to TMT1 overexpression. Immunoblot analysis using a polyclonal antibody raised against a synthetic peptide corresponding to the C-terminal region of TMT1 confirmed the presence of TMT1 protein in the tonoplast-enriched membrane fraction of overexpressor lines, with line 4 exhibiting the highest protein abundance. These results demonstrate the successful generation of transgenic Arabidopsis lines with stable and elevated TMT1 expression.

Example 2: Electrophysiological Confirmation of Enhanced Vacuolar Glucose Transport  
Vacuoles were isolated from mesophyll cells of wild-type, tmt1-2::tDNA, and TMT1-overexpressing lines 1, 4, and 10 using enzymatic digestion and osmotic shock, followed by purification through a Percoll gradient. Whole-vacuole patch-clamp recordings were performed in the whole-vacuole configuration using patch pipettes with resistances of 1.5–3 MΩ. The bath solution contained 200 mM KCl, 1 mM CaCl₂, 2 mM MgCl₂, and 10 mM MES-Tris (pH 7.5), while the pipette solution contained the same salts with 10 mM HEPES-Tris (pH 5.5) to establish a physiological pH gradient. The membrane potential was clamped at 0 mV, and glucose was applied to the bath solution by pressure ejection from a micropipette filled with 50 mM glucose in the same buffer. A robust inward current was observed upon glucose application in vacuoles from wild-type plants, consistent with glucose/proton antiport activity. In vacuoles from the tmt1-2::tDNA mutant, this current was reduced to approximately 20% of wild-type levels, confirming the essential role of TMT1 and TMT2 in vacuolar glucose transport. In contrast, vacuoles from TMT1-overexpressing line 4 exhibited a two-fold increase in current amplitude compared to wild-type vacuoles, while lines 1 and 10 showed intermediate increases of 1.4- and 1.6-fold, respectively. When the pH gradient was eliminated by equilibrating both bath and pipette solutions to pH 7.5, the glucose-induced current was reduced by approximately 50% in all genotypes, confirming that the transport mechanism is electrogenic and dependent on the proton motive force. The magnitude of the current correlated directly with TMT1 mRNA and protein levels, providing direct functional evidence that TMT1 overexpression enhances vacuolar glucose import capacity.

Example 3: Enhanced Growth of TMT1-Overexpressing Plants in High-Sugar Conditions  
Wild-type, tmt1-2::tDNA, and TMT1-overexpressing lines 1, 4, and 10 were grown in liquid Murashige and Skoog medium supplemented with either 0% or 5% glucose for 12 days under a 10-hour light/14-hour dark photoperiod. Fresh weight was measured after harvesting and blotting excess liquid from the tissue. In the absence of added glucose, all lines exhibited similar growth rates, with fresh weights averaging 185 mg per plant. In the presence of 5% glucose, wild-type plants showed severe growth inhibition, with fresh weight reduced to less than 25 mg. The tmt1-2::tDNA mutant exhibited even greater suppression, reaching only 9 mg of fresh weight. In contrast, all three TMT1-overexpressing lines exhibited significantly enhanced growth, with line 4 achieving a fresh weight of 78 mg, line 10 reaching 57 mg, and line 1 attaining 49 mg—representing increases of 312%, 228%, and 196%, respectively, compared to wild-type plants under the same conditions. The enhanced growth was not due to increased water uptake, as dry weight measurements showed proportional increases in biomass. The growth advantage was maintained across multiple generations and was not affected by the presence of sucrose or fructose in the medium, indicating that the phenotype is specific to the alleviation of glucose-mediated inhibition. These results demonstrate that TMT1 overexpression confers resistance to sugar-mediated growth repression, a trait with significant implications for in vitro propagation and high-carbon cultivation systems.

Example 4: Altered Subcellular Sugar Partitioning in TMT1-Overexpressing Plants  
Leaves from wild-type and TMT1-overexpressing lines 1 and 4 grown in liquid culture medium containing 5% glucose were subjected to nonaqueous fractionation to determine the subcellular distribution of glucose, fructose, and sucrose. Freeze-dried tissue was homogenized in a heptane-tetrachlorethylene solvent system and subjected to density gradient centrifugation to separate vacuolar, plastidic, and cytosolic compartments. Marker enzyme activities for alkaline pyrophosphatase (plastid), UGPase (cytosol), and acid phosphatase (vacuole) were used to validate fraction purity. Sugar content in each fraction was quantified by ion chromatography using a CarboPac PA1 column with amperometric detection. In wild-type plants, glucose was distributed as 40% vacuolar, 43% plastidic, and 17% cytosolic. In contrast, line 1 exhibited a dramatic shift, with 82% of glucose sequestered in the vacuole, 18% in plastids, and less than 1% in the cytosol. Line 4 showed a similar pattern, with 65% vacuolar, 35% plastidic, and no detectable cytosolic glucose. Fructose distribution followed a similar trend, with wild-type plants containing 45% vacuolar, 31% plastidic, and 24% cytosolic fructose, while lines 1 and 4 accumulated over 90% of fructose in the vacuole and less than 10% in plastids, with no detectable cytosolic fructose. Total sugar content remained unchanged across genotypes, confirming that the phenotypic differences were due to altered compartmentation, not total accumulation. These results demonstrate that TMT1 overexpression redirects monosaccharides from the cytosol into the vacuole, effectively decoupling sugar signaling from bulk sugar concentration.

Example 5: Reduced Respiratory Consumption of Glucose in TMT1-Overexpressing Plants  
Seedlings of wild-type, tmt1-2::tDNA, and TMT1-overexpressing lines 1, 4, and 10 were incubated in 100 mM [¹⁴C]glucose for six hours in the dark. Radiolabeled CO₂ released from respiration was captured in a 1 N KOH trap positioned above the seedlings and quantified by scintillation counting. Wild-type plants respired 18% of the imported glucose, while the tmt1-2::tDNA mutant exhibited a higher rate of 26%, indicating excessive carbon loss due to cytosolic sugar accumulation. In contrast, TMT1-overexpressing lines 1, 4, and 10 respired only 13%, 14%, and 14% of the glucose, respectively, representing reductions of 28–44% compared to wild-type plants. To confirm these findings in soil-grown plants, diurnal CO₂ exchange was measured using infrared gas analysis on whole rosettes. Nocturnal CO₂ release was significantly lower in lines 4 and 10 compared to wild-type and tmt1-2::tDNA plants, with reductions of 22% and 19%, respectively. Line 1 showed a smaller, statistically nonsignificant reduction of 8%. These results indicate that TMT1 overexpression reduces the respiratory consumption of imported sugars, thereby conserving carbon for growth and storage. The effect is most pronounced in lines with the highest TMT1 expression, suggesting a direct relationship between vacuolar sugar sequestration and metabolic efficiency.

Example 6: Enhanced Phloem Loading and SUC2 Expression in TMT1-Overexpressing Plants  
Leaves from 6-week-old wild-type and TMT1-overexpressing plants were excised at the end of the light period, and petioles were immersed in 15 mM EDTA solution (pH 7.25) to stimulate phloem exudation. Sugar export was allowed to proceed for 16 hours in a humidified chamber, after which exudates were collected and analyzed for sucrose, glucose, and fructose by HPLC. Wild-type leaves exported 12.5 mmol C₆ units per gram of fresh weight, while tmt1-2::tDNA leaves exported slightly less at 11.1 mmol C₆ units/g. In contrast, TMT1-overexpressing lines 1, 4, and 10 exported 16.6, 17.9, and 17.2 mmol C₆ units/g, respectively, representing increases of 33–43% over wild-type levels. Concurrently, quantitative RT-PCR analysis of SUC2 mRNA in the same leaf tissues revealed that wild-type plants expressed SUC2 at baseline levels, while line 4 exhibited a 150% increase in transcript abundance, and lines 1 and 10 showed 120% and 115% increases, respectively. To confirm the regulatory relationship, leaf discs from wild-type plants were incubated in 100 mM glucose for 4 or 16 hours, resulting in a 50% and 90% reduction in SUC2 mRNA, respectively. These results demonstrate that TMT1 overexpression indirectly upregulates SUC2 expression by lowering cytosolic glucose concentration, thereby enhancing phloem loading capacity and promoting carbon allocation to sink tissues.

Example 7: Increased Seed Weight and Composition in TMT1-Overexpressing Plants  
Seeds were harvested from wild-type and TMT1-overexpressing plants grown under standard soil conditions for 34 days. One thousand seed weight was measured gravimetrically, with wild-type seeds averaging 3.2 mg per 1,000 seeds. Seeds from line 1 weighed 3.6 mg (13% increase), line 4 weighed 4.1 mg (28% increase), and line 10 weighed 4.0 mg (25% increase). Lipid content was quantified by isopropanol extraction and gravimetric analysis, with wild-type seeds containing 4.4 mg of lipid per seed. Line 1 seeds contained 4.6 mg (+5%), line 4 seeds contained 4.9 mg (+12%), and line 10 seeds contained 4.9 mg (+12%). Protein content was measured using the bicinchoninic acid assay, with wild-type seeds containing 3.2 mg of protein per seed. Line 1 seeds contained 3.6 mg (+11%), line 4 seeds contained 3.7 mg (+14%), and line 10 seeds contained 3.5 mg (+10%). Total seed mass per plant was also measured, with wild-type plants producing 125 mg of seed per plant. Line 1 produced 218 mg (74% increase), line 4 produced 178 mg (42% increase), and line 10 produced 157 mg (26% increase). Seed number per silique remained unchanged across all lines, confirming that the increased seed mass was due to enhanced individual seed weight rather than increased seed number. These results demonstrate that TMT1 overexpression enhances both the quantity and quality of seed production, providing a dual advantage for agricultural applications.

Example 8: Altered Gene Expression Profiles in TMT1-Overexpressing Plants  
RNA was extracted from leaves of wild-type and TMT1-overexpressing plants grown under standard or high-glucose conditions and analyzed by quantitative RT-PCR for the expression of sugar-responsive genes CAB1 and NR1. Under control conditions, all lines exhibited similar levels of CAB1 and NR1 mRNA. In the presence of 5% glucose, wild-type plants showed a 70% reduction in CAB1 mRNA and a 2.1-fold increase in NR1 mRNA. In contrast, TMT1-overexpressing lines maintained CAB1 mRNA levels at 85–90% of control levels and exhibited only a 1.2- to 1.4-fold increase in NR1 mRNA, indicating a significant attenuation of sugar-mediated transcriptional responses. Similar results were observed in soil-grown plants, where TMT1-overexpressing lines exhibited higher CAB1 and lower NR1 mRNA levels compared to wild-type plants under ambient conditions. These findings confirm that the cytosolic glucose concentration, rather than total sugar content, governs sugar signaling. The ability of TMT1 overexpression to buffer cytosolic glucose levels and thereby modulate gene expression provides a novel mechanism for engineering plants with improved metabolic flexibility and stress resilience.

Example 9: Application of TMT1 Overexpression in Crop Species  
The TMT1 overexpression construct was introduced into Solanum tuberosum (potato) via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation using the same binary vector and promoter system as in Arabidopsis. Transgenic potato lines were selected and grown under greenhouse conditions. Tubers from TMT1-overexpressing lines exhibited a 25–30% increase in dry weight and a 20% increase in starch content compared to wild-type controls. In addition, tuber respiration rates during storage were reduced by 18–22%, leading to improved shelf life and reduced spoilage. In Brassica napus (canola), TMT1 overexpression resulted in a 15–20% increase in seed oil content and a 12–15% increase in seed protein content, with no change in seed number. In Zea mays (maize), TMT1 overexpression under the control of a leaf-specific promoter led to a 10–12% increase in kernel weight and a 15% increase in total grain yield per plant. These results demonstrate that the benefits of TMT1 overexpression are conserved across diverse plant species and can be effectively transferred to major agricultural crops to enhance yield and quality.

Example 10: Use of TMT1 Overexpression in Controlled Environment Agriculture  
TMT1-overexpressing Arabidopsis and tomato plants were grown in hydroponic systems supplemented with 10% sucrose, a concentration that typically causes severe growth inhibition and tissue necrosis in wild-type plants. Wild-type plants exhibited stunted growth, chlorosis, and reduced root development, with fresh weight reduced by 60–70%. In contrast, TMT1-overexpressing lines maintained normal growth, green leaf coloration, and robust root systems, with fresh weight exceeding that of wild-type plants grown in 0% sucrose by 25%. The enhanced growth was accompanied by higher chlorophyll content, increased photosynthetic efficiency, and improved nutrient uptake. These results demonstrate that TMT1 overexpression enables the use of high-sugar media in tissue culture and controlled environment agriculture without compromising plant health, thereby improving propagation efficiency, transformation success rates, and biomass production in vertical farming and bioreactor systems.
