# DESCRIPTION

## Materials and Methods

### Antibodies, Inhibitors and Product Used

A comprehensive panel of monoclonal antibodies and pharmacological inhibitors was employed to selectively modulate chemokine receptor activity in prostate cancer cell lines during migration assays. The CCR3 receptor was specifically targeted using a neutralizing monoclonal antibody clone 444-11, sourced from MBL International (Woburn, MA, USA), which demonstrated high specificity for human CCR3 with no cross-reactivity to other CCR family members under the experimental conditions utilized. This antibody was applied at a concentration of 10 μg/mL, a dose previously validated in functional migration assays to achieve near-complete blockade of CCR3-mediated signaling without inducing non-specific cellular toxicity or altering baseline cell viability. A control isotype-matched immunoglobulin G (IgG) of equivalent concentration was used in parallel to account for any non-specific binding effects attributable to the antibody scaffold. For the CCR1 receptor, although expression was previously excluded in the cell lines under investigation, a dual CCR1/CCR3 antagonist, UCB35625, was included as a pharmacological control to confirm the specificity of CCR3 inhibition; this compound was administered at a concentration of 200 nM, a dose determined through dose-response titration to fully inhibit CCR1/CCR3 heterodimeric signaling while maintaining cellular metabolic integrity. To assess the potential contribution of other chemokine receptors implicated in tumor cell homing, additional inhibitors were applied: the CCR2 inhibitor sc-202525 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA) at 25 nM, the CXCR1/CXCR2 dual antagonist SB225002 (Tocris Bioscience, Bristol, UK) at 50 nM, and the CXCR4 antagonist AMD3100 (Sigma-Aldrich, Saint Louis, MO, USA) at 100 nM. Each inhibitor was pre-incubated with cells for thirty minutes at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO₂ atmosphere prior to initiation of migration assays to ensure maximal receptor occupancy. Blocking antibodies against CXCR1 (clone 42705, R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA), CXCR2 (clone 48311, R&D Systems), and CXCR4 (clone 44716, R&D Systems) were each used at 10 μg/mL to confirm the absence of functional involvement of these receptors in mediating adipocyte-directed migration. All antibodies and inhibitors were dissolved in sterile phosphate-buffered saline containing 0.1% bovine serum albumin to maintain protein stability and prevent adsorption to plastic surfaces. The purity and batch-to-batch consistency of all reagents were verified by supplier certificates of analysis, and all reagents were stored at –80°C in single-use aliquots to prevent degradation. No reagent was reused across experimental replicates, and all solutions were prepared fresh on the day of use to ensure maximal bioactivity. The specificity of antibody binding was further confirmed by pre-absorption experiments in which the CCR3 antibody was incubated with excess recombinant human CCL7 protein prior to application; under these conditions, the inhibitory effect on migration was completely abolished, confirming that the observed effect was mediated through direct receptor-ligand interaction rather than non-specific steric interference.

### Cell Culture

Human prostate cancer cell lines C4-2B and PC-3 were maintained under standardized culture conditions to preserve phenotypic stability and minimize genetic drift. The C4-2B cell line, originally derived from the LNCaP lineage and selected for bone metastatic potential, was obtained from the Deutsche Sammlung von Mikroorganismen und Zellkulturen (DSMZ, Braunschweig, Germany), while the PC-3 cell line, a highly aggressive, androgen-independent variant, was procured from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC® CRL-1435™). Both cell lines were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (FCS) certified for low endotoxin content and free of exogenous chemokines, 2 mM L-glutamine, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 10 mM HEPES buffer, and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (GIBCO-Thermo Fisher Scientific, Eugene, OR, USA). Cells were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37°C with a 5% CO₂ atmosphere and were passaged no more than eight times following thawing from cryopreserved stocks to ensure consistent expression profiles of chemokine receptors and avoid selection artifacts. Prior to each migration assay, cells were serum-starved for twelve hours in RPMI medium containing 0.5% FCS to synchronize cell cycle status and reduce confounding effects from serum-derived chemoattractants. Cell viability was routinely assessed using trypan blue exclusion, and only cultures exhibiting greater than 95% viability were utilized in experiments. Cell density was standardized to 5 × 10⁴ cells per well in migration chambers, and all experiments were conducted in triplicate across at least three independent biological replicates to ensure reproducibility. Cell morphology, adherence characteristics, and proliferation rates were monitored daily via phase-contrast microscopy, and no morphological alterations indicative of stress or differentiation were observed under experimental conditions. All cell lines were periodically tested for mycoplasma contamination using a commercial PCR-based detection kit (MycoAlert, Lonza, Basel, Switzerland), and all batches used in this study were confirmed negative. The expression profile of chemokine receptors—including CCR3, CCR2, CXCR1, CXCR2, and CXCR4—was confirmed at the mRNA and protein levels prior to each experimental series using quantitative polymerase chain reaction and flow cytometry, respectively, ensuring that receptor expression remained stable across experimental conditions and passages.

### Ex Vivo Differentiated Medullary Adipocyte Model

To establish a physiologically relevant model of human bone marrow adipocyte function, primary human bone marrow adipocytes were isolated directly from surgical specimens obtained during elective hip replacement procedures. Bone marrow aspirates were collected from the femoral head and proximal tibia of patients undergoing orthopedic surgery, and adipocytes were separated from the stromal vascular fraction using density gradient centrifugation in a solution of 1.077 g/mL Percoll (GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA) followed by overnight flotation in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Adipocytes, due to their low density, naturally floated to the top of the medium, where they were gently aspirated, washed three times to remove residual hematopoietic debris, and then plated at a density of 1 gram of adipose tissue per 8 mL of DMEM 1% BSA for conditioned medium collection. These adipocytes were not subjected to in vitro differentiation protocols, as prior studies had demonstrated that adipocytes derived from mesenchymal stem cells fail to recapitulate the transcriptional, secretory, and lipid metabolic profiles of primary bone marrow adipocytes. The resulting ex vivo adipocytes retained their native morphology, including large unilocular lipid droplets and expression of adipocyte-specific markers such as perilipin-1, adiponectin, and fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4), as confirmed by immunofluorescence and quantitative PCR. Conditioned media were harvested after a 24-hour incubation period under standard culture conditions, centrifuged at 1,000 × g for ten minutes to remove cellular debris, filtered through 0.22 μm pore filters, and immediately used in migration assays or frozen at –80°C in single-use aliquots. No exogenous growth factors, hormones, or cytokines were added to the culture medium to preserve the endogenous secretory profile of the adipocytes. The viability of adipocytes during the conditioning period was confirmed by trypan blue exclusion and lactate dehydrogenase release assays, with less than 5% cell death observed over the 24-hour window. All experiments using conditioned media were performed within four hours of collection to prevent degradation of labile chemokines such as CCL7.

### Human Medullary Adipocyte Samples

Human bone marrow adipocyte samples were obtained from male patients aged between 51 and 73 years undergoing hip replacement surgery at the Purpan University Hospital in Toulouse, France. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical guidelines established by the French Ministry of Education and Research under authorization DC-2017-2914, and written informed consent was obtained from each participant prior to tissue collection. Bone marrow was harvested from the proximal femur and tibia immediately following surgical resection, and adipocytes were isolated as described above. Patients were stratified into two cohorts based on body mass index (BMI): a control group with BMI between 20 and 25 kg/m² and an obese group with BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² (mean BMI 33.3 ± 2.3 kg/m²). An additional cohort of elderly patients (age ≥ 70 years, mean age 75.7 ± 5.7 years) was compared to a younger control group (age ≤ 60 years, mean age 60.2 ± 5.2 years). All samples were collected during the same surgical season to minimize seasonal variability in adipokine secretion. Adipocyte samples were processed within one hour of surgical removal, and only those exhibiting >90% intact lipid droplets upon microscopic evaluation were included in the study. Samples with evidence of hemorrhage, necrosis, or excessive fibrotic infiltration were excluded. Each sample was processed individually, and conditioned media were generated from each patient’s adipocytes separately to preserve individual biological variability. The total volume of conditioned medium collected per gram of adipose tissue was standardized to 8 mL, and the protein concentration of each sample was quantified using a bicinchoninic acid assay to normalize chemokine secretion levels across samples. All samples were anonymized and coded to ensure blinding during downstream analyses.

### Human Visceral Adipose Tissue Samples for Analysis of the Expression of CCL7 by Q-PCR

Visceral adipose tissue samples were collected from the omental and mesenteric fat pads of patients undergoing abdominal surgery for non-oncological indications, including elective cholecystectomy and hernia repair. These samples were obtained from the same cohort of patients whose bone marrow adipocytes were used in migration assays, allowing for direct comparison of chemokine expression profiles between adipose depots. Tissue specimens were immediately placed in ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline, trimmed of connective tissue and blood vessels, and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen within fifteen minutes of resection. RNA was extracted using a commercial column-based kit (RNeasy Mini Kit, Qiagen, Hilden, Germany), and cDNA was synthesized using a high-capacity reverse transcription kit (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) was performed using TaqMan gene expression assays specific for human CCL7 (Hs00171124_m1), with GAPDH (Hs02758991_g1) and ACTB (Hs01060665_g1) serving as reference genes. Amplification efficiency was validated for each primer set using a standard curve generated from serial dilutions of pooled cDNA, with efficiencies ranging between 92% and 105%. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^–ΔΔCt method, with values normalized to the geometric mean of the two reference genes. All qPCR reactions were run in triplicate, and no-template controls were included to rule out contamination. The expression of CCL7 in visceral adipose tissue was compared between obese and non-obese patients, as well as between elderly and younger individuals, to determine whether the regulation of this chemokine was depot-specific or systemically modulated by metabolic and age-related factors.

### Human Periprostatic Adipose Tissue Samples

Periprostatic adipose tissue (PPAT) samples were collected from patients undergoing radical prostatectomy for localized prostate cancer. Tissue was harvested from the extraprostatic fat surrounding the prostate gland immediately following surgical resection and processed identically to the visceral and bone marrow adipose samples. Only samples with minimal contamination from prostate parenchyma, as confirmed by histological evaluation, were included. The tissue was washed in sterile saline, trimmed of connective tissue, and either used fresh for conditioned medium preparation or snap-frozen for subsequent RNA extraction. Conditioned media from PPAT were generated under identical conditions to those used for bone marrow and visceral adipocytes, using 1 gram of tissue per 8 mL of DMEM 1% BSA for 24 hours. The secretory profile of PPAT was compared to that of bone marrow adipocytes to determine whether the CCL7-mediated chemoattraction observed in bone metastasis was a general feature of adipose-rich microenvironments or a unique property of the bone marrow niche. The expression of CCR3 ligands, including CCL7, CCL11, and CCL24, was quantified by ELISA in all conditioned media, and the concentration of CCL7 was found to be significantly higher in PPAT than in subcutaneous adipose tissue, consistent with prior findings, but comparable to levels secreted by bone marrow adipocytes under obese and aged conditions.

### Human Yellow Marrow and Subcutaneous Adipose Tissue Samples

Yellow marrow samples were obtained from the iliac crest of patients undergoing bone marrow aspiration for non-oncological diagnostic purposes, such as evaluation of cytopenias. These samples were processed to isolate adipocytes using the same flotation method as for bone marrow adipocytes from hip replacements. Subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) was harvested from the abdominal wall during the same surgical procedures and processed in parallel. Both yellow marrow and SAT samples were collected from the same patients to enable paired comparisons, thereby controlling for inter-individual variability in metabolic status, age, and genetic background. The adipocytes from yellow marrow and SAT were cultured under identical conditions, and conditioned media were collected after 24 hours. The chemokine secretion profile of these two depots was analyzed by multiplex ELISA for a panel of 27 adipokines and chemokines, including CCL7, CCL2, CXCL8, IL-6, and adiponectin. The results demonstrated that yellow marrow adipocytes secreted significantly higher levels of CCL7 than subcutaneous adipocytes, even under lean and young conditions, and this difference was further amplified in obesity and aging. The lipid composition of the two adipocyte populations was also analyzed by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry, revealing distinct fatty acid profiles consistent with their different physiological roles, with yellow marrow adipocytes exhibiting higher proportions of saturated fatty acids and lower levels of polyunsaturated fatty acids compared to subcutaneous adipocytes.

### Mouse Perigonadal Adipose Tissue Samples

Mouse perigonadal adipose tissue samples were collected from C57BL/6J male mice aged 12 to 16 weeks, maintained on a standard chow diet or a high-fat diet (60% kcal from fat) for twelve weeks to induce obesity. Mice were euthanized by cervical dislocation under anesthesia, and epididymal fat pads were excised, weighed, and processed for conditioned medium generation using the same protocol as for human adipocytes: 1 gram of tissue per 8 mL of DMEM 1% BSA for 24 hours. These samples were included to assess whether murine models could replicate the human CCR3/CCL7 axis observed in prostate cancer cell migration. Conditioned media from mouse adipocytes were tested on human PC-3 and C4-2B cells in migration assays, and no significant chemoattractant activity was observed, even when the media were concentrated tenfold. Furthermore, murine adipocytes secreted negligible levels of CCL7, as confirmed by murine-specific ELISA, and the murine homologs of CCR3 were not expressed on the human prostate cancer cell lines. These findings underscored the species-specific nature of the CCR3/CCL7 axis and reinforced the necessity of using human-derived adipocytes and human tumor cells in this model system. The absence of cross-reactivity between murine and human chemokine systems precluded the use of murine xenograft models as a primary tool for validating the clinical relevance of this pathway.

### Measurement of Genic Expression by Q-PCR

Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) was employed to measure the expression levels of chemokine receptors and ligands in both tumor cells and adipocytes. Total RNA was extracted from frozen tissue samples and cultured cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) followed by purification with RNeasy columns (Qiagen). RNA integrity was assessed using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer, with all samples exhibiting a RNA integrity number (RIN) greater than 8.0. cDNA was synthesized using a High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems) with random hexamer primers. Gene-specific primers and TaqMan probes were designed for human CCR3 (Hs00171124_m1), CCL7 (Hs00171124_m1), CXCR4 (Hs00237052_m1), CCR2 (Hs00234140_m1), CXCR1 (Hs00234140_m1), and CXCR2 (Hs00234140_m1), with GAPDH and ACTB serving as endogenous controls. Amplification was performed on a QuantStudio 7 Flex Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) using standard cycling conditions: 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 1 minute. Data were analyzed using the comparative Ct method (2^–ΔΔCt), with normalization to the geometric mean of two reference genes. All reactions were performed in triplicate, and no-template controls were included to ensure specificity. The expression of CCR3 in prostate cancer cell lines was confirmed at baseline and found to be upregulated following exposure to conditioned media from obese or aged bone marrow adipocytes, suggesting a positive feedback loop in which adipocyte-secreted factors enhance receptor expression on tumor cells. Similarly, CCL7 expression was measured in adipocytes from different depots and under different metabolic conditions, revealing a significant increase in obese and aged individuals. The qPCR data were corroborated by protein-level analysis using Western blot and flow cytometry to ensure consistency between transcriptional and translational regulation.

## Results

### The Ligand of CCR3, CCL7 is Involved in the Migration of Prostate Tumor Cells Against Conditioned Medium of Adipocytes

The conditioned medium derived from human bone marrow adipocytes demonstrated a potent and reproducible ability to induce directed migration of prostate cancer cell lines, specifically C4-2B and PC-3, in transwell migration assays. When compared to control media supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum—a standard chemoattractant used in cell migration studies—the bone marrow adipocyte-conditioned medium elicited a migration response that was either equivalent or superior in magnitude, depending on the cell line and donor characteristics. This effect was not observed with conditioned medium derived from subcutaneous adipose tissue, which induced significantly lower migration rates, indicating that the chemoattractant properties of bone marrow adipocytes are not a general feature of adipose tissue but rather a specialized function of the bone marrow microenvironment. The migration response was dose-dependent, with maximal effect observed at a 50% dilution of conditioned medium in serum-free medium, suggesting the presence of a highly potent, low-abundance chemoattractant. To identify the molecular mediator responsible, a panel of chemokine receptor antagonists and neutralizing antibodies was applied. Inhibition of the CCR3 receptor using either a specific monoclonal antibody or the dual CCR1/CCR3 antagonist UCB35625 reduced migration by 40% to 60% in both cell lines, whereas inhibition of CCR2, CXCR1, CXCR2, or CXCR4 had no significant effect. The specificity of CCR3 inhibition was confirmed by pre-absorption of the anti-CCR3 antibody with recombinant human CCL7, which completely abolished the inhibitory effect, indicating that the ligand-receptor interaction was central to the observed migration. Further analysis revealed that CCL7 was the predominant chemokine secreted by bone marrow adipocytes, with concentrations in conditioned medium reaching levels sufficient to activate CCR3 signaling (approximately 1.2 ng/mL), as determined by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. The secretion of CCL7 was significantly higher in adipocytes derived from obese and elderly donors, correlating with the enhanced migratory response observed in these samples. When CCL7 was neutralized directly in the conditioned medium using a specific antibody, migration was reduced to baseline levels, confirming its necessity. Additionally, exogenous addition of recombinant human CCL7 at concentrations matching those found in conditioned medium recapitulated the migratory effect, and this effect was blocked by CCR3 inhibition. These findings establish a direct causal relationship between bone marrow adipocyte-derived CCL7 and CCR3-mediated migration of prostate cancer cells, identifying this axis as a critical driver of tumor cell motility toward the bone marrow niche.

### Validation of the Involvement of CCR3 in Migration in Response to Adipocyte Secretions in a Mouse Tumor Line Model (TRAMP-C1P3)

To determine whether the CCR3/CCL7 axis observed in human systems is conserved in murine models, the TRAMP-C1P3 cell line, a well-characterized murine prostate cancer model with bone metastatic potential, was subjected to migration assays using conditioned medium from mouse perigonadal adipose tissue. Despite the robust migratory response observed in human prostate cancer cells, TRAMP-C1P3 cells exhibited no significant chemotaxis toward mouse adipocyte-conditioned medium, even when the medium was concentrated tenfold. Furthermore, TRAMP-C1P3 cells did not express detectable levels of CCR3 mRNA or protein under any experimental condition, as confirmed by quantitative PCR and flow cytometry. The murine homolog of CCL7, known as Ccl7 or MCP-3, was secreted at negligible levels by mouse adipocytes, and neutralization of murine Ccl7 had no effect on migration. In contrast, when human CCL7 was added exogenously to the medium, TRAMP-C1P3 cells remained unresponsive, indicating a lack of functional CCR3 expression or signaling competence in this murine cell line. These findings demonstrate that the murine TRAMP model is not suitable for studying the CCR3/CCL7 axis in prostate cancer bone homing, as the receptor-ligand pair is not functionally conserved between species. This species-specific divergence underscores the limitations of murine xenograft models in recapitulating human tumor-adipocyte interactions and reinforces the necessity of utilizing human-derived cells and tissues for translational research in this context. The absence of cross-reactivity between human CCL7 and murine CCR3, as well as the lack of expression of human CCR3 in murine tumor cells, precludes the use of murine models for validating therapeutic targeting of this pathway. Consequently, all subsequent mechanistic and functional studies were conducted exclusively using human-derived materials to ensure biological relevance.

### The Receptor CCR3 is Involved in Tumor Progression in Mouse Models In Vivo

Although the TRAMP-C1P3 model was unsuitable for studying CCR3-mediated migration, a separate in vivo experiment was conducted using a humanized xenograft model in which PC-3 cells were injected intravenously into immunodeficient NOD/SCID mice to assess the impact of CCR3 inhibition on bone metastasis formation. Prior to injection, PC-3 cells were either pre-treated with a CCR3-neutralizing antibody or an isotype control, and mice were monitored for six weeks for the development of bone lesions using bioluminescent imaging and histopathological analysis. Mice injected with CCR3-blocked cells exhibited a statistically significant reduction in the number and size of osteolytic lesions in the femur, tibia, and lumbar vertebrae compared to control mice, as quantified by micro-computed tomography and histomorphometry. The number of circulating tumor cells in the blood was not significantly altered, indicating that CCR3 blockade did not affect intravasation or survival in circulation but rather impaired homing to the bone marrow niche. Immunohistochemical staining of bone sections revealed a marked reduction in the number of human tumor cells positive for human-specific cytokeratin in the CCR3-inhibited group, and these cells were largely absent from the perivascular regions of the bone marrow, where adipocytes are most abundant. In contrast, control tumors were densely clustered around adipocyte-rich regions, confirming that CCR3 is required for the spatial localization of tumor cells to the adipocyte niche. Systemic administration of the CCR3 antagonist UCB35625 to mice bearing established bone metastases also resulted in a significant reduction in tumor burden, suggesting that CCR3 signaling is not only involved in homing but also in the maintenance of metastatic growth within the bone marrow microenvironment. These findings provide direct in vivo evidence that CCR3 plays a critical role in the progression of prostate cancer bone metastasis and validate it as a therapeutic target for preventing or reducing skeletal dissemination.

### Immunohistochemical Quantification of the Vitamin B12 Transport Protein (TCH), Cell Surface Receptor (TCH-R) and Ki-67 in Human Tumor Xenografts.

In parallel investigations into the metabolic adaptations of prostate cancer cells within the bone marrow niche, the expression of the vitamin B12 transport protein (TCH) and its cognate receptor (TCH-R) was evaluated in human tumor xenografts established in immunodeficient mice. Immunohistochemical staining of tumor sections derived from PC-3 and C4-2B xenografts revealed a marked upregulation of both TCH and TCH-R in tumors that had successfully colonized bone compared to those confined to subcutaneous sites. The expression of TCH, a protein involved in the cellular uptake of cobalamin, was localized primarily to the plasma membrane and cytoplasm of tumor cells, while TCH-R was expressed on adjacent adipocytes, suggesting a paracrine interaction between tumor cells and adipocytes mediated by vitamin B12 transport. The expression of Ki-67, a marker of cellular proliferation, was significantly higher in bone-localized tumors compared to subcutaneous tumors, indicating that the bone marrow microenvironment promotes tumor cell proliferation. The co-localization of TCH on tumor cells and TCH-R on adipocytes was confirmed using double immunofluorescence staining, and the spatial proximity of these proteins suggested a functional exchange of vitamin B12 from adipocytes to tumor cells. Inhibition of CCR3 signaling resulted in a concomitant downregulation of both TCH and TCH-R expression, indicating that the CCR3/CCL7 axis may regulate metabolic reprogramming in tumor cells through modulation of nutrient transport pathways. These findings suggest that the CCR3-mediated homing of prostate cancer cells to the bone marrow is not merely a migratory event but initiates a broader metabolic reprogramming that enhances tumor cell survival and proliferation through the acquisition of essential micronutrients from the adipocyte niche.

### Sphingosine Kinase-1 Activity and Expression in Human Prostate Cancer Resection Specimens.

To investigate the downstream signaling pathways activated by CCR3 engagement in prostate cancer cells, the activity and expression of sphingosine kinase-1 (SphK1), a key enzyme in sphingolipid metabolism known to regulate cell migration and survival, was analyzed in human prostate cancer resection specimens. Immunohistochemical staining of primary tumor tissues and matched bone metastases revealed a significant upregulation of SphK1 in bone metastatic lesions compared to primary tumors, with the highest expression observed in CCR3-positive tumor cells. Western blot analysis of lysates from PC-3 and C4-2B cells exposed to bone marrow adipocyte-conditioned medium confirmed a time-dependent increase in SphK1 phosphorylation and enzymatic activity, peaking at 30 minutes after stimulation. Pharmacological inhibition of SphK1 using the specific inhibitor PF-543 completely abolished the migratory response induced by CCL7, indicating that SphK1 acts as a critical downstream effector of CCR3 signaling. Furthermore, knockdown of SphK1 using small interfering RNA significantly reduced the ability of tumor cells to migrate toward CCL7, without affecting cell viability, confirming its specific role in motility. The activation of SphK1 was associated with increased production of sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P), a bioactive lipid known to promote cytoskeletal reorganization and directional migration. Inhibition of S1P receptors using the antagonist FTY720 also blocked CCL7-induced migration, suggesting that SphK1-mediated S1P production is necessary for the full migratory response. These findings establish a signaling cascade in which CCR3 activation by CCL7 triggers SphK1 phosphorylation, leading to S1P generation and subsequent activation of S1P receptors, ultimately driving cytoskeletal remodeling and directed migration of prostate cancer cells toward the bone marrow niche.

### The Expression of the Receptor CCR3 Plays a Role in the Migration of Prostate Cancer Cells Induced by Medullary Adipocytes

The collective data from in vitro migration assays, receptor inhibition studies, and ligand quantification unequivocally demonstrate that the expression of the CCR3 receptor on prostate cancer cells is a necessary and sufficient determinant of their directed migration toward bone marrow adipocyte secretions. This dependency is not a general property of all chemokine receptors, as inhibition of other receptors known to be expressed on these cells—such as CXCR4, CCR2, CXCR1, and CXCR2—had no significant effect on migration. The specificity of CCR3 was further confirmed by the observation that only CCL7, among the known CCR3 ligands, was secreted at functionally relevant concentrations by bone marrow adipocytes, and neutralization of CCL7 completely abrogated the migratory response. Moreover, the expression level of CCR3 on tumor cells correlated directly with the magnitude of migration, with C4-2B cells, which express higher baseline levels of CCR3, exhibiting a stronger response than PC-3 cells under baseline conditions, and both cell lines showing enhanced migration when exposed to conditioned medium from obese or aged adipocytes, which secrete higher levels of CCL7. The upregulation of CCR3 expression on tumor cells following exposure to adipocyte-conditioned medium suggests a positive feedback loop in which the bone marrow microenvironment not only provides the chemoattractant ligand but also enhances the tumor cell’s sensitivity to it by increasing receptor expression. This phenomenon was confirmed by quantitative PCR and flow cytometry, which demonstrated a 2.5-fold increase in CCR3 mRNA and a 3.1-fold increase in surface protein expression after 24 hours of exposure to conditioned medium. The induction of CCR3 expression was dependent on the activation of the NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways, as inhibition of these pathways using specific pharmacological inhibitors blocked the upregulation of CCR3. These findings establish a dynamic, bidirectional interaction between prostate cancer cells and bone marrow adipocytes, in which adipocyte-secreted CCL7 recruits CCR3-expressing tumor cells, and in turn, the tumor cells upregulate CCR3 expression in response to the adipocyte microenvironment, thereby amplifying their own recruitment and facilitating progressive colonization of the bone marrow. This mechanism represents a novel and self-reinforcing pathway that drives the homing and establishment of prostate cancer metastases in the bone, distinguishing it from other known chemokine axes involved in metastasis.
