# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to novel engineered immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) Fc variants with selectively enhanced binding affinity for the inhibitory Fc gamma receptor IIb (FcγRIIb) while maintaining negligible or reduced binding to the activating Fc gamma receptors, particularly FcγRIIaR131 and FcγRIIaH131, as well as to FcγRI and FcγRIIIa. More specifically, the invention encompasses recombinant antibody molecules comprising an Fc region containing a defined set of amino acid substitutions that confer high selectivity for FcγRIIb over all other human FcγRs, including the closely related FcγRIIa allotypes. These engineered Fc variants are designed for use in therapeutic monoclonal antibodies intended to modulate immune responses through selective engagement of FcγRIIb, thereby enabling potent agonistic activity in the context of tumor necrosis factor receptor (TNFR) superfamily targeting antibodies, suppression of B-cell activation in autoimmune disorders, inhibition of mast cell degranulation in allergic conditions, and induction of peripheral tolerance in dendritic cell-mediated immune regulation—all without triggering FcγRIIa-mediated platelet activation, thrombosis, or accelerated clearance from circulation. The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions containing such engineered antibodies, methods of producing them, and their use in the treatment of cancer, autoimmune diseases, allergic disorders, and immune-mediated inflammatory conditions.

The technical field of the invention lies at the intersection of protein engineering, structural immunology, and therapeutic antibody development. It addresses a long-standing challenge in the field of antibody therapeutics: the inability to selectively enhance binding to FcγRIIb without concomitantly increasing affinity for FcγRIIaR131, a receptor that, when engaged by immune complexes, can induce platelet aggregation, thromboembolic events, and rapid antibody clearance. Prior approaches to enhancing FcγRIIb binding, such as the S267E/L328F double substitution, have been limited by their lack of selectivity, resulting in unacceptable safety profiles due to off-target activation of FcγRIIaR131-expressing cells. The present invention overcomes this limitation by introducing a unique combination of substitutions that exploit subtle structural differences between FcγRIIb and FcγRIIaR131, thereby achieving unprecedented selectivity. The engineered Fc variants described herein are structurally distinct from previously reported variants, as demonstrated by crystallographic analysis revealing novel conformational rearrangements in the CH2 domain interface that are uniquely compatible with FcγRIIb recognition but incompatible with FcγRIIa binding. This invention therefore represents a significant advancement in the design of next-generation therapeutic antibodies with improved efficacy, safety, and pharmacokinetic properties.

## BACKGROUND ART

The therapeutic application of monoclonal antibodies has revolutionized the treatment of cancer, autoimmune diseases, and infectious disorders. Among the most widely used antibody formats is the IgG1 subclass, which exerts its biological effects through interactions with Fc gamma receptors (FcγRs) expressed on immune effector cells. These receptors are divided into activating and inhibitory subtypes, with FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa, and FcγRIIIb mediating pro-inflammatory responses such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), phagocytosis, and cytokine release, while FcγRIIb serves as the sole inhibitory FcγR, dampening immune activation through immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM)-dependent signaling. The functional balance between activating and inhibitory FcγRs is critical for immune homeostasis, and dysregulation of this balance underlies numerous pathological conditions, including autoimmunity, chronic inflammation, and uncontrolled tumor immune evasion.

Historically, efforts to enhance the therapeutic potency of monoclonal antibodies have focused on augmenting interactions with activating FcγRs, particularly FcγRIIIa, to amplify ADCC. These efforts have yielded clinically successful antibodies with engineered Fc regions, such as those containing the S239D/I332E/A330L substitutions, which significantly improve binding to FcγRIIIa and enhance antitumor activity. However, such enhancements often come at the cost of increased risk of systemic inflammation and cytokine release syndrome. In contrast, the inhibitory receptor FcγRIIb has received comparatively less attention, despite its critical role in maintaining immune tolerance. FcγRIIb is uniquely expressed on B cells and dendritic cells, where it functions as a central checkpoint for immune regulation. Co-engagement of FcγRIIb with the B-cell receptor (BCR) by immune complexes suppresses B-cell proliferation, differentiation, and antibody production, thereby offering a powerful mechanism for treating autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus. Similarly, FcγRIIb on dendritic cells inhibits antigen presentation and costimulatory molecule expression, promoting peripheral tolerance and suppressing T-cell activation. Moreover, recent studies have revealed that FcγRIIb acts as a molecular scaffold for agonistic antibodies targeting members of the TNFR superfamily, including CD40, DR5, and CD137, enabling receptor clustering and downstream signaling that would otherwise be insufficient for full agonism in the absence of FcγRIIb.

Despite these compelling therapeutic opportunities, the development of Fc-engineered antibodies with enhanced FcγRIIb binding has been hampered by a fundamental structural challenge: the high sequence homology between FcγRIIb and FcγRIIaR131, the latter of which is expressed on platelets, monocytes, and macrophages. This homology has rendered it exceedingly difficult to design Fc variants that distinguish between these two receptors. The most widely studied Fc variant for enhancing FcγRIIb binding, S267E/L328F, increases affinity for FcγRIIb by more than 300-fold but simultaneously enhances binding to FcγRIIaR131 to an equal or greater extent. This lack of selectivity has profound clinical implications. FcγRIIaR131 is the primary receptor responsible for immune complex-mediated platelet activation and aggregation, and its engagement by therapeutic antibodies has been directly linked to thromboembolic complications in clinical trials of anti-CD154 and anti-VEGF antibodies. Furthermore, antibodies with enhanced FcγRIIa binding are rapidly internalized and degraded by FcγRIIa-expressing myeloid cells, resulting in shortened serum half-life and reduced bioavailability. These limitations have severely restricted the clinical translation of FcγRIIb-enhanced antibodies, despite their demonstrated efficacy in preclinical models.

Prior attempts to address this challenge have focused on glycoengineering, such as afucosylation at Asn297, which enhances binding to FcγRIIIa but has minimal effect on FcγRIIb. Other strategies have involved altering the lower hinge region or CH2 domain to modulate Fc conformation, but none have achieved the level of selectivity required for safe and effective therapeutic application. The structural basis for FcγRIIb recognition has been partially elucidated through crystallographic studies of Fc-FcγRIIb complexes, but these have not revealed a clear path to engineering selectivity over FcγRIIaR131. The key insight missing from prior art has been the identification of specific amino acid substitutions capable of inducing conformational rearrangements in the Fc region that are uniquely compatible with FcγRIIb binding but sterically or electrostatically incompatible with FcγRIIaR131. The present invention provides this missing insight through the discovery of a novel substitution, P238D, and its combinatorial optimization into a highly selective variant, V12, which achieves over 200-fold enhancement of FcγRIIb binding without increasing binding to FcγRIIaR131 or any other activating FcγR. This breakthrough enables the development of therapeutic antibodies with unprecedented safety and efficacy profiles, overcoming the fundamental limitations of prior Fc engineering strategies.

## PRIOR ART DOCUMENTS

The prior art includes numerous publications and patent disclosures describing Fc engineering strategies aimed at modulating interactions with Fc gamma receptors. U.S. Patent No. 7,655,778 B2 discloses Fc variants containing the S267E and L328F substitutions, which are reported to increase binding affinity for FcγRIIb by up to 430-fold relative to wild-type IgG1. However, this patent explicitly acknowledges that the same substitutions also increase binding to FcγRIIaR131 to a comparable extent, thereby failing to address the critical issue of selectivity. Similarly, U.S. Patent No. 8,470,297 B2 describes the use of afucosylated IgG1 antibodies to enhance ADCC through increased binding to FcγRIIIa, but does not disclose any modifications that selectively enhance FcγRIIb binding or distinguish it from FcγRIIa allotypes. The publication by Chu et al. (Nature Biotechnology, 2008) describes the S267E/L328F variant in detail, demonstrating its efficacy in suppressing B-cell activation in a lupus model and reducing IgE production in vivo, but also reports that the variant binds FcγRIIaR131 with affinity nearly identical to that of FcγRIIb, raising significant safety concerns for clinical application.

Another relevant prior art document is the study by Smith et al. (Journal of Immunology, 2012), which provides quantitative binding data demonstrating that the S267E/L328F variant exhibits a 355-fold increase in affinity for FcγRIIb and an 864-fold increase for FcγRIIaR131, confirming the lack of selectivity inherent to this approach. The authors further note that this dual enhancement correlates with increased platelet activation in vitro and suggests that such variants may pose unacceptable risks in patients homozygous for the FcγRIIaR131 allele, which is prevalent in multiple ethnic populations. In contrast, the present invention discloses an Fc variant that increases FcγRIIb binding by over 200-fold while maintaining binding to FcγRIIaR131 at levels indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1, thereby eliminating this risk entirely.

The crystal structure of the Fc-FcγRIIaR131 complex (PDB ID: 3RY6) has been previously described in the literature and reveals that the binding interface is dominated by hydrophobic interactions and a salt bridge between Glu267 and Arg131. This structural insight has been used to rationalize the non-selective enhancement observed with S267E/L328F, as the introduced Glu267 forms an identical salt bridge with Arg131 in both FcγRIIb and FcγRIIaR131. However, no prior art document has identified or exploited the structural divergence at position 238, where FcγRIIb contains Tyr160 and FcγRIIa contains Phe160, nor has any prior art described how substitution of Pro238 to Asp induces a conformational rearrangement that enables a novel hydrogen bond with Tyr160 of FcγRIIb while being sterically incompatible with Phe160 of FcγRIIa. The present invention is the first to elucidate this mechanism and to leverage it for the design of a highly selective Fc variant.

Additionally, prior art documents such as the study by White et al. (Nature Immunology, 2011) and Wilson et al. (Journal of Experimental Medicine, 2011) demonstrate the critical role of FcγRIIb as a scaffold for agonistic anti-CD40 and anti-DR5 antibodies, respectively, but do not provide any engineered Fc variants capable of enhancing this function without concomitant activation of FcγRIIa. Similarly, the work by Horton et al. (Journal of Clinical Investigation, 2011) shows that an anti-CD19 antibody with S267E/L328F Fc reduces B-cell activation in a lupus model, but the accompanying platelet activation and potential for thrombosis remain unaddressed. The present invention resolves this critical gap by providing an Fc variant that enhances agonistic activity through selective FcγRIIb engagement while avoiding platelet activation entirely, thereby enabling safe and effective application in autoimmune and oncologic indications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The present invention addresses several critical and previously unresolved problems in the field of therapeutic antibody engineering. First, there exists a significant unmet need for Fc-engineered monoclonal antibodies that can selectively enhance binding to the inhibitory FcγRIIb receptor without concomitantly increasing affinity for the activating FcγRIIaR131 receptor, which is known to mediate platelet aggregation, thromboembolic events, and accelerated antibody clearance. Prior Fc variants, such as S267E/L328F, have demonstrated potent enhancement of FcγRIIb binding but simultaneously increase binding to FcγRIIaR131 to an equal or greater degree, rendering them unsuitable for clinical use due to unacceptable safety risks. This lack of selectivity has severely limited the therapeutic potential of FcγRIIb-targeted antibodies, despite their proven efficacy in preclinical models of autoimmunity, allergy, and cancer immunotherapy.

Second, there is a need for Fc variants that maintain the structural integrity and pharmacokinetic properties of native IgG1, including stability at high concentrations, resistance to aggregation, and prolonged serum half-life mediated by FcRn binding. Many engineered Fc regions, particularly those involving substitutions in the CH2 domain, suffer from reduced thermal stability, increased formation of high-molecular-weight species upon storage, and altered binding to FcRn, all of which compromise their suitability for pharmaceutical development. The present invention solves this problem by identifying a combination of substitutions that not only enhance FcγRIIb selectivity but also preserve FcRn binding affinity and maintain stability comparable to wild-type IgG1, even at concentrations of 100 mg/ml.

Third, there is a need for Fc variants that enable enhanced agonistic activity of antibodies targeting the TNFR superfamily, such as CD40, DR5, and CD137, without triggering off-target immune activation or platelet aggregation. While it has been established that FcγRIIb serves as a critical scaffold for receptor clustering and downstream signaling in these antibodies, prior attempts to exploit this mechanism have been hindered by the unavoidable co-engagement of FcγRIIaR131, which introduces confounding pro-inflammatory effects. The present invention provides an Fc variant that selectively enhances FcγRIIb-mediated agonism while eliminating the risk of FcγRIIa-mediated platelet activation, thereby enabling the safe and effective development of next-generation agonistic antibodies for cancer immunotherapy.

Finally, there is a need for Fc variants that are broadly applicable across multiple therapeutic indications, including autoimmune diseases, allergic disorders, and oncology, without requiring patient stratification based on FcγRIIa genotype. The present invention solves this problem by providing an Fc variant that is functionally inert toward all FcγRIIa allotypes (R131 and H131), thereby ensuring consistent safety and efficacy across diverse patient populations, regardless of their genetic background. This represents a major advancement over prior art, which is inherently limited by population-specific risks associated with FcγRIIa polymorphisms.

### Means for Solving the Problems

The problems outlined above are solved by the invention through the identification and characterization of a novel class of engineered IgG1 Fc variants that contain a specific combination of amino acid substitutions at positions 233, 237, 238, 268, 271, and 330 of the Fc region, as defined by the EU numbering system. The most preferred variant, referred to herein as V12, comprises the substitutions E233D, G237D, P238D, H268D, P271G, and A330R. These substitutions collectively confer a more than 200-fold increase in binding affinity for human FcγRIIb relative to wild-type IgG1, while maintaining binding to FcγRIIaR131 and FcγRIIaH131 at levels indistinguishable from the wild-type receptor. This unprecedented selectivity is achieved through a unique structural mechanism in which the P238D substitution induces a conformational rearrangement in the CH2 domain that exposes novel interaction surfaces compatible with FcγRIIb but incompatible with FcγRIIa allotypes. Specifically, the substitution of Pro238 with Asp disrupts the hydrophobic core of the CH2 domain, leading to a repositioning of adjacent residues that enables the formation of a novel hydrogen bond between Gly237 and Tyr160 of FcγRIIb—a residue that is replaced by Phe in FcγRIIa, thereby conferring selectivity. Additional substitutions at positions 233, 237, 268, 271, and 330 further stabilize this conformation and enhance binding affinity through electrostatic and van der Waals interactions with FcγRIIb, without introducing any new interactions with FcγRIIa.

The invention further encompasses the use of these Fc variants in the context of monoclonal antibodies directed against a wide range of therapeutic targets, including but not limited to CD19, IgE, CD40, DR5, CD137, and other members of the TNFR superfamily. When incorporated into such antibodies, the V12 Fc variant enhances agonistic activity by promoting efficient crosslinking of target receptors through FcγRIIb-mediated scaffolding, without inducing platelet activation or accelerating clearance. The variant also retains high binding affinity for FcRn at acidic pH, ensuring a serum half-life comparable to that of wild-type IgG1, and demonstrates excellent stability at high concentrations, with minimal formation of high-molecular-weight aggregates upon storage. The structural basis for these properties has been elucidated through high-resolution crystallography of the Fc(V12)-FcγRIIb complex, which reveals a distinct binding interface characterized by multiple novel hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and van der Waals contacts that are absent in the wild-type Fc-FcγRIIb complex and incompatible with FcγRIIa binding.

The invention further provides methods for producing these Fc variants using recombinant DNA technology, including the use of site-directed mutagenesis, transient transfection in mammalian cell lines such as FreeStyle™ 293F cells, and purification via protein A affinity chromatography. The resulting antibodies are formulated into stable pharmaceutical compositions suitable for intravenous or subcutaneous administration. The invention also includes methods of treatment comprising the administration of such antibodies to patients suffering from autoimmune diseases, allergic disorders, or cancers, wherein the therapeutic effect is mediated through selective engagement of FcγRIIb without triggering FcγRIIa-mediated adverse events.

### Effects of the Invention

The invention produces a number of profound and unexpected effects that collectively represent a transformative advance in the field of therapeutic antibody engineering. First, the V12 Fc variant enables the safe and effective enhancement of FcγRIIb-mediated immune regulation without the risk of thromboembolic complications that have plagued prior Fc engineering efforts. This is demonstrated by the absence of platelet activation or aggregation in vitro when immune complexes are formed with the V12 variant, even in donors homozygous for the FcγRIIaR131 allele, a genotype previously associated with high risk of antibody-induced thrombosis. This effect is not observed with any prior Fc variant, making the invention uniquely suited for clinical development.

Second, the invention enables the potentiation of agonistic activity in TNFR superfamily-targeting antibodies without compromising safety. Antibodies containing the V12 Fc variant induce significantly higher levels of cytokine production and T-cell activation in co-culture assays compared to wild-type IgG1, demonstrating enhanced agonism, yet do not trigger off-target immune activation or platelet aggregation. This effect is achieved without increasing binding to any other FcγR, including FcγRIIIa, which is typically associated with ADCC and cytokine release. The result is a highly selective and potent agonistic antibody with a superior therapeutic index.

Third, the invention provides Fc variants that maintain the pharmacokinetic profile of native IgG1, with no reduction in FcRn binding affinity and no shortening of serum half-life in human FcRn transgenic mice. This is a critical advantage over prior art variants, which often exhibit accelerated clearance due to increased FcγRIIa-mediated endocytosis. The preservation of FcRn binding ensures that antibodies containing the V12 Fc variant can be administered at standard dosing regimens, without the need for increased frequency or higher doses, thereby improving patient compliance and reducing treatment burden.

Fourth, the invention provides Fc variants with exceptional biophysical stability, even at high concentrations of 100 mg/ml, with aggregation levels comparable to wild-type IgG1 after four weeks of storage at 25°C. This stability is essential for the development of subcutaneous formulations and for manufacturing scalability, and is not achieved by prior Fc variants, which often suffer from reduced thermal stability and increased viscosity due to structural perturbations in the CH2 domain.

Fifth, the invention enables broad applicability across diverse patient populations, regardless of FcγRIIa genotype, because the V12 variant does not bind to either FcγRIIaR131 or FcγRIIaH131 at levels above wild-type IgG1. This eliminates the need for pre-treatment genotyping and ensures consistent efficacy and safety across ethnic groups, a significant advantage over prior art that is inherently limited by population-specific polymorphisms.

Collectively, these effects enable the development of a new generation of therapeutic antibodies with enhanced potency, improved safety, and superior manufacturability, addressing long-standing limitations in the field and opening new avenues for the treatment of cancer, autoimmune diseases, and allergic disorders.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### <Substitution>

The invention encompasses a novel class of immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) Fc variants characterized by a specific combination of amino acid substitutions at positions 233, 237, 238, 268, 271, and 330 of the Fc region, as defined by the EU numbering system. The most preferred variant, designated V12, comprises the substitutions E233D, G237D, P238D, H268D, P271G, and A330R. Each of these substitutions contributes to the overall functional profile of the variant, and their combination results in synergistic effects that cannot be predicted from the individual substitutions alone. The substitution at position 238, P238D, is the cornerstone of the invention, as it initiates a conformational rearrangement in the CH2 domain that is essential for achieving selectivity for FcγRIIb over FcγRIIa allotypes. This substitution disrupts the hydrophobic core formed by Pro238, Val263, Val266, Val323, Asn325, and Leu328 in wild-type IgG1, forcing Asp238 to adopt an exposed conformation that enables novel interactions with FcγRIIb, including a hydrogen bond between the main chain of Gly237 and the side chain of Tyr160 on FcγRIIb—a residue that is replaced by Phe in FcγRIIa, thereby conferring selectivity. The additional substitutions at positions 233, 237, 268, 271, and 330 stabilize this rearranged conformation and enhance binding affinity through electrostatic and van der Waals interactions with FcγRIIb, without introducing any new interactions with FcγRIIa. The substitution E233D introduces a negatively charged residue near Lys113 of FcγRIIb, potentially forming a weak electrostatic interaction that contributes to binding. The substitution G237D enhances the hydrogen bond network with Tyr160 of FcγRIIb, reducing the distance from 3.1 Å to 2.9 Å and increasing binding stability. The substitution H268D introduces a negatively charged residue that forms an electrostatic interaction with Arg292 in the CH2 domain, which is repositioned due to the conformational change induced by P271G. The substitution P271G, replacing a rigid proline with a flexible glycine, allows for greater conformational freedom in the loop region spanning residues 266–273, facilitating optimal positioning of Asp270 to form a salt bridge with Arg131 of FcγRIIb. The substitution A330R introduces a positively charged residue near Glu86 of FcγRIIb, potentially forming a weak electrostatic interaction that further stabilizes the complex. The combination of these six substitutions results in a binding affinity for FcγRIIb that is over 200-fold greater than that of wild-type IgG1, while binding to FcγRIIaR131 and FcγRIIaH131 remains unchanged from wild-type levels. This level of selectivity is unprecedented in the field and is not achievable by any other known combination of substitutions. The invention further encompasses variants containing fewer than all six substitutions, provided that they retain the P238D substitution and demonstrate enhanced binding to FcγRIIb without increasing binding to FcγRIIa allotypes. Such variants may include E233D/P238D, G237D/P238D, H268D/P238D, P271G/P238D, A330R/P238D, or combinations thereof, as long as they meet the functional criteria of selective FcγRIIb enhancement. The invention also encompasses the use of these substitutions in the context of other IgG subclasses, including IgG2, IgG3, and IgG4, provided that the structural context permits the same conformational rearrangements to occur. The substitutions may be introduced into any antibody scaffold, including full-length IgG, Fab fragments, Fc fusion proteins, or bispecific antibodies, as long as the Fc region is intact and capable of binding FcγRIIb. The invention further encompasses the use of these substitutions in combination with other Fc modifications, such as glycoengineering, to further fine-tune effector function, provided that the selectivity for FcγRIIb over FcγRIIa is preserved.

The invention does not require modification of this residue, and the novel Fc variants are fully compatible with the presence of an asparagine at position 297. The glycan structure at this site may be modified independently of the amino acid substitutions described herein, including afucosylation, galactosylation, sialylation, or high-mannose content, to further modulate effector function. The presence of the asparagine residue at position 297 is essential for the structural stability of the Fc region, and its absence or mutation would result in loss of FcγR binding and reduced serum half-life. The invention further encompasses the use of the novel Fc variants in antibodies that contain additional glycosylation sites introduced by mutagenesis, provided that these sites do not interfere with the conformational rearrangements induced by the P238D substitution or other substitutions in the Fc region.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The invention is exemplified by the preparation of a panel of IgG1 antibody variants containing single or multiple amino acid substitutions in the Fc region, as defined by the EU numbering system. The wild-type human IgG1 antibody, designated as IgG1, was used as the template for all mutagenesis experiments. The heavy chain sequence of the IgG1 antibody was cloned into a mammalian expression vector under the control of the cytomegalovirus promoter, and the light chain sequence was cloned into a separate vector under the control of the same promoter. Site-directed mutagenesis was performed using the QuikChange Site-Directed Mutagenesis Kit (Agilent Technologies) to introduce the desired amino acid substitutions at positions 233, 237, 238, 268, 271, and 330. The mutations were introduced individually and in combination to generate a library of over 500 variants. The plasmids encoding the heavy and light chains were co-transfected into FreeStyle™ 293F cells using polyethyleneimine (PEI) at a ratio of 1:3 (DNA:PEI). The transfected cells were cultured in FreeStyle™ 293F Expression Medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific) at 37°C with 8% CO2 for seven days. The culture supernatants were harvested and filtered through a 0.22 μm filter, and the antibodies were purified using Protein A affinity chromatography (MabSelect™ SuRe, Cytiva). The purified antibodies were dialyzed against phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and concentrated to 1 mg/mL using ultrafiltration devices with a 10 kDa molecular weight cutoff (Amicon Ultra, Millipore). The purity of the antibodies was assessed by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) under reducing and non-reducing conditions, and all variants were found to be >95% pure. The concentration of each antibody was determined by absorbance at 280 nm using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and the extinction coefficient was calculated based on the amino acid composition. The binding affinity of each variant for human FcγRIIaR131, FcγRIIaH131, and FcγRIIb was evaluated using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) on a Biacore T200 instrument (Cytiva). The antibodies were captured on a CM5 sensor chip coated with protein A, and the FcγRs were injected at concentrations ranging from 1 nM to 1000 nM. The binding responses were normalized to the captured amount of antibody, and the dissociation constants (KD) were calculated using a 1:1 Langmuir binding model with Biacore Evaluation Software. The results demonstrated that the P238D substitution alone increased binding to FcγRIIb by approximately 15-fold while reducing binding to FcγRIIaR131 and FcγRIIaH131 by more than 90%. The combination of P238D with additional substitutions at positions 233, 237, 268, 271, and 330 further enhanced binding to FcγRIIb, with the V12 variant (E233D/G237D/P238D/H268D/P271G/A330R) achieving a 217-fold increase in binding affinity relative to wild-type IgG1, while maintaining binding to FcγRIIaR131 and FcγRIIaH131 at levels indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1. These results demonstrate that the V12 variant achieves unprecedented selectivity for FcγRIIb over all other FcγRs.

### Example 2

The invention is exemplified by the determination of the crystal structure of the complex between the Fc fragment of the V12 variant and human FcγRIIb. The Fc fragment corresponding to residues 216 to the C-terminus of the human IgG1 heavy chain was cloned into a pET-28a vector with a C-terminal hexahistidine tag. The P238D substitution was introduced by site-directed mutagenesis, and the V12 variant was generated by combining the P238D substitution with the additional substitutions E233D, G237D, H268D, P271G, and A330R. The Fc fragments were expressed in Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) cells and purified using nickel affinity chromatography followed by size exclusion chromatography. The extracellular domain of human FcγRIIb was expressed in FreeStyle™ 293F cells in the presence of the mannosidase I inhibitor kifunensine to produce homogeneous, high-mannose glycans. The FcγRIIb was purified by ion exchange chromatography and then deglycosylated using endoglycosidase F1 fused with glutathione S-transferase to remove heterogeneity in the glycan structure. The complex between the Fc(V12) fragment and the deglycosylated FcγRIIb was formed by mixing equimolar amounts of each protein and purified by size exclusion chromatography. The complex was concentrated to 10 mg/mL and crystallized using the hanging drop vapor diffusion method at 20°C. The crystallization solution contained 0.1 M Bis-Tris pH 6.5, 0.2 M potassium phosphate dibasic, and 19% PEG 3350. Crystals were cryoprotected with 20% ethylene glycol and flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen. X-ray diffraction data were collected at the SPring-8 beamline BL-32XU to a resolution of 2.86 Å. The data were processed using XDS and scaled using Scala. The structure was solved by molecular replacement using the Fc(P238D)/FcγRIIb structure (PDB ID: 6XYZ) as a search model. The model was built using Coot and refined using REFMAC5. The final model contained one Fc(V12)/FcγRIIb complex per asymmetric unit, with excellent geometry and low R-factors (Rwork = 0.21, Rfree = 0.26). The electron density maps clearly showed the side chains of all six substitutions in the Fc(V12) variant and their interactions with FcγRIIb. The structure revealed that the P238D substitution induced a conformational rearrangement in the CH2 domain that exposed novel interaction surfaces compatible with FcγRIIb but incompatible with FcγRIIa. The Asp238 residue formed a hydrogen bond with the main chain of Gly237, which in turn formed a hydrogen bond with Tyr160 of FcγRIIb. The Asp237 residue made van der Waals contacts with Trp87 and Tyr160 of FcγRIIb, and the distance between the main chain NH of Gly237 and the OH of Tyr160 was reduced from 3.1 Å in the P238D variant to 2.9 Å in the V12 variant, indicating a stronger interaction. The Asp270 residue formed a salt bridge with Arg131 of FcγRIIb, and the P271G substitution allowed for greater conformational flexibility in the loop region, enabling optimal positioning of Asp270. The H268D substitution formed an electrostatic interaction with Arg292 in the CH2 domain, which was repositioned due to the conformational change. The A330R substitution was positioned near Glu86 of FcγRIIb, suggesting a potential weak electrostatic interaction. The structure confirmed that none of the substitutions in the V12 variant introduced new interactions with residues that are conserved in FcγRIIa, thereby explaining the selectivity of the variant for FcγRIIb.

### Example 3

The invention is exemplified by the assessment of the thermal stability of the V12 variant and its comparison with wild-type IgG1. The melting temperature (Tm) of the CH2 domain was measured using a thermal shift assay with SYPRO Orange dye. The purified IgG1 and V12 variant antibodies were diluted to 0.1 mg/mL in a buffer containing 20 mM histidine, 150 mM NaCl, pH 6.0. SYPRO Orange dye was added to a final dilution of 1:500, and the fluorescence was monitored using a Rotor-Gene Q real-time PCR instrument (QIAGEN). The temperature was increased from 30°C to 99°C at a heating rate of 4°C/min, and the fluorescence signal was recorded at each temperature point. The Tm was determined as the temperature at which the fluorescence signal reached its maximum value. The Tm of the IgG1 antibody was 70°C, while the Tm of the V12 variant was 62°C, representing an 8°C decrease. This reduction in Tm is consistent with the structural rearrangements induced by the P238D substitution, which disrupts the hydrophobic core of the CH2 domain. However, the magnitude of the decrease is within the range observed for other clinically approved Fc-engineered antibodies, such as those containing the S267E/L328F substitutions, which exhibit Tm values of approximately 60–65°C. The reduction in Tm did not correlate with a loss of function, as the V12 variant retained full binding affinity for FcγRIIb and exhibited no aggregation in solution. The invention further encompasses the use of the V12 variant in pharmaceutical formulations that include stabilizing agents such as sucrose, trehalose, or arginine, which can mitigate the effects of reduced thermal stability. The invention also encompasses the use of the V12 variant in lyophilized formulations, which further enhance stability during long-term storage. The results demonstrate that the V12 variant, despite its reduced Tm, remains structurally and functionally stable under conditions relevant to pharmaceutical development.

### Example 4

The invention is exemplified by the assessment of the stability of the V12 variant at high concentration. The purified IgG1 and V12 variant antibodies were concentrated to 100 mg/mL in a histidine buffer (20 mM histidine, 150 mM NaCl, pH 6.0) using ultrafiltration devices with a 10 kDa molecular weight cutoff. The concentrated solutions were stored at 25°C for four weeks, and samples were taken at time zero and after four weeks for analysis. High-molecular-weight (HMW) species were analyzed by size exclusion high-performance liquid chromatography (SE-HPLC) using a TSK-GEL G3000SWXL column (TOSOH) with a mobile phase consisting of 300 mM NaCl in 50 mM phosphate buffer, pH 7.0. The percentage of HMW species was calculated as the area under the peak corresponding to aggregates divided by the total protein peak area. At time zero, the HMW content of the IgG1 sample was 0.3%, and the HMW content of the V12 variant was 0.3%. After four weeks of storage at 25°C, the HMW content of the IgG1 sample increased to 0.3%, and the HMW content of the V12 variant increased to 0.3%. These results demonstrate that the V12 variant exhibits no increase in aggregation compared to wild-type IgG1, even at a concentration of 100 mg/mL, which is significantly higher than the typical concentration used for subcutaneous administration. The stability of the V12 variant is attributed to the preservation of key structural elements in the Fc region, despite the conformational rearrangements induced by the P238D substitution. The invention further encompasses the use of the V12 variant in formulations containing stabilizing agents such as sucrose, trehalose, or arginine, which may further reduce aggregation. The results demonstrate that the V12 variant is suitable for development as a high-concentration subcutaneous formulation, which is a significant advantage over prior art variants that exhibit increased aggregation at high concentrations.

### Example 5

The invention is exemplified by the measurement of the binding affinity of the V12 variant for human FcRn at acidic pH. The soluble human FcRn (hFcRn) was expressed in HEK293 cells and purified using nickel affinity chromatography and size exclusion chromatography. The binding affinity of the IgG1 and V12 variant antibodies for hFcRn was measured using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) on a Biacore T200 instrument. The antibodies were captured on a CM5 sensor chip coated with protein A, and hFcRn was injected at concentrations ranging from 1 nM to 1000 nM at pH 6.0. The binding responses were normalized to the captured amount of antibody, and the dissociation constants (KD) were calculated using a 1:1 Langmuir binding model. The KD for IgG1 binding to hFcRn at pH 6.0 was 1.5 ± 0.01 μM, and the KD for the V12 variant binding to hFcRn at pH 6.0 was 1.4 ± 0.01 μM. These results demonstrate that the V12 variant retains binding affinity for hFcRn at levels indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1, indicating that the conformational rearrangements induced by the P238D substitution do not interfere with the FcRn binding interface. The invention further encompasses the use of the V12 variant in therapeutic applications where prolonged serum half-life is required, as the preservation of FcRn binding ensures that the antibody will be recycled and not degraded, thereby maintaining therapeutic concentrations in vivo. The results demonstrate that the V12 variant is suitable for development as a long-acting therapeutic antibody, with pharmacokinetic properties comparable to those of wild-type IgG1.

### Example 6

The invention is exemplified by the assessment of platelet activation and aggregation induced by immune complexes formed with the V12 variant. Fresh whole blood was collected from healthy volunteers with known FcγRIIa genotypes (R/R131 and H/H131) and anticoagulated with 3.8% sodium citrate. Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) was prepared by centrifugation at 200 g for 15 minutes, and washed platelets were resuspended in modified Tyrode buffer containing apyrase. The platelets were incubated with preformed immune complexes consisting of human IgE and anti-IgE antibodies containing either the wild-type IgG1 Fc, the S267E/L328F Fc variant, or the V12 Fc variant, at a molar ratio of 1:1. After five minutes of incubation, 30 μM ADP was added to induce platelet activation, and platelet aggregation was measured using an aggregometer (MCM Hema Tracer 712) at 37°C with stirring at 1000 rpm. The expression of CD62p and PAC-1 on the platelet surface was also measured by flow cytometry. In platelets from donors homozygous for the FcγRIIaR131 genotype, the immune complexes formed with the S267E/L328F variant induced strong upregulation of CD62p and PAC-1 and caused rapid and sustained platelet aggregation. In contrast, the immune complexes formed with the V12 variant induced no significant upregulation of CD62p or PAC-1 and caused no platelet aggregation. In platelets from donors homozygous for the FcγRIIaH131 genotype, the S267E/L328F variant induced only mild upregulation of CD62p and PAC-1 and no aggregation, while the V12 variant induced no detectable activation or aggregation. These results demonstrate that the V12 variant does not induce platelet activation or aggregation, even in the presence of high-affinity FcγRIIaR131, whereas the S267E/L328F variant does. The invention further encompasses the use of the V12 variant in therapeutic applications where platelet activation is a safety concern, such as in patients with autoimmune diseases or cancer, where the risk of thrombosis must be minimized. The results demonstrate that the V12 variant provides a significant safety advantage over prior art variants.

### Example 7

The invention is exemplified by the assessment of the agonistic activity of an anti-CD137 antibody containing the V12 Fc variant. The anti-CD137 antibody (clone 1D8) was engineered to contain the wild-type IgG1 Fc, the S267E/L328F Fc variant, or the V12 Fc variant. Mouse T lymphoma cells (CTLL-2) expressing mouse CD137 and human B lymphoma cells (Raji) expressing human FcγRIIb were co-cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 10 ng/mL mouse IL-2, 10 ng/mL phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate, and 0.5 μg/mL ionomycin. The cells were treated with 3 μg/mL of each antibody for 24 hours, and the concentration of mouse interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) in the culture supernatant was measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The results showed that the anti-CD137 antibody containing the V12 Fc variant induced a 5.2-fold increase in IFN-γ production compared to the wild-type IgG1 antibody, while the antibody containing the S267E/L328F Fc variant induced a 5.5-fold increase. These results demonstrate that the V12 variant enhances agonistic activity to a level comparable to that of the S267E/L328F variant, but without the associated risk of platelet activation. The invention further encompasses the use of the V12 variant in the development of agonistic antibodies for cancer immunotherapy, where enhanced T-cell activation is desired without triggering systemic inflammation or thrombosis. The results demonstrate that the V12 variant provides a superior therapeutic index compared to prior art variants, enabling safe and effective enhancement of agonistic activity.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The invention has significant industrial applicability in the field of therapeutic antibody development, particularly in the areas of oncology, autoimmune disease, allergy, and immune-mediated inflammatory disorders. The novel Fc variants described herein, particularly the V12 variant, provide a solution to a long-standing problem in the field: the inability to enhance binding to the inhibitory FcγRIIb receptor without concomitantly increasing binding to the activating FcγRIIaR131 receptor, which leads to platelet activation, thrombosis, and accelerated clearance. The V12 variant overcomes this limitation by achieving over 200-fold enhancement of FcγRIIb binding while maintaining binding to FcγRIIaR131 and FcγRIIaH131 at wild-type levels, thereby enabling the safe and effective development of next-generation therapeutic antibodies. The invention is applicable to a wide range of antibody formats, including full-length IgG, bispecific antibodies, Fc fusion proteins, and antibody fragments, and can be incorporated into existing manufacturing platforms using standard recombinant expression and purification techniques. The V12 variant retains the pharmacokinetic properties of wild-type IgG1, including FcRn binding and serum half-life, and exhibits excellent stability at high concentrations, making it suitable for subcutaneous and intravenous formulations. The invention is particularly valuable for the development of agonistic antibodies targeting the TNFR superfamily, such as CD40, DR5, and CD137, where enhanced FcγRIIb binding is required for receptor clustering and downstream signaling, but where prior art variants have been limited by safety concerns. The invention is also valuable for the development of antibodies for the treatment of autoimmune diseases, such as systemic lupus erythematosus, where suppression of B-cell activation through FcγRIIb co-engagement is desired, and for the treatment of allergic disorders, such as asthma and atopic dermatitis, where inhibition of mast cell degranulation through FcγRIIb engagement is beneficial. The invention further enables the development of therapies that are effective across diverse patient populations, regardless of FcγRIIa genotype, thereby eliminating the need for pre-treatment genotyping and improving accessibility. The industrial applicability of the invention is further enhanced by its compatibility with existing regulatory frameworks for monoclonal antibody therapeutics, as the Fc variants are based on the human IgG1 scaffold and do not introduce any novel or non-human sequences. The invention therefore represents a commercially viable and clinically transformative advancement in antibody engineering, with broad applicability across multiple therapeutic areas and significant potential for market adoption.
