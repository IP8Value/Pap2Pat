# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic antibodies and, more specifically, to engineered immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) Fc domains that exhibit substantially diminished or completely abrogated effector functions while preserving critical drug-like properties essential for clinical utility. The invention encompasses novel amino acid substitutions within the Fc region of IgG1 antibodies that eliminate binding to activating Fc gamma receptors (FcγRs) and complement component C1q, without compromising binding to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) or Protein A, and without inducing significant destabilization of the Fc structure under physiological or stress conditions. These engineered Fc domains are particularly useful in the context of bispecific antibodies, T cell-engaging therapeutics, and other applications where unintended immune activation through Fc-mediated effector mechanisms is undesirable. The invention further provides methods for the production, purification, and characterization of such antibodies, as well as pharmaceutical compositions comprising them for the treatment of cancer, autoimmune disorders, and other diseases where modulation of immune cell activity is therapeutically beneficial.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Therapeutic monoclonal antibodies have become a cornerstone of modern medicine, with over 100 approved agents targeting a wide array of disease states, including oncology, autoimmune disorders, and infectious diseases. The majority of these agents are based on the IgG1 subclass due to its favorable pharmacokinetic profile, high antigen-binding affinity, and potent effector functions, including antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). These effector functions are mediated through interactions between the constant fragment (Fc) of the antibody and various immune receptors and proteins, including activating Fc gamma receptors (FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa), the complement C1q protein, and the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), which governs serum half-life. While these properties are advantageous in certain therapeutic contexts—such as the elimination of tumor cells—they are often detrimental in others, particularly when the antibody is designed to engage T cells or modulate immune signaling without triggering inflammatory cascades.

In T cell-engaging bispecific antibodies, for example, the primary mechanism of action relies on the simultaneous binding of a tumor-associated antigen and the CD3 complex on T cells, thereby inducing T cell activation and target cell lysis. In such cases, Fc-mediated effector functions are not only unnecessary but can lead to excessive cytokine release, off-target cytotoxicity, systemic inflammation, and dose-limiting toxicities. Consequently, there has been a longstanding and well-documented need in the art to develop IgG1-based antibodies with silenced effector functions—often referred to as “Fc-silenced” or “effector-null” variants—while retaining the structural integrity, stability, and pharmacokinetic advantages inherent to the IgG1 scaffold.

Several strategies have been explored to achieve this goal. One approach involves switching the isotype from IgG1 to IgG2 or IgG4, which naturally exhibit lower affinity for FcγRs and C1q. However, this strategy is fraught with drawbacks. IgG2 and IgG4 are more prone to aggregation under acidic conditions, have reduced thermal stability, and may exhibit increased structural flexibility or half-antibody formation, all of which compromise manufacturability, storage stability, and clinical safety. Moreover, IgG2 and IgG4 do not bind FcRn with the same efficiency as IgG1, potentially leading to shorter serum half-lives and increased dosing frequency. Another approach involves genetic modification of the Fc region to disrupt key interactions with effector molecules while preserving interactions with FcRn and Protein A. Early efforts focused on mutations in the lower hinge region, such as L234A/L235A (LALA) and L234A/L235A/G237A (LALAGA), which significantly reduced but did not fully eliminate FcγR binding. Subsequent studies introduced additional mutations in the CH2 domain, including P329G, D265A, and P331S, often in combination with hinge mutations, to achieve more complete silencing.

Despite these advances, a critical gap remains in the art. Many of the existing Fc-silencing mutations, while effective at reducing effector function, concurrently destabilize the Fc domain, leading to increased susceptibility to acid-induced aggregation—a major concern during downstream processing, particularly during viral inactivation steps that require low pH hold conditions. This instability manifests as decreased melting temperature (Tm) of the CH2 domain, elevated self-interaction propensity, and increased aggregate formation under stress conditions, all of which negatively impact yield, purity, shelf life, and safety. Furthermore, some mutations, such as D265A and P331S, have been shown to impair biophysical properties despite achieving effector silencing, rendering them unsuitable for clinical development. There is therefore a pressing need in the field for an IgG1 Fc variant that simultaneously achieves complete abrogation of FcγR and C1q binding, maintains robust binding to FcRn and Protein A, and exhibits biophysical stability indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1 under both physiological and process-relevant stress conditions.

The prior art has not adequately addressed this multifaceted challenge. While certain combinations, such as LALA-P329G, have gained widespread clinical adoption, their biophysical profile has not been systematically compared against other potential candidates under identical experimental conditions. Moreover, the impact of mutations such as P329A, S267K, and LALE in combination with CH2 substitutions has been either underexplored or incompletely characterized. The present invention fills this critical gap by identifying and validating novel Fc variants that achieve complete effector silencing without compromising stability, manufacturability, or pharmacokinetic performance. These variants represent a significant advancement over existing technologies, offering a superior platform for the development of next-generation therapeutic antibodies with enhanced safety profiles and improved developability.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of engineered immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) Fc domains that exhibit complete abrogation of binding to activating Fc gamma receptors (FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa) and complement component C1q, while retaining full binding affinity for the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) and Protein A, and maintaining biophysical properties equivalent to those of wild-type IgG1. The invention is based on the discovery that specific combinations of mutations in the lower hinge and CH2 regions of the IgG1 Fc domain—particularly those involving the substitution of proline at position 329 with glycine or alanine, in combination with either L234A/L235A (LALA) or L234A/L235E (LALE) hinge mutations, or the substitution of serine at position 267 with lysine in combination with LALA or LALE—result in a synergistic silencing of effector functions without inducing destabilization of the Fc structure. These variants demonstrate no detectable binding to FcγRs under highly avid conditions, including those that mimic immune complex formation, and show no measurable interaction with C1q in sensitive, bead-based assays. Crucially, these mutations do not impair FcRn binding, ensuring normal serum half-life, nor do they interfere with Protein A affinity, preserving the efficiency of downstream purification processes.

The invention further encompasses the identification of a distinct biophysical signature associated with these novel Fc variants: a CH2 domain melting temperature (Tm) that is statistically indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1, minimal self-interaction as measured by affinity-capture self-interaction nanoparticle spectroscopy (AC-SINS), unchanged hydrophobicity profile as assessed by hydrophobic interaction chromatography (HIC), and no increase in aggregate formation under acidic stress conditions (pH 3.5), as determined by size exclusion chromatography. In contrast, other previously described silencing mutations, such as D265A and P331S, when combined with the same hinge modifications, result in significant reductions in CH2 Tm and marked increases in acid-induced aggregation, rendering them unsuitable for clinical development. The invention thus provides a clear and reproducible method for distinguishing between Fc variants that achieve functional silencing with acceptable developability and those that do not.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more of the novel Fc variants described herein, formulated for parenteral administration, optionally in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or stabilizer. The Fc variants may be incorporated into monospecific or bispecific antibodies, including T cell-engaging formats, where the absence of effector function is essential for safety and efficacy. The invention further provides methods for the production of such antibodies, including transient or stable expression in mammalian cell systems, purification via Protein A affinity chromatography, and formulation under conditions that maintain structural integrity. The invention also encompasses diagnostic and analytical methods for confirming the presence of the novel Fc variants, including biolayer interferometry, surface plasmon resonance, differential scanning fluorimetry, and size exclusion chromatography under acidic stress conditions.

The invention is based on the unexpected finding that the combination of L234A/L235A or L234A/L235E with P329G, P329A, or S267K results in a complete and consistent elimination of FcγR and C1q binding across multiple assay formats, including cell-based, bead-based, and label-free kinetic platforms, while preserving all other critical properties of the IgG1 scaffold. This combination of functional silencing and structural stability is not predicted by prior art and represents a significant departure from conventional wisdom, which has long assumed that complete effector silencing necessarily requires destabilizing mutations or glycan removal. The invention thus provides a robust, scalable, and clinically viable solution to a long-standing problem in therapeutic antibody engineering.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive and novel class of engineered immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) Fc domains that are specifically designed to eliminate all clinically relevant effector functions while preserving the structural, biophysical, and pharmacokinetic properties essential for therapeutic utility. The invention is grounded in the systematic evaluation of a broad panel of Fc mutations, including substitutions in the lower hinge and CH2 domains, and the identification of specific combinations that achieve complete silencing of Fc gamma receptor (FcγR) and complement component C1q binding without inducing destabilization of the Fc structure. The Fc domain of IgG1, which spans from approximately residue 216 to 447 using the EU numbering system, contains multiple regions critical for interactions with immune effector molecules. The lower hinge region, encompassing residues 230–238, is the primary site of contact for FcγRs, while residues in the CH2 domain, particularly around the N297 glycosylation site and the CH2-CH3 interface, mediate interactions with C1q, FcRn, and Protein A. The present invention identifies mutations that selectively disrupt the former while leaving the latter intact.

The core innovation of the invention lies in the discovery that the combination of L234A/L235A (LALA) or L234A/L235E (LALE) hinge mutations with either P329G, P329A, or S267K substitutions in the CH2 domain results in a complete and reproducible abrogation of binding to all activating FcγRs—FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIa (CD32a), and FcγRIIIa (CD16a)—as measured by multiple orthogonal assay formats, including biolayer interferometry, surface plasmon resonance, and highly sensitive cell-based binding assays under conditions of immune complex formation. These mutations are not merely additive; they act synergistically to eliminate residual binding that persists even in the presence of LALA or LALE alone. For example, while LALA or LALE alone reduce FcγR binding by approximately one order of magnitude, they still retain detectable binding under conditions of high antigen density, such as when the antibody is pre-complexed with a multivalent antigen conjugate. In contrast, the inclusion of P329G, P329A, or S267K completely abolishes this residual binding, rendering the Fc domain functionally inert with respect to FcγR engagement. This level of silencing is critical for therapeutic applications in which even low-level FcγR activation could lead to cytokine release syndrome, off-target cytotoxicity, or immune cell exhaustion.

Similarly, the invention demonstrates that these same combinations—LALA-P329G, LALA-P329A, LALE-P329G, LALE-P329A, LALA-S267K, and LALE-S267K—completely eliminate binding to C1q, the initiating component of the classical complement cascade. This was demonstrated using a highly sensitive bead-based assay in which the antibody was immobilized via a biotinylated antigen peptide on streptavidin-coated beads, followed by incubation with purified human C1q and complement component C3. Wild-type IgG1 showed robust C1q and C3 deposition, whereas all variants containing the novel combinations exhibited no detectable signal above background, even at concentrations exceeding 100 nM. This is in stark contrast to other commonly proposed silencing mutations, such as D265A and P331S, which, despite reducing C1q binding in some assays, still retain measurable interaction under more sensitive conditions. The invention thus provides a definitive solution to the problem of complement-mediated toxicity, which is particularly relevant in the context of antibodies targeting hematological malignancies or tissues with high complement expression.

Importantly, the invention establishes that these novel Fc variants do not compromise the binding of the Fc domain to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), which is essential for the long serum half-life characteristic of IgG1 antibodies. Binding to FcRn was assessed using surface plasmon resonance under both neutral and acidic pH conditions, and all variants exhibited association and dissociation kinetics indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1. This confirms that the mutations introduced do not alter the structural interface between the CH2 and CH3 domains, which is the primary site of FcRn interaction. Furthermore, binding to Protein A, a critical determinant for efficient purification via affinity chromatography, was preserved in all variants, as confirmed by biolayer interferometry and affinity capture assays. This ensures that the manufacturing process for these antibodies remains fully compatible with existing industrial workflows, eliminating the need for costly or complex purification alternatives.

The most significant and unexpected aspect of the invention is the preservation of biophysical stability in these Fc variants. Prior art has long assumed that complete effector silencing necessitates structural compromise, particularly in the CH2 domain, which is inherently less stable than other regions of the Fc. This assumption has led to the widespread use of mutations such as D265A and P331S, which, while effective at silencing effector functions, result in a substantial reduction in the melting temperature (Tm) of the CH2 domain, as measured by differential scanning fluorimetry. In wild-type IgG1, the CH2 Tm typically ranges from 67 to 68°C under standard assay conditions. In contrast, D265A and P331S variants exhibit Tm values as low as 60.5°C, indicating a significant loss of structural integrity. This destabilization directly correlates with increased susceptibility to acid-induced aggregation, a major concern during viral inactivation steps that require prolonged exposure to low pH (e.g., pH 3.5). The present invention demonstrates that the novel Fc variants described herein—LALA-P329G, LALA-P329A, LALE-P329G, LALE-P329A, LALA-S267K, and LALE-S267K—all maintain a CH2 Tm within 1.0°C of wild-type IgG1, with no statistically significant difference observed across multiple independent experiments. Furthermore, when subjected to pH 3.5 stress for one hour at room temperature, these variants exhibit aggregate levels that are indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1, as quantified by size exclusion chromatography. In contrast, D265A and P331S variants show aggregate increases of up to 11.3% under identical conditions, rendering them unsuitable for clinical development.

The invention further demonstrates that these novel Fc variants do not introduce new liabilities in terms of polyspecificity, hydrophobicity, or self-interaction. Polyspecificity reagent binding, which assesses non-specific interactions with cellular membrane and cytosolic proteins, was unchanged across all variants, with normalized binding scores falling within the range of wild-type IgG1. Hydrophobic interaction chromatography revealed no shift in retention time, indicating that the surface hydrophobicity of the Fc domain remains unaltered. Affinity-capture self-interaction nanoparticle spectroscopy (AC-SINS), a highly sensitive method for detecting weak self-interactions that can lead to viscosity issues and aggregation, showed no increase in maximal wavelength shift for any of the novel variants, whereas certain combinations, such as LALA-D270A and LALA-L309V, exhibited significant increases in self-interaction. This confirms that the novel mutations do not induce conformational changes or expose cryptic hydrophobic patches that could compromise developability.

The invention also encompasses the use of these Fc variants in a wide array of therapeutic formats, including monospecific antibodies, bispecific antibodies, T cell-engaging bispecifics, dual-variable domain immunoglobulins, and antibody-drug conjugates. In each case, the presence of the novel Fc mutations ensures that the antibody’s mechanism of action is driven solely by target binding and effector cell engagement (in the case of bispecifics), without the confounding influence of Fc-mediated immune activation. This is particularly advantageous in the context of T cell-engaging bispecifics, where FcγR binding on macrophages or dendritic cells can lead to premature clearance of the therapeutic, cytokine storm, or sequestration in non-target tissues. The invention further provides a method for selecting and validating Fc variants based on a defined set of criteria: complete abrogation of FcγR and C1q binding under highly avid conditions, retention of FcRn and Protein A binding, and maintenance of CH2 Tm and acid stability equivalent to wild-type IgG1. This selection criteria provides a clear, objective, and reproducible framework for the development of next-generation therapeutic antibodies with superior safety and manufacturability profiles.

The invention further includes methods for the production of these antibodies, including transient transfection of mammalian cells such as HEK293 or CHO cells, stable cell line generation, and purification using Protein A affinity chromatography followed by ion exchange and size exclusion chromatography. The invention further provides formulations comprising these antibodies in buffered solutions at physiological pH, optionally containing stabilizers such as sucrose, histidine, or polysorbate 80, and packaged in single-use vials or pre-filled syringes for subcutaneous or intravenous administration. The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits for the detection and quantification of these Fc variants in biological samples, comprising capture antibodies specific for the novel mutation combinations, detection reagents, and reference standards for quantitative analysis.

In summary, the invention provides a transformative advancement in the field of therapeutic antibody engineering by delivering Fc variants that achieve complete effector silencing without sacrificing stability, developability, or pharmacokinetic performance. This represents a paradigm shift from the prevailing assumption that functional silencing requires structural compromise and establishes a new gold standard for the design of next-generation biologics.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Antibodies

The antibodies utilized in the present invention were generated as IgG1 monoclonal antibodies targeting the CD3ε subunit of the T cell receptor complex, using the anti-CD3ε antibody ADI-26906 as the parental scaffold. The variable regions of ADI-26906 were cloned into an IgG1 expression vector containing the human IgG1 constant region with the EU numbering system, and a series of Fc mutations were introduced via site-directed mutagenesis to generate a panel of variants. The mutations included single and double substitutions in the lower hinge region (L234A, L235A, L235E, L235K, G237A) and single substitutions in the CH2 domain (D265A, S267K, H268Q, D270A, Y296F, L309A, L309V, K322A, A327G, L328A, P329A, P329G, A330S, P331S, A330S/P331S), as well as combinations thereof. All constructs were expressed in HEK293 cells via transient transfection using polyethyleneimine as the transfection reagent. Culture supernatants were harvested 7 days post-transfection and clarified by centrifugation and filtration. The antibodies were purified using Protein A affinity chromatography, followed by buffer exchange into phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at pH 7.4 and concentration to 10 mg/mL. Purity was confirmed by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) under reducing and non-reducing conditions, which showed single bands at approximately 150 kDa and 50 kDa, respectively, with no evidence of fragmentation or aggregation. Mass spectrometry analysis confirmed the expected molecular weight of each variant, with no detectable post-translational modifications beyond the conserved N297 glycosylation. All variants were stored at 4°C in PBS and used within two weeks of purification to ensure structural integrity. The identity and integrity of each variant were further confirmed by peptide mapping and LC-MS/MS analysis, which verified the presence of the intended mutations and the absence of unintended modifications. This panel of antibodies served as the foundation for all subsequent functional and biophysical assessments.

### Example 2: THP-1 Cell Binding and C1q Binding to Antibodies

To evaluate the binding of the engineered IgG1 Fc variants to activating Fc gamma receptors expressed on human immune cells, a cell-based binding assay was performed using the human monocytic leukemia cell line THP-1, which constitutively expresses FcγRI and FcγRII but not FcγRIII. Cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and maintained at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere. For standard binding assays, 100 nM of each antibody variant was incubated with 2 × 10⁵ THP-1 cells in PBS containing 0.1% bovine serum albumin (PBSF) for 30 minutes on ice. Following two washes with PBSF, bound antibodies were detected using a goat anti-human IgG secondary antibody conjugated to R-phycoerythrin (R-PE) at a 1:200 dilution, followed by a 20-minute incubation on ice. Cells were washed twice and analyzed by flow cytometry using a BD FACSCanto II instrument. Median fluorescence intensity (MFI) was recorded and normalized to wild-type IgG1. All lower hinge variants (LALA, LALE, LALAGA) reduced binding by approximately 10-fold compared to wild-type IgG1, but residual binding remained detectable. In contrast, variants containing the additional CH2 mutations P329G, P329A, or S267K showed no detectable binding above background, even at concentrations up to 500 nM.

To assess binding under highly avid conditions, a pre-complexing strategy was employed to mimic immune complex formation. A biotinylated CD3ε N-terminal peptide (residues 21–47) was conjugated to bovine serum albumin (BSA) to generate a dodecavalent antigen complex (CD3ε-BSA). Antibody variants (100 nM) were pre-incubated with 10 nM CD3ε-BSA for 20 minutes at room temperature to allow complex formation. The complexes were then added to THP-1 cells and incubated for 30 minutes on ice. Bound complexes were detected as described above. Under these conditions, LALA and LALE variants retained significant binding, whereas all variants containing P329G, P329A, or S267K showed no detectable signal, confirming complete silencing of FcγR engagement even under conditions of high antigen density.

To assess binding to complement component C1q, a bead-based assay was developed using streptavidin-coated Dynabeads. A biotinylated CD3ε peptide was immobilized on the beads, followed by saturation binding of each antibody variant. Beads were then incubated with purified human C1q alone or in the presence of human complement serum containing C3. After washing, C1q binding was detected using an anti-human C1q antibody conjugated to allophycocyanin (APC), and C3 deposition was detected using an anti-human C3 antibody conjugated to phycoerythrin (PE). Wild-type IgG1 showed robust C1q and C3 signal, whereas all variants containing the novel combinations of LALA or LALE with P329G, P329A, or S267K showed no detectable signal above isotype control. In contrast, variants containing D265A or P331S still exhibited measurable C1q binding, albeit reduced compared to wild-type. This confirmed that the novel Fc variants achieve complete abrogation of C1q engagement, a critical requirement for preventing complement-mediated toxicity.

### Example 3: FcγR Binding by Antibodies

To quantify the binding kinetics of the engineered Fc variants to human Fc gamma receptors, biolayer interferometry (BLI) and surface plasmon resonance (SPR) were employed using a ForteBio Octet HTX and a Biacore 8K instrument, respectively. For BLI, anti-human IgG Fc capture sensors were loaded with 100 nM of each antibody variant, followed by exposure to purified human FcγRI, FcγRIIa, and FcγRIIIa at concentrations ranging from 10 nM to 1 µM. Association and dissociation phases were monitored for 180 seconds each, and data were fitted to a 1:1 binding model. Wild-type IgG1 showed high-affinity binding to all three receptors, with dissociation constants (KD) in the low nanomolar range. LALA and LALE variants showed reduced binding, with KD values increased by 10- to 100-fold, but still measurable. In contrast, variants containing P329G, P329A, or S267K showed no detectable binding to any of the three FcγRs, with association rates indistinguishable from buffer-only controls. Similar results were obtained using SPR, where antibodies were captured on a CM5 sensor chip via a goat anti-human Fc antibody and exposed to FcγRs at 100 nM (FcγRI) or 1000 nM (FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa). No binding response was observed for the novel variants, whereas LALA and LALE retained low but detectable responses. These data confirm that the combination of lower hinge and CH2 mutations eliminates FcγR binding at the molecular level, regardless of receptor subtype or assay format. Furthermore, binding to FcRn was preserved in all variants, with association and dissociation kinetics matching those of wild-type IgG1, confirming that the mutations selectively disrupt effector interactions without affecting FcRn binding.

### Example 4: Developability Assays

To evaluate the biophysical developability of the engineered Fc variants, a comprehensive panel of assays was performed, including polyspecificity reagent (PSR) binding, affinity-capture self-interaction nanoparticle spectroscopy (AC-SINS), hydrophobic interaction chromatography (HIC), size exclusion chromatography (SEC), differential scanning fluorimetry (DSF), and pH 3.5 stress SEC. PSR binding was assessed using a FACS-based assay in which soluble membrane and cytosolic proteins from CHO cells were biotinylated and incubated with the antibody variants. Binding was detected using ExtrAvidin-R-PE and normalized to three control antibodies with known low, medium, and high polyspecificity. All novel variants exhibited PSR binding scores within the range of wild-type IgG1 (0.33–0.43), with no significant deviation observed. AC-SINS measurements, which detect weak self-interactions by monitoring the wavelength shift of gold nanoparticles coated with anti-human Fc, showed no increase in maximal wavelength shift for any of the novel variants, whereas LALA-D270A and LALA-L309V exhibited shifts more than double that of wild-type, indicating elevated self-interaction propensity. HIC analysis revealed no change in retention time for any variant, confirming that surface hydrophobicity was unaffected. SEC analysis under native conditions showed monomer content exceeding 98% for all variants, with no detectable high-molecular-weight species. DSF analysis, which measures the thermal unfolding of the Fc domain, showed that the CH2 domain Tm for all novel variants was 67.2–67.8°C, indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1 (67.5°C). In contrast, D265A and P331S variants exhibited Tm values as low as 60.5°C. Finally, pH 3.5 stress SEC, in which antibodies were incubated at pH 3.5 for one hour and then analyzed by SEC, showed no increase in aggregate formation for the novel variants, with Δ% monomer change less than 1.5% relative to wild-type. In contrast, D265A variants showed increases of 9.6–11.3%, and P331S variants showed increases of 3.1–8.6%. These data collectively demonstrate that the novel Fc variants possess a biophysical profile equivalent to wild-type IgG1, making them highly suitable for clinical development.

### Example 5: ADCC Activity Assay

To determine whether the engineered Fc variants retained any residual antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) activity, a standard ADCC assay was performed using target cells expressing CD3ε and effector cells derived from human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). Target cells were labeled with calcein-AM and co-cultured with PBMCs at an effector-to-target ratio of 10:1 in the presence of serial dilutions of each antibody variant. After 4 hours of incubation, supernatants were collected and fluorescence was measured to quantify target cell lysis. Wild-type IgG1 induced robust target cell lysis, with an EC50 of approximately 0.1 nM. LALA and LALE variants showed a 10- to 100-fold reduction in activity, with EC50 values in the range of 1–10 nM. In contrast, all variants containing P329G, P329A, or S267K showed no detectable ADCC activity at concentrations up to 100 nM, with lysis levels indistinguishable from buffer-only controls. This confirms that the novel Fc variants are completely devoid of ADCC function, even at concentrations far exceeding those expected in vivo. The absence of ADCC activity was further corroborated by the lack of cytokine release (IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-6) from PBMCs upon exposure to the variants, as measured by ELISA. These results demonstrate that the novel Fc variants are functionally silent with respect to ADCC, a critical requirement for T cell-engaging therapeutics.

### Example 6: Complement Activity Assay

To assess complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) activity, a CDC assay was performed using target cells expressing CD3ε and human complement serum as the source of complement components. Target cells were labeled with calcein-AM and incubated with serial dilutions of each antibody variant in the presence of 10% human complement serum for 4 hours at 37°C. Supernatants were collected and fluorescence was measured to quantify cell lysis. Wild-type IgG1 induced strong CDC activity, with an EC50 of approximately 0.5 nM. LALA and LALE variants showed partial reduction in CDC, with EC50 values increased to 5–10 nM, consistent with partial C1q binding. In contrast, all variants containing P329G, P329A, or S267K showed no detectable CDC activity at concentrations up to 100 nM, with lysis levels indistinguishable from buffer-only controls. This was further confirmed by ELISA measurement of C3b deposition on target cells, which showed no signal for the novel variants, whereas wild-type and LALA/LALE variants showed dose-dependent increases. These results confirm that the novel Fc variants completely abrogate complement activation, eliminating the risk of complement-mediated inflammation, tissue damage, or hemolysis. The absence of CDC activity, combined with the absence of ADCC and FcγR binding, establishes these variants as truly effector-silenced, making them ideal for use in T cell-engaging and other sensitive therapeutic applications.
