# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, Huntington’s disease, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis are characterized by progressive and irreversible loss of neuronal function, leading to profound motor, cognitive, and behavioral impairments that severely diminish quality of life. These conditions are frequently accompanied by pathological overproduction of nitric oxide within the central and peripheral nervous systems, a process mediated primarily by the neuronal isoform of nitric oxide synthase, or nNOS. While nitric oxide serves essential physiological roles in neurotransmission, synaptic plasticity, and vascular regulation under normal conditions, its excessive and sustained generation in response to excitotoxic stress, ischemic insult, or chronic neuroinflammation contributes directly to oxidative damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, protein nitration, and ultimately, neuronal apoptosis. The therapeutic potential of selectively inhibiting nNOS to mitigate this pathological cascade has been widely recognized, yet the development of clinically viable inhibitors has been hindered by the structural and functional homology shared among the three mammalian nitric oxide synthase isoforms: endothelial NOS (eNOS), inducible NOS (iNOS), and neuronal NOS (nNOS). Each isoform is a homodimeric enzyme composed of two distinct domains—a reductase domain responsible for electron transfer from NADPH through FAD and FMN cofactors, and an oxygenase domain housing the heme iron, tetrahydrobiopterin (H₄B), and the substrate L-arginine—wherein the catalytic conversion of L-arginine to L-citrulline and nitric oxide occurs. The high degree of sequence and structural conservation within the active sites of these isoforms, particularly between nNOS and eNOS, has rendered traditional competitive inhibitors based on L-arginine analogs ineffective in achieving sufficient selectivity. Such compounds often exhibit poor pharmacokinetic properties due to excessive polarity, high total polar surface area, and an abundance of hydrogen bond donors, which collectively impede blood-brain barrier penetration and limit their utility in treating central nervous system disorders. Furthermore, inhibition of eNOS, which is critical for maintaining vascular tone and endothelial integrity, can lead to hypertension and cardiovascular complications, while inhibition of iNOS may compromise host immune defenses. Consequently, the design of a potent, selective, and brain-penetrant nNOS inhibitor capable of distinguishing between these closely related isoforms remains an unmet medical need. Prior efforts to exploit subtle differences in the active site architecture have focused on residues that diverge between isoforms, particularly the substitution of aspartate in nNOS with asparagine in eNOS at a position adjacent to the heme propionate group. This single amino acid difference has been shown to confer substantial selectivity in certain inhibitor classes, as the negatively charged carboxylate of aspartate can form a strong electrostatic or hydrogen-bonding interaction with cationic amine functionalities in inhibitors, whereas the neutral amide of asparagine cannot. However, previous inhibitors designed to engage this aspartate residue have often suffered from conformational flexibility in their amine-containing side chains, which reduces binding affinity and selectivity by allowing multiple orientations that may not optimally engage the target residue. Additionally, modifications to the core scaffold of these inhibitors have frequently resulted in steric clashes with conserved residues such as heme propionates or hydrophobic pockets lined by methionine and leucine residues, thereby diminishing potency or altering binding modes unpredictably. The discovery of a novel class of 7-phenyl-2-aminoquinoline derivatives that simultaneously achieve high potency against nNOS, exceptional selectivity over eNOS and iNOS, and favorable physicochemical properties for central nervous system penetration represents a significant advancement in the field. These compounds are distinguished by a meta-substituted aminomethyl phenyl ring attached to the 7-position of a 4-methylquinolin-2-amine scaffold, which positions the terminal primary amine to optimally interact with the isoform-specific aspartate residue (Asp597 in rat nNOS, Asp602 in human nNOS) located within a polar pocket near the heme propionate A and tetrahydrobiopterin. Structural studies reveal that this meta-substitution pattern restricts rotational freedom of the aminomethyl tail, locking it into a conformation that maximizes hydrogen bonding and electrostatic stabilization with Asp597/Asp602, while simultaneously avoiding unfavorable interactions with residues in eNOS and iNOS. Moreover, the introduction of specific substituents at the 4-position of the central phenyl ring further enhances binding affinity and selectivity by engaging a hydrophobic subpocket formed by Met336 in rat nNOS and Met341 in human nNOS—residues that are replaced by smaller valine and phenylalanine residues in eNOS, respectively. This dual-targeting strategy, wherein the inhibitor simultaneously exploits the aspartate-asparagine divergence and the methionine-valine divergence, results in unprecedented selectivity profiles, with some analogs exhibiting over 800-fold preference for nNOS over eNOS. Importantly, these compounds demonstrate high metabolic stability in human liver microsomes, excellent permeability across artificial blood-brain barrier models, and minimal off-target interactions with a broad panel of CNS receptors, positioning them as viable candidates for the treatment of neurodegenerative diseases and acute neurological injuries characterized by nitric oxide-mediated neurotoxicity.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a novel class of 7-phenyl-2-aminoquinoline derivatives that function as highly selective inhibitors of neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with minimal activity against endothelial and inducible isoforms of the enzyme. These compounds are characterized by a core structure comprising a 4-methylquinolin-2-amine moiety substituted at the 7-position by a phenyl ring bearing a meta-substituted aminomethyl group, wherein the terminal amine is unsubstituted and capable of forming a direct hydrogen bond or electrostatic interaction with an aspartate residue located in the active site of neuronal nitric oxide synthase. The phenyl ring is further substituted at the 4-position with a hydrophobic moiety selected from the group consisting of alkyl, cycloalkyl, alkoxy, heteroalkoxy, and arylalkoxy groups, which occupies a hydrophobic subpocket defined by methionine residues unique to the neuronal isoform. The invention encompasses specific compounds including, but not limited to, 7-(3-(aminomethyl)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(cyclopropylmethoxy)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, and 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(pyridin-3-ylmethoxy)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine trihydrochloride, each of which demonstrates nanomolar inhibitory potency against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase and selectivity ratios exceeding 500-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. The compounds are synthesized through a streamlined synthetic route involving a palladium-catalyzed Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling reaction between a 7-boronic ester or trifluoroborate derivative of 4-methylquinolin-2-amine and a suitably protected meta-substituted bromobenzene precursor, followed by sequential deprotection steps to yield the final amine-containing inhibitors as water-soluble hydrochloride salts. The inhibitors are designed to bind competitively at the L-arginine binding site of neuronal nitric oxide synthase, wherein the aminoquinoline core mimics the guanidinium group of arginine by forming bifurcated hydrogen bonds with the main chain carbonyl of tryptophan and the side chain carboxylate of glutamate residues conserved across isoforms, while the meta-aminomethyl phenyl side chain extends into a region of the active site where the presence of aspartate in neuronal isoforms, as opposed to asparagine in endothelial isoforms, enables a high-affinity interaction that is absent in non-target isoforms. The 4-position substituent further enhances binding by engaging a hydrophobic cavity formed by methionine and leucine residues that are not conserved in endothelial nitric oxide synthase, thereby contributing to isoform discrimination. These compounds exhibit favorable physicochemical properties, including low molecular weight, moderate lipophilicity, and acceptable polar surface area, which collectively enable efficient passive diffusion across the blood-brain barrier, as demonstrated by high permeability values in parallel artificial membrane assays. Furthermore, the inhibitors demonstrate high stability in human liver microsomes, indicating resistance to hepatic metabolism, and show negligible binding to a broad panel of central nervous system receptors, minimizing the risk of off-target pharmacological effects. The invention provides a method of treating neurological disorders associated with pathological nitric oxide overproduction, including neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, Huntington’s disease, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, as well as acute neurological injuries such as stroke, traumatic brain injury, cerebral palsy, and ischemic encephalopathy, by administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a compound of the invention. The compounds may be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions suitable for oral, intravenous, intranasal, or intrathecal administration, and may be co-administered with other neuroprotective agents, antioxidants, or anti-inflammatory compounds to enhance therapeutic efficacy. The invention further encompasses the use of these compounds in the preparation of medicaments for the treatment of conditions characterized by excessive neuronal nitric oxide production, and the use of these compounds as research tools for the selective modulation of neuronal nitric oxide synthase activity in vitro and in vivo.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention provides a novel class of compounds that act as potent, selective, and brain-penetrant inhibitors of neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with applications in the treatment of neurological disorders driven by pathological nitric oxide signaling. These compounds are defined by a distinct chemical architecture that enables them to exploit subtle but critical differences in the active site topology of neuronal nitric oxide synthase relative to its closely related isoforms, endothelial and inducible nitric oxide synthase. The core structure of the invention comprises a 4-methylquinolin-2-amine scaffold, substituted at the 7-position by a phenyl ring that bears two key functional groups: a meta-substituted primary aminomethyl group and a 4-position substituent selected from a defined set of hydrophobic moieties. The 4-methylquinolin-2-amine moiety serves as a bioisostere of the guanidinium group of L-arginine, the natural substrate of nitric oxide synthase, and is positioned to form a conserved network of hydrogen bonds with residues in the oxygenase domain of the enzyme. Specifically, the exocyclic amino group of the quinoline ring forms a bifurcated hydrogen bond with the backbone carbonyl oxygen of tryptophan 587 in rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and tryptophan 592 in human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, while the nitrogen atom of the quinoline ring accepts a hydrogen bond from the side chain carboxylate of glutamate 592 in rat and glutamate 602 in human neuronal nitric oxide synthase. These interactions are conserved across all isoforms and are essential for anchoring the inhibitor within the substrate-binding pocket. The phenyl ring attached at the 7-position of the quinoline extends into the space between the heme propionates A and D, where its orientation is constrained by the surrounding protein environment. The meta-substituted aminomethyl group, positioned at the 3-position of this phenyl ring, is oriented such that its terminal primary amine points toward a polar pocket formed by the carboxylate of heme propionate A, the side chains of glutamine 478 in rat and glutamine 483 in human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, arginine 481 in rat and arginine 486 in human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, and, most critically, aspartate 597 in rat and aspartate 602 in human neuronal nitric oxide synthase. This aspartate residue is replaced by asparagine in both endothelial and inducible nitric oxide synthase isoforms, and the presence of the negatively charged carboxylate in neuronal isoforms enables a strong electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding interaction with the protonated amine of the inhibitor. The meta-substitution pattern is essential to this interaction, as ortho- and para-substituted analogs exhibit significantly reduced potency and selectivity due to suboptimal geometry and increased conformational flexibility, which prevent the amine from achieving the precise orientation required for maximal interaction with aspartate. In contrast, the meta-substituted derivatives lock the amine into a fixed orientation that aligns perfectly with the aspartate side chain, resulting in a substantial increase in binding affinity and isoform discrimination. The primary amine is not substituted with alkyl or aryl groups, as methylation or other modifications eliminate the ability to form a hydrogen bond with aspartate and lead to a marked loss of potency, indicating that the free amine is necessary for optimal activity. The 4-position substituent on the phenyl ring, which may be selected from alkyl, cycloalkyl, alkoxy, heteroalkoxy, or arylalkoxy groups, occupies a hydrophobic subpocket defined by methionine 336 in rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and methionine 341 in human neuronal nitric oxide synthase. This residue is replaced by valine in endothelial nitric oxide synthase and by phenylalanine in inducible nitric oxide synthase, and the larger methionine side chain creates a more spacious and hydrophobic cavity that can accommodate bulkier substituents. Substituents such as cyclopropylmethoxy, ethoxy, isopropoxy, and pyridinylmethoxy are particularly effective, as they provide favorable van der Waals contacts with methionine and leucine residues lining the pocket, while avoiding steric clashes that would occur in the smaller valine-containing cavity of endothelial nitric oxide synthase. The cyclopropylmethoxy substituent, for example, fits snugly into a cavity formed by methionine 336, leucine 337, and tyrosine 706 in rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase, resulting in a compound with sub-40 nanomolar inhibitory potency. The pyridinylmethoxy substituent, particularly the 3-pyridyl isomer, enhances binding through a dual mechanism: its hydrophobic character engages the methionine-rich pocket, while its nitrogen atom forms a hydrogen bond with a structured water molecule near the heme propionate A, which in turn stabilizes the inhibitor-enzyme complex. This water-mediated interaction is not observed in endothelial nitric oxide synthase, where the altered geometry of the pocket and the absence of the methionine residue prevent the formation of a comparable network. The combination of the meta-aminomethyl group and the 4-position hydrophobic substituent results in a synergistic effect on binding affinity and selectivity, as each moiety engages a distinct isoform-specific feature of the active site. The meta-aminomethyl group exploits the aspartate-asparagine divergence, while the 4-position substituent exploits the methionine-valine divergence, thereby achieving a dual-point selectivity mechanism that is not achievable with inhibitors targeting only one of these features. The result is a class of compounds with inhibitory constants against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase in the low nanomolar range, selectivity ratios over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase exceeding 800-fold, and minimal activity against human or murine inducible nitric oxide synthase. The compounds are synthesized through a modular and scalable route that begins with the preparation of a 7-boronic ester or trifluoroborate derivative of 4-methylquinolin-2-amine, which is then subjected to a palladium-catalyzed Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling reaction with a variety of meta-substituted bromobenzene derivatives bearing protected amine and 4-position substituents. The coupling reaction is performed under mild conditions using sodium bicarbonate as a base and microwave irradiation to accelerate the process, thereby minimizing side reactions such as deacetylation of the quinoline ring. The resulting protected intermediates are then subjected to sequential deprotection steps, first removing the acetyl group under basic conditions and then cleaving the Boc protecting group on the amine using methanolic hydrochloric acid to yield the final inhibitors as stable, water-soluble dihydrochloride or trihydrochloride salts. This synthetic strategy allows for the rapid generation of a diverse library of analogs, enabling systematic exploration of structure-activity relationships across both the aminomethyl tail and the 4-position substituent. The resulting compounds exhibit excellent physicochemical properties that are conducive to central nervous system delivery. Calculated log D values at physiological pH are within the optimal range for blood-brain barrier penetration, and the total polar surface area is sufficiently low to permit passive diffusion across lipid membranes. Experimental permeability measurements using a parallel artificial membrane assay that mimics the blood-brain barrier confirm that these compounds possess high effective permeability values, with the most potent analogs exceeding 10 × 10⁻⁶ cm/s, a threshold associated with robust brain penetration in vivo. Furthermore, the compounds demonstrate high stability in human liver microsomes, with half-lives exceeding 60 minutes, indicating resistance to oxidative metabolism by cytochrome P450 enzymes. Off-target profiling against a panel of 45 central nervous system receptors, including monoamine transporters, G-protein-coupled receptors, and ion channels, reveals no significant binding at concentrations up to 10 micromolar, with the majority of interactions classified as insignificant or weak. This clean pharmacological profile distinguishes these inhibitors from earlier generations of nitric oxide synthase inhibitors, which often exhibited undesirable off-target effects such as serotonin receptor antagonism or dopamine transporter inhibition. The compounds are suitable for formulation into a variety of pharmaceutical dosage forms, including oral tablets, capsules, and solutions; intravenous infusions; intranasal sprays; and formulations for direct intrathecal or intracerebroventricular delivery. They may be administered alone or in combination with other neuroprotective agents, such as antioxidants, anti-inflammatory compounds, or agents that modulate glutamate receptor activity, to provide synergistic therapeutic benefits in conditions characterized by excitotoxicity and oxidative stress. The invention further encompasses the use of these compounds in the preparation of medicaments for the treatment of neurological disorders associated with excessive neuronal nitric oxide production, including but not limited to Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, Huntington’s disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, stroke, traumatic brain injury, cerebral palsy, and ischemic encephalopathy. Administration of a therapeutically effective amount of a compound of the invention to a subject in need thereof results in the suppression of pathological nitric oxide synthesis within neurons, thereby reducing oxidative damage, preventing mitochondrial dysfunction, inhibiting protein nitration, and preserving neuronal viability. The compounds may be dosed orally, intravenously, or via other routes, and the dosage regimen may be adjusted based on the severity of the condition, the age and weight of the subject, and the pharmacokinetic profile of the specific compound employed. The invention also provides a method of screening for compounds with similar activity by assaying their ability to inhibit purified neuronal nitric oxide synthase in a hemoglobin capture assay, and by evaluating their selectivity over endothelial and inducible isoforms using the same methodology. The compounds may also be used as molecular probes in biochemical and cellular assays to investigate the role of neuronal nitric oxide in synaptic plasticity, neuroinflammation, and neurodegeneration. The structural insights gained from crystallographic studies of inhibitor-enzyme complexes provide a blueprint for the rational design of next-generation inhibitors with even greater potency, selectivity, and pharmacokinetic properties. The invention represents a significant advancement in the field of neuropharmacology, offering a new class of therapeutics that directly target the molecular source of nitric oxide-mediated neurotoxicity with unprecedented precision and safety.

### ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS

The invention is further illustrated by the following specific embodiments, which are provided for the purpose of exemplification and not limitation. One embodiment of the invention is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, which exhibits an inhibitory constant of 55 nanomolar against rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and 122 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with selectivity ratios of 440-fold over murine inducible nitric oxide synthase and 877-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. This compound is synthesized by coupling a 7-trifluoroborate derivative of 4-methylquinolin-2-amine with 1-bromo-3-(N-Boc-amino)methylbenzene under microwave-assisted Suzuki-Miyaura conditions, followed by sequential deprotection of the acetyl and Boc groups to yield the final product as a dihydrochloride salt. The compound is characterized by a clear and well-defined binding mode in crystal structures of both rat and human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, wherein the aminomethyl group is positioned to form a direct hydrogen bond with the carboxylate of aspartate 597 in rat and aspartate 602 in human, while the phenyl ring is sandwiched between heme propionates A and D. The 4-methyl group on the quinoline ring contributes to hydrophobic stabilization of the core scaffold without introducing steric hindrance. A second embodiment is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(cyclopropylmethoxy)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, which demonstrates an inhibitory constant of 39 nanomolar against rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and 49 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with selectivity ratios exceeding 1,000-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. This compound is prepared by coupling the same 7-trifluoroborate precursor with 1-bromo-3-(aminomethyl)-4-(cyclopropylmethoxy)benzene, followed by deprotection as described above. Crystallographic analysis reveals that the cyclopropylmethoxy group occupies a hydrophobic subpocket formed by methionine 336, leucine 337, and tyrosine 706 in rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase, forming multiple favorable van der Waals contacts that enhance binding affinity. The cyclopropyl ring’s constrained geometry prevents rotational freedom, ensuring optimal positioning within the pocket. A third embodiment is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(pyridin-3-ylmethoxy)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine trihydrochloride, which exhibits an inhibitory constant of 44 nanomolar against rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and 45 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with selectivity ratios of 879-fold over murine inducible nitric oxide synthase and 312-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. This compound is synthesized by coupling the 7-trifluoroborate precursor with 1-bromo-3-(aminomethyl)-4-(3-pyridylmethoxy)benzene, followed by deprotection. The 3-pyridyl group engages the methionine-rich pocket through hydrophobic interactions, while the nitrogen atom of the pyridine ring forms a hydrogen bond with a conserved water molecule adjacent to heme propionate A, a feature not present in endothelial nitric oxide synthase due to the substitution of methionine with valine. A fourth embodiment is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(4-cyanobenzyl)oxyphenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, which demonstrates an inhibitory constant of 58 nanomolar against rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and 61 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with selectivity ratios of 720-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. The cyano group on the benzyl ring enhances binding through dipole interactions with the protein backbone, while the benzyl ether linker provides optimal spacing for the substituent to reach the hydrophobic pocket. A fifth embodiment is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(3-fluorobenzyl)oxyphenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, which exhibits an inhibitory constant of 67 nanomolar against rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and 72 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with selectivity ratios exceeding 800-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. The fluorine atom on the benzyl ring contributes to binding through a favorable orthogonal dipole interaction with a nearby carbonyl group in the protein, while the benzyl ether linker maintains the necessary distance and flexibility for optimal positioning. A sixth embodiment is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(isopropoxy)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, which demonstrates an inhibitory constant of 73 nanomolar against rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and 81 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with selectivity ratios of 650-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. The isopropoxy group provides a compact, branched hydrophobic moiety that fills the methionine-rich pocket without introducing excessive polarity. A seventh embodiment is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(methoxycyclobutyl)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, which exhibits an inhibitory constant of 49 nanomolar against rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and 51 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with selectivity ratios exceeding 900-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. The cyclobutyl ring provides a rigid, three-dimensional hydrophobic surface that complements the shape of the pocket formed by methionine 336 and leucine 337. An eighth embodiment is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(methylcyclopropyl)oxyphenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, which demonstrates an inhibitory constant of 41 nanomolar against rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and 46 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with selectivity ratios exceeding 1,000-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. The methylcyclopropyl group enhances binding through additional hydrophobic contacts with the side chain of leucine 337. A ninth embodiment is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(oxazol-4-ylmethoxy)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, which exhibits an inhibitory constant of 63 nanomolar against rat neuronal nitric oxide synthase and 65 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, with selectivity ratios of 433-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. The oxazole ring provides a hydrogen bond acceptor that interacts with a structured water molecule near the heme propionate, while maintaining low polarity and favorable permeability. A tenth embodiment is the compound 7-(3-(aminomethyl)-4-(thiazol-4-ylmethoxy)phenyl)-4-methylquinolin-2-amine dihydrochloride, which demonstrates an inhibitory constant of 31 nanomolar against human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, making it the most potent inhibitor in the series, with selectivity ratios exceeding 700-fold over human endothelial nitric oxide synthase. The thiazole ring engages the hydrophobic pocket through sulfur-mediated van der Waals interactions and forms a hydrogen bond with a water molecule near asparagine 574 in human neuronal nitric oxide synthase, contributing to its exceptional potency. Each of these compounds is prepared using the same general synthetic strategy, beginning with the preparation of the 7-boronic ester or trifluoroborate derivative of 4-methylquinolin-2-amine, followed by palladium-catalyzed coupling with the appropriate brominated precursor, and sequential deprotection to yield the final amine-containing inhibitor as a hydrochloride salt. The compounds are characterized by high purity, as confirmed by nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, high-resolution mass spectrometry, and elemental analysis. They are stable under ambient conditions and exhibit solubility in aqueous buffers sufficient for in vivo administration. Their pharmacokinetic profiles demonstrate rapid absorption, low plasma protein binding, and extensive distribution into the central nervous system following systemic administration in rodent models. These compounds are suitable for use in pharmaceutical compositions, including tablets, capsules, injectable solutions, and nasal sprays, and may be administered alone or in combination with other therapeutic agents to treat neurological disorders associated with pathological nitric oxide production. The invention thus provides a new class of highly selective neuronal nitric oxide synthase inhibitors with superior potency, selectivity, and drug-like properties, representing a significant advancement in the treatment of neurodegenerative and acute neurological conditions.
