## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccine development, specifically to a novel multivalent glycoconjugate vaccine designed to elicit protective humoral immunity against two of the most clinically significant Gram-negative bacterial pathogens responsible for nosocomial infections: Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The invention encompasses a chemically synthesized conjugate comprising four distinct Klebsiella pneumoniae O-antigen polysaccharides covalently linked to two distinct recombinant Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins, thereby forming a single, unified immunogenic formulation capable of inducing broadly protective antibodies against the most prevalent and clinically relevant strains of both pathogens. This invention further extends to the compositions, methods of administration, and manufacturing processes necessary for the production, purification, and stabilization of said conjugate vaccine, as well as to the use of such a vaccine for the prevention of invasive and wound-associated infections caused by multidrug-resistant strains of Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa in human populations at risk for hospital-acquired infections. The invention represents a significant advancement in the field of antimicrobial prophylaxis by overcoming the limitations of traditional polysaccharide vaccines through the strategic use of heterologous carrier proteins and by simultaneously targeting two distinct pathogens with a single, synergistic immunogen.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nosocomial infections remain a persistent and escalating threat to global public health, particularly in intensive care units, surgical wards, and long-term care facilities where patients are frequently exposed to invasive devices, prolonged antibiotic regimens, and compromised immune defenses. Among the most prevalent and dangerous causative agents of these infections are the Gram-negative bacteria Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa, both of which have been classified by the World Health Organization and the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention as critical priority pathogens due to their rapid acquisition and dissemination of resistance mechanisms to nearly all classes of antibiotics, including carbapenems and colistin. The clinical burden of these organisms is compounded by their ability to colonize mucosal surfaces asymptomatically, persist in hospital environments for extended periods, and cause a spectrum of life-threatening conditions ranging from ventilator-associated pneumonia and catheter-related bloodstream infections to surgical site infections and septicemia. Despite decades of research and the development of numerous antimicrobial agents, the therapeutic landscape for infections caused by these pathogens continues to deteriorate, with treatment options becoming increasingly limited, costly, and associated with high rates of treatment failure and mortality.

Traditional approaches to preventing bacterial infections have relied heavily on antibiotics, but the relentless emergence of multidrug-resistant strains has rendered many of these agents ineffective, creating a therapeutic vacuum that demands alternative strategies. Vaccines, by contrast, offer a fundamentally different mechanism of protection—one that is not susceptible to the molecular mechanisms of antimicrobial resistance and that can confer durable, immune-mediated defense against both susceptible and resistant isolates. However, despite the clear clinical need, no licensed vaccine currently exists for either Klebsiella pneumoniae or Pseudomonas aeruginosa in humans. The challenges to vaccine development for these pathogens are substantial and distinct. For Klebsiella pneumoniae, the primary obstacle lies in the extreme antigenic diversity of its capsular polysaccharide, with over eighty distinct K-types identified, none of which dominate globally. While some studies have pursued multivalent capsule-based vaccines, the logistical and immunological burden of incorporating more than twenty distinct K-antigens into a single formulation has proven prohibitive. In contrast, the lipopolysaccharide O-antigen, though less variable, has been largely overlooked as a vaccine target despite evidence that antibodies against specific O-types—particularly O1, O2, O3, and O5—account for over eighty percent of clinical isolates worldwide and are capable of mediating opsonophagocytic killing and protective immunity in animal models. For Pseudomonas aeruginosa, the principal challenge has been the selection of an antigen capable of inducing broadly protective immunity across diverse serotypes. Flagellin, the structural subunit of the polar flagellum, has emerged as a leading candidate due to its essential role in motility, biofilm formation, and virulence, as well as its capacity to elicit functional antibodies that inhibit bacterial movement and enhance clearance. However, flagellin exists in two major serotypes, FlaA and FlaB, and immunity to one does not confer protection against the other, necessitating a bivalent approach. Moreover, prior attempts to develop flagellin-based vaccines have yielded inconsistent results, often due to the inherent immunogenicity of flagellin as a Toll-like receptor 5 agonist, which can trigger excessive inflammation or immune tolerance, and the difficulty in maintaining structural integrity and antigenic fidelity during purification and conjugation.

Historically, polysaccharide antigens have been notoriously poor immunogens in humans, particularly in vulnerable populations such as infants, the elderly, and immunocompromised individuals, due to their T-cell-independent nature, which fails to induce immunological memory, class switching, or affinity maturation. The breakthrough in overcoming this limitation came with the development of glycoconjugate vaccines, in which bacterial polysaccharides are covalently linked to immunogenic carrier proteins, thereby converting the immune response from a T-cell-independent to a T-cell-dependent mechanism. This strategy has been spectacularly successful in the case of Haemophilus influenzae type b and Streptococcus pneumoniae, where conjugate vaccines have dramatically reduced disease incidence and even provided herd immunity. However, the application of this approach to Klebsiella pneumoniae has been severely limited by the lack of a suitable carrier protein that is both immunologically inert in the context of prior human exposure and capable of eliciting robust, synergistic immunity to both the polysaccharide and the carrier itself. Tetanus toxoid and diphtheria toxoid, the most commonly used carriers in licensed conjugate vaccines, are administered routinely in childhood immunization schedules, resulting in high levels of pre-existing immunity that can suppress the response to the polysaccharide component through epitopic suppression. Alternative carrier proteins, such as outer membrane proteins or exotoxins, have been explored but often suffer from poor stability, low yield, or unintended immunopathogenic effects. Furthermore, no prior effort has successfully combined a multivalent Klebsiella O-antigen conjugate with a bivalent Pseudomonas flagellin carrier in a single formulation, despite the compelling epidemiological overlap between the two pathogens and the potential for synergistic protection. The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by introducing a novel, rationally designed glycoconjugate vaccine that uniquely integrates four Klebsiella pneumoniae O-antigens with two Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins, thereby achieving broad-spectrum coverage against the most clinically relevant strains of both pathogens while circumventing the limitations of traditional carrier proteins and polysaccharide immunogenicity.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel, multivalent glycoconjugate vaccine composition comprising four distinct Klebsiella pneumoniae O-antigen polysaccharides covalently linked to two distinct recombinant Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins, wherein the conjugate is formulated to elicit a robust, durable, and protective humoral immune response against both pathogens simultaneously. The invention further encompasses a method of inducing protective immunity in a mammalian subject by administering the conjugate vaccine in a therapeutically effective dose, thereby preventing or reducing the incidence of infection caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The four Klebsiella pneumoniae O-antigens selected for inclusion in the vaccine are O1, O2, O3, and O5, which collectively account for more than eighty percent of clinical isolates worldwide and are associated with the majority of invasive and multidrug-resistant infections. Each of these polysaccharides is chemically conjugated via a stable thioether linkage to lysine residues on either recombinant FlaA or FlaB flagellin protein, with the resulting conjugates arranged in a balanced, quadrivalent formulation comprising O1 conjugated to FlaB, O2 conjugated to FlaA, O3 conjugated to FlaB, and O5 conjugated to FlaA. This arrangement ensures that each flagellin protein serves as a carrier for two distinct O-antigens, thereby maximizing antigenic coverage while minimizing the number of individual conjugate components required for broad protection. The recombinant flagellin proteins are produced in Escherichia coli using codon-optimized synthetic genes and purified to homogeneity, with residual endotoxin levels reduced to less than 0.1 endotoxin units per microgram of protein, ensuring safety and immunological compatibility. The chemical conjugation process employs a two-step strategy involving the introduction of an aminooxy-thiol linker to the reducing end of each polysaccharide followed by maleimide-mediated coupling to lysine residues on the flagellin protein, resulting in a stable, defined, and reproducible conjugate structure in which the polysaccharide chain remains intact and fully accessible to the immune system. The final conjugate formulation exhibits a polysaccharide-to-protein molar ratio of approximately 1:1, with each component maintaining a narrow molecular size distribution consistent with monomeric or low-order oligomeric structures, as confirmed by high-performance liquid chromatography with size exclusion chromatography and mass spectrometry analyses.

The invention further provides a method of manufacturing the conjugate vaccine through a scalable, GMP-compatible process that includes the genetic engineering of Klebsiella pneumoniae reagent strains to produce non-encapsulated, guanine-auxotrophic mutants that facilitate high-yield, low-contaminant O-antigen extraction, the expression and purification of recombinant flagellin proteins in Escherichia coli, and the sequential chemical conjugation, purification, and formulation of the final product. The manufacturing process is designed to yield large quantities of highly pure, endotoxin-free, and sterility-compliant conjugate suitable for human use, with each step validated for reproducibility, consistency, and scalability. The resulting vaccine composition is stable when stored at 2–8°C for extended periods and may be formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients such as sodium chloride, phosphate buffer, and polysorbate 80 to maintain structural integrity and solubility. Administration of the vaccine to mammalian subjects, including humans, induces high-titer, functional IgG antibodies against each of the four O-antigens and both flagellin types, as demonstrated by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, flow cytometry, and opsonophagocytic uptake assays. These antibodies are capable of binding to encapsulated Klebsiella pneumoniae strains regardless of their capsular serotype, mediating opsonophagocytic killing by macrophages and neutrophils, and inhibiting the motility of Pseudomonas aeruginosa strains expressing the corresponding flagellin type. Passive transfer of immune sera from vaccinated animals confers significant protection against lethal intravenous challenge with Klebsiella pneumoniae and burn wound infection with Pseudomonas aeruginosa, with survival rates exceeding eighty percent in murine models. The vaccine does not induce detectable Toll-like receptor 5 activation following conjugation, eliminating the risk of excessive inflammation while preserving the immunogenicity of the flagellin carrier. The invention further provides a method of treating or preventing infection in a subject at risk for nosocomial infection, including patients undergoing surgery, mechanical ventilation, or prolonged hospitalization, by administering the conjugate vaccine prior to anticipated exposure. The vaccine is particularly suited for use in healthcare settings where multidrug-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa are endemic, and its broad-spectrum coverage eliminates the need for strain-specific diagnostics or multiple separate vaccines. The invention represents a transformative advance in the field of antimicrobial prophylaxis by providing the first single-agent vaccine capable of simultaneously targeting two of the most dangerous Gram-negative pathogens with a single, synergistic, and manufacturable formulation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Conjugate

The conjugate of the present invention is a chemically synthesized, covalent complex formed by the stable linkage of four distinct Klebsiella pneumoniae O-antigen polysaccharides to two distinct recombinant Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins, resulting in a multivalent immunogen capable of eliciting broad and protective humoral immunity against both pathogens. The Klebsiella pneumoniae O-antigens selected for inclusion in the conjugate are derived from the O1, O2, O3, and O5 serotypes, which together represent the most prevalent and clinically significant variants among clinical isolates worldwide, accounting for more than eighty percent of invasive infections associated with multidrug-resistant strains. These polysaccharides are extracted from genetically engineered Klebsiella pneumoniae reagent strains that have been modified to eliminate capsule expression and to render the organism auxotrophic for guanine, thereby facilitating high-yield, low-contaminant production and simplifying downstream purification. Each O-antigen is purified to homogeneity using a scalable manufacturing process involving nitrous acid deamination, tangential flow filtration, anion exchange chromatography, and ammonium sulfate precipitation, resulting in polysaccharides with minimal residual protein, nucleic acid, and endotoxin contamination, as confirmed by Bradford assay, spectrophotometric analysis at 260 nm, and chromogenic limulus amebocyte lysate assays. The molecular weight distribution of each purified polysaccharide is characterized by high-performance liquid chromatography with size exclusion chromatography, revealing a narrow, homogeneous population with an average molecular mass ranging between 50 and 150 kDa, consistent with the expected size of intact O-antigen repeats. Monosaccharide composition analysis by high-performance anion exchange chromatography with pulsed amperometric detection confirms that O1 and O2 are polygalactan structures, while O3 and O5 are predominantly composed of mannose residues, with each structure retaining its native linkage pattern and antigenic epitopes.

The recombinant flagellin proteins used in the conjugate are derived from the two major serotypes of Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin, FlaA and FlaB, which are encoded by distinct genes and exhibit minimal sequence homology, necessitating their inclusion as separate components to achieve broad coverage. These flagellin proteins are produced in Escherichia coli using synthetic genes that have been codon-optimized for high-level expression in the bacterial host, cloned into a pTrc-His-TOPO expression vector, and induced with isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside under controlled fermentation conditions. The recombinant proteins are purified from the soluble fraction of bacterial lysates using immobilized metal affinity chromatography with nickel-sepharose resin, followed by sequential tangential flow filtration, cation exchange, and anion exchange membrane chromatography to remove endotoxin, nucleic acids, and misfolded aggregates. The resulting flagellin preparations are characterized by a single band of approximately 55 kDa on SDS-PAGE under reducing conditions, with purity exceeding 95% as determined by densitometric analysis, and endotoxin levels below 0.1 endotoxin units per microgram of protein. The structural integrity of the flagellin proteins is confirmed by circular dichroism spectroscopy and high-performance liquid chromatography with size exclusion chromatography, demonstrating that the proteins exist predominantly in their monomeric form and retain a secondary structure consistent with the canonical flagellin fold. Importantly, the recombinant flagellin proteins lack the post-translational glycosylation modifications observed in native Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin, which are absent in the Escherichia coli expression system and have been shown in prior studies to interfere with antibody accessibility and functional activity.

The conjugation of the Klebsiella pneumoniae O-antigens to the Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins is achieved through a precisely controlled, two-step thioether-aminooxy chemistry that ensures site-specific linkage while preserving the structural and immunological integrity of both components. In the first step, each purified O-antigen is reacted with O-(3-mercaptopropyl)-hydroxylamine under mildly acidic conditions (pH 5.0) to introduce an aminooxy-thiol moiety at the reducing end of the polysaccharide chain, which is then stabilized by reduction with sodium cyanoborohydride. In the second step, the recombinant flagellin proteins are derivatized with N-γ-maleimidobutyryl-oxysuccinimide ester, which selectively reacts with lysine residues on the protein surface to generate reactive maleimide groups. The aminooxy-thiol-modified O-antigens are then mixed with the maleimide-derivatized flagellin proteins at a 1:1 weight-to-weight ratio and incubated under gentle rotation at 4°C for a period of 24 to 48 hours, allowing for the formation of a stable thioether linkage between the sulfhydryl group of the linker and the maleimide moiety on the flagellin. The reaction is monitored by SDS-PAGE and high-performance liquid chromatography with refractive index detection, which reveals a distinct upward shift in molecular weight corresponding to the conjugation of polysaccharide chains to the flagellin backbone. The resulting conjugates are purified by size exclusion chromatography on a Superdex 200 column to remove unreacted polysaccharide, free flagellin, and low-molecular-weight byproducts, yielding a homogeneous population of conjugate molecules with a defined polysaccharide-to-protein ratio of approximately 1:1. The final conjugate is sterile-filtered, formulated in phosphate-buffered saline containing 5 mM EDTA, and stored at 4°C until use. The conjugation process effectively ablates the innate immunostimulatory activity of flagellin through Toll-like receptor 5, as confirmed by luciferase reporter assays in HEK293 cells, while preserving the antigenic epitopes recognized by protective antibodies, as demonstrated by binding assays and functional opsonophagocytic assays. The resulting quadrivalent conjugate formulation comprises four distinct molecular species: O1 conjugated to FlaB, O2 conjugated to FlaA, O3 conjugated to FlaB, and O5 conjugated to FlaA, ensuring that each flagellin protein serves as a carrier for two distinct O-antigens, thereby maximizing antigenic coverage and minimizing formulation complexity.

### Compositions

The compositions of the present invention encompass the purified quadrivalent glycoconjugate vaccine as described above, formulated for parenteral administration to mammalian subjects, including humans, for the prevention of infection caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The vaccine composition is formulated as a sterile, pyrogen-free, isotonic aqueous solution suitable for intramuscular injection, containing the four conjugate species in equal molar proportions, with each conjugate present at a concentration of 5 micrograms of polysaccharide per dose. The total polysaccharide content per dose is therefore 20 micrograms, distributed equally among the four O-antigens, while the total flagellin content is 20 micrograms, with 10 micrograms of FlaA and 10 micrograms of FlaB, each serving as the carrier for two distinct O-antigens. The formulation is buffered in 20 mM sodium phosphate, 150 mM sodium chloride, and 5 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, adjusted to a pH of 7.4, and may optionally contain a stabilizing agent such as polysorbate 80 at a concentration of 0.01% to prevent adsorption to container surfaces and maintain colloidal stability during storage. The composition is free of adjuvants, preservatives, or other immunostimulatory agents, as the conjugate itself is sufficiently immunogenic to elicit a robust immune response without the need for additional components. The absence of adjuvants enhances the safety profile of the vaccine, particularly in vulnerable populations such as neonates, the elderly, and immunocompromised individuals, who may be more susceptible to inflammatory side effects.

The vaccine composition may be lyophilized for long-term storage and reconstituted immediately prior to administration, or it may be maintained in liquid form under refrigerated conditions (2–8°C) for up to 24 months without significant degradation of antigenic integrity or immunogenicity. Stability studies conducted over a period of 18 months demonstrate that the polysaccharide-to-protein ratio remains unchanged, the molecular size distribution is preserved, and the endotoxin content remains below the regulatory threshold of 0.1 endotoxin units per microgram of protein. The conjugate retains its ability to bind to target antigens in enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays and to mediate opsonophagocytic uptake in macrophage assays, confirming the preservation of functional epitopes. The composition may be administered as a single dose or as a series of doses, with a preferred regimen consisting of three intramuscular injections administered at intervals of 14 to 28 days, followed by a booster dose at 6 to 12 months to enhance immunological memory. The vaccine may be co-administered with other vaccines, including those for tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis, pneumococcus, and influenza, without evidence of interference in immunogenicity or increased adverse events. The composition is suitable for use in both pediatric and adult populations, with dosing adjusted based on body weight in pediatric subjects, although clinical trials have demonstrated equivalent immunogenicity across age groups when administered at the same microgram-per-dose concentration. The vaccine may be delivered via prefilled syringes, vials, or auto-injectors, and is compatible with standard cold chain logistics, making it suitable for deployment in both hospital and community-based vaccination programs. The composition is distinct from prior formulations that rely on admixed unconjugated antigens, which fail to induce significant anti-polysaccharide antibody responses, or those that utilize tetanus or diphtheria toxoid as carriers, which are subject to epitopic suppression due to pre-existing immunity in the majority of the population. The unique combination of Klebsiella O-antigens with Pseudomonas flagellin carriers in a single, chemically defined conjugate represents a novel and non-obvious advancement in vaccine design, offering a level of breadth, specificity, and durability of immune response that is unattainable with existing technologies.

### Methods of Treatment

The methods of treatment provided by the present invention involve the administration of the quadrivalent glycoconjugate vaccine to a mammalian subject, including a human, who is at risk for infection caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae or Pseudomonas aeruginosa, with the intent of preventing or reducing the incidence, severity, or duration of such infections. The subject may be identified as being at risk based on clinical factors such as prolonged hospitalization, admission to an intensive care unit, receipt of mechanical ventilation, presence of an indwelling urinary or central venous catheter, recent surgical intervention, immunosuppressive therapy, or underlying conditions such as diabetes, chronic lung disease, or malignancy. The vaccine is administered parenterally, preferably by intramuscular injection into the deltoid or anterolateral thigh muscle, using a sterile needle and syringe, at a dose of 5 micrograms of each O-antigen and 10 micrograms of each flagellin protein per dose, for a total of 20 micrograms of polysaccharide and 20 micrograms of protein per injection. The preferred regimen consists of three doses administered at intervals of 14 to 28 days, with the first dose given at least 14 days prior to anticipated exposure to a high-risk environment, such as preoperative vaccination prior to major surgery or pre-admission vaccination prior to transfer to an intensive care unit. A booster dose may be administered at six to twelve months following the primary series to enhance the durability of the immune response, particularly in subjects with impaired immune function or ongoing exposure to high-risk settings.

The vaccine is effective in eliciting a robust, functional, and long-lasting humoral immune response characterized by the production of high-titer IgG antibodies specific to each of the four Klebsiella pneumoniae O-antigens and both Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin types. These antibodies are capable of binding to the surface of live Klebsiella pneumoniae strains regardless of their capsular serotype, as demonstrated by flow cytometry and immunofluorescence assays, and mediate opsonophagocytic uptake by neutrophils and macrophages, as confirmed by in vitro killing assays using human peripheral blood mononuclear cells. The anti-flagellin antibodies induced by the vaccine are capable of inhibiting bacterial motility in Pseudomonas aeruginosa strains expressing the corresponding flagellin type, thereby impairing the ability of the organism to disseminate, colonize mucosal surfaces, and form biofilms. The protective efficacy of the vaccine is demonstrated in animal models by passive transfer of immune sera, which confers significant protection against lethal intravenous challenge with Klebsiella pneumoniae and burn wound infection with Pseudomonas aeruginosa, with survival rates exceeding eighty percent in both models. The vaccine is particularly effective in preventing invasive infections such as bacteremia, sepsis, and pneumonia, which are the most common and deadly manifestations of Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection in hospitalized patients. The vaccine may be administered prophylactically to patients prior to anticipated high-risk procedures, such as organ transplantation, chemotherapy, or major abdominal or thoracic surgery, or therapeutically in conjunction with antimicrobial therapy in patients who have already developed an infection but are at risk for recurrence or dissemination. The vaccine may also be administered to healthcare workers, first responders, and other individuals with occupational exposure to multidrug-resistant pathogens, thereby reducing the risk of transmission within healthcare settings. The method of treatment does not require prior microbiological identification of the infecting strain, as the vaccine provides broad coverage against the most prevalent and clinically relevant serotypes of both pathogens, eliminating the need for diagnostic delays or strain-specific interventions. The vaccine is safe and well-tolerated in preclinical and early clinical studies, with no evidence of systemic inflammation, local reactogenicity, or immune-mediated adverse events, making it suitable for repeated administration and use in vulnerable populations.

### Methods of Making the Conjugate

The methods of making the conjugate of the present invention comprise a series of precisely controlled, scalable, and reproducible steps designed to produce a highly pure, consistent, and immunologically active quadrivalent glycoconjugate vaccine from genetically engineered bacterial strains and recombinant protein expression systems. The process begins with the genetic modification of Klebsiella pneumoniae reagent strains to enable the high-yield production of non-encapsulated, guanine-auxotrophic mutants that facilitate the efficient extraction of O-antigen polysaccharides while minimizing contamination from capsule, outer membrane proteins, and endotoxin. This is accomplished through lambda red-mediated homologous recombination to delete the wzabc gene cluster responsible for capsule biosynthesis and the guaBA operon responsible for guanine biosynthesis, resulting in strains CVD 3001 (O1), CVD 3002 (O5), and the native O2 and O3 isolates, which are confirmed by PCR, sequencing, and phenotypic assays for capsule loss and guanine auxotrophy. The modified strains are then cultivated in large-scale fermentation systems under controlled conditions using a fully chemically defined medium supplemented with exogenous guanine to support growth, with temperature, pH, agitation, and aeration optimized to maximize polysaccharide yield. After 18 to 24 hours of growth, the culture is harvested and subjected to nitrous acid deamination to release the O-antigen from the lipopolysaccharide backbone, followed by centrifugation, microfiltration, and tangential flow filtration to remove cellular debris and concentrate the polysaccharide-containing supernatant. The concentrated solution is then subjected to anion exchange chromatography in negative mode to remove negatively charged contaminants such as nucleic acids and endotoxin, followed by ammonium sulfate precipitation and dialysis to further purify the polysaccharide. The final product is concentrated by ultrafiltration, dialyzed against sterile water, filtered through a 0.2-micron membrane, and lyophilized to obtain a dry powder with a purity exceeding 95% and endotoxin levels below 0.01 endotoxin units per microgram.

The recombinant flagellin proteins are produced by transforming Escherichia coli BL21 cells with synthetic genes encoding the FlaA and FlaB flagellin sequences, codon-optimized for high-level expression in the bacterial host and cloned into the pTrc-His-TOPO expression vector. Transformed cells are grown in Terrific Broth under controlled fermentation conditions, with induction of protein expression triggered by the addition of isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside when the culture reaches an optical density of approximately 3.0 at 600 nm. After a 90-minute induction period at 37°C, cells are harvested by centrifugation, lysed using B-PER reagent, and the soluble fraction is purified by immobilized metal affinity chromatography using nickel-sepharose resin to capture the 6xHis-tagged flagellin. The bound protein is eluted with imidazole-containing buffer and subjected to sequential purification steps including tangential flow filtration, cation exchange chromatography, and anion exchange chromatography to remove residual endotoxin, nucleic acids, and misfolded aggregates. The purified flagellin is concentrated, buffer-exchanged into phosphate-buffered saline, sterile-filtered, and stored at -80°C until use. The purity and integrity of the flagellin are confirmed by SDS-PAGE, size exclusion chromatography, and endotoxin assays, with each preparation meeting stringent quality control criteria.

The chemical conjugation process is initiated by modifying the reducing end of each purified Klebsiella O-antigen with O-(3-mercaptopropyl)-hydroxylamine under acidic conditions to introduce a thiol-containing linker, followed by reduction with sodium cyanoborohydride to stabilize the linkage. Simultaneously, the recombinant flagellin proteins are derivatized with N-γ-maleimidobutyryl-oxysuccinimide ester to generate reactive maleimide groups on lysine residues. The aminooxy-thiol-modified polysaccharides are then mixed with the maleimide-derivatized flagellins at a 1:1 weight-to-weight ratio and incubated under gentle rotation at 4°C for 24 to 48 hours to allow for thioether bond formation. The reaction mixture is then purified by size exclusion chromatography on a Superdex 200 column to isolate the conjugate from unreacted components, and the fractions containing the conjugate are pooled, concentrated, sterile-filtered, and formulated in phosphate-buffered saline with EDTA. The final product is characterized for polysaccharide and protein content using resorcinol and BCA assays, respectively, and for structural integrity by high-performance liquid chromatography with refractive index detection and SDS-PAGE. The entire manufacturing process is conducted under Good Manufacturing Practice conditions, with each step validated for reproducibility, yield, purity, and sterility, and with appropriate documentation and quality control testing performed at each stage to ensure batch-to-batch consistency and compliance with regulatory standards.

### Genetically Engineered Strains

The genetically engineered strains of Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa used in the production of the conjugate vaccine are integral components of the invention, providing the biological foundation for the reliable, scalable, and contaminant-free production of the key antigenic components. The Klebsiella pneumoniae reagent strains are derived from clinical isolates of known O-antigen serotypes, including B5055 (O1:K2), 4425/51 (O5:K57), 7380 (O2), and 390 (O3), which have been modified through targeted genetic deletions to enhance polysaccharide yield and simplify purification. The deletion of the wzabc gene cluster, which encodes the enzymes responsible for capsule biosynthesis, renders the strains non-encapsulated, thereby exposing the underlying O-antigen and facilitating its extraction without competition from the more abundant and structurally complex capsule. The deletion of the guaBA operon, which encodes enzymes involved in guanine biosynthesis, creates a guanine auxotrophy that attenuates the organism’s virulence and allows for controlled growth in defined media, minimizing the risk of contamination with wild-type strains and enabling the use of inexpensive, chemically defined media that reduce the burden of endotoxin and protein contaminants. These modifications are confirmed by phenotypic assays, including the absence of a capsule halo in India ink staining, the inability to grow in guanine-free medium, and the marked increase in LD50 in murine infection models, which exceeds 10^9 CFU compared to approximately 10^4 CFU for the wild-type parental strains. The resulting reagent strains are stable over multiple generations in culture, do not revert to a wild-type phenotype, and produce O-antigen yields that are three to five times higher than those of unmodified strains, making them ideal for industrial-scale production.

The recombinant flagellin proteins are produced in Escherichia coli BL21 cells transformed with synthetic genes encoding the FlaA and FlaB flagellin sequences derived from Pseudomonas aeruginosa strains PAK and PAO1, respectively. These synthetic genes are designed to optimize codon usage for high-level expression in E. coli, eliminate cryptic splice sites and secondary structures that impede translation, and incorporate a C-terminal 6xHis tag for efficient purification. The genes are cloned into the pTrc-His-TOPO vector, which provides a strong trc promoter for inducible expression and a kanamycin resistance marker for selection. The resulting plasmids, designated pTrcHis-flaAopt and pTrcHis-flaBopt, are stably maintained in E. coli BL21 cells under selective pressure, and protein expression is induced under controlled conditions to maximize yield and minimize inclusion body formation. The use of E. coli as the expression host ensures rapid growth, low cost, and compatibility with established fermentation and purification protocols, while the absence of native flagellin glycosylation machinery in E. coli ensures that the recombinant proteins are structurally consistent and free from the variable carbohydrate modifications that can interfere with antibody recognition in native Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin. The resulting recombinant flagellin proteins are functionally active in their ability to bind to Toll-like receptor 5 in their unconjugated form, as demonstrated by luciferase reporter assays, but lose this activity upon conjugation to the O-antigen, indicating that the conjugation process does not disrupt the overall protein fold but rather masks or sterically hinders the TLR5-binding domain. The genetic modifications and expression systems used in this invention are novel in their combination and application, as no prior effort has employed a guanine-auxotrophic, non-encapsulated Klebsiella pneumoniae strain for the purpose of O-antigen production in conjunction with a codon-optimized, His-tagged flagellin expression system in E. coli for the purpose of glycoconjugate vaccine development. The strains are maintained under strict biosafety conditions, with master and working cell banks established and characterized for genetic stability, purity, and absence of adventitious agents, and are used exclusively for the production of the conjugate vaccine in accordance with regulatory guidelines for biological product manufacturing.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, a quadrivalent glycoconjugate vaccine was prepared by chemically linking four Klebsiella pneumoniae O-antigen polysaccharides (O1, O2, O3, and O5) to two recombinant Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins (FlaA and FlaB) using a two-step thioether-aminooxy conjugation chemistry. The O-antigens were extracted from genetically engineered Klebsiella pneumoniae reagent strains CVD 3001 (O1), CVD 3002 (O5), and native isolates 7380 (O2) and 390 (O3), following fermentation in chemically defined medium, nitrous acid deamination, and sequential purification by tangential flow filtration, anion exchange chromatography, and ammonium sulfate precipitation. Each polysaccharide was analyzed for purity, molecular weight, and monosaccharide composition, with endotoxin levels consistently below 0.01 endotoxin units per microgram and protein contamination below 1%. The recombinant FlaA and FlaB proteins were expressed in Escherichia coli BL21 using codon-optimized synthetic genes cloned into the pTrc-His-TOPO vector, induced with IPTG, and purified by nickel affinity chromatography followed by cation and anion exchange membrane chromatography, yielding proteins with purity greater than 95% and endotoxin levels below 0.1 endotoxin units per microgram. The polysaccharides were modified with O-(3-mercaptopropyl)-hydroxylamine at a concentration of 2.5 mg/mL in 100 mM sodium acetate buffer (pH 5.0) and incubated for 16 hours at room temperature, followed by reduction with sodium cyanoborohydride at a 2:1 weight ratio to stabilize the thiol linkage. Separately, the flagellin proteins were derivatized with N-γ-maleimidobutyryl-oxysuccinimide ester at a concentration of 1 mM in 10 mM sodium phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) for one hour, then purified by tangential flow filtration and dialysis against PBS containing 5 mM EDTA. The aminooxy-thiol-modified polysaccharides were then mixed with the maleimide-derivatized flagellins at a 1:1 weight ratio and incubated under gentle rotation at 4°C for 48 hours. The conjugation reactions were monitored by SDS-PAGE, which revealed a distinct upward shift in molecular weight corresponding to polysaccharide attachment, and by high-performance liquid chromatography with refractive index detection, which confirmed the formation of a single, homogeneous conjugate peak. The conjugates were purified by size exclusion chromatography on a Superdex 200 column, pooled, concentrated, and sterile-filtered. The final quadrivalent formulation contained equal amounts of O1:FlaB, O2:FlaA, O3:FlaB, and O5:FlaA, each at a concentration of 5 μg polysaccharide per mL, with a total polysaccharide content of 20 μg/mL and a total flagellin content of 20 μg/mL. The conjugate was stable for at least 18 months at 4°C, with no degradation in molecular size, polysaccharide-to-protein ratio, or immunogenicity. When administered to rabbits via intramuscular injection at 0, 14, 28, and 42 days, the conjugate induced high-titer IgG antibodies against all four O-antigens and both flagellin types, with endpoint titers exceeding 10,000 ELISA units for each antigen. In contrast, rabbits immunized with an admixed mixture of unconjugated polysaccharides and flagellins failed to generate significant anti-polysaccharide antibody responses, demonstrating the critical role of covalent conjugation in enhancing immunogenicity. The conjugate-induced antibodies mediated opsonophagocytic uptake of Klebsiella pneumoniae strains B5055 (O1) and 700603 (O3) by J774 macrophages, with uptake rates increasing by more than tenfold compared to pre-immune sera, and inhibited the motility of Pseudomonas aeruginosa strain PAO1 (FlaB) by more than 80% in agar motility assays. Passive transfer of immune sera from vaccinated rabbits conferred 100% protection against lethal burn wound infection with PAO1 and 90% protection against intravenous challenge with B5055, whereas control sera provided no protection. These results demonstrate the efficacy, specificity, and functional potency of the quadrivalent glycoconjugate vaccine as a novel prophylactic agent against multidrug-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections.
