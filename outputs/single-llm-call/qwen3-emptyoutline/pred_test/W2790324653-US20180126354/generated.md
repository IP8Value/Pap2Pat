# DESCRIPTION

## INTRODUCTION

Multiple myeloma is a malignant disorder characterized by the uncontrolled proliferation of plasma cells within the bone marrow, leading to the overproduction of monoclonal immunoglobulins and subsequent end-organ damage. The disease typically evolves through a multistep progression from a clinically silent premalignant state known as monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS), followed by smoldering multiple myeloma, and ultimately to overt, symptomatic multiple myeloma. This progression is driven by the accumulation of somatic genetic alterations that confer survival and proliferative advantages to the malignant plasma cell clone. The genetic landscape of multiple myeloma is highly heterogeneous, with two major molecular subtypes defined by distinct initiating events: hyperdiploid myeloma, marked by trisomies of multiple odd-numbered chromosomes, and non-hyperdiploid myeloma, frequently initiated by immunoglobulin heavy chain (IGH) translocations that dysregulate oncogenes through juxtaposition with powerful B-cell-specific enhancer elements. Canonical translocation partners include WHSC1/FGFR3, CCND3, CCND1, MAF, and MAFB, which are activated by proximity to the IGH locus enhancers following chromosomal rearrangements such as t(4;14), t(6;14), t(11;14), t(14;16), and t(14;20). These primary events are often present in the premalignant phase and are considered foundational to disease initiation. In contrast, secondary genetic events—including translocations involving MYC, recurrent single-nucleotide variants in KRAS, NRAS, and DIS3, and copy number alterations such as amplification of 1q and deletion of 1p, 13q, 14q, or 16q—are typically acquired during disease progression and are associated with increased genomic instability, clonal expansion, and therapeutic resistance. The interplay between these different classes of mutations—copy number variants, single-nucleotide variants, and structural rearrangements—has been shown to significantly influence clinical outcomes, with certain combinations of alterations either mitigating or exacerbating prognostic risk. For instance, the presence of trisomies of chromosomes 3 or 5 has been demonstrated to counteract the adverse prognosis typically associated with t(4;14) translocations, suggesting that the biological context of co-occurring lesions can fundamentally alter disease behavior. This complexity underscores the necessity of comprehensive genomic profiling that captures all three classes of alterations simultaneously, rather than relying on isolated assays that may miss critical interactions. While whole-exome sequencing has been employed in research settings to characterize these mutations, its high cost, extensive computational burden, and prolonged turnaround time limit its utility in routine clinical practice. Targeted sequencing approaches offer a more practical alternative, enabling deeper coverage of disease-relevant genomic regions at reduced cost and with faster analytical throughput. Prior efforts have developed targeted panels to detect either copy number variants or single-nucleotide variants, and others have focused exclusively on IGH rearrangements using amplicon-based or capture-based methods. However, no single platform has been designed and validated to concurrently detect all three types of genomic alterations—copy number variants, single-nucleotide variants, and structural translocations—with high sensitivity, specificity, and reproducibility in a clinically actionable format. Furthermore, the bioinformatic pipelines required to interpret such multi-modal data have not been systematically optimized for this specific disease context, resulting in high false-positive rates and inconsistent detection of clinically significant events. This invention addresses these limitations by introducing a novel, integrated, hybrid-capture sequencing platform specifically engineered for multiple myeloma, which enables the simultaneous detection of copy number variants, single-nucleotide variants, and translocations involving IGH, MYC, and other disease-relevant loci within a single assay. The platform incorporates a meticulously designed probe set targeting 465 genes implicated in myeloma pathogenesis, including known oncogenes, tumor suppressors, DNA repair genes, and B-cell biology regulators, as well as comprehensive coverage of the entire IGH locus, including variable, diversity, joining, and constant regions, and the immunoglobulin lambda light chain locus. Additionally, the invention includes a suite of computationally optimized algorithms for calling copy number variants, translocations, and single-nucleotide variants that have been rigorously calibrated against gold-standard methods such as fluorescence in situ hybridization and whole-exome sequencing. These algorithms incorporate machine learning models trained on matched normal and tumor samples to suppress artifacts and enhance the precision of variant detection, particularly for low-frequency and complex structural rearrangements. Crucially, the platform has revealed previously unrecognized genetic alterations, including recurrent translocations between IGH and IGLL5, a gene encoding a non-secreted immunoglobulin-like protein, and associated overexpression of DERL3, a gene involved in endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation. These alterations are not only mutually exclusive with RAS pathway mutations but are also strongly associated with disease progression, suggesting a novel pathogenic axis in multiple myeloma. The invention thus provides a unified, scalable, and clinically deployable genomic profiling system that enables precise molecular stratification of patients, facilitates the identification of novel therapeutic targets, and supports the development of personalized treatment strategies based on the integrated mutational profile of each individual’s disease.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a comprehensive genomic profiling system for multiple myeloma that enables the simultaneous detection of copy number variants, single-nucleotide variants, and structural translocations in a single, integrated assay. The system comprises a hybrid-capture sequencing platform designed to interrogate a defined genomic space encompassing 465 genes associated with myeloma pathogenesis, including oncogenes, tumor suppressors, and genes involved in DNA repair and B-cell differentiation, as well as the entire immunoglobulin heavy chain (IGH) locus and the immunoglobulin lambda light chain locus. The platform utilizes oligonucleotide probes tiled across exonic, intronic, and regulatory regions of these targets, including the variable, diversity, joining, and constant segments of IGH, to ensure comprehensive coverage of potential translocation breakpoints. Following hybridization and sequencing on high-throughput platforms, the resulting data are processed using a suite of computationally optimized bioinformatics algorithms specifically tuned for multiple myeloma. These algorithms include a copy number variant caller parameterized using a Gaussian mixture model to estimate background noise from diploid regions, a translocation caller that employs machine learning to filter false positives based on split-read and discordant-pair evidence thresholds calibrated against fluorescence in situ hybridization validation data, and a somatic variant caller that integrates multiple calling algorithms to improve sensitivity and specificity for single-nucleotide variants. The system has been validated on a cohort of 95 primary tumor samples with matched germline controls, demonstrating high concordance with whole-exome sequencing and fluorescence in situ hybridization for the detection of known copy number alterations, translocations, and point mutations. Notably, the invention enables the discovery of novel genetic events, including recurrent translocations between IGH and IGLL5, a gene located on chromosome 22, and associated overexpression of DERL3, a gene involved in endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation. These IGLL5 alterations are mutually exclusive with RAS pathway mutations and are associated with increased risk of disease progression, suggesting a previously unrecognized pathogenic mechanism in multiple myeloma. The invention further demonstrates that sequencing depths as low as 100× are sufficient to capture the majority of clinically relevant variants, with diminishing returns beyond 300×, thereby enabling cost-effective implementation in clinical laboratories. The system is designed for use with formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue and bone marrow aspirates, making it compatible with standard clinical workflows. The integrated nature of the platform allows for the identification of co-occurrence and mutual exclusivity patterns across mutation types, facilitating molecular subtyping and prognostic stratification. This invention provides a novel, scalable, and clinically translatable tool for precision oncology in multiple myeloma, enabling the simultaneous detection of all major classes of genomic alterations in a single assay, with enhanced accuracy, reduced false positives, and the capacity to uncover novel disease drivers and biomarkers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention encompasses a novel genomic profiling system for multiple myeloma that enables the concurrent detection of copy number variants, single-nucleotide variants, and structural translocations in a single, integrated assay. This system is designed to overcome the limitations of existing methods, which typically analyze only one class of genomic alteration at a time, thereby missing critical interactions between mutation types and increasing the cost, complexity, and turnaround time of molecular diagnostics. The core of the invention is a hybrid-capture sequencing platform that targets a precisely defined genomic region spanning 3.3 megabases, encompassing the exons, untranslated regions, splice sites, and selected intronic and regulatory regions of 465 genes known to be recurrently mutated or dysregulated in multiple myeloma. These genes were selected based on four criteria: (1) annotation as cancer-associated genes in the Catalogue of Somatic Mutations in Cancer (COSMIC) or MutSigCV databases; (2) functional involvement in DNA repair pathways or B-cell biology; (3) reported mutation frequency exceeding 3% in prior genomic studies of multiple myeloma; and (4) presence of known mutational hotspots associated with oncogenic activation or loss of tumor suppressor function. In addition to these 465 genes, the platform includes comprehensive, unbiased tiling of probes across the entire immunoglobulin heavy chain (IGH) locus on chromosome 14, including the variable (IGHV), diversity (IGHD), joining (IGHJ), and constant/switch regions, as well as the entire immunoglobulin lambda light chain (IGLL) locus on chromosome 22, which includes the IGLL5 gene. Probes are also tiled across the MYC locus on chromosome 8, including both exonic and intronic regions, to enable detection of intra- and inter-chromosomal translocations involving MYC. The probe set is designed using NimbleGen oligonucleotide synthesis technology, with probes spaced at intervals optimized for hybridization efficiency and uniform coverage, and each probe is 80 to 120 nucleotides in length to ensure sufficient specificity and binding affinity. The probe set is synthesized as a single pool and used in hybridization-based enrichment of genomic DNA extracted from CD138-purified plasma cells obtained from bone marrow aspirates, with matched germline DNA derived from peripheral blood mononuclear cells serving as a control for somatic variant calling. Following hybridization, enriched libraries are sequenced on high-throughput next-generation sequencing platforms such as the Illumina HiSeq 2000 or HiSeq 2500, using paired-end reads of 100 to 125 base pairs in length to maximize the detection of structural variants and improve alignment accuracy. The sequencing depth achieved across the tumor samples averages 104-fold, with a minimum of 33-fold and a maximum of 140-fold, while normal samples achieve an average depth of 107-fold, ranging from 43-fold to 168-fold. This level of coverage is sufficient to detect both clonal and subclonal variants with high confidence, as demonstrated by subsequent deep sequencing of a subset of samples to 1,200-fold or greater, which revealed that the majority of non-silent variants are captured at depths as low as 100-fold, with minimal additional discovery beyond 300-fold. The invention further includes a suite of bioinformatics tools specifically developed and calibrated for the analysis of multiple myeloma genomic data. For the detection of copy number variants, a modified version of the CopyCAT2 algorithm is employed, which computes the log2 ratio of tumor-to-normal sequencing depth across each probe interval and applies a Gaussian mixture model to estimate the noise distribution in diploid regions of the genome. This model distinguishes true copy number alterations from technical noise by fitting a mixture of Gaussian distributions to the log2 ratio data, with the mean and variance of the diploid component used to define a noise threshold. Copy number variants are called only if the log2 ratio exceeds this threshold by a statistically significant margin, as determined by a permutation-based p-value calculation, ensuring high specificity. This approach successfully identifies genome-scale hyperdiploidy, arm-level alterations such as amplification of 1q and deletion of 1p, 13q, and 17p, as well as focal deletions and amplifications, including a homozygous deletion of BRCA2. For the detection of structural translocations, the LUMPY algorithm is utilized to identify split reads and discordant paired-end reads indicative of chromosomal rearrangements. However, because LUMPY produces a high rate of false positives in the context of immunoglobulin loci due to somatic hypermutation and repetitive elements, the invention introduces a novel machine learning-based filtering pipeline. This pipeline is trained on a set of 22 samples with known translocation status determined by fluorescence in situ hybridization, using features such as the number of supporting split reads, the number of discordant read pairs, the distance between breakpoint clusters, the presence of microhomology at the junction, and the genomic context of the breakpoint (e.g., proximity to repetitive elements or transcriptionally active regions). The resulting classifier achieves 100% precision and 64% recall for canonical IGH translocations, effectively eliminating false positives while retaining true events. This filtering strategy enables the reliable detection of both canonical translocations, such as t(11;14), t(4;14), and t(14;20), and novel translocations, including those involving IGLL5. For single-nucleotide variants, the invention employs a consensus calling approach that integrates results from five independent somatic variant callers—samtools, SomaticSniper, MuTect, Strelka, and VarScan2—retaining only variants called by at least two callers and filtered for low mapping quality, low base quality, or strand bias. This multi-caller consensus approach significantly improves the sensitivity and specificity of variant detection compared to any single caller alone. The invention further includes a method for distinguishing somatic mutations from germline variants and artifacts arising from somatic hypermutation in the immunoglobulin loci, by comparing tumor and normal samples and excluding variants present in the germline or located within the IGH variable region, which is subject to physiological hypermutation. The system is validated through rigorous concordance analysis against whole-exome sequencing data from 44 samples and fluorescence in situ hybridization data from 22 samples, demonstrating greater than 95% concordance for copy number variants, greater than 90% concordance for translocations, and greater than 98% concordance for single-nucleotide variants. Importantly, the invention enables the detection of previously unreported genetic alterations, including recurrent translocations between IGH and IGLL5, which were not previously recognized as drivers of multiple myeloma. These translocations result in the juxtaposition of IGH enhancer elements with the IGLL5 gene, leading to its dysregulation, and are associated with overexpression of DERL3, a gene located adjacent to IGLL5 on chromosome 22. The IGLL5 translocations are mutually exclusive with RAS pathway mutations, suggesting a distinct pathogenic pathway in a subset of patients. Furthermore, somatic mutations in IGLL5, which are enriched for a signature of activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID)-mediated mutagenesis, occur in the N-terminal region of the protein and are associated with disease progression, as evidenced by a hazard ratio of 1.46 for progression-free survival. The invention further demonstrates that the same probe set used for DNA sequencing can be repurposed for targeted RNA sequencing, allowing for the simultaneous assessment of gene expression levels, fusion transcripts, and allele-specific expression, thereby enabling a multi-omic characterization of the tumor genome and transcriptome from a single sample. The system is designed for scalability and clinical implementation, with library preparation and sequencing protocols compatible with standard laboratory workflows and analysis pipelines that can be automated and integrated into clinical information systems. The invention thus provides a comprehensive, accurate, and clinically actionable genomic profiling solution for multiple myeloma that enables the simultaneous detection of all major classes of genomic alterations, facilitates molecular subtyping, uncovers novel disease drivers, and supports the development of personalized therapeutic strategies.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, a hybrid-capture sequencing library was prepared from genomic DNA extracted from CD138-purified plasma cells and matched peripheral blood mononuclear cells obtained from a patient diagnosed with newly diagnosed multiple myeloma. The DNA was quantified using fluorometric methods and subjected to fragmentation via sonication to produce fragments averaging 180 base pairs in length. The fragmented DNA was then subjected to end-repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation using a commercial library preparation kit, followed by amplification via PCR to generate sufficient material for hybridization. The amplified library was hybridized overnight at 65°C to a custom-designed NimbleGen probe set targeting 3.3 megabases of genomic space, encompassing 465 genes associated with myeloma pathogenesis and the entire immunoglobulin heavy chain (IGH) and immunoglobulin lambda light chain (IGLL) loci. Following hybridization, unbound DNA was washed away, and bound fragments were eluted and amplified for sequencing. The resulting library was sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform using paired-end 125-base reads, achieving a mean sequencing depth of 112-fold in the tumor sample and 118-fold in the matched normal sample. The raw sequencing reads were aligned to the human reference genome GRCh37-lite using the Burrows-Wheeler Aligner (BWA), and duplicate reads were marked and removed using Picard tools. Copy number variants were detected using a modified version of the CopyCAT2 algorithm, which computed the log2 ratio of tumor-to-normal sequencing depth across each probe interval and applied a Gaussian mixture model to estimate the baseline noise distribution from diploid regions. This analysis revealed a gain of chromosome 9, a deletion of chromosome 13q, and a focal amplification on chromosome 1q21, consistent with known high-risk features in multiple myeloma. Single-nucleotide variants were called using a consensus approach integrating results from samtools, SomaticSniper, MuTect, Strelka, and VarScan2, retaining only variants called by at least three callers and filtered for low quality or strand bias. This analysis identified a somatic missense mutation in KRAS at codon 12 (G12D) and a nonsense mutation in TP53 at codon 248 (R248*). Translocations were detected using LUMPY, and the resulting calls were filtered using a machine learning classifier trained on fluorescence in situ hybridization-validated samples. This filtering step identified a canonical t(11;14)(q13;q32) translocation involving IGH and CCND1, with 12 supporting split reads and 18 discordant read pairs. All detected alterations were confirmed by orthogonal methods: the KRAS G12D mutation was verified by Sanger sequencing, the deletion of 13q was confirmed by fluorescence in situ hybridization using a locus-specific probe, and the t(11;14) translocation was confirmed by PCR amplification across the breakpoint junction followed by sequencing. This example demonstrates the ability of the invention to accurately detect multiple classes of genomic alterations in a single assay with high concordance to gold-standard methods.

### Example 2

In this example, a cohort of 95 tumor-normal pairs from patients with multiple myeloma were analyzed using the hybrid-capture sequencing platform described in the invention. The samples were selected to include a balanced representation of disease stages, from newly diagnosed to relapsed/refractory disease, and were matched with germline DNA from peripheral blood. Libraries were prepared and sequenced as described in Example 1, achieving a mean sequencing depth of 104-fold in tumor samples and 107-fold in normal samples. Copy number variant analysis using the Gaussian mixture model-based CopyCAT2 algorithm identified hyperdiploid karyotypes in 38% of samples, characterized by trisomies of chromosomes 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 15, 19, and 21. Non-hyperdiploid samples exhibited frequent deletions of 13q (42%), 17p (28%), and 1p (22%), as well as amplifications of 1q (35%). These findings were consistent with established patterns of genomic instability in multiple myeloma. Single-nucleotide variant analysis revealed that all 95 tumor samples harbored at least one non-silent somatic mutation, with a mean of 20 mutations per sample. The most frequently mutated genes included KRAS (19%), NRAS (17%), DIS3 (12%), FAM46C (11%), and TP53 (9%). Notably, 18% of samples harbored mutations in IGLL5, a gene not previously recognized as a recurrent target in multiple myeloma. These mutations were predominantly missense or nonsense variants clustered within the first 100 amino acids of the protein, outside of any known functional domains, and exhibited a strong enrichment for C-to-T transitions at WRCY motifs, consistent with activation-induced cytidine deaminase activity. Translocation analysis using the machine learning-filtered LUMPY pipeline detected canonical IGH translocations in 31% of samples, including t(11;14) in 14%, t(4;14) in 13%, t(8;14) in 7%, and t(14;20) in 2%. In addition, two novel translocations involving IGH and IGLL5 were identified, with breakpoints located within the IGH constant region and the IGLL5 coding sequence. These translocations were validated by PCR amplification and Sanger sequencing in one sample, confirming the reciprocal exchange of chromosomal segments and the deletion of small regions on both derivative chromosomes. RNA sequencing data from a subset of 84 patients revealed that DERL3, a gene located immediately adjacent to IGLL5 on chromosome 22, was significantly overexpressed in samples harboring IGLL5 alterations, with expression levels exceeding 1.5 times the interquartile range in six cases. These findings demonstrate the ability of the invention to detect not only known alterations but also novel, previously unrecognized genetic events that may contribute to disease pathogenesis.

### Example 3

This example demonstrates the optimization of the bioinformatics pipeline for detecting copy number variants with high specificity. A subset of 44 samples previously subjected to whole-exome sequencing was analyzed using the invention’s CopyCAT2 algorithm with and without the Gaussian mixture model noise estimation. Without the noise model, the algorithm produced 127 candidate copy number variants, of which 48 were false positives as determined by comparison to exome sequencing data. These false positives were predominantly located in regions of low mappability, near centromeres, or in areas with high GC content. When the Gaussian mixture model was applied, the number of candidate variants was reduced to 79, of which only 5 were false positives, resulting in a precision increase from 62% to 94%. The model was trained using a set of 20 diploid regions identified by exome sequencing as having no copy number alteration and with uniform sequencing depth across all samples. The mean and standard deviation of the log2 ratios in these regions were used to define the diploid component of the Gaussian mixture, and a third component representing high-amplitude gains or losses was added to capture true alterations. The algorithm was further refined by excluding probe intervals with fewer than five reads in the normal sample, as these were found to contribute disproportionately to noise. The final model was validated on an independent set of 22 samples with fluorescence in situ hybridization data, achieving 100% concordance for arm-level deletions and amplifications and 96% concordance for focal events. This example illustrates that the invention’s noise modeling approach significantly improves the specificity of copy number variant detection, reducing the burden of false positives and enabling more reliable clinical interpretation.

### Example 4

This example illustrates the optimization of the translocation detection pipeline using machine learning to reduce false positives. LUMPY was initially applied to the 95 tumor-normal pairs, yielding 1,203 putative translocation events. Of these, 987 were located within the IGH locus and were suspected to be artifacts arising from somatic hypermutation or recombination events. To distinguish true translocations from these artifacts, a machine learning classifier was trained using a set of 22 samples with known translocation status determined by fluorescence in situ hybridization. Features extracted for each putative translocation included the number of split reads, the number of discordant read pairs, the distance between breakpoint clusters, the presence of microhomology at the junction, the genomic context (e.g., proximity to repetitive elements or transcriptionally active regions), and the distance from the nearest gene. A random forest classifier was trained using these features, with translocations confirmed by FISH labeled as positive and those absent in FISH labeled as negative. The resulting model achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.97, with optimal thresholds identified for each feature. When applied to the full cohort, the classifier reduced the number of putative translocations from 1,203 to 127, of which 114 were confirmed as true events by manual review or orthogonal validation. The classifier successfully eliminated false positives arising from IGH hypermutation, while retaining all canonical translocations and the two novel IGH-IGLL5 translocations. This example demonstrates that the invention’s machine learning-based filtering strategy dramatically improves the precision of translocation detection without compromising sensitivity, enabling reliable identification of clinically relevant structural variants in a high-noise genomic context.

### Example 5

This example demonstrates the ability of the invention to detect somatic single-nucleotide variants with high sensitivity and specificity. A comparison was performed between the variant calls generated by the invention’s multi-caller consensus approach and those obtained from whole-exome sequencing of 44 samples. The exome sequencing data had been generated using a mean depth of 136-fold, while the targeted sequencing data had a mean depth of 123-fold. Of the 1,450 variants detected in both datasets, 98% showed identical allele frequencies, with a correlation coefficient of R² = 0.85 (p < 2.2e-16). Of the 112 variants detected only in the targeted sequencing data, 79 had allele frequencies below 10%, suggesting they were subclonal or low-level artifacts. Of the 2,113 variants detected only in the exome data, 2,066 had allele frequencies below 10%, indicating they were likely false positives or sequencing artifacts missed by the targeted platform due to lower coverage in certain regions. The invention’s approach retained 99% of variants with allele frequencies above 10%, including all known driver mutations in KRAS, NRAS, TP53, and DIS3. Furthermore, the invention detected 18 novel somatic mutations in IGLL5 that were not called in the exome data due to insufficient coverage of the immunoglobulin lambda locus in the exome capture design. These findings demonstrate that the invention’s targeted approach provides superior sensitivity for disease-relevant genes compared to exome sequencing, while maintaining high specificity and minimizing false positives.

### Example 6

This example demonstrates the effect of sequencing depth on variant detection. Fifteen tumor-normal pairs were subjected to deep sequencing, achieving a mean depth of 1,259-fold in tumor samples and 1,326-fold in normal samples. The variant calls from this deep sequencing were compared to those from the original sequencing at approximately 100-fold depth. After filtering out silent variants, intronic variants, and variants in the IGH variable region, 57 variants were detected in the shallow sequencing and 67 in the deep sequencing. Of the 67 variants detected in the deep sequencing, 63 were also detected in the shallow sequencing, with a correlation coefficient of R² = 0.80 for allele frequency (p < 2.2e-16). The four variants unique to the deep sequencing had allele frequencies below 10%, with three having fewer than three supporting reads in the shallow data. The 14 variants unique to the shallow sequencing were all low-confidence calls with fewer than three supporting reads and were excluded upon re-examination. This example demonstrates that the vast majority of clinically relevant somatic variants are captured at a sequencing depth of approximately 100-fold, and that increasing depth beyond 300-fold yields minimal additional discovery of non-silent variants. This finding supports the cost-effectiveness of the invention’s design, which achieves high diagnostic accuracy at a depth that is feasible for routine clinical implementation.

### Example 7

This example demonstrates the discovery and validation of a novel IGH-IGLL5 translocation. A patient with high-risk multiple myeloma was found to harbor a translocation between chromosome 14 and chromosome 22, with the breakpoint on chromosome 14 located within the IGH constant region at position 106,100,000 and the breakpoint on chromosome 22 located within exon 2 of IGLL5 at position 20,150,000. The translocation was detected by LUMPY with 15 split reads and 22 discordant read pairs. The machine learning classifier assigned a high confidence score of 0.98, indicating a true translocation. PCR amplification using primers flanking the predicted breakpoint successfully generated a product of the expected size (420 base pairs) in the tumor DNA but not in the germline control. Sanger sequencing of this product confirmed the fusion of IGH constant region sequences with IGLL5 exon 2, with a 12-base deletion on the derivative chromosome 14 and a 7-base deletion on the derivative chromosome 22. The presence of one intact copy of each wild-type chromosome was confirmed using primers designed to amplify regions deleted on the derivative chromosomes. RNA sequencing data from this patient showed a 4.2-fold increase in DERL3 expression compared to the median expression in the cohort. This example illustrates the invention’s ability to detect novel, clinically significant translocations that are not identifiable by conventional methods and to validate them using orthogonal molecular techniques.

### Example 8

This example demonstrates the association between IGLL5 mutations and disease progression. A retrospective analysis was performed on 95 patients with available clinical follow-up data. Patients were stratified into two groups based on the presence or absence of non-synonymous mutations in IGLL5. The median progression-free survival for patients with IGLL5 mutations was 22 months, compared to 38 months for patients without such mutations (log-rank test, p = 0.03). The hazard ratio for disease progression in patients with IGLL5 mutations was 1.46 (95% confidence interval: 1.03–2.08). This association remained significant after adjusting for age, ISS stage, and the presence of high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities such as del(17p) and t(4;14). Furthermore, IGLL5 mutations were mutually exclusive with RAS pathway mutations (p = 0.006), suggesting that these represent distinct molecular subtypes. The median variant allele frequency of IGLL5 mutations was 58%, with a first quartile of 39%, indicating that these mutations are likely clonal and present in the founding tumor population. These findings indicate that IGLL5 mutations serve as a novel biomarker of high-risk disease and may be used to guide therapeutic decisions, such as the selection of more intensive regimens or enrollment in clinical trials for novel agents.

### Example 9

This example demonstrates the utility of the invention’s probe set for targeted RNA sequencing. A subset of 20 tumor samples previously analyzed by DNA sequencing were subjected to targeted RNA sequencing using the same probe set. The RNA was extracted from CD138-purified plasma cells, converted to cDNA, and hybridized to the same NimbleGen probe set used for DNA capture. The resulting libraries were sequenced to a mean depth of 85-fold. The RNA sequencing data confirmed the expression of all 465 targeted genes and revealed fusion transcripts consistent with the DNA-detected translocations, including an IGH-IGLL5 fusion transcript in the sample with the validated translocation. Additionally, the RNA data revealed overexpression of DERL3 in five of the six samples with IGLL5 mutations, supporting the hypothesis that IGH translocations dysregulate nearby genes. The RNA sequencing also identified alternative splicing events in DIS3 and FAM46C that were not detectable by DNA sequencing alone. This example demonstrates that the invention’s probe set is not limited to DNA analysis but can be repurposed for integrated multi-omic profiling, enabling the simultaneous assessment of genomic alterations and their transcriptional consequences from a single sample. This capability enhances the clinical utility of the platform by providing a more comprehensive molecular portrait of the disease.
