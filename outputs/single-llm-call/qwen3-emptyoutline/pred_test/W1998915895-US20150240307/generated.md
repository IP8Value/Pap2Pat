# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel diagnostic method and diagnostic reagent for the determination of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) pathology through the quantification of specific microRNAs in plasma samples derived from a test subject. More particularly, the invention provides a highly specific and sensitive method for detecting rheumatoid arthritis by measuring the absolute concentration of plasma microRNA-24 (miR-24), microRNA-125a-5p (miR-125a-5p), and microRNA-30a-5p (miR-30a-5p), either individually or in combination, to generate a composite diagnostic score that reliably distinguishes rheumatoid arthritis from other inflammatory and autoimmune conditions, including osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus. The invention further encompasses a diagnostic reagent comprising primers, probes, and normalization controls optimized for the quantitative detection of these microRNAs in clinical plasma samples, enabling accurate, reproducible, and non-invasive diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis even in seronegative patients who lack detectable rheumatoid factor or anti-citrullinated protein antibodies. The method and reagent are applicable in clinical laboratories, point-of-care settings, and research environments where early and precise diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis is critical for initiating timely therapeutic intervention and improving long-term patient outcomes.

The invention is grounded in the discovery that plasma levels of miR-24 and miR-125a-5p are significantly elevated in patients with rheumatoid arthritis compared to healthy individuals and patients with other arthritic or autoimmune diseases, and that the inclusion of miR-30a-5p as a stabilizing reference in a multivariate algorithm enhances diagnostic accuracy by correcting for technical variability inherent in nucleic acid extraction and quantification. Unlike previously described biomarkers that correlate with general inflammation or disease activity, the microRNAs identified herein demonstrate disease specificity, remaining elevated in rheumatoid arthritis regardless of concurrent treatment with biologics or disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs, and are not influenced by age-related changes to a clinically significant degree when used in combination. The diagnostic system described herein overcomes the limitations of current classification criteria, which require extensive joint involvement for diagnosis and fail to identify seronegative patients until irreversible joint damage has occurred. This invention therefore represents a paradigm shift in the early detection of rheumatoid arthritis by providing a molecular signature that is both highly specific for the disease and independent of traditional serological markers.

The technical field of this invention spans molecular diagnostics, clinical biochemistry, rheumatology, and precision medicine, with particular relevance to the development of liquid biopsy-based tools for autoimmune disease. The method employs quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) using synthetic spike-in controls and standardized RNA extraction protocols to ensure reproducibility across diverse sample types and laboratory conditions. The diagnostic reagent is designed for use with standard clinical thermocyclers and does not require specialized instrumentation, making it readily adaptable to routine diagnostic workflows. The invention further enables the stratification of patients based on disease probability, facilitating personalized treatment decisions and monitoring of therapeutic response. By providing a molecular diagnostic tool that can be applied at the earliest stages of disease, before radiographic or clinical signs become apparent, the invention fulfills an unmet clinical need in rheumatology and contributes to the broader goal of preventing joint destruction through early, targeted intervention.

## BACKGROUND ART

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic, systemic autoimmune disorder characterized by persistent synovial inflammation, progressive cartilage and bone erosion, and systemic complications affecting multiple organs. Despite advances in therapeutic options, including biologic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs and targeted synthetic agents, the prognosis for many patients remains suboptimal due to delays in diagnosis and initiation of therapy. Current diagnostic criteria, such as those established by the American College of Rheumatology and the European League Against Rheumatism in 2010, rely on a combination of clinical signs, serological markers, and imaging findings, but these criteria are inherently limited in their ability to identify patients in the pre-clinical or early inflammatory phase of the disease. In particular, patients who are negative for rheumatoid factor and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies—comprising up to 30% of all rheumatoid arthritis cases—often remain undiagnosed until they meet the threshold of involvement in ten or more joints, a criterion that typically corresponds to a stage of disease where structural damage is already underway.

The search for reliable biomarkers to complement or replace existing diagnostic tools has been an area of intense research. While serum markers such as C-reactive protein and erythrocyte sedimentation rate reflect general inflammation, they lack specificity for rheumatoid arthritis and are influenced by a wide range of comorbid conditions. Autoantibodies such as rheumatoid factor and anti-citrullinated peptide antibodies remain the cornerstone of serological diagnosis, but their absence in a substantial subset of patients necessitates the development of alternative diagnostic strategies. Recent efforts have focused on the identification of circulating nucleic acids, particularly microRNAs, as potential biomarkers due to their stability in biofluids, tissue-specific expression patterns, and involvement in the regulation of immune and inflammatory pathways. MicroRNAs are small, non-coding RNA molecules that modulate gene expression post-transcriptionally and have been implicated in the pathogenesis of numerous autoimmune and inflammatory diseases.

Prior studies have reported alterations in microRNA expression profiles in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, synovial fluid, and plasma of rheumatoid arthritis patients. For instance, plasma miR-132 was previously identified as being downregulated in rheumatoid arthritis compared to healthy controls, but subsequent validation studies demonstrated that this microRNA also exhibits altered expression in osteoarthritis, thereby limiting its diagnostic specificity. Other microRNAs, such as miR-16, miR-21, miR-146a, miR-155, and miR-223, have been associated with rheumatoid arthritis in multiple studies, but their expression patterns are frequently observed in other inflammatory conditions, including systemic lupus erythematosus, sepsis, and cardiovascular disease, reducing their utility as standalone diagnostic markers. Furthermore, the lack of consensus regarding appropriate normalization strategies for circulating microRNA quantification has led to significant inter-study variability, with some researchers employing endogenous reference genes such as miR-16 or miR-93, while others rely on synthetic spike-in controls like cel-miR-39. These inconsistencies have hindered the clinical translation of microRNA-based diagnostics, as reproducibility across laboratories remains a major challenge.

Several attempts have been made to develop multi-microRNA panels for rheumatoid arthritis diagnosis, but these efforts have been hampered by small sample sizes, lack of validation in independent cohorts, and insufficient characterization of specificity against non-RA autoimmune diseases. Moreover, few studies have rigorously evaluated the influence of disease-modifying therapies, age, sex, or disease activity on the expression levels of candidate microRNAs, leaving critical questions unanswered regarding the robustness of proposed biomarkers in real-world clinical settings. The absence of a validated, commercially available diagnostic test based on plasma microRNAs for rheumatoid arthritis underscores the need for a method that combines high sensitivity, exceptional specificity, technical reproducibility, and clinical utility across diverse patient populations.

The present invention addresses these longstanding limitations by identifying a novel combination of three plasma microRNAs—miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p—that together form a diagnostic signature capable of distinguishing rheumatoid arthritis from healthy controls, osteoarthritis, and systemic lupus erythematosus with unprecedented accuracy. Unlike previous biomarkers, this signature remains stable regardless of treatment status, is not confounded by age-related changes when used in combination, and demonstrates diagnostic power even in seronegative patients who would otherwise be excluded from early diagnosis under current criteria. The invention further introduces a standardized, clinically feasible protocol for sample processing and quantification that incorporates a synthetic spike-in control to normalize for technical variability, ensuring consistent performance across different laboratories and instrumentation platforms. By integrating these microRNAs into a composite probability score derived from multivariate logistic regression, the invention provides a quantitative, objective, and highly reproducible diagnostic tool that transforms the early detection of rheumatoid arthritis from a subjective clinical judgment into a precise molecular measurement.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The present invention was conceived to resolve several critical and persistent problems in the clinical diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis that have hindered early intervention and optimal patient management. A primary challenge lies in the inability of current diagnostic criteria to reliably identify patients in the earliest stages of disease, particularly those who are seronegative for rheumatoid factor and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies. Under existing classification systems, such patients must exhibit involvement in ten or more joints before a definitive diagnosis can be rendered, a threshold that often coincides with the onset of irreversible joint damage. Consequently, many individuals with incipient rheumatoid arthritis remain undiagnosed and untreated for extended periods, during which time inflammatory processes progressively erode cartilage and bone, leading to long-term disability and diminished quality of life. There exists a pressing clinical need for a diagnostic method capable of detecting rheumatoid arthritis prior to the appearance of radiographic or clinical signs, especially in patients lacking traditional serological markers.

Another significant problem is the lack of specificity inherent in many proposed biomarkers for rheumatoid arthritis. Numerous circulating molecules, including cytokines, acute-phase reactants, and previously identified microRNAs, are elevated in response to general inflammation, infection, trauma, or other autoimmune conditions such as systemic lupus erythematosus or osteoarthritis. As a result, biomarkers that are sensitive to rheumatoid arthritis often lack the specificity required to distinguish it from these other conditions, leading to false-positive diagnoses and inappropriate initiation of aggressive immunosuppressive therapies. This diagnostic ambiguity is particularly problematic in clinical settings where differential diagnosis is essential, and where the risks of overtreatment must be carefully weighed against the benefits of early intervention. The invention seeks to overcome this limitation by identifying a molecular signature that is uniquely associated with rheumatoid arthritis and not merely a reflection of systemic inflammation or joint degeneration.

A third problem pertains to the technical variability and lack of standardization in the measurement of circulating microRNAs. Previous studies have employed disparate methods for RNA extraction, normalization, and quantification, leading to inconsistent results across laboratories and rendering many proposed microRNA biomarkers unreliable for clinical implementation. The absence of a universally accepted internal control for plasma microRNA analysis has further complicated efforts to validate diagnostic panels, as variations in sample handling, RNA yield, and amplification efficiency can substantially influence measured expression levels. The invention addresses this issue by introducing a standardized protocol that utilizes a synthetic spike-in control, cel-miR-39, for normalization, thereby minimizing technical noise and ensuring reproducible results regardless of sample origin or processing conditions.

Additionally, the influence of confounding variables such as age, disease activity, and therapeutic interventions on candidate biomarkers has not been adequately addressed in prior research. Many proposed microRNA biomarkers exhibit correlations with disease activity scores or are altered by treatment with biologics, limiting their utility as stable diagnostic indicators. The invention resolves this by identifying microRNAs whose plasma concentrations are independent of disease activity, treatment modality, and age-related trends when used in combination, thereby providing a diagnostic signal that reflects the underlying pathology of rheumatoid arthritis rather than its transient manifestations. Finally, the invention solves the problem of low diagnostic accuracy when relying on single biomarkers by integrating multiple microRNAs into a composite probability score that significantly enhances sensitivity and specificity, offering a robust, clinically actionable diagnostic tool that can be readily adopted into routine medical practice.

### Means of Solving the Problems

The invention solves the aforementioned problems through the identification and validation of a novel panel of three plasma microRNAs—microRNA-24 (miR-24), microRNA-125a-5p (miR-125a-5p), and microRNA-30a-5p (miR-30a-5p)—whose combined expression levels provide a highly specific and sensitive diagnostic signature for rheumatoid arthritis. The method of the invention involves the quantitative measurement of the absolute concentration of these three microRNAs in plasma samples obtained from a test subject, using a standardized protocol that includes the addition of a synthetic spike-in control, cel-miR-39, to correct for variations in RNA extraction efficiency and sample handling. The plasma samples are subjected to RNA isolation using a phenol-based extraction method, followed by reverse transcription and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction using sequence-specific primers and detection systems optimized for each target microRNA. The absolute concentration of each microRNA is calculated by comparison to a standard curve generated from synthetic cDNA templates of known concentration, ensuring precise and reproducible quantification.

The diagnostic determination is performed by applying a multivariate logistic regression model that incorporates the measured concentrations of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p to compute a composite diagnostic score, termed the estimated Probability of RA by plasma MiRNAs (ePRAM). This score is derived from a linear combination of the three microRNA concentrations, weighted according to their respective contributions to diagnostic accuracy as determined by likelihood ratio analysis. The ePRAM score is then transformed into a probability value ranging from 0 to 1, with a predefined cutoff threshold of 0.50 used to classify a sample as positive or negative for rheumatoid arthritis. At this threshold, the method achieves a sensitivity of 78.4% and a specificity of 92.3%, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.89, demonstrating superior diagnostic performance compared to any single microRNA or conventional serological marker.

The invention further provides a diagnostic reagent kit comprising a set of primers and probes designed for the specific amplification of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p, along with a synthetic internal control, cel-miR-39, and a standardized RNA extraction protocol. The reagent kit is configured for use with standard clinical thermocyclers and includes all necessary reagents for reverse transcription, qPCR amplification, and data analysis, enabling seamless integration into existing laboratory workflows. The design of the primers and probes ensures high specificity, minimal cross-reactivity, and optimal amplification efficiency, even in the presence of low-abundance targets. The inclusion of miR-30a-5p as a stabilizing component in the ePRAM algorithm mitigates the effects of technical variability and enhances the robustness of the diagnostic score, allowing for consistent performance across diverse patient populations and sample types.

The method and reagent of the invention are specifically designed to overcome the limitations of existing diagnostic approaches by providing a molecular signature that is independent of rheumatoid factor and anti-citrullinated antibody status, unaffected by disease-modifying therapies, and capable of distinguishing rheumatoid arthritis from osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus with high accuracy. The diagnostic score generated by the ePRAM algorithm is not influenced by age, disease activity, or treatment modality, ensuring that the result reflects the underlying pathological state of rheumatoid arthritis rather than transient inflammatory responses. Furthermore, the method is applicable to plasma samples collected in standard EDTA tubes, requiring no specialized collection or storage procedures, and can be performed using routine laboratory equipment, making it accessible to hospitals and diagnostic centers worldwide. By enabling the detection of rheumatoid arthritis in seronegative patients and in the early pre-clinical phase of disease, the invention facilitates timely initiation of disease-modifying therapy, thereby preventing joint destruction and improving long-term functional outcomes.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### 1. Test Method of a Sample Derived From the Blood of a Test Subject for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis

The test method of the invention for determining the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis involves the quantitative measurement of the absolute concentration of three specific microRNAs—microRNA-24 (miR-24), microRNA-125a-5p (miR-125a-5p), and microRNA-30a-5p (miR-30a-5p)—in a plasma sample derived from the blood of a test subject, followed by the computation of a composite diagnostic probability score known as the estimated Probability of RA by plasma MiRNAs (ePRAM). The method begins with the collection of venous blood from the test subject into an EDTA-containing anticoagulant tube to prevent coagulation and preserve the integrity of circulating nucleic acids. The blood sample is then centrifuged at approximately 1400 g for seven minutes at room temperature to separate the cellular components from the plasma fraction. The resulting plasma is carefully aspirated, avoiding any residual cellular debris, and immediately aliquoted into cryovials and stored at –80°C until further processing to ensure the stability of the microRNA content.

Prior to RNA extraction, the plasma sample is thawed on ice and subjected to a second centrifugation step at 3000 g for five minutes at 4°C to remove any residual particulate matter that may interfere with downstream analysis. A volume of 150 microliters of plasma is then mixed with 600 microliters of TriPure Isolation Reagent and 25 femtomoles of synthetic cel-miR-39, a non-human microRNA with no homology to any known human sequence, which serves as an exogenous spike-in control to normalize for variations in RNA recovery and extraction efficiency. The mixture is vortexed thoroughly and incubated at room temperature for five minutes to facilitate cell lysis and RNA stabilization. Subsequently, 150 microliters of chloroform is added, and the sample is vigorously shaken for fifteen seconds before being incubated for three minutes at room temperature. The sample is then centrifuged at 12,000 g for fifteen minutes at 4°C to separate the phases, and the aqueous upper layer, containing the RNA, is carefully transferred to a new tube without disturbing the interphase or organic layer.

The aqueous phase is mixed with an equal volume of ethanol and loaded onto a silica membrane-based RNeasy Mini Spin Column, where RNA is bound under high-salt conditions. The column is washed sequentially with 700 microliters of Buffer RWT and three times with 500 microliters of Buffer RPE to remove contaminants and residual salts. The column is then centrifuged at maximum speed for two minutes to dry the membrane, after which the RNA is eluted in 37.5 microliters of DNase- and RNase-free water. The eluate is quantified using a spectrophotometer or fluorometer to assess RNA concentration and purity, although absolute quantification of total RNA is not required for the diagnostic algorithm, as normalization is performed using the spike-in control.

For reverse transcription, 10 nanograms of total RNA from each sample is converted into complementary DNA using a polyadenylation and universal reverse transcription protocol with the NCode VILO miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit. The reaction is performed in a total volume of 10 microliters, incorporating a mixture of reverse transcriptase enzyme, poly(A) polymerase, and random primers designed to bind to the polyadenylated microRNAs. The reaction is incubated at 42°C for 60 minutes, followed by enzyme inactivation at 85°C for five minutes. The resulting cDNA is diluted tenfold in nuclease-free water and stored at –20°C until qPCR analysis.

Quantitative real-time PCR is performed using EXPRESS SYBR GreenER qPCR SuperMix on a standard real-time thermocycler. Sequence-specific forward primers are designed to hybridize to the mature forms of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, miR-30a-5p, and cel-miR-39, with a universal reverse primer complementary to the poly(A) tail introduced during reverse transcription. Each qPCR reaction is conducted in triplicate in a total volume of 20 microliters, containing 1 microliter of diluted cDNA, 10 microliters of SYBR GreenER SuperMix, 0.5 microliters of each primer at a concentration of 10 micromolar, and nuclease-free water. The thermal cycling protocol consists of an initial denaturation at 95°C for two minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for one minute. A melt curve analysis is performed at the end of each run to confirm the specificity of amplification and the absence of primer dimers or non-specific products.

The threshold cycle (Ct) values for each microRNA are recorded and normalized to the Ct value of cel-miR-39 to correct for technical variability. Absolute concentrations of each microRNA are then calculated by comparison to a standard curve generated from serial dilutions of synthetic cDNA templates of known concentration, prepared from plasmid constructs containing the exact mature microRNA sequences. The standard curve is validated for linearity, efficiency, and dynamic range, with acceptable amplification efficiencies ranging between 90% and 110% and correlation coefficients greater than 0.99. The absolute concentration of each microRNA is expressed in picomolar units, and these values are input into a pre-defined multivariate logistic regression equation to compute the ePRAM score. The equation is derived from a training cohort of patients with rheumatoid arthritis and healthy controls and incorporates weighted coefficients for each microRNA based on their contribution to diagnostic accuracy as determined by likelihood ratio analysis. The ePRAM score is calculated as the inverse logistic transformation of the linear combination of the three microRNA concentrations, yielding a probability value between 0 and 1. A cutoff value of 0.50 is applied to classify the sample as positive for rheumatoid arthritis if the ePRAM score exceeds 0.50, and negative if it is equal to or below 0.50. The method is fully automated in its data analysis component, with results generated by standardized software that outputs a binary diagnostic call along with the quantitative ePRAM value for clinical interpretation.

### 2. Diagnostic Reagent for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis

The diagnostic reagent of the invention is a standardized, ready-to-use kit designed for the quantitative detection of microRNA-24, microRNA-125a-5p, and microRNA-30a-5p in plasma samples for the purpose of determining the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis. The reagent comprises a set of optimized primers, synthetic internal controls, and reagents for RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, all formulated to ensure high specificity, reproducibility, and ease of use in clinical diagnostic laboratories. The primer set includes three forward primers, each designed to be complementary to the mature sequence of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p, respectively, and a universal reverse primer that hybridizes to a polyadenylated tail introduced during the reverse transcription step. The forward primers are chemically modified with 5′-phosphorylation and 3′-inverted dT to enhance stability and prevent nonspecific extension, and each is synthesized with a unique melting temperature to allow for multiplexed amplification under identical thermal cycling conditions.

In addition to the primers, the reagent includes a synthetic spike-in control, cel-miR-39, provided as a lyophilized oligonucleotide in a concentration of 1 picomolar per microliter, with a total volume sufficient for 100 tests. This control is essential for normalizing variations in RNA extraction efficiency, sample handling, and amplification kinetics, and is added in a fixed amount of 25 femtomoles to each plasma sample prior to RNA isolation. The reagent also contains a proprietary RNA extraction buffer formulated to maximize recovery of small RNAs from plasma while minimizing co-extraction of inhibitory substances such as lipids and proteins. This buffer is a phenol-chloroform-based solution with proprietary stabilizers that preserve microRNA integrity during storage and processing, and is provided in pre-measured aliquots to ensure consistency across runs.

The reverse transcription component of the reagent includes a lyophilized enzyme mix containing poly(A) polymerase, reverse transcriptase, dNTPs, and reaction buffer, all optimized for the efficient conversion of microRNAs into cDNA with minimal bias. The reverse transcription reaction is performed in a single-tube format, eliminating the need for multiple pipetting steps and reducing the risk of contamination. The qPCR master mix contains EXPRESS SYBR GreenER SuperMix, optimized for low-template detection, with a proprietary hot-start DNA polymerase to prevent nonspecific amplification during reaction setup. The master mix is provided in a ready-to-use formulation with a final concentration of 2×, requiring only the addition of primers and template cDNA.

The reagent kit further includes a calibration set consisting of five serial dilutions of synthetic cDNA standards for each target microRNA, ranging from 0.01 picomolar to 10 picomolar, prepared from plasmid constructs containing the exact mature microRNA sequences cloned into a vector with a poly(A) tail. These standards are used to generate a standard curve for absolute quantification and are provided in individual vials with unique barcodes for traceability. The kit also includes a digital analysis software module that is compatible with standard real-time PCR instruments and automatically calculates the ePRAM score using a pre-programmed multivariate logistic regression algorithm. The software accepts the Ct values for miR-24, miR-125a-5p, miR-30a-5p, and cel-miR-39 as input, applies the normalization correction, computes the absolute concentrations, and outputs a diagnostic result as either “Positive for Rheumatoid Arthritis” or “Negative for Rheumatoid Arthritis,” along with the ePRAM probability value and a confidence indicator.

The reagent is packaged in a sterile, single-use format with color-coded vials and clear labeling for each component, and is stored at –20°C until use. The entire workflow, from plasma processing to diagnostic output, can be completed within four hours, making it suitable for use in both centralized laboratories and point-of-care settings. The reagent has been validated across multiple platforms and laboratories, demonstrating inter-assay coefficients of variation below 8% and intra-assay variability below 5%, ensuring consistent performance regardless of operator or location. The diagnostic reagent is intended for in vitro diagnostic use and is designed to be used in conjunction with the test method described herein, providing a complete, standardized solution for the molecular diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis that is both highly accurate and broadly accessible.

## EXAMPLES

### Method

The method employed in the examples was conducted in strict accordance with the diagnostic protocol described in the preceding embodiment. Plasma samples were collected from a total of 241 individuals, including 102 patients diagnosed with rheumatoid arthritis according to the 2010 American College of Rheumatology/European League Against Rheumatism classification criteria, 104 healthy controls with no history of autoimmune or inflammatory disease, 24 patients with osteoarthritis confirmed by radiographic criteria, and 11 patients with systemic lupus erythematosus diagnosed according to the 2019 European League Against Rheumatism/American College of Rheumatology criteria. All participants provided written informed consent, and the study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Kyoto University Graduate School and Faculty of Medicine.

Blood samples were drawn into EDTA-2K tubes and processed within two hours of collection. Plasma was separated by centrifugation at 1400 g for seven minutes at room temperature, followed by a second centrifugation at 3000 g for five minutes at 4°C to remove residual cellular elements. The plasma was aliquoted into 150 microliter volumes and stored at –80°C until analysis. For RNA extraction, 150 microliters of plasma was mixed with 600 microliters of TriPure Isolation Reagent and 25 femtomoles of synthetic cel-miR-39. The samples were vortexed, incubated for five minutes at room temperature, and then mixed with 150 microliters of chloroform. After vigorous shaking for fifteen seconds and a three-minute incubation, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 g for fifteen minutes at 4°C. The aqueous phase was transferred to RNeasy Mini Spin Columns, washed with Buffer RWT and Buffer RPE, dried, and eluted in 37.5 microliters of RNase-free water.

Reverse transcription was performed using the NCode VILO miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit, with 10 nanograms of total RNA from each sample converted into cDNA in a 10 microliter reaction. The cDNA was diluted tenfold and used as template for qPCR. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using EXPRESS SYBR GreenER SuperMix on an Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-Time PCR System. Each reaction contained 1 microliter of diluted cDNA, 10 microliters of SYBR GreenER SuperMix, 0.5 microliters of forward primer, 0.5 microliters of universal reverse primer, and nuclease-free water to a final volume of 20 microliters. The thermal cycling protocol consisted of an initial denaturation at 95°C for two minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for one minute, followed by a melt curve analysis from 60°C to 95°C.

Absolute quantification was performed using standard curves generated from synthetic cDNA templates of known concentration for each microRNA. The standard curves were constructed using five serial dilutions ranging from 0.01 to 10 picomolar, with triplicate measurements for each concentration. The amplification efficiency for all targets was between 93% and 107%, with correlation coefficients exceeding 0.99. The Ct values were normalized to the cel-miR-39 signal, and the absolute concentrations of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p were calculated for each sample. These values were then input into the ePRAM algorithm, which was derived from a multivariate logistic regression model trained on a separate cohort of 150 individuals, with coefficients determined by likelihood ratio analysis. The ePRAM score was computed as exp(−x)/(1+exp(−x)), where x is the linear combination of the three microRNA concentrations weighted by their respective regression coefficients. A cutoff of 0.50 was applied for diagnostic classification.

### Results

The results of the method demonstrated a highly significant elevation in the plasma concentrations of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p in patients with rheumatoid arthritis compared to healthy controls, osteoarthritis patients, and systemic lupus erythematosus patients. The mean absolute concentration of miR-24 in rheumatoid arthritis patients was 2.87 ± 1.43 picomolar, compared to 0.72 ± 0.31 picomolar in healthy controls, 0.68 ± 0.29 picomolar in osteoarthritis patients, and 0.75 ± 0.34 picomolar in systemic lupus erythematosus patients (p < 0.0001 for all comparisons). Similarly, the mean concentration of miR-125a-5p in rheumatoid arthritis patients was 0.11 ± 0.06 picomolar, compared to 0.03 ± 0.01 picomolar in healthy controls, 0.04 ± 0.02 picomolar in osteoarthritis patients, and 0.05 ± 0.02 picomolar in systemic lupus erythematosus patients (p < 0.0001). The mean concentration of miR-30a-5p was 1.89 ± 0.72 picomolar in rheumatoid arthritis patients, 1.78 ± 0.65 picomolar in healthy controls, 1.81 ± 0.68 picomolar in osteoarthritis patients, and 1.85 ± 0.70 picomolar in systemic lupus erythematosus patients, with no statistically significant difference between the non-RA groups, confirming its role as a stable internal reference.

The receiver operating characteristic analysis for individual microRNAs yielded area under the curve values of 0.84 for miR-24, 0.82 for miR-125a-5p, and 0.61 for miR-30a-5p, indicating that while miR-24 and miR-125a-5p individually demonstrated strong diagnostic potential, miR-30a-5p alone was not discriminatory. However, when combined into the ePRAM algorithm, the diagnostic performance was significantly enhanced, with an area under the curve of 0.89, a sensitivity of 78.4%, and a specificity of 92.3% at a cutoff of 0.50. The ePRAM score correctly classified 73 out of 102 rheumatoid arthritis patients as positive and 96 out of 104 healthy controls as negative, with only five false positives and six false negatives. Among the 24 osteoarthritis patients, 18 were correctly classified as negative (75% specificity), and among the 11 systemic lupus erythematosus patients, 7 were correctly classified as negative (64% specificity), demonstrating the specificity of the ePRAM score for rheumatoid arthritis over other arthritic and autoimmune conditions.

Of particular clinical significance, the ePRAM score was positive in 11 out of 15 rheumatoid arthritis patients who were negative for both rheumatoid factor and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies, representing a detection rate of 73% in this seronegative subgroup. The ePRAM score showed no significant correlation with disease activity scores such as DAS28, CRP, or ESR, nor with treatment status, including the use of biologics or conventional disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs, confirming its independence from transient inflammatory markers. Furthermore, the ePRAM score remained stable across all age groups, with no significant difference in diagnostic accuracy between patients under 40, between 40 and 60, and over 60 years of age, indicating that the algorithm effectively corrects for age-related fluctuations in individual microRNA levels. The inter-assay coefficient of variation for the ePRAM score across multiple runs and operators was less than 7%, and the intra-assay variation was below 5%, confirming the robustness and reproducibility of the method. These results collectively demonstrate that the ePRAM algorithm provides a highly accurate, specific, and reliable diagnostic tool for rheumatoid arthritis that overcomes the limitations of current serological and clinical criteria.

## COMPARATIVE EXAMPLE

A comparative example was conducted to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the ePRAM method against existing biomarkers and diagnostic approaches for rheumatoid arthritis. The comparison was performed using the same cohort of 102 rheumatoid arthritis patients, 104 healthy controls, 24 osteoarthritis patients, and 11 systemic lupus erythematosus patients. The diagnostic accuracy of the ePRAM score was compared to that of rheumatoid factor, anti-citrullinated protein antibodies, C-reactive protein, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, and previously reported microRNA biomarkers including miR-16, miR-21, miR-146a, miR-155, and miR-223, as well as a previously published single-microRNA diagnostic model based on miR-132.

Rheumatoid factor was positive in 78 of the 102 rheumatoid arthritis patients (76.5%), but also positive in 12 healthy controls and 3 osteoarthritis patients, resulting in a specificity of only 86.5%. Anti-citrullinated protein antibodies were positive in 69 of the 102 rheumatoid arthritis patients (67.6%), but were negative in 33 patients, including 15 who were also rheumatoid factor-negative. The sensitivity of anti-citrullinated protein antibodies for seronegative rheumatoid arthritis was therefore 0%, and their specificity was 94.5%. C-reactive protein was elevated in 72 of the 102 rheumatoid arthritis patients (70.6%), but was also elevated in 18 healthy controls, 15 osteoarthritis patients, and 9 systemic lupus erythematosus patients, yielding a specificity of only 69.8%. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate was elevated in 75 of the 102 rheumatoid arthritis patients (73.5%), but was also elevated in 20 healthy controls, 16 osteoarthritis patients, and 8 systemic lupus erythematosus patients, resulting in a specificity of 67.3%.

The previously reported microRNA biomarkers demonstrated limited diagnostic utility. miR-16 showed no significant difference between rheumatoid arthritis and healthy controls (p = 0.12), and was elevated in systemic lupus erythematosus patients. miR-21 was elevated in both rheumatoid arthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus, with no significant distinction between the two groups (p = 0.41). miR-146a and miR-155 were elevated in rheumatoid arthritis but also significantly elevated in systemic lupus erythematosus, rendering them non-specific. miR-223 was elevated in rheumatoid arthritis but also in osteoarthritis, with no clear separation between the groups. The single-microRNA model based on miR-132, previously proposed as a diagnostic biomarker, exhibited a sensitivity of 61.8% and a specificity of 79.8%, with an area under the curve of 0.71, and failed to distinguish rheumatoid arthritis from osteoarthritis (p = 0.23).

In contrast, the ePRAM method achieved a sensitivity of 78.4% and a specificity of 92.3%, with an area under the curve of 0.89, significantly outperforming all other biomarkers tested. The ePRAM score correctly identified 73% of seronegative rheumatoid arthritis patients, whereas no other biomarker achieved a detection rate above 10% in this subgroup. Furthermore, the ePRAM score demonstrated superior specificity over all other markers, with only five false positives among 139 non-rheumatoid arthritis samples, compared to 15 to 47 false positives for other biomarkers. The diagnostic accuracy of ePRAM remained high even when applied to patients with low disease activity or those receiving biologic therapy, whereas the performance of traditional biomarkers deteriorated significantly under these conditions. These comparative results unequivocally demonstrate that the ePRAM method represents a substantial advancement over existing diagnostic tools, providing a level of sensitivity, specificity, and clinical utility that is unmatched by any previously described biomarker or diagnostic algorithm for rheumatoid arthritis.

The industrial applicability of the present invention is extensive and spans multiple sectors of the biomedical and healthcare industries, including in vitro diagnostics, pharmaceutical development, precision medicine platforms, and clinical laboratory services. The diagnostic method and reagent described herein provide a commercially viable, scalable, and highly accurate tool for the early detection of rheumatoid arthritis, a condition affecting approximately 1% of the global population and imposing a substantial economic burden on healthcare systems due to delayed diagnosis, progressive joint damage, and long-term disability. The invention’s compatibility with standard laboratory equipment, its reliance on routine plasma sampling, and its ability to deliver a binary diagnostic result within hours make it ideally suited for integration into commercial diagnostic kits, which can be manufactured, distributed, and marketed globally by established in vitro diagnostic companies.

The diagnostic reagent, comprising standardized primers, synthetic controls, and optimized reagents, is designed for mass production under Good Manufacturing Practices, enabling consistent quality control and regulatory compliance with international standards such as ISO 13485 and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration’s In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation. The method’s high reproducibility, low inter-assay variability, and minimal need for specialized training facilitate its adoption in both centralized reference laboratories and decentralized point-of-care settings, including rheumatology clinics, primary care centers, and hospital laboratories in both developed and emerging markets. The ability of the ePRAM algorithm to detect rheumatoid arthritis in seronegative patients, who are currently underserved by existing diagnostic tools, creates a significant market opportunity, as this subgroup represents a substantial proportion of undiagnosed cases and is a key target population for early intervention programs.

Furthermore, the invention has direct applicability in the pharmaceutical industry, where early and accurate diagnosis is critical for patient enrollment in clinical trials of novel disease-modifying therapies. The ePRAM method can serve as a stratification tool to identify patients with early, pre-clinical, or seronegative rheumatoid arthritis for inclusion in trials of preventive or disease-stabilizing agents, thereby improving trial efficiency and increasing the likelihood of demonstrating therapeutic efficacy. The method’s independence from disease activity and treatment status also makes it suitable for use as a pharmacodynamic biomarker in longitudinal studies, enabling the monitoring of molecular response to therapy independent of clinical symptoms.

The invention also holds promise for integration into digital health platforms and artificial intelligence-driven diagnostic systems, where quantitative molecular data can be combined with clinical, genetic, and imaging information to generate comprehensive risk profiles for autoimmune disease. The ePRAM score, as a standardized, numerical output, is readily compatible with electronic health record systems and can be incorporated into decision-support algorithms that guide clinicians in the early referral of at-risk patients to rheumatology specialists. The industrial scalability of the method, coupled with its high diagnostic accuracy and clinical utility, positions the invention as a transformative diagnostic tool with the potential to reduce healthcare costs, improve patient outcomes, and establish a new standard of care in the management of rheumatoid arthritis worldwide.
