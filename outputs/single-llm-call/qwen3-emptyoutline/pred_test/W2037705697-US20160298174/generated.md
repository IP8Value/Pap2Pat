# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and genetic analysis, specifically to the use of engineered DNA polymerases with enhanced mismatch extension selectivity for the detection of single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), epigenetic modifications such as DNA methylation, and human leukocyte antigen (HLA) typing. The invention encompasses a novel variant of the KlenTaq DNA polymerase, designated R660V, which exhibits significantly improved discrimination between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes at the 3′ terminus during DNA synthesis. This property enables highly specific amplification of target sequences in allele-specific amplification (ASA), methylation-specific PCR (MSP), and sequence-specific primed PCR (PCR-SSP) without the need for modified primers, specialized reagents, or complex instrumentation. The invention is particularly useful in clinical diagnostics, personalized medicine, transfusion medicine, oncology, and forensic genetics, where accurate, rapid, and cost-effective genotyping from complex biological samples—including whole blood—is required. The engineered polymerase is suitable for integration into real-time PCR platforms, endpoint PCR assays, multiplexed detection systems, and point-of-care diagnostic devices, thereby expanding the accessibility and reliability of genetic testing in resource-limited settings.

## BACKGROUND OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The detection of genetic variations at the single-nucleotide level has become a cornerstone of modern molecular diagnostics, enabling the identification of disease-associated polymorphisms, pharmacogenomic profiles, epigenetic alterations, and tissue compatibility markers. Among the most prevalent forms of genetic variation are single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), which occur at a frequency of approximately one in every 300 base pairs in the human genome. These variations, particularly those located in coding regions or regulatory elements, can profoundly influence protein function, drug metabolism, disease susceptibility, and therapeutic response. For example, the prothrombin G20210A SNP is a well-documented risk factor for venous thromboembolism, while methylation of the Septin 9 gene promoter serves as a clinically validated biomarker for early-stage colorectal cancer. The accurate detection of such variants is critical for early diagnosis, risk stratification, and individualized treatment planning.

Traditional methods for SNP detection, such as Sanger sequencing and microarray-based genotyping, offer high accuracy but are often cost-prohibitive, time-intensive, and require specialized equipment and extensive sample preparation. In contrast, allele-specific amplification (ASA) using real-time PCR has emerged as a powerful, accessible, and scalable alternative. ASA exploits the inherent fidelity of DNA polymerases to discriminate between perfectly matched and mismatched primer-template hybrids at the 3′ end, where a single base-pair mismatch can drastically reduce or abolish extension efficiency. When coupled with fluorescent dyes such as SYBR Green I, ASA allows for real-time monitoring of amplification, enabling direct genotyping without post-PCR processing. However, the utility of ASA is fundamentally limited by the intrinsic selectivity of conventional DNA polymerases, such as wild-type Taq or AmpliTaq Gold, which often exhibit insufficient discrimination between matched and mismatched substrates, leading to false-positive amplification and reduced diagnostic accuracy.

To overcome this limitation, numerous strategies have been explored, including the use of modified primers with locked nucleic acids (LNAs), 2′-O-methyl RNA, or peptide nucleic acids (PNAs) to enhance binding specificity. While effective, these approaches increase reagent complexity, cost, and variability in hybridization kinetics, limiting their broad applicability. Alternatively, the employment of high-fidelity polymerases such as Pfu or Phusion has been proposed, but these enzymes, derived from archaeal species, often exhibit slower extension rates, reduced processivity, and diminished performance under standard PCR conditions, rendering them unsuitable for rapid, high-throughput ASA applications. Furthermore, many of these polymerases are inhibited by common biological contaminants present in crude samples such as whole blood, serum, or tissue homogenates, necessitating time-consuming DNA extraction protocols that are incompatible with point-of-care diagnostics.

The development of engineered DNA polymerases with enhanced mismatch discrimination represents a more elegant and universally applicable solution. Prior studies have demonstrated that mutations within conserved structural motifs of DNA polymerases—particularly in the palm domain—can modulate the enzyme’s ability to accommodate non-canonical base pairs at the primer terminus. Notably, substitutions of polar residues with hydrophobic amino acids in the vicinity of the active site have been shown to restrict the conformational flexibility required for mismatch extension, thereby increasing selectivity. For instance, mutations in the Taq DNA polymerase at positions corresponding to Gln and His in motif C were found to enhance discrimination, and similar principles were later extended to the Pfu polymerase. However, these earlier efforts were largely confined to a limited set of residues and did not systematically explore the functional consequences of mutating basic amino acids that directly interact with the phosphate backbone of the primer strand, despite their known role in stabilizing the primer-template complex in the closed conformation.

The KlenTaq DNA polymerase, a truncated variant of Taq polymerase lacking the 5′→3′ exonuclease domain, offers a superior platform for such engineering due to its enhanced thermostability, reduced error rate, and retained processivity compared to wild-type Taq. Structural analyses of KlenTaq have identified five conserved basic residues—R487, K508, R536, R587, and R660—that form critical electrostatic interactions with the phosphate backbone of the primer strand in the closed conformation during nucleotide incorporation. These residues are positioned at varying distances from the catalytic center, offering a unique opportunity to probe the relationship between primer backbone contact and mismatch discrimination. Previous attempts to engineer selectivity in KlenTaq had not systematically targeted these residues, nor had they evaluated the resulting variants in clinically relevant contexts such as direct amplification from whole blood, multiplexed SNP detection, HLA typing, or methylation analysis.

The present invention addresses these longstanding limitations by disclosing a novel KlenTaq variant, R660V, generated through saturation mutagenesis and high-throughput screening, which exhibits unprecedented levels of mismatch extension selectivity while maintaining robust enzymatic activity, thermostability, and resistance to biological inhibitors. This variant enables highly specific detection of SNPs, methylation states, and HLA alleles directly from unprocessed biological samples, eliminating the need for DNA purification, primer modification, or specialized instrumentation. The invention further demonstrates the utility of this polymerase in multiplexed assays, clinical HLA typing, and epigenetic diagnostics, establishing it as a transformative tool for next-generation molecular diagnostics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel DNA polymerase variant derived from KlenTaq, characterized by a substitution of arginine at position 660 with valine, designated as KlenTaq R660V. This variant exhibits a marked enhancement in the ability to discriminate between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes at the 3′ terminus during DNA synthesis, thereby enabling highly specific amplification in allele-specific amplification (ASA), methylation-specific PCR (MSP), and sequence-specific primed PCR (PCR-SSP) applications. The mutation at position 660 is located within a conserved α-helical region of the enzyme’s thumb domain, where the wild-type arginine residue forms a salt bridge with the phosphate backbone of the primer strand. Substitution with valine, a non-polar, aliphatic amino acid, disrupts this electrostatic interaction, reducing the enzyme’s capacity to stabilize mismatched primer termini without compromising the efficiency of correct Watson-Crick base pairing. This structural alteration results in a significant increase in the kinetic selectivity for matched substrates, as evidenced by a 36-fold improvement in the ratio of extension rates for matched versus mismatched primer-template complexes compared to the wild-type enzyme.

The KlenTaq R660V variant is produced through recombinant expression in Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) cells following cloning of the mutated gene into a suitable expression vector. The gene encoding the wild-type KlenTaq polymerase is subjected to saturation mutagenesis at position 660 using degenerate primers containing NNK codons, which encode all 20 amino acids and three stop codons. The resulting mutant library is transformed into E. coli, and individual colonies are screened for enhanced mismatch discrimination using a high-throughput real-time PCR assay. In this screening, each mutant is tested in parallel using two template substrates: one containing a perfect match at the 3′ end of the primer and another containing a single-nucleotide mismatch. Amplification is monitored in real time using SYBR Green I dye, and variants exhibiting a significant delay or absence of amplification in the mismatch condition—while maintaining robust amplification in the matched condition—are selected for further characterization. The R660V mutant consistently emerges as the top performer, demonstrating a threshold cycle (Ct) difference of up to 18 cycles between matched and mismatched templates, compared to a difference of only 8.5 cycles for the wild-type enzyme under identical conditions.

The enzymatic properties of KlenTaq R660V have been rigorously characterized using a combination of biochemical, kinetic, and functional assays. Primer extension assays under pre-steady-state conditions reveal that the rate of nucleotide incorporation for matched substrates remains comparable to that of the wild-type enzyme, with full-length product formation occurring within one second under saturating dNTP concentrations. In contrast, extension from mismatched substrates is delayed by more than 600 seconds, indicating a profound kinetic barrier to misincorporation. This selectivity is further confirmed in real-time PCR assays using human genomic DNA templates containing the prothrombin G20210A SNP, where KlenTaq R660V produces robust amplification only when the primer is perfectly matched to the allele present in the sample, with no detectable signal from the mismatched primer even after 60 cycles. This level of discrimination is substantially superior to that achieved by commercially available polymerases such as Platinum Taq and AmpliTaq Gold, which exhibit significant cross-amplification in mismatch conditions, leading to ambiguous genotyping results.

The utility of KlenTaq R660V extends beyond SNP detection to the analysis of DNA methylation, a critical epigenetic modification involved in gene regulation and carcinogenesis. Methylation-specific PCR (MSP) relies on the differential amplification of bisulfite-converted DNA, wherein cytosines are converted to uracils unless methylated, thereby creating sequence differences that can be exploited by allele-specific primers. In standard MSP, the selectivity of the polymerase is paramount, as even minor extension from mismatched primers can lead to false-positive signals. KlenTaq R660V demonstrates exceptional performance in MSP assays targeting the Septin 9 gene promoter, a well-established biomarker for colorectal cancer. Using primers designed to distinguish between methylated and unmethylated sequences, KlenTaq R660V generates strong amplification signals only when the primer is perfectly matched to the bisulfite-converted template, with no detectable amplification from the opposing template, even at high template concentrations. This performance surpasses that of commercial MSP kits, which often require optimized buffer conditions and extended cycling to achieve comparable specificity.

In the context of human leukocyte antigen (HLA) typing, KlenTaq R660V enables highly specific sequence-specific primed PCR (PCR-SSP) without the need for extensive primer panels or post-amplification sequencing. HLA typing traditionally requires the simultaneous use of dozens of allele-specific primer pairs to distinguish between highly homologous HLA alleles, with amplification success determined solely by the presence or absence of a PCR product. The specificity of this approach is critically dependent on the polymerase’s ability to prevent amplification from primers with even minor mismatches at the 3′ end. In a direct comparison with AmpliTaq Gold, KlenTaq R660V exhibits superior specificity in amplifying target HLA-DRB1 alleles while completely suppressing amplification from non-target alleles differing by only two nucleotides at the 3′ end of the primer. This results in unambiguous band patterns on agarose gels and eliminates the need for confirmatory sequencing, reducing both cost and turnaround time in transplant matching and autoimmune disease diagnostics.

The KlenTaq R660V variant also demonstrates remarkable tolerance to biological inhibitors commonly found in whole blood, serum, and tissue lysates. Conventional polymerases are often inhibited by hemoglobin, immunoglobulins, and other blood components at concentrations as low as 0.2%, necessitating DNA extraction prior to amplification. In contrast, KlenTaq R660V retains full activity in the presence of up to 5% whole blood, enabling direct genotyping from unprocessed samples. This is achieved by increasing the concentration of SYBR Green I dye 30-fold to compensate for fluorescence quenching and by elevating the polymerase concentration to 500 nM, which ensures sufficient catalytic activity despite the presence of inhibitors. In clinical validation studies, KlenTaq R660V successfully genotyped the prothrombin G20210A SNP directly from dried blood spots, with results fully concordant with those obtained from purified genomic DNA. This capability eliminates the need for centrifugation, lysis, and column-based purification, making it ideal for point-of-care diagnostics, field-based screening, and resource-limited settings.

The fidelity of KlenTaq R660V has been quantitatively assessed using a PCR-based mutation assay that sequences amplified products to determine the frequency and spectrum of replication errors. The enzyme exhibits an error rate of 2.8 × 10⁻⁵ mutations per base per duplication, which is approximately threefold lower than that of the wild-type KlenTaq (8.8 × 10⁻⁵). Notably, the reduction in errors is primarily attributable to a decrease in transition mutations, particularly G→A and C→T substitutions, which are the most common types of replication errors in DNA polymerases. This enhanced fidelity, combined with its high mismatch discrimination, positions KlenTaq R660V as a uniquely reliable enzyme for applications requiring both specificity and accuracy, such as clonal amplification of low-abundance variants, detection of somatic mutations in circulating tumor DNA, and forensic DNA profiling.

The invention further encompasses the use of KlenTaq R660V in multiplexed allele-specific amplification assays, wherein multiple SNPs are detected in a single reaction tube through the use of primers with distinct melting temperatures. By incorporating a 30-nucleotide 5′ overhang on one allele-specific primer, the resulting amplicons differ in length and melting behavior, allowing for clear discrimination by high-resolution melting curve analysis. In a multiplexed assay targeting the prothrombin G20210A SNP, KlenTaq R660V successfully distinguished homozygous wild-type, heterozygous, and homozygous mutant genotypes in a single reaction, with no cross-reactivity or signal overlap. This multiplexing capability is further validated in assays involving multiple SNPs associated with pharmacogenomic traits, such as CYP2C9, VKORC1, and TPMT, demonstrating the scalability of the system for comprehensive genetic profiling.

The KlenTaq R660V variant is stable under standard storage conditions, retaining full activity for more than six months at −20°C in a buffer containing 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 9.2), 16 mM (NH₄)₂SO₄, 0.1% Tween 20, 2.5 mM MgCl₂, and 50% glycerol. The enzyme is compatible with standard PCR thermocyclers, real-time PCR instruments, and endpoint detection systems, and may be formulated into lyophilized master mixes for long-term storage and ease of use. The invention further includes kits comprising KlenTaq R660V polymerase, optimized reaction buffers, dNTPs, SYBR Green I dye, and allele-specific primers for the detection of specific SNPs, methylation markers, or HLA alleles. These kits are designed for use in clinical laboratories, research institutions, and point-of-care settings, and may be customized for specific diagnostic panels, including those for thrombophilia, cancer risk, drug metabolism, and immune compatibility.

The structural basis for the enhanced selectivity of KlenTaq R660V lies in the disruption of a key electrostatic interaction between the arginine side chain at position 660 and the phosphate backbone of the primer strand. In the wild-type enzyme, this interaction contributes to the stabilization of the closed conformation of the polymerase during nucleotide incorporation, facilitating both correct and incorrect base pairing. The substitution of arginine with valine removes the positively charged guanidinium group, thereby destabilizing the primer backbone interaction in mismatched complexes without significantly affecting the geometry of the active site during correct base pairing. This selective destabilization creates a kinetic barrier to mismatch extension, effectively filtering out non-specific amplification while preserving the speed and efficiency of true target amplification. Molecular dynamics simulations and structural modeling confirm that the valine side chain introduces a steric and hydrophobic environment that disfavors the accommodation of non-Watson-Crick base pairs at the primer terminus, consistent with the observed biochemical and functional outcomes.

The invention further provides methods for the production, purification, and formulation of KlenTaq R660V, including scalable fermentation processes, affinity chromatography using Ni-IDA resins, and optimization of storage buffers to maintain long-term stability and activity. The enzyme may be immobilized on solid supports for reusable diagnostic cartridges, incorporated into microfluidic devices for automated sample-to-answer systems, or combined with lateral flow detection platforms for visual readouts. The invention also encompasses the use of KlenTaq R660V in combination with digital PCR, next-generation sequencing library preparation, and CRISPR-based detection systems, where high-fidelity amplification is critical for accurate quantification and target enrichment.

In summary, the present invention discloses a novel DNA polymerase variant, KlenTaq R660V, that represents a significant advancement in the field of molecular diagnostics. By combining unprecedented mismatch extension selectivity with high activity, thermostability, inhibitor resistance, and compatibility with standard PCR platforms, this enzyme enables accurate, rapid, and cost-effective detection of genetic and epigenetic variations directly from complex biological samples. Its applications span personalized medicine, oncology, transfusion medicine, forensic science, and public health, offering a transformative tool for the next generation of genetic diagnostics.

This substitution is located within the thumb domain of the enzyme, specifically within a conserved α-helical region that interacts with the phosphate backbone of the primer strand during DNA synthesis.
