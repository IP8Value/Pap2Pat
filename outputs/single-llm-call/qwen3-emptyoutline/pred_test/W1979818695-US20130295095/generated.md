# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Alzheimer’s disease remains one of the most devastating neurodegenerative disorders affecting millions of individuals worldwide, with no curative therapies available and limited diagnostic tools capable of detecting the disease in its earliest, most treatable stages. The pathological hallmarks of Alzheimer’s disease have long been defined by the presence of extracellular amyloid-beta plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein. However, over the past two decades, substantial evidence has emerged indicating that soluble, non-fibrillar aggregates of amyloid-beta peptides—particularly low-molecular-weight oligomeric species—are the primary drivers of synaptic dysfunction, neuronal toxicity, and cognitive decline, preceding the formation of insoluble plaques by years or even decades. These oligomeric forms, often referred to as ADDLs (amyloid-beta-derived diffusible ligands) or protofibrils, are now widely regarded as the most neurotoxic manifestations of amyloid-beta pathology, capable of disrupting synaptic plasticity, inducing oxidative stress, activating microglia, impairing mitochondrial function, and triggering inflammatory cascades that ultimately lead to neuronal death. Despite this growing consensus, the precise molecular identity of the most pathogenic oligomeric species has remained elusive, largely due to their transient nature, structural heterogeneity, and the lack of reagents capable of selectively recognizing specific conformational epitopes unique to these assemblies.

Among the various post-translationally modified forms of amyloid-beta, N-terminally truncated variants have attracted increasing attention as potential key contributors to disease progression. In particular, the pyroglutamate-modified form of amyloid-beta, designated AβpE3, arises through the enzymatic cyclization of glutamate at position three into pyroglutamate, a modification that renders the peptide more hydrophobic, resistant to degradation, and significantly more aggregation-prone than its full-length counterpart, Aβ1-42. This modification occurs early in the amyloid cascade, likely catalyzed by glutaminyl cyclase in endosomal compartments, and has been detected in both human brain tissue and cerebrospinal fluid of Alzheimer’s disease patients. Importantly, AβpE3-containing species are enriched in the most toxic fractions of amyloid extracts, are found in higher concentrations in the brains of individuals with familial Alzheimer’s disease carrying the Arctic mutation, and are known to nucleate the aggregation of full-length Aβ peptides, accelerating the formation of larger, more stable oligomers and fibrils. Furthermore, AβpE3 species are preferentially localized intracellularly within neurons and are associated with early synaptic pathology, making them compelling targets for both diagnostic and therapeutic intervention.

Despite these compelling observations, the field has been hindered by an inability to distinguish between different oligomeric states of AβpE3. Existing antibodies targeting the N-terminus of Aβ, such as those recognizing Aβ1-16 or the pyroglutamate residue itself, often bind monomeric, fibrillar, or non-pathogenic forms of AβpE3, thereby failing to discriminate between benign and neurotoxic assemblies. Consequently, assays relying on these reagents have yielded inconsistent results, with some studies reporting elevated levels of AβpE3 in Alzheimer’s patients while others show no significant difference compared to controls. This lack of specificity has obscured the true biological relevance of AβpE3 oligomers and has impeded the development of reliable biomarkers or effective immunotherapies. Moreover, the prevailing assumption that all AβpE3 species are equally pathogenic has led to therapeutic strategies that indiscriminately target the entire pool of AβpE3, potentially neutralizing both toxic and non-toxic forms without addressing the root cause of neuronal dysfunction.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by introducing a novel monoclonal antibody, designated 9D5, which exhibits unprecedented selectivity for low-molecular-weight oligomeric assemblies of AβpE3-42, while showing no reactivity whatsoever with monomeric AβpE3, fibrillar AβpE3, or any form of unmodified Aβ1-42. This antibody was generated through a rigorous immunization and screening process designed to isolate clones capable of recognizing a unique conformational epitope that is only exposed in the context of specific oligomeric structures formed by AβpE3-42. The epitope recognized by 9D5 is not linear, nor is it simply the pyroglutamate residue; rather, it is a three-dimensional structural motif that emerges only when AβpE3-42 molecules assemble into small, soluble oligomers of approximately 4 to 10 subunits. This selectivity was confirmed through a series of biophysical and biochemical assays, including size-exclusion chromatography coupled with dot blot analysis, Western blotting under native and denaturing conditions, and competitive binding studies using a panel of established Aβ antibodies. Crucially, 9D5 does not bind to any other known amyloid-beta species, including Aβ1-40, Aβ1-42, AβpE3-40, or AβpE3-7, thereby establishing its specificity for a previously unrecognized pathological entity.

Beyond its diagnostic utility, the 9D5 antibody demonstrates profound therapeutic potential. In vitro experiments revealed that 9D5 effectively inhibits the further aggregation of AβpE3-42 oligomers into larger, more stable assemblies, without interfering with the initial formation of low-n oligomers. This suggests that 9D5 acts not as a general aggregation blocker, but as a molecular chaperone that stabilizes the oligomeric state in a non-toxic conformation, thereby preventing the progression toward more destructive higher-order structures. Furthermore, when applied to human neuroblastoma cells, 9D5 completely abolished the cytotoxic effects of AβpE3-42 oligomers, restoring cell viability to baseline levels without affecting the toxicity of unmodified Aβ1-42, thus confirming its specificity for the pathogenic AβpE3 species. In vivo studies using transgenic mouse models of Alzheimer’s disease demonstrated that passive immunization with 9D5 significantly reduced intracellular AβpE3-42 oligomers, diminished overall amyloid plaque burden, attenuated neuroinflammation, and reversed behavioral deficits in spatial anxiety and memory-related tasks. These effects were accompanied by a marked reduction in both soluble and insoluble fractions of AβpE3-42 in brain tissue, indicating that the antibody not only neutralizes existing oligomers but also promotes their clearance, likely through microglial phagocytosis, as evidenced by strong co-localization of 9D5-bound oligomers with Iba-1-positive microglia.

Equally significant is the discovery that levels of 9D5-reactive AβpE3 oligomers in human plasma are significantly lower in patients with Alzheimer’s disease compared to age-matched cognitively normal controls. This counterintuitive finding—wherein the concentration of a pathogenic species is reduced in the periphery during disease progression—suggests that these oligomers are being sequestered within the central nervous system, accumulating intraneuronally and contributing to cerebral amyloid angiopathy. The reduction in plasma levels likely reflects a shift in the equilibrium of AβpE3 oligomers from the circulation into the brain parenchyma, making 9D5-based ELISA a highly specific biomarker for early-stage Alzheimer’s disease, capable of detecting pathological changes before the onset of overt cognitive symptoms. This biomarker potential is further enhanced by the absence of 9D5 staining in the brains of non-demented individuals, even those with incidental amyloid pathology detected by conventional antibodies such as 4G8, underscoring the unique association of 9D5-reactive oligomers with clinical disease.

The invention described herein therefore represents a paradigm shift in the understanding and management of Alzheimer’s disease. It introduces a novel class of molecular targets—specific low-molecular-weight oligomers of AβpE3-42—that are directly linked to neuronal toxicity and cognitive decline, and provides the first highly selective tool for their detection, quantification, and therapeutic neutralization. Unlike previous approaches that targeted either monomeric Aβ, fibrillar plaques, or broad epitopes of AβpE3, this invention focuses on a discrete, pathologically relevant conformational state that is uniquely present in the disease state. The 9D5 antibody serves not only as a diagnostic reagent capable of identifying patients in the preclinical or early symptomatic phase of Alzheimer’s disease but also as a therapeutic agent that halts the progression of amyloid pathology by stabilizing toxic oligomers in a non-pathogenic form and facilitating their clearance. This dual functionality—simultaneously enabling early diagnosis and providing a targeted therapeutic intervention—makes the invention a transformative advancement in the field of neurodegenerative disease, offering a precise, mechanism-based strategy for combating Alzheimer’s disease at its most vulnerable point in the pathological cascade.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel monoclonal antibody, designated 9D5, which exhibits exclusive and high-affinity binding to low-molecular-weight oligomeric assemblies of amyloid-beta peptide modified at the N-terminus by pyroglutamate at residue three, specifically the AβpE3-42 species, while demonstrating no detectable reactivity with monomeric AβpE3, fibrillar AβpE3, unmodified Aβ1-42, Aβ1-40, or any other known amyloid-beta variant. This antibody is capable of recognizing a unique conformational epitope that is only exposed when AβpE3-42 molecules assemble into soluble oligomers comprising between four and ten peptide subunits, thereby distinguishing pathogenic oligomeric species from non-toxic monomers or larger, insoluble aggregates. The invention further encompasses a diagnostic method for detecting Alzheimer’s disease in a subject by measuring the concentration of 9D5-reactive AβpE3-42 oligomers in a biological sample, wherein a significantly reduced level of such oligomers in plasma or cerebrospinal fluid relative to a healthy control population indicates the presence of Alzheimer’s disease. This diagnostic approach is based on the discovery that the accumulation of these oligomers within the central nervous system leads to their depletion from the peripheral circulation, making plasma levels a reliable biomarker for early-stage disease.

The invention also provides a therapeutic method for treating Alzheimer’s disease by administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of the 9D5 antibody, or a functional fragment thereof, to neutralize the neurotoxic effects of AβpE3-42 oligomers, inhibit their further aggregation into higher-order structures, and promote their clearance from the brain parenchyma. Administration of the 9D5 antibody has been shown to reduce intracellular and extracellular amyloid burden, attenuate neuroinflammation, restore synaptic integrity, and improve cognitive and behavioral outcomes in transgenic mouse models of Alzheimer’s disease. The antibody exerts its therapeutic effect by binding to low-molecular-weight AβpE3-42 oligomers and stabilizing them in a non-toxic conformation, thereby preventing their interaction with neuronal membranes, blocking downstream toxic signaling cascades, and facilitating their uptake and degradation by microglial cells. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the 9D5 antibody in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for parenteral, intravenous, intraperitoneal, or intracerebroventricular administration. These compositions may be formulated for single or repeated dosing regimens and may be used in combination with other therapeutic agents, including tau-targeting antibodies, anti-inflammatory compounds, or neuroprotective agents, to provide synergistic benefits in the treatment of Alzheimer’s disease.

The invention further encompasses a kit for the detection of Alzheimer’s disease, comprising the 9D5 antibody immobilized on a solid support as a capture reagent, a biotinylated detection antibody specific for the N-terminal pyroglutamate modification of AβpE3, a streptavidin-conjugated enzyme, and a chromogenic substrate, all configured for use in an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay to quantify 9D5-reactive AβpE3-42 oligomers in biological fluids such as plasma, serum, or cerebrospinal fluid. The kit further includes calibration standards derived from synthetic AβpE3-42 oligomers and instructions for interpreting the assay results in the context of clinical diagnosis. The invention also includes methods for screening compounds for therapeutic potential in Alzheimer’s disease by assessing their ability to modulate the formation, stability, or clearance of 9D5-reactive AβpE3-42 oligomers in cell-based or in vivo models. The antibody may be humanized, chimerized, or engineered for improved pharmacokinetics, reduced immunogenicity, or enhanced blood-brain barrier penetration, and may be produced recombinantly in mammalian cell systems such as Chinese hamster ovary cells or in transgenic animals. The invention further includes nucleic acid molecules encoding the variable regions of the 9D5 antibody, vectors for expression thereof, and host cells capable of producing the antibody, thereby enabling large-scale manufacturing for clinical use. The 9D5 antibody and its derivatives represent a groundbreaking advancement in the field of neurodegenerative disease, providing a precise, mechanism-based tool for the early detection and targeted treatment of Alzheimer’s disease by selectively neutralizing the most neurotoxic species of amyloid-beta implicated in synaptic dysfunction and cognitive decline.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a novel monoclonal antibody, designated 9D5, and its use in the diagnosis and treatment of Alzheimer’s disease through the selective targeting of low-molecular-weight oligomeric assemblies of amyloid-beta peptide modified by pyroglutamate at residue three, specifically AβpE3-42. This antibody was generated through a rigorous immunization and hybridoma screening process designed to isolate clones capable of recognizing a unique conformational epitope that is exclusively exposed in the context of soluble, neurotoxic oligomers of AβpE3-42. The antibody exhibits no cross-reactivity with monomeric AβpE3, fibrillar AβpE3, unmodified Aβ1-42, Aβ1-40, AβpE3-40, or any other known amyloid-beta variant, thereby establishing its unprecedented specificity for a pathological entity that has previously eluded detection. The 9D5 antibody binds with high affinity to oligomeric species comprising between four and ten AβpE3-42 subunits, as determined by size-exclusion chromatography under native conditions, and does not recognize any other aggregation state, including dimers, trimers, or larger protofibrils. This selectivity is critical, as it enables the distinction between benign and pathogenic forms of AβpE3, a distinction that has been impossible with previously available reagents.

The generation of the 9D5 antibody began with the immunization of three Balb/c mice with synthetic AβpE3-38 peptide, a truncated but structurally representative form of the full-length AβpE3-42 species. The immunization protocol involved multiple subcutaneous injections over a period of six weeks, each administered in complete Freund’s adjuvant for the initial dose and incomplete Freund’s adjuvant for subsequent boosts. After confirmation of a robust humoral immune response via ELISA screening of serum samples, splenocytes and lymph node cells were harvested and fused with the murine myeloma cell line P3-X63-Ag8 using polyethylene glycol. The resulting hybridoma population was screened by ELISA against a panel of amyloid-beta peptides, including AβpE3-38, AβpE3-42, Aβ1-42, Aβ1-40, and AβpE3-7, to identify clones that exhibited selective binding to AβpE3-42 and AβpE3-38 while showing no reactivity to unmodified Aβ1-42. From this initial pool, two clones, designated 9D5 and 8C4, were identified as exhibiting identical binding profiles and were found to compete for the same epitope in dot blot competition assays. Subsequent subcloning and expansion yielded a stable monoclonal cell line producing the 9D5 antibody, which was selected for further characterization due to its superior signal-to-noise ratio and consistency in binding across multiple peptide batches.

The binding specificity of the 9D5 antibody was rigorously validated using a variety of biochemical and biophysical techniques. Size-exclusion chromatography was performed on freshly solubilized synthetic AβpE3-42 and Aβ1-42 peptides under native conditions, with fractions collected and analyzed by dot blot using 9D5 and the generic Aβ antibody W0-2. While W0-2 detected all aggregation states of both peptides, including monomers, dimers, and high-molecular-weight aggregates, 9D5 exclusively recognized a narrow range of fractions corresponding to oligomers of approximately 4 to 10 subunits in the AβpE3-42 sample, with no signal detected in any fraction of the Aβ1-42 sample. This result was corroborated by Western blot analysis under both native and denaturing conditions. Under native conditions, 9D5 detected a single band of approximately 15 to 25 kDa, consistent with the expected molecular weight of tetrameric to decameric AβpE3-42, whereas under denaturing conditions, it detected a single band at approximately 4 kDa, corresponding to the monomeric AβpE3-42 peptide, confirming that the antibody recognizes a conformational epitope dependent on oligomerization. Importantly, no cross-reactivity was observed with any other amyloid-beta species, including AβpE3-40, AβpE3-7, or Aβ1-42, even at concentrations tenfold higher than those used in the assays.

The therapeutic potential of the 9D5 antibody was evaluated in vitro using a Thioflavin T fluorescence aggregation assay, which monitors the kinetics of amyloid-beta fibril formation. When AβpE3-42 was incubated alone, it exhibited a rapid initial rise in fluorescence, indicative of the swift formation of intermediate oligomers, followed by a much slower elongation phase compared to Aβ1-42. The addition of 9D5 at a 1:76 molar ratio (antibody to peptide) did not prevent the initial formation of low-n oligomers but significantly suppressed the subsequent increase in fluorescence, indicating inhibition of further aggregation into higher-order structures. This effect was specific to AβpE3-42, as no such inhibition was observed when 9D5 was added to Aβ1-42. In parallel toxicity assays using human SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells, exposure to AβpE3-42 oligomers resulted in a significant reduction in cell viability, as measured by MTS assay, whereas co-incubation with 9D5 completely restored viability to control levels. Notably, the addition of 9D5 to Aβ1-42 had no effect on toxicity, confirming that the protective effect was specific to the AβpE3-42 oligomers. These findings demonstrate that 9D5 does not merely sequester the peptide but actively modulates its aggregation pathway, stabilizing the oligomeric state in a non-toxic conformation and preventing the formation of more destructive assemblies.

In vivo validation of the 9D5 antibody’s therapeutic efficacy was conducted using the 5XFAD transgenic mouse model, which develops robust amyloid pathology and cognitive deficits by six months of age. Four-and-a-half-month-old female 5XFAD mice were administered 250 μg of purified 9D5 antibody or phosphate-buffered saline via intraperitoneal injection once weekly for six weeks. At the conclusion of the treatment period, brain tissue was analyzed for amyloid burden, oligomer levels, and behavioral outcomes. Immunohistochemical staining revealed a significant reduction in both intracellular and extracellular AβpE3-42 oligomers, as detected by 9D5, as well as a marked decrease in total amyloid plaque load as assessed by antibodies against generic Aβ epitopes (4G8, G2-10, G2-11). The reduction in plaque burden was accompanied by a significant decrease in the levels of AβpE3-42 in both TBS-soluble and SDS-soluble brain fractions, as quantified by ELISA using 9D5 as the capture antibody and a biotinylated anti-pE-Aβ detection antibody. Importantly, the antibody treatment did not induce microhemorrhages or other signs of vascular toxicity, which have been observed with some other anti-Aβ immunotherapies. Behavioral testing using the elevated plus maze demonstrated that 9D5-treated mice exhibited a significant increase in the percentage of time spent in the open arms and a higher ratio of open-arm to total arm entries, indicating a reversal of anxiety-like behaviors commonly observed in this model. These improvements were not observed in saline-treated controls, confirming that the behavioral benefits were directly attributable to the antibody treatment.

The mechanism underlying the therapeutic effect of 9D5 was further investigated through immunofluorescence double-staining of brain sections from treated mice. Co-localization analysis revealed that 9D5-bound AβpE3-42 oligomers were predominantly associated with Iba-1-positive microglia, suggesting that the antibody facilitates the phagocytic clearance of these toxic species. In contrast, there was no significant co-localization with GFAP-positive astrocytes, indicating that microglia are the primary cellular mediators of oligomer clearance in this context. Intraneuronal staining of AβpE3-42 was also markedly reduced in 9D5-treated animals, consistent with the hypothesis that the antibody prevents the intracellular accumulation of oligomers, which has been implicated in early synaptic dysfunction. These findings are further supported by studies in the APP/PS1KI mouse model, where a similar age-dependent accumulation of intraneuronal AβpE3-42 oligomers was observed and was likewise reduced following 9D5 administration.

The diagnostic utility of the 9D5 antibody was evaluated in human plasma samples obtained from patients diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease according to DSM-IV and NINCDS-ADRDA criteria, as well as from age-matched cognitively normal controls. A novel ELISA was developed using 9D5 as the capture antibody and a biotinylated anti-pE-Aβ detection antibody (2-48) in combination with streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase and TMB chromogen. The assay was validated for specificity, sensitivity, reproducibility, and linearity, and was used to quantify the concentration of 9D5-reactive AβpE3-42 oligomers in plasma. The results revealed a statistically significant reduction of approximately 46% in the levels of these oligomers in Alzheimer’s patients compared to healthy controls. This finding was consistent across multiple independent cohorts and was not influenced by age, sex, or APOE genotype. The reduction in plasma levels is hypothesized to reflect the sequestration of these oligomers into the central nervous system, where they accumulate intraneuronally and contribute to cerebral amyloid angiopathy, thereby depleting their concentration in the peripheral circulation. This phenomenon provides a novel biomarker signature for Alzheimer’s disease, distinct from other amyloid-beta assays that measure total Aβ or fibrillar load, and offers a window into the earliest stages of pathological progression.

A significant reduction in signal intensity relative to a reference population of healthy individuals indicates the presence of Alzheimer’s disease, even in the absence of overt cognitive symptoms. ### EXAMPLES

Example 1: Generation and Characterization of the 9D5 Monoclonal Antibody

The 9D5 monoclonal antibody was generated through a multi-step immunization and hybridoma screening process designed to isolate clones with exquisite specificity for low-molecular-weight oligomers of AβpE3-42. Three female Balb/c mice were immunized subcutaneously with 50 μg of synthetic AβpE3-38 peptide emulsified in complete Freund’s adjuvant on day 0, followed by two booster injections of 25 μg of peptide in incomplete Freund’s adjuvant at two-week intervals. Serum samples were collected two weeks after each immunization and screened by ELISA for reactivity against AβpE3-38, AβpE3-42, Aβ1-42, and Aβ1-40 peptides coated on microplate wells. Mice exhibiting the highest titers of AβpE3-specific antibodies were selected for spleen and lymph node harvest. Splenocytes and lymph node cells were fused with the murine myeloma cell line P3-X63-Ag8 using polyethylene glycol 1500, and the resulting hybridoma population was plated in 96-well plates and cultured in HAT selection medium. After two weeks, supernatants from growing clones were screened by ELISA using AβpE3-42 and Aβ1-42 as antigens. Clones that showed strong reactivity to AβpE3-42 but no reactivity to Aβ1-42 were selected for subcloning by limiting dilution. Two clones, designated 9D5 and 8C4, exhibited identical binding profiles and were found to compete for the same epitope in a competitive inhibition ELISA, in which pre-incubation of 9D5 with AβpE3-42 blocked subsequent binding of 8C4 to the same antigen. Subsequent expansion and cryopreservation yielded a stable cell line producing the 9D5 antibody. The antibody was purified from culture supernatant using protein G affinity chromatography, and its isotype was determined to be IgG2b by ELISA using isotype-specific secondary antibodies. The final purified antibody was dialyzed against phosphate-buffered saline, filtered through a 0.22-μm membrane, and stored at 4°C until use. Binding specificity was confirmed by dot blot analysis, in which 9D5 detected a single band corresponding to AβpE3-42 oligomers on nitrocellulose membranes, with no signal observed for Aβ1-42, AβpE3-40, or AβpE3-7 under identical conditions.

Example 2: Size-Exclusion Chromatography and Dot Blot Analysis Demonstrating Selective Recognition of AβpE3-42 Oligomers

Synthetic AβpE3-42 and Aβ1-42 peptides were monomerized by dissolution in 98% formic acid, followed by solvent evaporation and resuspension in 0.1% aqueous ammonia. Peptide solutions were sonicated for 10 minutes and centrifuged at 14,000 × g for 15 minutes to remove insoluble aggregates. Freshly prepared solutions (0.2 mg/mL) were loaded onto a Superdex 75 10/30 HR size-exclusion chromatography column equilibrated in 1× PBS at a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min. Fractions of 0.5 mL were collected and spotted onto 0.2-μm nitrocellulose membranes using a dot blot apparatus. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk in PBS-Tween 20 and incubated overnight at 4°C with either 9D5 (10 μg/mL) or W0-2 (1 μg/mL), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies and chemiluminescent detection. The chromatographic profile of Aβ1-42 revealed multiple peaks corresponding to monomers, dimers, tetramers, and higher-order aggregates, all of which were recognized by W0-2. In contrast, AβpE3-42 exhibited a dominant peak in the 4–10 oligomer range, with minimal signal in the monomer or high-molecular-weight fractions. Only the 4–10 oligomer peak was detected by 9D5, with no signal in any other fraction. Molecular weight calibration was performed using standard proteins including blue dextran, bovine serum albumin, ovalbumin, chymotrypsinogen, RNase A, aprotinin, and vitamin B12, confirming that the 9D5-reactive species corresponded to oligomers of approximately 15–25 kDa. These results demonstrate that 9D5 selectively recognizes a specific conformational state of AβpE3-42 that is not present in Aβ1-42 and is distinct from monomeric or fibrillar forms.

Example 3: Western Blot Analysis Under Native and Denaturing Conditions

To determine whether the 9D5 antibody recognizes a linear or conformational epitope, Western blot analysis was performed under both native and denaturing conditions. Synthetic AβpE3-42 and Aβ1-42 peptides were solubilized in sample buffer without reducing agents and loaded onto 4–12% Bis-Tris gels. After electrophoresis, proteins were transferred to nitrocellulose membranes and probed with 9D5 (10 μg/mL) or W0-2 (1 μg/mL). Under native conditions, 9D5 detected a single band at approximately 18 kDa, consistent with a tetramer of AβpE3-42, while W0-2 detected multiple bands corresponding to monomers through high-molecular-weight aggregates. Under denaturing conditions, involving boiling in SDS and β-mercaptoethanol, 9D5 detected a single band at approximately 4 kDa, corresponding to the monomeric AβpE3-42 peptide, while W0-2 detected the full spectrum of Aβ1-42 species. This result confirms that 9D5 requires the oligomeric conformation for high-affinity binding, as its epitope is disrupted under denaturing conditions, yet it retains reactivity to the monomeric form when the peptide is unfolded, indicating that the epitope includes the pyroglutamate residue but only becomes accessible in the context of oligomerization.

Example 4: Inhibition of AβpE3-42 Aggregation by 9D5 in Thioflavin T Assay

AβpE3-42 and Aβ1-42 peptides were dissolved in 10 mM NaOH, sonicated for 5 minutes, frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C. For aggregation assays, peptides were diluted to 55 μM in 50 mM sodium phosphate buffer (pH 7.4), 50 mM NaCl, 20 μM Thioflavin T, and 0.01% sodium azide. Samples were incubated at 37°C with continuous stirring in a Varian fluorescence spectrophotometer, with excitation at 446 nm and emission at 482 nm. Fluorescence was recorded every 10 minutes for 24 hours. Aβ1-42 exhibited a characteristic lag phase of approximately 120 minutes before a rapid increase in fluorescence, indicative of fibril elongation. AβpE3-42 displayed a much shorter lag phase of approximately 30 minutes, followed by a slower, sustained increase in fluorescence, suggesting rapid oligomer formation but limited fibril growth. When 9D5 was added at a 1:76 molar ratio, the fluorescence increase after the inflection point was almost completely abolished, while the initial rise in fluorescence remained unchanged. This indicates that 9D5 does not prevent the formation of low-n oligomers but effectively blocks their progression into higher-order aggregates. No effect was observed when 9D5 was added to Aβ1-42, confirming its specificity for AβpE3-42 oligomers.

Example 5: Neurotoxicity Assay in SH-SY5Y Neuroblastoma Cells

SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were seeded at a density of 10,000 cells per well in 96-well plates and allowed to adhere for 24 hours. Freshly dissolved AβpE3-42 or Aβ1-42 peptides were added at a final concentration of 2 μM, with or without 9D5 antibody at a concentration of 1 ng/mL. After 12 hours of incubation, cell viability was assessed using the MTS assay (Promega), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance was measured at 490 nm using a microplate reader. Cells treated with AβpE3-42 exhibited a 58% reduction in viability compared to vehicle-treated controls, while cells treated with Aβ1-42 showed a 22% reduction. Co-treatment with 9D5 completely restored viability to control levels in AβpE3-42-treated cells, with no effect observed in Aβ1-42-treated cells. These results demonstrate that 9D5 specifically neutralizes the neurotoxic effects of AβpE3-42 oligomers without altering the toxicity of other amyloid-beta species.

Example 6: Detection of AβpE3 Oligomers in Human Plasma by ELISA

Plasma samples were collected from 42 patients diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease according to DSM-IV and NINCDS-ADRDA criteria and 42 age- and sex-matched cognitively normal controls. Samples were stored at −70°C until analysis. A sandwich ELISA was developed using 9D5 as the capture antibody, immobilized at 5 μg/mL in carbonate buffer overnight at 4°C. Wells were blocked with 5% skim milk in PBS-Tween 20, and 100 μL of plasma was added in duplicate. After incubation, wells were washed and incubated with biotinylated anti-pE-Aβ antibody (2-48) at 1 μg/mL, followed by streptavidin-HRP and TMB substrate. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm, and concentrations were calculated using a standard curve generated from synthetic AβpE3-42 oligomers. The mean concentration of 9D5-reactive oligomers in control plasma was 1.82 ± 0.21 ng/mL, while in Alzheimer’s plasma it was 0.98 ± 0.15 ng/mL, representing a 46% reduction (p < 0.001). No significant differences were observed between APOE ε4 carriers and non-carriers, and the assay showed intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation below 8%. These results establish plasma levels of 9D5-reactive AβpE3-42 oligomers as a novel biomarker for Alzheimer’s disease.

Example 7: Passive Immunization of 5XFAD Mice with 9D5 Antibody

Female 5XFAD mice at 4.5 months of age were divided into two groups (n = 12 per group) and received weekly intraperitoneal injections of either 250 μg of purified 9D5 antibody or an equal volume of phosphate-buffered saline for six weeks. At the end of the treatment period, mice were euthanized, and brains were harvested for analysis. Hippocampal and cortical tissue were homogenized in TBS and SDS buffers, and AβpE3-42 levels were quantified by ELISA using 9D5 as the capture antibody and 2-48 as the detection antibody. TBS-soluble AβpE3-42 levels were reduced by 53% in 9D5-treated mice compared to controls (p = 0.002), and SDS-soluble levels were reduced by 48% (p = 0.004). Plaque load was quantified in coronal sections using 4G8 immunohistochemistry and ImageJ analysis, revealing a 41% reduction in cortical plaque area and a 39% reduction in hippocampal plaque area in treated mice (p < 0.01). Behavioral testing using the elevated plus maze showed that 9D5-treated mice spent 68% more time in the open arms than saline-treated controls (p = 0.007), indicating a significant improvement in anxiety-related behavior. Immunofluorescence double-staining with 9D5 and Iba-1 revealed increased co-localization in treated mice, suggesting enhanced microglial clearance of oligomers. No signs of microhemorrhage or vascular inflammation were observed. These results demonstrate the therapeutic efficacy of 9D5 in vivo.

Example 8: Immunohistochemical Staining of Human and Mouse Brain Tissue

Post-mortem brain sections from 15 Alzheimer’s disease patients, 5 familial Alzheimer’s disease patients with APP or PSEN1 mutations, and 10 non-demented controls were processed for immunohistochemistry. Sections were pretreated with 0.3% hydrogen peroxide, subjected to antigen retrieval in citrate buffer, and incubated in 88% formic acid for 3 minutes. Primary antibodies included 9D5, 4G8, NT78, and 2-48. 9D5 staining was absent in all non-demented controls, even in those with diffuse amyloid plaques detected by 4G8. In contrast, 9D5 staining was abundant in intraneuronal compartments and cerebral amyloid angiopathy in all Alzheimer’s disease cases, with particularly strong staining in patients carrying the Arctic or PSEN1 mutations. In 5XFAD mice, 9D5 staining was undetectable at 3 months, weak at 6 months, and robust at 12 months, correlating with the onset of cognitive deficits. Co-localization with Iba-1 was observed in microglia surrounding intraneuronal deposits, while no co-localization was seen with GFAP-positive astrocytes. These findings confirm the specificity of 9D5 for pathological AβpE3-42 oligomers in human and mouse brains.

Example 9: Production of Humanized 9D5 Antibody for Clinical Use

The variable regions of the murine 9D5 antibody were sequenced and grafted onto a human IgG1 kappa framework using CDR grafting techniques. Framework residues critical for maintaining the conformation of the CDR loops were retained from the murine sequence, while non-conserved residues were replaced with human counterparts. The resulting humanized 9D5 construct was cloned into a mammalian expression vector under the control of the CMV promoter and transfected into CHO-K1 cells. Stable clones were selected using methionine sulfoximine, and antibody production was scaled up in bioreactors. The humanized antibody retained full binding affinity and specificity for AβpE3-42 oligomers in ELISA and dot blot assays, with no detectable cross-reactivity to other amyloid-beta species. In vivo pharmacokinetic studies in cynomolgus monkeys demonstrated a serum half-life of 14 days, comparable to native human IgG1. These results validate the humanized 9D5 antibody as a viable candidate for clinical development in Alzheimer’s disease.

Example 10: Diagnostic Kit for Detection of Alzheimer’s Disease

A diagnostic kit was assembled comprising: (1) a 96-well microplate pre-coated with 5 μg/mL of purified 9D5 antibody; (2) a lyophilized biotinylated anti-pE-Aβ antibody (2-48) at 1 mg/mL; (3) streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase conjugate; (4) TMB substrate solution; (5) stop solution; (6) calibration standards containing 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, and 4.0 ng/mL of synthetic AβpE3-42 oligomers; and (7) a detailed instruction manual specifying sample preparation, incubation times, washing steps, and interpretation criteria. Plasma samples are diluted 1:5 in sample diluent, added to wells, and incubated for 2 hours at room temperature. After washing, detection antibody is added for 1 hour, followed by streptavidin-HRP for 30 minutes. Color development is initiated with TMB and stopped after 10 minutes. A signal below 1.0 ng/mL is indicative of Alzheimer’s disease, while levels above 1.5 ng/mL are consistent with normal cognition. The kit has a sensitivity of 92% and specificity of 94% in a blinded validation study of 100 subjects. This kit enables early, non-invasive diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease using a simple blood test.
