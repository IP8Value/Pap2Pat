# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global swine industry continues to face substantial economic challenges due to the persistent and widespread prevalence of porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2), a small, non-enveloped, single-stranded DNA virus belonging to the genus Circovirus within the family Circoviridae. First identified in the early 1990s in association with postweaning multisystemic wasting syndrome, PCV-2 has since been linked to a spectrum of clinical manifestations including dermatitis and nephropathy syndrome, reproductive failure, respiratory distress, and enteric complications, all of which contribute to significant mortality, reduced growth rates, and increased veterinary intervention costs. The virus’s compact genome, approximately two kilobases in length, encodes a single structural capsid protein of approximately 27 kilodaltons, which is both necessary and sufficient for the formation of virus-like particles that closely mimic the native virion in morphology and antigenic presentation. This capsid protein has emerged as the principal target for vaccine development due to its ability to elicit robust and durable humoral immune responses in swine, including the production of neutralizing antibodies that confer protective immunity against viral challenge.

Current commercial vaccines against PCV-2 are predominantly based on the recombinant expression of the capsid protein in heterologous systems such as insect cells, yeast, or bacterial hosts, followed by purification and formulation as subunit vaccines or virus-like particle (VLP) preparations. While these approaches have demonstrated efficacy in reducing disease incidence and improving herd health, they are inherently constrained by high production costs, complex downstream processing requirements, and the necessity for expensive bioreactor infrastructure, sterile environments, and specialized media formulations. Insect cell culture systems, for instance, require serum-free media supplemented with growth factors and precise temperature and pH control, while yeast and bacterial fermentation systems necessitate stringent aseptic conditions and extensive purification protocols involving multiple chromatographic steps such as affinity, ion-exchange, and size-exclusion chromatography. These methods, although effective, are not easily scalable to meet the demands of large-scale global production, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where swine production is expanding but access to advanced biomanufacturing infrastructure remains limited.

Moreover, the inclusion of affinity tags—commonly used to facilitate purification of recombinant proteins—introduces additional complexity, as these tags must be enzymatically cleaved post-purification to avoid potential immunogenicity or interference with antigenic conformation. The removal of such tags often requires additional purification steps, increasing both time and cost. Furthermore, the risk of contamination with adventitious agents from animal-derived cell lines, such as porcine endogenous retroviruses or other zoonotic pathogens, remains a persistent concern in mammalian and insect expression systems, necessitating rigorous and costly quality control measures. Inactivated whole-virus vaccines, while capable of eliciting strong immune responses, carry inherent biosafety risks during production due to the necessity of handling live, replication-competent virus, which demands high-containment facilities and extensive inactivation validation procedures.

In contrast, plant-based expression systems offer a compelling alternative to conventional production platforms. Plants, particularly Nicotiana benthamiana, have been established as robust and versatile hosts for the transient expression of recombinant proteins through agroinfiltration with Agrobacterium tumefaciens. This method leverages the natural DNA transfer mechanism of Agrobacterium to deliver gene constructs into plant cells, resulting in rapid, high-level protein accumulation within days following infiltration. Importantly, plant cells are capable of performing essential post-translational modifications—including proper folding, disulfide bond formation, and glycosylation patterns compatible with mammalian immune recognition—while simultaneously eliminating the risk of contamination with mammalian or insect pathogens. The upstream production requirements for plant-based systems are minimal: a biosafety level 1 laboratory and a controlled greenhouse environment suffice, rendering the technology accessible, cost-effective, and scalable without reliance on expensive bioreactors or sterile infrastructure. Additionally, plant biomass can be harvested and processed in bulk, enabling the potential for decentralized manufacturing models that could significantly lower the cost of goods and improve vaccine accessibility in resource-limited regions.

Despite these advantages, prior efforts to express PCV-2 capsid protein in plants had not been successfully demonstrated, nor had the assembly of the expressed protein into structurally authentic virus-like particles been confirmed. Previous studies in other expression systems had shown that the PCV-2 capsid protein self-assembles into VLPs with diameters of approximately 17 nanometers, closely resembling the native virion, and that these particles are highly immunogenic. However, the unique cellular environment of plant cells—characterized by distinct organelles, compartmentalized protein trafficking, and a different complement of chaperones and folding enzymes—raised uncertainty as to whether the capsid protein would fold correctly, form stable oligomers, or assemble into particles of the appropriate size and morphology. Furthermore, the feasibility of purifying such particles from plant tissue using simple, scalable, and cost-efficient methods had not been explored. Conventional purification techniques for VLPs derived from other systems typically involve multi-step chromatographic processes or density gradient centrifugation using cesium chloride, which are labor-intensive, expensive, and difficult to scale for industrial production.

This invention addresses these critical gaps by demonstrating, for the first time, the successful transient expression of the PCV-2 capsid protein in Nicotiana benthamiana plants via Agrobacterium-mediated infiltration, the spontaneous self-assembly of this protein into morphologically authentic virus-like particles within plant cells, and the efficient purification of these particles using a single-step sucrose density gradient centrifugation method that requires no affinity tags, no chromatography columns, and no complex instrumentation beyond standard laboratory centrifuges. The purified particles exhibit the expected size, shape, and buoyant density consistent with native PCV-2 virions and retain full immunogenicity, eliciting high-titer, antigen-specific antibody responses in a murine model comparable to those induced by a commercially available subunit vaccine. Crucially, the yield of purified capsid protein obtained from plant biomass—approximately 6.5 milligrams per kilogram of leaf fresh weight—exceeds the productivity of many bacterial expression systems and is sufficient to support large-scale vaccine manufacturing. The entire process, from gene delivery to purified antigen, can be completed in under two weeks, with minimal capital investment and no requirement for sterile conditions beyond standard laboratory practices. This represents a paradigm shift in the production of veterinary vaccines, offering a scalable, safe, affordable, and sustainable platform for the global control of PCV-2 and potentially other circoviral diseases affecting swine populations worldwide.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel, scalable, and cost-effective method for the production of immunogenic porcine circovirus type 2 virus-like particles using a plant-based transient expression system. The invention comprises the expression of the PCV-2 capsid protein in Nicotiana benthamiana plants via agroinfiltration with recombinant Agrobacterium tumefaciens harboring a codon-optimized gene sequence encoding the full-length capsid protein, the spontaneous assembly of the expressed protein into virus-like particles within plant cells, and the purification of these particles from plant tissue using a single-step sucrose density gradient centrifugation technique that does not require affinity tags, chromatography columns, or complex purification equipment. The resulting purified virus-like particles are structurally and immunologically authentic, exhibiting a diameter of approximately 16 nanometers, a buoyant density of 1.564 grams per cubic centimeter in cesium chloride gradients, and the ability to elicit a robust, antigen-specific humoral immune response in mammalian hosts comparable to that induced by commercially available subunit vaccines.

The invention further encompasses the use of the purified virus-like particles as a vaccine antigen for the prevention of porcine circovirus type 2 infection in swine, as well as their use in diagnostic assays for the detection of PCV-2-specific antibodies in serum samples. The method of production described herein eliminates the need for expensive bioreactors, sterile fermentation conditions, or complex downstream purification processes, thereby significantly reducing the cost of goods and enabling decentralized manufacturing in low-resource settings. The plant-based system utilized in the invention is inherently safe, as it carries no risk of contamination with mammalian or insect pathogens, and the purification protocol is simple, reproducible, and amenable to scale-up using standard centrifugation equipment found in most research and industrial laboratories.

The invention also includes a purified composition comprising virus-like particles of porcine circovirus type 2, wherein the particles are assembled from the capsid protein encoded by the ORF2 gene of PCV-2, are free of plant-derived nucleic acids or other viral components, and are substantially free of contaminating plant proteins, as confirmed by SDS-PAGE, mass spectrometry, and transmission electron microscopy. The composition may be formulated with an adjuvant suitable for veterinary use, such as mineral oil, saponin, or aluminum salts, and may be administered via subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intranasal routes to induce protective immunity in swine. The invention further includes a kit comprising the purified virus-like particles in a stable, lyophilized or liquid formulation, along with instructions for reconstitution, dosing, and administration, as well as control reagents for diagnostic applications.

The method of the invention is characterized by a production timeline of less than fourteen days from agroinfiltration to purified antigen, with yields of approximately 6.5 milligrams of capsid protein per kilogram of leaf fresh weight, which is comparable to or exceeds the productivity of bacterial and yeast expression systems. The purification process requires only a single centrifugation step through a sucrose gradient, eliminating the need for affinity chromatography, tag cleavage, or multi-step purification protocols. The resulting particles are stable under standard storage conditions, retain their structural integrity for extended periods, and demonstrate high immunogenicity in preclinical models, making them suitable for use as a next-generation vaccine candidate against PCV-2. The invention thus represents a transformative advancement in veterinary biotechnology, providing a safe, affordable, and scalable platform for the production of virus-like particle vaccines that can be deployed globally to mitigate the economic and welfare impacts of porcine circovirus type 2 infection.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention relates to a method for producing immunogenic virus-like particles of porcine circovirus type 2 using a plant-based transient expression system, wherein the virus-like particles are formed by the self-assembly of the capsid protein encoded by the ORF2 gene of PCV-2, and are purified from plant tissue using a single-step sucrose density gradient centrifugation process. The method begins with the construction of a recombinant expression vector containing a plant-optimized nucleotide sequence encoding the full-length PCV-2 capsid protein, which is then introduced into Agrobacterium tumefaciens via electroporation or other standard transformation techniques. The recombinant Agrobacterium is cultivated in liquid medium under controlled conditions to achieve a desired optical density, after which it is used to infiltrate the leaves of Nicotiana benthamiana plants via vacuum or syringe infiltration. Following a period of incubation under controlled environmental conditions, the infiltrated leaves are harvested, homogenized in a buffered solution, and clarified by centrifugation to remove cellular debris and insoluble material. The clarified supernatant is then layered onto a pre-formed sucrose density gradient and subjected to ultracentrifugation, during which the virus-like particles, due to their density and structural properties, pellet at the bottom of the tube while soluble plant proteins remain distributed throughout the gradient. The pellet is resuspended in a physiologically compatible buffer, such as phosphate-buffered saline, to yield a purified preparation of virus-like particles.

The nucleotide sequence encoding the capsid protein is codon-optimized for expression in plant cells, with modifications made to enhance translation efficiency, increase GC content, eliminate cryptic splice sites, and remove potential regulatory elements that could interfere with mRNA stability or protein accumulation. The sequence is cloned into a plant expression vector under the control of a strong constitutive promoter, such as the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter, and may include a translational enhancer sequence to further boost protein yield. The vector is designed to retain the native capsid protein sequence without the addition of affinity tags, fusion partners, or signal peptides, ensuring that the expressed protein retains its natural conformation and self-assembly properties. The Agrobacterium strain used for infiltration is selected for its high transformation efficiency and compatibility with the expression vector, with LBA4404 being the preferred strain due to its ability to induce high levels of protein expression within four days post-infiltration. The infiltration is performed at an optical density of approximately 1.0 at 600 nanometers, which has been determined to maximize protein accumulation without inducing excessive plant stress or tissue necrosis. The infiltrated plants are maintained under controlled environmental conditions, including a 16-hour light/8-hour dark photoperiod and a temperature of approximately 22 degrees Celsius, to support optimal protein expression and accumulation.

Following harvest, the plant tissue is homogenized in a buffer containing sodium chloride, calcium chloride, glycerol, and a protease inhibitor cocktail to preserve protein integrity and prevent degradation. The homogenate is clarified by centrifugation at high speed to remove cell walls, chloroplasts, and other particulate matter, yielding a clear supernatant that contains the expressed capsid protein in soluble and assembled forms. The supernatant is then filtered through multiple layers of Miracloth or a similar membrane to ensure complete removal of any residual particulates before being layered onto a sucrose gradient prepared in a stepwise manner, typically consisting of a 45% sucrose layer overlaid with a 65% sucrose layer. The gradient is centrifuged at high speed for several hours under controlled temperature conditions, during which the virus-like particles, due to their density and structural integrity, sediment through the gradient and accumulate at the interface or in the pellet, while soluble plant proteins remain dispersed in the upper fractions. The pellet is then carefully resuspended in a small volume of phosphate-buffered saline or another biocompatible buffer to yield a concentrated preparation of purified virus-like particles.

The purified preparation is analyzed by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, which reveals a predominant protein band at approximately 27 kilodaltons corresponding to the monomeric capsid protein, as well as a secondary band at approximately 54 kilodaltons, which corresponds to a stable dimeric form of the capsid protein. Mass spectrometry analysis confirms the identity of the 27-kilodalton band as the PCV-2 capsid protein, with no detectable contamination from plant or bacterial proteins. Transmission electron microscopy of the purified preparation reveals the presence of spherical particles with diameters ranging from 12 to 24 nanometers, with a mean diameter of approximately 16 nanometers, closely matching the size and morphology of native PCV-2 virions. The particles exhibit a uniform, icosahedral structure with no evidence of envelope or nucleocapsid components, confirming their identity as virus-like particles. Further analysis using cesium chloride density gradient centrifugation demonstrates a buoyant density of 1.564 grams per cubic centimeter, which is consistent with the known density of PCV-2 VLPs produced in other systems and confirms the structural integrity of the particles.

The purified virus-like particles are highly immunogenic, as demonstrated by their ability to elicit a strong, antigen-specific antibody response in mice following subcutaneous immunization with an adjuvant. Mice immunized with the plant-produced VLPs develop serum antibody titers comparable to those induced by a commercially available subunit vaccine, with no detectable immune response observed in control animals immunized with plant extracts lacking the capsid protein. The immune response is characterized by the production of IgG antibodies capable of binding to the native PCV-2 capsid protein, suggesting that the plant-produced particles retain conformational epitopes critical for neutralization. The particles are stable under standard storage conditions for extended periods, and may be lyophilized or formulated in liquid suspension with an adjuvant for long-term storage and ease of administration. The entire production process, from gene delivery to purified antigen, requires no sterile conditions beyond standard laboratory practices, no expensive bioreactors, no chromatography columns, and no affinity tags, making it significantly more cost-effective and scalable than existing production platforms.

The invention further encompasses the use of the purified virus-like particles as a vaccine for the prevention of porcine circovirus type 2 infection in swine. The particles may be administered alone or in combination with an immunological adjuvant, such as mineral oil, saponin, or aluminum hydroxide, to enhance the magnitude and duration of the immune response. The vaccine may be administered via subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intranasal routes, with one or more booster doses given at intervals of two to four weeks to ensure sustained immunity. The vaccine is suitable for use in piglets, weaners, sows, and boars, and may be incorporated into multivalent vaccine formulations targeting other swine pathogens such as porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus or swine influenza virus. The invention also includes diagnostic applications, wherein the purified virus-like particles are used as capture antigens in enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays to detect PCV-2-specific antibodies in serum samples from swine, enabling serological surveillance and herd health monitoring.

The method of the invention is characterized by a production yield of approximately 6.5 milligrams of purified capsid protein per kilogram of leaf fresh weight, which is sufficient to produce thousands of vaccine doses per kilogram of plant biomass. The process is scalable to industrial levels using automated infiltration systems, large-scale greenhouse cultivation, and high-throughput centrifugation equipment, enabling the production of millions of doses annually at a fraction of the cost of conventional systems. The absence of affinity tags and chromatographic purification steps eliminates the need for expensive resins, enzymes, and consumables, while the use of plant biomass as a renewable and non-animal-derived production platform ensures sustainability and reduces the risk of zoonotic contamination. The invention thus provides a transformative solution to the global challenge of PCV-2 control, offering a safe, affordable, and scalable vaccine production platform that can be deployed in both developed and developing regions of the world.

### Example 1

In a representative example of the invention, a recombinant plasmid was constructed containing the codon-optimized open reading frame 2 (ORF2) gene of porcine circovirus type 2, derived from a Lithuanian isolate and modified to enhance expression in plant cells. The gene sequence was synthesized with an added 5′ AgeI restriction site and a 3′ XhoI restriction site to facilitate directional cloning into the pEAQ-HT plant expression vector, which contains the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter, a tobacco etch virus 5′ untranslated region for enhanced translation, and a nopaline synthase terminator. The resulting construct, designated PCV-2_pEAQ, was transformed into Escherichia coli for amplification and verified by colony PCR and DNA sequencing. The plasmid was then introduced into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404 via electroporation, and transformants were selected on media containing kanamycin and rifampicin. The recombinant Agrobacterium culture was grown in liquid medium to an optical density of 1.0 at 600 nanometers, diluted in infiltration buffer containing magnesium chloride and acetosyringone, and incubated for one hour to induce virulence gene expression.

Six-week-old Nicotiana benthamiana plants were vacuum-infiltrated with the bacterial suspension, and the infiltrated leaves were harvested at four days post-infiltration. The leaves were weighed, homogenized in extraction buffer containing sodium chloride, calcium chloride, glycerol, and a protease inhibitor cocktail, and clarified by centrifugation at 8,000 × g for 20 minutes. The supernatant was filtered through four layers of Miracloth to remove residual particulates and layered onto a sucrose gradient consisting of 6 milliliters of 45% sucrose overlaid with 2 milliliters of 65% sucrose in ultracentrifuge tubes. The gradient was centrifuged at 120,000 × g for four hours at 4 degrees Celsius. After centrifugation, the pellet was resuspended in one milliliter of endotoxin-free phosphate-buffered saline, and the resulting suspension was analyzed by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, which revealed a dominant protein band at 27 kilodaltons and a secondary band at 54 kilodaltons. Mass spectrometry analysis confirmed the identity of the 27-kilodalton band as the PCV-2 capsid protein, with no detectable contamination from plant or bacterial proteins. The 54-kilodalton band was identified as a stable dimer of the capsid protein, consistent with previously reported oligomerization behavior in other expression systems.

Transmission electron microscopy of the resuspended pellet revealed the presence of spherical particles with diameters ranging from 12 to 24 nanometers, with a mean diameter of 16 nanometers and a morphology indistinguishable from native PCV-2 virions. No particles were observed in control samples derived from plants infiltrated with empty vector. Further purification using cesium chloride density gradient centrifugation yielded a band at a buoyant density of 1.564 grams per cubic centimeter, corresponding to the most concentrated fraction of virus-like particles. Immunogenicity testing in mice demonstrated that animals immunized with the purified particles developed high-titer, antigen-specific IgG antibodies, with serum titers comparable to those induced by a commercial subunit vaccine. No immune response was detected in control animals immunized with plant extract lacking the capsid protein. The yield of purified capsid protein was determined to be approximately 6.5 milligrams per kilogram of leaf fresh weight, and the entire process from infiltration to purified antigen was completed in less than seven days. The purified preparation was stable for over six months at 4 degrees Celsius and retained full immunogenicity upon reconstitution.

### Discussion

The successful expression and purification of porcine circovirus type 2 virus-like particles from Nicotiana benthamiana plants represents a significant advancement in the field of veterinary vaccinology, offering a novel, scalable, and economically viable alternative to existing production platforms. The demonstration that the PCV-2 capsid protein can spontaneously assemble into morphologically authentic virus-like particles within the plant cellular environment underscores the remarkable capacity of plant cells to support the correct folding and oligomerization of complex viral proteins, even in the absence of the native viral genome or specialized chaperone systems typically found in animal cells. The observation that the capsid protein forms stable dimers in addition to monomers suggests that the plant cellular milieu provides sufficient structural guidance for the protein to adopt its native conformation, and that these dimers may serve as intermediates in the assembly pathway leading to the formation of complete virus-like particles. The absence of any detectable contamination from plant or bacterial proteins in the purified preparation further confirms the specificity and efficiency of the purification process, which relies solely on the physical properties of the assembled particles rather than biochemical affinity.

The use of a single-step sucrose density gradient centrifugation for purification is a critical innovation of the invention, as it eliminates the need for expensive and labor-intensive chromatographic methods that are standard in other expression systems. This approach not only reduces production costs but also simplifies the manufacturing process, making it accessible to laboratories and facilities without advanced bioprocessing infrastructure. The fact that the purified particles retain their structural integrity and immunogenicity after this simple purification process indicates that the plant-produced VLPs are not only physically stable but also functionally equivalent to those produced in more complex systems. The buoyant density of 1.564 grams per cubic centimeter observed in cesium chloride gradients, while slightly higher than previously reported values for PCV-2 VLPs, may reflect subtle differences in protein composition, hydration, or the presence of plant-derived molecules that co-purify with the particles, yet does not appear to impair their biological function. The high yield of approximately 6.5 milligrams per kilogram of leaf fresh weight is particularly noteworthy, as it exceeds the productivity of many bacterial expression systems and is sufficient to support large-scale vaccine production without requiring extensive biomass cultivation.

The immunogenicity data presented in this example provide compelling evidence that the plant-produced VLPs are capable of eliciting a robust and specific immune response in a mammalian model, with antibody titers comparable to those induced by a commercially licensed subunit vaccine. This finding is of critical importance, as it validates the functional equivalence of the plant-derived antigen and supports its potential for use in swine vaccination programs. The lack of immune response in control animals immunized with plant extract confirms that the observed immunogenicity is attributable solely to the PCV-2 capsid protein and not to non-specific immune stimulation by plant components. The stability of the purified particles under standard storage conditions further enhances their practical utility, as it allows for long-term storage, transport, and distribution without the need for cold chain logistics, which is a major barrier to vaccine access in many parts of the world.

The implications of this invention extend far beyond the control of PCV-2. The methodology described herein establishes a generalizable platform for the production of virus-like particles for other economically significant animal pathogens, including porcine circovirus type 3, porcine parvovirus, and other members of the Circoviridae family. The ability to rapidly express and purify immunogenic antigens from plants using a simple, tag-free, chromatography-free method opens the door to the development of next-generation vaccines for a wide range of veterinary diseases, particularly in regions where access to advanced biotechnology is limited. Moreover, the scalability of the system, combined with its low capital and operational costs, makes it uniquely suited for decentralized manufacturing models that can be implemented at the regional or even farm level, thereby reducing dependence on centralized pharmaceutical supply chains.

The invention also has broader implications for global food security and animal welfare. By providing a cost-effective means of preventing PCV-2-related disease, the technology has the potential to reduce mortality rates, improve growth efficiency, and decrease the use of antibiotics in swine production, all of which contribute to more sustainable and ethical farming practices. The absence of animal-derived components in the production process further aligns the technology with growing consumer and regulatory demands for ethically produced and environmentally sustainable agricultural products. In summary, the invention represents a paradigm shift in veterinary vaccine development, offering a safe, affordable, and scalable solution to one of the most economically devastating diseases in global swine production, with the potential to transform the landscape of animal health care worldwide.
