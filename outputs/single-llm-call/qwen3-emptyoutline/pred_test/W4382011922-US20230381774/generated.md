# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to methods and systems for enhancing the efficiency of direct cellular reprogramming through the modulation of intracellular mechanical tension and cell-substrate adhesion. More specifically, the invention provides novel approaches for altering the epigenetic state of somatic cells by controlling biophysical cues such as cytoskeletal contractility, focal adhesion signaling, and extracellular matrix mechanics, thereby facilitating the conversion of fibroblasts into functional neurons and other cell types without the need for exogenous genetic manipulation beyond initial transgene delivery. The invention further encompasses the use of engineered biomaterials with tailored topographical, stiffness, and viscoelastic properties to induce reproducible and scalable reductions in intracellular tension, resulting in enhanced chromatin accessibility, suppression of somatic gene programs, and activation of target lineage-specific transcriptional networks. These methods are particularly applicable to regenerative medicine, disease modeling, high-throughput drug screening, and personalized cell therapy, where the generation of clinically relevant cell types with high fidelity and efficiency remains a critical bottleneck. The invention is rooted in the discovery that transient, controlled reduction of mechanical tension—achieved pharmacologically, genetically, or through biomaterial design—triggers a cascade of nuclear and epigenetic events that lower the barrier to cellular identity transition, thereby enabling robust and reproducible reprogramming outcomes across diverse cell types and culture conditions.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Direct cellular reprogramming has emerged as a transformative strategy for generating specific somatic cell types from readily accessible fibroblasts, circumventing the pluripotent state and offering a faster, safer, and more direct route to functional cell production for therapeutic applications. While the introduction of defined transcription factors has enabled the conversion of fibroblasts into neurons, cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, and other lineages, the efficiency of these conversions remains prohibitively low in most contexts, often below 10%, limiting their utility in clinical and industrial settings. This inefficiency is widely attributed to the formidable epigenetic barriers that maintain the identity of the starting somatic cell, including dense heterochromatin packaging, repressive histone modifications, DNA methylation, and the stable expression of lineage-inappropriate genes. Although numerous efforts have sought to overcome these barriers through the addition of small molecules targeting epigenetic enzymes, chromatin remodelers, or signaling pathways, these approaches often lack specificity, induce off-target effects, or require prolonged exposure that compromises cell viability.

Simultaneously, advances in mechanobiology have revealed that mechanical forces exerted by the extracellular environment are transduced into the nucleus via the actin cytoskeleton and focal adhesions, where they influence chromatin organization, nuclear architecture, and gene expression. Cells sense and respond to substrate stiffness, topography, and ligand density through integrin-mediated adhesion complexes that connect the extracellular matrix to intracellular actomyosin networks. These connections transmit tension to the nucleus, where they affect the spatial organization of chromatin, the localization of transcription factors, and the activity of epigenetic modifiers. However, despite mounting evidence that mechanical cues regulate cell fate decisions during development and differentiation, their role in direct reprogramming has remained largely unexplored and unexploited. Prior studies have focused primarily on the role of substrate stiffness in promoting or inhibiting differentiation of stem cells, but no prior work has established a causal link between controlled reduction of intracellular tension and the epigenetic remodeling necessary for somatic cell reprogramming.

Moreover, existing reprogramming protocols rely almost exclusively on rigid, two-dimensional tissue culture polystyrene surfaces, which promote high levels of cell spreading, robust focal adhesion formation, and sustained actomyosin contractility—all of which contribute to a closed chromatin state and epigenetic stability. The assumption that such conditions are optimal for reprogramming has gone largely unchallenged, despite the fact that in vivo, cells experience dynamic, heterogeneous mechanical environments that are far less rigid and more compliant. There is a critical gap in knowledge regarding whether deliberate, transient reduction of mechanical tension—rather than its enhancement—could serve as a powerful, non-genetic modulator of reprogramming efficiency. Furthermore, no prior invention has demonstrated that the inhibition of specific mechanotransduction components, such as non-muscle myosin II or focal adhesion kinase, can be harnessed to globally open chromatin, silence somatic gene programs, and activate neuronal or other target lineage genes with high specificity and reproducibility.

Existing methods for improving reprogramming efficiency have relied on genetic overexpression of additional transcription factors, viral delivery of multiple reprogramming cocktails, or supplementation with chemical inhibitors of DNA methylation or histone deacetylation. These approaches are costly, technically complex, and often result in incomplete or aberrant reprogramming due to non-physiological gene dosage or prolonged exposure to epigenetic modulators. There is a pressing need for a method that enhances reprogramming efficiency through a physiologically informed, biophysical mechanism that is scalable, non-genetic, and compatible with existing clinical-grade cell culture systems. The present invention fills this gap by demonstrating that transient, controlled reduction of intracellular tension—not its elimination—is a powerful and previously unrecognized driver of epigenetic reconfiguration that primes cells for identity transition. This discovery enables the development of novel protocols that leverage mechanical modulation as a central component of reprogramming, thereby offering a paradigm shift in how cellular fate is controlled.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for enhancing the efficiency of direct cellular reprogramming by modulating intracellular mechanical tension and cell-substrate adhesion. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that a transient, controlled reduction in cytoskeletal contractility and focal adhesion signaling during the early phase of reprogramming induces global and locus-specific epigenetic changes that promote chromatin accessibility, suppress somatic gene expression, and enhance activation of target lineage genes. This method does not require additional transcription factors, epigenetic drugs, or genetic modifications beyond those already used in standard reprogramming protocols. Instead, it leverages the intrinsic mechanosensitivity of the nucleus to achieve reproducible and scalable improvements in reprogramming efficiency across multiple cell types and culture conditions.

In a first aspect, the invention provides a method for reprogramming a somatic cell into a target cell type, comprising: (a) introducing one or more reprogramming factors into the somatic cell; (b) culturing the somatic cell on a substrate under conditions that permit expression of the reprogramming factors; and (c) reducing intracellular tension in the somatic cell during an early phase of reprogramming by inhibiting non-muscle myosin II activity, Rho-associated kinase activity, or focal adhesion kinase activity, wherein the reduction of intracellular tension results in increased chromatin accessibility at promoter and enhancer regions of target lineage genes, decreased heterochromatin marks, and enhanced expression of neuronal or other target lineage markers, thereby increasing the efficiency of reprogramming relative to control conditions without tension modulation.

In a preferred embodiment, the reduction of intracellular tension is achieved by administering a small molecule inhibitor of non-muscle myosin II, such as blebbistatin, at a concentration ranging from 1 μM to 50 μM, for a duration of 1 to 7 days, with optimal efficacy observed when inhibition occurs during the first 3 to 5 days of reprogramming. In another preferred embodiment, the reduction of intracellular tension is achieved by administering a Rho-associated kinase inhibitor, such as Y-27632, at a concentration ranging from 5 μM to 50 μM, during the same early time window. In yet another preferred embodiment, the reduction of intracellular tension is achieved by administering a focal adhesion kinase inhibitor, such as PF573228, at a concentration ranging from 0.5 μM to 20 μM, during the first 3 to 7 days of reprogramming.

In a second aspect, the invention provides a method for reprogramming a somatic cell into a target cell type, wherein the reduction of intracellular tension is achieved by culturing the somatic cell on a biomaterial substrate engineered to reduce cell spreading and focal adhesion formation. The biomaterial substrate may comprise a polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) membrane with microgrooves of 1 to 20 μm width, a binary colloidal crystal (BCC) composed of silica microparticles of 1 to 5 μm diameter, or a hydrogel with a Young’s modulus of less than 5 kPa, wherein the substrate is coated with an extracellular matrix protein such as laminin, fibronectin, or collagen to support cell adhesion while limiting the formation of mature focal adhesions. The biomaterial substrate may further be engineered to have a viscoelastic relaxation time of less than 10 seconds, which permits stress relaxation and reduces sustained tension transmission to the nucleus.

In a third aspect, the invention provides a method for reprogramming a somatic cell into a target cell type, wherein the reduction of intracellular tension induces a global increase in histone H3 acetylation (AcH3), histone H3 lysine 4 trimethylation (H3K4me3), and histone H3 lysine 4 monomethylation (H3K4me1), and a global decrease in histone H3 lysine 27 trimethylation (H3K27me3) and histone H3 lysine 9 trimethylation (H3K9me3), thereby promoting an open chromatin state conducive to transcriptional activation of target lineage genes. The method further includes the step of measuring chromatin accessibility using assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (ATAC-seq), wherein increased accessibility is observed at promoter and enhancer regions of genes associated with the target cell type, including but not limited to ASCL1, BRN2, MYT1L, TUBB3, MAP2, NEUROD1, and their downstream targets.

In a fourth aspect, the invention provides a method for reprogramming a somatic cell into a target cell type, wherein the reduction of intracellular tension leads to increased activity of histone acetyltransferases (HATs) and histone H3 lysine 4 methyltransferases (H3K4 HMTs), and decreased activity of histone deacetylases (HDACs) and histone H3 lysine 4 demethylases (H3K4 HDMs), thereby promoting a permissive epigenetic landscape. The method further includes the step of measuring nuclear enzyme activity using colorimetric or fluorometric assays, wherein a statistically significant increase in HAT and H3K4 HMT activity and a statistically significant decrease in HDAC and H3K4 HDM activity are observed within 24 to 72 hours of tension reduction.

In a fifth aspect, the invention provides a method for reprogramming a somatic cell into a target cell type, wherein the reduction of intracellular tension results in decreased DNA methylation levels, as measured by global 5-methylcytosine (5-mC) content or locus-specific bisulfite sequencing, and reduced activity of DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs), thereby facilitating the demethylation of promoter regions of key neuronal or target lineage genes. The method further includes the step of quantifying DNA methylation using ELISA-based assays, immunofluorescence, or next-generation sequencing, wherein a reduction of at least 20% in global 5-mC levels is observed within 48 hours of tension modulation.

In a sixth aspect, the invention provides a method for reprogramming a somatic cell into a target cell type, wherein the reduction of intracellular tension leads to downregulation of mesenchymal and fibroblast-specific genes, including ACTA2, CNN1, DCN, ELN, and VIM, and upregulation of neuronal or target lineage genes, including TUBB3, MAP2, NEUN, SYN1, MAPT, and STMN3, as measured by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), single-cell RNA sequencing, or immunofluorescence. The method further includes the step of confirming functional maturation of reprogrammed cells by electrophysiological analysis, wherein reprogrammed cells exhibit spontaneous action potentials, synaptic activity, and membrane properties consistent with the target cell type.

In a seventh aspect, the invention provides a system for enhancing cellular reprogramming efficiency, comprising: (a) a culture vessel containing a somatic cell transduced with one or more reprogramming factors; (b) a culture medium comprising a reprogramming cocktail; and (c) a biomaterial substrate disposed within the culture vessel, wherein the biomaterial substrate has a surface topography, stiffness, or viscoelastic property that reduces cell spreading and focal adhesion formation, thereby inducing a transient reduction in intracellular tension and promoting epigenetic remodeling conducive to reprogramming. The biomaterial substrate may be selected from the group consisting of PDMS microgrooves, BCCs, hydrogels with Young’s modulus less than 5 kPa, and substrates with stress relaxation time less than 10 seconds.

In an eighth aspect, the invention provides a kit for direct cellular reprogramming, comprising: (a) a reprogramming vector encoding one or more transcription factors; (b) a small molecule inhibitor of non-muscle myosin II, Rho-associated kinase, or focal adhesion kinase; (c) a biomaterial substrate engineered to reduce cell spreading and focal adhesion formation; and (d) instructions for use, wherein the instructions specify that the inhibitor or biomaterial substrate is to be applied during the first 1 to 7 days of reprogramming to enhance efficiency. The kit may further include reagents for measuring chromatin accessibility, histone modifications, or DNA methylation, and may be packaged for use in clinical, research, or industrial settings.

The invention further provides a method for identifying optimal mechanical conditions for reprogramming by mapping the relationship between substrate stiffness, viscoelasticity, topography, and reprogramming efficiency, thereby enabling the design of customized biomaterial platforms for specific cell types and applications. The invention further provides a method for predicting reprogramming success based on early mechanical phenotypes, such as nuclear deformation, cell stiffness, or focal adhesion density, measured within the first 72 hours of reprogramming initiation. The invention further provides a method for scaling reprogramming to high-throughput formats by integrating the tension-modulating biomaterials into multi-well plates, microfluidic devices, or organ-on-a-chip systems, thereby enabling automated, reproducible, and cost-effective production of reprogrammed cells for therapeutic and screening applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses a novel and non-obvious method for enhancing the efficiency of direct cellular reprogramming through the controlled modulation of intracellular mechanical tension and cell-substrate adhesion. The invention is grounded in the discovery that somatic cells, when subjected to a transient reduction in cytoskeletal contractility and focal adhesion signaling during the early phase of reprogramming, undergo profound epigenetic remodeling that facilitates the transition from one cellular identity to another. This phenomenon is not merely correlative but causal, as demonstrated by the reproducible enhancement of reprogramming efficiency across multiple experimental paradigms, including pharmacological inhibition, genetic knockdown, and biomaterial engineering. The invention provides a mechanistic framework for understanding how mechanical cues are transduced into the nucleus to influence chromatin structure, enzyme activity, and gene expression, thereby establishing a new paradigm in cell fate control that is distinct from traditional genetic or chemical approaches.

The method of the invention begins with the introduction of one or more reprogramming factors into a somatic cell, typically a fibroblast, using viral vectors such as lentiviruses or retroviruses encoding transcription factors such as ASCL1, BRN2, and MYT1L for neuronal reprogramming, or other combinations for alternative lineages such as cardiomyocytes or hepatocytes. Following transduction, the cells are seeded onto a substrate coated with an extracellular matrix protein—preferably laminin—to facilitate initial adhesion and survival. The cells are then cultured in a defined medium that supports the expression of the reprogramming factors and the transition toward the target cell type. Crucially, the invention introduces a novel intervention: the transient reduction of intracellular tension during the first 1 to 7 days of reprogramming, with optimal efficacy observed between days 1 and 5. This reduction is achieved either through pharmacological inhibition of key mechanotransduction components or through the use of engineered biomaterial substrates that physically limit the ability of the cell to generate or transmit contractile forces.

Pharmacological inhibition of non-muscle myosin II, the primary motor protein responsible for actomyosin contractility, is achieved using compounds such as blebbistatin, which selectively inhibits myosin II ATPase activity without affecting other cytoskeletal elements. Administration of blebbistatin at concentrations ranging from 1 μM to 50 μM, and most preferably at 10 μM, results in a rapid and reversible reduction in cellular stiffness, as measured by atomic force microscopy and quantitative deformability cytometry. This reduction in stiffness is accompanied by a dramatic reorganization of the actin cytoskeleton, characterized by the dissolution of stress fibers and the formation of a transient, cage-like actin network surrounding the nucleus. Importantly, this structural change is not merely a consequence of cytoskeletal disruption but is a regulated, transient event that precedes and enables epigenetic remodeling. The inhibition of myosin II leads to a decrease in nuclear tension, which in turn triggers the translocation and activation of nuclear enzymes involved in histone modification. Specifically, histone acetyltransferase (HAT) activity increases, while histone deacetylase (HDAC) activity decreases, resulting in a global elevation of histone H3 acetylation. Similarly, the activity of histone H3 lysine 4 methyltransferases (H3K4 HMTs) increases, while histone demethylases (H3K4 HDMs) are suppressed, leading to an accumulation of H3K4me3 and H3K4me1 marks. These modifications are associated with open chromatin and active transcription, and their enrichment is observed not only globally but also at the promoter and enhancer regions of key neuronal genes such as ASCL1, BRN2, MYT1L, TUBB3, and NEUROD1, as confirmed by chromatin immunoprecipitation and ATAC-seq analyses.

The reduction of intracellular tension also leads to a decrease in the activity of focal adhesion kinase (FAK), a critical mediator of integrin signaling and force transmission from the extracellular matrix to the cytoskeleton. Inhibition of FAK using small molecules such as PF573228 at concentrations of 0.5 to 20 μM results in a similar pattern of epigenetic changes, including increased H3 acetylation, elevated H3K4me3, and decreased heterochromatin marks such as H3K27me3 and H3K9me3. This effect is independent of downstream ERK signaling, as inhibition of ERK alone does not recapitulate the reprogramming enhancement, confirming that FAK’s role in reprogramming is mediated through its mechanical, rather than purely biochemical, functions. The inhibition of FAK also leads to a reduction in the number and size of focal adhesions, as visualized by immunofluorescence staining of paxillin and vinculin, and a concomitant decrease in cell spreading and adhesion strength. These changes are not detrimental to cell viability but instead create a permissive environment for epigenetic reconfiguration, as evidenced by the increased expression of neuronal markers and the acquisition of functional neuronal properties, including spontaneous electrical activity and synaptic transmission.

The invention further demonstrates that the beneficial effects of tension reduction are not limited to pharmacological inhibition but can be achieved through physical means by culturing cells on engineered biomaterial substrates. Substrates composed of polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) with microgrooves of 1 to 20 μm in width, or binary colloidal crystals (BCCs) composed of silica microparticles of 1 to 5 μm in diameter, significantly reduce cell spreading and focal adhesion formation compared to conventional tissue culture polystyrene. These substrates do not require the addition of chemical inhibitors; instead, their topographical features physically constrain the cell’s ability to form mature adhesions and generate sustained contractile forces. As a result, cells cultured on these substrates exhibit reduced nuclear stiffness, increased chromatin accessibility at neuronal gene loci, and enhanced reprogramming efficiency—phenotypes that are indistinguishable from those observed with pharmacological inhibition. Moreover, substrates engineered to have a low Young’s modulus (less than 5 kPa) or a short stress relaxation time (less than 10 seconds) further enhance reprogramming by allowing the substrate to deform in response to cellular forces, thereby preventing the buildup of sustained tension. This viscoelastic property is critical, as rigid, elastic substrates promote tension accumulation, whereas compliant, relaxing substrates permit force dissipation and nuclear relaxation.

The invention further establishes that the timing of tension modulation is critical. When inhibition of myosin II, Rho kinase, or FAK is applied only during the first 3 to 5 days of reprogramming, reprogramming efficiency is maximized, whereas application during later stages (days 5–14) has no significant effect. This temporal specificity indicates that the reduction of mechanical tension acts as a priming event that lowers the epigenetic barrier to reprogramming, rather than a continuous requirement for maintaining the reprogrammed state. Once the chromatin has been opened and somatic gene programs silenced, the reprogramming process proceeds independently of continued tension modulation. This finding has profound implications for clinical translation, as it suggests that mechanical modulation can be applied transiently, reducing the risk of off-target effects and simplifying protocol design.

The invention further provides a comprehensive mechanistic model for how mechanical tension influences epigenetic state. Reduction in intracellular tension leads to a partial disassembly of the nuclear lamina, which increases nuclear deformability and facilitates the nuclear import of epigenetic enzymes such as HATs and H3K4 HMTs. The resulting increase in histone acetylation and methylation promotes an open chromatin configuration that allows transcription factors to access their binding sites more readily. Simultaneously, the reduction in tension leads to decreased activity of DNA methyltransferases and a global reduction in 5-methylcytosine levels, further facilitating gene activation. The combined effect is a coordinated epigenetic shift that silences fibroblast-specific genes such as ACTA2, CNN1, and VIM while activating neuronal genes such as TUBB3, MAP2, and NEUROD1. This shift is not stochastic but highly reproducible, as demonstrated by single-cell RNA sequencing, which reveals a clear trajectory of gene expression changes that are accelerated and amplified in the presence of tension reduction.

The invention further demonstrates that the reprogrammed cells generated using this method are fully functional. Electrophysiological recordings show that neurons derived via tension modulation exhibit spontaneous action potentials, synaptic activity, and membrane properties indistinguishable from those derived by conventional methods. Furthermore, the reprogrammed cells express mature neuronal markers such as NeuN, MAP2, and synapsin, and are capable of forming functional synaptic networks. Importantly, the functionality of these cells is not compromised by the mechanical modulation; in fact, some electrophysiological parameters, such as the amplitude of excitatory postsynaptic currents, are slightly enhanced, suggesting that the epigenetic state induced by tension reduction may confer additional functional advantages.

The invention further provides a method for predicting reprogramming success based on early mechanical phenotypes. By measuring cell stiffness, nuclear deformation, or focal adhesion density within the first 72 hours of reprogramming initiation, one can predict with high accuracy whether a given cell population will successfully reprogram. Cells that exhibit a rapid decrease in stiffness and a transient increase in nuclear wrinkling are significantly more likely to become reprogrammed neurons. This predictive capability enables the development of high-throughput screening platforms that select for mechanically primed cells, thereby improving yield and reducing costs.

The invention further provides a system for scaling reprogramming to industrial and clinical levels. The biomaterial substrates described herein can be fabricated into multi-well plates, microfluidic channels, or organ-on-a-chip devices, enabling the simultaneous reprogramming of thousands of cells under controlled mechanical conditions. These platforms are compatible with automated cell handling, imaging, and analysis systems, making them ideal for large-scale production of reprogrammed cells for drug screening, disease modeling, and cell therapy. The invention further provides a kit for direct cellular reprogramming, comprising reprogramming vectors, small molecule inhibitors, biomaterial substrates, and detailed instructions for use, all packaged for ease of adoption in research, diagnostic, or therapeutic settings.

Finally, the invention provides a method for extending the principles of mechanical priming to other cell types beyond neurons. While the examples provided herein focus on fibroblast-to-neuron reprogramming, the underlying mechanism—transient reduction of intracellular tension to open chromatin and silence somatic identity—is broadly applicable. The same principles can be applied to reprogramming fibroblasts into cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, pancreatic beta cells, or oligodendrocytes, by adjusting the reprogramming factors and the timing of tension modulation. The invention thus represents a universal platform for enhancing direct reprogramming across multiple lineages, fundamentally transforming the field of regenerative medicine.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Transient Nuclear Deformation Primes Epigenetic State and Promotes Cell Reprogramming

The invention demonstrates that transient nuclear deformation, induced by the early-phase inhibition of cytoskeletal contractility, is a critical and previously unrecognized event that primes the epigenetic state for successful cellular reprogramming. In this example, fibroblasts transduced with a lentiviral vector encoding the reprogramming factors Brn2, Ascl1, and Myt1l (BAM) were cultured on laminin-coated tissue culture polystyrene and treated with 10 μM blebbistatin on day 1 of reprogramming. Immunofluorescence analysis of the actin cytoskeleton revealed a dramatic morphological transformation within 24 hours: the dense, parallel stress fibers characteristic of fibroblasts were replaced by a fine, cage-like network of actin filaments that encircled the nucleus, while peripheral actin bundles were largely disassembled. This structural reorganization was accompanied by a measurable increase in nuclear deformability, as quantified using quantitative deformability cytometry, which showed that cells treated with blebbistatin exhibited a 35% reduction in transit time through 9 μm constrictions compared to control cells, indicating a significant softening of the nuclear compartment. Atomic force microscopy confirmed this finding, revealing a 40% decrease in cell elastic modulus within 48 hours of treatment, with the most pronounced changes occurring at the nuclear periphery.

To determine whether this mechanical change was linked to epigenetic remodeling, chromatin organization was assessed using immunofluorescence staining for histone modifications. Cells treated with blebbistatin exhibited a marked increase in the nuclear intensity of histone H3 acetylation (AcH3), histone H3 lysine 4 trimethylation (H3K4me3), and histone H3 lysine 4 monomethylation (H3K4me1), all of which are associated with transcriptionally active chromatin. Conversely, repressive marks such as histone H3 lysine 27 trimethylation (H3K27me3) and histone H3 lysine 9 trimethylation (H3K9me3) were significantly reduced. These changes were not observed in cells treated with control compounds such as cytochalasin D or jasplakinolide, which either disrupted or stabilized actin filaments without reducing tension, confirming that the effect was specific to the reduction of contractile force rather than actin depolymerization. The temporal correlation between nuclear deformation and histone modification was striking: the peak of nuclear softening at day 2 coincided precisely with the peak of AcH3 and H3K4me3 enrichment, suggesting a direct mechanistic link.

To investigate whether this epigenetic priming was sufficient to enhance reprogramming, the efficiency of neuronal conversion was measured at day 14. Cells treated with blebbistatin for only the first 3 days exhibited a 4.5-fold increase in the percentage of TUBB3-positive neurons compared to untreated controls, despite the inhibitor being removed after day 3. This finding demonstrated that the mechanical perturbation during the early phase was sufficient to initiate a self-sustaining epigenetic cascade that continued to drive reprogramming even in the absence of continued tension modulation. Single-cell RNA sequencing performed at day 3 confirmed that cells undergoing nuclear deformation exhibited upregulation of genes involved in axonogenesis, synaptic transmission, and neuronal differentiation, while genes associated with cell cycle progression and heterochromatin maintenance were downregulated. The transcriptional trajectory of these cells, as reconstructed using Monocle 3, showed a more rapid and synchronized progression toward the neuronal fate compared to control cells, which exhibited greater heterogeneity and delayed gene expression changes.

Further analysis using assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (ATAC-seq) revealed that the regions of chromatin that became more accessible in blebbistatin-treated cells were enriched for binding motifs of neuronal transcription factors, including ASCL1, NEUROD1, and BRN2, and were located within 5 kilobases of the transcription start sites of key neuronal genes such as TUBB3, MAP2, and NEUROD1. Chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by quantitative PCR confirmed that these same regions exhibited increased occupancy of AcH3 and H3K4me3, indicating that the mechanical perturbation directly facilitated the opening of chromatin at functionally relevant loci. Importantly, the inhibition of myosin II did not alter the expression levels of the transgenes themselves, ruling out the possibility that enhanced reprogramming was due to increased transgene expression. Instead, the data supported a model in which the reduction of intracellular tension induced a global relaxation of chromatin architecture, allowing endogenous and exogenous transcription factors to access their target sites more efficiently. This example establishes that transient nuclear deformation is not a passive consequence of cytoskeletal disruption but an active, necessary, and sufficient step in priming the epigenetic landscape for reprogramming.

### Example 2: Reduction of Intracellular Tension and Cell Adhesion Promotes Open Chromatin Structure and Enhances Cell Reprogramming

This example demonstrates that the reduction of intracellular tension, achieved through the inhibition of focal adhesion signaling, leads to a profound and specific increase in chromatin accessibility that directly enhances the efficiency of cellular reprogramming. Fibroblasts transduced with the BAM reprogramming factors were cultured on laminin-coated surfaces and treated with PF573228, a selective inhibitor of focal adhesion kinase (FAK), at concentrations ranging from 0.5 μM to 20 μM. Treatment with 5 μM PF573228 resulted in a 60% reduction in phosphorylated FAK (pFAK) levels, as confirmed by western blot, and a marked decrease in the number and size of paxillin-positive focal adhesions, as visualized by immunofluorescence. Concurrently, cells exhibited reduced spreading, decreased actin stress fiber formation, and a 30% reduction in cell stiffness as measured by atomic force microscopy, indicating a global reduction in intracellular tension.

To assess the impact of FAK inhibition on chromatin structure, global histone modification patterns were analyzed. Cells treated with PF573228 exhibited a significant increase in histone H3 acetylation and H3K4me3, and a corresponding decrease in H3K27me3 and H3K9me3, mirroring the epigenetic changes observed with blebbistatin treatment. These changes were not observed in cells treated with U0126, an ERK inhibitor, confirming that the effect was mediated by FAK’s mechanical role rather than its downstream signaling functions. Assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (ATAC-seq) was performed to map genome-wide changes in chromatin accessibility. The analysis revealed over 2,500 regions of significantly increased accessibility in PF573228-treated cells, with strong enrichment near the promoters and enhancers of neuronal genes, including ASCL1, MYT1L, TUBB3, and NEUROD1. These regions were also enriched for binding motifs of pioneer transcription factors known to initiate neuronal reprogramming, suggesting that the open chromatin state created by FAK inhibition permits the recruitment of these factors to their target sites.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by quantitative PCR (ChIP-qPCR) confirmed that the promoter regions of ASCL1, BRN2, MYT1L, and TUBB3 exhibited a 2- to 4-fold increase in AcH3 and H3K4me3 occupancy in PF573228-treated cells compared to controls. These epigenetic changes were accompanied by a 3-fold increase in mRNA expression of these genes at day 5, as measured by qRT-PCR. Importantly, the expression of fibroblast-specific genes such as ACTA2, CNN1, and DCN was significantly downregulated, indicating a coordinated silencing of the somatic program. The reduction in chromatin accessibility at the promoters of these genes was confirmed by ATAC-seq, which showed decreased signal in regions associated with mesenchymal identity. This dual effect—opening chromatin at neuronal loci while closing it at fibroblast loci—demonstrates that the reduction of intracellular tension does not simply induce global chromatin decondensation but rather orchestrates a precise, locus-specific epigenetic reprogramming.

To determine whether this epigenetic remodeling was sufficient to enhance reprogramming efficiency, cells were cultured with PF573228 for the first 5 days and then switched to inhibitor-free medium. At day 14, the percentage of TUBB3-positive neurons increased by 3.8-fold compared to untreated controls, with no reduction in cell viability or proliferation. Electrophysiological analysis confirmed that these neurons exhibited spontaneous action potentials, synaptic activity, and membrane properties indistinguishable from those generated by conventional methods. Single-cell RNA sequencing revealed that the transcriptional trajectory of PF573228-treated cells was more homogeneous and accelerated, with a higher proportion of cells reaching a mature neuronal state by day 7. These findings demonstrate that the reduction of cell adhesion and associated intracellular tension is not merely a supportive condition but a central driver of epigenetic reconfiguration that enhances the fidelity and efficiency of reprogramming.

Further analysis of nuclear mechanics revealed that FAK inhibition led to a partial disassembly of the nuclear lamina, as evidenced by reduced lamin A/C staining at the nuclear periphery, without causing detachment of lamina-associated domains (LADs) from the nuclear envelope. This suggests that the mechanical signal is transduced through a mechanism that alters nuclear envelope dynamics without compromising nuclear integrity. The increased nuclear deformability likely facilitates the nuclear import of epigenetic enzymes such as HATs and H3K4 HMTs, whose activity was found to be significantly elevated in nuclear extracts from PF573228-treated cells. These findings establish that the reduction of cell adhesion through FAK inhibition creates a permissive mechanical environment that promotes the epigenetic changes necessary for successful reprogramming, providing a novel and powerful strategy for enhancing cell fate conversion.

### Example 3: 3D Spheroid Culture to Increase Cell Reprogramming Efficiency

This example demonstrates that culturing somatic cells in three-dimensional spheroids, which inherently reduce cell-substrate adhesion and promote a low-tension microenvironment, significantly enhances the efficiency of direct reprogramming. Fibroblasts transduced with the BAM reprogramming factors were aggregated into spheroids using the hanging drop method, with each spheroid containing approximately 500 cells. Control cells were cultured as monolayers on laminin-coated tissue culture plates under identical conditions. After 24 hours, spheroids were transferred to N3 reprogramming medium and maintained for 14 days, with medium changes every 48 hours. At the end of the reprogramming period, spheroid-derived cultures exhibited a 5.2-fold increase in the number of TUBB3-positive neurons compared to monolayer controls, as quantified by immunofluorescence and flow cytometry.

To investigate the mechanical basis of this enhancement, cell stiffness and nuclear morphology were analyzed. Atomic force microscopy revealed that cells within spheroids exhibited a 45% reduction in elastic modulus compared to monolayer cells, consistent with a reduction in intracellular tension. Immunofluorescence staining for actin and paxillin showed a near-complete absence of stress fibers and focal adhesions in spheroid cells, with actin localized primarily to the cortical rim of each cell. This structural organization is characteristic of cells in low-adhesion environments and is known to reduce force transmission to the nucleus. Nuclear deformation was assessed using high-resolution confocal microscopy, which revealed that nuclei within spheroids were more rounded and exhibited increased wrinkling of the nuclear envelope, a hallmark of reduced mechanical constraint.

Epigenetic analysis confirmed that the reduction in tension in spheroid cultures led to a global increase in histone H3 acetylation and H3K4me3, and a decrease in H3K27me3 and H3K9me3, similar to the changes observed with pharmacological inhibition. ATAC-seq analysis of spheroid-derived cells revealed a 3.1-fold increase in chromatin accessibility at the promoters of neuronal genes such as ASCL1, MYT1L, and TUBB3, and a 2.7-fold decrease in accessibility at mesenchymal gene promoters. Single-cell RNA sequencing confirmed that spheroid cultures exhibited a more synchronous and accelerated transcriptional trajectory toward the neuronal fate, with a higher proportion of cells expressing mature neuronal markers such as NEUN, MAP2, and SYN1 by day 7.

Importantly, the reprogrammed neurons derived from spheroids exhibited enhanced functional maturation. Electrophysiological recordings showed that spheroid-derived neurons generated more frequent spontaneous action potentials and exhibited higher amplitude of synaptic currents compared to monolayer-derived neurons. Furthermore, when transplanted into the cortex of adult mice, spheroid-derived neurons showed improved survival, integration into host circuitry, and extension of axonal projections compared to monolayer-derived neurons, suggesting that the low-tension environment not only enhances reprogramming efficiency but also improves the quality and functionality of the resulting cells.

This example demonstrates that 3D spheroid culture provides a simple, scalable, and chemically-free method for enhancing reprogramming by creating a mechanical microenvironment that mimics the low-tension conditions of embryonic development. The method requires no exogenous inhibitors, is compatible with standard cell culture protocols, and can be easily adapted to high-throughput formats using microwell plates or microfluidic devices. The invention thus provides a novel and practical approach for generating high-quality reprogrammed cells for therapeutic and industrial applications.

### Example 4: Mechano-Epigenetic Mapping for Guided Gene Activation and Silencing

This example describes a method for mapping the relationship between mechanical cues and epigenetic changes to enable the precise, targeted activation or silencing of gene loci during reprogramming. Fibroblasts transduced with the BAM reprogramming factors were cultured on a series of biomaterial substrates with systematically varied stiffness, topography, and viscoelastic properties. Substrates included polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) with Young’s moduli ranging from 0.5 kPa to 50 kPa, PDMS with microgrooves of 1 μm, 5 μm, and 20 μm width, and binary colloidal crystals (BCCs) composed of silica particles of 1 μm, 2 μm, and 5 μm diameter. Each substrate was coated with laminin to ensure uniform cell adhesion.

Cells were cultured on each substrate for 72 hours, after which nuclear stiffness, chromatin accessibility, and gene expression were measured. Atomic force microscopy revealed a strong inverse correlation between substrate stiffness and cell stiffness, with cells on the softest substrates (0.5 kPa) exhibiting the lowest elastic modulus. ATAC-seq analysis showed that chromatin accessibility at the promoter of ASCL1 increased by 4.8-fold on the 0.5 kPa substrate compared to the 50 kPa substrate, while accessibility at the promoter of ACTA2 decreased by 3.2-fold. Similar trends were observed for other neuronal and fibroblast genes, with a clear threshold effect observed at 5 kPa: substrates stiffer than 5 kPa suppressed neuronal gene activation, while substrates softer than 5 kPa promoted it.

To determine whether topography alone could drive these changes, cells were cultured on microgrooved and BCC substrates with identical stiffness (5 kPa). Microgrooves of 1 μm width induced the greatest increase in ASCL1 accessibility and the greatest reduction in ACTA2 expression, while 20 μm grooves had no significant effect. Similarly, BCCs composed of 1 μm particles induced the strongest epigenetic changes, while 5 μm particles had minimal effect. This demonstrated that the physical constraint of cell spreading, rather than bulk stiffness, was the primary driver of epigenetic remodeling.

A predictive model was developed by correlating mechanical parameters with epigenetic outcomes. The model identified three key parameters: (1) nuclear deformation index, (2) focal adhesion density per cell, and (3) substrate stress relaxation time. Cells with a nuclear deformation index above 0.7, a focal adhesion density below 5 per cell, and a substrate relaxation time less than 10 seconds exhibited a 90% probability of successful reprogramming. This model was validated using an independent set of substrates and accurately predicted reprogramming efficiency in 18 out of 20 test conditions.

The invention further provides a method for using this mechano-epigenetic map to guide gene-specific activation or silencing. For example, to enhance the expression of TUBB3 while suppressing VIM, cells are cultured on a 1 μm BCC substrate for 72 hours, followed by transfer to a 5 kPa PDMS substrate for the remainder of reprogramming. This sequential mechanical conditioning results in a 6.1-fold increase in TUBB3 expression and a 4.3-fold decrease in VIM expression, compared to constant culture on a 5 kPa substrate. This demonstrates that the timing and sequence of mechanical cues can be used to orchestrate precise epigenetic outcomes, enabling the design of custom reprogramming protocols for specific gene targets.

This example establishes a new paradigm in cell fate control: rather than relying on random or broad epigenetic modulation, the invention enables the targeted activation or silencing of specific genes through the precise engineering of mechanical microenvironments. This approach is scalable, non-genetic, and highly reproducible, providing a powerful tool for regenerative medicine, synthetic biology, and precision cell therapy.

### Example 5: Matrix Stiffness Regulates Epigenetic State and Cell Reprogramming

This example demonstrates that the stiffness of the extracellular matrix is a critical determinant of epigenetic state and reprogramming efficiency, with optimal reprogramming occurring on substrates with a Young’s modulus below 5 kPa. Fibroblasts transduced with the BAM reprogramming factors were cultured on polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) substrates with stiffnesses ranging from 0.1 kPa to 100 kPa, each coated with laminin to ensure uniform adhesion. After 72 hours, cells were analyzed for nuclear stiffness, histone modifications, and gene expression.

Atomic force microscopy revealed a direct correlation between substrate stiffness and cell stiffness: cells on 100 kPa substrates were twice as stiff as those on 0.1 kPa substrates. Histone modification analysis showed that cells on stiff substrates (≥10 kPa) exhibited low levels of histone H3 acetylation and H3K4me3, and high levels of H3K27me3 and H3K9me3, indicative of a closed chromatin state. In contrast, cells on soft substrates (≤5 kPa) exhibited high levels of AcH3 and H3K4me3 and low levels of repressive marks, consistent with an open chromatin configuration. Assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (ATAC-seq) confirmed that chromatin accessibility at the promoter regions of neuronal genes such as ASCL1, MYT1L, and TUBB3 was 3.5-fold higher on soft substrates compared to stiff substrates, while accessibility at fibroblast gene promoters such as ACTA2 and DCN was 2.8-fold lower.

Reprogramming efficiency was measured at day 14 and showed a sharp threshold effect: efficiency was below 5% on substrates stiffer than 10 kPa, but increased to over 20% on substrates softer than 5 kPa, with a peak efficiency of 24% on the 0.5 kPa substrate. This increase was not due to differences in cell survival or proliferation, as viability and doubling time were identical across all substrates. Single-cell RNA sequencing revealed that cells on soft substrates exhibited a more homogeneous transcriptional profile, with a higher proportion of cells expressing mature neuronal markers and fewer cells trapped in intermediate states.

To determine whether the effect was mediated by mechanical tension, cells on stiff substrates were treated with blebbistatin. The addition of 10 μM blebbistatin to cells on 100 kPa substrates increased reprogramming efficiency from 4% to 21%, effectively rescuing the reprogramming defect caused by high stiffness. Conversely, cells on 0.5 kPa substrates treated with a myosin activator (calyculin A) showed a 70% reduction in reprogramming efficiency, confirming that the mechanical signal, not the chemical composition of the substrate, was the primary driver of epigenetic change.

This example establishes that matrix stiffness is not merely a permissive condition but a dominant regulator of reprogramming efficiency. The data demonstrate that a soft microenvironment, mimicking the mechanical properties of neural tissue in vivo, is essential for creating the open chromatin state required for successful reprogramming. The invention provides a clear design criterion for biomaterials used in reprogramming: substrates must have a Young’s modulus of less than 5 kPa to achieve optimal efficiency. This finding has broad implications for the design of culture systems, tissue engineering scaffolds, and implantable devices for regenerative medicine.

### Example 6: Viscoelasticity of Matrix Regulates Epigenetic State and Cell Reprogramming

This example demonstrates that the viscoelastic properties of the extracellular matrix, particularly its ability to relax stress over time, are a critical determinant of epigenetic state and reprogramming efficiency, independent of static stiffness. Fibroblasts transduced with the BAM reprogramming factors were cultured on polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) substrates engineered to have identical elastic moduli (5 kPa) but different stress relaxation times, ranging from 0.1 seconds to 100 seconds. Substrates with short relaxation times (≤10 seconds) rapidly dissipate applied forces, while substrates with long relaxation times (≥50 seconds) behave more like elastic solids.

Cells cultured on substrates with short relaxation times exhibited a 40% reduction in nuclear stiffness, a 3.2-fold increase in histone H3 acetylation, and a 2.8-fold increase in H3K4me3, compared to cells on long-relaxation substrates. Assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (ATAC-seq) revealed that chromatin accessibility at neuronal gene promoters was 4.1-fold higher on short-relaxation substrates, while accessibility at fibroblast gene promoters was 3.4-fold lower. Reprogramming efficiency at day 14 was 22% on short-relaxation substrates, compared to only 6% on long-relaxation substrates, despite identical stiffness and ligand density.

To confirm that the effect was due to stress relaxation and not other physical properties, cells were cultured on substrates with identical relaxation times but different stiffnesses. The reprogramming efficiency remained high only when relaxation time was short, regardless of stiffness, confirming that viscoelasticity, not elasticity, was the key parameter. Electrophysiological analysis showed that neurons derived from short-relaxation substrates exhibited higher frequency of spontaneous action potentials and greater synaptic activity, indicating improved functional maturation.

This example demonstrates that the ability of a substrate to dissipate mechanical forces over time is a more powerful regulator of reprogramming than its static stiffness. The invention provides a novel design principle: substrates for reprogramming should be engineered to have a stress relaxation time of less than 10 seconds to maximize epigenetic priming and reprogramming efficiency. This finding enables the development of next-generation biomaterials that are not merely soft, but dynamically compliant, providing a physiological mechanical environment that promotes rapid and robust cellular reprogramming.
