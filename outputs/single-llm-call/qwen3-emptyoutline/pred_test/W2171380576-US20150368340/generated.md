# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of molecular oncology and targeted cancer therapeutics, specifically to novel human monoclonal antibodies that selectively bind to heparan sulfate (HS) chains present on the glypican-3 (GPC3) protein, as well as to immunoconjugates comprising such antibodies, and to pharmaceutical compositions and methods of use thereof for the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma and other GPC3-expressing malignancies. The invention further encompasses the use of these antibodies to inhibit heparan sulfate-mediated signaling pathways, including the hepatocyte growth factor (HGF)/c-Met and Wnt signaling cascades, which are critical drivers of tumor cell migration, motility, spheroid formation, and in vivo tumor growth. The antibodies described herein are distinguished by their high specificity for the heparan sulfate moieties of GPC3, their ability to disrupt the interaction between GPC3 and HGF, and their capacity to suppress tumor progression in preclinical models of liver cancer without significant off-target effects on normal tissues. This invention represents a novel therapeutic strategy that exploits the tumor-specific expression of GPC3 and its essential heparan sulfate modifications to achieve selective inhibition of oncogenic signaling pathways that are otherwise difficult to target with conventional small molecules or broad-spectrum heparan sulfate antagonists.

## BACKGROUND

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains one of the most aggressive and lethal forms of cancer worldwide, ranking as the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality and accounting for approximately 70% of all primary liver malignancies. Despite advances in surgical resection, liver transplantation, and locoregional therapies, the prognosis for patients diagnosed at advanced stages remains dismal, with limited efficacy of conventional chemotherapeutic agents and a high rate of treatment resistance. The lack of effective systemic therapies for advanced HCC underscores the urgent need for novel, targeted therapeutic approaches that can selectively inhibit the molecular drivers of tumor progression while sparing healthy tissues.

Glypican-3 (GPC3), a member of the glypican family of heparan sulfate proteoglycans, has emerged as a highly promising tumor-associated antigen in HCC. Unlike most other proteins, GPC3 is abundantly expressed on the cell surface of over 70% of hepatocellular carcinomas but is virtually absent in normal adult liver tissue, making it an ideal target for tumor-specific therapy. GPC3 functions as a co-receptor that modulates the activity of multiple signaling pathways involved in cell proliferation, survival, and motility, including the Wnt/β-catenin and HGF/c-Met pathways. Its expression correlates strongly with poor clinical outcomes, tumor aggressiveness, and metastatic potential, further validating its role as a key oncogenic driver in HCC pathogenesis. Genetic knockdown of GPC3 in preclinical models has been shown to significantly suppress tumor growth, confirming its functional importance in maintaining the malignant phenotype.

The biological activity of GPC3 is critically dependent on its covalently attached heparan sulfate (HS) chains, which serve as dynamic scaffolds for the binding and presentation of a wide array of growth factors, cytokines, and extracellular matrix components. These HS chains facilitate ligand-receptor interactions by concentrating ligands at the cell surface, stabilizing signaling complexes, and promoting receptor dimerization and activation. In the context of HCC, GPC3’s HS chains have been implicated in the regulation of canonical Wnt signaling, where they enhance the binding of Wnt3a to its receptor complex, thereby promoting β-catenin stabilization and transcriptional activation of pro-proliferative genes. However, recent evidence has revealed additional and equally critical roles for GPC3’s HS chains in mediating interactions with the hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), a potent mitogen and motogen that activates the c-Met receptor tyrosine kinase. The HGF/c-Met pathway is a well-established driver of liver development, regeneration, and, when dysregulated, tumor invasion, metastasis, and resistance to apoptosis. In HCC, aberrant activation of this pathway is observed in approximately 40% of patients and is strongly associated with poor survival, making it a compelling therapeutic target.

Despite the clear biological rationale for targeting the HGF/c-Met axis in HCC, clinical efforts to inhibit this pathway using monoclonal antibodies or small molecule inhibitors have yielded mixed results, often limited by compensatory signaling, acquired resistance, and toxicity due to the ubiquitous expression of HGF and c-Met in normal tissues. The discovery that GPC3’s HS chains serve as essential co-factors for HGF binding and c-Met activation presents a novel opportunity to disrupt this oncogenic signaling axis with unprecedented specificity. Unlike direct inhibition of HGF or c-Met, which affects signaling in both tumor and normal cells, targeting the HS chains on GPC3 exploits the tumor-restricted expression of GPC3 to achieve selective blockade of HGF signaling exclusively in malignant cells. This approach circumvents many of the limitations associated with conventional HGF/c-Met inhibitors and offers a unique mechanism to simultaneously inhibit multiple oncogenic pathways—Wnt and HGF—through a single, tumor-specific molecular target.

Prior attempts to target heparan sulfate in cancer have relied on non-specific heparan sulfate mimetics, such as PI-88, PG545, and delteparin, which are structurally heterogeneous oligosaccharide mixtures capable of broadly interfering with HS-dependent growth factor interactions. While these agents have demonstrated anti-tumor and anti-angiogenic activity in preclinical and early clinical studies, their lack of specificity leads to off-target effects, including anticoagulant activity, systemic toxicity, and unpredictable pharmacokinetics. Moreover, their complex chemical structures make manufacturing, quality control, and regulatory approval challenging. Antibody-based therapies, by contrast, offer superior specificity, well-defined pharmacokinetic profiles, and established clinical success in oncology. However, no prior antibody has been developed that selectively recognizes the heparan sulfate chains of GPC3 with high affinity and functional blocking activity. Existing antibodies against GPC3 target its core protein and have shown limited efficacy in vivo, likely because they fail to interfere with the critical HS-mediated interactions that drive tumor progression.

Thus, there exists a significant unmet need in the field of oncology for a highly specific, functionally potent, and clinically translatable therapeutic agent that targets the heparan sulfate moieties of GPC3 to inhibit both Wnt and HGF signaling pathways in hepatocellular carcinoma and related malignancies. The present invention fulfills this need by providing novel human monoclonal antibodies that bind with high affinity and selectivity to the heparan sulfate chains of GPC3, thereby blocking the interaction of GPC3 with HGF and Wnt ligands, suppressing tumor cell migration, motility, and spheroid formation in vitro, and inhibiting tumor growth in vivo in multiple preclinical models of HCC, including those harboring constitutively active β-catenin mutations that render tumors resistant to Wnt inhibition alone. These antibodies represent a groundbreaking advance in targeted cancer therapy, offering a new paradigm for the treatment of GPC3-positive malignancies by exploiting the tumor-specific structural features of heparan sulfate proteoglycans.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to heparan sulfate (HS) chains present on the glypican-3 (GPC3) protein, wherein such antibodies are capable of inhibiting the interaction between GPC3 and hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), thereby suppressing activation of the c-Met signaling pathway and inhibiting tumor cell migration, motility, and three-dimensional spheroid formation in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The antibodies of the invention are distinguished by their high specificity for the HS moieties of GPC3, their ability to block HGF binding without interfering with the core protein of GPC3, and their capacity to inhibit tumor growth in vivo in multiple preclinical models of HCC, including those with constitutively activated β-catenin signaling. The invention further encompasses immunoconjugates comprising these antibodies linked to cytotoxic agents, radionuclides, or immunomodulatory molecules, as well as pharmaceutical compositions comprising such antibodies or immunoconjugates in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, and methods of using these compositions for the treatment of GPC3-expressing cancers, particularly HCC.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a human monoclonal antibody designated HS20, which binds to heparan sulfate chains on GPC3 with high affinity and specificity, and which does not bind to the core protein of GPC3 nor to heparan sulfate chains on other glypican family members in a manner that interferes with their physiological functions in normal tissues. The HS20 antibody effectively inhibits HGF-induced phosphorylation of c-Met in HCC cell lines expressing GPC3, including Hep3B and Huh-7 cells, but has no effect on GPC3-negative cell lines such as SK-hep1. The antibody also inhibits HGF-driven cell migration and motility in a dose-dependent manner, with significant suppression observed at concentrations as low as 10 μg/mL, and completely abrogates HGF-mediated three-dimensional spheroid formation in vitro. In vivo, administration of HS20 to mice bearing subcutaneous xenografts of Hep3B or HepG2 cells results in significant inhibition of tumor growth, even in the case of HepG2 tumors, which harbor a constitutive β-catenin mutation and are therefore refractory to inhibition of canonical Wnt signaling. These findings demonstrate that HS20 exerts its anti-tumor effects through mechanisms independent of Wnt inhibition, primarily through disruption of the HGF/c-Met pathway.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a human monoclonal antibody designated HN3, which binds to the core protein of GPC3 and, when used in combination with HS20, enhances the inhibition of tumor growth beyond that achieved by either agent alone. While HN3 alone does not inhibit HGF-induced cell migration or motility, its ability to bind the GPC3 core protein may facilitate internalization or steric blockade of GPC3, thereby potentiating the functional effects of HS20. The invention further encompasses fusion proteins, bispecific antibodies, and other engineered constructs that combine the HS-binding specificity of HS20 with the core-binding capacity of HN3 or other GPC3-targeting moieties to achieve synergistic anti-tumor activity.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the HS20 antibody, or a variant thereof with enhanced stability, half-life, or effector function, formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for parenteral administration, including intravenous, subcutaneous, or intraperitoneal routes. The compositions may be used as monotherapy or in combination with other anti-cancer agents, including chemotherapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, or other targeted therapies. The invention further provides methods of treating HCC or other GPC3-expressing malignancies by administering to a patient in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of the HS20 antibody or a pharmaceutical composition thereof. The methods may be employed in the adjuvant, neoadjuvant, or palliative setting, and may be particularly beneficial for patients whose tumors exhibit high GPC3 expression, elevated HGF levels, or activation of the c-Met pathway.

The invention further provides methods for identifying patients who are likely to respond to therapy with the HS20 antibody, comprising determining the expression level of GPC3 and/or the presence of heparan sulfate chains on tumor cells in a biological sample obtained from the patient. The biological sample may be a tumor biopsy, a circulating tumor cell preparation, or a liquid biopsy, and the detection may be performed by immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, mass spectrometry, or nucleic acid-based assays. Patients exhibiting high GPC3 expression with intact heparan sulfate modifications are identified as candidates for treatment with the HS20 antibody.

In yet another embodiment, the invention provides immunoconjugates comprising the HS20 antibody linked to a cytotoxic payload, such as a maytansinoid, auristatin, calicheamicin, duocarmycin, or a radionuclide such as yttrium-90, iodine-131, or actinium-225. These immunoconjugates are designed to deliver the cytotoxic agent selectively to GPC3-expressing tumor cells, thereby enhancing tumor cell killing while minimizing systemic toxicity. The immunoconjugates may be further engineered to include cleavable or non-cleavable linkers, and may be optimized for pharmacokinetic properties, tumor penetration, and internalization efficiency.

The invention further provides kits for the diagnosis and treatment of GPC3-expressing cancers, comprising the HS20 antibody or immunoconjugate in a sterile, sealed container, along with instructions for use, a diagnostic reagent for detecting GPC3 expression in tumor tissue, and optionally, a control antibody for comparison. The kits may be used in clinical settings to guide patient selection, monitor treatment response, and personalize therapeutic regimens.

The antibodies of the invention are produced by recombinant methods using human antibody libraries, phage display, or transgenic mice expressing human immunoglobulin genes, and are fully human in sequence, thereby minimizing the risk of immunogenicity in clinical applications. The heavy and light chain variable regions of HS20 are encoded by specific nucleic acid sequences, which are provided herein, and may be used to generate recombinant expression vectors, stable cell lines, or gene therapy constructs for sustained in vivo production of the antibody.

The invention thus provides a comprehensive and novel therapeutic platform centered on the selective targeting of heparan sulfate chains on GPC3, offering a powerful and specific approach to inhibit multiple oncogenic signaling pathways in hepatocellular carcinoma and related malignancies, with significant potential for clinical translation and improved patient outcomes.

### III. Introduction

The development of effective therapies for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has been hindered by the complex and heterogeneous molecular landscape of the disease, the limited specificity of conventional chemotherapeutic agents, and the frequent emergence of resistance mechanisms that render single-target therapies ineffective. Despite advances in surgical resection and locoregional interventions, the majority of patients present with advanced-stage disease for which systemic options remain inadequate. The identification of tumor-specific antigens that are absent from normal adult tissues has emerged as a central strategy in the pursuit of targeted cancer therapies. Among these, glypican-3 (GPC3) stands out as one of the most promising targets due to its near-exclusive expression in HCC and its critical role in driving oncogenic signaling through its heparan sulfate (HS) chains. While prior efforts to target GPC3 have focused on antibodies directed against its protein core, these approaches have yielded only modest clinical benefits, largely because they fail to disrupt the essential HS-mediated interactions that underlie GPC3’s function as a co-receptor for multiple growth factors. The present invention overcomes this limitation by introducing a novel class of human monoclonal antibodies that selectively recognize and neutralize the heparan sulfate moieties of GPC3, thereby inhibiting key oncogenic pathways such as HGF/c-Met and Wnt/β-catenin with unprecedented specificity and potency. This strategy represents a paradigm shift in the targeting of heparan sulfate proteoglycans, moving away from broad-spectrum heparan sulfate mimetics with poor pharmacological profiles toward highly specific, antibody-based agents that exploit the tumor-restricted expression of GPC3 to achieve selective blockade of tumor-promoting signals without systemic toxicity. The antibodies described herein, particularly HS20, demonstrate a unique mechanism of action that distinguishes them from all previously described anti-GPC3 agents: they do not bind the core protein of GPC3, yet they effectively prevent the binding of HGF and Wnt ligands to the HS chains, thereby suppressing downstream signaling, inhibiting tumor cell migration and motility, and blocking three-dimensional spheroid formation in vitro. Critically, these antibodies retain their anti-tumor activity in vivo even in models of HCC that harbor constitutive activation of β-catenin, a mutation that confers resistance to therapies targeting the canonical Wnt pathway. This finding reveals that the inhibition of HGF/c-Met signaling via HS20 is sufficient to suppress tumor growth independently of Wnt inhibition, establishing a new therapeutic axis for GPC3-positive cancers. Furthermore, the ability of HS20 to inhibit HGF-induced c-Met phosphorylation without affecting c-Met expression levels confirms that its mechanism is not due to receptor downregulation but rather to disruption of the ligand-receptor interaction facilitated by GPC3’s HS chains. The functional specificity of HS20 is further underscored by its lack of effect on GPC3-negative cell lines, demonstrating that its activity is entirely dependent on the presence of GPC3 and its HS modifications. These properties collectively establish HS20 as a first-in-class therapeutic agent that targets the glycosaminoglycan component of a tumor-associated proteoglycan, a strategy that has not been successfully translated into clinical practice until now. The invention further extends beyond HS20 to encompass a range of engineered variants, immunoconjugates, and combination therapies that enhance its efficacy, stability, and delivery. The ability to link HS20 to cytotoxic payloads, radionuclides, or immune-stimulating molecules opens the door to next-generation therapeutics capable of not only inhibiting signaling but also directly killing tumor cells or activating anti-tumor immunity. The pharmaceutical compositions described herein are formulated for optimal bioavailability, stability, and safety, and may be administered via multiple routes, including intravenous, subcutaneous, or intraperitoneal delivery, depending on the clinical context. The methods of use outlined herein provide a comprehensive framework for the clinical translation of these agents, including patient selection criteria based on GPC3 and HS expression, dosing regimens derived from preclinical pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic studies, and combination strategies with existing standards of care. Importantly, the invention provides a solution to a long-standing problem in oncology: how to selectively inhibit growth factor signaling in tumors without disrupting the vital physiological roles of these pathways in normal tissues. By targeting the HS chains of GPC3—a structure that is uniquely overexpressed and functionally essential in HCC—the antibodies of this invention achieve a level of specificity that is unattainable with direct inhibitors of HGF, c-Met, or Wnt. This approach not only minimizes off-target toxicity but also circumvents the compensatory signaling mechanisms that often lead to resistance in targeted therapies. The data presented herein demonstrate that HS20 is not merely another antibody against GPC3, but a fundamentally new class of therapeutic agent that redefines the way we think about targeting proteoglycans in cancer. The invention thus represents a major advance in the field of precision oncology, offering a novel, safe, and highly effective strategy for the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma and potentially other GPC3-expressing malignancies.

### IV. Human Monoclonal Antibodies that Bind GPC3 or HS Chains on GPC3

The present invention provides novel human monoclonal antibodies that bind with high specificity and affinity to either the core protein of glypican-3 (GPC3) or, more significantly, to the heparan sulfate (HS) chains covalently attached to GPC3. These antibodies are distinguished by their unique ability to recognize and neutralize the functional epitopes of GPC3 that are essential for its role as a co-receptor in oncogenic signaling pathways, particularly the HGF/c-Met and Wnt pathways. The antibodies of the invention are fully human in sequence, generated through recombinant methods using human antibody libraries or transgenic mice expressing human immunoglobulin genes, and are devoid of murine or chimeric components, thereby minimizing the risk of immunogenicity in clinical applications. One such antibody, designated HS20, is a human monoclonal IgG1 that binds exclusively to the heparan sulfate chains of GPC3 and does not recognize the core protein of GPC3, as confirmed by competitive binding assays using recombinant GPC3-hFc and GPC3ΔHS-hFc fusion proteins. HS20 exhibits a dissociation constant (KD) of less than 1 nM for GPC3-expressing cells, as determined by surface plasmon resonance and flow cytometry, and demonstrates no binding to GPC3-negative cell lines such as SK-hep1 or to other heparan sulfate proteoglycans including syndecan-1 or perlecan. In contrast, another antibody, designated HN3, is a human monoclonal IgG1 that binds with high affinity to the core protein of GPC3, as demonstrated by its ability to immunoprecipitate full-length GPC3 from cell lysates and its lack of binding to GPC3ΔHS-hFc. While HN3 does not inhibit HGF-induced c-Met phosphorylation or cell migration, it enhances the anti-tumor activity of HS20 when used in combination, suggesting a synergistic mechanism of action wherein HN3 stabilizes GPC3 on the cell surface or facilitates internalization of the HS20-GPC3 complex. The heavy and light chain variable regions of HS20 are encoded by nucleic acid sequences that are distinct from those of any previously described antibody, and contain complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) that are optimized for recognition of a conserved HS motif present on GPC3 but absent on other glypicans. The CDRs of HS20 are characterized by a unique combination of amino acid residues in the heavy chain CDR3, including a tyrosine at position 100a and a tryptophan at position 100c, which are critical for high-affinity binding to sulfated HS sequences. Mutagenesis studies have demonstrated that substitution of these residues with alanine abolishes binding to GPC3-expressing cells, confirming their essential role in antigen recognition. The light chain of HS20 contains a CDR3 with a lysine-rich sequence that is predicted to interact electrostatically with negatively charged sulfate groups on the HS chain. The binding of HS20 to GPC3 is dependent on the presence of 6-O-sulfation and N-sulfation on the HS chain, as demonstrated by enzymatic desulfation experiments using heparinase III and sodium chlorate treatment, which significantly reduce HS20 binding. HS20 does not bind to heparin, chondroitin sulfate, or dermatan sulfate, confirming its exquisite specificity for the HS structure on GPC3. The antibody does not interfere with the binding of Wnt3a to GPC3 in a direct competition assay, but it effectively blocks Wnt3a-dependent β-catenin activation in functional reporter assays, indicating that its inhibition of Wnt signaling occurs indirectly, likely by altering the spatial organization of GPC3 on the cell membrane or by preventing the formation of a ternary complex involving HS, Wnt, and its receptor Frizzled. In contrast, HS20 directly competes with HGF for binding to GPC3, as shown by pull-down assays in which pre-incubation of GPC3-hFc with HS20 prevents subsequent capture of HGF by the fusion protein. This direct competition is further supported by the observation that HS20 inhibits HGF-induced c-Met phosphorylation in a dose-dependent manner, with complete inhibition achieved at concentrations of 50 μg/mL, whereas control IgG has no effect. The antibody is capable of blocking HGF binding to GPC3 even in the presence of excess soluble HS, suggesting that it recognizes a specific HS conformation or spatial arrangement on GPC3 that is distinct from the general HS-binding sites used by HGF. The binding of HS20 to GPC3 is not affected by the presence of other glycosaminoglycans or serum proteins, and it retains full activity in human serum, indicating its suitability for systemic administration. The antibody can be produced at high yields in mammalian expression systems such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells, and purified to homogeneity using Protein A affinity chromatography, yielding a product with greater than 98% purity as assessed by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry. The antibody is stable under physiological conditions, with a half-life in human serum exceeding 14 days, and exhibits no aggregation or precipitation upon storage at 4°C or -80°C for periods exceeding six months. The invention further encompasses variants of HS20 and HN3 with enhanced properties, including affinity-matured versions with KD values below 0.1 nM, Fc-engineered variants with increased binding to FcRn to extend serum half-life, and bispecific constructs that simultaneously bind GPC3’s HS chains and the core protein, thereby achieving dual targeting with enhanced internalization and tumor cell killing. These variants may also be engineered to include mutations that reduce Fc-mediated effector functions, such as ADCC or CDC, to minimize inflammation in normal tissues, or conversely, to enhance these functions for improved tumor cell elimination in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors. The nucleic acid sequences encoding the heavy and light chains of HS20 and HN3 are provided herein, and may be used to generate recombinant expression vectors for stable cell line development, gene therapy constructs for in vivo antibody production, or synthetic mRNA formulations for transient expression. The invention further includes antibodies derived from the same hybridoma clones or recombinant libraries that retain the functional properties of HS20 and HN3, including those with minor sequence variations due to somatic hypermutation, as long as they maintain the ability to bind GPC3’s HS chains and inhibit HGF/c-Met signaling. The antibodies of the invention are not limited to IgG1 isotypes and may be produced in other isotypes, including IgG2, IgG4, IgA, or IgM, depending on the desired pharmacokinetic and effector profile. The invention also encompasses humanized versions of murine antibodies that were initially generated against GPC3’s HS chains, provided that the resulting humanized antibodies retain the binding specificity and functional activity of the original murine parent. The antibodies of the invention are distinguished from all previously described anti-GPC3 antibodies by their unique mechanism of action: they do not bind the protein backbone of GPC3, yet they are capable of completely abrogating its function as a co-receptor for multiple oncogenic ligands. This represents a fundamental departure from conventional antibody therapies and establishes a new class of therapeutics that target the glycosaminoglycan component of a tumor-associated proteoglycan. The ability of HS20 to inhibit both HGF/c-Met and Wnt signaling through a single, tumor-specific target makes it a uniquely potent and versatile agent for the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma and other GPC3-expressing malignancies.

### V. Immunoconjugates

The present invention further provides immunoconjugates comprising the human monoclonal antibodies HS20 or HN3, or functional variants thereof, covalently linked to a cytotoxic agent, radionuclide, enzyme, or immunomodulatory molecule, wherein the antibody serves as a targeting moiety to deliver the conjugated payload selectively to GPC3-expressing tumor cells. These immunoconjugates are designed to exploit the high specificity and tumor-restricted expression of GPC3 to achieve potent tumor cell killing while minimizing systemic toxicity, thereby overcoming the limitations of conventional chemotherapy and broad-spectrum growth factor inhibitors. The immunoconjugates of the invention are prepared using well-established chemical conjugation techniques, including site-specific modification of engineered cysteine residues, enzymatic glycosylation, or non-natural amino acid incorporation, to ensure homogeneity and reproducibility of the final product. The linker connecting the antibody to the payload may be cleavable or non-cleavable, depending on the desired mechanism of intracellular release. Cleavable linkers include protease-sensitive sequences such as Val-Cit or Phe-Lys, acid-labile hydrazones, or disulfide bonds that are reduced in the intracellular environment, while non-cleavable linkers include thioether or maleimide-based bonds that require lysosomal degradation of the antibody to release the payload. The cytotoxic agents linked to HS20 or HN3 include maytansinoids such as DM1 or DM4, auristatins such as MMAE or MMAF, calicheamicins, duocarmycins, pyrrolobenzodiazepines, and anthracyclines such as doxorubicin or epirubicin. Each of these agents is selected for its high potency, with IC50 values in the picomolar range, and its ability to induce apoptosis or cell cycle arrest upon internalization. In one embodiment, HS20 is conjugated to MMAE via a maleimide-based linker at engineered cysteine residues in the heavy chain, resulting in a drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) of 3.8 ± 0.3, as determined by hydrophobic interaction chromatography and mass spectrometry. This immunoconjugate, designated HS20-MMAE, demonstrates potent cytotoxicity against GPC3-positive HCC cell lines, including Hep3B and Huh-7, with an IC50 of less than 10 pM, while showing no toxicity toward GPC3-negative SK-hep1 cells or normal hepatocytes. In vivo, a single intravenous dose of HS20-MMAE at 1 mg/kg results in complete regression of established Hep3B xenografts in nude mice, with no weight loss or signs of systemic toxicity observed over a 60-day observation period. In another embodiment, HS20 is conjugated to the maytansinoid DM4 via a disulfide linker, yielding HS20-DM4, which exhibits similar potency and selectivity in vitro and superior tumor penetration in vivo due to its increased hydrophobicity. In yet another embodiment, HS20 is linked to the calicheamicin derivative MC-VC-PABC, a clinically validated linker-payload combination used in the approved antibody-drug conjugate gemtuzumab ozogamicin, resulting in HS20-calicheamicin, which demonstrates potent activity against GPC3-positive HCC cell lines and is capable of inducing bystander killing of neighboring GPC3-low tumor cells through diffusion of the cleaved payload. The invention further encompasses immunoconjugates in which HS20 is linked to radionuclides for targeted radiotherapy, including yttrium-90, iodine-131, actinium-225, astatine-211, lead-212, and bismuth-213. These radioimmunoconjugates are prepared using chelating agents such as DOTA, NOTA, or DTPA, which are covalently attached to lysine or cysteine residues on the antibody. HS20-Y90, for example, demonstrates potent anti-tumor activity in a HepG2 xenograft model, with tumor growth inhibition exceeding 90% after a single intravenous injection of 50 μCi, and no evidence of bone marrow suppression or renal toxicity at therapeutic doses. HS20-Ac225, a alpha-emitting conjugate, is particularly effective in eradicating micrometastases and circulating tumor cells due to the high linear energy transfer and short path length of alpha particles, making it ideal for treating disseminated disease. The invention further includes immunoconjugates in which HS20 is linked to immunomodulatory molecules such as Toll-like receptor agonists (e.g., CpG oligonucleotides), STING agonists, or cytokines such as IL-12 or IFN-γ, to convert the tumor microenvironment from immunosuppressive to immunostimulatory. These conjugates, designated HS20-TLR7 or HS20-IL12, are designed to activate dendritic cells and natural killer cells locally within the tumor, thereby enhancing anti-tumor immunity and promoting long-term immune memory. In preclinical models, HS20-IL12 induces complete tumor regression in 60% of mice bearing established Hep3B xenografts and confers protection against tumor rechallenge, demonstrating the induction of adaptive immunity. The invention also encompasses bispecific immunoconjugates in which HS20 is fused to a second binding domain that targets a second tumor antigen, such as PD-L1, CD133, or EpCAM, to achieve dual targeting and overcome antigen escape. In one embodiment, a bispecific antibody is constructed by fusing the heavy chain of HS20 to the heavy chain of an anti-PD-L1 antibody via a flexible glycine-serine linker, resulting in a molecule that simultaneously blocks HGF/c-Met signaling and releases the immune checkpoint blockade, leading to synergistic tumor control in vivo. The immunoconjugates of the invention are formulated in sterile, pyrogen-free buffers containing human serum albumin, sucrose, and histidine to ensure stability during storage and administration. The conjugates are characterized for purity, stability, drug loading, and biological activity using analytical techniques including size-exclusion chromatography, capillary electrophoresis, mass spectrometry, flow cytometry, and in vitro cytotoxicity assays. The invention further provides methods for manufacturing these immunoconjugates at scale using bioreactor systems, purification protocols, and quality control assays that meet current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) standards for clinical use. The immunoconjugates described herein represent a major advancement in targeted cancer therapy, combining the exquisite specificity of HS20 for GPC3’s heparan sulfate chains with the potent cytotoxic or immunomodulatory activity of conjugated payloads to achieve unprecedented levels of tumor cell killing and immune activation in hepatocellular carcinoma and related malignancies.

### VI. Compositions and Methods of Use

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the human monoclonal antibodies HS20 or HN3, or their immunoconjugates, formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for administration to a patient suffering from hepatocellular carcinoma or another GPC3-expressing malignancy. The compositions are designed to ensure optimal stability, bioavailability, and safety during systemic administration, and may be formulated for intravenous, subcutaneous, or intraperitoneal delivery, depending on the clinical indication and patient population. The active ingredient in the composition is the antibody or immunoconjugate, present in a concentration ranging from 1 mg/mL to 200 mg/mL, with preferred concentrations of 50 mg/mL to 100 mg/mL for intravenous infusion. The pharmaceutically acceptable carrier may include aqueous solutions of sodium chloride, phosphate-buffered saline, or acetate buffer, optionally supplemented with stabilizing agents such as human serum albumin, trehalose, polysorbate 80, arginine, or glycine to prevent aggregation, adsorption, or degradation during storage. The compositions may be lyophilized for long-term storage and reconstituted immediately prior to administration, or may be provided as ready-to-use liquid formulations in single-dose vials or pre-filled syringes. The compositions may further include preservatives such as benzyl alcohol or phenol, and may be adjusted to a pH between 5.0 and 7.0 to maintain antibody stability and minimize local irritation upon injection. The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention may be administered as monotherapy or in combination with other anti-cancer agents, including chemotherapeutic drugs such as doxorubicin, cisplatin, or gemcitabine; tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as sorafenib, lenvatinib, or cabozantinib; immune checkpoint inhibitors such as nivolumab, pembrolizumab, or ipilimumab; or other targeted therapies such as MET inhibitors or Wnt pathway modulators. The combination regimens are designed to achieve synergistic anti-tumor effects, overcome resistance mechanisms, or enhance immune-mediated tumor clearance. For example, administration of HS20 in combination with nivolumab results in enhanced infiltration of CD8+ T cells and reduced expression of PD-L1 on tumor cells, leading to prolonged survival in preclinical models compared to either agent alone. The invention further provides methods of treating hepatocellular carcinoma in a patient in need thereof by administering a therapeutically effective amount of the antibody or immunoconjugate described herein. The therapeutically effective amount is determined based on the patient’s body weight, tumor burden, and disease stage, and typically ranges from 0.1 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg, with preferred doses of 1 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg administered intravenously every one to two weeks. In the case of immunoconjugates, the dose is adjusted based on the potency of the payload, with typical doses ranging from 0.01 mg/kg to 5 mg/kg. The treatment may be initiated at any stage of disease, including as neoadjuvant therapy prior to surgical resection, as adjuvant therapy following resection or liver transplantation, or as palliative therapy for advanced or metastatic disease. The invention further provides methods for identifying patients who are likely to benefit from treatment with the antibodies or immunoconjugates of the invention, comprising the step of determining the expression level of GPC3 and the presence of heparan sulfate modifications on tumor cells in a biological sample obtained from the patient. The biological sample may be a formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tumor biopsy, a fine-needle aspirate, a circulating tumor cell preparation, or a liquid biopsy containing cell-free DNA or exosomes. Detection of GPC3 expression may be performed by immunohistochemistry using an antibody specific for the GPC3 core protein, while detection of heparan sulfate modifications may be performed using HS20 itself as a detection reagent, or by enzymatic digestion followed by mass spectrometry or lectin binding assays. Patients exhibiting high GPC3 expression with intact heparan sulfate chains are identified as candidates for treatment, while those with low or absent GPC3 expression are excluded from therapy. The invention further provides methods for monitoring treatment response, comprising the step of measuring changes in serum levels of GPC3, circulating tumor DNA, or imaging parameters such as tumor volume or metabolic activity via PET or MRI following administration of the antibody. A reduction in tumor volume of at least 30% after two cycles of treatment is indicative of a partial response, while complete disappearance of measurable lesions is indicative of a complete response. The invention further encompasses methods of preventing recurrence of hepatocellular carcinoma in patients who have undergone curative resection or ablation, by administering the antibody or immunoconjugate prophylactically to eliminate residual micrometastatic disease. The treatment duration may range from a single dose to multiple cycles over a period of several months, depending on the clinical response and tolerability. The invention further provides kits for the diagnosis and treatment of GPC3-expressing cancers, comprising a vial containing the antibody or immunoconjugate, a vial containing a control antibody for comparison, instructions for use, and a diagnostic reagent for detecting GPC3 expression in tumor tissue. The kits may be used in clinical laboratories to guide patient selection, monitor treatment response, and personalize therapeutic regimens. The methods of use described herein are supported by extensive preclinical data demonstrating the safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetic profile of the antibodies and immunoconjugates in multiple animal models, including xenograft models of Hep3B, HepG2, and Huh-7 tumors, as well as patient-derived xenograft models that recapitulate the heterogeneity of human HCC. The antibodies are well tolerated in non-human primates, with no evidence of hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, or hematologic toxicity at doses up to 100 mg/kg, and no neutralizing antibody response observed after repeated administration. The invention thus provides a comprehensive and clinically translatable platform for the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma and other GPC3-expressing malignancies, combining novel therapeutic agents with precise patient selection strategies and optimized delivery protocols to achieve superior outcomes compared to existing standards of care.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The human monoclonal antibody HS20 was generated by immunizing transgenic mice expressing human immunoglobulin genes with recombinant GPC3-hFc fusion protein purified from mammalian cell culture supernatants. Mice were immunized intraperitoneally with 10 μg of GPC3-hFc emulsified in Freund’s complete adjuvant on day 0, followed by booster immunizations with 5 μg of antigen in Freund’s incomplete adjuvant on days 14, 28, and 42. Spleen cells were harvested on day 49 and fused with SP2/0 myeloma cells using polyethylene glycol to generate hybridomas. Hybridoma supernatants were screened by ELISA for binding to GPC3-hFc and GPC3ΔHS-hFc, the latter of which lacks heparan sulfate chains due to enzymatic removal of HS moieties. Clones exhibiting strong binding to GPC3-hFc but no binding to GPC3ΔHS-hFc were selected for further characterization. One such clone, designated HS20, was subcloned by limiting dilution and expanded in culture. The antibody was purified from culture supernatant using Protein A affinity chromatography, followed by size-exclusion chromatography to remove aggregates. The purified antibody was analyzed by SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions, revealing a single band at approximately 150 kDa consistent with a fully assembled IgG1 molecule. The antibody was confirmed to be human IgG1κ by isotype-specific ELISA. Binding specificity was further confirmed by flow cytometry using Hep3B cells, which express high levels of GPC3, and SK-hep1 cells, which are GPC3-negative. HS20 bound robustly to Hep3B cells but showed no binding to SK-hep1 cells, whereas a control IgG1 antibody showed no binding to either cell line. To confirm that HS20 binds specifically to the heparan sulfate chains of GPC3, Hep3B cells were treated with heparinase III to remove HS chains, resulting in complete loss of HS20 binding, as measured by flow cytometry. In contrast, treatment with chondroitinase ABC, which removes chondroitin sulfate, had no effect on HS20 binding. HS20 did not bind to recombinant CD22-hFc, a control Fc-fusion protein, confirming its specificity for GPC3. These data demonstrate that HS20 is a human monoclonal antibody that selectively recognizes the heparan sulfate chains of GPC3 and does not bind to the core protein or to unrelated proteins.

### Example 2

To evaluate the functional activity of HS20 in inhibiting hepatocyte growth factor (HGF)-mediated signaling, Hep3B cells were serum-starved for 24 hours and then treated with 50 ng/mL recombinant human HGF in the presence or absence of 50 μg/mL HS20 or a control human IgG. After 10 minutes of stimulation, cells were lysed and analyzed by Western blotting for phosphorylation of c-Met at tyrosine residues 1234 and 1235, which are indicative of receptor activation. HGF treatment resulted in a robust increase in phospho-c-Met levels compared to untreated controls, whereas pre-treatment with HS20 completely abolished this phosphorylation, with no effect observed in cells treated with control IgG. Similar results were obtained in Huh-7 cells, which also express GPC3, but not in SK-hep1 cells, which lack GPC3 expression. To determine whether HS20 affects total c-Met levels, blots were stripped and re-probed with an antibody against total c-Met, revealing no change in total protein levels, indicating that HS20 inhibits c-Met activation without altering receptor expression. To confirm that the inhibition of c-Met phosphorylation was dependent on GPC3, Hep3B cells stably expressing GPC3 shRNA were generated and treated with HGF and HS20. In GPC3-knockdown cells, HGF-induced c-Met phosphorylation was significantly reduced compared to control shRNA cells, and HS20 had no additional inhibitory effect, demonstrating that the antibody’s activity requires the presence of GPC3. These results establish that HS20 inhibits HGF-induced c-Met activation by blocking the interaction between HGF and GPC3’s heparan sulfate chains, thereby preventing ligand-induced receptor dimerization and autophosphorylation.

### Mutation of HS20 to Remove an N-Glycosylation Site does not Alter Binding Affinity and Specificity

The HS20 antibody contains a single N-glycosylation site in its heavy chain variable region at position Asn-30, which is conserved in the germline sequence. To determine whether this glycosylation affects antigen binding or antibody stability, a point mutation was introduced to replace asparagine with glutamine (N30Q), thereby eliminating the N-glycosylation motif. The mutant HS20-N30Q was expressed in CHO cells alongside wild-type HS20, and both variants were purified to homogeneity. Binding affinity was assessed by surface plasmon resonance using immobilized GPC3-hFc, revealing identical dissociation constants (KD = 0.8 nM for wild-type and 0.7 nM for N30Q). Flow cytometry analysis on Hep3B cells showed identical staining intensity and binding kinetics between the two variants, confirming that removal of the N-glycan does not impair antigen recognition. Furthermore, functional assays demonstrated that HS20-N30Q retained full ability to inhibit HGF-induced c-Met phosphorylation and cell migration, with no significant difference in potency compared to wild-type HS20. Stability studies under accelerated conditions (40°C for 4 weeks) revealed no difference in aggregation or fragmentation between the two variants, as assessed by size-exclusion chromatography. These results indicate that the N-glycosylation site in HS20 is not required for antigen binding, functional activity, or structural stability, and that the antibody can be produced in non-glycosylated form without compromising its therapeutic properties. This finding is significant because it allows for the production of HS20 in bacterial or yeast expression systems, which do not perform mammalian-type N-glycosylation, thereby reducing manufacturing costs and complexity.

### HS20 Inhibits HCC Tumor Growth In Vivo

To evaluate the anti-tumor activity of HS20 in vivo, female BALB/c nu/nu mice were subcutaneously injected with 10 × 10⁶ Hep3B cells suspended in PBS. Once tumors reached an average volume of 100 mm³, mice were randomized into two groups (n = 8 per group) and treated with either 25 mg/kg HS20 or an equal volume of phosphate-buffered saline via intravenous injection twice weekly for three weeks. Tumor volumes were measured twice weekly using calipers, and the formula V = ab²/2 was used to calculate volume. HS20-treated mice exhibited a marked reduction in tumor growth compared to vehicle-treated controls, with tumor volumes in the HS20 group being reduced by 68% at day 21 (p < 0.001). Histological analysis of excised tumors revealed decreased proliferation (Ki-67 staining), increased apoptosis (cleaved caspase-3 staining), and reduced microvessel density (CD31 staining) in HS20-treated tumors compared to controls. Western blot analysis of tumor lysates confirmed a significant reduction in phospho-c-Met and phospho-ERK levels in HS20-treated tumors, indicating inhibition of downstream signaling pathways. No significant weight loss or signs of toxicity were observed in HS20-treated mice, and serum levels of liver enzymes (ALT, AST) remained within normal ranges, indicating no hepatotoxicity. These results demonstrate that HS20 effectively inhibits the growth of GPC3-positive hepatocellular carcinoma in vivo by blocking HGF/c-Met signaling and that it is well tolerated at therapeutically effective doses.

### The HN3 Human Antibody Inhibits HCC Tumor Growth In Vivo

To evaluate the anti-tumor activity of HN3, a human monoclonal antibody that binds the core protein of GPC3, female BALB/c nu/nu mice were subcutaneously injected with 5 × 10⁶ HepG2 cells, a cell line expressing GPC3 and harboring a constitutive β-catenin mutation. Once tumors reached an average volume of 100 mm³, mice were randomized into three groups (n = 10 per group) and treated with either 20 mg/kg HN3, 20 mg/kg HS20, or vehicle control via intravenous injection twice weekly for two weeks. Tumor volumes were measured twice weekly. HN3 treatment resulted in a 42% reduction in tumor volume compared to vehicle controls (p < 0.01), while HS20 treatment resulted in a 51% reduction (p < 0.001). When HN3 and HS20 were administered in combination, tumor growth was suppressed by 83% compared to controls (p < 0.0001), demonstrating a synergistic effect. Immunohistochemical analysis of tumor sections revealed that HN3 treatment led to increased internalization of GPC3, as evidenced by reduced membrane staining and increased intracellular accumulation, while HS20 treatment reduced phospho-c-Met and phospho-β-catenin levels. Combination therapy resulted in the most profound suppression of both signaling pathways. No signs of toxicity were observed in any treatment group. These results demonstrate that HN3, despite lacking direct inhibitory activity on HGF/c-Met signaling in vitro, contributes to tumor suppression in vivo by promoting GPC3 internalization and degradation, and that its combination with HS20 leads to enhanced anti-tumor efficacy through complementary mechanisms.

### Example 6

To determine whether HS20 is effective against metastatic HCC, a tail vein injection model of lung metastasis was established. Hep3B cells were injected intravenously into BALB/c nu/nu mice, and after 14 days, mice were treated with 25 mg/kg HS20 or vehicle control twice weekly for three weeks. Mice were sacrificed on day 42, and lungs were harvested for histological analysis. Vehicle-treated mice exhibited numerous large metastatic nodules on the lung surface, whereas HS20-treated mice showed a 75% reduction in the number of metastatic foci and a 90% reduction in total metastatic burden as quantified by histomorphometry. Hematoxylin and eosin staining confirmed the presence of viable tumor cells in control lungs, while HS20-treated lungs contained only scattered apoptotic cells and fibrotic areas. Immunohistochemistry for phospho-c-Met revealed strong staining in control lung metastases but minimal staining in HS20-treated lungs. These findings demonstrate that HS20 not only inhibits primary tumor growth but also suppresses the formation of distant metastases in vivo, likely by blocking HGF/c-Met-driven motility and invasion. The results support the potential of HS20 as a therapeutic agent for preventing metastatic spread in patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma.
