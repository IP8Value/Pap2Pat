# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and therapeutic protein engineering, specifically to recombinant IgA1 protease polypeptides and their use in the treatment of IgA1 deposition disorders. More particularly, the invention provides novel, soluble, and enzymatically active IgA1 protease polypeptides comprising only the catalytic protease domain of the native serine-type IgA1 protease, devoid of the signal peptide, α-protein domain, and β-core domain, which are typically present in the full-length precursor protein produced by pathogenic bacteria such as *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Neisseria meningitidis*, and *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*. The invention further encompasses methods for the high-yield recombinant expression of these truncated IgA1 protease polypeptides in non-pathogenic bacterial hosts, particularly *Escherichia coli*, under optimized culture conditions that maximize solubility, stability, and enzymatic activity. Additionally, the invention includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising these purified recombinant IgA1 proteases and methods of using them to degrade pathogenic IgA1 immune complexes deposited in tissues, particularly within the glomeruli of the kidney, thereby ameliorating or preventing the progression of IgA1 deposition disorders such as IgA nephropathy.

The invention is distinguished from prior art by the unprecedented yield and purity of soluble, active IgA1 protease achieved through the expression of the isolated protease domain without accessory domains that complicate folding, secretion, and purification. Unlike previous approaches that attempted to express the full-length precursor protein or employed pathogenic bacterial systems with inherent limitations in scalability and safety, the present invention enables the production of therapeutic-grade IgA1 protease at yields exceeding 100 mg/L of total soluble protein and 20–40 mg/L of purified, active enzyme—more than one thousand-fold greater than previously reported. This breakthrough permits the development of clinically viable, scalable, and safe biologic therapies for IgA1-mediated renal and extrarenal diseases, which were previously considered infeasible due to the inability to produce sufficient quantities of the enzyme in a functional and purifiable form.

The invention further encompasses novel analytical methods for quantifying the enzymatic activity of IgA1 protease using automated electrophoresis systems, enabling precise, reproducible, and high-throughput measurement of proteolytic activity based on the real-time degradation of human IgA1 substrate. These methods facilitate quality control, batch consistency, and dose optimization for therapeutic applications. The IgA1 protease polypeptides disclosed herein are engineered to retain full specificity for the hinge region of human IgA1, with no detectable activity against IgA2, IgG, IgM, or IgA from non-primate species, ensuring targeted degradation of pathogenic immune complexes without off-target proteolysis of other plasma or tissue proteins. This specificity is critical for therapeutic safety and distinguishes the invention from non-specific proteases such as chymopapain or subtilisin, which have been tested in prior studies but failed due to their broad substrate range and associated systemic toxicity.

The invention is applicable to the fields of nephrology, immunology, and biopharmaceutical manufacturing, and provides a foundation for the development of first-in-class biologic therapies for IgA nephropathy, a leading cause of chronic kidney disease worldwide. The recombinant IgA1 protease polypeptides of the invention are suitable for parenteral administration, including intravenous, intraperitoneal, or subcutaneous delivery, and may be formulated with stabilizing agents, buffers, and excipients to ensure shelf stability and in vivo bioavailability. The invention thus represents a transformative advancement in the treatment of IgA1 deposition disorders, offering a mechanism-based, enzyme replacement strategy that directly targets the pathogenic molecule responsible for disease initiation and progression.

## BACKGROUND

IgA nephropathy, also known as Berger’s disease, is the most common form of primary glomerulonephritis worldwide and a leading cause of end-stage renal disease, particularly in young adults. The disease is characterized by the aberrant deposition of galactose-deficient IgA1-containing immune complexes within the mesangial matrix of the glomerulus, triggering complement activation, inflammation, mesangial cell proliferation, and progressive fibrosis. The pathogenesis of IgA nephropathy is intimately linked to the structural features of human IgA1, particularly the unique extended hinge region that contains multiple O-linked oligosaccharides. In healthy individuals, IgA1 is efficiently cleared from circulation, but in patients with IgA nephropathy, a subset of IgA1 molecules exhibits incomplete galactosylation of these hinge region O-glycans, rendering them immunogenic and prone to form immune complexes with autoantibodies directed against the exposed N-acetylgalactosamine residues. These circulating immune complexes become trapped in the glomerular mesangium, initiating a cascade of inflammatory events that ultimately lead to glomerular scarring and loss of renal function.

Despite decades of research, no disease-modifying therapy has been approved for IgA nephropathy. Current standard-of-care interventions, including angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, corticosteroids, and immunosuppressive agents, are largely palliative and do not address the underlying pathogenic mechanism of IgA1 deposition. The development of targeted therapies that directly remove or neutralize the pathogenic IgA1 immune complexes has long been a goal of nephrology research. Early attempts to achieve this goal involved the use of exogenous proteolytic enzymes such as chymopapain and subtilisin, which demonstrated the feasibility of enzymatic degradation of IgA1 deposits in animal models. However, these enzymes lack specificity and cleave a broad array of proteins, including albumin, fibrinogen, and extracellular matrix components, resulting in systemic toxicity and unacceptable safety profiles that preclude clinical use.

A more promising approach emerged with the discovery that certain bacterial pathogens, including *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Neisseria meningitidis*, and *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, naturally produce IgA1-specific proteases that cleave human IgA1 at a single, highly conserved site within its hinge region, leaving IgA2, IgG, and IgM intact. These proteases, which belong to the serine protease family, recognize a unique amino acid sequence motif in the IgA1 hinge region characterized by a proline-serine or proline-threonine cleavage site, depending on the protease subtype. The specificity of these enzymes for human IgA1, combined with their ability to degrade immune complexes without affecting other immunoglobulins, made them attractive candidates for therapeutic development. Indeed, early studies demonstrated that purified IgA1 protease from *H. influenzae* could reduce glomerular IgA1 deposition in passive mouse models of IgA nephropathy, providing proof-of-concept that targeted proteolysis of IgA1 could be therapeutically beneficial.

However, the clinical translation of this approach was severely hampered by fundamental technical and biological challenges. First, the natural producers of IgA1 protease—*H. influenzae*, *N. meningitidis*, and *N. gonorrhoeae*—are human pathogens with complex growth requirements, including the need for hemin and other undefined factors, making large-scale fermentation impractical, unsafe, and cost-prohibitive. Second, attempts to express the full-length IgA1 protease precursor in heterologous hosts such as *Escherichia coli* consistently resulted in poor yields, misfolding, aggregation into insoluble inclusion bodies, or secretion inefficiency due to the presence of accessory domains required for native bacterial secretion. The full-length precursor protein consists of four domains: an N-terminal signal peptide, the catalytic protease domain, an α-protein domain, and a C-terminal β-core domain. The β-core domain functions as a translocation channel embedded in the outer membrane, while the α-protein domain acts as a chaperone and secretion facilitator. In the native bacterial context, the precursor undergoes autocatalytic cleavage at three sites to release the mature protease domain into the extracellular space. However, when expressed in *E. coli*, which lacks the appropriate outer membrane machinery, the full-length precursor fails to be properly processed, leading to intracellular accumulation, misfolding, and loss of solubility.

Prior attempts to overcome these limitations included the expression of truncated variants lacking the β-core domain but retaining the α-protein domain and signal peptide. While these constructs improved solubility compared to the full-length precursor, they still suffered from low expression yields, poor stability, and inconsistent enzymatic activity. Moreover, the presence of the α-protein domain, although not directly involved in catalysis, appeared to interfere with proper folding or to introduce steric hindrance that reduced enzymatic efficiency. Other studies attempted to express the protease domain alone but failed to achieve detectable levels of soluble protein, likely due to the intrinsic instability of the isolated catalytic domain in the absence of its native structural context. As a result, the highest reported yield of purified, active IgA1 protease from any recombinant system prior to this invention was less than 0.03 mg per liter of culture, a quantity insufficient for even preclinical testing, let alone clinical application.

Furthermore, the analytical methods available for measuring IgA1 protease activity were crude and unreliable, relying on gel-based Western blotting to assess cleavage of IgA1 substrate, which provided only qualitative or semi-quantitative data and was highly susceptible to variability in sample preparation, transfer efficiency, and antibody binding. These limitations rendered it impossible to accurately compare enzyme batches, determine specific activity, or establish dose-response relationships necessary for therapeutic development.

The present invention overcomes these longstanding barriers by demonstrating, for the first time, that the catalytic protease domain of *H. influenzae* IgA1 protease, when expressed in isolation without the signal peptide, α-protein domain, or β-core domain, can be produced in *Escherichia coli* as a soluble, stable, and fully active enzyme at yields exceeding 100 mg/L of total soluble protein and 20–40 mg/L of purified enzyme. This breakthrough was achieved through systematic screening of multiple expression constructs, host strains, induction conditions, and purification protocols. The invention further introduces a novel, high-precision method for quantifying IgA1 protease activity using automated capillary electrophoresis, enabling accurate determination of specific activity and batch consistency. The resulting recombinant IgA1 protease polypeptides are structurally and functionally equivalent to the native enzyme, exhibit no cross-reactivity with non-IgA1 substrates, and can be manufactured at scales sufficient for clinical use. This invention thus transforms IgA1 protease from a scientifically intriguing but practically inaccessible tool into a viable therapeutic candidate for the treatment of IgA nephropathy and related IgA1 deposition disorders.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious solution to the long-standing problem of producing sufficient quantities of a highly specific, soluble, and enzymatically active IgA1 protease for therapeutic use in IgA1 deposition disorders. The invention is based on the surprising discovery that the catalytic protease domain of *Haemophilus influenzae* serine-type IgA1 protease, when expressed in isolation without the signal peptide, α-protein domain, or β-core domain, retains full proteolytic activity against human IgA1 and can be produced in *Escherichia coli* at unprecedented yields and purity. This finding contradicts prior assumptions that the accessory domains are essential for proper folding, stability, or secretion of the protease and demonstrates that the catalytic domain is intrinsically capable of adopting its functional conformation independently in a heterologous host.

The invention encompasses recombinant IgA1 protease polypeptides consisting essentially of the protease domain of *H. influenzae* IgA1 protease, wherein the polypeptide lacks the N-terminal signal peptide, the α-protein domain, and the C-terminal β-core domain of the native precursor protein. In a preferred embodiment, the polypeptide comprises amino acids 1–850 of the mature *H. influenzae* IgA1 protease, corresponding to the catalytic domain as defined by the cleavage sites a, b, and c of the precursor protein, and includes a C-terminal hexahistidine tag for purification. The polypeptide is expressed in a non-pathogenic bacterial host, preferably *Escherichia coli*, under conditions that promote solubility and prevent aggregation, including low-temperature induction (12–25°C), low concentrations of inducer (0.1–0.6 mM IPTG), and the use of specialized host strains such as C41(DE3) or C43(DE3), which are known to reduce the burden of recombinant protein expression and enhance the solubility of difficult-to-express proteins.

The invention further provides methods for the production of these polypeptides, including culturing a recombinant *E. coli* strain harboring an expression vector encoding the isolated IgA1 protease domain, inducing expression under optimized conditions, lysing the cells, and purifying the polypeptide using a combination of affinity chromatography, anion-exchange chromatography, and size-exclusion chromatography to achieve purity greater than 95%. The purified polypeptide exhibits a specific activity of at least 25 ng/μL/min per ng of enzyme, as measured by the degradation of human IgA1 substrate using an automated electrophoresis-based assay, and retains full specificity for human IgA1, with no detectable cleavage of IgA2, IgG, IgM, or IgA from non-human primates or rodents.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the purified IgA1 protease polypeptide in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, buffer, or stabilizing agent, wherein the composition is formulated for parenteral administration and contains no detectable endotoxin, host cell protein, or nucleic acid contamination. The compositions may be lyophilized for long-term storage and reconstituted immediately prior to administration. The invention also provides methods of treating IgA1 deposition disorders, including IgA nephropathy, by administering an effective amount of the purified IgA1 protease polypeptide to a subject in need thereof, wherein the administration results in the degradation of pathogenic IgA1 immune complexes in the glomerulus or other affected tissues, leading to a reduction in proteinuria, stabilization of renal function, or improvement in histological markers of glomerular injury.

The invention further includes a novel method for quantifying the enzymatic activity of IgA1 protease using an Experion Automated Electrophoresis System, wherein a known concentration of human IgA1 is incubated with a sample of the protease, the reaction is stopped, and the remaining intact IgA1 is quantified by capillary electrophoresis using a standardized calibration curve. This method provides a rapid, reproducible, and quantitative measure of specific activity that is superior to traditional Western blot-based assays and enables precise batch-to-batch comparison, quality control, and dose determination for therapeutic applications.

The invention represents a paradigm shift in the therapeutic development of IgA1 proteases by demonstrating that the catalytic domain alone is sufficient for function, that it can be produced at high yields in a safe, scalable, and cost-effective manner, and that it possesses the necessary purity, stability, and specificity for clinical use. The invention thus enables the development of the first targeted, mechanism-based therapy for IgA nephropathy, a disease with no approved disease-modifying treatments, and opens new avenues for the treatment of other IgA1-mediated disorders, including Henoch-Schönlein purpura, IgA vasculitis, and IgA1-containing immune complex glomerulonephritis in transplant recipients.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “IgA1 protease” refers to a serine protease enzyme that specifically cleaves human immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1) at a conserved site within its hinge region, between a proline residue and a serine or threonine residue, without cleaving other immunoglobulin classes such as IgA2, IgG, IgM, or IgA from non-human species. The term encompasses both native IgA1 proteases derived from pathogenic bacteria and recombinant variants thereof, including those that have been modified to enhance solubility, stability, or expression yield, provided that the core catalytic function and substrate specificity are retained. The term “IgA1 protease polypeptide” refers to a linear chain of amino acids that constitutes the functional unit of the enzyme, whether expressed in its native form or as a recombinant variant, and may include additional amino acid sequences such as affinity tags, linkers, or fusion partners that do not interfere with its enzymatic activity or specificity.

The term “protease domain” refers to the portion of the IgA1 protease precursor protein that contains the catalytic triad responsible for peptide bond hydrolysis and the substrate-binding pocket that confers specificity for the IgA1 hinge region. In the context of *Haemophilus influenzae* IgA1 protease, the protease domain corresponds to amino acid residues 1–850 of the mature enzyme, as defined by the autocatalytic cleavage sites a, b, and c that separate the signal peptide, protease domain, α-protein domain, and β-core domain in the full-length precursor. The protease domain does not include the signal peptide, which directs translocation across the inner membrane, nor the α-protein domain, which facilitates secretion through the β-core channel, nor the β-core domain, which forms a transmembrane pore. The protease domain, when expressed in isolation, retains full enzymatic activity and specificity, as demonstrated herein.

The term “recombinant” refers to a polypeptide or nucleic acid molecule that has been artificially generated through genetic engineering techniques, including the insertion of a gene encoding the polypeptide into an expression vector, transformation into a host cell, and subsequent expression and purification of the encoded protein. The term excludes polypeptides isolated directly from natural bacterial sources, such as *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Neisseria meningitidis*, or *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, regardless of whether they are purified or partially characterized.

The term “soluble” refers to a polypeptide that remains in the aqueous phase following cell lysis and centrifugation, as opposed to being aggregated into insoluble inclusion bodies. Solubility is confirmed by the presence of the polypeptide in the supernatant fraction after high-speed centrifugation of lysed cells and by its ability to be purified by standard chromatographic techniques without requiring denaturation and refolding procedures.

The term “active” refers to a polypeptide that possesses enzymatic function, as demonstrated by its ability to cleave human IgA1 at the characteristic hinge region site, as detected by Western blot, mass spectrometry, or automated electrophoresis, and to produce a measurable decrease in intact IgA1 substrate over time under defined conditions. Activity is quantified as specific activity, expressed as nanograms of IgA1 cleaved per minute per nanogram of protease enzyme.

The term “specificity” refers to the ability of the IgA1 protease to cleave human IgA1 without cleaving other immunoglobulin classes or unrelated proteins. Specificity is confirmed by the absence of cleavage products when the enzyme is incubated with IgA2, IgG, IgM, murine IgA, rat IgA, or non-primate IgA, as determined by immunoblotting or mass spectrometry.

The term “pharmaceutical composition” refers to a formulation containing the IgA1 protease polypeptide in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, buffer, or excipient, suitable for administration to a mammalian subject. The composition may be in liquid or lyophilized form and may contain stabilizers such as sugars, polyols, surfactants, or amino acids to prevent aggregation, denaturation, or adsorption to container surfaces.

The term “effective amount” refers to the quantity of IgA1 protease polypeptide sufficient to produce a therapeutic effect in a subject, such as a reduction in glomerular IgA1 deposition, a decrease in proteinuria, or stabilization of serum creatinine levels, without causing significant adverse effects. The effective amount may vary depending on the route of administration, the severity of the disease, the patient’s body weight, and other clinical factors, but is typically in the range of 0.1 to 10 mg per kilogram of body weight per administration.

The term “IgA1 deposition disorder” refers to any pathological condition in which IgA1-containing immune complexes accumulate in tissues, leading to inflammation, tissue damage, or organ dysfunction. Preferred embodiments include IgA nephropathy, Henoch-Schönlein purpura, IgA vasculitis, and IgA1-associated glomerulonephritis occurring in the context of liver disease, chronic infection, or post-transplantation. The term excludes non-IgA1-mediated forms of glomerulonephritis, such as lupus nephritis, membranous nephropathy, or focal segmental glomerulosclerosis.

The term “host cell” refers to a living cell capable of expressing a recombinant polypeptide encoded by an exogenous nucleic acid molecule. Preferred host cells include *Escherichia coli* strains such as BL21(DE3), C41(DE3), C43(DE3), Origami B(DE3), and ArcticExpress(DE3), which are engineered for high-level protein expression and enhanced solubility of difficult-to-express proteins. The term excludes pathogenic bacterial species such as *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Neisseria meningitidis*, or *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, which are not used in the production of the polypeptides of the invention.

The term “expression vector” refers to a nucleic acid molecule, typically a plasmid or viral construct, that contains a promoter, a coding sequence for the polypeptide of interest, and regulatory elements necessary for transcription and translation in a host cell. Preferred vectors include pET21a, pColdIV, and derivatives thereof, which contain T7 or cold shock promoters, ribosome binding sites, and selectable markers such as antibiotic resistance genes.

The term “affinity tag” refers to a short amino acid sequence fused to the polypeptide to facilitate purification, detection, or immobilization. Preferred tags include hexahistidine (His6), FLAG, or Strep-tag II, with His6 being the most preferred due to its compatibility with immobilized metal affinity chromatography and minimal impact on protein structure or function.

The term “specific activity” refers to the enzymatic activity of the IgA1 protease per unit mass of protein, expressed as nanograms of IgA1 cleaved per minute per nanogram of protease enzyme. This parameter is used to assess the functional quality of the purified enzyme and is critical for determining dosing in therapeutic applications.

The term “automated electrophoresis system” refers to a capillary-based electrophoresis platform, such as the Experion System (Bio-Rad), that separates proteins based on size and charge using microfluidic chips and detects them via fluorescence or absorbance. This system is used herein to quantify the residual intact IgA1 substrate after protease digestion, enabling precise calculation of enzymatic activity.

The term “lyophilized” refers to a solid, dry formulation of the IgA1 protease polypeptide obtained by freeze-drying an aqueous solution under reduced pressure, which enhances long-term stability and allows for storage at ambient or refrigerated temperatures without loss of activity.

The term “endotoxin-free” refers to a preparation of the IgA1 protease polypeptide in which the concentration of bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is below the threshold established for parenteral pharmaceuticals, typically less than 0.1 endotoxin units per milligram of protein, as determined by the Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) assay.
