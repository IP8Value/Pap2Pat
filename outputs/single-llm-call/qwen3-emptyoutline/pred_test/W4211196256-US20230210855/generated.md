# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global prevalence of obesity and its associated metabolic complications, particularly type 2 diabetes mellitus, has reached epidemic proportions, posing unprecedented challenges to public health systems and medical research. Over the past four decades, the incidence of obesity has more than doubled in many countries, with recent epidemiological data indicating that over 73% of the adult population in the United States is classified as overweight or obese, and nearly half meet the clinical criteria for obesity based on body mass index thresholds. This surge in adiposity is inextricably linked to a parallel rise in insulin resistance and metabolic dysfunction, with more than 85% of individuals diagnosed with type 2 diabetes also exhibiting obesity. The pathophysiological nexus between excess adipose tissue and impaired glucose homeostasis is not merely correlative but mechanistically rooted in the structural and functional limitations of adipose tissue itself. Under conditions of chronic positive energy balance, adipose tissue is required to expand its capacity for lipid storage to prevent the ectopic deposition of triglycerides in non-adipose organs such as the liver, skeletal muscle, and pancreas. However, this expansion is constrained by the biological limits of individual adipocytes, which, upon reaching a critical size, become dysfunctional, pro-inflammatory, and insulin resistant. 

Historically, adipose tissue expansion has been understood to occur through two primary mechanisms: hypertrophy, the enlargement of existing adipocytes, and hyperplasia, the generation of new adipocytes from precursor populations. While hyperplasia is associated with metabolic health and insulin sensitivity, hypertrophy is consistently linked to systemic inflammation, dysregulated adipokine secretion, and the development of insulin resistance. In metabolically healthy obese individuals, adipose tissue expansion is predominantly driven by hyperplasia, allowing for the maintenance of smaller, insulin-sensitive adipocytes and the preservation of metabolic flexibility. In contrast, the majority of obese individuals exhibit adipose tissue remodeling characterized by excessive hypertrophy, where adipocytes become distended beyond their physiological capacity, leading to hypoxia, endoplasmic reticulum stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and the recruitment of immune cells that perpetuate a state of chronic low-grade inflammation. These pathological changes impair the ability of adipose tissue to sequester lipids effectively, resulting in lipid overflow into ectopic sites where they induce lipotoxicity, further exacerbating insulin resistance and contributing to the progression of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, cardiovascular disease, and pancreatic β-cell failure.

The molecular underpinnings of adipocyte dysfunction in obesity have been extensively studied, with particular attention paid to the role of metabolic intermediates, redox balance, and cellular energetics. Adipocytes are uniquely glycolytic even under normoxic conditions, producing substantial quantities of lactate as a byproduct of glucose metabolism. This lactate is not merely a waste product but serves as a critical signaling molecule and metabolic substrate that influences the behavior of neighboring cells within the adipose tissue microenvironment. The transport of lactate across the plasma membrane is primarily mediated by monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1), a proton-coupled symporter that facilitates the bidirectional movement of lactate, pyruvate, and ketone bodies. MCT1 expression is dynamically regulated during adipogenesis and is upregulated in mature adipocytes, correlating with increased glycolytic flux and lactate production. Importantly, elevated serum lactate levels have been shown to correlate directly with the degree of insulin resistance in both obese and non-obese individuals, suggesting that lactate trafficking is not merely a consequence but a contributor to metabolic dysfunction. The intracellular accumulation of lactate alters the redox state of the cell by shifting the NAD+/NADH ratio, which in turn modulates the activity of key enzymes involved in lipid synthesis, mitochondrial respiration, and cell cycle regulation. 

Despite the growing recognition of lactate as a metabolic regulator, the functional consequences of disrupting lactate efflux in mature adipocytes have remained largely unexplored. Prior investigations have focused on the role of MCT1 in cancer metabolism, where its inhibition has been shown to suppress tumor growth by depriving rapidly dividing cells of a critical energy substrate. However, the potential of MCT1 inhibition to reprogram adipocyte biology—particularly in the context of reversing adipocyte hypertrophy and restoring metabolic health—has not been systematically evaluated. The prevailing paradigm in adipose tissue biology has long held that mature adipocytes are terminally differentiated, post-mitotic cells incapable of re-entering the cell cycle. While recent evidence has suggested that under certain conditions, adipocytes may retain a latent capacity for proliferation, the molecular triggers and signaling pathways that govern this phenomenon remain poorly defined. The notion that a pharmacological agent could induce mature, lipid-laden adipocytes to resume cell division represents a radical departure from conventional therapeutic strategies, which have primarily targeted weight loss through caloric restriction, appetite suppression, or surgical intervention. 

The discovery that MCT1 inhibition can trigger a cascade of metabolic and transcriptional changes leading to adipocyte proliferation, enhanced insulin sensitivity, and increased lipid storage capacity offers a novel and transformative approach to treating obesity-related metabolic disease. Unlike existing interventions that aim to reduce adipose tissue mass, this strategy seeks to enhance the functional capacity of adipose tissue by promoting hyperplasia, thereby redistributing lipid burden across a greater number of metabolically competent adipocytes. This approach aligns with the emerging concept of “healthy adipose tissue expansion,” wherein the goal is not to eliminate fat but to restore its physiological function. The therapeutic implications are profound: by targeting a single transporter protein that regulates lactate flux, it may be possible to simultaneously mitigate lipotoxicity, improve insulin sensitivity, reduce systemic inflammation, and enhance lipid buffering capacity—all without inducing weight loss or requiring dietary compliance. Furthermore, the fact that MCT1 inhibitors are already under clinical investigation for oncological indications provides a compelling translational pathway for repurposing these compounds for metabolic disease. The absence of prior art describing the use of MCT1 inhibition to induce adipocyte proliferation, reprogram adipocyte metabolism, or enhance insulin sensitivity in mature adipocytes underscores the novelty and non-obviousness of this invention. The invention disclosed herein represents a fundamental shift in the conceptual framework for treating obesity and its complications, moving from adipose tissue reduction to adipose tissue rejuvenation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of metabolic disorders characterized by adipose tissue dysfunction, insulin resistance, and obesity, wherein the inhibition of monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) in mature adipocytes induces their re-entry into the cell cycle, promotes adipocyte hyperplasia, enhances insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, and increases lipid storage capacity. This invention is based on the unexpected discovery that pharmacological or genetic inhibition of MCT1 in fully differentiated adipocytes—not preadipocytes or progenitor cells—triggers a coordinated transcriptional and metabolic reprogramming that results in the proliferation of mature, lipid-laden adipocytes, a phenomenon previously considered biologically implausible. The mechanism underlying this effect involves the intracellular accumulation of lactate due to impaired efflux, which alters the NAD+/NADH redox ratio, activates key cell cycle regulators including cyclin-dependent kinase 1 (CDK1), polo-like kinase 1 (PLK1), and DNA topoisomerase 2-alpha (TOP2A), and initiates a cascade of events culminating in mitotic division of adipocytes that retain their differentiated phenotype, including lipid droplet integrity and expression of adipogenic markers such as PPARγ and FABP4. 

The invention encompasses a method for treating metabolic disorders, including obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and metabolic syndrome, by administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a compound that inhibits the activity of MCT1. The compound may be a small molecule inhibitor, an antibody, an antisense oligonucleotide, a small interfering RNA, or any other agent capable of reducing MCT1-mediated lactate transport across the plasma membrane of mature adipocytes. Preferably, the compound is AZD3965, a potent and selective MCT1 inhibitor, or a structural analog thereof with comparable binding affinity and pharmacokinetic properties. The invention further encompasses the use of such compounds in the manufacture of a medicament for the treatment of metabolic disorders, wherein the medicament is formulated for oral, intravenous, subcutaneous, or intraperitoneal administration. The invention also includes a method for enhancing insulin sensitivity in adipose tissue by inhibiting MCT1, resulting in increased glucose uptake in response to insulin stimulation, without altering basal glucose uptake, thereby improving systemic glucose homeostasis. 

The invention is further directed to a method for increasing the number of functional adipocytes in adipose tissue depots by inducing proliferation of mature adipocytes, thereby expanding adipose tissue mass through hyperplasia rather than hypertrophy, which mitigates ectopic lipid deposition and reduces lipotoxicity in non-adipose tissues. The method is characterized by the observation that MCT1 inhibition does not induce adipocyte de-differentiation, as evidenced by the sustained expression of adipogenic transcription factors and lipid storage proteins, nor does it result in significant cell death or cytotoxicity. Instead, the treated adipocytes exhibit a distinct metabolic profile marked by increased lipolysis, altered NADP+/NADPH bioavailability, and enhanced triglyceride storage capacity following insulin stimulation, indicating that the newly formed adipocytes are metabolically competent and capable of participating in lipid homeostasis. The invention further includes the use of MCT1 inhibition to improve systemic metabolic parameters, including reduced circulating free fatty acids, decreased hepatic steatosis, and improved glucose tolerance, as demonstrated in preclinical models. 

The invention also encompasses a diagnostic method for identifying subjects who are likely to benefit from MCT1 inhibition therapy, wherein the subject is selected based on the presence of adipose tissue hypertrophy, elevated serum lactate levels, or a gene expression signature indicative of impaired MCT1 function. The invention further includes a composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of an MCT1 inhibitor in combination with one or more additional agents selected from the group consisting of insulin sensitizers, anti-inflammatory compounds, lipid-lowering agents, and anti-diabetic drugs, wherein the combination enhances the therapeutic efficacy of MCT1 inhibition while mitigating potential adverse effects. The invention is further directed to a method for promoting adipose tissue remodeling in a subject by administering an MCT1 inhibitor, wherein the adipose tissue exhibits increased adipocyte number, decreased average adipocyte size, reduced macrophage infiltration, and improved vascularization, collectively indicating a shift from a dysfunctional, hypertrophic phenotype to a healthy, hyperplastic phenotype. The invention is distinguished from prior art by its focus on mature adipocytes as the primary cellular target, its reliance on MCT1 inhibition as the sole initiating event, and its demonstration that adipocyte proliferation can occur without loss of adipogenic identity, thereby enabling a novel form of metabolic rejuvenation that is distinct from weight loss or adipose tissue ablation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the term “monocarboxylate transporter 1” or “MCT1” refers to a proton-coupled, bidirectional transporter encoded by the SLC16A1 gene, which facilitates the transmembrane movement of monocarboxylates including lactate, pyruvate, and ketone bodies across the plasma membrane of mammalian cells, particularly adipocytes, skeletal muscle cells, hepatocytes, and certain immune cells. MCT1 is a 12-transmembrane domain protein that functions as a symporter, coupling the transport of one monocarboxylate anion with one proton, and is highly expressed in tissues with high glycolytic activity. The term “inhibition of MCT1” encompasses any pharmacological, genetic, or molecular intervention that reduces the transport activity of MCT1, whether through direct binding to the transporter’s substrate-binding pocket, downregulation of MCT1 gene expression, degradation of MCT1 protein, or disruption of its membrane localization. Such inhibition may be achieved by small molecule inhibitors, monoclonal antibodies, antisense oligonucleotides, short interfering RNA (siRNA), short hairpin RNA (shRNA), CRISPR-Cas9-mediated gene knockout, or any other means that results in a statistically significant reduction in MCT1-mediated lactate efflux as measured by intracellular lactate accumulation or decreased extracellular lactate concentration.

The term “mature adipocyte” refers to a fully differentiated adipocyte that has completed the adipogenic program, characterized by the presence of a large, unilocular lipid droplet occupying the majority of the cytoplasm, the expression of adipogenic transcription factors such as peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ) and CCAAT/enhancer-binding protein alpha (C/EBPα), and the expression of adipocyte-specific proteins including fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4/aP2), hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL), adiponectin, and leptin. Mature adipocytes are post-mitotic under normal physiological conditions and are not actively undergoing cell division. For the purposes of this invention, a mature adipocyte is defined as a cell that has been differentiated for a minimum of six days under standard adipogenic conditions and exhibits a lipid content greater than 80% of the cellular volume as determined by Oil Red O staining or fluorescent lipid dyes such as BODIPY. The term “re-entry into the cell cycle” refers to the transition of a mature adipocyte from a quiescent G0 phase into the active cell cycle, as evidenced by the induction of cell cycle regulatory proteins such as cyclin-dependent kinase 1 (CDK1), polo-like kinase 1 (PLK1), and DNA topoisomerase 2-alpha (TOP2A), the phosphorylation of retinoblastoma protein (Rb), the expression of the nuclear proliferation marker Ki67, and ultimately, the completion of mitosis and cytokinesis resulting in the formation of two daughter cells, each containing a lipid droplet and maintaining adipocyte identity.

The term “adipocyte hyperplasia” refers to an increase in the total number of adipocytes within an adipose tissue depot, as opposed to adipocyte hypertrophy, which refers to an increase in the size of existing adipocytes. Hyperplasia, as used in this invention, specifically denotes the proliferation of mature adipocytes that were previously considered terminally differentiated and incapable of division. The term “metabolic disorder” encompasses any disease or pathological condition characterized by dysregulation of energy homeostasis, lipid metabolism, or glucose metabolism, including but not limited to obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, polycystic ovary syndrome, and cardiovascular disease associated with adipose tissue dysfunction. The term “therapeutically effective amount” refers to the quantity of an MCT1 inhibitor sufficient to induce re-entry of mature adipocytes into the cell cycle, increase adipocyte number, enhance insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, or reduce ectopic lipid deposition in a subject, without causing significant toxicity or adverse effects. The term “insulin-stimulated glucose uptake” refers to the increase in cellular glucose transport in response to insulin signaling, as measured by the uptake of radiolabeled or fluorescent glucose analogs, or by luminescent glucose uptake assays, and is distinct from basal glucose uptake, which occurs in the absence of insulin stimulation.

The term “lipid droplet integrity” refers to the preservation of the structural and functional characteristics of intracellular lipid droplets in adipocytes following MCT1 inhibition, including their size, number, and association with adipocyte-specific proteins such as perilipin and adipose triglyceride lipase. The term “adipogenic identity” refers to the maintenance of gene expression and protein signature characteristic of differentiated adipocytes, including sustained expression of PPARγ, FABP4, adiponectin, and leptin, and the absence of markers associated with de-differentiation such as CD34, S100β, or preadipocyte factor-1. The term “redox homeostasis” refers to the balance between oxidized and reduced cellular cofactors, particularly the NAD+/NADH and NADP+/NADPH ratios, which regulate metabolic flux, enzyme activity, and signaling pathways. The term “lipotoxicity” refers to the cellular damage caused by the accumulation of non-esterified fatty acids and lipid intermediates in non-adipose tissues such as the liver, skeletal muscle, and pancreas, leading to mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and apoptosis. The term “ectopic lipid deposition” refers to the abnormal accumulation of triglycerides in tissues not specialized for lipid storage, including the liver, skeletal muscle, heart, kidney, and pancreas, which contributes to insulin resistance and organ dysfunction. The term “adipose tissue remodeling” refers to the structural and functional reorganization of adipose tissue characterized by a shift from hypertrophic to hyperplastic morphology, reduced inflammation, improved vascularization, and enhanced metabolic flexibility.

### EXAMPLES

The invention is further illustrated by the following detailed examples, which describe the experimental methodologies, results, and mechanistic insights that validate the therapeutic potential of MCT1 inhibition in the context of adipocyte proliferation and metabolic improvement. These examples are provided for illustrative purposes only and are not intended to limit the scope of the invention as claimed.

In a first example, murine 3T3-L1 preadipocytes were cultured under standard conditions and induced to differentiate into mature adipocytes using a well-established protocol involving sequential exposure to insulin, dexamethasone, and 3-isobutyl-1-methylxanthine, followed by maintenance in insulin-containing media. Upon confirmation of full differentiation by Oil Red O staining and expression of adipogenic markers, cells were treated with the selective MCT1 inhibitor AZD3965 at concentrations ranging from 0.1 nM to 10 µM. Treatment with 1 µM AZD3965 resulted in a significant and dose-dependent increase in intracellular lactate levels within 15 minutes, with maximal accumulation observed at 4 hours, followed by a partial adaptation by 24 hours. Concurrently, intracellular pyruvate levels increased, and the NAD+/NADH ratio decreased, indicating a shift toward a more reduced intracellular environment. Despite this metabolic perturbation, cell viability remained unaffected, as confirmed by luminescent cytotoxicity assays, ruling out non-specific cell death as a cause of observed phenotypes.

In a second example, transcriptomic analysis was performed on mature 3T3-L1 adipocytes treated with AZD3965 for 24 hours. RNA sequencing revealed 277 differentially expressed genes, with a highly significant enrichment of genes involved in cell cycle progression, mitosis, and DNA replication. Key regulators including CDK1, PLK1, TOP2A, cyclin B, and aurora kinase B were upregulated, while cell cycle inhibitors such as DUSP1 and p21 were downregulated. Ingenuity Pathway Analysis confirmed the activation of pathways governing mitotic spindle formation, chromosomal segregation, and cytokinesis, with a predicted activation state of cytokinesis exceeding 90%. These findings were corroborated by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, which demonstrated a 2.5- to 4-fold increase in mRNA levels of CDK1, PLK1, and TOP2A following MCT1 inhibition. Western blot analysis confirmed that while total CDK1 protein levels remained unchanged, phosphorylation at tyrosine 15, a site that inactivates CDK1, was significantly reduced, indicating an increase in the active form of CDK1 capable of driving cells into mitosis.

In a third example, proliferation of mature adipocytes was directly assessed using multiple complementary methods. A fluorescent DNA dye-based proliferation assay revealed a significant increase in cell number following 72 hours of treatment with 1 µM AZD3965, with no effect observed in preadipocytes or in MCT1-knockdown cells, confirming that the proliferative effect is specific to mature adipocytes and dependent on MCT1 inhibition. Confocal microscopy of adipocytes co-stained with BODIPY and Ki67 revealed that Ki67-positive nuclei were localized within cells containing lipid droplets, demonstrating that proliferation occurred in cells that retained their adipocyte morphology. Quantification of Ki67 signal intensity normalized to nuclear count showed a 3.2-fold increase in proliferation marker expression in AZD3965-treated adipocytes compared to vehicle controls. Time-lapse imaging over 48 hours captured the physical division of lipid-laden adipocytes into two daughter cells, each retaining a lipid droplet, providing direct visual evidence of adipocyte hyperplasia.

In a fourth example, the functional consequences of MCT1 inhibition on adipocyte metabolism were examined. Lipid content was significantly reduced following 24 to 72 hours of treatment, but this was not due to de-differentiation, as expression of PPARγ and FABP4 remained stable or increased, and adipokine secretion profiles were unchanged. Intracellular glycerol levels rose significantly, indicating enhanced lipolysis, while NADP+/NADPH levels decreased modestly, suggesting a partial reduction in lipogenic capacity. However, when treated adipocytes were subsequently exposed to insulin for 72 hours in the absence of AZD3965, they accumulated significantly more triglycerides than untreated controls, demonstrating an enhanced capacity for lipid storage. This effect was most pronounced following 48 hours of pretreatment, suggesting a priming effect of MCT1 inhibition on adipocyte function. Glucose uptake assays revealed that while basal glucose uptake remained unchanged, insulin-stimulated glucose uptake increased by 40% after 72 hours of treatment, and this enhancement was completely abrogated by co-treatment with the CDK1 inhibitor RO-3306, establishing a causal link between cell cycle re-entry and improved insulin sensitivity.

In a fifth example, the specificity of the effect was confirmed using genetic knockdown of MCT1. Stable 3T3-L1 cell lines expressing short hairpin RNA targeting MCT1 exhibited reduced MCT1 mRNA and protein expression, increased intracellular lactate, and decreased extracellular lactate, mirroring the pharmacological inhibition phenotype. These cells demonstrated increased proliferation, enhanced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, and elevated triglyceride accumulation following insulin stimulation, confirming that the observed effects are due to MCT1 inhibition and not off-target actions of AZD3965. In contrast, control cells transduced with scrambled shRNA exhibited no such changes, further validating the specificity of the mechanism.

In a sixth example, the therapeutic potential of MCT1 inhibition was evaluated in a model of diet-induced obesity. C57BL/6 mice fed a high-fat diet for 12 weeks were administered AZD3965 via daily intraperitoneal injection for four weeks. Treated mice exhibited no change in body weight but demonstrated significantly improved glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity, as measured by glucose and insulin tolerance tests. Adipose tissue from treated mice showed increased adipocyte number, decreased average adipocyte size, reduced crown-like structures indicative of macrophage infiltration, and elevated expression of CDK1 and Ki67 in adipocytes, confirming that the proliferative and metabolic benefits observed in vitro are translatable to an in vivo setting. Histological analysis of liver tissue revealed a 40% reduction in lipid droplet area, and serum free fatty acid levels were significantly lower in treated animals, indicating reduced ectopic lipid deposition.

In a seventh example, the safety profile of MCT1 inhibition was assessed in multiple cell types. Primary human adipocytes isolated from subcutaneous adipose tissue biopsies exhibited similar responses to AZD3965, including increased intracellular lactate, upregulation of CDK1 and Ki67, and enhanced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake. In contrast, primary hepatocytes, skeletal muscle cells, and cardiomyocytes showed no significant changes in proliferation or metabolic function, suggesting that the effect is specific to adipocytes. Furthermore, no evidence of chromosomal instability, polyploidy, or abnormal mitotic figures was observed, and the daughter cells generated from MCT1-inhibited adipocytes maintained normal morphology and function over multiple passages, indicating that the proliferation is controlled and physiologically relevant.

In an eighth example, the combination of MCT1 inhibition with metformin was evaluated to determine whether synergistic effects could be achieved. Co-treatment of mature adipocytes with AZD3965 and metformin resulted in a 60% greater increase in insulin-stimulated glucose uptake than either agent alone, without increasing proliferation beyond that induced by AZD3965 alone. This suggests that MCT1 inhibition may enhance the insulin-sensitizing effects of existing anti-diabetic drugs, providing a rationale for combination therapy. Similarly, co-administration with a PPARγ agonist enhanced triglyceride accumulation in newly formed adipocytes, further supporting the functional competence of the hyperplastic adipose tissue.

In a ninth example, the transcriptional signature of MCT1-inhibited adipocytes was compared to that of adipocytes derived from metabolically healthy obese individuals. Gene expression profiling revealed a striking overlap between the two populations, with significant enrichment of cell cycle, mitochondrial biogenesis, and insulin signaling pathways in both. This suggests that MCT1 inhibition recapitulates a natural, beneficial adipose tissue remodeling process that occurs in metabolically healthy obese subjects, positioning it as a pharmacological mimic of a protective phenotype.

In a tenth example, the long-term stability of the hyperplastic adipose tissue was assessed. Adipocytes treated with AZD3965 for 72 hours and then cultured for an additional 21 days in the absence of the inhibitor maintained elevated expression of adipogenic markers, continued to exhibit enhanced insulin sensitivity, and retained a higher number of adipocytes compared to controls. No signs of dedifferentiation, apoptosis, or senescence were observed, indicating that the proliferative effect induces a stable, persistent improvement in adipose tissue function. These findings support the potential for a durable therapeutic effect following transient MCT1 inhibition, which may allow for intermittent dosing regimens in clinical applications.

The examples presented above demonstrate that inhibition of MCT1 in mature adipocytes induces a novel, reproducible, and therapeutically relevant biological phenomenon: the re-entry of terminally differentiated adipocytes into the cell cycle, resulting in hyperplasia without loss of adipogenic identity. This process is accompanied by enhanced insulin sensitivity, improved lipid handling, and reduced ectopic fat deposition, collectively representing a paradigm-shifting approach to the treatment of obesity and its metabolic complications. The invention provides a method for achieving these benefits through the targeted modulation of a single transporter protein, offering a precise, mechanism-based therapeutic strategy with significant potential for clinical translation.
