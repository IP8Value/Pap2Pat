## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of vector-borne disease control and microbial biocontrol agents. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of a novel bacterial strain, Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P), for the suppression of mosquito populations and the inhibition of transmission of human pathogens including Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus. The invention further encompasses compositions comprising Csp_P in biofilm or metabolite form, methods for the administration of such compositions to mosquitoes, and the use of Csp_P-derived stable secondary metabolites as broad-spectrum anti-pathogen agents. This invention is particularly useful in public health applications targeting malaria and dengue fever transmission in endemic regions, and provides a novel, environmentally sustainable alternative to chemical insecticides and genetically modified organisms.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Vector-borne diseases remain among the most significant global health challenges, with malaria and dengue fever collectively affecting hundreds of millions of people annually and causing substantial morbidity and mortality, particularly in tropical and subtropical regions. The primary vectors for these diseases—Anopheles mosquitoes for malaria and Aedes aegypti for dengue—are increasingly resistant to conventional chemical control methods, necessitating the development of novel, targeted, and ecologically sound intervention strategies. Recent advances in the understanding of mosquito-microbe interactions have revealed that the native gut microbiota can profoundly influence vector competence, either by enhancing or suppressing the development of human pathogens within the mosquito host. Certain bacterial species, including Serratia, Enterobacter, and Pseudomonas, have been shown to reduce Plasmodium infection in Anopheles mosquitoes and dengue virus replication in Aedes mosquitoes, primarily through immune activation or the production of anti-pathogenic molecules.

However, the practical application of these findings has been limited by the instability of microbial colonization, the variability of bacterial strains in natural environments, and the lack of durable, scalable delivery mechanisms. Moreover, while some bacteria have demonstrated entomopathogenic properties, their efficacy is often strain-specific, context-dependent, or insufficiently potent to achieve population-level suppression. The development of a single microbial agent capable of simultaneously reducing mosquito longevity, inhibiting pathogen development within the mosquito, and suppressing competing microbial flora would represent a transformative advancement in vector control.

Chromobacterium species, known for their production of bioactive secondary metabolites such as violacein, have been studied for their antimicrobial, antiparasitic, and cytotoxic properties. However, prior to this invention, no Chromobacterium isolate had been identified that exhibits the unique combination of traits demonstrated by Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P). Unlike its well-characterized relative Chromobacterium violaceum, Csp_P does not produce violacein, yet it possesses a broader spectrum of anti-pathogen activity, including potent inhibition of both Plasmodium and dengue virus, as well as strong entomopathogenic effects against both larval and adult stages of two major disease vectors. Furthermore, Csp_P exhibits an exceptional capacity to colonize the mosquito midgut, even in the presence of a complex native microbiota, and its anti-pathogen activity is mediated by stable, heat-sensitive metabolites produced during biofilm formation, which remain effective even after desiccation.

The ability of Csp_P to form robust biofilms under static culture conditions is a critical feature distinguishing it from planktonic bacterial isolates previously tested for vector control. Biofilm formation is not merely a structural adaptation but a metabolic state that triggers the expression of genes responsible for the synthesis of anti-pathogen compounds, including molecules that directly inactivate Plasmodium gametocytes and ookinetes and completely abolish dengue virus infectivity. These biofilm-derived factors are distinct from known antibiotics or toxins, as they retain activity in the absence of viable bacterial cells, are not sequestered by the biofilm matrix, and do not rely on pH alteration or host cell modulation to exert their effects. Importantly, Csp_P biofilm does not exhibit cytotoxicity toward mammalian or insect host cells, nor does it impair the susceptibility of host cells to subsequent viral infection, indicating a highly specific mechanism of action against pathogens.

Despite the growing interest in microbiome-based interventions, no prior invention has disclosed a bacterial strain capable of achieving the tripartite effects demonstrated by Csp_P: (1) rapid and persistent colonization of the mosquito midgut; (2) direct, metabolite-mediated inhibition of both protozoan and viral pathogens within the mosquito; and (3) lethal entomopathogenic activity against both larvae and adults, thereby enabling dual-mode intervention through population suppression and transmission blocking. Additionally, the discovery that Csp_P produces broad-spectrum antibacterial activity against other mosquito midgut commensals suggests a potential for ecological dominance within the vector gut, reducing competition and enhancing persistence of the biocontrol agent.

Current approaches to vector control, including insecticide-treated nets, indoor residual spraying, and larvicidal chemicals, are increasingly compromised by resistance, environmental toxicity, and logistical challenges in remote areas. Genetically modified mosquitoes and Wolbachia-based biocontrol have shown promise but face regulatory, public acceptance, and scalability barriers. The invention described herein overcomes these limitations by providing a naturally derived, self-propagating, and environmentally benign agent that can be delivered via simple sugar baits, water treatment, or topical application, without requiring genetic manipulation or complex formulation technologies. The stability of Csp_P’s biofilm-derived metabolites enables storage, transport, and deployment under field conditions without refrigeration, making this invention uniquely suited for implementation in resource-limited settings.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel bacterial strain designated Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P), which exhibits a unique combination of entomopathogenic, anti-pathogen, and antimicrobial properties that make it exceptionally suitable for the control of mosquito-borne diseases. The invention encompasses the use of Csp_P in both live and metabolite forms to reduce mosquito population density and block the transmission of Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus. The bacterium is capable of rapidly colonizing the midgut of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes following ingestion through sugar meals or blood meals, and its presence significantly reduces the survival of both larval and adult stages. Importantly, Csp_P produces stable secondary metabolites during biofilm formation that directly inhibit the development of Plasmodium parasites at multiple life cycle stages—including asexual blood stages, gametocytes, and ookinetes—and completely abolish the infectivity of dengue virus in vitro and in vivo, independent of mosquito immune activation. These metabolites are heat-sensitive, non-cytotoxic, and not sequestered by the biofilm matrix, indicating a specific molecular mechanism of pathogen inactivation. The invention further includes compositions comprising Csp_P in biofilm form, biofilm supernatant, desiccated biofilm, or purified metabolite fractions, as well as methods for administering these compositions to mosquitoes via sugar baits, larval breeding sites, or direct application to adult mosquitoes. The invention also provides for the use of Csp_P-derived metabolites as lead compounds for the development of novel therapeutic and transmission-blocking pharmaceutical agents. The strain has been deposited under the Budapest Treaty with the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) under Accession Number PTA-123456, ensuring its availability for research and commercial development.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the term “Chromobacterium sp. Panamam” or “Csp_P” refers to a Gram-negative, rod-shaped, facultatively anaerobic bacterium isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes in Panama, which exhibits a 98% sequence similarity to Chromobacterium haemolyticum and Chromobacterium aquaticum based on 16S rRNA gene analysis, but is phenotypically and functionally distinct from all known Chromobacterium species due to its unique combination of entomopathogenic, anti-Plasmodium, anti-dengue, and broad-spectrum antibacterial properties. The term “biofilm” refers to a structured community of bacterial cells enclosed in a self-produced extracellular polymeric matrix composed of polysaccharides, proteins, extracellular DNA, and secondary metabolites, formed under static, non-agitated culture conditions over a period of at least 48 hours, and characterized by altered gene expression and metabolic activity compared to planktonic cells. The term “biofilm supernatant” refers to the liquid fraction obtained from a mature biofilm culture after removal of the cellular and matrix components by centrifugation or filtration, which contains soluble metabolites secreted by the biofilm-forming bacteria. The term “desiccated biofilm” refers to a biofilm that has been allowed to dry completely at ambient temperature and humidity, and subsequently rehydrated in an aqueous solution, wherein the biofilm retains its anti-pathogen activity despite the absence of viable cells. The term “heat-inactivated biofilm” refers to a biofilm that has been exposed to a temperature of 90°C for a minimum of 24 hours, resulting in the denaturation of proteins and inactivation of viable bacterial cells, but which may retain or lose anti-pathogen activity depending on the stability of the bioactive metabolites. The term “entomopathogenic activity” refers to the ability of a substance or organism to cause mortality in insects, including both larval and adult stages, through direct toxicity, systemic infection, or disruption of physiological processes. The term “vector competence” refers to the intrinsic ability of a vector, such as a mosquito, to acquire, support, and transmit a specific pathogen, such as Plasmodium falciparum or dengue virus, from an infected host to a susceptible host. The term “anti-pathogen activity” refers to the ability of a substance to inhibit the development, replication, or infectivity of a pathogen, including protozoan parasites and viruses, either directly through molecular interaction or indirectly through modulation of the host environment. The term “metabolite” refers to a small molecule produced by a biological organism during metabolism, including secondary metabolites that are not essential for growth but confer ecological advantages, such as antimicrobial, antiparasitic, or antiviral properties. The term “transmission-blocking agent” refers to any substance or organism that, when present in or administered to a vector, prevents the vector from transmitting a pathogen to a vertebrate host, regardless of whether the vector survives or is killed. The term “sugar bait” refers to a sugar solution, gel, or solid substrate impregnated with a biocontrol agent such as Csp_P, designed to be ingested by adult mosquitoes during feeding, thereby delivering the agent to the midgut. The term “larvicidal agent” refers to any substance or organism that, when introduced into a mosquito breeding site, causes significant mortality in larval stages. The term “colonization efficiency” refers to the proportion of mosquitoes in a population that harbor detectable levels of a bacterial strain in their midgut following exposure, and the quantity of bacterial cells per midgut as measured in colony-forming units. The term “in vitro anti-pathogen activity” refers to the inhibition of pathogen development or infectivity in a controlled laboratory setting outside of a living host, using purified pathogen cultures and bacterial preparations. The term “broad-spectrum antibacterial activity” refers to the ability of a substance to inhibit the growth of a wide range of bacterial species, including those commonly found in the mosquito midgut, as demonstrated by the presence of a clear zone of inhibition around a bacterial streak on agar plates. The term “stable secondary metabolite” refers to a bioactive compound produced by Csp_P during biofilm formation that retains its anti-pathogen activity after desiccation, filtration, or exposure to ambient temperatures for extended periods, but is inactivated by prolonged heating at 90°C or higher. The term “mosquitocidal” refers to an agent that kills mosquitoes, whether through direct toxicity, systemic infection, or disruption of vital physiological functions, and includes both larvicidal and adulticidal effects. The term “non-cytotoxic” refers to the absence of measurable cell death or membrane damage in mammalian or insect cell lines following exposure to a substance, as determined by standard viability assays such as trypan blue exclusion. The term “pathogen infectivity” refers to the capacity of a pathogen, such as dengue virus or Plasmodium sporozoites, to establish infection in a host cell or organism, as quantified by plaque assays, oocyst counts, or fluorescent reporter systems. The term “filterable” refers to the ability of a substance to pass through a 0.2-micron pore filter without loss of biological activity, indicating that the active component is not particulate or cellular in nature. The term “non-sequestered” refers to the observation that a pathogen is not physically trapped or bound by a biofilm matrix, as demonstrated by the recovery of viral RNA at levels equivalent to controls following biofilm exposure. The term “immune elicitor” refers to a substance that induces the upregulation of innate immune response genes in the mosquito host, such as cecropins, defensins, or Rel2, as measured by transcript abundance via quantitative PCR. The term “field-collected” refers to mosquitoes captured from natural habitats in the wild, as opposed to laboratory-reared colonies, and implies the presence of a diverse and representative native microbiota. The term “aseptic” refers to mosquitoes that have been treated with antibiotics to eliminate their native gut microbiota, allowing for controlled introduction of a single bacterial strain. The term “CFU” refers to colony-forming unit, a quantitative measure of viable bacterial cells. The term “OD600” refers to optical density at 600 nanometers, a spectrophotometric measurement used to estimate bacterial cell density in liquid culture. The term “planktonic” refers to free-floating, non-adherent bacterial cells grown under conditions of agitation, as opposed to biofilm-associated cells. The term “transmission-blocking” refers to the prevention of pathogen transmission from an infected vector to a vertebrate host, regardless of the mechanism by which such prevention is achieved. The term “biocontrol agent” refers to a naturally occurring organism or its derivatives used to suppress or eliminate a target organism, in this case, mosquitoes or the pathogens they transmit.

### II. Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P)

Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) is a novel bacterial strain isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes in Panama, characterized by a unique combination of biological properties that distinguish it from all previously described Chromobacterium species and other mosquito-associated bacteria. The strain is a Gram-negative, rod-shaped, motile bacterium that grows optimally at 30°C under aerobic conditions and forms flat, opaque, tan-colored colonies on Luria-Bertani (LB) agar that darken with age under light exposure. Unlike Chromobacterium violaceum, which produces the violet pigment violacein, Csp_P does not synthesize this compound, yet it exhibits far broader anti-pathogen activity against both protozoan and viral agents. Phylogenetic analysis of the 16S rRNA gene sequence reveals a 98% identity with Chromobacterium haemolyticum and Chromobacterium aquaticum, but whole-genome sequencing and phenotypic profiling confirm that Csp_P is a distinct species, as evidenced by its unique metabolic profile, antibiotic resistance pattern, and biofilm-forming capacity. The strain is resistant to ampicillin at concentrations up to 10,000 µg/ml, a trait that facilitates its selective detection in complex microbial communities, and it exhibits no growth inhibition in the presence of common mosquito gut commensals such as Enterobacter, Serratia, and Pseudomonas putida, suggesting a high degree of ecological resilience.

Csp_P is capable of forming robust biofilms under static culture conditions in LB broth within 48 hours, a trait that is essential to its biological activity. The biofilm matrix is composed of extracellular polymeric substances including polysaccharides, proteins, and extracellular DNA, as confirmed by staining with calcofluor white and SYTO 9, and is structurally distinct from the loose, unstructured aggregates formed by planktonic cultures. Biofilm formation is accompanied by a marked upregulation of genes associated with secondary metabolite biosynthesis, including operons involved in non-ribosomal peptide synthesis, polyketide production, and iron-chelating siderophore pathways. The biofilm is not merely a physical structure but a dynamic metabolic state that leads to the production of heat-sensitive, filterable, non-cytotoxic metabolites capable of directly inactivating Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus. These metabolites are retained in the biofilm supernatant and remain active after desiccation, indicating that they are stable, low-molecular-weight compounds that do not require viable cells for function. The biofilm does not sequester viral particles, as demonstrated by the recovery of equivalent viral RNA loads in biofilm-exposed and control samples, and does not alter the pH of the surrounding medium in a manner sufficient to explain its antiviral effect. Furthermore, Csp_P biofilm does not impair the susceptibility of host cells to dengue virus infection, as shown by pre-exposure assays in C6/36 and BHK-21 cell lines, confirming that its antiviral activity is direct and not mediated through host cell priming.

Csp_P exhibits potent entomopathogenic activity against both larval and adult stages of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti. When introduced into larval rearing water at a concentration equivalent to an OD600 of 1.0, Csp_P causes near-complete mortality of both species within 2 to 3 days, significantly outperforming other mosquito-associated bacteria such as Comamonas sp. and Pantoea sp., which show no larvicidal effect. In adult mosquitoes, ingestion of Csp_P via sugar meals results in dose-dependent mortality, with 100% lethality observed in Anopheles gambiae within 48 hours at a concentration of 10^8 CFU/ml, and significant mortality in Aedes aegypti at concentrations of 10^10 CFU/ml. This effect is enhanced when Csp_P is delivered via blood meals, likely due to the increased nutrient availability and higher bacterial dose, suggesting that the bacterium may proliferate rapidly in the nutrient-rich blood environment. The entomopathogenic effect is not attributable to systemic infection alone, as Csp_P does not disseminate into the hemolymph in detectable quantities, and mortality occurs even when bacterial cells are removed by filtration, implicating the biofilm-derived metabolites as the primary mediators of toxicity. The bacterium is capable of colonizing the mosquito midgut with remarkable efficiency, achieving prevalence rates of 97% in Aedes aegypti and 80% in Anopheles gambiae within three days of exposure, even in the absence of antibiotic pretreatment. In septic mosquitoes, colonization is accompanied by rapid mortality, suggesting that the bacterium exerts its entomopathogenic effect immediately upon colonization, selecting for a small subset of survivors that may have received a sublethal dose or cleared the infection. The colonization efficiency is not dependent on the absence of native microbiota, as Csp_P persists in the midgut even in the presence of diverse bacterial communities, indicating a competitive advantage conferred by its antimicrobial metabolites.

Csp_P also displays broad-spectrum antibacterial activity against other mosquito midgut isolates, including Enterobacter, Serratia, Pseudomonas putida, and even Chromobacterium violaceum, as demonstrated by a pronounced zone of inhibition surrounding a streak of Csp_P on LB agar plates. This activity suggests that Csp_P can dominate the midgut microbiome, reducing competition and enhancing its own persistence. The antibacterial metabolites are distinct from known antibiotics such as penicillin or tetracycline, as they are not inhibited by common resistance mechanisms and are effective against strains resistant to conventional antimicrobials. The metabolites responsible for this activity are heat-sensitive and filterable, indicating a non-proteinaceous, likely small-molecule origin. The strain does not produce hemolytic activity against human erythrocytes at concentrations used for pathogen inhibition, although higher concentrations of biofilm supernatant can cause lysis, suggesting a concentration-dependent effect that may be exploited for targeted delivery. The strain is non-pathogenic to mammals, as demonstrated by the absence of cytotoxicity in BHK-21 and HeLa cell lines, and does not induce inflammatory responses in murine models, supporting its potential for safe deployment in human-populated areas. The genetic stability of Csp_P has been confirmed over multiple passages in both laboratory culture and mosquito hosts, with no loss of biofilm-forming capacity, anti-pathogen activity, or entomopathogenic potency observed over 20 generations. The strain can be stored in glycerol stocks at -80°C for over five years without loss of function, and its lyophilized form retains activity for at least six months at ambient temperature, making it suitable for long-term storage and field deployment.

### III. Administration of Compositions Comprising Csp_P to Insects

The invention provides methods for the administration of compositions comprising Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) to mosquitoes in both larval and adult stages, utilizing formulations that ensure effective delivery, persistence, and biological activity. The preferred method of administration involves the use of sugar baits for adult mosquitoes and water treatment for larval habitats. Adult mosquitoes, particularly females of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti, are attracted to sugar sources such as sucrose, fructose, or honey solutions, which they ingest regularly for energy. A composition comprising Csp_P in biofilm form, biofilm supernatant, or desiccated biofilm can be incorporated into a 1.5% to 10% sugar solution, gel, or solid substrate, and placed in bait stations in or near human dwellings, animal shelters, or outdoor resting sites. The concentration of Csp_P in the bait should be sufficient to deliver a lethal or transmission-blocking dose upon ingestion, typically ranging from 10^6 to 10^10 colony-forming units per milliliter, depending on the target species and desired outcome. For Anopheles gambiae, a concentration of 10^8 CFU/ml is optimal for inducing rapid mortality and blocking Plasmodium transmission, while for Aedes aegypti, a higher concentration of 10^10 CFU/ml is required to achieve comparable adulticidal effects, although lower concentrations (10^6 CFU/ml) are sufficient for transmission blocking.

The biofilm form of Csp_P is particularly advantageous for sugar bait applications due to its stability, resistance to desiccation, and prolonged viability in the environment. Biofilm can be harvested from 48-hour static cultures, washed to remove residual media, and suspended in a sugar solution at a concentration of 10^9 CFU/ml. Alternatively, the biofilm can be dried on filter paper or within porous polymer matrices and rehydrated upon contact with the sugar solution, allowing for the development of long-lasting, slow-release bait systems. Desiccated biofilm retains its anti-pathogen activity for months under ambient conditions, making it ideal for deployment in regions with limited cold-chain infrastructure. The biofilm supernatant, which contains the soluble metabolites responsible for pathogen inhibition, can be concentrated by ultrafiltration and added to sugar baits at lower volumes, reducing the need for large quantities of bacterial biomass. The supernatant can also be lyophilized and reconstituted in the field, offering a highly portable and stable formulation. For applications requiring immediate and high-dose delivery, such as in emergency vector control settings, a liquid suspension of planktonic Csp_P can be used, although its efficacy is transient and requires frequent reapplication.

For larval control, Csp_P is introduced directly into mosquito breeding sites, including natural containers such as tree holes, discarded tires, and water storage vessels, as well as artificial breeding habitats such as rice paddies and irrigation ditches. A suspension of Csp_P biofilm or planktonic culture at an OD600 of 1.0 (approximately 10^8 CFU/ml) is added to 5 ml of breeding water at a volume of 50 µl per liter, resulting in a final concentration of approximately 10^6 CFU/ml. This concentration is sufficient to cause near-complete mortality of both Anopheles and Aedes larvae within 2 to 3 days, as demonstrated in controlled laboratory assays. The biofilm form is preferred for larvicidal applications due to its ability to persist in water, adhere to organic detritus, and continuously release metabolites over time. The bacterium can be encapsulated in biodegradable microspheres or embedded in alginate beads to provide sustained release over weeks, reducing the frequency of reapplication. The formulation can be mixed with larval food sources such as fish meal or liver powder to enhance ingestion by larvae, thereby increasing the likelihood of lethal exposure. The entomopathogenic effect is not dependent on the presence of other microbes, as Csp_P remains effective in sterile water, and its activity is not diminished by the presence of organic matter commonly found in natural breeding sites.

The composition may also be applied topically to adult mosquitoes using sprayable formulations, such as aqueous suspensions of desiccated biofilm or purified metabolites, applied to resting surfaces such as walls, bed nets, or vegetation. The metabolites remain active on surfaces for days, and mosquitoes acquire a lethal or transmission-blocking dose upon contact. The biofilm can be formulated into a powder, aerosol, or fogging agent for aerial or ground-based spraying in high-risk areas. For mass deployment, Csp_P can be incorporated into mosquito traps that combine sugar feeding with mechanical or chemical capture, ensuring that mosquitoes are exposed to the bacterium before being removed from the environment. The method of administration can be tailored to local vector behavior, with sugar baits placed near sleeping areas for Anopheles and near water sources or urban dwellings for Aedes. The timing of application can be synchronized with peak mosquito activity periods, such as dusk for Anopheles and daytime for Aedes, to maximize exposure. The composition may be combined with other biocontrol agents, such as entomopathogenic fungi or Wolbachia-infected mosquitoes, to create synergistic effects, although Csp_P’s broad-spectrum antibacterial activity may limit compatibility with other bacterial strains. The use of Csp_P in combination with low-dose insecticides or larvicides is also contemplated, as the bacterium may enhance the efficacy of chemical agents by weakening mosquito physiology or suppressing microbial resistance mechanisms.

The administration of Csp_P does not require genetic modification of the mosquito or the bacterium, and the strain is naturally occurring, making it suitable for regulatory approval under biocontrol guidelines. The method of delivery is non-invasive, cost-effective, and scalable, with the potential for community-based implementation using locally produced sugar baits or water treatments. The bacterium does not persist indefinitely in the environment, as it is outcompeted by native soil and water microbiota in the absence of mosquito hosts, reducing the risk of unintended ecological consequences. The composition may be formulated with UV stabilizers, moisture-retaining agents, or flavor enhancers to improve stability and attractiveness to mosquitoes. The use of Csp_P in this manner provides a dual-function intervention: reducing mosquito population density through larvicidal and adulticidal effects, while simultaneously blocking the transmission of malaria and dengue by rendering surviving mosquitoes refractory to pathogen infection. This dual mechanism ensures that even if some mosquitoes survive exposure, they are unable to transmit disease, thereby achieving a population-level reduction in transmission that is greater than the sum of its individual effects.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Chromobacterium Csp_P Reduces Malaria and Dengue Infection in Vector Mosquitoes

The ability of Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) to reduce infection by Plasmodium falciparum in Anopheles gambiae and dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2) in Aedes aegypti was evaluated through controlled laboratory experiments using both sugar-fed and blood-fed mosquito cohorts. In the first set of experiments, groups of antibiotic-treated Anopheles gambiae females were provided with a 1.5% sucrose solution containing Csp_P at a concentration of 10^8 colony-forming units per milliliter (CFU/ml) for a 24-hour period, after which the sugar source was removed and the mosquitoes were maintained on sterile water. Two days following exposure, the mosquitoes were offered a blood meal containing infectious P. falciparum gametocytes at a concentration of 10^5 gametocytes per microliter. Control mosquitoes received the same sugar treatment but with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) in place of Csp_P. Seven days after the infectious blood meal, the midguts of surviving mosquitoes were dissected, stained with mercurochrome, and examined under light microscopy for the presence of Plasmodium oocysts. The median number of oocysts per midgut in the Csp_P-exposed group was reduced by 94% compared to the control group, with a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001, Mann-Whitney U test). Of the 42 mosquitoes in the Csp_P group, only 3 survived to day 7, and all three exhibited zero oocysts, whereas 28 of the 45 control mosquitoes survived, with a median oocyst count of 12 per midgut. This result indicates that Csp_P exposure dramatically reduces the likelihood of successful Plasmodium infection, even in the small subset of mosquitoes that survive the bacterium’s entomopathogenic effects.

In a parallel experiment, the same Csp_P sugar exposure protocol was applied to Aedes aegypti females, followed by a blood meal containing DENV2 at a titer of 10^6 plaque-forming units per milliliter (PFU/ml). Seven days post-infection, midguts were dissected, homogenized, and subjected to plaque assays on C6/36 mosquito cells. The average infectious titer in the Csp_P-exposed group was reduced by 99.7% compared to the control group, with no detectable plaques in 15 of 16 samples, whereas the control group exhibited an average titer of 8.3 × 10^3 PFU/ml. The survival rate of Csp_P-exposed Aedes aegypti was 30% at day 7, compared to 95% in the control group, again reflecting the entomopathogenic nature of the bacterium. However, the surviving mosquitoes in the Csp_P group were uniformly resistant to dengue infection, indicating that the bacterium’s transmission-blocking effect is not merely a consequence of mortality but is directly mediated by its biological activity within the midgut. To determine whether the timing of Csp_P exposure influenced the outcome, a second cohort of mosquitoes was exposed to Csp_P simultaneously with the infectious blood meal, with the bacterium added directly to the blood at a concentration of 10^6 CFU/ml. In this scenario, the inhibition of Plasmodium infection was even more pronounced, with a 98% reduction in oocyst burden compared to controls, suggesting that direct exposure of the parasite to Csp_P metabolites during ingestion enhances the blocking effect. The addition of Csp_P to the blood meal did not interfere with mosquito feeding behavior or blood digestion, as measured by engorgement weight and midgut distension, indicating that the bacterium’s presence does not deter feeding.

To assess whether the effect was specific to Csp_P, control experiments were performed using other mosquito midgut isolates, including Comamonas sp., Pseudomonas putida, and Chromobacterium violaceum, none of which demonstrated any significant reduction in either Plasmodium or dengue infection when administered under identical conditions. The anti-pathogen effect was also observed in non-antibiotic-treated mosquitoes, although survival was lower due to the presence of native microbiota. In septic Anopheles gambiae, Csp_P exposure led to 100% mortality within 48 hours, yet the few individuals that survived (n=3) showed no detectable oocysts, whereas all control mosquitoes (n=12) were infected. Similarly, in septic Aedes aegypti fed on a 10^10 CFU/ml Csp_P sugar solution, 79% of mosquitoes were colonized by Csp_P at day 1, and by day 3, only 15% remained alive, all of which were negative for dengue virus. These findings confirm that Csp_P’s transmission-blocking effect is independent of antibiotic pretreatment and occurs in the context of a natural microbial environment. The consistency of the results across multiple biological replicates, with no variation in the magnitude of inhibition, suggests a highly reproducible and robust mechanism of action. The fact that even a small number of surviving mosquitoes were completely refractory to infection strongly supports the hypothesis that Csp_P induces a persistent, metabolite-mediated state of pathogen resistance that is not simply a byproduct of immune activation or physiological stress. These results demonstrate that Csp_P is capable of rendering mosquitoes incapable of transmitting either malaria or dengue, even under conditions of high pathogen exposure and variable survival, making it a uniquely effective transmission-blocking agent.

### Example 2: Live Chromobacterium Csp_P is a General Mosquitocidal, and Malaria and Dengue Transmission-Blocking Agent

The dual functionality of Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) as both a general mosquitocidal agent and a potent transmission-blocking agent was demonstrated through a series of comprehensive experiments evaluating its effects on mosquito survival, pathogen infection, and microbial competition. In larval exposure assays, groups of 10 Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti larvae were placed in 5 ml of distilled water supplemented with 50 µl of a Csp_P culture at an OD600 of 1.0 (approximately 10^8 CFU/ml). Control larvae were maintained in water containing an equivalent volume of PBS. Over a 72-hour observation period, 98% of Anopheles gambiae larvae and 95% of Aedes aegypti larvae exposed to Csp_P died, compared to less than 5% mortality in the control groups. The onset of mortality occurred within 24 hours for Aedes aegypti and within 48 hours for Anopheles gambiae, with no larvae surviving beyond day 3 in the Csp_P-treated groups. The mortality was rapid, uniform, and dose-dependent, with no recovery observed even in larvae that appeared initially unaffected. The effect was specific to Csp_P, as identical experiments with other mosquito-associated bacteria, including Enterobacter sp., Serratia sp., and Pantoea sp., resulted in no significant mortality. The larvicidal effect was maintained even when the Csp_P culture was filtered through a 0.2-µm membrane, indicating that the active component is a soluble metabolite rather than a living cell. The metabolite remained active after desiccation, as dried biofilm resuspended in water induced identical mortality rates, confirming the stability of the compound under field-relevant conditions.

In adult mosquito assays, Csp_P was delivered via sugar meals at concentrations ranging from 10^6 to 10^10 CFU/ml. At 10^8 CFU/ml, Anopheles gambiae exhibited 100% mortality within 48 hours, while Aedes aegypti required a higher concentration of 10^10 CFU/ml to achieve similar lethality. Survival curves showed a sharp decline in mortality within the first 24 to 48 hours, consistent with a rapid-acting toxin rather than a slow-acting pathogen. When Csp_P was delivered via blood meal, mortality occurred more rapidly and at lower bacterial concentrations, with 90% of Anopheles gambiae females dying within 24 hours of feeding on blood containing 10^6 CFU/ml. This suggests that the nutrient-rich environment of the blood meal enhances bacterial proliferation or metabolite production, leading to accelerated lethality. Importantly, the entomopathogenic effect was not observed in non-blood-feeding males, indicating a sex-specific susceptibility, likely due to differences in feeding behavior, physiology, or midgut environment. The bacterium did not induce mortality in non-target insects such as Drosophila melanogaster or Culex quinquefasciatus, suggesting a high degree of specificity for Anopheles and Aedes species.

The transmission-blocking capacity of Csp_P was confirmed in parallel experiments using the same mosquito cohorts. In Anopheles gambiae exposed to Csp_P via sugar meal and subsequently fed on Plasmodium falciparum-infected blood, the median oocyst count was reduced by 94% compared to controls, with 100% of surviving mosquitoes showing zero oocysts. In Aedes aegypti exposed to Csp_P and subsequently fed on DENV2-infected blood, infectious virus titers were reduced by 99.7%, with no detectable plaques in the majority of samples. The inhibition was observed regardless of whether Csp_P was introduced before or during the infectious blood meal, and the effect was consistent across multiple independent trials. The bacterium’s ability to block transmission was not attributable to immune activation, as transcriptomic analysis of midgut tissues showed only modest and inconsistent upregulation of immune genes such as cecropins and defensins, and the anti-pathogen activity was fully recapitulated in vitro using filtered biofilm supernatants in the absence of mosquito cells. Furthermore, the metabolites did not alter the susceptibility of host cells to infection, as demonstrated by pre-exposure assays in C6/36 and BHK-21 cell lines, confirming that the inhibition is direct and not mediated through host priming. The metabolites were also shown to be non-cytotoxic to mammalian and insect cells, ruling out general toxicity as a mechanism.

The broad-spectrum antibacterial activity of Csp_P was demonstrated by streaking the bacterium on LB agar plates and observing zones of inhibition around its growth that encompassed all other mosquito midgut isolates tested, including Chromobacterium violaceum, Enterobacter sp., Serratia sp., and Pseudomonas putida. This suggests that Csp_P can dominate the mosquito midgut microbiome, eliminating competitors and ensuring its own persistence. The antibacterial metabolites were heat-sensitive and filterable, indicating a small-molecule origin, and were identical to those responsible for anti-pathogen activity, suggesting a single class of compounds with dual functions. The strain’s ability to simultaneously kill mosquitoes, block pathogen transmission, and suppress competing microbes makes it a uniquely powerful agent for vector control. Unlike other biocontrol candidates that require genetic modification or complex delivery systems, Csp_P can be deployed using simple, low-cost sugar baits or water treatments, and its metabolites remain active for months in dried form. The combination of entomopathogenic, anti-pathogen, and antimicrobial properties in a single, naturally occurring strain represents a paradigm shift in vector control, offering a sustainable, scalable, and ecologically sound solution to two of the world’s most devastating diseases.

## CONCLUSION

The invention described herein represents a significant and transformative advancement in the field of vector-borne disease control. Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) is a novel bacterial strain that uniquely combines entomopathogenic, anti-pathogen, and antimicrobial properties in a single, naturally occurring organism. This strain is capable of rapidly colonizing the midgut of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, inducing high rates of mortality in both larval and adult stages, and simultaneously blocking the transmission of Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus through the production of stable, heat-sensitive, non-cytotoxic metabolites. These metabolites, produced during biofilm formation, act directly on pathogens in a manner independent of mosquito immune activation, host cell modulation, or physical sequestration, and retain their activity even after desiccation and filtration. The bacterium’s broad-spectrum antibacterial activity allows it to dominate the mosquito midgut microbiome, ensuring persistence and reducing competition from other microbes. The invention provides compositions comprising Csp_P in biofilm, supernatant, desiccated, or purified metabolite form, and methods for their administration via sugar baits, larval breeding site treatment, or topical application. The strain has been deposited under the Budapest Treaty and is available for research and commercial use. The invention offers a scalable, cost-effective, and environmentally sustainable alternative to chemical insecticides and genetically modified organisms, with the potential to drastically reduce the global burden of malaria and dengue fever. The dual-action mechanism—simultaneously suppressing vector populations and blocking pathogen transmission—makes Csp_P an unparalleled tool for integrated vector management, and its stability, specificity, and efficacy position it as a leading candidate for field deployment in endemic regions worldwide.
