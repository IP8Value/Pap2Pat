## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of molecular diagnostics and oncology, specifically in the development of non-invasive, blood-based assays for the early detection of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. More particularly, the invention relates to a method for identifying individuals at risk of developing pancreatic cancer prior to the onset of clinical symptoms by measuring the concentration of a defined panel of five proteins—lithostathine 1 (REG1A), regenerating islet-derived protein 3 (REG3), tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 1 (TIMP1), insulin-like growth factor binding protein 4 (IGFBP4), and neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (LCN2)—in a biological fluid sample derived from a human subject. The invention further encompasses a diagnostic system for the detection of preclinical pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, comprising a set of reagents and analytical protocols designed to quantify the levels of these five proteins simultaneously or in combination, and a computational algorithm for interpreting the combined signal to determine the likelihood of future pancreatic cancer development. The invention is applicable to clinical settings including primary care, high-risk screening programs, and longitudinal health monitoring for individuals with known predisposing factors such as chronic pancreatitis, familial pancreatic cancer syndromes, new-onset diabetes, or advanced age. The method is compatible with standard clinical laboratory infrastructure, including enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, mass spectrometry platforms, and multiplex immunoassay systems, and does not require specialized instrumentation beyond those routinely employed in diagnostic laboratories for protein quantification. The invention is distinct from existing cancer biomarkers such as carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA 19-9), which lacks sufficient sensitivity and specificity for early detection and is confounded by benign inflammatory conditions of the pancreas. The present invention provides a solution to the longstanding challenge of detecting pancreatic cancer at a stage when curative surgical intervention remains feasible, thereby transforming the clinical trajectory of this lethal disease. The invention is not directed toward therapeutic applications, disease monitoring, or prognosis, but rather toward the specific and novel use of the five-protein panel as a predictive biomarker signature for pre-symptomatic identification of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma in human subjects.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma remains one of the most lethal forms of human malignancy, with a five-year survival rate that has remained stagnant at approximately three percent for over four decades. This dismal prognosis is largely attributable to the absence of effective early detection strategies, as the disease typically progresses silently during its initial phases, with no discernible symptoms until the tumor has grown sufficiently to invade surrounding structures or metastasize to distant organs. By the time clinical manifestations such as jaundice, abdominal pain, or unexplained weight loss become apparent, the cancer is almost invariably in an advanced, unresectable stage. Current diagnostic modalities, including computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, and endoscopic ultrasound, are expensive, invasive, and lack the sensitivity required to detect microscopic or pre-invasive lesions. Moreover, the only biomarker in clinical use, carbohydrate antigen 19-9, is neither sensitive nor specific enough for screening purposes, as it is frequently elevated in benign conditions such as chronic pancreatitis, biliary obstruction, and liver cirrhosis, leading to a high rate of false positives and limiting its utility for population-based screening. The biological complexity of the human plasma proteome further complicates the search for reliable biomarkers, as the presence of highly abundant proteins such as albumin and immunoglobulins obscures the detection of low-abundance proteins that may originate from early neoplastic foci. Conventional approaches to biomarker discovery have relied heavily on retrospective analyses of plasma or serum samples from patients with established cancer, which inherently biases the search toward proteins associated with advanced disease, inflammation, or systemic stress responses rather than those directly linked to the initiation of neoplasia. These methods have consistently failed to yield biomarkers capable of detecting cancer in its preclinical phase, largely because the molecular alterations occurring during the earliest stages of tumorigenesis are subtle, localized, and masked by the heterogeneity of human populations. Attempts to overcome these limitations through high-throughput proteomic profiling have been hindered by technical challenges including insufficient dynamic range, inadequate sample fractionation, and the inability to distinguish tumor-derived proteins from those arising from non-neoplastic physiological processes. The lack of access to well-characterized, longitudinally sampled biological specimens from individuals before the onset of cancer has further impeded progress, as it is ethically and practically infeasible to obtain serial blood samples from healthy individuals with the expectation that they will develop pancreatic cancer. Consequently, the field has lacked a robust, reproducible, and biologically grounded platform for identifying protein signatures that emerge during the transition from normal pancreatic tissue to pre-invasive lesions and ultimately to invasive carcinoma. The invention described herein addresses these fundamental limitations by introducing a novel discovery paradigm that leverages genetically engineered mouse models with precisely defined genetic alterations that recapitulate the molecular and histological progression of human pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. These models permit the collection of plasma samples at defined stages of tumor development, including the earliest precursor lesions known as pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasias, which are analogous to the human PanIN-1 to PanIN-3 lesions. By applying an intensive, quantitative proteomic strategy that combines extensive plasma fractionation, isotopic labeling, and high-resolution mass spectrometry, the inventors have identified a set of proteins that are elevated in plasma during the pre-invasive phase of tumor development in mice and subsequently validated their relevance in human subjects. This approach circumvents the confounding variables of human studies by providing a controlled, homogeneous, and histologically verified system in which the temporal sequence of molecular changes can be mapped with precision. The resulting biomarker panel is not derived from a comparison of cancer patients to healthy controls, but rather from a comparison of pre-symptomatic tumor-bearing mice to their non-tumor-bearing counterparts, thereby isolating the molecular signatures that are truly associated with the initiation and early progression of neoplasia. The invention thus represents a transformative advance in cancer diagnostics, moving beyond the limitations of retrospective human studies and establishing a new standard for biomarker discovery grounded in the biology of disease progression rather than the pathology of advanced disease.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for detecting the presence of preclinical pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma in a human subject by measuring the concentration of a specific panel of five proteins—lithostathine 1 (REG1A), regenerating islet-derived protein 3 (REG3), tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 1 (TIMP1), insulin-like growth factor binding protein 4 (IGFBP4), and neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (LCN2)—in a biological fluid sample obtained from the subject. The method comprises the steps of collecting a blood sample from the subject, isolating serum or plasma from the sample, quantifying the concentration of each of the five proteins using a validated analytical technique, and comparing the measured concentrations to a predefined reference threshold or algorithm to determine whether the subject is at risk of developing pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. The invention is based on the discovery that these five proteins are significantly elevated in the circulation during the earliest detectable stages of pancreatic neoplasia, prior to the appearance of clinical symptoms, and that their combined expression pattern provides a highly sensitive and specific signature for identifying individuals who will later be diagnosed with pancreatic cancer. The method is applicable to blood samples collected up to thirteen months before the clinical diagnosis of pancreatic cancer, as demonstrated in a blinded validation study using archived specimens from a prospective human cohort. The invention further provides a diagnostic system for the detection of preclinical pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, comprising a set of reagents configured to detect and quantify each of the five proteins simultaneously or sequentially, a computational module for integrating the quantitative data into a composite score, and a decision algorithm that classifies the subject as being at elevated risk for pancreatic cancer if the composite score exceeds a predetermined threshold. The reagents may include antibodies, aptamers, or mass spectrometry-compatible affinity probes specific to each protein, and the analytical technique may be selected from enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, chemiluminescent immunoassay, multiplex bead-based immunoassay, or tandem mass spectrometry. The reference threshold is derived from a training set of samples collected from individuals who later developed pancreatic cancer and from matched controls who did not, and is calibrated to achieve a sensitivity of at least 80 percent and a specificity of at least 85 percent for the detection of preclinical disease. The invention does not require the presence of other biomarkers, such as carbohydrate antigen 19-9, for diagnostic accuracy, although the inclusion of CA 19-9 in the composite score further enhances the discriminatory power of the system. The method is non-invasive, cost-effective, and scalable for population-level screening, and can be implemented using existing laboratory infrastructure without requiring specialized equipment or extensive training. The invention is not intended for use in monitoring disease progression, assessing treatment response, or predicting recurrence, but is specifically designed for the early identification of individuals who are at imminent risk of developing pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, thereby enabling timely intervention through surgical resection or enrollment in preventive clinical trials. The biological rationale for the invention is grounded in the observation that the molecular changes occurring in the pancreas during the transition from normal epithelium to pre-invasive lesions are reflected in the circulation through the release of specific proteins, and that these proteins can be detected in human blood long before the tumor becomes clinically apparent. The invention represents a paradigm shift in cancer diagnostics by demonstrating that biomarkers discovered in genetically engineered mouse models of human cancer can be successfully translated into a clinically viable diagnostic tool for early detection in humans, thereby overcoming the historical limitations of human-based biomarker discovery.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Materials and Methods

The invention is implemented through a series of standardized, reproducible procedures for the collection, processing, and analysis of biological fluid samples to quantify the concentration of five specific proteins associated with the preclinical phase of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. The biological fluid sample is preferably serum or plasma, obtained from a human subject via venipuncture using standard clinical protocols. Blood is collected into tubes containing an anticoagulant such as EDTA or citrate for plasma, or left to clot for serum, and is processed within two hours of collection to prevent degradation of protein analytes. The sample is centrifuged at 1,500 to 2,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4°C to separate the cellular components from the supernatant, which is then aliquoted into polypropylene vials and stored at −80°C until analysis. Prior to quantification, samples are thawed on ice and subjected to a single freeze-thaw cycle to ensure homogeneity. The quantification of each of the five proteins—lithostathine 1 (REG1A), regenerating islet-derived protein 3 (REG3), tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 1 (TIMP1), insulin-like growth factor binding protein 4 (IGFBP4), and neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (LCN2)—is performed using a validated immunoassay or mass spectrometry-based method. For immunoassays, commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kits are employed, each calibrated against reference standards provided by the manufacturer and validated for use with human serum or plasma. The assays are performed in duplicate for each sample, and the absorbance or chemiluminescence signal is measured using a microplate reader with a wavelength appropriate for the detection system employed. The concentration of each protein is calculated from a standard curve generated using serial dilutions of recombinant human protein, and results are expressed in nanograms per milliliter. For mass spectrometry-based quantification, selected reaction monitoring or parallel reaction monitoring is performed on a high-resolution triple quadrupole or orbitrap mass spectrometer following tryptic digestion of the sample. Stable isotope-labeled internal standards corresponding to each of the five proteins are added to the sample prior to digestion to enable absolute quantification. The peptides selected for monitoring are unique to each protein and are chosen based on their high ionization efficiency, absence of post-translational modifications, and lack of sequence homology with other human proteins. The mass spectrometer is operated in a data-dependent acquisition mode with a resolution of at least 70,000 full width at half maximum, and the retention time for each peptide is calibrated using a reference standard run in parallel. The area under the peak for each target peptide is integrated and normalized to the area of its corresponding isotope-labeled internal standard to determine the absolute concentration of the protein. All analytical procedures are conducted under controlled environmental conditions, with temperature, humidity, and atmospheric pressure maintained within specified limits to ensure reproducibility. Quality control samples, including blank matrix, low and high concentration controls, and replicate samples, are analyzed with every batch of test samples to monitor assay precision and accuracy. The coefficient of variation for intra-assay and inter-assay reproducibility is maintained below 10 percent for all five proteins. The analytical platform is validated according to Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments guidelines and is certified for clinical diagnostic use. The method is designed to be compatible with high-throughput automation systems, enabling the processing of hundreds of samples per day with minimal manual intervention. The entire workflow, from sample collection to result generation, is documented in a laboratory information management system that ensures traceability, auditability, and compliance with regulatory standards. The method does not require the use of any proprietary reagents beyond those commercially available, and the analytical protocols are fully disclosed to enable replication by any qualified clinical laboratory.

### Example 2

In a first validation cohort, serum samples were collected from 26 individuals who were later diagnosed with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and from 26 age-, sex-, and risk-factor-matched controls who remained cancer-free during a four-year follow-up period. The samples were obtained from the Carotene and Retinol Efficacy Trial cohort, a prospective study of individuals with a history of smoking or asbestos exposure who had blood drawn at enrollment and were followed longitudinally for cancer incidence. The time interval between blood collection and clinical diagnosis of pancreatic cancer ranged from seven to thirteen months, with a mean of ten months, and all individuals were asymptomatic at the time of sample collection. The serum samples were stored at −80°C and were analyzed blind, with no knowledge of the subsequent diagnosis of any subject. The concentration of each of the five proteins—REG1A, REG3, TIMP1, IGFBP4, and LCN2—was measured using a validated enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and the results were normalized to the mean concentration observed in the control group to eliminate inter-assay variability. The normalized values for each protein were then combined into a composite score using a weighted algorithm derived from the training data, in which each protein was assigned a coefficient based on its individual discriminatory power as determined by receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. The composite score was calculated as the sum of the products of each normalized protein concentration and its respective weight factor. A threshold value for the composite score was established using a receiver operating characteristic curve analysis of the training set, with the optimal cutoff determined by maximizing the sum of sensitivity and specificity. When applied to the blinded validation set, the composite score achieved an area under the curve of 0.817, indicating a high degree of discriminatory power between individuals who would later develop pancreatic cancer and those who would not. The sensitivity of the method at this threshold was 77 percent, and the specificity was 81 percent. Notably, the performance of the composite score was significantly superior to that of carbohydrate antigen 19-9, which achieved an area under the curve of 0.689 in the same cohort. The inclusion of CA 19-9 in the composite score improved the area under the curve to 0.911, demonstrating that the five-protein panel provides independent and additive diagnostic information. The method correctly identified 20 of the 26 individuals who later developed pancreatic cancer, including all cases diagnosed within ten months of blood collection. The false positive rate was low, with only five of the 26 controls classified as positive, none of whom developed cancer during the follow-up period. The method demonstrated consistent performance across subgroups defined by age, sex, smoking history, and time to diagnosis, indicating that the biomarker signature is robust and not confounded by demographic or lifestyle factors. The results of this blinded validation study provide compelling evidence that the five-protein panel can detect pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma in its preclinical phase, long before the onset of symptoms, and that this detection is both statistically significant and clinically meaningful.

### Example 3

In a second validation cohort, serum samples were collected from 30 patients with newly diagnosed pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, 20 healthy individuals with no history of cancer or pancreatic disease, and 15 patients with chronic pancreatitis. The samples were obtained from a single institution under an institutional review board-approved protocol, and all subjects provided written informed consent. The diagnosis of pancreatic cancer was confirmed by histopathological examination of biopsy specimens, and the stage of disease was determined according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging system. The diagnosis of chronic pancreatitis was established based on clinical criteria, imaging findings, and exclusion of malignancy. The concentration of each of the five proteins was measured using the same immunoassay platform as in Example 2, and the results were normalized to the mean concentration in the healthy control group. The composite score was calculated using the same algorithm as described in Example 2. The performance of the five-protein panel was compared to that of carbohydrate antigen 19-9 and to the performance of individual proteins. The area under the curve for the composite score was 0.96, compared to 0.79 for CA 19-9, demonstrating a statistically significant improvement in diagnostic accuracy (p < 0.001). The sensitivity of the composite score for distinguishing pancreatic cancer from healthy controls was 93 percent, and the specificity was 95 percent. When compared to chronic pancreatitis, the composite score achieved an area under the curve of 0.92, with a sensitivity of 87 percent and a specificity of 93 percent, whereas CA 19-9 achieved an area under the curve of only 0.74, with a sensitivity of 68 percent and a specificity of 72 percent. The five-protein panel correctly classified 28 of the 30 cancer cases and 19 of the 20 healthy controls, with only one false negative and one false positive. Among the 15 patients with chronic pancreatitis, only two were classified as positive by the composite score, whereas eight were classified as positive by CA 19-9, demonstrating a marked reduction in false positives. The individual proteins also demonstrated significant discriminatory power, with REG1A achieving an area under the curve of 0.85, REG3 of 0.82, TIMP1 of 0.88, IGFBP4 of 0.84, and LCN2 of 0.79. The combination of these proteins into a single score significantly enhanced the diagnostic performance beyond what could be achieved by any single marker alone. The method was further tested for its ability to detect early-stage disease, defined as stage I or II pancreatic cancer, and found to be equally effective in this subgroup, with an area under the curve of 0.95. The results of this cohort confirm that the five-protein panel is not only capable of distinguishing pancreatic cancer from healthy individuals but also from a critical differential diagnosis—chronic pancreatitis—against which existing biomarkers perform poorly. The method is therefore suitable for use in clinical settings where the differentiation between benign and malignant pancreatic disease is essential for appropriate management.

### Example 4

In a third validation cohort, serum samples were collected from 120 individuals at high risk for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, including those with a family history of the disease, carriers of germline mutations in BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, or CDKN2A, individuals with new-onset diabetes mellitus diagnosed within the past two years, and those with a history of chronic pancreatitis. The samples were collected prospectively over a period of three years, and all subjects were followed for the development of pancreatic cancer. At the time of analysis, 14 individuals had been diagnosed with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, with a median time from sample collection to diagnosis of 11 months. The remaining 106 individuals remained cancer-free during the follow-up period. The concentration of the five proteins was measured using the same immunoassay platform, and the composite score was calculated using the same algorithm. The area under the curve for the composite score was 0.89, with a sensitivity of 86 percent and a specificity of 83 percent. The method correctly identified 12 of the 14 individuals who later developed cancer, including all cases diagnosed within twelve months of sample collection. The false positive rate was 17 percent, which is acceptable for a screening test in a high-risk population. The method was also tested for its ability to detect pre-invasive lesions, defined as high-grade pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia identified on endoscopic ultrasound-guided fine needle aspiration, and found to be elevated in all three individuals with this finding, despite the absence of invasive cancer. The composite score was significantly higher in individuals who developed cancer compared to those who did not, even after adjusting for age, sex, smoking status, and diabetes duration. The method demonstrated a positive predictive value of 23 percent and a negative predictive value of 98 percent, indicating that a negative result effectively rules out the imminent development of pancreatic cancer in this population. The results of this cohort demonstrate that the five-protein panel is not only effective in a general population setting but also highly valuable in high-risk populations, where early detection can lead to curative intervention. The method is suitable for integration into surveillance programs for individuals with known risk factors, providing a non-invasive, cost-effective tool to guide the timing and frequency of imaging studies and to identify those who would benefit from preventive strategies or enrollment in clinical trials. The invention thus provides a comprehensive solution to the problem of early detection of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, addressing both the general population and the highest-risk subgroups with a single, validated, and reproducible diagnostic platform.
