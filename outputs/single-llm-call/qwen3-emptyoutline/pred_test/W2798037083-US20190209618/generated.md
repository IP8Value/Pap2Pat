# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell biology, specifically to methods and compositions for the ex vivo expansion of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells through the transcriptional activation of the Musashi-2 gene by the cooperative action of the transcription factors USF2 and PLAG1. The invention further encompasses the use of these methods to enhance the therapeutic potential of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells for transplantation, regenerative medicine, and the treatment of hematological disorders. Additionally, the invention provides novel compositions comprising expanded hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, as well as methods for culturing leukemic cells by modulating the expression of USF2 and PLAG1, thereby enabling the study of leukemogenesis and the screening of therapeutic agents targeting hematopoietic malignancies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) are a rare population of multipotent cells capable of self-renewal and differentiation into all lineages of mature blood cells. Their unique biological properties make them indispensable for the treatment of a wide array of hematological diseases, including leukemia, lymphoma, aplastic anemia, and inherited metabolic disorders. The clinical utility of HSPCs is largely dependent on their availability in sufficient quantities, particularly in the context of umbilical cord blood transplantation, where the limited cell dose often restricts their use in adult recipients. Despite decades of research, the ex vivo expansion of functional, long-term repopulating HSPCs has remained a persistent challenge in translational hematology. Conventional culture systems that promote proliferation frequently result in the loss of stemness, leading to the exhaustion of repopulating capacity and the dominance of differentiated progeny.

Efforts to overcome this limitation have focused on identifying molecular regulators that maintain the balance between self-renewal and differentiation. Among the most promising candidates is the RNA-binding protein Musashi-2 (MSI2), which has been shown to be highly expressed in primitive HSPCs and to decline as cells commit to lineage-specific differentiation. Functional studies have demonstrated that MSI2 is essential for the maintenance of HSPC reconstitution potential in vivo and that its overexpression enhances the ex vivo expansion of human cord blood-derived HSPCs. Mechanistically, MSI2 exerts its effects by post-transcriptionally repressing key differentiation-promoting genes, including CYP1B1, a cytochrome P450 enzyme implicated in the metabolism of endogenous signaling molecules that influence stem cell fate. However, while the downstream effects of MSI2 are increasingly understood, the upstream transcriptional mechanisms that govern its expression in human HSPCs have remained largely obscure.

Prior investigations into the transcriptional regulation of MSI2 have been limited, with no comprehensive identification of transcription factors capable of directly activating its promoter in a physiologically relevant context. Although certain transcription factors have been associated with hematopoietic stem cell maintenance—such as HOXB4, NOTCH1, and GATA2—none have been shown to directly and specifically regulate MSI2 expression in human HSPCs. Furthermore, the absence of a defined transcriptional regulatory network controlling MSI2 has hindered the development of targeted strategies to enhance its expression without the need for genetic manipulation or viral transduction. The discovery of novel transcriptional activators of MSI2 would therefore represent a significant advance in the field, offering a precise and potentially pharmacologically tractable means to expand HSPCs for clinical applications.

In parallel, the role of MSI2 in leukemogenesis has garnered increasing attention. Aberrantly elevated levels of MSI2 have been documented in aggressive subtypes of acute myeloid leukemia, chronic myeloid leukemia, and myelodysplastic syndromes, where it contributes to the maintenance of leukemic stem cells and the suppression of differentiation. The identification of upstream regulators of MSI2 in this context may therefore not only facilitate the expansion of healthy HSPCs but also provide novel therapeutic targets for the selective inhibition of leukemic stem cells. To date, no small molecules or biologics have been developed that specifically target the transcriptional activation of MSI2, and the absence of such agents underscores a critical gap in the therapeutic landscape for hematological malignancies.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by uncovering a previously unrecognized transcriptional regulatory axis composed of two transcription factors—USF2 and PLAG1—that act in concert to drive the expression of MSI2 in human HSPCs. Through systematic promoter dissection, mutagenesis, chromatin immunoprecipitation, and functional validation, it has been demonstrated that USF2 and PLAG1 bind cooperatively to a conserved minimal promoter region upstream of the MSI2 gene, and that their co-expression results in a synergistic increase in MSI2 transcription, leading to the robust expansion of primitive CD34+ HSPCs in culture. This discovery reveals a novel mechanism by which stem cell fate is controlled at the transcriptional level and provides the foundation for a new class of interventions aimed at enhancing HSPC expansion for transplantation and suppressing leukemic stem cell survival through targeted modulation of this axis.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for the ex vivo expansion of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells by increasing the transcriptional activity of the Musashi-2 gene through the simultaneous activation of the transcription factors USF2 and PLAG1. The method comprises culturing hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells in the presence of a composition that enhances the expression or activity of both USF2 and PLAG1, thereby inducing a synergistic upregulation of MSI2 and resulting in a significant increase in the number of primitive, CD34-positive cells without loss of long-term repopulating potential. The invention further encompasses the use of recombinant nucleic acid constructs, viral vectors, or small molecule modulators to elevate the intracellular levels of USF2 and PLAG1 in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, thereby promoting their expansion under defined culture conditions.

The invention further provides a composition comprising hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells that have been expanded ex vivo by the method described herein, wherein the expanded cells exhibit increased expression of MSI2, reduced expression of CYP1B1, and enhanced engraftment potential in immunodeficient mouse models. These expanded cells are suitable for transplantation into subjects requiring hematopoietic reconstitution, including patients with hematological malignancies, bone marrow failure syndromes, or inherited blood disorders.

The invention further provides a method for inhibiting the proliferation of leukemic cells by reducing the expression or activity of USF2 and PLAG1, thereby downregulating MSI2 expression and promoting differentiation or apoptosis of leukemic stem cells. This method may be employed in the development of therapeutic agents that target the USF2-PLAG1-MSI2 axis to selectively eliminate leukemic stem cells while sparing normal hematopoietic stem cells.

The invention further provides a method for identifying compounds that modulate the transcriptional activity of the MSI2 promoter by screening for agents that enhance or inhibit the binding of USF2 and PLAG1 to their respective consensus sequences within the minimal promoter region. Such screening methods may be implemented in high-throughput formats using reporter gene assays, chromatin immunoprecipitation-based platforms, or cell-based phenotypic screens to identify novel small molecules, peptides, or oligonucleotides capable of modulating the USF2-PLAG1 interaction or their binding to the MSI2 promoter.

The invention further provides a method for isolating and enriching hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells based on the co-expression of USF2 and PLAG1, wherein cells exhibiting high levels of both transcription factors are enriched for primitive, multipotent, and self-renewing properties. This method enables the purification of a highly potent HSPC subpopulation without reliance on surface markers alone, thereby improving the quality and consistency of cell products for clinical use.

The invention further provides a method for predicting the clinical efficacy of hematopoietic stem cell grafts by measuring the endogenous expression levels of USF2 and PLAG1 in donor-derived HSPCs, wherein higher co-expression correlates with greater ex vivo expansion potential and improved engraftment outcomes in transplant recipients.

The invention further provides a method for enhancing the survival and engraftment of transplanted hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells by pre-treating the cells with a composition that upregulates USF2 and PLAG1 prior to transplantation, thereby priming the cells for enhanced self-renewal and resistance to differentiation-inducing signals in the recipient microenvironment.

The invention further provides a method for generating induced pluripotent stem cell-derived hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells with enhanced repopulating capacity by introducing exogenous USF2 and PLAG1 during the differentiation protocol, thereby recapitulating the endogenous transcriptional program that governs primitive hematopoietic identity.

The invention further provides a method for studying the molecular mechanisms of leukemogenesis by culturing leukemic cell lines or primary leukemic blasts under conditions that modulate the expression of USF2 and PLAG1, thereby enabling the identification of downstream effectors and vulnerabilities specific to leukemic stem cells.

The invention further provides a method for screening therapeutic agents for their ability to selectively inhibit leukemic stem cells by assessing their impact on the USF2-PLAG1-MSI2 axis, wherein agents that suppress this axis are identified as potential candidates for the treatment of hematological malignancies.

The invention further provides a method for producing a therapeutic cell product enriched for hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells by combining the expansion method described herein with a purification step that selects for cells expressing high levels of CD34 and low levels of lineage commitment markers, wherein the resulting product exhibits superior engraftment kinetics and multilineage reconstitution capacity compared to conventional HSPC products.

The invention further provides a method for maintaining the quiescent state of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells during ex vivo culture by transiently modulating the expression of USF2 and PLAG1 to achieve a controlled, non-exhaustive expansion profile, thereby preserving the long-term repopulating potential of the cells for serial transplantation.

The invention further provides a method for improving the homing efficiency of transplanted hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells by modulating the expression of USF2 and PLAG1 prior to transplantation, wherein the resulting cells exhibit enhanced expression of homing receptors such as CXCR4 and CD44, leading to improved migration to the bone marrow niche.

The invention further provides a method for generating a library of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell clones with defined levels of USF2 and PLAG1 expression for functional genomics studies, wherein each clone serves as a model system for dissecting the downstream transcriptional and epigenetic networks controlled by this axis.

The invention further provides a method for identifying biomarkers of hematopoietic stem cell potency by correlating the expression levels of USF2 and PLAG1 with functional outcomes such as colony-forming unit frequency, long-term culture-initiating cell activity, and in vivo engraftment potential.

The invention further provides a method for optimizing culture media formulations for hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell expansion by incorporating factors that stabilize or enhance the activity of USF2 and PLAG1, including cytokines, metabolites, or epigenetic modulators that promote their nuclear localization, DNA binding, or transcriptional activation.

The invention further provides a method for preventing the differentiation of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells during ex vivo culture by co-administering agents that sustain the expression of USF2 and PLAG1, thereby maintaining the cells in a primitive, undifferentiated state for extended periods without loss of functionality.

The invention further provides a method for enhancing the engraftment of gene-edited hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells by transiently upregulating USF2 and PLAG1 following gene modification, thereby counteracting the differentiation pressure induced by the editing process and improving the recovery of edited, functional stem cells.

The invention further provides a method for producing a cryopreserved cell product comprising expanded hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells generated by the method described herein, wherein the cells retain their expanded phenotype and functional potential following thawing and transplantation.

The invention further provides a method for monitoring the stability of the USF2-PLAG1-MSI2 axis during ex vivo culture by periodic assessment of transcription factor binding, MSI2 mRNA levels, and CYP1B1 protein expression, wherein deviations from the expected profile are used to adjust culture conditions in real time to optimize cell expansion.

The invention further provides a method for identifying patient-specific responses to HSPC expansion protocols by measuring baseline expression levels of USF2 and PLAG1 in donor-derived cells, wherein patients with low co-expression are identified as candidates for adjunctive therapy to enhance the axis prior to expansion.

The invention further provides a method for generating a standardized, clinically compliant cell product for allogeneic transplantation by using a defined, serum-free culture system supplemented with recombinant USF2 and PLAG1 activators to achieve consistent and reproducible expansion of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells across multiple donors.

The invention further provides a method for reducing the incidence of graft failure in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation by preconditioning the recipient with agents that upregulate USF2 and PLAG1 in the bone marrow niche, thereby creating a more receptive microenvironment for the engraftment of transplanted HSPCs.

The invention further provides a method for expanding hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells derived from induced pluripotent stem cells by introducing exogenous USF2 and PLAG1 during the hematopoietic specification phase, thereby enhancing the yield and quality of functional HSPCs for regenerative applications.

The invention further provides a method for identifying novel transcriptional co-factors of USF2 and PLAG1 by performing proteomic analyses of complexes formed at the MSI2 promoter, wherein the identified co-factors serve as additional targets for the modulation of HSPC expansion or leukemic cell suppression.

The invention further provides a method for predicting the risk of leukemic transformation in patients with myelodysplastic syndromes or clonal hematopoiesis by monitoring the expression levels of USF2 and PLAG1 in peripheral blood or bone marrow cells, wherein elevated co-expression correlates with increased risk of progression to acute myeloid leukemia.

The invention further provides a method for enhancing the therapeutic index of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation by combining the expansion of HSPCs via USF2 and PLAG1 activation with the selective depletion of residual leukemic cells through inhibition of the same axis, thereby achieving a dual benefit of increasing graft potency while reducing disease burden.

The invention further provides a method for generating a genetically engineered hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell line with constitutive co-expression of USF2 and PLAG1 for use as a standardized model system for drug screening, mechanistic studies, and cell therapy development.
