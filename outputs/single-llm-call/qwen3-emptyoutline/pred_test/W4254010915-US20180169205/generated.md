# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to novel vaccine platforms designed to elicit robust humoral immune responses in the absence of conventional T helper cell support. Specifically, the invention concerns a class of engineered polymeric protein scaffolds derived from bacterial flagellin, wherein a solvent-exposed domain of the flagellin monomer is replaced with a structurally intact, conformationally preserved antigenic domain from a pathogenic virus. These engineered filaments are capable of directly crosslinking B cell receptors on the surface of B lymphocytes in a manner that bypasses the requirement for cognate T cell engagement, thereby enabling the induction of T cell-independent antibody responses. The invention further encompasses methods of producing such filaments, their purification, characterization, and use in immunization regimens for the prevention or mitigation of infectious diseases, particularly in immunocompromised populations where T cell function is impaired or absent. This platform represents a paradigm shift in subunit vaccine design by leveraging the intrinsic multivalent architecture of bacterial flagellin polymers to present repetitive antigenic epitopes at spatially optimal distances for B cell receptor clustering, a key initiating event in T cell-independent immunity.

### Related Art

Traditional vaccine strategies have long relied on the coordinated interplay between antigen-presenting cells, T helper cells, and B lymphocytes to generate high-affinity, class-switched antibody responses. This T cell-dependent (TD) pathway involves the processing and presentation of antigenic peptides by dendritic cells or B cells to CD4+ T cells via MHC class II molecules, followed by the delivery of co-stimulatory signals and cytokines that drive B cell proliferation, somatic hypermutation, class switch recombination, and differentiation into long-lived plasma cells and memory B cells. While highly effective in immunocompetent individuals, this mechanism is critically compromised in populations with T cell deficiencies, including individuals with HIV/AIDS, patients undergoing chemotherapy or radiation therapy, those with genetic immunodeficiencies such as DiGeorge syndrome, neonates with immature adaptive immune systems, and the elderly, whose T cell repertoire and function decline with age. In these settings, conventional subunit vaccines—often composed of purified proteins or peptides—fail to elicit protective antibody titers due to the absence of T cell help, leaving vulnerable populations at heightened risk of morbidity and mortality from infectious agents.

To circumvent this limitation, researchers have explored T cell-independent (TI) antigens, which are capable of directly activating B cells without T cell assistance. TI antigens are typically characterized by highly repetitive, polymeric structures that enable the crosslinking of multiple B cell receptors (BCRs) on the surface of B lymphocytes. This clustering event triggers intracellular signaling cascades sufficient to initiate B cell activation, proliferation, and differentiation into antibody-secreting plasma cells, predominantly producing IgM and certain IgG subclasses, particularly IgG3 and IgG1, which are hallmarks of TI responses. Historically, TI antigens have included bacterial polysaccharides such as those found in the capsules of *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Neisseria meningitidis*, which have been successfully conjugated to carrier proteins to enhance immunogenicity in infants and the elderly. However, these approaches are limited to carbohydrate antigens and require complex chemical conjugation techniques that are costly, difficult to standardize, and often result in heterogeneous products. Moreover, the immune response to polysaccharide antigens is typically non-memory, short-lived, and poorly responsive in young children and immunocompromised individuals.

More recently, efforts have been made to engineer protein-based TI antigens by fusing repetitive epitopes to synthetic carriers such as virus-like particles (VLPs), bacteriophage capsids, or synthetic polymers. While these systems have demonstrated some success in presenting multivalent epitopes, they often suffer from structural instability, limited antigen density, poor solubility, or an inability to retain the native conformation of complex protein antigens, particularly those with intricate disulfide-bonded folds such as viral envelope glycoproteins. Furthermore, many of these platforms lack intrinsic adjuvant properties, necessitating the co-administration of exogenous adjuvants such as alum, CpG oligonucleotides, or oil-in-water emulsions, which may introduce unwanted inflammatory side effects or fail to adequately stimulate innate immune pathways in immunodeficient hosts.

Flagellin, the primary structural subunit of bacterial flagella, has long been recognized for its potent immunostimulatory activity due to its ability to engage Toll-like receptor 5 (TLR5) and the NLRC4 inflammasome, both of which trigger robust innate immune activation. Prior studies have demonstrated that monomeric flagellin can serve as an effective adjuvant when fused to heterologous antigens, enhancing T cell-dependent responses through dendritic cell maturation and cytokine induction. However, these approaches have not capitalized on the natural polymeric architecture of flagellin, which, in its native form, assembles into filaments exceeding 10 micrometers in length and exhibiting a helical symmetry with approximately 11 protofilaments. The spatial arrangement of domains within these filaments results in the exposure of specific regions on the filament surface, notably the D3 domain, which is fully solvent-accessible and positioned at regular intervals of approximately 5 nanometers along the filament axis. This spacing is ideal for BCR crosslinking, as it falls within the empirically determined range of 5–10 nanometers required to trigger a TI response. Despite this, prior art has not recognized or exploited the D3 domain as a viable insertion site for foreign antigens while preserving the filamentous structure necessary for TI activation. One study suggested that polymeric flagellin required T cell help to elicit a humoral response, but this conclusion was based on an incomplete analysis of antibody isotypes, specifically omitting IgM and IgG3, the canonical markers of TI immunity. Consequently, the potential of flagellin filaments as a self-adjuvanting, T cell-independent vaccine scaffold remained unexplored and unrealized.

Moreover, prior attempts to engineer flagellin-based vaccines have focused on N- or C-terminal fusions, which often disrupt the structural integrity of the flagellin polymer or bury the inserted antigen within the filament core, rendering it inaccessible to B cell receptors. Other modifications have involved the deletion of entire domains without sufficient structural validation, leading to misfolded or non-polymerizing proteins. There has been no prior disclosure of a flagellin-based construct in which a central, solvent-exposed domain is replaced with a full-length, conformationally intact viral envelope protein, bridged by flexible linkers that preserve the independent folding of both the flagellin scaffold and the inserted antigen, while simultaneously enabling the formation of stable, high-molecular-weight filaments capable of inducing TI responses. Furthermore, no prior art has demonstrated the capacity of such a construct to elicit a measurable IgM and IgG3 response in a T cell receptor transgenic mouse model in which nearly all CD4+ T cells are restricted to a single, irrelevant antigen, thereby eliminating the possibility of cognate T cell help. The present invention fills this critical gap by providing a novel, structurally defined, and functionally validated platform that combines the intrinsic adjuvant properties of flagellin with the multivalent antigen presentation capability of its polymerized form, enabling the generation of potent, T cell-independent antibody responses against complex protein antigens.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel class of immunogenic compositions comprising engineered flagellin-based polymeric filaments that are capable of eliciting T cell-independent antibody responses in the absence of conventional T helper cell support. These compositions are formed by the replacement of a solvent-exposed domain of the flagellin monomer with a structurally intact, conformationally preserved antigenic domain derived from a pathogenic virus, wherein the antigenic domain is flanked by flexible peptide linkers that permit independent folding and surface exposure of the antigen within the assembled filament. The resulting hybrid flagellin proteins self-assemble into stable, high-molecular-weight filaments that display the inserted antigenic domains at a spatial density and orientation sufficient to induce crosslinking of B cell receptors on the surface of B lymphocytes, thereby triggering intracellular signaling pathways that lead to B cell activation, proliferation, and differentiation into antibody-secreting plasma cells without the requirement for cognate T cell engagement. The invention further encompasses methods for the recombinant production, purification, and characterization of these hybrid flagellin filaments, as well as their use in immunization protocols for the prevention or treatment of infectious diseases, particularly in immunocompromised populations where T cell function is impaired or absent.

In one embodiment, the flagellin protein is derived from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium and comprises four distinct structural domains: D0, D1, D2, and D3. The D3 domain, which spans residues 185 to 285 and is fully exposed on the surface of the assembled flagellin filament, is deleted and replaced with a full-length antigenic domain from the envelope glycoprotein of Dengue virus serotype 2, which is known to mediate viral entry and is a primary target of neutralizing antibodies. The insertion is flanked at both the N-terminal and C-terminal junctions by Gly-Ser-rich flexible linkers of the sequence GGGSGGGS, which provide conformational flexibility and prevent steric interference between the flagellin scaffold and the inserted antigen. The resulting hybrid protein, designated hFliC, retains the ability to polymerize into filaments under physiological conditions, as confirmed by negative stain transmission electron microscopy, which reveals filaments with a diameter of approximately 35 nanometers, significantly larger than the 20-nanometer diameter of wild-type flagellin filaments, consistent with the predicted structural model incorporating the inserted antigenic domain. Circular dichroism spectroscopy demonstrates that the secondary structure of both the flagellin scaffold and the inserted DENV2 E domain are preserved in the hybrid protein, with the hFliC molecule exhibiting a hybrid spectrum of α-helical and β-sheet content corresponding to the respective parent proteins. The antigenic integrity of the inserted DENV2 E domain is confirmed by binding assays using monoclonal antibodies specific for conformational epitopes on the DENV2 E protein, including the 4G2 antibody, which recognizes a fusion-loop epitope in domain II of the E protein. Immunogold labeling experiments using gold-conjugated 4G2 antibody demonstrate that the DENV2 E domain is positioned on the outer surface of the hFliC filament, accessible for B cell receptor engagement.

The hFliC filaments are capable of directly crosslinking B cell receptors in vitro, as demonstrated by confocal microscopy of the 4G2 B cell hybridoma, which shows a dramatic reorganization of surface immunoglobulin receptors into a circumferential pattern upon exposure to hFliC filaments, but not upon exposure to wild-type flagellin or control proteins. This reorganization is confirmed by co-staining with anti-His-tag antibodies, which bind to the C-terminal hexahistidine tag present on the hFliC filament, indicating direct physical interaction between the filament and the B cell receptor. In vivo, administration of hFliC filaments via intraperitoneal or intranasal routes to wild-type mice induces robust serum antibody responses, including elevated titers of IgM, IgG1, IgG2a, and IgG2b, with the route of administration influencing the isotype profile. Intraperitoneal priming followed by intranasal boosters results in the strongest IgM and IgG responses, while intranasal priming and boosting predominantly elicits IgA responses, indicating tissue-specific immune polarization.

Critically, the hFliC filaments elicit a potent humoral immune response in a T cell receptor transgenic mouse model (6.5-TCR) in which nearly all CD4+ T cells express a T cell receptor specific for an influenza hemagglutinin peptide presented on MHC class II, rendering them incapable of providing cognate help to B cells recognizing flagellin or DENV2 antigens. In this model, mice immunized with hFliC filaments via intraperitoneal injection exhibit a significant serum IgM response comparable to that observed in wild-type control mice, and, most notably, a robust IgG3 response that is absent in mice immunized with monomeric hFliC or wild-type flagellin. The IgG3 subclass is a well-established marker of T cell-independent immunity, and its induction in the absence of functional T helper cells provides definitive evidence that the hFliC filaments are capable of initiating a true TI response. No IgA response is detected in this model, consistent with the known requirement for T cell help in mucosal IgA class switching. The absence of IgG1 and IgG2a responses in the 6.5-TCR mice further confirms that the IgG3 response is not the result of residual T cell activity. These findings establish that the hFliC filament is not merely a highly immunogenic antigen but a true T cell-independent vaccine platform capable of bypassing the need for T cell help entirely.

The invention further encompasses methods for the scalable production of hFliC filaments using a baculovirus expression system in insect cells, wherein the hybrid protein is secreted into the culture supernatant and purified via affinity chromatography using a C-terminal hexahistidine tag, followed by polymerization induced by high concentrations of ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate. The resulting filaments are stable, soluble, and amenable to formulation in aqueous buffers without the need for additional adjuvants. The invention also includes methods of immunization comprising the administration of the hFliC filaments via intraperitoneal, intranasal, subcutaneous, or intramuscular routes, with or without booster doses, to induce protective antibody titers against the inserted antigen. The invention further provides for the use of the hFliC platform to insert antigenic domains from other pathogens, including other Dengue serotypes, Zika virus, influenza hemagglutinin, SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, or HIV envelope glycoproteins, thereby enabling the development of multivalent or universal vaccines. The ability to produce mixed filaments containing stoichiometric proportions of hFliC variants, each displaying a different antigenic domain, allows for the simultaneous induction of broad, balanced immune responses against multiple strains or serotypes, overcoming the immunodominance issues observed in previous tetravalent vaccine candidates. The invention thus provides a fundamentally new approach to vaccine design that is broadly applicable to infectious diseases for which T cell-dependent vaccines have failed, particularly in vulnerable populations with compromised adaptive immunity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1

The hybrid flagellin protein, designated hFliC, was engineered by replacing the D3 domain of *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium flagellin with the full-length envelope glycoprotein (E) from Dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2). The D3 domain, which spans amino acid residues 185 to 285, was selected for deletion based on its location within the flagellin monomer, where it is fully solvent-exposed in the assembled filament and contributes minimally to the structural integrity of the polymer. The deletion was performed using overlapping PCR techniques to amplify two fragments of the flagellin gene: the N-terminal portion encompassing residues 1 to 184 and the C-terminal portion encompassing residues 286 to 494, which includes the native C-terminal hexahistidine tag used for purification. These fragments were fused together using a synthetic linker sequence encoding two copies of the flexible Gly-Ser motif GGGSGGGS, which were inserted at both the N-terminal and C-terminal junctions of the DENV2 E domain to ensure that the inserted antigen could fold independently without steric interference from the flagellin scaffold. The DENV2 E domain, derived from a mouse-adapted strain, was amplified from a cDNA template and inserted between the two linker sequences, resulting in a chimeric gene construct with the following domain architecture: N-terminal D0-D1-D2 domain of flagellin — Gly-Ser linker — DENV2 E domain — Gly-Ser linker — D2-C-terminal domain of flagellin — hexahistidine tag. The entire construct was cloned into the pENTR 3C expression vector for recombination into a baculovirus transfer vector, which was then used to transfect SF21 insect cells to generate recombinant baculovirus stocks. These stocks were amplified and used to infect SF9 insect cells for large-scale protein production. The hFliC protein was secreted into the culture supernatant due to the retention of the native flagellin signal peptide, which facilitated its release into the extracellular medium. After 72 hours of incubation at 27°C, the culture supernatant was harvested, filtered to remove cellular debris, and subjected to ammonium sulfate precipitation to concentrate the protein. The precipitated protein was resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline containing 0.05% Tween-20 and dialyzed overnight against PBS to remove ammonium sulfate and other small molecules. The protein was then purified by immobilized metal affinity chromatography using cobalt resin, which binds specifically to the C-terminal hexahistidine tag. Bound protein was eluted with increasing concentrations of imidazole, and fractions containing the full-length hFliC protein were pooled and dialyzed again to remove residual imidazole and Tween-20. The final preparation was concentrated using polyethylene oxide and analyzed for purity and molecular weight by SDS-PAGE under denaturing conditions, which revealed a major band at approximately 85 kDa, consistent with the predicted molecular weight of the hFliC construct. Western blot analysis using an anti-His-tag antibody confirmed the identity of the band and revealed a minor degradation product near the N-terminus, likely resulting from proteolytic cleavage during expression or purification, but this did not interfere with the overall yield or functionality of the protein. Densitometric analysis indicated that the full-length hFliC constituted approximately 15% of the total protein in the crude preparation, with the remainder comprising host cell proteins and minor degradation products, which were subsequently removed during purification.

To assess the secondary structure of the purified hFliC protein, circular dichroism spectroscopy was performed and compared to that of wild-type flagellin and the isolated DENV2 E domain. The wild-type flagellin protein exhibited a characteristic α-helical spectrum with strong minima at 208 nm and 222 nm, consistent with its known structural composition dominated by α-helical segments in the D0, D1, and D2 domains. The DENV2 E domain, in contrast, displayed a spectrum dominated by β-sheet and random coil features, with a pronounced minimum at 215 nm, consistent with its known immunoglobulin-like fold composed primarily of β-strands. The hFliC protein exhibited a hybrid spectrum that combined features of both parent proteins, with clear minima at both 208 nm and 215 nm, indicating the presence of both α-helical and β-sheet structural elements. Quantitative analysis of the spectra using the K2D3 algorithm revealed that hFliC contained approximately 56% α-helix, 18.4% β-sheet, and 25.6% random coil, values that closely approximated the weighted average of the individual components, suggesting that neither the flagellin scaffold nor the inserted DENV2 E domain underwent significant structural perturbation upon fusion. This was further supported by the observation that the hFliC protein remained soluble and stable under physiological conditions, with no evidence of aggregation or precipitation, indicating that the inserted domain had folded correctly and was not causing misfolding of the flagellin scaffold. The preservation of secondary structure was critical to the functional integrity of the construct, as the DENV2 E domain must retain its native conformation to be recognized by neutralizing antibodies and to serve as a target for B cell receptors.

To determine whether the hFliC protein retained the ability to polymerize into filaments, purified monomeric protein was subjected to polymerization conditions previously established for wild-type flagellin, namely incubation in 1.5 M ammonium sulfate or 1.5 M sodium citrate at room temperature overnight. After polymerization, samples were subjected to high-speed centrifugation to pellet the filaments, which were then resuspended in PBS for analysis. Negative stain transmission electron microscopy was performed on the polymerized hFliC, and the resulting images revealed the presence of long, filamentous structures with a diameter of approximately 35 nanometers, significantly larger than the 20-nanometer diameter of wild-type flagellin filaments. This increase in diameter was consistent with the predicted model in which the DENV2 E domain, which is approximately 10 nanometers in diameter, is inserted into the solvent-exposed region of the filament, thereby increasing the overall girth of the structure. The filaments exhibited a uniform morphology, with no evidence of aggregation, branching, or irregularity, suggesting that the insertion of the DENV2 E domain did not disrupt the helical symmetry or the intermolecular interactions required for filament assembly. The inner core of the filaments displayed high contrast, similar to that observed in Tobacco Mosaic Virus filaments, indicating a dense, ordered protein core, while the outer regions exhibited lower contrast, suggesting that the DENV2 E domains were flexible and mobile on the filament surface, a feature that may enhance their accessibility to B cell receptors. To confirm that the DENV2 E domain was indeed exposed on the surface of the filament and retained its antigenic integrity, binding assays were performed using a recombinant monoclonal IgA antibody specific for the DENV2 E protein. This antibody was generated by cloning the variable regions of the 4G2 hybridoma, which had been shown to produce multiple non-specific antibody chains, into a mammalian expression system to yield a pure, single-species IgA antibody. ELISA plates coated with hFliC filaments, wild-type flagellin, or purified DENV2 E protein were incubated with the recombinant IgA, and binding was detected using an alkaline phosphatase-conjugated anti-IgA antibody. The hFliC filaments and purified DENV2 E protein both showed strong binding to the IgA antibody, while wild-type flagellin showed no binding, confirming that the DENV2 E domain was accessible and immunoreactive in the context of the filament. To further validate the surface exposure of the DENV2 E domain, immunogold labeling was performed using the 4G2 IgG monoclonal antibody, which recognizes a conformational epitope in domain II of the DENV2 E protein. Filaments were incubated with 4G2 IgG, washed, and then labeled with 10-nanometer gold-conjugated anti-mouse IgG antibodies. Transmission electron microscopy revealed that the gold particles were consistently localized to the outer surface of the hFliC filaments, with no labeling observed on wild-type flagellin filaments. This provided direct visual evidence that the DENV2 E domain was not only exposed on the filament surface but was oriented in a manner that allowed antibody access to its critical epitopes. The combination of these findings established that the hFliC protein was not only capable of polymerizing into filaments but that the inserted antigenic domain was correctly folded, accessible, and immunoreactive, fulfilling the essential criteria for a functional TI vaccine scaffold.

### Example 2

The functional capacity of the hFliC filaments to induce a T cell-independent antibody response was evaluated in both immunocompetent and T cell-restricted mouse models. In immunocompetent C57BL/6J mice, a five-week immunization protocol was employed in which animals received a priming dose of either hFliC filaments or monomeric hFliC via intraperitoneal or intranasal routes, followed by three weekly intranasal booster doses. Serum samples were collected at the end of the protocol and analyzed for antigen-specific antibody titers by ELISA using plates coated with purified DENV2 E protein. Mice immunized with hFliC filaments via intraperitoneal priming exhibited significantly higher serum IgG and IgM titers compared to mice immunized with monomeric hFliC, regardless of the route of booster administration. The IgG response was dominated by the IgG1 subclass, with moderate levels of IgG2a and IgG2b, indicating a mixed Th1/Th2 response. In contrast, mice receiving intranasal priming and boosting showed the highest levels of serum IgA, with minimal IgG and IgM responses, suggesting that mucosal delivery preferentially induced a local IgA response, likely mediated by mucosal-associated lymphoid tissue. These findings demonstrated that the polymeric nature of the hFliC filament was essential for eliciting a strong systemic antibody response, as the monomeric form was significantly less immunogenic, even when delivered via the same route. The enhanced immunogenicity of the filament was not attributable to the presence of residual endotoxin or other contaminants, as all protein preparations were rigorously purified and tested for endotoxin content using a Limulus amebocyte lysate assay, with levels below the detection threshold of 0.1 EU/mL. Furthermore, the inclusion of the flagellin scaffold itself did not appear to drive a T cell-dependent response, as the antibody titers elicited by hFliC filaments were comparable to those elicited by wild-type flagellin when tested in the same protocol, yet wild-type flagellin did not induce a DENV2-specific response, confirming that the DENV2 E domain was the source of antigenic specificity.

To test the capacity of hFliC filaments to induce a T cell-independent response, a T cell receptor transgenic mouse model (6.5-TCR) was employed. These mice express a transgenic T cell receptor specific for an influenza hemagglutinin peptide presented on MHC class II I-Ed, and due to stringent allelic exclusion during thymic selection, nearly all CD4+ T cells in these mice are restricted to this single epitope and are incapable of recognizing any other antigen, including flagellin or DENV2 E. As a result, B cells specific for these antigens receive no cognate T cell help, rendering the mice functionally deficient in T cell-dependent antibody responses. B10.D2 mice, which share the same genetic background but lack the transgenic T cell receptor, were used as wild-type controls. Both strains were immunized using the same protocol: a priming dose of hFliC filaments or monomeric hFliC via intraperitoneal or intranasal injection, followed by three intranasal boosters. Serum antibody titers were measured at the end of the five-week period. In the 6.5-TCR mice, no IgA response was detected in any group, consistent with the known requirement for T cell help in class switching to IgA. However, mice receiving intraperitoneal priming with hFliC filaments exhibited a robust serum IgM response, with titers comparable to those observed in wild-type B10.D2 mice immunized with the same construct. This was a critical finding, as IgM is the primary isotype produced in T cell-independent responses and is typically not induced in the absence of T cell help. More significantly, these mice also exhibited a marked increase in serum IgG3, a subclass of IgG that is strongly associated with TI responses and is rarely produced in the absence of T cell-derived cytokines such as IL-21 or IFN-γ. The IgG3 response was not observed in 6.5-TCR mice immunized with monomeric hFliC or wild-type flagellin, nor was it observed in mice receiving intranasal priming, indicating that the route of administration and the polymeric nature of the antigen were both essential for the induction of this response. The IgG2b subclass, which is typically associated with Th1 responses, was elevated in mice immunized with monomeric hFliC, suggesting that in the absence of T cell help, the monomeric form may be processed and presented by antigen-presenting cells in a manner that engages residual innate pathways, but this response was insufficient to induce class switching to IgG3. In contrast, the filamentous form of hFliC induced a response that was qualitatively and quantitatively distinct, with IgG3 being the dominant IgG subclass, a hallmark of TI immunity. The absence of IgG1 and IgG2a responses in the 6.5-TCR mice further confirmed that the IgG3 response was not the result of residual T cell activity or cross-reactivity, as these subclasses are typically induced by T cell-dependent mechanisms. The IgG3 response in the 6.5-TCR mice was statistically significant compared to all control groups, with p-values below 0.005, and was reproducible across multiple independent experiments. These findings provided definitive evidence that the hFliC filaments were capable of inducing a true T cell-independent antibody response, characterized by the production of IgM and IgG3 in the complete absence of cognate T cell help.

To confirm that the hFliC filaments were capable of directly crosslinking B cell receptors, in vitro experiments were performed using the 4G2 B cell hybridoma, which expresses a surface immunoglobulin receptor specific for the DENV2 E protein. Cells were incubated with hFliC filaments, monomeric hFliC, or wild-type flagellin for 30 minutes, after which they were fixed, permeabilized, and stained with fluorescently labeled antibodies against surface immunoglobulin and against the C-terminal His-tag on the hFliC filament. Confocal microscopy revealed that unstimulated 4G2 cells exhibited a diffuse, random distribution of surface immunoglobulin receptors across the cell membrane. In contrast, cells incubated with hFliC filaments showed a striking reorganization of the immunoglobulin receptors into a circumferential pattern, with the receptors clustering along the periphery of the cell membrane. This reorganization was not observed in cells treated with monomeric hFliC or wild-type flagellin, which maintained a random distribution. Co-staining with anti-His-tag antibodies confirmed that the hFliC filaments were in direct contact with the cell surface, and the spatial correlation between the filament binding sites and the BCR clusters indicated that the filaments were physically crosslinking multiple BCRs simultaneously. This clustering event is the key initiating signal for T cell-independent B cell activation, as it triggers the phosphorylation of immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs (ITAMs) in the Igα and Igβ signaling subunits, leading to downstream activation of the Syk kinase pathway, calcium flux, and ultimately, B cell proliferation and differentiation. The ability of the hFliC filament to induce this clustering event without the need for additional adjuvants, co-stimulatory molecules, or antigen-presenting cells underscores its potential as a standalone vaccine platform. The spatial arrangement of the DENV2 E domains on the filament, spaced approximately 5 nanometers apart, is ideal for engaging multiple BCRs simultaneously, as this distance falls within the empirically determined range of 5–10 nanometers required for efficient BCR crosslinking. The flexibility of the DENV2 E domains on the filament surface, as evidenced by the low contrast in electron microscopy, likely enhances this crosslinking efficiency by allowing the antigenic domains to orient themselves optimally for receptor engagement, even if the BCRs are not uniformly distributed on the cell surface. This dynamic flexibility may also contribute to the high avidity of the interaction, as it allows for multivalent binding even if individual epitope-BCR affinities are modest. The combination of high antigen density, optimal spacing, and conformational flexibility makes the hFliC filament a uniquely effective platform for inducing TI responses.

The implications of this invention extend far beyond the specific application to Dengue virus. The modular nature of the hFliC platform allows for the replacement of the DENV2 E domain with any other conformationally intact antigenic domain from a pathogenic virus or bacterium, provided that the domain can be expressed as a soluble, folded protein and that its insertion does not disrupt the polymerization of the flagellin scaffold. For example, the envelope glycoproteins of Zika virus, West Nile virus, or other flaviviruses could be inserted in place of DENV2 E to generate a universal flavivirus vaccine. Similarly, the hemagglutinin protein of influenza virus, the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2, or the envelope glycoprotein of HIV could be inserted to generate vaccines against these pathogens. The ability to produce mixed filaments containing multiple hFliC variants, each displaying a different antigenic domain, allows for the simultaneous induction of immune responses against multiple strains or serotypes. This is particularly important for pathogens such as Dengue virus, where prior vaccine candidates have failed due to immunodominance, in which the immune response is skewed toward one serotype at the expense of others. By co-administering stoichiometric amounts of four different hFliC filaments, each displaying a different DENV serotype envelope protein, it is possible to generate a balanced, broad neutralizing response against all four serotypes without the need for complex chemical conjugation or live-attenuated formulations. This approach overcomes the limitations of previous tetravalent vaccines, which have struggled to achieve uniform immunogenicity across serotypes. Furthermore, because the hFliC platform does not require adjuvants, it is particularly well-suited for use in populations with compromised immune systems, including neonates, the elderly, and immunocompromised individuals, where traditional adjuvants may be ineffective or even harmful. The stability of the filaments under physiological conditions, their solubility in aqueous buffers, and their compatibility with standard purification and formulation techniques make them ideal candidates for inclusion in lyophilized or liquid vaccine formulations that can be stored and transported without refrigeration. The invention thus provides a fundamentally new approach to vaccine design that is broadly applicable, highly adaptable, and uniquely suited for the most vulnerable populations in need of effective immunization strategies.

## CONCLUSIONS

The present invention establishes a novel and transformative platform for the development of vaccines capable of eliciting robust, T cell-independent antibody responses in the absence of conventional T helper cell support. Through the strategic replacement of the solvent-exposed D3 domain of bacterial flagellin with a structurally intact, conformationally preserved antigenic domain from a pathogenic virus, a hybrid flagellin protein has been engineered that retains the ability to self-assemble into stable, high-molecular-weight filaments while simultaneously presenting the inserted antigen in a multivalent, spatially optimized manner on the filament surface. This architectural design enables the direct crosslinking of B cell receptors on the surface of B lymphocytes, triggering intracellular signaling pathways sufficient to initiate B cell activation, proliferation, and differentiation into antibody-secreting plasma cells without the requirement for cognate T cell engagement. The functional validation of this platform in a T cell receptor transgenic mouse model, in which nearly all CD4+ T cells are restricted to a single, irrelevant antigen, provides unequivocal evidence that the hFliC filaments induce a true T cell-independent immune response, as demonstrated by the robust production of IgM and, critically, IgG3—two isotypes that are hallmark indicators of TI immunity and are typically not generated in the absence of T cell-derived cytokines. The preservation of the native conformation of the inserted antigenic domain, confirmed through circular dichroism spectroscopy and antibody binding assays, ensures that the immune response is directed against biologically relevant epitopes, thereby enhancing the likelihood of generating neutralizing antibodies. The ability of the filaments to be produced at scale using a baculovirus expression system, purified via affinity chromatography, and polymerized under simple, physiologically compatible conditions renders the platform highly scalable, reproducible, and suitable for industrial manufacturing. The modular nature of the design allows for the facile substitution of the inserted antigenic domain with any other conformationally stable protein antigen, enabling the rapid development of vaccines against a wide array of pathogens, including other flaviviruses, coronaviruses, influenza strains, and HIV. The capacity to generate mixed filaments containing multiple antigenic variants further enables the creation of multivalent or universal vaccines that overcome the immunodominance issues plaguing previous vaccine candidates. Importantly, the absence of a requirement for exogenous adjuvants, coupled with the intrinsic adjuvant properties conferred by the flagellin scaffold through TLR5 and NLRC4 engagement, provides a dual mechanism of immune stimulation that enhances innate activation while preserving the T cell-independent nature of the humoral response. This combination of features makes the hFliC filament platform uniquely suited for use in immunocompromised populations—including neonates, the elderly, individuals with HIV/AIDS, patients undergoing chemotherapy, and those with genetic immunodeficiencies—who are most vulnerable to infectious disease and least likely to benefit from conventional vaccines. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in vaccine design, moving away from reliance on T cell help toward a structure-based, antigen-driven model of B cell activation that is both broadly applicable and uniquely capable of protecting those who have been historically underserved by existing immunization technologies.
