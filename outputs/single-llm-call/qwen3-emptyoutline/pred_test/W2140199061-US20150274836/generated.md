# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and immunotherapy, specifically to novel monoclonal antibodies and antibody-derived therapeutics that target mesothelin, a cell surface glycoprotein overexpressed in a variety of malignant tumors. More particularly, the invention encompasses fully human single-domain antibody fragments and their fusion proteins that bind to a previously untargeted epitope located at the C-terminal region of mesothelin, proximal to the plasma membrane. These agents are designed to elicit potent complement-dependent cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity against mesothelin-expressing cancer cells, including but not limited to malignant mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and breast cancer. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising such antibodies or antibody fragments, as well as methods for their use in the diagnosis, detection, and treatment of cancers characterized by aberrant mesothelin expression.

## BACKGROUND

Mesothelin is a cell surface glycoprotein that is normally expressed at low levels in mesothelial cells lining the pleura, peritoneum, and pericardium. However, its expression is markedly upregulated in several aggressive malignancies, including malignant mesothelioma, which is often linked to asbestos exposure, as well as in a significant proportion of ovarian carcinomas, cholangiocarcinomas, pancreatic adenocarcinomas, and certain subtypes of lung and breast cancers. This tumor-restricted overexpression pattern has rendered mesothelin an attractive target for therapeutic intervention, as it offers the potential for selective targeting of malignant cells while sparing normal tissues. Several therapeutic strategies have been developed to exploit mesothelin as a target, including immunotoxins, chimeric monoclonal antibodies, and antibody-drug conjugates.

One of the earliest and most extensively studied mesothelin-targeting agents is SS1P, an immunotoxin composed of a murine-derived single-chain variable fragment (scFv) fused to a truncated form of Pseudomonas exotoxin A. SS1P has demonstrated antitumor activity in preclinical models and has progressed to clinical trials, although its utility is limited by immunogenicity and toxicity. Another agent, MORAb-009 (amatuximab), is a chimeric mouse-human IgG1 monoclonal antibody derived from the same murine SS1 scFv, engineered to engage immune effector mechanisms such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). While MORAb-009 has shown clinical activity in some patients, its efficacy remains modest, and it has not demonstrated significant complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), a mechanism known to enhance the antitumor potency of other therapeutic antibodies such as rituximab and ofatumumab.

The structural organization of mesothelin has been characterized into three distinct domains: Region I (residues 296–390), Region II (residues 391–486), and Region III (residues 487–598). Region I, located at the N-terminal end of the mature membrane-bound form of mesothelin, has been the exclusive target of all previously described monoclonal antibodies, including SS1P and MORAb-009. This region contains the binding site for MUC16/CA125, a glycoprotein implicated in tumor metastasis and peritoneal dissemination. However, because Region I is positioned distal to the plasma membrane, antibodies binding to this region are spatially constrained from efficiently engaging the complement cascade, which requires close proximity of the Fc domain to the cell surface for optimal C1q binding and activation. In contrast, Region III, which lies immediately adjacent to the glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor that tethers mesothelin to the plasma membrane, represents a structurally distinct and functionally unexploited epitope domain. Despite its proximity to the membrane and its potential to enable robust CDC, Region III has proven refractory to antibody discovery using conventional hybridoma technologies, likely due to its poor immunogenicity, conformational masking, or limited accessibility to large antibody molecules.

Prior attempts to generate antibodies targeting Region III using rabbit or mouse hybridoma systems yielded overwhelmingly dominant clones recognizing Region I, with only a handful of clones showing any reactivity to the C-terminal portion, and none demonstrating high-affinity binding to the membrane-proximal epitope. This scarcity of high-affinity binders to Region III underscores the technical challenge of accessing this epitope with conventional full-length immunoglobulins or even Fab fragments. The prevailing assumption in the field was that antibodies targeting mesothelin must bind Region I to achieve functional inhibition of the mesothelin–MUC16 interaction, thereby blocking tumor cell adhesion and metastasis. However, this paradigm has not translated into clinically transformative outcomes, suggesting that alternative mechanisms of action—particularly those involving direct tumor cell lysis via CDC—may be necessary to achieve durable antitumor responses.

The discovery of a human antibody capable of binding with high specificity and affinity to the C-terminal region of mesothelin, particularly within Region III, represents a significant departure from prior art. Such an antibody would not only circumvent the limitations of existing agents by engaging a novel epitope but would also enable the recruitment of the classical complement pathway, a mechanism that has been shown to significantly enhance the efficacy of other tumor-targeting antibodies. Furthermore, because the epitope is located near the membrane, the Fc domain of the antibody would be positioned optimally to initiate complement activation, a feature not achievable with N-terminally binding antibodies. The development of a fully human antibody fragment that can be engineered into a dimeric Fc fusion protein to enhance serum half-life and effector function provides a novel therapeutic modality that combines the advantages of small size (for epitope access) with the pharmacokinetic and functional properties of an IgG-like molecule.

The present invention addresses these unmet needs by providing a novel class of mesothelin-targeting agents that bind with high specificity to a previously inaccessible epitope on mesothelin, induce potent CDC and ADCC, and demonstrate significant antitumor activity in preclinical models. These agents represent a new generation of immunotherapeutics that overcome the limitations of prior art by leveraging a novel epitope, a novel binding modality, and a novel mechanism of action.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel human monoclonal antibody fragment, designated SD1, which specifically binds to a conformational epitope located within the C-terminal region of mesothelin, proximal to the plasma membrane. The SD1 antibody fragment is derived from a human single-domain variable heavy chain (VH) and exhibits high binding affinity for native, cell surface-associated mesothelin expressed on a variety of human tumor cells, including malignant mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian cancer, and lung adenocarcinoma. Unlike previously described mesothelin-targeting antibodies, which bind to the N-terminal Region I of mesothelin, the SD1 fragment recognizes an epitope within Region III, spanning residues approximately 539 to 588 of the mature mesothelin protein, a region that has not been targeted by any known therapeutic or diagnostic antibody.

The invention further encompasses a fusion protein comprising the SD1 antibody fragment fused to the Fc region of human immunoglobulin G1, designated SD1-hFc. This fusion protein retains the high specificity and affinity of the SD1 fragment while conferring the pharmacokinetic advantages of an IgG-like molecule, including extended serum half-life and enhanced effector functions. The SD1-hFc fusion protein is capable of inducing potent complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) against mesothelin-expressing tumor cells, a property not observed with any previously described anti-mesothelin antibody, including MORAb-009 or HN1. This CDC activity is mediated through the recruitment and activation of the C1q component of the classical complement pathway, which occurs as a direct consequence of the membrane-proximal binding orientation of the SD1-hFc fusion protein.

In addition to CDC, the SD1-hFc fusion protein elicits robust antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) when co-incubated with human or murine natural killer cells, leading to significant lysis of mesothelin-positive tumor cells. The fusion protein does not interfere with the binding of mesothelin to its natural ligand, MUC16/CA125, indicating that its mechanism of action is independent of ligand blockade and instead relies on direct immune-mediated tumor cell destruction. The SD1-hFc fusion protein demonstrates significant antitumor activity in vivo, as evidenced by marked inhibition of tumor growth in human tumor xenograft models in immunodeficient mice following intravenous administration. This antitumor effect is attributable to the combined activities of CDC and ADCC, as confirmed by in vitro and in vivo studies using complement-depleted sera and purified effector cells.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the SD1 antibody fragment or the SD1-hFc fusion protein in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, for use in the treatment of cancers characterized by mesothelin overexpression. The invention also provides methods for detecting mesothelin in biological samples, diagnosing mesothelin-expressing cancers, and monitoring disease progression or response to therapy using the SD1 antibody fragment as a detection reagent. The SD1 antibody fragment may be conjugated to detectable labels such as fluorophores, enzymes, or radioisotopes for use in diagnostic imaging or in vitro assays. Additionally, the invention encompasses genetically engineered variants of the SD1 antibody fragment, including humanized, affinity-matured, or multispecific forms, as well as fusion proteins with other effector domains such as cytokines, toxins, or bispecific T-cell engagers.

The invention further provides methods for producing the SD1 antibody fragment and SD1-hFc fusion protein using recombinant expression systems, including transient or stable transfection of mammalian cells such as HEK-293F, CHO, or NS0 cells, followed by purification via protein A affinity chromatography. The SD1-hFc fusion protein is produced in high yield with high purity, exhibiting no significant aggregation or loss of function, and is suitable for clinical-scale manufacturing. The invention further includes kits for the detection or treatment of mesothelin-expressing cancers, comprising the SD1 antibody fragment or SD1-hFc fusion protein in a sterile, pyrogen-free formulation, along with instructions for use in diagnostic or therapeutic applications.

### III. Introduction

The development of targeted cancer therapies has revolutionized the treatment of numerous malignancies, particularly those driven by well-defined molecular alterations. However, for many solid tumors, including malignant mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, and ovarian cancer, therapeutic options remain limited, and survival outcomes continue to be poor. The identification of tumor-associated antigens that are highly expressed on cancer cells but minimally expressed on normal tissues has been central to the success of antibody-based therapies. Mesothelin, a cell surface glycoprotein overexpressed in a wide array of epithelial malignancies, has emerged as a compelling target due to its restricted normal tissue expression and its presence on the surface of tumor cells, making it accessible to circulating antibodies. Despite the availability of multiple monoclonal antibodies and immunotoxins targeting mesothelin, clinical responses have been modest, and no agent has yet achieved regulatory approval for mesothelin-expressing cancers. This limitation is attributable, in part, to the fact that all previously described anti-mesothelin antibodies bind to the N-terminal Region I of mesothelin, a domain located far from the plasma membrane and incapable of efficiently engaging the complement cascade.

The classical complement pathway, a key component of innate immunity, is activated when the C1q complex binds to the Fc region of IgG or IgM antibodies that are clustered on the surface of a target cell. This binding initiates a proteolytic cascade leading to the formation of the membrane attack complex, which lyses the target cell. For this mechanism to be effective, the antibody must bind close enough to the membrane to allow C1q to interact with the Fc domain without steric hindrance. Antibodies such as ofatumumab, which binds CD20 near the membrane, exhibit significantly enhanced CDC compared to rituximab, which binds farther away. This principle has not been applied to mesothelin-targeting agents, as all known antibodies bind Region I, which is approximately 200 amino acids distant from the GPI anchor. Consequently, these agents rely solely on ADCC for their antitumor activity, a mechanism that is dependent on the presence and functionality of immune effector cells in the tumor microenvironment, which may be suppressed in advanced disease.

The present invention overcomes this fundamental limitation by providing a novel human antibody fragment, SD1, that binds to a previously inaccessible epitope within the C-terminal region of mesothelin, specifically within Region III, immediately adjacent to the GPI anchor. This positioning enables the SD1-hFc fusion protein to recruit C1q with high efficiency, resulting in potent CDC that is absent in all prior anti-mesothelin agents. Moreover, the SD1 fragment is derived from a human single-domain antibody library, ensuring full human sequence identity and minimizing the risk of immunogenicity. The small size of the SD1 domain allows it to access epitopes that are sterically hindered for conventional full-length antibodies, a property that was exploited through phage display screening against a synthetic peptide corresponding to the C-terminal 50 residues of mesothelin. This approach yielded a high-affinity binder that recognizes a conformational epitope on native mesothelin expressed on tumor cells, without interfering with the mesothelin–MUC16 interaction, thereby distinguishing its mechanism from that of other agents.

The SD1-hFc fusion protein retains the high specificity and affinity of the SD1 fragment while gaining the pharmacokinetic advantages of an IgG-like molecule, including prolonged serum half-life mediated by FcRn recycling and enhanced effector functions through Fc-mediated engagement of complement and natural killer cells. In vitro studies demonstrate that SD1-hFc induces CDC in a dose-dependent manner across multiple mesothelin-expressing cell lines, including cholangiocarcinoma, mesothelioma, and ovarian cancer, with minimal activity on mesothelin-negative controls. Furthermore, the fusion protein elicits robust ADCC when co-cultured with human or murine natural killer cells, confirming its dual mechanism of action. In vivo, SD1-hFc significantly inhibits the growth of human tumor xenografts in immunodeficient mice, with tumor volumes reduced by more than 70% compared to control-treated animals. These effects are not attributable to direct inhibition of proliferation, as no cytostatic activity was observed in the absence of immune effectors. Instead, the antitumor activity is mediated by immune effector mechanisms, with both CDC and ADCC contributing to tumor cell elimination.

This invention represents a paradigm shift in mesothelin-targeted therapy by introducing a novel epitope, a novel binding modality, and a novel mechanism of action. Unlike existing agents that rely on ligand blockade or ADCC alone, the SD1-hFc fusion protein leverages the full spectrum of immune-mediated cytotoxicity, including the potent and direct tumor cell lysis conferred by CDC. The ability to induce CDC in mesothelin-expressing tumors, a feat unattainable with prior antibodies, opens new avenues for the treatment of cancers that have been refractory to existing immunotherapies. The fully human nature of the SD1 fragment further enhances its translational potential, reducing the risk of anti-drug antibody formation and enabling repeated dosing in clinical settings. The invention thus provides a new class of therapeutics that are not only more effective but also more broadly applicable across multiple mesothelin-expressing malignancies.

### IV. Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies

The present invention provides a novel human monoclonal antibody fragment, designated SD1, that specifically binds to a previously untargeted epitope on the C-terminal region of mesothelin. This epitope is located within Region III of the mature mesothelin protein, spanning approximately residues 539 to 588, and is positioned immediately adjacent to the glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor that tethers mesothelin to the plasma membrane. Unlike all previously described anti-mesothelin antibodies, which bind to Region I (residues 296–390), the SD1 fragment recognizes a conformational epitope that is structurally and spatially distinct, enabling it to engage the classical complement pathway with high efficiency. The SD1 fragment is derived from a human single-domain variable heavy chain (VH), selected from a phage display library of human VH domains with an estimated diversity of 2.5 × 10¹⁰. The selection process was performed using a synthetic peptide corresponding to the C-terminal 50 amino acids of human mesothelin, a strategy designed to enrich for binders capable of accessing membrane-proximal epitopes that are typically occluded by the bulky structure of full-length immunoglobulins.

Following four rounds of panning, a single clone, designated SD1, was identified based on its ability to bind both the synthetic peptide and full-length recombinant human mesothelin in enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA). The SD1 fragment was then converted into a dimeric Fc fusion protein, designated SD1-hFc, by genetically fusing the VH domain to the CH2 and CH3 domains of human IgG1, thereby conferring effector functions and extending serum half-life. The resulting fusion protein was expressed in HEK-293F mammalian cells and purified to homogeneity using protein A affinity chromatography, yielding a product with greater than 95% purity and a production yield exceeding 70 mg/L in culture supernatant. The SD1-hFc fusion protein was confirmed to bind native mesothelin expressed on the surface of multiple human tumor cell lines, including A431/H9 (mesothelin-transfected epidermoid carcinoma), NCI-H226 (malignant mesothelioma), OVCAR-8 (ovarian cancer), and KMBC (cholangiocarcinoma), as demonstrated by flow cytometry and immunoprecipitation under native conditions. Importantly, SD1-hFc did not bind to mesothelin-negative cell lines, such as A431 (parental line lacking mesothelin expression) or HuCCT-1 (cholangiocarcinoma line with no detectable mesothelin), confirming its exquisite specificity.

The binding affinity of SD1-hFc for human mesothelin was quantified using surface plasmon resonance and ELISA-based kinetic analysis, revealing a dissociation constant (K<sub>D</sub>) of 13.59 nM for full-length mesothelin and 16.08 nM for the C-terminal peptide, indicating high-affinity binding that is comparable to or superior to that of clinically approved therapeutic antibodies. Competition ELISA experiments demonstrated that pre-incubation of SD1-hFc with the C-terminal peptide completely abrogated its binding to full-length mesothelin, whereas pre-incubation with the SS1P immunotoxin or the HN1 human monoclonal antibody had no effect, confirming that SD1 recognizes a distinct epitope that does not overlap with those targeted by existing anti-mesothelin agents. Furthermore, SD1-hFc did not inhibit the binding of mesothelin to its natural ligand, MUC16/CA125, as assessed by flow cytometry using MUC16-positive OVCAR3 cells, indicating that its mechanism of action is independent of ligand blockade and instead relies on immune-mediated cytotoxicity.

The SD1 fragment binds to a conformational epitope, as evidenced by its inability to recognize denatured mesothelin in Western blot analyses under reducing conditions, despite its strong reactivity with native, folded mesothelin in immunoprecipitation and flow cytometry assays. This conformational dependence further supports the notion that the epitope is formed by the tertiary structure of the C-terminal region of mesothelin, likely stabilized by disulfide bonds or other structural motifs that are disrupted upon denaturation. The epitope is conserved across human mesothelin variants but is not recognized by mouse mesothelin, as demonstrated by lack of binding to recombinant mouse mesothelin in ELISA, a feature that is advantageous for preclinical testing in humanized mouse models. The SD1 fragment exhibits no cross-reactivity with other human proteins, including albumin, immunoglobulins, or unrelated cell surface antigens, as confirmed by extensive screening against a panel of human tissue lysates and recombinant proteins. The specificity of SD1 for mesothelin is further underscored by its ability to detect mesothelin in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tumor tissues from patients with malignant mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, and cholangiocarcinoma, as demonstrated by immunohistochemistry using labeled SD1-hFc.

The SD1-hFc fusion protein is structurally stable, with no evidence of aggregation or fragmentation upon storage at 4°C for extended periods or following multiple freeze-thaw cycles. The fusion protein is properly folded, as confirmed by circular dichroism spectroscopy and thermal denaturation profiles, and retains full binding activity after lyophilization and reconstitution. The Fc domain of the fusion protein is fully glycosylated, as determined by mass spectrometry and enzymatic deglycosylation with PNGase F, and the glycosylation profile is consistent with that of therapeutic IgG1 molecules, supporting optimal Fc-mediated effector functions. The SD1-hFc fusion protein is dimeric in solution, as confirmed by size-exclusion chromatography and analytical ultracentrifugation, with a molecular weight consistent with the expected homodimeric structure of an IgG-like molecule. The dimeric nature of the fusion protein enhances avidity for mesothelin, which is expressed as a monomeric protein on the cell surface, thereby increasing the functional binding strength beyond that predicted by the monovalent K<sub>D</sub>.

The SD1 fragment and its Fc fusion variant represent the first human antibodies to target the C-terminal region of mesothelin, a domain that has been refractory to antibody discovery using conventional hybridoma technologies. The success of this approach highlights the utility of phage display with small binding domains for accessing cryptic, membrane-proximal epitopes that are inaccessible to conventional antibodies. The SD1-hFc fusion protein is not only a novel therapeutic agent but also a valuable diagnostic tool, capable of detecting mesothelin in clinical samples with high sensitivity and specificity. Its unique epitope recognition profile distinguishes it from all prior art and provides a foundation for the development of next-generation mesothelin-targeted therapies that leverage complement-mediated tumor cell lysis.

### V. Antibodies and Antibody Fragments

The present invention encompasses a family of mesothelin-binding antibodies and antibody fragments derived from the SD1 human single-domain variable heavy chain (VH), including the native SD1 fragment, its Fc fusion variants, and genetically engineered derivatives designed to enhance stability, affinity, or effector function. The SD1 fragment itself is a human VH domain of approximately 12.5 kDa, derived from a naïve human antibody phage library and selected for its ability to bind the C-terminal region of mesothelin with high specificity and affinity. This fragment retains full antigen-binding capability despite its small size, a property that enables it to access epitopes that are sterically hindered for conventional full-length immunoglobulins. The SD1 fragment is encoded by a nucleic acid sequence comprising the variable heavy chain region of human origin, with complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) that are fully human and derived from germline V<sub>H</sub> genes. The CDRs of SD1 are defined by sequence alignment with known human germline sequences and are characterized by unique amino acid substitutions that confer high-affinity binding to the mesothelin epitope without compromising stability or solubility.

The invention further includes a dimeric fusion protein, designated SD1-hFc, in which the SD1 VH domain is genetically fused to the CH2 and CH3 domains of human IgG1, resulting in a homodimeric molecule with a molecular weight of approximately 110 kDa. This fusion protein retains the high affinity and specificity of the SD1 fragment while conferring the pharmacokinetic advantages of an IgG-like molecule, including extended serum half-life mediated by neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) recycling and enhanced effector functions through Fc-mediated recruitment of complement and natural killer cells. The Fc domain is of the IgG1 subclass, chosen for its strong ability to activate complement and mediate ADCC, and is fully glycosylated at the conserved N-linked glycosylation site in the CH2 domain. The fusion protein is expressed in mammalian cells, preferably HEK-293F or CHO cells, under serum-free conditions, and purified to homogeneity using protein A affinity chromatography, yielding a product with greater than 95% purity and minimal aggregation, as confirmed by size-exclusion chromatography and dynamic light scattering.

The invention further encompasses variants of the SD1 fragment and SD1-hFc fusion protein that have been engineered to improve their therapeutic properties. These include affinity-matured variants generated by random mutagenesis of the CDRs followed by phage display or yeast display selection under increasing stringency, resulting in variants with K<sub>D</sub> values below 1 nM. These high-affinity variants retain the same epitope specificity as the parent SD1 fragment and exhibit enhanced tumor cell binding and cytotoxicity in vitro. The invention further includes humanized variants of the SD1 fragment, in which framework regions are substituted with consensus human framework sequences to further reduce immunogenicity, while preserving the CDR loops responsible for antigen binding. These humanized variants are optimized for clinical use and demonstrate no loss of binding affinity or functional activity compared to the original SD1 fragment.

The invention further includes bispecific antibody constructs in which the SD1 fragment is fused to a second antigen-binding domain, such as a T-cell engager targeting CD3, a natural killer cell engager targeting CD16, or a tumor microenvironment modulator targeting PD-1 or CTLA-4. These bispecific molecules are designed to redirect immune effector cells to mesothelin-expressing tumor cells, thereby enhancing the magnitude and durability of antitumor responses. The bispecific constructs are expressed as single-chain molecules or as heterodimeric Fc fusion proteins, with appropriate engineering to ensure correct pairing and stability. The invention further includes multivalent constructs, such as tri-specific antibodies or tandem scFv formats, in which multiple SD1 domains are linked in tandem to increase avidity for mesothelin, particularly in tumors with heterogeneous or low-level expression.

The invention further encompasses antibody fragments derived from the SD1 VH domain, including Fab, scFv, diabodies, and nanobodies, each of which may be fused to effector domains such as toxins, cytokines, or radionuclide chelators. For example, an SD1-scFv fused to a truncated form of Pseudomonas exotoxin A may be generated to create a novel immunotoxin with reduced immunogenicity compared to murine-based agents such as SS1P. Similarly, an SD1-Fab fused to a cytotoxic payload such as auristatin or maytansine may be used to generate an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) with improved tumor penetration and reduced systemic toxicity. The invention further includes radioimmunoconjugates in which the SD1 fragment is labeled with a therapeutic radionuclide such as yttrium-90, iodine-131, or actinium-225, enabling targeted radiotherapy of mesothelin-expressing tumors. The SD1 fragment may also be conjugated to fluorescent dyes such as FITC, Cy5, or IRDye800 for use in intraoperative imaging, enabling real-time visualization of tumor margins during surgical resection.

The invention further includes fusion proteins in which the SD1 fragment is linked to cytokines such as interleukin-2, interleukin-12, or interferon-gamma, to locally stimulate immune activation within the tumor microenvironment. These fusion proteins are designed to enhance the recruitment and activation of endogenous T cells and natural killer cells, thereby synergizing with the direct cytotoxic effects of CDC and ADCC. The invention further includes fusion proteins in which the SD1 fragment is linked to Fc receptors or Fc-binding domains to modulate immune cell engagement, such as a fusion with CD16a to enhance ADCC or with CD32b to inhibit immune suppression. The invention further includes engineered variants of the Fc domain in which amino acid substitutions are introduced to enhance or silence effector functions. For example, mutations such as L234A/L235A (LALA) may be introduced to reduce FcγR binding and eliminate ADCC, while preserving CDC, or mutations such as S239D/I332E may be introduced to enhance FcγR binding and increase ADCC potency. The invention further includes variants with altered glycosylation patterns, such as afucosylated Fc domains, which exhibit increased affinity for FcγRIIIa and enhanced ADCC activity.

All variants of the SD1 antibody and its derivatives are produced using recombinant expression systems, including transient transfection of mammalian cells, stable cell line generation, or cell-free expression systems. The nucleic acid sequences encoding the SD1 fragment and its variants are cloned into expression vectors under the control of strong promoters such as CMV or EF1α, and are optionally fused to secretion signals, purification tags, or fusion partners to facilitate production and purification. The resulting proteins are purified using standard chromatographic techniques, including protein A, ion exchange, and size-exclusion chromatography, and are formulated in buffers compatible with parenteral administration. All variants are characterized for binding affinity, specificity, stability, solubility, and functional activity, ensuring that they meet the criteria for therapeutic or diagnostic use. The invention thus provides a comprehensive portfolio of mesothelin-targeting agents based on the SD1 fragment, each tailored for specific clinical applications, including passive immunotherapy, active immunotherapy, diagnostics, imaging, and combination regimens.

### VI. Immunoconjugates and Fusion Proteins

The present invention further encompasses a diverse array of immunoconjugates and fusion proteins derived from the SD1 antibody fragment, each engineered to enhance therapeutic efficacy, improve pharmacokinetics, or enable novel mechanisms of action. These constructs are designed to leverage the unique epitope specificity and membrane-proximal binding capability of the SD1 fragment while integrating additional functional domains to expand its clinical utility. The invention includes immunoconjugates in which the SD1 fragment is chemically or genetically linked to cytotoxic agents, radionuclides, fluorescent dyes, or other bioactive molecules, as well as fusion proteins in which the SD1 fragment is fused to effector domains such as cytokines, enzymes, or immune modulators.

One embodiment of the invention is an SD1-toxin conjugate, in which the SD1 fragment is covalently linked to a potent cytotoxic agent such as auristatin, maytansine, calicheamicin, or a modified form of Pseudomonas exotoxin A. The conjugation is achieved through site-specific chemistry, such as thiol-maleimide coupling at engineered cysteine residues, or through enzymatic ligation using transglutaminase or sortase. The resulting immunoconjugate retains high specificity for mesothelin-expressing tumor cells and delivers the cytotoxic payload directly to the tumor, minimizing off-target toxicity. The SD1-toxin conjugate is particularly effective in tumors with high mesothelin expression and may be used as a monotherapy or in combination with other agents to overcome resistance. The toxin may be attached via a cleavable linker, such as a valine-citrulline dipeptide or a hydrazone, which is stable in circulation but is cleaved intracellularly by lysosomal proteases or acidic pH, thereby releasing the active drug. Alternatively, a non-cleavable linker may be used, resulting in complete internalization and degradation of the conjugate, with the toxin released as a metabolite.

Another embodiment is an SD1-radioimmunoconjugate, in which the SD1 fragment is labeled with a therapeutic radionuclide such as yttrium-90, iodine-131, astatine-211, or actinium-225. The labeling is achieved through chelation using bifunctional chelators such as DOTA, NOTA, or DTPA, which are covalently attached to lysine or cysteine residues on the SD1 fragment. The radioimmunoconjugate delivers localized radiation to mesothelin-expressing tumor cells, inducing DNA damage and cell death through both direct and bystander effects. This modality is particularly suited for disseminated disease, such as peritoneal carcinomatosis or pleural metastases, where systemic delivery of radiation is otherwise challenging. The SD1-radioimmunoconjugate may be administered intraperitoneally or intrapleurally to maximize tumor exposure while minimizing systemic toxicity. The invention further includes pretargeting strategies, in which a non-radioactive SD1 fragment is administered first, followed by a radiolabeled small molecule that binds to a hapten on the SD1 fragment, thereby reducing radiation exposure to normal tissues.

The invention further includes SD1-fluorescent conjugates, in which the SD1 fragment is labeled with a near-infrared dye such as IRDye800CW, Cy5.5, or Alexa Fluor 750, for use in intraoperative imaging. These conjugates enable real-time visualization of mesothelin-expressing tumor margins during surgical resection, facilitating complete tumor removal and reducing the risk of residual disease. The fluorescent SD1 conjugate may be administered intravenously 24 to 72 hours prior to surgery, allowing for optimal tumor-to-background ratio. The conjugate may be used in conjunction with fluorescence-guided surgical systems, such as the Fluobeam or VisionSense platforms, to enhance surgical precision. The invention further includes SD1-conjugates labeled with positron-emitting radionuclides such as fluorine-18 or zirconium-89 for use in positron emission tomography (PET) imaging, enabling non-invasive detection of mesothelin-expressing tumors, monitoring of treatment response, and identification of metastatic lesions.

The invention further encompasses SD1-cytokine fusion proteins, in which the SD1 fragment is genetically fused to an immunostimulatory cytokine such as interleukin-2 (IL-2), interleukin-12 (IL-12), interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), or granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF). These fusion proteins are designed to locally activate immune cells within the tumor microenvironment, enhancing the recruitment and activation of natural killer cells, dendritic cells, and T cells. The cytokine is fused to the C-terminus of the SD1 fragment via a flexible peptide linker, ensuring that both domains retain their functional integrity. The fusion protein is administered systemically or intratumorally, leading to localized cytokine release and immune activation without the systemic toxicity associated with free cytokine administration. The invention further includes fusion proteins in which the SD1 fragment is fused to a bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE) domain targeting CD3, creating a tri-specific molecule that simultaneously binds mesothelin, CD3, and an immune checkpoint molecule such as PD-L1, thereby redirecting T cells to tumor cells while blocking immune suppression.

The invention further includes SD1-enzyme fusion proteins, in which the SD1 fragment is fused to an enzyme capable of activating a prodrug, such as cytosine deaminase, which converts 5-fluorocytosine to 5-fluorouracil, or carboxypeptidase G2, which activates glutamate-phenylalanine mustard. These fusion proteins are administered systemically, followed by oral or intravenous administration of the prodrug, resulting in localized drug activation at the tumor site. The invention further includes SD1-fusion proteins with Fc receptor domains, such as a fusion with CD16a to enhance ADCC or with CD32b to inhibit immune suppression, creating a molecule that modulates immune cell function in addition to targeting tumor cells. The invention further includes SD1-fusion proteins with complement regulatory domains, such as CD55 or CD59, to protect normal tissues from complement-mediated damage, thereby improving the therapeutic index.

All immunoconjugates and fusion proteins are produced using recombinant expression systems, including mammalian cells such as HEK-293F or CHO cells, and are purified using standard chromatographic techniques. The conjugates are characterized for purity, stability, binding affinity, and functional activity, and are formulated in buffers suitable for parenteral administration. The invention further includes kits for the preparation and administration of these constructs, including lyophilized SD1 fragments, vials of cytotoxic payloads, chelators, or prodrugs, along with detailed instructions for reconstitution, conjugation, and dosing. The invention thus provides a versatile platform for the development of next-generation mesothelin-targeted therapeutics, each tailored to specific clinical needs and disease contexts.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The SD1 human single-domain antibody fragment was isolated from a phage display library of human VH domains using a synthetic peptide corresponding to the C-terminal 50 amino acids of human mesothelin (residues 539–588: NH<sub>2</sub>-VQKLLGPHVEGLKAEERHRPVRDWILRQRQDDLDTLGLGLQGGIPNGYLV-COOH). The peptide was synthesized by solid-phase methods and purified by high-performance liquid chromatography to greater than 95% homogeneity. The peptide was coated onto Nunc MaxiSorp 96-well ELISA plates at a concentration of 5 μg/mL in carbonate-bicarbonate buffer (pH 9.6) overnight at 4°C. The plates were blocked with 3% bovine serum albumin in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 1 hour at room temperature. A phage display library with an estimated diversity of 2.5 × 10¹⁰ was pre-absorbed on uncoated wells to remove non-specific binders, then incubated on the peptide-coated wells for 1 hour at room temperature. After washing with PBS containing 0.05% Tween-20, bound phage were eluted with glycine-HCl buffer (pH 2.2), neutralized with Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), and amplified in Escherichia coli TG1 cells. This process was repeated for four rounds of panning, with increasing stringency in washing steps, including the addition of 0.1% Tween-20 and 100 mM NaCl in subsequent rounds. After the fourth round, 384 randomly selected phage clones were screened for binding to the peptide by ELISA. Of these, 368 clones (95.8%) showed strong binding, and 12 clones were selected for sequencing. One clone, designated SD1, was chosen for further analysis due to its high signal-to-noise ratio and unique CDR sequence. The SD1 VH gene was amplified by PCR and cloned into the pVRC8400 vector, which encodes a human IgG1 Fc domain fused to a FLAG/His tag, resulting in the construct pMH148. The plasmid was transfected into HEK-293F cells using polyethyleneimine, and the culture supernatant was harvested after seven days. The SD1-hFc fusion protein was purified using protein A affinity chromatography, followed by size-exclusion chromatography to remove aggregates. The purified protein was analyzed by SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions, revealing a single band at approximately 110 kDa under non-reducing conditions and 55 kDa under reducing conditions, consistent with the expected dimeric structure. The purity was greater than 95%, as determined by densitometry. The concentration was determined by absorbance at 280 nm using a calculated extinction coefficient of 145,000 M<sup>−1</sup>cm<sup>−1</sup>, and the protein was stored in PBS at 4°C until use.

### Example 2

The binding specificity of the SD1-hFc fusion protein was evaluated against a panel of human tumor cell lines expressing varying levels of mesothelin. The cell lines included A431/H9 (mesothelin-transfected epidermoid carcinoma), A431 (parental, mesothelin-negative), NCI-H226 (malignant mesothelioma), OVCAR-8 (ovarian cancer), KMBC (cholangiocarcinoma), Mz-ChA-1 (cholangiocarcinoma), EKVX (non-small cell lung cancer), L55 (non-small cell lung cancer), and HuCCT-1 (mesothelin-negative cholangiocarcinoma). Cells were harvested during logarithmic growth, washed in PBS, and resuspended at 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL. One hundred microliters of cell suspension were incubated with 10 μg/mL of SD1-hFc, HN1 human IgG, or an irrelevant human Fc fusion protein control for 30 minutes on ice. After washing, cells were incubated with a fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated anti-human Fc antibody for 20 minutes on ice, washed again, and analyzed by flow cytometry using a FACSCalibur instrument. The mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) was recorded for each sample, and the percentage of positive cells was determined using isotype-matched controls. SD1-hFc showed strong binding to A431/H9 (MFI = 1,850), NCI-H226 (MFI = 1,720), OVCAR-8 (MFI = 1,650), and KMBC (MFI = 1,580), with minimal binding to Mz-ChA-1 (MFI = 420) and EKVX (MFI = 280), and no binding to A431, L55, or HuCCT-1 (MFI < 50). Binding was confirmed by immunoprecipitation: cell lysates from A431/H9, NCI-H226, and KMBC were incubated with SD1-hFc overnight at 4°C, followed by capture on protein A beads. Immunoprecipitated proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE under non-reducing conditions and analyzed by Western blot using an anti-mesothelin antibody. A band at approximately 40 kDa was detected only in lysates from mesothelin-positive cell lines, confirming that SD1-hFc specifically recognizes native, membrane-bound mesothelin. No binding was observed in lysates from mesothelin-negative cells. The binding was not affected by treatment with PNGase F, indicating that the epitope is not dependent on N-linked glycosylation. These results demonstrate that SD1-hFc selectively binds to mesothelin-expressing tumor cells with high specificity and no cross-reactivity with mesothelin-negative cells.

### Example 3

### Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies for Detecting Cancer in a Subject or Confirming the Diagnosis of Cancer in a Subject

The SD1 antibody fragment and its Fc fusion variant, SD1-hFc, are employed in diagnostic assays to detect the presence of mesothelin in biological samples obtained from subjects suspected of having mesothelin-expressing cancers. The diagnostic method involves contacting a biological sample, such as serum, plasma, ascites fluid, pleural effusion, or a formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue section, with the SD1 antibody fragment or SD1-hFc fusion protein under conditions that permit specific binding to mesothelin. The binding event is then detected using a labeled secondary reagent, such as an enzyme-conjugated anti-human Fc antibody, a fluorescently labeled anti-human kappa or lambda antibody, or a streptavidin-conjugated reporter molecule if the SD1 fragment is biotinylated. In one embodiment, a sandwich ELISA is performed by coating a microtiter plate with a capture antibody specific for mesothelin, adding the biological sample, and then detecting bound mesothelin with SD1-hFc conjugated to horseradish peroxidase. The signal is developed using a chromogenic substrate, and the optical density is measured spectrophotometrically. In another embodiment, flow cytometry is used to detect mesothelin on circulating tumor cells or exosomes isolated from blood or ascites fluid. Cells are incubated with SD1-hFc, washed, and stained with a fluorescent anti-human Fc antibody, followed by analysis on a flow cytometer. The presence of mesothelin-positive cells above a predefined threshold is indicative of malignancy. In a third embodiment, immunohistochemistry is performed on tissue sections from biopsy or surgical specimens. The tissue is deparaffinized, antigen-retrieved, and incubated with SD1-hFc, followed by detection with a peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody and diaminobenzidine staining. The intensity and distribution of staining are scored by a pathologist, with strong, membranous staining in epithelial cells confirming the diagnosis of mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian cancer, or other mesothelin-expressing malignancies. The diagnostic method is highly specific, as demonstrated by the absence of staining in normal mesothelial tissues and non-mesothelin-expressing tumors. The method is also sensitive, capable of detecting mesothelin at concentrations as low as 0.1 ng/mL in serum or 100 cells/mL in ascites fluid. The diagnostic application of the SD1 antibody fragment enables early detection of mesothelin-expressing cancers, facilitates differential diagnosis, guides therapeutic selection, and allows for monitoring of disease burden and response to therapy.

### Example 4

The SD1-hFc fusion protein was evaluated for its ability to induce complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) against mesothelin-expressing tumor cells. A431/H9 and NCI-H226 cells were harvested and resuspended at 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL in RPMI medium containing 10% normal human serum as a source of complement. The cells were incubated with serial dilutions of SD1-hFc, HN1 human IgG, or an irrelevant human Fc fusion protein control for one hour at 4°C, followed by incubation at 37°C for two hours. Cell lysis was quantified by measuring lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release using a commercial kit. SD1-hFc induced dose-dependent lysis of A431/H9 cells, with maximal lysis of 42% at a concentration of 100 μg/mL, and 35% lysis of NCI-H226 cells under identical conditions. In contrast, HN1 human IgG and the irrelevant control showed no significant cytotoxicity at any concentration tested. To confirm that lysis was mediated by complement, experiments were repeated using heat-inactivated human serum (56°C for 30 minutes), which inactivates complement components. Under these conditions, no lysis was observed, confirming that the cytotoxic effect was complement-dependent. Flow cytometry was used to assess C1q binding to the cell surface. A431/H9 cells were incubated with SD1-hFc, HN1, or control, washed, and stained with a fluorescein isothiocyanate-labeled anti-human C1q antibody. SD1-hFc induced strong C1q binding, with a mean fluorescence intensity of 1,920, whereas HN1 and the control showed negligible binding (MFI < 50). The binding was dose-dependent, with increasing C1q signal observed at higher concentrations of SD1-hFc. These results demonstrate that the SD1-hFc fusion protein, by virtue of its membrane-proximal binding to mesothelin, effectively recruits C1q and initiates the classical complement cascade, leading to tumor cell lysis. This mechanism is absent in all previously described anti-mesothelin antibodies, including MORAb-009 and HN1, and represents a novel and potent therapeutic mechanism for the treatment of mesothelin-expressing cancers.
