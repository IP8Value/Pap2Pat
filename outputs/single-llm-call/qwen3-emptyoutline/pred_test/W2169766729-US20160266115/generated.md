# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The clinical management of patients with complex immune-mediated conditions has long been hindered by an inability to accurately characterize the systemic state of the immune system in a manner that reliably correlates with disease progression, therapeutic response, or survival outcomes. Traditional approaches to immune monitoring have relied heavily on the measurement of individual cell populations as proportions or percentages relative to broader parental populations—such as reporting regulatory T cells as a percentage of CD4+ T cells or lymphocytes as a percentage of total white blood cells. While these methods offer simplicity and have been widely adopted, they fundamentally fail to account for the absolute abundance of each cell type within the circulatory volume, thereby obscuring critical biological insights. For instance, a patient with lymphopenia may exhibit a seemingly normal percentage of regulatory T cells within the CD4+ compartment, yet the absolute number of those regulatory T cells may be profoundly diminished, rendering any interpretation of immune suppression or activation misleading. Similarly, an elevation in granulocytes due to infection or stress may mask an underlying depletion of mononuclear cells, leading to misclassification of immune status when only relative frequencies are considered. These limitations are particularly acute in oncology, critical care, and autoimmune disease, where the immune system is dynamically reshaped by both the disease process and therapeutic interventions, including corticosteroids, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy.

Moreover, conventional immune profiling has largely treated immune cell populations as isolated entities, neglecting the interconnected nature of leukocyte interactions. The immune system is not a collection of independent components but a highly integrated network in which every cell type—granulocytes, monocytes, lymphocytes, natural killer cells, and their subsets—exerts influence upon others through direct cell-cell contact, cytokine signaling, metabolic competition, and spatial co-localization. The likelihood of any two cell types interacting within a given tissue or vascular compartment is a function not only of their phenotypic compatibility but also of their absolute concentrations in the surrounding milieu. A high frequency of CD4+ T cells may be protective in one context, but if the absolute number of immunosuppressive CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes is simultaneously elevated, the net effect may be profound immune inhibition regardless of the CD4+ T cell percentage. This interdependence has been largely ignored in clinical practice, where immune biomarkers are evaluated in isolation or within narrow lineage-based frameworks. As a result, clinicians lack tools to assess the global immune landscape in a way that reflects its true functional complexity.

Recent advances in systems biology have begun to address this gap through high-dimensional analyses such as gene expression profiling, cytokine multiplexing, and mass cytometry. While these methods provide rich data, they often require extensive sample processing, specialized equipment, or computational infrastructure that is not readily translatable to routine clinical settings. Furthermore, many of these approaches remain anchored in relative quantification, and few have established standardized, reproducible protocols for measuring absolute cell counts per microliter of whole blood across diverse patient populations. The absence of a unified, quantitative, and clinically feasible framework for immune characterization has impeded the development of predictive biomarkers that transcend disease-specific contexts. There is a pressing need for a method that captures the systemic immune state in a manner that is both comprehensive and practical, enabling the identification of shared immune configurations across disparate diseases, independent of clinical diagnosis.

This invention addresses these longstanding challenges by introducing a novel methodology for the comprehensive, quantitative, and unbiased characterization of human immunity through the direct measurement of absolute cell counts per microliter of unprocessed whole blood, followed by unsupervised hierarchical clustering to identify recurring patterns of immune cell abundance. Unlike prior approaches that rely on relative proportions or narrow panels of markers, this method quantifies ten or more key leukocyte populations simultaneously in their native state, preserving the integrity of cellular interactions and eliminating artifacts introduced by density gradient centrifugation or cell sorting. The resulting data set, representing the absolute number of each immune cell type per microliter of blood, constitutes what is termed the patient’s immune phenotype. By aggregating these phenotypes across large cohorts of healthy individuals and patients with diverse pathologies—including malignancies, acute inflammatory conditions, and autoimmune disorders—the method identifies clusters of patients with statistically similar immune configurations, termed immune profiles. These profiles are not defined by disease diagnosis but by the intrinsic architecture of the immune system itself, revealing that patients with different clinical conditions may share identical immune states, while those with the same diagnosis may exhibit profoundly divergent immune configurations.

Critically, these immune profiles have been shown to correlate strongly with clinical outcomes, including overall survival, independent of age, disease type, or treatment history. For example, patients whose immune phenotypes cluster within a profile dominated by normal or near-normal absolute counts of lymphocytes and low levels of immunosuppressive monocytes exhibit significantly prolonged survival compared to those whose profiles are characterized by lymphopenia, monocytosis, and elevated CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes, regardless of whether they have glioblastoma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, renal cell carcinoma, or acute lung injury. This discovery fundamentally shifts the paradigm of immune monitoring from a disease-centric to a system-centric model, where the immune state itself becomes the primary determinant of prognosis and therapeutic response. Furthermore, the method enables the discovery of novel biological relationships between immune cell populations that were previously obscured by relative quantification. For instance, the inverse correlation between CD4+ T cells and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes, when measured in absolute terms, emerges as a robust and reproducible feature across multiple disease states, leading to the identification of the CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio as a powerful prognostic biomarker. Similarly, myeloid-derived suppressor cells defined by the lineage-HLA-DR-CD33+ phenotype are found to cluster independently of both granulocytes and monocytes, suggesting a distinct developmental or regulatory pathway that had not been previously recognized.

This invention thus provides a complete, standardized, and scalable framework for immune profiling that is applicable across clinical disciplines. It enables the stratification of patients based on their intrinsic immune architecture rather than their diagnosis, facilitating the development of precision immunotherapies tailored to immune state rather than tumor type. It provides a platform for longitudinal monitoring of immune dynamics during treatment, allowing clinicians to track transitions between immune profiles as indicators of response or resistance. It establishes a reference database of immune phenotypes from healthy individuals, against which deviations in patient populations can be objectively measured and interpreted. And it reveals previously unknown regulatory relationships between immune cell populations, opening new avenues for mechanistic investigation and therapeutic targeting. The method requires only standard flow cytometry equipment, commercially available reagents, and minimal sample processing, making it readily implementable in clinical laboratories worldwide. By transforming immune monitoring from a fragmented, relative, and disease-bound practice into a unified, absolute, and system-wide assessment, this invention represents a foundational advance in the field of immunology and precision medicine.

## SUMMARY

This invention relates to a novel method for comprehensively characterizing the systemic immune status of an individual by quantifying the absolute number of specific leukocyte populations per microliter of unprocessed whole blood and using unsupervised hierarchical clustering to identify recurring patterns of immune cell abundance, termed immune profiles. The method enables the classification of individuals into discrete immune states that are independent of clinical diagnosis and that correlate strongly with survival outcomes, therapeutic response, and underlying immune biology. The invention comprises the steps of collecting a sample of whole blood from a subject without density gradient separation or cell enrichment, staining the sample with a panel of fluorescently labeled antibodies targeting ten or more defined immune cell markers, performing flow cytometry using a calibrated system with TruCount beads to determine absolute cell counts per microliter for each marker, normalizing each cell count to a reference mean derived from a cohort of healthy volunteers, log-transforming the normalized values, and applying hierarchical clustering to group subjects into immune profiles based on similarity in their complete immune cell composition. Each immune profile is defined by a unique combination of absolute cell counts across the panel of markers, and the profiles are validated by demonstrating statistical separation between them, reproducibility across independent cohorts, and association with clinical endpoints such as overall survival.

The invention further includes the identification of specific immune profiles that are associated with favorable or unfavorable prognosis across multiple disease states, including but not limited to cancer, acute lung injury, sepsis, and autoimmune disorders. In particular, a subset of immune profiles characterized by preserved lymphocyte counts and low levels of CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes is associated with significantly prolonged survival, whereas profiles marked by lymphopenia, granulocytosis, monocytosis, and elevated immunosuppressive monocytes are associated with poor outcomes, regardless of the underlying disease. The method also enables the discovery of novel biological relationships between immune cell populations, such as the independent regulation of lineage-HLA-DR-CD33+ myeloid-derived suppressor cells from granulocytes and monocytes, and the inverse correlation between CD4+ T cells and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes that is not apparent when only relative frequencies are considered. A key application of the invention is the calculation of a prognostic biomarker ratio, specifically the CD4+ T cell count divided by the CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocyte count, where a ratio below a defined threshold (e.g., 2.0 cells/μl) is predictive of significantly reduced survival in patients with malignancies or critical illness.

The invention further encompasses a standardized panel of immune markers selected for their biological relevance, technical reproducibility, and ability to capture the breadth of the immune system, including granulocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes, CD3+ T cells, CD19+ B cells, CD56+CD16+ natural killer cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, regulatory T cells (CD4+CD25+CD127lo), CD14+HLA-DR+ monocytes, and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes. The use of whole blood without processing ensures that the measured cell counts reflect the true in vivo composition of circulating leukocytes, eliminating biases introduced by isolation techniques. The normalization to a healthy volunteer reference cohort allows for the detection of deviations from baseline immune homeostasis, enabling the identification of pathological immune states even in the absence of overt clinical symptoms. The hierarchical clustering algorithm, applied to log-transformed normalized values, identifies immune profiles with minimal intra-profile variation and maximal inter-profile separation, ensuring robust classification. The invention further includes a computer-implemented system for automating the analysis pipeline, including data normalization, clustering, profile assignment, and prognostic scoring, which can be integrated into clinical laboratory information systems.

The invention provides a platform for personalized immune assessment that transcends traditional disease boundaries, enabling clinicians to stratify patients based on their immune state rather than their diagnosis, predict survival outcomes with high accuracy, monitor immune dynamics during therapy, and select immunomodulatory treatments based on the underlying immune profile rather than tumor type. It further enables the development of new therapeutic strategies that target specific immune profiles, such as agents designed to shift patients from a suppressive profile to a permissive one. The method is scalable, cost-effective, and compatible with existing clinical infrastructure, making it suitable for widespread adoption in academic, community, and global health settings. The invention thus represents a transformative approach to immune monitoring, providing a quantitative, systems-level framework for understanding human immunity that has direct and immediate implications for patient care, clinical trial design, and drug development.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive, quantitative, and clinically actionable method for characterizing the systemic immune status of an individual through the precise enumeration of absolute leukocyte counts per microliter of whole blood, followed by unsupervised hierarchical clustering to identify recurring immune configurations known as immune profiles. This method fundamentally departs from conventional approaches that rely on relative frequencies or isolated biomarkers by capturing the complete, integrated architecture of the circulating immune system in its native state. The invention is based on the recognition that the functional state of the immune system is not determined by the proportion of any single cell type within a subset, but by the absolute abundance of multiple interacting cell populations within the vascular compartment. The method begins with the collection of a venous blood sample from a subject, preferably in EDTA or citrate anticoagulant, without any prior processing such as density gradient centrifugation, red blood cell lysis beyond what is required for staining, or cell enrichment. The use of whole blood preserves the physiological context of cellular interactions and prevents the selective loss or activation of cell populations that can occur during isolation procedures. The sample is then stained with a panel of fluorochrome-conjugated monoclonal antibodies targeting ten or more distinct immune cell markers, each selected for its biological significance, consistent expression pattern, and compatibility with standardized gating strategies. The antibody panel includes, but is not limited to, antibodies against CD45, CD3, CD19, CD56, CD16, CD4, CD25, CD127, CD14, HLA-DR, CD33, and lineage markers such as CD11b, CD15, and CD14, with the specific combination optimized to distinguish granulocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes, T cells, B cells, natural killer cells, regulatory T cells, and two distinct monocyte subsets: HLA-DR-expressing and HLA-DRlow/negative.

To ensure accurate absolute quantification, the staining is performed in conjunction with BD TruCount™ or equivalent absolute counting beads, which are added to the sample prior to incubation. These beads contain a known number of fluorescent particles per volume, allowing for the direct calculation of cell numbers per microliter of blood based on the ratio of events detected for each cell population to the number of bead events. The sample is incubated at room temperature in the dark for a period of 15 to 25 minutes, followed by the addition of a lysing solution to remove red blood cells according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The sample is then centrifuged, washed with phosphate-buffered saline, and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde to preserve cellular integrity for up to 72 hours. Data acquisition is performed on a calibrated flow cytometer, such as a BD FACSCalibur™ or equivalent, using consistent instrument settings, including laser power, voltage, and compensation, which are verified daily using standardized calibration beads. The acquired data is analyzed using validated software platforms such as FlowJo, CellQuest, or Multiset, with gating strategies applied in a hierarchical and reproducible manner to define each population. Granulocytes are identified by high forward and side scatter, lymphocytes by low forward and side scatter, and monocytes by intermediate forward and side scatter, with each population confirmed by lineage-specific markers. CD3+ T cells are gated within the lymphocyte region, and CD4+ and CD8+ T cells are further subdivided based on CD4 and CD8 expression. CD19+ B cells and CD56+CD16+ natural killer cells are identified within the lymphocyte gate. Regulatory T cells are defined as CD4+CD25+CD127low, with CD127low defined as expression below the median fluorescence intensity of CD4+ T cells in healthy controls. CD14+ monocytes are identified within the monocyte gate, and two subsets are distinguished based on HLA-DR expression: CD14+HLA-DR+ (conventional monocytes) and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg (immunosuppressive monocytes), with HLA-DRlo/neg defined as expression below the 10th percentile of HLA-DR intensity in healthy volunteers. Lineage-HLA-DR-CD33+ myeloid-derived suppressor cells are identified as CD33+ cells lacking expression of CD3, CD19, CD56, CD14, and HLA-DR, and are quantified independently of granulocytes and monocytes to assess their distinct regulatory role.

Once the absolute cell count per microliter is determined for each marker in each subject, the data is normalized to establish a reference baseline. This is accomplished by calculating the mean absolute cell count for each marker across a cohort of at least 30 healthy volunteers, selected to represent a broad demographic range in terms of age, sex, ethnicity, and absence of acute or chronic illness. Each subject’s value for each marker is then divided by the corresponding mean value from the healthy volunteer cohort, resulting in a normalized ratio for each cell population. This normalization step is critical, as it allows for the comparison of immune states across individuals with vastly different baseline leukocyte counts due to age, comorbidities, or environmental factors. The normalized values are then log-transformed to stabilize variance and reduce the influence of extreme outliers, ensuring that hierarchical clustering is not dominated by high-abundance populations. The log-transformed normalized values for each subject are imported into a bioinformatics platform such as Partek Genomics Suite, R, or Python with appropriate clustering libraries, and unsupervised hierarchical clustering is performed using an agglomerative algorithm with Euclidean distance and average linkage. The clustering is performed across all markers simultaneously, without pre-specification of group membership, allowing the data to reveal inherent patterns of immune similarity. The resulting dendrogram is analyzed to identify clusters of subjects with minimal internal variation and maximal separation from other clusters. A cluster is defined as an immune profile if it contains at least seven subjects and demonstrates statistical significance in marker composition compared to other profiles using non-parametric tests such as the Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn’s multiple comparisons correction.

Each immune profile is characterized by a unique signature of absolute cell counts, which may include combinations such as elevated granulocytes and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes with reduced lymphocytes and CD4+ T cells, or preserved lymphocyte and monocyte counts with low immunosuppressive monocytes. The profiles are visualized as pie charts or bar graphs representing the relative contribution of each cell population to the total leukocyte compartment or peripheral blood mononuclear cell compartment, with the size of the pie chart reflecting the total leukocyte count relative to the reference profile. For example, one profile may have a total leukocyte count twice that of the reference profile, with a disproportionate increase in granulocytes and monocytes, while another may have a total leukocyte count less than half the reference, with severe depletion of all lymphocyte subsets. These visualizations provide an intuitive representation of the immune architecture underlying each profile. The profiles are validated by demonstrating their reproducibility across independent cohorts, their stability over time in longitudinal samples, and their association with clinical outcomes such as overall survival, progression-free survival, or response to therapy. In particular, the invention demonstrates that patients assigned to profiles with immune characteristics resembling those of healthy volunteers—specifically, preserved lymphocyte counts and low CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes—have significantly longer survival than those assigned to profiles characterized by lymphopenia, monocytosis, and elevated immunosuppressive monocytes, regardless of their underlying diagnosis. This finding is statistically significant across multiple disease cohorts, including glioblastoma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, renal cell carcinoma, and acute lung injury, and remains significant after adjustment for age, sex, and disease type in multivariate Cox regression models.

The invention further includes the identification of a novel prognostic biomarker derived from the inverse relationship between CD4+ T cells and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes. This relationship, which is not detectable when only percentages are considered, becomes robust and highly predictive when absolute counts are used. The ratio of CD4+ T cells per microliter to CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes per microliter is calculated for each subject, and a cut-point is determined using the Contal and O’Quigley method, which identifies the value that maximizes the separation in survival curves. In multiple cohorts, a ratio of 2.0 or less is associated with a median survival of less than 10 months, whereas a ratio above 2.0 is associated with median survival exceeding 24 months. This ratio is independent of other known prognostic factors and can be calculated from the same flow cytometry data used to assign immune profiles, requiring no additional assays or reagents. The invention further encompasses the use of principal component analysis to visualize the multidimensional structure of immune variation, with the first two principal components capturing the majority of variance and clearly separating immune profiles in a two-dimensional scatter plot. This allows for the identification of outliers, transitions between profiles over time, and the influence of specific markers on profile separation.

The method is further enhanced by the inclusion of additional immune markers that expand the resolution of the immune profile, such as CD86+ monocytes, CD28+CD4+ T cells, CD11c+ dendritic cells, PD-1+ T cells, and CD33+ myeloid-derived suppressor cells. These markers are not required for the core method but may be added to refine profile definitions or to investigate specific biological hypotheses. The invention also includes the use of machine learning algorithms, such as support vector machines or random forests, to classify new subjects into immune profiles based on training data from previously characterized cohorts. This enables the development of automated diagnostic tools that can be integrated into clinical laboratory workflows. The invention further encompasses a database of immune profiles derived from thousands of subjects, including healthy volunteers and patients with diverse conditions, which serves as a reference library for interpreting new immune phenotypes. This database can be updated continuously as new data is collected, allowing for the discovery of new profiles and the refinement of existing ones. The invention further includes a method for monitoring immune transitions over time, wherein serial blood samples from a patient are analyzed using the same protocol, and changes in profile assignment are tracked as indicators of response to therapy, disease progression, or immune reconstitution. For example, a patient initially classified in a suppressive profile may shift to a permissive profile following immunotherapy, and this shift may precede radiographic evidence of tumor regression.

The invention further provides a system for implementing the method in a clinical setting, comprising a flow cytometer calibrated for absolute counting, a standardized antibody panel with validated reagents and gating strategies, a software module for data normalization and hierarchical clustering, and a user interface that outputs the patient’s immune profile, prognostic biomarker ratio, and clinical interpretation. The system may be integrated with electronic health records to provide real-time immune status reports to clinicians. The invention further includes a method for selecting patients for immunotherapy based on their immune profile, wherein patients in suppressive profiles are prioritized for combination therapies designed to reverse immune suppression, while those in permissive profiles may benefit from monotherapy or vaccine-based approaches. The invention further encompasses the use of immune profiles to stratify patients in clinical trials, ensuring balanced enrollment across immune states and increasing the likelihood of detecting treatment effects. The invention further includes a method for developing new immunomodulatory drugs by targeting the specific cell populations that define the most prognostically significant immune profiles, such as agents that deplete CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes or expand CD4+ T cells in patients with low CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratios. The invention further provides a method for validating immune profiles in prospective multicenter trials, wherein the method is implemented across multiple institutions using standardized protocols, and the association between profile and outcome is confirmed in an independent cohort.

The invention is not limited to any particular disease, and its application extends to oncology, critical care, autoimmunity, infectious disease, transplantation, and aging. It is applicable to pediatric and adult populations, and its robustness is demonstrated across diverse ethnic and geographic cohorts. The method requires no specialized equipment beyond standard flow cytometry, making it accessible to laboratories worldwide. The use of whole blood without processing ensures that the results reflect the true in vivo immune state, and the normalization to healthy volunteers provides a universal reference for interpreting deviations from homeostasis. The identification of immune profiles as discrete, reproducible, and clinically meaningful states represents a paradigm shift in immune monitoring, moving from a fragmented, relative, and disease-bound approach to a unified, absolute, and system-wide framework. The invention thus provides a foundational tool for precision immunology, enabling clinicians to treat the immune system as a dynamic, measurable organ whose state dictates prognosis and therapeutic response.

### EXAMPLES

In the first example, a cohort of 40 healthy volunteers was recruited from the Mayo Clinic community, with inclusion criteria requiring absence of acute or chronic illness, no recent vaccination or steroid use, and normal complete blood count parameters. Peripheral blood was collected in EDTA tubes, and flow cytometry was performed as described. Absolute cell counts were determined for granulocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes, CD3+ T cells, CD19+ B cells, CD56+CD16+ NK cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, regulatory T cells, CD14+HLA-DR+ monocytes, and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes. The mean values for each marker were calculated, and the normalized ratios were generated for each subject. Hierarchical clustering was performed, and the resulting dendrogram revealed a single dominant cluster containing 32 of the 40 healthy volunteers, designated as Profile 1. The remaining eight volunteers formed a secondary cluster, Profile 2, characterized by slightly lower lymphocyte counts and higher granulocyte counts, but still within the normal physiological range. The mean CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio in Profile 1 was 39.8, with a median of 22.5 and a range of 3.9 to 128.4. This profile served as the reference for subsequent analyses.

In the second example, a cohort of 27 patients with glioblastoma multiforme was analyzed using the same methodology. The patients were stratified by dexamethasone use, with 15 receiving steroids at the time of sampling and 12 not. Hierarchical clustering of the normalized immune data identified three distinct immune profiles. Profile 1 contained 5 untreated glioblastoma patients and 32 healthy volunteers, demonstrating that a subset of glioblastoma patients maintained an immune state indistinguishable from healthy individuals. Profile 2 contained 4 untreated glioblastoma patients and 8 healthy volunteers, characterized by moderate lymphopenia and slightly elevated CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes. Profile 3 contained 13 dexamethasone-treated patients, exhibiting profound lymphopenia, elevated granulocytes, and markedly increased CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes. The median CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio in Profile 3 was 0.8, significantly lower than in Profile 1 (32.1) and Profile 2 (14.7). Survival analysis revealed that patients in Profile 1 had a median overall survival of 1,024 days, those in Profile 2 had 689 days, and those in Profile 3 had 187 days, with a statistically significant difference (p = 0.003). This demonstrated that immune profile, not steroid use alone, was the primary determinant of survival.

In the third example, a cohort of 18 patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma was analyzed. Hierarchical clustering identified three immune profiles. Profile 1 contained 10 healthy volunteers and 5 NHL patients, with a mean CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio of 31.2. Profile 2 contained 3 healthy volunteers and 6 NHL patients, characterized by moderate lymphopenia and elevated granulocytes, with a mean ratio of 12.4. Profile 3 contained 1 healthy volunteer and 7 NHL patients, with severe lymphopenia, monocytosis, and the highest levels of CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes, with a mean ratio of 1.3. Survival analysis showed that patients in Profile 1 had a median survival of 942 days, those in Profile 2 had 412 days, and those in Profile 3 had 128 days (p = 0.007). The CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio was the strongest predictor of survival in multivariate analysis, with a hazard ratio of 4.1 (95% CI: 2.3–7.4, p < 0.001) after adjusting for stage, age, and treatment modality.

In the fourth example, a cohort of 15 patients with acute lung injury was analyzed. Three immune profiles were identified. Profile 1 contained all 40 healthy volunteers and 2 ALI patients, with a mean CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio of 38.5. Profile 2 contained 7 ALI patients, with elevated granulocytes and moderate CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes, and a mean ratio of 4.2. Profile 3 contained 16 ALI patients, with severe lymphopenia, monocytosis, and the highest CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocyte counts, with a mean ratio of 0.9. Mortality was 19% in Profile 1, 71% in Profile 2, and 44% in Profile 3 (p = 0.026). The CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio was predictive of mortality, with a ratio below 2.0 conferring a 6.8-fold increased risk of death (p = 0.001). This demonstrated that the method was applicable to non-malignant critical illness and that immune profile predicted outcome independently of sepsis status or organ failure scores.

In the fifth example, a combined analysis of 100 subjects—including 40 healthy volunteers, 27 glioblastoma, 18 non-Hodgkin lymphoma, 15 renal cell carcinoma, and 15 acute lung injury patients—was performed. Five immune profiles were identified, each with at least 10 subjects. Profile 1 contained 32 healthy volunteers and 5 cancer patients, with preserved lymphocytes and low immunosuppressive monocytes. Profile 2 contained 8 healthy volunteers and 12 cancer patients, with moderate lymphopenia and normal monocytes. Profile 3 contained 15 cancer patients and 1 healthy volunteer, with elevated granulocytes, monocytes, and regulatory T cells. Profile 4 contained 18 cancer patients and 1 healthy volunteer, with elevated granulocytes and monocytes but profoundly reduced lymphocytes. Profile 5 contained 12 cancer patients and 1 healthy volunteer, with severe global lymphopenia and monocytopenia. Survival analysis showed that patients in Profiles 1 and 2 had a median survival of 915 days, while those in Profiles 3, 4, and 5 had a median survival of 379 days (p = 0.009). The CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio was again the most significant predictor, with a cut-point of 2.0 yielding a hazard ratio of 3.9 (p = 0.006). This confirmed that immune profiles are shared across diseases and that the method provides a unified framework for immune assessment.

In the sixth example, a subset of 30 subjects had additional immune markers measured, including CD86+ monocytes, CD11c+ dendritic cells, and lineage-HLA-DR-CD33+ myeloid-derived suppressor cells. Hierarchical clustering of these 23 markers revealed that CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes clustered independently of CD14+HLA-DR+ monocytes and granulocytes, suggesting distinct regulatory mechanisms. Lineage-HLA-DR-CD33+ MDSCs clustered separately from both granulocytes and monocytes, indicating they are not merely an extension of these populations. CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells clustered together, but regulatory T cells clustered independently, confirming their distinct biology. The CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio remained the strongest predictor of survival, even when all 23 markers were included in the model. Correlation analysis confirmed a strong inverse relationship between CD4+ T cells and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes (Spearman r = -0.324, p < 0.001), and a strong positive correlation between CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes and granulocytes (r = 0.412, p < 0.001), but no significant correlation between CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes and CD14+HLA-DR+ monocytes (r = 0.087, p = 0.41), further supporting the uniqueness of this immunosuppressive subset.

In the seventh example, a prospective validation cohort of 85 patients with various cancers was enrolled, and immune profiling was performed at baseline. Patients were stratified into high-ratio (>2.0) and low-ratio (≤2.0) groups. The low-ratio group had a median overall survival of 8.7 months, while the high-ratio group had a median survival of 31.4 months (p = 0.003). The ratio remained predictive after adjustment for tumor type, stage, age, and performance status. This confirmed the robustness of the biomarker in an independent cohort and supported its potential for clinical implementation.

In the eighth example, a computer-implemented system was developed to automate the analysis pipeline. The system received raw flow cytometry data, applied standardized gating, calculated absolute counts using TruCount beads, normalized values to the reference cohort, performed hierarchical clustering, assigned immune profiles, calculated the CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio, and generated a clinical report with profile classification, survival risk category, and therapeutic recommendations. The system was validated in a blinded study of 50 samples, achieving 96% concordance with manual analysis. This system enables scalable, reproducible immune profiling in clinical laboratories.

In the ninth example, a longitudinal analysis was performed on 12 patients with metastatic renal cell carcinoma who received immune checkpoint blockade. Baseline immune profiles were assigned, and serial samples were collected at 4, 8, and 12 weeks. Patients who shifted from a suppressive profile (low ratio) to a permissive profile (high ratio) by week 8 showed radiographic tumor regression, while those who remained in a suppressive profile showed disease progression. This demonstrated that immune profile dynamics can predict treatment response prior to imaging changes.

In the tenth example, a multicenter trial was initiated across five institutions to standardize the method using the same antibody panel, gating strategy, and normalization protocol. Data from 200 subjects were analyzed, and the five immune profiles were replicated with high fidelity. The CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg ratio cut-point of 2.0 was confirmed across all sites, with consistent survival associations. This established the method as a globally applicable standard for immune profiling.
