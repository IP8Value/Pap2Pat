## BACKGROUND

The analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing data has emerged as a transformative approach for dissecting cellular heterogeneity, identifying rare cell populations, and reconstructing developmental trajectories in complex biological systems. Despite significant advances in high-throughput sequencing technologies and computational methods, the interpretation of single-cell transcriptomes remains hindered by several persistent technical and conceptual challenges. Chief among these is the pervasive issue of dropout events—instances in which transcripts are present in a cell but fail to be detected due to technical limitations in library preparation, amplification efficiency, or sequencing depth. These spurious zeros distort the true expression landscape, leading to inaccurate clustering, misleading dimensionality reductions, and erroneous inferences about lineage relationships. Conventional methods such as principal component analysis (PCA) and its derivatives, while widely adopted, are fundamentally ill-suited to handle the non-negative, count-based nature of RNA-seq data and the asymmetric noise structure introduced by dropouts. These methods assume Gaussian noise distributions and linear relationships, which are inconsistent with the biological reality of gene expression as a discrete, stochastic, and highly sparse process. Furthermore, existing algorithms for pseudotime inference and trajectory reconstruction, including Monocle, Wishbone, and Mpath, often impose rigid tree-like topologies that assume a single progenitor giving rise to multiple distinct lineages, thereby failing to capture the dynamic, multi-faceted, and asynchronous nature of differentiation in developing tissues. These approaches are also insensitive to the combinatorial expression patterns that define progenitor states, where cells simultaneously express genes associated with multiple potential fates, and they lack a principled mechanism to quantify the degree of cellular plasticity or commitment. As a result, critical intermediate cell states—particularly those representing multipotent progenitors poised at lineage bifurcation points—are frequently missed, misclassified, or obscured by dominant transcriptional programs. In the context of embryonic development, where precise temporal and spatial coordination of lineage specification governs organogenesis, the inability to accurately resolve the continuum from pluripotency to commitment has impeded the discovery of novel regulatory mechanisms and the identification of key molecular drivers of cell fate decisions. Moreover, current methods do not integrate the spatial organization of cellular states into their analytical framework, treating each cell as an isolated data point rather than a node in a continuous, topologically structured differentiation landscape. This limitation prevents the visualization of developmental trajectories as smooth, gradient-like paths analogous to Waddington’s epigenetic landscape, where cells transition through states of increasing commitment along energetically favorable routes. The absence of a unified framework that simultaneously accounts for technical noise, quantifies differentiation potential, and maps lineage relationships in a biologically interpretable, two-dimensional space has created a critical gap in the field of single-cell genomics. There is therefore a compelling need for a novel analytical pipeline that overcomes these limitations by incorporating a probabilistic model of dropout events, introducing a quantitative metric of cellular multipotency, and organizing cells into a structured metacell landscape that preserves both local similarity and global developmental directionality. Such a method would enable researchers to not only identify known cell types with greater accuracy but also discover previously unrecognized progenitor populations, predict their lineage potential, and infer the signaling pathways that govern their fate decisions—all from a single, coherent computational framework.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel computational pipeline, referred to herein as the Dpath pipeline, for the analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing data that overcomes the limitations of existing methods by integrating weighted Poisson non-negative matrix factorization, self-organizing map-based metacell organization, and random walk with restart-based trajectory inference. The pipeline enables the identification of latent transcriptional programs—termed metagenes—that represent coherent biological functions such as lineage specification, and assigns each cell a probabilistic signature of these metagenes through non-negative coefficient vectors. Unlike conventional dimensionality reduction techniques, the Dpath pipeline explicitly models dropout events using a weighted Poisson likelihood function, thereby preserving the integrity of low-expression signals and enhancing the separation of biologically distinct cell populations. The invention further introduces a novel quantitative metric, termed metagene entropy, which measures the uncertainty in a cell’s metagene expression profile and serves as a robust indicator of cellular differentiation potential: high entropy corresponds to multipotent progenitor states, while low entropy reflects lineage-committed cells. These metagene signatures are then mapped onto a two-dimensional hexagonal grid of metacells using a self-organizing map algorithm, creating a topologically organized landscape where neighboring metacells exhibit similar transcriptional profiles and spatial position correlates with developmental progression. The metacell landscape is further refined by partitioning into discrete clusters using partitioning around medoids, enabling the identification of major cellular populations and their hierarchical relationships. A heterogeneous graph is constructed to model transitions between metagenes and metacells, with directed edges defined by metagene entropy gradients, ensuring that flow follows the direction of increasing commitment. A random walk with restart algorithm is then applied to this graph to prioritize metacells as either progenitor or committed states based on their connectivity and entropy profile, thereby inferring the most probable developmental trajectories from multipotent progenitors to terminally differentiated lineages. The invention further enables the ranking of genes according to their correlation with these prioritized states, allowing for the identification of novel marker genes and regulatory drivers of lineage specification. The pipeline has been validated in the context of murine embryonic development using a dataset of 281 Etv2-EYFP+ cells across three developmental time points, where it successfully resolved the endothelial, haematopoietic, and endocardial lineages, identified a previously unrecognized population of haemato-endothelial progenitors expressing Sox7 and Runx1, predicted the existence of endocardial cushion progenitors undergoing endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition, and correctly inferred the temporal order of gene expression during primitive haematopoiesis. Functional validation using embryonic stem cell differentiation assays confirmed the critical role of sonic hedgehog signaling in regulating the emergence of these progenitor populations. The Dpath pipeline is implemented as a standalone software package written in the R programming language, fully documented, and freely distributable, enabling widespread adoption by the research community. The invention provides a paradigm shift in single-cell analysis by replacing rigid, tree-based models with a continuous, entropy-driven, topologically structured framework that accurately recapitulates biological development, identifies novel progenitor states, and uncovers regulatory mechanisms with unprecedented precision.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed below. A “metagene” refers to a latent transcriptional program derived from the decomposition of a single-cell gene expression matrix, represented as a non-negative vector of gene weights that collectively define a biological function such as lineage specification, cellular state, or developmental process. A “metagene basis” is the matrix whose columns are the individual metagenes, each column representing the relative contribution of each gene to that metagene. A “metagene coefficient” is a non-negative, unit-sum vector assigned to each cell that indicates the relative weight or proportion of each metagene expressed in that cell. “Metagene entropy” is a quantitative measure of the uncertainty or diversity in a cell’s metagene coefficient vector, calculated using the Shannon entropy formula applied to the normalized metagene weights, and serves as a proxy for cellular multipotency or differentiation potential, with higher entropy indicating a greater number of active lineage programs and thus a more progenitor-like state. A “metacell” is a representative unit on a two-dimensional self-organizing map that aggregates multiple single cells with similar metagene expression profiles, such that each metacell corresponds to a prototype expression signature and occupies a spatial position that reflects its transcriptional similarity to neighboring metacells. A “self-organizing map” or “SOM” is an unsupervised machine learning algorithm that projects high-dimensional data onto a low-dimensional, typically hexagonal or rectangular, grid while preserving topological relationships, such that cells with similar transcriptional profiles are mapped to adjacent or nearby metacells. A “heterogeneous metagene–metacell graph” is a directed probabilistic graph structure comprising two types of nodes—metagenes and metacells—with edges representing transition probabilities between them, where the directionality of edges is determined by metagene entropy gradients, ensuring that transitions flow from higher to lower entropy states. A “random walk with restart” or “RWR” is a stochastic algorithm in which a virtual walker moves probabilistically through the graph, occasionally returning to a predefined starting node, and the steady-state probability distribution over the nodes is used to rank their relevance to the starting node; in this invention, RWR is applied to prioritize metacells as progenitor or committed states based on their connectivity to metagenes representing specific lineages. A “developmental trajectory” is a path through the metacell landscape that connects a progenitor metacell to one or more committed metacells, representing the most probable sequence of transcriptional transitions a cell undergoes during differentiation. A “committed state” refers to a metacell or cell population whose metagene entropy is low and whose metagene coefficients are dominated by a single or a small number of metagenes corresponding to a specific lineage, such as endothelial, haematopoietic, or endocardial. A “progenitor state” refers to a metacell or cell population whose metagene entropy is high and whose metagene coefficients reflect the co-expression of multiple metagenes associated with distinct lineages, indicating multipotency and the capacity to differentiate into multiple fates. A “weighted Poisson non-negative matrix factorization” or “wp-NMF” is a matrix decomposition technique that factorizes a non-negative gene expression matrix into a product of two non-negative matrices—the metagene basis and the metagene coefficients—while incorporating cell- and gene-specific weights derived from a weighted Poisson likelihood model that accounts for the probability of dropout events. A “dropout event” is a technical artifact in single-cell RNA sequencing wherein a transcript is present in a cell but fails to be detected due to stochastic sampling, amplification bias, or low capture efficiency, resulting in a zero count despite biological expression. A “gene enrichment score” is a metric that quantifies the association between a gene’s expression level across metacells and the steady-state probability of those metacells being prioritized as a specific cellular state, calculated as the weighted sum of expression levels, where the weights are the normalized steady-state probabilities from the RWR algorithm. A “transcriptional program” is a coordinated set of genes whose co-expression defines a biological function, such as a developmental process, signaling pathway, or cellular identity, and is captured by a metagene in this invention. A “lineage bifurcation point” is a developmental stage or cellular state at which a multipotent progenitor gives rise to two or more distinct committed lineages, and which is identified in this invention as a metacell with high entropy and connections to multiple downstream committed metacells. A “pseudotime” is a computational estimate of the relative developmental progression of cells along a trajectory, independent of chronological time, and in this invention is derived from the position of cells along the RWR-inferred trajectories through the metacell landscape. A “cellular plasticity” refers to the capacity of a cell to adopt multiple lineage fates, and is quantified in this invention by metagene entropy. A “topological organization” refers to the spatial arrangement of metacells on the self-organizing map such that the Euclidean distance between any two metacells reflects the similarity of their metagene expression profiles. A “partitioning around medoids” or “PAM” is a clustering algorithm that partitions a set of objects into a specified number of clusters by minimizing the sum of dissimilarities between each object and its assigned cluster medoid, used in this invention to segment the metacell landscape into discrete cell populations. A “transition probability matrix” is a square matrix whose entries represent the likelihood of moving from one node to another in the heterogeneous metagene–metacell graph, calculated based on the similarity of metagene coefficients and the directionality imposed by metagene entropy gradients. A “steady-state probability” is the long-term probability distribution over the nodes of a Markov chain, reached after an infinite number of steps, and in this invention represents the likelihood that a random walker will be found at a given metacell, thereby ranking its relevance to the specified cellular state. A “biological validation” refers to experimental confirmation of computational predictions using techniques such as immunohistochemistry, fluorescence-activated cell sorting, or functional assays in model systems. A “software package” refers to a collection of computer-executable instructions, data files, and documentation implemented in the R programming language that enables the full execution of the Dpath pipeline on user-provided single-cell RNA sequencing datasets.

### ASPECTS OF THE INVENTION

The invention comprises multiple interdependent aspects that collectively form a novel and non-obvious analytical framework for single-cell transcriptomic analysis. The first aspect is a method for decomposing a single-cell gene expression matrix into metagenes using weighted Poisson non-negative matrix factorization, wherein the decomposition is performed under non-negativity constraints and incorporates a cell- and gene-specific weighting scheme derived from a weighted Poisson likelihood function that models dropout events as a low-magnitude Poisson process superimposed on the true expression signal. This weighting scheme assigns higher weights to expression values with a high probability of being genuine and lower weights to those likely to represent technical dropouts, thereby improving the accuracy of metagene recovery in the presence of sparsity. The second aspect is the calculation of metagene entropy for each cell as the Shannon entropy of its normalized metagene coefficient vector, which provides a quantitative, continuous measure of cellular multipotency that correlates inversely with differentiation state and enables the discrimination of progenitor from committed cells without reliance on predefined marker genes. The third aspect is the organization of cells into a two-dimensional metacell landscape via a self-organizing map algorithm, wherein each metacell represents a prototype metagene expression profile and the spatial arrangement of metacells reflects their transcriptional similarity, such that developmental progression is visualized as a continuous gradient from high-entropy progenitor regions to low-entropy committed regions. The fourth aspect is the construction of a heterogeneous metagene–metacell graph, wherein directed edges are established between metacells based on metagene entropy gradients and between metagenes and metacells based on expression correlation, creating a probabilistic network that encodes lineage relationships as flows from multipotent to committed states. The fifth aspect is the application of a random walk with restart algorithm to this graph, wherein the algorithm is seeded at metagenes representing specific lineages and iteratively propagates through the network to compute steady-state probabilities for each metacell, thereby prioritizing metacells as progenitor or committed states according to their likelihood of being reached from the seed nodes. The sixth aspect is the computation of gene enrichment scores for each gene based on the correlation between its expression profile across metacells and the steady-state probabilities of those metacells, enabling the ranking of genes according to their association with specific cellular states and the identification of novel lineage markers and regulatory drivers. The seventh aspect is the inference of developmental trajectories as the shortest paths between prioritized progenitor metacells and committed metacells on the metacell landscape, wherein trajectories are constrained to follow the direction of decreasing metagene entropy and are validated by their agreement with known biological timelines and functional assays. The eighth aspect is the implementation of the entire pipeline as a software package in the R programming language, comprising modular functions for wp-NMF, SOM construction, PAM clustering, graph generation, RWR computation, and gene ranking, with built-in visualization tools for metacell landscapes, entropy gradients, and trajectory paths. The ninth aspect is the use of the invention to identify novel progenitor populations, such as a population of Etv2+ cells co-expressing Sox7 and Runx1 that reside at the bifurcation point between haematopoietic and endothelial lineages, and to predict their regulatory dependencies, such as the critical role of sonic hedgehog signaling in their maintenance and differentiation. The tenth aspect is the application of the invention to any single-cell RNA sequencing dataset, regardless of tissue type, developmental stage, or species, provided that the data are in the form of a gene-by-cell expression matrix with counts or normalized expression values, and that the number of cells exceeds a minimum threshold sufficient to support stable metagene decomposition and metacell organization. The eleventh aspect is the use of the invention to generate a comprehensive atlas of cellular states and trajectories for a given biological system, enabling hypothesis generation, target discovery, and experimental validation in regenerative medicine, cancer biology, developmental genetics, and immunology. The twelfth aspect is the integration of the invention with other omics modalities, such as ATAC-seq, proteomics, or spatial transcriptomics, to extend the metagene–metacell framework to multi-layered biological networks and to infer regulatory hierarchies linking chromatin accessibility, gene expression, and cell fate decisions. The thirteenth aspect is the use of the invention to guide the isolation of rare progenitor populations for functional assays, transplantation studies, or in vitro differentiation protocols by identifying unique gene expression signatures associated with high-entropy metacells. The fourteenth aspect is the use of the invention to compare developmental trajectories across conditions, such as wild-type versus mutant embryos, drug-treated versus control cultures, or healthy versus diseased tissues, to identify perturbations in lineage specification and to uncover novel disease-associated progenitor states. The fifteenth aspect is the use of the invention to reconstruct lineage relationships in non-developmental contexts, such as tumor evolution, immune cell differentiation, or cellular reprogramming, where the concept of multipotency and commitment is equally applicable and where existing methods have failed to resolve complex branching dynamics.

### Materials and Methods

The Dpath pipeline was implemented using the R programming language (version 4.1.2) and leveraged established packages for matrix factorization, machine learning, and graph analysis. The input data consisted of a gene-by-cell expression matrix derived from single-cell RNA sequencing experiments, where each entry represented the normalized expression level of a gene in a single cell, typically in transcripts per million (TPM) or log-transformed counts. Prior to analysis, genes with low expression variance across cells were filtered out to reduce technical noise, and cells with fewer than 100,000 mapped reads were excluded to ensure sufficient sequencing depth. The weighted Poisson non-negative matrix factorization (wp-NMF) was performed by first estimating the probability of dropout for each gene-cell pair using a model that assumed the observed expression followed a mixture of a Poisson distribution with mean μ and a low-magnitude Poisson distribution with mean λ₀ = 0.1, representing the dropout event. The weight πₙₘ for gene n in cell m was computed as the posterior probability that the observed expression was not a dropout, derived from Bayes’ theorem using the estimated expression mean and variance across the dataset. The metagene basis matrix U and metagene coefficient matrix V were then optimized iteratively using multiplicative update rules to maximize the weighted log-likelihood function under non-negativity constraints, with initialization provided by non-negative singular value decomposition. The number of metagenes K was determined by performing multiple runs of wp-NMF with varying K values and selecting the value that maximized the cophenetic correlation coefficient of the consensus clustering matrix, indicating stable and reproducible metagene recovery. Metagene entropy for each cell was computed as the Shannon entropy of its normalized metagene coefficient vector, Hₘ = −∑ₖ Vₖₘ log(Vₖₘ), where Vₖₘ was the coefficient of metagene k in cell m, and the sum was constrained to one. The self-organizing map (SOM) was constructed using the Kohonen package in R, with a hexagonal grid of 15 × 15 metacells (225 total), where the input vectors were the mean metagene coefficient profiles across multiple runs of wp-NMF to ensure robustness. The SOM was trained using default parameters, including a Gaussian neighborhood function and a linearly decreasing learning rate over 500 iterations. Metacell entropy was computed as the average entropy of all cells mapped to each metacell. The partitioning around medoids (PAM) algorithm was applied to the metacell coordinates to segment the SOM into discrete clusters, with the number of clusters chosen to ensure each cluster contained at least 15 metacells and remained topologically connected. A heterogeneous metagene–metacell graph was constructed by defining transition probabilities between metacells based on their Euclidean distance on the SOM and the directionality imposed by metagene entropy gradients: an edge was directed from metacell p to metacell q only if the entropy of p was greater than that of q. Transition probabilities from metagenes to metacells and vice versa were computed as the normalized correlation between metagene expression profiles and metacell metagene coefficients. The random walk with restart (RWR) algorithm was implemented using the igraph package, with restart probability set to 0.85 and seeded at metagenes representing specific lineages (e.g., endothelial, haematopoietic, endocardial). Steady-state probabilities were computed until convergence, and metacells were ranked by their RWR scores. Gene enrichment scores were calculated as the weighted sum of a gene’s expression across metacells, with weights equal to the normalized RWR scores of those metacells. Developmental trajectories were inferred as the shortest paths between the highest-ranked progenitor metacells and the highest-ranked committed metacells, using Dijkstra’s algorithm on the directed metacell graph. All analyses were performed on a Linux-based server with 64 GB RAM and 16 CPU cores. The entire pipeline is packaged as an R package named “dpath,” which includes functions for data preprocessing, wp-NMF, SOM construction, PAM clustering, graph generation, RWR computation, trajectory inference, and visualization, with comprehensive documentation, example datasets, and tutorial scripts provided. The software is distributed under an open-source license and is available for download from a public repository.

### Results

The application of the Dpath pipeline to a dataset of 281 Etv2-EYFP+ cells isolated from murine embryos at E7.25, E7.75, and E8.25 revealed a comprehensive and biologically coherent organization of haemato-endothelial lineage development. Weighted Poisson non-negative matrix factorization successfully decomposed the gene expression matrix into four distinct metagenes, each representing a major transcriptional program: MG1 corresponded to endothelial lineage genes such as Plvap, Emcn, and Icam1; MG2 to haematopoietic genes including Gata1, Itga2b, and Hba-a1; MG3 to endocardial and cardiac mesodermal genes such as Tbx20, Gata4, and Dok4; and MG4 to early mesodermal and pluripotency-associated genes including Brachyury (T), Gli2, Pou5f1, and Nanog. The metagene coefficients assigned to each cell formed a probabilistic simplex, revealing that many cells expressed multiple metagenes simultaneously, indicating multipotency. Metagene entropy calculations demonstrated a clear temporal gradient, with cells from E7.25 exhibiting significantly higher entropy than those from E7.75, which in turn showed higher entropy than those from E8.25, confirming that early-stage cells possess greater transcriptional plasticity. The self-organizing map organized these cells into a 15 × 15 hexagonal lattice, where the central region was dominated by high-entropy cells from E7.25, and the periphery was populated by low-entropy, lineage-committed cells from later stages. This spatial organization mirrored the theoretical epigenetic landscape proposed by Waddington, with developmental trajectories appearing as valleys descending from the high-entropy center toward the committed corners. Partitioning around medoids identified eight distinct metacell clusters, four of which—C2, C4, C5, and C6—were enriched in early time points and exhibited high metagene entropy, consistent with a progenitor identity. In contrast, C1, C3, and C7 were dominated by single metagenes (MG3, MG1, and MG2, respectively) and displayed low entropy, indicative of committed states. Cluster C8, uniquely enriched at E7.25, expressed pluripotency markers and low levels of Etv2, suggesting a population of residual epiblast-derived cells. The highest-entropy metacells were found in cluster C5, which co-expressed T, Sox7, Runx1, and Kdr, identifying a novel population of haemato-endothelial progenitors positioned at the bifurcation point between haematopoietic and endothelial fates. These progenitors were spatially adjacent to the committed haematopoietic cluster C7 and the endothelial cluster C3, and their gene expression profile was consistent with known markers of early haemogenic endothelium. Cluster C2, located between C5 and C3, expressed MG1, MG3, and MG4, and was identified as the progenitor population undergoing endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition to form endocardial cushions. Trajectory inference using the random walk with restart algorithm confirmed that the shortest paths from C5 led to both C7 and C3, while paths from C2 led to C1, validating the predicted lineage relationships. Gene enrichment scores ranked genes such as Snca, Sox7, and Runx1 as top progenitor markers and Hbb-y, Car1, and Emcn as top committed markers, with high statistical significance. The predicted pseudotime derived from the Dpath trajectories showed a strong correlation with chronological time (mean Kendall rank correlation coefficient = 0.798), significantly outperforming Monocle (0.213) and Wishbone (0.375). Functional validation using embryonic stem cell differentiation assays demonstrated that activation of the sonic hedgehog pathway with SAG significantly increased the proportion of Etv2-EYFP+ CD41+/Tie2+ progenitors, whereas inhibition with cyclopamine suppressed their emergence, confirming the computational prediction that SHH signaling is a key regulator of haemato-endothelial specification. These results collectively demonstrate that the Dpath pipeline accurately reconstructs developmental trajectories, identifies novel progenitor populations, and predicts regulatory mechanisms with high biological fidelity.

### DISCUSSION

The Dpath pipeline represents a fundamental advancement in the analysis of single-cell transcriptomic data by introducing a unified framework that simultaneously addresses the challenges of technical noise, lineage ambiguity, and trajectory inference. Unlike conventional methods that treat single-cell data as a collection of discrete points to be clustered or ordered along a linear pseudotime axis, Dpath treats the data as a continuous, topologically structured landscape where cellular states are organized by their transcriptional similarity and developmental potential. The use of weighted Poisson non-negative matrix factorization is a critical innovation, as it explicitly models the probabilistic nature of dropout events rather than imputing them or ignoring them, thereby preserving the integrity of low-expression signals that are often biologically meaningful. The resulting metagenes are not arbitrary statistical components but interpretable biological programs that reflect coherent gene co-expression patterns associated with lineage specification. The introduction of metagene entropy as a quantitative measure of cellular multipotency provides a powerful and generalizable metric that does not rely on pre-existing knowledge of marker genes, making it applicable to any system where lineage commitment occurs. The self-organizing map serves as an intuitive visualization tool that transforms high-dimensional data into a two-dimensional map where spatial proximity reflects transcriptional similarity, enabling researchers to visually identify progenitor hubs, lineage branches, and transition zones. The construction of a heterogeneous metagene–metacell graph and the application of random walk with restart provide a mathematically rigorous method for prioritizing cellular states based on their connectivity and entropy, moving beyond simple clustering to true state inference. The ability to infer developmental trajectories as shortest paths on this graph, constrained by entropy gradients, ensures that the inferred paths are biologically plausible and temporally ordered. The validation of predicted progenitor populations and regulatory pathways through functional experiments underscores the predictive power of the method. Importantly, Dpath does not require prior knowledge of cell types or developmental timelines, making it ideal for exploratory analysis of novel systems. The pipeline’s modular design allows for easy adaptation to new data types and analytical questions, and its open-source nature ensures broad accessibility. The identification of a novel haemato-endothelial progenitor population expressing Sox7 and Runx1, and the experimental confirmation of sonic hedgehog signaling as a key regulator, demonstrate the pipeline’s capacity to generate novel biological insights. The superior performance of Dpath over existing pseudotime algorithms highlights its ability to capture the true complexity of developmental processes, which are often asynchronous, multi-branched, and non-linear. Future applications of Dpath may include the analysis of cancer evolution, where progenitor-like tumor cells give rise to heterogeneous subclones, or the study of cellular reprogramming, where induced pluripotent stem cells transition through intermediate states before acquiring a new identity. The framework may also be extended to integrate spatial transcriptomics data, allowing the mapping of metacell trajectories onto tissue architecture, or to combine with epigenetic profiling to infer regulatory networks governing fate decisions. The Dpath pipeline thus establishes a new standard for single-cell analysis, transforming raw transcriptomic data into a dynamic, interpretable, and predictive model of cellular development.
