## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of natural language processing and, more specifically, to systems and methods for improving the efficiency, scalability, and adversarial robustness of question answering systems that operate over large textual corpora. The invention provides a novel architecture for question answering that decouples the process of context selection from the process of answer extraction, enabling the system to dynamically identify and retrieve only the minimal set of sentences necessary to answer a given question, thereby significantly reducing computational overhead and improving performance in the presence of misleading or adversarial content. The invention is particularly suited for deployment in environments where large volumes of unstructured text must be processed in real time, such as in enterprise knowledge bases, digital assistants, legal document review systems, medical record retrieval platforms, and automated customer support applications. The technical problem addressed by this invention is the inefficiency and vulnerability of conventional question answering models that process entire documents or paragraphs in their entirety, leading to excessive computational demands, slow inference times, and susceptibility to adversarial perturbations introduced through irrelevant or deceptive sentences. The solution provided herein leverages a two-stage framework comprising a sentence-level selector and a downstream question answering module, where the selector operates independently and is trained using transfer learning, data modification, and score normalization techniques to identify the most relevant sentences with high precision and minimal redundancy. This approach fundamentally alters the way question answering systems consume contextual information, shifting from a brute-force, full-context processing paradigm to a targeted, minimal-context paradigm that is both computationally efficient and inherently resistant to adversarial manipulation.

## BACKGROUND

Prior systems for automated question answering have relied heavily on deep neural architectures that analyze entire documents or long passages of text in order to locate the answer to a posed query. These models, often based on bidirectional attention mechanisms, coattention networks, or transformer-based encoders, are designed to capture complex dependencies between the question and the full context, enabling them to reason over multiple sentences and infer answers that require cross-sentence synthesis. While such models have achieved impressive performance on benchmark datasets like SQuAD, NewsQA, and TriviaQA, they suffer from significant limitations that hinder their practical deployment in real-world scenarios. First, these systems are computationally expensive, requiring substantial memory and processing power to encode and attend to every word in a document, even when the majority of the content is irrelevant to the question. This inefficiency becomes especially pronounced when operating over long documents, multi-document collections, or entire knowledge bases such as Wikipedia, where the average document length may exceed hundreds of sentences. Second, these models are highly sensitive to adversarial inputs—intentionally inserted distractor sentences that are semantically plausible but factually unrelated to the question. Such adversarial examples, which have been shown to mislead even state-of-the-art models into producing incorrect answers, expose a critical vulnerability: the models lack the ability to distinguish between relevant and irrelevant context, and instead rely on superficial statistical correlations that can be easily exploited. Third, existing systems typically operate with a fixed context window, selecting a predetermined number of sentences or paragraphs regardless of the complexity or specificity of the question. This rigidity leads to unnecessary computational load in cases where a single sentence suffices, and insufficient context in cases where multiple sentences are required, resulting in suboptimal performance across the board. Furthermore, prior approaches to context selection, such as those based on term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) or simple keyword matching, fail to capture the semantic alignment between the question and the candidate sentence, often retrieving sentences that contain overlapping vocabulary but lack the necessary logical or factual support to answer the question. Other methods that attempt to jointly train sentence selection and answer extraction in an end-to-end manner are constrained by their need for massive labeled datasets and are difficult to adapt to new domains or question types without retraining the entire system. Additionally, these joint models do not provide interpretability or control over the number of sentences used in inference, making it impossible to balance efficiency and accuracy dynamically. The need for a system that can autonomously determine the minimal amount of context required to answer a question, while maintaining or even improving accuracy, has remained unmet. No prior system has demonstrated the ability to consistently identify, with high precision, the exact subset of sentences that contain the answer, while simultaneously rejecting irrelevant or adversarial content, without requiring end-to-end retraining or fixed context sizes. The invention disclosed herein overcomes these limitations by introducing a novel sentence selection mechanism that is lightweight, adaptable, and decoupled from the answer extraction component, enabling it to be seamlessly integrated with any existing question answering model while delivering substantial gains in speed, accuracy, and robustness.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention comprises a novel question answering system that operates through a two-stage architecture: a sentence selector and a downstream question answering model. The sentence selector is responsible for identifying, from a given set of sentences derived from a document or collection of documents, the minimal subset of sentences that collectively contain sufficient information to answer a posed question. The downstream question answering model then processes only this minimal subset to generate the final answer. This architectural separation enables the system to achieve unprecedented efficiency, scalability, and adversarial robustness without compromising accuracy. The sentence selector is trained independently of the question answering model and employs three key techniques—weight transfer, data modification, and score normalization—to enhance its ability to distinguish between sentences that are relevant to the question and those that are not. The weight transfer technique leverages pre-trained representations from a high-performing question answering model that has been trained on oracle sentences—sentences known to contain the correct answer—to initialize the encoder portion of the sentence selector. This initialization ensures that the sentence selector begins training with a strong semantic understanding of what constitutes a relevant sentence, significantly accelerating convergence and improving generalization. The data modification technique involves augmenting the training data by replacing full documents with their corresponding oracle sentences, thereby forcing the sentence selector to learn to identify the precise sentence containing the answer without being distracted by surrounding context. This method effectively simulates the ideal scenario in which the answer is contained within a single sentence, allowing the selector to develop a refined sensitivity to the linguistic and semantic cues that distinguish answer-bearing sentences from irrelevant ones. The score normalization technique applies a per-paragraph or per-document normalization to the raw selection scores generated by the sentence selector, ensuring that scores are comparable across different contexts and preventing bias toward longer documents or paragraphs with higher sentence counts. This normalization enables the system to dynamically threshold the selection scores, allowing the number of sentences selected to vary on a per-question basis, rather than enforcing a fixed number of sentences for all queries. This dynamic selection mechanism, referred to herein as “Dyn,” is a critical innovation that distinguishes the invention from prior art, which relies on static top-k selection strategies that either under-select or over-select context, leading to inefficiencies or inaccuracies. The sentence selector operates by first encoding both the question and each candidate sentence using a shared encoder module, which is typically implemented using a bidirectional recurrent neural network with long short-term memory units or a transformer-based encoder. The encoder produces a high-dimensional representation for the question and for each sentence, capturing their semantic content and syntactic structure. These representations are then passed to a decoder module, which computes a bilinear similarity score between the question encoding and each sentence encoding. This score reflects the likelihood that the sentence contains sufficient information to answer the question, with higher scores indicating greater relevance. The decoder is trained using a binary cross-entropy loss function, where the ground truth labels are derived from the oracle sentences identified during data preprocessing. The use of bilinear similarity, as opposed to simpler dot-product or cosine similarity measures, allows the model to capture more nuanced, non-linear relationships between the question and sentence representations, enabling it to detect relevance even when the lexical overlap between the two is minimal. The sentence selector is trained on a diverse set of question-answer pairs drawn from multiple domains, including encyclopedic text, news articles, and scientific literature, ensuring that it generalizes across question types, answer formats, and document structures. Once trained, the sentence selector is deployed in conjunction with any downstream question answering model, such as a BERT-based span predictor, a DCN+ model, or a transformer-based answer generator. During inference, the system receives a question and a set of candidate sentences, typically extracted from a document or retrieved from a knowledge base. The sentence selector computes a selection score for each sentence and applies a dynamic threshold to determine the minimal set of sentences to pass to the question answering model. This threshold is not fixed but is instead tuned based on the desired balance between efficiency and accuracy, allowing the system to adapt to different operational constraints. For example, in a low-latency application such as a voice assistant, the threshold may be set to select only the top one or two sentences, whereas in a high-accuracy legal or medical application, the threshold may be relaxed to include additional sentences to ensure completeness. The dynamic nature of this selection process ensures that the system never processes more context than necessary, resulting in substantial reductions in computational time and memory usage. In experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, the system achieved up to a fifteen-fold improvement in training speed and a thirteen-fold improvement in inference speed compared to full-context models, while maintaining or exceeding their accuracy. On the SQuAD dataset, the system achieved an F1 score of 94.6 when selecting an average of 3.9 sentences per question, compared to 94.7 when using the full document, demonstrating that the minimal context is sufficient to achieve near-optimal performance. On the more challenging NewsQA dataset, where documents are significantly longer, the system achieved a 6.9-fold speedup in inference while matching the F1 score of the full-context model. On the TriviaQA and SQuAD-Open datasets, which involve reasoning over multiple documents, the system outperformed both TF-IDF-based retrieval and state-of-the-art end-to-end models by a significant margin, achieving a 59.5 F1 score with an average of five sentences per question, compared to 51.9 F1 with TF-IDF and 57.2 F1 with the next best model. Crucially, the system demonstrated exceptional robustness on the SQuAD-Adversarial dataset, where adversarial sentences were inserted into every document to mislead models into selecting incorrect answers. While conventional models experienced a dramatic drop in performance—falling from an F1 score of 79.8 to as low as 54.3—the system maintained an F1 score of 85.4, achieving a new state-of-the-art result and demonstrating its ability to effectively ignore misleading context. The system’s success in this setting is attributable to the sentence selector’s ability to identify and prioritize the oracle sentence, even in the presence of semantically similar but factually incorrect distractors. The sentence selector does not rely on surface-level lexical cues or keyword matching, but instead learns to recognize the underlying semantic structure that links the question to the correct answer. This capability is further enhanced by the score normalization technique, which ensures that the selection scores are calibrated relative to the local context, preventing the model from being biased toward documents with more sentences or higher lexical density. The system is also highly modular and extensible. The sentence selector can be trained once and then applied to any downstream question answering model, regardless of its architecture, enabling seamless integration with existing systems without requiring retraining of the entire pipeline. This modularity allows the invention to be deployed in a wide range of applications, from cloud-based question answering services to on-device assistants, where computational resources are limited. The sentence selector can be implemented as a lightweight service that runs on edge devices, preprocessing incoming queries and documents before sending only the minimal context to a more powerful backend server for answer generation. This distributed architecture minimizes bandwidth usage, reduces latency, and enhances privacy by limiting the amount of sensitive data that must be transmitted. The system can also be adapted to handle multilingual questions by training the sentence selector on parallel corpora, or to operate over structured knowledge graphs by augmenting the sentence encodings with entity and relation embeddings. Furthermore, the dynamic selection mechanism enables the system to scale gracefully with document length: as documents grow larger, the system does not become proportionally slower, because it continues to process only a small, dynamically determined subset of sentences. This scalability makes the invention uniquely suited for applications involving massive document collections, such as legal discovery, patent analysis, clinical trial review, and regulatory compliance monitoring. The invention also introduces a novel training methodology that combines supervised learning with weak supervision derived from oracle sentences. During training, the system is provided with question-answer pairs and the corresponding full documents, but the labels for sentence selection are derived not from human annotation, but from the position of the answer span within the document. This eliminates the need for costly and time-consuming sentence-level annotations, making the system practical to train at scale. The use of oracle sentences as training targets ensures that the sentence selector learns to identify the minimal context required for accurate answering, rather than simply memorizing patterns or correlations. The data modification technique further reinforces this learning by exposing the model exclusively to oracle sentences during training, thereby forcing it to generalize from a highly focused signal. The weight transfer technique ensures that the model inherits the semantic understanding of a high-performing QA model, allowing it to leverage existing knowledge without requiring extensive training from scratch. Together, these techniques create a powerful synergy that enables the sentence selector to achieve high precision with minimal training data. The system’s performance is further enhanced by the use of adaptive thresholding, which allows the number of selected sentences to vary based on the complexity of the question. For example, simple factual questions such as “Who wrote Hamlet?” may require only one sentence, while complex inferential questions such as “What were the long-term consequences of the Treaty of Versailles on European diplomacy?” may require three or more sentences to provide sufficient context. The system automatically determines the optimal number of sentences for each question, eliminating the need for manual tuning or hyperparameter adjustment. This adaptability is a fundamental departure from prior systems, which treat all questions uniformly and apply a fixed context window regardless of the question’s complexity. The invention also includes a novel evaluation framework that measures not only the accuracy of the final answer but also the efficiency and robustness of the sentence selection process. Metrics such as mean average precision, the average number of selected sentences per question, and the percentage of questions answered correctly with one sentence provide a comprehensive assessment of the system’s performance. These metrics demonstrate that the system consistently selects fewer sentences than competing methods while achieving higher accuracy, confirming that the dynamic selection mechanism is not only efficient but also more precise. The system’s ability to achieve high accuracy with minimal context also has important implications for explainability and interpretability. Because the system explicitly identifies the sentences used to generate each answer, it provides a transparent and auditable trail of reasoning that can be reviewed by human users. This feature is particularly valuable in high-stakes domains such as healthcare, law, and finance, where decisions must be justified and verified. The system can be extended to include confidence scores for each selected sentence, allowing users to assess the reliability of the reasoning process. In addition, the system can be combined with a feedback loop in which human reviewers correct misselected sentences, and these corrections are used to further refine the sentence selector through active learning. This closed-loop system ensures continuous improvement and adaptation to domain-specific nuances. The invention also encompasses methods for deploying the system in real-time streaming environments, where questions are received continuously and documents are updated dynamically. In such settings, the sentence selector can be incrementally updated as new documents are added to the corpus, using online learning techniques to adapt its scoring function without requiring full retraining. The system can also be integrated with retrieval systems that use approximate nearest neighbor search to identify candidate documents from a large index, ensuring that the sentence selector operates only on the most promising candidates, further enhancing efficiency. The invention is not limited to any specific hardware implementation and can be deployed on a variety of computing platforms, including central processing units, graphics processing units, tensor processing units, and specialized neural accelerators. The software components of the system are implemented in standard programming languages such as Python and TensorFlow or PyTorch, and can be containerized and deployed using cloud-native frameworks such as Kubernetes or Docker. The system’s modular design allows for easy integration with existing natural language processing pipelines, including tokenizers, part-of-speech taggers, named entity recognizers, and coreference resolvers, which can be used to enhance the quality of the sentence encodings. The invention also includes methods for handling questions that require reasoning over multiple documents, where the sentence selector operates across document boundaries to identify the most relevant sentences from each document. This capability enables the system to answer complex questions that span multiple sources, such as those encountered in research synthesis or policy analysis. The system’s performance on the SQuAD-Open and TriviaQA datasets demonstrates its effectiveness in such open-domain settings, where the source of the answer is not provided and must be discovered from a vast corpus. The invention’s ability to outperform state-of-the-art models in these settings, while using a fraction of the computational resources, represents a significant advancement in the field of open-domain question answering. The system’s robustness to adversarial inputs is further validated through extensive testing on intentionally perturbed datasets, where the sentence selector consistently identifies the correct answer-bearing sentence even when it is surrounded by multiple distractors that are grammatically correct, semantically plausible, and lexically similar. This resilience is achieved not through brute-force filtering or rule-based heuristics, but through the learned ability of the sentence selector to distinguish between genuine and deceptive context based on deep semantic alignment. The invention thus provides a solution to a long-standing problem in natural language processing: how to answer questions accurately and efficiently without being misled by irrelevant or malicious context. The system’s combination of dynamic selection, weight transfer, data modification, and score normalization constitutes a novel and non-obvious advancement over the prior art, which has relied on fixed-context models, keyword-based retrieval, or end-to-end training without explicit context minimization. The invention’s ability to achieve superior performance with significantly reduced computational cost, while maintaining or improving accuracy and robustness, represents a paradigm shift in the design of question answering systems. The invention is not merely an incremental improvement but a fundamental rethinking of how machines consume and reason over textual information, enabling a new class of efficient, scalable, and trustworthy question answering applications that were previously infeasible due to computational constraints or vulnerability to manipulation. The invention is therefore patentable under 35 U.S.C. § 101, § 102, and § 103, as it satisfies the requirements of novelty, non-obviousness, and utility, and provides a concrete, practical solution to a well-documented technological problem.
