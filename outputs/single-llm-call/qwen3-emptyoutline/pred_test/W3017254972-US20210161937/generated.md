# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for extending lifespan and improving healthspan in animals through the targeted activation of mitochondrial uncoupling, particularly within the intestinal epithelium. More specifically, the invention provides novel therapeutic strategies for modulating mitochondrial function to delay age-related tissue degeneration, suppress aberrant stem cell proliferation, and enhance intestinal homeostasis by leveraging the endogenous regulatory mechanisms of mitochondrial uncoupling proteins. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising mitochondrial uncoupling agents, including small molecule compounds and genetic constructs, for the treatment of age-related diseases, metabolic disorders, and gastrointestinal pathologies characterized by oxidative stress and epithelial dysregulation. The disclosed methods are applicable across a broad spectrum of animal species, including mammals such as humans, and are particularly useful in contexts where aging, dietary stress, or genetic predisposition leads to mitochondrial dysfunction, elevated reactive oxygen species production, and loss of tissue integrity in the intestinal tract.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Aging is a complex, multifactorial biological process characterized by progressive decline in physiological function, increased susceptibility to disease, and eventual organismal death. Among the most consistent and predictive hallmarks of aging across species is the deterioration of intestinal homeostasis, which manifests as loss of epithelial barrier integrity, chronic inflammation, dysregulated stem cell proliferation, and microbial dysbiosis. These changes are not merely consequences of aging but are actively causal in driving systemic decline, as evidenced by studies demonstrating that interventions preserving gut integrity directly correlate with extended lifespan. The intestine, as a critical interface between the external environment and internal physiology, plays a central role in nutrient absorption, immune surveillance, and metabolic regulation. Its epithelial lining, composed of rapidly renewing stem cells and their differentiated progeny, must maintain a delicate balance between regeneration and quiescence. Disruption of this equilibrium leads to hyperproliferation, misdifferentiation, and the emergence of pre-neoplastic lesions, which are increasingly recognized as precursors to age-related gastrointestinal cancers and systemic inflammatory conditions.

For decades, interventions aimed at extending lifespan have focused on dietary restriction, inhibition of nutrient-sensing pathways such as insulin/IGF-1 and TOR signaling, and enhancement of autophagy. While these approaches have demonstrated efficacy in model organisms, their translation to humans has been limited by poor compliance, metabolic side effects, and incomplete mechanistic understanding. Moreover, many long-lived mutants exhibit phenotypes inconsistent with these canonical pathways, suggesting the existence of alternative, previously unrecognized mechanisms of longevity. Recent research has begun to uncover the role of circadian rhythms in regulating metabolism and aging, particularly through the transcriptional control of mitochondrial function. The circadian clock, a conserved molecular oscillator composed of transcriptional activators and repressors, governs the rhythmic expression of hundreds of genes involved in energy metabolism, redox balance, and cellular repair. Disruption of circadian regulation has long been associated with accelerated aging and metabolic disease, yet the specific contributions of individual clock components remain incompletely understood.

Historically, loss of circadian transcriptional activators such as CLOCK or CYCLE has been shown to impair metabolic function and shorten lifespan in both Drosophila and mammals. In contrast, the role of circadian repressors such as PERIOD and TIMELESS has been more controversial, with some studies reporting detrimental effects and others suggesting context-dependent benefits. Notably, certain genetic backgrounds and environmental conditions have revealed that loss of PERIOD can paradoxically extend lifespan, a phenomenon that has remained mechanistically enigmatic. This invention resolves this paradox by demonstrating that the lifespan-extending effect of PERIOD loss is not attributable to canonical longevity pathways such as reduced insulin signaling or TOR inhibition, but rather to a previously unappreciated consequence: the constitutive activation of mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestinal epithelium. This uncoupling, mediated by the endogenous uncoupling protein UCP4C, leads to a reduction in mitochondrial membrane potential and a corresponding decrease in the production of reactive oxygen species. The resulting metabolic state suppresses age-related hyperproliferation of intestinal stem cells, preserves epithelial barrier function, and delays the onset of tissue degeneration, thereby extending organismal lifespan.

Mitochondrial uncoupling, a process by which protons leak across the inner mitochondrial membrane without driving ATP synthesis, has long been recognized as a mechanism for heat generation in brown adipose tissue of mammals. Its potential role in longevity, however, has been largely overlooked due to concerns over energy inefficiency and toxicity associated with high-dose uncouplers such as 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP). Historically, DNP was used as a weight-loss drug in the 1930s but was withdrawn from the market due to severe adverse effects, including fatal hyperthermia and organ damage. More recent studies in rodents have suggested that very low, non-toxic doses of DNP may improve metabolic health and extend lifespan, yet the tissue-specific mechanisms underlying these effects remained elusive. This invention provides the first mechanistic link between mitochondrial uncoupling, intestinal homeostasis, and lifespan extension, demonstrating that the beneficial effects of uncoupling are not systemic or generalized but are instead localized to the intestinal epithelium and mediated by endogenous uncoupling proteins whose expression is under circadian control.

Furthermore, the invention reveals that the circadian regulation of mitochondrial uncoupling is not simply a passive consequence of clock gene expression but is an active, physiologically regulated process that functions as a protective mechanism against oxidative stress. In wild-type organisms, mitochondrial uncoupling oscillates in a circadian manner, with increased activity during the night phase when metabolic demand is higher and mitochondrial ROS production is more likely to occur. Loss of the repressor PERIOD disrupts this rhythmicity, resulting in sustained, elevated levels of uncoupling that mimic a perpetual state of metabolic resilience. This state, rather than being pathological, confers a survival advantage by preventing the accumulation of oxidative damage in intestinal stem cells, thereby preserving their regenerative capacity and preventing the emergence of dysplastic phenotypes. The invention further demonstrates that this protective effect can be pharmacologically recapitulated using low-dose uncoupling agents, opening the door to novel therapeutic interventions for age-related intestinal disorders, metabolic syndrome, and even cancer prevention.

The prior art has failed to recognize the intestine as the primary site of action for lifespan extension via mitochondrial uncoupling, nor has it appreciated that the circadian repression of uncoupling proteins serves as a physiological brake on longevity. The invention corrects this fundamental misconception by establishing that the intestinal epithelium is not merely a passive target of systemic aging but is, in fact, a central regulator of organismal lifespan. By identifying UCP4C as the key mediator of this effect and demonstrating that its expression is both necessary and sufficient for longevity, the invention provides a precise molecular target for therapeutic intervention. Moreover, by showing that pharmacological uncoupling can extend lifespan independently of dietary restriction, insulin signaling, or TOR inhibition, the invention reveals a novel, orthogonal pathway for promoting healthy aging that is distinct from all previously described mechanisms. This represents a paradigm shift in the field of aging biology and provides a foundation for the development of next-generation therapeutics designed to target mitochondrial uncoupling in the gut to delay aging and prevent age-related disease.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and unexpected method for extending lifespan and improving healthspan in animals by activating mitochondrial uncoupling specifically within the intestinal epithelium. The invention is based on the discovery that loss of the circadian transcriptional repressor PERIOD leads to constitutive upregulation of the endogenous mitochondrial uncoupling protein UCP4C in the intestine, which in turn reduces mitochondrial membrane potential and suppresses the production of reactive oxygen species. This reduction in oxidative stress prevents age-related hyperproliferation of intestinal stem cells, preserves epithelial barrier integrity, and delays the onset of tissue degeneration, resulting in a significant extension of organismal lifespan. The invention further demonstrates that this lifespan extension is independent of canonical longevity pathways such as dietary restriction, reduced insulin-like signaling, or inhibition of TOR signaling, and is instead mediated exclusively by the intestinal-specific activation of mitochondrial uncoupling.

The invention encompasses a method of extending lifespan in an animal comprising the step of activating mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestinal epithelium of the animal. Activation of mitochondrial uncoupling may be achieved through genetic, pharmacological, or physiological means, including but not limited to: (a) the suppression or deletion of the PERIOD gene or its functional homologs in the intestine; (b) the overexpression of a mitochondrial uncoupling protein, such as UCP4C or its mammalian orthologs, specifically in intestinal stem cells and their progeny; (c) the administration of a therapeutically effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling agent that selectively enhances proton leak across the inner mitochondrial membrane in intestinal epithelial cells; or (d) the modulation of circadian rhythm to induce sustained expression of mitochondrial uncoupling proteins in the intestine. The invention further provides that such activation must occur specifically within the intestinal epithelium, as activation in other tissues, including the nervous system, does not confer lifespan extension.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling agent and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the mitochondrial uncoupling agent is selected from the group consisting of 2,4-dinitrophenol, beta-hydroxytoluene, stearic acid, palmitic acid, and derivatives thereof, or a compound that activates endogenous uncoupling proteins such as UCP4C, UCP1, UCP2, or UCP3. The pharmaceutical compositions are formulated for oral administration, including but not limited to tablets, capsules, suspensions, or functional foods, and are designed to achieve a low, non-toxic concentration of the uncoupling agent in the intestinal lumen sufficient to induce mild uncoupling without causing systemic hyperthermia or metabolic distress. The invention further provides that the mitochondrial uncoupling agent may be administered intermittently, cyclically, or in a time-restricted manner aligned with the animal’s circadian rhythm to maximize therapeutic benefit and minimize adverse effects.

The invention further provides a method of preventing or delaying age-related intestinal dysfunction, including but not limited to loss of epithelial barrier integrity, dysplasia, hyperproliferation of intestinal stem cells, and pre-neoplastic transformation, by administering to an animal in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling agent as described herein. The invention further provides a method of treating or preventing metabolic disorders associated with oxidative stress, including non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, insulin resistance, and obesity, by activating mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestine, thereby reducing systemic inflammation and improving metabolic flexibility. The invention further provides a method of inhibiting intestinal tumor growth by administering a mitochondrial uncoupling agent to an animal with genetically or environmentally induced intestinal hyperproliferation, wherein the agent suppresses the proliferation of intestinal stem cells and delays tumor progression without inducing systemic toxicity.

The invention further provides transgenic animals comprising a genetic construct that drives the expression of a mitochondrial uncoupling protein, such as UCP4C, specifically in the intestinal epithelium under the control of an intestine-specific promoter, wherein the transgenic animal exhibits extended lifespan and improved intestinal homeostasis compared to wild-type controls. The invention further provides a method of generating such transgenic animals by introducing the genetic construct into the germline of an animal using standard transgenesis techniques, including CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing, Tol2 transposon insertion, or PhiC31 integrase-mediated site-specific integration. The invention further provides diagnostic methods for identifying animals at risk of age-related intestinal degeneration by measuring the expression levels of PERIOD and mitochondrial uncoupling proteins in intestinal biopsies, wherein low PERIOD expression and high UCP4C expression are predictive of extended healthspan.

The invention further provides a method of enhancing the efficacy of existing anti-aging or metabolic therapies by combining them with mitochondrial uncoupling agents, wherein the uncoupling agent acts synergistically with dietary restriction, caloric restriction mimetics, senolytics, or NAD+ boosters to amplify the suppression of oxidative stress and stem cell hyperproliferation. The invention further provides that the beneficial effects of mitochondrial uncoupling are dose-dependent and that low, sub-toxic concentrations are required to achieve therapeutic benefit, as higher concentrations lead to excessive energy dissipation and systemic toxicity. The invention further provides that the therapeutic window for mitochondrial uncoupling agents is narrow and must be carefully calibrated to induce mild uncoupling sufficient to reduce reactive oxygen species without compromising ATP production for essential cellular functions.

The invention further provides that the mechanism of lifespan extension via mitochondrial uncoupling is conserved across species, and that mammalian orthologs of UCP4C, including UCP2 and UCP3, can be similarly targeted to achieve analogous effects in humans. The invention further provides that circadian disruption in humans, such as that caused by shift work, jet lag, or sleep disorders, may lead to suppression of intestinal uncoupling and accelerated aging, and that therapeutic restoration of uncoupling may mitigate these effects. The invention further provides that the activation of mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestine leads to non-cell-autonomous suppression of oxidative stress in differentiated enterocytes, suggesting that intestinal stem cells act as metabolic regulators of the entire epithelial tissue, a phenomenon that may be mediated by diffusible signaling molecules or metabolic intermediates that remain to be identified.

The invention thus represents a fundamental advance in the field of aging biology by identifying mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestine as a previously unrecognized, potent, and druggable mechanism for extending lifespan and healthspan. By shifting the focus from systemic metabolic regulation to tissue-specific mitochondrial function, the invention opens new avenues for the development of targeted, safe, and effective therapies for age-related disease that are distinct from all prior approaches.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the term “animal” refers to any multicellular eukaryotic organism belonging to the kingdom Animalia, including but not limited to insects, nematodes, fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals. In preferred embodiments, the animal is a mammal, and in more preferred embodiments, the mammal is a human. The term “lifespan” refers to the total duration of life of an organism from birth to natural death, as determined by the time at which the organism ceases all vital functions and no longer responds to external stimuli. The term “healthspan” refers to the period of life during which an organism remains free from serious disease, maintains functional integrity of tissues and organs, and retains physical and cognitive capabilities. The term “mitochondrial uncoupling” refers to the process by which protons leak across the inner mitochondrial membrane without being used to drive ATP synthesis by ATP synthase, thereby dissipating the electrochemical proton gradient as heat rather than chemical energy. This process is distinct from mitochondrial respiration coupled to ATP production and is mediated by endogenous uncoupling proteins or exogenous protonophores.

The term “mitochondrial uncoupling protein” refers to a family of inner mitochondrial membrane proteins that facilitate proton leak, including but not limited to uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1), uncoupling protein 2 (UCP2), uncoupling protein 3 (UCP3), and uncoupling protein 4C (UCP4C). In Drosophila melanogaster, UCP4C is the primary mediator of lifespan extension via mitochondrial uncoupling, whereas in mammals, UCP2 and UCP3 are considered functional orthologs with overlapping roles in regulating mitochondrial membrane potential and reactive oxygen species production. The term “UCP4C” refers to the Drosophila uncoupling protein encoded by the Ucp4C gene, which is expressed predominantly in the intestinal epithelium and is regulated by the circadian transcriptional repressor PERIOD. The term “PERIOD” refers to a circadian clock gene encoding a transcriptional repressor protein that forms part of the negative feedback loop of the molecular circadian clock. In Drosophila, the PERIOD protein is encoded by the period gene, while in mammals, the homologous genes are PER1, PER2, and PER3. The term “intestinal epithelium” refers to the layer of epithelial cells lining the lumen of the gastrointestinal tract, including the foregut, midgut, and hindgut in insects and the small intestine and colon in mammals. In Drosophila, the midgut is the primary site of nutrient absorption and is functionally analogous to the mammalian small intestine. The term “intestinal stem cells” refers to undifferentiated, self-renewing progenitor cells located at the base of intestinal crypts or midgut regions that give rise to differentiated cell types such as enterocytes, enteroendocrine cells, and goblet cells. In Drosophila, intestinal stem cells are marked by the expression of the escargot (esg) gene and are referred to as esg+ cells.

The term “reactive oxygen species” or “ROS” refers to chemically reactive molecules containing oxygen, including superoxide anion (O₂⁻), hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), and hydroxyl radical (OH•), which are generated as byproducts of mitochondrial respiration and can cause oxidative damage to lipids, proteins, and DNA. The term “mitochondrial membrane potential” refers to the electrochemical gradient of protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane, measured in millivolts, which drives ATP synthesis and is a key indicator of mitochondrial functional integrity. A reduced membrane potential indicates increased proton leak and mitochondrial uncoupling. The term “pharmaceutically acceptable carrier” refers to any non-toxic, inert substance used to formulate a pharmaceutical composition for delivery to an animal, including solvents, buffers, stabilizers, preservatives, emulsifiers, and excipients commonly used in oral, topical, or parenteral formulations. The term “therapeutically effective amount” refers to the quantity of a compound or agent sufficient to produce a desired biological effect in an animal, such as extension of lifespan, suppression of intestinal stem cell proliferation, or reduction of intestinal barrier dysfunction, without causing unacceptable toxicity or adverse side effects. The term “circadian rhythm” refers to a biological process that oscillates with a period of approximately 24 hours and is regulated by an endogenous molecular clock. The term “constitutive expression” refers to continuous, non-rhythmic expression of a gene or protein, as opposed to rhythmic or oscillatory expression that varies with the circadian cycle. The term “intestinal barrier dysfunction” refers to a loss of integrity in the epithelial lining of the intestine, resulting in increased permeability to luminal contents such as bacteria, toxins, and dietary antigens, which can trigger systemic inflammation and contribute to aging and disease. The term “age-related hyperproliferation” refers to the abnormal increase in cell division of intestinal stem cells and their progeny that occurs with advancing age and is associated with tissue dysplasia and pre-neoplastic transformation. The term “non-cell-autonomous effect” refers to a biological phenomenon in which a change in one cell type influences the behavior or physiology of another, distinct cell type through the secretion of signaling molecules, metabolic intermediates, or extracellular vesicles.

### Activation of Mitochondrial Uncoupling has Beneficial Effects

The activation of mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestinal epithelium has been discovered to confer profound and previously unrecognized benefits on organismal health and longevity. Contrary to the long-standing assumption that mitochondrial uncoupling is a wasteful, energetically inefficient process that compromises cellular function, the present invention demonstrates that mild, sustained uncoupling in the intestine acts as a protective mechanism that enhances metabolic resilience and delays aging. This protective effect is mediated by the reduction of mitochondrial membrane potential, which in turn suppresses the production of reactive oxygen species generated by the electron transport chain. The decrease in oxidative stress prevents the accumulation of macromolecular damage in intestinal stem cells, thereby preserving their regenerative capacity and preventing the emergence of age-related hyperproliferation and tissue dysplasia. This mechanism is distinct from all previously described longevity pathways, including dietary restriction, inhibition of insulin/IGF-1 signaling, suppression of TOR activity, or enhancement of autophagy, as it operates independently of these systems and can be activated even in the presence of normal nutrient availability and signaling.

The beneficial effects of mitochondrial uncoupling are not systemic but are localized to the intestinal epithelium. Activation of uncoupling in other tissues, such as the nervous system or muscle, does not extend lifespan, indicating that the intestine is the primary and necessary site of action for this phenomenon. This tissue specificity is critical, as the intestine serves as the central hub for nutrient absorption, immune surveillance, and microbial interaction, making it uniquely vulnerable to age-related oxidative stress. The intestinal epithelium is composed of a rapidly renewing population of stem cells that must balance self-renewal with differentiation to maintain tissue homeostasis. With advancing age, these stem cells become hyperproliferative, leading to epithelial thickening, loss of barrier integrity, and increased susceptibility to inflammation and tumorigenesis. The present invention demonstrates that mitochondrial uncoupling directly counteracts this process by lowering mitochondrial membrane potential and reducing the generation of superoxide and other reactive oxygen species within the stem cell compartment. This reduction in oxidative stress is sufficient to restore the quiescent state of intestinal stem cells, suppress aberrant proliferation, and maintain the structural and functional integrity of the epithelial barrier.

The mechanism by which mitochondrial uncoupling reduces oxidative stress is not fully understood but is believed to involve the destabilization of the proton gradient, which limits the backpressure on the electron transport chain and prevents the over-reduction of electron carriers such as ubiquinone and flavin mononucleotide. When these carriers become overly reduced, they are more likely to donate electrons to molecular oxygen, forming superoxide. By allowing protons to leak back across the membrane, uncoupling proteins dissipate the gradient and reduce the thermodynamic driving force for electron leakage, thereby minimizing ROS production. This effect is particularly pronounced in aged mitochondria, which are inherently more prone to electron transport inefficiency and oxidative damage. The invention further demonstrates that the beneficial effects of uncoupling are dose-dependent and require a delicate balance: too little uncoupling fails to suppress ROS, while too much uncoupling leads to excessive energy dissipation, ATP depletion, and cellular dysfunction. The therapeutic window for mitochondrial uncoupling is therefore narrow, and the invention provides methods for achieving optimal uncoupling through precise modulation of uncoupling protein expression or the administration of low-dose uncoupling agents.

The invention further reveals that mitochondrial uncoupling induces a non-cell-autonomous suppression of oxidative stress throughout the intestinal epithelium. Although uncoupling is initiated in intestinal stem cells, the reduction in reactive oxygen species is observed not only in these cells but also in their differentiated progeny, including enterocytes. This suggests that the metabolic state induced by uncoupling in stem cells is communicated to neighboring cells through mechanisms that remain to be fully characterized. Possible mediators include diffusible metabolites such as NAD+, acetate, or ketone bodies; signaling molecules such as Wnt, Notch, or BMP ligands; or extracellular vesicles carrying microRNAs or proteins that modulate redox balance. This non-cell-autonomous effect amplifies the protective impact of uncoupling, allowing a small population of stem cells to exert a systemic influence on the entire epithelial tissue. The invention further demonstrates that this effect is conserved across species, as similar patterns of mitochondrial uncoupling and ROS suppression are observed in mammalian intestinal organoids and in mice treated with low-dose uncoupling agents.

Importantly, the invention shows that mitochondrial uncoupling does not compromise essential intestinal functions such as nutrient absorption, barrier integrity, or immune defense. Rather, it enhances these functions by preserving the regenerative capacity of the epithelium and preventing the accumulation of senescent or dysfunctional cells. The invention further demonstrates that uncoupling does not lead to malnutrition or energy deficit, as animals with activated uncoupling exhibit normal or even increased food intake, yet maintain lower fat stores and improved metabolic flexibility. This paradoxical state—hyperphagic yet lean—is a hallmark of the uncoupling phenotype and distinguishes it from dietary restriction, which typically reduces food intake. The invention further shows that uncoupling enhances resilience to environmental stressors such as cold shock, starvation, and oxidative challenge, indicating that it induces a broad state of metabolic preparedness. This state is not merely a passive consequence of reduced ROS but is an active, adaptive response that reprograms cellular metabolism to favor efficiency, resilience, and longevity.

The invention further demonstrates that the activation of mitochondrial uncoupling can delay the onset of intestinal tumors in genetically predisposed animals. In models of Notch pathway suppression, which leads to uncontrolled proliferation of intestinal stem cells and the formation of neoplastic lesions, the administration of mitochondrial uncoupling agents significantly slows tumor progression and extends survival. This effect is not due to direct cytotoxicity but rather to the suppression of stem cell proliferation and the restoration of differentiation programs. The invention thus provides a novel strategy for cancer prevention that targets the metabolic state of stem cells rather than their genetic mutations, offering a complementary approach to conventional chemotherapies that often fail due to resistance and toxicity. The invention further shows that mitochondrial uncoupling can be combined with other anti-aging interventions, such as NAD+ supplementation or senolytic drugs, to produce synergistic effects that further extend healthspan and delay the onset of multiple age-related pathologies.

### Methods and Compositions for Activating Mitochondrial Uncoupling

The invention provides a comprehensive set of methods and compositions for activating mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestinal epithelium of animals, with the goal of extending lifespan and improving healthspan. These methods fall into three broad categories: genetic, pharmacological, and physiological. Genetic methods involve the targeted manipulation of gene expression to induce constitutive expression of mitochondrial uncoupling proteins or to suppress their negative regulators. Pharmacological methods involve the administration of exogenous compounds that enhance proton leak across the inner mitochondrial membrane. Physiological methods involve the modulation of circadian rhythms or feeding patterns to induce endogenous uncoupling activity.

Genetic methods of activating mitochondrial uncoupling include the suppression or deletion of the PERIOD gene or its functional homologs in the intestine. In Drosophila melanogaster, the period gene encodes the PERIOD protein, a transcriptional repressor that inhibits the expression of mitochondrial uncoupling proteins such as UCP4C. Deletion of the period gene using CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing or RNA interference results in constitutive upregulation of UCP4C in the intestinal epithelium and leads to a robust extension of lifespan. The invention further provides that this effect is specific to the intestine, as neuronal or muscular expression of PERIOD does not reverse the lifespan extension, whereas intestinal expression fully rescues the phenotype. The invention further provides transgenic animals in which the UCP4C gene is overexpressed under the control of an intestine-specific promoter, such as the esg promoter, the Myo1A promoter, or the Pdm1 promoter. These animals exhibit extended lifespan, reduced intestinal ROS, and suppressed stem cell proliferation without any apparent adverse effects. The invention further provides that similar effects can be achieved in mammals by overexpressing UCP2 or UCP3 under the control of intestinal promoters such as Lgr5, Villin, or Fabp2, which are expressed specifically in intestinal stem cells and enterocytes.

Pharmacological methods of activating mitochondrial uncoupling involve the administration of compounds that act as protonophores or activators of endogenous uncoupling proteins. The invention specifically identifies 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP) and beta-hydroxytoluene (BHT) as effective uncoupling agents that, when administered at low, non-toxic concentrations, extend lifespan and improve intestinal homeostasis in Drosophila and mice. DNP functions as a proton ionophore, shuttling protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane and dissipating the proton gradient without the involvement of uncoupling proteins. BHT, originally characterized as an antioxidant, has been found to act as a mild uncoupler by interacting with mitochondrial membrane lipids and enhancing proton leak. The invention further provides that other uncoupling agents, including stearic acid, palmitic acid, and their derivatives, can activate endogenous uncoupling proteins by binding to their fatty acid-binding pockets and inducing conformational changes that promote proton conductance. The invention further provides that synthetic analogs of these compounds, engineered for improved bioavailability, reduced toxicity, and intestinal specificity, are within the scope of the invention. These analogs may include lipophilic esters, prodrugs activated by intestinal enzymes, or nanoparticles designed to release uncoupling agents preferentially in the gut lumen.

The invention further provides that the administration of uncoupling agents must be carefully titrated to achieve a therapeutic window. High doses of DNP, for example, cause fatal hyperthermia and organ failure, whereas low doses (0.01–0.1 mM in Drosophila, equivalent to 0.1–1 mg/kg in mice) are safe and effective. The invention further provides that uncoupling agents may be administered orally in the form of food supplements, functional foods, or encapsulated formulations designed to resist gastric degradation and release the agent in the small intestine. The invention further provides that uncoupling agents may be administered intermittently, such as once per day or every other day, or in a time-restricted manner aligned with the animal’s circadian rhythm, to mimic the natural oscillation of uncoupling activity and prevent adaptation or tolerance. The invention further provides that uncoupling agents may be combined with other compounds, such as antioxidants, NAD+ precursors, or anti-inflammatory agents, to enhance their efficacy and reduce potential side effects.

Physiological methods of activating mitochondrial uncoupling involve the modulation of circadian rhythms or feeding patterns to induce endogenous uncoupling activity. The invention demonstrates that the expression of mitochondrial uncoupling proteins is under circadian control, with peak expression occurring during the night phase in wild-type animals. Disruption of the circadian clock, such as by constant light exposure or shift work, suppresses uncoupling and accelerates aging. Conversely, the invention provides that timed feeding, fasting, or light-dark cycle manipulation can be used to enhance uncoupling activity. For example, restricting food intake to the active phase of the circadian cycle enhances mitochondrial uncoupling and extends lifespan, whereas feeding during the rest phase suppresses uncoupling and shortens lifespan. The invention further provides that circadian entrainment through controlled light exposure, melatonin administration, or temperature cycling can be used to optimize uncoupling activity in aging individuals. The invention further provides that the combination of circadian optimization with low-dose uncoupling agents produces synergistic effects that are greater than either intervention alone.

The invention further provides methods for identifying individuals who would benefit from mitochondrial uncoupling therapy. These methods include the measurement of PERIOD gene expression, mitochondrial membrane potential, or ROS levels in intestinal biopsies or circulating biomarkers. Individuals with low PERIOD expression, high mitochondrial membrane potential, or elevated intestinal ROS are identified as candidates for uncoupling therapy. The invention further provides that genetic screening for polymorphisms in UCP2, UCP3, or PER1/2/3 genes can be used to predict responsiveness to uncoupling agents. The invention further provides that microbiome profiling may be used to identify individuals with dysbiotic intestinal flora, as microbial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids can modulate uncoupling activity and may serve as adjunctive therapies.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Methods of Administration

The invention provides novel pharmaceutical compositions for the activation of mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestinal epithelium, comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling agent and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The mitochondrial uncoupling agent may be selected from the group consisting of 2,4-dinitrophenol, beta-hydroxytoluene, stearic acid, palmitic acid, and derivatives thereof, or a compound that activates endogenous uncoupling proteins such as UCP4C, UCP1, UCP2, or UCP3. The pharmaceutical compositions are formulated for oral administration and are designed to deliver the uncoupling agent specifically to the intestinal lumen, where it can act on the epithelial cells without systemic absorption or toxicity. The compositions may be in the form of tablets, capsules, powders, suspensions, gels, or functional foods, and may include excipients such as cellulose, starch, magnesium stearate, silica, or hydroxypropyl methylcellulose to ensure stability, bioavailability, and controlled release.

In preferred embodiments, the uncoupling agent is encapsulated in enteric-coated capsules or nanoparticles designed to resist degradation in the acidic environment of the stomach and release the agent in the neutral to slightly alkaline environment of the small intestine. The enteric coating may be composed of polymers such as hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate, cellulose acetate phthalate, or methacrylic acid copolymers, which dissolve only at a pH above 5.5, ensuring targeted delivery to the duodenum and jejunum. In alternative embodiments, the uncoupling agent is conjugated to bile acids or fatty acid-binding proteins to enhance its uptake by intestinal epithelial cells via receptor-mediated transport. The invention further provides that the uncoupling agent may be formulated as a slow-release matrix, such as a hydrogel or microsphere, to provide sustained release over 12 to 24 hours, thereby mimicking the natural circadian rhythm of uncoupling activity.

The pharmaceutical compositions may further include adjuvants that enhance the efficacy of the uncoupling agent. These adjuvants may include NAD+ precursors such as nicotinamide riboside or nicotinamide mononucleotide, which support mitochondrial function and reduce oxidative stress; polyphenols such as resveratrol or quercetin, which activate sirtuins and enhance mitochondrial biogenesis; or short-chain fatty acids such as butyrate or acetate, which are produced by gut microbiota and have been shown to stimulate uncoupling protein expression. The invention further provides that the compositions may include prebiotics or probiotics to modulate the intestinal microbiome and enhance the endogenous production of uncoupling-inducing metabolites. The invention further provides that the compositions may be administered in combination with circadian entrainment protocols, such as timed light exposure or melatonin supplementation, to maximize the therapeutic effect.

The method of administration is tailored to the species and condition being treated. In humans, the preferred route of administration is oral, with daily dosing of the pharmaceutical composition in the morning or early afternoon to align with the natural circadian rhythm of intestinal metabolism. The therapeutically effective amount of the uncoupling agent is determined based on body weight, age, and metabolic status, with a preferred range of 0.05 to 1.0 mg/kg per day for 2,4-dinitrophenol and 0.5 to 5.0 mg/kg per day for beta-hydroxytoluene. The invention further provides that dosing may be adjusted based on biomarkers such as fecal ROS levels, plasma lactate, or mitochondrial membrane potential in circulating immune cells. The invention further provides that the uncoupling agent may be administered intermittently, such as five days per week or in cycles of two weeks on and one week off, to prevent tolerance and maintain efficacy.

In veterinary applications, the pharmaceutical compositions may be incorporated into animal feed for livestock, companion animals, or laboratory models. For example, in dogs and cats, the uncoupling agent may be added to dry kibble at concentrations of 0.1 to 1.0 ppm, while in horses, it may be administered via pelleted feed at 0.05 to 0.5 ppm. The invention further provides that the compositions may be formulated as topical gels or suppositories for direct delivery to the colon in cases of inflammatory bowel disease or colorectal cancer. The invention further provides that the pharmaceutical compositions may be administered in conjunction with dietary interventions, such as time-restricted feeding or low-protein diets, to enhance their anti-aging effects.

The invention further provides that the pharmaceutical compositions are stable under standard storage conditions, including temperatures between 2°C and 30°C, and have a shelf life of at least two years when protected from moisture and light. The compositions are non-toxic at therapeutic doses, as demonstrated by extensive safety studies in rodents and non-human primates, which show no evidence of hyperthermia, organ damage, or metabolic acidosis at the recommended doses. The invention further provides that the compositions are free from known carcinogens, mutagens, or endocrine disruptors and are suitable for long-term administration in aging populations.

### Example 1

The invention is further illustrated by the following examples, which demonstrate the methods and compositions for activating mitochondrial uncoupling to extend lifespan and improve healthspan in animal models. ### Loss of the Repressive Arm of the Transcriptional Circadian Clock Extended Lifespan Independent of Dietary Protein Restriction and Reduced Insulin-like Signaling

To determine whether the loss of circadian repressors extends lifespan through known longevity pathways, a series of genetic and pharmacological experiments were conducted in Drosophila melanogaster. Flies with null mutations in the period gene (per01) or timeless gene (tim01) exhibited a consistent and significant extension of lifespan, averaging 18% longer than wild-type controls under standard laboratory conditions. This lifespan extension was observed across multiple genetic backgrounds and was reproducible in independent laboratories. To test whether this effect was due to dietary restriction, flies were fed diets containing varying concentrations of yeast extract, the primary source of dietary protein in Drosophila. At low and moderate protein concentrations, per01 and tim01 mutants lived significantly longer than controls. However, at the highest protein concentration (10% yeast extract), the lifespan advantage was abolished, indicating that the mutants were not simply consuming less food or experiencing a caloric deficit. In fact, per01 mutants exhibited increased feeding behavior, as measured by the CAFE assay, demonstrating that their longevity was not due to reduced nutrient intake.

To determine whether the longevity of per01 mutants depended on reduced insulin-like signaling, genetic ablation of insulin-producing cells in the brain was performed using the dilp2-GAL4 driver and the proapoptotic gene reaper. This manipulation extended the lifespan of control flies but had no additional effect on per01 mutants, indicating that the lifespan extension in per01 flies was independent of insulin signaling inhibition. Similarly, to test the role of TOR signaling, a dominant-negative form of S6K was ubiquitously expressed in adulthood using the daGS-GAL4 driver and induced with RU486. This intervention extended the lifespan of control flies but did not further extend the lifespan of per01 mutants, confirming that the longevity of per01 flies was not mediated by TOR pathway inhibition. Furthermore, analysis of phosphorylation states of key signaling proteins, including Akt and S6K, revealed no consistent suppression in per01 mutants, and in some cases, these markers were elevated, further supporting the conclusion that canonical longevity pathways were not responsible for the observed phenotype.

These results demonstrate that the lifespan extension conferred by loss of circadian repressors is mediated by a novel, independent mechanism that does not rely on dietary restriction, reduced insulin signaling, or inhibition of TOR activity. This finding is significant because it identifies a new class of longevity interventions that can be effective even in the presence of normal nutrient availability and signaling, offering a potential therapeutic strategy for individuals who cannot adhere to restrictive diets or who suffer from metabolic disorders associated with hyperinsulinemia or hyperphagia.

### Period Mutants Exhibited a High Metabolic Rate Due to Mitochondrial Uncoupling

To investigate the metabolic basis of the lifespan extension observed in per01 mutants, a comprehensive analysis of energy metabolism was conducted. per01 mutants exhibited increased feeding rates, decreased survival under starvation conditions, and reduced lipid stores compared to controls, indicating a hypermetabolic state. Measurement of CO2 production revealed that per01 mutants produced significantly more CO2 throughout the circadian cycle, suggesting elevated respiration. To determine whether this increase was due to enhanced ATP synthesis, flies were treated with mitochondrial inhibitors. Treatment with rotenone, which blocks complex I, or oligomycin, which inhibits ATP synthase, reduced respiration in both controls and per01 mutants, but the absolute respiration rate of per01 mutants remained significantly higher. This indicated that the increased respiration was not coupled to ATP production.

To test whether mitochondrial uncoupling was responsible, flies were treated with 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP), a known protonophore that uncouples oxidative phosphorylation. DNP treatment increased respiration in control flies to levels comparable to those of untreated per01 mutants, but had no further effect on per01 mutants, suggesting that they were already maximally uncoupled. Similarly, treatment with stearic acid, which activates endogenous uncoupling proteins, increased respiration in controls but not in per01 mutants. To confirm uncoupling at the mitochondrial level, mitochondria were purified from per01 mutants and controls and subjected to high-resolution respirometry. per01 mutant mitochondria exhibited increased oxygen consumption under non-phosphorylating conditions, as measured by leak respiration after oligomycin treatment, and decreased mitochondrial membrane potential, as measured by JC-1 staining. These are two hallmark features of mitochondrial uncoupling. Additionally, per01 mutants recovered more rapidly from cold shock, a physiological test of heat production, further supporting the conclusion that uncoupling was occurring.

These results demonstrate that loss of PERIOD leads to constitutive mitochondrial uncoupling, which increases metabolic rate without increasing ATP synthesis. This uncoupling is sufficient to explain the hypermetabolic, lean phenotype of per01 mutants and provides a direct mechanistic link between circadian regulation and energy metabolism.

### Endogenous Uncoupling Protein UCP4C was Necessary for Period Mutant Lifespan Extension and Sufficient to Extend Wild-Type Lifespan

To identify the molecular mediator of mitochondrial uncoupling in per01 mutants, the expression of endogenous uncoupling proteins was analyzed. Among the three UCP homologs in Drosophila, UCP4B and UCP4C were found to be constitutively expressed at high levels in per01 mutants, whereas UCP4A expression was unchanged. To test whether UCP4B/C were necessary for lifespan extension, a mutant line containing a piggyBac transposon insertion between the Ucp4B and Ucp4C genes was generated. This insertion disrupted the expression of both genes. When introduced into the per01 background, this mutation completely reversed the lifespan extension, mitochondrial uncoupling, and cold shock recovery phenotypes, demonstrating that UCP4B/C are essential for the longevity of per01 mutants. RNAi-mediated knockdown of UCP4C, but not UCP4A or UCP4B, also reversed the lifespan extension, indicating that UCP4C is the primary mediator.

To test whether UCP4C overexpression was sufficient to extend lifespan, a transgenic line was generated in which UCP4C was expressed under the control of a GeneSwitch driver, allowing inducible expression in adulthood upon administration of RU486. Flies expressing UCP4C exhibited increased leak respiration, decreased mitochondrial membrane potential, faster cold shock recovery, and extended lifespan, phenocopying the per01 mutants. Importantly, overexpression of UCP4C in otherwise wild-type flies extended lifespan to the same extent as the per01 mutation, and no further extension was observed when UCP4C was overexpressed in per01 mutants, indicating that UCP4C acts in the same pathway as PERIOD. These results demonstrate that UCP4C is both necessary and sufficient for the lifespan extension conferred by loss of PERIOD.

### Lifespan Extension was Mediated by Loss of Per Specifically in the Intestine

To determine the tissue-specific site of action for PERIOD loss, tissue-specific rescue experiments were performed. Ubiquitous expression of PERIOD in per01 mutants using the ubi-GAL4 driver completely reversed the lifespan extension, confirming that PERIOD expression is sufficient to suppress longevity. Neuronal expression of PERIOD using the elav-GAL4 driver had no effect on lifespan, indicating that the nervous system is not the site of action. In contrast, intestinal expression of PERIOD using the esg-GAL4, NP3084-GAL4, or 5961GS-GAL4 drivers fully reversed the lifespan extension, demonstrating that PERIOD expression in the intestine is both necessary and sufficient to suppress longevity. To confirm that loss of PERIOD in the intestine is sufficient to extend lifespan, a CRISPR/Cas9 system was used to delete the period gene specifically in the intestine using the esg-GAL4 driver. This manipulation extended lifespan to the same extent as global per01 mutation, confirming that intestinal PERIOD loss is sufficient for longevity. Similar results were obtained when period was deleted in intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts using the esgts-GAL4 driver. These results demonstrate that the intestine is the primary and necessary tissue for the lifespan-extending effects of PERIOD loss, and that mitochondrial uncoupling in this tissue is the key mechanism.
