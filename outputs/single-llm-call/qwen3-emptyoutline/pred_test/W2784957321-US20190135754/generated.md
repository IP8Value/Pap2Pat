# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The construction of chiral quaternary stereocenters remains one of the most persistent and strategically significant challenges in modern organic synthesis, particularly within the context of pharmaceutical discovery and the development of bioactive molecules. The presence of such stereocenters imparts three-dimensional complexity and conformational rigidity to molecular architectures, which are often essential for high-affinity binding to biological targets, metabolic stability, and selective pharmacological activity. Despite decades of intensive research, the catalytic, enantioselective formation of quaternary carbon centers—especially those bearing multiple heteroatom-functionalized substituents—continues to present substantial synthetic hurdles. Traditional approaches relying on stoichiometric chiral auxiliaries, resolution techniques, or chiral pool derivatization are often labor-intensive, low-yielding, and economically unsustainable at scale. Consequently, the development of catalytic, asymmetric methodologies that enable direct, atom-economical, and stereocontrolled construction of these motifs has become a central objective in synthetic organic chemistry.

Among the most powerful strategies for achieving this goal, transition metal-catalyzed allylic alkylation stands out as a transformative and widely adopted methodology. Since its inception in 1965 by Tsuji, this class of reactions has evolved into a cornerstone of asymmetric synthesis, enabling the formation of carbon–carbon bonds with precise stereocontrol through the intermediacy of π-allyl-metal complexes. The utility of allylic alkylation lies in its ability to couple readily available electrophilic allylic substrates with a diverse array of nucleophiles, including stabilized enolates, malonates, and other carbon-based nucleophiles, under mild catalytic conditions. Over the past five decades, palladium-catalyzed variants have dominated the field, particularly in the context of enantioselective transformations, owing to the well-established ligand frameworks and mechanistic understanding associated with Pd(0)/Pd(II) catalytic cycles. However, despite the remarkable progress in palladium-mediated systems, significant limitations persist, particularly in terms of substrate scope, functional group tolerance, and the requirement for pre-activated electrophiles such as allylic acetates, carbonates, or halides. These pre-functionalized substrates often necessitate multi-step syntheses, increasing cost, waste, and procedural complexity.

In parallel, the use of prochiral nucleophiles that generate tetrasubstituted enolates upon deprotonation has emerged as a particularly attractive route to quaternary stereocenters. Among these, α-substituted carbonyl compounds—especially β-keto esters, α-cyano esters, and related systems—have been extensively explored in both palladium- and nickel-catalyzed systems. However, the application of this strategy to α-acyl lactones and lactams, which represent structurally distinct and highly valuable scaffolds, has remained largely unexplored. Lactones and lactams are ubiquitous structural motifs in natural products, pharmaceuticals, and agrochemicals, and their α-quaternary derivatives offer unique opportunities for downstream functionalization. For instance, lactone rings can be selectively opened to yield acyclic carboxylic acid derivatives bearing chiral quaternary centers, while lactam reduction provides direct access to enantioenriched piperidines—privileged nitrogen-containing heterocycles found in over 60% of FDA-approved small-molecule drugs. Despite this clear pharmaceutical relevance, the catalytic enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-acyl lactones and lactams using free, unactivated allylic alcohols as electrophiles had not been achieved prior to the present invention.

The few existing reports in this area are severely constrained. One notable example involves a palladium-catalyzed decarboxylative allylic alkylation of enol carbonates derived from γ-butyrolactones, which delivers α-acyl quaternary lactones with moderate enantioselectivity. However, this method requires the prior synthesis of enol carbonates—a non-trivial, multi-step process involving activation of the lactone carbonyl and installation of a carbonate leaving group. Furthermore, the electrophile scope is limited to a narrow range of allylic systems, and the overall transformation lacks the convergence and operational simplicity that would make it broadly applicable in medicinal chemistry or process development. The reliance on pre-functionalized nucleophiles and the absence of a general protocol for nitrogen-containing analogs further diminish its practical utility. No prior method has enabled the direct, intermolecular coupling of unmodified α-acyl lactones or lactams with free allylic alcohols under enantioselective catalytic conditions. The absence of such a method represents a significant gap in synthetic methodology, particularly given the abundance and low cost of allylic alcohols as starting materials and the structural diversity accessible through lactone and lactam scaffolds.

Moreover, while nickel catalysis has gained increasing attention in recent years for its ability to mediate transformations that are challenging for palladium—such as cross-couplings with unactivated electrophiles, reductive processes, and reactions involving challenging nucleophiles—its application to enantioselective allylic alkylations of lactones and lactams had not been demonstrated. Nickel’s distinct electronic properties, including its lower electronegativity, greater propensity for single-electron transfer pathways, and enhanced oxidative addition kinetics with alcohols, suggest that it may be uniquely suited to activate free allylic alcohols without the need for pre-activation. Indeed, a recent report by Mashima demonstrated the nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation of β-keto esters with allylic alcohols, using a chiral bisphosphine ligand and a mild base, thereby circumventing the need for pre-formed enolates or activated electrophiles. This breakthrough provided a conceptual foundation for extending the methodology to structurally analogous but more complex systems such as α-acyl lactones and lactams. However, the inherent differences in ring strain, electronic distribution, and steric environment between β-keto esters and α-acyl lactones/lactams render direct extrapolation of reaction conditions infeasible. The lactone and lactam rings introduce additional conformational constraints, alter the acidity and geometry of the α-proton, and modify the chelation behavior of the carbonyl oxygen with the metal center—all of which critically influence the efficiency and stereochemical outcome of the transformation.

The present invention addresses these long-standing challenges by introducing a novel catalytic system that enables the first nickel-catalyzed, intermolecular, enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-substituted lactones and lactams using free allylic alcohols as electrophiles. This methodology overcomes the limitations of prior art by eliminating the need for pre-functionalized nucleophiles or activated electrophiles, leveraging the intrinsic reactivity of the lactone and lactam carbonyls to facilitate deprotonation and enolate formation under mild conditions. The use of a commercially available chiral bisphosphine ligand, in combination with a simple nickel precursor, allows for high levels of enantiocontrol across a broad substrate scope, including both oxygen- and nitrogen-containing heterocycles. The reaction proceeds under operationally simple conditions, tolerates a wide array of functional groups, and delivers products in high yields and enantiomeric excesses. Importantly, the resulting α-quaternary lactones and lactams serve as versatile synthetic intermediates, amenable to a variety of downstream transformations—including ring-opening, reduction, functionalization, and cyclization—that lead to structurally complex, enantioenriched architectures with high pharmaceutical relevance. This invention thus represents a paradigm shift in the synthesis of chiral quaternary centers, providing a direct, scalable, and broadly applicable route to molecular scaffolds that were previously inaccessible through catalytic asymmetric means.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a novel and highly efficient method for the enantioselective construction of α-quaternary lactones and lactams via nickel-catalyzed intermolecular allylic alkylation using free allylic alcohols as electrophiles and unmodified α-acyl lactones or lactams as prochiral nucleophiles. This method represents the first successful application of such a transformation, overcoming longstanding limitations in substrate scope, functional group tolerance, and operational simplicity that have hindered prior approaches. The invention enables the direct coupling of readily available, commercially accessible starting materials under mild reaction conditions, without the need for pre-activation of either the nucleophile or the electrophile. The resulting products are enantioenriched α-quaternary lactones and lactams, which serve as valuable chiral building blocks for the synthesis of complex bioactive molecules, including pharmaceutical agents, natural product analogs, and advanced synthetic intermediates.

The method comprises the reaction of an α-acyl lactone or lactam with an allylic alcohol in the presence of a nickel catalyst and a chiral bisphosphine ligand, under an inert atmosphere and in an appropriate solvent, to afford the corresponding α-quaternary lactone or lactam product with high enantiomeric excess. The nickel catalyst is selected from nickel(0) or nickel(II) precursors, including but not limited to nickel(II) 1,5-cyclooctadiene, nickel(II) acetylacetonate, nickel(II) chloride, and nickel(II) triflate, with nickel(II) 1,5-cyclooctadiene being particularly preferred. The chiral bisphosphine ligand is selected from a family of C2-symmetric, electron-rich, and sterically demanding bisphosphines, including (R)-P-phos, (R)-BINAP, (R)-Segphos, (R)-DTBM-Segphos, (R)-MeO-BIPHEP, and (R)-Synphos, with (R)-P-phos being the most effective in terms of enantioselectivity and yield. The reaction is typically conducted in an ethereal solvent such as diethyl ether, tetrahydrofuran, or 1,4-dioxane, with diethyl ether being optimal, at temperatures ranging from –20 °C to 20 °C, with –10 °C to 0 °C being most preferred. A mild base, such as potassium carbonate, cesium carbonate, or potassium tert-butoxide, is employed in stoichiometric amounts relative to the nucleophile to facilitate deprotonation and enolate generation. The reaction proceeds without the need for additives, co-catalysts, or specialized equipment, and can be performed on scales ranging from milligrams to multi-gram quantities with consistent enantioselectivity and yield.

The scope of the invention encompasses a broad range of α-acyl lactones, including those substituted at the α-position with ester groups such as methoxycarbonyl, ethoxycarbonyl, isopropoxycarbonyl, benzyloxycarbonyl, and tert-butoxycarbonyl, as well as those incorporating fused or bicyclic ring systems such as γ-butyrolactones, δ-valerolactones, and fused [3.3.0] or [4.3.0] lactone systems. The lactam component includes N-protected α-acyl pyrrolidinones, piperidinones, and azepinones, with benzoyl, acetyl, pivaloyl, and methoxycarbonyl protecting groups being particularly suitable. The allylic alcohol electrophiles encompass a wide variety of structures, including aryl-substituted allylic alcohols bearing electron-donating or electron-withdrawing substituents at the ortho, meta, or para positions; heteroaryl-substituted allylic alcohols such as furanyl, thienyl, pyridyl, and indolyl derivatives; aliphatic allylic alcohols including linear and branched alkyl chains; and alkenyl-substituted allylic alcohols such as cinnamyl, prenyl, and geranyl derivatives. The reaction is compatible with functional groups such as halides, ethers, esters, ketones, silyl ethers, and protected amines, allowing for the synthesis of highly functionalized products without the need for protection/deprotection sequences.

The products of the invention are enantioenriched α-quaternary lactones and lactams, which contain a carbon stereocenter bearing four distinct substituents: the lactone or lactam ring, the acyl group, the allyl-derived chain, and a hydrogen atom. These products exhibit enantiomeric excesses ranging from 80% to 95%, with the majority of examples exceeding 85% ee. The stereochemical outcome is consistently controlled by the chirality of the bisphosphine ligand, with (R)-configured ligands delivering products with (R)-absolute configuration at the quaternary center. The reaction is highly selective for the linear allylation product, even when branched allylic alcohols are employed, indicating a well-defined migratory insertion pathway and a single dominant π-allyl nickel intermediate. The method is scalable, robust, and reproducible, with no significant loss in yield or enantioselectivity observed upon scale-up to 10-gram quantities. The reaction times are typically between 12 and 48 hours, with completion monitored by thin-layer chromatography or gas chromatography.

The utility of the invention extends beyond the direct formation of α-quaternary lactones and lactams. The products serve as versatile synthetic intermediates that can be transformed into a wide array of structurally complex and pharmacologically relevant molecules. For instance, lactone products can undergo selective reduction to yield enantioenriched 1,4-diols, which contain a chiral quaternary center and two hydroxyl functionalities suitable for further derivatization. Alternatively, nucleophilic addition to the lactone carbonyl, such as with vinyl Grignard reagents, leads to enone products that retain the chiral quaternary center and can be subjected to ring-closing metathesis to form spirocyclic systems. Lactam products can be reduced with hydride reagents to yield enantioenriched piperidine derivatives, which are core structures in numerous CNS-active drugs. The benzoyl protecting group on the lactam nitrogen can be selectively cleaved under mild basic conditions to afford unprotected lactams, which are valuable intermediates for peptide mimetics and alkaloid synthesis. Additionally, the allylic chain can be functionalized via hydroboration, epoxidation, dihydroxylation, or cross-metathesis, enabling the installation of additional stereocenters and functional handles. These transformations demonstrate that the products of the invention are not merely endpoints but serve as springboards for the synthesis of diverse molecular architectures with high complexity and biological relevance.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the enantioenriched α-quaternary lactones and lactams as active ingredients, either as isolated compounds or in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents. These compositions may be formulated for oral, parenteral, topical, or inhalation administration and are suitable for the treatment of conditions such as neurological disorders, infectious diseases, metabolic syndromes, and cancer. The invention also includes synthetic schemes for the preparation of exemplary pharmaceutical agents derived from the α-quaternary lactone and lactam products, including analogs of pregabalin, gabapentin, and other GABAergic agents, as well as novel piperidine-based kinase inhibitors and serotonin receptor modulators. The method disclosed herein provides a direct, efficient, and scalable route to these agents, circumventing the need for resolution, chiral auxiliaries, or multi-step sequences that have historically plagued their synthesis.

In summary, the invention provides a fundamentally new and broadly applicable method for the catalytic enantioselective synthesis of α-quaternary lactones and lactams, overcoming significant limitations in prior art and opening new avenues for the construction of complex, bioactive molecules. The method is operationally simple, uses inexpensive and commercially available reagents, exhibits exceptional enantioselectivity and functional group tolerance, and delivers products that are directly useful in medicinal chemistry and drug discovery. The invention thus represents a significant advancement in the field of asymmetric catalysis and synthetic methodology, with far-reaching implications for the efficient production of chiral pharmaceuticals and fine chemicals.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention provides a novel catalytic methodology for the enantioselective construction of α-quaternary lactones and lactams through the direct intermolecular allylic alkylation of unmodified α-acyl lactones and lactams with free allylic alcohols, mediated by a nickel catalyst in the presence of a chiral bisphosphine ligand. This transformation represents a significant departure from conventional approaches, which have historically required pre-activated nucleophiles such as enol carbonates, silyl enol ethers, or metal enolates, as well as activated electrophiles such as allylic acetates, halides, or carbonates. By contrast, the method of the invention utilizes the inherent acidity of the α-proton in lactones and lactams, enabling direct deprotonation under mild basic conditions to generate a nucleophilic enolate species in situ. This enolate then engages in a stereoselective bond-forming event with an allylic alcohol, which is activated through coordination to a nickel center, leading to the formation of a π-allyl nickel intermediate. The subsequent nucleophilic attack by the enolate, followed by reductive elimination, delivers the desired α-quaternary lactone or lactam product with high enantiomeric excess.

The key innovation of this invention lies in the synergistic combination of a nickel catalyst and a chiral bisphosphine ligand, which together enable the activation of both the nucleophile and the electrophile under conditions that are chemoselective, operationally simple, and scalable. Nickel, in its low oxidation state, exhibits a unique ability to coordinate and activate allylic alcohols without the need for prior functionalization, a feature that distinguishes it from palladium-based systems, which typically require the installation of a leaving group. The chiral bisphosphine ligand, with its well-defined C2-symmetric geometry and tunable steric and electronic properties, controls the facial selectivity of the enolate attack on the π-allyl intermediate, thereby dictating the absolute configuration of the newly formed quaternary stereocenter. The reaction proceeds under mild conditions—typically at temperatures between –20 °C and 20 °C, in ethereal solvents, and without the need for anhydrous or oxygen-free environments beyond standard inert atmosphere techniques—making it highly accessible to synthetic chemists in both academic and industrial settings.

The scope of the invention is broad and encompasses a wide variety of α-acyl lactones and lactams, including monocyclic, fused, and bridged systems, as well as a diverse array of allylic alcohols bearing aryl, heteroaryl, aliphatic, and alkenyl substituents. The method is compatible with a range of functional groups, including halogens, ethers, esters, ketones, silyl-protected alcohols, and protected amines, allowing for the synthesis of highly functionalized products without the need for protection/deprotection sequences. The resulting α-quaternary lactones and lactams are obtained in high yields, typically ranging from 70% to 95%, and with enantiomeric excesses of 80% to 95%, depending on the specific substrate combination. The stereochemical outcome is reliably controlled by the chirality of the bisphosphine ligand, with (R)-configured ligands consistently producing (R)-configured products, and vice versa.

The synthetic utility of the products extends far beyond their initial formation. The lactone ring can be selectively opened under acidic or reductive conditions to yield acyclic carboxylic acids bearing a chiral quaternary center, which are valuable intermediates for the synthesis of non-natural amino acids, prostaglandin analogs, and other bioactive molecules. The lactam ring can be reduced to yield enantioenriched piperidines, pyrrolidines, or azepanes—core scaffolds in a multitude of pharmaceutical agents targeting the central nervous system, cardiovascular system, and infectious diseases. The allylic side chain can be further elaborated through a variety of transformations, including hydrofunctionalization, oxidation, cyclopropanation, and cross-coupling, enabling the rapid assembly of molecular complexity from simple building blocks. The method is also amenable to automation and continuous flow processes, making it suitable for large-scale manufacturing of chiral intermediates for pharmaceutical production.

Importantly, the invention eliminates the need for expensive or toxic reagents, avoids the use of heavy metals beyond nickel, and generates minimal byproducts, primarily water and the corresponding alkoxide salt, rendering it environmentally favorable and in alignment with green chemistry principles. The use of commercially available ligands and catalysts further enhances the practicality and economic viability of the method. The invention thus provides a powerful, general, and scalable platform for the synthesis of enantioenriched α-quaternary lactones and lactams, addressing a long-standing gap in synthetic methodology and enabling the efficient production of complex, biologically relevant molecules that were previously inaccessible through catalytic asymmetric means.

### Exemplary Methods

The method of the invention is carried out by combining an α-acyl lactone or lactam with an allylic alcohol in the presence of a nickel catalyst and a chiral bisphosphine ligand, in an appropriate solvent, under an inert atmosphere, and at a controlled temperature. The reaction is typically initiated by degassing the solvent and reaction vessel with an inert gas such as nitrogen or argon, followed by the sequential addition of the nickel precursor, the chiral ligand, the nucleophile, the electrophile, and a mild base. The reaction mixture is then stirred for a period ranging from 12 to 48 hours, during which time the enantioselective bond formation occurs. The progress of the reaction is monitored by analytical techniques such as thin-layer chromatography, gas chromatography, or high-performance liquid chromatography. Upon completion, the reaction is quenched with an aqueous solution, extracted with an organic solvent, dried over anhydrous sodium sulfate, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product is purified by flash chromatography on silica gel or preparative high-performance liquid chromatography to afford the desired α-quaternary lactone or lactam in high purity and enantiomeric excess.

The α-acyl lactone or lactam is employed in a molar amount ranging from 0.5 to 2.0 equivalents relative to the allylic alcohol, with 1.0 to 1.2 equivalents being most preferred to ensure complete consumption of the electrophile while minimizing side reactions. The allylic alcohol is typically used in slight excess, ranging from 1.1 to 2.0 equivalents, to drive the reaction to completion and compensate for any volatility or side reactions. The nickel catalyst is employed in catalytic amounts, typically between 0.5 mol% and 10 mol%, with 5 mol% being optimal for most substrates. The chiral bisphosphine ligand is used in a molar ratio of 1.1 to 2.0 times that of the nickel catalyst, with 1.2:1 being the most effective for achieving high enantioselectivity. The base is employed in stoichiometric amounts, typically 1.0 to 1.5 equivalents relative to the nucleophile, with potassium carbonate, cesium carbonate, and potassium tert-butoxide being the most effective. The solvent is selected from ethereal solvents such as diethyl ether, tetrahydrofuran, 1,4-dioxane, or methyl tert-butyl ether, with diethyl ether being the most preferred due to its ability to solubilize all components while promoting high enantioselectivity. The reaction temperature is maintained between –20 °C and 20 °C, with –10 °C to 0 °C being optimal for balancing reaction rate and enantioselectivity. The reaction is typically conducted under an inert atmosphere, although the use of technical-grade solvents and reagents without rigorous drying is acceptable, as the system is tolerant to trace moisture.

The method is scalable and can be performed on laboratory scales ranging from 0.1 mmol to 100 mmol, with yields and enantioselectivities remaining consistent across scales. The reaction is amenable to continuous flow processing, wherein the nickel catalyst and ligand are immobilized on a solid support, and the nucleophile and electrophile are delivered as continuous streams through a heated reactor. The method is also compatible with automation platforms, enabling high-throughput screening of substrate combinations and optimization of reaction parameters.

The products are isolated as colorless to pale yellow oils or low-melting solids, depending on the substituents present. The enantiomeric excess is determined by chiral high-performance liquid chromatography using columns such as Chiralpak AD-H, Chiralcel OD-H, or Chiralpak IB, with eluents consisting of hexane/isopropanol mixtures. Absolute configuration is assigned by comparison with known standards, X-ray crystallography, or optical rotation data. The products are characterized by nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, high-resolution mass spectrometry, and infrared spectroscopy to confirm structure and purity.

Alternative embodiments of the method include the use of pre-mixed catalyst systems, wherein the nickel precursor and chiral ligand are combined in advance to form a pre-catalyst complex prior to addition to the reaction mixture. This approach may improve reproducibility and reduce induction periods. The method may also be conducted in the presence of molecular sieves (3Å or 4Å) to scavenge trace water, although this is not required. The reaction may be performed in sealed vessels or under reflux conditions for substrates with higher boiling points. The use of microwave irradiation or ultrasound may accelerate the reaction rate without compromising enantioselectivity.

The method is particularly advantageous in that it avoids the use of stoichiometric chiral auxiliaries, toxic heavy metals, or hazardous reagents. It does not require cryogenic temperatures, specialized equipment, or extensive purification procedures. The starting materials are commercially available or readily synthesized using standard organic transformations. The reaction produces minimal waste, primarily in the form of alkali metal salts and water, which are easily removed during workup. The method is therefore suitable for industrial-scale manufacturing of chiral intermediates for pharmaceutical production.

### Transition Metal Catalysts

The catalytic system of the invention relies on the use of nickel-based transition metal complexes to mediate the enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-acyl lactones and lactams with allylic alcohols. Nickel is uniquely suited for this transformation due to its ability to activate unfunctionalized allylic alcohols through direct coordination and deprotonation, a capability that is not shared by other transition metals such as palladium, which typically require pre-activated electrophiles. The nickel catalyst operates through a low-valent Ni(0) or Ni(II) species that is generated in situ from commercially available precursors. Preferred nickel precursors include nickel(II) 1,5-cyclooctadiene (Ni(COD)₂), nickel(II) acetylacetonate, nickel(II) chloride, nickel(II) bromide, nickel(II) triflate, and nickel(II) nitrate. Among these, Ni(COD)₂ is the most effective, likely due to its ability to readily undergo ligand exchange and reduction under the reaction conditions to generate the active Ni(0) species.

The catalytic cycle is initiated by the coordination of the chiral bisphosphine ligand to the nickel center, forming a chiral Ni(0) complex that is capable of oxidative addition into the allylic alcohol. This step is believed to proceed via a concerted deprotonation and metalation process, wherein the nickel center facilitates the abstraction of the hydroxyl proton and simultaneously coordinates to the allylic system, forming a π-allyl nickel intermediate. This intermediate is stabilized by the electron-donating nature of the bisphosphine ligand and the steric environment imposed by its chiral backbone. The nucleophile, generated in situ by deprotonation of the α-acyl lactone or lactam by a mild base, then attacks the π-allyl complex in a stereoselective manner, with the chiral ligand dictating the facial bias of the approach. The resulting nickel enolate undergoes reductive elimination to release the product and regenerate the Ni(0) catalyst, completing the catalytic cycle.

The choice of nickel precursor significantly influences the reaction rate, yield, and enantioselectivity. Nickel(II) acetylacetonate, while stable and easy to handle, requires longer reaction times and higher temperatures to achieve comparable results, likely due to the slower reduction to Ni(0). Nickel(II) chloride and bromide are less effective, producing lower yields and inconsistent enantioselectivity, possibly due to halide-induced catalyst poisoning or the formation of inactive nickel halide aggregates. Nickel(II) triflate, although highly electrophilic, tends to promote side reactions such as ester hydrolysis or lactam ring-opening, particularly in the presence of trace water. Nickel(II) nitrate is unsuitable due to its oxidizing properties, which lead to decomposition of the chiral ligand and formation of byproducts.

The optimal catalyst loading is between 5 mol% and 10 mol%, with 5 mol% being sufficient for most substrates and 10 mol% being employed for sterically hindered or electronically deactivated systems. Lower loadings, such as 1 mol% or 2 mol%, result in incomplete conversion and prolonged reaction times exceeding 72 hours, whereas higher loadings, such as 15 mol% or 20 mol%, do not improve yield or enantioselectivity and may lead to increased formation of dimeric or oligomeric byproducts. The use of pre-formed Ni(0) complexes, such as Ni(COD)₂, is preferred over in situ reduction methods, as they provide greater reproducibility and eliminate variability associated with reducing agents.

Alternative nickel sources, such as nickel nanoparticles, nickel supported on alumina or carbon, or nickel complexes with N-heterocyclic carbene ligands, have been tested but are less effective. Nickel nanoparticles exhibit poor enantioselectivity due to the lack of a chiral environment, while supported catalysts suffer from leaching and reduced activity. N-heterocyclic carbene ligands, although effective in other nickel-catalyzed transformations, fail to induce high enantioselectivity in this system, likely due to their strong σ-donor character, which alters the electronic properties of the nickel center and disrupts the delicate balance required for enantiocontrol.

The catalytic system is tolerant to a variety of additives, including Lewis acids such as magnesium perchlorate or zinc triflate, which may enhance reactivity in certain cases, but are not required. The presence of molecular sieves (3Å or 4Å) may improve yields in moisture-sensitive substrates, but the reaction proceeds efficiently without them. The catalyst system is also compatible with air-stable ligands and reagents, allowing for simplified handling procedures in both academic and industrial laboratories.

Importantly, the nickel catalyst is not interchangeable with other transition metals. Palladium, copper, rhodium, iridium, and cobalt catalysts, when tested under identical conditions, fail to produce the desired α-quaternary products in any significant yield or enantioselectivity. Palladium systems, even with chiral phosphine ligands, require pre-activated allylic carbonates and yield only trace amounts of product from allylic alcohols. Copper catalysts promote racemic background reactions, while rhodium and iridium systems lead to decomposition of the lactone or lactam ring. Cobalt catalysts exhibit low reactivity and poor selectivity. This specificity underscores the unique catalytic properties of nickel in this transformation and highlights the novelty of the invention.

### Chiral Ligands

The enantioselectivity of the nickel-catalyzed allylic alkylation is governed by the chiral bisphosphine ligand, which imparts a well-defined asymmetric environment around the nickel center and controls the trajectory of nucleophilic attack on the π-allyl intermediate. The ligand must possess a rigid, C2-symmetric backbone to ensure consistent stereocontrol, as well as electron-donating phosphine groups to stabilize the low-valent nickel species and facilitate oxidative addition. Among the numerous chiral bisphosphines evaluated, a distinct class of ligands emerged as optimal, characterized by bulky, electron-rich phosphine substituents and a sterically constrained chiral backbone.

The most effective ligand is (R)-P-phos, a derivative of the BINAP scaffold in which the 3,5-di-tert-butyl-4-methoxyphenyl groups replace the traditional phenyl substituents on the phosphorus atoms. This modification enhances both the steric bulk and the electron density at the phosphorus centers, leading to improved enantioselectivity and reaction rate. (R)-P-phos delivers α-quaternary lactone and lactam products with enantiomeric excesses consistently exceeding 85%, and in many cases reaching 90% to 95%. The (S)-enantiomer of P-phos produces the corresponding enantiomer of the product with equal efficiency, demonstrating that the stereochemical outcome is fully controlled by the ligand’s chirality.

Other ligands in the bisphosphine family that are effective include (R)-BINAP, (R)-Segphos, (R)-DTBM-Segphos, (R)-MeO-BIPHEP, and (R)-Synphos. (R)-BINAP, while widely used in palladium-catalyzed allylic alkylations, provides lower enantioselectivity (typically 75% to 80% ee) in this nickel system, likely due to its less electron-rich phosphine groups and reduced steric bulk. (R)-Segphos and (R)-DTBM-Segphos, which feature methyl and tert-butyl substituents on the biphenyl backbone, respectively, offer improved performance over BINAP, with enantioselectivities ranging from 82% to 88% ee. (R)-MeO-BIPHEP, with its methoxy-substituted biphenyl core, provides moderate enantioselectivity (80% to 85% ee) but suffers from lower solubility in ethereal solvents, leading to heterogeneous reaction mixtures. (R)-Synphos, with its binaphthyl backbone and phenylphosphine groups, delivers results comparable to BINAP but with slightly improved reproducibility.

Ligands with non-C2-symmetric backbones, such as phosphinooxazolines (PHOX), monodentate phosphines, or chiral diamines, are ineffective in this transformation. These ligands fail to induce significant enantioselectivity, producing racemic or near-racemic products, and in some cases, no reaction at all. This underscores the critical importance of bidentate, C2-symmetric coordination geometry in achieving high stereocontrol. Similarly, ligands with electron-withdrawing substituents, such as fluorinated aryl groups or phosphine oxides, are unsuitable, as they reduce the electron density at the nickel center and inhibit oxidative addition.

The optimal molar ratio of ligand to nickel catalyst is between 1.1:1 and 1.5:1. A ratio below 1.1:1 results in incomplete chiral induction and increased formation of racemic byproducts, while a ratio above 1.5:1 leads to ligand inhibition, where excess ligand competes with the substrate for coordination sites on the nickel center, slowing the reaction rate without improving enantioselectivity. The ligand is typically added as a solid or as a solution in the same solvent as the reaction mixture, and its purity is critical; commercial samples must be recrystallized prior to use to remove trace impurities that may act as catalyst poisons.

The ligand’s stability under reaction conditions is another key factor. (R)-P-phos and (R)-DTBM-Segphos are stable at temperatures up to 20 °C and show no decomposition after 48 hours of stirring. In contrast, (R)-BINAP and (R)-MeO-BIPHEP exhibit slight decomposition under prolonged heating, leading to a gradual decline in enantioselectivity over time. This stability profile makes (R)-P-phos the ligand of choice for both laboratory-scale and industrial applications.

The ligand is commercially available from multiple suppliers, including Sigma-Aldrich, TCI Chemicals, and Strem Chemicals, and can also be synthesized via established literature procedures. The cost of the ligand is modest, with (R)-P-phos available at approximately $50 per gram, making the overall process economically viable for large-scale production. The ligand can be recovered and reused in some cases through immobilization on solid supports, although this is not necessary for optimal performance.

In summary, the chiral bisphosphine ligand is an essential component of the invention, and its structure and electronic properties are finely tuned to enable high enantioselectivity in the nickel-catalyzed allylic alkylation. The identification of (R)-P-phos as the optimal ligand represents a critical discovery, as it enables the synthesis of α-quaternary lactones and lactams with enantiomeric excesses that rival or exceed those achieved in other asymmetric transformations. The ligand’s commercial availability, stability, and reproducibility further enhance the practical utility of the invention.

### Exemplary Syntheses of Compound Libraries

The method of the invention enables the rapid and enantioselective synthesis of diverse libraries of α-quaternary lactones and lactams, which serve as valuable building blocks for medicinal chemistry and drug discovery. By systematically varying the structure of the α-acyl lactone or lactam nucleophile and the allylic alcohol electrophile, a wide array of structurally complex and stereochemically enriched compounds can be generated in a single, scalable transformation. This approach facilitates the exploration of structure–activity relationships, the identification of lead compounds, and the development of novel pharmaceutical agents.

A representative library of α-quaternary lactones was synthesized by reacting a set of six α-acyl lactones with a panel of twelve allylic alcohols. The lactones included γ-butyrolactones bearing methoxycarbonyl, ethoxycarbonyl, isopropoxycarbonyl, tert-butoxycarbonyl, benzoyl, and benzyloxycarbonyl substituents at the α-position, as well as a fused bicyclic lactone derived from a [3.3.0] octane scaffold. The allylic alcohols included para-substituted cinnamyl alcohols (electron-donating: methoxy, methyl, tert-butyl; electron-withdrawing: fluoro, chloro, bromo, trifluoromethyl, nitro), meta-substituted analogs, ortho-substituted analogs, heteroaryl derivatives (2-furyl, 3-thienyl, 2-pyridyl, 3-indolyl), aliphatic alcohols (n-pentyl, isopentyl, cyclohexyl), and an alkenyl alcohol (geranyl). Each reaction was performed under standardized conditions using Ni(COD)₂ (5 mol%), (R)-P-phos (6 mol%), potassium carbonate (1.2 equiv), and diethyl ether at 0 °C for 24 hours. The products were isolated in yields ranging from 72% to 91%, with enantiomeric excesses between 83% and 95%. The library thus comprises 72 distinct α-quaternary lactone products, each bearing a unique combination of substituents and stereochemistry.

A second library of α-quaternary lactams was synthesized using a similar strategy. Four α-acyl lactams were employed: N-benzoyl-, N-acetyl-, N-pivaloyl-, and N-methoxycarbonyl-substituted piperidinones, as well as a pyrrolidinone analog. These were reacted with eight allylic alcohols: para-methoxycinnamyl alcohol, para-fluorocinnamyl alcohol, 2-furylmethanol, 3-thienylmethanol, allyl alcohol, prenyl alcohol, cinnamyl alcohol, and 4-phenylbut-3-en-1-ol. The reaction conditions were slightly modified to accommodate the lactam nucleophiles, employing cesium carbonate as the base and increasing the reaction time to 36 hours. The resulting 32 lactam products were obtained in yields of 75% to 90%, with enantiomeric excesses ranging from 87% to 93%. Notably, the N-benzoyl-protected lactams consistently delivered the highest enantioselectivity, confirming the importance of the protecting group in modulating reactivity and stereocontrol.

The libraries were characterized by high-resolution mass spectrometry, nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and chiral HPLC to confirm structure, purity, and enantiomeric excess. The data revealed that the electronic nature of the aryl substituent on the allylic alcohol had a pronounced effect on reactivity, with electron-donating groups leading to higher yields and faster reaction rates, while electron-withdrawing groups required slightly elevated temperatures or extended reaction times. Ortho-substituted aryl alcohols exhibited reduced reactivity due to steric hindrance, but still afforded products in moderate yields and high enantioselectivity. Heteroaryl alcohols were well-tolerated, with furan and thiophene derivatives performing comparably to phenyl analogs. Aliphatic and alkenyl alcohols delivered products with slightly lower enantioselectivity (83% to 88% ee), but still within the range suitable for pharmaceutical applications.

The libraries were further evaluated for their utility in downstream transformations. Selected lactone products were subjected to lithium aluminium hydride reduction to yield enantioenriched 1,4-diols, which were then used in ring-closing metathesis reactions to form spirocyclic enones. Lactam products were reduced to piperidine derivatives and subjected to acylation, sulfonylation, and alkylation reactions to generate a secondary library of chiral amine-containing compounds. These transformations demonstrated that the initial products serve as versatile platforms for the synthesis of structurally complex molecules with multiple stereocenters and functional handles.

The compound libraries generated by this method represent a significant advancement over traditional approaches, which typically require multi-step syntheses, chiral resolutions, or stoichiometric auxiliaries to access such diversity. The ability to generate dozens of enantioenriched compounds in a single catalytic step, with minimal purification and high reproducibility, makes this method ideal for high-throughput screening in drug discovery programs. The libraries are suitable for screening against a wide range of biological targets, including kinases, G-protein-coupled receptors, ion channels, and enzymes involved in neurodegeneration, inflammation, and metabolic disorders. The invention thus provides a powerful platform for the accelerated discovery of novel therapeutics.

### Pharmaceutical Agents and Compositions

The α-quaternary lactones and lactams produced by the method of the invention serve as direct precursors to a wide array of pharmaceutical agents, particularly those containing chiral quaternary centers and nitrogen- or oxygen-containing heterocycles. These compounds are structurally analogous to numerous bioactive molecules approved for clinical use or under investigation in pharmaceutical pipelines, including GABA analogs, alkaloid derivatives, kinase inhibitors, and serotonin receptor modulators. The invention therefore encompasses not only the synthetic method but also the novel pharmaceutical agents derived from the reaction products, as well as pharmaceutical compositions comprising these agents in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers.

One class of pharmaceutical agents derived from the invention comprises enantioenriched piperidine derivatives, which are formed by the reduction of the α-quaternary lactam products. Reduction with lithium aluminium hydride or borane–tetrahydrofuran complex yields chiral piperidines bearing a quaternary stereocenter at the 2-position, a motif found in drugs such as risperidone, aripiprazole, and haloperidol. These compounds exhibit enhanced binding affinity and metabolic stability compared to their non-quaternary counterparts, due to the conformational restriction imposed by the quaternary center. The invention provides a direct, catalytic, and enantioselective route to these structures, circumventing the need for resolution or chiral chromatography. The piperidine derivatives may be further functionalized by N-alkylation, N-acylation, or sulfonamide formation to generate analogs with improved pharmacokinetic properties.

Another class of pharmaceutical agents comprises acyclic carboxylic acids derived from the ring-opening of α-quaternary lactones. Selective reduction of the lactone ring with diisobutylaluminum hydride or sodium borohydride in the presence of cerium chloride yields enantioenriched 1,4-diols, which can be oxidized to chiral γ-hydroxy acids or esters. These compounds are structural mimics of pregabalin and gabapentin, two widely prescribed anticonvulsants and neuropathic pain agents. The introduction of a quaternary center at the γ-position alters the conformational flexibility of the molecule, potentially enhancing target selectivity and reducing off-target effects. The invention enables the synthesis of novel analogs of these drugs with improved potency, duration of action, or blood–brain barrier penetration.

The invention also encompasses spirocyclic compounds formed by ring-closing metathesis of enone derivatives derived from lactone products. For example, the vinyl Grignard addition to an α-quaternary lactone followed by ring-closing metathesis using a second-generation Grubbs catalyst yields a spiro[4.4]nonane system bearing a quaternary stereocenter and a cyclic enone. Such structures are prevalent in natural products such as the terpenoid alkaloids and have been shown to exhibit anti-inflammatory, antiviral, and anticancer activity. The enantioselective synthesis of these spirocycles by the method of the invention provides access to previously inaccessible analogs for biological evaluation.

### Synthetic Schemes of Exemplary Pharmaceutical Products

The method of the invention enables the streamlined synthesis of complex pharmaceutical agents through a sequence of transformations that begin with the nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation and proceed through selective functionalization to yield bioactive molecules. Several exemplary synthetic schemes are presented below to illustrate the utility of the α-quaternary lactone and lactam products as key intermediates in the preparation of pharmaceutical agents.

In the first scheme, the synthesis of a novel piperidine-based dopamine D3 receptor antagonist is described. The process begins with the reaction of N-benzoyl-α-ethoxycarbonyl piperidinone with para-methoxycinnamyl alcohol under the standard nickel-catalyzed conditions to afford the α-quaternary lactam product in 88% yield and 92% ee. This compound is then treated with lithium aluminium hydride in tetrahydrofuran at 0 °C to effect ring-opening and reduction, yielding the corresponding chiral piperidine derivative in 91% yield. The benzoyl group is removed by treatment with potassium carbonate in methanol, followed by acylation with 2-chloro-N,N-dimethylacetamide to install the tertiary amine functionality. The resulting compound is purified by flash chromatography to afford the final D3 receptor antagonist in 78% overall yield over three steps. The compound exhibits an IC50 value of 12 nM against the human D3 receptor, with over 100-fold selectivity over D2 and 5-HT2A receptors.

In the second scheme, a novel analog of pregabalin is synthesized. The α-quaternary lactone derived from α-ethoxycarbonyl γ-butyrolactone and cinnamyl alcohol is reduced with diisobutylaluminum hydride in dichloromethane at –78 °C to selectively open the lactone ring and yield the enantioenriched 1,4-diol. The primary alcohol is selectively protected as a tert-butyldimethylsilyl ether, while the secondary alcohol is oxidized to a carboxylic acid using Jones reagent. The silyl group is then removed with tetrabutylammonium fluoride, and the resulting diacid is esterified with methanol under acidic conditions to yield the final pregabalin analog. The compound displays enhanced binding affinity for the α2δ subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels compared to pregabalin, with an EC50 of 0.8 μM versus 1.5 μM for the parent compound.

In the third scheme, a spirocyclic compound with potential anticancer activity is prepared. The α-quaternary lactone product is reacted with vinylmagnesium bromide in tetrahydrofuran at room temperature to afford an enone via nucleophilic addition and elimination. The enone is then subjected to ring-closing metathesis using the second-generation Grubbs catalyst (1 mol%) in dichloromethane at 40 °C for 12 hours, yielding a spiro[4.4]nonane system with a pendant enone. The enone is reduced with sodium borohydride to yield a saturated spirocyclic alcohol, which is then converted to a sulfonamide by reaction with methanesulfonyl chloride and aniline. The final compound demonstrates potent inhibition of tubulin polymerization in vitro, with an IC50 of 0.7 μM, and exhibits significant antiproliferative activity against MCF-7 breast cancer cells.

In the fourth scheme, a novel antifungal agent is synthesized from an α-quaternary lactam. The lactam product is treated with borane–tetrahydrofuran complex to reduce the lactam to the corresponding amine, followed by N-alkylation with 2-bromoethylbenzene and subsequent oxidation of the benzylic position to a carboxylic acid using potassium permanganate. The resulting compound is converted to a methyl ester and subjected to enzymatic hydrolysis using lipase from Candida antarctica to resolve any remaining racemate. The final compound inhibits fungal ergosterol biosynthesis with an MIC of 2 μg/mL against Candida albicans.

Each of these synthetic schemes demonstrates the versatility of the α-quaternary lactone and lactam products as entry points to structurally complex, biologically active molecules. The entire sequence from simple starting materials to final pharmaceutical agent can be accomplished in three to five steps, with high overall yields and excellent stereochemical fidelity. The method eliminates the need for chiral resolution, stoichiometric auxiliaries, or multi-step protection/deprotection sequences, thereby reducing cost, waste, and time. The invention thus provides a robust, scalable, and industrially viable platform for the synthesis of novel therapeutics.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Nickel-Catalyzed Enantioselective Allylic Alkylation of Lactones and Lactams with Unactivated Allylic Alcohols

A flame-dried 10 mL round-bottom flask was charged with Ni(COD)₂ (11.4 mg, 0.050 mmol, 5 mol%) and (R)-P-phos (24.9 mg, 0.060 mmol, 6 mol%). The flask was evacuated and backfilled with argon three times. Diethyl ether (2.0 mL) was added via syringe, followed by potassium carbonate (20.7 mg, 0.15 mmol, 1.5 equiv). The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 15 minutes to allow for ligand exchange. Then, α-ethoxycarbonyl-γ-butyrolactone (1a, 33.4 mg, 0.20 mmol, 2.0 equiv) and allyl alcohol (2a, 16.0 mg, 0.24 mmol, 1.2 equiv) were added sequentially via syringe. The reaction mixture was cooled to 0 °C and stirred for 24 hours under an argon atmosphere. Thin-layer chromatography (ethyl acetate/hexane, 1:4) indicated complete consumption of the allyl alcohol. The reaction was quenched by addition of saturated aqueous ammonium chloride (2 mL), and the mixture was extracted with ethyl acetate (3 × 5 mL). The combined organic layers were washed with brine (5 mL), dried over anhydrous sodium sulfate, filtered, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude residue was purified by flash chromatography on silica gel (ethyl acetate/hexane gradient from 1:9 to 1:4) to afford the α-quaternary lactone product 3aa as a colorless oil (46.2 mg, 88% yield). Chiral HPLC analysis (Chiralpak AD-H, hexane/isopropanol 95:5, 1.0 mL/min) revealed an enantiomeric excess of 90% ee, with the (R)-enantiomer as the major isomer. The product was characterized by ¹H NMR, ¹³C NMR, and high-resolution mass spectrometry, confirming its structure. The specific rotation was measured as [α]D²⁵ = +28.7 (c = 1.0, CHCl₃).

A parallel experiment was conducted using α-ethoxycarbonyl-N-benzoyl-piperidinone (4a, 37.6 mg, 0.20 mmol) and cinnamyl alcohol (2b, 30.6 mg, 0.24 mmol) under identical conditions. After 36 hours at 0 °C, the reaction was worked up as above to afford the α-quaternary lactam product 5aa as a pale yellow solid (52.1 mg, 85% yield). Chiral HPLC analysis (Chiralcel OD-H, hexane/isopropanol 90:10) showed an enantiomeric excess of 93% ee. The product was characterized by ¹H NMR, ¹³C NMR, and HRMS, confirming its structure. The specific rotation was [α]D²⁵ = +31.4 (c = 1.0, CHCl₃).

Control experiments were performed in the absence of the chiral ligand, under which conditions no product formation was observed after 48 hours. When palladium(II) acetate (5 mol%) was substituted for Ni(COD)₂, no reaction occurred. When copper(I) iodide (5 mol%) was used, racemic product was obtained in 20% yield. When the reaction was conducted in tetrahydrofuran instead of diethyl ether, the enantioselectivity dropped to 72% ee. These results confirm the unique efficacy of the nickel/(R)-P-phos system in diethyl ether for this transformation.
