# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to novel promoter elements derived from human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6) and their use in driving high-level expression of heterologous genes in eukaryotic cells, particularly in T lymphocytes. More specifically, the invention encompasses a recombinant DNA construct comprising a promoter region derived from the major immediate-early gene locus of HHV-6, optionally in combination with the first intron of the HHV-6 IE1 gene, which exhibits significantly enhanced transcriptional activity compared to the well-established cytomegalovirus (CMV) major immediate-early promoter in T-cell lines. The invention further includes methods for utilizing this promoter to express foreign genes in mammalian cells, including but not limited to primary T cells, T-cell lines, and recombinant viral vectors such as varicella-zoster virus (VZV). The promoter elements disclosed herein are particularly useful in the fields of gene therapy, immunotherapy, vaccine development, and biopharmaceutical production, where efficient and cell-type-specific gene expression is required. The invention provides a powerful alternative to the CMV promoter, especially in contexts where CMV-driven expression is suboptimal or subject to silencing in immune cells, thereby enabling more robust and sustained transgene expression in clinically relevant cell populations.

## BACKGROUND ART

The regulation of gene expression in mammalian cells has long been a cornerstone of molecular biology and biotechnology. Among the most widely employed tools for driving high-level transgene expression are viral promoters, which have evolved to efficiently hijack the host transcriptional machinery. The cytomegalovirus (CMV) major immediate-early promoter, derived from human cytomegalovirus, has become the gold standard in this regard. Its robust activity across a broad spectrum of cell types—including fibroblasts, epithelial cells, and many immortalized lines—has rendered it indispensable in research laboratories and commercial applications alike. It is routinely used in plasmid vectors for transient transfection, in viral vectors for gene delivery, and in transgenic animal models to achieve constitutive expression of reporter genes, therapeutic proteins, and immunomodulatory molecules. Despite its widespread utility, the CMV promoter is not without limitations. In certain cell types, particularly primary T lymphocytes and other immune cells, its activity is often diminished or subject to epigenetic silencing over time, leading to inconsistent or transient transgene expression. This phenomenon has posed significant challenges in the development of T-cell-based therapies, including chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapies, T-cell receptor (TCR) engineering, and viral vector-based vaccines targeting immune responses.

Human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6), a member of the beta-herpesvirus subfamily, shares considerable genomic and biological homology with CMV and HHV-7. HHV-6 exists in two distinct variants, HHV-6A and HHV-6B, both of which exhibit a striking tropism for T lymphocytes. While HHV-6B is the primary etiological agent of exanthema subitum in infants, HHV-6A remains enigmatic in terms of its pathogenic role, though it is increasingly associated with neurological and immunological disorders. Importantly, HHV-6 replicates efficiently in T cells, suggesting that its regulatory elements have evolved to optimally interact with the transcriptional environment of these cells. The HHV-6 genome contains a major immediate-early (MIE) locus that is structurally analogous to the CMV MIE locus, encoding homologs of the CMV IE1 and IE2 proteins, designated U89 and U86, respectively. These proteins are critical for initiating the viral lytic cycle and are expressed immediately upon infection, prior to viral DNA replication. The promoter region upstream of the U89 gene, which drives expression of the MIE transcripts, has been the subject of limited investigation. Prior studies have identified a region known as R3, located near the right end of the HHV-6 genome, as a potent enhancer of the U95 gene, another immediate-early transcript. However, the precise boundaries of the promoter responsible for driving the MIE genes, its functional domains, and its comparative strength relative to the CMV promoter had not been systematically characterized.

Moreover, while introns have long been recognized as potential modulators of gene expression—through mechanisms such as enhancing transcriptional initiation, promoting mRNA stability, or facilitating nuclear export—their role in the context of herpesvirus promoters has been incompletely explored. In CMV, the inclusion of intron A within the MIE expression cassette has been shown to modestly enhance promoter activity, but this effect is neither universal nor sufficient to overcome the inherent limitations of the CMV promoter in T cells. No prior work has demonstrated that an intron from an HHV-6 gene can substantially augment the activity of its cognate promoter, nor has any study established that an HHV-6-derived promoter can outperform the CMV promoter in T-cell lines. Furthermore, the functional contribution of specific transcription factor binding sites within the HHV-6 MIE promoter region, such as NF-κB and AP-1 motifs, had not been experimentally validated in the context of promoter-driven gene expression. Consequently, there has remained a critical unmet need in the field for a promoter system that is not only highly active in T cells but also superior in performance to the CMV promoter in this specific cellular context, without compromising its utility in other cell types. The absence of such a tool has hindered progress in T-cell engineering, where persistent and high-level transgene expression is essential for therapeutic efficacy and durability.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The primary problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of a promoter system capable of driving high-level, sustained, and cell-type-specific expression of heterologous genes in T lymphocytes. While the cytomegalovirus (CMV) major immediate-early promoter has been the dominant tool for transgene expression across diverse cell types, its performance in T cells is consistently suboptimal. In primary T cells and many T-cell lines, CMV promoter activity is often weak, transient, or subject to silencing due to epigenetic modifications, chromatin remodeling, or the absence of key transcription factors that are abundant in other cell types but scarce or inactive in lymphocytes. This limitation has impeded the development of effective T-cell-based therapies, including adoptive cell transfer, CAR-T cell engineering, and T-cell-targeted vaccines, where prolonged and robust expression of transgenes such as receptors, cytokines, or co-stimulatory molecules is critical for therapeutic success. Additionally, in recombinant viral vectors that infect or replicate in T cells, the use of the CMV promoter may lead to inefficient transgene expression, reduced vector immunogenicity, or premature loss of transgene function. There is therefore a pressing need for a promoter that is not merely functional in T cells, but that actively outperforms the CMV promoter in this context, providing a significant and reproducible advantage in gene expression levels.

A further problem lies in the absence of a comprehensive understanding of the regulatory elements within the HHV-6 major immediate-early locus. Although HHV-6 is known to replicate efficiently in T cells, the promoter region responsible for driving expression of its immediate-early genes had not been definitively identified, nor had its transcriptional architecture been mapped in relation to known enhancer elements or intronic sequences. Previous studies had suggested that the R3 region, located upstream of the U95 gene, might possess enhancer activity, but its potential role in regulating the MIE genes had not been tested. Moreover, the possibility that introns within the HHV-6 MIE transcripts themselves could modulate promoter activity had never been investigated. Without a clear delineation of the minimal functional promoter, the identification of critical cis-regulatory elements, or an understanding of how intronic sequences influence transcriptional output, it was impossible to engineer a superior promoter system based on HHV-6. The invention solves these problems by identifying the precise promoter region responsible for driving HHV-6 MIE gene expression, characterizing its functional domains, demonstrating its superior activity over the CMV promoter in T-cell lines, and revealing that the inclusion of the first intron of the IE1 gene dramatically enhances this activity, resulting in a promoter system with unprecedented performance in T cells.

### Solutions to the Problems

The invention provides a solution to the aforementioned problems through the identification and characterization of a novel promoter element derived from human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6). This promoter, designated the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter (HHV-6 MIEp), is located upstream of the U89 gene and encompasses a region extending from approximately -983 to +130 base pairs relative to the transcription start site, including the R3 enhancer domain and the first intron of the IE1 gene. The invention demonstrates that this promoter exhibits significantly higher transcriptional activity than the CMV major immediate-early promoter in multiple T-cell lines, including Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1 cells, while maintaining comparable or slightly reduced activity in non-T-cell lines such as MRC-5 fibroblasts and MeWo melanoma cells. This cell-type-specific enhancement is attributed to the presence of a functional NF-κB binding site within the R3 region, which is essential for full promoter activity, as deletion of this site results in a dramatic reduction in transcriptional output across all cell types. Furthermore, the invention reveals that the inclusion of the first intron of the IE1 gene, which is naturally spliced from the primary transcript, substantially augments promoter activity, increasing expression levels by up to fivefold in T-cell lines compared to the promoter lacking this intron. The resulting construct, comprising the HHV-6 MIE promoter and the first intron (HHV-6 MIEp-in1), achieves expression levels that are consistently and significantly higher than those driven by the CMV promoter in T cells, while remaining highly active in other cell types, thereby providing a broadly applicable yet T-cell-optimized expression system.

The invention further provides a method for constructing recombinant gene expression cassettes using the HHV-6 MIE promoter with or without the first intron, wherein the promoter is operably linked to a heterologous coding sequence and a polyadenylation signal. These cassettes can be incorporated into plasmid vectors, viral vectors, or bacterial artificial chromosomes (BACs) for delivery into mammalian cells. The invention demonstrates the utility of this system by successfully incorporating the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 cassette into the genome of a recombinant varicella-zoster virus (VZV) vaccine strain, where it drives robust expression of the DsRed2 fluorescent reporter gene. The recombinant virus retains full infectivity, and the transgene is expressed at high levels under the control of the HHV-6 promoter, confirming its functionality in the context of a complex viral genome. This establishes that the HHV-6 MIE promoter is not only competent in plasmid-based systems but also fully compatible with large DNA viruses, making it suitable for use in live attenuated vaccines, oncolytic virotherapy, and other advanced delivery platforms. The invention thus provides a complete, validated, and broadly applicable solution to the long-standing problem of inadequate transgene expression in T cells, offering a superior alternative to the CMV promoter in both research and therapeutic contexts.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The invention delivers a number of substantial and unexpected advantages over existing promoter systems. First and foremost, the HHV-6 MIE promoter, particularly when combined with the first intron of the IE1 gene, provides a level of transcriptional activity in T-cell lines that exceeds that of the CMV promoter by a statistically significant and biologically meaningful margin. This enhancement is not a marginal improvement but a fundamental shift in performance, enabling higher protein yields, more consistent transgene expression across cell populations, and prolonged expression kinetics that are critical for therapeutic applications. The cell-type specificity of the promoter—strong in T cells, moderately active in other cell types—provides a unique advantage in applications where targeted expression is desired, reducing off-target effects and minimizing immune activation in non-relevant tissues. Second, the invention identifies the NF-κB binding site within the R3 region as a critical determinant of promoter strength, providing a molecular rationale for its enhanced activity in T cells, where NF-κB signaling is frequently activated. This mechanistic insight allows for the rational design of future promoter variants, including synthetic promoters with optimized transcription factor binding motifs or chimeric constructs incorporating elements from other immune-responsive promoters.

Third, the discovery that the first intron of the IE1 gene acts as a powerful transcriptional enhancer is entirely novel and unexpected. Introns are often viewed as non-essential or even detrimental to expression due to potential splicing inefficiencies or mRNA instability. The invention demonstrates the opposite: that a single intron, when derived from the HHV-6 MIE transcript, can dramatically amplify promoter output without compromising mRNA integrity or translational efficiency. This finding opens new avenues for optimizing gene expression cassettes by incorporating intronic sequences from herpesviruses, a strategy previously unexplored in the field. Fourth, the successful integration and function of the HHV-6 MIE promoter in a recombinant VZV genome establishes its compatibility with complex viral systems, proving that it can function in the context of a large DNA virus undergoing its own complex transcriptional regulation. This is a critical validation, as many promoters that work well in plasmids fail in viral vectors due to interference with viral promoters, chromatin structure, or replication timing. The fact that the HHV-6 promoter drives robust expression of a foreign gene in VZV-infected cells confirms its robustness and versatility.

Finally, the invention provides a commercially viable and patentable alternative to the CMV promoter, which is extensively used but subject to intellectual property restrictions and licensing fees. The HHV-6 MIE promoter is derived from a naturally occurring viral sequence that is not currently patented for gene expression purposes, allowing for unrestricted use in research, diagnostics, and therapeutics without the need for third-party licensing. This freedom-to-operate advantage, combined with superior performance, positions the invention as a transformative tool for the biotechnology and pharmaceutical industries, with direct applications in gene therapy, vaccine development, cell engineering, and protein production. The invention thus represents a significant advancement in the field of gene expression control, offering a more powerful, more specific, and more freely usable promoter system than any previously available.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### DEFINITION

For the purposes of this patent application, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed herein. The term “promoter” refers to a DNA sequence located upstream of a coding region that is recognized by RNA polymerase and associated transcription factors to initiate transcription of a downstream gene. A “major immediate-early promoter” is a promoter that drives the expression of viral genes immediately following infection, prior to viral DNA replication, and is typically characterized by high transcriptional activity and responsiveness to cellular signaling pathways. The term “HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter” or “HHV-6 MIEp” refers to a specific DNA sequence derived from the human herpesvirus 6 genome, located upstream of the U89 gene, which is capable of directing transcription of a heterologous gene in eukaryotic cells, and which includes the region spanning from approximately -983 to +130 nucleotides relative to the transcription start site of the U89 gene, including the R3 enhancer element and optionally the first intron of the IE1 gene. The “first intron of the IE1 gene” refers to the intronic sequence located between the first and second exons of the U89 transcript, which is naturally spliced out during mRNA maturation and has been found to enhance transcriptional activity when included in a promoter construct. The term “operably linked” means that a promoter is positioned in a functional relationship with a coding sequence such that it is capable of initiating transcription of that sequence. A “heterologous gene” or “heterologous coding sequence” refers to a nucleic acid sequence that is not naturally associated with the promoter in its native context and is introduced for the purpose of expressing a foreign protein, such as a reporter gene, therapeutic protein, antigen, or receptor. The term “T-cell line” refers to an immortalized cell line derived from human T lymphocytes, including but not limited to Jurkat, Molt-3, SupT1, and CEM cells, which retain key features of primary T cells including expression of T-cell-specific transcription factors and signaling pathways. The term “recombinant virus” refers to a virus whose genome has been genetically modified to include a heterologous gene under the control of a non-native promoter, such as the HHV-6 MIE promoter, and which retains the ability to infect and replicate in host cells. The term “expression cassette” refers to a DNA construct comprising a promoter, a coding sequence, and a polyadenylation signal, optionally including introns, splice sites, and other regulatory elements necessary for efficient gene expression. The term “NF-κB binding site” refers to a specific DNA sequence motif recognized by the nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells transcription factor, typically characterized by the consensus sequence GGGRNYYYCC, where R is a purine, Y is a pyrimidine, and N is any nucleotide, and which is essential for the transcriptional activity of the HHV-6 MIE promoter. The term “R3 region” refers to a repetitive sequence element located within the HHV-6 genome, approximately 1.5 kb upstream of the U89 gene, which contains multiple transcription factor binding sites including NF-κB and AP-1 motifs and contributes to the enhancer activity of the promoter. The term “transfection efficiency” refers to the proportion of cells in a population that have successfully taken up and expressed an exogenous nucleic acid construct, which may be normalized using a co-transfected control reporter such as Renilla luciferase under the control of the herpes simplex virus thymidine kinase promoter. The term “cell-type-specific promoter activity” refers to the differential ability of a promoter to drive gene expression in one cell type compared to another, such that the promoter exhibits significantly higher activity in T cells than in non-T cells. The term “recombinant varicella-zoster virus” refers to a genetically modified strain of varicella-zoster virus, such as the Oka vaccine strain, that has been engineered to contain a heterologous gene under the control of the HHV-6 MIE promoter and that retains the ability to infect human cells and produce infectious progeny.

## DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### (Enhancer for Promoter)

The enhancer element critical to the function of the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter resides within a region designated R3, which is located approximately 550 to 380 base pairs upstream of the transcription start site of the U89 gene. This region, which is part of the direct repeat structure at the right end of the HHV-6 genome, contains multiple conserved transcription factor binding motifs, including a functional NF-κB binding site and an AP-1 binding site. Deletion analysis of the promoter region revealed that removal of the segment spanning nucleotides -552 to -381 resulted in a dramatic reduction in transcriptional activity across all tested cell lines, indicating that this segment is indispensable for promoter function. Further mutagenesis studies confirmed that point mutations or small deletions within the NF-κB binding site, specifically within the core sequence GGGAATTTCC, led to a near-complete loss of promoter activity, whereas mutations in the adjacent AP-1 site had a comparatively minor effect. The NF-κB binding site is conserved between HHV-6A and HHV-6B strains, suggesting its fundamental role in viral biology. The enhancer activity of the R3 region is not dependent on orientation or distance, as demonstrated by its ability to activate a minimal promoter when placed upstream, downstream, or in an inverted configuration relative to the transcription start site. However, its maximal effect is observed when positioned in its native location, approximately 450 base pairs upstream of the transcription start site, indicating that its function is optimized by the surrounding chromatin context and the presence of other nearby regulatory elements. The R3 enhancer is responsive to cellular stimuli that activate the NF-κB pathway, including tumor necrosis factor-alpha, interleukin-1, and phorbol esters, which further supports its role as a signal-responsive enhancer. This responsiveness is particularly advantageous in T cells, where NF-κB signaling is a central component of activation, proliferation, and survival pathways. The enhancer does not function independently as a promoter in the absence of the core promoter elements, but rather acts synergistically with the basal transcription machinery assembled at the transcription start site. The R3 enhancer is distinct from classical viral enhancers such as those found in simian virus 40 or adenovirus, in that it lacks tandem repeats of identical motifs and instead contains a complex arrangement of overlapping and adjacent binding sites that likely facilitate cooperative binding of multiple transcription factors. This architectural complexity may contribute to its cell-type specificity, as the availability and phosphorylation state of NF-κB subunits vary significantly between cell types. The enhancer is also resistant to silencing in T cells, a common problem with other viral enhancers, suggesting that it is recognized by T-cell-specific co-activators or chromatin remodelers that maintain an open chromatin configuration. The R3 enhancer, when fused to heterologous promoters, can significantly boost their activity in T cells, making it a modular component that can be incorporated into synthetic expression systems to enhance transgene expression in immune cells.

### (Promoter)

The HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter is a DNA sequence of approximately 1,100 to 1,300 base pairs in length, depending on the inclusion of the first intron, and is derived from the 5’ flanking region of the U89 gene of human herpesvirus 6. The minimal functional promoter, lacking the first intron, spans from nucleotide position -983 to +130 relative to the transcription start site, and contains a TATA box at approximately -30, a CAAT box at approximately -80, and the critical R3 enhancer region located between -552 and -381. This core promoter is sufficient to drive detectable levels of gene expression in a variety of mammalian cell types, including fibroblasts, epithelial cells, and astrocytoma lines, although its activity in these cells is generally lower than that of the CMV promoter. However, in T-cell lines such as Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1, the same minimal promoter exhibits transcriptional activity that is two- to fivefold greater than that of the CMV promoter, as measured by luciferase reporter assays and quantitative RT-PCR. The promoter is capable of initiating transcription from a single major start site, as determined by 5’ RACE analysis, and produces a single predominant transcript in transfected cells. The promoter sequence is highly conserved between HHV-6A and HHV-6B isolates, with greater than 95% nucleotide identity in the core promoter and R3 regions, indicating strong evolutionary pressure to maintain its function. The promoter does not contain any known silencer elements, and its activity is not suppressed by CpG methylation, which is a common mechanism of CMV promoter inactivation in immune cells. The promoter is functional in both transient and stable transfection systems, and its activity remains stable over multiple cell divisions in clonal cell lines, suggesting that it is not subject to rapid epigenetic silencing. The promoter is also compatible with a wide range of vector backbones, including plasmids, adenoviral vectors, lentiviral vectors, and bacterial artificial chromosomes, without loss of function. The promoter does not contain any known recombination hotspots or repetitive sequences that would compromise its stability during cloning or propagation in bacterial systems. The promoter sequence is free from known immunostimulatory motifs such as CpG islands that might trigger unwanted innate immune responses, making it suitable for in vivo applications. The promoter is active in both human and non-human primate cells, and shows cross-species functionality in murine T-cell lines, albeit at reduced levels, indicating broad utility across mammalian species. The promoter can be chemically synthesized, cloned from viral genomic DNA using PCR, or obtained from recombinant plasmid libraries, and is readily amenable to modification for the insertion of restriction sites, epitope tags, or other regulatory elements. The promoter sequence is provided in SEQ ID NO: 1, and its functional variants, including those with deletions or point mutations in the NF-κB site, are provided in SEQ ID NOs: 2 through 7.

### (Gene Construct)

The invention provides a gene construct comprising a transcriptional unit operably linked to the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter, wherein the transcriptional unit includes a heterologous coding sequence and a polyadenylation signal. The gene construct may be in the form of a plasmid, a viral vector, or a bacterial artificial chromosome, and may be linear or circular. The promoter is positioned immediately upstream of the coding sequence, with no intervening introns or untranslated regions between the promoter and the start codon, although the first intron of the IE1 gene may be included between the promoter and the coding sequence to further enhance expression. The heterologous coding sequence may encode any protein of interest, including but not limited to fluorescent proteins such as green fluorescent protein (GFP), red fluorescent protein (DsRed2), or luciferase; therapeutic proteins such as interleukins, interferons, or tumor necrosis factor receptors; chimeric antigen receptors (CARs); T-cell receptors (TCRs); enzymes for metabolic correction; or antigens for vaccine development. The polyadenylation signal may be derived from any source, including the bovine growth hormone (BGH) poly(A) signal, the SV40 late poly(A) signal, or the human β-globin poly(A) signal, and is positioned downstream of the coding sequence to ensure proper mRNA processing and stability. The gene construct may further include a splice donor site and a splice acceptor site flanking the first intron of the IE1 gene to ensure its efficient removal during mRNA maturation, although the intron may also be retained in the mature transcript if desired. The gene construct may be designed for use in transient expression systems, where it is delivered by transfection or electroporation, or for stable integration into the host genome, where it is delivered via viral vectors such as lentivirus, retrovirus, or adeno-associated virus. The construct may also be incorporated into the genome of a recombinant virus, such as varicella-zoster virus, herpes simplex virus, or vaccinia virus, using homologous recombination or site-specific recombination techniques such as Tn7 transposition. The gene construct may be flanked by loxP or FRT sites for Cre- or Flp-mediated excision, or by attB/attP sites for PhiC31-mediated integration, to enable controlled expression or removal of the transgene cassette. The construct may include a selectable marker, such as a gene conferring resistance to antibiotics like neomycin, puromycin, or hygromycin, or a fluorescent marker such as GFP, to facilitate selection and tracking of transfected or transduced cells. The gene construct is free from bacterial origin of replication and antibiotic resistance genes when intended for use in human therapeutic applications, and may be purified using endotoxin-free methods to meet regulatory standards. The gene construct may be formulated in liposomes, nanoparticles, or other delivery vehicles for in vivo administration. The gene construct, when expressed in T cells, produces protein levels that are at least two-fold higher than those produced by an identical construct containing the CMV promoter, and in some cases up to ten-fold higher, depending on the cell type and transfection method.

The invention provides a method for enhancing the expression of a heterologous gene in a eukaryotic cell, particularly a T lymphocyte, comprising the steps of introducing into the cell a gene construct comprising the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter operably linked to the heterologous gene and a polyadenylation signal, wherein the promoter includes the R3 enhancer region and optionally the first intron of the IE1 gene. The method may further comprise the step of selecting cells that express the heterologous gene using fluorescence-activated cell sorting, antibiotic selection, or magnetic bead separation. The method may be used to express a therapeutic protein, such as a chimeric antigen receptor, in T cells for adoptive cell therapy, wherein the resulting engineered T cells are infused back into the patient to target cancer cells. The method may be used to express an antigen, such as a viral glycoprotein, in antigen-presenting cells to stimulate an immune response, thereby serving as a vaccine platform. The method may be used to express a reporter gene, such as luciferase, for in vivo imaging of T-cell trafficking and persistence. The method may be used in combination with CRISPR-Cas9 or other genome editing tools to simultaneously knock out endogenous genes and introduce the transgene under the control of the HHV-6 promoter. The method may be used in high-throughput screening platforms to identify novel regulators of T-cell function by expressing libraries of cDNAs or shRNAs under the control of the HHV-6 promoter. The method may be used to generate transgenic animals, such as mice, in which the heterologous gene is expressed specifically in T cells under the control of the HHV-6 promoter, enabling the study of immune responses in vivo. The method is characterized by its ability to produce sustained, high-level expression of the heterologous gene in T cells over extended periods, with minimal silencing or loss of expression, thereby overcoming the limitations of conventional promoters such as CMV. The method does not require the use of exogenous inducers or chemical activators, as the promoter is constitutively active in T cells due to endogenous signaling pathways. The method is scalable and compatible with current Good Manufacturing Practices for the production of clinical-grade gene therapy products.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

To determine the relative transcriptional activity of the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter compared to the cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter, a series of luciferase reporter assays were performed in multiple mammalian cell lines. A DNA fragment spanning nucleotides -983 to +130 relative to the transcription start site of the HHV-6 U89 gene was amplified by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) from genomic DNA of HHV-6B strain HST and cloned into the pGL3-basic vector (Promega) upstream of the firefly luciferase gene, generating the construct designated pGL3-HHV6MIEp. A parallel construct containing the CMV major immediate-early promoter was generated by excising the CMV promoter from pcDNA3.1(+) (Invitrogen) and inserting it into the same vector at the SmaI and BglII sites, yielding pGL3-CMV. The pRL-TK plasmid, encoding Renilla luciferase under the control of the herpes simplex virus thymidine kinase promoter, was used as an internal control for transfection efficiency. Adherent cell lines—MRC-5 (human lung fibroblasts), MeWo (human melanoma), and U373 (human astrocytoma)—were plated at a density of 1 × 10⁵ cells per well in 24-well plates and transfected the following day with 1 μg of the reporter plasmid and 0.25 μg of pRL-TK using Lipofectamine 2000 according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Suspended T-cell lines—Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1—were transfected in suspension using the same reagent with 1 μg of reporter plasmid and 0.25 μg of pRL-TK. Forty-eight hours after transfection, cells were lysed in passive lysis buffer, and firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were measured sequentially using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega) on a TriStar LB941 luminometer. Luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity to account for variations in transfection efficiency. The data were expressed as fold induction relative to the background activity of the empty pGL3-basic vector. In MRC-5, MeWo, and U373 cells, the CMV promoter drove luciferase expression at levels 1.8- to 2.5-fold higher than the HHV-6 MIE promoter. However, in Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1 cells, the HHV-6 MIE promoter drove expression at levels 3.1-, 4.2-, and 3.8-fold higher than the CMV promoter, respectively. The differences in promoter activity between the two constructs in T-cell lines were statistically significant (p < 0.001, two-tailed Student’s t-test), while no significant difference was observed in non-T-cell lines. These results demonstrate that the HHV-6 MIE promoter exhibits superior transcriptional activity in T-cell lines compared to the CMV promoter, while maintaining comparable activity in other cell types, establishing its potential as a T-cell-optimized expression system.

### Example 2

To investigate the role of intronic sequences in modulating the activity of the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter, a series of promoter constructs containing different combinations of introns from the IE1 gene were generated. The full-length HHV-6 MIE promoter (pGL3-HHV6MIEp) was used as the baseline. Introns 1 through 4 of the IE1 gene were individually and in combination amplified by PCR using primers designed to flank each intron and inserted into the promoter construct immediately downstream of the transcription start site but upstream of the luciferase coding sequence, generating constructs designated pGL3-HHV6MIEp-in1, pGL3-HHV6MIEp-in1in2, pGL3-HHV6MIEp-in1in2in3, and pGL3-HHV6MIEp-in1in2in3in4. The intron sequences were verified by sequencing to ensure fidelity. These constructs, along with the parental promoter lacking introns, were transfected into MRC-5, MeWo, U373, Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1 cells as described in Example 1. Luciferase activity was measured 48 hours post-transfection and normalized to Renilla luciferase. In all six cell lines, the inclusion of intron 1 alone resulted in a significant increase in promoter activity: in MRC-5 cells, activity increased by 1.7-fold; in MeWo cells, by 1.9-fold; in U373 cells, by 1.8-fold; in Jurkat cells, by 4.8-fold; in Molt-3 cells, by 5.2-fold; and in SupT1 cells, by 4.6-fold. When intron 2 was added to intron 1, promoter activity decreased in all cell lines, returning to levels similar to or slightly below those of the promoter without any introns. Further addition of introns 3 and 4 did not restore activity and in some cases further reduced expression. The highest activity was consistently observed with the promoter containing only intron 1. The increase in activity conferred by intron 1 was statistically significant (p < 0.001) in all T-cell lines and remained significant in non-T-cell lines. These results indicate that the first intron of the IE1 gene functions as a potent transcriptional enhancer of the HHV-6 MIE promoter, and that the presence of additional introns may have a repressive effect, possibly due to the introduction of splicing inefficiencies, premature polyadenylation signals, or inhibitory RNA secondary structures. The construct containing the HHV-6 MIE promoter and intron 1 (HHV-6 MIEp-in1) was selected for all subsequent experiments as it provided the optimal balance of high activity and simplicity.

### Example 3

To identify the cis-regulatory elements within the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter that are essential for its transcriptional activity, a series of 5’ deletion mutants were constructed and analyzed. Starting from the full-length promoter (pGL3-HHV6MIEp-in1), progressive deletions were made from the 5’ end using PCR with primers designed to remove increasing segments upstream of the transcription start site. The resulting constructs included pGL3-HHV6MIEp-d1 (deletion from -983 to -214), pGL3-HHV6MIEp-d2 (deletion from -983 to -381), pGL3-HHV6MIEp-d3 (deletion from -983 to -552), and pGL3-HHV6MIEp-d4 (deletion from -983 to -700). Each construct was transfected into Jurkat, Molt-3, SupT1, MRC-5, MeWo, and U373 cells, and luciferase activity was measured as described in Example 1. The deletion of the region between -552 and -381 (construct d3) resulted in a drastic reduction in promoter activity in all cell lines, with a decrease of 85–92% compared to the full-length promoter. In contrast, deletion of the region upstream of -552 (construct d4) had a minimal effect, reducing activity by only 10–15%. Deletion of the region between -381 and -214 (construct d2) reduced activity by approximately 40–50%, indicating that this region contains additional enhancer elements. The most significant loss of activity was observed when the region between -552 and -381 was removed, suggesting that this segment contains a critical enhancer. Bioinformatics analysis of this region revealed the presence of a consensus NF-κB binding site (GGGAATTTCC) centered at nucleotide -465 and an AP-1 binding site (TGACTCA) at -485. To test the functional importance of the NF-κB site, a mutant construct was generated in which the core NF-κB sequence was mutated from GGGAATTTCC to GGTAATTTCC using site-directed mutagenesis, resulting in pGL3-HHV6MIEp-in1-ΔNF-κB. This construct was transfected into all six cell lines, and luciferase activity was measured. The mutant promoter exhibited a 90–95% reduction in activity compared to the wild-type promoter, regardless of cell type. Mutation of the AP-1 site alone resulted in only a 20–30% reduction in activity. These results demonstrate that the NF-κB binding site within the R3 region is indispensable for the full transcriptional activity of the HHV-6 MIE promoter and that its function is conserved across diverse cell types. The AP-1 site contributes modestly to activity but is not essential. The data confirm that the R3 region, and specifically the NF-κB binding site within it, is the primary enhancer element responsible for the high activity of the HHV-6 MIE promoter in T cells.

### Example 4

To demonstrate the utility of the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter in the context of a recombinant viral vector, a gene expression cassette containing the HHV-6 MIE promoter with intron 1 (HHV-6 MIEp-in1) was inserted into the genome of the varicella-zoster virus (VZV) Oka vaccine strain. The HHV-6 MIEp-in1 sequence was amplified by PCR and cloned into the pFastBac1 plasmid (Invitrogen) in place of the polyhedrin promoter and gentamicin resistance gene, generating the plasmid HHV-6MIEpin1-pFastBac. The DsRed2 coding sequence, encoding a red fluorescent protein, was amplified from pDsRed2-C1 (Clontech) using primers introducing HindIII sites at both ends and cloned into the HHV-6MIEpin1-pFastBac plasmid downstream of the promoter, followed by the bovine growth hormone polyadenylation signal, yielding the construct HHV-6MIEpin1-DsRed-pFastBac. The VZV Oka strain was maintained as a bacterial artificial chromosome (vOka-BAC) in E. coli DH10B cells. A LacZα-mini-attTn7 cassette was inserted into the vOka-BAC genome using RecA-mediated recombination, generating vOka-BAC-Tn. The HHV-6MIEpin1-DsRed cassette was then inserted into the vOka-BAC-Tn genome via Tn7-mediated site-specific transposition, using the helper plasmid pMON7124. Transposition events were selected on LB agar plates containing X-gal and IPTG, and white colonies (indicative of successful insertion) were screened by PCR for the presence of the DsRed2 gene. Correct insertion was confirmed by restriction fragment length polymorphism (RFLP) analysis using BamHI digestion, which showed a shift in band size from 7.8 kb in the parental vOka-BAC to 7.5 kb in the recombinant construct, consistent with the expected size of the inserted cassette. Southern blot analysis using probes specific for the HHV-6 MIE promoter and the DsRed2 gene confirmed the presence of the cassette only in the recombinant genome and not in the parental vOka-BAC. The recombinant vOka-BAC DNA was purified and transfected into MRC-5 cells by electroporation. Five days post-transfection, cytopathic effects consistent with VZV infection were observed, and red fluorescence from DsRed2 was detected by fluorescence microscopy, indicating expression of the transgene. Green fluorescence from the GFP marker included in the BAC backbone was also observed, confirming successful reconstitution of infectious virus. Western blot analysis of cell lysates from infected MRC-5 cells using an anti-DsRed monoclonal antibody revealed a specific band at 29 kDa, corresponding to the expected molecular weight of DsRed2, in cells infected with the recombinant virus but not in those infected with the parental vOka-BAC. Expression of the VZV glycoprotein B (gB), a late viral protein, was detected in both samples, confirming productive infection. These results demonstrate that the HHV-6 MIE promoter is fully functional within the context of a large DNA virus and can drive high-level expression of a heterologous gene in VZV-infected cells. The recombinant virus retains full infectivity and replicative capacity, validating the HHV-6 MIE promoter as a viable and robust tool for gene expression in recombinant herpesviruses.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The invention has substantial and diverse industrial applicability across multiple sectors of biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, and medical research. In the field of gene therapy, the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter, particularly in combination with the first intron of the IE1 gene, provides a superior alternative to the cytomegalovirus promoter for driving sustained transgene expression in T lymphocytes, which are central to the success of adoptive cell therapies such as CAR-T and TCR-T treatments. The enhanced and persistent expression of chimeric antigen receptors, cytokines, or safety switches under the control of this promoter will lead to more effective and durable clinical outcomes, reducing the need for repeated dosing and improving patient safety. In the vaccine development industry, the promoter can be incorporated into viral vectors such as varicella-zoster virus, adenovirus, or vaccinia virus to express pathogen-derived antigens in antigen-presenting cells, thereby eliciting potent and long-lasting immune responses. The promoter’s T-cell specificity may reduce off-target immune activation, enhancing vaccine safety profiles. In the field of biopharmaceutical manufacturing, the promoter can be used in T-cell-based expression systems to produce recombinant proteins, monoclonal antibodies, or enzymes with high yield and purity, circumventing the limitations of traditional mammalian cell lines such as CHO or HEK293, which often exhibit low productivity for certain immunomodulatory proteins. In diagnostic and imaging applications, the promoter can be used to generate transgenic reporter systems for tracking T-cell migration, activation, and persistence in vivo, enabling non-invasive monitoring of immune responses in preclinical models and clinical trials. In synthetic biology, the promoter serves as a constitutive, cell-type-specific input module for constructing genetic circuits in engineered T cells, enabling sophisticated control over therapeutic behavior. The promoter is compatible with current Good Manufacturing Practices, can be chemically synthesized at scale, and is free from intellectual property restrictions that encumber the CMV promoter, making it an attractive commercial asset for licensing and product development. The promoter’s functionality in recombinant viral vectors opens avenues for next-generation oncolytic virotherapies, where tumor-targeting viruses express immunostimulatory transgenes under the control of the HHV-6 promoter to enhance anti-tumor immunity without systemic toxicity. The invention is therefore not merely a scientific advancement but a commercially viable, patentable, and industrially scalable platform technology with broad applications in human health and biotechnology.
