# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and ophthalmology, specifically to the identification and utilization of molecular markers for the differentiation, maturation, and quality assessment of corneal endothelial cells derived from pluripotent or progenitor cell sources. The invention provides novel, highly specific transcriptional and protein markers that enable the precise monitoring of corneal endothelial lineage commitment and terminal differentiation in vitro. These markers are particularly valuable for the development of cell-based therapies aimed at restoring corneal transparency in patients suffering from corneal endothelial dysfunction, a condition that currently relies on scarce donor tissue for treatment. The invention further encompasses methods for the directed differentiation of stem cells into functional corneal endothelial cells by modulating the expression of these markers, thereby enabling scalable, reproducible, and clinically viable production of corneal endothelial cell products without reliance on human donor tissue. The disclosed markers, particularly POU6F2, represent a breakthrough in the field due to their exclusive expression in mature corneal endothelial cells, absence in all other ocular tissues and non-ocular cell types, and strong correlation with functional maturation, including the acquisition of ion transport and barrier-forming capabilities essential for corneal dehydrating function.

## BACKGROUND ART

Corneal endothelial cells constitute a single layer of specialized epithelial cells lining the posterior surface of the cornea, adjacent to Descemet’s membrane. These cells are critical for maintaining corneal transparency by actively regulating fluid and ion transport between the aqueous humor and the corneal stroma, thereby preventing stromal edema and subsequent light scattering. Unlike many other epithelial cell types, human corneal endothelial cells exhibit minimal proliferative capacity in vivo after birth, rendering them highly vulnerable to cumulative loss due to aging, trauma, surgical injury, or genetic disorders. When the cell density falls below a critical threshold, typically around 500 to 800 cells per square millimeter, the remaining cells are unable to maintain proper corneal hydration, leading to corneal swelling, loss of visual acuity, and ultimately, blindness. The current standard of care for corneal endothelial failure is allogeneic corneal transplantation, which involves the surgical replacement of the diseased endothelial layer with a donor cornea. However, this approach is severely limited by a global shortage of viable donor tissue, the risk of immune rejection, the need for long-term immunosuppression, and the lack of standardized quality control for donor grafts. Moreover, the aging population and increasing incidence of endothelial dystrophies have exacerbated the demand for alternative therapeutic strategies.

To address these limitations, researchers have pursued the generation of corneal endothelial cells from alternative cellular sources, including ex vivo expansion of primary cells, transdifferentiation of somatic cells, and directed differentiation of pluripotent stem cells. While early efforts demonstrated the feasibility of expanding human corneal endothelial cells in culture, these cells rapidly undergo phenotypic drift, losing their characteristic polygonal morphology, tight junction formation, and pump function, and instead adopt a fibroblastic, mesenchymal phenotype through a process known as endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition. This instability has rendered long-term culture and large-scale production of functional corneal endothelial cells impractical. Subsequent investigations have explored the reprogramming of other cell types, such as iris pigment epithelial cells or corneal stromal cells, into corneal endothelial-like cells, but these approaches suffer from low efficiency, incomplete reprogramming, and insufficient functional validation. More recently, the advent of induced pluripotent stem cell technology has opened new avenues for generating patient-specific corneal endothelial cells. However, the successful differentiation of pluripotent stem cells into authentic corneal endothelial cells remains a formidable challenge, primarily due to the lack of reliable, specific, and functionally relevant molecular markers that can be used to track lineage commitment, assess differentiation efficiency, and validate the maturity and functionality of the resulting cell products.

Prior art has identified several genes associated with corneal endothelial function, including SLC4A11, COL8A2, and ATP1A1, which are commonly used as surrogate markers in differentiation protocols. However, these markers are not exclusive to corneal endothelial cells; they are expressed in other ocular tissues, such as the trabecular meshwork and ciliary body, and in some non-ocular tissues, thereby limiting their utility as definitive identifiers of corneal endothelial lineage. Furthermore, these markers do not reliably distinguish between progenitor-like cells and terminally differentiated, functional corneal endothelial cells. Consequently, current protocols for generating corneal endothelial cells from stem cells lack a robust, standardized method for quality control, leading to heterogeneous cell populations, inconsistent therapeutic outcomes, and an inability to meet regulatory requirements for clinical translation. The absence of a definitive maturation marker has also hindered the mechanistic understanding of how human corneal endothelial cells acquire their unique functional properties during development. Although transcription factors such as PITX2 and TFAP2B have been implicated in early ocular development and neural crest differentiation, their expression persists in multiple anterior segment tissues, and their levels do not correlate with terminal differentiation. Thus, there remains an unmet need in the field for a highly specific, functionally relevant, and universally applicable marker that can reliably identify mature, functional corneal endothelial cells and serve as a benchmark for the successful differentiation of stem cells into this lineage. The present invention fulfills this critical gap by identifying and validating POU6F2 as a novel, exclusive, and maturation-dependent marker of human corneal endothelial cells.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the absence of a molecular marker that is both uniquely and consistently expressed in mature, functional human corneal endothelial cells and absent in all other ocular and non-ocular cell types, thereby enabling the precise identification, isolation, and quality assessment of corneal endothelial cells derived from stem cell sources. Existing markers used in the field are either non-specific, expressed in multiple cell lineages, or fail to distinguish between progenitor states and terminally differentiated functional cells. This deficiency has impeded the development of reliable, scalable, and clinically translatable cell therapies for corneal endothelial dysfunction. Without a definitive marker of maturation, current differentiation protocols yield heterogeneous cell populations with variable functionality, making it impossible to ensure therapeutic efficacy or meet regulatory standards for cell-based products. Furthermore, the lack of a marker that correlates with functional maturation has hindered the elucidation of the molecular mechanisms governing human corneal endothelial cell development, limiting the ability to rationally design differentiation protocols. The problem is further compounded by the fact that human corneal endothelial cells exhibit unique biological properties, including minimal proliferative capacity and distinct gene expression patterns, which are not fully recapitulated in animal models, necessitating the identification of human-specific markers. The technical challenge, therefore, is to identify a molecular signature that is strictly confined to mature human corneal endothelial cells, that is detectable at both the transcriptional and protein levels, that is upregulated during terminal differentiation, and that can be used to monitor and validate the production of functional corneal endothelial cells in vitro.

### Solution to Problem

The solution to this technical problem is the identification and validation of POU6F2, a member of the POU family of transcription factors, as a highly specific and maturation-dependent marker of human corneal endothelial cells. The invention demonstrates, for the first time, that POU6F2 is expressed at high levels exclusively in mature human corneal endothelial cells, both in vivo and in vitro, and is undetectable in all other ocular tissues, including the corneal epithelium, stroma, iris, ciliary body, lens, and retinal pigment epithelium, as well as in all non-ocular tissues tested, including brain, kidney, pancreas, liver, and salivary gland, with the exception of negligible expression in a single hepatocyte sample. This expression pattern is not shared by any previously known corneal endothelial markers, including PITX2, TFAP2B, LMX1B, SLC4A11, or COL8A2, all of which exhibit broader expression profiles. The invention further establishes that POU6F2 expression is dynamically regulated during the differentiation of corneal endothelial progenitor cells into mature corneal endothelial cells, with transcript and protein levels increasing significantly during the maturation process, coinciding with the acquisition of functional characteristics such as tight junction formation, ion transport activity, and Descemet’s membrane synthesis. The invention provides a method for determining the maturity and authenticity of corneal endothelial cells by detecting the presence and abundance of POU6F2 mRNA or protein, using techniques such as quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, RNA sequencing, immunofluorescence staining, or Western blotting. The invention further provides a method for directing the differentiation of pluripotent stem cells or progenitor cells into functional corneal endothelial cells by modulating the expression of POU6F2, either through genetic overexpression, epigenetic activation, or by incorporating POU6F2 expression as a selection criterion during differentiation protocols. The invention also provides a composition comprising a population of corneal endothelial cells that express POU6F2 at a level above a defined threshold, thereby ensuring their functional maturity and suitability for therapeutic use. The invention further includes a diagnostic or quality control kit comprising reagents for detecting POU6F2 expression, including specific primers, probes, antibodies, or sequencing libraries, for use in evaluating the purity and maturity of corneal endothelial cell products.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The advantageous effects of the invention are profound and multifaceted. First, the identification of POU6F2 as a corneal endothelial-specific marker enables the precise and unambiguous identification of mature corneal endothelial cells, eliminating the ambiguity associated with existing markers and allowing for the development of standardized, reproducible differentiation protocols. This significantly enhances the quality control of cell-based therapies, ensuring that only functionally mature cells are used for transplantation, thereby improving therapeutic outcomes and reducing the risk of graft failure. Second, the exclusive expression of POU6F2 in corneal endothelial cells allows for the efficient purification of these cells from heterogeneous populations using fluorescence-activated cell sorting or magnetic-activated cell sorting, based on POU6F2 protein expression, which is not feasible with previously known markers. Third, the upregulation of POU6F2 during terminal differentiation provides a dynamic, real-time biomarker for monitoring the progression of differentiation, enabling researchers and manufacturers to optimize culture conditions, timing, and media formulations to maximize yield and functionality. Fourth, the invention facilitates the development of gene therapy approaches for corneal endothelial disorders by providing a target for the restoration of POU6F2 expression in patients with mutations or epigenetic silencing affecting this gene, potentially reversing endothelial dysfunction. Fifth, the invention enables the creation of a reference standard for corneal endothelial cell products, allowing regulatory agencies to establish clear criteria for approval of cell-based therapies based on POU6F2 expression levels. Sixth, the discovery that POU6F2 is expressed in a specific isoform containing a 36-amino acid insertion in the POU-specific domain provides an additional layer of specificity, as this isoform is uniquely associated with mature corneal endothelial cells and may serve as a novel therapeutic target. Seventh, the invention contributes to the fundamental understanding of human corneal endothelial development by identifying POU6F2 as a key regulator of terminal differentiation, potentially opening new avenues for investigating the transcriptional networks governing corneal endothelial cell identity. Finally, the invention eliminates reliance on donor tissue by enabling the scalable, ethical, and cost-effective production of autologous or allogeneic corneal endothelial cells from induced pluripotent stem cells, thereby addressing the global shortage of donor corneas and making corneal regeneration accessible to millions of patients worldwide.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the practical application and implementation of the invention in the context of generating, identifying, and validating mature human corneal endothelial cells using POU6F2 as a definitive marker. These examples are presented to demonstrate the utility, reproducibility, and clinical relevance of the invention, and are not intended to limit its scope. Each example is conducted under strict ethical guidelines and in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki, using human tissues obtained with informed consent from donors or their next of kin.

In a first embodiment, human corneal endothelial tissues were obtained from cadaveric donors within hours of death and immediately preserved in RNA stabilization reagent to prevent degradation. Descemet’s membrane, with its attached endothelial monolayer, was carefully dissected using sterile forceps and processed for total RNA extraction. Cap analysis of gene expression (CAGE) was performed on triplicate samples using the HeliScope single molecule sequencer, following established protocols. The resulting promoter activity profiles were compared against the FANTOM5 expression atlas, which contains transcriptome data from over 1,000 human tissue and cell samples. Promoters were considered corneal endothelial-specific if they exhibited expression levels exceeding 10 transcripts per million in all corneal endothelial samples, while remaining below 3 transcripts per million in all other samples, with a log2 fold-change greater than five and a Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p-value less than 0.01. This stringent analysis identified 45 promoters associated with 28 gene loci that were uniquely expressed in corneal endothelial cells. Among these, the promoter p3@POU6F2 was found to be expressed at approximately 100 transcripts per million in corneal endothelial tissues, while being undetectable in all other primary cell types, including brain, kidney, liver, and salivary gland, with the exception of a single hepatocyte sample exhibiting negligible expression at 1.77 transcripts per million. This level of specificity was unprecedented among all known corneal endothelial markers.

In a second embodiment, the expression of POU6F2 was validated across a panel of human ocular and non-ocular tissues using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction. Total RNA was extracted from central cornea, limbus, iris, ciliary body, retina, optic nerve, kidney, pancreas, liver, and salivary gland. cDNA was synthesized and amplified using TaqMan probes specific for POU6F2 and the housekeeping gene GAPDH. The results confirmed that POU6F2 expression was restricted exclusively to corneal endothelial tissues and cultured corneal endothelial cells, with no detectable signal in any other tissue tested. This finding was corroborated by immunofluorescence staining of human corneal tissue sections, where a polyclonal antibody against POU6F2 specifically labeled the endothelial monolayer, with no staining observed in the corneal epithelium, stroma, or other ocular structures. The specificity of the antibody was further confirmed by pre-absorption with the immunizing peptide, which abolished staining. In contrast, antibodies against previously known markers such as TFAP2B and LMX1B showed broad expression in multiple anterior segment tissues, including the trabecular meshwork and iris stroma, demonstrating their lack of specificity for mature corneal endothelial cells.

In a third embodiment, the dynamics of POU6F2 expression during corneal endothelial differentiation were investigated using a well-established in vitro model of human corneal endothelial progenitor cells (HCEPs) and their differentiated progeny (dHCEPs). HCEPs were isolated from primary corneal endothelial cells by culturing them in serum-free medium supplemented with basic fibroblast growth factor and Knockout Serum Replacement. After reaching confluence, HCEPs were induced to differentiate by switching to a medium containing fetal bovine serum and culturing for 28 days. RNA and protein were harvested at multiple time points during differentiation. CAGE analysis revealed that p3@POU6F2 was among the most significantly upregulated promoters during differentiation, with transcript levels increasing by more than 15-fold between HCEPs and dHCEPs. Western blot analysis confirmed a corresponding increase in POU6F2 protein expression, with a distinct band at approximately 65 kDa corresponding to the isoform containing the 36-amino acid insertion in the POU-specific domain. Immunofluorescence staining of dHCEPs showed strong nuclear localization of POU6F2, consistent with its role as a transcription factor, and co-localization with ZO-1 at cell junctions, indicating the acquisition of mature epithelial characteristics. In contrast, HCEPs exhibited minimal POU6F2 expression, consistent with their progenitor state.

In a fourth embodiment, the expression of POU6F2 was examined in flat-mounted human corneal tissues to determine its spatial distribution relative to the corneal periphery, where progenitor-like cells are believed to reside. Immunofluorescence staining for p75 neurotrophin receptor, a known marker of neural crest-derived progenitor cells, revealed strong expression in the extreme peripheral cornea. In contrast, POU6F2 expression was absent in these peripheral regions and was instead concentrated in the central cornea, where mature endothelial cells predominate. This inverse relationship between p75NTR and POU6F2 expression provides compelling evidence that POU6F2 is a marker of terminal differentiation and not of progenitor status. The transitional zone between the peripheral progenitor region and the central mature region showed a gradient of POU6F2 upregulation and p75NTR downregulation, further supporting its role as a maturation marker.

In a fifth embodiment, the invention was applied to the differentiation of human induced pluripotent stem cells into corneal endothelial-like cells. Pluripotent stem cells were directed toward a neural crest lineage using a combination of Wnt activators and BMP inhibitors, followed by exposure to a corneal endothelial differentiation medium containing FGF2, TGFβ inhibitors, and ROCK inhibitors. At day 21, cells were analyzed for the expression of POU6F2. Cells that exhibited high levels of POU6F2 mRNA and protein, as determined by qRT-PCR and flow cytometry using a fluorescently labeled anti-POU6F2 antibody, were selected and further characterized. These POU6F2-positive cells exhibited polygonal morphology, formed confluent monolayers with tight junctions, expressed SLC4A11 and ZO-1, and demonstrated functional pump activity in a transepithelial electrical resistance assay. In contrast, POU6F2-negative cells remained fibroblastic, failed to form junctions, and lacked pump function. This demonstrates that POU6F2 expression is not merely a correlative marker but a functional determinant of corneal endothelial maturity and can be used as a selection criterion to isolate therapeutically viable cells.

In a sixth embodiment, a diagnostic kit for quality control of corneal endothelial cell products was developed. The kit comprises a set of primers and probes for the detection of POU6F2 transcript variants, including the 36-amino acid insertion isoform, designed to distinguish it from other POU family members. The kit further includes a monoclonal antibody specific for the POU6F2 protein, conjugated to a fluorescent dye or enzyme for use in immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, or Western blotting. The kit also includes a reference standard curve generated from purified mature corneal endothelial cells, allowing users to quantify POU6F2 expression levels and determine whether a cell product meets a predefined threshold for clinical use, such as a minimum of 50 transcripts per million or 80% of cells expressing POU6F2 above a defined fluorescence intensity. The kit is accompanied by a standardized protocol for sample preparation, data analysis, and interpretation, enabling laboratories to implement consistent quality control measures across different production sites.

In a seventh embodiment, the invention was applied to the generation of corneal endothelial cell sheets for transplantation. Human corneal endothelial cells, selected for high POU6F2 expression, were seeded onto a temperature-responsive culture dish coated with laminin-511E8. After reaching confluence, the temperature was lowered to induce detachment of the intact cell sheet, which was then transplanted onto the posterior cornea of a rabbit model with experimentally induced endothelial dysfunction. Control animals received either non-selected cells or no treatment. After four weeks, corneal thickness was measured by optical coherence tomography, and endothelial cell density was assessed by specular microscopy. Animals transplanted with POU6F2-high cells exhibited significantly reduced corneal edema, restored endothelial cell density, and improved visual clarity compared to controls. Histological analysis confirmed the presence of a continuous, monolayered endothelial sheet expressing POU6F2 and ZO-1, integrated with the host Descemet’s membrane. These results demonstrate the therapeutic efficacy of POU6F2-selected cells and validate the invention as a critical tool for clinical translation.

In an eighth embodiment, the invention was extended to the study of corneal endothelial dystrophies. RNA was extracted from corneal tissues of patients with Fuchs’ endothelial corneal dystrophy and compared to tissues from healthy donors. Quantitative analysis revealed a significant reduction in POU6F2 transcript levels in dystrophic tissues, correlating with the degree of endothelial cell loss and functional impairment. This suggests that POU6F2 downregulation may contribute to disease pathogenesis and that restoring its expression could be a viable therapeutic strategy. Gene editing techniques, such as CRISPR-Cas9-mediated activation of the POU6F2 promoter, were employed in patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cells, resulting in the restoration of POU6F2 expression and the generation of functional corneal endothelial cells. These findings establish POU6F2 not only as a marker but also as a potential therapeutic target for corneal endothelial disorders.

In a ninth embodiment, the invention was used to develop a bioreactor system for large-scale production of corneal endothelial cells. The system incorporates real-time monitoring of POU6F2 expression using a genetically encoded fluorescent reporter under the control of the POU6F2 promoter. As cells differentiate, the fluorescence intensity increases, allowing automated, non-destructive tracking of differentiation efficiency. The system is integrated with a cell sorting module that harvests only those cells exhibiting fluorescence above a predetermined threshold, ensuring a homogeneous, high-quality product. The bioreactor is operated under closed, sterile conditions and is compliant with Good Manufacturing Practice guidelines, enabling the production of clinical-grade corneal endothelial cells at scale.

In a tenth embodiment, the invention was applied to the development of a cryopreservation protocol for corneal endothelial cells. Cells were selected for high POU6F2 expression prior to freezing and stored in a cryoprotectant solution containing dimethyl sulfoxide and trehalose. Post-thaw analysis revealed that cells retaining high POU6F2 expression maintained their morphology, junctional integrity, and pump function, whereas cells with low POU6F2 expression exhibited significant loss of viability and function. This demonstrates that POU6F2 expression serves as a predictive biomarker for cryosurvival and functional recovery, enabling the selection of optimal cell batches for storage and future transplantation.

These embodiments collectively demonstrate the transformative potential of the invention. By providing a definitive, specific, and functionally relevant marker of mature human corneal endothelial cells, the invention overcomes the longstanding limitations of the field and establishes a new paradigm for the development of cell-based therapies for corneal endothelial dysfunction. The invention enables the precise identification, purification, and validation of therapeutic cells, ensures consistency and safety in manufacturing, and opens new avenues for diagnosis, gene therapy, and regenerative medicine. The exclusive nature of POU6F2 expression makes it an unparalleled tool for advancing corneal regeneration and restoring sight to millions of individuals worldwide.
