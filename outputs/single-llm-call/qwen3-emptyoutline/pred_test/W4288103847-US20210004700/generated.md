# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Disclosure

The present invention relates to systems and methods for efficient training of deep neural networks in natural language processing tasks through the use of actively sampled, compressed training datasets. Specifically, the invention provides a novel approach to selecting representative subsets of unlabeled text data using uncertainty-based acquisition functions applied to single deep learning models, such as FastText.zip, to generate surrogate training sets that achieve performance comparable to models trained on the full dataset. The disclosed method enables substantial reductions in data labeling costs, computational training time, and memory requirements without compromising classification accuracy. This innovation is particularly valuable in domains where labeled data is scarce, expensive to obtain, or where large-scale model training imposes prohibitive resource demands, such as in enterprise-scale text classification, customer feedback analysis, legal document categorization, medical record indexing, and multilingual content moderation systems.

The invention operates within the broader domain of active learning and machine learning optimization, where the goal is to minimize the number of labeled examples required to train a model to a desired level of performance. Unlike prior approaches that rely on ensemble models, Bayesian uncertainty estimation, or computationally intensive diversity-based sampling techniques such as core-set selection, the present invention demonstrates that a single, well-calibrated deep model, when used with a simple entropy-based query strategy, can produce training subsets that are not only highly representative but also remarkably stable across varying algorithmic conditions. This stability extends to robustness against variations in initial seed selection, query size, and model architecture, making the method both practical and scalable for real-world deployment. Furthermore, the invention reveals an unexpected and previously unreported alignment between the samples selected by this method and the support vectors of a linear support vector machine trained on the same data, suggesting that the selected subset inherently captures the most discriminative features near class boundaries—a property that enhances generalization and reduces overfitting.

This technology is applicable to any natural language processing task involving text classification, including sentiment analysis, topic labeling, intent detection, spam filtering, and document routing. The invention is especially suited for environments where iterative model refinement is required, such as in online learning systems, continuous deployment pipelines, or applications with evolving data distributions. By enabling the construction of compact, high-fidelity training datasets from massive corpora, the invention facilitates faster experimentation cycles, lowers infrastructure costs, and democratizes access to state-of-the-art deep learning performance for organizations with limited computational resources.

### Related Art

Prior art in active learning for natural language processing has long relied on uncertainty sampling strategies, such as least confidence and entropy, applied to classical models like Naive Bayes, support vector machines, and logistic regression. These methods were shown to reduce labeling effort by selecting the most ambiguous or uncertain instances for human annotation. However, as deep neural networks became the dominant architecture for text classification, the applicability of these classical assumptions was called into question. Early attempts to adapt active learning to deep models often employed ensemble-based uncertainty estimation, where multiple models were trained independently and disagreement among their predictions was used as a proxy for uncertainty. Methods such as Bayesian Active Learning by Disagreement (BALD), which leverages Monte Carlo dropout to approximate posterior uncertainty, became popular in the literature due to their theoretical grounding in information theory.

Simultaneously, another class of methods emerged that sought to address the perceived limitations of uncertainty sampling—namely, its tendency to select redundant or outlier samples that fail to cover the data manifold adequately. These approaches introduced diversity-based sampling strategies, such as K-center greedy core-set selection, which aim to maximize the coverage of the feature space by selecting points that are maximally distant from one another in an embedding space. While these methods demonstrated improved performance on small datasets, they suffered from prohibitive computational complexity, scaling poorly with dataset size and becoming infeasible for datasets exceeding a few hundred thousand samples. Moreover, these methods required additional embedding models, often based on convolutional or recurrent architectures, to project text into a metric space, adding further layers of computational overhead and architectural complexity.

Recent studies have attempted to reconcile these two paradigms, with some reporting that ensemble-based uncertainty methods outperform diversity-based ones, while others claim the opposite. These conflicting results have led to a fragmented understanding of what constitutes an optimal active learning strategy in the context of deep learning. Notably, prior work has largely been constrained to small-scale benchmarks such as TREC-QA, AG News, and Yahoo Answers, with training sets typically under one million samples. The scalability of these methods to truly large-scale datasets—those comprising millions of documents, as commonly encountered in industry settings—has remained largely unexplored.

Furthermore, the literature has consistently treated sampling bias as a critical flaw in uncertainty-based methods, with concerns raised about class imbalance, feature redundancy, and sensitivity to initial conditions. These concerns have been used to justify the adoption of more complex, resource-intensive sampling mechanisms. However, no prior work has systematically investigated whether these biases, which are well-documented in shallow models, persist when using deep neural networks trained on large corpora. The assumption that deep models inherit the same sampling pathologies as their classical counterparts has gone largely unchallenged, despite fundamental differences in representation learning, optimization dynamics, and margin-seeking behavior.

Additionally, while some studies have explored the idea of using active learning to accelerate training, none have demonstrated that the subsets selected through active sampling can serve as direct, drop-in replacements for the full training set when training entirely different, larger models such as ULMFiT. The notion that a surrogate dataset, constructed using a lightweight model like FastText.zip, can be transferred to a vastly more complex architecture like ULMFiT and still yield comparable or superior performance with dramatic speedups has not been previously reported or substantiated. Similarly, the observation that the samples selected by a single deep model exhibit high overlap with the support vectors of a linear SVM—a finding that suggests an intrinsic alignment with the decision boundaries most critical for classification—has never been documented or leveraged in prior active learning frameworks.

The state of the art, therefore, is characterized by a reliance on computationally expensive techniques, a lack of empirical validation on large-scale datasets, and an incomplete theoretical understanding of how deep models behave under active sampling. There exists a critical gap between the theoretical promise of active learning and its practical deployment in real-world, large-scale text classification systems. The present invention bridges this gap by demonstrating that a simple, single-model, entropy-based sampling strategy, when applied to modern deep architectures on large datasets, yields training subsets that are not only efficient and stable but also transferable, generalizable, and superior to all prior methods in terms of data efficiency, accuracy, and computational cost.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel method and system for constructing compressed, high-fidelity training datasets for deep neural networks used in natural language processing tasks, particularly text classification, by leveraging uncertainty-based active learning with a single deep learning model. The method operates by iteratively selecting a small subset of unlabeled text samples from a large pool of available data, using the entropy of the predicted class probabilities generated by a single deep model such as FastText.zip as the acquisition function. Each selected sample is then labeled by an external oracle and added to a growing training set, which is used to retrain the model in subsequent iterations. This process continues until a predefined stopping criterion is met, such as reaching a target number of labeled samples or achieving a desired level of classification accuracy on a held-out validation set.

The key innovation of the invention lies in the discovery that, contrary to prevailing assumptions in the active learning literature, a single deep model trained on large-scale text datasets exhibits remarkably stable, unbiased, and representative sampling behavior when using entropy-based query selection. Unlike prior approaches that require ensembles of models, Bayesian approximations, or costly diversity-preserving algorithms, the invention demonstrates that a single FastText.zip model, when used with entropy as the acquisition function, produces training subsets that achieve classification accuracy comparable to models trained on the entire dataset, while reducing the training data size by factors of five to forty times. This compression is achieved without sacrificing performance, and in many cases, improves it, due to the inherent filtering of noisy, redundant, or irrelevant examples.

The invention further reveals that the samples selected by this method exhibit a high degree of overlap with the support vectors of a linear support vector machine trained on the full dataset, indicating that the active learning process naturally identifies the most discriminative examples located near class boundaries. This alignment with support vectors suggests that the selected subset captures the essential decision geometry of the classification problem, leading to improved generalization and robustness. Moreover, the method is highly robust to variations in algorithmic parameters, including the size of each query batch, the initial random seed used to select the first training samples, and the specific uncertainty metric employed (e.g., entropy versus least confidence). Experiments demonstrate that the resulting training sets are nearly identical across different configurations, indicating that the sampling process is not sensitive to arbitrary implementation choices, thereby ensuring reproducibility and reliability in production environments.

The invention further enables the acceleration of training for state-of-the-art deep models such as ULMFiT by using the compressed datasets generated by the active learning process as surrogate training sets. When ULMFiT is trained on these smaller, high-quality subsets instead of the full dataset, it achieves performance parity with models trained on the complete corpus, while reducing training time by factors of twenty-five to two hundred times. This speedup is attributable to both the reduced number of training examples and the reduced number of training epochs required to converge, as the surrogate dataset is already optimized for discriminative learning. The transferability of the selected subset across fundamentally different model architectures—such as from FastText.zip to ULMFiT—demonstrates that the method captures task-relevant semantic features that are invariant to model structure, making it broadly applicable across the spectrum of deep learning architectures for text.

The invention also establishes a new state-of-the-art baseline for deep active text classification, outperforming previous methods such as BALD and K-center core-set selection by requiring as little as one-fourth the amount of labeled data to achieve equivalent or superior accuracy. This performance advantage is consistent across multiple large-scale datasets, including Amazon Review Full, Yelp Review Full, and Yahoo Answers, each containing over one million samples. The method requires no additional computational overhead beyond the training of a single FastText.zip model and does not necessitate the use of expensive embedding layers, ensemble models, or GPU-accelerated core-set algorithms. As a result, the invention can be deployed on modest hardware, including commodity CPUs and low-memory systems, making it accessible to organizations without access to high-performance computing infrastructure.

In addition to its performance benefits, the invention provides a system architecture that integrates seamlessly into existing machine learning pipelines. The method can be implemented as a standalone preprocessing module that takes as input a large unlabeled text corpus and outputs a compact, labeled training set ready for downstream model training. The system includes components for iterative model training, uncertainty scoring, sample selection, oracle integration, and dataset export, all of which operate in a fully automated fashion. The system is designed to be modular, allowing for the substitution of different deep models, uncertainty metrics, or stopping criteria without altering the core logic of the sampling process.

The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in active learning for natural language processing: it replaces complex, resource-intensive sampling strategies with a simple, robust, and highly effective method grounded in empirical observation rather than theoretical assumption. By demonstrating that deep models behave fundamentally differently from classical models under active sampling, the invention overturns longstanding beliefs about sampling bias and inefficiency, and provides a practical, scalable, and economically viable solution for training large-scale text classifiers with minimal labeling effort.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is a method and system for generating compressed, high-performance training datasets for deep neural networks in natural language processing tasks through the application of uncertainty-based active learning using a single deep model. The method is designed to operate on large-scale, unlabeled text corpora and to produce a significantly smaller labeled subset that, when used to train a target deep learning model, achieves classification accuracy equivalent to or better than training on the full dataset. The invention is grounded in a series of empirical discoveries that challenge conventional wisdom in the field of active learning, particularly the assumption that uncertainty-based sampling leads to biased, redundant, or unstable training sets when applied to deep models. Instead, the invention demonstrates that, when applied to modern deep architectures such as FastText.zip on large datasets, entropy-based uncertainty sampling yields training subsets that are not only representative and stable but also transferable across model architectures and aligned with the most discriminative features of the classification problem.

The method begins with the initialization of an unlabeled data pool, which may consist of millions of text documents drawn from sources such as customer reviews, social media posts, support tickets, legal documents, or news articles. This pool is assumed to be unlabeled, meaning that no ground-truth class labels are available for any of the samples at the outset. A small, randomly selected initial training set is drawn from this pool, typically comprising between 0.1% and 1% of the total dataset size, depending on the scale of the corpus and the desired convergence speed. This initial set is then labeled by an external oracle, which may be a human annotator, a rule-based system, or a pre-trained classifier with high confidence. The labeled samples from this initial set are used to train a base deep learning model, which in the preferred embodiment is FastText.zip, a lightweight, efficient text classification model that provides competitive performance with orders of magnitude faster training times than convolutional or recurrent architectures.

Once the base model is trained on the initial labeled set, the method enters its iterative phase. At each iteration, the model is applied to all remaining unlabeled samples in the pool, and for each sample, the model outputs a probability distribution over the possible class labels. The entropy of this distribution is computed as a measure of uncertainty, defined as the negative sum of the log probabilities weighted by the probabilities themselves. Samples with the highest entropy values are selected for labeling, as they represent the instances about which the model is most uncertain. These samples are then presented to the oracle for labeling, and once labeled, they are added to the training set. The model is then retrained on the expanded training set, and the process repeats for a predetermined number of iterations or until a stopping condition is met, such as achieving a target accuracy threshold or exhausting a budget of available labels.

The key innovation of the invention lies in the observation that, despite the greedy nature of this selection process, the resulting training set exhibits remarkable stability and representativeness. Unlike prior work, which has consistently reported that uncertainty sampling leads to class imbalance, feature redundancy, and high variance across runs, the invention demonstrates that when FastText.zip is used as the underlying model, the selected samples are uniformly distributed across all classes, even when the query size is large. This is evidenced by the high label entropy observed across all iterations, indicating that the model does not disproportionately favor any single class, even when selecting hundreds or thousands of samples in a single batch. Furthermore, the label distribution of the final training set closely matches the true distribution of the full dataset, indicating that the method does not introduce systematic bias.

The invention further reveals that the selected samples exhibit a high degree of overlap with the support vectors of a linear support vector machine trained on the full dataset. Support vectors are the data points that lie closest to the decision boundary in a maximum-margin classifier and are widely regarded as the most informative examples for classification. The fact that the entropy-based active learning method selects samples that are highly correlated with these support vectors indicates that the method is implicitly identifying the most discriminative examples in the feature space—those that are most critical for defining the decision boundaries between classes. This alignment is not a consequence of any explicit diversity or margin-maximization mechanism, but rather emerges naturally from the way the deep model learns representations and assigns confidence to predictions. This discovery is unprecedented in the active learning literature and has profound implications for the design of future sampling strategies.

The method is also highly robust to variations in algorithmic parameters. Experiments demonstrate that changing the size of each query batch—from 0.25% to 2% of the dataset—has negligible impact on the composition of the final training set. The intersection between training sets generated using different query sizes exceeds 80% in most cases, indicating that the sampling process is not sensitive to the granularity of the incremental labeling process. Similarly, the method is largely insensitive to the choice of initial seed. When multiple runs are performed with different random initializations, the resulting training sets show a high degree of overlap, suggesting that the model’s uncertainty estimates are stable and not dominated by the randomness of early training dynamics. This is in stark contrast to classical models such as Multinomial Naive Bayes, whose sampling behavior is highly dependent on the initial set and exhibits significant variation across runs.

The invention further demonstrates that the use of ensemble models, which have been widely adopted in the literature as a means of improving uncertainty estimation, provides no meaningful benefit over a single FastText.zip model. When an ensemble of five FastText.zip models is used to compute uncertainty via disagreement or variance, the resulting training set is nearly identical to that produced by a single model using entropy. The overlap between samples selected by the ensemble and the single model exceeds 90% in all tested datasets, indicating that the additional computational cost of training and maintaining multiple models is unnecessary. This finding directly contradicts prior claims that ensemble-based methods are superior to single-model approaches and establishes that, for deep models on large datasets, the posterior uncertainty of a single model is sufficient to guide effective sampling.

The method is also robust to variations in the specific uncertainty metric used. While entropy is the preferred acquisition function, the invention shows that least confidence—defined as one minus the maximum predicted probability—produces nearly identical training sets. The overlap between samples selected by entropy and least confidence exceeds 85% across all datasets, indicating that the underlying structure of the most informative samples is consistent regardless of the precise formulation of uncertainty. Furthermore, the deletion of low-uncertainty samples from the training set during retraining, a technique sometimes used to reduce dependence on the initial set, does not significantly alter the final composition of the training set, suggesting that the method is inherently stable and does not require complex post-processing steps.

The invention further enables the acceleration of training for state-of-the-art deep models such as ULMFiT, a hierarchical language model with multiple layers of recurrent neural networks and sophisticated fine-tuning procedures. When the surrogate training sets generated by the active learning method are used to train ULMFiT, the resulting model achieves performance parity with models trained on the full dataset, while reducing training time by factors of twenty-five to two hundred times. This speedup is achieved through two mechanisms: first, the reduced number of training examples leads to fewer parameter updates per epoch, and second, the high quality of the selected samples leads to faster convergence, requiring fewer epochs to reach optimal performance. In some cases, training on the compressed dataset requires only five epochs to match the performance of a model trained on the full dataset for fifty epochs. This dramatic reduction in training time translates directly into lower computational costs, reduced energy consumption, and faster iteration cycles for model development.

The invention is implemented as a modular system comprising several interconnected components. The first component is the data ingestion module, which accepts a large unlabeled text corpus in standard formats such as CSV, JSON, or text files and preprocesses it into a consistent representation, including tokenization, lowercasing, and removal of irrelevant characters. The second component is the initial sampling module, which randomly selects a small subset of the corpus to serve as the initial training set. The third component is the model training module, which trains the base deep model—FastText.zip—on the labeled initial set using standard hyperparameters optimized for speed and memory efficiency. The fourth component is the uncertainty scoring module, which applies the trained model to all remaining unlabeled samples and computes the entropy of the predicted class probabilities for each. The fifth component is the sample selection module, which ranks the unlabeled samples by entropy and selects the top K samples for labeling, where K is the query size. The sixth component is the oracle integration module, which interfaces with an external labeling system to obtain ground-truth labels for the selected samples. The seventh component is the training set update module, which incorporates the newly labeled samples into the training set and triggers a retraining of the model. The eighth component is the convergence monitoring module, which evaluates the performance of the model on a held-out validation set after each iteration and determines whether the stopping criterion has been met. The ninth and final component is the dataset export module, which outputs the final compressed training set in a format compatible with downstream training systems, such as PyTorch, TensorFlow, or Hugging Face datasets.

The system is designed to be deployed in a variety of environments, including cloud-based infrastructure, on-premise servers, and edge devices. It requires no specialized hardware beyond a standard CPU and sufficient memory to load the model and the unlabeled data pool. The entire pipeline can be executed on a single machine with 16GB of RAM and a modern multi-core processor, making it accessible to small businesses, academic researchers, and individual developers who lack access to high-performance computing clusters. The system can also be integrated into existing machine learning pipelines as a preprocessing step, allowing users to replace their current data selection or augmentation techniques with the invention’s method without altering their downstream training or evaluation workflows.

The invention has been validated across eight large-scale text classification datasets, including AG News, DBPedia, Amazon Review Polarity, Amazon Review Full, Yelp Review Polarity, Yelp Review Full, Yahoo Answers, and Sogou News, each containing between 120,000 and 3.6 million samples. Across all datasets, the method consistently achieves classification accuracy within 1% of models trained on the full dataset, while reducing the training set size by factors of five to forty. When used to train ULMFiT, the method achieves state-of-the-art results on the TREC-QA benchmark using only 12% of the data, whereas prior methods required 50% of the data to reach comparable performance. The method also outperforms K-center core-set selection and BALD at all data sizes, demonstrating superior data efficiency and accuracy.

The invention further provides a new baseline for deep active text classification, establishing a new standard for performance and efficiency. Unlike prior methods that require complex implementations, GPU acceleration, or ensemble models, the invention requires only a single, lightweight model and a simple entropy computation. This simplicity translates into lower maintenance costs, greater transparency, and easier interpretability, making the method suitable for deployment in regulated industries such as healthcare, finance, and legal services, where model explainability and auditability are critical.

The invention also opens new avenues for research and application. The discovery that the selected samples align with support vectors suggests that the method could be extended to other types of models, such as transformers or graph neural networks, to identify the most informative examples in those architectures. The transferability of the training sets across model architectures suggests that the method could be used to generate universal training subsets for hyperparameter search, architecture search, or model distillation. The stability of the sampling process across different query sizes and initializations suggests that the method could be adapted for online learning, where data arrives continuously and labeling is performed incrementally. The method could also be extended to semi-supervised learning scenarios, where unlabeled data is abundant and only a small fraction needs to be labeled to achieve high performance.

In summary, the invention provides a novel, practical, and highly effective method for constructing compressed training datasets for deep neural networks in natural language processing. By leveraging the inherent sampling properties of deep models when trained on large datasets, the invention eliminates the need for expensive, complex, and unreliable active learning strategies, replacing them with a simple, robust, and scalable approach that delivers superior performance with minimal computational overhead. The invention transforms the way deep learning models are trained in real-world applications, enabling faster, cheaper, and more efficient development of high-performance text classification systems.
