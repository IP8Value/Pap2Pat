## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel synthetic peptide compounds that function as potent and selective antagonists of the growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor, and to their use in the treatment, prevention, or amelioration of pulmonary diseases characterized by inflammation, fibrosis, or uncontrolled cellular proliferation. More specifically, the invention encompasses a class of modified GHRH receptor antagonists designed to inhibit signaling pathways downstream of the GHRH receptor in lung epithelial cells, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, immune cells, and neoplastic tissues, thereby suppressing pathological processes such as extracellular matrix deposition, myofibroblast activation, chronic inflammation, angiogenesis, and tumor growth. The compounds of this invention are distinguished by their high binding affinity for the GHRH receptor, their resistance to enzymatic degradation in vivo, their ability to penetrate lung tissue effectively, and their capacity to modulate intracellular signaling cascades—including the PI3K/Akt, MAPK/ERK, JAK2/STAT3, AMPK, and p53 pathways—without significantly altering systemic growth hormone or insulin-like growth factor-1 levels. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising these antagonists, methods of administering them to patients suffering from interstitial lung diseases, pulmonary sarcoidosis, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, lung cancer, and other GHRH receptor-expressing pulmonary pathologies, and diagnostic methods for identifying patients who are likely to respond to such therapy based on the expression profile of the GHRH receptor or its splice variant SV1 in lung tissue. The compounds described herein represent a new therapeutic class with a unique mechanism of action that directly targets the paracrine and autocrine roles of GHRH in the lung, independent of the classical endocrine GHRH–GH–IGF-1 axis, thereby offering a novel approach to treating complex, multifactorial lung diseases that have proven refractory to conventional therapies.

## BACKGROUND

The regulation of lung homeostasis involves a complex interplay of endocrine, paracrine, and autocrine signaling systems that coordinate tissue repair, immune surveillance, and cellular proliferation. Among these, the growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) system has historically been regarded as a hypothalamic-pituitary regulator of somatic growth and metabolism through stimulation of growth hormone secretion and subsequent hepatic production of insulin-like growth factor-1. However, emerging evidence has demonstrated that GHRH and its receptor are expressed in a wide array of extra-pituitary tissues, including the lung, where they participate in local physiological and pathological processes unrelated to systemic endocrine function. The GHRH receptor, a G-protein-coupled receptor of the secretin/glucagon family, is expressed not only in pituitary somatotrophs but also in alveolar epithelial cells, airway club cells, lung fibroblasts, pulmonary endothelial cells, dendritic cells, lymphocytes, and multiple types of lung cancer cells. Furthermore, a truncated splice variant of the receptor, designated SV1, has been identified in human and murine lung tissues and is known to exhibit ligand-independent signaling activity that promotes cell survival and proliferation. The presence of GHRH and its receptor in the lung suggests that GHRH functions as a local growth and survival factor, particularly in contexts of injury, inflammation, and neoplasia. In conditions such as idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, sarcoidosis, and non-small cell lung carcinoma, elevated levels of GHRH and its receptor have been correlated with disease progression, fibrotic remodeling, and poor prognosis. In idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, for instance, fibroblasts exposed to profibrotic stimuli such as bleomycin or transforming growth factor-beta exhibit increased expression of the GHRH receptor, which in turn activates pro-survival pathways including PI3K/Akt and ERK, leading to resistance to apoptosis and excessive collagen deposition. Similarly, in lung cancer, autocrine GHRH signaling enhances tumor cell proliferation, inhibits apoptosis, and promotes angiogenesis through upregulation of vascular endothelial growth factor and matrix metalloproteinases. The discovery that synthetic GHRH receptor antagonists can selectively block these local signaling events without affecting systemic growth hormone levels has opened a new therapeutic avenue. Prior attempts to treat pulmonary fibrosis and lung cancer have relied on broad-spectrum immunosuppressants, antifibrotics with limited efficacy, or cytotoxic chemotherapies that cause significant systemic toxicity. None of these approaches target the specific molecular drivers of disease progression at the level of the GHRH receptor. Moreover, existing therapies fail to address the underlying metabolic dysregulation and mitochondrial dysfunction observed in diseased lung cells. The development of synthetic GHRH receptor antagonists with enhanced pharmacokinetic properties, including resistance to proteolytic degradation and improved tissue penetration, represents a significant advance in the field. These compounds are capable of modulating multiple pathogenic pathways simultaneously—suppressing inflammation by downregulating cytokine production and T-cell activation, inhibiting fibrosis by promoting fibroblast apoptosis and reducing extracellular matrix synthesis, and arresting tumor growth by inducing metabolic stress and blocking oncogenic signaling. Despite the compelling preclinical evidence supporting the therapeutic potential of GHRH receptor antagonists in pulmonary diseases, no such compound has been approved for clinical use, and there remains a critical unmet need for targeted, mechanism-based therapies that address the root causes of these debilitating conditions. The present invention fulfills this need by providing novel, structurally optimized GHRH receptor antagonists with superior biological activity, metabolic stability, and tissue specificity, along with detailed methods for their use in the treatment of a broad spectrum of pulmonary disorders.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel synthetic peptide compounds that act as potent and selective antagonists of the growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor, wherein the compounds are designed to inhibit pathological signaling in the lung without disrupting systemic endocrine function. These compounds are characterized by a core peptide sequence derived from the active region of GHRH but modified at multiple positions to enhance receptor binding affinity, resist enzymatic degradation, and improve pharmacokinetic properties. Key structural modifications include the substitution of L-arginine at position 2 with D-arginine, the replacement of lysine residues at positions 12 and 21 with ornithine, the incorporation of hydroxyarginine at position 9, the use of pentafluorophenylalanine at position 6, methylation of tyrosine at position 10, the substitution of leucine at position 15 with α-aminobutyric acid, and the replacement of the C-terminal leucine with a non-natural amino acid derivative to form a carboxamide terminus. Additionally, the N-terminus of certain analogs is modified with a phenoxyacetyl or adamantyl group to enhance membrane permeability and prolong half-life. These modifications collectively confer upon the compounds a binding affinity for the GHRH receptor that exceeds that of native GHRH by more than an order of magnitude, while simultaneously reducing or eliminating their ability to stimulate growth hormone release from the pituitary. The compounds of the invention are capable of inhibiting the activation of multiple intracellular signaling pathways in lung cells, including the phosphoinositide 3-kinase/protein kinase B (PI3K/Akt) pathway, the extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK1/2) pathway, the Janus kinase 2/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (JAK2/STAT3) pathway, and the p21-activated kinase 1/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3/nuclear factor-kappa B (PAK1-STAT3/NF-κB) axis, all of which are implicated in the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis, inflammation, and cancer. In lung fibroblasts, the compounds induce apoptosis by enhancing mitochondrial respiration, increasing spare respiratory capacity, and promoting the release of cytochrome c, thereby overcoming the anti-apoptotic phenotype characteristic of fibrotic tissue. In lung epithelial cells and tumor cells, the compounds suppress proliferation by downregulating cyclin-dependent kinases, upregulating p27kip1, and inhibiting cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB) activity. In immune cells, the compounds reduce the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-2, interleukin-12, interleukin-17A, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and decrease the infiltration of CD4+ T-cells and CD68+ macrophages into lung tissue. The compounds also inhibit the expression of inducible nitric oxide synthase and cyclooxygenase-2, thereby reducing nitrosative and oxidative stress in the lung microenvironment. In animal models of bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis, administration of the compounds results in a significant reduction in hydroxyproline content, collagen deposition, and histopathological fibrosis scores, while preserving lung architecture and function. In xenograft models of human lung cancer, the compounds suppress tumor growth, reduce metastasis, and enhance the efficacy of chemotherapy agents such as cisplatin by increasing intracellular reactive oxygen species and disrupting metabolic adaptation. The compounds are administered via subcutaneous, intravenous, or inhalation routes and are formulated in stable, pharmaceutically acceptable carriers suitable for chronic administration. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more of the disclosed compounds, methods of treating pulmonary diseases by administering these compositions to a patient in need thereof, and diagnostic methods for selecting patients based on the expression of the GHRH receptor or its splice variant SV1 in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid, lung biopsy tissue, or circulating tumor cells. The invention provides a novel, targeted, and mechanism-based therapeutic strategy for the treatment of interstitial lung diseases, sarcoidosis, and lung cancer, offering significant advantages over existing therapies in terms of efficacy, safety, and specificity.

### Compositions

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more of the synthetic GHRH receptor antagonists described herein, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, diluents, or excipients. The compositions are formulated for administration via multiple routes, including subcutaneous injection, intravenous infusion, and inhalation, to ensure optimal delivery to the lung parenchyma and target cells. The active ingredient in the composition is a peptide compound selected from the group consisting of MIA-602, MIA-604, MIA-610, MIA-690, and structurally related analogs thereof, each of which possesses a modified amino acid sequence designed to maximize receptor binding affinity, metabolic stability, and tissue penetration while minimizing endocrine activity. The peptide compound is present in the composition in a concentration ranging from approximately 0.1 mg/mL to 10 mg/mL, depending on the route of administration and the intended therapeutic dose. For subcutaneous administration, the composition may be formulated as a sterile, isotonic, aqueous solution buffered at a pH between 5.0 and 7.4, containing a stabilizing agent such as human serum albumin or polysorbate 80, an isotonicity agent such as sodium chloride or dextrose, and a preservative such as benzyl alcohol or methylparaben. For intravenous administration, the composition is prepared as a pyrogen-free, sterile solution in a physiological saline or dextrose vehicle, with the peptide concentration adjusted to allow for slow infusion over a period of 15 to 60 minutes. For inhalation delivery, the composition is formulated as a dry powder, aerosol, or nebulized suspension using a carrier such as lactose, trehalose, or mannitol, with particle size distribution optimized for deep lung deposition (1–5 micrometers in aerodynamic diameter). The dry powder formulation may be delivered via a dry powder inhaler device, while the nebulized suspension may be administered using a jet or ultrasonic nebulizer. The composition may further include one or more additional therapeutic agents selected from the group consisting of corticosteroids, antifibrotics such as pirfenidone or nintedanib, chemotherapeutic agents such as cisplatin or paclitaxel, immune checkpoint inhibitors such as pembrolizumab or nivolumab, and antioxidants such as N-acetylcysteine or glutathione. The inclusion of these agents may enhance the therapeutic efficacy of the GHRH receptor antagonist by synergistically targeting complementary pathways involved in disease progression. The composition may also contain one or more enhancers of pulmonary absorption, such as chitosan, cyclodextrins, or fatty acid derivatives, to improve bioavailability following inhalation. The composition is packaged in a sterile, sealed container suitable for single or multiple doses, and is labeled with instructions for storage at 2°C to 8°C for long-term stability or at room temperature for short-term use. The composition is intended for use in the treatment of pulmonary diseases characterized by GHRH receptor overexpression, including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, sarcoidosis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with fibrotic components, and non-small cell lung carcinoma. The composition may be administered once daily, twice daily, or at longer intervals depending on the pharmacokinetic profile of the specific antagonist and the severity of the disease. In chronic conditions such as idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, the composition may be administered continuously for a period of at least three months, with periodic monitoring of lung function, biomarkers of fibrosis, and imaging studies to assess therapeutic response. In cancer settings, the composition may be administered in cycles, in combination with chemotherapy or immunotherapy, with dosing schedules tailored to maximize tumor suppression while minimizing systemic toxicity. The pharmaceutical composition of the invention is distinguished from prior art formulations by its ability to deliver a potent, metabolically stable GHRH receptor antagonist directly to the lung tissue, thereby achieving high local concentrations of the active compound while avoiding systemic exposure that could lead to off-target effects. The composition is designed to maintain the structural integrity of the peptide during storage and delivery, ensuring that the biological activity of the compound is preserved until the moment of administration. The use of novel N-terminal modifications, such as phenoxyacetyl or adamantyl groups, enhances the lipophilicity of the peptide, facilitating its passage across cellular membranes and improving its distribution within the lung interstitium. The inclusion of D-amino acids and non-natural amino acids renders the peptide resistant to degradation by endogenous proteases, thereby extending its half-life in vivo and reducing the frequency of dosing required for therapeutic efficacy. The composition may be further optimized for sustained release using microencapsulation techniques, nanoparticle delivery systems, or hydrogel-based depots, allowing for prolonged exposure of lung cells to the antagonist and minimizing fluctuations in plasma concentration. The pharmaceutical composition of the invention represents a significant advancement in the field of pulmonary therapeutics, providing a targeted, mechanism-based approach to treating complex and progressive lung diseases that have previously lacked effective therapies.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Synthesis of GHRH Antagonists

The synthetic GHRH receptor antagonists described herein were prepared using solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) on a Rink amide resin, employing Fmoc (9-fluorenylmethyloxycarbonyl) chemistry and standard coupling protocols. The synthesis was carried out using an automated peptide synthesizer (e.g., Liberty Blue, CEM Corporation) under controlled temperature and inert atmosphere conditions. The C-terminal amino acid, an Nle-D-Arg-Ha-NH2 tripeptide sequence, was first loaded onto the resin via esterification, followed by sequential deprotection and coupling of the remaining amino acids in the C-to-N direction. Each amino acid was activated using HBTU (O-benzotriazole-N,N,N',N'-tetramethyluronium hexafluorophosphate) and DIPEA (N,N-diisopropylethylamine) in DMF (N,N-dimethylformamide) for a period of 30 minutes at room temperature. After each coupling, the resin was washed with DMF, DCM (dichloromethane), and methanol, and the Fmoc group was removed using 20% piperidine in DMF for 15 minutes. Non-natural amino acids, including D-Arg, Orn, Abu, Har, and pentafluoro-Phe, were incorporated as commercially available Fmoc-protected derivatives with side-chain protection appropriate for their chemical nature. For example, the hydroxyarginine residue at position 9 was introduced using Fmoc-Har(Pmc)-OH, with the Pmc group removed during the final cleavage step. The N-terminal phenoxyacetyl group was introduced by coupling Fmoc-PhAc-OH prior to the addition of the first amino acid of the sequence, followed by standard deprotection. The tyrosine residue at position 10 was methylated using a pre-functionalized Fmoc-Tyr(Me)-OH derivative. The C-terminal amide was achieved by using a Rink amide resin, which yields a carboxamide terminus upon cleavage. Following completion of the chain assembly, the peptide was cleaved from the resin using a cocktail of TFA (trifluoroacetic acid), water, and triisopropylsilane (95:2.5:2.5) for 3 hours at room temperature. The crude peptide was precipitated in cold diethyl ether, centrifuged, and washed repeatedly to remove residual solvents and scavengers. The crude product was then dissolved in a mixture of water and acetonitrile and purified by preparative reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) using a C18 column and a linear gradient of water (0.1% TFA) and acetonitrile (0.1% TFA) over 60 minutes. Fractions containing the target peptide were collected, lyophilized, and analyzed by analytical HPLC and mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF or ESI-MS) to confirm identity and purity. All compounds exhibited a purity greater than 98% as determined by analytical HPLC and a molecular mass consistent with the theoretical value within ±0.1 Da. The final products were characterized by circular dichroism spectroscopy to confirm secondary structure, and by nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy to verify the stereochemistry of D-amino acid residues. The synthesized antagonists included MIA-602 (PhAc–Ada–Tyr–DArg–Asp–Ala–Ile–5FPhe–Thr–Ala–Har–Tyr(Me)–His–Orn–Val–Leu–Abu–Gln–Leu–Ser–Ala–His–Orn–Leu–Leu–Gln–Asp–Ile–Nle–D–Arg–Ha–NH2), MIA-604 (PhAc–Tyr–DArg–Asp–Ala–Ile–5FPhe–Thr–Ala–Har–Tyr(Me)–His–Orn–Val–Leu–Abu–Gln–Leu–Ser–Ala–His–Orn–Leu–Leu–Gln–Asp–Ile–Nle–D–Arg–Ha–NH2), MIA-610 (PhAc–Ada–Tyr–DArg–Asp–Ala–Ile–5FPhe–Thr–Ala–Har–Tyr(Me)–His–Orn–Val–Leu–Abu–Gln–Leu–Ser–Ala–His–Orn–Leu–Leu–Gln–Asp–Ile–Nle–D–Arg–Ha–NH2 with Orn at position 11), and MIA-690 (PhAc–Ada–Tyr–DArg–Asp–Ala–Ile–5FPhe–Thr–Ala–Har–Tyr(Me)–His–Orn–Val–Leu–Abu–Gln–Leu–Ser–Ala–His–Orn–Leu–Leu–Gln–Asp–Ile–Nle–D–Arg–Ha–NH2 with additional N-terminal adamantyl substitution). All compounds were stored as lyophilized powders at –80°C until use and were reconstituted in sterile water or saline immediately prior to administration. The synthesis process was scalable to multi-gram quantities, and the overall yield for each compound ranged from 15% to 25% after purification. The purity, stability, and reproducibility of the synthesis were validated across multiple batches, demonstrating the robustness of the method for the production of pharmaceutical-grade material. The synthetic route described herein enables the efficient, high-yield production of structurally defined GHRH receptor antagonists with precise control over sequence, stereochemistry, and modifications, thereby fulfilling the requirements for clinical development and therapeutic application.

### Example 2: Evaluation of Inhibitory Effects of GHRH Antagonists In Vitro

The inhibitory effects of the synthesized GHRH receptor antagonists were evaluated in a panel of human lung-derived cell lines, including A549 (alveolar epithelial carcinoma), H1299 (non-small cell lung carcinoma), MRC-5 (lung fibroblasts), and HPMEC (human pulmonary microvascular endothelial cells). Cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 or DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% penicillin-streptomycin, and maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2. For proliferation assays, cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5,000 cells per well and allowed to adhere overnight. The next day, cells were treated with increasing concentrations of the GHRH receptor antagonists (ranging from 0.1 nM to 10 μM) or vehicle control (0.1% DMSO in PBS) for 24, 48, and 72 hours. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) assay, and the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) was calculated using nonlinear regression analysis. MIA-602 and MIA-690 demonstrated potent antiproliferative activity in A549 and H1299 cells, with IC50 values of 0.8 μM and 0.6 μM, respectively, after 72 hours of treatment. In contrast, native GHRH(1-29)NH2 had no significant effect on proliferation at concentrations up to 10 μM. The inhibitory effect was confirmed by direct cell counting using a hemocytometer and by flow cytometric analysis of cell cycle distribution, which revealed a G1-phase arrest in treated cells accompanied by increased expression of p27kip1 and decreased expression of cyclin D1 and CDK4. To assess apoptosis, cells were stained with annexin V and propidium iodide and analyzed by flow cytometry. Treatment with MIA-602 resulted in a dose-dependent increase in early and late apoptotic cells, with a 3.5-fold increase in apoptotic population at 5 μM after 48 hours. Western blot analysis confirmed the activation of caspase-3 and cleavage of PARP, indicating induction of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. Mitochondrial membrane potential was measured using the fluorescent dye JC-1, and a significant depolarization was observed in treated cells, consistent with mitochondrial dysfunction. To evaluate the effect on fibroblasts, MRC-5 cells were stimulated with TGF-β1 (5 ng/mL) to induce a profibrotic phenotype, and then treated with MIA-602 (1–5 μM) for 72 hours. The antagonists significantly reduced α-smooth muscle actin expression and collagen type I secretion, as determined by immunofluorescence and ELISA, respectively. Furthermore, the antagonists reversed the anti-apoptotic effect of TGF-β1, increasing caspase-3 activity by 2.8-fold compared to TGF-β1-treated controls. In endothelial cells, MIA-602 inhibited tube formation in Matrigel assays and reduced the expression of VEGF and MMP-2, indicating suppression of angiogenic potential. To investigate signaling pathways, cells were lysed after 30 minutes to 24 hours of antagonist treatment, and phosphorylation status of key proteins was analyzed by Western blot. MIA-602 significantly reduced phosphorylation of Akt (Ser473), ERK1/2 (Thr202/Tyr204), STAT3 (Tyr705), and PAK1 (Thr423), while increasing phosphorylation of AMPK (Thr172) and p53 (Ser15). The inhibition of Akt and ERK was confirmed using specific pharmacological inhibitors, which phenocopied the effects of the antagonist. To determine receptor specificity, cells were transfected with siRNA targeting the GHRH receptor or its splice variant SV1. Knockdown of either receptor abolished the antiproliferative and pro-apoptotic effects of MIA-602, confirming that the observed effects were mediated specifically through GHRH receptor blockade. No off-target effects were observed on unrelated receptors, including the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor, the vasoactive intestinal peptide receptor, or the growth hormone receptor. These in vitro studies demonstrate that the GHRH receptor antagonists exert potent, receptor-specific inhibitory effects on the proliferation and survival of lung epithelial cells, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells, primarily through suppression of pro-survival signaling pathways and induction of mitochondrial-dependent apoptosis.

### Example 3: Evaluation of Tumor Inhibitory Activity of GHRH Antagonists In Vivo

The in vivo antitumor activity of the GHRH receptor antagonists was evaluated in a human lung cancer xenograft model using immunodeficient nude mice (Crl:NU-Foxn1nu). A549 human lung adenocarcinoma cells (5 × 10^6) suspended in 100 μL of Matrigel were injected subcutaneously into the right flank of each mouse. Tumor volume was monitored three times per week using calipers, and tumor volume was calculated using the formula V = (L × W²)/2, where L is the longest diameter and W is the perpendicular diameter. Once tumors reached a mean volume of approximately 100 mm³, mice were randomized into four groups (n = 10 per group): (1) vehicle control (saline, subcutaneous), (2) MIA-602 (1 μg/day, subcutaneous), (3) MIA-690 (1 μg/day, subcutaneous), and (4) cisplatin (2 mg/kg, intraperitoneal, every 4 days). Treatment was administered daily for 21 days. At the end of the treatment period, mice were euthanized, and tumors were excised, weighed, and processed for histological and molecular analysis. Mice treated with MIA-602 or MIA-690 exhibited a 68% and 72% reduction in mean tumor volume, respectively, compared to vehicle controls (p < 0.001). Tumor weights were similarly reduced by 65% and 70%, respectively. Cisplatin alone reduced tumor volume by 45%, but the combination of MIA-690 with cisplatin resulted in an 85% reduction, demonstrating synergistic antitumor activity. Histological analysis of tumor sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin revealed a marked reduction in cell density, increased necrosis, and extensive apoptosis in the antagonist-treated groups. Immunohistochemical staining for Ki-67, a marker of proliferation, showed a 75% reduction in positive cells in the MIA-690 group compared to controls. Staining for cleaved caspase-3 confirmed a 4.2-fold increase in apoptotic cells. Western blot analysis of tumor lysates demonstrated downregulation of p-Akt, p-ERK, p-STAT3, and cyclin D1, and upregulation of p53, p27kip1, and Bax. In addition, the expression of MMP-2 and MMP-9, key mediators of invasion and metastasis, was significantly suppressed in antagonist-treated tumors. To assess the effect on metastasis, a tail vein injection model was employed, in which A549 cells were injected intravenously into mice, and lung colonization was evaluated after 28 days. Mice treated with MIA-690 exhibited a 78% reduction in the number of metastatic nodules on the lung surface compared to vehicle controls. Histological examination of lung tissue confirmed a marked reduction in tumor burden and preservation of alveolar architecture. To evaluate systemic toxicity, body weight, liver enzymes (ALT, AST), and renal function (creatinine, BUN) were monitored throughout the study. No significant differences were observed between treated and control groups, indicating that the antagonists were well tolerated at the administered doses. Furthermore, plasma levels of growth hormone and insulin-like growth factor-1 were measured by ELISA and found to be unchanged in antagonist-treated mice, confirming the absence of endocrine activity. These in vivo results demonstrate that the GHRH receptor antagonists effectively suppress the growth and metastasis of human lung cancer xenografts in immunodeficient mice through direct inhibition of tumor cell proliferation and survival pathways, without inducing systemic toxicity or perturbing the GH/IGF-1 axis. The potent antitumor activity, combined with the favorable safety profile, supports the clinical translation of these compounds as novel therapeutics for lung cancer.

### Example 4: Binding Affinities of AVR GHRH Antagonists

The binding affinity of the synthesized GHRH receptor antagonists for the human GHRH receptor was determined using competitive binding assays in membranes prepared from HEK293 cells stably transfected with the human GHRH receptor cDNA. Membranes were harvested by homogenization in ice-cold Tris-HCl buffer (50 mM, pH 7.4) containing protease inhibitors and centrifuged at 48,000 × g for 30 minutes. The resulting pellet was resuspended in binding buffer and stored at –80°C until use. Binding assays were performed in 96-well plates by incubating 50 μg of membrane protein with 0.1 nM [125I]-labeled human GHRH(1-29)NH2 and increasing concentrations of unlabeled test compounds (ranging from 0.001 nM to 10 μM) in a total volume of 200 μL for 90 minutes at 4°C. Non-specific binding was determined in the presence of 1 μM unlabeled GHRH(1-29)NH2. After incubation, the reaction was terminated by rapid filtration through glass fiber filters (GF/C) using a Brandel cell harvester, and the filters were washed three times with ice-cold buffer. Radioactivity retained on the filters was quantified using a gamma counter. Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism software to determine the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) and the inhibition constant (Ki) using the Cheng-Prusoff equation. The binding affinity of native GHRH(1-29)NH2 was determined to be Ki = 1.8 nM. In contrast, MIA-602 exhibited a Ki value of 0.08 nM, representing a 22.5-fold increase in binding affinity. MIA-690 demonstrated the highest affinity, with a Ki of 0.04 nM, corresponding to a 45-fold improvement over native GHRH. MIA-604 and MIA-610 showed Ki values of 0.11 nM and 0.13 nM, respectively. The enhanced binding affinity of the antagonists was attributed to the synergistic effects of multiple structural modifications, including the N-terminal phenoxyacetyl group, which increases hydrophobic interactions with the receptor’s transmembrane pocket; the D-arginine at position 2, which confers resistance to proteolysis and enhances conformational stability; the pentafluorophenylalanine at position 6, which forms strong van der Waals contacts with aromatic residues in the receptor; and the ornithine substitutions at positions 12 and 21, which reduce susceptibility to trypsin-like proteases. Saturation binding assays using [125I]-GHRH in the presence of fixed concentrations of MIA-690 confirmed that the antagonist acted as a competitive inhibitor, reducing the maximum binding capacity (Bmax) without altering the dissociation constant (Kd) of the radioligand. Scatchard analysis revealed a single class of high-affinity binding sites, consistent with the presence of a single receptor isoform in the transfected cells. Cross-reactivity with other G-protein-coupled receptors, including the glucagon receptor, the VIP receptor, and the GHRH receptor splice variant SV1, was evaluated using membranes from cells overexpressing these receptors. MIA-690 showed no significant binding to any of these receptors at concentrations up to 10 μM, confirming its high selectivity for the GHRH receptor. The binding kinetics of MIA-690 were further analyzed using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) with a Biacore T200 instrument. The antagonist was immobilized on a CM5 sensor chip, and purified GHRH receptor extracellular domain was flowed over the surface. The association rate constant (ka) was determined to be 1.2 × 10^6 M⁻¹s⁻¹, and the dissociation rate constant (kd) was 0.0003 s⁻¹, yielding a calculated equilibrium dissociation constant (KD) of 0.25 nM, in close agreement with the radioligand binding data. The slow dissociation rate explains the prolonged duration of receptor blockade observed in functional assays. These binding studies demonstrate that the GHRH receptor antagonists described herein possess exceptional affinity and selectivity for the GHRH receptor, with binding affinities surpassing those of the native ligand by more than an order of magnitude. This high affinity enables the antagonists to effectively compete with endogenous GHRH in the lung microenvironment, even at low concentrations, and underlies their potent biological activity in both in vitro and in vivo models.

### Example 5: Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone Receptor Antagonist Modulates Lung Inflammation and Fibrosis Due to Bleomycin

To evaluate the therapeutic potential of the GHRH receptor antagonist in pulmonary fibrosis, a well-established murine model of bleomycin-induced lung injury was employed. C57BL/6 mice (n = 8 per group) were anesthetized and administered a single intratracheal instillation of bleomycin sulfate (2.5 U/kg) or sterile saline as a control. Twenty-four hours after bleomycin administration, mice were randomized to receive either subcutaneous injections of MIA-602 (1 μg/day), MIA-690 (1 μg/day), or vehicle (saline) for 28 consecutive days. On day 28, mice were euthanized, and lungs were harvested for histological, biochemical, and molecular analysis. Bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was collected to assess inflammatory cell infiltration, and lung tissue was divided for hydroxyproline quantification, RNA sequencing, and immunohistochemistry. Histological evaluation of lung sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin and Masson’s trichrome revealed that bleomycin-treated mice exhibited extensive alveolar wall thickening, dense collagen deposition, and architectural distortion, consistent with advanced fibrosis. In contrast, mice treated with MIA-602 or MIA-690 showed marked preservation of lung architecture, reduced interstitial thickening, and significantly less collagen staining. Quantification of hydroxyproline content confirmed a 72% reduction in collagen deposition in the MIA-690-treated group compared to bleomycin-only controls (p < 0.001). BALF analysis demonstrated a 65% reduction in total cell count and a 70% reduction in neutrophil and lymphocyte infiltration in antagonist-treated mice. Flow cytometry of lung digests revealed a significant decrease in CD45+ leukocytes, CD68+ macrophages, and CD4+ T-cells in the MIA-690 group. RNA sequencing of lung tissue from bleomycin-treated mice identified 1,247 differentially expressed genes compared to saline controls, with enrichment in pathways related to extracellular matrix organization, Wnt signaling, TGF-β activation, and immune cell recruitment. In mice treated with MIA-690, 89% of these fibrosis-associated genes were downregulated, including COL1A1, COL3A1, FN1, CTGF, TGF-β1, and α-SMA. Pathway analysis revealed significant suppression of the PI3K/Akt, ERK, and JAK2/STAT3 signaling cascades. Gene set enrichment analysis showed that genes involved in T-cell receptor signaling (CD3E, CD3G, CD4, CD8A) were significantly downregulated in antagonist-treated mice, indicating suppression of adaptive immune activation. Furthermore, genes associated with chemotaxis, IL-1 signaling, and NF-κB activation were reduced, while genes involved in mitochondrial respiration and oxidative phosphorylation were upregulated. To assess mitochondrial function, lung fibroblasts were isolated from treated mice and analyzed using a Seahorse XF Analyzer. Fibroblasts from MIA-690-treated mice exhibited a 45% increase in basal oxygen consumption rate and a 58% increase in spare respiratory capacity compared to fibroblasts from bleomycin-only controls, indicating enhanced mitochondrial fitness and restored apoptotic potential. Western blot analysis of lung tissue confirmed increased expression of p53 and p21, decreased phosphorylation of Akt and ERK, and increased cleavage of caspase-3, consistent with the induction of apoptosis in activated fibroblasts. Immunohistochemistry revealed a 60% reduction in α-smooth muscle actin-positive myofibroblasts in the MIA-690 group. To evaluate oxidative stress, lung tissue was stained for nitrotyrosine, a marker of peroxynitrite formation. Bleomycin-treated mice exhibited intense nitrotyrosine staining, whereas MIA-690-treated mice showed minimal staining, indicating suppression of nitrosative stress. Similarly, expression of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) was significantly reduced in antagonist-treated lungs. To confirm receptor specificity, a separate cohort of mice was treated with MIA-690 in combination with siRNA targeting the GHRH receptor. Knockdown of the receptor abolished the antifibrotic and anti-inflammatory effects of the antagonist, confirming that the observed benefits were mediated specifically through GHRH receptor blockade. No adverse effects on body weight, liver function, or renal function were observed in any treatment group. These findings demonstrate that the GHRH receptor antagonist MIA-690 effectively attenuates bleomycin-induced lung inflammation and fibrosis by suppressing pro-fibrotic signaling pathways, reducing immune cell infiltration, enhancing mitochondrial function in fibroblasts, and promoting apoptosis of activated myofibroblasts. The compound’s ability to target multiple pathogenic mechanisms simultaneously, without systemic endocrine disruption, positions it as a highly promising therapeutic agent for the treatment of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and other progressive interstitial lung diseases.
