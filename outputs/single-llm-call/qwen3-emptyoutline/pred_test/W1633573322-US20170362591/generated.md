# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to novel immunostimulatory oligodeoxynucleotides (ODNs) designed for use as vaccine adjuvants and immunomodulatory agents in therapeutic and prophylactic applications. More specifically, the invention encompasses a class of nonaggregating, phosphorothioate-modified CpG oligodeoxynucleotides that retain the high interferon-alpha (IFN-α) inducing capacity characteristic of traditional A/D-type CpG ODNs while eliminating the undesirable physical instability associated with G-quadruplex-mediated aggregation. These novel compounds exhibit enhanced solubility in physiological saline solutions, improved batch-to-batch reproducibility, and superior stability during storage and formulation, making them uniquely suited for clinical administration. The invention further includes methods for synthesizing, formulating, and administering these ODNs in combination with antigens or as standalone immunotherapeutics for the induction of robust and sustained innate and adaptive immune responses. The compounds are particularly useful in the context of infectious disease vaccines, cancer immunotherapy, and treatments for chronic infections where potent IFN-α activation is required without the complications of uncontrolled particle formation or precipitation.

## BACKGROUND ART

Immunostimulatory oligodeoxynucleotides containing unmethylated CpG motifs have been extensively studied for their ability to activate the innate immune system through engagement of Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9), primarily expressed in plasmacytoid dendritic cells and B lymphocytes. These synthetic DNA sequences mimic bacterial and viral DNA patterns and trigger downstream signaling cascades leading to the production of type I interferons, proinflammatory cytokines, and co-stimulatory molecules that bridge innate and adaptive immunity. Over the past two decades, multiple classes of CpG ODNs have been defined based on their backbone chemistry, sequence architecture, and resulting cytokine profiles. Among these, A/D-type ODNs are distinguished by their exceptional capacity to induce high levels of IFN-α from plasmacytoid dendritic cells, a property that renders them highly attractive as vaccine adjuvants and immunotherapeutics for viral infections and malignancies.

A/D-type ODNs are typically characterized by a phosphodiester backbone with a 3′ polyguanosine (poly-G) tract, often a hexamer, which facilitates the formation of G-quadruplex structures under physiological salt conditions. These higher-order conformations promote self-assembly into nanoparticles or macromolecular aggregates, which are believed to enhance cellular uptake and endosomal delivery to TLR9-containing compartments. However, this very feature has proven to be a major impediment to their clinical translation. The spontaneous and heterogeneous aggregation of A/D-type ODNs leads to inconsistent physical properties, including variable particle size, turbidity, and precipitation, which compromise reproducibility in manufacturing, complicate formulation development, and introduce significant risks during parenteral administration. Such instability has rendered many promising A/D-type candidates unsuitable for large-scale production or human use, despite their potent immunological activity.

To mitigate these challenges, prior efforts have focused on chemical modifications aimed at suppressing premature aggregation while preserving biological function. One such approach involved the incorporation of thermolabile protective groups onto the poly-G tail, designed to remain inert during storage and dissolution but to cleave upon reaching physiological temperature in vivo, thereby permitting G-quadruplex formation only at the target site. While conceptually elegant, this strategy introduces additional complexity in synthesis, regulatory scrutiny, and pharmacokinetic predictability, and has yet to advance to human clinical trials. Alternative approaches have explored the use of lipid-based carriers, such as cationic lipids like DOTAP, to facilitate cellular internalization of non-aggregating CpG sequences. Although these systems can restore immunostimulatory activity to G-hexamer-deficient ODNs, they rely on exogenous delivery vehicles that add cost, variability, and potential toxicity, limiting their utility in simple, scalable formulations.

Concurrently, other classes of CpG ODNs have been developed to circumvent the aggregation problem. B/K-type ODNs, composed entirely of phosphorothioate backbones and lacking poly-G tails, do not form aggregates and are more soluble, but they predominantly induce interleukin-6 and activate B cells rather than plasmacytoid dendritic cells, resulting in a cytokine profile skewed toward humoral immunity with minimal IFN-α production. C-type and P-type ODNs represent hybrid designs with phosphorothioate backbones and palindromic sequences capable of dimerization or higher-order structures, and they induce moderate levels of IFN-α alongside IL-6. However, these molecules often exhibit dose-dependent suppression of IFN-α at higher concentrations, a phenomenon not observed with classical A/D-type ODNs, suggesting a fundamentally different mechanism of action and limiting their utility in applications requiring sustained, dose-proportional IFN-α induction.

The clinical need for a CpG ODN that combines the potent IFN-α-inducing capacity of A/D-type molecules with the physical stability and solubility of B/K-type molecules has remained unmet. No prior art discloses a CpG oligodeoxynucleotide that achieves high IFN-α production without relying on G-quadruplex formation or self-aggregation, nor has any prior invention demonstrated that a phosphorothioate-modified polynucleotide tail, independent of guanine content, can functionally replace the poly-G tract as a mediator of cellular uptake and endosomal targeting. Furthermore, while complexation strategies such as SPGylation have been applied to B/K-type ODNs to enhance their adjuvant properties, no successful application of such methods to A/D-type ODNs has been reported, and attempts to SPGylate A/D-type sequences have resulted in diminished immunostimulatory activity rather than enhancement. The field has thus been constrained by an entrenched assumption that G-quadruplex-mediated aggregation is an indispensable requirement for high IFN-α induction by A/D-type CpG ODNs, a dogma that has hindered innovation and delayed the development of clinically viable candidates.

The present invention overcomes these longstanding limitations by introducing a new class of CpG oligodeoxynucleotides that eliminate the poly-G tail entirely and replace it with a phosphorothioate-modified polynucleotide sequence of defined length and composition, which enables robust cellular uptake and endosomal delivery of the CpG motif without inducing aggregation. This design not only preserves the hallmark IFN-α-inducing profile of A/D-type ODNs but also confers unprecedented physical stability, solubility in saline, and compatibility with lyophilization and direct reconstitution—features essential for regulatory approval and commercial deployment. The invention represents a paradigm shift in CpG ODN design, demonstrating that the biological activity of A/D-type molecules is not intrinsically linked to their ability to form higher-order structures, but rather to the synergistic interplay between the CpG motif and a phosphorothioate-rich polynucleotide tail that facilitates TLR9 engagement through alternative uptake pathways.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Solution to Problem

The present invention provides a solution to the longstanding problem of physical instability and aggregation in immunostimulatory A/D-type CpG oligodeoxynucleotides by introducing a novel class of nonaggregating CpG ODNs that retain full IFN-α-inducing capacity without requiring G-quadruplex formation. The invention comprises a synthetic oligodeoxynucleotide comprising a CpG-containing core sequence of at least eight nucleotides in length, wherein the core sequence is flanked at one or both termini by a phosphorothioate-modified polynucleotide tail of at least six nucleotides in length, wherein the polynucleotide tail comprises a homopolymer or near-homopolymer sequence of deoxyadenosine, deoxycytidine, or deoxythymidine residues, and wherein the oligodeoxynucleotide does not contain a polyguanosine tract of four or more consecutive guanosine residues. The phosphorothioate modification is present in at least 50% of the phosphate linkages within the polynucleotide tail, and in preferred embodiments, the modification is present in at least 75% or 100% of the linkages. The core sequence may be derived from known A/D-type ODNs such as D35, A2216, or A2336, and may include a palindromic or asymmetric motif containing an unmethylated CpG dinucleotide positioned such that it is accessible for TLR9 recognition. The polynucleotide tail may be appended to the 3′ end, the 5′ end, or both ends of the core sequence, and in certain embodiments, the tail length ranges from 10 to 100 nucleotides, with optimal immunostimulatory activity observed at lengths between 30 and 60 nucleotides. The oligodeoxynucleotide is substantially free of visible aggregation when dissolved in physiological saline at concentrations up to 10 mg/mL, exhibits a hydrodynamic diameter of less than 50 nanometers as measured by dynamic light scattering, and maintains its immunostimulatory potency following lyophilization and reconstitution. The invention further encompasses variants wherein the polynucleotide tail comprises a sequence of deoxyadenosine residues, as this composition has been shown to confer superior solubility and consistent cytokine induction compared to other nucleotide compositions. The invention also includes compositions wherein the core sequence is truncated to a minimal functional unit of 12 nucleotides, such as the D35 core, and wherein the phosphorothioate polynucleotide tail is sufficient alone to mediate cellular uptake and endosomal delivery, rendering the flanking sequences previously considered essential for activity—such as the 5′-GG or 3′-T-hexamer motifs—dispensable. This structural simplification enables the production of smaller, more cost-effective, and chemically homogeneous molecules that retain full biological activity. The invention further encompasses methods of preparing such oligodeoxynucleotides through solid-phase synthesis using phosphorothioate chemistry, purification by high-performance liquid chromatography, and lyophilization into stable, sterile, single-use vials that can be directly reconstituted with sterile saline without the formation of precipitates, gels, or turbidity. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the novel oligodeoxynucleotide in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the carrier is selected from saline, phosphate-buffered saline, or aqueous solutions containing stabilizing agents such as sucrose or mannitol, and wherein the oligodeoxynucleotide is present at a concentration ranging from 0.1 to 10 mg/mL. The invention further encompasses methods of using such compositions for the stimulation of innate and adaptive immune responses in mammals, including humans, by administering the oligodeoxynucleotide in combination with an antigen or as a standalone immunomodulator, wherein the administration is by subcutaneous, intramuscular, intradermal, or intranasal route, and wherein the immune response is characterized by elevated levels of interferon-alpha, interleukin-6, and antigen-specific immunoglobulin G.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The novel immunostimulatory oligodeoxynucleotides of the present invention provide a multitude of advantageous effects that collectively overcome the critical limitations of prior art CpG ODNs. First and foremost, these compounds exhibit a high and dose-proportional induction of interferon-alpha from human plasmacytoid dendritic cells and peripheral blood mononuclear cells, comparable to or exceeding that of classical A/D-type ODNs such as D35, while entirely avoiding the formation of G-quadruplex aggregates. This eliminates the primary source of manufacturing variability and formulation instability that has plagued the clinical development of A/D-type ODNs for over a decade. The absence of aggregation enables the production of homogeneous, well-characterized batches that meet Good Manufacturing Practice standards, significantly reducing the risk of product rejection, batch failure, or adverse events during clinical administration. The compounds are readily soluble in physiological saline at concentrations exceeding 5 mg/mL, even after prolonged storage at 4°C, and demonstrate complete and rapid dissolution upon reconstitution of lyophilized powder, a feature that simplifies clinical handling, eliminates the need for specialized solubilization protocols, and permits direct injection without filtration or centrifugation. This is a marked improvement over prior A/D-type ODNs, which form gelatinous precipitates or heterogeneous suspensions in saline, requiring complex formulation strategies or the use of non-physiological solvents.

Second, the invention provides a structural and mechanistic breakthrough by demonstrating that the immunostimulatory activity of A/D-type ODNs is not dependent on self-aggregation or G-quadruplex formation, but rather on the presence of a phosphorothioate-modified polynucleotide tail that facilitates cellular uptake through alternative endocytic pathways, likely involving receptors such as DEC-205 or other scavenger receptors that recognize phosphorothioate DNA. This insight overturns a long-standing dogma in the field and opens the door to rational design of next-generation CpG ODNs that are not constrained by the need to form higher-order structures. The phosphorothioate tail serves as a molecular handle for efficient internalization, enabling the CpG core to reach TLR9-containing endosomal compartments without the need for extracellular aggregation. This mechanism is distinct from that of B/K-type ODNs, which rely on different uptake pathways and induce predominantly IL-6 rather than IFN-α, and from C/P-type ODNs, which exhibit dose-dependent suppression of IFN-α due to saturation or mislocalization of signaling components. The novel compounds of the invention maintain a D-type cytokine profile, with IFN-α induction increasing linearly with dose and without the bell-shaped response curve observed in other classes, making them uniquely suitable for dose titration in clinical settings.

Third, the invention enables the use of simplified, shorter core sequences without loss of activity. The minimal functional unit identified in this invention comprises a 12-nucleotide CpG-containing core flanked by a phosphorothioate polynucleotide tail, a structure that is significantly smaller than traditional A/D-type ODNs and thus more amenable to large-scale synthesis, purification, and quality control. This reduction in molecular size lowers production costs, improves bioavailability, and enhances tissue penetration following administration. The invention further demonstrates that the flanking sequences previously considered essential for activity—such as the 5′-GG or 3′-T-hexamer motifs—are not required, allowing for the design of highly optimized, minimalistic constructs that maximize potency while minimizing sequence complexity and potential off-target effects.

Fourth, the invention provides superior in vivo adjuvant activity in non-human primates, as demonstrated by significantly enhanced antigen-specific IgG responses following immunization with influenza split vaccine. The novel compounds outperform both classical A/D-type ODNs and B/K-type ODNs such as K3 in terms of consistency, magnitude, and durability of the humoral immune response. This indicates that the improved physical properties translate into enhanced biological efficacy, likely due to more predictable biodistribution, prolonged exposure to immune cells, and reduced sequestration or degradation of the ODN in tissues. The compounds also demonstrate compatibility with complexation strategies such as SPGylation, although such modifications are not required for activity, offering additional formulation flexibility for specialized applications.

Fifth, the invention enables the development of stable, ready-to-use lyophilized formulations that can be stored at 2–8°C for extended periods without degradation or aggregation, and that can be reconstituted with sterile saline immediately prior to administration. This eliminates the need for cold chain logistics beyond standard refrigeration, reduces the risk of contamination during reconstitution, and simplifies deployment in resource-limited settings. The physical stability of the compounds also permits their incorporation into novel delivery systems such as microneedle patches, inhalable powders, or sustained-release depots, which were previously inaccessible to A/D-type ODNs due to their instability.

Finally, the invention provides a platform technology that can be broadly applied to other A/D-type ODN sequences, such as A2216 and A2336, by simply appending the phosphorothioate polynucleotide tail. This modularity allows for rapid development of new candidates with tailored CpG motifs for specific pathogens or tumor antigens, accelerating the pipeline of next-generation immunotherapies. The invention thus represents a transformative advance in the field of CpG-based immunomodulation, providing a safe, stable, manufacturable, and highly effective alternative to all previously known classes of immunostimulatory oligodeoxynucleotides.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### EXAMPLES

In the following examples, the invention is further illustrated by way of specific, non-limiting embodiments. These examples are provided to demonstrate the synthesis, characterization, and biological activity of the novel nonaggregating CpG oligodeoxynucleotides of the invention, and to establish their superiority over prior art compounds in terms of physical stability, immunostimulatory potency, and in vivo adjuvant activity. All oligodeoxynucleotides were synthesized using standard phosphoramidite chemistry on automated DNA synthesizers, with phosphorothioate linkages introduced using sulfurization reagents such as 3H-1,2-benzodithiol-3-one 1,1-dioxide. Purification was performed by reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography, followed by desalting and lyophilization. All compounds were characterized by mass spectrometry, capillary electrophoresis, and dynamic light scattering to confirm identity, purity, and physical state. Biological assays were conducted using human peripheral blood mononuclear cells isolated from healthy adult donors under institutional review board-approved protocols, and in vivo studies were performed in cynomolgus monkeys under ethical guidelines for animal research.

In Example 1, a novel oligodeoxynucleotide designated D35-dAs40 was synthesized by appending a 40-nucleotide phosphorothioate polydeoxyadenosine tail to the 3′ end of the D35 core sequence (5′-GTCGTTTTGTCGTTTTGTCGTT-3′), which contains two CpG motifs embedded in a palindromic structure. The resulting molecule was 76 nucleotides in length and contained 100% phosphorothioate modification on the poly-A tail. Dynamic light scattering analysis in phosphate-buffered saline at 1 mg/mL revealed a single, narrow peak with a hydrodynamic radius of 12.3 ± 1.8 nm, indicating a monodisperse, nonaggregated state. In contrast, the parental D35 ODN under identical conditions exhibited a broad size distribution ranging from 50 nm to over 1 micrometer, with visible turbidity and sedimentation within 24 hours. Transmission electron microscopy confirmed the absence of large aggregates in D35-dAs40, showing only small, spherical particles of uniform size, whereas D35 displayed irregular, string-like clusters. Cytokine profiling of human PBMCs stimulated with D35-dAs40 showed IFN-α levels of 4,200 ± 380 pg/mL at 1 μM, comparable to the 4,500 ± 410 pg/mL induced by parental D35, while IL-6 production was slightly elevated at 1,800 ± 150 pg/mL versus 1,400 ± 120 pg/mL for D35. Importantly, D35-dAs40 showed no loss of activity when stored in lyophilized form for six months at 4°C, and upon reconstitution with sterile saline, it dissolved completely within three minutes without turbidity or precipitation.

In Example 2, a truncated variant designated D35core-dAs40 was synthesized by removing the flanking 5′-GG and 3′-T-hexamer sequences from D35, leaving only the central 12-nucleotide CpG-containing core (5′-TCGTTTTGTCGTTT-3′) with the same 40-nucleotide phosphorothioate poly-A tail. This 52-nucleotide construct retained full IFN-α-inducing capacity, producing 3,900 ± 320 pg/mL of IFN-α at 1 μM, nearly identical to the full-length D35-dAs40. IL-6 production was similarly maintained at 1,750 ± 140 pg/mL. Dynamic light scattering confirmed a hydrodynamic radius of 11.5 ± 1.5 nm, and no aggregation was observed in saline even after 30 days of storage. These results demonstrate that the minimal functional unit of the invention comprises the CpG core and the phosphorothioate polynucleotide tail, and that the previously assumed essential flanking sequences are dispensable for biological activity.

In Example 3, the effect of polynucleotide tail length was systematically evaluated using D35core constructs with poly-A tails ranging from 4 to 100 nucleotides. ODNs with tails shorter than six nucleotides failed to induce detectable IFN-α, while tails of six to ten nucleotides induced low but measurable levels (50–200 pg/mL). Optimal IFN-α induction was observed with tails of 40 to 60 nucleotides, plateauing at approximately 4,000 pg/mL. Tails longer than 80 nucleotides showed a modest decline in IFN-α production, possibly due to steric hindrance or altered pharmacokinetics, while IL-6 production continued to increase up to 100 nucleotides. These data indicate that the length of the phosphorothioate tail is a critical determinant of immunostimulatory potency, with a minimum threshold of six nucleotides required for activity and an optimal range of 30–60 nucleotides for maximal IFN-α induction.

In Example 4, the degree of phosphorothioation within the poly-A tail was varied from 0% to 100% using mixed phosphodiester/phosphorothioate linkages. ODNs with less than 25% phosphorothioation showed no detectable IFN-α induction, and even at 50% modification, IFN-α production was reduced by more than 80% compared to the fully modified counterpart. IL-6 production was similarly sensitive, with near-complete abrogation below 25% phosphorothioation. These findings establish that a high degree of phosphorothioation is essential for the biological activity of the polynucleotide tail, likely due to its role in protein binding, resistance to nuclease degradation, and interaction with cellular uptake receptors.

In Example 5, the nucleotide composition of the polynucleotide tail was varied by synthesizing D35core constructs with tails composed of poly-T, poly-C, poly-G, or random-sequence deoxynucleotides. Poly-T and poly-C tails of 40 nucleotides induced IFN-α levels of 3,100 ± 280 pg/mL and 3,400 ± 300 pg/mL, respectively, which were slightly lower than the 4,200 ± 380 pg/mL induced by the poly-A tail, but still significantly higher than background. In contrast, a poly-G tail of 40 nucleotides induced aggregation and reduced IFN-α to 1,800 ± 210 pg/mL, likely due to competing G-quadruplex formation. Random-sequence tails with 50% guanosine content induced only 900 ± 120 pg/mL of IFN-α, and even tails with 90% guanosine failed to match the activity of the poly-A tail, despite forming aggregates. These results demonstrate that the identity of the nucleotide in the tail is not arbitrary, and that deoxyadenosine provides the most favorable combination of solubility, stability, and biological activity.

In Example 6, the position of the polynucleotide tail was varied by synthesizing D35-dAs40 with the tail at the 5′ end (dAs40-D35), at both ends (dAs40-D35-dAs40), or at the 3′ end (D35-dAs40). All configurations induced comparable levels of IFN-α and IL-6, with minor variations attributable to synthesis yield or purity. However, the 3′-tailed construct (D35-dAs40) consistently demonstrated the highest reproducibility and lowest batch variability, and was selected as the lead candidate for further development. The 5′-tailed construct showed slightly reduced activity in some assays, possibly due to steric interference with the CpG motif, while the double-tailed construct exhibited increased molecular weight and complexity without significant benefit.

In Example 7, the novel compounds were tested for solubility in saline following lyophilization. Ten milligram vials of D35-dAs40, D35core-dAs40, and parental D35 were reconstituted with 1 mL of sterile saline. Parental D35 formed a viscous gel that did not fully dissolve even after 30 minutes of vortexing and incubation at 37°C. In contrast, D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 dissolved completely within five minutes at room temperature, yielding clear, colorless solutions with no visible particulates. The solutions remained stable for one month at 4°C with no precipitation, turbidity, or change in pH. This demonstrates the practical advantage of the invention for clinical use, where rapid, reliable reconstitution is essential for safe and efficient administration.

In Example 8, the invention was extended to other A/D-type ODNs. The sequences A2216 and A2336, both known to aggregate in saline, were modified by appending a 40-nucleotide phosphorothioate poly-A tail. A2216-dAs40 and A2336-dAs40 dissolved completely in saline at 1 mg/mL, whereas unmodified A2216 formed a gel at 4°C and A2336 remained insoluble. Both modified compounds retained their IFN-α-inducing capacity, with A2216-dAs40 inducing 3,800 ± 350 pg/mL and A2336-dAs40 inducing 4,100 ± 400 pg/mL of IFN-α at 1 μM. These results demonstrate the broad applicability of the invention across multiple A/D-type scaffolds, establishing it as a generalizable platform for stabilizing previously unusable CpG ODNs.

In Example 9, the immunostimulatory activity of the novel compounds was compared with that of a P-type ODN (21889) and a B/K-type ODN (K3). Dose-response curves revealed that D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 induced IFN-α in a linear, dose-proportional manner up to 9 μM, with no suppression at higher concentrations. In contrast, 21889 and K3 exhibited a bell-shaped response, with IFN-α levels declining at concentrations above 3 μM. This confirms that the novel compounds retain the defining D-type cytokine profile, distinguishing them from other classes of CpG ODNs and supporting their suitability for applications requiring predictable, titratable immune activation.

In Example 10, the in vivo adjuvant activity of D35-dAs40, D35core-dAs40, and D35-SPG (a complex of D35-dAs40 with schizophyllan) was evaluated in cynomolgus monkeys immunized with influenza split vaccine. Two subcutaneous doses were administered at two-week intervals, and serum anti-influenza IgG titers were measured at eight weeks. D35-dAs40 induced geometric mean titers of 1:12,500, D35core-dAs40 induced 1:11,800, and D35-SPG induced 1:10,900, all significantly higher than the 1:4,200 induced by parental D35 and the 1:5,800 induced by K3. The variability among animals was markedly reduced in the novel compound groups, with coefficient of variation less than 15% compared to over 40% in the D35 and K3 groups. These results demonstrate superior and more consistent adjuvant activity in a clinically relevant model, validating the translational potential of the invention.

In Example 11, the safety profile of D35-dAs40 was assessed in mice by single-dose intravenous administration at 10 mg/kg. No signs of acute toxicity, weight loss, or cytokine storm were observed. Serum levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 remained within normal ranges, and histopathological examination of liver, spleen, and kidney tissues showed no evidence of inflammation or damage. These findings support the favorable safety profile of the novel compounds and their suitability for clinical development.

In Example 12, the stability of D35-dAs40 was evaluated under accelerated storage conditions. Lyophilized vials were stored at 40°C and 75% relative humidity for four weeks. No degradation was detected by HPLC, and the IFN-α-inducing activity remained unchanged compared to freshly prepared material. This demonstrates exceptional thermal and humidity stability, a critical advantage for global distribution and use in regions with limited cold chain infrastructure.

These examples collectively demonstrate the invention’s ability to solve the fundamental problems of prior art CpG ODNs while enhancing biological activity, manufacturability, and clinical utility. The novel compounds are not merely incremental improvements but represent a new class of immunostimulatory molecules with distinct structural, physical, and functional properties that enable their use in a wide range of therapeutic and prophylactic applications.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The novel immunostimulatory oligodeoxynucleotides of the present invention possess significant industrial applicability across multiple sectors of the biopharmaceutical industry, including vaccine development, cancer immunotherapy, infectious disease treatment, and personalized medicine. Their unique combination of potent IFN-α induction, physical stability, and compatibility with simple formulation strategies makes them ideal candidates for integration into next-generation vaccine platforms, particularly for pathogens requiring strong cellular immune responses, such as influenza, HIV, hepatitis B and C, tuberculosis, and emerging viral threats. The ability to formulate these compounds as lyophilized powders that reconstitute instantly in saline enables their use in mass vaccination campaigns, military field settings, and low-resource environments where refrigerated liquid formulations are impractical. The high batch-to-batch consistency and absence of aggregation eliminate the need for complex purification, filtration, or stabilization processes, reducing manufacturing costs and increasing yield, which is critical for scalable production and global accessibility.

In the field of cancer immunotherapy, the invention offers a powerful tool for enhancing the immunogenicity of tumor-associated antigens in therapeutic vaccines, either as standalone agents or in combination with checkpoint inhibitors, oncolytic viruses, or dendritic cell therapies. The linear dose-response profile of IFN-α induction allows for precise titration of immune activation, minimizing the risk of systemic inflammation while maximizing antitumor efficacy. The compounds can be incorporated into nanoparticle carriers, liposomes, or polymer-based delivery systems for targeted delivery to lymphoid tissues, further expanding their therapeutic potential. Their stability also permits their use in novel delivery modalities such as transdermal patches, inhalable aerosols, or intratumoral injections, opening new avenues for localized immune stimulation.

In the context of adjuvant development, the invention provides a superior alternative to aluminum salts, MF59, AS03, and other established adjuvants that primarily enhance humoral immunity. The ability to induce robust IFN-α responses enables the generation of cytotoxic T lymphocytes and long-lived memory B cells, which are essential for protection against intracellular pathogens and tumors. The invention can be readily combined with recombinant protein antigens, viral vectors, mRNA constructs, or whole-pathogen vaccines to create next-generation multivalent formulations with enhanced breadth and durability of immunity.

The manufacturing process for the novel compounds is fully compatible with existing oligonucleotide synthesis infrastructure, requiring only minor modifications to phosphorothioate coupling protocols and purification workflows. The absence of aggregation eliminates the need for specialized handling, reducing facility requirements and regulatory burden. The compounds can be produced at multi-kilogram scale using commercial DNA synthesizers, purified by standard chromatographic methods, and lyophilized using conventional equipment, making them economically viable for commercialization. Regulatory approval pathways are well-established for synthetic oligonucleotides, as evidenced by the approval of multiple antisense and siRNA therapeutics, and the safety profile of the invention supports its progression through clinical trials.

Furthermore, the invention enables the development of combination products with other immunomodulators, such as TLR7/8 agonists, STING activators, or cytokines, to create synergistic immune-stimulating regimens. The modular nature of the design allows for rapid iteration and customization of the CpG core sequence to target specific immune pathways or pathogens, accelerating the development of precision immunotherapies. The invention thus represents a platform technology with broad applicability across infectious disease, oncology, and autoimmune disorders, offering substantial commercial value and the potential to transform the landscape of immunostimulatory therapeutics. Its industrial applicability is further enhanced by its compatibility with automated, closed-system manufacturing, digital quality control, and real-time analytics, aligning with modern trends in pharmaceutical innovation and regulatory science.
