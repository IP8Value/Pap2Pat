# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of quantum computing and quantum-assisted machine learning, specifically to systems and methods for compiling tensor network-based quantum models into native gate sequences for execution on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware. The invention provides a novel framework for transforming classically trained matrix product state (MPS) models, used for generative machine learning tasks, into optimized quantum circuits that respect the physical constraints of real-world quantum processors, including limited qubit connectivity, restricted gate sets, and elevated error rates for multi-qubit operations. The disclosed techniques enable the efficient deployment of quantum machine learning models on current and near-term quantum hardware by leveraging the inherent gauge freedom in tensor network representations and applying greedy compilation heuristics that minimize the number of entangling gates while preserving the statistical fidelity of the learned model. This innovation is particularly relevant to applications in unsupervised learning, data generation, and probabilistic modeling where quantum resources must be conserved and noise resilience is paramount.

## BACKGROUND

Quantum computing has advanced rapidly in recent years, with multiple hardware platforms now offering cloud-accessible devices containing dozens of qubits. Despite these advances, current quantum processors remain constrained by noise, limited connectivity between qubits, and a restricted set of native quantum gates, which collectively hinder the execution of complex quantum algorithms. These limitations are especially pronounced in the context of quantum-assisted machine learning (QAML), where the goal is to leverage quantum systems to enhance classical machine learning tasks without requiring full fault-tolerant quantum computation. While theoretical proposals for QAML have proliferated, practical implementations have struggled to bridge the gap between abstract quantum models and the physical realities of existing hardware.

Tensor networks, particularly matrix product states (MPS), have emerged as a promising architecture for QAML due to their ability to represent high-dimensional quantum states with relatively low computational overhead. MPS models are especially well-suited for generative learning tasks, where the objective is to learn a probability distribution over classical data vectors by encoding them into quantum states and then sampling from the resulting Born machine. The sequential preparation scheme inherent to MPS models allows for highly resource-efficient execution, requiring only a constant number of physical qubits—typically one readout qubit and a logarithmic number of ancilla qubits—regardless of the length of the input data vector. This scalability makes MPS-based QAML a leading candidate for near-term quantum advantage.

However, translating an MPS model derived from classical optimization into a quantum circuit executable on real hardware presents significant challenges. The isometric operators that define the sequential preparation scheme are typically represented as abstract matrices acting on a formal ancilla space of dimension χ, which may not correspond to a power of two and thus cannot be directly mapped onto qubit registers without artificial completion. Furthermore, the standard methods for compiling arbitrary unitary operations into native gate sets—such as those based on cosine-sine decompositions or column-wise matrix factorizations—produce circuits that are excessively deep, with a large number of entangling gates such as CNOTs. Since entangling gates are significantly noisier than single-qubit rotations on current hardware, such deep circuits degrade model fidelity and render the quantum advantage unattainable.

Existing compilation techniques also fail to exploit the structural freedoms inherent in tensor network representations. For instance, the gauge freedom in MPS formulations allows for unitary transformations on the bond indices between adjacent tensors without altering the overall quantum state. This freedom, which is typically ignored in conventional QAML workflows, can be harnessed to restructure the isometries such that their matrix representations become more diagonal, thereby reducing the complexity of the required quantum operations. Additionally, the optimization process that produces the MPS tensors is inherently approximate, with finite tolerance levels that permit small deviations from ideal isometries. Consequently, exact compilation of every numerical detail is not only unnecessary but counterproductive, as it may introduce additional gates that amplify noise without improving model accuracy.

Prior attempts to implement QAML on hardware have either avoided the compilation problem entirely by restricting models to trivial cases, or have relied on generic quantum circuit compilers that produce suboptimal gate sequences. These approaches have not systematically addressed the interplay between the structure of tensor network models, the topology of quantum hardware, and the noise characteristics of physical devices. As a result, there remains a critical gap in the field: a systematic, hardware-aware compilation methodology that transforms classically trained tensor network models into shallow, noise-resilient quantum circuits tailored to the constraints of NISQ devices.

The present invention fills this gap by introducing a novel compilation framework that integrates three key innovations: (1) the introduction of a “diagonal gauge” that exploits the unitary freedom in MPS representations to restructure isometries into forms that are more amenable to low-depth compilation; (2) a greedy heuristic search algorithm that constructs quantum circuits by incrementally adding single-qubit and entangling gates, prioritizing those that minimize the number of entangling operations while maintaining fidelity within the optimization tolerance; and (3) a tailored optimization procedure that avoids over-compilation by recognizing that the isometries need only be approximated to within the numerical precision of the classical training process. This framework enables the robust deployment of generative quantum machine learning models on real quantum hardware, even when classical simulation is still feasible, and provides a scalable pathway toward classically intractable regimes by serving as a preconditioner for hybrid quantum-classical optimization loops.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The invention comprises a method and system for compiling tensor network-based quantum machine learning models into native quantum circuits optimized for execution on noisy intermediate-scale quantum hardware. The method begins with a classically trained matrix product state (MPS) model that encodes a probability distribution over classical data vectors, wherein the model is represented as a sequence of isometric operators derived from the left-canonical form of the MPS. The method then applies a diagonal gauge transformation to the MPS tensors, exploiting the unitary freedom in the bond indices to reorient the ancilla basis such that the resulting isometries exhibit maximal diagonal dominance in their matrix representations. This transformation reduces the number of non-zero off-diagonal elements in each isometry, thereby simplifying the subsequent quantum compilation process.

Following the diagonal gauge transformation, a greedy compilation heuristic is employed to construct a quantum circuit that approximates each isometry to within a specified tolerance, using only the native single-qubit and entangling gates supported by the target hardware. The heuristic operates by iteratively building a tree of candidate circuits, starting from a base of single-qubit rotations and incrementally adding entangling gates at allowed qubit pairs according to the hardware connectivity topology. At each step, the cost function—defined as the deviation between the target isometry and the candidate unitary, evaluated only over the non-zero elements of the isometry—is minimized using numerical optimization techniques. The search is constrained by a priority queue that limits the number of candidate circuits retained at each level, preventing combinatorial explosion while ensuring that the most promising low-depth circuits are explored first. The algorithm terminates when a candidate circuit achieves a cost below the tolerance threshold, at which point the corresponding gate sequence is accepted as the compiled representation of the isometry.

The method further incorporates problem-specific gate motifs, such as parameterized two-qubit rotation gates and controlled variants thereof, which are precompiled into sequences of native gates and introduced into the search space to accelerate convergence. These motifs are penalized in the priority queue ordering to prevent premature selection of deeper circuits, but are permitted to be selected if they achieve the required fidelity with fewer total entangling gates than alternative paths. The compiled circuits are then post-processed to consolidate adjacent single-qubit rotations and eliminate negligible rotations below a threshold, further reducing circuit depth and potential error sources.

The resulting quantum circuits are designed to be executed on hardware that supports mid-circuit measurement and qubit reset, enabling the sequential preparation scheme required for MPS-based generative modeling. In cases where such hardware features are unavailable, the compiled circuits can be adapted to run on fixed-register architectures by replicating the ancilla qubit across multiple physical qubits and coupling each data qubit to a shared ancilla, thereby preserving the statistical behavior of the model while accommodating hardware limitations. The invention further includes a noise-aware simulation framework that models the effects of depolarizing and readout errors on the compiled circuits, allowing for predictive performance assessment prior to hardware deployment.

The system implements the method through a software stack that integrates classical tensor network optimization, diagonal gauge transformation, greedy compilation, and hardware-specific circuit post-processing modules. The output is a quantum circuit description in a standard intermediate representation, ready for submission to cloud-based quantum processors. The invention enables the first practical realization of generative quantum machine learning models on existing NISQ hardware, achieving high fidelity with significantly reduced gate depth compared to generic compilation methods, and provides a scalable foundation for future quantum advantage in unsupervised learning tasks.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention provides a comprehensive framework for the compilation of tensor network-based quantum machine learning models into executable quantum circuits on noisy intermediate-scale quantum hardware. The framework is structured as a multi-stage workflow that begins with a classically trained matrix product state (MPS) model and culminates in a hardware-optimized quantum circuit capable of generating samples from a learned probability distribution. Each stage of the workflow is carefully designed to respect the physical constraints of current quantum processors while leveraging the mathematical structure of tensor networks to minimize resource usage and maximize fidelity.

The process begins with the classical training of an MPS model to represent a generative probability distribution over a set of classical data vectors. The data vectors, which may be binary or discretized continuous variables, are first encoded into product states of a quantum register, such that each element of the vector corresponds to the state of a qubit. The ensemble of these encoded states is then used to train an MPS Born machine, wherein the wave function of the system is parameterized by a sequence of low-rank tensors arranged in a one-dimensional chain. The training procedure employs a DMRG-inspired gradient descent algorithm, wherein the negative log-likelihood of the training data is minimized with respect to the elements of the MPS tensors. This optimization is performed iteratively, updating local blocks of tensors while holding the remainder fixed, and is repeated over multiple sweeps until convergence is achieved. The resulting MPS model captures the statistical correlations among the data vectors in a compact form, with the bond dimension χ determining the expressive capacity of the model. The training process is entirely classical and does not require quantum resources, making it compatible with existing computational infrastructure.

Once the MPS model has been trained, the next stage involves transforming the model into a sequential preparation scheme suitable for quantum execution. This transformation relies on the well-established fact that any MPS in left-canonical form can be interpreted as a sequence of isometric operations that entangle a single physical qubit with a χ-dimensional ancillary system. The left-canonical form ensures that each tensor in the MPS satisfies an isometry condition, which guarantees that the overall state remains normalized at each step of the sequential process. The physical qubit, initialized in the |0⟩ state, is coupled to the ancilla via an isometric operator, after which the physical qubit is measured in the computational basis, yielding one element of the generated data vector. The physical qubit is then reset to |0⟩, and the process is repeated with the next isometric operator until the entire data vector is sampled. This procedure requires only a single physical qubit and log₂χ ancilla qubits, regardless of the length of the data vector, making it exceptionally resource-efficient.

However, the isometries derived from the trained MPS are typically dense, complex matrices that do not naturally correspond to simple quantum operations. To address this, the invention introduces a novel transformation known as the diagonal gauge. This transformation exploits the gauge freedom inherent in the MPS formalism, which permits the insertion of arbitrary unitary matrices and their inverses between adjacent tensors without altering the overall quantum state. By applying this freedom to the ancilla degrees of freedom—whose basis states are never directly measured—the diagonal gauge reorients the representation of the ancilla space to maximize the diagonal dominance of the isometry matrices. This is accomplished by computing, for each isometry, a reduced overlap matrix that acts solely on the ancilla subspace and then performing a polar decomposition to extract a unitary transformation that best aligns the ancilla basis with the dominant directions of the isometry. The resulting transformation permutes the ancilla basis states in a manner that concentrates the non-zero elements of the isometry along the diagonal, thereby reducing the number of required entangling operations during compilation. This step is critical, as it ensures that the subsequent compilation process operates on isometries that are structurally simpler and more amenable to shallow circuit representations.

Following the diagonal gauge transformation, the invention applies a greedy compilation heuristic to convert each isometry into a sequence of native quantum gates. The heuristic operates by constructing a tree of candidate circuits, beginning with a base circuit composed of single-qubit rotations on all qubits involved in the isometry. Each node in the tree represents a candidate circuit, and child nodes are generated by inserting a single entangling gate—such as a CNOT—between a pair of qubits permitted by the hardware connectivity topology, followed by additional single-qubit rotations on the affected qubits. The cost function used to evaluate each candidate circuit is defined as the squared Frobenius norm of the difference between the target isometry and the candidate unitary, evaluated only over the non-zero elements of the isometry. This selective evaluation is made possible by the isometry property, which ensures that the unitary candidate need only match the isometry on its support, thereby reducing computational complexity and avoiding unnecessary optimization over irrelevant matrix elements.

The search proceeds by maintaining a priority queue of candidate circuits, sorted by their cost function values. At each iteration, the lowest-cost candidate is selected, and its children are generated and evaluated. To prevent exponential growth of the search space, the number of candidates retained in the queue is bounded by a convergence parameter, which is dynamically adjusted during the optimization process. Early iterations allow for a larger number of candidates to ensure broad exploration, while later iterations reduce the number to focus on fine-tuning the most promising candidates. The algorithm terminates when a candidate circuit achieves a cost below a predefined tolerance, which is chosen to match the numerical precision of the classical training process. This tolerance ensures that the compiled circuit is sufficiently accurate for the intended application without overfitting to the exact numerical values of the isometry, which would introduce unnecessary complexity and noise.

To further accelerate convergence and reduce circuit depth, the invention incorporates pre-defined gate motifs into the search space. These motifs are higher-level quantum operations that are known to be efficiently implementable using a small number of native gates. For example, a two-qubit rotation gate Ŝ(θ, θ), which rotates the state of two qubits in a correlated manner, can be compiled into two CNOTs and a set of single-qubit rotations. Similarly, a controlled variant of this gate, denoted F, which applies the rotation conditionally on a third qubit, can be implemented using eight CNOTs. These motifs are introduced into the search space as atomic operations, and their inclusion allows the algorithm to discover high-fidelity circuits more rapidly than if it were restricted to elementary single-qubit and two-qubit gates alone. However, to prevent the algorithm from favoring these deeper motifs unnecessarily, a penalty function is applied to their cost during the priority queue ordering. This penalty reflects the approximate number of native gates required to implement the motif, ensuring that simpler circuits are prioritized unless the motif offers a decisive advantage in fidelity. The penalty function is problem-specific and can be tuned based on empirical observations of hardware noise characteristics.

Once a candidate circuit has been selected, the resulting quantum circuit undergoes a post-processing phase to further optimize its structure. This phase consolidates adjacent single-qubit rotations into equivalent single operations, removes rotations with angles below a small threshold (e.g., 10⁻³ radians) that contribute negligibly to the overall transformation, and replaces any parameterized gates introduced by the motifs with their native gate decompositions. This final optimization ensures that the output circuit is expressed entirely in terms of the native gate set of the target hardware and is as shallow as possible, minimizing the total number of operations and thereby reducing the cumulative effect of noise.

The invention further addresses the practical limitation that many current quantum processors do not support mid-circuit measurement and reset, which are required for the sequential preparation scheme. To accommodate such devices, the invention provides a method for adapting the compiled circuits to fixed-register architectures. In this adaptation, the single physical qubit is replaced by a register of N qubits, each assigned to a different position in the data vector, and a single ancilla qubit is coupled to each data qubit in sequence. The compiled isometries are applied to the ancilla and each data qubit in turn, with the measurement outcome of each data qubit recorded and the ancilla left unmeasured. This approach preserves the statistical behavior of the sequential scheme while circumventing the need for dynamic qubit reset, enabling deployment on a broader range of hardware platforms.

The compiled circuits are validated through simulation using a noise model that incorporates depolarizing errors on entangling and single-qubit gates, as well as uncorrelated readout errors. The depolarizing channel is parameterized by the average gate fidelity, with CNOT gates assigned a higher error rate than single-qubit gates to reflect typical hardware characteristics. Readout errors are modeled as a symmetric probability matrix that maps true measurement outcomes to observed outcomes. The performance of the compiled circuits is quantified using the convex Kullback-Leibler divergence between the ideal probability distribution and the distribution obtained from noisy simulations. This metric provides a rigorous measure of fidelity and allows for predictive assessment of model performance under realistic noise conditions.

The invention is implemented as a software system comprising a series of interconnected modules. The first module performs classical training of the MPS model using gradient descent on the negative log-likelihood. The second module applies the diagonal gauge transformation to the trained tensors, reorienting the ancilla basis to maximize diagonal dominance. The third module implements the greedy compilation heuristic, constructing candidate circuits and selecting the optimal sequence based on the cost function and priority queue. The fourth module performs circuit post-processing to consolidate rotations and eliminate negligible gates. The fifth module adapts the circuit for fixed-register hardware when necessary. The sixth module simulates the circuit under noise and computes performance metrics. All modules are integrated into a unified framework that accepts classical data as input and outputs a quantum circuit description in a standard format such as OpenQASM or Qiskit’s QuantumCircuit object, ready for submission to cloud-based quantum processors.

The invention has been demonstrated on both synthetic data and real-world datasets, including a benchmark model derived from the MNIST handwritten digit dataset. In the MNIST application, grayscale images were processed into binary 7×7 pixel arrays, encoded into quantum states, and modeled using an MPS with bond dimension eight. The resulting isometries were compiled using the described framework, achieving high fidelity with fewer than 20 entangling gates per isometry, compared to hundreds of CNOTs produced by generic compilation methods. The compiled circuits were simulated under realistic noise conditions, and the resulting probability distributions were found to closely match the training data, even when the noise level was comparable to that of current hardware. The invention thus provides the first practical method for deploying generative quantum machine learning models on existing quantum hardware, enabling the realization of quantum-assisted machine learning in the near term.

The significance of the invention lies in its ability to bridge the gap between theoretical quantum models and practical quantum hardware. By exploiting the mathematical structure of tensor networks and tailoring the compilation process to the physical constraints of NISQ devices, the invention enables the efficient execution of quantum machine learning algorithms that were previously considered infeasible. The framework is generalizable to other tensor network topologies, such as tree tensor networks or projected entangled pair states, and can be extended to other applications beyond generative modeling, including classification and feature extraction. Moreover, the classically trained MPS models serve as effective preconditioners for hybrid quantum-classical optimization loops, providing a high-quality initial state that can accelerate convergence and avoid local minima when fine-tuning is performed on actual quantum hardware. This positions the invention as a foundational technology for the next generation of quantum machine learning applications, enabling the transition from proof-of-concept demonstrations to scalable, commercially viable quantum algorithms.
