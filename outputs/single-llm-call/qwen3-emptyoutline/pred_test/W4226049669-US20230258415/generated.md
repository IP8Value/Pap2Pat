# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel class of liquid-infused surfaces engineered to simultaneously enhance convective heat transfer and reduce hydrodynamic drag in laminar and turbulent fluid flows. Specifically, the invention pertains to structured surfaces comprising periodic transverse grooves infused with a liquid that exhibits thermal conductivity comparable to or less than that of the solid substrate forming the grooves. This configuration enables a previously unexploited mechanism of heat transfer enhancement—dispersive convection within the groove cavities—while concurrently inducing a slip effect at the fluid-interface boundary that reduces wall shear stress. The invention is particularly applicable to thermal management systems where space constraints, energy efficiency, and material compatibility are critical, including microheat exchangers, electronic cooling devices, nuclear reactor components, aerospace thermal control systems, and industrial heat exchange apparatuses operating under low-to-moderate Reynolds number conditions. The invention overcomes longstanding limitations in surface engineering by decoupling the traditional trade-off between heat transfer augmentation and drag penalty, thereby enabling a net improvement in thermal-hydraulic performance that is both measurable and scalable across multiple fluid regimes.

The technical domain encompasses the intersection of microfluidics, interfacial science, and non-isothermal transport phenomena, wherein the thermal and hydrodynamic behavior of complex fluid-solid-liquid interfaces is governed by the interplay of viscous forces, thermal diffusion, and momentum transfer across heterogeneous surfaces. Unlike conventional roughened or textured surfaces that rely on increased surface area or turbulent mixing to enhance heat transfer—often at the cost of elevated pressure drop—this invention exploits the internal recirculation dynamics induced by the external shear flow within liquid-infused cavities to generate a net convective flux that supplements conductive transport. The key innovation lies in the deliberate selection of material properties such that the thermal conductivity of the solid structure is matched to or subordinated by that of the infusing liquid, thereby enabling the recirculating vortices within the grooves to actively participate in heat transport rather than merely acting as passive thermal barriers. This condition, previously overlooked in the literature, transforms the groove geometry from a geometric perturbation into an active thermal amplifier, fundamentally altering the energy balance at the interface and enabling a new paradigm in surface-mediated heat exchange.

The invention is further distinguished by its applicability to both laminar and turbulent flows, with the dispersive convection mechanism remaining effective across a broad range of Péclet numbers defined by the slip velocity and groove depth. The system operates without requiring external energy input, phase change, or active control, relying solely on the inherent hydrodynamic response of the liquid-infused texture to the imposed shear flow. The design is compatible with a wide array of solid materials—including polymers, metals, ceramics, and composites—and infusing liquids—including water, organic solvents, ionic liquids, and low-toxicity liquid metals such as gallium-based alloys—thereby enabling tailored implementations for specific thermal, chemical, and mechanical environments. The invention is not limited to planar geometries and may be extended to curved, cylindrical, or conformal surfaces, making it suitable for integration into pipes, channels, heat sinks, turbine blades, and other complex geometries commonly found in engineering systems. The resulting thermal-hydraulic performance metrics, including enhanced Nusselt numbers and reduced friction coefficients, are quantifiable, repeatable, and predictable through the analytical relationships derived herein, enabling robust design and optimization for industrial deployment.

## BACKGROUND

Historically, the enhancement of convective heat transfer in fluid systems has been pursued through a variety of surface modification techniques, each of which has been constrained by a fundamental trade-off between increased heat flux and increased hydrodynamic resistance. Conventional approaches such as surface roughening, the addition of fins, ribs, grooves, or corrugations have long been employed to increase the effective surface area available for heat exchange. However, these modifications invariably introduce flow separation, vortex shedding, and increased form drag, resulting in elevated pressure losses that necessitate greater pumping power to maintain a given flow rate. This penalty is particularly acute in systems operating under laminar flow conditions, where the absence of turbulent mixing renders the system highly sensitive to geometric perturbations. Even in turbulent flows, where enhanced mixing can partially offset the drag penalty, the net thermal-hydraulic efficiency—the ratio of heat transfer enhancement to drag increase—often remains marginal or negative, especially when the surface modifications are not optimized for both phenomena simultaneously.

Alternative strategies have focused on reducing drag through the use of superhydrophobic surfaces, which trap air pockets beneath the fluid to induce a slip boundary condition. While these surfaces have demonstrated significant reductions in wall shear stress, their thermal performance has been severely limited by the low thermal conductivity of the entrapped gas, which acts as a high-resistance barrier to heat conduction. This limitation has been widely documented in the literature, where the thermal resistance of the gas layer dominates the overall heat transfer process, effectively negating any potential benefit from drag reduction. More recently, liquid-infused surfaces (LIS) have emerged as a promising alternative, replacing the gas phase with a low-viscosity, immiscible liquid that can maintain a stable, slippery interface while offering higher thermal conductivity than gases. However, prior investigations into LIS have largely assumed either that the solid substrate is isothermal or that its thermal conductivity is significantly higher than that of the infusing liquid, thereby treating the solid as a passive conduit for heat rather than an active participant in the thermal transport process.

This assumption has led to a critical oversight in the field: the neglect of convective heat transfer within the groove cavities themselves. In all prior analytical and numerical studies, the heat flux through the LIS was decomposed into conductive contributions from the external fluid, the solid substrate, and the infusing liquid, with convection within the texture being either ignored or assumed negligible. This simplification was justified under the premise that the recirculation within the grooves was too weak or too localized to contribute meaningfully to the overall heat transfer budget. However, this view fails to account for the fact that when the thermal conductivity of the solid is comparable to or lower than that of the infusing liquid, the temperature gradient across the groove interface becomes sufficiently steep to drive a net convective flux from the recirculating vortices. This phenomenon, which has never been systematically quantified or exploited, represents a previously unrecognized thermal amplification mechanism that fundamentally alters the energy balance at the interface.

Furthermore, prior attempts to model LIS heat transfer have relied on simplified thermal resistance networks that assume unit Nusselt number for the textured region, effectively treating the groove as a purely conductive element with no internal convective transport. This assumption leads to systematic underprediction of the actual heat flux, particularly in systems where the solid and liquid thermal conductivities are matched. The resulting design models are therefore inherently conservative, leading to oversized systems, excessive material usage, and suboptimal thermal performance. The absence of a predictive framework for the dispersive convection component has also hindered the development of scalable design rules, leaving engineers without a reliable method to optimize groove geometry, liquid selection, or operating conditions for maximum thermal-hydraulic efficiency.

The present invention addresses these shortcomings by introducing a novel class of liquid-infused surfaces designed explicitly to activate dispersive convection within the groove cavities through the strategic selection of solid and liquid material properties. By ensuring that the thermal conductivity of the solid structure is equal to or less than that of the infusing liquid, the invention enables the recirculating flow within the grooves to actively participate in heat transport, thereby generating a net increase in surface heat flux that is both measurable and predictable. This mechanism operates independently of turbulence, making it effective in laminar flows where traditional enhancement techniques fail, and it synergizes with turbulent mixing in high-Reynolds-number environments to produce a multiplicative enhancement effect. The invention further provides a quantitative, analytical relationship between the dispersive convection contribution and the Péclet number based on the slip velocity and groove depth, enabling precise prediction of heat transfer performance across a wide range of operating conditions. This represents a paradigm shift in surface engineering, moving beyond passive geometry modification to active thermal design through material-property matching and flow-structure coupling.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The invention comprises a liquid-infused surface configured to simultaneously enhance convective heat transfer and reduce hydrodynamic drag in fluid flows by exploiting dispersive convection within transverse grooves, wherein the thermal conductivity of the solid substrate is equal to or less than that of the infusing liquid. The surface consists of a solid base structure containing a periodic array of transverse grooves, each groove being filled with a liquid that is immiscible with the external flowing fluid and has a thermal conductivity equal to or greater than that of the solid material. The grooves are oriented perpendicular to the direction of the primary flow, and their depth, width, and pitch are selected to induce stable recirculation vortices under the action of the external shear stress. The infusing liquid forms a dynamic, continuous interface with the external fluid, enabling slip at the boundary while maintaining thermal continuity across the interface. The combination of these features results in a net increase in surface heat flux compared to a smooth surface of identical geometry, even in the absence of turbulent fluctuations, due to the convective transport of heat by the recirculating flow within the grooves.

The invention further provides a method for predicting the enhancement of heat transfer based on a surface Péclet number defined by the slip velocity and the groove depth, wherein the ratio of dispersive convection to total heat flux increases logarithmically with increasing surface Péclet number over the range of 10 to 1000. This relationship holds for both laminar and turbulent flows and is independent of the viscosity ratio between the infusing and external fluids, provided that the thermal conductivities of the solid and infusing liquid are matched. The dispersive convection mechanism is most effective when the solid-to-liquid thermal conductivity ratio is less than or equal to unity, and the groove geometry satisfies the condition that the vertical dimension of the recirculation vortex is approximately equal to the groove depth. The invention further enables a reduction in hydrodynamic drag due to the slip effect at the liquid-infused interface, resulting in a net improvement in thermal-hydraulic efficiency as quantified by the product of the heat flux enhancement ratio and the inverse drag ratio, which exceeds unity across a broad range of Prandtl numbers.

The invention is applicable to systems operating under laminar or turbulent flow conditions, with the dispersive convection mechanism contributing a measurable fraction of the total heat flux even in turbulent regimes, where it acts as an amplifier of the random convective fluctuations. The enhancement is scalable with groove depth relative to the system height, with the maximum possible contribution of dispersive convection bounded by the ratio of groove depth to total channel height. The invention further permits the use of a wide range of solid materials—including polymers, metals, ceramics, and composites—and infusing liquids—including water, organic solvents, ionic liquids, and liquid metals such as gallium-based alloys—provided that the thermal conductivity constraint is satisfied. The surface may be integrated into planar, cylindrical, or conformal geometries, and is compatible with standard manufacturing techniques such as micro-machining, lithography, and additive manufacturing. The invention enables the design of compact, high-efficiency thermal systems with reduced pumping power requirements, improved energy efficiency, and enhanced thermal stability, particularly in applications where space, weight, or material constraints limit conventional heat transfer augmentation methods.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention is a liquid-infused surface engineered to achieve simultaneous enhancement of convective heat transfer and reduction of hydrodynamic drag through the activation of dispersive convection within transverse grooves, wherein the thermal conductivity of the solid substrate is intentionally matched to or subordinated by that of the infusing liquid. This configuration fundamentally alters the thermal transport mechanism at the interface by enabling the recirculating flow within the grooves to actively participate in heat exchange, rather than acting as a passive thermal barrier or a mere source of slip. The surface consists of a rigid solid base structure, typically fabricated from a metallic, polymeric, or ceramic material, which contains a periodic array of transverse grooves oriented perpendicular to the direction of the primary fluid flow. Each groove is filled with a second liquid that is immiscible with the external flowing fluid and is selected such that its thermal conductivity is equal to or greater than that of the solid substrate. The infusing liquid forms a stable, continuous, and dynamic interface with the external fluid, maintained by capillary forces and surface tension, which prevents the external fluid from penetrating the grooves while allowing the infusing liquid to remain in place under the influence of the imposed shear stress.

The key innovation lies in the deliberate selection of the solid-to-liquid thermal conductivity ratio, κ_s/κ_i, to be less than or equal to unity. In prior art, surfaces with similar geometries were analyzed under the assumption that the solid substrate was either isothermal or had a thermal conductivity significantly higher than that of the infusing liquid, thereby treating the solid as a perfect heat conductor and the groove as a purely conductive region. This assumption led to the erroneous conclusion that the recirculation within the grooves contributed negligibly to the overall heat flux. However, when the thermal conductivity of the solid is comparable to or less than that of the infusing liquid, the temperature gradient across the groove interface becomes sufficiently steep to drive a net convective flux from the recirculating vortices. This phenomenon, termed dispersive convection, arises because the recirculating flow transports fluid with a temperature distinct from the mean flow into and out of the groove, thereby creating a time-averaged deviation in the temperature field that is spatially correlated with the groove geometry. This deviation, quantified as the dispersive component of the convective flux, ṽT, contributes directly to the total surface heat flux and is absent in smooth-wall configurations.

The dispersive convection mechanism is activated by the interaction between the external shear flow and the groove geometry. As the external fluid flows over the surface, the no-slip condition at the solid walls of the groove induces a pressure gradient that drives a secondary recirculation within the cavity. This recirculation forms a stable vortex whose vertical extent is approximately equal to the groove depth, and whose horizontal scale is determined by the groove width and pitch. The recirculating fluid within the vortex is continuously exchanged with the external flow, resulting in a net transport of thermal energy from the bottom of the groove toward the interface and from the interface toward the downstream wall of the groove. This process generates a net positive contribution to the surface heat flux, which is absent in systems where the solid is a superior thermal conductor, as the temperature gradient across the groove is then too shallow to drive significant convective transport. The magnitude of this contribution is governed by the surface Péclet number, Pe_i, defined as the product of the slip velocity and the groove depth, divided by the thermal diffusivity of the infusing liquid. Over the practical range of Pe_i from 10 to 1000, the ratio of dispersive convection to total heat flux increases logarithmically with Pe_i, following the relationship q_conv,d/q ≈ 0.73(k/(h+2k))·ln(Pe_i), where k is the groove depth, h is the channel height, and q is the total surface heat flux. This relationship enables the precise prediction of heat transfer enhancement without the need for full-scale numerical simulations, facilitating rapid design optimization.

The invention further incorporates a geometric constraint that the groove width-to-depth ratio, w/k, must be greater than one to ensure the formation of a coherent recirculation vortex, and that the pitch-to-depth ratio, p/k, must lie between two and eight to maintain stability of the interface and prevent coalescence of adjacent vortices. The solid fraction, φ_s, defined as the ratio of the solid area to the total surface area, must not exceed one-half to ensure sufficient open area for the recirculation to develop fully. These geometric constraints, combined with the thermal conductivity criterion, define the operational envelope of the invention. The infusing liquid may be selected from a broad class of materials, including water, ethylene glycol, silicone oils, ionic liquids, and low-toxicity liquid metals such as eutectic gallium-indium alloys, provided that the thermal conductivity of the liquid is equal to or greater than that of the solid. The solid substrate may be fabricated from materials such as aluminum, copper, stainless steel, polydimethylsiloxane, or silicon carbide, depending on the thermal, mechanical, and chemical requirements of the application. The interface between the infusing liquid and the external fluid is stabilized by surface chemistry, such as the use of fluorinated or siloxane-based coatings, which reduce interfacial tension and prevent wetting of the solid walls by the external fluid.

The invention is effective in both laminar and turbulent flow regimes. In laminar flows, the dispersive convection is the dominant mechanism of heat transfer enhancement, as there are no turbulent fluctuations to contribute to the convective flux. In turbulent flows, the dispersive convection remains a significant contributor, acting as an amplifier of the random convective fluctuations by increasing the local temperature gradients near the interface. The presence of the dispersive convection modifies the turbulent heat flux budget such that the random component, vT, increases proportionally to the total heat flux, leading to a multiplicative enhancement effect. This effect is quantified by the relationship q/q_0 = 1 + Nu_0·(q_conv,d/q_0), where q_0 is the heat flux over a smooth surface, Nu_0 is the Nusselt number of the smooth-wall flow, and q_conv,d/q_0 is the dispersive convection contribution normalized by the smooth-wall heat flux. This equation demonstrates that even a small dispersive convection contribution, on the order of one percent, can lead to a substantial overall heat transfer enhancement when amplified by a high Nusselt number, as is typical in turbulent flows. The invention thus enables a synergistic effect between the dispersive convection and turbulent mixing, resulting in a net heat transfer increase of up to 14 percent in turbulent channel flows, while simultaneously reducing drag by up to three percent.

The reduction in drag arises from the slip effect at the liquid-infused interface, which reduces the wall shear stress by allowing the external fluid to move relative to the solid surface. The slip velocity, U_s, is determined by the viscosity ratio between the infusing and external fluids, the groove geometry, and the shear stress imposed by the external flow. The resulting drag reduction, quantified as DR = (τ_0 - τ)/τ_0, where τ_0 is the wall shear stress over a smooth surface and τ is the shear stress over the textured surface, is typically in the range of 1 to 8 percent, depending on the viscosity ratio and groove geometry. Crucially, the invention achieves a net improvement in thermal-hydraulic efficiency, as measured by the product of the heat flux enhancement ratio and the inverse drag ratio, (q/q_0)·(τ_0/τ), which exceeds unity across all tested conditions. This metric, which represents the ratio of the thermal performance gain to the hydraulic penalty, is a direct measure of the system’s overall efficiency and is significantly higher than that of conventional roughened surfaces or superhydrophobic surfaces with gas pockets.

The invention is scalable and compatible with a wide range of manufacturing techniques. The solid substrate may be fabricated using precision machining, laser etching, photolithography, electrochemical etching, or additive manufacturing methods such as selective laser melting or stereolithography. The infusing liquid may be introduced through capillary action, vacuum impregnation, or pressure-assisted infiltration, and may be replenished through passive diffusion or active reservoir systems in long-duration applications. The surface may be applied to planar geometries, such as heat exchanger plates, or to curved geometries, such as the inner walls of pipes, turbine blades, or microchannels. The invention is particularly suited for applications in microscale thermal management systems, where the high surface-to-volume ratio amplifies the benefit of enhanced heat transfer, and where the reduction in pumping power is critical for energy efficiency. Examples include cooling systems for high-power electronics, microfluidic heat exchangers, nuclear fuel rod cladding, aerospace thermal control panels, and biomedical devices requiring precise temperature regulation.

The invention further enables the design of multi-functional surfaces that combine thermal enhancement with other properties, such as anti-fouling, corrosion resistance, or self-cleaning, through the selection of appropriate infusing liquids and surface coatings. For instance, the use of fluorinated oils as infusing liquids can impart both slip and low-friction properties, while the use of liquid metals can provide high thermal conductivity and electrical conductivity, enabling simultaneous thermal and electrical management. The surface may also be rendered responsive to external stimuli, such as temperature or electric fields, by incorporating phase-change materials or electroactive liquids into the infusing layer, thereby enabling adaptive thermal control. The analytical relationships derived herein provide a robust framework for the design and optimization of such surfaces, allowing engineers to predict heat transfer performance and drag reduction based on a small set of input parameters: the solid and liquid thermal conductivities, the groove geometry, the viscosity ratio, and the operating Reynolds and Péclet numbers.

The invention represents a paradigm shift in surface engineering, moving beyond the traditional paradigm of increasing surface area or inducing turbulence to enhance heat transfer, and instead leveraging the intrinsic coupling between hydrodynamic flow and thermal transport in structured interfaces. By intentionally matching the thermal conductivities of the solid and liquid phases, the invention activates a previously unexploited mechanism of heat transfer enhancement that is both passive and scalable. The resulting surfaces offer a unique combination of high thermal performance, low hydraulic penalty, and material flexibility, enabling the design of next-generation thermal systems that are more efficient, compact, and reliable than those currently available. The invention is not limited to the specific geometries or materials described herein, but encompasses any liquid-infused surface that satisfies the thermal conductivity criterion and induces dispersive convection through transverse groove geometry, thereby achieving simultaneous heat transfer enhancement and drag reduction.
