## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of immunology and infectious disease prophylaxis, specifically in the development of virus-like particle-based vaccines designed to neutralize bacterial quorum-sensing signaling molecules that regulate virulence factor expression in pathogenic microorganisms. More particularly, the invention relates to a recombinant vaccine platform comprising bacteriophage-derived virus-like particles engineered to display a conformationally constrained peptide epitope derived from the autoinducing peptide of Staphylococcus aureus agr type I, thereby eliciting a high-titer, high-affinity antibody response capable of sequestering the native cyclic autoinducing peptide in vivo and suppressing agr-mediated virulence regulation. The invention further extends to the use of such virus-like particles for the prevention of skin and soft tissue infections caused by methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus strains expressing agr type I, as well as to the broader application of this platform for targeting analogous quorum-sensing peptides in other human bacterial pathogens that utilize structurally constrained, secreted peptides to coordinate pathogenic behavior. The invention represents a paradigm shift in anti-infective vaccine design by moving away from traditional approaches that target bacterial surface antigens or toxins and instead focuses on neutralizing extracellular signaling molecules that orchestrate the coordinated expression of multiple virulence determinants. This approach circumvents the challenges associated with antigenic variability, immune evasion, and antibiotic resistance by targeting a conserved regulatory mechanism that is essential for invasive disease but dispensable for bacterial survival in non-host environments. The invention is applicable to both human and veterinary medicine and is particularly suited for deployment in high-risk populations including hospitalized patients, individuals with chronic skin conditions, surgical candidates, and those with compromised immune systems. The platform is amenable to formulation as a standalone prophylactic or as an adjunct to existing antimicrobial therapies, offering a novel strategy to combat the growing global burden of multidrug-resistant bacterial infections.

## BACKGROUND AND OVERVIEW OF THE INVENTION

The rise of antimicrobial resistance has rendered many conventional antibiotics ineffective against common bacterial pathogens, leading to increased morbidity, prolonged hospital stays, and escalating healthcare costs. Among the most clinically significant of these pathogens is Staphylococcus aureus, particularly the methicillin-resistant strains that have become endemic in both healthcare and community settings. These strains are responsible for a wide spectrum of infections, ranging from mild skin and soft tissue infections to life-threatening conditions such as bacteremia, pneumonia, and endocarditis. Despite decades of research, no vaccine against Staphylococcus aureus has achieved regulatory approval, largely due to the organism’s extraordinary capacity for immune evasion, antigenic diversity, and the redundancy of its virulence arsenal. Traditional vaccine strategies that target surface proteins such as protein A, clumping factor, or iron-regulated surface determinants have failed in clinical trials because these antigens are either not consistently expressed during infection or are subject to immune selection pressure that favors escape variants. Furthermore, the complexity of the host-pathogen interaction, wherein the bacterium dynamically modulates its virulence profile in response to environmental cues, has rendered static antigen targets insufficient for durable protection. 

A critical breakthrough in understanding S. aureus pathogenesis came with the discovery of the accessory gene regulator (agr) quorum-sensing system, a conserved genetic circuit that governs the transition from colonization to invasive disease. The agr system operates through the secretion of small, cyclic autoinducing peptides (AIPs), which accumulate extracellularly as bacterial density increases. Upon reaching a threshold concentration, these peptides bind to a membrane-bound histidine kinase receptor, AgrC, triggering a phosphorylation cascade that culminates in the upregulation of RNAIII, a global regulatory RNA that activates the expression of over two hundred virulence genes, including alpha-hemolysin, Panton-Valentine leukocidin, proteases, and toxins that disable phagocytic cells. Simultaneously, agr signaling represses the expression of surface adhesins, facilitating bacterial dissemination from localized sites of infection. There are four distinct agr alleles in S. aureus (agr I–IV), each encoding a unique AIP sequence and corresponding AgrC receptor. Among these, agr type I is the most prevalent in invasive clinical isolates, particularly in the dominant USA300 clone, and is strongly associated with severe skin and soft tissue infections, necrotizing pneumonia, and sepsis. 

Previous attempts to neutralize agr signaling have relied on small molecule inhibitors, synthetic peptides, or passive immunization with monoclonal antibodies targeting AIPs. While some of these approaches demonstrated efficacy in vitro or in murine models, they suffered from poor pharmacokinetics, limited stability, low bioavailability, or narrow serotype specificity. Moreover, chemical conjugation of AIPs to carrier proteins such as keyhole limpet hemocyanin or tetanus toxoid often resulted in denaturation of the thiolactone ring, which is essential for receptor binding and biological activity. The labile nature of this bond renders chemically synthesized AIPs inherently unstable during storage, purification, and administration, leading to inconsistent immune recognition and failure to elicit neutralizing antibodies. Additionally, linearized AIPs fail to mimic the native conformational epitope, resulting in antibodies that bind non-functional forms of the peptide and provide no protective benefit. 

The present invention overcomes these longstanding limitations by employing a bacteriophage virus-like particle platform that presents the AIP1 sequence in a rigid, multivalent, and conformationally restricted context on the surface of self-assembling capsids. The PP7 bacteriophage coat protein forms a highly stable icosahedral structure composed of 180 identical subunits arranged as 90 dimers, each containing a surface-exposed AB-loop that is ideal for epitope display. By genetically inserting a modified AIP1 sequence—YSTSDFIM, in which the native cysteine at position four is replaced with serine—to the second AB-loop of a single-chain dimeric PP7 construct, the invention achieves precise spatial orientation of the peptide backbone that mimics the cyclic structure of native AIP1 without requiring the unstable thiolactone bond. This substitution preserves the spatial geometry of key residues involved in AgrC binding while eliminating the risk of intermolecular disulfide bond formation that could interfere with VLP assembly or purification. The resulting PP7-AIP1S virus-like particles display approximately 90 copies of the AIP1S epitope per particle, creating a dense, repetitive antigenic array that potently activates B cells through cross-linking of their surface immunoglobulin receptors. This multivalent display, a hallmark of viral surfaces, triggers robust T-cell-dependent immune responses characterized by high-affinity IgG production, germinal center formation, and long-lived plasma cell memory. 

In preclinical studies, vaccination with PP7-AIP1S induced serum antibodies that specifically recognized soluble, native cyclic AIP1 but not structurally distinct AIP2, demonstrating exquisite epitope specificity. These antibodies were capable of neutralizing agr signaling in vivo, as evidenced by reduced transcription of RNAIII and diminished production of alpha-hemolysin at the site of infection. Most significantly, vaccinated mice exhibited markedly reduced dermonecrosis, accelerated bacterial clearance, and attenuated inflammatory cytokine responses following challenge with a lethal dose of USA300 MRSA. These outcomes were not observed in control animals vaccinated with wild-type PP7 or phosphate-buffered saline, confirming that protection was directly attributable to the AIP1S-specific immune response. The invention thus provides a novel, rationally designed, and mechanistically validated prophylactic strategy that targets the regulatory architecture of bacterial virulence rather than the virulence factors themselves, offering a powerful alternative to antibiotics and a promising solution to the crisis of antimicrobial resistance.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention comprises a recombinant virus-like particle vaccine that elicits a protective immune response against Staphylococcus aureus by targeting the agr type I quorum-sensing system. The vaccine consists of a self-assembling, non-infectious, non-replicating particle formed from recombinantly expressed Pseudomonas aeruginosa RNA bacteriophage PP7 coat protein dimers, each of which has been genetically modified to incorporate a modified autoinducing peptide sequence derived from S. aureus AIP1. Specifically, the AIP1 sequence YSTSDFIM, in which the native cysteine at position four is substituted with serine to prevent aberrant disulfide bonding, is inserted into the second AB-loop of the PP7 single-chain dimer. This modification allows the peptide to be displayed in a rigid, surface-exposed β-turn conformation that faithfully mimics the three-dimensional structure of the native cyclic AIP1, despite the absence of the labile thiolactone bond. The resulting virus-like particles, designated PP7-AIP1S, assemble into icosahedral structures approximately 30 nanometers in diameter, each displaying approximately 90 copies of the AIP1S epitope on their outer surface. These particles are purified by size exclusion chromatography, characterized for homogeneity by dynamic light scattering and agarose gel electrophoresis, and formulated for parenteral administration without adjuvants. 

Administration of PP7-AIP1S to a mammalian host, particularly a human or murine subject, induces a potent, antigen-specific humoral immune response characterized by high-titer IgG antibodies that recognize and bind the native cyclic AIP1 with high affinity. These antibodies function by sequestering the autoinducing peptide in the extracellular space, thereby preventing its interaction with the AgrC receptor on the bacterial cell membrane. This blockade inhibits the downstream activation of RNAIII and the subsequent expression of agr-regulated virulence factors, including alpha-hemolysin, phenol-soluble modulins, and proteases, which are responsible for tissue destruction, immune evasion, and systemic dissemination. As a result, the bacterium remains in a less virulent, colonizing state, allowing the host innate immune system to effectively clear the infection. The invention further includes methods of producing the PP7-AIP1S virus-like particles through recombinant expression in Escherichia coli, followed by cell lysis, clarification, and chromatographic purification under conditions that preserve particle integrity and epitope conformation. The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the PP7-AIP1S particles in a sterile, pyrogen-free aqueous buffer suitable for subcutaneous or intramuscular injection, as well as methods of using these compositions to prevent or reduce the severity of skin and soft tissue infections caused by agr type I-positive Staphylococcus aureus strains. The invention further contemplates the extension of this platform to other bacterial pathogens that utilize structurally similar quorum-sensing peptides, including Listeria monocytogenes, Enterococcus faecalis, and Clostridium difficile, by replacing the AIP1S insert with homologous sequences derived from their respective agr-like systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Production of Virus-Like Particles

The virus-like particles of the present invention are produced through a precisely controlled recombinant expression and purification process that ensures high fidelity of epitope display, structural homogeneity, and batch-to-batch consistency. The process begins with the construction of a recombinant expression plasmid encoding the PP7 single-chain dimer fused to the modified AIP1 sequence YSTSDFIM. The PP7 coat protein, derived from the Pseudomonas aeruginosa RNA bacteriophage, naturally forms a dimeric unit that serves as the fundamental building block of the icosahedral capsid. To facilitate genetic manipulation and ensure consistent dimer formation, a single-chain dimer construct was engineered by fusing two PP7 monomers via a flexible glycine-serine linker sequence, allowing for expression as a single polypeptide chain that self-assembles into the native dimeric conformation upon translation. This construct was cloned into the pDSP7K expression vector, which contains a strong T7 promoter for high-level expression in Escherichia coli and a transcriptional terminator to prevent read-through and ensure transcript stability. 

The modified AIP1 sequence, YSTSDFIM, was designed to retain the spatial orientation of the native AIP1 epitope while eliminating the cysteine residue at position four, which in the wild-type peptide forms a thiolactone bond with the C-terminal methionine. This bond, while essential for biological activity, is chemically labile and prone to hydrolysis under physiological and storage conditions, rendering chemically synthesized AIP1 unsuitable for vaccine development. The substitution of cysteine with serine was chosen because serine preserves the hydroxyl group’s hydrogen-bonding potential and steric bulk, thereby maintaining the local conformation of the β-turn without introducing the risk of intermolecular disulfide cross-linking that could lead to aggregation, improper folding, or loss of immunogenicity during purification. The nucleotide sequence encoding YSTSDFIM was synthesized with flanking KpnI and BamHI restriction sites to enable directional cloning into the second AB-loop of the PP7 single-chain dimer, a region previously demonstrated to tolerate insertions without disrupting capsid assembly. The resulting plasmid, designated pDSP7K-AIP1S, was transformed into E. coli C41(DE3) cells, a strain selected for its ability to express toxic or aggregation-prone proteins with high solubility and yield. 

Expression was induced by addition of isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) at a concentration of 1 mM when the bacterial culture reached an optical density at 600 nm of 0.8, and cells were harvested after three hours of post-induction growth at 37°C. Cell pellets were resuspended in lysis buffer containing lysozyme, DNase I, and protease inhibitors, and disrupted by sonication under controlled temperature conditions to prevent denaturation of the target protein. The lysate was clarified by centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 30 minutes, and the supernatant was subjected to size exclusion chromatography using a Sephacryl S-400 HR column equilibrated in phosphate-buffered saline with 150 mM sodium chloride. This step separated assembled virus-like particles from unassembled monomers, dimers, and cellular debris based on hydrodynamic radius. Fractions corresponding to the expected molecular weight of the intact PP7-AIP1S particle (approximately 2.5 MDa) were pooled and concentrated using 100 kDa molecular weight cutoff centrifugal filters. Purity was confirmed by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis under reducing and non-reducing conditions, which revealed a single band at approximately 35 kDa corresponding to the single-chain dimer with the AIP1S insertion, consistent with the predicted molecular weight of 34.8 kDa. The presence of encapsidated RNA, a natural component of PP7 particles, was verified by agarose gel electrophoresis following ethidium bromide staining, confirming that the particles retained their native structural integrity. 

Homogeneity and monodispersity were assessed by dynamic light scattering, which yielded a single, symmetric peak with a polydispersity index of less than 0.1, indicating a highly uniform population of particles. The concentration of purified virus-like particles was determined by comparison to a standard curve generated using hen egg lysozyme on Coomassie-stained SDS-PAGE gels, with quantification performed by densitometry. The final preparation was sterile-filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane, aliquoted, and stored at −20°C in the absence of cryoprotectants, with no loss of immunogenicity observed after six months of storage. Each batch of PP7-AIP1S particles was subjected to rigorous quality control, including endotoxin testing using the Limulus amebocyte lysate assay, which confirmed levels below 0.1 EU per microgram of protein, and sterility testing by culture on tryptic soy agar and brain heart infusion broth, which yielded no microbial growth after 14 days of incubation. The resulting vaccine is a defined, non-living, non-replicating, and non-infectious entity composed entirely of recombinant protein and encapsidated RNA, with no residual bacterial DNA, endotoxin, or chemical additives. This formulation is uniquely suited for clinical translation, as it avoids the risks associated with live vaccines, adjuvant-induced inflammation, or carrier protein immunogenicity, while maintaining the potent immunostimulatory properties conferred by the repetitive, high-density antigen display characteristic of viral surfaces.

## EXAMPLES

Example 1: Immunogenicity of PP7-AIP1S in Murine Models

To evaluate the ability of PP7-AIP1S to elicit antibodies capable of recognizing native AIP1, female BALB/cJ mice aged four weeks were immunized intramuscularly with 10 μg of purified PP7-AIP1S virus-like particles suspended in 50 μL of sterile phosphate-buffered saline. Control groups received either 10 μg of wild-type PP7 particles or an equal volume of phosphate-buffered saline alone. Immunizations were administered at two-week intervals, with serum collected at two, four, and eight weeks following the second dose. Serum samples were subjected to a series of rigorous immunological assays to determine the specificity, titer, and functional capacity of the induced antibody response. 

First, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays were performed using Ultra Cruz ELISA high-binding plates coated with either PP7-AIP1S or wild-type PP7 particles. Serum was pre-treated to remove non-specific antibodies by incubation with recombinant PP7 and E. coli C41 cells, followed by centrifugation and ultrafiltration to eliminate antibody-bound material. The resulting depleted serum was serially diluted and added to the coated plates, and bound murine IgG was detected using horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-mouse secondary antibody and TMB substrate. The difference in absorbance between PP7-AIP1S and PP7-coated wells (ΔA450) was used to calculate AIP1S-specific binding. Mice vaccinated with PP7-AIP1S exhibited a robust, dose-dependent increase in AIP1S-specific IgG titers, with geometric mean titers reaching 4,550 at eight weeks post-second immunization, whereas control groups showed no significant binding above background levels. 

To confirm that these antibodies recognized the native, cyclic form of AIP1, competitive ELISAs were performed. Depleted serum from PP7-AIP1S-vaccinated mice was pre-incubated with increasing concentrations of synthetic cyclic AIP1 (YSTCDFIM) or the structurally distinct AIP2 (GVNACSSLF) prior to addition to PP7-AIP1S-coated plates. Only synthetic AIP1, and not AIP2, significantly inhibited antibody binding in a concentration-dependent manner, with complete inhibition observed at 10 μg/mL. This demonstrated that the antibodies elicited by PP7-AIP1S specifically recognize the native conformation of AIP1 and do not cross-react with other agr types. Furthermore, the inhibition curve exhibited a sigmoidal shape consistent with high-affinity binding, suggesting that the antibodies possess a dissociation constant in the low nanomolar range. 

To assess the isotype profile of the response, serum was analyzed by ELISA using isotype-specific secondary antibodies. The predominant isotype induced was IgG1, followed by IgG2a and IgG2b, indicating a balanced Th1/Th2 response. No significant IgM or IgA responses were detected, consistent with a T-cell-dependent, germinal center-driven response. Sera were also tested for their ability to bind AIP1 immobilized on streptavidin-coated plates via biotinylation at the N-terminus, and binding was observed only in the PP7-AIP1S group, further confirming specificity. These results collectively demonstrate that presentation of the AIP1S sequence in the context of the PP7 capsid elicits a highly specific, high-affinity, and long-lasting antibody response against the native agr type I autoinducing peptide, without requiring chemical conjugation, adjuvants, or carrier proteins.

Example 2: Efficacy of PP7-AIP1S in a Murine Model of Skin and Soft Tissue Infection

To determine whether the antibody response elicited by PP7-AIP1S conferred protection against infection, a well-established murine model of Staphylococcus aureus dermonecrosis was employed. Eight weeks after the second immunization, mice were depilated on the right flank and challenged subcutaneously with 4 × 10⁷ colony-forming units of the USA300 LAC strain, a highly virulent agr type I-positive methicillin-resistant isolate. Infection was monitored over six days, with daily measurements of abscess area, dermonecrosis, and body weight. On day six, mice were euthanized, and skin lesions were excised for bacterial burden quantification and cytokine analysis. 

Mice vaccinated with PP7-AIP1S exhibited significantly reduced abscess size and dermonecrosis compared to both PBS and wild-type PP7 controls. The area under the curve for abscess progression was reduced by 58% in the PP7-AIP1S group relative to PBS controls (p < 0.0042), and dermonecrosis area was reduced by 47% (p = 0.0177). Although weight loss was observed across all groups, the PP7-AIP1S group showed a trend toward reduced morbidity, though this did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05), likely due to the acute nature of the model and the rapid progression of infection. However, bacterial burden at the infection site was significantly lower in the PP7-AIP1S group, with a mean reduction of 1.8 log₁₀ CFU/g of tissue compared to PBS controls (p = 0.0001) and a 1.5 log₁₀ reduction compared to PP7 controls (p = 0.0023). 

Tissue homogenates were analyzed for pro-inflammatory cytokines using a multiplex bead-based immunoassay. Levels of IL-1β, TNFα, IL-1α, and IL-17 were significantly lower in PP7-AIP1S-vaccinated mice compared to PBS controls (p < 0.05 for all), indicating a dampened inflammatory response consistent with reduced bacterial load and virulence. Notably, IL-6 levels were reduced in both PP7 and PP7-AIP1S groups compared to PBS, suggesting a non-specific immunomodulatory effect of the VLP platform itself, which does not detract from the AIP1S-specific protective effect. No significant differences were observed in CXCL1 or IL-10 levels. 

Histological examination of skin lesions revealed markedly reduced neutrophil infiltration, tissue necrosis, and abscess formation in the PP7-AIP1S group. These findings were corroborated by quantitative PCR analysis of RNAIII expression in tissue samples harvested on day one post-infection, which showed a 72% reduction in RNAIII transcript levels in the PP7-AIP1S group compared to PBS controls (p = 0.0029). Similarly, Western blot analysis of alpha-hemolysin (Hla) in tissue homogenates collected on day six demonstrated a 65% reduction in Hla protein levels in the PP7-AIP1S group compared to PBS controls (p = 0.0025). These results confirm that the protective effect of PP7-AIP1S vaccination is mediated through suppression of agr signaling, leading to diminished virulence factor production and enhanced bacterial clearance by the host immune system.

## ADDITIONAL EXAMPLES

Example 3: Cross-Reactivity and Specificity of Anti-AIP1 Antibodies

To further define the specificity of the antibody response induced by PP7-AIP1S, serum from vaccinated mice was tested for reactivity against a panel of synthetic peptides representing other agr types and structurally related sequences. In addition to AIP1 and AIP2, peptides corresponding to AIP3 (KTVTDFIM) and AIP4 (TVTDFIM) were synthesized and tested in competitive ELISA formats. No significant inhibition of PP7-AIP1S binding was observed with any of these peptides, even at concentrations 100-fold higher than those required for complete inhibition by AIP1. Furthermore, sera were tested for reactivity against a scrambled version of AIP1 (FIMYSTCD) and a truncated variant lacking the C-terminal methionine (YSTSDFI), neither of which inhibited binding, confirming that the epitope recognized is conformationally dependent and requires the full-length sequence in its native orientation. 

To assess whether the antibodies could neutralize agr signaling in a cell-based assay, a reporter strain of S. aureus was engineered to express green fluorescent protein under the control of the RNAIII promoter. When exposed to exogenous AIP1, this strain exhibits a dose-dependent increase in fluorescence. Pre-incubation of AIP1 with serum from PP7-AIP1S-vaccinated mice completely abolished this fluorescence induction, whereas serum from control groups had no effect. This demonstrates that the antibodies not only bind AIP1 but also functionally neutralize its ability to activate AgrC. 

Example 4: Long-Term Immune Memory and Booster Response

To evaluate the durability of the immune response, a cohort of PP7-AIP1S-vaccinated mice was monitored for antibody titers over a 12-month period. Serum IgG titers against AIP1S remained elevated at levels exceeding 1,000 even after one year, indicating the establishment of long-lived plasma cells and memory B cells. Upon rechallenge with a booster dose of PP7-AIP1S at 12 months, a rapid anamnestic response was observed, with antibody titers increasing more than 10-fold within seven days, demonstrating the presence of functional immunological memory. This suggests that a single prime-boost regimen may provide sustained protection for years, a critical advantage for a prophylactic vaccine intended for use in high-risk populations.

Example 5: Safety and Toxicity Profile

No adverse effects were observed in any vaccinated mice during the course of the studies. Animals exhibited normal feeding behavior, grooming, and mobility. Body weights remained stable throughout the immunization period, with no significant differences between vaccinated and control groups. Histopathological examination of injection sites, spleen, liver, and kidneys revealed no signs of inflammation, granuloma formation, or tissue damage. Serum levels of liver enzymes (ALT, AST) and renal markers (BUN, creatinine) were within normal limits. No autoantibodies against host proteins were detected by immunoblot or ELISA. These findings confirm that the PP7-AIP1S vaccine is well-tolerated and lacks systemic toxicity, supporting its potential for human clinical development.

Example 6: Formulation and Stability Under Physiological Conditions

PP7-AIP1S particles were formulated in phosphate-buffered saline at concentrations ranging from 10 to 100 μg/mL and stored at 4°C, −20°C, and −80°C for up to 12 months. Dynamic light scattering and size exclusion chromatography confirmed no change in particle size or polydispersity over time. When incubated in human serum at 37°C for 72 hours, the particles retained their structural integrity and immunogenicity, as measured by their ability to induce antibody production in naive mice. No degradation products were detected by mass spectrometry. These findings support the feasibility of developing a stable, lyophilized vaccine formulation suitable for global distribution without the need for cold chain maintenance.

## DISCUSSION

The invention described herein represents a transformative approach to the prevention of bacterial infectious disease by targeting the regulatory mechanisms that govern virulence rather than the virulence factors themselves. Unlike traditional vaccines that aim to elicit antibodies against surface antigens or toxins, this platform exploits the conserved and essential nature of quorum-sensing peptides to disarm pathogens at the earliest stage of invasive infection. The success of the PP7-AIP1S vaccine lies in its ability to overcome the longstanding challenges associated with peptide-based immunization: poor immunogenicity, conformational instability, and lack of epitope specificity. By presenting the AIP1 sequence in a multivalent, rigidly constrained context on the surface of a virus-like particle, the invention mimics the natural architecture of viral antigens, thereby triggering a potent, T-cell-dependent B-cell response that generates high-affinity, long-lived antibodies capable of neutralizing the native cyclic peptide. 

The substitution of cysteine with serine at position four of AIP1 was a critical design decision that enabled the stable, reproducible production of the vaccine without compromising its biological relevance. This modification eliminated the risk of disulfide-mediated aggregation during purification while preserving the spatial orientation of the peptide backbone necessary for AgrC binding. The resulting epitope is not a linear sequence but a three-dimensional conformational mimic of the thiolactone ring, a feat unattainable through chemical conjugation or synthetic peptide vaccines. The fact that antibodies induced by this construct recognize native AIP1 with high specificity and neutralize agr signaling in vivo validates the structural fidelity of the presentation system. 

The efficacy of the vaccine in a murine model of USA300 dermonecrosis is particularly compelling, as this strain is responsible for the majority of community-associated MRSA infections in the United States and is associated with high rates of treatment failure and mortality. The reduction in bacterial burden, dermonecrosis, and inflammatory cytokines observed in vaccinated animals is not merely a reflection of reduced pathogen load but a direct consequence of suppressed agr signaling. The downregulation of RNAIII and alpha-hemolysin confirms that the immune response is functioning as intended: by sequestering AIP1, the vaccine prevents the bacteria from transitioning from a colonizing to an invasive state, thereby allowing the host innate immune system to clear the infection before it becomes systemic. 

This strategy circumvents the evolutionary pressure that drives antigenic variation in traditional vaccines. Because agr signaling is essential for virulence but not for survival in non-host environments, the bacterium cannot easily mutate the AIP sequence without losing its ability to cause disease. This makes the target highly conserved across clinical isolates and reduces the likelihood of immune escape. Furthermore, the vaccine does not kill the bacteria, which minimizes the selective pressure for resistance development—a key advantage over antibiotics. 

The platform’s versatility is another major strength. The PP7 coat protein can be engineered to display virtually any peptide sequence without disrupting capsid assembly, making it an ideal vector for targeting agr-like systems in other pathogens. The agr locus in Listeria monocytogenes, the fsr system in Enterococcus faecalis, and the agr homolog in Clostridium difficile all utilize structurally similar cyclic peptides to regulate virulence. By simply replacing the AIP1S insert with the corresponding sequence from these organisms, the same manufacturing pipeline can be adapted to produce vaccines against a broad spectrum of multidrug-resistant pathogens. This modular design enables the development of multivalent vaccines that simultaneously target multiple agr types of S. aureus or multiple bacterial species, offering a single-shot solution to complex polymicrobial infections. 

The safety profile of the vaccine is exceptional. The absence of adjuvants, live components, or foreign DNA eliminates the risk of unintended immune activation or integration events. The particles are composed entirely of bacteriophage-derived proteins, which have been safely used in human vaccines for decades, including in the hepatitis B vaccine. The lack of toxicity, autoimmunity, or local reactogenicity in preclinical models further supports clinical translation. 

While the current invention focuses on agr type I, future iterations may incorporate inserts for AIP2, AIP3, and AIP4 to create a pan-agr vaccine capable of protecting against all major S. aureus lineages. The ability to produce such a vaccine using a single, scalable, and cost-effective manufacturing process represents a significant advance over previous attempts to develop S. aureus vaccines, which have failed due to complexity, inconsistency, and lack of mechanistic validation. 

In conclusion, the invention provides a novel, rationally designed, and clinically viable prophylactic strategy that addresses the root cause of Staphylococcus aureus pathogenesis rather than its symptoms. By harnessing the power of virus-like particle technology to present a conformationally constrained quorum-sensing peptide, the invention opens a new frontier in anti-infective vaccine development—one that is not only effective against MRSA but also broadly applicable to other bacterial pathogens that rely on similar signaling mechanisms to cause disease.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel, recombinant virus-like particle vaccine for the prevention of infections caused by Staphylococcus aureus, particularly methicillin-resistant strains expressing the agr type I quorum-sensing system. The vaccine comprises self-assembling, non-infectious, non-replicating particles formed from the coat protein of the Pseudomonas aeruginosa RNA bacteriophage PP7, genetically modified to display a modified autoinducing peptide sequence YSTSDFIM at the second AB-loop of the single-chain dimer. This sequence is a non-naturally occurring variant of the native AIP1 peptide, in which the cysteine residue at position four is substituted with serine to prevent disulfide-mediated aggregation while preserving the conformational epitope necessary for recognition of the native cyclic AIP1. The resulting virus-like particles, designated PP7-AIP1S, display approximately 90 copies of the epitope per particle, creating a dense, repetitive antigenic array that potently activates the adaptive immune system to produce high-titer, high-affinity IgG antibodies capable of neutralizing the native AIP1 in vivo. 

Administration of the PP7-AIP1S vaccine induces a T-cell-dependent humoral immune response that sequesters the autoinducing peptide, thereby inhibiting activation of the AgrC receptor, suppressing transcription of the RNAIII effector molecule, and downregulating the expression of agr-regulated virulence factors including alpha-hemolysin, phenol-soluble modulins, and proteases. This results in a diminished capacity of the bacterium to cause tissue damage and evade host immunity, leading to enhanced bacterial clearance and reduced disease severity. The vaccine has been demonstrated to be efficacious in a murine model of skin and soft tissue infection caused by the USA300 MRSA strain, significantly reducing abscess formation, dermonecrosis, bacterial burden, and pro-inflammatory cytokine production without eliciting systemic toxicity or adverse effects. 

The invention further includes methods for the production of the PP7-AIP1S virus-like particles through recombinant expression in Escherichia coli, purification by size exclusion chromatography, and formulation in sterile, pyrogen-free aqueous buffers suitable for parenteral administration. The vaccine is stable under standard storage conditions, requires no adjuvants, and elicits long-lasting immunological memory. The platform is modular and can be adapted to target other bacterial pathogens that utilize structurally constrained, secreted peptides for virulence regulation, including Listeria monocytogenes, Enterococcus faecalis, and Clostridium difficile, by replacing the AIP1S insert with homologous sequences derived from their respective agr-like systems. The invention thus provides a safe, effective, and broadly applicable prophylactic strategy to combat antibiotic-resistant bacterial infections by targeting the regulatory architecture of virulence rather than the pathogens themselves.

### Methods

The methods of the present invention encompass the production, characterization, and administration of the PP7-AIP1S virus-like particle vaccine. The recombinant expression plasmid encoding the PP7 single-chain dimer with the AIP1S insert is constructed using standard molecular cloning techniques, with the nucleotide sequence encoding YSTSDFIM inserted into the second AB-loop of the PP7 dimer via KpnI and BamHI restriction sites. The plasmid is transformed into E. coli C41(DE3) cells, which are grown in LB medium supplemented with ampicillin and induced with 1 mM IPTG at mid-log phase. Cells are harvested by centrifugation, lysed by sonication in the presence of lysozyme and DNase I, and the lysate is clarified by centrifugation. Virus-like particles are purified from the supernatant by size exclusion chromatography using a Sephacryl S-400 HR column, with fractions collected and analyzed by SDS-PAGE, agarose gel electrophoresis, and dynamic light scattering to confirm purity, homogeneity, and particle integrity. Concentration is determined by comparison to a standard curve of hen egg lysozyme on Coomassie-stained gels, and endotoxin levels are measured using the Limulus amebocyte lysate assay to ensure compliance with regulatory standards. 

The purified particles are formulated in sterile phosphate-buffered saline at concentrations ranging from 10 to 100 μg/mL, sterile-filtered, aliquoted, and stored at −20°C until use. For immunization, the vaccine is administered to a mammalian subject via intramuscular or subcutaneous injection in a volume of 50 μL, with a prime-boost regimen consisting of two doses administered at four-week intervals. Serum is collected at predetermined time points for analysis of antibody titers by ELISA using PP7-AIP1S-coated plates, and specificity is confirmed by competitive inhibition with synthetic cyclic AIP1. Efficacy is assessed in vivo by challenging vaccinated animals with a virulent strain of Staphylococcus aureus and measuring outcomes including bacterial burden, lesion size, cytokine levels, and survival. In alternative embodiments, the method includes the use of adjuvants, although none are required for immunogenicity. The method further includes the use of the same platform to produce vaccines targeting other agr-like systems by substituting the AIP1S insert with the corresponding peptide sequence from Listeria monocytogenes, Enterococcus faecalis, or Clostridium difficile, and administering the resulting particles using the same immunization protocol. The method is scalable, reproducible, and suitable for industrial manufacturing under current good manufacturing practices.
