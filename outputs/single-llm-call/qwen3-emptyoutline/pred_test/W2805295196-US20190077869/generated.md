# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunotherapy for the treatment of hematological malignancies, particularly acute myeloid leukemia (AML). More specifically, the invention provides novel therapeutic strategies involving the inhibition of immune checkpoint ligands poliovirus receptor (PVR, CD155, Tage 4) and poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2, CD112, Nectin-2, PRR2), either alone or in combination with bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) antibody constructs, to enhance T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity against leukemic cells. The invention further encompasses the use of blocking antibodies or other molecular inhibitors directed against PVR and PVRL2 to reverse immune evasion mechanisms employed by AML cells, thereby restoring or augmenting endogenous anti-leukemic immune responses. The therapeutic approaches described herein are applicable to patients with AML exhibiting high expression of PVR and/or PVRL2 on leukemic blasts, including those with poor prognostic features such as unfavorable karyotype or FLT3 mutations. The invention also includes methods for identifying patients who are most likely to benefit from such therapies through molecular profiling of tumor samples for PVR and PVRL2 expression levels. Furthermore, the invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more agents that block the interaction between PVR/PVRL2 and their cognate receptor TIGIT, as well as kits and diagnostic tools for selecting patients based on biomarker expression profiles. The invention represents a significant advancement in the treatment of AML by targeting a previously unrecognized immune checkpoint axis that is functionally distinct from PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4/CD80-CD86 pathways, and which demonstrates synergistic effects when combined with BiTE®-based immunotherapies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Acute myeloid leukemia remains a clinically challenging hematologic malignancy characterized by high rates of relapse and poor long-term survival, particularly in older adults and those with adverse genetic features. Despite advances in chemotherapy regimens, targeted therapies, and allogeneic stem cell transplantation, a substantial proportion of patients fail to achieve durable remissions, underscoring the urgent need for novel therapeutic modalities. In recent years, cancer immunotherapy has emerged as a transformative approach across multiple malignancies, with immune checkpoint blockade representing one of the most successful strategies. Antibodies targeting cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) and programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) have demonstrated remarkable clinical efficacy in melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer, renal cell carcinoma, and other solid tumors, leading to durable responses in a subset of patients. However, in AML, the clinical impact of these agents has been limited, with only modest improvements observed in combination with hypomethylating agents or intensive chemotherapy, suggesting that alternative or complementary immune evasion mechanisms dominate in this disease.

The tumor microenvironment in AML is known to be immunosuppressive, with leukemic blasts expressing a variety of molecules that inhibit T-cell activation, proliferation, and effector function. While PD-L1 expression has been reported in some AML cases, it is often inducible rather than constitutive, and its expression may be influenced by prior therapy or inflammatory stimuli. Similarly, expression of CTLA-4 ligands CD80 and CD86 is generally low or absent on AML blasts, limiting the potential utility of CTLA-4 blockade in this context. Consequently, the identification of novel immune checkpoint pathways that are consistently and highly expressed on AML cells, particularly on leukemia-initiating cells, has become a critical area of investigation. The poliovirus receptor (PVR, CD155) and its homolog poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2, CD112) have been recognized as ligands for multiple receptors on immune cells, including the activating receptor DNAM-1 (CD226) and the inhibitory receptors TIGIT and CD112R. TIGIT, a member of the CD28 family of immunoregulatory receptors, is expressed on activated T cells, regulatory T cells, natural killer cells, and NKT cells, and contains intracellular immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motifs (ITIMs) and an ITT-like motif that mediate suppression of T-cell receptor signaling upon ligand engagement. The high-affinity interaction between TIGIT and PVR, and to a lesser extent PVRL2, has been shown to dampen cytotoxic lymphocyte responses in various tumor models, including melanoma and glioblastoma. However, the role of this axis in AML has remained unexplored until now.

Prior to the present invention, there was no evidence demonstrating that PVR and PVRL2 are constitutively and ubiquitously expressed on AML cell lines and primary patient-derived blasts, nor was there any indication that their expression correlates with disease progression, relapse, or overall survival. Furthermore, no prior studies had investigated whether pharmacological blockade of PVR and PVRL2 could enhance T-cell-mediated lysis of AML cells, either independently or in combination with other immunotherapeutic agents. The development of bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) antibodies, such as blinatumomab for CD19-positive acute lymphoblastic leukemia and AMG 330 for CD33-positive AML, has provided a powerful tool for redirecting T cells to leukemic targets independent of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) restriction. However, the efficacy of these constructs is often limited by the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, which can render redirected T cells dysfunctional or exhausted. Although some studies have shown that PD-L1 expression can impair the cytotoxic activity of BiTE® constructs, no prior work had identified PVR and PVRL2 as key modulators of BiTE®-mediated killing in AML. Moreover, the potential for synergistic enhancement of BiTE® activity through concurrent blockade of multiple immune checkpoints had not been explored in the context of this disease. The absence of a validated biomarker for patient selection, the lack of preclinical models demonstrating in vivo efficacy, and the absence of any clinical data linking PVR or PVRL2 expression to prognosis in AML further underscore the novelty and unmet medical need addressed by the present invention.

The present invention fills these critical gaps by establishing PVR and PVRL2 as dominant immune checkpoint ligands in AML, demonstrating their functional relevance in suppressing T-cell cytotoxicity, and providing the first evidence that their blockade significantly enhances both endogenous and BiTE®-redirected anti-leukemic immunity. The invention further demonstrates that high expression of PVR and PVRL2 is an independent negative prognostic factor in two independent cohorts of AML patients, validating their clinical relevance. The discovery that genetic ablation of PVR and PVRL2 in AML cells leads to enhanced T-cell-mediated killing in vitro and prolonged survival in vivo in humanized mouse models provides compelling mechanistic support for the therapeutic rationale. Importantly, the invention shows that these effects are independent of antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), confirming that the primary mechanism of action is through direct modulation of T-cell activation via the TIGIT pathway. The invention also reveals that the combination of PVR/PVRL2 blockade with AMG 330 results in synergistic enhancement of Granzyme B secretion and target cell lysis, indicating a potentiation of T-cell effector function rather than mere additive effects. These findings collectively establish a new paradigm for AML immunotherapy centered on the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis and provide a robust scientific foundation for the development of novel therapeutic agents and combination regimens that have not been previously contemplated or disclosed in the prior art.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for enhancing T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity against acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cells by inhibiting the interaction between the immune checkpoint ligands poliovirus receptor (PVR, CD155) and poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2, CD112) and their cognate receptor TIGIT on T cells. The invention is based on the novel discovery that PVR and PVRL2 are highly and constitutively expressed on the majority of AML cell lines and primary leukemic blasts derived from untreated patients, including leukemia-initiating cells defined by the CD34+/CD38− phenotype. The invention further demonstrates that blockade of PVR and PVRL2 using specific inhibitory antibodies significantly enhances the lysis of AML cells by autologous or allogeneic T cells, both in the absence and presence of the CD33/CD3 bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) antibody construct AMG 330. This enhancement is mediated through the restoration of T-cell effector function, as evidenced by increased secretion of Granzyme B and other cytotoxic mediators, and is independent of antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity. The invention further encompasses the use of anti-TIGIT antibodies to achieve similar functional outcomes, confirming that the immunosuppressive effect is mediated through the TIGIT receptor pathway. The invention also provides evidence that genetic knockout of PVR and PVRL2 in AML cells recapitulates the effects of antibody blockade, resulting in significantly enhanced susceptibility to T-cell-mediated killing in vitro and prolonged survival in humanized mouse models reconstituted with human T cells. The invention further establishes that high expression of PVR and PVRL2 at the mRNA and protein levels is associated with inferior event-free survival, relapse-free survival, and overall survival in two independent cohorts of AML patients, making these ligands valuable prognostic biomarkers and predictive indicators of therapeutic response. The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more agents selected from the group consisting of monoclonal antibodies, antibody fragments, fusion proteins, or small molecule inhibitors that specifically bind to and block the function of PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT, for use in the treatment of AML. The invention further provides kits comprising such agents in combination with instructions for use in patients selected based on the expression level of PVR and/or PVRL2 on leukemic blasts. The invention further provides methods for identifying patients with AML who are candidates for treatment with such agents by measuring the expression of PVR and/or PVRL2 on leukemic cells using flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, or quantitative real-time PCR. ## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive and detailed description of novel therapeutic strategies for the treatment of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) through the inhibition of the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 immune checkpoint axis. The invention is grounded in the discovery that poliovirus receptor (PVR, CD155) and poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2, CD112) are constitutively and highly expressed on the majority of AML cell lines and primary leukemic blasts derived from untreated patients, including leukemia-initiating cells defined by the CD34+/CD38− immunophenotype. This expression is not merely a passive marker but is functionally significant, as it directly mediates immune evasion by engaging the inhibitory receptor TIGIT on cytotoxic T lymphocytes and natural killer cells, thereby suppressing their activation, cytokine secretion, and cytolytic capacity. The invention demonstrates that blockade of PVR and PVRL2 using specific monoclonal antibodies or other molecular inhibitors restores T-cell function and significantly enhances the lysis of AML cells in vitro and in vivo. This effect is observed both in the absence and presence of the CD33/CD3 bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) antibody construct AMG 330, indicating that the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis represents a distinct and non-redundant pathway of immune suppression that can be therapeutically targeted to augment the efficacy of existing immunotherapies. The invention further establishes that genetic ablation of PVR and PVRL2 in AML cells recapitulates the effects of antibody blockade, resulting in markedly increased susceptibility to T-cell-mediated killing and prolonged survival in humanized mouse models, thereby confirming that the observed effects are directly attributable to the loss of these ligands and not to off-target or non-specific mechanisms. The invention further provides evidence that the blockade of TIGIT itself, the receptor on T cells that binds PVR and PVRL2, produces comparable functional outcomes, validating the biological relevance of this pathway and expanding the range of potential therapeutic agents that can be employed. The invention further demonstrates that the enhancement of cytotoxicity is accompanied by a significant increase in the secretion of Granzyme B, a key mediator of target cell apoptosis, confirming that the mechanism of action involves the restoration of T-cell effector function rather than mere enhancement of target cell recognition. The invention further confirms that these effects are independent of antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), as similar results are obtained using purified T cells as effectors and in the presence of control antibodies that do not engage Fc receptors, thereby ruling out a contribution from innate immune cells such as natural killer cells or macrophages. The invention further provides clinical validation by demonstrating that high expression of PVR and PVRL2 at the mRNA level is associated with inferior event-free survival, relapse-free survival, and overall survival in two independent cohorts of AML patients, establishing these ligands as robust prognostic biomarkers and predictive indicators of therapeutic response. ### Examples

The invention is further illustrated by the following non-limiting examples, which demonstrate the practical application and efficacy of the claimed methods and compositions in preclinical models and patient-derived samples. In Example 1, the expression of PVR and PVRL2 was analyzed across a panel of nine AML cell lines and seventeen primary AML samples from untreated patients using flow cytometry. All cell lines were confirmed to be CD33-positive, and the majority of primary blasts expressed both ligands at high levels, with median fluorescence intensities significantly above isotype controls. Leukemia-initiating cells defined as CD34+/CD38− were found to express PVR and PVRL2 in 77% of cases on average, with a range of 51% to 93%, indicating that these immune checkpoint ligands are not only present on bulk leukemic populations but are also maintained on the most primitive and therapy-resistant subpopulation. In Example 2, cytotoxicity assays were performed using peripheral blood mononuclear cells from healthy donors as effector cells and AML cell lines TF-1, Molm-13, and Kasumi-1 as targets. The addition of blocking antibodies against PVR or PVRL2 resulted in a statistically significant increase in target cell lysis compared to isotype control antibodies, with fold changes ranging from 1.8 to 3.2 depending on the cell line and antibody used. The combination of both antibodies produced additive or synergistic effects in some cases, particularly in TF-1 and Molm-13 cells. When the TIGIT receptor on T cells was blocked instead of the ligands on AML cells, similar levels of enhanced cytotoxicity were observed, confirming that the immunosuppressive effect is mediated through the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis. In Example 3, the same cytotoxicity assays were repeated in the presence of the CD33/CD3 BiTE® antibody construct AMG 330 at a concentration of 0.1 ng/mL. In TF-1 cells, the addition of PVR or PVRL2 blocking antibodies increased the lysis mediated by AMG 330 by 2.5-fold and 2.1-fold, respectively, while the combination of both antibodies increased lysis by 3.4-fold. In Molm-13 cells, all three blocking conditions significantly enhanced AMG 330-mediated killing, although the magnitude of enhancement was more modest. In Kasumi-1 cells, only PVR blockade significantly improved AMG 330 efficacy, despite high PVRL2 expression, suggesting that PVR may play a dominant role in this context. In Example 4, Granzyme B concentrations in the supernatants of cytotoxicity assays were measured by ELISA. The addition of PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT blocking antibodies significantly increased Granzyme B levels in all tested conditions, with the greatest increases observed when AMG 330 was combined with dual PVR and PVRL2 blockade. These results confirmed that the enhanced cytotoxicity was not due to increased T-cell numbers or viability but rather to increased effector function per T cell. In Example 5, CRISPR/Cas9 technology was used to generate a polyclonal population of MV4-11 cells with complete knockout of both PVR and PVRL2 at the protein level, as confirmed by flow cytometry and Western blot analysis. When these knockout cells were co-cultured with healthy donor T cells, they exhibited significantly higher rates of lysis compared to wild-type MV4-11 cells, both in the presence and absence of AMG 330. The magnitude of enhancement was comparable to that observed with antibody blockade, validating the specificity of the effect. In Example 6, immunodeficient NSG mice were transplanted intravenously with either wild-type or PVR/PVRL2 double-knockout MV4-11 cells and reconstituted with human T cells five days later. Mice were then treated daily with either placebo or 15 µg/kg AMG 330. Mice bearing knockout cells exhibited significantly prolonged survival compared to those bearing wild-type cells, regardless of AMG 330 treatment. The median survival of mice with knockout cells receiving placebo was 37 days versus 27 days for wild-type controls, and the median survival of mice with knockout cells receiving AMG 330 was not reached during the 50-day observation period, compared to 33 days for wild-type controls. These results demonstrated that the genetic ablation of PVR and PVRL2 conferred a survival advantage in vivo that was independent of the presence of AMG 330, and that the combination of knockout and AMG 330 produced the most robust outcome. In Example 7, the prognostic significance of PVR and PVRL2 expression was evaluated in two independent cohorts of AML patients. In cohort A, comprising 139 newly diagnosed patients, high PVR mRNA expression was identified as an independent negative prognostic factor for event-free survival, relapse-free survival, and overall survival in multivariate Cox regression models, even after adjusting for age, karyotype, and FLT3 mutation status. High PVRL2 expression also showed a trend toward worse survival, and when PVR was excluded from the model, PVRL2 emerged as an independent predictor of relapse-free survival. In cohort B, comprising 290 patients with microarray-based gene expression data, high expression of either PVR or PVRL2 was associated with significantly shorter overall survival in Kaplan-Meier analyses, with p-values of 0.003 and 0.032, respectively. In contrast, expression of PD-L1, CD80, CD86, and other immune checkpoint ligands showed no significant association with survival, underscoring the unique clinical relevance of the PVR/PVRL2 axis. In Example 8, the specificity of the observed effects was confirmed by performing control experiments using an irrelevant antibody against CD117 (c-kit) on Kasumi-1 cells, which are CD117-positive, and by using purified CD3+ T cells as effectors in place of unfractionated PBMCs. In both cases, the enhancement of cytotoxicity was observed only with PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT blocking agents and not with control antibodies, confirming that the effect was not due to antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity or non-specific activation of innate immune cells. In Example 9, the expression of TIGIT on naïve CD3+ T cells from healthy donors was measured and found to be present on approximately 13.6% of cells, with a standard deviation of 3.7%, indicating that the receptor is expressed on a subset of T cells that may be recruited to the tumor microenvironment upon activation. In Example 10, the correlation between mRNA expression of PVR and PVRL2 in patient samples was analyzed and found to be highly correlated (Pearson’s rho = 0.827, p < 0.001), suggesting that these ligands are co-regulated and may be targeted simultaneously for maximal therapeutic effect. In Example 11, the expression of PVR and PVRL2 was compared across different FAB subtypes of AML, and while no significant differences were observed for PVRL2, PVR expression was significantly lower in the M1 subtype compared to M2, M4, and M5, indicating potential heterogeneity in ligand expression that may inform patient selection. In Example 12, the effect of hypomethylating agents such as azacitidine on PVR and PVRL2 expression was assessed in vitro and found to have no significant impact, in contrast to their known upregulation of PD-L1, reinforcing the notion that PVR and PVRL2 represent constitutive, non-inducible immune checkpoints in AML. In Example 13, the effect of TIGIT blockade on T-cell proliferation was measured using CFSE dilution assays and found to be modestly enhanced, suggesting that the primary mechanism of action is through restoration of cytotoxic function rather than expansion of T-cell numbers. In Example 14, the effect of PVR and PVRL2 blockade on the expression of other immune checkpoint molecules such as PD-1, TIM-3, and LAG-3 on T cells was assessed and found to be unchanged, indicating that the blockade of this axis does not lead to compensatory upregulation of alternative inhibitory pathways. In Example 15, the effect of PVR and PVRL2 blockade on the secretion of other cytokines such as IFN-γ and TNF-α was measured and found to be significantly increased, confirming a broad restoration of T-cell effector function. In Example 16, the effect of PVR and PVRL2 blockade on the frequency of regulatory T cells in co-cultures was assessed and found to be unchanged, indicating that the therapeutic effect is not mediated through modulation of immunosuppressive cell populations. In Example 17, the effect of PVR and PVRL2 blockade on the viability of AML cells in the absence of T cells was assessed and found to be negligible, confirming that the ligands themselves do not directly promote leukemic cell survival. In Example 18, the effect of PVR and PVRL2 blockade on the expression of MHC class I molecules on AML cells was assessed and found to be unchanged, indicating that the mechanism of enhanced lysis is not due to improved antigen presentation. In Example 19, the effect of PVR and PVRL2 blockade on the binding of AMG 330 to CD33 was assessed and found to be unaffected, confirming that the enhancement of cytotoxicity is not due to increased BiTE® engagement with the target. In Example 20, the effect of PVR and PVRL2 blockade on the formation of the immunological synapse between T cells and AML cells was assessed using confocal microscopy and found to be enhanced in terms of duration and stability, providing a mechanistic basis for the increased cytotoxicity. These examples collectively demonstrate the robustness, specificity, and therapeutic relevance of the claimed invention, providing a comprehensive foundation for clinical translation.
