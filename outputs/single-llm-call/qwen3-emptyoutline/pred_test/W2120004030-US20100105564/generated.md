# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and personalized oncology, specifically to the identification, characterization, and clinical application of gene expression signatures derived from morphologically normal breast epithelium and stroma. The invention provides novel nucleic acid and polypeptide-based compositions, diagnostic methods, and therapeutic selection tools grounded in the discovery that gene expression profiles of histologically normal breast tissues—whether derived from patients with invasive breast carcinoma or from healthy individuals undergoing reduction mammoplasty—are molecularly indistinguishable and do not reflect the clinical or pathological characteristics of the associated tumor. This finding enables the development of reference signatures for normal breast tissue that can be used to interpret tumor gene expression data with unprecedented precision, identify biologically relevant cancer subtypes, and distinguish between tumor-intrinsic and microenvironment-driven expression patterns. The invention further encompasses methods for classifying breast carcinomas based on their similarity to these normal tissue signatures, thereby enabling the prediction of clinical behavior, prognosis, and response to therapy in ways not previously possible. These compositions and methods are applicable across diverse clinical settings, including diagnostic laboratories, research institutions, and personalized medicine platforms, and are particularly suited for integration into high-throughput genomic platforms such as microarrays, next-generation sequencing, quantitative PCR, and digital spatial profiling systems.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Breast cancer remains one of the most prevalent and lethal malignancies affecting women worldwide, with approximately one in eight women developing invasive disease during their lifetime. Despite advances in early detection, surgical intervention, and systemic therapies, a significant proportion of patients with early-stage disease—up to 26%—will ultimately develop metastatic recurrence and succumb to their illness. Current clinical prognostic tools, including tumor size, lymph node status, histological grade, and hormone receptor expression, provide only partial predictive power and fail to accurately stratify patients into distinct risk categories. Molecular profiling technologies, particularly gene expression microarrays, have been widely adopted in an effort to uncover more robust biomarkers capable of predicting clinical outcomes. Several gene expression signatures have been proposed to classify breast cancer into intrinsic subtypes, including luminal A, luminal B, HER2-enriched, basal-like, and normal-like, each associated with distinct clinical behaviors and therapeutic responses. However, many of these signatures were derived from whole-tissue analyses that contain complex mixtures of tumor cells, stromal fibroblasts, immune infiltrates, endothelial cells, and adipocytes, making it difficult to determine whether observed expression patterns originate from the neoplastic epithelium or from the surrounding microenvironment.

Historically, the assumption has been that morphologically normal tissue adjacent to a tumor represents a passive bystander, unaffected by the presence of malignancy. This assumption has underpinned the design of many diagnostic assays, which treat adjacent normal tissue as a neutral reference. However, emerging evidence suggests that even histologically normal cells may harbor molecular alterations, including loss of heterozygosity, epigenetic modifications, and subtle shifts in gene expression, potentially reflecting field cancerization or early neoplastic transformation. These observations have fueled speculation that gene expression patterns in normal breast tissue might serve as predictive biomarkers for tumor behavior, risk of recurrence, or even predisposition to cancer development. Prior studies attempting to address this question have been limited by small sample sizes, lack of cell-type specificity, and the use of heterogeneous tissue samples that fail to distinguish epithelial from stromal contributions. Moreover, most prior gene expression studies have relied on bulk tissue analysis, which obscures the distinct transcriptional programs of individual cellular compartments and introduces confounding signals from non-neoplastic elements.

Laser capture microdissection (LCM) has emerged as a powerful tool for isolating pure populations of epithelial and stromal cells from complex tissue architectures, enabling the generation of cell-type-specific gene expression profiles. Previous efforts using LCM to compare normal epithelium adjacent to tumor with epithelium from reduction mammoplasty specimens reported no significant differences in gene expression, but these studies were limited to small cohorts and did not analyze the stromal compartment. Consequently, a comprehensive understanding of the molecular landscape of normal breast tissue—particularly the stroma, which constitutes more than 80% of the breast’s volume—remained elusive. The stroma, composed of fibroblasts, extracellular matrix components, immune cells, and vasculature, plays an active role in maintaining tissue homeostasis and has been implicated in tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis. Yet, no prior study had systematically characterized the transcriptome of morphologically normal stroma from a large cohort of patients, nor had any study tested whether such normal stromal profiles could be used to interpret tumor expression data in a clinically meaningful way.

Furthermore, prior attempts to correlate gene expression in normal tissues with clinical outcomes have yielded inconsistent results, often due to methodological inconsistencies, inadequate statistical power, or failure to account for inter-individual variation. Many published gene signatures for breast cancer prognosis were derived from datasets that did not account for the cellular origin of the expressed transcripts, leading to the misattribution of stromal signals as epithelial tumor signatures, or vice versa. For instance, signatures associated with wound healing, fibroblast activation, or angiogenesis—commonly found in tumor stroma—have been erroneously interpreted as tumor-intrinsic biomarkers of poor prognosis, despite their origin in non-neoplastic cells. This has led to the development of prognostic models that are not only unreliable but also biologically misleading, as they conflate microenvironmental responses with tumor cell behavior.

The need for a definitive, cell-type-specific reference framework for normal breast tissue has therefore become urgent. Such a framework would not only clarify the origins of expression signals observed in tumor samples but also enable the accurate identification of true tumor-specific alterations, distinguish between reactive and intrinsic stromal changes, and uncover novel biological subtypes of breast cancer that are defined by their resemblance to normal epithelial or stromal programs. The present invention fulfills this critical unmet need by providing a comprehensive, validated, and clinically actionable set of gene expression signatures derived from morphologically normal epithelium and stroma, demonstrating their molecular homogeneity across patient populations, and establishing their utility in interpreting tumor gene expression data to improve diagnostic accuracy, prognostic stratification, and therapeutic decision-making.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and transformative approach to the diagnosis, classification, and management of breast cancer through the identification and application of gene expression signatures derived from morphologically normal breast epithelium and stroma. Contrary to prevailing assumptions, the invention demonstrates that gene expression profiles of histologically normal epithelial and stromal cells isolated from patients with invasive breast carcinoma are indistinguishable from those of epithelial and stromal cells isolated from healthy individuals undergoing reduction mammoplasty. This finding establishes that morphologically normal breast tissue, regardless of its proximity to a tumor, does not harbor a cancer-associated gene expression signature detectable at the whole-transcriptome level. The invention further reveals that variations in gene expression within normal tissues are primarily attributable to differences in tissue type (epithelium versus stroma) and inter-individual variability, rather than to clinical characteristics of the associated tumor, such as estrogen receptor status, lymph node involvement, tumor size, grade, or recurrence.

Based on these discoveries, the invention provides a set of reference gene expression signatures that define the molecular identity of normal breast epithelium and stroma. These signatures comprise a panel of genes that are consistently and differentially expressed in epithelial versus stromal compartments, including known markers such as KIT, CDH1, and KRT5 in epithelium, and COL1A1, VWF, CD31, and FN1 in stroma, as well as novel transcripts not previously associated with normal breast tissue biology. These signatures are not predictive of tumor characteristics but serve as a foundational reference for interpreting gene expression profiles of breast carcinomas. When applied to tumor datasets, the invention enables the identification of distinct molecular subtypes of breast cancer based on their similarity to normal tissue programs. Specifically, a subset of breast tumors exhibits a gene expression profile highly enriched for epithelium-specific genes and is molecularly analogous to normal ductal epithelium; these tumors are consistently ER-negative, PR-negative, and HER2-negative, and correspond to the basal-like and normal-like subtypes associated with poor clinical outcomes. In contrast, other tumor subtypes exhibit enrichment for stroma-specific genes, reflecting a microenvironmental influence rather than tumor cell identity.

The invention further provides nucleic acid compositions comprising oligonucleotides designed to detect and quantify the expression of genes within these normal tissue signatures, including primers, probes, and hybridization arrays optimized for use in clinical diagnostic platforms. These compositions are capable of distinguishing between epithelial and stromal gene expression patterns in tumor samples, thereby enabling the deconvolution of tumor-intrinsic signals from microenvironmental contributions. The invention also provides polypeptide binding compositions, including monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies, that specifically recognize proteins encoded by the identified epithelial and stromal signature genes, enabling immunohistochemical validation and spatial localization of these signatures in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections.

The invention further encompasses methods for diagnosing breast cancer and assigning or selecting treatment regimens based on the relative expression of epithelial and stromal genes within a tumor sample. By comparing the expression profile of a tumor to the reference signatures of normal epithelium and stroma, clinicians can determine whether a tumor’s molecular identity resembles that of normal epithelial cells, suggesting a tumor-intrinsic basal-like phenotype, or whether it exhibits a stromal gene expression signature, indicating a microenvironmentally driven phenotype. This distinction has profound implications for prognosis and therapy: tumors resembling normal epithelium are more likely to be aggressive, resistant to endocrine therapy, and responsive to chemotherapy, while tumors exhibiting stromal enrichment may benefit from anti-fibrotic or anti-angiogenic agents. The invention further provides methods for predicting recurrence risk, survival outcomes, and response to targeted therapies by integrating the normal tissue signatures into predictive algorithms that account for the cellular origin of gene expression signals.

Importantly, the invention eliminates the confounding influence of microenvironmental gene expression in tumor profiling by providing a validated reference framework that allows for the accurate attribution of expression signals to their cellular source. This enables the development of more precise, biologically grounded diagnostic tools that are not misled by reactive stromal changes, thereby improving the reliability of prognostic models and reducing the rate of false-positive or false-negative predictions. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in breast cancer molecular diagnostics, moving beyond the analysis of bulk tumor tissue to a cell-type-resolved understanding of tumor biology that is both more accurate and more therapeutically actionable.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Identifying Classes and Genes for Predicting Clinical Outcome

The invention is grounded in the systematic identification and validation of gene expression signatures that define the molecular identity of morphologically normal breast epithelium and stroma, and the subsequent demonstration that these signatures are not predictive of clinical outcomes associated with breast cancer. Through the use of laser capture microdissection and whole-genome microarray analysis, a cohort of 44 patients—including 34 with invasive ductal carcinoma and 10 undergoing reduction mammoplasty—was analyzed to generate high-resolution transcriptomic profiles from pure populations of epithelial and stromal cells. The epithelial compartment was defined by the presence of intact ductal structures with polarized epithelial cells, while the stromal compartment was defined as extralobular connective tissue containing fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and extracellular matrix components, with minimal epithelial contamination. Each sample was subjected to two rounds of T7-based linear RNA amplification to preserve relative mRNA abundance, followed by hybridization to Agilent whole-genome 44 K microarrays using a universal human reference RNA as a common reference. Duplicate hybridizations with reverse-dye labeling were performed for all samples to control for dye bias, and stringent quality control measures ensured that only arrays meeting predefined thresholds for signal intensity, signal-to-noise ratio, and replicate concordance were included in the analysis.

Class discovery analyses, including hierarchical clustering and multidimensional scaling, revealed that samples clustered exclusively by tissue type—epithelium versus stroma—and not by patient origin (cancer versus reduction), tumor subtype, or any clinical parameter. This clustering was robust across multiple distance metrics and was confirmed through 10,000 bootstrap iterations, demonstrating statistical significance with p-values below 0.01. The three outlier samples that initially appeared to miscluster were re-examined and confirmed to be technical artifacts resulting from minor epithelial contamination in stromal samples, a known limitation of microdissection that was accounted for in subsequent analyses. To identify the genes responsible for this tissue-specific clustering, class distinction analyses were performed using both linear models for microarray analysis (LIMMA) and significance analysis of microarrays (SAM), with stringent filtering criteria requiring a false discovery rate-adjusted p-value of ≤0.01, a fold change of ≥2.0, and an intensity above background (A > 6.0). This analysis yielded a set of 883 genes that were consistently differentially expressed between epithelium and stroma in at least 50% of individual patient pairs. These genes formed the core of the normal tissue signature and were used to re-cluster the entire dataset, achieving perfect classification of all samples into epithelial and stromal clusters, including the previously misclassified outliers.

To determine whether these gene expression patterns were associated with clinical characteristics, a series of class prediction analyses were performed using the prediction around medoids (PAM) algorithm, with cross-validation employed at every step to prevent overfitting. Predictive models were constructed for estrogen receptor status, progesterone receptor status, HER2 amplification, tumor grade, tumor size, lymph node status, menopausal status, age, and recurrence. None of the models achieved predictive accuracy beyond random chance, with average cross-validation error rates of approximately 50%. This result was consistent across multiple gene filtering thresholds and validation strategies, demonstrating that gene expression in morphologically normal breast tissue does not reflect, predict, or correlate with the clinical or pathological features of the associated tumor. Principal component analysis further confirmed that the primary sources of variation in the dataset were attributable to tissue type (explaining 3.98% of total variation) and inter-individual differences (explaining 3.58%), while the vast majority of variation (84.58%) was due to gene-specific expression differences within individual samples. These findings conclusively demonstrate that the molecular identity of normal breast tissue is determined by cellular lineage and individual genetic background, not by the presence or characteristics of a nearby malignancy.

### Cancers

The invention provides a novel framework for the molecular classification of breast cancers based on their similarity to normal epithelial and stromal gene expression signatures. When the normal tissue signature was applied to a large, independently derived dataset of 295 breast carcinomas from the Netherlands Cancer Institute, hierarchical clustering revealed two distinct clusters of tumors. The larger cluster, comprising 257 tumors, exhibited significant enrichment for stroma-specific genes and underrepresentation of epithelium-specific genes, consistent with a microenvironment-driven transcriptional profile. This cluster included tumors classified as luminal A, luminal B, and HER2-enriched subtypes, as defined by established gene expression classifiers. In contrast, the smaller cluster, comprising 38 tumors, was characterized by a striking enrichment for epithelium-specific genes and a near-complete absence of stroma-specific signals. These tumors were uniformly negative for estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2, and exhibited high expression of basal epithelial markers such as KRT5, KRT17, and GGH, aligning precisely with the molecular definition of basal-like and normal-like breast cancer subtypes. Notably, this cluster was only observed when clustering was performed using epithelium-specific genes; when stroma-specific genes were used, the cluster dissolved, and tumors co-clustered regardless of receptor status.

This finding demonstrates that a subset of breast cancers—specifically those with basal-like and normal-like phenotypes—retain a gene expression program that closely resembles that of normal ductal epithelium, suggesting that these tumors arise from or retain the transcriptional identity of normal epithelial cells. In contrast, other subtypes, including luminal and HER2-enriched tumors, exhibit gene expression patterns that reflect a dominant stromal influence, indicating that their molecular profile is shaped more by the surrounding microenvironment than by intrinsic epithelial characteristics. This distinction has profound implications for prognosis and therapy: tumors resembling normal epithelium are associated with poor survival outcomes, while those exhibiting stromal enrichment may be more responsive to therapies targeting the tumor microenvironment. The invention thus provides a method for classifying breast cancers not by receptor status alone, but by their underlying transcriptional architecture, enabling a more biologically accurate stratification of patients that correlates with clinical behavior.

### Nucleic Acid Compositions

The invention provides novel nucleic acid compositions comprising oligonucleotides designed to detect and quantify the expression of genes within the normal breast epithelium and stroma signatures. These compositions include primers, probes, and hybridization sequences optimized for use in a variety of molecular diagnostic platforms, including quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), microarrays, next-generation sequencing, and digital spatial transcriptomics. The nucleic acid compositions are designed to specifically hybridize to target sequences corresponding to the 883-gene normal tissue signature, with particular emphasis on the most discriminative epithelial and stromal markers. For epithelial-specific genes, the compositions include primers and probes targeting KRT5, KRT17, CDH1, EPCAM, KIT, CLDN3, and GGH, while for stromal-specific genes, the compositions include primers and probes targeting COL1A1, COL3A1, FN1, VWF, CD31, PDGFRB, and ACTA2. Each primer and probe is designed with a melting temperature between 58°C and 62°C, a GC content of 40–60%, and minimal secondary structure or dimer formation, ensuring high specificity and efficiency in multiplexed assays.

The nucleic acid compositions are provided in the form of pre-designed assay kits, each containing a panel of forward and reverse primers, TaqMan probes labeled with distinct fluorophores, or oligonucleotide probes for microarray hybridization. The kits are compatible with standard laboratory instrumentation and are optimized for use with as little as 1 ng of total RNA extracted from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections. The compositions are further engineered to distinguish between splice variants and isoforms of key genes, ensuring accurate quantification of biologically relevant transcripts. In addition, the compositions include internal control genes selected from housekeeping genes with stable expression across both epithelial and stromal compartments, such as RPLP0 and GAPDH, to normalize for RNA input and amplification efficiency. The nucleic acid compositions may be provided as lyophilized reagents, pre-aliquoted in multi-well plates, or integrated into automated diagnostic platforms for high-throughput clinical use.

### SDPP Genes and Nucleic Acids

The invention further encompasses a subset of genes identified as stroma-derived, disease-associated, patient-specific, and prognostic (SDPP) genes, which are differentially expressed in morphologically normal stroma and exhibit consistent patterns of variation across patient cohorts. These genes, while not predictive of tumor characteristics, serve as robust biomarkers of stromal identity and are essential for the accurate interpretation of tumor gene expression profiles. The SDPP genes include COL1A1, COL3A1, FN1, VWF, CD31, PDGFRB, ACTA2, THY1, and FAP, each of which is expressed at significantly higher levels in stroma compared to epithelium and exhibits low inter-individual variability. The nucleic acid sequences of these genes are provided in the invention as reference sequences for the design of detection assays, and their expression levels are used as a baseline for normalizing stromal signals in tumor samples. The invention further provides synthetic nucleic acid constructs encoding the full-length coding sequences of these SDPP genes, which are used as calibration standards in quantitative assays and as positive controls in diagnostic validation studies. These constructs are cloned into plasmid vectors with T7 or SP6 promoters for in vitro transcription, enabling the generation of standardized RNA transcripts for use in spike-in controls and assay calibration. The SDPP genes are also used to define a stromal index, a quantitative measure of stromal contribution to a tumor’s gene expression profile, which is calculated as the average expression level of the SDPP genes normalized to epithelial reference genes.

### Nucleic Acids, Primers and Probes

The invention provides a comprehensive set of nucleic acid primers and probes specifically designed to detect and quantify the expression of genes within the normal epithelial and stromal signatures. Each primer pair is designed to amplify a unique region of the target gene with a product length of 80–150 base pairs, ensuring compatibility with both qRT-PCR and digital PCR platforms. The probes are labeled with fluorescent dyes such as FAM, HEX, Cy5, or TAMRA, and are designed with a quencher moiety to enable real-time detection during amplification. The primers and probes are optimized for use in multiplexed assays, allowing for the simultaneous detection of up to 20 epithelial and 20 stromal targets in a single reaction. The invention further provides locked nucleic acid (LNA)-modified probes to enhance binding specificity and reduce off-target hybridization, particularly for genes with high sequence homology. The probes are also designed to span exon-exon junctions to avoid amplification of genomic DNA contamination, and include mismatched bases to discriminate between closely related isoforms. Each primer and probe set is validated for sensitivity, specificity, dynamic range, and reproducibility using a panel of 50 normal breast tissue samples, with coefficients of variation below 10% across replicate runs. The primers and probes are provided in a ready-to-use format, pre-mixed in master mixes optimized for reverse transcription and amplification, and are compatible with commercial platforms such as the Applied Biosystems QuantStudio, Bio-Rad CFX, and Fluidigm Biomark HD systems.

### Polypeptide Binding Compositions

The invention provides novel polypeptide binding compositions, including monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies, that specifically recognize proteins encoded by the genes comprising the normal epithelial and stromal signatures. These antibodies are generated by immunizing animals with recombinant peptides corresponding to unique epitopes of the target proteins, followed by hybridoma screening or phage display selection to isolate high-affinity, high-specificity binders. The polypeptide binding compositions include antibodies directed against KIT, CDH1, EPCAM, KRT5, KRT17, COL1A1, VWF, CD31, PDGFRB, and ACTA2, each of which has been validated for use in immunohistochemistry on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded breast tissue sections. The antibodies are provided in purified form at concentrations of 0.1–1.0 mg/mL, with each lot tested for cross-reactivity against non-target tissues and for batch-to-batch consistency. The invention further provides antibody pairs for use in double-labeling immunofluorescence assays, enabling the simultaneous visualization of epithelial and stromal markers in the same tissue section. The polypeptide binding compositions are also formulated for use in flow cytometry, Western blotting, and immunoprecipitation assays, expanding their utility beyond diagnostic histopathology. The antibodies are supplied with standardized protocols for antigen retrieval, blocking, incubation times, and detection systems, ensuring reproducible results across laboratories. The invention further includes antibody-conjugated beads for magnetic separation of epithelial and stromal cells from tissue digests, enabling the enrichment of specific cell populations for downstream molecular analysis.

### Microarrays

The invention provides a novel microarray platform comprising a high-density oligonucleotide array designed to interrogate the expression of the 883-gene normal tissue signature in a single assay. The microarray contains at least two distinct probes per gene, selected to represent unique exonic regions and avoid cross-hybridization with homologous sequences. The probes are synthesized in situ using photolithographic or inkjet printing technologies and are arranged in a 44 K format compatible with standard Agilent and Affymetrix hybridization systems. The microarray includes control probes for housekeeping genes, spike-in RNA controls, and negative controls to monitor background hybridization and technical variability. The platform is optimized for use with as little as 50 ng of amplified RNA and provides a dynamic range of five logs, enabling the detection of both high- and low-abundance transcripts. The microarray is accompanied by proprietary software for data normalization, quality control, and signature scoring, which calculates an epithelial index and a stromal index for each sample based on the average expression of the respective gene sets. The software also generates a tumor classification score, which quantifies the degree of similarity between a tumor’s expression profile and the normal epithelial or stromal signatures, enabling the automated classification of tumors as epithelial-like, stromal-like, or mixed. The microarray platform is validated for use in clinical laboratories and is compliant with CLIA and CAP standards for diagnostic testing. The invention further provides a version of the microarray with embedded barcodes for sample tracking and multiplexed processing, enabling high-throughput analysis of hundreds of samples per run.

### Methods of Diagnosis

The invention provides a method for diagnosing breast cancer by analyzing the gene expression profile of a tumor sample in relation to the reference signatures of normal epithelium and stroma. The method comprises the steps of obtaining a tissue sample from a patient suspected of having breast cancer, isolating RNA from the sample, quantifying the expression of the 883-gene normal tissue signature using a nucleic acid composition as described herein, and comparing the resulting expression profile to the reference signatures. The method further comprises calculating an epithelial index and a stromal index for the sample, where the epithelial index is the average expression level of epithelial-specific genes normalized to housekeeping genes, and the stromal index is the average expression level of stromal-specific genes normalized to the same reference. The method then classifies the tumor as epithelial-like if the epithelial index exceeds a predetermined threshold and the stromal index is below a second threshold, as stromal-like if the stromal index exceeds the second threshold and the epithelial index is below the first threshold, and as mixed if both indices fall within intermediate ranges. The method further comprises correlating the classification with clinical outcomes, wherein epithelial-like tumors are associated with a higher risk of recurrence and poor survival, while stromal-like tumors are associated with a more favorable prognosis and potential responsiveness to microenvironment-targeting therapies. The method may be performed on tissue obtained by core needle biopsy, excisional biopsy, or surgical resection, and is applicable to both fresh-frozen and formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded samples. The method is further adapted for use in liquid biopsy applications, wherein circulating tumor cells or cell-free RNA are captured and analyzed using the same signature to infer tumor subtype without the need for invasive tissue sampling.

### Methods of Assigning or Selecting Treatment

The invention provides a method for assigning or selecting treatment for a patient diagnosed with breast cancer based on the classification of the tumor according to its similarity to normal epithelial or stromal gene expression signatures. The method comprises the steps of performing the diagnostic method described above to classify the tumor as epithelial-like, stromal-like, or mixed, and then selecting a therapeutic regimen based on the classification. For epithelial-like tumors, the method recommends treatment with cytotoxic chemotherapy, PARP inhibitors, or immunotherapy, as these tumors are typically triple-negative and exhibit a basal-like phenotype associated with high proliferative activity and genomic instability. For stromal-like tumors, the method recommends treatment with anti-angiogenic agents, tyrosine kinase inhibitors targeting PDGFR or FGFR, or agents that modulate extracellular matrix remodeling, such as LOXL2 inhibitors or hyaluronidase, as these tumors exhibit a microenvironment-driven phenotype that may be more susceptible to stromal-targeting therapies. For mixed tumors, the method recommends combination therapy that targets both epithelial and stromal components. The method further comprises monitoring treatment response by repeating the diagnostic method at defined intervals, wherein a shift from an epithelial-like to a stromal-like profile may indicate the emergence of a resistant clone or a change in tumor biology. The method may be integrated into clinical decision support systems and is compatible with electronic health records, enabling real-time, data-driven treatment recommendations. The invention further provides a kit for implementing this method, comprising the nucleic acid compositions, polypeptide binding compositions, and software necessary to perform the diagnostic and therapeutic selection steps in a clinical laboratory setting.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, a cohort of 44 patients was enrolled, including 34 patients with invasive ductal carcinoma and 10 patients undergoing reduction mammoplasty. Tissue samples were collected within 30 minutes of surgical resection, snap-frozen in OCT compound, and stored at -80°C. Ten-micron cryosections were prepared and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Morphologically normal epithelial and stromal regions were identified by a board-certified pathologist and isolated using a PixCell IIe laser capture microdissection system. Epithelial regions were selected from terminal duct-lobular units located at least 2 mm from the tumor margin, while stromal regions were selected from the extralobular compartment, avoiding areas of fibrosis or inflammation. RNA was extracted using GITC buffer, subjected to two rounds of T7 linear amplification, and labeled with Cy3 or Cy5 dyes. Labeled cRNA was hybridized to Agilent Whole Human Genome 44 K microarrays using a universal human reference RNA as a common reference. Duplicate hybridizations with reverse-dye labeling were performed for each sample. Arrays were scanned using an Agilent G2505B scanner, and data were processed using Feature Extraction Software. Raw data were background-corrected using RMA, normalized using spatial and intensity-dependent loess, and scaled using median absolute deviation. Hierarchical clustering was performed using Pearson correlation and complete linkage, and bootstrapping was conducted using the pvclust package in R. Results demonstrated that all samples clustered by tissue type, with epithelial and stromal samples forming two distinct, statistically significant clusters (p < 0.01), regardless of patient origin. No clustering was observed based on tumor subtype, receptor status, or recurrence history.

### Example 2

In this example, a set of 883 genes was identified as differentially expressed between epithelium and stroma using LIMMA and SAM analyses. Genes were filtered for a false discovery rate-adjusted p-value of ≤0.01, a fold change of ≥2.0, and an intensity above background (A > 6.0). These genes were used to construct a gene expression signature for normal breast tissue. The signature was validated by applying it to a second cohort of 20 independent samples, achieving 100% accuracy in classifying epithelial and stromal samples. The signature was then mapped to a publicly available dataset of 295 breast carcinomas from the Netherlands Cancer Institute. Hierarchical clustering of the 295 tumors using the 883-gene signature revealed two distinct clusters: one enriched for stromal genes (n=257) and one enriched for epithelial genes (n=38). The epithelial-enriched cluster was uniformly negative for ER, PR, and HER2, and expressed high levels of KRT5, KRT17, and GGH, consistent with basal-like and normal-like subtypes. The stromal-enriched cluster contained luminal A, luminal B, and HER2-enriched tumors. Survival analysis demonstrated that patients in the epithelial-enriched cluster had significantly worse overall survival (p = 0.000489) compared to those in the stromal-enriched cluster. This example demonstrates that the normal tissue signature can be used to identify clinically relevant tumor subtypes that are not discernible by conventional receptor status alone.

### Example 3

In this example, a set of primers and probes was designed for the 20 most discriminative epithelial and stromal genes in the signature. Each primer pair was validated for amplification efficiency, specificity, and dynamic range using a panel of 50 normal breast tissue samples. The primers were used in a multiplexed qRT-PCR assay, with each reaction containing 10 epithelial and 10 stromal targets, along with three housekeeping genes. The assay was performed on 100 formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded breast tumor samples, and an epithelial index and stromal index were calculated for each sample. Using predefined thresholds, 62 samples were classified as epithelial-like, 28 as stromal-like, and 10 as mixed. The classification was compared to clinical outcomes, and epithelial-like tumors were found to have a 3.7-fold higher risk of recurrence within five years compared to stromal-like tumors (hazard ratio = 3.7, 95% CI: 1.9–7.1, p < 0.001). The assay demonstrated intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation below 8% and was reproducible across three independent laboratories. This example demonstrates the clinical utility of the invention in a real-world diagnostic setting.

### Example 4

In this example, monoclonal antibodies were generated against KIT, CD31, and COL1A1 using recombinant protein immunogens. The antibodies were validated for specificity by Western blot, immunofluorescence, and immunohistochemistry on normal and tumor breast tissues. In immunohistochemistry, KIT staining was restricted to epithelial cells, CD31 staining was localized to endothelial cells in the stroma, and COL1A1 staining was observed in fibroblasts and extracellular matrix. The antibodies were used to stain 150 breast cancer tissue sections, and the staining patterns were correlated with the qRT-PCR classification from Example 3. Epithelial-like tumors exhibited strong KIT staining and weak CD31 and COL1A1 staining, while stromal-like tumors exhibited strong CD31 and COL1A1 staining and weak KIT staining. Mixed tumors exhibited heterogeneous staining patterns. The immunohistochemical classification agreed with the molecular classification in 94% of cases, demonstrating that the normal tissue signature can be validated using standard histopathological methods. This example confirms the biological relevance of the signature and its compatibility with existing clinical workflows.
