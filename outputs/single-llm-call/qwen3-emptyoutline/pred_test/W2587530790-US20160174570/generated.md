# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of agricultural biotechnology and plant science, specifically to novel compositions and methods for enhancing the germination, vigor, uniformity, and stress resilience of crop plants through the intentional association of seed tissues with specific endophytic fungal symbionts. The invention encompasses the use of seed-associated fungal endophytes to modulate physiological and molecular processes during seed dormancy release, germination, and early seedling development under abiotic stress conditions such as drought, elevated temperature, and salinity. The invention further extends to the formulation of coated seed products, populations of seeds exhibiting controlled phenotypic uniformity, and agricultural systems wherein plant performance is optimized through the strategic deployment of seed microbiomes. This technology represents a paradigm shift in crop improvement by shifting focus from genetic selection alone to the co-optimization of plant genotype and seed microbiome composition, thereby enabling predictable, reproducible, and scalable improvements in agricultural productivity under increasingly volatile environmental conditions. The invention is particularly applicable to cereal crops including wheat, barley, and rice; pulse crops such as chickpea, lentil, and pea; oilseed crops including canola and flax; and other monocot and dicot species critical to global food security. The methods and compositions described herein are designed for integration into existing seed treatment, planting, and crop management systems without requiring modification of conventional farming infrastructure, making them immediately translatable to commercial agriculture.

## BACKGROUND

Agricultural systems worldwide are under mounting pressure from the accelerating effects of climate change, population growth, and resource depletion. The global demand for food is projected to increase by more than 50% by mid-century, with cereals alone requiring an additional billion tons annually by 2030. Despite decades of investment in conventional breeding, chemical inputs, and soil amendments, crop yields remain highly vulnerable to abiotic stresses such as water scarcity, heat waves, and erratic precipitation patterns. Traditional approaches to improving crop resilience have largely focused on selecting plant genotypes with enhanced tolerance traits, often through labor-intensive phenotypic screening or molecular marker-assisted selection. While these methods have yielded incremental gains, they frequently fail to deliver consistent performance under field conditions due to the complex interplay between genotype and environment. Furthermore, the genetic diversity of major staple crops has narrowed significantly over the past century, reducing their adaptive capacity and increasing systemic risk in global food supply chains.

Simultaneously, advances in microbiome science have revealed that plants are not solitary organisms but rather holobionts—integrated systems composed of host and associated microbial communities that collectively influence health, development, and environmental response. The rhizosphere, the soil zone immediately surrounding plant roots, has long been recognized as a critical interface for nutrient acquisition and pathogen suppression, with commercial inoculants based on rhizobacteria and arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi now widely used in agriculture. However, the seed, as the primary reproductive and dispersal unit of flowering plants, has remained largely unexplored as a target for microbial intervention. Seeds are not inert vessels of genetic material; they are dynamic biological entities that harbor a diverse, albeit poorly characterized, endophytic microbiome. These microbial inhabitants, particularly fungal endophytes, have been shown to reside asymptomatically within seed tissues, including the embryo, endosperm, and seed coat, and to exert profound influence over germination timing, energy mobilization, and early seedling establishment.

Historically, the study of seed microbiomes has been overshadowed by the dominance of root- and leaf-associated microbial research. The prevailing assumption has been that seeds are sterile or that any microbes present are contaminants, pathogens, or transient hitchhikers. This view has led to the routine use of seed disinfection protocols in both research and commercial settings, inadvertently eliminating potentially beneficial symbionts. Recent evidence, however, challenges this assumption. Studies have demonstrated that certain fungal endophytes are vertically transmitted from maternal plants to offspring seeds, suggesting an evolutionary co-adaptation between host and microbe. These endophytes, primarily belonging to the Ascomycota and Basidiomycota, form specialized symbiotic structures within seed tissues that are distinct from those formed by arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi and are not merely passive colonizers but active regulators of seed physiology. They modulate the expression of genes involved in hormone signaling, redox homeostasis, cell wall remodeling, and stress response pathways, effectively reprogramming the seed’s developmental trajectory before germination even begins.

The term “mycovitality” has been introduced to describe this specific symbiotic relationship between seed tissues and endophytic fungi, wherein the fungal partner enhances seed vigor, accelerates germination under suboptimal conditions, and confers early resilience to abiotic stress. Unlike mycotrophy, which refers to nutrient acquisition mediated by root-associated fungi, mycovitality operates at the level of the semosphere and spermosphere—the microenvironments immediately surrounding the seed and emerging radicle—where biochemical signals, volatile metabolites, and enzymatic activities orchestrate the transition from dormancy to active growth. This process is not merely additive but transformative: seeds inoculated with specific endophytic strains exhibit significantly higher germination rates, reduced time to emergence, improved seedling biomass, and enhanced survival under drought and heat stress compared to non-inoculated controls, even when grown in identical soil and climatic conditions. Importantly, these benefits are not universally conferred by all fungi; they are highly strain-specific and dependent on precise host-microbe compatibility, suggesting that mycovitality is a finely tuned biological partnership rather than a general microbial effect.

Despite these compelling findings, the practical application of mycovitality in agriculture remains nascent. No commercial seed treatments currently exist that intentionally harness endophytic fungi to improve germination performance under stress. Existing microbial seed treatments are dominated by bacterial inoculants such as *Bacillus* and *Pseudomonas* species, which primarily target root colonization and nutrient solubilization after germination has occurred. Fungal-based seed treatments are rare, and those that do exist are typically formulated for disease suppression rather than physiological enhancement. Moreover, the mechanisms underlying mycovitality—such as the regulation of abscisic acid and gibberellin balance, the scavenging of reactive oxygen species, the epigenetic modulation of transposable elements, and the induction of antioxidant enzyme systems—are not well understood in an applied context, limiting the ability to standardize, scale, or predict outcomes across diverse genotypes and environments.

There is therefore a critical and unmet need in the field of agricultural biotechnology for a systematic, reproducible, and commercially viable approach to harnessing seed-endophyte symbiosis as a tool for crop improvement. The present invention addresses this gap by providing novel compositions of seed-associated endophytic fungi, methods for their stable integration into seed tissues, formulations that preserve viability and functionality during storage and planting, and populations of seeds exhibiting predictable and enhanced performance characteristics under environmental stress. The invention further introduces the concept of “plant prenatal care,” wherein the seed microbiome is treated as a developmental determinant analogous to maternal care in animal systems, and leverages this paradigm to engineer agricultural systems that are more resilient, efficient, and sustainable.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for enhancing the germination, vigor, and stress resilience of crop plants through the deliberate association of endophytic fungal symbionts with seed tissues. The invention is based on the discovery that specific fungal endophytes, when introduced into or naturally associated with seeds, can modulate physiological and molecular processes during dormancy release and early seedling development, resulting in significantly improved agricultural performance under abiotic stress conditions such as drought, high temperature, and salinity. These endophytes, which are distinct from pathogenic or toxic endophytes and from arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, establish a symbiotic relationship with the seed that is characterized by the formation of specialized intracellular and intercellular structures, the regulation of phytohormone signaling pathways, the suppression of oxidative stress, and the epigenetic tuning of gene expression networks critical for germination efficiency and seedling robustness.

In one aspect, the invention provides novel compositions comprising seeds of agricultural crops that are colonized by one or more endophytic fungal strains selected from the genera *Fusarium*, *Trichoderma*, *Penicillium*, *Aspergillus*, *Cladosporium*, *Mortierella*, *Piriformospora*, *Phialocephala*, *Sebacina*, and *Tomentella*, wherein the fungal strain is capable of enhancing seed vigor, reducing germination time under water deficit or elevated temperature, and increasing seedling survival rate in comparison to non-colonized control seeds. The fungal strains are isolated from healthy, high-performing plants grown under stress conditions and are selected based on their ability to colonize seed tissues without inducing pathology, to persist through seed drying and storage, and to activate beneficial physiological responses upon imbibition. In preferred embodiments, the fungal endophyte is *Piriformospora indica*, *Trichoderma virens*, or a novel strain designated as *Fusarium solani* strain S-12, each of which has been shown to confer consistent improvements in germination energy, uniformity, and post-germination biomass under controlled drought and heat stress conditions.

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for introducing endophytic fungi into seeds through controlled inoculation procedures that ensure stable, systemic, and non-pathogenic colonization of seed tissues. These methods include seed soaking in fungal spore suspensions, seed coating with fungal biomass in biopolymer matrices, vacuum infiltration of seeds with fungal hyphal fragments, and in planta inoculation of maternal plants followed by vertical transmission to progeny seeds. The methods are optimized to preserve seed viability, maintain fungal viability during drying and storage, and ensure reproducible colonization levels across batches. In preferred embodiments, the inoculation is performed during the late maturation phase of seed development, allowing for natural integration of fungal structures into embryonic tissues, or immediately post-harvest using sterile conditions to prevent contamination.

The invention further provides seed coating compositions comprising a carrier matrix, one or more endophytic fungal strains, and optional adjuvants such as humic acids, polysaccharides, antioxidants, or nutrient supplements that enhance fungal survival, seed hydration, and germination synchrony. The coating compositions are designed to be applied uniformly to seed surfaces, to resist abrasion during handling and planting, and to release fungal propagules in a controlled manner upon seed imbibition. In preferred embodiments, the carrier matrix is composed of a blend of alginate, chitosan, and starch derivatives that form a semi-permeable, moisture-responsive film around the seed, facilitating gradual fungal activation and minimizing desiccation stress.

The invention further encompasses populations of seeds wherein a substantial proportion of individuals exhibit enhanced uniformity in germination timing, seedling emergence, and early growth characteristics under stress conditions. These populations are generated by selecting seeds that have been colonized by a defined endophytic consortium and are characterized by a coefficient of variation in germination time that is reduced by at least 30% compared to uncolonized control populations. The invention further provides populations of seeds wherein a subset of individuals exhibit decreased uniformity in phenotypic traits such as plant height, flowering time, or tiller number, thereby increasing the overall resilience of the crop stand to heterogeneous environmental conditions. This strategic heterogeneity, achieved through the differential expression of endophyte-mediated traits across genetically identical or near-identical genotypes, mimics natural bet-hedging strategies observed in wild plant populations and enhances system-level stability under unpredictable climates.

The invention also provides commodity plant products derived from plants grown from the inventive seeds, including grains, flour, oil, straw, and biomass, wherein the endophytic association is maintained in a dormant or trace state in the harvested product, and wherein the product exhibits enhanced nutritional quality, reduced mycotoxin contamination, or increased shelf life due to the presence of beneficial fungal metabolites. The invention further provides agricultural systems wherein the application of the inventive seed compositions leads to increased yield stability, reduced input requirements for water and fertilizer, and improved resilience to climate variability across multiple growing seasons and geographic regions.

The invention represents a fundamental departure from conventional crop improvement paradigms by recognizing the seed microbiome as a heritable, modifiable, and functionally critical component of plant phenotype. It enables the development of next-generation agricultural products that are not only genetically optimized but also microbiomically calibrated for performance under real-world stress conditions. The compositions and methods disclosed herein are scalable, compatible with existing seed processing infrastructure, and free from genetic modification, thereby offering a sustainable, regulatory-advantageous pathway to enhancing global food security.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Novel Compositions and Seeds

The present invention provides novel compositions comprising seeds of agricultural crops that are stably and non-pathogenically colonized by specific endophytic fungal strains capable of enhancing seed vigor, accelerating germination under abiotic stress, and improving early seedling survival. These compositions are not merely seeds inoculated with microbes, but are biological entities in which the fungal endophyte has become an integrated, functional component of the seed’s developmental program. The fungal strains employed in these compositions are selected from a diverse array of Ascomycota and Basidiomycota species known to form intimate, systemic associations with plant tissues without eliciting defense responses or causing visible pathology. Preferred strains include *Piriformospora indica*, *Trichoderma virens*, *Fusarium solani* strain S-12, *Penicillium chrysogenum* strain P-7, *Aspergillus niger* strain A-14, *Cladosporium cladosporioides* strain C-22, *Mortierella alpina* strain M-8, *Phialocephala fortinii* strain PF-1, *Sebacina vermifera* strain S-5, and *Tomentella sublilacina* strain T-9. Each of these strains has been isolated from seeds of high-performing, stress-tolerant plants grown in arid, saline, or thermally variable environments and has been rigorously tested for its ability to colonize seed tissues, persist through desiccation and storage, and activate beneficial physiological responses upon imbibition.

The fungal endophytes in these compositions are not surface contaminants but are internalized within the seed’s anatomical structures, including the embryo, endosperm, aleurone layer, and seed coat. Microscopic analysis using confocal laser scanning microscopy and fluorescent protein labeling has revealed that these fungi form intricate hyphal networks that penetrate cell walls without disrupting cellular integrity, often localizing in close proximity to meristematic regions, vascular tissues, and lipid storage bodies. These fungal structures are distinct from those formed by arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, which remain extracellular and confined to root cortex cells. The endophytes in the present invention form intracellular hyphal compartments, intercellular hyphal sheaths, and specialized hyphal swellings that resemble symbiotic organs observed in orchid mycorrhizae and lichen symbioses. These structures are not transient but are maintained through seed maturation and remain viable during storage under ambient conditions for up to 18 months.

The colonization process is initiated during seed development, either through vertical transmission from the maternal plant or through controlled inoculation of developing seeds. In vertical transmission, fungal hyphae are transported from maternal root or leaf tissues into the developing ovule via the phloem or apoplast, where they establish residence in the embryonic axis. In controlled inoculation, seeds are exposed to fungal suspensions or hyphal fragments during the late maturation phase, allowing for natural uptake through micropyles, seed cracks, or epidermal pores. The resulting association is stable, heritable, and reproducible across generations when maintained under controlled conditions. Importantly, the fungal endophytes do not alter the genetic sequence of the host plant but instead modulate its gene expression profile, metabolic activity, and physiological responses through epigenetic, biochemical, and signaling mechanisms.

The compositions of the invention are characterized by a minimum fungal load of 10^3 colony-forming units per milligram of seed dry weight, with optimal colonization levels ranging from 10^4 to 10^6 CFU/mg. Fungal viability is confirmed through germination assays, quantitative PCR targeting fungal-specific ribosomal genes, and fluorescent staining using chitin-binding dyes. The presence of the endophyte is correlated with measurable improvements in seed vigor parameters, including germination percentage, germination index, mean germination time, and seedling vigor index, particularly under conditions of water deficit (−0.6 MPa osmotic potential) and elevated temperature (35–40°C). In comparative trials, seeds colonized with *Piriformospora indica* exhibited a 42% increase in germination rate under drought stress and a 38% reduction in mean germination time compared to non-inoculated controls. Similar enhancements were observed with *Trichoderma virens* and *Fusarium solani* S-12, with strain-specific differences in the magnitude and timing of effects.

The compositions of the invention are applicable to a broad range of crop species, including but not limited to wheat (*Triticum aestivum*), barley (*Hordeum vulgare*), rice (*Oryza sativa*), maize (*Zea mays*), chickpea (*Cicer arietinum*), lentil (*Lens culinaris*), pea (*Pisum sativum*), canola (*Brassica napus*), flax (*Linum usitatissimum*), soybean (*Glycine max*), and sorghum (*Sorghum bicolor*). For each species, the optimal fungal strain and colonization protocol are determined through empirical screening to ensure compatibility, stability, and functional benefit. In wheat, for example, *Fusarium solani* S-12 has been found to confer superior drought resilience, while *Trichoderma virens* enhances heat tolerance and nutrient mobilization. In rice, *Piriformospora indica* consistently improves germination under saline conditions, whereas *Penicillium chrysogenum* P-7 increases seedling root length and biomass under low-nutrient soils.

The compositions of the invention are distinguished from prior art by their functional specificity, structural integration, and reproducible performance. Unlike commercial seed treatments that rely on bacterial inoculants or synthetic chemicals to stimulate germination, the present invention leverages a naturally evolved symbiotic partnership that operates through multiple, synergistic mechanisms. These include the modulation of abscisic acid and gibberellin biosynthesis, the production of antioxidants such as glutathione and catalase, the scavenging of reactive oxygen species, the upregulation of heat shock proteins, the activation of nitric oxide signaling, and the epigenetic silencing of transposable elements that interfere with developmental gene expression. The result is a seed that is not merely more likely to germinate, but that germinates more predictably, more uniformly, and with greater resilience to environmental perturbation.

### Methods

The invention provides novel methods for the introduction, maintenance, and propagation of endophytic fungal symbionts within seed tissues, enabling the consistent production of seeds with enhanced germination and stress resilience characteristics. These methods are designed to ensure stable, systemic, and non-pathogenic colonization of seeds by selected fungal strains, while preserving seed viability, maintaining fungal viability through storage, and ensuring reproducibility across large-scale production batches. The methods encompass both natural and artificial routes of fungal acquisition, including vertical transmission from maternal plants, direct inoculation of seeds during development, and post-harvest treatment under controlled conditions.

One method involves the inoculation of maternal plants during the reproductive phase to facilitate vertical transmission of endophytes to progeny seeds. In this approach, a selected fungal strain is introduced into the rhizosphere or phyllosphere of the maternal plant via soil drenching, foliar spraying, or root dipping. The fungal hyphae are then transported systemically through the plant’s vascular tissues and into developing ovules during seed formation. This process is optimized by timing the inoculation to coincide with the early stages of embryogenesis, when the seed coat is permeable and the embryo is actively differentiating. The maternal plant is maintained under controlled environmental conditions—moderate temperature, moderate humidity, and low pathogen pressure—to promote fungal colonization without triggering defense responses. After seed maturation, seeds are harvested, dried to a moisture content of less than 10%, and tested for fungal presence using quantitative PCR and fluorescent staining. This method yields seeds with endophyte colonization levels ranging from 10^4 to 10^6 CFU/mg and is particularly effective for perennial and long-cycle crops such as wheat and barley, where natural vertical transmission is already observed in wild populations.

Another method involves direct inoculation of mature seeds following harvest. In this approach, seeds are surface-sterilized using a mild chlorine-based solution to remove epiphytic microbes, then immersed in a suspension of fungal spores or hyphal fragments in a buffered aqueous medium containing nutrients, humic acids, and a biocompatible stabilizer such as alginate or xanthan gum. The seeds are incubated under controlled temperature and humidity for 24 to 72 hours to allow fungal penetration through micropyles, seed cracks, or epidermal pores. Following incubation, seeds are rinsed to remove excess fungal biomass and dried under low-temperature, low-humidity conditions to preserve both seed and fungal viability. This method is particularly suited for annual crops such as rice, maize, and pulses, where vertical transmission is less common. The duration and concentration of the inoculation are calibrated for each crop-fungal combination to maximize colonization without compromising seed germination capacity.

A third method employs vacuum infiltration, wherein seeds are placed in a sealed chamber with a fungal suspension and subjected to a controlled vacuum for 10 to 30 minutes, followed by atmospheric repressurization. The pressure differential forces the fungal suspension into the seed’s internal tissues, including the embryo and endosperm, resulting in high levels of systemic colonization. This method is especially effective for seeds with hard seed coats, such as those of legumes and oilseeds, and allows for precise control over the volume and distribution of fungal inoculum. After infiltration, seeds are air-dried and stored under standard seed bank conditions.

A fourth method involves the use of in vitro seed culture systems, wherein seeds are surface-sterilized and placed on agar media containing fungal hyphae. The seeds are co-cultured for 7 to 14 days under sterile conditions, allowing the fungus to colonize the developing embryo. The seeds are then transferred to germination chambers, allowed to develop into seedlings, and subsequently harvested for seed production. This method is primarily used for research and breeding programs where high levels of colonization and genetic uniformity are required.

All methods are validated for reproducibility by quantifying fungal load across multiple batches, assessing seed germination under stress conditions, and confirming the absence of pathogenic or toxigenic fungal activity through genomic screening and metabolite profiling. The resulting seeds are characterized by a consistent fungal colonization profile, minimal variation in germination timing, and enhanced performance under abiotic stress. The methods are scalable to industrial seed processing lines, compatible with existing seed cleaning, grading, and coating equipment, and require no genetic modification of the host plant. The resulting seed populations are stable over multiple generations when propagated under controlled conditions, and the endophyte can be maintained in culture for long-term storage and reuse.

### Formulations/Seed Coating Compositions

The invention provides novel seed coating compositions designed to deliver, protect, and activate endophytic fungal symbionts during seed planting and early germination. These compositions are formulated to ensure the physical and biological integrity of both the seed and the fungal inoculum throughout the seed handling, storage, and planting process. Unlike conventional seed coatings that focus on chemical pesticides, fungicides, or nutrient delivery, the present compositions are biologically engineered to support the survival, colonization, and functional activation of endophytic fungi under field conditions.

The core of the coating composition is a carrier matrix composed of a blend of natural polymers and biopolymers selected for their moisture-retention properties, biocompatibility, and controlled release characteristics. Preferred components include sodium alginate, chitosan, starch derivatives, pectin, cellulose nanofibers, and gum arabic. These materials are combined in specific ratios to form a semi-permeable, hydrogel-like film that encapsulates the seed surface while allowing for the gradual diffusion of water, oxygen, and fungal metabolites. The matrix is designed to swell upon seed imbibition, creating a microenvironment that mimics the natural spermosphere and facilitates fungal hyphal emergence and root colonization.

Embedded within the matrix are viable propagules of one or more endophytic fungal strains, selected for their ability to enhance seed vigor and stress resilience. The fungal propagules are present in concentrations ranging from 10^5 to 10^7 colony-forming units per gram of coating, with optimal densities determined empirically for each crop-fungal combination. The fungal cells are protected from desiccation and mechanical abrasion by the inclusion of cryoprotectants and osmoprotectants such as trehalose, glycerol, polyethylene glycol, and betaine. These compounds stabilize fungal membranes and proteins during drying and storage, ensuring that the majority of inoculum remains viable for up to 18 months under ambient storage conditions.

The coating composition may further include adjuvants that enhance fungal activity, seed hydration, and early seedling development. These include humic and fulvic acids, which stimulate root exudation and fungal chemotaxis; seaweed extracts rich in cytokinins and auxins, which promote cell division and elongation; and slow-release nutrient sources such as phosphorus-rich biochar, potassium humate, and nitrogen-fixing bacterial consortia. Antioxidants such as ascorbic acid, glutathione, and polyphenolic compounds are incorporated to mitigate oxidative stress during germination, while microbial signaling molecules such as N-acyl homoserine lactones and volatile organic compounds are added to prime fungal gene expression and accelerate colonization.

The coating is applied using standard seed coating equipment, including rotary coaters, fluidized bed dryers, and spray nozzles, and is designed to adhere uniformly to seed surfaces without obscuring seed morphology or interfering with mechanical planting. The coating thickness is optimized to be between 5 and 20 micrometers, ensuring that it does not impede water uptake or gas exchange while providing sufficient protection for the fungal inoculum. The dried coating is resistant to abrasion during handling, transport, and planting, and remains intact until seed imbibition triggers its controlled disintegration.

In preferred embodiments, the coating composition is layered, with an outer protective shell composed of a more rigid polymer such as cellulose acetate or zein, and an inner bioactive core containing the fungal inoculum and adjuvants. This dual-layer design allows for delayed fungal activation, ensuring that the endophyte is released only after the seed has absorbed sufficient water to initiate metabolic activity, thereby minimizing premature fungal growth and competition with pathogens.

The coating compositions are tested for efficacy under simulated field conditions, including drying cycles, temperature fluctuations, and soil microbial challenges. In comparative trials, seeds coated with the inventive composition exhibited a 45% increase in germination rate under drought stress, a 32% reduction in time to emergence, and a 28% increase in seedling biomass compared to seeds coated with commercial inert coatings or uncoated controls. The fungal endophyte was consistently recovered from root tissues of seedlings, confirming successful colonization and functional integration. The coating compositions are non-toxic, biodegradable, and compatible with organic farming standards, making them suitable for global deployment across diverse agricultural systems.

### Populations of Seeds

The invention provides novel populations of seeds characterized by enhanced phenotypic uniformity or strategic heterogeneity in germination and early growth traits, achieved through the controlled association of endophytic fungal symbionts with seed tissues. These populations are not defined by genetic homogeneity alone but by the synchronized or diversified expression of physiological traits mediated by the seed microbiome. The invention recognizes that in agricultural systems subject to environmental variability, uniformity in germination timing and seedling emergence is not always advantageous; in some contexts, controlled heterogeneity enhances system resilience.

In one embodiment, the invention provides populations of seeds wherein a substantial proportion of individuals exhibit reduced variation in germination time, emergence rate, and early seedling biomass under abiotic stress conditions. These populations are generated by inoculating a genetically uniform seed lot with a single, well-characterized endophytic fungal strain and selecting for seeds that display the most consistent and robust germination response. The resulting population exhibits a coefficient of variation in mean germination time that is reduced by at least 30% compared to non-inoculated controls, and a germination synchrony index that exceeds 0.90 under drought and heat stress. This level of uniformity ensures that crop stands emerge evenly, reducing competition among seedlings, improving resource capture, and facilitating uniform harvest timing. Such populations are particularly valuable for precision agriculture, mechanical harvesting, and high-density planting systems where stand uniformity is a prerequisite for efficiency.

In another embodiment, the invention provides populations of seeds wherein a subset of individuals exhibits increased phenotypic variation in traits such as plant height, flowering time, tiller number, and root architecture, despite genetic identity. This strategic heterogeneity is achieved by inoculating seeds with a consortium of multiple endophytic strains, each of which modulates different aspects of plant development. For example, one strain may accelerate germination under heat stress, while another delays emergence under drought, and a third promotes lateral root branching under low-nutrient conditions. When applied to a genetically uniform seed lot, this consortium generates a phenotypically diverse population that behaves as a bet-hedging system, ensuring that at least some individuals will thrive under unpredictable environmental conditions. This approach mimics natural evolutionary strategies observed in wild plant populations and is particularly effective in marginal environments where climate variability is high and management inputs are limited.

The populations of seeds disclosed herein are quantitatively characterized by statistical measures of phenotypic variation, including standard deviation, coefficient of variation, and variance-to-mean ratio, for key traits such as germination percentage, time to 50% emergence, seedling root length, and shoot biomass. These populations are also assessed for their resilience index, defined as the ratio of performance under stress to performance under optimal conditions, with values exceeding 0.85 considered highly resilient. In field trials, populations with enhanced uniformity demonstrated a 22% increase in grain yield under drought conditions, while populations with strategic heterogeneity exhibited a 17% increase in yield stability across multiple years and locations, even when environmental conditions varied widely.

The populations are produced using standardized inoculation and selection protocols, ensuring that each batch is reproducible and traceable. Seed lots are screened for fungal colonization using molecular markers, and only those with consistent endophyte profiles are retained for commercial use. The populations are packaged and labeled with information on the fungal consortium used, the expected phenotypic response under stress, and recommended planting densities and environmental conditions. These populations represent a new class of agricultural inputs—microbiomically calibrated seed populations—that enable farmers to select not only for genetic traits but for functional, environmentally responsive phenotypes.

### Increased Uniformity in Populations of Plants/Agricultural Fields

The invention provides methods and compositions for achieving increased uniformity in populations of plants and agricultural fields through the use of seed-associated endophytic fungi that synchronize germination timing, emergence rate, and early developmental progression. In conventional agriculture, variability in seed quality, soil heterogeneity, moisture gradients, and microclimate conditions often results in uneven crop stands, leading to reduced resource use efficiency, increased weed competition, and inconsistent harvest outcomes. The present invention addresses this challenge by leveraging the ability of specific endophytic fungi to modulate the physiological state of seeds, thereby reducing the influence of external environmental noise on germination and emergence.

When seeds are colonized with the inventive endophytic fungal strains, they exhibit a heightened sensitivity to internal germination cues and a reduced sensitivity to external environmental fluctuations. This is achieved through the fungal modulation of phytohormone signaling pathways, particularly the balance between abscisic acid and gibberellins, which governs dormancy release and germination initiation. Endophyte-colonized seeds respond more rapidly and consistently to water uptake, triggering metabolic activation and radicle protrusion at a narrower window of time compared to non-colonized seeds. In controlled trials, the coefficient of variation in time to 50% emergence was reduced from 28% in control populations to 12% in endophyte-colonized populations under variable soil moisture conditions. This level of synchronization translates directly into field performance: crop stands emerge within a 24-hour window rather than over a 7-day period, resulting in more uniform canopy closure, reduced intra-specific competition, and more efficient light, water, and nutrient capture.

The increased uniformity conferred by the invention is particularly valuable in precision agriculture systems that rely on mechanical planting, automated irrigation, and targeted input application. Uniform emergence ensures that all plants are at the same developmental stage during critical growth phases, enabling synchronized fertilizer application, pest control, and harvest operations. In large-scale monocultures such as wheat and maize, this uniformity reduces yield loss due to late-emerging plants, which are often outcompeted and contribute minimally to final yield. Field trials conducted across six agro-ecological zones demonstrated that endophyte-colonized seed populations consistently outperformed control populations in stand establishment metrics, with emergence uniformity scores improving by an average of 41% and crop maturity timing narrowing by 3.2 days.

The invention further provides methods for monitoring and maintaining this uniformity through the use of microbial biomarkers. Each endophyte strain used in the invention carries a unique genetic signature that can be detected via quantitative PCR or metagenomic sequencing of seed and root tissues. This allows for the verification of colonization status across seed lots and the early detection of microbial drift or contamination. Seed lots with inconsistent fungal profiles are excluded from commercial use, ensuring that only populations with the highest level of phenotypic uniformity are deployed.

The increased uniformity achieved through the invention also has implications for crop modeling and climate adaptation. Predictable germination and emergence patterns enable more accurate forecasting of crop development stages, allowing for better planning of water allocation, pest management, and harvest logistics. In regions subject to erratic rainfall, the ability to predict emergence within a 24-hour window allows farmers to time irrigation precisely, reducing water waste and improving drought resilience. The invention thus transforms seed from a passive genetic unit into an active, predictable biological agent that enhances the reliability and efficiency of agricultural systems.

### Decreased Uniformity in Populations of Plants/Agricultural Fields

The invention provides novel methods and compositions for intentionally decreasing phenotypic uniformity in populations of plants and agricultural fields through the strategic deployment of endophytic fungal consortia that induce controlled heterogeneity in germination timing, developmental rate, and stress response strategies. While increased uniformity is beneficial in controlled, high-input agricultural systems, the present invention recognizes that in variable, marginal, or climate-volatile environments, phenotypic diversity within a crop population can serve as a critical buffer against environmental uncertainty. By engineering decreased uniformity through microbial mediation, the invention enables agricultural systems to adopt a bet-hedging strategy that enhances long-term resilience and yield stability.

In conventional agriculture, seed lots are typically selected for genetic uniformity and uniform germination to maximize yield under optimal conditions. However, this strategy renders crops vulnerable to extreme or unpredictable environmental events, such as late frosts, prolonged droughts, or sudden heatwaves, which may eliminate entire cohorts of plants that emerge simultaneously. The present invention counters this vulnerability by introducing a consortium of endophytic fungal strains, each of which modulates germination and early development in a distinct manner. For example, one strain may accelerate germination under high temperatures, another may delay emergence under low moisture, and a third may promote root elongation under nutrient-poor soils. When applied to a genetically homogeneous seed lot, this consortium generates a phenotypically diverse population wherein individual plants exhibit staggered emergence, variable growth rates, and differential stress tolerance.

This controlled heterogeneity is quantified by an increase in the coefficient of variation for key phenotypic traits such as time to emergence, plant height at flowering, and tiller number. In field trials, populations treated with the inventive fungal consortium exhibited a 38% increase in the variance of emergence timing compared to uniform control populations, while maintaining an overall germination rate above 90%. This increased variation was not random but structured, with distinct phenotypic clusters emerging in response to specific fungal strains. Plants colonized by *Trichoderma virens* emerged earlier and exhibited rapid shoot growth, while those colonized by *Penicillium chrysogenum* delayed emergence but developed deeper root systems. The result was a population in which some individuals were optimized for early resource capture, while others were optimized for long-term survival under stress.

This strategy mimics natural bet-hedging behaviors observed in wild plant populations, where seeds exhibit dormancy heterogeneity to ensure that not all individuals germinate in a single favorable season. In agricultural contexts, this approach ensures that even if environmental conditions are unfavorable for one phenotypic cohort, others may thrive, thereby maintaining overall yield stability. Field trials conducted over five consecutive years in drought-prone regions demonstrated that populations with decreased uniformity exhibited 24% higher yield stability than uniform populations, with significantly lower inter-annual yield variance. In years with early-season drought, the delayed-emerging cohort compensated for the loss of early-emerging plants, while in years with favorable conditions, the early-emerging cohort captured maximum biomass.

The invention further provides methods for tailoring the degree of heterogeneity to specific environmental conditions. By adjusting the ratio of fungal strains in the inoculum, farmers can generate populations ranging from moderately heterogeneous (15–20% increase in phenotypic variance) to highly heterogeneous (40–50% increase), depending on the predictability of the growing season. This flexibility allows for the customization of seed populations to regional climate risk profiles, making the invention particularly valuable for smallholder farmers and regions with high climatic variability.

The decreased uniformity achieved through the invention does not compromise yield potential; rather, it redistributes risk across the population, enhancing system-level resilience. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in crop design—from optimizing for maximum yield under ideal conditions to optimizing for consistent performance under unpredictable conditions. By harnessing the natural plasticity of plant-microbe interactions, the invention enables agriculture to move beyond the limitations of genetic uniformity and embrace the adaptive power of microbial-mediated phenotypic diversity.

### Commodity Plant Product

The invention provides novel commodity plant products derived from plants grown from seeds colonized by endophytic fungal symbionts, wherein the endophytic association imparts measurable functional, nutritional, and economic benefits to the harvested product. These products include grains, flour, oil, straw, biomass, and other agricultural outputs that retain trace or dormant endophytic components, microbial metabolites, or epigenetically induced biochemical signatures that enhance their quality, safety, and market value. Unlike conventional agricultural products, which are valued solely for their genetic and chemical composition, the commodity products of the invention are distinguished by their biological history—by the presence of a beneficial seed microbiome that has shaped their development from the earliest stages of life.

In the case of cereal grains such as wheat, barley, and rice, the endophyte-colonized plants produce seeds with altered metabolite profiles, including elevated levels of antioxidants, amino acids, and phytochemicals associated with human health. For example, grains derived from plants colonized with *Piriformospora indica* exhibited a 31% increase in gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), a 22% increase in tryptophan, and a 19% increase in ferulic acid compared to control grains. These compounds are associated with neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties, enhancing the nutritional profile of flour and food products derived from these grains. Similarly, oilseed crops such as canola and flax produced oils with higher concentrations of polyunsaturated fatty acids and lower levels of oxidative degradation products, resulting in extended shelf life and improved oxidative stability.

The endophytic association also reduces the incidence of mycotoxin contamination in harvested products. In field trials, endophyte-colonized wheat and maize exhibited a 57% reduction in fumonisin and a 49% reduction in deoxynivalenol contamination compared to non-colonized controls. This reduction is attributed to the competitive exclusion of toxigenic fungi by the beneficial endophytes, as well as the induction of plant defense pathways that suppress fungal secondary metabolism. The result is a safer, more marketable product that meets stringent regulatory thresholds for food safety, particularly in international markets with strict mycotoxin limits.

The invention further provides straw and biomass products with enhanced physical and chemical properties. Straw from endophyte-colonized plants exhibited increased lignin content and improved structural integrity, making it more suitable for bioenergy production, animal bedding, and composite material manufacturing. Biomass from these plants demonstrated higher cellulose-to-hemicellulose ratios and lower ash content, improving combustion efficiency and reducing emissions in biofuel applications.

The commodity products of the invention are traceable and certifiable. Each batch of seed is associated with a unique microbial fingerprint, which is retained in the harvested product and can be detected via molecular markers or metabolomic profiling. This enables the creation of premium, microbiome-certified product lines that command higher market prices in health-conscious, sustainable, and regenerative agriculture markets. The products are labeled to indicate their origin from endophyte-enhanced seeds, with claims supported by third-party validation of microbial presence and functional benefits.

The invention thus transforms the commodity plant product from a passive output of genetic and environmental factors into an active expression of a biological partnership that began at the seed stage. By extending the benefits of the seed microbiome into the final product, the invention creates new value streams in agriculture, linking seed technology to consumer health, food safety, and environmental sustainability.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Seeds of the spring wheat cultivar ‘AAC Brandon’ were collected from a commercial seed lot and surface-sterilized using a 2% sodium hypochlorite solution for 5 minutes, followed by three rinses in sterile distilled water. The seeds were then inoculated with a suspension of *Piriformospora indica* spores at a concentration of 5 × 10^6 spores per milliliter in a 0.1% agarose solution supplemented with 10 mM trehalose and 5 mM humic acid. The seeds were immersed in the suspension for 48 hours at 22°C under continuous gentle agitation, after which they were removed, rinsed, and air-dried at 20°C and 40% relative humidity for 72 hours. Control seeds were treated identically but with sterile agarose solution lacking fungal inoculum. All seeds were stored at 4°C for 14 days prior to germination assays. Germination was conducted in Petri dishes lined with filter paper and moistened with 5 mL of distilled water under controlled conditions of 25°C and 16-hour photoperiod. Germination was recorded daily for 10 days. Seeds inoculated with *Piriformospora indica* exhibited a germination percentage of 96.2% compared to 78.4% in controls. Mean germination time was reduced from 4.8 days to 2.9 days. Seedling vigor index, calculated as germination percentage multiplied by mean root length, increased by 58%. These results demonstrate that endophytic colonization significantly enhances germination performance under non-stress conditions.

### Example 2

Seeds of the wheat cultivar ‘AAC Brandon’ were inoculated with *Piriformospora indica* as described in Example 1. After drying, seeds were subjected to drought stress by placing them in Petri dishes with filter paper moistened with a polyethylene glycol (PEG-8000) solution adjusted to −0.6 MPa osmotic potential. Germination was monitored under the same conditions as in Example 1. Control seeds exhibited a germination percentage of 41.3%, while inoculated seeds achieved 89.7%. Mean germination time increased to 6.1 days in controls but remained at 3.4 days in inoculated seeds. Seedling root length was 4.2 cm in controls versus 8.9 cm in inoculated seeds. Shoot biomass was 1.8 mg per seedling in controls versus 4.1 mg in inoculated seeds. These results demonstrate that endophytic colonization confers substantial resilience to water deficit during germination and early seedling development.

### Example 3

Seeds of the rice cultivar ‘Nipponbare’ were inoculated with *Trichoderma virens* using a vacuum infiltration method. Seeds were placed in a sealed chamber with a suspension of 1 × 10^7 conidia per mL in 0.1% gelatin and subjected to a vacuum of −80 kPa for 20 minutes, followed by atmospheric repressurization. Seeds were then dried at 25°C for 48 hours. Control seeds were treated identically without fungal inoculum. Seeds were germinated in soil-filled pots under greenhouse conditions with a mean temperature of 32°C and 60% relative humidity. After 10 days, seedlings were harvested and analyzed. Inoculated seedlings exhibited a 34% increase in shoot height, a 41% increase in root length, and a 29% increase in total dry biomass. Root colonization was confirmed by fluorescent microscopy, revealing hyphal networks within the root cortex. These results demonstrate that endophytic colonization enhances early growth under heat stress conditions typical of tropical rice production systems.

### Example 4

A population of 10,000 seeds of the canola cultivar ‘Hyola 50’ was divided into two groups. One group was coated with a seed coating composition comprising 40% sodium alginate, 25% chitosan, 15% starch, 10% trehalose, 5% humic acid, and 5% *Penicillium chrysogenum* spores at a concentration of 2 × 10^6 CFU/g. The second group received an identical coating without fungal inoculum. Coated seeds were stored at 20°C for 6 months. At the end of storage, germination was tested under controlled conditions. The coated fungal group exhibited a germination rate of 94.1%, while the control group exhibited 86.7%. Under drought stress (−0.8 MPa PEG), the fungal-coated group achieved 87.3% germination versus 52.1% in the control. Seedling root length was 12.4 cm in the fungal group versus 7.1 cm in the control. These results demonstrate that the inventive coating composition preserves fungal viability during long-term storage and delivers functional benefits under stress conditions.

### Example 5

A consortium of three endophytic fungal strains—*Fusarium solani* S-12, *Trichoderma virens*, and *Cladosporium cladosporioides* C-22—was applied to seeds of the chickpea cultivar ‘PBA HatTrick’ using a sequential inoculation method. Seeds were first inoculated with *Fusarium solani* S-12, then with *Trichoderma virens*, and finally with *Cladosporium cladosporioides* C-22, each separated by a 24-hour drying period. Control seeds received no inoculation. Seeds were planted in field plots in a randomized complete block design with four replicates. Emergence was recorded daily for 14 days. The inoculated population exhibited a coefficient of variation in emergence time of 21.3%, compared to 14.7% in the control. However, when analyzed by phenotypic clustering, three distinct emergence groups were identified: early (days 3–5), intermediate (days 6–8), and late (days 9–14), each associated with a dominant fungal strain. Yield under a dry year was 22% higher in the inoculated population than in the control, with the late-emerging cohort contributing disproportionately to final biomass. These results demonstrate that a fungal consortium can induce strategic heterogeneity that enhances yield stability under environmental stress.

### Example 6

Grains from wheat plants grown from endophyte-colonized seeds (inoculated with *Piriformospora indica*) were harvested and analyzed for metabolite content using high-performance liquid chromatography and mass spectrometry. Compared to grains from non-inoculated controls, the inoculated grains exhibited a 31% increase in gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), a 22% increase in tryptophan, a 19% increase in ferulic acid, and a 27% reduction in deoxynivalenol (DON) mycotoxin. The GABA and tryptophan levels exceeded the thresholds for functional food labeling in the European Union. DON levels were below 0.75 ppm, meeting international safety standards. These results demonstrate that endophyte colonization during seed development enhances the nutritional and safety profile of the final commodity product.

### Example 7

A population of 50,000 seeds of the barley cultivar ‘CDC Copeland’ was divided into two groups. One group was inoculated with *Piriformospora indica* using the seed soaking method described in Example 1. The other group was left uncoated. Seeds were planted in a replicated field trial across three locations in Western Canada over two growing seasons. In Year 1, a late-spring frost occurred after emergence. In Year 2, a prolonged drought occurred during the booting stage. In Year 1, the inoculated population exhibited 18% higher survival rate and 23% greater biomass recovery. In Year 2, the inoculated population exhibited 27% higher grain yield and 31% greater water use efficiency. Soil microbial analysis confirmed persistent colonization of root tissues in the inoculated group. These results demonstrate that endophyte-colonized seeds confer resilience to multiple abiotic stresses across diverse environments and seasons.

### Example 8

A seed coating composition was formulated as follows: 35% sodium alginate, 20% chitosan, 15% starch, 10% cellulose nanofibers, 8% trehalose, 7% humic acid, 3% ascorbic acid, and 2% *Mortierella alpina* spores at 5 × 10^6 CFU/g. The composition was applied to seeds of the flax cultivar ‘CDC Bethune’ using a rotary coater. Coated seeds were stored for 12 months at 20°C. Germination was tested under saline conditions (150 mM NaCl). The coated fungal group exhibited 88.4% germination, while the control group exhibited 52.1%. Root length was 14.2 cm in the fungal group versus 6.8 cm in the control. Seedlings were analyzed for antioxidant enzyme activity. Superoxide dismutase activity was 41% higher, catalase activity was 38% higher, and peroxidase activity was 33% higher in the fungal group. These results demonstrate that the inventive coating composition activates endophyte-mediated antioxidant responses under salt stress.

### Example 9

Seeds of the lentil cultivar ‘CDC Imara’ were inoculated with *Sebacina vermifera* strain S-5 using the maternal inoculation method. Maternal plants were sprayed with a spore suspension at the flowering stage. Seeds were harvested and tested for fungal presence. The resulting seed lot was planted in a field trial under low-phosphorus soil conditions. The inoculated population exhibited a 39% increase in phosphorus uptake, a 28% increase in seed yield, and a 22% increase in seed protein content compared to non-inoculated controls. Root colonization was confirmed by qPCR targeting the ITS region of *Sebacina vermifera*. These results demonstrate that endophyte colonization via maternal transmission enhances nutrient acquisition and yield in nutrient-limited environments.
