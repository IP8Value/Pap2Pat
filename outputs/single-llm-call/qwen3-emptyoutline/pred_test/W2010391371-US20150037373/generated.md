# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) derived from the cones of Pinus sylvestris, and its use as an immunomodulatory agent capable of enhancing the proliferative phase of a primary T cell response in vivo. More specifically, the invention pertains to the oral administration of PPC in combination with an immunogenic stimulus to significantly increase the expansion of antigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes during the early phase of a primary immune response, without altering the rate of activation-induced cell death during the subsequent contraction phase. The invention further encompasses the use of PPC to augment the magnitude of the secondary T cell response by increasing the pool of antigen-specific effector T cells generated during the primary response, thereby improving the efficacy of vaccines and other immunotherapeutic interventions. The composition and method of administration described herein are particularly useful in contexts requiring robust cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) priming, including but not limited to cancer immunotherapy, chronic viral infections, and prophylactic or therapeutic vaccination against intracellular pathogens. The invention also provides a purified, standardized, and stable powdered formulation of PPC that is free from microbial, endotoxin, and heavy metal contamination, and which can be consistently administered in precise dosages via oral, sublingual, or enteral routes to achieve reproducible immunological outcomes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The modulation of the immune system through natural plant-derived compounds has been a subject of interest for centuries, with historical records documenting the use of pinecone extracts in ancient Mediterranean and East Asian medicinal traditions. The earliest known written account of such use dates to the first century AD, when Pedanius Dioscorides, a Greek physician and pharmacologist practicing in Rome, described in his seminal work De Materia Medica the application of pinecone extracts for the treatment of renal and gastrointestinal disorders, as well as for topical management of cutaneous lesions resembling psoriasis. These early observations, though anecdotal and lacking mechanistic understanding, laid the foundation for later ethnopharmacological investigations into the bioactive properties of coniferous plant materials. In the 20th century, indigenous populations on the island of Kyushu in Japan developed a traditional practice of preparing an aqueous infusion from pinecones, which was consumed regularly to treat a broad spectrum of ailments, including infectious diseases and malignancies. The perceived broad-spectrum efficacy of this preparation suggested an underlying influence on systemic immune function, prompting modern scientific inquiry into its molecular constituents and biological effects.

Recent research has identified a unique polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) isolated from the cones of Pinus sylvestris as the principal bioactive component responsible for the observed immunomodulatory effects. This complex, composed of a heterogeneous mixture of polyphenolic propanoid units covalently linked to high-molecular-weight polysaccharide chains, exhibits remarkable stability under physiological conditions and retains biological activity following oral administration. Prior studies have demonstrated that PPC, when administered orally at the time of immunization, profoundly alters the quality of the adaptive immune response by biasing T helper cell differentiation toward a T helper type 1 (Th1) phenotype, while simultaneously suppressing the development of a T helper type 2 (Th2) response. This polarization is evidenced by a marked increase in the production of interferon-gamma (IFNγ) and interleukin-12 (IL-12), coupled with a significant reduction in interleukin-4 (IL-4) secretion from antigen-stimulated splenocytes. Furthermore, PPC has been shown to enhance the generation of antigen-specific CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) during secondary immune responses, with studies reporting a consistent doubling in the number of IFNγ-producing CD8+ T cells detected by enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISPOT) assays following immunization with protein, DNA, or dendritic cell-based vaccines.

Despite these promising findings, the precise mechanism by which PPC exerts its effects on T cell dynamics remained elusive. While it was hypothesized that PPC might function as an antioxidant to inhibit activation-induced cell death (AICD), thereby increasing T cell survival during the contraction phase, direct experimental evidence supporting this hypothesis was lacking. Moreover, the temporal window during which PPC exerts its most critical influence—whether during priming, expansion, or memory formation—had not been definitively established. The prevailing assumption in the field was that any enhancement of secondary T cell responses must arise from improved survival of memory precursors or enhanced dendritic cell maturation, both of which occur during or after the primary response. However, no prior study had systematically evaluated the impact of PPC on the kinetics of T cell proliferation, contraction, or apoptosis during the initial 96 hours following antigenic challenge.

The present invention addresses these critical gaps in knowledge by providing the first comprehensive analysis of PPC’s effects on the primary T cell response in vivo, using the well-characterized staphylococcal enterotoxin B (SEB) model of superantigen-driven T cell activation. This model permits the selective and synchronous activation of a defined population of T cells expressing the Vβ8 T cell receptor, enabling precise quantification of expansion and contraction dynamics. Through rigorous experimental design involving flow cytometry, cytokine profiling, reactive oxygen species (ROS) detection, apoptosis assays, and quantitative gene expression analysis, the present invention demonstrates that PPC does not act by inhibiting apoptosis or enhancing T cell survival during the contraction phase, as previously hypothesized. Instead, the invention reveals that PPC exerts its primary effect during the early proliferative phase of the primary T cell response, leading to a significant and reproducible increase in the number of antigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cells before the onset of contraction. This finding fundamentally redefines the mechanistic understanding of how PPC enhances secondary immune responses: rather than preserving memory cells, PPC amplifies the initial wave of effector T cell generation, thereby increasing the size of the precursor pool from which memory cells are subsequently derived. This insight not only resolves longstanding ambiguities in the literature but also establishes a new paradigm for the rational design of immunoadjuvants that target the expansion phase of T cell responses.

The prior art contains no disclosure or suggestion that a plant-derived polysaccharide-polyphenol complex can selectively enhance T cell proliferation without altering apoptotic pathways, nor does it describe the specific dosing regimen, route of administration, or biochemical characterization required to achieve reproducible immunological outcomes. Furthermore, while other natural compounds such as curcumin, resveratrol, and epigallocatechin gallate have been reported to possess antioxidant or anti-inflammatory properties, none have been shown to induce a selective, dose-dependent, and antigen-context-dependent expansion of antigen-specific T cells in vivo without suppressing activation-induced cell death. The invention disclosed herein is therefore novel, non-obvious, and represents a significant advancement over the state of the art in immunomodulatory therapeutics.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for enhancing the magnitude of a primary T cell response in a mammalian subject by orally administering a purified polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) derived from the cones of Pinus sylvestris in conjunction with an immunogenic stimulus. The invention is based on the discovery that PPC, when administered orally within a specific temporal window surrounding antigen exposure, significantly increases the number of antigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cells during the proliferative phase of the primary immune response, without affecting the rate of activation-induced cell death during the subsequent contraction phase. This effect is observed in the absence of any alteration in the expression of key apoptosis-regulating genes such as Bcl-2, Bim, or Bax, and without modification of intracellular levels of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 per cell. The invention further demonstrates that the enhancement of T cell expansion is not attributable to a reduction in reactive oxygen species (ROS) or to the suppression of apoptosis, as previously hypothesized, but rather to a direct or indirect augmentation of T cell proliferation driven by PPC’s interaction with antigen-presenting cells or the lymph node microenvironment.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for enhancing the primary T cell response in a subject, comprising the steps of: (a) administering to the subject an effective amount of a polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) derived from Pinus sylvestris, wherein the PPC is in a purified, standardized, and stable powdered form; and (b) concurrently or sequentially administering an immunogenic stimulus to the subject, wherein the immunogenic stimulus is selected from the group consisting of peptides, proteins, nucleic acids, whole inactivated pathogens, live attenuated pathogens, dendritic cell vaccines, and tumor-associated antigens. The PPC is administered orally, sublingually, or via enteral tube, and the immunogenic stimulus is delivered via parenteral, mucosal, or intradermal routes. The PPC is administered in a dosage range of 10 to 500 μg per kg of body weight, with a preferred dosage of 200 μg per kg, and is given in multiple doses over a period of 24 to 72 hours, beginning no later than 24 hours prior to the immunogenic stimulus and continuing for up to 48 hours thereafter. The method results in a statistically significant increase in the number of antigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cells in secondary lymphoid organs, including lymph nodes and spleen, as measured by flow cytometry using antigen-specific T cell receptor Vβ chain expression or peptide-MHC tetramer staining.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of PPC in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the PPC is characterized by a polyphenylpropanoid content of at least 85% by weight, as determined by UV spectrophotometry at 280 nm using an extinction coefficient of 22.8 L·g⁻¹·cm⁻¹, and wherein the composition is free of detectable levels of endotoxin, heavy metals, pesticides, herbicides, and viable microorganisms. The composition is formulated as a dry powder, capsule, tablet, or oral suspension, and is stable for at least 24 months when stored at room temperature in a desiccated environment. The composition is intended for oral administration in conjunction with an immunogenic agent to enhance the primary T cell response, and is particularly useful in the context of cancer immunotherapy, where the goal is to generate a robust and sustained cytotoxic T lymphocyte response against tumor antigens.

In a further embodiment, the invention provides a method for improving the efficacy of a vaccine by co-administering PPC with the vaccine, wherein the vaccine is administered to a subject in need thereof, and wherein the PPC is administered orally in a dose of 200 μg per kg of body weight, three times daily for two consecutive days, beginning on the day of vaccination. The method results in a doubling of the number of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells detected during the secondary immune response, as compared to vaccination without PPC, and is associated with increased production of IFNγ and reduced production of IL-4 in antigen-stimulated splenocytes. The invention further provides a kit for enhancing T cell responses, comprising a first container containing a predetermined dose of PPC in powdered form and a second container containing an immunogenic agent, wherein the kit includes instructions for oral administration of PPC prior to, concurrently with, or immediately following administration of the immunogenic agent.

The invention further encompasses the use of PPC to modulate the balance between Th1 and Th2 immune responses, wherein the administration of PPC suppresses the development of antigen-specific IgE-mediated allergic responses and prevents the induction of Th2-associated cytokines such as IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, while promoting the secretion of IFNγ and IL-12. This makes the invention particularly valuable for the prevention or treatment of allergic disorders, asthma, and atopic dermatitis, wherein a Th1-skewed immune response is beneficial. The invention also provides a method for enhancing the efficacy of dendritic cell-based vaccines by co-administering PPC, wherein the PPC promotes the migration, maturation, or antigen-presenting capacity of dendritic cells in vivo, leading to increased T cell priming in secondary lymphoid tissues. The invention further provides a method for treating chronic viral infections, such as hepatitis B, hepatitis C, or human immunodeficiency virus, by enhancing the expansion of virus-specific CD8+ T cells during the primary response, thereby increasing the likelihood of viral clearance and reducing the establishment of immunological exhaustion.

The invention is distinguished from the prior art by its precise definition of the temporal and dosing parameters necessary to achieve reproducible immunological enhancement, its exclusion of any mechanism involving suppression of apoptosis or modulation of Bcl-2 family proteins, and its demonstration that the effect of PPC is specific to the proliferative phase of the primary T cell response. The invention further provides a standardized, reproducible, and scalable method for the production of PPC, including a purification process involving extraction at elevated pH and temperature, followed by spray-drying, filtration, and analytical standardization, resulting in a product with consistent biological activity across batches. The invention thus represents a significant advancement in the field of immunomodulation by providing a novel, orally bioavailable, non-toxic, and highly effective agent for enhancing the magnitude of adaptive immune responses without compromising the natural regulatory mechanisms of T cell homeostasis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

The present invention is directed to a polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) derived from the cones of Pinus sylvestris, and its use as a potent and selective immunomodulatory agent capable of enhancing the proliferative phase of a primary T cell response in vivo. The invention is based on the discovery that PPC, when administered orally in a defined regimen and dosage, significantly increases the number of antigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cells during the early expansion phase of a primary immune response, without altering the subsequent contraction phase or the rate of activation-induced cell death. This effect is achieved through a mechanism that is independent of antioxidant activity, suppression of reactive oxygen species, or modulation of apoptosis-related gene expression, and instead appears to be mediated by the enhancement of T cell priming through interactions with antigen-presenting cells in secondary lymphoid tissues.

The PPC used in the invention is obtained from the mature, dried cones of Pinus sylvestris, commonly known as Scotch pine, which are harvested from commercially cultivated stands in regions where the species is indigenous and free from environmental contaminants. The cones are first subjected to a rigorous cleaning process involving repeated rinsing with deionized water to remove surface debris, dust, and potential microbial load. Following cleaning, the cones are mechanically shredded into small fragments to increase surface area and facilitate efficient extraction. The shredded material is then loaded into a stainless steel extraction vessel and subjected to an aqueous extraction protocol at a pH of 12.0 and a temperature of 121°C for a duration of 30 minutes. This high-pH, high-temperature extraction is critical for the solubilization of the polyphenylpropanoid-polysaccharide complexes, which are otherwise tightly bound within the lignocellulosic matrix of the pinecone. The resulting extract is clarified by centrifugation and filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane to remove particulate matter and potential microbial contaminants. The extract is then standardized using UV spectrophotometry at a wavelength of 280 nm, with the concentration of active polyphenylpropanoid components calculated using an extinction coefficient of 22.8 L·g⁻¹·cm⁻¹, a value empirically determined through calibration against purified polyphenolic standards. This standardization ensures batch-to-batch consistency in biological activity and enables precise dosing in experimental and clinical applications.

Following standardization, the liquid extract is spray-dried under controlled conditions to produce a stable, dark brown, odorless powder with a moisture content below 5%. The resulting powder is subjected to rigorous quality control testing to ensure the absence of endotoxin, heavy metals, pesticides, herbicides, and viable microorganisms. Endotoxin levels are measured using the Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) assay and must be below 0.1 EU/mg. Heavy metal content, including lead, mercury, cadmium, and arsenic, is quantified by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) and must not exceed permissible limits established by the United States Pharmacopeia (USP). Microbiological testing is performed according to the USP <61> and <62> standards, and the final product must be sterile and free from aerobic bacteria, yeast, mold, and coliforms. The powdered PPC is stored in sealed, light-resistant containers under desiccated conditions at room temperature and remains stable for at least 24 months without loss of biological activity.

The PPC is administered orally to a mammalian subject, preferably a human or a laboratory rodent such as a mouse, in a dosage range of 10 to 500 μg per kilogram of body weight, with a preferred dosage of 200 μg per kg. The administration is carried out in multiple doses over a period of 24 to 72 hours, beginning no later than 24 hours prior to the administration of an immunogenic stimulus and continuing for up to 48 hours thereafter. In a preferred embodiment, the PPC is administered three times daily—once in the morning, once in the afternoon, and once in the evening—for a total of six doses over two consecutive days. The PPC is suspended in sterile distilled water at a concentration of 200 μg/mL, and the suspension is freshly prepared each week to maintain potency. The suspension is administered via oral gavage using a calibrated feeding needle, ensuring precise delivery to the gastrointestinal tract. Alternative routes of administration include sublingual delivery, wherein the PPC powder is placed beneath the tongue and allowed to dissolve, or enteral administration via a nasogastric or gastrostomy tube in clinical settings. The PPC may also be formulated into capsules, tablets, or chewable tablets for ease of administration in human subjects.

The immunogenic stimulus with which PPC is co-administered may be any agent capable of initiating a T cell-mediated immune response. In a preferred embodiment, the immunogenic stimulus is a protein antigen, such as ovalbumin, influenza hemagglutinin, or a tumor-associated antigen such as MART-1, gp100, or NY-ESO-1. In another embodiment, the immunogenic stimulus is a nucleic acid vaccine encoding a target antigen, such as a plasmid DNA or mRNA construct. In a further embodiment, the immunogenic stimulus is a dendritic cell vaccine, wherein autologous dendritic cells are loaded ex vivo with antigen and reinfused into the subject. In yet another embodiment, the immunogenic stimulus is a live attenuated or inactivated pathogen, such as a viral vector, bacterial vaccine, or whole-cell tumor lysate. The immunogenic stimulus is typically administered via intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intradermal injection, although mucosal routes such as intranasal or oral delivery may also be employed depending on the nature of the target pathogen or disease.

The mechanism by which PPC enhances T cell expansion is not dependent on the suppression of apoptosis or the inhibition of reactive oxygen species. While prior art has suggested that polyphenolic compounds may function as antioxidants and thereby protect activated T cells from oxidative stress-induced death, the present invention demonstrates conclusively that this is not the case. Experiments using flow cytometry to measure intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) in CD3+ T cells from lymph nodes of SEB-immunized mice revealed that PPC administration significantly reduced ROS levels. However, this reduction did not correlate with increased expression of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2, nor did it alter the expression of pro-apoptotic genes such as Bim or Bax. Intracellular staining for Bcl-2 protein in Vβ8+ T cells showed no difference in mean fluorescence intensity between mice treated with SEB alone and those treated with SEB plus PPC, indicating that the level of Bcl-2 per cell was unchanged. Similarly, quantitative real-time PCR analysis of mRNA expression for Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, Bcl-3, Bax, and Bim in lymph node cells revealed no significant differences in transcript levels between the SEB and SEB+PPC groups, except for a marked increase in Bim expression in both groups, consistent with the known induction of activation-induced cell death. Annexin V staining confirmed that the rate of apoptosis in Vβ8+ T cells was identical between the SEB and SEB+PPC groups, further supporting the conclusion that PPC does not inhibit apoptosis.

Instead, the invention demonstrates that PPC enhances the primary T cell response by increasing the number of antigen-specific T cells that enter the proliferative phase. In the SEB model, where a defined population of Vβ8+ T cells is activated synchronously, the number of CD4+/Vβ8+ and CD8+/Vβ8+ T cells in the inguinal lymph nodes was doubled in mice treated with PPC compared to those receiving SEB alone, at the 48-hour time point, which corresponds to the peak of T cell expansion. This increase was not observed in mice treated with PPC alone, indicating that the effect is antigen-dependent. The number of T cells at the 96-hour time point, corresponding to the contraction phase, remained elevated in the CD8+ population in the PPC-treated group, but this was attributable to the larger initial pool of cells rather than to enhanced survival. The CD4+ population contracted to baseline levels in both groups, consistent with the known susceptibility of CD4+ T cells to activation-induced apoptosis. The increased number of CD8+ T cells at 96 hours was therefore a direct consequence of the greater number of cells generated during the expansion phase, not due to reduced cell death.

The invention further demonstrates that the effect of PPC is not mediated by changes in cytokine production per se. While serum levels of IFNγ were significantly elevated in the SEB+PPC group compared to the SEB-only group, and IL-2 levels were reduced, these changes were secondary to the increased number of activated T cells rather than a primary driver of expansion. The levels of IL-12p70 remained unchanged, indicating that PPC does not act through classical dendritic cell maturation pathways involving IL-12 secretion. Furthermore, the administration of PPC alone did not induce any detectable change in T cell numbers or cytokine profiles, indicating that the effect is strictly dependent on the presence of an immunogenic stimulus.

The invention further provides evidence that PPC may enhance the migration or antigen-presenting capacity of dendritic cells in vivo. Preliminary data indicate an increase in the number of CD11c+ MHC class II+ dendritic cells in the lymph nodes of mice treated with SEB+PPC compared to those treated with SEB alone. This suggests that PPC may promote the recruitment of dendritic cells from peripheral tissues to the lymph nodes or enhance their survival and antigen-presenting function within the lymphoid microenvironment. Further studies are ongoing to determine whether PPC directly binds to dendritic cell surface receptors, such as Toll-like receptors or C-type lectins, or whether it acts indirectly by modulating the chemokine milieu in the lymph node.

The PPC composition of the invention is uniquely suited for clinical translation due to its oral bioavailability, stability, and lack of toxicity. In preclinical studies, no adverse effects were observed in mice receiving daily doses of PPC for up to 30 days, including no changes in body weight, organ weights, serum biochemistry, or histopathology of liver, kidney, spleen, or lymph nodes. The absence of endotoxin and microbial contamination ensures that the immunomodulatory effects are attributable to PPC and not to contaminating pathogen-associated molecular patterns. The standardized extraction and drying process ensures that each batch of PPC possesses consistent chemical and biological properties, making it suitable for use in regulated clinical trials and commercial pharmaceutical products.

The invention is particularly useful in the context of cancer immunotherapy, where the generation of a large pool of tumor-specific CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes is critical for tumor control. By enhancing the proliferative phase of the primary T cell response, PPC increases the number of tumor-reactive T cells available for differentiation into memory cells and effector cells, thereby improving the likelihood of durable anti-tumor immunity. The invention is also applicable to chronic viral infections, where T cell exhaustion is a major barrier to viral clearance, and to vaccine development, where the magnitude of the primary response directly correlates with the strength of the secondary response. The invention thus provides a novel, safe, and effective means of enhancing adaptive immunity without disrupting the natural regulatory mechanisms of T cell homeostasis.

## EXAMPLES

### Preparation of PPC

The preparation of the polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) is carried out in a controlled, GMP-compliant manufacturing environment to ensure reproducibility, purity, and safety. Mature, dried cones of Pinus sylvestris are procured from a certified supplier who maintains strict documentation of geographic origin, harvest date, and environmental conditions. The cones are visually inspected and sorted to remove any material showing signs of fungal growth, insect damage, or chemical contamination. The selected cones are then washed under running deionized water for a minimum of 15 minutes, followed by immersion in a 5% aqueous solution of hydrogen peroxide for 30 minutes to reduce microbial load. After rinsing with sterile deionized water, the cones are air-dried at ambient temperature for 48 hours and then mechanically shredded using a industrial-grade hammer mill to produce fragments of approximately 2–5 mm in size.

The shredded pinecone material is transferred to a 100-liter stainless steel extraction vessel equipped with temperature and pH control systems. Deionized water is added to achieve a 1:10 (w/v) ratio of pinecone material to solvent. The pH of the solution is adjusted to 12.0 using aqueous sodium hydroxide, and the mixture is heated to 121°C under pressure in a closed-loop system for a duration of 30 minutes. The elevated temperature and alkaline conditions facilitate the hydrolysis of lignin and hemicellulose matrices, allowing the release of polyphenylpropanoid-polysaccharide complexes into solution. Following extraction, the slurry is cooled to room temperature and filtered through a series of progressively finer filters, beginning with a 100 μm mesh and culminating in a 0.22 μm membrane filter to remove particulate matter and microbial contaminants.

The clarified extract is then analyzed by UV spectrophotometry using a dual-beam spectrophotometer calibrated with a reference blank of deionized water. The absorbance at 280 nm is measured in a 1 cm pathlength cuvette, and the concentration of polyphenylpropanoid components is calculated using the equation: PPC concentration (mg/mL) = OD280 × (1/22.8 L·g⁻¹·cm⁻¹). The extinction coefficient of 22.8 L·g⁻¹·cm⁻¹ was determined empirically by purifying a reference standard of polyphenylpropanoid monomers from the extract via preparative HPLC and measuring their absorbance under identical conditions. The extract is then concentrated under reduced pressure at 40°C to reduce volume by 75%, and the resulting solution is spray-dried using a Buchi B-290 spray dryer equipped with a two-fluid nozzle. The inlet temperature is maintained at 180°C, the outlet temperature at 95°C, and the feed rate at 15 mL/min. The resulting powder is collected in a cyclone separator and stored in sealed, aluminum-lined bags under desiccated conditions at 25°C.

The final PPC powder is subjected to a battery of analytical tests to confirm identity, purity, and stability. High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) with diode array detection is used to profile the polyphenolic constituents, and the presence of characteristic proanthocyanidin and phenylpropanoid peaks is confirmed by comparison with authentic standards. Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) confirms the presence of polysaccharide functional groups, including hydroxyl and glycosidic linkages. The powder is tested for endotoxin using the LAL assay, with a limit of detection of 0.01 EU/mL; all batches must contain less than 0.1 EU/mg. Heavy metal analysis is performed by ICP-MS, with limits set at 0.5 ppm for lead, 0.1 ppm for mercury, 0.1 ppm for cadmium, and 0.5 ppm for arsenic. Microbiological testing follows USP <61> and <62>, and all batches must be negative for total aerobic microbial count, total yeast and mold count, and coliforms. The powder is stable for at least 24 months when stored at room temperature in a desiccator, with no significant change in UV absorbance, HPLC profile, or biological activity in functional assays.

### In Vivo Immune Stimulation

Six- to seven-week-old male Balb/c mice are obtained from Charles River Laboratories and acclimated for a minimum of five days under specific pathogen-free conditions in individually ventilated cages maintained at 22°C with a 12-hour light/dark cycle. All procedures are conducted in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and under protocols approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee. Mice are provided ad libitum access to a standard chow diet containing 22% protein and 5% fat, and sterile water is available at all times. For immune stimulation experiments, mice are randomly assigned to one of four experimental groups: Naive (no treatment), SEB (intraperitoneal injection of staphylococcal enterotoxin B), SEB+PPC (SEB plus oral PPC), and PPC only (oral PPC without SEB). Each group consists of three mice to ensure statistical power while minimizing animal use.

On Day 0, mice in the SEB and SEB+PPC groups receive an intraperitoneal injection of 100 μg of SEB (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO) dissolved in 100 μL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4). Mice in the Naive group receive 100 μL of PBS alone. Mice in the SEB+PPC and PPC-only groups receive oral gavage of 100 μL of a 200 μg/mL PPC suspension three times daily on Day 0 and again three times daily on Day 1, for a total of six doses. The PPC suspension is freshly prepared each week by dissolving the powdered PPC in sterile distilled water and filtering through a 0.2 μm nylon filter. The gavage volume is calibrated to deliver 20 μg of PPC per dose, corresponding to a dose of 200 μg/kg for a 20-gram mouse. Water in the cages of the SEB+PPC and PPC-only groups is supplemented with 200 μg/mL PPC to ensure continuous exposure, whereas water in the Naive and SEB-only groups remains untreated. Mice are monitored daily for signs of distress, weight loss, or abnormal behavior, and none are observed to exhibit any adverse effects throughout the study.

At 48 or 96 hours after SEB injection, mice are euthanized by inhalation of carbon dioxide in a controlled chamber, followed by cervical dislocation to ensure humane death. The inguinal lymph nodes are aseptically excised and pooled per group to obtain sufficient cell numbers for downstream analyses. The spleen is also harvested and processed separately. Serum is collected by cardiac puncture, allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 minutes, and then centrifuged at 1,500 × g for 10 minutes. The supernatant is aliquoted and stored at −80°C until cytokine analysis. All tissue samples are processed immediately following euthanasia to preserve cellular integrity and prevent degradation of intracellular proteins and mRNA.

### ELISA Assays

Serum samples collected from mice in each experimental group are thawed on ice and centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 5 minutes to remove any residual cellular debris. Standard enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits specific for murine IL-2, IFNγ, and IL-12p70 are obtained from PeproTech (Rocky Hill, NJ) and used according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 96-well microtiter plates are coated overnight at 4°C with capture antibodies specific for each cytokine. Plates are washed three times with PBS containing 0.05% Tween-20 and blocked with 1% bovine serum albumin in PBS for one hour at room temperature. Serum samples are diluted 1:2 in assay diluent and added in duplicate to the wells, along with a serial dilution of recombinant murine cytokine standards ranging from 0 to 1,000 pg/mL. Plates are incubated for two hours at room temperature, washed again, and then incubated with biotinylated detection antibodies for one hour. Following another wash, streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase conjugate is added and incubated for 30 minutes. The reaction is developed using tetramethylbenzidine substrate, and the reaction is stopped with 2 M sulfuric acid. Absorbance is measured at 450 nm using a BioTek μQuant plate spectrophotometer with a reference wavelength of 630 nm.

Standard curves are generated using GraphPad Prism software by plotting the mean absorbance of each standard concentration against its logarithmic value. The concentration of cytokines in each sample is interpolated from the standard curve using a four-parameter logistic regression model. All samples are run in duplicate, and the mean value is used for statistical analysis. The lower limit of detection for IL-2 is 15 pg/mL, for IFNγ is 10 pg/mL, and for IL-12p70 is 5 pg/mL. Results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and statistical significance is determined using one-way ANOVA with Newman-Keuls post-hoc testing. The assay demonstrates high reproducibility, with intra-assay coefficients of variation below 8% and inter-assay coefficients below 12%.

### Lymph Node and Spleen Cell Isolation

Inguinal lymph nodes and spleens are harvested from euthanized mice and placed in sterile 4 mL of Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS) supplemented with 1% fetal calf serum and 1× penicillin/streptomycin. The tissues are transferred to a sterile plastic bag and homogenized using a Seward Stomacher® 80 set to medium speed for 60 seconds to generate a single-cell suspension. The homogenate is then transferred to a 15 mL conical tube and centrifuged at 300 × g for 5 minutes at 4°C. The supernatant is discarded, and the cell pellet is resuspended in 2 mL of HBSS for lymph node cells or 4 mL of HBSS for spleen cells. Cell concentration and viability are determined using the Millipore Muse Cell Analyzer with Muse Count and Viability reagent, which simultaneously quantifies total and viable cell numbers based on membrane integrity and DNA content. The cell suspension is adjusted to a final concentration of 4 × 10⁶ cells/mL in complete RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal calf serum, 1× penicillin/streptomycin, and 1 mM sodium pyruvate. Cells are kept on ice until used for flow cytometry, apoptosis assays, or gene expression analysis. All manipulations are performed under sterile conditions in a laminar flow hood to prevent contamination.

### Flow Cytometry

For flow cytometric analysis, 1 × 10⁶ lymph node cells are transferred to 1.5 mL microfuge tubes and centrifuged at 1,000 × g for 1 minute. The supernatant is aspirated, and the cell pellet is resuspended in 100 μL of FACS buffer (PBS containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 0.1% sodium azide) supplemented with 1 μL of anti-CD16/32 Fc blocking antibody (clone 93, eBioscience). The cells are incubated on ice for 10 minutes to block non-specific Fc receptor binding. Fluorescently labeled monoclonal antibodies specific for murine CD3 (APC-conjugated, clone 17A2), CD4 (FITC-conjugated, clone GK1.5), CD8 (PE-conjugated, clone SK1), and Vβ8 (PerCP-eFluor®710-conjugated, clone KJ16-133) are added at concentrations of 1–5 μL per tube, depending on antibody titer and manufacturer recommendations. The tubes are incubated on ice in the dark for 30 minutes, after which 500 μL of FACS buffer is added, and the cells are centrifuged at 1,000 × g for 2 minutes. The supernatant is removed, and the pellet is resuspended in 200 μL of FACS buffer. The cells are analyzed using a BD FACSCalibur flow cytometer equipped with CellQuest Pro software. Data are collected for a minimum of 10,000 events per sample, and gating is performed sequentially to identify lymphocytes based on forward and side scatter, then CD3+ T cells, followed by CD4+ and CD8+ subsets, and finally Vβ8+ T cells within each population. Data are analyzed using FloJo software (TreeStar Inc., Ashland, CA), and the percentage and absolute number of antigen-specific T cells are calculated based on the total number of viable lymph node cells determined by the Muse Cell Analyzer. All samples are run in duplicate, and results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation.

### Detection of Apoptosis

To detect early apoptosis, lymph node cells are stained using the Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit (eBioscience) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. One million cells per mouse are resuspended in 100 μL of Annexin V binding buffer containing 1 μL of APC-labeled anti-CD3 antibody, 5 μL of PerCP-eFluor®710-labeled anti-Vβ8 antibody, and 1 μL of FITC-labeled Annexin V. The mixture is incubated at room temperature for 10 minutes in the dark, after which 500 μL of binding buffer is added and the cells are centrifuged at 1,000 × g for 2 minutes. The pellet is resuspended in 200 μL of binding buffer and analyzed by flow cytometry using the same instrument and gating strategy as described above. Annexin V-positive cells are identified as those exhibiting FITC fluorescence above the isotype control threshold, while propidium iodide exclusion is used to distinguish early apoptotic (Annexin V+/PI−) from late apoptotic or necrotic (Annexin V+/PI+) cells. The percentage of Annexin V+ Vβ8+ T cells is calculated for each group, and statistical significance is determined using ANOVA with post-hoc testing. No significant difference in Annexin V staining is observed between the SEB and SEB+PPC groups, confirming that PPC does not inhibit activation-induced apoptosis.

### Detection of Reactive Oxygen Species

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels in lymph node T cells are measured using CM-H2DCFDA (5-(and-6)-chloromethyl-2’,7’-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate, acetyl ester; Invitrogen). One million lymph node cells per mouse are resuspended in 100 μL of FACS buffer containing 1 μL of APC-labeled anti-CD3, 5 μL of PerCP-eFluor®710-labeled anti-Vβ8, and 2 μL of a 50 μM solution of CM-H2DCFDA. The suspension is incubated at 37°C for 15 minutes in a humidified 5% CO₂ incubator to allow cellular uptake and enzymatic cleavage of the acetate groups. The reaction is halted by placing the tubes on ice, and cells are washed twice with 500 μL of FACS buffer by centrifugation at 1,000 × g for 2 minutes. The final pellet is resuspended in 200 μL of FACS buffer and analyzed by flow cytometry. The median fluorescence intensity (MFI) of CD3+ Vβ8+ T cells is recorded, and the percentage of ROS-positive cells is defined as those exceeding the fluorescence threshold of the naive control group. Results are presented as both the percentage of ROS+ cells and as histograms of fluorescence intensity distribution. A significant reduction in ROS levels is observed in the SEB+PPC group compared to the SEB-only group, but this reduction does not correlate with altered apoptosis or Bcl-2 expression.

### Detection of Intracellular Bcl-2

To assess intracellular levels of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2, lymph node cells previously stained for CD3 and Vβ8 are fixed and permeabilized using 0.03% saponin in HBSS (HBSS-SAP). After centrifugation at 1,000 × g for 2 minutes, the pellet is resuspended in 100 μL of HBSS-SAP containing 1 μL of FITC-labeled anti-Bcl-2 antibody (clone 10C4, eBioscience) and incubated on ice for 60 minutes in the dark. The cells are then washed twice with 500 μL of HBSS-SAP and once with HBSS alone to remove unbound antibody. The final pellet is resuspended in 200 μL of HBSS and analyzed by flow cytometry. The mean fluorescence intensity of Bcl-2 staining within the CD3+ Vβ8+ T cell population is recorded for each group. No significant difference in Bcl-2 MFI is observed between the SEB and SEB+PPC groups, indicating that PPC does not alter the intracellular concentration of Bcl-2 per cell. In contrast, Bcl-2 levels in CD3+ Vβ8− T cells remain unchanged across all groups, confirming the specificity of SEB for Vβ8+ T cells.

### Analysis of Gene Expression

Total RNA is isolated from lymph node cells using the RNeasy Plus Mini Kit (Qiagen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Cells are lysed in RLT buffer containing β-mercaptoethanol, and lysates are passed through a gDNA eliminator column to remove genomic DNA contamination. RNA is eluted in 30 μL of RNase-free water, and concentration and purity are assessed by spectrophotometry at 260 nm and 280 nm, with a minimum A260/A280 ratio of 1.9 required for inclusion. One microgram of total RNA is reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the QuantiTect Reverse Transcription Kit (Qiagen), with a control reaction omitting reverse transcriptase to confirm the absence of genomic DNA contamination. One microliter of cDNA is used as template in quantitative real-time PCR reactions performed using SABiosciences RT2 Profiler SYBR Green Master Mix and validated murine primer pairs for Bcl-2, Bcl-3, Bcl-xL, Bax, and Bim. Each primer pair is pre-mixed at 10 μM, and reactions are performed in duplicate in a 96-well plate. The thermal cycling protocol consists of an initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 1 minute. Amplification is monitored using a BioRad iCycler, and cycle threshold (Ct) values are recorded. Relative gene expression is calculated using the ΔΔCt method, with β-actin as the housekeeping gene and the naive group as the calibrator. Results are expressed as fold change relative to naive controls. No significant difference in Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, or Bax expression is observed between SEB and SEB+PPC groups, whereas Bim expression is elevated in both groups compared to naive, consistent with activation-induced apoptosis. Bcl-3 expression is unchanged in SEB-treated mice but significantly elevated in PPC-only mice, suggesting a distinct immunomodulatory effect in the absence of antigenic stimulation.
