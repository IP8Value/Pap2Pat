# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of vaccinology and immunology, specifically to novel vaccine compositions and methods for inducing protective immune responses against multiple arboviral pathogens and orthopoxviruses using a replication-deficient poxvirus vector platform. The invention encompasses a recombinant vaccine based on the Copenhagen strain of vaccinia virus (VACV), genetically modified to lack the D13L gene encoding the essential viral assembly protein D13, thereby rendering the vector incapable of producing infectious progeny in non-complementing host cells. This vector, referred to herein as the Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV), is engineered to express structural antigenic cassettes derived from Zika virus (ZIKV) and chikungunya virus (CHIKV), enabling the induction of broad and durable neutralizing antibody responses against both arboviruses in a single immunization regimen. The invention further includes methods for manufacturing the vaccine using Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells stably transfected with the D13L gene and the host range protein CP77, thereby overcoming historical limitations associated with the use of primary chicken embryo fibroblasts in poxvirus-based vaccine production. The vaccine composition and its administration route, dosage, and schedule are specifically configured to elicit robust humoral immunity sufficient to prevent viremia following challenge with pathogenic strains of ZIKV and to confer cross-neutralizing protection against multiple genotypes of CHIKV, including Asian, West African, and Indian Ocean lineage isolates. The invention also provides for the concurrent induction of anti-VACV neutralizing antibodies, suggesting potential utility as a next-generation smallpox vaccine. The platform is uniquely suited for single-dose or homologous prime-boost regimens in human populations, eliminating the need for heterologous prime-boost strategies commonly required by other viral vector systems, and is compatible with cold chain-independent storage and needle-free delivery modalities.

## BACKGROUND

Vaccination remains the most effective public health intervention for preventing infectious disease, particularly those caused by arthropod-borne viruses that pose significant global health threats. Among these, chikungunya virus (CHIKV) and Zika virus (ZIKV) have emerged as major public health concerns due to their rapid geographic expansion, high rates of morbidity, and severe clinical consequences, including chronic arthralgia and congenital neurological abnormalities, respectively. CHIKV has caused explosive outbreaks affecting millions across Africa, Asia, and the Americas, with the most recent pandemic originating in 2004 and spreading to over 100 countries. ZIKV, which was declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern by the World Health Organization in 2016, is now recognized as the causative agent of Congenital Zika Syndrome, a spectrum of fetal neurodevelopmental defects including microcephaly, ocular abnormalities, and limb contractures. Both viruses are transmitted primarily by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes, which co-circulate in tropical and subtropical regions, leading to frequent co-infections in human populations and complicating disease surveillance and control efforts. Despite extensive international efforts, no licensed vaccine is currently available for either pathogen, and existing vaccine candidates have faced challenges related to immunogenicity, durability, cross-strain coverage, manufacturing scalability, and safety in vulnerable populations such as pregnant women.

Traditional vaccine platforms, including inactivated whole-virus preparations and subunit vaccines, have demonstrated limited success in eliciting robust and long-lasting neutralizing antibody responses, often requiring multiple doses and adjuvants to achieve protective immunity. Live attenuated vaccines, while potent, carry inherent risks of reversion, neurotropism, and teratogenicity, particularly concerning for ZIKV in pregnant women. Viral vector platforms, particularly those based on adenoviruses and poxviruses, have emerged as promising alternatives due to their ability to present antigens in a native conformation, stimulate both humoral and cellular immunity, and accommodate large foreign gene inserts. Among poxvirus vectors, Modified Vaccinia Ankara (MVA) and ALVAC have been extensively studied and deployed in clinical trials for a variety of infectious diseases, including HIV, malaria, and Ebola. However, these vectors are typically replication-deficient in human cells due to extensive genomic deletions accumulated during attenuation, and they have consistently demonstrated poor immunogenicity when administered as stand-alone vaccines in naïve individuals. As a result, heterologous prime-boost regimens—such as DNA priming followed by MVA boosting—have become the standard approach to overcome this limitation, despite the increased complexity, cost, and logistical burden associated with multiple injections and distinct manufacturing processes.

The production of poxvirus-based vaccines has historically relied on primary chicken embryo fibroblasts (CEFs), a system fraught with regulatory, scalability, and safety challenges. CEFs are not amenable to large-scale, consistent, and GMP-compliant manufacturing due to their primary cell nature, batch-to-batch variability, and potential for contamination with avian retroviruses or other adventitious agents. Furthermore, the use of animal-derived components in culture media introduces additional regulatory scrutiny and complicates the approval pathway for human use. The development of continuous cell lines capable of supporting high-titer poxvirus propagation under serum-free and protein-free conditions represents a critical advancement in vaccine manufacturing. Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells, widely used in the biopharmaceutical industry for the production of monoclonal antibodies, recombinant proteins, and other biologics, offer a superior alternative due to their well-characterized genetics, scalability in bioreactors, compatibility with defined culture media, and established regulatory acceptance by global health authorities.

A novel poxvirus vector platform, the Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV), has been developed to address these longstanding challenges. SCV is derived from the Copenhagen strain of vaccinia virus, a historically potent and immunogenic strain, but is genetically engineered to lack the D13L gene, which encodes the major structural protein responsible for the formation of the viral envelope during morphogenesis. This deletion renders SCV incapable of producing infectious virions in standard mammalian cells, thereby eliminating the risk of systemic replication and dissemination in vaccine recipients. However, when propagated in complementing CHO cell lines engineered to stably express the D13L protein and the host range protein CP77, SCV achieves high-titer yields suitable for vaccine production. Importantly, SCV retains the full immunogenic potential of the parental Copenhagen strain, including the ability to activate innate immune pathways, promote dendritic cell maturation, and induce strong B and T cell responses. Previous studies in murine models have demonstrated that a single immunization with an SCV-based bivalent vaccine encoding CHIKV and ZIKV structural antigens induces potent neutralizing antibodies and confers complete protection against lethal challenge with both viruses. However, the translational relevance of these findings in non-human primates—the gold standard for predicting human immune responses—remained unproven.

The prior art lacks a vaccine platform that simultaneously achieves the following: (1) the capacity to induce high-titer, durable, and broadly cross-neutralizing antibody responses against multiple arboviral genotypes following a single or homologous prime-boost regimen; (2) the use of a replication-deficient poxvirus vector that is non-pathogenic in immunocompromised hosts; (3) scalable, GMP-compatible manufacturing in a continuous mammalian cell line devoid of animal-derived components; (4) the ability to elicit protective immunity without the need for heterologous priming; and (5) the induction of cross-reactive immunity against orthopoxviruses, thereby enabling dual-purpose protection against both arboviruses and smallpox. The present invention fulfills these unmet needs by demonstrating, for the first time in a non-human primate model, that a single administration of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK induces robust neutralizing antibody responses against both ZIKV and CHIKV, confers significant protection against ZIKV viremia, and elicits cross-genotype neutralizing immunity against multiple CHIKV isolates, all while maintaining an excellent safety profile and enabling manufacturing in a clinically relevant cell system. Furthermore, the invention demonstrates that homologous boosting with the same vector significantly enhances the magnitude and breadth of the immune response, obviating the necessity for complex multi-platform regimens. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in the design and deployment of next-generation multivalent vaccines for global infectious disease threats.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel vaccine composition comprising a replication-deficient poxvirus vector, specifically the Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV), engineered to express structural antigenic cassettes derived from Zika virus (ZIKV) and chikungunya virus (CHIKV). The SCV vector is genetically modified to lack the D13L gene, which encodes the essential viral envelope protein D13, thereby rendering the vector incapable of producing infectious progeny in non-complementing mammalian cells. The vector is propagated in a Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cell line that has been stably transfected with the D13L gene and the host range protein CP77, enabling high-yield production under serum-free and protein-free conditions. The vaccine composition is formulated as a sterile, lyophilized or liquid suspension suitable for parenteral administration, containing a therapeutically effective amount of SCV particles encoding the CHIKV structural polyprotein cassette (C-E3-E2-6K-E1) derived from the 2006 Reunion Island Indian Ocean lineage isolate (06–021) and the ZIKV prME polyprotein derived from the 2015 Brazilian isolate SPH2015. The vaccine is designed to be administered via intramuscular injection in a single dose of at least 10⁷ plaque-forming units (pfu) or in a homologous prime-boost regimen comprising two doses administered at an interval of at least 21 days, each dose containing at least 10⁸ pfu.

The invention further provides a method for inducing protective immunity against ZIKV and CHIKV infection in a mammalian subject, comprising administering the vaccine composition as described above. In one embodiment, the method comprises a single intramuscular administration of 10⁷ pfu of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK, which induces neutralizing antibody titers against ZIKV and CHIKV sufficient to reduce serum viral load below the limit of reliable quantification following challenge with pathogenic ZIKV strain PRVABC59. In another embodiment, the method comprises two intramuscular administrations of 10⁸ pfu of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK at an interval of 35 days, which results in a significant increase in neutralizing antibody titers against both ZIKV and CHIKV, with a fold increase of at least five-fold for ZIKV and at least 40-fold for CHIKV Asian genotype isolates, and confers complete protection against detectable viremia following ZIKV challenge. The invention further provides a method for inducing broad cross-neutralizing immunity against multiple CHIKV genotypes, wherein the neutralizing antibody response evolves over time to encompass isolates from the Asian, West African, and Indian Ocean lineages, with the response to Asian isolates exhibiting delayed but accelerated boosting kinetics compared to other lineages, resulting in equivalent neutralizing titers across all genotypes by day 70 post-initial vaccination.

The invention further provides a method for inducing protective immunity against orthopoxvirus infection, wherein administration of the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine induces neutralizing antibodies against vaccinia virus (Western Reserve strain) with a mean reciprocal 50% plaque reduction neutralization titer of at least 79 by day 70 following two doses of 10⁸ pfu, comparable to titers induced by licensed MVA and ACAM2000 smallpox vaccines. The vaccine composition is non-pathogenic in immunocompromised hosts, does not induce fever, weight loss, or systemic adverse events, and is associated with only rare, non-specific cutaneous reactions that are not causally linked to the vaccine. The manufacturing process for the vaccine is carried out in a continuous CHO cell line under defined, serum-free conditions, eliminating the need for primary chicken embryo fibroblasts and enabling scalable, GMP-compliant production. The vaccine is stable under refrigerated conditions and is amenable to lyophilization, enabling cold chain-independent distribution. The invention further provides a method for reducing the burden of multiple vaccinations by enabling a single-dose or homologous prime-boost regimen to confer protection against two distinct arboviral pathogens and one orthopoxvirus, thereby simplifying immunization schedules, improving compliance, and reducing overall healthcare costs.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Construction of Vaccines

The vaccine composition of the present invention is constructed through a series of precise genetic, cellular, and biochemical manipulations designed to ensure safety, immunogenicity, manufacturability, and stability. The foundational component of the vaccine is the Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV), a replication-deficient poxvirus derived from the Copenhagen strain of vaccinia virus (VACV). The Copenhagen strain was selected for its high immunogenicity, robust replication kinetics in permissive cells, and historical use in smallpox vaccination campaigns, which demonstrated an excellent safety profile in millions of individuals. To render SCV replication-deficient in standard mammalian cells, the D13L gene was precisely deleted from the viral genome using homologous recombination techniques. The D13L gene encodes a 25-kDa structural protein that is essential for the formation of the viral envelope during morphogenesis; its absence prevents the assembly of mature, infectious virions in non-complementing cells. This deletion was confirmed by whole-genome sequencing, PCR amplification across the deletion junction, and functional assays demonstrating the absence of extracellular enveloped virions in infected cell lysates. The resulting SCV genome retains all other open reading frames, including those encoding immunomodulatory proteins, structural antigens, and replication machinery, thereby preserving the full antigenic and immunostimulatory capacity of the parental virus.

The SCV vector is engineered to express two distinct recombinant antigenic cassettes derived from the structural proteins of chikungunya virus (CHIKV) and Zika virus (ZIKV). The CHIKV cassette comprises the entire structural polyprotein precursor C-E3-E2-6K-E1, derived from the 2006 Reunion Island Indian Ocean lineage isolate 06–021 (GenBank: AM258992), which encodes a 1249-amino acid polyprotein. This cassette is inserted into the thymidine kinase (TK) locus of the SCV genome under the control of the synthetic early/late VACV promoter p7.5, ensuring robust expression during both early and late phases of the viral replication cycle. The ZIKV cassette comprises the prME polyprotein (pre-membrane and envelope proteins), derived from the 2015 Brazilian isolate SPH2015 (GenBank: KU321639), which encodes a 692-amino acid precursor. This cassette is inserted into the vaccinia virus hemagglutinin (HA) locus under the control of the synthetic late promoter p11, which ensures high-level expression during the late phase of infection, coinciding with maximal virion assembly and antigen presentation. The insertion sites were selected to minimize disruption of essential viral genes and to allow for independent transcription and processing of each antigenic cassette. The resulting recombinant virus, designated SCV-ZIKA/CHIK, expresses both CHIKV and ZIKV antigens as polyproteins that are cleaved by host and viral proteases into their respective mature structural proteins, thereby preserving native conformational epitopes critical for the induction of neutralizing antibodies.

The SCV-ZIKA/CHIK virus is propagated in a genetically engineered Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cell line, designated SCS-1, which has been stably transfected with two expression constructs: one encoding the full-length D13L gene under the control of a constitutive cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter, and another encoding the host range protein CP77 under the control of a simian virus 40 (SV40) promoter. The D13L transgene complements the deletion in the viral genome, enabling the assembly and release of infectious SCV particles. The CP77 transgene enhances viral replication efficiency in CHO cells by counteracting host antiviral restriction factors, thereby increasing viral yield without altering the replication-deficient phenotype in non-complementing cells. The SCS-1 cell line was derived from CHO-S cells, a suspension-adapted variant widely used in biopharmaceutical manufacturing, and has been extensively characterized for karyotype stability, absence of adventitious agents, and compatibility with serum-free, protein-free culture media. The cells are maintained in a chemically defined, animal-component-free medium (e.g., CD CHO, Thermo Fisher Scientific) in stirred-tank bioreactors under controlled conditions of temperature, pH, and dissolved oxygen. Infection is initiated at a low multiplicity of infection (MOI ≤ 0.01) to minimize the risk of recombination events and to ensure high fidelity of recombinant virus propagation. Infected cells are harvested at 48 to 72 hours post-infection, when viral titers peak and cellular lysis is minimal.

The harvested cell culture supernatant and cell lysates are subjected to a multi-step purification process to isolate SCV particles free of cellular debris, host proteins, and endotoxin. First, the lysate is clarified by low-speed centrifugation to remove intact cells and large aggregates. The supernatant is then concentrated and purified by ultracentrifugation through a 36% sucrose cushion, which separates intact virions from smaller contaminants based on buoyant density. The resulting viral pellet is resuspended in a sterile, endotoxin-free buffer comprising 10 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0) and 150 mM NaCl, and subjected to tangential flow filtration (TFF) for buffer exchange and concentration. The final formulation is sterile-filtered through a 0.22 µm membrane and tested for sterility, mycoplasma contamination, endotoxin levels, and residual host cell DNA and protein. The vaccine is formulated at a concentration of 10⁸ plaque-forming units per 0.5 mL, with an overfill of 100 µL to ensure accurate dosing. The formulation may be stored as a liquid suspension at −80°C or lyophilized using a cryoprotectant such as sucrose or trehalose to enable long-term storage at 2–8°C. The lyophilized formulation is reconstituted immediately prior to administration with sterile water for injection.

The identity and integrity of the recombinant virus are confirmed by multiple analytical methods. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) with primers specific to the CHIKV and ZIKV cassettes confirms the presence of the inserted genes, while absence of wild-type SCV is confirmed by amplification across the D13L deletion junction. Next-generation sequencing of the entire viral genome verifies the absence of unintended mutations and confirms the stability of the inserted cassettes after multiple passages. Immunoblot analysis of infected SCS-1 cells demonstrates the expression of full-length CHIKV E2 and E1 proteins and ZIKV prM and E proteins, with appropriate molecular weights and post-translational modifications. Electron microscopy confirms the presence of mature, enveloped poxvirus particles with characteristic brick-shaped morphology, and immunogold labeling with anti-CHIKV and anti-ZIKV antibodies confirms the incorporation of the recombinant antigens into the viral envelope. The final product is released for use only after meeting predefined specifications for potency, purity, sterility, and stability, as defined by regulatory guidelines for viral vaccines.

### Example 2

In a controlled, blinded, randomized study conducted in Indian rhesus macaques (Macaca mulatta), the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine were evaluated following single and homologous prime-boost regimens. A total of sixteen adult animals, aged between one and six years and weighing between 2.0 and 8.0 kilograms, were stratified by sex and body weight and randomly assigned to four experimental groups: (1) a single-dose group receiving 10⁷ plaque-forming units (pfu) of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK (n = 5); (2) a two-dose group receiving 10⁸ pfu of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK at days 0 and 35 (n = 5); (3) a control group receiving two doses of 10⁸ pfu of SCV-control, a replication-deficient SCV vector lacking any foreign antigen inserts (n = 4); and (4) a positive control group receiving two doses of formalin-inactivated ZIKV PRVABC59 (n = 2). Vaccinations were administered via intramuscular injection into the right quadriceps muscle in a volume of 0.5 mL, using aseptic technique and a 22-gauge needle. All animals were monitored daily for clinical signs of morbidity, including changes in body weight, appetite, activity level, and temperature. No fever, weight loss, or systemic adverse events were observed in any animal following vaccination. A single animal in the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK 10⁷ pfu group developed a mild, bilateral, flat, erythematous rash in the inguinal region by day 35, which persisted throughout the study but was not associated with systemic inflammation, histopathological changes, or elevated inflammatory markers. This finding was consistent with previously documented, non-specific cutaneous reactions observed in naïve non-human primates housed in conditions of prolonged sitting and reduced airflow to the perineal region, and was deemed unrelated to the vaccine by the attending veterinarian. No other adverse events were recorded.

Blood samples were collected at baseline (day 0), day 20, day 35, day 70, and day 84 for serological analysis. Serum was separated and stored at −80°C until analysis. Neutralizing antibody responses against ZIKV were measured using a focus reduction neutralization test (FRNT50) with the ZIKV PRVABC59 strain. All animals in the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK groups developed detectable neutralizing antibody titers by day 35, whereas no significant responses were observed in the SCV-control group. In the single-dose group (10⁷ pfu), the mean FRNT50 titer at day 35 was 187, and by day 70, it had increased to 214, with no statistically significant change between time points. In the two-dose group (10⁸ pfu), the mean FRNT50 titer at day 35 was 142, and after the second dose, it increased to 781 by day 70, representing a mean fold increase of 5.5 ± 1.4 (p = 0.009). These results demonstrate that a homologous prime-boost regimen with SCV-ZIKA/CHIK significantly enhances the magnitude of the anti-ZIKV neutralizing antibody response, whereas a single low-dose vaccination induces a modest but sustained response. The two-dose regimen induced titers comparable to those observed in animals vaccinated with formalin-inactivated ZIKV, which served as a positive control and achieved a mean FRNT50 titer of 1,240 at day 70.

To evaluate the breadth of the neutralizing response against CHIKV, serum samples were tested against four geographically and genetically distinct CHIKV isolates: LR2006-OPY1 (Indian Ocean lineage, Reunion Island), 37997 (West African lineage, Senegal), R99659 (Asian lineage, British Virgin Islands), and AF15561 (Asian lineage, Thailand). At day 35, following a single dose of 10⁷ pfu, neutralizing titers against the IOL and WA isolates were significantly higher than those against the Asian isolates, with a mean difference of 5.6-fold. However, by day 70, the titers against the Asian isolates had increased substantially, reaching levels equivalent to those against the IOL and WA isolates, with a mean fold increase of 5.2-fold (p = 0.009). In the two-dose group, the disparity between Asian and non-Asian isolates was even more pronounced at day 35, with titers against Asian isolates being 30-fold lower than those against IOL and WA isolates (p < 0.001). Following the second dose, the titers against the Asian isolates increased by a mean of 44-fold (p = 0.005), while titers against IOL and WA isolates increased by 7.1-fold, resulting in no significant difference between any of the four isolates at day 70. This temporal evolution of the neutralizing antibody response indicates that the vaccine induces an initial epitope-focused response, likely targeting conserved linear epitopes such as E2EP3, followed by a broadening of the response over time through epitope spreading, resulting in comprehensive cross-genotype neutralization.

The anti-VACV neutralizing antibody response was also assessed using a plaque reduction neutralization test (PRNT50) with the Western Reserve strain of vaccinia virus. Following a single dose of 10⁷ pfu, only one of five animals developed a detectable neutralizing antibody titer (reciprocal PRNT50 = 40), with no sustained response observed by day 70. In contrast, following two doses of 10⁸ pfu of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK, the mean reciprocal PRNT50 titer reached 79 ± 24 by day 70, with all animals in the group developing titers above the limit of detection (1:10). The SCV-control group, which received two doses of the same vector without foreign inserts, exhibited a comparable mean PRNT50 titer of 82 ± 21, confirming that the anti-VACV response is driven by the vector backbone and not by the inserted antigens. The mean endpoint IgG ELISA titer against VACV in the two-dose SCV-ZIKA/CHIK group was 800 ± 163, with similar titers observed in the SCV-control group, further confirming that the vector induces robust humoral immunity against its own structural antigens. These findings demonstrate that the SCV platform is capable of inducing anti-orthopoxvirus immunity comparable to that elicited by licensed smallpox vaccines, suggesting potential utility as a dual-purpose vaccine against both arboviruses and orthopoxviruses.

### Example 3

To evaluate the protective efficacy of the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine against live virus challenge, all animals were inoculated subcutaneously on day 70 with 10⁵ plaque-forming units of wild-type ZIKV strain PRVABC59, a clinically relevant isolate responsible for the 2015–2016 pandemic in the Americas. Serum samples were collected daily for 14 days post-challenge and analyzed for ZIKV RNA copy number using quantitative reverse-transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) with primers targeting the envelope protein gene. The lower limit of reliable quantification (LLoRQ) was established at 860 ZIKV RNA copies per milliliter based on serial dilutions of in vitro-transcribed viral RNA. In the SCV-control group, all four animals exhibited sustained viremia with peak viral loads exceeding 10⁵ copies/mL, with detectable RNA persisting for up to 10 days. In contrast, none of the five animals in the two-dose SCV-ZIKA/CHIK group (10⁸ pfu) exhibited serum ZIKV RNA levels above the LLoRQ at any time point, indicating complete suppression of detectable viremia. In the single-dose group (10⁷ pfu), two animals (NHP 5552 and NHP 5550) exhibited low-level viremia, with RNA levels exceeding the LLoRQ for one and three days, respectively. These two animals also had the lowest neutralizing antibody titers at the time of challenge, with reciprocal FRNT50 values of 100 and 120, respectively, compared to the mean titer of 214 in the remaining three animals of this group. A strong inverse correlation (Spearman’s rho = −0.89, p < 0.001) was observed between pre-challenge neutralizing antibody titers and the area under the curve (AUC) of serum ZIKV RNA levels, confirming that the level of pre-existing neutralizing antibodies is a key determinant of protection against viremia.

The total AUC of ZIKV RNA over the 14-day post-challenge period was significantly lower in both SCV-ZIKA/CHIK groups compared to the SCV-control group (Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, p < 0.01), with the two-dose group showing complete suppression of detectable viremia and the single-dose group showing a significant reduction in viral load duration and magnitude. The two animals in the positive control group vaccinated with formalin-inactivated ZIKV also exhibited no detectable viremia, confirming the efficacy of the inactivated vaccine as a comparator. Importantly, no clinical signs of illness, fever, or weight loss were observed in any animal following ZIKV challenge, and all animals recovered without intervention. The absence of detectable viremia in the two-dose group suggests that the vaccine-induced neutralizing antibodies are sufficient to prevent systemic dissemination of ZIKV, a critical endpoint for preventing transplacental transmission and congenital disease. Although sterilizing immunity—defined as complete absence of detectable viral RNA—was not achieved in all animals, the degree of viremia suppression observed in the two-dose group is considered clinically meaningful, particularly given the sensitivity of qRT-PCR and the known threshold of viral load required for fetal infection in animal models.

The data further suggest that the vaccine induces a functional immune response capable of rapidly clearing or preventing the establishment of infection upon exposure to a pathogenic strain. The fact that the two animals with the lowest neutralizing titers in the single-dose group exhibited low-level viremia supports the hypothesis that a threshold level of neutralizing antibodies, likely above 150–200 reciprocal FRNT50, is required to confer complete protection against ZIKV challenge in non-human primates. This threshold is readily achieved with the two-dose regimen, which consistently induced titers exceeding 700. The absence of viremia in the two-dose group, despite the use of a high-dose challenge (10⁵ pfu), demonstrates that the vaccine confers robust protection against a stringent challenge model. These findings are particularly significant given the lack of a validated animal model for congenital Zika syndrome in non-human primates, and suggest that the vaccine may be capable of preventing fetal infection in pregnant women, although direct testing in pregnant animals remains a future objective.

### Example 4

The manufacturing process for the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine was optimized to ensure scalability, reproducibility, and compliance with current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP). The SCS-1 CHO cell line, which expresses both D13L and CP77, was banked at a master cell bank (MCB) and working cell bank (WCB) level, with full characterization including karyotyping, mycoplasma testing, sterility testing, and absence of retroviral sequences. The MCB was tested for the presence of the D13L and CP77 transgenes by PCR and Southern blot, and for the stability of the transgene expression over 50 population doublings. The WCB was used for all production runs, with cells expanded in 10-L and 100-L wave bioreactors under serum-free, protein-free conditions. Infection was initiated at a cell density of 1.5 × 10⁶ viable cells per milliliter and an MOI of 0.005, allowing for a single infection cycle and minimizing the risk of recombination. The viral harvest was collected at 72 hours post-infection, when cell viability was approximately 70% and viral titers peaked at 1 × 10⁹ pfu/mL. The harvest was clarified by centrifugation at 3,000 × g for 20 minutes, followed by filtration through a 0.45 µm membrane. The clarified supernatant was concentrated 20-fold using a 100 kDa molecular weight cutoff tangential flow filtration system and then purified by ultracentrifugation through a 36% sucrose cushion at 100,000 × g for 2 hours at 4°C. The resulting viral pellet was resuspended in 10 mL of sterile 10 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 150 mM NaCl buffer and subjected to sterile filtration through a 0.22 µm membrane.

The purified virus was formulated into single-use vials containing 0.5 mL of vaccine at a concentration of 10⁸ pfu/mL, with an overfill of 100 µL to account for dead volume in syringes and needles. The formulation was tested for sterility by inoculation into thioglycollate and soybean-casein digest media, with no growth observed after 14 days. Mycoplasma contamination was assessed using a commercial PCR-based ELISA kit, with all samples testing negative. Endotoxin levels were measured using a Limulus amebocyte lysate assay and were consistently below 0.1 endotoxin units per milliliter. The viral titer was determined by plaque assay on SCS-1 cells, with a coefficient of variation of less than 10% across ten independent production runs. The identity of the virus was confirmed by PCR amplification of the CHIKV and ZIKV cassettes, and the absence of wild-type SCV was confirmed by sequencing across the D13L deletion junction. The vaccine was stored at −80°C for long-term stability or lyophilized using a lyophilization cycle comprising freezing at −40°C for 4 hours, primary drying at −30°C under vacuum for 12 hours, and secondary drying at 20°C for 8 hours. The lyophilized vaccine was reconstituted with sterile water for injection and showed no loss of potency after 12 months of storage at 2–8°C. The reconstituted vaccine remained stable for at least 24 hours at room temperature, supporting its use in resource-limited settings where cold chain maintenance may be challenging.

The entire manufacturing process was validated for scalability, with a single 100-L bioreactor run yielding approximately 10,000 doses of vaccine. The process was transferred to a GMP facility for pilot-scale production, and the final product was subjected to a full battery of analytical tests, including purity by SDS-PAGE and silver staining, integrity by electron microscopy, immunogenicity by ELISA and neutralization assays, and safety by testing in immunocompromised mice. No adverse events were observed in SCID mice following intraperitoneal administration of 10⁹ pfu of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK, confirming the non-pathogenic nature of the vector in the absence of a functional immune system. The vaccine was also tested for potential interference with other vaccines, and no reduction in immunogenicity was observed when co-administered with a licensed yellow fever vaccine in a separate study. The manufacturing process is fully automated, with real-time monitoring of cell density, glucose consumption, pH, and dissolved oxygen, and is amenable to continuous manufacturing platforms. The use of a defined cell line and serum-free medium eliminates the need for animal-derived components, reducing regulatory risk and facilitating global licensure.

### Example 5

The immunological profile of the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine was further characterized by analyzing the kinetics, magnitude, and durability of the neutralizing antibody response over an extended period. Animals from the two-dose group (10⁸ pfu) were monitored for an additional 90 days following the second vaccination (day 160), and serum samples were collected at day 105, day 135, and day 160. The mean reciprocal FRNT50 titer against ZIKV remained stable at approximately 750 from day 70 to day 160, with no significant decline observed, suggesting that the antibody response is durable and does not wane over time. Similarly, the mean reciprocal FRNT50 titers against all four CHIKV isolates remained stable at levels exceeding 500 from day 70 to day 160, with no significant difference between any of the genotypes. The anti-VACV PRNT50 titer also remained stable, with a mean of 81 ± 18 at day 160, comparable to the level observed at day 70. These findings indicate that the vaccine induces a long-lived humoral immune response, likely mediated by long-lived plasma cells residing in the bone marrow, and do not support the need for booster immunizations beyond the two-dose regimen.

The breadth of the CHIKV neutralizing response was further analyzed by testing serum samples against a panel of additional CHIKV isolates, including an ECSA genotype isolate from Brazil (strain BR01/2016) and a recent Asian lineage isolate from Singapore (strain SG2018). The neutralizing titers against these additional isolates were comparable to those observed against the original four isolates, with no significant difference in mean titers, suggesting that the vaccine induces broadly cross-reactive immunity across the entire CHIKV phylogenetic tree. This is particularly significant given that the vaccine antigen is derived from an Indian Ocean lineage isolate, which is phylogenetically distinct from the Asian and ECSA lineages responsible for recent outbreaks in the Americas and Southeast Asia. The ability of the vaccine to induce cross-neutralizing antibodies against such divergent strains suggests that the immune response is directed against conserved conformational epitopes on the E2 and E1 glycoproteins, rather than strain-specific linear epitopes. This is further supported by the observation that the neutralizing response evolves over time to include epitopes that are not initially dominant, a phenomenon consistent with epitope spreading and affinity maturation of B cell receptors.

The specificity of the neutralizing antibodies was confirmed by competition assays using monoclonal antibodies known to target key neutralizing epitopes on the CHIKV and ZIKV envelope proteins. Pre-incubation of serum with a monoclonal antibody targeting the E2EP3 epitope on CHIKV reduced neutralization by approximately 40% at day 35, but by day 70, this reduction was diminished to less than 10%, indicating that the antibody response has diversified beyond the initial E2EP3-focused response. Similarly, pre-incubation with a monoclonal antibody targeting the fusion loop of ZIKV E protein reduced neutralization by approximately 30% at day 70, suggesting that a significant proportion of the neutralizing response is directed against this conserved, but potentially ADE-prone, region. However, no enhancement of dengue virus infection was observed in an IFNAR1−/− mouse model of antibody-dependent enhancement, suggesting that the vaccine-induced antibodies do not mediate pathogenic enhancement, a critical safety consideration for flavivirus vaccines.

### Example 6

The safety profile of the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine was rigorously evaluated in multiple animal models beyond the non-human primate study. In a separate study, SCID mice, which lack functional T and B cells, were administered a single intraperitoneal dose of 10⁹ pfu of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK, which is 10,000-fold higher than the human equivalent dose. No weight loss, fever, lethargy, or histopathological changes were observed in any animal over a 28-day observation period, and viral DNA was undetectable in all tissues by qPCR at day 7, confirming the inability of the vector to replicate or disseminate in the absence of a complementing cell line. In a separate study, pregnant rhesus macaques were administered a single dose of 10⁸ pfu of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK at gestational day 60, and fetal tissues were collected at day 120. No viral DNA was detected in placental, fetal brain, or fetal liver tissues by qPCR, and no histopathological abnormalities were observed in any fetal organ. These findings suggest that the vaccine is non-teratogenic and does not cross the placental barrier, a critical safety feature for a vaccine intended for use in women of childbearing age.

The vaccine was also tested for potential interference with the immune response to other pathogens. Animals were vaccinated with SCV-ZIKA/CHIK and then challenged with a heterologous pathogen, dengue virus serotype 2, 30 days later. No enhancement of dengue viremia was observed, and the neutralizing antibody response to dengue was unaffected, suggesting that the vaccine does not induce cross-reactive antibodies that could mediate antibody-dependent enhancement of other flaviviruses. In addition, the vaccine did not induce autoantibodies or immune complex deposition in the kidneys or skin, as assessed by immunofluorescence and histology, further supporting its safety profile.

The vaccine was also tested for stability under extreme environmental conditions. Lyophilized vaccine vials were subjected to accelerated aging at 40°C for 4 weeks, and the viral titer was measured before and after exposure. No significant loss of potency was observed, with a mean reduction of less than 0.5 log₁₀ pfu/mL, indicating that the vaccine is stable under conditions that simulate prolonged transport in hot climates. The vaccine was also tested for resistance to freeze-thaw cycles, and no loss of infectivity was observed after five cycles of freezing at −80°C and thawing at 37°C. These findings support the potential for deployment in low-resource settings where cold chain infrastructure is limited or unreliable.

### Example 7

The present invention represents a transformative advancement in the field of vaccinology by providing a single-platform, homologous prime-boost vaccine capable of inducing robust, durable, and broadly protective immunity against two major arboviral pathogens and one orthopoxvirus. Unlike existing vaccine candidates, which require multiple doses, adjuvants, heterologous priming, or complex manufacturing systems, the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine achieves high-level immunogenicity and protection following a simple two-dose regimen administered via intramuscular injection. The use of a replication-deficient poxvirus vector derived from the Copenhagen strain, propagated in a defined CHO cell line, enables scalable, GMP-compliant manufacturing without reliance on primary chicken embryo fibroblasts, thereby reducing regulatory risk and facilitating global licensure. The vaccine induces neutralizing antibody titers against ZIKV and CHIKV that are comparable to or exceed those induced by licensed inactivated vaccines, and confers complete protection against viremia following challenge with a pathogenic ZIKV strain. The ability of the vaccine to elicit cross-neutralizing immunity against multiple CHIKV genotypes, including those responsible for recent outbreaks in the Americas, represents a significant advantage over strain-specific candidates. The concurrent induction of anti-VACV immunity further enhances the public health value of the vaccine, enabling a single product to protect against both emerging arboviral threats and the re-emergence of smallpox. The safety profile of the vaccine is exceptional, with no systemic adverse events observed in non-human primates and no evidence of pathogenicity in immunocompromised hosts. The stability of the lyophilized formulation under refrigerated conditions and resistance to freeze-thaw cycles make it ideal for deployment in remote and resource-limited regions. The invention thus provides a novel, safe, effective, and manufacturable solution to the global challenge of arboviral disease, with the potential to be deployed as a single-dose or two-dose vaccine in both pediatric and adult populations, including women of childbearing age, without the need for complex cold chain logistics or multiple injections. The invention further provides a platform technology that can be readily adapted to encode additional antigens from other pathogens, such as dengue, yellow fever, or chikungunya variants, thereby enabling the development of multivalent vaccines for a broad spectrum of infectious diseases.
