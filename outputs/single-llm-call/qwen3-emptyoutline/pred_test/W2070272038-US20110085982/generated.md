## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Breast cancer remains one of the most prevalent and lethal malignancies affecting women worldwide, with a substantial proportion of deaths attributable to metastatic disease that is resistant to conventional therapies. Among the various molecular subtypes of breast cancer, triple-negative breast cancer—defined by the absence of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 amplification—is particularly aggressive, frequently recurs after initial treatment, and is associated with poor overall survival. Unlike other subtypes that can be targeted with endocrine or anti-HER2 therapies, triple-negative breast cancer lacks established molecular targets, leaving chemotherapy as the primary systemic treatment option. Despite initial responses in some patients, a significant subset of tumors rapidly develop resistance, leading to widespread metastasis and inevitable mortality. The biological basis for this aggressive behavior has been the subject of intense investigation, with particular focus on the epithelial-mesenchymal transition, a developmental program reactivated in cancer cells that confers increased motility, invasiveness, and resistance to apoptosis. While in vitro studies using established breast cancer cell lines have demonstrated that forced expression of certain transcription factors can induce a mesenchymal phenotype, these models are largely artificial, irreversible, and do not reflect the dynamic, context-dependent nature of epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity observed in human tumors. Clinical evidence suggests that metastatic lesions often retain epithelial characteristics despite the presence of mesenchymal features in the primary tumor, leading to the hypothesis that cancer cells undergo transient and reversible phenotypic shifts rather than permanent lineage commitment. This concept, known as epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity, posits that cancer cells can dynamically alternate between epithelial and mesenchymal states in response to microenvironmental stimuli, thereby enhancing their adaptability and survival during dissemination. However, until now, no human-derived breast cancer cell line has been shown to exhibit such reversible transitions in a controlled and reproducible manner under defined culture conditions. Existing models, including MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7, are either constitutively mesenchymal or epithelial and fail to switch phenotypes upon changes in media composition or growth factors. Furthermore, the molecular mechanisms that govern this plasticity remain poorly understood, with conflicting reports on the roles of Snail, Twist, and Zeb family transcription factors across different models. The absence of a reliable in vitro system that mirrors the phenotypic heterogeneity and adaptability of human triple-negative breast cancer has severely limited the ability to identify novel therapeutic targets or to test drugs designed to lock cancer cells in a less aggressive state. The clinical observation that circulating tumor cells often co-express both epithelial and mesenchymal markers further underscores the need for a model that can recapitulate this intermediate, hybrid phenotype. The present invention addresses these critical gaps by providing a novel human-derived cell line that faithfully reproduces the reversible epithelial-mesenchymal transitions observed in patient-derived tumors, thereby enabling the mechanistic dissection of phenotypic plasticity and the development of therapies aimed at disrupting this adaptive process.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel human-derived epithelial cell line, designated DKAT, isolated from the malignant pleural effusion of a patient with aggressive, treatment-resistant, metastatic triple-negative breast cancer, which exhibits a unique and reversible capacity to transition between epithelial and mesenchymal phenotypes in response to alterations in culture conditions. The DKAT cell line, when cultured in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media, displays a predominantly epithelial phenotype characterized by the expression of E-cadherin, cytokeratins 5, 8, 17, and 18, membrane localization of epithelial junctional proteins, and a cobblestone morphology. Upon switching to Stromal Cell Growth Media containing insulin, basic fibroblast growth factor, and fetal bovine serum, DKAT cells undergo a rapid and reproducible phenotypic shift to a mesenchymal state, marked by the downregulation of epithelial markers, upregulation of vimentin and N-cadherin, loss of cell-cell adhesion, acquisition of a spindle-shaped morphology, and enhanced migratory capacity. This transition is fully reversible: when mesenchymal DKAT cells are returned to Mammary Epithelial Growth Media, they regain epithelial characteristics, including re-expression of E-cadherin and cytokeratins, re-establishment of junctional complexes, and restoration of cobblestone morphology. The DKAT cell line is genetically stable, has been maintained in continuous culture for more than seventy passages, and retains its phenotypic plasticity across multiple passages without selection pressure. The cell line harbors a heterozygous missense mutation in exon 8 of the TP53 gene at codon 273, consistent with loss of p53 function, and exhibits low levels of PTEN protein, resulting in constitutive activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, features commonly observed in aggressive triple-negative breast cancers. The DKAT cell line also expresses a high proportion of CD44+/CD24−/low cells, a phenotype associated with cancer stem cell activity, and demonstrates high tumorigenic potential in vivo, with as few as ten cells capable of forming tumors in immunocompromised mice. Xenograft tumors derived from DKAT cells recapitulate the phenotypic heterogeneity of the original patient tumor, exhibiting regions of epithelial, mesenchymal, and hybrid phenotypes within the same tumor mass, confirming that the plasticity observed in vitro is not an artifact of culture but reflects the intrinsic biology of the tumor. The invention further identifies Zeb1 as a critical regulator of this phenotypic plasticity, as overexpression of Zeb1 in epithelial DKAT cells is sufficient to induce mesenchymal characteristics, while knockdown of Zeb1 prevents the induction of migration and mesenchymal marker expression upon exposure to Stromal Cell Growth Media. The DKAT cell line is uniquely capable of undergoing these reversible transitions in a manner not observed in other commonly used breast cancer cell lines, including MDA-MB-231, MCF-7, SUM-149, and SUM-190, which remain phenotypically static under identical culture conditions. The invention further includes methods for inducing, detecting, and quantifying epithelial-mesenchymal transitions in the DKAT cell line using defined media, immunofluorescence, western blotting, quantitative PCR, and functional migration assays. The invention also encompasses the use of the DKAT cell line for high-throughput screening of compounds that inhibit epithelial-mesenchymal transition, promote mesenchymal-epithelial transition, or otherwise disrupt phenotypic plasticity, as well as for the development of diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic strategies aimed at preventing metastasis and overcoming therapy resistance in triple-negative breast cancer.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive and novel system for modeling the dynamic phenotypic plasticity that underlies the aggressiveness and therapeutic resistance of triple-negative breast cancer. At its core is the DKAT cell line, a human-derived epithelial cell line isolated from the malignant pleural effusion of a 35-year-old woman with a rapidly progressing, metastatic triple-negative breast cancer that was refractory to multiple lines of chemotherapy. The DKAT cell line is not a genetically engineered construct, nor is it derived from a xenograft or transgenic model; it is a direct, clonally expanded, and stably maintained culture of human tumor cells that retain the molecular and phenotypic characteristics of the original patient tumor. The cell line has been propagated continuously for more than seventy passages without loss of its defining properties, including its ability to reversibly transition between epithelial and mesenchymal states in response to environmental cues. This capability distinguishes DKAT from all previously described breast cancer cell lines, which exhibit fixed phenotypes regardless of culture conditions. The DKAT cell line is maintained in a serum-free, defined medium known as Mammary Epithelial Growth Media, which supports proliferation while preserving an epithelial phenotype characterized by the expression of E-cadherin, cytokeratins 5, 8, 17, and 18, and the presence of adherens and tight junctions localized at the cell membrane. When these cells are transitioned to Stromal Cell Growth Media, which contains insulin, basic fibroblast growth factor, and five percent fetal bovine serum, they undergo a rapid and coordinated phenotypic shift. Within two hours of exposure to this media, E-cadherin is internalized from the membrane into the cytoplasm, and cells adopt a more elongated, spindle-like morphology. By twenty-four hours, vimentin expression is markedly increased, and by fourteen days, E-cadherin protein levels are reduced by more than ninety percent, while vimentin and N-cadherin are upregulated by more than tenfold. This mesenchymal state is accompanied by a significant increase in migratory capacity, as demonstrated by scratch wound assays in which DKAT cells in Stromal Cell Growth Media close a wound in twelve hours, compared to only sixty percent closure by cells maintained in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media over the same period. Crucially, when these mesenchymal DKAT cells are returned to Mammary Epithelial Growth Media, they revert to an epithelial phenotype within five passages, with restoration of E-cadherin expression, relocalization of junctional proteins to the membrane, and downregulation of vimentin and N-cadherin. This reversibility is not due to selection of pre-existing subpopulations, as demonstrated by clonal derivation experiments in which single cells isolated from either epithelial or mesenchymal states are capable of undergoing the full transition in either direction. The DKAT cell line is genetically stable, with a modal chromosome number of fifty-six and a complex but consistent karyotype that includes hyperdiploid, near-tetraploid, and composite clones, with no evidence of contamination or cross-contamination with other cell lines. The cell line harbors a well-characterized missense mutation in exon 8 of the TP53 gene at codon 273 (CGT to CAT), which results in a substitution of arginine for histidine and is known to impair p53’s transcriptional activity and tumor suppressor function. This mutation is identical to that found in the MDA-MB-468 cell line and is consistent with the loss of p53 function observed in many triple-negative breast cancers. Additionally, the DKAT cell line exhibits low levels of PTEN protein, a negative regulator of the PI3K/AKT pathway, resulting in constitutive phosphorylation of AKT at serine 473, a hallmark of aggressive breast cancer biology. The cell line also expresses a high percentage of CD44-positive, CD24-low/negative cells, a surface marker profile associated with breast cancer stem cells and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. This stem-like phenotype is maintained across both epithelial and mesenchymal states, suggesting that phenotypic plasticity is not a property of a distinct subpopulation but rather an inherent capability of the majority of DKAT cells. The tumorigenic potential of the DKAT cell line is exceptional, as evidenced by xenograft experiments in which as few as ten cells injected into the mammary fat pad of immunocompromised mice are sufficient to form palpable tumors within two to four weeks. These tumors exhibit remarkable heterogeneity, with distinct regions displaying epithelial, mesenchymal, and hybrid phenotypes, mirroring the multi-focal architecture of the original patient tumor. Immunohistochemical analysis of these xenografts reveals that human DKAT cells express both cytokeratin 5 and smooth muscle actin within the same tumor mass, confirming that the phenotypic diversity observed in vivo is intrinsic to the DKAT cells and not due to infiltration of host stromal elements. The DKAT cell line also forms tumorspheres in non-adherent, serum-free conditions, and these spheres contain distinct populations of cells expressing either epithelial or mesenchymal markers, demonstrating that a single DKAT cell is capable of generating progeny with divergent phenotypes. This finding strongly supports the hypothesis that phenotypic plasticity is a fundamental property of the DKAT cell line and not a consequence of clonal selection. The molecular mechanism underlying this plasticity has been identified as the transcription factor Zeb1. Zeb1 expression is low in epithelial DKAT cells but is dramatically upregulated upon transition to Stromal Cell Growth Media. Forced overexpression of Zeb1 in epithelial DKAT cells is sufficient to induce mesenchymal characteristics, including downregulation of E-cadherin and claudin-1 and upregulation of vimentin. Conversely, stable knockdown of Zeb1 using lentiviral shRNA constructs completely abrogates the ability of DKAT cells to undergo epithelial-mesenchymal transition and eliminates the enhanced migratory phenotype induced by Stromal Cell Growth Media. These findings establish Zeb1 as a necessary and sufficient regulator of phenotypic plasticity in the DKAT model. Importantly, no such effect is observed with knockdown of Snail1 or Twist, indicating that the regulatory network governing plasticity in DKAT cells is distinct from that in other breast cancer models. The DKAT cell line is unique in its ability to undergo these reversible transitions; other commonly used breast cancer cell lines, including MDA-MB-231, MCF-7, SUM-149, and SUM-190, show no change in epithelial or mesenchymal marker expression when cultured in either Mammary Epithelial Growth Media or Stromal Cell Growth Media. Even immortalized human mammary epithelial cells, which are not transformed, fail to undergo full phenotypic switching and instead undergo growth arrest in the presence of serum. This specificity underscores the novelty of the DKAT cell line and its relevance as a model for aggressive, plastic triple-negative breast cancer. The invention further encompasses a comprehensive set of methods for utilizing the DKAT cell line in research and drug discovery. These include protocols for inducing epithelial-mesenchymal transition by switching from Mammary Epithelial Growth Media to Stromal Cell Growth Media, methods for reversing the transition by returning cells to Mammary Epithelial Growth Media, techniques for quantifying the extent of transition using immunofluorescence, western blotting, and quantitative PCR, and assays for measuring functional outcomes such as migration, invasion, and tumorsphere formation. The invention also includes kits comprising pre-formulated Mammary Epithelial Growth Media and Stromal Cell Growth Media, along with antibodies and reagents for detecting epithelial and mesenchymal markers, and instructions for performing phenotypic transition assays. The DKAT cell line can be used for high-throughput screening of small molecules, biologics, or genetic modifiers that inhibit epithelial-mesenchymal transition or promote mesenchymal-epithelial transition, thereby identifying compounds capable of locking cancer cells in a less aggressive state. The invention further enables the development of diagnostic assays to detect circulating tumor cells exhibiting hybrid epithelial-mesenchymal phenotypes, which may serve as prognostic biomarkers for metastatic risk. The DKAT cell line is also suitable for co-culture studies with immune cells, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells to model the tumor microenvironment and investigate how stromal interactions influence phenotypic plasticity. The invention thus provides not only a novel cell line but a complete experimental platform for understanding and targeting the dynamic biology of metastatic triple-negative breast cancer.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The DKAT cell line was established from a malignant pleural effusion obtained from a 35-year-old woman with a rapidly progressing, metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. The patient had initially presented with a 4 cm, ER-negative, PR-negative, HER2-negative breast tumor, which was treated with cyclophosphamide, methotrexate, and 5-fluorouracil. After seven months of treatment, the cancer recurred in the chest wall radiation field, and by ten months, metastases were detected in the lung, pleura, liver, and bone. The patient’s disease became refractory to taxotere and navelbine, and she succumbed to pancytopenia and multi-organ failure at twelve months from initial diagnosis. The pleural effusion was collected under institutional review board–approved protocols with informed consent, and the fluid was centrifuged to pellet the cellular component. The pellet was resuspended in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media supplemented with bovine pituitary extract, insulin, human recombinant epidermal growth factor, and hydrocortisone. The cells were plated in tissue culture flasks and maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing five percent carbon dioxide. Within forty-eight hours, cells began to proliferate, forming adherent colonies with a doubling time of approximately twenty-four hours. The cells were passaged every three to four days using trypsin-EDTA, and mycoplasma testing was performed monthly using a commercial PCR-based detection kit. After ten passages, the cell line was designated DKAT and has been maintained continuously for more than seventy passages without loss of viability or phenotypic characteristics. The DKAT cell line exhibits a consistent epithelial morphology in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media, with cells displaying a cobblestone appearance, strong membrane localization of E-cadherin, and expression of cytokeratins 5, 8, 17, and 18. The cells are negative for estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 amplification, as confirmed by immunohistochemistry and fluorescence in situ hybridization, respectively. The DKAT cell line is not contaminated with other cell lines, as verified by short tandem repeat profiling and comparison to the ATCC database. The cell line has been cryopreserved in multiple vials at passage levels 5, 15, 25, 35, 45, 55, and 65, and all cryopreserved stocks retain the ability to undergo phenotypic transitions upon switching media. The DKAT cell line is distinct from other breast cancer cell lines in its origin, phenotype, and behavior, and represents the first human-derived cell line capable of reversible epithelial-mesenchymal transitions under defined culture conditions.

### Example 2

To characterize the baseline molecular profile of the DKAT cell line, a series of immunohistochemical, immunofluorescent, and biochemical analyses were performed. Immunohistochemistry on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded sections of the original patient tumor revealed that six distinct microfocal lesions exhibited varying patterns of epithelial and mesenchymal marker expression. Five of the six lesions were strongly positive for E-cadherin and weakly positive for vimentin, consistent with an epithelial phenotype. The sixth lesion, however, was strongly positive for vimentin, CK5, and CK8/18, with diffuse cytoplasmic E-cadherin staining, indicative of an intermediate or hybrid state. The chest wall recurrence showed a similar pattern, with reduced E-cadherin and increased vimentin, while the bone marrow metastasis reverted to a strong epithelial phenotype. These observations suggested phenotypic heterogeneity within the tumor. In vitro, DKAT cells were analyzed for expression of epithelial and mesenchymal markers using immunofluorescence and western blotting. Cells grown in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media expressed high levels of E-cadherin, localized predominantly at the cell membrane, and showed strong staining for cytokeratins 5, 8, 17, and 18. Vimentin expression was minimal and scattered. In contrast, DKAT cells grown in Stromal Cell Growth Media for fourteen days exhibited a dramatic reduction in E-cadherin expression, loss of membrane localization, and a complete reorganization of the cytoskeleton, with vimentin filaments extending throughout the cytoplasm. N-cadherin expression was upregulated, and claudin-1 and occludin were downregulated. Western blot analysis confirmed these findings, with a greater than tenfold increase in vimentin and N-cadherin protein levels and a greater than ninety percent decrease in E-cadherin and claudin-1. The DKAT cell line also expressed high levels of phosphorylated AKT at serine 473, as detected by phospho-specific antibodies, and low levels of PTEN protein, as confirmed by densitometric analysis of western blots. Flow cytometry analysis revealed that seventy-eight percent of DKAT cells in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media expressed the CD44+/CD24−/low phenotype, a marker profile associated with cancer stem cells. These findings demonstrate that the DKAT cell line recapitulates the molecular signature of aggressive triple-negative breast cancer, including loss of tumor suppressor function, activation of oncogenic signaling, and enrichment for stem-like properties.

### Example 3

To determine whether the phenotypic changes observed in DKAT cells were reversible, a series of transition experiments were conducted. DKAT cells were first grown in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media for ten passages to establish a stable epithelial phenotype. These cells were then switched to Stromal Cell Growth Media, and samples were collected at 1 hour, 24 hours, and 14 days. Immunofluorescence analysis at one hour revealed that E-cadherin was no longer localized at the cell membrane but was instead concentrated in the cytoplasm, while vimentin expression was already elevated. At twenty-four hours, cells adopted a spindle-shaped morphology, and by fourteen days, E-cadherin protein levels were reduced by more than ninety percent, while vimentin was upregulated by more than tenfold. To test for reversibility, these mesenchymal DKAT cells were then switched back to Mammary Epithelial Growth Media. After five passages, E-cadherin expression was restored, vimentin levels were reduced to baseline, and the cobblestone morphology was re-established. Western blot analysis confirmed these changes, with a greater than eightfold increase in E-cadherin and a greater than ninefold decrease in vimentin. The same transition was observed in clonal populations derived from single DKAT cells. A single epithelial DKAT cell was isolated by limiting dilution and expanded into a clonal line. This clonal line, when exposed to Stromal Cell Growth Media, underwent the same phenotypic transition as the bulk population, with identical kinetics and marker expression profiles. Similarly, a clonal line derived from a single mesenchymal DKAT cell was switched back to Mammary Epithelial Growth Media and regained epithelial characteristics, including membrane E-cadherin and reduced vimentin. These experiments demonstrate that phenotypic plasticity is not a property of a minor subpopulation but is an intrinsic capability of individual DKAT cells. The reversibility of the transition was further confirmed by functional assays: mesenchymal DKAT cells exhibited significantly increased migration in scratch assays, but after five passages in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media, their migratory capacity returned to baseline levels. These findings establish that the DKAT cell line exhibits true phenotypic plasticity, with the ability to reversibly switch between epithelial and mesenchymal states without genetic selection or permanent alteration.

### Example 4

To assess whether the phenotypic plasticity of the DKAT cell line is unique among breast cancer models, a comparative analysis was performed with other commonly used cell lines. MDA-MB-231, a mesenchymal triple-negative breast cancer cell line, was cultured in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media for fourteen days. No change in E-cadherin or vimentin expression was observed, and the cells retained their spindle-shaped morphology and high migratory capacity. MCF-7, SUM-149, and SUM-190, which are epithelial cell lines normally cultured in serum-containing media, were switched to Stromal Cell Growth Media for fourteen days. None of these cell lines exhibited upregulation of vimentin or downregulation of E-cadherin, and their morphology remained unchanged. HMEC-hTERT, immortalized human mammary epithelial cells, were also cultured in Stromal Cell Growth Media. While a slight decrease in E-cadherin and a minor increase in vimentin were observed, the cells ceased proliferation and underwent growth arrest within seven days, failing to acquire a fully mesenchymal phenotype. In contrast, DKAT cells continued to proliferate robustly in Stromal Cell Growth Media and underwent a complete phenotypic transition. These results demonstrate that the ability to reversibly transition between epithelial and mesenchymal states is not a general property of breast cancer cells or even of transformed epithelial cells, but is a unique and defining characteristic of the DKAT cell line. The specificity of this phenotype suggests that the DKAT cell line captures a rare and clinically relevant biological state that is not represented in existing models. This uniqueness underscores the value of the DKAT cell line as a tool for studying the mechanisms of phenotypic plasticity and for identifying therapeutic strategies that target this specific vulnerability in aggressive triple-negative breast cancer.

### Example 5

To determine the molecular mechanisms underlying the phenotypic plasticity of the DKAT cell line, the expression of key EMT-regulating transcription factors was analyzed. Western blot analysis was performed on DKAT cells grown in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media versus Stromal Cell Growth Media for fourteen days. No significant difference in the expression of Snail1 or Twist was observed between the two conditions. However, Zeb1 protein levels were markedly increased in mesenchymal DKAT cells, with a greater than fivefold increase compared to epithelial cells. To test whether Zeb1 overexpression was sufficient to induce a mesenchymal phenotype, the full-length Zeb1 cDNA was cloned into a retroviral expression vector and transduced into epithelial DKAT cells. Stable clones expressing Zeb1 were selected using G418. These Zeb1-overexpressing cells exhibited a dramatic reduction in E-cadherin and claudin-1 expression, a marked increase in vimentin, and a spindle-shaped morphology indistinguishable from DKAT cells grown in Stromal Cell Growth Media. Conversely, stable knockdown of Zeb1 was achieved using two independent lentiviral shRNA constructs targeting distinct regions of the Zeb1 mRNA. DKAT cells transduced with these shRNAs and selected with puromycin showed a greater than ninety percent reduction in Zeb1 protein levels. When these Zeb1-knockdown cells were switched to Stromal Cell Growth Media, they failed to upregulate vimentin or downregulate E-cadherin, and their morphology remained epithelial. Furthermore, the enhanced migratory capacity induced by Stromal Cell Growth Media was completely abolished in Zeb1-knockdown cells, as demonstrated by scratch assays in which the wound closure rate was reduced by more than seventy percent compared to control cells. These findings establish Zeb1 as a necessary and sufficient regulator of phenotypic plasticity in the DKAT cell line. The absence of a role for Snail1 or Twist indicates that the regulatory network governing plasticity in DKAT cells is distinct from that in other models, highlighting the unique biology of this cell line. The identification of Zeb1 as the central regulator provides a specific molecular target for therapeutic intervention aimed at blocking phenotypic plasticity in triple-negative breast cancer.

### Example 6

To evaluate the tumorigenic potential of the DKAT cell line, a limiting dilution xenograft assay was performed in immunocompromised NOD.CB17-PrkdcSCID/J mice. DKAT cells grown in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media were resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline and injected into the fourth mammary fat pad of mice at concentrations of ten, one hundred, one thousand, and ten thousand cells per injection. Four independent experiments were conducted, with eight mice per dose group. Palpable tumors were detected within two to four weeks in all mice injected with ten thousand, one thousand, or one hundred cells. Remarkably, two out of eight mice injected with only ten cells developed palpable tumors within three weeks, and these tumors grew to a diameter of fifteen millimeters by twelve weeks. Tumor growth was monitored weekly by caliper measurement, and mice were sacrificed when tumors reached 1.5 centimeters in diameter or after 150 days. Histological analysis of excised tumors revealed a heterogeneous architecture, with regions of epithelial, mesenchymal, and hybrid phenotypes. Immunohistochemistry confirmed the presence of human-specific p53-positive tumor cells expressing both cytokeratin 5 and smooth muscle actin within the same tumor mass, demonstrating that the phenotypic diversity observed in vitro is recapitulated in vivo. The tumors exhibited invasive growth patterns, infiltrating surrounding adipose tissue and, in some cases, invading the ductal structures of the host mammary gland. The ability of ten DKAT cells to form tumors in vivo demonstrates exceptional tumorigenic potential, far exceeding that of other breast cancer cell lines, which typically require at least one thousand to ten thousand cells for reliable tumor formation. This high tumorigenicity, combined with the phenotypic plasticity of the cells, suggests that DKAT cells possess stem-like properties that enable them to initiate and sustain tumor growth from minimal numbers of cells.

### Example 7

To investigate whether the phenotypic plasticity of DKAT cells is reflected in three-dimensional culture, tumorsphere assays were performed. DKAT cells were plated at a density of one cell per well in ultra-low attachment 96-well plates in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media supplemented with B27, heparin, epidermal growth factor, and basic fibroblast growth factor. After ten to fourteen days, tumorspheres were counted, and the tumorsphere formation efficiency was calculated as the percentage of wells containing spheres. The DKAT cell line exhibited a tumorsphere formation efficiency of between three and ten percent, indicating a high frequency of self-renewing cells. Immunofluorescence staining of pooled tumorspheres revealed the presence of distinct populations of cells: some expressed high levels of E-cadherin and cytokeratins 5 and 18, while others expressed vimentin and N-cadherin. Dual staining for E-cadherin and vimentin showed that many spheres contained both epithelial and mesenchymal cells, often in close proximity. Similarly, staining for cytokeratin 5 and cytokeratin 18 revealed subpopulations of basal and luminal epithelial cells within the same sphere. These findings demonstrate that a single DKAT cell is capable of generating progeny with divergent phenotypes, supporting the hypothesis that phenotypic plasticity is an intrinsic property of the DKAT cell line and not a consequence of clonal selection. The presence of hybrid phenotypes within tumorspheres further mirrors the intermediate epithelial-mesenchymal states observed in circulating tumor cells from patients with metastatic breast cancer. The ability of DKAT cells to generate such heterogeneous structures in a defined, serum-free environment underscores their developmental flexibility and reinforces their relevance as a model for studying the origins of tumor heterogeneity.

### Example 8

To confirm that the phenotypic heterogeneity observed in DKAT xenografts was derived from the implanted human tumor cells and not from host stromal infiltration, three-color immunofluorescence staining was performed on frozen sections of xenograft tumors. Sections were stained with antibodies specific for human p53, keratin 14, and DAPI. Human p53-positive cells were identified as those with nuclear staining that did not cross-react with murine p53. Keratin 14, a marker of basal and myoepithelial cells, was expressed in a subset of these human p53-positive cells, while other human p53-positive cells were keratin 14-negative. The majority of DAPI-positive cells that were negative for human p53 were also negative for keratin 14, confirming that these were murine stromal cells. The presence of keratin 14-positive cells that were also positive for human p53 demonstrates that the myoepithelial differentiation observed in the tumors originated from the DKAT cells themselves and not from host fibroblasts or other stromal elements. This finding is critical, as it establishes that the phenotypic plasticity of DKAT cells extends beyond simple epithelial-mesenchymal transitions to include lineage diversification into basal and myoepithelial cell types, a feature that is rarely observed in other breast cancer models. The ability of DKAT cells to generate multiple lineages within a single tumor mass further supports the idea that these cells possess stem-like properties and that their plasticity is not limited to a binary switch but encompasses a broader spectrum of differentiation states. This level of lineage flexibility is consistent with the clinical observation that triple-negative breast cancers often exhibit metaplastic or sarcomatoid features, and it suggests that the DKAT cell line captures a more comprehensive aspect of tumor biology than previously modeled systems.

### Example 9

To demonstrate the utility of the DKAT cell line for drug discovery and therapeutic screening, a high-throughput compound screening assay was developed. A library of one thousand small molecules, including FDA-approved drugs, kinase inhibitors, epigenetic modulators, and natural compounds, was screened for their ability to inhibit epithelial-mesenchymal transition in DKAT cells. DKAT cells were grown in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media and then switched to Stromal Cell Growth Media in the presence of each compound at a concentration of ten micromolar. After fourteen days, cells were fixed and stained for E-cadherin and vimentin using immunofluorescence. The ratio of E-cadherin to vimentin fluorescence intensity was quantified using automated image analysis software. Compounds that maintained an E-cadherin/vimentin ratio greater than 0.8 were considered inhibitors of phenotypic transition. From the initial screen, thirty compounds were identified as hits. These hits were then validated in dose-response experiments, and three compounds were found to completely block the transition at concentrations below one micromolar. These compounds were further tested for their effects on cell migration, tumorsphere formation, and in vivo tumor growth. One compound, designated DKAT-1, was found to prevent the upregulation of Zeb1, restore E-cadherin expression, reduce vimentin levels, and inhibit migration by more than eighty percent. When administered orally to mice bearing DKAT xenografts, DKAT-1 significantly reduced tumor volume and delayed metastasis to the lung and bone. Histological analysis of tumors from treated mice revealed a predominance of epithelial phenotypes and a marked reduction in hybrid and mesenchymal regions. These findings demonstrate that the DKAT cell line can be used as a platform for identifying novel therapeutics that target phenotypic plasticity, and that such agents have the potential to suppress metastasis and overcome therapy resistance in triple-negative breast cancer. The DKAT cell line thus represents not only a novel biological model but also a powerful tool for the development of next-generation cancer therapies.
