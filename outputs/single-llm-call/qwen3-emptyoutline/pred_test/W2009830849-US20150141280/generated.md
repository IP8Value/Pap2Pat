# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and oncology, specifically to the use of circulating microRNAs as non-invasive biomarkers for the detection and diagnosis of Luminal A-like breast cancer. More particularly, the invention provides a method for identifying individuals with Luminal A-like breast cancer through the quantitative measurement of specific microRNA species in whole blood samples, without the need for tissue biopsy or invasive procedures. The invention further encompasses a diagnostic panel comprising three microRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—whose combined expression profile in peripheral blood exhibits a statistically significant and clinically meaningful discriminatory capacity between individuals with Luminal A-like breast cancer and those without malignancy. This diagnostic approach is particularly suited for use in routine clinical settings where minimally invasive, reproducible, and subtype-specific diagnostic tools are urgently needed to complement existing imaging modalities such as mammography. The invention is applicable to population-based screening, early detection programs, and longitudinal monitoring of disease status, and is designed to function independently of tumor stage, grade, or nodal involvement, thereby offering a robust and broadly applicable diagnostic solution for the most common molecular subtype of breast cancer.

The invention resides in the discovery that the circulating levels of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 are consistently and significantly downregulated in the whole blood of individuals with Luminal A-like breast cancer, regardless of the clinical stage or pathological features of the tumor. This downregulation is not merely a secondary consequence of tumor burden but reflects a systemic molecular signature associated with the underlying biology of this specific breast cancer subtype. Unlike traditional serum or plasma-based biomarker assays, the present invention utilizes whole blood collected in EDTA anticoagulant tubes, a sample type that is logistically simpler to collect, store, and transport under routine clinical conditions, thereby enhancing the feasibility of widespread adoption in diverse healthcare environments. The method does not require immediate centrifugation, plasma separation, or cryopreservation, which are common limitations of other circulating biomarker approaches. The invention further includes a standardized protocol for RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and quantitative real-time PCR using TaqMan assays, with normalization to miR-16 as a stable endogenous control, ensuring reproducibility across laboratories and diagnostic platforms. The diagnostic utility of the disclosed miRNA panel is validated through receiver operating characteristic analysis, demonstrating an area under the curve of 0.80, with a sensitivity of 77% and specificity of 74%, surpassing the diagnostic performance of currently used serum biomarkers such as CA 15-3 and CEA, which lack sufficient sensitivity for early-stage detection. The invention therefore represents a paradigm shift in breast cancer diagnostics by providing the first circulating biomarker panel capable of specifically identifying Luminal A-like breast cancer—a subtype that accounts for over seventy percent of all breast cancer cases—through a simple blood test, thereby enabling earlier, more precise, and less invasive clinical decision-making.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women globally, with an estimated incidence exceeding one million new cases annually in developed regions alone. Despite significant advances in surgical techniques, radiation therapy, and targeted systemic treatments, the leading cause of mortality in this disease continues to be the development of distant metastases, often arising from undetected or inadequately characterized primary tumors. The clinical management of breast cancer is increasingly guided by molecular subtyping, which categorizes tumors based on the expression profiles of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/neu). Among these subtypes, Luminal A-like breast cancer represents the most prevalent group, characterized by ER and PR positivity in the absence of HER2/neu overexpression, and is generally associated with a favorable prognosis when diagnosed early. However, the current standard of care for diagnosis relies heavily on mammographic screening followed by invasive core needle biopsy for histopathological confirmation and receptor status determination. This two-step process is not only resource-intensive and psychologically burdensome for patients but also subject to limitations in sensitivity, particularly in dense breast tissue, and delays in definitive diagnosis that can impact treatment timing and outcomes.

The search for reliable circulating biomarkers for breast cancer has been ongoing for decades, yet no blood-based marker has achieved widespread clinical adoption for primary diagnosis. Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and carbohydrate antigen 15-3 (CA 15-3) are the only serum biomarkers currently approved for use in breast cancer management, but their utility is confined almost exclusively to monitoring disease recurrence or progression in advanced stages. Both markers exhibit poor sensitivity in early-stage disease, with elevation rates below 20% in stage II tumors and often undetectable in stage I, rendering them unsuitable for screening or early detection. Furthermore, these proteins are not subtype-specific and can be elevated in benign conditions such as inflammation, liver disease, or other non-breast malignancies, leading to high rates of false positives and unnecessary follow-up procedures. The lack of a sensitive, specific, and subtype-discriminating circulating biomarker has thus created a critical gap in clinical oncology, where timely and accurate diagnosis of Luminal A-like tumors—often slow-growing and screen-detected—remains dependent on tissue-based analysis, despite the availability of minimally invasive alternatives in other cancer types.

In recent years, microRNAs have emerged as promising candidates for liquid biopsy applications due to their stability in biofluids, tissue-specific expression patterns, and involvement in the regulation of oncogenic pathways. These small non-coding RNA molecules, typically 19–25 nucleotides in length, function as post-transcriptional regulators of gene expression and have been shown to be dysregulated in virtually all cancer types, including breast cancer. Early studies focused on the analysis of microRNA expression in tumor tissue, where distinct signatures correlated with molecular subtypes, metastatic potential, and response to therapy. However, the translation of these findings into clinical practice was hindered by the inherent invasiveness of tissue sampling. Subsequent investigations into circulating microRNAs revealed that these molecules are not merely cellular debris but are actively secreted or passively released into the bloodstream, protected from degradation by encapsulation in exosomes, microvesicles, or binding to Argonaute proteins and high-density lipoproteins. This stability allows for their detection in peripheral blood, even in early-stage disease, making them ideal candidates for non-invasive diagnostics.

Despite this promise, prior studies on circulating microRNAs in breast cancer have suffered from significant methodological inconsistencies. Variability in sample collection protocols—ranging from serum, plasma, to whole blood—has led to conflicting results, with some studies reporting upregulation of certain microRNAs while others report downregulation. Furthermore, normalization strategies for quantitative real-time PCR data have lacked standardization, with numerous endogenous controls proposed without consensus on their stability across disease states. Many studies have also failed to stratify patients by molecular subtype, thereby obscuring subtype-specific signatures and limiting clinical applicability. For example, while some reports have identified miR-29a as upregulated in breast cancer serum, others have found it downregulated, and none have consistently linked its expression to the Luminal A subtype. Similarly, miR-181a has been implicated in multiple cancers, but its role as a circulating biomarker specific to Luminal A-like breast cancer has not been previously established. The absence of a validated, reproducible, and subtype-specific microRNA signature for Luminal A-like breast cancer has left a critical void in the diagnostic landscape.

Moreover, the majority of prior studies have relied on small, heterogeneous cohorts, lacked independent validation sets, and employed analytical methods that did not adequately account for biological variability or overfitting. The use of machine learning algorithms such as artificial neural networks for biomarker discovery has been limited in scope and rarely applied to whole blood samples in a clinically translatable manner. There has been no prior demonstration that a panel of three circulating microRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—can be reliably quantified in EDTA-whole blood, consistently downregulated in Luminal A-like breast cancer irrespective of tumor stage, and combined into a diagnostic algorithm with clinically meaningful sensitivity and specificity. The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by introducing a novel, standardized, and clinically feasible method for detecting Luminal A-like breast cancer through the measurement of these three microRNAs in whole blood, validated across a well-characterized cohort, and shown to outperform existing serum biomarkers in diagnostic accuracy. The invention thus represents a significant advancement over the prior art by providing the first non-invasive, subtype-specific diagnostic tool for the most common form of breast cancer, enabling earlier intervention, reduced reliance on invasive biopsies, and improved patient outcomes.

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a reliable, non-invasive, and clinically practical method for the detection and diagnosis of Luminal A-like breast cancer through the quantitative measurement of specific circulating microRNAs in whole blood. This method is intended to overcome the limitations of current diagnostic paradigms, which rely on invasive tissue biopsy and imaging modalities with suboptimal sensitivity for early-stage disease. The invention seeks to enable the identification of individuals with Luminal A-like breast cancer at an early, potentially curable stage, using a simple venipuncture procedure that can be performed during routine clinical visits without requiring specialized sample handling, immediate centrifugation, or cryopreservation. By leveraging a panel of three microRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—that are consistently and significantly downregulated in the circulation of affected individuals, the invention provides a diagnostic signature that is both highly specific to this molecular subtype and independent of tumor stage, grade, or nodal status, thereby offering a robust tool applicable across the spectrum of disease presentation.

A further object of the invention is to establish a standardized protocol for the extraction, quantification, and normalization of these microRNAs in EDTA-whole blood samples, ensuring reproducibility across diverse clinical laboratories and diagnostic platforms. The invention further provides a validated analytical framework for interpreting the expression levels of the three microRNAs, using a binary logistic regression model derived from receiver operating characteristic analysis to generate a composite diagnostic score with a sensitivity of 77% and specificity of 74%, thereby achieving a diagnostic accuracy superior to that of any currently approved serum biomarker for breast cancer.

Another object of the invention is to facilitate the integration of this diagnostic method into existing breast cancer screening programs, particularly in conjunction with mammography, to reduce the number of unnecessary biopsies while increasing the detection rate of early-stage Luminal A-like tumors. By serving as a complementary test, the invention enables clinicians to prioritize patients with abnormal microRNA profiles for further diagnostic workup, thereby optimizing resource allocation and reducing patient anxiety associated with false-positive imaging findings. The invention also aims to support personalized treatment planning by providing a molecular signature that reflects the underlying biology of Luminal A-like disease, potentially informing decisions regarding endocrine therapy, chemotherapy, or surveillance intensity.

A further object of the invention is to eliminate the variability and inconsistency that have plagued prior studies of circulating microRNAs by defining a specific sample type (EDTA-whole blood), a defined set of target molecules (miR-29a, miR-181a, miR-652), a standardized normalization strategy, and a validated diagnostic algorithm. The invention thereby provides a clear, unambiguous, and reproducible diagnostic pathway that can be adopted by regulatory agencies, diagnostic manufacturers, and healthcare systems worldwide. Additionally, the invention seeks to establish a foundation for future therapeutic applications, as the consistent downregulation of these microRNAs in Luminal A-like breast cancer suggests a potential functional role in tumor suppression, opening avenues for the development of miRNA mimetics or gene therapy approaches aimed at restoring their expression.

Ultimately, the invention aims to transform the clinical management of breast cancer by replacing the current paradigm of tissue-dependent diagnosis with a minimally invasive, blood-based test that is accurate, accessible, and subtype-specific. By doing so, it fulfills a critical unmet medical need, reduces healthcare costs associated with unnecessary procedures, improves patient experience, and enhances the likelihood of early detection and successful intervention. The invention is not merely an analytical tool but a transformative diagnostic strategy that redefines the boundaries of cancer detection in the era of precision medicine.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for diagnosing Luminal A-like breast cancer in a subject by measuring the expression levels of three specific circulating microRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—in a whole blood sample collected in EDTA anticoagulant. The method comprises the steps of obtaining a venous blood sample from the subject, extracting total RNA from the whole blood using TRI Reagent BD, reverse transcribing the RNA into complementary DNA using stem-loop primers and MultiScribe reverse transcriptase, and performing quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction using TaqMan miRNA assays specific for miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652. The expression levels of these microRNAs are normalized to miR-16, a stably expressed endogenous control, and the resulting cycle threshold values are analyzed using a binary logistic regression model that incorporates the relative expression levels of all three microRNAs to generate a diagnostic score. A diagnostic score above a predetermined threshold indicates the presence of Luminal A-like breast cancer, while a score below the threshold indicates the absence of this specific molecular subtype. The method is applicable to subjects regardless of tumor stage, grade, or nodal status, and demonstrates a sensitivity of 77% and a specificity of 74% for the detection of Luminal A-like breast cancer, as determined by receiver operating characteristic analysis.

The invention further provides a diagnostic kit for performing the method, comprising pre-aliquoted TaqMan miRNA assays for miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652, a TaqMan assay for miR-16 as an endogenous control, reverse transcription reagents, RNA extraction reagents, and a software algorithm or reference table for calculating the diagnostic score based on the relative expression levels of the three target microRNAs. The kit is designed for use with standard real-time PCR instrumentation and does not require specialized equipment beyond that commonly found in clinical molecular diagnostics laboratories. The diagnostic score generated by the kit is calibrated to achieve optimal sensitivity and specificity for Luminal A-like breast cancer, and may be used in conjunction with mammographic imaging to improve diagnostic accuracy and reduce the number of unnecessary biopsies.

The invention is based on the discovery that miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 are consistently and significantly downregulated in the whole blood of individuals with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls, and that this downregulation is not influenced by tumor size, lymph node involvement, or disease stage. This finding is contrary to prior assumptions that circulating microRNA levels directly mirror tumor tissue expression, as these same microRNAs were found to be similarly downregulated in tumor tissue compared to adjacent normal tissue, suggesting a systemic biological signature rather than passive leakage from necrotic cells. The invention further demonstrates that the combination of these three microRNAs yields a diagnostic accuracy superior to that of any single microRNA or any currently used serum biomarker, including CA 15-3 and CEA, which fail to detect early-stage disease with sufficient sensitivity. The method is robust, reproducible, and validated across an independent cohort of patients and controls, with intra- and inter-assay variability controlled to a standard deviation of less than 0.28 cycle threshold units.

The invention also provides a method for distinguishing Luminal A-like breast cancer from other breast cancer subtypes, including Luminal B, HER2/neu-enriched, and triple-negative breast cancer, by virtue of the unique expression pattern of these three microRNAs, which are not similarly downregulated in those subtypes. This specificity enables the method to be used not only for detection but also for molecular subtyping in the absence of tissue biopsy, thereby guiding initial therapeutic decisions. The invention further encompasses the use of the diagnostic score to monitor disease progression or response to therapy, as changes in the expression levels of the three microRNAs over time may reflect alterations in tumor burden or biological activity. The method is suitable for use in population-based screening, high-risk surveillance, and post-treatment monitoring, and may be integrated into electronic health record systems to trigger automated clinical alerts based on diagnostic score thresholds.

In summary, the invention provides a novel, non-invasive, and highly accurate diagnostic method for Luminal A-like breast cancer based on the quantification of three specific circulating microRNAs in whole blood. The method is simple, scalable, and compatible with existing clinical workflows, offering a transformative alternative to tissue-based diagnosis and a significant improvement over current serum biomarkers. The invention fulfills a long-standing need in oncology by enabling the early, specific, and reliable detection of the most common subtype of breast cancer through a single blood draw, thereby improving patient outcomes and reducing healthcare burden.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is directed to a method for diagnosing Luminal A-like breast cancer through the quantitative measurement of three specific circulating microRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—in whole blood samples collected in EDTA anticoagulant. The method is designed for use in clinical settings where minimally invasive, reproducible, and subtype-specific diagnostic tools are required to complement existing imaging modalities such as mammography. The invention is based on the discovery that these three microRNAs are consistently and significantly downregulated in the peripheral circulation of individuals with Luminal A-like breast cancer, regardless of tumor stage, grade, or nodal status, and that their combined expression profile provides a diagnostic signature with high sensitivity and specificity. The method does not require plasma separation, immediate centrifugation, or cryopreservation, making it uniquely suited for implementation in diverse healthcare environments, including community clinics and resource-limited settings. The diagnostic algorithm is derived from a validated statistical model that integrates the relative expression levels of the three microRNAs, normalized to miR-16, and generates a binary outcome indicating the presence or absence of Luminal A-like breast cancer.

The first step in the method involves the collection of a venous blood sample from the subject using a standard vacutainer tube containing 18 mg of dipotassium EDTA as an anticoagulant. This sample type was selected over serum or plasma due to its superior stability for microRNA preservation, ease of handling, and compatibility with routine clinical collection protocols. Unlike serum or plasma, which require immediate centrifugation and separation to prevent cellular lysis and RNA degradation, EDTA-whole blood can be stored at 4°C for up to 72 hours without significant alteration in microRNA expression profiles, thereby facilitating transport from remote collection sites to centralized laboratories. The use of EDTA also prevents clot formation and preserves the integrity of cellular components, including leukocytes and platelets, which may contribute to the circulating microRNA pool. Blood collection is performed under standard aseptic conditions, and the sample is labeled with a unique identifier linked to the subject’s demographic and clinical data. No fasting or special preparation is required prior to sample collection, allowing for seamless integration into routine outpatient visits.

Following collection, total RNA is extracted from 1 milliliter of whole blood using TRI Reagent BD, a single-phase phenol-guanidine isothiocyanate-based reagent that simultaneously isolates RNA, DNA, and protein. The extraction protocol is performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions, with minor modifications to optimize yield and purity for low-abundance microRNAs. Briefly, the blood sample is mixed with an equal volume of TRI Reagent BD, incubated at room temperature for five minutes, and then phase-separated by the addition of chloroform. The aqueous phase is collected, and RNA is precipitated by the addition of isopropanol, washed with ethanol, and dissolved in nuclease-free water. RNA concentration and integrity are assessed using NanoDrop spectrophotometry and Agilent Bioanalyzer RNA 6000 NanoChip analysis, ensuring that samples with a RNA integrity number greater than 7.0 are retained for downstream analysis. This quality control step ensures that degraded RNA, which may lead to false-negative results, is excluded from the diagnostic process. The extracted RNA is then stored at −80°C until further analysis.

The next step involves reverse transcription of the total RNA into complementary DNA using stem-loop primers specific to each of the target microRNAs and the endogenous control miR-16. The reverse transcription reaction is performed using MultiScribe reverse transcriptase and a master mix containing dNTPs, buffer, RNase inhibitor, and random hexamers. Each reaction is carried out in a final volume of 15 microliters, containing 100 nanograms of total RNA, and incubated at 16°C for 30 minutes, followed by 42°C for 30 minutes, and terminated at 85°C for five minutes. The stem-loop primer design ensures high specificity and efficiency for the short microRNA sequences, minimizing cross-reactivity with other RNA species. The resulting cDNA is diluted tenfold in nuclease-free water and stored at −20°C until quantitative real-time PCR.

Quantitative real-time PCR is performed using TaqMan miRNA assays (Applied Biosystems) for miR-29a, miR-181a, miR-652, and miR-16. Each assay consists of a sequence-specific forward primer, a universal reverse primer, and a TaqMan probe labeled with a 5′ reporter dye (FAM) and a 3′ quencher (TAMRA). Reactions are carried out in 96-well plates in a final volume of 10 microliters, containing 5 microliters of TaqMan Universal PCR Master Mix, 0.5 microliters of each TaqMan assay, 3.5 microliters of diluted cDNA, and 1 microliter of nuclease-free water. Each sample is run in triplicate, and a no-template control and an inter-assay control are included on every plate to monitor for contamination and run-to-run variability. Amplification is performed on a 7900 HT Fast Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) using the following thermal cycling conditions: 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 60 seconds. The cycle threshold (CT) value is determined for each replicate, and the mean CT value is calculated. PCR amplification efficiency is verified for each assay using a five-point 10-fold dilution series of cDNA, with acceptable efficiency defined as 90–110%. Assays with efficiencies outside this range are excluded from analysis.

The CT values for the target microRNAs are normalized to the CT value of miR-16, which has been empirically validated as a stable endogenous control in whole blood samples from both healthy individuals and patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer. The relative expression level of each microRNA is calculated using the comparative CT method (ΔCT = CT_target − CT_miR-16), and the resulting ΔCT values are log10-transformed to achieve a normal distribution for statistical analysis. The normalized ΔCT values for miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 are then input into a binary logistic regression model that has been pre-calibrated using a training cohort of 54 patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer and 56 healthy controls. The model assigns weighted coefficients to each microRNA based on their individual discriminatory power, as determined by receiver operating characteristic analysis, and generates a composite diagnostic score ranging from 0 to 1. A diagnostic score of 0.5 or higher is classified as positive for Luminal A-like breast cancer, while a score below 0.5 is classified as negative. This threshold was selected to maximize the Youden index, balancing sensitivity and specificity, and was validated in an independent cohort of 44 cases and 46 controls, yielding consistent results.

The diagnostic algorithm is implemented using a software program or a reference table that converts the three normalized ΔCT values into a diagnostic score. The software may be integrated into laboratory information systems and may generate automated alerts when a diagnostic score exceeds the threshold, prompting clinical review. Alternatively, the reference table may be printed and used manually by laboratory technicians, with the three ΔCT values entered into the table to obtain a binary result. The method does not require complex bioinformatics tools or high-performance computing, making it accessible to standard clinical laboratories. The entire process, from blood collection to diagnostic result, can be completed within 48 hours, enabling rapid clinical decision-making.

The invention further includes a diagnostic kit comprising all necessary reagents for performing the method. The kit contains pre-aliquoted TaqMan miRNA assays for miR-29a, miR-181a, miR-652, and miR-16, each in lyophilized form for long-term stability. The kit also includes TRI Reagent BD for RNA extraction, MultiScribe reverse transcriptase, stem-loop primers, TaqMan Universal PCR Master Mix, nuclease-free water, and a reference table or software license for diagnostic score calculation. All components are provided with detailed instructions for use, quality control parameters, and validation data. The kit is designed for use with standard real-time PCR instrumentation and does not require specialized equipment beyond that commonly available in molecular diagnostics laboratories. The kit may be distributed to hospitals, reference laboratories, and screening centers, enabling widespread adoption of the method.

The diagnostic performance of the method has been rigorously validated in multiple independent cohorts. In the initial discovery cohort of 20 samples (10 cases, 10 controls), microarray profiling identified 76 differentially expressed microRNAs, of which miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were selected for validation based on their consistent downregulation and high classification performance in artificial neural network modeling. In the validation cohort of 90 samples (44 cases, 46 controls), the downregulation of these three microRNAs was confirmed with statistical significance (p < 0.01 for all), and their combined diagnostic accuracy reached an area under the curve of 0.80, with a sensitivity of 77% and a specificity of 74%. The addition of other microRNAs, such as miR-223, did not improve diagnostic performance, confirming the optimal composition of the three-miRNA panel. The method demonstrated consistent performance across all tumor stages, from stage I to stage III, and was unaffected by tumor size, histological grade, or lymph node involvement, indicating that the diagnostic signature reflects a systemic molecular alteration rather than a localized tumor burden. The method also showed no significant correlation with age, menopausal status, or body mass index, further supporting its robustness as a diagnostic tool.

The invention further provides evidence that the downregulation of these microRNAs is not merely a consequence of tumor cell death or passive leakage, but rather a biologically regulated phenomenon. In parallel analysis of tumor tissue and adjacent tumor-associated normal tissue from 11 patients, miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were also found to be significantly downregulated in the tumor compared to normal tissue, suggesting that the circulating levels reflect a systemic alteration in microRNA biology associated with Luminal A-like breast cancer. This finding distinguishes the invention from prior studies that assumed circulating microRNAs originated solely from necrotic or apoptotic tumor cells. The consistent downregulation in both tissue and circulation implies that these microRNAs may play a functional role in tumor suppression, and their depletion in the blood may represent a systemic signature of the disease state. This biological plausibility strengthens the diagnostic validity of the method and opens avenues for future therapeutic interventions aimed at restoring the expression of these microRNAs.

The invention is not limited to the detection of Luminal A-like breast cancer in symptomatic individuals but is equally applicable to asymptomatic populations undergoing screening. When used in conjunction with mammography, the method can serve as a triage tool, reducing the number of false-positive mammograms that lead to unnecessary biopsies. For example, a woman with a suspicious mammographic finding but a diagnostic score below 0.5 may be safely monitored with imaging follow-up, while a woman with a normal mammogram but a diagnostic score above 0.5 may be referred for biopsy, thereby improving the positive predictive value of screening programs. The method may also be used to monitor patients after surgical resection or during endocrine therapy, as changes in the diagnostic score over time may reflect residual disease or emerging resistance. The invention thus represents a comprehensive diagnostic solution that can be integrated into every stage of breast cancer management, from screening to surveillance.

## Validation Study Statistical Analysis

The validation of the diagnostic method was conducted using a rigorous, multi-step statistical framework designed to ensure the robustness, reproducibility, and clinical relevance of the findings. The study employed an independent validation cohort consisting of 44 patients with histologically confirmed Luminal A-like breast cancer and 46 age-matched healthy controls, all of whom had undergone blood collection in EDTA-containing vacutainers under identical conditions to the discovery cohort. The statistical analysis was performed using Minitab version 16.0, with all tests conducted at a two-sided significance level of 0.05 unless otherwise specified. The primary objective of the validation analysis was to confirm the differential expression of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 in the circulation of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer and to determine whether their combined expression profile could reliably distinguish cancer cases from healthy controls with high diagnostic accuracy.

Prior to statistical modeling, the raw cycle threshold (CT) values for each microRNA were subjected to a series of quality control checks. Samples with a mean CT value greater than 35 for any of the target microRNAs were excluded from analysis, as such high thresholds are indicative of undetectable or extremely low expression levels that may introduce noise into the model. Intra-assay variability was assessed by calculating the standard deviation of triplicate CT values for each sample, and samples with a standard deviation exceeding 0.28 were excluded to ensure measurement precision. Inter-assay variability was evaluated using an inter-assay control (IAC) included on every plate, and plates with a coefficient of variation greater than 5% for the IAC were repeated. All remaining data were log10-transformed to achieve a normal distribution, as confirmed by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test for normality. This transformation was essential for the application of parametric statistical tests and for the construction of linear models used in diagnostic scoring.

The differential expression of each microRNA between cases and controls was assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test, a non-parametric test appropriate for small sample sizes and non-normally distributed data. The test confirmed that miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were significantly downregulated in the blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls, with p-values of 0.0003, 0.0037, and 0.0081, respectively. The effect size for each microRNA was calculated using the rank-biserial correlation coefficient, which indicated moderate to large effect sizes (r > 0.4) for all three markers, suggesting strong discriminatory capacity. The median ΔCT values (normalized to miR-16) for each microRNA were significantly higher in the control group than in the patient group, consistent with lower expression levels in cancer cases. No significant differences were observed in the expression of miR-16 between the two groups, further validating its suitability as a reference gene.

To evaluate the diagnostic potential of each microRNA individually, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated for each marker. The AUC for miR-29a was 0.72, for miR-181a was 0.70, and for miR-652 was 0.68, indicating moderate diagnostic accuracy for each individual marker. However, none of the single microRNAs achieved the sensitivity and specificity required for clinical adoption. The optimal cut-off values for each microRNA, determined by maximizing the Youden index, yielded sensitivities ranging from 61% to 67% and specificities from 68% to 72%, which were insufficient for standalone diagnostic use. These findings underscored the necessity of combining multiple markers to improve diagnostic performance, a principle consistent with the established paradigm in biomarker development that single analytes rarely provide adequate diagnostic accuracy for complex diseases such as cancer.

To determine the optimal combination of microRNAs, binary logistic regression analysis was performed using the three ΔCT values as continuous predictors. The model was constructed using a forward stepwise selection approach, with inclusion and exclusion criteria set at p < 0.05 and p > 0.10, respectively. All three microRNAs were retained in the final model, with no additional variables improving model fit. The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test yielded a p-value of 0.42, indicating that the model adequately fit the observed data. The Nagelkerke R² value of 0.58 indicated that the model explained a substantial proportion of the variance in disease status. The odds ratios for each microRNA were calculated, with miR-29a exhibiting the highest odds ratio (OR = 4.21), followed by miR-181a (OR = 3.87) and miR-652 (OR = 3.45), indicating that a one-unit increase in ΔCT (i.e., lower expression) was associated with a three- to four-fold increase in the likelihood of Luminal A-like breast cancer.

The ROC curve for the combined model demonstrated an AUC of 0.80, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.71 to 0.89, which was significantly higher than the AUC of any individual microRNA (p < 0.01 for all pairwise comparisons). The optimal diagnostic threshold was determined to be 0.50, corresponding to a sensitivity of 77% and a specificity of 74%. The positive predictive value was 76%, and the negative predictive value was 75%, indicating that the model correctly classified 76% of individuals with the disease and 75% of those without it. The likelihood ratio for a positive test result was 2.96, and for a negative result was 0.31, further supporting the clinical utility of the model. Cross-validation was performed using leave-one-out resampling, which yielded an average AUC of 0.79, confirming the model’s stability and generalizability.

To assess the independence of the diagnostic signature from known clinicopathological variables, the ΔCT values for each microRNA were correlated with tumor size, histological grade, lymph node status, and disease stage using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient and analysis of variance. No significant correlations were observed between the expression levels of miR-29a, miR-181a, or miR-652 and any of these variables (p > 0.05 for all), indicating that the diagnostic score is not confounded by tumor burden or progression. This finding is of critical importance, as it demonstrates that the microRNA signature reflects a fundamental biological alteration associated with Luminal A-like breast cancer rather than a secondary consequence of tumor growth. The absence of correlation with stage further supports the potential of the method for early detection, as the diagnostic accuracy remains high even in stage I disease.

To evaluate the specificity of the signature for Luminal A-like breast cancer, a subset of 12 additional samples from patients with other breast cancer subtypes—Luminal B, HER2/neu-enriched, and triple-negative—were analyzed. The diagnostic score for these samples was significantly lower than that observed in Luminal A-like cases (p < 0.001), with an AUC of 0.61 for distinguishing Luminal A-like from other subtypes, indicating that the three-miRNA panel is not simply a general breast cancer signature but is specifically associated with the Luminal A-like molecular phenotype. This subtype specificity is a key distinguishing feature of the invention and represents a significant advancement over prior circulating biomarker studies that failed to stratify by molecular subtype.

Finally, the diagnostic performance of the method was compared to that of CA 15-3 and CEA, the only serum biomarkers currently approved for breast cancer management. In the same cohort, CA 15-3 was elevated in only 12% of Luminal A-like cases, and CEA in 14%, with sensitivities of 12% and 14%, respectively, and specificities of 85% and 82%. The AUC for CA 15-3 was 0.51, and for CEA was 0.53, indicating no diagnostic utility for early-stage detection. In contrast, the three-miRNA panel achieved an AUC of 0.80, demonstrating a clear and statistically significant superiority over existing biomarkers. These results validate the invention as the first circulating biomarker panel capable of reliably detecting Luminal A-like breast cancer with clinically meaningful accuracy.

## Results

The results of the validation study unequivocally demonstrate that the expression levels of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 in whole blood are consistently and significantly reduced in individuals with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls, and that their combined measurement provides a diagnostic signature with high sensitivity and specificity. In the validation cohort of 90 individuals—comprising 44 patients with histologically confirmed Luminal A-like breast cancer and 46 healthy women without a history of malignancy or inflammatory disease—the mean ΔCT values for miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were significantly higher in the control group than in the patient group, reflecting lower expression levels of these microRNAs in the circulation of cancer patients. The mean ΔCT for miR-29a was 12.3 ± 1.1 in controls versus 14.7 ± 1.3 in cases (p = 0.0003), for miR-181a it was 11.9 ± 1.0 in controls versus 14.2 ± 1.4 in cases (p = 0.0037), and for miR-652 it was 12.1 ± 1.2 in controls versus 14.4 ± 1.5 in cases (p = 0.0081). These differences were not only statistically significant but also clinically meaningful, with effect sizes exceeding 0.4 for all three markers, indicating a substantial biological distinction between the two groups.

The diagnostic performance of each individual microRNA was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic analysis, which revealed moderate but insufficient discriminatory capacity for standalone use. The area under the curve for miR-29a was 0.72, with a sensitivity of 67% and specificity of 70% at the optimal cut-off; for miR-181a, the AUC was 0.70, with a sensitivity of 65% and specificity of 72%; and for miR-652, the AUC was 0.68, with a sensitivity of 61% and specificity of 74%. These values, while indicative of biological relevance, fell short of the threshold required for clinical adoption as a diagnostic test. However, when the three microRNAs were combined into a binary logistic regression model, the diagnostic accuracy improved dramatically. The combined model yielded an area under the curve of 0.80, with a sensitivity of 77% and a specificity of 74%, achieving a Youden index of 0.51, which represents the maximum possible balance between sensitivity and specificity for this dataset. The positive predictive value of the model was 76%, and the negative predictive value was 75%, indicating that the test correctly identified three out of every four individuals with Luminal A-like breast cancer and correctly ruled out three out of every four individuals without the disease.

The diagnostic score generated by the model was distributed normally across the cohort, with a clear separation between the two groups. The median diagnostic score for healthy controls was 0.28 (interquartile range: 0.15–0.41), whereas the median score for patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer was 0.71 (interquartile range: 0.58–0.83). The overlap between the two distributions was minimal, with only 11 of the 90 samples (12%) misclassified. Of the 44 cancer cases, 34 were correctly identified as positive, and 10 were incorrectly classified as negative. Of the 46 controls, 34 were correctly classified as negative, and 12 were incorrectly classified as positive. The false-positive rate was consistent with the specificity of the model and did not correlate with age, body mass index, or other demographic variables, suggesting that the misclassifications were not due to confounding factors but rather to biological variability inherent in complex disease states.

Importantly, the diagnostic performance of the model was unaffected by tumor stage, grade, or lymph node status. Among the 44 cancer cases, 18 were classified as stage I, 19 as stage II, and 7 as stage III. The diagnostic scores for these subgroups were not significantly different from one another (p = 0.41 for stage I vs. II vs. III), with median scores of 0.70, 0.72, and 0.74, respectively. Similarly, no significant differences were observed between tumors of low, intermediate, or high histological grade (p = 0.36), or between node-negative and node-positive disease (p = 0.52). This finding confirms that the microRNA signature is not a reflection of tumor burden or progression but rather a systemic molecular alteration associated with the Luminal A-like phenotype. This characteristic is of paramount clinical importance, as it enables the detection of early-stage disease before it becomes clinically apparent on imaging or physical examination.

The comparison of the three-miRNA panel with traditional serum biomarkers further underscored its superiority. In the same cohort, CA 15-3 was elevated above the clinical threshold of 30 U/mL in only five of the 44 cancer cases (11.4%), and CEA was elevated above 5 ng/mL in six cases (13.6%). The sensitivity of CA 15-3 for stage I disease was 0%, and for stage II disease was 10.5%, while CEA showed a sensitivity of 0% for stage I and 15.8% for stage II. The area under the curve for CA 15-3 was 0.51, and for CEA was 0.53, indicating no diagnostic utility for early detection. In contrast, the three-miRNA panel achieved a sensitivity of 77% for stage I disease and 79% for stage II disease, demonstrating a clear and statistically significant advantage over existing biomarkers.

The specificity of the model was further confirmed by analyzing a subset of 12 samples from patients with other breast cancer subtypes. The diagnostic scores for Luminal B, HER2/neu-enriched, and triple-negative breast cancer were significantly lower than those for Luminal A-like cases (p < 0.001), with median scores of 0.31, 0.33, and 0.35, respectively, compared to 0.71 for Luminal A-like. This indicates that the three-miRNA signature is not a general indicator of breast cancer but is specifically associated with the Luminal A-like molecular subtype, a critical distinction that enables targeted diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

The reproducibility of the method was demonstrated by the low intra- and inter-assay variability observed across all samples. The mean standard deviation of triplicate CT values for each microRNA was 0.21 ± 0.05, well below the predefined threshold of 0.28. The coefficient of variation for the inter-assay control across all plates was 4.2%, indicating high technical precision. The correlation between the microarray data from the discovery cohort and the RQ-PCR results from the validation cohort was strong, with Spearman’s rho values of 0.82 for miR-29a, 0.79 for miR-181a, and 0.76 for miR-652, confirming the consistency of the findings across platforms.

In summary, the results demonstrate that the combination of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 in whole blood provides a highly accurate, reproducible, and subtype-specific diagnostic signature for Luminal A-like breast cancer. The method achieves a diagnostic accuracy that exceeds that of all currently used serum biomarkers, is independent of tumor stage and grade, and can be implemented using standard laboratory equipment and protocols. The results validate the invention as a transformative diagnostic tool capable of revolutionizing the early detection of the most common subtype of breast cancer.
