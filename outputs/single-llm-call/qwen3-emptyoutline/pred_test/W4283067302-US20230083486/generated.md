# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of reinforcement learning has long grappled with the challenge of efficient exploration in environments where extrinsic rewards are sparse, delayed, or structurally hidden within complex sequences of interactions. Traditional exploration strategies, such as ε-greedy policies or entropy regularization, often fail to scale to high-dimensional, partially observable, or procedurally generated domains because they lack the capacity to distinguish between regions of the environment that are merely novel and those that are meaningfully informative. In such settings, random exploration becomes prohibitively inefficient, leading to exponentially long training times or complete failure to discover rewarding trajectories. To address this limitation, a class of methods known as curiosity-driven exploration has emerged, which seeks to internalize the notion of learning progress by rewarding the agent for encountering states that are difficult to predict. These methods are grounded in the principle that an agent’s intrinsic motivation to reduce uncertainty about its environment naturally leads it to explore regions of high information gain. Among the most prominent approaches in this category are Random Network Distillation (RND), which uses the prediction error of a randomly initialized neural network as an intrinsic reward, and the Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM), which trains a forward dynamics model to predict next-state representations and uses the residual error as a signal. While these methods have demonstrated success in certain domains—particularly in 2D Atari games—they often falter in more complex environments characterized by continuous control, partial observability, multi-step dependencies, and procedural generation. This is because their underlying predictive models are either too simplistic, operating at the pixel level where noise dominates signal, or too narrowly focused on one-step transitions, failing to capture the temporal structure necessary for long-horizon exploration. Furthermore, these approaches typically decouple the learning of the world model from the learning of the policy, resulting in suboptimal representations that are not explicitly optimized for exploration. As a result, the representations learned by RND and ICM are often brittle, sensitive to architectural choices, and incapable of generalizing across diverse tasks without extensive hyperparameter tuning or auxiliary objectives. Recent advances in self-supervised representation learning, particularly the Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) framework, have shown that predictive models trained via bootstrapped latent targets—where a moving average of the model’s own parameters generates stable, slowly evolving targets—can learn rich, semantically meaningful representations without the need for contrastive losses or data augmentations. These representations have proven effective in domains ranging from computer vision to graph learning, yet their potential for driving exploration in reinforcement learning has remained largely unexplored. The critical insight underpinning this invention is that the same predictive mechanism that enables robust representation learning can, when applied in a recurrent, multi-step, latent-space setting, simultaneously serve as the foundation for an intrinsic reward signal that is both temporally coherent and semantically grounded. By unifying the objectives of representation learning and exploration into a single predictive loss, the proposed method eliminates the need for separate modules, auxiliary networks, or hand-engineered reward shaping. This integration not only simplifies the architecture but also ensures that the agent’s curiosity is intrinsically aligned with its ability to model the world, thereby fostering a virtuous cycle in which improved predictions lead to better exploration, and richer exploration data, in turn, refine the predictive model. The resulting system, named BYOL-Explore, operates without external supervision, human demonstrations, or task-specific priors, and is capable of achieving human-level and even superhuman performance on benchmark environments that have previously required such aids. Its generality is demonstrated across vastly different domains—from the pixel-based, fully observable Atari games to the first-person, 3D, procedurally generated, and partially observable DM-HARD-8 suite—where it consistently outperforms state-of-the-art alternatives. Moreover, BYOL-Explore achieves these results using a single, shared policy and world model across all tasks, a feat that underscores its capacity for transfer and scalability. The method’s success lies not merely in its technical novelty but in its conceptual elegance: it treats exploration not as a separate problem to be solved with an external reward function, but as an emergent property of an agent that is fundamentally committed to understanding its environment through predictive consistency. This paradigm shift—where the agent’s curiosity is not imposed from outside but arises naturally from its own learning dynamics—represents a foundational advancement in the design of autonomous learning systems.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a novel method and system for enabling autonomous agents to perform efficient, curiosity-driven exploration in complex reinforcement learning environments characterized by sparse extrinsic rewards, partial observability, and high-dimensional state spaces. The invention comprises a unified framework in which a single predictive model, trained via a self-supervised bootstrapping mechanism, simultaneously learns a latent representation of the environment and generates an intrinsic reward signal that guides the agent’s exploratory behavior. The core innovation lies in the integration of two previously distinct objectives—representation learning and exploration—into a single, coherent learning objective derived from a recurrent latent-predictive architecture inspired by the Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) methodology. The system operates by maintaining two parallel neural networks: an online network and a target network. The online network processes sequences of observations and actions to produce latent representations of the agent’s history and predicts future latent states over a multi-step horizon. The target network, which is an exponentially moving average of the online network’s parameters, generates stable, slowly evolving targets for these future predictions. The learning signal is derived from the cosine distance between the online network’s predictions and the target network’s outputs, with the stop-gradient operator applied to the targets to prevent backpropagation through them. This loss function serves a dual purpose: it trains the online network to develop increasingly accurate and informative latent representations of the environment, while simultaneously providing a measure of prediction uncertainty that is directly converted into an intrinsic reward for the agent’s policy. To ensure numerical stability and adapt to the non-stationary nature of the intrinsic reward signal, the raw prediction errors are normalized using an exponentially moving average of their mean and variance, and optionally clipped to prioritize regions of highest uncertainty. The resulting intrinsic reward is then combined with the extrinsic reward from the environment and used to train a policy via any standard reinforcement learning algorithm, such as VMPO or PPO. Crucially, the encoder and recurrent state components of the predictive model may be shared with the policy network, allowing the policy to benefit from the same latent representations that drive exploration, thereby creating a synergistic feedback loop between perception, prediction, and action. The method requires no external supervision, human demonstrations, task-specific priors, or hand-crafted features, and operates effectively across a wide range of environments, including fully observable 2D games with complex dynamics and partially observable 3D environments with continuous control and procedural generation. The invention demonstrates superior performance on benchmark tasks such as the 10 hardest exploration Atari games and the DM-HARD-8 suite, achieving human-level and superhuman performance in the majority of tasks without relying on auxiliary objectives or reward shaping. Notably, the method achieves these results using a single, unified agent trained across multiple tasks simultaneously, highlighting its scalability and generalization capacity. The system’s robustness is further evidenced by its ability to perform meaningful exploration even in the absence of extrinsic rewards, discovering complex behavioral sequences such as navigating multi-room dungeons or manipulating physical objects in 3D space purely through intrinsic motivation. The underlying architecture is computationally efficient, scalable to large-scale distributed training, and compatible with modern reinforcement learning frameworks, making it suitable for deployment in real-world robotic systems, simulated training environments, and adaptive learning platforms. By eliminating the need for separate curiosity modules, contrastive losses, or random projections, the invention provides a conceptually simple, theoretically grounded, and empirically powerful solution to the long-standing problem of exploration in reinforcement learning, establishing a new paradigm in which curiosity emerges naturally from the agent’s commitment to predictive consistency.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention comprises a comprehensive system and method for autonomous exploration in reinforcement learning environments through the use of a unified latent-predictive model that simultaneously learns a representation of the environment and generates an intrinsic reward signal to guide policy learning. The system is composed of three interdependent components: a latent-predictive world model, a reward normalization and prioritization mechanism, and a reinforcement learning agent that may optionally share representational components with the world model. Each component is designed to operate in concert, ensuring that the agent’s exploratory behavior is not an externally imposed heuristic but an emergent consequence of its internal predictive dynamics. The world model is the central innovation of the invention and is responsible for encoding observations into a latent space, predicting future latent states over a multi-step horizon, and generating the intrinsic reward signal based on the discrepancy between its predictions and the targets produced by a slowly evolving target network. The world model operates in a recurrent, multi-step framework that captures the temporal structure of the environment, distinguishing it from prior methods that rely on one-step prediction errors or pixel-level reconstruction losses. The model begins with an encoder network that transforms raw observations—such as RGB images, sensor readings, or text instructions—into a fixed-dimensional latent representation. This encoder is implemented as a deep convolutional neural network, such as a ResNet architecture, which is capable of extracting hierarchical features from high-dimensional inputs. The latent representation is then fed, along with the previous action taken by the agent, into a recurrent neural network cell, specifically a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) unit, which maintains a hidden state that encodes the entire history of observations and actions up to the current time step. This recurrent state, referred to as the closed-loop representation, serves as the context for predicting future latent states. The prediction is made by a separate open-loop recurrent network that unrolls the future trajectory of the agent’s latent state over a fixed horizon, without receiving new observations, thereby simulating the agent’s internal model of how the environment will evolve based on its current state and planned actions. The targets for these predictions are not derived from external supervision or fixed random projections, as in prior methods, but are generated by a target network that is an exponentially moving average of the online network’s parameters. This target network consists of a copy of the encoder and a separate, non-trainable recurrent cell that processes future observations to produce latent representations that serve as ground truth for the online network’s predictions. The parameters of the target network are updated after each training step using a slow-moving average, ensuring that the targets evolve gradually and remain stable over time, which prevents the online network from chasing rapidly changing or noisy signals. The loss function used to train the online network is defined as the average cosine distance between the predicted future latent states and the corresponding targets generated by the target network. Importantly, the stop-gradient operator is applied to the targets, preventing gradients from flowing back through them and ensuring that the learning signal arises solely from the online network’s ability to align its predictions with the slowly evolving targets. This mechanism, borrowed from the BYOL framework, ensures that the model learns to extract features that are predictive of future states without collapsing into trivial solutions such as memorizing the input or producing constant outputs. The resulting loss is computed across a batch of trajectories collected by the agent and is backpropagated through the online network’s parameters, updating both the encoder and the recurrent state dynamics. The same loss function that trains the world model is used to compute the intrinsic reward for the agent’s policy. Specifically, for each transition in the collected trajectory, the intrinsic reward is defined as the cumulative prediction error over the entire open-loop horizon, summed across all time steps leading to the next observation. This reward reflects the agent’s uncertainty about how the environment will evolve from its current state, with higher uncertainty corresponding to higher intrinsic reward. This formulation ensures that the agent is motivated to explore states that are difficult to predict, which are precisely the states that contain the most information for improving the world model. To account for the non-stationary nature of the intrinsic reward signal during training, the raw prediction errors are normalized using an exponentially moving average of their mean and mean squared value. This normalization process ensures that the intrinsic reward remains within a consistent scale, preventing the policy from being overwhelmed by early-stage high-variance signals or becoming insensitive to later, more refined predictions. The normalized reward is then optionally clipped to prioritize only those transitions with the highest prediction errors, effectively ignoring regions of the environment where the model has already achieved high predictive accuracy. This prioritization mechanism ensures that the agent focuses its learning efforts on the most informative regions of the state space, rather than attempting to optimize all sources of uncertainty simultaneously. The clipped reward is computed by subtracting the exponentially moving average of the normalized reward from each sample and setting negative values to zero, thereby creating a dynamic threshold that adapts to the agent’s learning progress. The resulting intrinsic reward is then combined with the extrinsic reward provided by the environment, either through a weighted sum or a multiplicative scheme, and used to train the agent’s policy via a standard reinforcement learning algorithm. The policy itself may be implemented using any off-the-shelf algorithm, such as VMPO, PPO, or A2C, and is typically composed of a policy head and a value head that operate on the same latent representation as the world model. In the shared representation variant of the invention, the encoder and recurrent state components of the world model are directly shared with the policy network, meaning that the policy learns its representations not only from the extrinsic reward signal but also from the predictive loss of the world model. This shared architecture creates a powerful feedback loop: as the policy explores new regions of the environment, it generates data that improves the world model’s predictions, which in turn generates more informative intrinsic rewards that guide the policy toward even more valuable regions. This synergy ensures that the representations learned by the policy are not only useful for maximizing cumulative reward but are also semantically meaningful, capturing the underlying structure of the environment rather than superficial correlations. The invention is designed to be highly scalable and compatible with distributed training architectures. The agent collects trajectories from multiple parallel actors running in separate environments, which are then batched and processed on high-performance hardware such as TPUs or GPUs. The world model and policy are updated using gradient descent on the combined loss, which includes both the predictive loss from the BYOL-Explore framework and the reinforcement learning loss from the policy optimization algorithm. The system requires no external supervision, human demonstrations, or task-specific priors, and operates in a completely self-supervised manner. It is capable of handling a wide variety of observation modalities, including visual inputs, continuous sensor readings, and symbolic instructions, and is robust to partial observability, procedural generation, and stochastic dynamics. The method has been evaluated on two benchmark suites: the 10 hardest exploration games in the Atari Learning Environment and the DM-HARD-8 suite of 3D navigation and manipulation tasks. In the Atari domain, the system achieves superhuman performance on all 10 games when trained with a longer sequence length and a larger model, demonstrating that the method scales effectively with computational resources. In the DM-HARD-8 suite, the system achieves human-level performance on five of the eight tasks and surpasses human performance on four, including the notoriously difficult Throw-across task, which previously required human demonstrations to solve. Remarkably, the system achieves these results using a single agent trained across all tasks simultaneously, without any task-specific parameters or reward shaping. The ablation studies conducted as part of the evaluation confirm the necessity of each component of the system: removing the bootstrapping mechanism by using fixed random targets leads to a dramatic drop in performance, demonstrating that the slowly evolving targets are essential for learning meaningful representations; reducing the prediction horizon to one step significantly impairs performance in 3D environments, confirming the importance of multi-step prediction for capturing long-horizon dependencies; and eliminating the reward prioritization mechanism reduces performance in tasks requiring fine-grained exploration, such as Montezuma’s Revenge, indicating that dynamic focus on high-uncertainty regions is critical for top-tier performance. The method also demonstrates exceptional performance in the purely intrinsic exploration regime, where no extrinsic reward is provided to the agent. In this setting, the agent is able to explore more than 20 rooms in Montezuma’s Revenge, surpassing the best previously reported results, and discovers complex behavioral sequences such as collecting keys, avoiding traps, and navigating ladders—all without ever receiving an extrinsic reward signal. This ability to generate meaningful, goal-directed behavior purely through curiosity underscores the depth of the representation learned by the system and its capacity to internalize the structure of the environment. The invention further demonstrates its generality by operating effectively in both discrete and continuous action spaces, handling tasks with mixed action types such as discrete button presses and continuous movement controls. The architecture is modular and extensible, allowing for the integration of additional components such as memory modules, attention mechanisms, or hierarchical planning layers without altering the core predictive framework. The system’s computational efficiency, scalability, and lack of reliance on external supervision make it uniquely suited for deployment in real-world applications, including robotic manipulation in unstructured environments, autonomous navigation in dynamic settings, and adaptive learning systems that must operate without human oversight. The invention represents a fundamental shift in the design of exploration algorithms, moving away from ad hoc reward shaping and external curiosity signals toward a principled, unified framework in which curiosity arises naturally from the agent’s commitment to predictive accuracy. By integrating representation learning and exploration into a single, self-supervised objective, the invention provides a robust, general, and scalable solution to one of the most enduring challenges in artificial intelligence.
