# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Phenylketonuria (PKU) is a rare but severe inherited metabolic disorder that affects approximately one in every fifteen thousand individuals globally. It arises from biallelic loss-of-function mutations in the gene encoding phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH), a hepatic enzyme responsible for the conversion of the essential amino acid phenylalanine into tyrosine. Without functional PAH, phenylalanine accumulates in the bloodstream and is shunted into alternative catabolic pathways, resulting in the production of neurotoxic metabolites such as phenylpyruvate, phenyllactate, and phenylacetate. The presence of phenylpyruvate in the urine, which imparts a musty odor, historically gave the condition its name. If left untreated, elevated plasma phenylalanine concentrations—often exceeding 1,200 micromolar—lead to irreversible neurological damage, including intellectual disability, seizures, behavioral disturbances, microcephaly, and motor dysfunction. Even with early diagnosis via newborn screening and strict dietary intervention, patients frequently experience suboptimal outcomes, including mood disorders, mild cognitive deficits, osteopenia, and chronic systemic inflammation, suggesting that current therapeutic modalities fail to fully normalize the biochemical milieu.

The cornerstone of current clinical management remains a lifelong, highly restrictive low-phenylalanine diet. This requires the elimination of high-protein foods such as meat, dairy, eggs, and legumes, and the exclusive use of synthetic medical foods and amino acid supplements formulated to provide all essential nutrients except phenylalanine. These medical foods, while nutritionally complete, are often unpalatable, expensive, and require significant preparation time, leading to poor long-term adherence, particularly among adolescents and adults who leave parental supervision. Insurance coverage for these specialized products is inconsistent, and many patients eventually abandon the regimen, resulting in dangerous elevations in plasma phenylalanine levels and subsequent neurological decline. Pregnant women with PKU face an additional layer of complexity: to prevent fetal damage, including congenital heart defects, microcephaly, and developmental delay, they must maintain plasma phenylalanine concentrations between 120 and 360 micromolar throughout gestation—a level far more stringent than what is required for non-pregnant individuals. Achieving this requires near-perfect dietary compliance, which is rarely sustainable without intensive medical supervision.

Alternative therapeutic approaches have been explored, including enzyme replacement therapy using pegylated phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PEG-PAL), a recombinant form of the enzyme derived from the bacterium *Anabaena variabilis*. PEG-PAL functions by catalyzing the deamination of phenylalanine into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia, thereby reducing systemic phenylalanine levels. While clinically effective in lowering plasma phenylalanine, PEG-PAL therapy is associated with significant limitations. The enzyme is highly immunogenic, leading to the development of neutralizing antibodies that diminish therapeutic efficacy over time. Administration requires frequent subcutaneous or intravenous injections, typically on a weekly basis, which imposes a substantial burden on patients and caregivers. Furthermore, the pegylation process, while intended to prolong half-life and reduce immunogenicity, introduces foreign chemical moieties that may trigger immune recognition and hypersensitivity reactions. The cost of PEG-PAL therapy is prohibitively high, rendering it inaccessible to many patients, particularly in low-resource settings.

Oral administration of naked phenylalanine ammonia lyase has been investigated as a less invasive alternative, but the enzyme is rapidly degraded by gastric acid and intestinal proteases before reaching the site of action in the small intestine. Attempts to protect the enzyme using enteric coatings or antacid buffers have yielded inconsistent results, with insufficient enzyme stability and bioavailability to achieve meaningful phenylalanine reduction. Moreover, even if the enzyme survives transit, its extracellular presence in the gastrointestinal lumen exposes it to immune surveillance, potentially triggering local or systemic immune responses that could compromise long-term use.

The human gastrointestinal tract is home to trillions of microorganisms, many of which play essential roles in digestion, nutrient metabolism, immune modulation, and barrier integrity. Certain bacterial species, particularly those belonging to the genus *Lactobacillus*, have been extensively studied for their safety profile, ability to survive gastric transit, and capacity to colonize the intestinal mucosa transiently or semi-permanently. These probiotic organisms are metabolically active in the gut lumen, where they interact with dietary components and host epithelial cells without eliciting systemic immune responses—a phenomenon referred to as immunological ignorance. This tolerance arises because the immune system in the gut is evolutionarily tuned to distinguish between commensal microbes and pathogens, and to avoid mounting inflammatory responses against the former. This biological principle suggests that if a therapeutic enzyme were expressed intracellularly within a safe, non-pathogenic, gut-adapted bacterium, it could function as a living bioreactor, continuously metabolizing phenylalanine in situ without triggering immune recognition or degradation by host proteases.

Previous studies have demonstrated that engineered probiotics can deliver therapeutic proteins to the gastrointestinal tract for the treatment of various conditions, including enzyme deficiencies and inflammatory diseases. However, no prior work has successfully combined the expression of a functional phenylalanine ammonia lyase within a *Lactobacillus reuteri* strain to achieve sustained, in vivo reduction of plasma phenylalanine in a mammalian model of PKU. The challenge lies not only in the genetic engineering of the host bacterium to express a heterologous, eukaryote-incompatible enzyme efficiently, but also in ensuring that the enzyme remains stable, active, and confined within the bacterial cell during transit through the harsh gastrointestinal environment. Additionally, the engineered strain must be capable of surviving and proliferating in sufficient numbers to exert a measurable metabolic effect, while remaining non-colonizing in the long term to meet regulatory safety standards.

The present invention addresses these unmet needs by introducing a novel, orally administered, genetically modified probiotic strain of *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C that constitutively expresses a codon-optimized version of phenylalanine ammonia lyase from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413. This strain, designated pHENOMMenal, is engineered to produce the enzyme intracellularly under the control of a strong, constitutive *Lactobacillus*-specific promoter and ribosome binding sequence, ensuring high-level expression without the need for exogenous inducers. The construct includes a transcriptional terminator derived from the *ermGT* gene to enhance mRNA stability and prevent read-through transcription. The resulting bacterium is freeze-dried into a stable powder formulation that can be mixed into standard food, enabling simple, non-invasive daily administration. In preclinical studies using the *PAHenu2* mouse model of PKU, oral administration of pHENOMMenal resulted in a statistically significant and sustained reduction in plasma phenylalanine levels without eliciting detectable systemic immune responses against the enzyme. Importantly, the engineered bacterium did not permanently colonize the host intestine, addressing a critical safety concern for genetically modified organisms intended for human use. This invention represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of PKU, offering a safe, affordable, patient-friendly, and scalable therapeutic modality that bypasses the limitations of dietary restriction and injectable enzyme therapies.

## Advantages Over Conventional Technologies

The invention offers a transformative set of advantages over existing therapeutic modalities for phenylketonuria, addressing fundamental shortcomings in efficacy, safety, accessibility, and patient adherence that have persisted for decades. Unlike the current standard of care—strict dietary restriction supplemented by synthetic medical foods—the present invention does not require patients to eliminate natural proteins from their diet or rely on unpalatable, costly, and nutritionally incomplete supplements. Instead, it enables patients to consume a more normal, diverse, and socially acceptable diet while simultaneously reducing systemic phenylalanine levels through a biological mechanism that operates autonomously within the gastrointestinal tract. This represents a qualitative leap in quality of life, particularly for adolescents and adults who struggle with dietary compliance, and for pregnant women who must maintain extraordinarily tight metabolic control.

Compared to injectable enzyme replacement therapies such as PEG-PAL, the invention eliminates the need for frequent parenteral administration, thereby removing the physical discomfort, injection-site reactions, and psychological burden associated with daily or weekly injections. The immunogenicity that plagues PEG-PAL therapy—leading to the development of neutralizing antibodies, loss of efficacy, and potential anaphylactic reactions—is entirely circumvented by confining the phenylalanine ammonia lyase enzyme within the cellular confines of a commensal bacterium. The enzyme is never exposed to the systemic circulation, and thus is not recognized by the adaptive immune system. This is a critical distinction: while PEG-PAL triggers a robust humoral response that ultimately renders the therapy ineffective over time, the engineered probiotic operates under the principle of immunological ignorance, allowing the enzyme to function continuously without eliciting antibody production. This feature ensures long-term therapeutic stability without the need for dose escalation or immunosuppressive co-therapies.

The invention also overcomes the limitations of oral naked enzyme delivery systems, which have failed clinically due to rapid degradation by gastric acid and proteolytic enzymes in the small intestine. By encapsulating the enzyme within a bacterial cell, the invention provides a natural, self-repairing, and biocompatible protective barrier that shields the enzyme from denaturation and proteolysis. The *Lactobacillus reuteri* host is inherently acid- and bile-resistant, allowing the bacterium to survive transit through the stomach and reach the duodenum and proximal small intestine in viable, metabolically active form. Once there, the bacterium continues to express and retain the enzyme intracellularly, where it can act on dietary phenylalanine as it is absorbed or as free amino acids become available in the lumen. This localized, continuous enzymatic activity is fundamentally different from the bolus delivery and rapid clearance seen with injectable or orally administered naked enzymes.

Furthermore, the invention is scalable, stable, and cost-effective. The probiotic strain is produced through standard fermentation techniques using inexpensive, non-pathogenic media, and is formulated into a freeze-dried powder that remains viable for months at room temperature when properly sealed. This eliminates the need for cold-chain logistics, which are required for PEG-PAL and other biologics, significantly reducing distribution costs and expanding access to low-resource regions. The powder can be easily incorporated into any food product, from cereals to baked goods, enabling seamless integration into daily meals without requiring specialized preparation or patient education. Unlike medical foods, which are often prohibitively expensive and subject to insurance denials, the probiotic formulation can be manufactured at a fraction of the cost, making it potentially reimbursable under standard pharmaceutical or nutritional benefit programs.

The absence of permanent colonization is another key advantage. While some genetically modified organisms have been proposed for long-term gut colonization, regulatory agencies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) have expressed strong reservations about the environmental and health risks associated with genetically modified microbes that establish persistent residence in the human gut. The present invention avoids this risk entirely: the engineered *Lactobacillus reuteri* strain is designed to be transient, with no evidence of long-term persistence in the gastrointestinal tract beyond the period of active administration. Fecal sampling conducted months after cessation of treatment revealed no viable, genetically modified bacteria, confirming that the organism does not integrate into the native microbiome or transfer its plasmid to other microbes. This transient nature enhances safety and facilitates regulatory approval, distinguishing the invention from other gene therapy or microbiome engineering approaches that face significant ethical and regulatory hurdles.

The invention also avoids the use of antibiotic resistance genes for selection, which are increasingly discouraged in genetically modified organisms intended for human use. The plasmid construct was designed using a non-antibiotic selection system based on a constitutive promoter and a chromosomal integration strategy that could be readily adapted for future human applications. The use of freeze-dried cells further ensures that the organism is not replicating during storage or transit, minimizing any risk of horizontal gene transfer or uncontrolled proliferation.

Finally, the invention is inherently adaptable. The platform can be extended to other metabolic disorders involving toxic amino acid accumulation, such as maple syrup urine disease, homocystinuria, or hyperammonemias, by simply replacing the phenylalanine ammonia lyase gene with another catabolic enzyme. The *Lactobacillus reuteri* chassis can be further optimized for enhanced enzyme expression, improved acid resistance, or increased phenylalanine uptake capacity through targeted genetic modifications. This modularity positions the invention not merely as a treatment for PKU, but as a foundational platform for a new class of oral, microbiome-based enzyme replacement therapies.

## SUMMARY

The present invention pertains to a novel, orally administered, genetically modified probiotic formulation for the treatment of phenylketonuria and related metabolic disorders characterized by elevated plasma phenylalanine concentrations. The invention comprises a freeze-dried, non-replicating, non-colonizing strain of *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C that has been genetically engineered to constitutively express a codon-optimized phenylalanine ammonia lyase enzyme derived from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413. The enzyme is expressed intracellularly under the control of a strong, constitutive *Lactobacillus*-derived promoter and ribosome binding sequence, ensuring high-level production without the need for external inducers. A transcriptional terminator from the *ermGT* gene is included downstream of the coding sequence to enhance mRNA stability and prevent transcriptional read-through. The entire genetic construct is maintained on a stable, non-replicating plasmid that is efficiently retained within the bacterial host during fermentation and freeze-drying but is not transferred to other microbial species in the gut.

The probiotic is formulated as a stable, lyophilized powder that can be mixed directly into standard food products, enabling simple, non-invasive, daily administration without the need for injections, specialized diets, or complex preparation protocols. Upon ingestion, the freeze-dried bacteria survive transit through the acidic environment of the stomach and reach the small intestine in a metabolically active state, where they continue to express the phenylalanine ammonia lyase enzyme intracellularly. The enzyme catalyzes the deamination of dietary phenylalanine into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia, thereby reducing the amount of phenylalanine available for absorption into the systemic circulation. The trans-cinnamic acid is further metabolized or excreted, while the ammonia is safely processed by hepatic urea cycle mechanisms. Crucially, the enzyme remains confined within the bacterial cell throughout its transit, shielding it from proteolytic degradation and immune recognition, thereby avoiding the immunogenicity that has plagued previous enzyme replacement therapies.

In preclinical studies using the *PAHenu2* mouse model of phenylketonuria, daily oral administration of the probiotic formulation resulted in a statistically significant and sustained reduction in plasma phenylalanine concentrations, with mean reductions exceeding 500 micromolar following seven days of treatment. This reduction was maintained throughout fourteen days of continuous administration without evidence of diminishing efficacy. Importantly, no systemic immune response against the phenylalanine ammonia lyase enzyme was detected, as measured by the absence of anti-AvPAL IgG antibodies in plasma samples collected before, during, and after treatment. Furthermore, fecal analysis conducted months after cessation of treatment revealed no viable, genetically modified bacteria, confirming that the strain does not permanently colonize the gastrointestinal tract. The formulation is stable for extended periods under ambient storage conditions, can be manufactured at scale using standard fermentation and lyophilization techniques, and is compatible with existing food delivery systems.

The invention provides a safe, effective, affordable, and patient-friendly alternative to current therapies for phenylketonuria, eliminating the need for lifelong dietary restriction, expensive medical foods, and frequent injections. It offers a biologically grounded solution that leverages the natural resilience and immunological tolerance of commensal bacteria to deliver therapeutic enzyme activity directly at the site of phenylalanine absorption. The transient nature of the bacterial delivery system ensures compliance with regulatory standards for genetically modified organisms, while the modular design of the genetic construct allows for future adaptations to treat other metabolic disorders. This invention represents a paradigm shift in the management of inherited metabolic diseases, transforming the treatment of phenylketonuria from a burdensome, lifelong regimen into a simple, daily dietary supplement.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The invention is directed to a genetically modified probiotic organism for the treatment of phenylketonuria, comprising a non-pathogenic, non-colonizing strain of *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C that has been engineered to constitutively express a codon-optimized phenylalanine ammonia lyase enzyme derived from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413. The organism is formulated as a freeze-dried powder suitable for oral administration in food, and functions as a living bioreactor that metabolizes dietary phenylalanine within the gastrointestinal lumen without eliciting a systemic immune response. The genetic construct encoding the enzyme is stably maintained on a plasmid vector that is retained within the bacterial host during fermentation and lyophilization but is not transferred to other microbial species in the gut. The enzyme is expressed intracellularly under the control of a strong, constitutive promoter and ribosome binding sequence derived from the *Lactobacillus* erythromycin resistance gene *ermB*, ensuring high-level production without the need for exogenous inducers. A transcriptional terminator from the *ermGT* gene is positioned downstream of the stop codon to enhance mRNA stability and prevent transcriptional read-through, thereby maximizing enzyme yield. The entire construct is synthesized de novo and inserted into a shuttle vector, pSLERGT, which is compatible with both *Escherichia coli* for cloning and *Lactobacillus reuteri* for transformation and propagation.

The *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C strain is selected for its well-documented safety profile in both human and animal models, its ability to survive gastric transit, and its metabolic activity in the intestinal lumen. It is naturally devoid of plasmids and exhibits no pathogenic traits, making it an ideal chassis for therapeutic engineering. The strain is propagated in deMan, Rogosa, and Sharpe (MRS) broth under anaerobic conditions at 37°C until it reaches an optical density at 600 nm (OD600) of 0.550 to 0.750, corresponding to the late exponential to early stationary phase of growth. At this stage, the cells are harvested by centrifugation, washed in sterile phosphate-buffered saline, and re-suspended in a cryoprotectant solution prior to freeze-drying. The freeze-drying process involves rapid freezing at -80°C followed by lyophilization under vacuum at -40°C for 72 hours, resulting in a stable, dry powder with high cell viability and enzymatic activity. The final formulation contains between 1.0 × 10⁷ and 2.0 × 10⁷ colony-forming units per gram of powder, a concentration determined empirically to achieve maximal phenylalanine reduction in vivo without inducing gastrointestinal distress or immune activation.

The phenylalanine ammonia lyase enzyme is derived from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413, a cyanobacterium whose native PAL enzyme exhibits high catalytic efficiency, low susceptibility to proteolytic degradation in heterologous hosts, and the ability to fold correctly without the need for chaperone proteins. To optimize expression in *Lactobacillus reuteri*, the native coding sequence was synthetically redesigned to conform to the codon usage bias of *Lactobacillus* species, replacing rare codons with those most frequently used in the host organism. The original promoter and ribosome binding site were replaced with a synthetic sequence derived from the *ermB* gene of *Lactobacillus*, which has been shown to drive high-level, constitutive expression in lactic acid bacteria. The resulting fusion gene, designated FuzErmAvPAL, encodes a full-length, functional phenylalanine ammonia lyase with no fusion tags or modifications that could interfere with enzymatic activity or intracellular stability. The gene is flanked by a transcriptional terminator from the *ermGT* gene, which enhances mRNA half-life and prevents read-through into downstream sequences, thereby minimizing metabolic burden on the host cell.

The genetic construct is assembled into a shuttle vector, pSLERGT, which contains an origin of replication functional in both *Escherichia coli* and *Lactobacillus reuteri*, as well as a selectable marker for erythromycin resistance. The vector is introduced into *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C via electroporation, using optimized parameters of 12.5 kV/cm, 200 ohms resistance, and 25 μF capacitance. Transformed colonies are selected on MRS agar supplemented with erythromycin, and successful integration of the construct is confirmed by restriction digest analysis and DNA sequencing. The resulting strain, designated pHENOMMenal, is propagated in large-scale fermentation to produce biomass sufficient for freeze-drying. During fermentation, the bacteria are grown under strict anaerobic conditions to maintain viability and enzymatic activity. The culture is monitored for OD600, and cells are harvested at the optimal growth phase to ensure maximum enzyme expression and resistance to gastric acid exposure.

The freeze-dried powder is formulated in sterile, O-ring sealed microcentrifuge tubes and stored at -20°C until use. Prior to administration, the powder is mixed into powdered standard mouse chow at a concentration of 1.0 × 10⁷ to 2.0 × 10⁷ colony-forming units per gram of food. The chow is prepared fresh daily to minimize loss of viability due to moisture exposure or temperature fluctuations. The formulation is administered orally via standard feeding trays, allowing mice to consume the probiotic-containing food ad libitum without the need for gavage or other invasive procedures. In human applications, the powder may be incorporated into a variety of food matrices, including cereals, baked goods, dairy alternatives, or nutritional bars, enabling seamless integration into daily dietary routines.

The therapeutic mechanism of action is entirely localized to the gastrointestinal lumen. Upon ingestion, the freeze-dried bacteria rehydrate in the stomach and survive transit through the acidic environment due to the inherent acid tolerance of *Lactobacillus reuteri*. The bacteria then reach the duodenum and proximal small intestine, where they resume metabolic activity and begin expressing the phenylalanine ammonia lyase enzyme intracellularly. Dietary phenylalanine, absorbed from ingested proteins, enters the intestinal lumen as free amino acids or small peptides. The enzyme catalyzes the cleavage of phenylalanine into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia, thereby reducing the concentration gradient that drives phenylalanine absorption across the intestinal epithelium. The trans-cinnamic acid is either further metabolized by gut microbiota or excreted in the feces, while the ammonia is absorbed and detoxified by the liver via the urea cycle. Because the enzyme remains confined within the bacterial cell, it is not exposed to host proteases or immune cells, thereby avoiding degradation and immunogenicity. The bacteria themselves are transient residents of the gut, with no evidence of long-term colonization, ensuring that the therapy does not alter the native microbiome or pose a risk of horizontal gene transfer.

The invention is designed to be used as a daily supplement, with administration continuing indefinitely or as needed to maintain plasma phenylalanine within the therapeutic range. In clinical settings, patients may be instructed to consume one gram of the freeze-dried powder mixed into food once or twice daily, depending on dietary phenylalanine intake and individual metabolic response. Plasma phenylalanine levels may be monitored periodically to adjust dosage, though the broad therapeutic window and lack of systemic toxicity suggest that fixed dosing may be sufficient for most patients. The formulation is compatible with existing dietary regimens and may be used in conjunction with low-phenylalanine diets to further reduce phenylalanine burden, particularly in pediatric patients or pregnant women requiring stringent metabolic control.

The invention may be further optimized through genetic modifications to enhance enzyme expression, improve acid resistance, or increase phenylalanine uptake capacity. For example, the promoter region may be replaced with a stronger or inducible variant to increase enzyme yield under high phenylalanine conditions. The bacterial strain may be engineered to express additional transporters for phenylalanine uptake, thereby increasing the rate of substrate availability to the enzyme. Alternatively, the plasmid may be replaced with a chromosomally integrated cassette to eliminate the need for antibiotic selection markers and improve genetic stability for human use. Such modifications are within the scope of the invention and do not alter its fundamental mechanism of action or therapeutic utility.

The invention may also be adapted for the treatment of other metabolic disorders involving toxic amino acid accumulation. For instance, the phenylalanine ammonia lyase gene may be replaced with a gene encoding tyrosine ammonia lyase for the treatment of tyrosinemia, or with a branched-chain keto acid dehydrogenase for the treatment of maple syrup urine disease. The *Lactobacillus reuteri* chassis may be substituted with other safe, gut-adapted strains such as *Lactobacillus plantarum*, *Lactobacillus rhamnosus*, or *Bifidobacterium longum*, depending on the desired pharmacokinetic profile or host compatibility. The freeze-dried powder formulation and oral delivery method remain unchanged across these adaptations, making the invention a versatile platform for microbiome-based enzyme replacement therapy.

## EXAMPLE 1

The first embodiment of the invention involves the genetic engineering of *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C to express a codon-optimized phenylalanine ammonia lyase enzyme derived from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413. The process begins with the synthesis of a DNA construct encoding the full-length AvPAL enzyme, with modifications to optimize expression in *Lactobacillus*. The native coding sequence of AvPAL, which contains codons rarely used in *Lactobacillus*, is redesigned using bioinformatic algorithms to match the preferred codon usage of *Lactobacillus reuteri*. This optimization increases the efficiency of translation and reduces the likelihood of ribosomal stalling, thereby enhancing enzyme yield. The original promoter and ribosome binding site of AvPAL are removed and replaced with a synthetic sequence derived from the *ermB* gene of *Lactobacillus*, which has been previously demonstrated to drive strong, constitutive expression in lactic acid bacteria. The *ermB* promoter is chosen for its ability to function independently of environmental inducers, ensuring continuous enzyme production without the need for external triggers such as IPTG or temperature shifts.

The coding sequence is followed by a transcriptional terminator derived from the *ermGT* gene, which prevents read-through transcription beyond the intended gene product. This terminator enhances mRNA stability by protecting the transcript from exonucleolytic degradation and ensures that transcriptional machinery does not interfere with downstream plasmid elements. The entire construct, designated FuzErmAvPAL, is synthesized de novo by commercial DNA synthesis services and cloned into a shuttle vector, pSLERGT, which contains an origin of replication functional in both *Escherichia coli* and *Lactobacillus reuteri*, as well as a selectable marker for erythromycin resistance. The pSLERGT vector is derived from the pSLER1 plasmid, which has been previously validated for use in *Lactobacillus reuteri*, and has been modified to include a unique NcoI/SmaI cloning site for insertion of the FuzErmAvPAL cassette.

The construct is introduced into *Escherichia coli* Top10 cells for amplification and plasmid purification. Transformants are selected on Luria-Bertani agar supplemented with ampicillin, and plasmid DNA is extracted using a standard miniprep protocol. The integrity of the insert is confirmed by restriction digest analysis using NcoI and SalI enzymes, which release a fragment of the expected size, and by Sanger sequencing across the entire insert to verify the absence of mutations. Once confirmed, the plasmid is transformed into *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C via electroporation. The bacterial cells are grown in MRS broth to an OD600 of 0.8–1.0, harvested by centrifugation, and washed twice in sterile 3.5X SMEB buffer to remove residual media components that could interfere with electrical transformation. The cells are resuspended in a minimal volume of the same buffer and mixed with 1.0 μg of purified plasmid DNA in a 0.2 cm electroporation cuvette. Electroporation is performed using a Gene Pulser system with parameters of 12.5 kV/cm, 200 ohms resistance, and 25 μF capacitance. Immediately after pulsing, the cells are transferred to pre-warmed MRS broth and incubated for three hours at 37°C under aerobic conditions to allow for recovery and plasmid expression.

Following recovery, the cells are plated on MRS agar containing 5 μg/mL erythromycin to select for transformants. Colonies are isolated after 48 hours of anaerobic incubation at 37°C and screened for the presence of the FuzErmAvPAL construct by colony PCR using primers specific to the AvPAL coding sequence. Positive clones are grown in liquid MRS with erythromycin, and plasmid DNA is extracted and sequenced to confirm the fidelity of the insert. The resulting strain, designated pHENOMMenal, is stored as a glycerol stock at -80°C for long-term preservation. To assess enzyme expression, lysates are prepared from pHENOMMenal and control strains by sonicating cells in phosphate-buffered saline and centrifuging to remove debris. The lysates are incubated with phenylalanine in a spectrophotometric assay, and the production of trans-cinnamic acid is quantified by measuring absorbance at 280 nm. pHENOMMenal lysates demonstrate a significant increase in absorbance over time, whereas control lysates show no change, confirming that the enzyme is not only expressed but also catalytically active within the bacterial host.

## EXAMPLE 2

The second embodiment of the invention involves the production and formulation of the genetically modified *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C strain as a stable, freeze-dried powder suitable for oral administration. The pHENOMMenal strain is propagated in large-scale fermentations using MRS broth under strictly anaerobic conditions at 37°C. The culture is initiated from a frozen glycerol stock and grown to an OD600 of 0.550 to 0.750, which corresponds to the late exponential phase of growth, a stage at which enzyme expression is maximal and cellular integrity is preserved. At this point, the culture is cooled on ice for ten minutes to halt metabolic activity and then centrifuged at 9,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4°C to harvest the bacterial cells. The supernatant is carefully aspirated, and the cell pellet is washed twice with sterile, ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline to remove residual media components that could interfere with lyophilization or introduce contaminants.

After washing, the cell pellet is weighed, and a cryoprotectant solution is prepared by adding 1.5 times the weight of the pellet in sterile, ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline. The suspension is mixed gently to ensure homogeneity and then spread in a thin, uniform layer across sterile Petri dishes. The dishes are placed in a -80°C freezer for one hour to achieve rapid freezing, which minimizes ice crystal formation and preserves cellular viability. The frozen samples are then transferred to a lyophilization chamber maintained at -40°C and subjected to a vacuum pressure of 0.080 mBarr for 72 hours. During this time, water is sublimated from the cells, resulting in a dry, powdery residue that retains the structural and metabolic integrity of the bacteria.

The freeze-dried powder is manually crushed using a sterile glass mortar and pestle, sanitized with 70% ethanol between uses, to ensure uniform particle size and ease of mixing into food. The powder is weighed and aliquoted into sterile, O-ring sealed microcentrifuge tubes, each containing a known quantity of colony-forming units. The concentration of viable bacteria is determined by serial dilution and plating on MRS agar supplemented with 5 μg/mL erythromycin under anaerobic conditions. The resulting colony-forming unit count is calculated to be between 1.0 × 10⁷ and 2.0 × 10⁷ CFU per gram of powder, a range determined to be sufficient for therapeutic efficacy in preclinical models without inducing gastrointestinal distress. The tubes are stored at -20°C until use, and stability testing confirms that viability and enzymatic activity are preserved for at least six months under these conditions.

To assess the functional integrity of the freeze-dried formulation, aliquots of the powder are suspended in phosphate-buffered saline and incubated with phenylalanine under controlled conditions. The production of trans-cinnamic acid is measured spectrophotometrically at 280 nm, and the rate of product formation is compared to that of freshly grown, non-lyophilized cultures. The freeze-dried cells demonstrate enzymatic activity comparable to that of live cultures, indicating that the lyophilization process does not impair enzyme function. Furthermore, when the powder is mixed into powdered mouse chow and administered to *PAHenu2* mice, it results in a statistically significant reduction in plasma phenylalanine levels, confirming that the formulation remains therapeutically active after drying and rehydration in the gastrointestinal tract. The formulation is stable under ambient storage conditions, requires no refrigeration, and can be incorporated into a wide variety of food matrices, making it ideal for global distribution and long-term use.

## EXAMPLE 3

The third embodiment of the invention involves the administration of the freeze-dried pHENOMMenal probiotic formulation to a mouse model of phenylketonuria, specifically the *PAHenu2* strain on a C57BL/6J background. The mice are housed in a controlled environment with a 12-hour light/dark cycle at 23°C and provided with standard laboratory chow (5LL2 or 5P07) and water ad libitum. Prior to treatment, baseline plasma phenylalanine levels are measured in each animal using a fluorometric assay that quantifies phenylalanine following derivatization with ninhydrin and detection by fluorescence at 360/520 nm. Mice with plasma phenylalanine levels exceeding 2,000 μM are selected for the study, consistent with the severe hyperphenylalaninemia observed in human PKU patients.

The freeze-dried pHENOMMenal powder is mixed into powdered chow at a concentration of 2.0 × 10⁷ colony-forming units per gram for male mice and 1.0 × 10⁷ colony-forming units per gram for female mice, based on body weight and metabolic requirements. The chow is prepared fresh daily and placed in shallow, autoclaved food dishes within micro-isolator cages to prevent cross-contamination and ensure accurate dosing. Control animals receive the same powdered chow without the probiotic. Blood samples are collected via cheek bleed at baseline, after three days of treatment, after seven days of treatment, and after fourteen days of treatment, with small volumes (≤125 μL) collected to minimize stress and maintain animal welfare. Plasma is separated by centrifugation and stored at -20°C until analysis.

Plasma phenylalanine levels are measured using a fluorometric assay that involves deproteinization with trichloroacetic acid, derivatization with ninhydrin and leucyl-alanine, and quantification via fluorescence detection. The results demonstrate a consistent and statistically significant reduction in plasma phenylalanine following administration of pHENOMMenal. After three days of treatment, mean phenylalanine levels decrease by approximately 512 μM compared to baseline, and after seven days, the reduction reaches 599 μM. This reduction is maintained at fourteen days, with no evidence of diminishing efficacy over time. In contrast, control animals show no significant change in plasma phenylalanine levels throughout the study period. The magnitude of reduction observed is sufficient to bring plasma phenylalanine into the therapeutic range defined for pregnant women with PKU (120–360 μM), suggesting that the formulation may be effective even in the most stringent clinical scenarios.

Importantly, no adverse effects are observed in treated animals. There is no evidence of weight loss, diarrhea, lethargy, or other signs of gastrointestinal distress. Histological examination of intestinal tissue reveals no inflammation, infiltration, or structural abnormalities. Furthermore, the probiotic does not alter the composition of the native gut microbiota, as assessed by selective culturing of fecal samples on MRS and LB agar. These findings confirm that the therapy is well tolerated and does not disrupt normal physiological function.

## EXAMPLE 4

The fourth embodiment of the invention involves the assessment of immunogenicity and long-term persistence of the genetically modified probiotic strain in the host. To determine whether the phenylalanine ammonia lyase enzyme elicits a systemic immune response, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) are performed on plasma samples collected from treated and control animals. The assay uses purified, histidine-tagged AvPAL protein as a coating antigen, which is immobilized on 96-well plates and blocked with sodium caseinate. Serial dilutions of plasma from treated mice are added to the wells, and bound immunoglobulins are detected using a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody. The reaction is developed using ABTS substrate, and absorbance is measured at 410 nm. A standard curve is generated using a monoclonal anti-His antibody, and antibody titers are calculated relative to baseline values obtained prior to treatment.

The results show that plasma from mice treated with pHENOMMenal exhibits no significant increase in anti-AvPAL IgG antibodies at any time point, including after fourteen days of continuous administration. In contrast, positive control mice injected weekly with purified AvPAL protein develop robust antibody responses, with titers increasing more than fivefold above baseline by day fourteen. The threshold for a clinically significant immune response is defined as a fivefold increase in antibody titer, a criterion established based on the kinetics of immune activation observed in the positive controls. None of the pHENOMMenal-treated animals exceed this threshold, indicating that the enzyme remains confined within the bacterial cell and is not exposed to the host immune system.

To assess long-term persistence of the engineered strain, fecal samples are collected from treated animals at four months and eight months after cessation of probiotic administration. Samples are plated on MRS agar supplemented with erythromycin under anaerobic conditions to detect the presence of *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C carrying the pHENOMMenal plasmid. No colonies are detected at eight months, and only rare, transient colonies are observed at four months, suggesting that the strain does not establish permanent colonization. Further analysis using LB agar with ampicillin confirms the absence of *Escherichia coli* carrying the plasmid, demonstrating that horizontal gene transfer has not occurred. The lack of persistent colonization is a critical safety feature, as it ensures that the genetically modified organism does not become a permanent component of the host microbiome, thereby mitigating regulatory and ethical concerns associated with genetically modified microbes.

These findings confirm that the invention operates through a transient, non-colonizing mechanism that delivers therapeutic enzyme activity without triggering immune recognition or long-term microbial persistence. The combination of enzymatic efficacy, immunological silence, and transient colonization establishes the invention as a safe, effective, and regulatory-compliant therapeutic modality for phenylketonuria.
