# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of lipid biochemistry and molecular biology, specifically to the biosynthesis, regulation, and physiological function of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids in eukaryotic organisms. More particularly, the invention encompasses novel genetic and enzymatic components involved in the de novo biosynthesis of C15ISO and C17ISO fatty acids, the identification of transcriptional regulators controlling their production, and the discovery of their indispensable role in larval development and metabolic homeostasis. The invention further includes methods for modulating the levels of these fatty acids through genetic, dietary, or pharmacological intervention, and their application in therapeutic, agricultural, and biotechnological contexts where regulation of growth, development, or metabolic balance is required. The invention is particularly relevant to understanding and treating human metabolic disorders associated with branched-chain fatty acid deficiency, enhancing the nutritional quality of food sources, and engineering organisms for the sustainable production of high-value lipid metabolites.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Fatty acids constitute a fundamental class of lipid molecules essential for energy storage, membrane architecture, and cellular signaling across all domains of life. While the biosynthesis of straight-chain saturated and unsaturated fatty acids has been extensively characterized in both prokaryotes and eukaryotes, the metabolic pathways governing the production of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids—particularly those with iso and anteiso configurations—have remained poorly understood in multicellular eukaryotes. Historically, these molecules were considered primarily bacterial in origin, with their presence in eukaryotic tissues regarded as incidental, derived from dietary intake or microbial symbiosis. However, accumulating evidence over the past several decades has demonstrated that monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids are not merely contaminants or dietary byproducts but are endogenously synthesized and functionally integrated into the physiology of diverse eukaryotic organisms, including fungi, plants, and mammals.

In mammals, monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids such as C21anteISO have been identified as major components of covalently bound lipids in hair fibers, where they confer critical hydrophobic properties necessary for structural integrity. Their absence has been correlated with pathological conditions such as maple syrup urine disease, a genetic disorder caused by mutations in the branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase complex, which results in drastically reduced levels of these fatty acids in skin and hair tissues. Similarly, altered levels of C15ISO and C17ISO have been observed in cancer cells, brain tissue, and sebaceous glands, suggesting a potential role in cellular differentiation, membrane dynamics, or signaling. Despite these associations, the enzymatic machinery responsible for their biosynthesis in eukaryotes remained unidentified, and their physiological necessity had never been experimentally demonstrated in a genetically tractable model organism.

In bacteria, the biosynthesis of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids is well characterized. It proceeds via the utilization of branched-chain amino acid-derived α-keto acids as primers, which are activated to their coenzyme A thioesters and subsequently elongated by a fatty acid synthase system distinct from that used for straight-chain fatty acids. This pathway requires the coordinated action of branched-chain aminotransferase and branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase to generate the initiating substrates, followed by iterative condensation with malonyl-CoA units. However, no homologous pathway had been identified in any eukaryote prior to this invention. While elongation of fatty acids in eukaryotes is mediated by a family of membrane-bound enzymes known as fatty acid elongases, these enzymes had previously been associated exclusively with the elongation of straight-chain fatty acids, particularly in the context of very-long-chain fatty acid synthesis for sphingolipid production or membrane remodeling.

The absence of a genetically validated system for studying monomethyl branched-chain fatty acid metabolism in eukaryotes has severely limited progress in understanding their biological significance. Conventional mammalian models are unsuitable for high-throughput genetic screening due to long generation times, complex physiology, and ethical constraints. Furthermore, the lack of a clear phenotypic readout for deficiency of these fatty acids in mammals has rendered it impossible to determine whether their presence is essential for viability or merely contributory to tissue-specific functions. Consequently, the fundamental question of whether monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids serve as indispensable metabolic regulators in eukaryotic development has remained unanswered.

Recent advances in the use of the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans as a model organism for lipid metabolism have provided an unprecedented opportunity to address this gap. C. elegans possesses a compact genome, a short life cycle, and a fully annotated set of conserved metabolic pathways, including those for fatty acid biosynthesis and elongation. Moreover, its transparent body and the availability of powerful genetic tools—including RNA interference, CRISPR-mediated mutagenesis, and fluorescent reporter constructs—make it uniquely suited for the functional dissection of lipid pathways. Prior studies had demonstrated that C. elegans synthesizes long-chain fatty acids via a conserved set of elongase enzymes, but no link had been established between these enzymes and branched-chain fatty acid production. The discovery that suppression of specific elongase genes leads to selective depletion of C15ISO and C17ISO, coupled with the observation that these deficiencies result in a complete and reversible arrest of larval development, represents a paradigm shift in the understanding of lipid biology in eukaryotes.

This invention arises from the identification and characterization of a previously unrecognized metabolic pathway in eukaryotes that is essential for development and governed by a novel regulatory network. The invention provides the first definitive evidence that monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids are not merely structural or incidental components of eukaryotic lipids but are, in fact, obligate signaling or metabolic molecules whose absence triggers a developmental checkpoint. It further identifies the specific enzymes responsible for their biosynthesis, the transcription factor that regulates their expression, and the compensatory mechanisms that respond to their depletion. These discoveries form the foundation for a new class of interventions targeting lipid homeostasis, with broad implications for human health, agriculture, and industrial biotechnology.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive and novel understanding of the biosynthesis, regulation, and physiological necessity of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids in eukaryotic organisms, particularly the C15ISO and C17ISO species. It is based on the discovery that these fatty acids are synthesized de novo in a genetically controlled pathway that is essential for larval development and metabolic homeostasis, and that their deficiency triggers a reversible developmental arrest that cannot be compensated by any other known fatty acid species. The invention discloses for the first time two novel fatty acid elongase enzymes, designated ELO-5 and ELO-6, which are specifically required for the production of C15ISO and C17ISO, respectively, and which represent the first eukaryotic enzymes identified to catalyze the elongation of branched-chain fatty acid primers. The invention further identifies a conserved transcriptional regulator, designated LPD-1, which is homologous to the mammalian sterol regulatory element-binding protein SREBP-1c and which directly controls the expression of ELO-5 and ELO-6, thereby integrating the biosynthesis of branched-chain fatty acids with conventional lipid metabolism.

The invention demonstrates that the levels of C17ISO serve as a metabolic signal that determines the progression of larval development in C. elegans, with a critical threshold required for exit from the L1 arrest state. This finding establishes a direct link between a specific lipid metabolite and a developmental checkpoint, revealing a previously unknown mechanism by which nutrient sensing is mediated through lipid composition rather than through classical nutrient sensors such as insulin or TOR pathways. The invention further reveals that the deficiency of C15ISO and C17ISO triggers a compensatory transcriptional response involving multiple genes, including acs-1, pnk-1, nhr-49, and C27H6.2, which encode enzymes and regulatory factors involved in fatty acid activation, coenzyme A metabolism, nuclear hormone signaling, and chromatin remodeling. These genes are upregulated in response to branched-chain fatty acid depletion, suggesting the existence of a feedback loop that monitors lipid homeostasis and adjusts metabolic flux accordingly.

The invention further provides methods for modulating the levels of C15ISO and C17ISO in eukaryotic cells and organisms through genetic manipulation, dietary supplementation, or pharmacological intervention. Specifically, the invention discloses that exogenous administration of C17ISO or C17anteISO completely rescues the developmental arrest caused by loss of ELO-5 function, whereas all other tested fatty acids—including saturated, unsaturated, polyunsaturated, and other branched-chain variants—are ineffective. This specificity confirms that C17ISO is not merely a structural component but a non-redundant signaling molecule. The invention further demonstrates that the expression of ELO-5 and ELO-6 is coordinately regulated by LPD-1, and that the transcriptional activity of LPD-1 is itself modulated by the levels of branched-chain fatty acids, establishing a reciprocal regulatory circuit that maintains lipid balance.

The invention also provides compositions and methods for the production of C15ISO and C17ISO in recombinant systems, including engineered bacteria, yeast, and plant cells, using the identified elongase and regulatory genes. These systems enable the scalable and sustainable production of these high-value fatty acids for use in nutritional supplements, pharmaceutical formulations, and cosmetic applications. The invention further provides diagnostic tools for detecting deficiencies in branched-chain fatty acid metabolism in humans and other mammals, based on the homology between the C. elegans pathway and human orthologs of ELO-5, ELO-6, and LPD-1. The invention further provides therapeutic strategies for treating metabolic disorders associated with impaired branched-chain fatty acid synthesis, including maple syrup urine disease, certain forms of neurodevelopmental delay, and skin barrier dysfunction, by supplementing with C17ISO or its derivatives or by pharmacologically enhancing the expression or activity of the corresponding human elongases and transcription factors.

In summary, the invention provides a foundational understanding of a previously unrecognized metabolic pathway essential for eukaryotic development, identifies novel enzymes and regulators governing this pathway, establishes a causal link between a specific lipid metabolite and a developmental checkpoint, and offers a suite of applications for medicine, agriculture, and biotechnology based on this discovery. The invention fundamentally transforms the perception of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids from passive lipid components to active regulators of cellular and organismal physiology.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is directed to the identification and functional characterization of a novel biosynthetic pathway for monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids in eukaryotic organisms, with a focus on the C15ISO and C17ISO species. This pathway is distinct from conventional fatty acid biosynthesis in that it utilizes branched-chain primers derived from leucine, isoleucine, and valine metabolism, and employs a specialized set of elongation enzymes that act upon these primers to generate fatty acids with methyl branches at the penultimate carbon. The pathway is conserved across eukaryotes, as evidenced by the high sequence homology between the identified C. elegans genes and their orthologs in humans, mice, and other mammals. The invention provides the first definitive evidence that these fatty acids are not merely structural elements but are essential signaling molecules whose absence triggers a developmental arrest that is both penetrant and reversible.

The biosynthetic pathway begins with the transamination and oxidative decarboxylation of branched-chain amino acids to generate the corresponding α-keto acids, which are then activated to their coenzyme A thioesters by an as-yet-unidentified acyl-CoA synthetase. These activated primers—specifically, 2-methylbutyryl-CoA and 2-methylvaleryl-CoA—serve as the starting units for fatty acid elongation. Unlike bacterial systems, which employ a type I fatty acid synthase complex to iteratively add two-carbon units from malonyl-CoA, eukaryotic elongation proceeds through a series of discrete membrane-bound enzyme complexes composed of four core activities: condensation, reduction, dehydration, and a second reduction. In this invention, two novel elongase enzymes, designated ELO-5 and ELO-6, are identified as the key catalysts responsible for the elongation of these branched-chain primers. ELO-5 is shown to catalyze the elongation of 2-methylbutyryl-CoA to produce C15ISO, and may also contribute to the elongation of 2-methylvaleryl-CoA to produce C17ISO. ELO-6, in contrast, is specifically required for the elongation of C15ISO to C17ISO, functioning as a dedicated elongase for the second elongation step in the pathway. This functional specialization is unprecedented among fatty acid elongases, which typically exhibit broad substrate promiscuity and act on multiple chain lengths.

The expression of ELO-5 and ELO-6 is tightly regulated by a conserved transcription factor designated LPD-1, which is the C. elegans ortholog of the mammalian sterol regulatory element-binding protein SREBP-1c. LPD-1 is localized to the endoplasmic reticulum under normal conditions and undergoes proteolytic cleavage in response to lipid depletion, releasing its N-terminal bHLH domain, which translocates to the nucleus and binds to sterol regulatory elements in the promoters of target genes. The invention demonstrates that the promoters of both elo-5 and elo-6 contain functional sterol regulatory elements that are directly bound by LPD-1, and that RNA interference-mediated knockdown of lpd-1 results in a dramatic reduction in the transcription of both elongase genes, leading to a near-complete depletion of C15ISO and C17ISO. Importantly, this depletion is not rescued by supplementation with other fatty acids, confirming that LPD-1’s role in branched-chain fatty acid biosynthesis is independent of its previously known function in regulating acetyl-CoA carboxylase and fatty acid synthase.

The physiological consequence of C15ISO and C17ISO deficiency is a complete and uniform arrest of development at the first larval stage (L1). This arrest is not due to general metabolic failure, as the animals remain viable for several days, retain normal morphology, and exhibit intact pharyngeal pumping and neural activity. Rather, the arrest is a programmed response triggered by the absence of C17ISO, which acts as a metabolic signal that must reach a critical threshold to permit progression beyond L1. This conclusion is supported by the observation that exogenous supplementation of C17ISO or C17anteISO fully rescues the developmental arrest, whereas all other tested fatty acids—including C15ISO, C18:0, C18:1, C18:2, and even other branched-chain variants such as C13ISO and C19ISO—are incapable of restoring normal development. Furthermore, the rescue is specific to the 17-carbon branched-chain fatty acids, as even the structurally similar C17:0 straight-chain fatty acid fails to substitute. This specificity indicates that the function of C17ISO is not merely structural but likely involves direct interaction with a receptor, enzyme, or signaling complex that recognizes the branched methyl group at the penultimate carbon.

The invention further reveals that the levels of C17ISO are dynamically regulated during development, with concentrations rising progressively from the egg stage through larval development to peak in gravid adults. This accumulation is likely mediated by maternal deposition into oocytes, as neither elo-5 nor elo-6 is significantly expressed in embryos or L1 larvae, suggesting that the embryo relies entirely on maternally derived C17ISO to initiate development. When maternal supply is depleted due to RNAi-mediated knockdown of elo-5 in the parent, the progeny are born with insufficient C17ISO and arrest at L1. This arrest is reversible: when rescued larvae are transferred to plates supplemented with C17ISO, they resume development and progress to adulthood with normal fertility. This reversibility confirms that the arrest is not due to irreversible damage but to a transient metabolic block that can be overcome by restoration of the missing signal.

The invention further identifies a network of compensatory genes that are transcriptionally upregulated in response to C15ISO and C17ISO deficiency. These include acs-1, which encodes a very-long-chain acyl-CoA synthetase; pnk-1, which encodes pantothenate kinase; nhr-49, a nuclear hormone receptor homologous to mammalian HNF4; and C27H6.2, a RuvB-like DNA-binding protein implicated in chromatin remodeling. The upregulation of these genes suggests the existence of a sophisticated feedback mechanism that attempts to restore lipid homeostasis by increasing the activation of alternative fatty acid substrates, enhancing coenzyme A availability, modulating transcriptional programs, and potentially altering chromatin accessibility to promote the expression of compensatory pathways. Notably, the upregulation of acs-1 and pnk-1 is dependent on the presence of LPD-1, as their induction is abolished in lpd-1(RNAi) backgrounds, indicating that LPD-1 serves as a central integrator of both biosynthetic and compensatory responses.

The invention further demonstrates that the regulation of this pathway is not unidirectional. Chronic deficiency of C15ISO and C17ISO leads to an upregulation of lpd-1 transcription in a subset of neurons, particularly the amphid sensory neurons, suggesting that lipid levels are monitored not only in the intestine—the primary site of lipid synthesis—but also in the nervous system, possibly as a mechanism to coordinate feeding behavior or metabolic state with developmental progression. This reciprocal regulation establishes a homeostatic loop in which LPD-1 drives the synthesis of C15ISO and C17ISO, while their absence feeds back to increase LPD-1 expression, thereby amplifying the biosynthetic response to deficiency. This loop may serve as a metabolic rheostat that ensures sufficient production of these essential fatty acids under conditions of dietary scarcity or increased demand.

The invention further provides methods for the production of C15ISO and C17ISO in heterologous systems. By expressing the C. elegans elo-5, elo-6, and lpd-1 genes in bacterial strains such as Escherichia coli or yeast such as Saccharomyces cerevisiae, it is possible to engineer strains that produce high yields of these fatty acids. The invention further provides vectors and expression cassettes containing the coding sequences of these genes under the control of inducible or constitutive promoters, along with co-expression of the branched-chain amino acid catabolic enzymes required to generate the primers. These engineered strains can be cultivated in fermenters using defined media containing branched-chain amino acids, and the resulting fatty acids can be extracted and purified for use in nutritional, pharmaceutical, or cosmetic applications. The invention further provides methods for the genetic modification of plants such as Arabidopsis thaliana or oilseed crops to express these elongases, thereby enabling the production of C15ISO and C17ISO in seed oils, which can be used as functional food ingredients or as precursors for the synthesis of bioactive lipids.

The invention further provides diagnostic methods for detecting deficiencies in branched-chain fatty acid metabolism in humans. Given the high sequence conservation between C. elegans ELO-5 and human ELOVL5, ELO-6 and human ELOVL7, and LPD-1 and human SREBF1, the invention enables the screening of patients with unexplained developmental delays, skin disorders, or neurological impairments for mutations in these orthologous genes. The invention further provides assays for measuring the levels of C15ISO and C17ISO in blood, skin, or hair samples using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry, and correlates low levels with clinical phenotypes. The invention further provides therapeutic compositions comprising purified C17ISO or its derivatives, such as esters or phospholipid conjugates, for oral or topical administration to patients with maple syrup urine disease, ichthyosis, or other conditions associated with branched-chain fatty acid deficiency.

The invention further provides methods for modulating the activity of the ELO-5 and ELO-6 enzymes using small molecule inhibitors or activators. High-throughput screening of chemical libraries has identified compounds that selectively inhibit ELO-5 activity without affecting other elongases, and these compounds can be used as research tools to probe the physiological roles of C15ISO and C17ISO in mammalian systems. Conversely, compounds that enhance the expression or stability of LPD-1 or its binding to the elo-5 and elo-6 promoters may be developed as therapeutics for conditions characterized by low levels of branched-chain fatty acids. The invention further provides gene therapy vectors encoding functional copies of ELO-5, ELO-6, or LPD-1 for delivery to tissues with impaired biosynthesis, such as the liver or skin.

In sum, the invention provides a complete and integrated understanding of a previously unknown lipid biosynthetic pathway that is essential for eukaryotic development, identifies its core enzymatic and regulatory components, establishes its physiological necessity, and provides a wide array of applications for medicine, agriculture, and biotechnology. The discovery that a single lipid molecule can act as a developmental checkpoint fundamentally alters our understanding of how organisms sense and respond to metabolic status, and opens new avenues for the treatment of metabolic and developmental disorders.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 2

The invention was further validated through the generation and characterization of a null mutant allele of the elo-5 gene, designated elo-5(gk208), which contains a 245-base pair deletion that removes the entire first exon of the gene, including the start codon and the first transmembrane domain predicted to anchor the protein in the endoplasmic reticulum. Homozygous elo-5(gk208) animals were viable when maintained on standard laboratory conditions, but exhibited a complete developmental arrest at the L1 stage when transferred to fresh plates lacking exogenous sources of branched-chain fatty acids. This arrest was indistinguishable from that observed in elo-5(RNAi) animals, confirming that the RNAi phenotype was due to specific loss of ELO-5 function and not off-target effects. The homozygous mutants were rescued to full fertility and normal development when supplemented with C17ISO or C17anteISO, but not with any other fatty acid tested, including C15ISO, C18:1, C18:2, or C17:0. The rescue was dose-dependent, with optimal recovery observed at concentrations of 10–50 μM, and was reversible upon removal of the supplement, indicating that the fatty acid must be continuously present to sustain development. These results confirm that ELO-5 is essential for the biosynthesis of C15ISO and C17ISO and that the developmental arrest is solely attributable to their absence.

#### Example 3

To determine whether the elongation of C15ISO to C17ISO occurs via ELO-6 in the absence of ELO-5, a series of feeding experiments were conducted using a bacterial strain, Stenotrophomonas maltophilia, which naturally produces high levels of C15ISO and C15anteISO but lacks the capacity to synthesize C17ISO. When elo-5(RNAi) animals were fed this bacterial strain, they accumulated bacterial C15ISO and C15anteISO in their tissues, yet still produced significant quantities of C17ISO and C17anteISO, as confirmed by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry. This demonstrated that the elongation of C15ISO to C17ISO was intact in the absence of ELO-5, indicating that ELO-6 retains full catalytic activity and can utilize exogenous C15ISO-CoA as a substrate. However, when free C15ISO was added directly to the growth medium, no elongation to C17ISO was observed, suggesting that the free fatty acid must first be activated to its CoA ester form before being recognized by ELO-6. This implies that the activation step is rate-limiting and that ELO-6 has a strict substrate preference for acyl-CoA esters over free fatty acids. The finding that bacterial C15ISO can be elongated while exogenous C15ISO cannot further suggests that the bacterial fatty acids are incorporated into lipid pools that are accessible to the elongase machinery, whereas the exogenous fatty acids are either sequestered, degraded, or fail to be efficiently activated.

#### Example 4

To investigate whether the developmental arrest caused by C17ISO deficiency is linked to altered membrane properties, the saturation index of fatty acids was calculated for wild-type and elo-5(RNAi) animals. The saturation index, defined as the ratio of saturated fatty acids to the sum of monounsaturated and branched-chain fatty acids, was found to be statistically indistinguishable between the two groups, indicating that the overall membrane fluidity was not significantly perturbed. This argues against the hypothesis that the arrest is due to a general disruption of membrane integrity or function. Furthermore, no changes in lipid droplet morphology, mitochondrial structure, or endoplasmic reticulum morphology were observed by electron microscopy in arrested larvae, suggesting that the arrest is not due to gross cellular organelle dysfunction. Instead, the arrest appears to be a highly specific signaling event triggered by the absence of C17ISO, likely mediated through a receptor or transcriptional regulator that directly senses the concentration of this fatty acid or a derivative thereof. The specificity of the rescue by C17ISO and C17anteISO, and the failure of other structurally similar molecules to substitute, further supports the notion that the molecule acts as a ligand for a highly selective binding site.

#### Example 5

To determine whether the transcriptional upregulation of compensatory genes such as acs-1 and pnk-1 is a direct consequence of LPD-1 activity, reporter constructs were generated in which the promoters of these genes were fused to green fluorescent protein. When these constructs were introduced into worms and subjected to lpd-1(RNAi), the fluorescence intensity of both acs-1Prom::GFP and pnk-1Prom::GFP was significantly reduced, indicating that LPD-1 is required for their basal expression. However, when the same constructs were introduced into elo-5(RNAi) animals, the fluorescence intensity was markedly increased, demonstrating that the upregulation of these genes in response to C17ISO deficiency is mediated through LPD-1. This establishes a feedforward loop in which LPD-1 activates the expression of elongases to produce C17ISO, and when C17ISO is low, LPD-1 activity is enhanced to drive the expression of compensatory genes that support lipid metabolism. This dual role of LPD-1—as both a biosynthetic activator and a compensatory regulator—positions it as a master controller of lipid homeostasis in the worm.

#### Example 6

To test whether the developmental arrest caused by C17ISO deficiency is analogous to other known developmental checkpoints, such as dauer formation or starvation-induced arrest, a series of genetic crosses were performed between elo-5(RNAi) animals and mutants in the insulin/IGF-1 signaling pathway, including daf-2 and daf-16. It was found that the L1 arrest in elo-5(RNAi) animals persisted even in the background of daf-2(mu150) loss-of-function mutants, which are constitutively dauer-constitutive, and that the arrest was not suppressed by daf-16 RNAi, which abolishes the dauer program. This indicates that the C17ISO-dependent arrest is mechanistically distinct from the insulin-mediated dauer pathway and represents a novel developmental checkpoint that operates independently of known nutrient-sensing circuits. Furthermore, the arrest was not rescued by supplementation with glucose, amino acids, or other nutrients, reinforcing the conclusion that the signal is specific to C17ISO and not a general indicator of metabolic sufficiency.

#### Example 7

To assess the conservation of this pathway in mammals, human orthologs of ELO-5, ELO-6, and LPD-1 were identified by sequence homology and cloned into expression vectors. Human ELOVL5 and ELOVL7 were expressed in HEK293 cells alongside the C. elegans branched-chain amino acid catabolic enzymes, and the cells were supplemented with leucine and isoleucine. Gas chromatography analysis revealed the de novo production of C15ISO and C17ISO in these cells, which was not observed in control cells lacking the elongase expression. Furthermore, knockdown of SREBF1 in these cells using siRNA led to a significant reduction in the levels of C15ISO and C17ISO, mirroring the phenotype observed in C. elegans. These results demonstrate that the entire pathway—from branched-chain amino acid catabolism to elongation and transcriptional regulation—is conserved in human cells, suggesting that C15ISO and C17ISO are not merely bacterial contaminants in human tissues but are endogenously synthesized and functionally important. This finding opens the door to the development of human therapies targeting these fatty acids.

#### Example 8

To determine whether C17ISO can be used as a therapeutic agent in a mammalian model of branched-chain fatty acid deficiency, a mouse model of maple syrup urine disease was generated by knocking out the BCKDHA gene, which encodes the E1α subunit of the branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase complex. These mice exhibited a severe reduction in C21anteISO levels in their hair and skin, along with neurological abnormalities and failure to thrive. Oral administration of C17ISO at a dose of 50 mg/kg/day for four weeks resulted in a significant increase in C21anteISO levels in the hair follicles and a partial rescue of neurological symptoms, including improved motor coordination and reduced tremor. Histological analysis of the skin revealed restoration of the lipid lamellar structure in the stratum corneum, which had been disrupted in untreated mice. These results demonstrate that C17ISO can be absorbed, transported, and metabolized in mammals to partially compensate for the deficiency of endogenous branched-chain fatty acids, and that it has therapeutic potential for treating skin and neurological disorders associated with impaired branched-chain fatty acid synthesis.

#### Example 9

To develop a scalable system for the industrial production of C15ISO and C17ISO, the genes elo-5, elo-6, and lpd-1 were cloned into a plasmid under the control of a strong, inducible promoter and transformed into Escherichia coli BL21(DE3). The strain was co-transformed with plasmids expressing the C. elegans branched-chain aminotransferase and branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase complex to generate the primers. The culture was grown in minimal medium supplemented with leucine and isoleucine, and induction with IPTG led to the accumulation of C15ISO and C17ISO to levels exceeding 15% of total fatty acids. The fatty acids were extracted using hexane and purified by silver-ion chromatography to isolate the branched-chain species. The yield was 2.3 grams per liter of culture, representing a commercially viable titer for use in nutritional supplements. The purified C17ISO was formulated into a lipid emulsion for oral delivery and tested for stability, bioavailability, and toxicity in rodent models, with no adverse effects observed at doses up to 500 mg/kg. This example demonstrates the feasibility of large-scale production of these fatty acids for commercial applications in medicine, cosmetics, and functional foods.
