# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology, specifically to predictive biomarkers for patient selection in antiangiogenic cancer therapy. More particularly, the invention provides a protein-based prognostic signature score, herein referred to as the VEGF Inhibition Response Predictor (ViRP) score, for identifying patients with HER2-negative breast cancer who are likely to achieve a favorable clinical response to treatment with a vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitor in combination with chemotherapy. The ViRP score is derived from the quantitative measurement of nine specific proteins or phosphoproteins in pre-treatment tumor tissue samples, and it enables the stratification of patients into those who are likely to achieve pathologic complete response or low residual disease burden following neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy with a VEGF-targeting agent and cytotoxic chemotherapy. This invention further encompasses the use of corresponding mRNA expression levels of the genes encoding these nine proteins as a surrogate for the protein-based ViRP score, thereby enabling non-invasive or less invasive molecular assessment using RNA extracted from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue or other clinically accessible biospecimens. The invention also includes methods for calculating the ViRP score using predefined mathematical coefficients derived from statistical modeling of protein expression data, and for applying the score to guide therapeutic decision-making in clinical practice. The utility of the ViRP score extends beyond breast cancer to other solid tumors where VEGF inhibition is a component of standard or investigational treatment regimens, including but not limited to ovarian, colorectal, lung, renal, and cervical cancers. The invention represents a significant advancement over prior art by providing a robust, reproducible, and biologically grounded molecular tool that directly correlates with tumor shrinkage and pathologic response, thereby enabling precision medicine approaches to antiangiogenic therapy that have previously been unattainable due to the lack of validated predictive biomarkers.

## BACKGROUND

The clinical management of solid tumors has long been hindered by the inability to reliably predict which patients will benefit from specific therapeutic interventions, particularly those targeting the tumor vasculature. Antiangiogenic therapy, which aims to inhibit the formation of new blood vessels that supply nutrients and oxygen to growing tumors, has been a focus of oncology research for over three decades. The discovery of vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) as a central mediator of tumor angiogenesis led to the development of bevacizumab, a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody that binds and neutralizes VEGF-A, thereby disrupting signaling through the VEGF receptor pathway. Clinical trials demonstrated that the addition of bevacizumab to standard chemotherapy regimens improved progression-free survival in multiple cancer types, including metastatic breast, colorectal, and non-small cell lung cancers. However, despite these promising results, the overall survival benefit in many populations remained marginal or statistically insignificant, and regulatory approvals for bevacizumab in breast cancer were subsequently withdrawn in several jurisdictions due to an unfavorable risk-benefit profile in unselected populations.

The fundamental challenge in the clinical application of bevacizumab and other VEGF inhibitors lies in the heterogeneity of tumor biology and the lack of validated biomarkers to identify the subset of patients most likely to respond. Early efforts to identify predictive biomarkers focused primarily on circulating levels of VEGF-A in plasma or serum, under the assumption that higher baseline levels would correlate with greater dependence on angiogenesis and thus greater sensitivity to VEGF blockade. However, large prospective clinical trials, including the MERiDiAN trial, failed to demonstrate that plasma VEGF-A levels could reliably predict which patients would derive clinical benefit from bevacizumab. Other molecular markers, such as germline mutations in BRCA1 or BRCA2, DNA methylation patterns, and expression of soluble carbonic anhydrase IX, have been investigated with similarly inconsistent results. These biomarkers, while biologically plausible, have not demonstrated sufficient sensitivity, specificity, or reproducibility across independent cohorts to be adopted into routine clinical practice.

Moreover, the majority of prior studies have relied on transcriptomic data derived from mRNA expression profiling, which, while informative, fails to capture critical post-translational modifications such as phosphorylation that directly regulate protein activity and signaling pathway dynamics. Protein expression levels, particularly those of signaling molecules and kinases, often do not correlate linearly with their corresponding mRNA levels due to translational regulation, protein degradation, and compartmentalization. Consequently, signatures developed from mRNA data frequently lack the biological precision required to predict response to targeted therapies that act on protein networks. Furthermore, existing prognostic signatures in breast cancer, such as PAM50, Oncotype DX, and MammaPrint, were designed to predict recurrence risk or chemotherapy benefit in the absence of targeted agents and are not calibrated to assess response to antiangiogenic therapy. There has been no prior attempt to develop a protein signature that directly models the molecular response to VEGF inhibition in combination with chemotherapy, nor has any study demonstrated that a composite protein score derived from pre-treatment tumor biopsies can significantly enrich for patients achieving pathologic complete response or low residual disease burden.

The clinical need for such a biomarker is acute. In breast cancer, approximately 70% of cases are estrogen receptor-positive and HER2-negative, and for these patients, neoadjuvant chemotherapy remains the standard of care prior to surgery in locally advanced disease. The addition of bevacizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy has shown modest improvements in pathologic response rates in some trials, but without a predictive biomarker, its use remains indiscriminate, exposing many patients to unnecessary toxicity, cost, and delay in effective therapy. The current standard of care offers no molecular guidance for selecting patients who would benefit from the addition of a VEGF inhibitor, resulting in suboptimal therapeutic decisions and inefficient resource allocation. The absence of a validated predictive signature has also impeded the development of next-generation antiangiogenic agents, as clinical trials cannot efficiently enroll patients most likely to respond. The present invention addresses these critical gaps by introducing a novel, empirically derived protein signature that quantifies the likelihood of response to VEGF inhibition in combination with chemotherapy, thereby enabling a paradigm shift from empirical to precision-based treatment selection in oncology.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel method for predicting response to antiangiogenic therapy in patients with solid tumors, particularly HER2-negative breast cancer, through the calculation of a protein-based prognostic signature score known as the VEGF Inhibition Response Predictor (ViRP) score. The ViRP score is derived from the quantitative measurement of nine specific proteins or phosphoproteins in pre-treatment tumor tissue samples, each selected through rigorous statistical modeling using adaptive Lasso regression to correlate with tumor shrinkage following treatment with a VEGF inhibitor in combination with chemotherapy. The nine proteins comprising the ViRP signature are selected from a panel of 210 cancer-relevant proteins measured by reverse-phase protein array technology, and include ACC-pS79, Syk, PAK1, ERK1/2-pT202/Y204, FAK-pY397, PLCγ1-pY783, STAT3-pY705, CrkL-pY207, and SHP2-pY542. Each protein’s expression level is weighted by a predefined coefficient derived from multivariate modeling, and the sum of these weighted values, plus a fixed intercept, constitutes the ViRP score. The score is calculated using a mathematical formula that integrates the log2-transformed, median-centered protein expression values with their respective beta coefficients and a constant term, and it is designed to be applied to tumor tissue obtained prior to initiation of therapy.

The ViRP score has been validated in two independent clinical cohorts and demonstrates a strong and statistically significant association with pathologic response to treatment, as measured by pathologic complete response (pCR) and residual cancer burden (RCB) class. Patients with low ViRP scores are significantly more likely to achieve pCR or low RCB (RCB 0/I) following neoadjuvant treatment with a VEGF inhibitor and chemotherapy, whereas patients with high ViRP scores are unlikely to benefit from the addition of the antiangiogenic agent. The predictive performance of the ViRP score, as measured by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, exceeds 0.80 for both pCR and low RCB, indicating high diagnostic accuracy. The score also demonstrates superior predictive capability compared to conventional clinical and pathological variables, including tumor stage, hormone receptor status, PAM50 subtype, and TP53 mutation status, none of which were independently associated with response in the development cohort. Importantly, the ViRP score is not dependent on estrogen receptor expression, which distinguishes it from other prognostic tools and enables its application across both ER-positive and ER-negative subtypes.

The invention further encompasses a surrogate mRNA-based ViRP score, which is computed using the same mathematical formula but applied to the mRNA expression levels of the genes encoding the nine proteins in the signature, including the parent genes of phosphoproteins. This mRNA surrogate demonstrates a high degree of correlation with the original protein-based ViRP score, with a Pearson correlation coefficient exceeding 0.75, and retains significant predictive power for pCR in both the development and validation cohorts. This feature enables the ViRP score to be implemented using standard clinical molecular diagnostics platforms such as RNA sequencing or quantitative PCR, thereby facilitating broader adoption in routine pathology laboratories without requiring specialized protein detection technologies. The invention also includes a method for determining an optimal cutoff value for dichotomizing patients into high- and low-ViRP score groups based on maximizing the Youden index or other clinically relevant performance metrics, such that patients with scores below the cutoff are recommended for treatment with a VEGF inhibitor plus chemotherapy, while those above the cutoff are directed toward alternative therapeutic strategies.

The ViRP score is applicable not only in the neoadjuvant setting but also in the metastatic and adjuvant contexts, and its biological underpinnings suggest potential utility in other solid tumors where VEGF inhibition is a component of standard therapy, including ovarian, colorectal, renal, and non-small cell lung cancers. The signature proteins are enriched in signaling pathways related to angiogenesis, immune cell infiltration, cytoskeletal remodeling, and receptor tyrosine kinase signaling, suggesting that the ViRP score reflects a composite biological state of the tumor microenvironment conducive to response to VEGF blockade. The invention further provides a method for validating the ViRP score in independent cohorts using the same mathematical model and predefined coefficients, ensuring consistency and reproducibility across institutions. The invention eliminates the need for trial-and-error treatment selection and enables clinicians to avoid exposing non-responders to the toxicity, cost, and delay associated with ineffective therapy. The ViRP score thus represents a transformative diagnostic tool that bridges the gap between molecular biology and clinical decision-making in precision oncology.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Treatment Regimen

The present invention provides a method for selecting patients for treatment with a vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitor in combination with chemotherapy based on the calculation of a protein-based prognostic signature score, known as the VEGF Inhibition Response Predictor (ViRP) score. The method is applicable to patients diagnosed with solid tumors, particularly those with HER2-negative breast cancer, who are candidates for neoadjuvant, adjuvant, or metastatic treatment regimens that include a VEGF inhibitor and cytotoxic chemotherapy. The VEGF inhibitor may be any agent that directly or indirectly inhibits the VEGF signaling pathway, including but not limited to monoclonal antibodies such as bevacizumab, ramucirumab, or aflibercept; tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as sunitinib, pazopanib, or axitinib; or biosimilar versions thereof. The chemotherapy component may include any standard cytotoxic regimen commonly used in the treatment of breast cancer, including but not limited to anthracycline-taxane combinations, capecitabine, gemcitabine, carboplatin, or cyclophosphamide-based regimens, administered in any sequence or schedule consistent with clinical guidelines.

The method begins with the acquisition of a tumor tissue sample from the patient prior to initiation of treatment. The sample may be obtained via core needle biopsy, fine-needle aspiration, or surgical excision, and must be preserved in a manner that maintains protein integrity, such as snap-freezing in liquid nitrogen or fixation in formalin followed by embedding in paraffin. The tissue sample is then processed to extract total protein, which is subjected to quantitative analysis using reverse-phase protein array (RPPA) technology. In this process, serial dilutions of the protein lysate are spotted onto a solid support, probed with validated antibodies specific to each of the nine signature proteins, and detected using a colorimetric or chemiluminescent system. The resulting signal intensities are normalized for total protein loading, log2-transformed, and median-centered across the cohort to account for technical variability. The normalized expression values for each of the nine proteins are then multiplied by their respective predefined beta coefficients, which were derived from multivariate statistical modeling of a training cohort of patients treated with VEGF inhibitor plus chemotherapy. The sum of these weighted values, plus a fixed intercept term, yields the ViRP score for the individual patient.

Once the ViRP score is calculated, it is compared to a predetermined cutoff value that has been established through receiver operating characteristic analysis to optimize the balance between sensitivity and specificity for predicting pathologic complete response or low residual cancer burden. If the patient’s ViRP score is below the cutoff, the patient is classified as a likely responder and is recommended for treatment with the VEGF inhibitor in combination with chemotherapy. If the ViRP score is at or above the cutoff, the patient is classified as a likely non-responder and is directed toward alternative therapeutic strategies, such as chemotherapy alone, endocrine therapy, or participation in clinical trials of novel agents. The cutoff value may be adjusted based on the clinical context, such as the desired level of sensitivity for identifying responders in a high-risk population or the need to minimize false positives in a low-prevalence setting. The method may be implemented in clinical laboratories using automated platforms for protein quantification and software algorithms for score calculation, ensuring reproducibility and scalability across healthcare systems.

The invention further encompasses the use of an mRNA-based surrogate of the ViRP score, which is calculated using the same mathematical formula but applied to the mRNA expression levels of the genes encoding the nine signature proteins. This approach enables the use of RNA extracted from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue, which is more routinely available in pathology departments than frozen protein lysates. The mRNA expression values are quantile-normalized and averaged across probe sets corresponding to each gene, then multiplied by the same beta coefficients and intercept used in the protein-based model. The resulting mRNA ViRP score is then interpreted using the same cutoff value as the protein-based score. This surrogate method provides a practical alternative for institutions that lack access to RPPA technology but possess RNA sequencing or microarray capabilities, thereby broadening the clinical applicability of the invention. The invention does not require the simultaneous measurement of all nine proteins; rather, the full signature must be assessed in its entirety, as the predictive power arises from the combinatorial interaction of the nine proteins, not from any single component.

The treatment regimen guided by the ViRP score may be administered in any clinical setting, including outpatient infusion centers, hospital-based oncology units, or specialized cancer centers. The duration of treatment may vary depending on the stage of disease and the specific chemotherapy regimen employed, but typically spans multiple cycles over a period of several months. For neoadjuvant treatment, therapy is initiated prior to surgery and continued until the time of definitive resection. For metastatic disease, treatment may be continued until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. The ViRP score may be re-evaluated during the course of treatment if new tissue is obtained, but its primary utility lies in pre-treatment patient stratification. The invention does not preclude the use of other biomarkers or clinical parameters in conjunction with the ViRP score; however, the score alone has been demonstrated to be independently predictive of response, and its inclusion in decision-making algorithms has been shown to significantly improve outcomes compared to standard clinical criteria.

### Relative Importance of Proteins and Modelling of Protein Combinations

The predictive power of the VEGF Inhibition Response Predictor (ViRP) score arises not from the individual activity of any single protein, but from the synergistic interaction of nine specific proteins and phosphoproteins whose combined expression pattern reflects a biological state of the tumor microenvironment that is uniquely responsive to VEGF inhibition in combination with chemotherapy. Each of the nine proteins—ACC-pS79, Syk, PAK1, ERK1/2-pT202/Y204, FAK-pY397, PLCγ1-pY783, STAT3-pY705, CrkL-pY207, and SHP2-pY542—has been rigorously selected through a multivariate statistical modeling process that accounts for co-linearity, variance, and predictive contribution across a cohort of patients treated with VEGF inhibitor plus chemotherapy. The relative importance of each protein within the signature was quantified using the lmg metric from the relaimpo package, which decomposes the total model R-squared into contributions attributable to each variable. This analysis revealed that Syk, a spleen tyrosine kinase involved in immune cell signaling and endothelial cell migration, contributed the largest proportion of predictive variance, followed by FAK-pY397, a focal adhesion kinase critical for integrin-mediated cell survival and angiogenesis, and ERK1/2-pT202/Y204, a key effector of the MAPK pathway involved in proliferation and survival signaling.

The inclusion of phosphorylated forms of these proteins is a critical innovation of the invention, as phosphorylation status directly reflects the activation state of signaling pathways and is often more closely associated with functional outcomes than total protein abundance. For example, the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 at threonine 202 and tyrosine 204 indicates active signaling through the RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK cascade, which has been implicated in resistance to antiangiogenic therapy. Similarly, phosphorylation of FAK at tyrosine 397 is a marker of integrin activation and is associated with increased tumor cell motility and vascular invasion. The presence of these phosphoproteins in the signature ensures that the ViRP score captures dynamic signaling states rather than static protein expression levels, thereby enhancing its biological relevance and predictive accuracy. The inclusion of ACC-pS79, a phosphorylated form of acetyl-CoA carboxylase, further reflects metabolic reprogramming in response to angiogenic inhibition, as this enzyme is involved in fatty acid synthesis and has been linked to tumor cell survival under nutrient stress.

The mathematical model underlying the ViRP score was developed using adaptive Lasso regression, a penalized regression technique that simultaneously selects variables and estimates their coefficients by applying a data-driven penalty that shrinks irrelevant predictors toward zero while preserving those with strong predictive associations. The model was trained on a cohort of 54 patients with HER2-negative breast cancer treated with VEGF inhibitor plus chemotherapy, using relative tumor size after neoadjuvant treatment as the continuous outcome variable. This approach, rather than dichotomizing response as pCR or non-pCR, allowed the model to capture the full spectrum of tumor shrinkage, thereby improving the robustness and generalizability of the signature. The final model retained nine proteins with non-zero coefficients, one of which was eliminated in an iterative refinement step, resulting in a parsimonious yet highly predictive signature. The intercept term was determined as the mean residual value after accounting for the weighted contributions of the nine proteins, ensuring that the score is calibrated to the population from which it was derived.

The relative importance of the proteins is not static and may vary depending on the tumor subtype, treatment regimen, or microenvironmental context. However, the coefficients used in the ViRP score were derived from a population that included both ER-positive and ER-negative patients, and the signature was validated across both subgroups, demonstrating that its predictive power is independent of hormone receptor status. This is a significant departure from prior biomarkers, which often fail in heterogeneous populations. The signature proteins are not randomly selected but are biologically interconnected, forming a network of signaling nodes that regulate angiogenesis, cell adhesion, immune modulation, and metabolic adaptation. Syk, for instance, is expressed not only in immune cells but also in endothelial and epithelial cells, where it modulates VEGF receptor signaling and vascular permeability. FAK and PAK1 interact with Rho GTPases to control cytoskeletal dynamics and cell migration, processes essential for vascular sprouting. ERK1/2 and STAT3 are downstream effectors of multiple receptor tyrosine kinases, including VEGFR, and their phosphorylation status reflects the integrated activity of these pathways. The presence of PLCγ1-pY783, CrkL-pY207, and SHP2-pY542 further indicates activation of receptor-proximal signaling cascades that regulate cell survival and proliferation in response to growth factor stimulation.

The invention does not rely on the assumption that any single protein is causally responsible for response; rather, it recognizes that tumor response to antiangiogenic therapy is a complex, multi-factorial phenomenon that requires the simultaneous assessment of multiple biological dimensions. The ViRP score functions as a composite readout of this complexity, integrating signals from cell-autonomous tumor pathways, stromal interactions, and immune modulation into a single numerical value. This approach is fundamentally different from prior biomarker strategies that attempted to identify single-gene or single-protein predictors, which have consistently failed due to biological redundancy and heterogeneity. The signature’s predictive performance, as measured by area under the curve exceeding 0.80, is attributable to this combinatorial design, which captures emergent properties of the tumor ecosystem that cannot be discerned from individual components. The model’s stability was confirmed through leave-one-out cross-validation, and its generalizability was validated in an independent cohort of 122 patients from the PROMIX trial, where the mRNA surrogate of the ViRP score maintained significant predictive power for pathologic response. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift from reductionist biomarker discovery to systems-level modeling of therapeutic response, providing a clinically actionable tool grounded in the complexity of cancer biology.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Identification of Ten Protein Prognostic Signature

In the development of the VEGF Inhibition Response Predictor (ViRP) score, a cohort of 109 patients with HER2-negative breast cancer who had undergone neoadjuvant treatment with either chemotherapy alone or chemotherapy in combination with bevacizumab was analyzed using reverse-phase protein arrays (RPPA) to quantify the expression levels of 210 cancer-relevant proteins, including 54 phosphorylated variants. The primary objective was to identify a protein signature that could predict tumor shrinkage following treatment with bevacizumab plus chemotherapy, as measured by the continuous variable of relative tumor size after 24 weeks of therapy. Initial unsupervised hierarchical clustering of the protein expression data revealed distinct molecular subgroups that correlated with both PAM50 subtypes and the degree of tumor response, suggesting that protein expression patterns contain information relevant to therapeutic outcome. Univariate Spearman correlation analysis of each protein against relative tumor size identified 103 proteins with inverse relationships to response, indicating that lower expression of these proteins was associated with greater tumor shrinkage. However, the number of significantly correlated proteins was greater in the chemotherapy-alone arm (54 proteins) than in the bevacizumab-plus-chemotherapy arm (38 proteins), and only ten proteins showed significant correlation in both arms, with two of these exhibiting opposing directions of association, indicating divergent biological mechanisms of response.

To isolate a signature specific to response to bevacizumab, a subset of 54 patients treated with bevacizumab plus chemotherapy was selected for model development. Given that proteins with low expression variance are unlikely to provide discriminatory power in a clinical setting, a mixed-model distribution analysis was performed on the variance of all 210 proteins, and only those with variance exceeding a defined threshold (N = 114) were retained for further analysis. Adaptive Lasso regression was then applied to this reduced set of proteins, using relative tumor size as the outcome variable and applying a penalty parameter (lambda) determined by leave-one-out cross-validation with mean absolute error as the loss function. The initial Lasso model identified ten proteins with non-zero coefficients, indicating their contribution to the prediction of tumor shrinkage. A second iterative round of Lasso regression was performed on this subset of ten proteins to refine the model and eliminate redundancy. In this refinement step, one protein was shrunk to a coefficient of zero, resulting in a final signature of nine proteins: ACC-pS79, Syk, PAK1, ERK1/2-pT202/Y204, FAK-pY397, PLCγ1-pY783, STAT3-pY705, CrkL-pY207, and SHP2-pY542. The intercept and beta coefficients for each of these nine proteins were determined from the final model and are presented in Table 1.

The ViRP score for each patient was calculated as the sum of the intercept and the product of each protein’s expression value and its corresponding beta coefficient. The resulting score demonstrated a strong and statistically significant correlation with relative tumor size after treatment, with a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.67 (P < 0.001), indicating that higher ViRP scores were associated with less tumor shrinkage and lower scores with greater shrinkage. When dichotomized using pathologic complete response (pCR) as the outcome, patients achieving pCR had significantly lower ViRP scores than those who did not (P < 0.001), with a mean difference of 0.84 units. Similar results were observed when residual cancer burden (RCB) was used as the response metric, with patients classified as low RCB (RCB 0/I) exhibiting significantly lower ViRP scores than those with high RCB (RCB II/III) (P < 0.001). The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve for the ViRP score in predicting pCR was 0.85 (95% confidence interval: 0.74 to 0.97), and for low RCB it was 0.80 (95% confidence interval: 0.68 to 0.93), demonstrating high diagnostic accuracy. The optimal cutoff value for dichotomizing patients into likely responders and non-responders was determined to be 0.366, based on the Youden index, which maximizes the sum of sensitivity and specificity. At this cutoff, the proportion of patients achieving pCR increased from 26% in the unselected population to 47% in the ViRP score-selected group, representing a near doubling of response rate. The predictive performance of the ViRP score was superior to all conventional clinical and pathological variables, including tumor size, nodal status, hormone receptor expression, PAM50 subtype, and TP53 mutation status, none of which achieved statistical significance in univariate analysis.

To assess the feasibility of translating the protein-based ViRP score into a clinically accessible mRNA-based assay, the corresponding mRNA expression levels of the genes encoding the nine signature proteins were extracted from the same cohort. The mRNA expression values were quantile-normalized and averaged across probe sets, then multiplied by the same beta coefficients and intercept derived from the protein model. The resulting mRNA ViRP score exhibited a high degree of correlation with the original protein-based score, with a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.75 (P < 0.001). When applied to the pCR outcome, the mRNA ViRP score also demonstrated a significant difference between responders and non-responders (P = 0.016), confirming that the mRNA surrogate retains predictive power despite the loss of phosphorylation information. The area under the curve for the mRNA ViRP score in predicting pCR was 0.73, indicating that while the protein-based score remains the gold standard, the mRNA surrogate is sufficiently accurate for clinical implementation in settings where protein measurement is not feasible. The signature was further validated in an external cohort of 122 patients from the PROMIX trial, where the mRNA ViRP score again showed a significant difference between pCR and non-pCR patients (P < 0.001), confirming its generalizability across independent populations. These findings establish the ViRP score as a robust, reproducible, and biologically grounded predictor of response to antiangiogenic therapy, providing a foundation for its integration into clinical decision-making algorithms.
