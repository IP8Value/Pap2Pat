# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of neuropharmacology and cognitive enhancement, specifically to the use of ampakine compounds for the restoration and enhancement of cognitive performance in conditions of sleep deprivation and normal alert states. More particularly, the invention concerns the administration of specific positive allosteric modulators of the α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptor, including but not limited to CX717, to mitigate the detrimental effects of prolonged wakefulness on memory, attention, decision-making, and response latency in non-human primates and, by extension, in human subjects. The invention further encompasses the use of such compounds to normalize regional cerebral metabolic activity in brain regions critically involved in executive function, working memory, and sensory-motor integration, as demonstrated through positron emission tomography and electroencephalographic correlates. The therapeutic utility of these compounds extends to clinical and occupational settings where sustained cognitive performance is essential despite circadian disruption, including military operations, emergency response, long-haul transportation, shift work, and medical care during extended duty cycles. The invention provides a novel, non-stimulant pharmacological approach to cognitive resilience that operates through the modulation of glutamatergic synaptic transmission, thereby avoiding the adverse effects associated with traditional psychostimulants such as amphetamines, caffeine, or modafinil.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Electrophysiology of Sleep and Sleep-Deprivation

Sleep is a complex, actively regulated neurobiological state characterized by distinct patterns of electrical activity in the central nervous system, observable through electroencephalographic (EEG) recordings. These patterns include synchronized slow-wave activity during non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep, particularly in the delta frequency band (0.5–5 Hz), and desynchronized, high-frequency activity during rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, notably in the beta range (18–25 Hz). The transition between wakefulness and sleep is governed by intricate interactions among subcortical nuclei, including the hypothalamic orexin system, the brainstem reticular formation, and the thalamocortical circuitry. Prolonged wakefulness disrupts this delicate balance, leading to the emergence of pathological electrophysiological signatures that reflect a brain struggling to maintain functional integrity. These include the appearance of transient, localized delta wave bursts during periods of attempted wakefulness, commonly referred to as “microsleep” episodes, which are associated with momentary lapses in attention and impaired sensory processing. Such episodes are not uniformly distributed across the cortex but tend to occur preferentially in regions under high cognitive load, such as the prefrontal cortex and medial temporal lobe. The persistence of these aberrant oscillations during extended periods of sleep deprivation correlates strongly with declining task accuracy, increased response variability, and slowed reaction times. Importantly, these electrophysiological disturbances are not merely epiphenomena of fatigue but represent active, maladaptive neural processes that interfere with the temporal precision required for information encoding, maintenance, and retrieval. The current invention addresses these disruptions by targeting the underlying synaptic mechanisms that govern cortical excitability and signal fidelity, thereby restoring the electrophysiological profile of the awake, alert brain without inducing artificial arousal or altering the fundamental architecture of sleep-wake regulation.

### Neuroanatomical Substrates of Sleep Deprivation

The cognitive deficits observed during prolonged sleep deprivation are not uniformly distributed across the brain but are instead localized to specific neural circuits that support executive control, working memory, and attentional vigilance. Functional neuroimaging studies in both humans and non-human primates have consistently identified the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) as a critical locus of vulnerability to sleep loss. This region, essential for maintaining goal-directed behavior, suppressing irrelevant stimuli, and coordinating sequential cognitive operations, exhibits marked reductions in glucose metabolism and neural activation during extended wakefulness. Similarly, the dorsal striatum, a key node in the corticostriatal loop involved in motor planning and response selection, demonstrates diminished metabolic activity under sleep-deprived conditions, suggesting a breakdown in the integration of cognitive and motor commands. The medial temporal lobe, encompassing the hippocampus and adjacent cortical structures, is also profoundly affected, with reduced metabolic engagement impairing the consolidation and retrieval of recent episodic and spatial information. Paradoxically, other regions such as the precuneus and posterior parietal cortex show compensatory increases in metabolic activity during sleep deprivation, possibly reflecting the recruitment of alternative networks to sustain performance in the face of primary system failure. The thalamus, traditionally viewed as a passive relay station, emerges as a critical hub whose metabolic suppression under sleep deprivation is not reversed by pharmacological intervention, suggesting a unique role in the generation or maintenance of sleep-like states independent of cortical AMPA receptor modulation. These neuroanatomical findings reveal a distributed pattern of dysfunction in which the brain’s most sophisticated cognitive circuits are selectively compromised, while more primitive or compensatory regions attempt to maintain baseline function. The present invention exploits this differential vulnerability by selectively enhancing synaptic efficacy in the DLPFC, striatum, and medial temporal lobe, thereby restoring the functional connectivity necessary for complex cognitive tasks without overactivating regions that may contribute to maladaptive compensation.

### Role of Stress in Sleep Deprivation and Cognitive Performance

Sleep deprivation is not merely a state of physical exhaustion but is accompanied by a cascade of neuroendocrine and autonomic responses that further exacerbate cognitive decline. The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis becomes chronically activated during prolonged wakefulness, resulting in elevated circulating levels of cortisol and other glucocorticoids. These stress hormones exert widespread effects on the central nervous system, including the downregulation of glutamate receptor expression, the suppression of synaptic plasticity in the hippocampus, and the impairment of long-term potentiation—a cellular correlate of learning and memory. Moreover, chronic stress alters the balance between excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmission, increasing GABAergic tone and reducing the availability of glutamate at key synapses. This creates a neurochemical environment in which even intact neural circuits are unable to generate the robust, sustained firing patterns required for accurate task performance. The interaction between sleep deprivation and stress is synergistic: the longer an individual remains awake, the greater the accumulation of metabolic byproducts, oxidative stress, and inflammatory cytokines, all of which further depress neuronal function. The resulting state is one of neural inefficiency, in which the brain must expend disproportionately greater energy to achieve the same level of performance, leading to accelerated fatigue and increased error rates. The ampakine compounds described herein counteract this stress-induced synaptic depression by enhancing the amplitude and duration of postsynaptic currents mediated by AMPA receptors, effectively raising the signal-to-noise ratio in glutamatergic pathways. This mechanism allows neurons to maintain functional output despite the presence of elevated stress hormones and reduced receptor availability, thereby preserving cognitive performance under conditions that would otherwise lead to catastrophic failure. The invention thus provides a pharmacological strategy that directly targets the synaptic consequences of stress, independent of its systemic hormonal manifestations.

### Effects of Sleep-Deprivation on Memory

Memory, particularly working memory and short-term retention, is among the most vulnerable cognitive functions to the effects of prolonged sleep deprivation. The ability to hold information in mind for brief intervals—such as the memory of a visual stimulus presented seconds earlier—is critically dependent on the coordinated activity of the prefrontal cortex and medial temporal lobe. Sleep deprivation disrupts this coordination by reducing the temporal precision of neuronal firing, increasing response variability, and diminishing the fidelity of synaptic transmission. In behavioral paradigms such as the delayed-match-to-sample task, animals and humans exhibit a dose-dependent decline in accuracy as the delay between stimulus presentation and response requirement increases. This decline is not simply a function of time passing but reflects an active degradation of the neural representation of the stimulus, likely due to the failure of recurrent excitatory loops to sustain persistent activity. The introduction of distractor stimuli further compounds this deficit, as the brain’s ability to filter irrelevant information becomes impaired. The result is a breakdown in the hierarchical organization of memory systems, in which the prefrontal cortex loses its capacity to guide retrieval from the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex. The present invention demonstrates that the administration of ampakine compounds restores the temporal dynamics of synaptic transmission in these circuits, allowing for the maintenance of stimulus representations over extended delays and improving the ability to discriminate target from distractor stimuli. This restoration is not achieved by increasing general arousal or motivation but by directly enhancing the biophysical properties of glutamatergic synapses, thereby reinstating the neural mechanisms that underlie successful memory performance. The invention thus provides a targeted intervention for memory deficits caused by sleep loss, with implications for clinical populations suffering from insomnia, circadian rhythm disorders, and neurodegenerative conditions characterized by synaptic hypofunction.

### II. Non-Human Primate Model for Testing Effects of Sleep Deprivation on a Delayed-Match-to-Sample Task (DMTS)

The invention is grounded in a robust, validated non-human primate model of cognitive performance that closely mirrors the neurobiological and behavioral characteristics of human cognition under conditions of sleep deprivation. The model employs adult male rhesus macaques (Macaca mulatta), a species whose prefrontal cortical architecture, memory systems, and sleep-wake regulation are highly analogous to those of humans. These animals are trained to perform a delayed-match-to-sample (DMS) task, a paradigm in which a visual stimulus is presented for a brief duration, followed by a variable delay interval, after which the subject must select the previously presented stimulus from among a set of distractor images. The task is designed to engage multiple cognitive domains simultaneously: attention during stimulus encoding, working memory during the delay period, perceptual discrimination during the match phase, and motor planning during response execution. The parameters of the task—specifically, the duration of the delay (ranging from 1 to 30 seconds) and the number of distractor images (ranging from two to six)—are systematically varied to create a gradient of cognitive difficulty, allowing for the detection of subtle impairments in performance. The animals are trained to operate a cursor using a hand-held manipulandum, which requires fine motor control and sustained attention, and are rewarded with liquid reinforcement to maintain motivation. This behavioral setup ensures high levels of compliance and minimizes confounding variables such as food deprivation or stress-induced hyperactivity. The DMS task is performed daily under controlled environmental conditions, with lighting, sound, and temperature maintained at consistent levels to eliminate external influences on performance. To assess the effects of sleep deprivation, animals are subjected to 30 to 36 hours of continuous wakefulness, during which they are housed in a brightly lit room and kept engaged through intermittent interaction, auditory stimulation, and gentle physical cues to prevent the onset of sleep. This protocol reliably induces profound deficits in task accuracy and increased response latencies, mirroring the cognitive impairments observed in sleep-deprived humans. Critically, the same animals are tested under multiple conditions: normal alert with vehicle administration, normal alert with ampakine administration, sleep-deprived with vehicle administration, and sleep-deprived with ampakine administration. This within-subjects design eliminates inter-individual variability and allows for the precise quantification of drug effects across states of arousal. The model further incorporates concurrent neurophysiological and neuroimaging assessments, including electroencephalographic recordings to detect microsleep episodes and positron emission tomography to measure regional cerebral glucose metabolism. These multimodal measurements provide a comprehensive picture of the relationship between synaptic modulation, neural activity, and behavioral output. The invention leverages this model to demonstrate that the ampakine CX717, administered intravenously at doses ranging from 0.3 to 1.5 mg/kg, produces a dose-dependent enhancement of task performance in normal alert animals and completely reverses the deficits induced by sleep deprivation. The magnitude of this effect is such that performance in sleep-deprived animals treated with CX717 not only returns to baseline levels but frequently exceeds them, indicating a true enhancement of cognitive capacity rather than mere restoration. The model further reveals that the drug’s effects are temporally specific, with improvements in performance emerging within minutes of administration and persisting for the duration of the testing session. The consistency of these findings across multiple animals, multiple sessions, and multiple task parameters establishes the reliability and reproducibility of the effect, forming the foundation for translation to human applications. The non-human primate model thus serves as a critical bridge between molecular pharmacology and clinical cognition, providing a physiologically relevant system in which the therapeutic potential of ampakines can be rigorously evaluated.

### Salts of Compounds

The ampakine compounds described herein, including but not limited to CX717, may be formulated in various salt forms to optimize their physicochemical properties for pharmaceutical use. The chemical structure of CX717 contains functional groups capable of forming stable ionic interactions with pharmaceutically acceptable counterions, thereby enhancing solubility, stability, bioavailability, and ease of formulation. Suitable salts include, but are not limited to, hydrochloride, sulfate, phosphate, acetate, citrate, tartrate, malate, fumarate, maleate, succinate, benzoate, methanesulfonate, ethanesulfonate, tosylate, and hydroxypropyl-beta-cyclodextrin complexes. The hydroxypropyl-beta-cyclodextrin complex, in particular, has been demonstrated to be an effective vehicle for intravenous administration in non-human primates, as it enhances aqueous solubility without inducing hemolytic or immunogenic responses. The formation of such salts is achieved through standard pharmaceutical techniques, including acid-base neutralization, crystallization, lyophilization, and co-precipitation. The resulting salt forms are characterized by their melting point, solubility profile, hygroscopicity, crystalline structure, and dissolution rate, all of which are optimized for parenteral delivery. The salt form may also influence the pharmacokinetic profile of the compound, including its rate of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion. For instance, the hydrochloride salt may exhibit faster dissolution in physiological fluids, leading to a more rapid onset of action, while the citrate salt may offer improved stability in solution over extended storage periods. The selection of the appropriate salt form is guided by the intended route of administration, the desired duration of effect, and the need for compatibility with existing delivery systems such as intravenous infusion pumps or implantable reservoirs. The invention encompasses all pharmaceutically acceptable salts of the ampakine compounds, including racemic mixtures, enantiomers, diastereomers, and hydrates thereof, provided that the salt form retains the positive allosteric modulatory activity of the parent compound at the AMPA receptor. The salts may be administered in solution, suspension, or lyophilized powder form, and may be combined with other excipients such as buffers, stabilizers, tonicity agents, and preservatives to ensure compatibility with biological systems. The invention further includes methods of preparing these salts, including the use of organic solvents, supercritical fluids, and spray-drying techniques to produce particles of controlled size and morphology suitable for inhalation or parenteral delivery. The salt forms are not merely inert carriers but are integral to the therapeutic efficacy of the invention, as they determine the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profile of the active moiety in vivo.

### Pharmaceutical Formulations

The ampakine compounds of the invention may be formulated into a variety of pharmaceutical compositions suitable for administration to mammalian subjects, including humans and non-human primates. The formulations are designed to deliver the active compound in a stable, bioavailable, and safe manner, with particular emphasis on parenteral routes such as intravenous, intramuscular, and subcutaneous administration, given the rapid onset of action required for acute cognitive enhancement. The primary formulation for intravenous use comprises CX717 dissolved in a solution of 10% w/v hydroxypropyl-beta-cyclodextrin in 0.45% saline, a vehicle that has been shown to be non-toxic, non-irritating, and compatible with prolonged infusion in primates. Alternative formulations may include aqueous solutions of the compound in buffered saline (pH 6.5–7.5), isotonic dextrose solutions, or phosphate-buffered saline containing stabilizing agents such as mannitol, glycine, or albumin. For extended-release applications, the compound may be encapsulated in biodegradable polymers such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA), polyethylene glycol (PEG), or chitosan, allowing for sustained delivery over hours or days. Microsphere and nanoparticle formulations may be employed to target specific brain regions via intranasal or intracerebroventricular delivery, bypassing the blood-brain barrier. Oral formulations may be prepared as tablets, capsules, or liquid suspensions containing the ampakine compound in combination with excipients such as lactose, microcrystalline cellulose, starch, magnesium stearate, and hypromellose, with optional enteric coatings to protect the compound from gastric degradation. Transdermal patches may be developed using permeation enhancers such as oleic acid or ethanol to facilitate systemic absorption through the skin. Inhalation formulations may be prepared as dry powders or aerosols using propellants or nebulization systems suitable for pulmonary delivery. Each formulation is optimized for stability, sterility, and compatibility with standard pharmaceutical manufacturing processes, and is tested for pyrogenicity, endotoxin content, and particulate matter in accordance with United States Pharmacopeia (USP) and International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) guidelines. The formulations may also include preservatives such as benzalkonium chloride or parabens for multi-dose containers, or may be provided as single-use, sterile, preservative-free units for parenteral administration. The invention encompasses all such formulations, whether for acute, intermittent, or chronic administration, and includes methods of preparing and packaging these compositions for clinical use. The choice of formulation is determined by the clinical context, the desired pharmacokinetic profile, and the route of administration most suitable for the intended patient population, including military personnel, shift workers, or patients with neurological disorders characterized by synaptic hypofunction.

### Dosage

The dosage of the ampakine compound for the purpose of enhancing cognitive performance in conditions of sleep deprivation or normal alert states is determined based on the subject’s body weight, the route of administration, the desired speed of onset, and the duration of effect required. In non-human primates, a dose range of 0.3 to 1.5 mg/kg, administered intravenously, has been demonstrated to produce a dose-dependent enhancement of task performance, with maximal efficacy observed at 0.8 mg/kg. This dose produces a rapid increase in accuracy and a significant reduction in response latency within 5 to 10 minutes of administration, with effects persisting for the duration of the testing session, typically 60 to 90 minutes. Lower doses (0.3–0.5 mg/kg) produce modest but statistically significant improvements, while higher doses (1.2–1.5 mg/kg) do not confer additional benefit and may be associated with transient increases in heart rate or mild autonomic activation, suggesting a narrow therapeutic window. For human applications, the dosage is extrapolated based on body surface area scaling, resulting in an equivalent human dose of approximately 0.05 to 0.25 mg/kg, which corresponds to a total dose of 3.5 to 17.5 mg for a 70 kg adult. Intravenous administration is preferred for acute, time-sensitive applications such as emergency response or military operations, where rapid cognitive enhancement is critical. For longer-duration applications, such as shift work or chronic sleep disruption, subcutaneous or intramuscular formulations may be employed, with dosing intervals of 4 to 8 hours to maintain therapeutic plasma concentrations. Oral formulations may be administered at higher doses (e.g., 5–20 mg) to account for first-pass metabolism and lower bioavailability, with peak plasma concentrations expected within 30 to 60 minutes. The invention encompasses single-dose, multiple-dose, and continuous-infusion regimens, with the total daily dose not exceeding 0.5 mg/kg in humans. The dosage may be adjusted based on individual pharmacokinetic parameters, including clearance rate, volume of distribution, and half-life, which may vary with age, sex, hepatic function, and concomitant medication use. The invention further includes methods of titrating the dosage based on real-time cognitive performance metrics, such as those derived from computerized neuropsychological testing or wearable neurophysiological monitors, allowing for personalized, adaptive dosing strategies. The dosage is selected to achieve a therapeutic plasma concentration of 10 to 100 ng/mL, which has been shown to correlate with maximal AMPA receptor modulation and cognitive enhancement without inducing adverse effects. The invention thus provides a precise, evidence-based framework for the administration of ampakine compounds across a range of clinical and occupational settings, ensuring optimal efficacy and safety.
