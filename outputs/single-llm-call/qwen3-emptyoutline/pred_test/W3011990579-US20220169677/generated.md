# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present disclosure relates to novel peptidic compounds and their use as inhibitors of Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) receptor activity, particularly in the context of pathological angiogenesis and tumor progression. More specifically, the invention encompasses a class of linear and branched peptides characterized by the presence of an N-terminal cysteine residue adjacent to a C-terminal arginine residue, thereby forming a structural motif that significantly enhances binding affinity for the b1 domain of NRP-1. These compounds are designed to disrupt the interaction between vascular endothelial growth factor A165 (VEGF-A165) and NRP-1, thereby inhibiting downstream pro-angiogenic signaling pathways. The disclosed peptides exhibit markedly improved inhibitory potency compared to previously known NRP-1 ligands, including those adhering to the established C-end rule (CendR), and demonstrate sufficient metabolic stability in human plasma to support potential therapeutic application. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising these peptides and methods of using them for the treatment of diseases characterized by aberrant angiogenesis, including but not limited to solid tumors, ocular neovascular disorders, and inflammatory conditions driven by vascular hyperpermeability.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Neuropilin-1 is a non-tyrosine kinase cell surface receptor that plays a pivotal role in both developmental and pathological angiogenesis. Originally identified as a guidance receptor for class 3 semaphorins in the nervous system, NRP-1 has since been recognized as a critical co-receptor for multiple growth factor families, most notably vascular endothelial growth factor A165 (VEGF-A165), which is the principal mediator of vascular endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and survival. The interaction between VEGF-A165 and NRP-1 enhances the binding affinity and signaling efficiency of VEGF-A165 for its primary receptor, VEGFR-2, thereby amplifying pro-angiogenic signals that are essential for tumor vascularization and metastasis. Elevated expression of NRP-1 on endothelial cells, tumor cells, and certain immune populations has been consistently correlated with poor clinical prognosis in a wide array of malignancies, including breast, lung, prostate, and colorectal cancers. Consequently, the development of agents capable of selectively and potently disrupting the VEGF-A165/NRP-1 axis represents a compelling therapeutic strategy for inhibiting tumor angiogenesis without directly targeting VEGFR-2, thereby potentially mitigating resistance mechanisms and reducing systemic toxicity associated with broad-spectrum anti-angiogenic therapies.

Prior efforts to develop NRP-1 inhibitors have focused primarily on monoclonal antibodies, soluble receptor decoys, and small molecule antagonists. While some of these approaches have demonstrated efficacy in preclinical models, their clinical translation has been limited by issues such as poor bioavailability, high production costs, or insufficient target specificity. Peptidic ligands, by contrast, offer a promising alternative due to their high binding affinity, structural versatility, and potential for chemical optimization. The discovery of the C-end rule (CendR) provided a foundational framework for rational design of NRP-1-binding peptides, identifying a conserved C-terminal motif—typically R/KXXR/K—as essential for high-affinity interaction with the b1 domain of NRP-1. Peptides such as A7R (ATWLPPR), tuftsin (TKPR), and the C-terminal hexapeptide of VEGF-A165 (CDKPRR) exemplify this principle, with their terminal arginine residue serving as the primary anchor point for binding. However, despite their specificity, many CendR peptides suffer from suboptimal binding potency, rapid enzymatic degradation, or poor pharmacokinetic profiles, limiting their utility as therapeutic agents.

Recent structural and biochemical investigations have suggested that residues adjacent to the canonical CendR motif may contribute to ligand-receptor recognition in ways not fully captured by the original rule. In particular, the presence of cysteine residues in the N-terminal region of VEGF-A165, specifically within the sequence CDKPRR encoded by exons 7 and 8, has been implicated in stabilizing the ligand-receptor complex through non-covalent interactions involving the thiol group. Experimental evidence indicates that deletion or substitution of these cysteine residues leads to a dramatic reduction in binding affinity, even when the C-terminal arginine is preserved. This observation suggests that the cysteine moiety may participate in interactions beyond simple steric complementarity, potentially forming hydrogen bonds, sulfur-π interactions with aromatic residues in the receptor pocket, or transient disulfide exchanges that stabilize the bound conformation. Despite this emerging insight, no prior art has systematically exploited the presence of an N-terminal cysteine residue as a structural determinant to enhance the potency of NRP-1-binding peptides, nor has any study demonstrated that the addition of cysteine to diverse CendR scaffolds consistently and significantly improves inhibitory activity across multiple peptide classes.

Furthermore, existing peptidic inhibitors of NRP-1, including branched constructs such as Lys(hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg, while showing improved potency over linear analogues, remain limited by their susceptibility to proteolytic cleavage in biological fluids, particularly at the junctions between basic amino acids and non-natural residues. The metabolic instability of these compounds in human plasma restricts their in vivo half-life and diminishes their therapeutic potential. There remains, therefore, a substantial unmet need for NRP-1 inhibitors that combine high binding affinity, structural simplicity, resistance to enzymatic degradation, and compatibility with pharmaceutical formulation. The present disclosure addresses this need by introducing a novel class of peptides that integrate an N-terminal cysteine residue with a C-terminal arginine-containing motif, resulting in compounds with unprecedented inhibitory potency against the VEGF-A165/NRP-1 interaction, while maintaining sufficient stability in human plasma to support further development as therapeutic agents. The discovery that the addition of cysteine at the N-terminus consistently enhances binding affinity across multiple peptide scaffolds—regardless of whether the parent sequence is linear or branched—constitutes a fundamental advance in the field and establishes a new design principle, herein termed the CysR rule, which complements and extends the CendR rule.

## SUMMARY

The present disclosure provides novel peptidic compounds of Formula (I):  
H₂N–Cys–X₁–X₂–X₃–Arg–COOH  
wherein X₁, X₂, and X₃ are independently selected from the group consisting of naturally occurring amino acids, non-naturally occurring amino acids, and amino acid mimetics, with the proviso that at least one of X₁, X₂, or X₃ is not arginine, lysine, or histidine; or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, or prodrug thereof.  

In a preferred embodiment, the compound of Formula (I) is selected from the group consisting of: H₂N–Cys–Lys–Dab–Dhp–Arg–COOH; H₂N–Cys–hArg–Dab–Dhp–Arg–COOH; H₂N–Cys–A7R–COOH; H₂N–Cys–TKPR–COOH; and H₂N–Cys–DKPRR–COOH, wherein A7R is ATWLPPR, hArg is homoarginine, Dab is 2,4-diaminobutyric acid, Dhp is dehydrophenylalanine, and TKPR is threonine-lysine-proline-arginine.  

In another preferred embodiment, the compound of Formula (I) is H₂N–Cys–hArg–Dab–Dhp–Arg–COOH, or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof.  

The compounds of Formula (I) are characterized by their ability to inhibit the binding of vascular endothelial growth factor A165 (VEGF-A165) to Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) with an IC₅₀ value of less than 200 nM, as determined in a competitive binding assay using biotinylated VEGF-A165 and recombinant human NRP-1. In particularly preferred embodiments, the IC₅₀ value is less than 100 nM, and in the most preferred embodiments, the IC₅₀ value is less than 80 nM.  

The disclosure further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a compound of Formula (I) and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent. Such compositions may be formulated for parenteral, intravenous, intratumoral, subcutaneous, intraperitoneal, or intravitreal administration, and may include stabilizing agents, penetration enhancers, or controlled-release matrices to optimize bioavailability and duration of action.  

The disclosure also encompasses methods of inhibiting angiogenesis in a mammal in need thereof, comprising administering to the mammal a therapeutically effective amount of a compound of Formula (I) or a pharmaceutical composition thereof. The method is particularly useful for the treatment of cancer, age-related macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, or other pathological conditions characterized by excessive or aberrant vascularization.  

The disclosure further provides methods of inhibiting the binding of VEGF-A165 to NRP-1 in a biological sample, comprising contacting the sample with a compound of Formula (I) under conditions sufficient to permit binding, thereby reducing the formation of the VEGF-A165/NRP-1 complex.  

The compounds of the disclosure exhibit a synergistic structural motif wherein the N-terminal cysteine residue, through its thiol group, engages in non-covalent interactions with the b1 domain of NRP-1, thereby stabilizing the ligand-receptor complex in a manner distinct from and additive to the canonical C-terminal arginine interaction. These interactions may include hydrogen bonding between the thiol group and polar residues in the receptor pocket, sulfur-π stacking with aromatic side chains such as tyrosine or phenylalanine, or transient electrostatic interactions involving the partial negative charge on the sulfur atom. The presence of the cysteine residue at the N-terminus, in conjunction with the C-terminal arginine, creates a bidentate binding architecture that significantly enhances affinity and specificity for NRP-1 over other related receptors.  

The compounds of the disclosure are also distinguished by their enhanced metabolic stability in human plasma relative to previously described peptidic NRP-1 ligands. When incubated in human plasma at 37°C, compounds of Formula (I) retain at least 40% of their initial concentration after 96 hours, with the most stable analogues retaining up to 60% of their concentration over the same period. This stability is attributed to the positioning of the cysteine residue and the nature of the adjacent amino acid linkages, which reduce susceptibility to cleavage by plasma proteases such as aminopeptidases and endopeptidases.  

The invention further includes methods for synthesizing the compounds of Formula (I) using solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques with Fmoc chemistry, wherein the cysteine residue is introduced at the N-terminus either during chain elongation or via post-synthetic modification of a pre-assembled peptide backbone. The synthesis may be performed on Wang resin or other suitable solid supports, with orthogonal protecting groups employed to preserve the thiol functionality until final deprotection. The resulting peptides are purified by preparative high-performance liquid chromatography and characterized by mass spectrometry and analytical chromatography to ensure structural integrity and homogeneity.  

The compounds of the disclosure represent a significant advancement in the field of angiogenesis inhibition, providing a new class of potent, stable, and synthetically accessible NRP-1 antagonists that overcome the limitations of prior art peptides. The discovery of the CysR rule, which defines the critical role of an N-terminal cysteine residue in enhancing NRP-1 binding, opens new avenues for the rational design of next-generation anti-angiogenic therapeutics with improved efficacy and pharmacokinetic properties.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSURE

### Compounds of the Disclosure

The compounds of the present disclosure are defined by a novel structural motif characterized by the presence of an N-terminal cysteine residue directly adjacent to a C-terminal arginine residue, with intervening amino acid residues that modulate conformation, solubility, and receptor interaction dynamics. These compounds are represented by the general formula: H₂N–Cys–X₁–X₂–X₃–Arg–COOH, wherein X₁, X₂, and X₃ are independently selected from the group consisting of naturally occurring amino acids, non-naturally occurring amino acids, and amino acid mimetics, with the proviso that at least one of X₁, X₂, or X₃ is not arginine, lysine, or histidine. This structural arrangement is distinct from all previously described NRP-1 ligands, which rely exclusively on the C-terminal arginine for receptor recognition under the CendR rule. The inclusion of the N-terminal cysteine residue introduces a second, independent binding determinant that significantly enhances the affinity and specificity of the peptide for the b1 domain of NRP-1, resulting in inhibitory potency that is orders of magnitude greater than that of prior art compounds.

In a preferred embodiment, the compound is H₂N–Cys–Lys–Dab–Dhp–Arg–COOH, wherein Lys is lysine, Dab is 2,4-diaminobutyric acid, and Dhp is dehydrophenylalanine. This compound exhibits an IC₅₀ value of approximately 80 nM in the competitive VEGF-A165/NRP-1 binding assay, representing a more than 50-fold improvement over the parent peptide lacking the N-terminal cysteine. The lysine residue contributes a positively charged side chain that may interact electrostatically with negatively charged residues in the NRP-1 binding pocket, while the Dab residue provides a flexible spacer that orients the cysteine and arginine moieties into optimal spatial alignment. The dehydrophenylalanine residue introduces conformational rigidity through its unsaturated backbone, reducing the entropic penalty associated with binding and promoting a bioactive conformation that is complementary to the receptor surface.

In another preferred embodiment, the compound is H₂N–Cys–hArg–Dab–Dhp–Arg–COOH, wherein hArg is homoisarginine. This analogue demonstrates the lowest IC₅₀ value of approximately 60 nM, making it the most potent compound disclosed herein. The homoisarginine residue, with its extended guanidinium side chain, enhances the electrostatic complementarity with the aspartate and glutamate residues lining the NRP-1 binding pocket, while simultaneously increasing the overall hydrophobic character of the peptide core. The presence of the N-terminal cysteine in this context appears to synergize with the homoisarginine side chain, enabling a dual-point anchoring mechanism wherein the thiol group of cysteine forms a hydrogen bond with Tyr297 or Tyr353 of NRP-1, while the terminal arginine engages in a salt bridge with Asp320 and a π-cation interaction with Arg304. This cooperative binding mode results in a binding energy that is substantially greater than the sum of the individual interactions, a phenomenon consistent with the concept of avidity enhancement.

In a further preferred embodiment, the compound is H₂N–Cys–A7R–COOH, wherein A7R is the sequence ATWLPPR. This compound, derived from the well-characterized phage-display-derived peptide A7R, exhibits an IC₅₀ value of 8.5 nM, representing a seven-fold improvement over the parent A7R peptide. The N-terminal cysteine in this context appears to stabilize the β-turn conformation adopted by the LPPR segment, which is known to be critical for receptor engagement. The cysteine thiol may interact with the hydrophobic cleft formed by Phe278 and Tyr353 of NRP-1, thereby enhancing van der Waals contacts and reducing the flexibility of the peptide backbone. This structural stabilization is not observed in the absence of the cysteine residue, suggesting that the thiol group serves not merely as a passive appendage but as an active participant in the binding event.

Another preferred embodiment is H₂N–Cys–TKPR–COOH, wherein TKPR is the endogenous tetrapeptide tuftsin. This compound demonstrates an IC₅₀ value of 9.2 nM, an over eleven-fold improvement over native tuftsin. The threonine residue at the N-terminus of tuftsin is replaced by cysteine, which not only introduces the critical thiol group but also alters the hydrogen-bonding network at the peptide-receptor interface. The hydroxyl group of threonine, which may have contributed to weak polar interactions in the native sequence, is replaced by a thiol group capable of forming stronger hydrogen bonds with backbone carbonyls or side-chain hydroxyls in the receptor. The lysine residue, retained in the second position, continues to provide electrostatic stabilization, while the proline residue enforces a turn structure that positions the terminal arginine for optimal engagement with the b1 domain.

The most significant improvement is observed in the compound H₂N–Cys–DKPRR–COOH, which is derived from the C-terminal hexapeptide of VEGF-A165. The native sequence DKPRR, lacking the N-terminal cysteine, exhibits negligible inhibitory activity at concentrations up to 100 μM. In contrast, the cysteine-containing analogue, H₂N–Cys–DKPRR–COOH, achieves an IC₅₀ value of 190 nM, representing an extraordinary enhancement in potency. This finding is particularly remarkable because it demonstrates that the addition of a single cysteine residue can restore high-affinity binding to a peptide sequence that was previously considered inactive. This result strongly supports the hypothesis that the cysteine residue is not merely a passive modifier but a critical structural element that enables the peptide to adopt a conformation compatible with the NRP-1 binding pocket. The aspartic acid residue in this sequence may contribute to solubility and charge distribution, while the arginine residues at positions 4 and 5 serve as secondary anchors that reinforce the primary interaction mediated by the C-terminal arginine.

The compounds of the disclosure may also be synthesized with non-natural amino acids or amino acid mimetics substituted for one or more of X₁, X₂, or X₃. For example, the lysine residue may be replaced by ornithine, homoarginine, or 2,4-diaminopropionic acid to modulate side-chain length and charge density. The Dab residue may be substituted with β-alanine, γ-aminobutyric acid, or 1,3-diaminopropane to alter backbone flexibility. The Dhp residue may be replaced by phenylglycine, α-methylphenylalanine, or 2,3-didehydrophenylalanine to enhance conformational rigidity or alter π-stacking potential. The arginine residue may be substituted with canavanine, agmatine, or guanidinylated lysine analogues to preserve the guanidinium functionality while improving metabolic stability.

The compounds may also be synthesized as branched peptides, wherein the N-terminal cysteine is attached to a side chain of a lysine or ornithine residue located at the center of the peptide sequence. For example, a compound of the structure H₂N–Cys–NH–(CH₂)₄–NH–CO–Lys–Dab–Dhp–Arg–COOH may be prepared, wherein the cysteine is linked via an amide bond to the ε-amino group of lysine, creating a branched architecture that mimics the spatial orientation of the cysteine and arginine residues observed in the linear analogues. This configuration may further enhance binding avidity by allowing simultaneous engagement of multiple receptor subsites.

All compounds of the disclosure are preferably synthesized in their free acid form, although pharmaceutically acceptable salts may be prepared for improved solubility, stability, or bioavailability. Suitable salts include hydrochloride, sulfate, acetate, citrate, maleate, fumarate, tartrate, mesylate, besylate, and phosphate salts. The compounds may also be formulated as prodrugs, wherein the carboxylic acid group is masked as an ester, amide, or carbamate, or wherein the thiol group is protected as a disulfide, thioether, or thioester. Prodrug forms are particularly useful for enhancing oral bioavailability or for targeted delivery to tumor tissues via enzymatic activation.

The compounds of the disclosure are substantially free of dimeric or oligomeric forms under physiological conditions. Although cysteine residues are capable of forming disulfide bonds, the compounds disclosed herein exhibit minimal dimerization under the conditions of the binding assays, with dimer formation occurring at less than 5% of the total peptide concentration. This low propensity for dimerization is attributed to the steric constraints imposed by the peptide backbone and the local environment of the cysteine residue, which limits access of the thiol group to neighboring molecules. The monomeric form is therefore the predominant and biologically active species.

The compounds of the disclosure are characterized by a molecular weight range of approximately 600 to 1200 Da, which is within the optimal range for peptide therapeutics, balancing membrane permeability, renal clearance, and target affinity. Their calculated logP values range from −1.5 to +0.8, indicating moderate hydrophilicity that supports aqueous solubility while retaining sufficient lipophilicity for membrane interaction. The isoelectric points of the compounds typically fall between 8.5 and 10.5, reflecting the presence of multiple basic residues and the protonation state of the arginine and cysteine side chains under physiological conditions.

### Methods for Preparing Compounds of Formula (I)

The compounds of Formula (I) are prepared using solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) employing Fmoc-based chemistry on Wang resin or other suitable solid supports. The synthesis begins with the loading of Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin, which provides a stable anchor point for the C-terminal arginine residue. The resin is pre-swollen in dimethylformamide (DMF) for 30 minutes prior to the initiation of chain elongation. The Fmoc group is removed by treatment with a 20% solution of piperidine in DMF, followed by thorough washing with DMF, dichloromethane, and methanol. The coupling of each subsequent amino acid is performed using a 2-fold molar excess of the Fmoc-protected amino acid, activated by HATU (O-(7-azabenzotriazol-1-yl)-N,N,N',N'-tetramethyluronium hexafluorophosphate) in the presence of a 5-fold excess of N,N-diisopropylethylamine (DIPEA) in DMF. The coupling reaction is allowed to proceed for 1 to 2 hours at room temperature, with monitoring of completion by the Kaiser test or chloranil test. In cases where the amino acid contains a side-chain functionality that requires orthogonal protection, such as the thiol group of cysteine, the Alloc (allyloxycarbonyl) protecting group is employed. The Alloc group is removed selectively using tetrakis(triphenylphosphine)palladium(0) in the presence of phenylsilane in dichloromethane, followed by neutralization and washing.

The N-terminal cysteine residue is introduced as the final amino acid in the chain elongation sequence. This ensures that the thiol group remains protected throughout the synthesis and is only exposed upon final deprotection. The cysteine residue is coupled using the same HATU/DIPEA protocol, followed by a second coupling if necessary to ensure complete reaction. After assembly of the full peptide sequence, the resin is washed extensively with DMF, methanol, and dichloromethane, and then subjected to cleavage from the solid support using a mixture of trifluoroacetic acid (TFA), dithiothreitol (DTT), water, and triisopropylsilane (TIS) in a ratio of 88:5:5:2 (v/v/v/v). The cleavage reaction is carried out for 3 hours at room temperature to ensure complete removal of all side-chain protecting groups, including Pbf from arginine, Trt from cysteine, and Boc from lysine, while minimizing side reactions such as alkylation or oxidation.

The crude peptide is precipitated by dropwise addition of the cleavage mixture into cold diethyl ether, followed by centrifugation and removal of the supernatant. The peptide pellet is washed three times with cold diethyl ether and dried under vacuum. The crude product is then purified by preparative reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) using a Phenomenex Jupiter Proteo C12 90 Å, 10 µm, 250 × 21.2 mm column, with a gradient of water containing 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid and acetonitrile containing 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid over a 60-minute period. Fractions corresponding to the target compound are collected, lyophilized, and analyzed by analytical RP-HPLC and high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) to confirm identity and purity. Analytical purity is typically greater than 95%, as determined by UV detection at 214 nm.

For the synthesis of branched analogues, the process is modified to include a branching point at the ε-amino group of lysine or the side chain of ornithine. In this case, the lysine residue is introduced with its ε-amino group protected by Alloc, while the α-amino group is protected by Fmoc. After coupling of the lysine residue, the Alloc group is selectively removed using the Pd(0)/PhSiH₃ system, and the N-terminal cysteine is coupled to the free ε-amino group. The remainder of the synthesis proceeds as described above. This approach allows for the preparation of both linear and branched variants from a common synthetic pathway, enabling systematic structure-activity relationship studies.

The compounds may also be synthesized using microwave-assisted SPPS to reduce coupling times and improve yields. In such cases, coupling reactions are performed at 50–75°C for 10–20 minutes under microwave irradiation, with the same reagent stoichiometry and solvent system. The resulting peptides exhibit comparable purity and biological activity to those synthesized by conventional methods.

Alternative synthetic routes include solution-phase synthesis for small-scale preparation of analogues, or chemoenzymatic methods involving the use of transpeptidases or sortases to ligate pre-synthesized peptide fragments. However, these methods are less preferred due to lower yields, higher cost, and reduced scalability.

The final compounds are characterized by a combination of analytical techniques, including high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) using a Sciex 6600TOF instrument, which confirms the molecular weight within ±0.01 Da of the theoretical value. High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) using a Shimadzu Prominence system with a C18 column and UV detection at 214 nm confirms homogeneity, with a single major peak corresponding to the target compound. Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy may be employed for structural confirmation in selected cases, particularly for novel non-natural amino acid derivatives. The thiol group is confirmed to be in the free reduced state by Ellman’s assay or by mass spectrometric detection of the protonated thiol species.

All compounds are stored as lyophilized powders at −20°C under inert atmosphere, protected from light and moisture. Prior to biological testing, compounds are dissolved in sterile water or phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at concentrations not exceeding 10 mM, and solutions are filtered through 0.22 µm membranes to ensure sterility and remove any particulate matter.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The compounds of Formula (I) may be formulated into a variety of pharmaceutical compositions suitable for administration to mammals, including humans, for the treatment of diseases characterized by pathological angiogenesis. The compositions comprise a therapeutically effective amount of a compound of Formula (I), or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof, in combination with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, diluents, or adjuvants. The choice of formulation depends on the intended route of administration, the physicochemical properties of the compound, and the desired pharmacokinetic profile.

For parenteral administration, including intravenous, intratumoral, subcutaneous, or intraperitoneal delivery, the compounds are formulated as sterile aqueous solutions or suspensions. The aqueous solution typically contains the compound dissolved in a buffered saline medium, such as 0.9% sodium chloride or phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), at a concentration ranging from 0.1 mg/mL to 10 mg/mL. The pH of the solution is adjusted to between 5.0 and 7.4 using pharmaceutically acceptable buffers such as citrate, acetate, or histidine to ensure stability of the peptide and minimize aggregation. Stabilizing agents such as human serum albumin, polysorbate 80, or polyethylene glycol may be added at concentrations of 0.01% to 0.5% (w/v) to prevent adsorption to container surfaces and to reduce proteolytic degradation. Antioxidants such as ascorbic acid, methionine, or dithiothreitol may be included at concentrations of 0.1 to 1.0 mM to maintain the thiol group of cysteine in its reduced state and prevent disulfide formation.

For sustained-release applications, the compounds may be encapsulated in biodegradable polymers such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA), polycaprolactone (PCL), or chitosan. These formulations may be administered as microspheres, nanoparticles, or hydrogels, providing controlled release of the peptide over a period of days to weeks. The polymer matrix may be engineered to degrade in response to enzymatic activity at the tumor site, thereby enabling targeted delivery. The encapsulation efficiency typically exceeds 80%, and the burst release is minimized to less than 20% within the first 24 hours.

For topical or intravitreal administration, particularly in the treatment of ocular neovascular diseases such as age-related macular degeneration or diabetic retinopathy, the compounds may be formulated as ophthalmic solutions, gels, or implants. The formulation may include viscosity-enhancing agents such as hyaluronic acid, carboxymethylcellulose, or hydroxypropyl methylcellulose to prolong contact time with the ocular surface. Preservatives such as benzalkonium chloride or chlorhexidine may be included at low concentrations to ensure sterility, while tonicity agents such as glycerol or mannitol are added to match the osmolarity of the tear film.

Oral formulations are less preferred due to the susceptibility of peptides to enzymatic degradation in the gastrointestinal tract. However, for systemic delivery, the compounds may be encapsulated in enteric-coated capsules or nanoparticles coated with pH-sensitive polymers that protect the peptide until it reaches the small intestine. Alternatively, the compounds may be conjugated to absorption enhancers such as fatty acids, bile salts, or cell-penetrating peptides to facilitate transmucosal transport. Prodrug forms, such as esterified carboxylates or thioether-linked derivatives, may be employed to improve oral bioavailability and resist peptidase cleavage.

The pharmaceutical compositions may further include penetration enhancers such as cell-penetrating peptides, lipid nanoparticles, or surfactants to facilitate transport across biological barriers, including the blood-brain barrier or tumor stroma. For example, the compound may be co-administered with a peptide that binds to the transferrin receptor or the insulin receptor to enable receptor-mediated transcytosis. The compositions may also be formulated as lyophilized powders for reconstitution immediately prior to administration, ensuring maximum stability during storage.

The dosage form may be adapted for single-dose or multi-dose administration. For intravenous infusion, a typical dose ranges from 0.1 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg body weight, administered once daily or every other day. For intratumoral injection, doses of 1 to 50 µg per injection site are administered weekly. For intravitreal injection, doses of 1 to 10 µg per eye are administered monthly. The exact dosage is determined by the severity of the disease, the patient’s body weight, renal and hepatic function, and the route of administration.

The pharmaceutical compositions are manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) and are subject to rigorous quality control testing, including assays for identity, purity, potency, sterility, endotoxin content, and particulate matter. Stability studies are conducted under accelerated conditions (40°C, 75% relative humidity) and real-time conditions (2–8°C) to determine shelf life, which is typically greater than 24 months for lyophilized formulations.

### Therapeutic Uses

The compounds of Formula (I) are useful in the treatment of a wide range of pathological conditions characterized by aberrant or excessive angiogenesis. These include, but are not limited to, solid tumors, ocular neovascular diseases, chronic inflammatory disorders, and vascular malformations. The therapeutic efficacy of the compounds is based on their ability to selectively inhibit the interaction between vascular endothelial growth factor A165 (VEGF-A165) and Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1), thereby disrupting the pro-angiogenic signaling cascade without directly targeting VEGFR-2. This mechanism of action offers several advantages over existing anti-angiogenic therapies, including reduced risk of resistance, lower systemic toxicity, and preservation of physiological vascular homeostasis.

In the treatment of solid tumors, the compounds are administered to patients diagnosed with cancers that overexpress NRP-1, including breast cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, prostate cancer, colorectal cancer, melanoma, glioblastoma, and pancreatic adenocarcinoma. The compounds inhibit tumor vascularization by blocking the recruitment of endothelial cells and pericytes to the tumor microenvironment, thereby reducing tumor perfusion, increasing hypoxia, and limiting nutrient delivery. In preclinical models, administration of the compounds results in significant reductions in tumor volume, microvessel density, and metastatic burden. The compounds may be administered as monotherapy or in combination with other anti-cancer agents, including chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, or other anti-angiogenic drugs such as bevacizumab or sunitinib. In combination regimens, the compounds may enhance the efficacy of these therapies by normalizing tumor vasculature, improving drug penetration, and reducing interstitial pressure.

In the treatment of ocular neovascular diseases, the compounds are administered via intravitreal injection to patients suffering from age-related macular degeneration (AMD), diabetic retinopathy, retinopathy of prematurity, or macular edema secondary to retinal vein occlusion. In these conditions, pathological neovascularization of the retina leads to hemorrhage, fibrosis, and irreversible vision loss. By inhibiting NRP-1-mediated VEGF signaling, the compounds prevent the formation of abnormal, leaky blood vessels while preserving the integrity of the existing retinal vasculature. Clinical studies demonstrate that intravitreal administration of the compounds reduces vascular leakage, decreases retinal thickness, and improves visual acuity over a period of several weeks. The compounds may be formulated as sustained-release implants or injectable gels to reduce the frequency of administration and improve patient compliance.

In the treatment of chronic inflammatory disorders, the compounds are administered to patients with rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, inflammatory bowel disease, or atherosclerosis. In these conditions, NRP-1 is expressed on activated endothelial cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells, where it promotes leukocyte recruitment, vascular permeability, and tissue remodeling. Inhibition of NRP-1 reduces the infiltration of inflammatory cells into affected tissues, decreases edema, and attenuates cytokine production. In animal models of collagen-induced arthritis, administration of the compounds significantly reduces joint swelling, cartilage erosion, and bone destruction. In models of psoriasis, the compounds reduce epidermal thickening, vascular density, and expression of pro-inflammatory mediators such as IL-17 and TNF-α.

In the treatment of vascular malformations, the compounds are administered to patients with hemangiomas, arteriovenous malformations, or lymphatic malformations. These conditions are characterized by abnormal vascular architecture and excessive endothelial proliferation. The compounds inhibit the growth and expansion of these lesions by blocking NRP-1-dependent signaling pathways, leading to regression of the abnormal vasculature. In pediatric patients with infantile hemangiomas, the compounds may be administered orally or intravenously to induce involution without the need for surgical intervention.

The compounds may also be used in the prevention of transplant rejection, where NRP-1 plays a role in graft vascularization and immune cell trafficking. Administration of the compounds to transplant recipients reduces the incidence of chronic allograft vasculopathy and improves long-term graft survival.

The compounds may further be employed in diagnostic imaging applications, wherein they are conjugated to radionuclides, fluorescent dyes, or magnetic nanoparticles to enable visualization of NRP-1-expressing tissues. For example, a compound labeled with technetium-99m or fluorine-18 may be used for positron emission tomography (PET) imaging of tumors or inflamed tissues, providing a non-invasive method for disease staging and treatment monitoring.

The compounds are administered in a therapeutically effective amount, which is determined based on the disease state, route of administration, and patient characteristics. Typical dosing regimens range from 0.01 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg per day, administered once daily or in divided doses. The duration of treatment varies from a single administration to chronic therapy lasting several months, depending on the clinical indication. The compounds exhibit a favorable safety profile in preclinical toxicology studies, with no significant hematological, hepatic, or renal toxicity observed at doses up to 100 mg/kg in rodents. The absence of off-target effects on VEGFR-2 or other growth factor receptors further supports their potential for long-term use.

The following examples illustrate the preparation, characterization, and biological evaluation of representative compounds of the disclosure. These examples are provided for illustrative purposes only and are not intended to limit the scope of the invention.

**Example 1: Synthesis of H₂N–Cys–hArg–Dab–Dhp–Arg–COOH**

Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin (0.5 g, 0.195 mmol) was swelled in DMF for 30 minutes. The Fmoc group was removed with 20% piperidine in DMF (2 × 10 min), followed by washing with DMF, DCM, and methanol. Fmoc-Dhp-OH (0.4 mmol) was coupled using HATU (0.4 mmol) and DIPEA (1 mmol) in DMF for 2 hours. The resin was washed and deprotected as above. Fmoc-Dab-OH (0.4 mmol) was coupled similarly. Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH (0.4 mmol) was coupled using the same protocol. The Alloc group on the ε-amino group of hArg was removed using Pd(PPh₃)₄ (0.016 mmol) and PhSiH₃ (3.2 mmol) in DCM for 2 hours. Fmoc-Cys(Alloc)-OH (0.4 mmol) was coupled to the free ε-amino group. After deprotection of the Fmoc group, the resin was washed and cleaved with TFA:DTT:H₂O:TIS (88:5:5:2, v/v/v/v) for 3 hours. The crude peptide was precipitated in cold diethyl ether, washed, and dried. The product was purified by preparative RP-HPLC (Phenomenex Jupiter Proteo C12, 10 µm, 250 × 21.2 mm) using a gradient of 5–60% acetonitrile in water with 0.1% TFA over 60 minutes. The main peak was collected, lyophilized, and analyzed by HRMS (m/z calculated for C₃₉H₆₄N₁₂O₁₀S⁺: 885.46; found: 885.47). Purity was >98% by analytical HPLC.

**Example 2: Competitive Binding Assay**

Recombinant human NRP-1 (200 ng/well) was immobilized on a 96-well plate overnight at 4°C. Wells were blocked with 0.5% BSA in PBS. Test compounds were serially diluted in PBS and mixed with biotinylated VEGF-A165 (400 ng/mL) and heparin (4 µg/mL). The mixture was added to the wells and incubated for 2 hours at room temperature. After washing, streptavidin-HRP (1:8000) was added for 45 minutes. Chemiluminescent substrate was added, and luminescence was measured using a Tecan Infinite F200Pro microplate reader. Percent inhibition was calculated as 100% − [(S − NS)/(P − NS)] × 100%, where S is signal with peptide, P is positive control (VEGF-A165 only), and NS is negative control (no NRP-1). IC₅₀ values were determined using nonlinear regression analysis in GraphPad Prism. The compound from Example 1 exhibited an IC₅₀ of 60 nM.

**Example 3: Plasma Stability Assay**

Human plasma was pre-incubated at 37°C for 20 minutes. Compound from Example 1 was added to a final concentration of 1 µM. Aliquots (100 µL) were taken at 0, 2, 4, 8, 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours. Plasma proteins were precipitated with 100 µL of 98% ethanol, centrifuged at 11,000 g for 10 minutes, and the supernatant was lyophilized. Samples were reconstituted in water and analyzed by RP-HPLC. The concentration of intact peptide was quantified by peak area relative to a standard curve. After 96 hours, 42% of the compound remained intact, demonstrating significant metabolic stability.

**Example 4: Synthesis of H₂N–Cys–DKPRR–COOH**

Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin (0.5 g) was used as above. Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-OH (0.4 mmol) was coupled, followed by Fmoc-Lys(Boc)-OH, Fmoc-Pro-OH, Fmoc-Asp(OtBu)-OH, and finally Fmoc-Cys(Alloc)-OH. After deprotection and Alloc removal, the resin was cleaved as above. The crude peptide was purified by RP-HPLC and analyzed by HRMS (m/z calculated for C₃₄H₅₆N₁₀O₁₂S⁺: 785.39; found: 785.40). Purity was >97%. IC₅₀ = 190 nM.

**Example 5: Inhibition of Tumor Angiogenesis in a Murine Xenograft Model**

Female nude mice (n=10 per group) were subcutaneously injected with 5 × 10⁶ MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells. When tumors reached 100 mm³, mice were treated with intravenous injections of the compound from Example 1 (2 mg/kg) or vehicle (PBS) every other day for 14 days. Tumor volume was measured every 2 days. At day 14, tumors were excised and analyzed for microvessel density by CD31 immunohistochemistry. Tumor volume in the treated group was reduced by 68% compared to controls (p < 0.001). Microvessel density was decreased by 72%. No signs of systemic toxicity were observed. These results demonstrate the in vivo efficacy of the compound in inhibiting tumor angiogenesis.
