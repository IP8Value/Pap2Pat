# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of metabolic medicine and molecular diagnostics, specifically to the use of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT) expression levels in adipose tissue and circulating concentrations of its enzymatic product, 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA), as biomarkers for the detection, stratification, and monitoring of insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes mellitus. The invention further encompasses therapeutic methods targeting NNMT activity to restore metabolic homeostasis in individuals exhibiting pathological elevations in adipose tissue NNMT expression and/or plasma MNA levels. The invention also provides diagnostic kits and analytical methods for the quantitative measurement of NNMT mRNA or protein in adipose tissue biopsies and for the precise determination of MNA concentration in biological fluids, particularly plasma, to enable early identification of individuals at risk for progressive metabolic dysfunction. The invention is particularly useful in clinical settings where conventional markers such as fasting glucose, HbA1c, or HOMA-IR lack sufficient sensitivity to detect subclinical insulin resistance prior to the onset of overt hyperglycemia. By establishing a direct causal link between adipose tissue NNMT overexpression and systemic insulin resistance, the invention opens new avenues for precision medicine approaches in the prevention and treatment of obesity-related metabolic disorders.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global prevalence of insulin resistance and its progression to type 2 diabetes mellitus has reached epidemic proportions, with millions of individuals suffering from metabolic dysfunction long before clinical diagnosis becomes apparent through standard biochemical markers. Despite decades of research into the pathophysiology of metabolic syndrome, the molecular mechanisms linking visceral adiposity to systemic insulin resistance remain incompletely understood. While chronic inflammation, ectopic lipid deposition, and mitochondrial dysfunction have been widely implicated, a critical gap in knowledge persists regarding the role of specific enzymatic pathways within adipose tissue that directly modulate cellular energy metabolism and insulin signaling. Nicotinamide, a form of vitamin B3, has long been recognized as a precursor to the essential coenzymes NAD+ and NADP+, which are central to redox reactions, DNA repair, and epigenetic regulation. However, the metabolic fate of excess nicotinamide, particularly in the context of obesity and metabolic stress, has received limited attention. The enzyme nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT), which catalyzes the transfer of a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine to nicotinamide to produce 1-methylnicotinamide and S-adenosylhomocysteine, has emerged as a key player in this metabolic network. Prior studies have suggested that NNMT expression is elevated in adipose tissue under conditions of dietary-induced obesity in rodent models, yet the translational relevance of this finding to human metabolic disease remained speculative. Moreover, while elevated urinary excretion of MNA and its metabolites had been reported in diabetic patients and animal models, the origin of this elevation, its relationship to adipose tissue biology, and its potential as a functional mediator rather than a mere byproduct were not established. Clinical studies investigating the effects of high-dose nicotinamide supplementation in humans revealed paradoxical impairments in insulin sensitivity, but these effects were attributed to direct pharmacological actions of nicotinamide itself, without consideration of downstream enzymatic metabolism. The absence of longitudinal data linking NNMT expression dynamics to changes in insulin sensitivity following therapeutic interventions further limited the clinical utility of these observations. Importantly, no prior study had demonstrated a consistent, quantifiable, and clinically actionable correlation between adipose tissue NNMT expression and the degree of insulin resistance as measured by the gold-standard hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp technique. Nor had any study established that reductions in NNMT expression following weight loss or improved metabolic health were directly associated with normalization of insulin sensitivity. The lack of validated biomarkers capable of reflecting real-time adipose tissue metabolic dysfunction has hindered the development of targeted therapies and the identification of high-risk individuals prior to irreversible beta-cell failure. Consequently, there exists a significant unmet need for a molecular biomarker system that accurately reflects the functional state of adipose tissue in relation to systemic insulin resistance, enables early intervention, and provides a measurable endpoint for therapeutic efficacy.

### Technical Problems Underlying the Present Invention

The technical problems addressed by the present invention stem from the inability of existing clinical tools to detect and monitor adipose tissue-specific metabolic dysfunction that precedes and drives the development of insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes. Conventional diagnostic parameters such as fasting plasma glucose, glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), and the homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) are insensitive to early-stage metabolic perturbations and often remain within normal ranges until significant beta-cell compensation has been exhausted. Furthermore, these parameters do not reflect the underlying tissue-specific pathophysiology occurring within adipose depots, particularly visceral and subcutaneous fat, which are now recognized as active endocrine organs contributing to systemic inflammation and insulin signaling disruption. While imaging techniques such as MRI and CT can quantify fat distribution, they provide no insight into the molecular activity within adipocytes that governs metabolic behavior. Similarly, circulating inflammatory markers like C-reactive protein or interleukin-6 are non-specific and influenced by a multitude of non-metabolic conditions, rendering them unreliable as standalone indicators of insulin resistance. The absence of a direct, quantifiable, and mechanistically linked biomarker derived from adipose tissue metabolism has impeded the development of precision therapies aimed at correcting the root cause of metabolic dysfunction rather than merely managing its downstream consequences. Moreover, prior attempts to modulate nicotinamide metabolism through supplementation or inhibition have lacked a rational basis due to the absence of validated patient stratification criteria. For instance, while high-dose nicotinamide has been shown to induce insulin resistance in some clinical trials, it remains unclear which individuals are most susceptible to this effect or whether the metabolic disruption is mediated by NNMT activity itself. The lack of a reliable assay to measure adipose tissue NNMT expression or plasma MNA concentration in a clinically feasible manner has further prevented the translation of preclinical findings into human applications. Additionally, no prior method has demonstrated that therapeutic interventions known to improve insulin sensitivity—such as exercise or bariatric surgery—produce a measurable and reproducible reduction in adipose tissue NNMT expression that correlates with the magnitude of metabolic improvement. This disconnect between clinical outcomes and molecular biomarkers has created a critical barrier to the development of targeted NNMT inhibitors, as there is no way to identify patients who would benefit most from such therapy or to monitor their response in real time. The present invention resolves these technical problems by establishing NNMT expression in adipose tissue and plasma MNA concentration as interdependent, quantifiable, and clinically relevant biomarkers that reflect the functional state of adipose tissue metabolism and its direct contribution to systemic insulin resistance. The invention further provides the methodological framework and diagnostic tools necessary to implement these biomarkers in clinical practice, enabling early detection, patient stratification, and therapeutic monitoring in a manner that was previously unattainable.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for the diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic monitoring of insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes mellitus through the quantitative assessment of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT) expression in adipose tissue and the concentration of its enzymatic product, 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA), in plasma. The invention is based on the discovery that elevated NNMT mRNA and protein levels in both subcutaneous and omental adipose tissue are consistently and significantly associated with the presence and severity of insulin resistance, as determined by hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp measurements, independent of body mass index or age. Furthermore, the invention demonstrates that plasma MNA concentration is not merely a passive byproduct of NNMT activity but serves as a reliable circulating biomarker that reflects the level of NNMT expression specifically in individuals exhibiting insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes. In contrast to individuals with normal glucose metabolism, where NNMT expression and plasma MNA levels are uncorrelated, a strong, statistically significant positive correlation exists between adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA concentration in insulin-resistant and diabetic subjects. This correlation is maintained across multiple independent clinical cohorts and is reversed following interventions that improve insulin sensitivity, such as structured exercise programs or bariatric surgery, where reductions in NNMT expression are paralleled by proportional decreases in plasma MNA. The invention further provides a diagnostic system comprising a set of threshold values for adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA concentration that distinguish individuals with subclinical insulin resistance from those with normal metabolic function, thereby enabling early intervention prior to the onset of hyperglycemia. The invention also encompasses therapeutic methods involving the inhibition of NNMT enzymatic activity in adipose tissue to restore insulin sensitivity, with the efficacy of such inhibition being monitored by serial measurements of plasma MNA concentration. The invention further includes a kit for the simultaneous quantification of NNMT mRNA in adipose tissue biopsy samples and MNA in plasma, utilizing standardized protocols for RNA extraction, quantitative real-time PCR, and liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry, thereby enabling standardized clinical implementation. The invention further provides a method for selecting patients for NNMT inhibitor therapy based on elevated adipose tissue NNMT expression and/or plasma MNA levels, and for adjusting therapeutic dosage based on the degree of MNA reduction over time. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in the management of metabolic disease by shifting the focus from systemic glucose levels to the molecular drivers of insulin resistance within adipose tissue, providing a mechanistic, measurable, and actionable framework for precision metabolic medicine.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed below. “Adipose tissue” refers to connective tissue composed primarily of adipocytes, including subcutaneous adipose tissue located beneath the skin and visceral adipose tissue, particularly omental adipose tissue, located within the abdominal cavity surrounding internal organs. “Nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase” or “NNMT” refers to the enzyme encoded by the NNMT gene, which catalyzes the transfer of a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine to nicotinamide, resulting in the formation of 1-methylnicotinamide and S-adenosylhomocysteine. “1-Methylnicotinamide” or “MNA” refers to the primary metabolite of nicotinamide produced by NNMT activity, detectable in biological fluids such as plasma, serum, and urine. “Insulin resistance” refers to a physiological state in which target tissues, particularly skeletal muscle, liver, and adipose tissue, exhibit a diminished response to insulin, resulting in impaired glucose uptake and elevated circulating insulin levels, as confirmed by a glucose infusion rate below 50 μmol·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ during a hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp procedure. “Type 2 diabetes mellitus” refers to a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by persistent hyperglycemia resulting from insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency, diagnosed according to established clinical criteria including fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, HbA1c ≥6.5%, or a 2-hour plasma glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L following an oral glucose tolerance test. “Plasma” refers to the liquid component of blood from which cellular elements have been removed, typically obtained by centrifugation of anticoagulated whole blood and used for the measurement of soluble biomarkers such as MNA. “NNMT expression” refers to the level of NNMT mRNA or protein in adipose tissue, as determined by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction or immunoblotting, respectively, and normalized to endogenous reference genes such as GAPDH, ACTB, or HPRT1. “Therapeutic intervention” refers to any clinical or pharmacological procedure designed to improve insulin sensitivity, including but not limited to caloric restriction, structured physical exercise, bariatric surgery, or administration of pharmacological agents that inhibit NNMT activity. “Biomarker” refers to a measurable biological indicator that reflects a physiological or pathological process, and in the context of this invention, specifically refers to adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA concentration as indicators of insulin resistance. “Quantitative measurement” refers to the precise determination of a molecular or biochemical parameter using standardized, validated, and reproducible analytical techniques, including but not limited to RT-qPCR, mass spectrometry, or enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. “Correlation” refers to a statistical association between two variables, as determined by Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient, wherein a value greater than 0.5 is considered a strong positive correlation in the context of this invention. “Clinical cohort” refers to a defined group of human subjects recruited under standardized inclusion and exclusion criteria for the purpose of evaluating a diagnostic or therapeutic intervention. “Therapeutic efficacy” refers to the degree to which a treatment produces a desired physiological outcome, in this case, the improvement of insulin sensitivity as measured by changes in glucose infusion rate or plasma MNA concentration. “Diagnostic kit” refers to a packaged set of reagents, controls, and instructions for the quantitative determination of NNMT expression and MNA concentration in clinical samples, designed for use in clinical laboratories under regulatory guidelines. “Subclinical insulin resistance” refers to a state of impaired insulin sensitivity in individuals who have not yet developed hyperglycemia or met diagnostic criteria for type 2 diabetes, as identified by elevated adipose tissue NNMT expression and/or plasma MNA concentration in the absence of abnormal fasting glucose or HbA1c.

### EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

The invention encompasses multiple embodiments, each directed toward distinct yet interrelated applications of the discovery that adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA concentration serve as biomarkers of insulin resistance. In a first embodiment, the invention provides a method for diagnosing insulin resistance in a human subject by obtaining a sample of subcutaneous or omental adipose tissue via biopsy, extracting total RNA, quantifying NNMT mRNA expression using quantitative real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, and comparing the resulting value to a predefined reference threshold. The reference threshold is established based on population data derived from cohorts of individuals with confirmed insulin resistance and normal glucose metabolism, wherein a NNMT expression level exceeding the 75th percentile of the normal cohort is indicative of insulin resistance. In a preferred embodiment, the reference threshold is set at a relative expression level of 1.8-fold higher than the median NNMT expression observed in individuals with normal glucose tolerance, as determined by the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method using GAPDH as a reference gene. In another preferred embodiment, the method further includes the concurrent measurement of plasma MNA concentration by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry, wherein a plasma MNA concentration above 1.2 μmol/L is considered indicative of insulin resistance when NNMT expression is elevated. In a second embodiment, the invention provides a method for stratifying patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus according to the severity of their insulin resistance, wherein patients are categorized into low, moderate, or high insulin resistance groups based on the magnitude of NNMT expression in adipose tissue and the corresponding plasma MNA concentration, with the highest tertile of NNMT expression and MNA levels associated with the greatest degree of impairment in glucose infusion rate during hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp studies. In a third embodiment, the invention provides a method for monitoring the efficacy of a therapeutic intervention designed to improve insulin sensitivity, wherein a baseline measurement of adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA concentration is obtained prior to intervention, and subsequent measurements are performed at defined intervals following intervention, with a reduction in NNMT expression of at least 20% and a reduction in plasma MNA concentration of at least 15% being indicative of a positive therapeutic response. In a fourth embodiment, the invention provides a method for selecting candidates for pharmacological NNMT inhibition, wherein individuals with adipose tissue NNMT expression above the predefined threshold and plasma MNA concentration above 1.2 μmol/L are identified as eligible for treatment with an NNMT inhibitor, and the dosage of the inhibitor is titrated based on the rate of decline in plasma MNA concentration over a 4- to 12-week treatment period. In a fifth embodiment, the invention provides a method for predicting the likelihood of progression from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes, wherein individuals with elevated NNMT expression and plasma MNA concentration in the absence of hyperglycemia are classified as high-risk, and are recommended for intensified lifestyle intervention or prophylactic therapy. In a sixth embodiment, the invention provides a diagnostic kit comprising a set of primers and probes for the amplification of NNMT mRNA in adipose tissue, a set of standards for the quantification of MNA in plasma, internal controls for RNA integrity and mass spectrometry calibration, and a written instruction manual detailing the steps for sample collection, processing, analysis, and interpretation of results according to the thresholds established in this invention. In a seventh embodiment, the invention provides a method for validating the specificity of NNMT as a biomarker by demonstrating that plasma MNA concentration does not correlate with NNMT expression in healthy, lean individuals with normal insulin sensitivity, thereby establishing that the biomarker relationship is dependent on the presence of metabolic dysfunction. In an eighth embodiment, the invention provides a method for differentiating between primary adipose tissue-driven insulin resistance and hepatic insulin resistance by comparing NNMT expression in adipose tissue with markers of liver fat content or hepatic insulin sensitivity, wherein elevated adipose NNMT with normal hepatic markers confirms adipose-specific pathophysiology. In a ninth embodiment, the invention provides a method for determining the contribution of adipose tissue versus other organs to systemic MNA levels by comparing NNMT expression in adipose tissue with NNMT expression in liver tissue obtained from surgical or post-mortem samples, wherein a stronger correlation between plasma MNA and adipose NNMT than between plasma MNA and liver NNMT supports the adipose tissue as the dominant source of circulating MNA in insulin-resistant individuals. In a tenth embodiment, the invention provides a method for assessing the reversibility of metabolic dysfunction by demonstrating that reductions in NNMT expression and plasma MNA concentration following bariatric surgery or intensive lifestyle intervention are sustained over a period of at least six months, indicating durable metabolic improvement. Each of these embodiments is supported by empirical data derived from multiple independent clinical cohorts, demonstrating reproducibility, clinical relevance, and biological plausibility.

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the practical application and validation of the invention in clinical and analytical settings. Example 1 describes the methodology for measuring NNMT expression and plasma MNA concentration in a cross-sectional cohort of 199 individuals undergoing abdominal surgery. Adipose tissue biopsies were collected from both subcutaneous and omental depots immediately after excision and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Total RNA was extracted using a commercial kit, and cDNA was synthesized using reverse transcriptase. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using TaqMan probes specific for human NNMT and reference genes GAPDH and ACTB. The relative expression of NNMT was calculated using the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method, with data normalized to the geometric mean of reference genes. Plasma samples were collected in EDTA tubes, centrifuged within 30 minutes, and stored at -80°C until analysis. MNA concentration was quantified using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry with electrospray ionization, employing a stable isotope-labeled internal standard (d4-MNA) for absolute quantification. Calibration curves were constructed using serial dilutions of synthetic MNA in plasma matrix, with a lower limit of quantification of 0.05 μmol/L. Statistical analysis revealed that NNMT expression in both adipose depots was significantly elevated in individuals with type 2 diabetes compared to those without, with a geometric mean ratio of 1.82 for omental tissue and 1.67 for subcutaneous tissue. Plasma MNA concentration was not correlated with NNMT expression in non-diabetic individuals, but a strong positive correlation (r = 0.60, p < 0.001) was observed in diabetic subjects. Linear regression analysis confirmed that the relationship between NNMT expression and plasma MNA was significantly modified by diabetes status, with a p-value of 0.001 for subcutaneous tissue. Example 2 describes the application of the invention in an exercise intervention cohort of 60 individuals with normal glucose tolerance, impaired glucose tolerance, or type 2 diabetes. Participants underwent a 12-week supervised exercise program consisting of three 60-minute sessions per week of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic activity. Subcutaneous adipose tissue biopsies and plasma samples were collected before and after the intervention. NNMT expression decreased by 21% in individuals with impaired glucose tolerance and by 16% in those with type 2 diabetes, but remained unchanged in the normal glucose tolerance group. Plasma MNA concentration decreased by 18% in the type 2 diabetes group and by 15% in the impaired glucose tolerance group, with changes correlating significantly with improvements in glucose infusion rate (r = 0.70, p < 0.001). Example 3 describes the application of the invention in a bariatric surgery cohort of 55 morbidly obese individuals. Adipose tissue and plasma samples were collected at the time of sleeve gastrectomy and again 12 months later following Roux-en-Y gastric bypass. NNMT expression in subcutaneous adipose tissue decreased by 50% and in omental adipose tissue by 41%, while plasma MNA concentration decreased by 24%. These reductions were statistically significant (p < 0.001) and correlated with improvements in HbA1c, fasting insulin, and glucose infusion rate. Example 4 describes the construction of a diagnostic kit for clinical use. The kit contains lyophilized primers and probes for NNMT and reference genes, a master mix optimized for RT-qPCR, a set of six MNA calibration standards ranging from 0.1 to 5.0 μmol/L, a d4-MNA internal standard, RNA extraction columns, plasma storage tubes, and a printed manual with step-by-step instructions, interpretation guidelines, and decision thresholds. The kit has been validated for inter-assay precision with a coefficient of variation less than 8% for NNMT expression and less than 5% for MNA quantification. Example 5 describes the use of the invention to identify patients eligible for NNMT inhibitor therapy. A patient with a BMI of 38 kg/m², HbA1c of 5.8%, and fasting glucose of 5.9 mmol/L was identified as having subclinical insulin resistance based on elevated NNMT expression in subcutaneous adipose tissue (relative expression = 2.1) and plasma MNA concentration of 1.4 μmol/L. This patient was enrolled in a clinical trial of a small-molecule NNMT inhibitor, and after 12 weeks of treatment, plasma MNA concentration decreased to 0.9 μmol/L, and glucose infusion rate increased by 32%, confirming therapeutic efficacy. Example 6 describes the exclusion of confounding variables. Multiple regression analysis was performed to assess the influence of age, sex, BMI, and inflammatory markers on the relationship between NNMT expression and plasma MNA. The correlation remained significant after adjustment for all covariates, confirming that the biomarker relationship is independent of these factors. Example 7 describes the use of the invention to predict progression to type 2 diabetes. A cohort of 120 individuals with prediabetes was followed for three years. Those with baseline NNMT expression above the 75th percentile and plasma MNA above 1.2 μmol/L had a 78% incidence of developing type 2 diabetes, compared to 12% in those below the threshold. Example 8 describes the validation of the biomarker in an independent cohort of 85 individuals undergoing hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp studies. NNMT expression in omental adipose tissue was 3.5-fold higher in insulin-resistant individuals (GIR <50 μmol·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹) than in insulin-sensitive individuals (GIR ≥50 μmol·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹), and plasma MNA concentration was inversely correlated with GIR (r = -0.44, p = 0.01). These examples collectively demonstrate the robustness, reproducibility, and clinical utility of the invention across diverse populations and settings, establishing its suitability for widespread adoption in metabolic medicine.
