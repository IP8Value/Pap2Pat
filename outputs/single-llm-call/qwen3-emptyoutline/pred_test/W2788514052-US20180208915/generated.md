# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of enzymatic glycoengineering, specifically to novel mutant endoglycosidases derived from Endo-S of Streptococcus pyogenes, and their use in the precise and efficient remodeling of N-linked glycans on immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies. The invention further encompasses methods for the one-pot transglycosylation of antibodies using combinations of these mutant endoglycosidases with auxiliary endoglycosidases from other microbial sources, thereby enabling the direct transfer of complex glycan structures from naturally occurring sialoglycopeptide donors without the need for prior chemical synthesis of activated glycan oxazoline intermediates. This technology is particularly applicable to the large-scale production of homogeneous glycoforms of therapeutic monoclonal antibodies, where precise control over glycosylation is critical for optimizing effector functions, pharmacokinetics, stability, and safety profiles. The disclosed mutants and methods represent a significant advancement over prior art by enabling high-yield, high-fidelity glycan remodeling under mild, aqueous conditions, with minimal hydrolytic side reactions, and without reliance on synthetic glycan donors, thus facilitating scalable, cost-effective, and environmentally benign manufacturing processes for next-generation biotherapeutics.

## BACKGROUND ART

The biological activity of monoclonal antibodies is profoundly influenced by the structure of their conserved N-linked glycan located in the Fc region of the immunoglobulin G (IgG) molecule. This glycan, typically a biantennary complex-type structure with core fucosylation and variable degrees of galactosylation and sialylation, modulates interactions with Fcγ receptors and complement proteins, thereby governing critical effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). The heterogeneity of glycoforms arising from conventional expression systems—whether mammalian, insect, or yeast—has long posed a challenge for the development of consistent, high-purity therapeutic antibodies. To address this, researchers have pursued strategies to engineer host cells to produce antibodies with defined glycosylation patterns, but these approaches remain limited by cellular metabolic complexity, batch-to-batch variability, and the inability to introduce non-natural or highly modified glycan structures.

An alternative approach, known as chemoenzymatic glycan remodeling, has emerged as a powerful tool for achieving homogeneity and structural precision. This method leverages endoglycosidases—enzymes capable of cleaving the chitobiose core of N-glycans—to remove native glycans from antibodies and subsequently transfer synthetic or natural oligosaccharide donors to the exposed GlcNAc residue on the antibody’s asparagine. The most successful variants of this strategy have employed endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidases (ENGases) that operate via a transglycosylation mechanism, wherein the enzyme forms a covalent glycosyl-enzyme intermediate with the donor substrate, which is then transferred to the acceptor antibody. Among these, Endo-S from Streptococcus pyogenes has garnered particular attention due to its high specificity for IgG Fc glycans and its ability to tolerate a broad range of glycan structures.

Early efforts to enhance transglycosylation efficiency focused on the D233Q mutant of Endo-S, which was shown to reduce hydrolytic activity while preserving transglycosylation capability. However, even this improved variant required a substantial excess of glycan oxazoline donor—typically 20 molar equivalents or more—relative to the antibody acceptor, due to competing hydrolysis of the product and instability of the oxazoline under physiological conditions. The requirement for large quantities of chemically synthesized oxazoline not only increases cost and complexity but also introduces scalability limitations, as oxazoline synthesis involves hazardous reagents, multi-step purification, and poor stability, rendering it impractical for industrial manufacturing. Moreover, the use of oxazoline donors restricts the repertoire of accessible glycoforms to those that can be chemically synthesized, excluding many biologically relevant structures derived from natural glycoproteins.

Attempts to circumvent the need for synthetic oxazoline have been explored through the use of auxiliary ENGases capable of processing complex glycopeptides into reactive intermediates. For instance, Endo-M from Mucor hiemalis and Endo-CC from Coprinopsis cinerea exhibit activity toward complex-type glycans on glycopeptides but are unable to act on core-fucosylated Fc glycans, making them theoretically compatible with Endo-S for sequential transglycosylation. However, prior attempts to combine wild-type Endo-S with wild-type Endo-M or Endo-CC resulted in negligible transglycosylation yields due to the hydrolytic degradation of both the donor glycan and the transglycosylation product. The inherent hydrolytic activity of these enzymes, even in the presence of high donor concentrations, rendered the one-pot approach ineffective. Furthermore, no prior art has demonstrated a systematic engineering strategy to simultaneously suppress hydrolysis while enhancing transglycosylation efficiency across multiple structural domains of Endo-S, nor has any prior disclosure described a functional synergy between mutant Endo-S and mutant auxiliary ENGases to achieve high-yield, hydrolysis-free glycan transfer directly from natural sialoglycopeptides.

Thus, despite significant advances in glycoengineering, there remains a critical unmet need for a robust, scalable, and economically viable method to remodel antibody glycans using natural, non-synthetic glycan donors, with minimal enzyme loading, high conversion efficiency, and negligible product degradation. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing a new class of Endo-S mutants with dramatically reduced hydrolytic activity and enhanced transglycosylation kinetics, as well as a novel one-pot enzymatic cascade that couples these mutants with engineered auxiliary ENGases to enable direct, efficient, and selective transfer of complex glycans from sialoglycopeptides to antibodies without the intervention of synthetic intermediates.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The primary technical problem addressed by the present invention is the inability of existing enzymatic methods to efficiently and selectively remodel the N-linked glycan of IgG antibodies using natural, non-synthetic glycan donors without significant hydrolytic degradation of the reaction product. Conventional chemoenzymatic approaches rely on chemically synthesized glycan oxazolines as activated donors, which are expensive to produce, unstable under aqueous conditions, and incompatible with large-scale manufacturing due to their hazardous synthesis and purification requirements. Furthermore, attempts to use naturally occurring glycopeptides as donors have been thwarted by the hydrolytic activity of endoglycosidases, which rapidly cleave both the donor glycopeptide and the newly formed glycosylated antibody product, leading to low yields and poor reproducibility. Even the most advanced Endo-S mutants, such as D233Q, require high molar excesses of oxazoline donor (≥20 eq) and still exhibit measurable hydrolysis of the transglycosylation product over time, limiting their utility in industrial settings. There exists no prior solution that enables the direct, high-yield transfer of complex, native-like glycans from sialoglycopeptides to antibodies using a single enzymatic reaction mixture, without the need for intermediate purification or chemical activation. The absence of such a method has impeded the development of next-generation antibody therapeutics with precisely defined glycoforms derived from natural sources, thereby restricting the ability to tailor effector functions, improve serum half-life, and reduce immunogenicity in a scalable and cost-effective manner.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention solves the aforementioned technical problem by providing a novel family of mutant endoglycosidases derived from Endo-S of Streptococcus pyogenes, which exhibit markedly reduced hydrolytic activity while maintaining or enhancing transglycosylation efficiency toward IgG Fc glycans. These mutants are generated through site-directed mutagenesis targeting specific residues within the substrate-binding and catalytic domains of Endo-S, including but not limited to positions D233, Q303, E350, D405, Y402, D279, and H122, with the D233Q mutation serving as a foundational scaffold. Through iterative mutagenesis and functional screening of over 120 variants, the invention identifies double and triple mutants—such as D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, and D233Q/D405A—that achieve transglycosylation efficiencies exceeding 90% within 24 hours using as little as five molar equivalents of glycan donor relative to the antibody acceptor, while demonstrating negligible hydrolysis of the product even after 48 hours of reaction. These mutants are structurally optimized to stabilize the glycosyl-enzyme intermediate, reduce water access to the active site, and enhance binding affinity for the glycan donor without compromising catalytic turnover.

In addition to these improved Endo-S mutants, the invention provides a novel one-pot enzymatic transglycosylation system that combines a mutant Endo-S with an auxiliary endoglycosidase from a different microbial source, such as Endo-M from Mucor hiemalis or Endo-CC from Coprinopsis cinerea, each engineered to possess reduced hydrolytic activity. Specifically, the Endo-M N175Q mutant and the Endo-CC N180H mutant are employed to cleave the glycan from a sialoglycopeptide donor, generating a reactive glycosyl-enzyme intermediate that is subsequently transferred to the deglycosylated antibody via the mutant Endo-S. The auxiliary ENGase is selected for its inability to act on core-fucosylated Fc glycans, thereby ensuring substrate specificity and preventing unwanted side reactions. The combination of these two enzymes in a single reaction vessel enables the direct, sequential transfer of a native glycan from a sialoglycopeptide to an antibody without the isolation or chemical activation of any intermediate. This cascade reaction proceeds with efficiencies exceeding 95% under optimized conditions, using naturally derived sialoglycopeptides as the sole glycan source, and without the need for synthetic oxazoline. The reaction is conducted under mild aqueous conditions at temperatures between 28°C and 37°C, with reaction times of less than five hours, and achieves near-complete conversion with minimal enzyme loading, making it uniquely suited for industrial-scale production.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The present invention delivers a multitude of advantageous effects that collectively overcome the limitations of prior art and establish a new paradigm for antibody glycoengineering. First, the novel Endo-S mutants enable transglycosylation at donor-to-acceptor ratios as low as five molar equivalents, representing a four-fold reduction compared to the previously reported D233Q mutant and a ten-fold reduction compared to earlier methods requiring 20–50 equivalents. This dramatic reduction in donor requirement translates directly into significant cost savings, simplified purification processes, and reduced waste generation, making the technology economically viable for commercial manufacturing. Second, the mutants exhibit negligible hydrolysis of the transglycosylation product over extended reaction periods, a critical improvement that ensures high product purity and eliminates the need for rapid reaction quenching or intermediate purification steps. Third, the one-pot transglycosylation system using mutant Endo-S in combination with mutant auxiliary ENGases eliminates the necessity for chemical synthesis of glycan oxazoline, thereby removing hazardous reagents, complex purification protocols, and stability concerns associated with oxazoline intermediates. This enables the direct use of naturally derived sialoglycopeptides—such as those isolated from egg yolk or other biological sources—as glycan donors, expanding the diversity of accessible glycoforms to include sialylated, galactosylated, and other biologically relevant structures that are difficult or impossible to synthesize chemically.

Fourth, the method is highly scalable, as demonstrated by the successful transglycosylation of 10 mg of antibody in a single reaction, yielding 8.0 mg of homogeneous glycosylated product with greater than 95% purity, as confirmed by liquid chromatography–electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (LC-ESI-MS). The reaction is compatible with standard bioprocessing equipment, requires no specialized instrumentation, and can be easily integrated into existing antibody purification workflows via affinity and hydroxyapatite chromatography. Fifth, the system exhibits broad applicability across different antibody isotypes and glycan donors, as evidenced by successful transfer using both full-length sialoglycopeptides and truncated SG-Asn substrates, and by the interchangeable use of Endo-M N175Q and Endo-CC N180H as auxiliary enzymes. Sixth, the reaction conditions are gentle and aqueous, preserving the structural integrity of the antibody and avoiding denaturation or aggregation, which are common pitfalls of chemical glycoconjugation methods. Seventh, the method is environmentally sustainable, as it replaces toxic organic solvents and chemical activators with enzymatic catalysis under aqueous, pH-neutral conditions, aligning with green chemistry principles. Finally, the invention enables the production of antibodies with precisely defined, homogeneous glycoforms that are otherwise inaccessible through cell-based expression systems, thereby facilitating the development of next-generation therapeutics with optimized pharmacokinetics, enhanced effector functions, and reduced immunogenicity—critical attributes for next-generation biologics in oncology, autoimmunity, and infectious disease indications.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### <Mutant Enzyme of the Present Invention>

The present invention encompasses a family of mutant endoglycosidases derived from Endo-S of Streptococcus pyogenes, characterized by a combination of amino acid substitutions that synergistically suppress hydrolytic activity while preserving or enhancing transglycosylation efficiency toward the Fc region of IgG antibodies. These mutants are constructed upon a foundational mutation at position D233, which is substituted with glutamine (D233Q), a modification previously shown to reduce hydrolysis by stabilizing the glycosyl-enzyme intermediate and impeding nucleophilic attack by water. Building upon this scaffold, additional mutations are introduced at residues that modulate substrate binding, active site architecture, and solvent accessibility. The most effective variants include double mutants such as D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, and D233Q/D405A, as well as triple mutants such as D233Q/Q303L/E350Q and D233Q/Y402F/D405A, each of which demonstrates transglycosylation efficiencies exceeding 90% within 24 hours under standard reaction conditions using five molar equivalents of glycan donor.

The Q303L substitution, located in close proximity to the catalytic center, is believed to enhance the hydrophobic packing around the glycan-binding pocket, thereby reducing water penetration and stabilizing the transition state during transglycosylation. The E350Q mutation, situated near the α1–3 arm of the glycan, likely alters electrostatic interactions that favor donor binding over water activation, while the D405A substitution, buried within the interior of the enzyme, may induce subtle conformational changes that tighten the active site and restrict the mobility of water molecules. The Y402F mutation, positioned adjacent to the branched mannose residue, appears to optimize van der Waals contacts with the glycan core, improving binding affinity without compromising catalytic turnover. Other beneficial mutations include D279S, which may reduce steric hindrance near the catalytic nucleophile, and H122Q, which likely modulates interactions with the α1–6 arm of the glycan. Importantly, the introduction of these mutations in combination yields synergistic effects that are not predictable from the individual contributions of single mutations, as evidenced by the fact that none of the single mutants outperformed the D233Q parent, whereas the double and triple mutants achieved unprecedented levels of efficiency and stability.

The mutant enzymes are produced recombinantly in Escherichia coli using a glutathione-S-transferase (GST) fusion expression system, followed by affinity purification and buffer exchange into physiological conditions. The purified enzymes exhibit specific activities comparable to wild-type Endo-S, with no loss of solubility or stability, and retain their ability to bind IgG Fc glycans with high specificity. Structural modeling and comparative analysis suggest that the mutations collectively narrow the active site cleft, reduce the accessibility of water to the catalytic center, and increase the residence time of the glycan donor in the binding pocket, thereby favoring transglycosylation over hydrolysis. These mutants are stable for extended periods under refrigerated storage and retain full activity after multiple freeze-thaw cycles, making them suitable for industrial use. The mutant enzymes are capable of transglycosylating a variety of IgG subclasses, including IgG1, IgG2, and IgG4, with equal efficiency, and function optimally at pH 7.0–7.5 and temperatures between 28°C and 37°C. Notably, the D233Q/E350Q and D233Q/D405A mutants exhibit faster transglycosylation kinetics than the D233Q variant, achieving 78% conversion within two hours, which further enhances their utility in high-throughput manufacturing settings. The absence of detectable hydrolysis over 48 hours in several variants, including D233Q/Y402F/D405A, represents a critical breakthrough, as it permits extended reaction times without product degradation, enabling complete conversion even with suboptimal donor concentrations or lower enzyme loadings.

### <Gene, Host Cell, Method of Producing Enzyme>

The present invention further provides isolated nucleic acid sequences encoding the novel mutant endoglycosidases described above, as well as recombinant expression vectors, host cells, and methods for their large-scale production. The gene encoding Endo-S from Streptococcus pyogenes is synthesized de novo or amplified from genomic DNA and cloned into a bacterial expression vector under the control of a strong, inducible promoter, such as the tac or T7 promoter. The vector is engineered to include an N-terminal glutathione-S-transferase (GST) tag to facilitate purification, followed by a thrombin or TEV protease cleavage site to allow for removal of the tag after purification. The desired mutations are introduced via site-directed mutagenesis using the QuikChange II protocol or other high-fidelity PCR-based methods, and the resulting plasmids are transformed into Escherichia coli BL21(DE3), a strain optimized for high-level protein expression. Transformants are cultured in Luria-Bertani broth supplemented with ampicillin, and expression is induced with isopropyl-β-d-1-thio-galactopyranoside (IPTG) at a concentration of 0.1 mM, followed by incubation at 16°C for 20 hours to promote proper folding and solubility.

After induction, cells are harvested by centrifugation and lysed by sonication in phosphate-buffered saline containing lysozyme and DNase I to reduce viscosity and degrade nucleic acids. The soluble fraction is recovered by centrifugation and loaded onto a glutathione-sepharose affinity column, where the GST-tagged mutant enzyme binds with high specificity. After washing to remove contaminants, the enzyme is eluted with reduced glutathione and subsequently buffer-exchanged into phosphate-buffered saline using dialysis or ultrafiltration. For large-scale production, the purified enzyme is further subjected to size-exclusion chromatography using a Superdex 200 column to remove residual glutathione and aggregates, yielding a highly pure, monodisperse enzyme preparation suitable for therapeutic applications. The final product is concentrated to a range of 2–7 mg/mL, sterile-filtered, aliquoted, and stored at −80°C in the presence of glycerol to prevent freeze-thaw damage.

The invention also encompasses alternative expression systems, including yeast (e.g., Pichia pastoris), insect cells (e.g., Sf9), and mammalian cells (e.g., CHO), which may be employed to produce the mutant enzymes with post-translational modifications if required for enhanced stability or activity. However, bacterial expression remains the preferred embodiment due to its simplicity, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. The nucleic acid sequences encoding the mutant enzymes are fully characterized and include codon-optimized variants for expression in E. coli, as well as sequences encoding the GST fusion and protease cleavage sites. The invention further includes recombinant vectors comprising these sequences, operably linked to regulatory elements such as ribosome binding sites, transcription terminators, and selection markers. Host cells transformed with these vectors are also part of the invention, and include both laboratory strains and industrially approved strains suitable for cGMP manufacturing. The method of producing the mutant enzyme is further characterized by its reproducibility, consistency across batches, and compatibility with standard bioprocessing equipment, making it directly transferable to commercial-scale production environments.

### <Sugar Remodeling>

The present invention provides a novel method for the enzymatic remodeling of N-linked glycans on IgG antibodies using a one-pot transglycosylation system that combines a mutant Endo-S enzyme with an auxiliary endoglycosidase capable of processing sialoglycopeptide donors. The method begins with the preparation of a deglycosylated antibody, which is obtained by incubating a purified IgG preparation with wild-type Endo-S under controlled conditions to remove the native glycan, leaving a single N-acetylglucosamine residue at the conserved asparagine site. This deglycosylated antibody is then mixed in a single reaction vessel with a sialoglycopeptide donor, such as sialoglycopeptide derived from egg yolk, and a combination of the mutant Endo-S enzyme and a mutant auxiliary ENGase, such as Endo-M N175Q or Endo-CC N180H. The auxiliary enzyme cleaves the glycan from the sialoglycopeptide, forming a transient glycosyl-enzyme intermediate, which is then accepted by the mutant Endo-S enzyme and transferred to the deglycosylated antibody, resulting in a homogeneous glycoform.

The reaction is carried out in a buffered aqueous solution, typically 50 mM Tris-HCl at pH 7.4, at temperatures between 28°C and 37°C, with the sialoglycopeptide donor present at a molar excess of 50 to 400 equivalents relative to the antibody. The mutant Endo-S is used at a concentration of approximately 20 μg per mg of antibody, while the auxiliary ENGase is used at a catalytic unit concentration of 5–33 mU per mg of antibody. The reaction proceeds for a duration of 2 to 8 hours, during which time the transglycosylation efficiency exceeds 90%, as confirmed by electrophoretic analysis and mass spectrometry. The reaction is terminated by heat denaturation or by dilution, and the product is purified using standard chromatographic techniques, including protein A affinity chromatography to remove unreacted antibody and auxiliary enzymes, followed by hydroxyapatite chromatography to remove residual sialoglycopeptide and enzyme fragments. The final product is a homogeneous glycosylated antibody with a single, well-defined glycoform, as verified by LC-ESI-MS, with a mass shift corresponding to the transferred glycan and a purity exceeding 95%.

The method is uniquely capable of utilizing naturally derived sialoglycopeptides as glycan donors, eliminating the need for synthetic oxazoline and enabling the incorporation of complex, biologically relevant glycan structures such as sialylated, galactosylated, and fucosylated variants that are difficult to synthesize chemically. The use of the Endo-M N175Q mutant in combination with Endo-S D233Q/E350Q achieves transglycosylation efficiencies of up to 97%, even when the sialoglycopeptide donor is truncated to SG-Asn, demonstrating the robustness and flexibility of the system. The reaction is compatible with a variety of antibody formats, including full-length IgG, Fc fragments, and bispecific antibodies, and can be scaled from microgram to multi-gram quantities without loss of efficiency. The method further enables the production of antibodies with tailored glycoforms for specific therapeutic applications, such as enhanced ADCC through reduced core fucosylation, prolonged serum half-life through increased sialylation, or reduced immunogenicity through glycan homogeneity. The absence of chemical activators, organic solvents, or hazardous reagents renders this method environmentally sustainable and compliant with green chemistry standards, while its simplicity and scalability make it ideal for integration into existing biopharmaceutical manufacturing workflows.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The purpose of this example is to demonstrate the generation and characterization of novel Endo-S mutants with enhanced transglycosylation efficiency and reduced hydrolysis activity. A plasmid encoding the wild-type Endo-S gene from Streptococcus pyogenes, fused to an N-terminal GST tag, was used as the template for site-directed mutagenesis. Mutations were introduced at residues D233, Q303, E350, D405, Y402, D279, H122, and R406 using the QuikChange II Site-Directed Mutagenesis Kit, with each mutation introduced individually and in combination. Over 120 distinct mutant variants were generated, including single, double, and triple mutants, and all constructs were verified by DNA sequencing. The mutants were expressed in Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) under IPTG induction at 16°C for 20 hours, and the recombinant proteins were purified via glutathione-affinity chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography. Protein concentrations were determined by UV absorbance at 280 nm, and all mutants exhibited expression yields comparable to wild-type Endo-S, with no evidence of aggregation or misfolding.

Hydrolysis and transglycosylation activities were assessed using deglycosylated trastuzumab as the acceptor and SG-oxazoline as the donor. Hydrolysis assays were performed by incubating 10 μg of each mutant with 500 μg of commercial trastuzumab in 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) at 30°C for up to 48 hours. Transglycosylation assays were performed by incubating 16 μg of each mutant with 800 μg of deglycosylated trastuzumab and 53.5 μg of SG-oxazoline (5 molar equivalents) under identical conditions. Aliquots were taken at 1, 2, 4, 8, 24, and 48 hours, quenched with reducing sample buffer, and analyzed by Experion Pro260 Automated Electrophoresis System to quantify the relative abundance of deglycosylated, transglycosylated, and native heavy chain species. The results revealed that while single mutants such as Q303L, E350Q, and D405A showed only modest improvements over D233Q, the double mutants D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, and D233Q/D405A exhibited significantly enhanced transglycosylation efficiencies, reaching 96%, 94%, and 78% at 24 hours, respectively, with hydrolysis levels below 5% even at 48 hours. The triple mutant D233Q/Q303L/E350Q achieved 95% transglycosylation with negligible hydrolysis, while D233Q/Y402F/D405A showed no detectable hydrolysis over the entire time course, despite achieving 79% transglycosylation. These results demonstrate that the combination of mutations at D233 with substitutions at Q303, E350, and D405 synergistically enhances transglycosylation while suppressing hydrolysis, establishing a new benchmark for Endo-S enzyme performance.

### Example 2

This example demonstrates the application of the novel Endo-S mutants in a one-pot transglycosylation system using sialoglycopeptide as a natural glycan donor in combination with the Endo-M N175Q mutant. Deglycosylated trastuzumab was prepared by incubating 100 mg of Herceptin with 500 μg of wild-type Endo-S in 50 mM phosphate buffer (pH 6.0) at 37°C for four hours, followed by purification using protein A and hydroxyapatite chromatography, yielding 85.7 mg of deglycosylated antibody with a confirmed mass of 49505.5 Da by LC-MS. A one-pot reaction was then set up by combining 1 mg of this deglycosylated trastuzumab with 5.82 mg of sialoglycopeptide (SGP) derived from egg yolk (300 equivalents), 20 μg of Endo-S D233Q mutant, and 5 mU of Endo-M N175Q mutant in 64.1 μL of 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4). The reaction was incubated at 28°C for 48 hours, with aliquots taken at 1, 2, 4, 8, 24, and 48 hours and analyzed by Experion Pro260 electrophoresis. The transglycosylation efficiency reached 95% at 24 hours and remained stable thereafter, with no detectable hydrolysis of the product. In contrast, reactions using wild-type Endo-S and wild-type Endo-M yielded less than 13% transglycosylation, with rapid degradation of the product observed within 8 hours. When the Endo-S D233Q/E350Q mutant was substituted for D233Q, transglycosylation efficiency increased to 97%, demonstrating the additive benefit of the E350Q mutation. The use of SG-Asn as a truncated donor substrate resulted in a slightly slower reaction rate but a marginally higher maximum efficiency of 97%, confirming the flexibility of the system. These results establish the feasibility of direct glycan transfer from a natural glycopeptide to an antibody using a single enzymatic cascade, without the need for synthetic oxazoline.

### Example 3

This example illustrates the scalability and practical utility of the one-pot transglycosylation method for the production of homogeneous glycosylated antibodies at a medium scale. A reaction mixture containing 10 mg of deglycosylated trastuzumab, 58.2 mg of sialoglycopeptide (300 equivalents), 200 μg of Endo-S D233Q mutant, and 0.05 U of Endo-M N175Q mutant was prepared in 600 μL of 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) and incubated at 28°C for 4.5 hours. The reaction was then terminated by dilution and subjected to sequential purification using HiTrap rProteinA FF affinity chromatography and CHT Type I hydroxyapatite chromatography. The final product was concentrated to 11.82 mg/mL in 680 μL, yielding 8.0 mg of glycosylated trastuzumab. LC-ESI-MS analysis of the heavy chain revealed a molecular mass of 51508.0 Da, consistent with the expected mass of the antibody bearing the transferred sialoglycan (calculated: 51508.7 Da), with a deviation of less than 0.1%. The purity of the product, as assessed by electrophoretic analysis, exceeded 95%, with no detectable traces of unmodified antibody or free glycan. A parallel reaction using SG-Asn as the donor yielded identical results, with 8.0 mg of product obtained at a concentration of 10.31 mg/mL and a mass of 51508.0 Da. These results confirm that the method is robust, reproducible, and scalable, capable of producing gram quantities of homogeneous glycosylated antibody in a single reaction, with minimal downstream processing and no requirement for synthetic intermediates.

### Example 4

This example evaluates the compatibility of the one-pot transglycosylation system with an alternative auxiliary ENGase, Endo-CC from Coprinopsis cinerea. Deglycosylated trastuzumab (1 mg) was combined with sialoglycopeptide (5.82 mg, 300 equivalents), 20 μg of Endo-S D233Q mutant, and either 17 mU of wild-type Endo-CC or 33 mU of Endo-CC N180H mutant in 74.1 μL of 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4). Reactions were conducted at both 28°C and 37°C, with sampling at 1, 2, 4, 8, and 24 hours. At 37°C, the wild-type Endo-CC produced no detectable transglycosylation product, while the Endo-CC N180H mutant achieved 57% transglycosylation at 8 hours, followed by a rapid decline due to product hydrolysis. When the reaction temperature was lowered to 28°C, the hydrolysis of the product was significantly suppressed, and the transglycosylation efficiency remained stable at 57% for up to 24 hours. Although the efficiency was lower than that achieved with Endo-M N175Q, this result demonstrates that the one-pot system is not limited to a single auxiliary enzyme and can be adapted to other ENGases with appropriate engineering. The use of Endo-CC N180H at 28°C provides an alternative pathway for glycan transfer, particularly useful when Endo-M is unavailable or when specific glycan structures are better processed by Endo-CC. This expands the versatility of the invention and establishes a platform for future optimization using additional ENGase variants.
