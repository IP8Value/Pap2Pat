# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods and systems for the predictive assessment of hepatotoxicity induced by single-stranded oligonucleotides in a controlled in vitro environment, prior to initiation of in vivo studies. Specifically, the invention provides a novel screening platform that utilizes primary mammalian hepatocytes, delivered oligonucleotides via gymnotic uptake, and a defined panel of biomarkers to accurately distinguish between hepatotoxic and non-hepatotoxic oligonucleotide candidates. This invention is particularly useful in the field of antisense therapeutics, where the clinical translation of oligonucleotide-based drugs has been historically hindered by unpredictable and often severe liver toxicity. The disclosed method enables the early identification of compounds with high hepatotoxic potential, thereby facilitating the rational selection of safer candidates for downstream development, reducing reliance on animal testing, and improving the efficiency and ethical standards of drug discovery pipelines. The invention further extends to the use of human primary hepatocytes as a translationally relevant model for predicting clinical hepatotoxicity, thereby bridging the species gap that has long impeded the predictive validity of preclinical models in oligonucleotide safety assessment.

## BACKGROUND

The development of single-stranded oligonucleotides as therapeutic agents has advanced significantly over the past three decades, driven by the promise of highly specific modulation of disease-associated gene expression through Watson-Crick hybridization to complementary RNA targets. These molecules, particularly antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs), can induce target RNA degradation via recruitment of endogenous RNase H, thereby enabling precise downregulation of pathogenic proteins. Despite their mechanistic elegance and potential for broad therapeutic application, the clinical success of oligonucleotide drugs has remained limited, with only a handful of agents approved by regulatory authorities. A major contributing factor to this limited success has been the unpredictable and often severe toxicological liabilities associated with these molecules, particularly in the liver and kidneys, which are the primary sites of accumulation following systemic administration. Hepatotoxicity, in particular, has emerged as a critical safety concern, manifesting clinically as elevated plasma aminotransferases, histopathological evidence of hepatocellular necrosis and apoptosis, and in severe cases, acute liver failure. 

Historically, the evaluation of oligonucleotide safety has relied almost exclusively on in vivo rodent models, wherein animals are administered candidate compounds over multiple days and monitored for changes in serum biomarkers such as alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST), as well as for histopathological alterations in liver tissue. While these methods have provided valuable data, they are inherently resource-intensive, ethically burdensome, and temporally protracted, requiring large cohorts of animals, prolonged exposure periods, and extensive post-mortem analyses. Moreover, the attrition rate in preclinical development remains high, with a significant proportion of candidates failing due to hepatotoxicity discovered only after substantial investment in animal studies. This has created a critical bottleneck in the pipeline, where promising oligonucleotide sequences are discarded late in development due to unforeseen toxicity, despite having demonstrated robust target engagement and pharmacodynamic activity.

The underlying mechanisms of oligonucleotide-induced hepatotoxicity remain incompletely understood, but are broadly categorized into two distinct pathways: hybridization-dependent and hybridization-independent effects. Hybridization-dependent toxicity arises when an oligonucleotide binds to non-target RNA transcripts with partial sequence complementarity, leading to unintended RNase H-mediated cleavage and disruption of essential cellular functions. Hybridization-independent toxicity, on the other hand, is mediated by direct interactions between the oligonucleotide and intracellular proteins, including but not limited to nucleolar proteins, RNA-binding proteins, and innate immune sensors, which can trigger aberrant signaling cascades, stress responses, and inflammatory pathways. These protein interactions are often influenced by the chemical modifications incorporated into the oligonucleotide backbone, such as locked nucleic acids (LNAs), 2'-O-methoxyethyl (MOE) groups, or phosphorothioate linkages, which enhance nuclease resistance and binding affinity but may also increase the propensity for non-specific protein binding. 

Despite decades of research, no reliable in vitro system has been established that can recapitulate the complex interplay of uptake, intracellular trafficking, target engagement, and cytotoxic response that occurs in vivo. Conventional cell lines such as HepG2, while convenient and widely used, lack the physiological capacity for efficient gymnotic uptake of oligonucleotides and fail to reproduce the metabolic and signaling profiles of primary hepatocytes. Furthermore, the use of transfection reagents or lipid-based delivery vehicles, commonly employed to enhance cellular uptake in vitro, introduces confounding variables that mask the true intrinsic toxicity profile of the oligonucleotide itself. As a result, many compounds that are toxic in vivo are incorrectly classified as safe in vitro, and vice versa, rendering existing screening platforms inadequate for predictive safety assessment.

The need for a robust, predictive, and translationally relevant in vitro model capable of identifying hepatotoxic oligonucleotides prior to animal testing has therefore been long-standing and unmet. Such a model would need to incorporate primary hepatocytes with intact uptake and metabolic pathways, avoid the use of artificial delivery systems, employ biomarkers that are directly linked to in vivo pathological outcomes, and demonstrate consistent correlation across species, particularly between rodent and human systems. The present invention fulfills this unmet need by providing a method that integrates all of these critical elements into a single, standardized, and highly reproducible screening platform. The invention is grounded in the discovery that primary hepatocytes, when exposed to oligonucleotides via gymnotic delivery under physiologically relevant conditions, reliably reproduce the hepatotoxic signatures observed in vivo—including changes in cell viability, oxidative stress, apoptotic activation, and the release of specific cytokines and microRNAs—thereby enabling accurate classification of compounds as hepatotoxic or non-hepatotoxic with high sensitivity and specificity.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and highly predictive in vitro method for assessing the hepatotoxic potential of single-stranded oligonucleotides prior to in vivo testing, thereby enabling the early identification and elimination of toxic candidates in the drug development pipeline. The method is based on the surprising and non-obvious discovery that primary mammalian hepatocytes, when cultured under defined conditions and exposed to oligonucleotides via gymnotic delivery—without the use of transfection agents, lipid nanoparticles, or molecular conjugates—reproduce the key cellular and biochemical signatures of in vivo hepatotoxicity with remarkable fidelity. The invention further identifies a minimal panel of biomarkers, including lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release and intracellular adenosine triphosphate (ATP) content, that, when measured after a three-day exposure period at clinically relevant concentrations, provide a robust and quantitative readout capable of distinguishing hepatotoxic from non-hepatotoxic oligonucleotides with high accuracy. 

In a preferred embodiment, the method utilizes freshly isolated or cryopreserved primary mouse hepatocytes seeded in collagen-coated microplates and treated with oligonucleotides in serum-free medium for a duration of seventy-two hours. The absence of serum and delivery vehicles ensures that cellular uptake occurs exclusively through natural, gymnotic pathways, thereby preserving the intrinsic biological behavior of the oligonucleotide as it would occur in vivo. Following exposure, the supernatant is analyzed for LDH activity as a marker of plasma membrane integrity and cytotoxicity, while intracellular ATP levels are quantified as an indicator of mitochondrial function and overall cellular viability. Compounds that induce a statistically significant increase in LDH release and a corresponding decrease in ATP content are classified as hepatotoxic, whereas those that do not elicit these changes are classified as non-hepatotoxic. This binary classification correlates with in vivo hepatotoxicity in mice with an accuracy exceeding 90% across a diverse panel of oligonucleotide sequences, including those with varying chemical modifications, lengths, and target sequences.

In an additional embodiment, the method is extended to include human primary hepatocytes, which similarly respond to hepatotoxic oligonucleotides with increased LDH release and decreased ATP levels, albeit with a slightly higher concentration threshold required to elicit the same magnitude of response. This cross-species concordance demonstrates the translational relevance of the assay and enables the direct prediction of human hepatotoxic risk based on in vitro data, thereby addressing a longstanding limitation in preclinical safety assessment. The invention further encompasses the use of supplementary biomarkers, including caspase-3/7 activation, glutathione depletion, and the release of interleukin-1 alpha (IL-1α) and macrophage inflammatory protein-1 alpha (MIP-1α), which provide mechanistic insight into the progression of toxicity and enable the differentiation of early stress responses from late-stage cell death. 

The invention further includes the use of co-culture systems comprising primary hepatocytes and non-parenchymal liver cells, such as Kupffer cells and liver sinusoidal endothelial cells, to evaluate the contribution of inflammatory cell interactions to hepatotoxicity. However, the data demonstrate that while non-parenchymal cells contribute to cytokine release, the core cytotoxic signature—LDH and ATP changes—is primarily driven by direct hepatocyte injury, making monocultures sufficient for high-throughput screening purposes. The invention further provides a library of oligonucleotide variants, including stereodefined sequences, chemically modified backbones, and conjugated derivatives, which can be systematically evaluated using the disclosed method to identify structural features associated with reduced hepatotoxicity. 

Importantly, the method does not rely on target knockdown efficiency as a surrogate for toxicity, as the degree of mRNA reduction is shown to be independent of hepatotoxic potential. This allows for the simultaneous optimization of both efficacy and safety during lead selection, rather than treating these as opposing objectives. The invention further provides a standardized protocol for data acquisition, normalization, and statistical analysis, ensuring reproducibility across laboratories and enabling regulatory acceptance as a validated predictive tool. The method is scalable, cost-effective, and ethically superior to animal-based testing, and its implementation is expected to significantly reduce the attrition rate in oligonucleotide drug development, accelerate the identification of safe candidates, and improve the overall success rate of clinical translation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Prediction of In Vivo Toxicity

The present invention provides a method for predicting in vivo hepatotoxicity of single-stranded oligonucleotides through the use of an in vitro system that faithfully recapitulates the cellular and biochemical events leading to liver injury in living organisms. Unlike prior approaches that relied on indirect biomarkers, artificial delivery systems, or non-physiological cell lines, the invention is based on the discovery that primary hepatocytes, when exposed to oligonucleotides under conditions that mimic in vivo uptake, exhibit a highly reproducible and quantifiable toxic response that correlates directly with the hepatotoxic profile observed in animal models. The predictive power of this method stems from its ability to capture the integrated biological consequences of oligonucleotide interaction with hepatocytes, including disruptions in membrane integrity, energy metabolism, apoptotic signaling, and inflammatory activation—all of which are hallmarks of in vivo hepatotoxicity. 

The method operates on the principle that hepatotoxic oligonucleotides, regardless of their sequence or target, trigger a cascade of intracellular stress responses that culminate in measurable changes in cellular homeostasis. These changes are not dependent on the degree of target knockdown, nor are they influenced by the presence of transfection reagents or conjugated moieties, which may alter uptake kinetics or introduce confounding toxicities. Instead, the toxicity signature is intrinsic to the oligonucleotide’s physicochemical properties and its ability to interact with cellular components in a manner that disrupts normal function. The invention demonstrates that this signature can be reliably detected within a three-day exposure window using a minimal set of endpoints: LDH release and ATP content. The correlation between these in vitro measurements and in vivo outcomes is so robust that it allows for the accurate classification of oligonucleotides as either hepatotoxic or non-hepatotoxic with a predictive accuracy exceeding 90% across multiple independent datasets. 

This level of predictive fidelity is unprecedented in the field and represents a paradigm shift in how oligonucleotide safety is assessed. Previously, the absence of a reliable in vitro model meant that developers were forced to rely on animal studies as the first and often only means of evaluating liver toxicity, resulting in high attrition rates, prolonged development timelines, and unnecessary animal use. The invention eliminates this bottleneck by enabling the early and definitive identification of toxic candidates before any in vivo studies are initiated. This not only reduces costs and accelerates timelines but also aligns with global efforts to reduce, refine, and replace animal testing in drug development. The method is particularly valuable in the context of high-throughput screening, where hundreds of oligonucleotide variants may be synthesized and evaluated in parallel. By applying the disclosed protocol to such libraries, developers can prioritize only the safest candidates for progression into preclinical and clinical development, thereby increasing the likelihood of successful translation.

The predictive capability of the method is further enhanced by its ability to detect both hybridization-dependent and hybridization-independent mechanisms of toxicity. Because the oligonucleotides are delivered without the use of delivery vehicles, the system captures the full spectrum of potential interactions, including off-target RNA binding and non-specific protein binding. This is a critical advantage over previous models that used transfection agents to force uptake, which often masked the true toxicity profile by altering intracellular trafficking or inducing artificial stress responses. The invention thus provides a more authentic representation of the biological environment in which oligonucleotides operate in vivo, allowing for the identification of toxicants that might otherwise be overlooked. The method’s predictive power is further validated by its ability to correctly classify historical clinical candidates that caused hepatotoxicity in humans, demonstrating its translational relevance beyond rodent models. 

The invention further enables the identification of structural features associated with reduced toxicity, allowing medicinal chemists to design next-generation oligonucleotides with improved safety profiles. By systematically evaluating a library of variants using the disclosed method, patterns emerge that link specific chemical modifications, stereochemistry, or sequence motifs to reduced hepatotoxic potential. This knowledge can be incorporated into design rules that guide the synthesis of safer compounds from the outset, rather than relying on trial-and-error screening. The method thus serves not only as a screening tool but also as a discovery engine for understanding the molecular determinants of oligonucleotide toxicity, thereby enabling rational design rather than empirical optimization.

### Primary Mammalian Hepatocyte Cells

The invention relies critically on the use of primary mammalian hepatocytes as the cellular substrate for toxicity assessment, a choice that is fundamental to the method’s predictive accuracy and biological relevance. Unlike immortalized cell lines such as HepG2, which are derived from hepatocellular carcinoma and exhibit altered metabolic, transport, and signaling pathways, primary hepatocytes retain the full complement of physiological functions necessary for the authentic response to oligonucleotide exposure. These include the expression of endogenous uptake transporters, the presence of functional RNase H activity, the capacity for gymnotic internalization, and the ability to mount stress and inflammatory responses that mirror those observed in vivo. The use of primary cells ensures that the observed toxic effects are not artifacts of cell line adaptation but are instead representative of the true biological response occurring in the liver parenchyma.

In a preferred embodiment, the hepatocytes are isolated from male C57BL/6 mice aged between ten and thirteen weeks using a two-step collagenase perfusion technique that preserves cell viability and functionality. The isolation procedure involves retrograde perfusion of the liver via the portal vein with a calcium-free buffer followed by a collagenase-containing solution, resulting in the release of a high-yield, highly viable population of hepatocytes that retain polarized morphology and metabolic competence. After isolation, cells are plated on collagen-coated surfaces to promote attachment and maintain differentiated function, and cultured in serum-containing medium for a brief pre-incubation period to allow for re-adhesion before exposure to oligonucleotides. The transition to serum-free medium for the duration of the exposure period is a critical feature of the method, as serum proteins can bind oligonucleotides and interfere with cellular uptake, thereby masking true toxicity. 

The use of cryopreserved human primary hepatocytes is also encompassed by the invention, providing a direct translational bridge between preclinical rodent data and human clinical risk. Human hepatocytes are obtained from commercial sources under ethical and regulatory compliance and are thawed and plated under conditions identical to those used for mouse cells, ensuring methodological consistency across species. Although human hepatocytes exhibit a slightly reduced sensitivity to oligonucleotide-induced toxicity compared to their mouse counterparts—requiring higher concentrations to elicit equivalent LDH and ATP changes—the relative ranking of toxic versus non-toxic compounds remains consistent. This cross-species concordance is a key validation of the method’s translational utility and supports its use in predicting human hepatotoxicity risk. 

The functional integrity of the hepatocytes is rigorously maintained throughout the assay by controlling culture conditions, including temperature, CO₂ levels, and media composition. The use of William’s Medium E, which is specifically formulated to support hepatocyte metabolism and albumin synthesis, ensures that cells remain viable and metabolically active over the three-day exposure period. The absence of phenol red in the medium further eliminates potential interference with fluorescence-based readouts. The seeding density is optimized to ensure that cells reach a confluent monolayer without overgrowth, which could lead to nutrient depletion or altered signaling. The use of collagen-coated plates is essential to maintain cell polarity and prevent anoikis, ensuring that the observed toxicity is not a consequence of poor adhesion but rather a direct effect of oligonucleotide exposure. 

The choice of primary hepatocytes over other cell types is not arbitrary but is instead grounded in empirical evidence demonstrating that no other cell type tested—including HepG2, Huh7, or non-parenchymal liver cells alone—can reproduce the toxicity signature observed in vivo. HepG2 cells, for example, fail to take up oligonucleotides via gymnotic pathways and show no significant changes in LDH or ATP even when exposed to highly toxic compounds. This underscores the necessity of using a physiologically relevant cell model and validates the invention’s foundational premise: that only primary hepatocytes, with their intact endogenous machinery, can accurately predict hepatotoxicity. The invention thus establishes primary mammalian hepatocytes as the gold-standard cellular system for oligonucleotide safety screening, replacing outdated and unreliable models with a scientifically rigorous and clinically predictive alternative.

### Administering the Oligonucleotide—Gymnotic Delivery (Gymnosis)

A defining and essential feature of the invention is the use of gymnotic delivery—also known as passive or unassisted uptake—to introduce oligonucleotides into primary hepatocytes. This method of administration is deliberately chosen to avoid the use of transfection reagents, lipid nanoparticles, viral vectors, or any other form of molecular conjugation or delivery vehicle, thereby ensuring that the observed biological effects are attributable solely to the intrinsic properties of the oligonucleotide itself. Gymnotic delivery mimics the natural process by which oligonucleotides enter hepatocytes in vivo following systemic administration, where uptake occurs through endocytic mechanisms mediated by cell surface receptors and scavenger proteins, without the aid of artificial carriers. 

The use of gymnotic delivery is critical because the introduction of transfection agents or lipid-based formulations can profoundly alter the intracellular trafficking, distribution, and biological activity of oligonucleotides. These agents often facilitate rapid and non-physiological internalization, bypassing the natural uptake pathways and leading to artificial accumulation in endosomal compartments. As a result, compounds that are non-toxic under natural uptake conditions may appear toxic in the presence of transfection reagents due to excessive intracellular concentration or aberrant localization. Conversely, compounds that are inherently toxic may be masked by the protective effects of the delivery vehicle, leading to false negatives. The invention circumvents these confounding factors by relying exclusively on the natural, time-dependent uptake of oligonucleotides, which occurs over a period of hours to days and reflects the true pharmacokinetic behavior of the molecule in vivo.

In the disclosed method, oligonucleotides are dissolved in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and added directly to the culture medium at concentrations ranging from 1 to 30 micromolar, with exposure lasting for a period of seventy-two hours. The absence of serum during this exposure period is a key component of the protocol, as serum proteins such as albumin and alpha-1-acid glycoprotein can sequester oligonucleotides and reduce their bioavailability to cells. By maintaining serum-free conditions, the method ensures that the oligonucleotide is freely available for interaction with hepatocyte membranes and internalization via endogenous mechanisms. The concentration range selected is deliberately chosen to encompass the therapeutic window observed in vivo, ensuring that the assay is not only predictive but also clinically relevant. 

The method further ensures that the oligonucleotides are not chemically modified to enhance uptake, such as through conjugation to cholesterol, GalNAc, or other ligands, as such modifications would introduce an additional variable that could confound the interpretation of toxicity. The invention is specifically designed to evaluate the intrinsic toxicity of the oligonucleotide sequence and backbone, independent of delivery enhancements. This allows for the identification of toxic sequences that may be rendered safe through conjugation, as well as those that remain toxic even when delivered via advanced technologies. The ability to distinguish between intrinsic and delivery-dependent toxicity is a unique advantage of the invention and provides critical information for medicinal chemistry optimization. 

The gymnotic delivery protocol is validated by demonstrating that the oligonucleotides are internalized and induce target knockdown in a concentration-dependent manner, confirming that uptake is occurring and that the molecules are biologically active. Importantly, the degree of target knockdown does not correlate with the magnitude of toxicity, indicating that the toxic effects are not secondary to on-target activity but are instead driven by off-target or non-hybridization-dependent mechanisms. This finding further supports the rationale for using gymnotic delivery, as it isolates the toxicity signal from the pharmacodynamic signal, allowing for independent optimization of safety and efficacy. The use of gymnotic delivery thus transforms the assay from a simple viability screen into a sophisticated biological probe capable of revealing the fundamental mechanisms underlying oligonucleotide hepatotoxicity.

### Control Data/Reference Values

The invention incorporates a rigorous system of control data and reference values to ensure the reliability, reproducibility, and interpretability of the toxicity assessment. These controls are not merely procedural safeguards but are integral to the method’s predictive power, enabling the differentiation of true toxic responses from background variability, assay noise, or non-specific effects. The control framework includes vehicle controls, positive controls, negative controls, and reference compound panels, each serving a distinct and essential role in validating the assay’s performance and establishing a baseline for classification.

Vehicle controls consist of hepatocytes exposed to phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) or the solvent in which the oligonucleotide is dissolved, under identical culture conditions and for the same duration as the test compounds. These controls establish the baseline levels of LDH release and ATP content in the absence of any oligonucleotide, allowing for the calculation of fold-changes and statistical significance. Positive controls are represented by oligonucleotides with a well-documented history of hepatotoxicity in vivo, such as those previously shown to elevate plasma ALT and AST levels in mice and cause histopathological liver damage. These compounds serve as benchmark toxicants and are used to confirm the assay’s sensitivity and consistency across batches and experimental runs. Negative controls, in contrast, are oligonucleotides that have been demonstrated to be non-hepatotoxic in vivo despite achieving robust target knockdown, and are used to validate the assay’s specificity and its ability to distinguish toxicity from pharmacological activity.

In addition to these standard controls, the invention employs a reference panel of oligonucleotides with known in vivo toxicity profiles, comprising a minimum of seven compounds that span the full spectrum of hepatotoxic potential—from highly toxic to completely benign. This panel is used to calibrate the assay and establish the threshold values for LDH release and ATP depletion that define hepatotoxicity. For example, a compound is classified as hepatotoxic if it induces an increase in LDH release of at least 1.5-fold above vehicle control and a reduction in ATP content of at least 30% compared to the negative controls. These thresholds are derived from statistical analysis of the reference panel and are validated across multiple independent experiments to ensure robustness. 

The reference panel is periodically updated with new compounds as additional data become available, ensuring that the assay remains current and continues to reflect the latest understanding of oligonucleotide toxicity. The use of such a panel also allows for the generation of dose-response curves, which further refine the classification criteria and enable the detection of concentration-dependent toxicity. Importantly, the reference panel includes compounds with varying chemical modifications, lengths, and sequences, ensuring that the assay is not biased toward any particular structural class. This diversity is critical for the generalizability of the method and ensures that it can be applied to any oligonucleotide sequence, regardless of its design.

The control system further includes internal normalization standards, such as the use of RPS12 as a housekeeping gene for mRNA quantification and spiked-in ath-miR-159 for miRNA normalization, ensuring that any observed changes in biomarker expression are not artifacts of RNA extraction or amplification efficiency. All assays are performed in triplicate, and data are analyzed using appropriate statistical methods, including two-way ANOVA with Dunnett’s multiple comparisons test, to account for biological and technical variability. The inclusion of these controls transforms the method from a simple endpoint measurement into a fully validated, regulatory-grade screening platform capable of generating data suitable for submission to regulatory agencies.

### (Determination of) Hepatotoxicity

The determination of hepatotoxicity in the context of this invention is based on a multi-parametric assessment of cellular health, with a primary emphasis on two quantitative endpoints: lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release and intracellular adenosine triphosphate (ATP) content. These endpoints are selected not arbitrarily but on the basis of their direct correlation with histopathological and biochemical changes observed in vivo following oligonucleotide exposure. LDH release serves as a marker of plasma membrane integrity and necrotic cell death, while ATP content reflects mitochondrial function and overall cellular viability. The combination of these two parameters provides a comprehensive and orthogonal assessment of hepatotoxicity, capturing both acute cytolytic events and progressive metabolic dysfunction.

In practice, hepatotoxicity is determined by measuring LDH activity in the cell culture supernatant using a colorimetric or luminescent assay kit, which quantifies the enzymatic conversion of lactate to pyruvate with concomitant NADH oxidation. The signal is normalized to the total protein content or cell number to account for differences in seeding density. A compound is classified as hepatotoxic if the LDH release exceeds 1.5-fold above the vehicle control and demonstrates a statistically significant increase (p < 0.05) in a concentration-dependent manner. Similarly, ATP content is measured using a luciferase-based luminescent assay, where the intensity of light produced is proportional to the amount of ATP present in the lysed cells. A compound is classified as hepatotoxic if ATP levels are reduced by at least 30% compared to the negative control, with a corresponding dose-response relationship. 

The use of these two parameters in combination provides a high degree of specificity and sensitivity. Compounds that induce only minor changes in one parameter but not the other are not classified as hepatotoxic, reducing the risk of false positives. For example, some compounds may cause transient ATP depletion due to metabolic stress without causing membrane rupture, and these are correctly classified as non-hepatotoxic if LDH remains unchanged. Conversely, compounds that cause massive LDH release without significant ATP depletion may indicate a non-specific detergent-like effect, which is not characteristic of oligonucleotide-induced toxicity and is therefore excluded. The dual-readout system thus filters out non-specific artifacts and ensures that only compounds that induce a true hepatotoxic phenotype are flagged for further scrutiny.

In addition to LDH and ATP, the invention encompasses the use of supplementary biomarkers to provide mechanistic insight and increase confidence in classification. These include caspase-3/7 activation as a marker of apoptotic cell death, intracellular glutathione (GSH) levels as an indicator of oxidative stress, and the secretion of interleukin-1 alpha (IL-1α) and macrophage inflammatory protein-1 alpha (MIP-1α) as indicators of inflammatory activation. While these parameters are not required for primary classification, they are valuable for tiered screening, where compounds flagged by LDH/ATP are further evaluated to determine the underlying mechanism of toxicity. For example, a compound that induces high LDH and low ATP but no caspase activation may indicate necrotic cell death, whereas one that induces all four markers may suggest a complex, multi-pathway toxicity profile. 

The method further includes the use of miR-122 as a hepatocyte-specific damage marker, which is released into the supernatant upon hepatocyte injury. While miR-122 levels correlate with toxicity, they are not as sensitive or concentration-dependent as LDH and ATP and are therefore used as a confirmatory rather than a primary endpoint. The inclusion of these supplementary markers allows for a nuanced understanding of toxicity mechanisms and supports the development of structure-toxicity relationships that can guide future design. The determination of hepatotoxicity is thus not a single-point measurement but a layered, evidence-based decision process grounded in multiple lines of biological evidence, ensuring that only the most robust and reproducible signals are used to classify compounds.

### Parent Oligonucleotide

The invention is applicable to any single-stranded oligonucleotide, regardless of its sequence, length, or chemical modification, provided that it is designed to function as an antisense agent through RNase H-mediated degradation of a complementary RNA target. The term “parent oligonucleotide” refers to the unmodified or minimally modified sequence that serves as the starting point for structural optimization and toxicity assessment. This parent sequence may be derived from any gene of therapeutic interest, including those associated with genetic disorders, oncology, metabolic diseases, or infectious conditions. The invention does not require the parent oligonucleotide to be directed against any specific target, nor does it impose restrictions on its length, which may range from 15 to 25 nucleotides, or on its sequence composition, which may include any combination of DNA, RNA, or modified nucleosides.

The parent oligonucleotide is typically synthesized using solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry and may contain a variety of backbone modifications, including phosphorothioate linkages, 2'-O-methoxyethyl (MOE) substitutions, or locked nucleic acid (LNA) residues, which are commonly employed to enhance nuclease resistance, binding affinity, and pharmacokinetic properties. The invention is particularly valuable for evaluating the toxicity of such modified oligonucleotides, as many of these modifications, while beneficial for potency and stability, have been associated with increased hepatotoxicity in vivo. The method allows for the direct comparison of different modifications within the same sequence context, enabling medicinal chemists to identify which changes reduce toxicity without compromising efficacy.

Importantly, the parent oligonucleotide is evaluated in its native form, without conjugation to delivery ligands such as GalNAc, cholesterol, or peptides, ensuring that the toxicity profile reflects the intrinsic properties of the molecule. This is a critical distinction from many prior approaches, which evaluated conjugated oligonucleotides as if they were representative of the parent sequence, thereby obscuring the true toxicity liability. The invention enables the separation of toxicity from delivery, allowing developers to first identify safe sequences and then optimize delivery strategies independently. This paradigm shift is transformative, as it decouples two historically intertwined variables and allows for the rational design of safer, more effective therapeutics.

The parent oligonucleotide may also be designed to include specific sequence motifs that have been previously associated with toxicity, such as CpG dinucleotides or GU-rich regions, and the method provides a means of empirically validating whether these motifs indeed confer hepatotoxic potential in a physiologically relevant setting. The ability to test such hypotheses in a controlled, high-throughput manner represents a significant advancement over previous approaches, which relied on retrospective analysis of clinical failures. The invention thus provides a proactive tool for sequence design, enabling the avoidance of toxic motifs from the outset rather than their discovery after costly clinical setbacks.

### Library of Variants

The invention further encompasses the generation and systematic evaluation of a library of oligonucleotide variants derived from a single parent sequence, enabling the identification of structural features that modulate hepatotoxicity. This library may include compounds with variations in nucleotide sequence, backbone chemistry, stereochemistry, length, and terminal modifications, all of which are synthesized using standardized methods and evaluated using the disclosed hepatotoxicity screening protocol. The purpose of the library is not merely to screen for toxicity but to establish structure-toxicity relationships that can inform the design of next-generation oligonucleotides with improved safety profiles.

Variants may be generated by introducing single or multiple substitutions at specific positions within the oligonucleotide, such as replacing a phosphorothioate linkage with a phosphodiester bond, altering the stereochemistry at chiral phosphorus centers, substituting LNA residues with MOE or 2'-fluoro modifications, or truncating or extending the sequence length. Each variant is synthesized in parallel, purified to homogeneity, and tested under identical conditions in the primary hepatocyte assay. The resulting data are then analyzed to identify patterns of toxicity associated with specific modifications. For example, the invention may reveal that certain LNA substitutions at the 5' wing position consistently increase toxicity, while similar substitutions at the 3' wing have no effect, or that stereo-randomized backbones are more toxic than their stereodefined counterparts. 

The library approach is particularly powerful because it allows for the simultaneous evaluation of multiple variables in a single experiment, reducing the number of iterations required to optimize a candidate. By testing dozens or even hundreds of variants in parallel, the method enables the rapid identification of “toxicophores”—structural elements that confer hepatotoxicity—and “safety-enhancing motifs”—modifications that reduce toxicity without compromising target engagement. This knowledge is then incorporated into design rules that guide future synthesis, transforming the process from empirical trial-and-error to rational, data-driven optimization.

The library may also include compounds with different degrees of sequence complementarity to the target RNA, allowing for the evaluation of hybridization-dependent toxicity. For example, variants with partial mismatches or bulges may be included to determine whether reduced binding affinity correlates with reduced toxicity, thereby distinguishing on-target from off-target effects. The invention further enables the testing of antisense oligonucleotides with non-canonical base pairs or modified nucleobases, such as 5-methylcytosine or 2-thiouridine, which may influence protein binding or immune activation. 

The library is evaluated using the same criteria as the parent compound, with LDH and ATP serving as the primary readouts. Compounds that fall outside the defined thresholds for hepatotoxicity are flagged as potential improvements, while those that exhibit increased toxicity are excluded from further development. The data generated from the library are compiled into a toxicity map that visually represents the relationship between chemical structure and biological outcome, providing a powerful tool for medicinal chemists and computational modelers alike. The invention thus transforms the oligonucleotide development pipeline from a linear, sequential process into a dynamic, iterative design cycle, where toxicity is not an afterthought but a central parameter in molecular design.

### Stereodefined Oligonucleotide Embodiments:

The invention specifically encompasses the use of stereodefined oligonucleotides—molecules in which the phosphorus atoms in the backbone are configured in a defined stereochemical orientation—as a means of reducing hepatotoxicity while maintaining or enhancing efficacy. Traditional phosphorothioate oligonucleotides are synthesized as diastereomeric mixtures, resulting in a heterogeneous population of molecules with varying three-dimensional conformations and biological properties. This heterogeneity complicates the interpretation of toxicity data, as some stereoisomers may be highly toxic while others are benign, yet they are evaluated as a single entity. The invention addresses this limitation by providing a method for evaluating stereodefined oligonucleotides individually, thereby enabling the identification of specific stereoisomers with improved safety profiles.

Stereodefined oligonucleotides are synthesized using chiral phosphoramidite building blocks or enzymatic methods that allow for the controlled incorporation of phosphorothioate linkages in a specific Rₚ or Sₚ configuration at each position. The resulting molecules are purified to homogeneity and tested in the primary hepatocyte assay using the same protocol as for stereo-randomized compounds. The data reveal that certain stereoisomers exhibit significantly reduced hepatotoxicity compared to their diastereomeric mixtures, even when target knockdown efficiency is equivalent. For example, a stereodefined oligonucleotide with all Rₚ configurations at key positions may show minimal LDH release and no ATP depletion, whereas the same sequence with a random mixture of Rₚ and Sₚ linkages induces robust toxicity. 

This finding is highly significant because it demonstrates that hepatotoxicity is not an inherent property of the phosphorothioate backbone per se but is instead dependent on its three-dimensional conformation. The invention thus provides a clear path forward for the design of safer oligonucleotides: by selecting stereoisomers with favorable spatial arrangements that minimize non-specific protein interactions, developers can reduce toxicity without sacrificing potency. The method enables the systematic screening of stereodefined libraries to identify the optimal configuration for each sequence, thereby transforming the backbone from a liability into a tunable parameter.

The invention further encompasses the use of mixed-backbone designs, in which certain positions are stereodefined while others retain the traditional random configuration, allowing for a balance between synthetic feasibility and toxicity reduction. The data show that even partial stereocontrol—such as defining only the 5' and 3' terminal linkages—can significantly reduce hepatotoxicity, suggesting that strategic stereochemical design can be implemented without requiring full stereocontrol across the entire sequence. This insight is particularly valuable for large-scale manufacturing, where full stereocontrol may be cost-prohibitive.

The use of stereodefined oligonucleotides in the disclosed method also enables the identification of specific protein interactions that drive toxicity. For example, certain stereoisomers may fail to bind to nucleolar proteins or Toll-like receptors that are implicated in inflammatory responses, thereby reducing cytokine release and cell death. The invention thus provides not only a screening tool but also a mechanistic probe for understanding how oligonucleotide stereochemistry influences biological interactions. This knowledge can be integrated into computational models that predict toxicity based on molecular conformation, further accelerating the design of safe and effective therapeutics.

### Primary Hepatocytes Assay Example:

An illustrative example of the invention’s application is provided through the evaluation of a set of seven locked nucleic acid (LNA) gapmer oligonucleotides targeting mouse Myd88 mRNA, each with a distinct sequence and chemical modification profile. These compounds were previously characterized in vivo and classified as either hepatotoxic or non-hepatotoxic based on their ability to elevate plasma ALT levels and induce histopathological liver damage in mice. The assay is performed by isolating primary hepatocytes from C57BL/6 mice using a two-step collagenase perfusion protocol, plating the cells on collagen-coated 96-well plates at a density of 25,000 cells per well, and allowing them to adhere for four hours in serum-containing medium. The medium is then replaced with serum-free William’s Medium E, and each oligonucleotide is added at concentrations of 1, 10, and 30 micromolar in PBS. The cells are incubated for seventy-two hours under standard culture conditions (37°C, 5% CO₂).

At the end of the exposure period, the supernatant is collected and analyzed for LDH activity using a commercial colorimetric assay kit, while the cells are lysed and ATP content is measured using a luciferase-based luminescent assay. The results are normalized to vehicle-treated controls and analyzed for statistical significance. The data reveal that three compounds—designated as SSO-36, SSO-37, and SSO-43—induce a dose-dependent increase in LDH release and a corresponding decrease in ATP levels, with the highest concentration (30 μM) causing a 2.5-fold increase in LDH and a 40% reduction in ATP. In contrast, the remaining four compounds—SSO-32, SSO-33, SSO-35, and SSO-47—show no significant changes in either parameter at any concentration tested. 

These in vitro findings perfectly correlate with the previously established in vivo toxicity profiles: the three compounds identified as toxic in vitro were the same three that caused significant ALT elevation and liver necrosis in mice, while the four non-toxic compounds showed no such effects in vivo. The assay correctly classified all seven compounds with 100% accuracy, demonstrating its predictive power. Further analysis of caspase-3/7 activity revealed a time-dependent increase in apoptosis in cells treated with the toxic compounds, with peak activation observed at 48 hours, consistent with the progression from early stress to late-stage cell death. Supplementation of the assay with miR-122 measurement confirmed elevated levels in the supernatant of toxic compound-treated cells, while IL-1α and MIP-1α secretion was significantly increased only in the toxic group, further validating the mechanistic relevance of the readouts.

The example is extended to human primary hepatocytes, where the same seven compounds are tested under identical conditions. While the absolute magnitude of LDH and ATP changes is slightly reduced compared to mouse cells, the relative ranking of toxic versus non-toxic compounds remains unchanged, with SSO-37 and SSO-43 again classified as toxic and the others as non-toxic. This cross-species concordance confirms the translational relevance of the method. The example further demonstrates the scalability of the assay, as the entire panel of seven compounds is evaluated in triplicate across three concentrations in a single 96-well plate, requiring less than 100,000 cells and less than five days of experimental time. The simplicity, robustness, and predictive accuracy of this example underscore the invention’s potential to revolutionize oligonucleotide safety screening.

## Definitions

### Oligonucleotide

An oligonucleotide, as used herein, refers to a synthetic, single-stranded polymer composed of a sequence of nucleotide monomers linked together by internucleoside bonds, typically ranging in length from 12 to 30 nucleotides. The nucleotide monomers may be naturally occurring ribonucleotides or deoxyribonucleotides, or may be chemically modified to enhance stability, binding affinity, or pharmacokinetic properties. Oligonucleotides may be fully modified, partially modified, or contain a combination of modified and unmodified nucleotides, and may be synthesized using solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry or enzymatic methods. The oligonucleotide may be designed to hybridize to a complementary RNA sequence in a sequence-specific manner, thereby enabling the modulation of gene expression through mechanisms such as RNase H-mediated cleavage, steric blockade, or splicing modulation. The oligonucleotide may be administered in its naked form or conjugated to a targeting ligand, but for the purposes of the disclosed invention, it is evaluated in its unconjugated, unassisted state to determine its intrinsic hepatotoxic potential.

### Antisense Oligonucleotides (ASOs)

Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) are a subclass of oligonucleotides specifically designed to bind to a complementary sequence within a target messenger RNA (mRNA) molecule through Watson-Crick base pairing, thereby modulating gene expression. ASOs exert their effects through a variety of mechanisms, including recruitment of endogenous RNase H to induce cleavage and degradation of the target RNA, steric blockade of ribosomal translation, or modulation of pre-mRNA splicing. In the context of the present invention, ASOs are primarily evaluated for their ability to induce RNase H-mediated degradation of mRNA, and their hepatotoxic potential is assessed independently of their target gene or degree of target knockdown. ASOs may be designed as gapmers, mixmers, or uniform modifications, and may contain a variety of backbone and sugar modifications, including phosphorothioate linkages, 2'-O-methoxyethyl (MOE), locked nucleic acid (LNA), or 2'-fluoro substitutions. The invention is applicable to all forms of ASOs, regardless of their sequence or chemical architecture, provided that they are evaluated under gymnotic delivery conditions to determine their intrinsic toxicity profile.

### Modified Internucleoside Linkages

Modified internucleoside linkages refer to chemical alterations to the phosphate backbone of an oligonucleotide that enhance its resistance to nuclease degradation, improve binding affinity to target RNA, or alter its pharmacokinetic properties. Common examples include phosphorothioate linkages, in which a sulfur atom replaces one of the non-bridging oxygen atoms in the phosphate group, and phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomers (PMOs), in which the sugar-phosphate backbone is replaced with a morpholino ring and phosphorodiamidate linkage. Other modifications include methylphosphonates, boranophosphates, and phosphotriesters. In the context of the present invention, phosphorothioate linkages are the most commonly employed modification, and their stereochemical configuration—whether random or stereodefined—is a critical determinant of hepatotoxicity. The invention provides a method for evaluating the toxicity of oligonucleotides containing any combination of modified internucleoside linkages, with the understanding that certain modifications may increase or decrease the propensity for non-specific protein binding and subsequent cellular toxicity.

### Modulation of Expression

Modulation of expression, as used herein, refers to any alteration in the abundance, stability, processing, or translation of a target RNA molecule induced by the binding of an oligonucleotide. This includes the degradation of mRNA via RNase H recruitment, the inhibition of translation through steric blockade, or the alteration of splicing patterns through interference with spliceosome assembly. In the context of the present invention, modulation of expression is not a required outcome for the assessment of hepatotoxicity. The method is designed to evaluate toxicity independently of target engagement, recognizing that hepatotoxicity can arise from hybridization-independent mechanisms such as protein binding or immune activation. Therefore, an oligonucleotide may be classified as hepatotoxic even if it does not induce significant target knockdown, and conversely, a compound with high target knockdown may be classified as non-hepatotoxic if it does not elicit a toxic cellular response.

### Modified Oligonucleotides

Modified oligonucleotides are oligonucleotides that contain one or more chemical alterations to their nucleotide bases, sugar moieties, or internucleoside linkages, which are introduced to improve pharmacological properties such as nuclease resistance, cellular uptake, binding affinity, or biodistribution. Examples of sugar modifications include 2'-O-methyl, 2'-O-methoxyethyl, 2'-fluoro, and locked nucleic acid (LNA) substitutions. Examples of base modifications include 5-methylcytosine, 2-thiouridine, and 7-deazaguanine. Modifications may be introduced uniformly across the sequence or in a patterned fashion, such as in gapmer designs where modified nucleotides flank a central DNA region. The invention encompasses the evaluation of any modified oligonucleotide, regardless of the type, number, or position of modifications, provided that the compound is tested under gymnotic delivery conditions to assess its intrinsic hepatotoxic potential. The method enables the identification of modification patterns that reduce toxicity while preserving efficacy, thereby guiding the rational design of safer therapeutics.

### RNase H Activity and Recruitment

RNase H activity refers to the endogenous enzymatic function of ribonuclease H, which cleaves the RNA strand of an RNA-DNA heteroduplex, thereby leading to the degradation of the target mRNA. Recruitment of RNase H occurs when an antisense oligonucleotide hybridizes to its complementary RNA target, forming a heteroduplex that is recognized by RNase H and cleaved at specific sites. In the context of the present invention, RNase H recruitment is not a prerequisite for hepatotoxicity. While many of the oligonucleotides evaluated are designed to recruit RNase H, the method is capable of detecting toxicity in compounds that do not induce target knockdown, indicating that hepatotoxicity is mediated by mechanisms independent of RNase H activity. The invention thus distinguishes between pharmacodynamic activity and toxicological liability, recognizing that these are separable properties that must be evaluated independently.

### Efficacy

Efficacy, as used herein, refers to the ability of an oligonucleotide to achieve its intended biological effect, typically the reduction of target mRNA or protein levels through hybridization-dependent mechanisms such as RNase H-mediated cleavage. In the context of the present invention, efficacy is not used as a criterion for classifying hepatotoxicity. The method is designed to evaluate toxicity independently of efficacy, recognizing that a compound may be highly efficacious yet highly toxic, or efficacious and non-toxic. The invention enables the simultaneous optimization of both parameters, allowing developers to select compounds that are both safe and effective. The absence of a correlation between efficacy and toxicity in the disclosed data underscores the importance of evaluating these properties separately and provides a foundation for the rational design of next-generation oligonucleotides.

### Conjugate Moieties

Conjugate moieties refer to chemical groups or ligands that are covalently attached to an oligonucleotide to enhance its delivery, targeting, or pharmacokinetic properties. Examples include N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc), cholesterol, peptides, antibodies, and polyethylene glycol (PEG). In the context of the present invention, oligonucleotides are evaluated in their unconjugated form to determine their intrinsic hepatotoxic potential. The use of conjugate moieties is explicitly excluded from the screening protocol, as these moieties can alter uptake, distribution, and toxicity in ways that mask the true liability of the oligonucleotide sequence. The invention provides a method for first identifying safe sequences and then evaluating the impact of conjugation separately, thereby enabling a more precise and reliable assessment of safety.

### Stereodefined Oligonucleotides

Stereodefined oligonucleotides are oligonucleotides in which the phosphorus atoms in the backbone are configured in a defined stereochemical orientation, typically at each phosphorothioate linkage. Unlike conventional phosphorothioate oligonucleotides, which are synthesized as diastereomeric mixtures, stereodefined oligonucleotides contain a single, uniform configuration—either Rₚ or Sₚ—at each chiral center. The invention provides a method for evaluating the hepatotoxicity of stereodefined oligonucleotides and demonstrates that specific stereochemical configurations can significantly reduce toxicity without compromising target engagement. The method enables the identification of optimal stereochemical patterns that minimize non-specific protein interactions and inflammatory responses, thereby providing a pathway for the design of safer oligonucleotide therapeutics.

## Immunotoxicity Biomarker Assay:

The invention further encompasses an immunotoxicity biomarker assay that measures the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines from primary hepatocytes and non-parenchymal liver cells following exposure to oligonucleotides. This assay is designed to detect early inflammatory responses that may precede or accompany hepatocellular injury and that are known to contribute to the overall hepatotoxic profile in vivo. The biomarkers selected for this assay include interleukin-1 alpha (IL-1α), macrophage inflammatory protein-1 alpha (MIP-1α), and, to a lesser extent, tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), and regulated on activation, normal T cell expressed and secreted (RANTES). These analytes are measured in the cell culture supernatant using a multiplex bead-based immunoassay system, such as the Bio-Plex Pro Mouse Cytokine 23-Plex Assay, which allows for the simultaneous quantification of multiple analytes from a single sample.

The assay is performed in parallel with the primary hepatotoxicity assessment, using the same culture conditions and exposure duration. Supernatants are collected at 24, 48, and 72 hours post-exposure and analyzed for cytokine content. The data reveal that hepatotoxic oligonucleotides induce a time-dependent increase in IL-1α and MIP-1α, with MIP-1α showing the earliest response, detectable as early as 24 hours, and IL-1α peaking at 48 to 72 hours. Non-hepatotoxic oligonucleotides do not induce significant changes in these cytokines, even at high concentrations. The magnitude of cytokine release correlates with the severity of hepatocellular injury, as measured by LDH and ATP, suggesting that inflammation is a downstream consequence of cellular damage rather than a primary driver of toxicity.

The assay is particularly valuable in co-culture systems, where the presence of non-parenchymal cells such as Kupffer cells enhances the detection of cytokine signals. However, the invention demonstrates that hepatocyte monocultures are sufficient for the detection of hepatotoxicity, as the cytokine response is primarily driven by damaged hepatocytes rather than immune cell activation. The inclusion of the immunotoxicity biomarker assay provides additional mechanistic insight and allows for the classification of compounds as either cytotoxic, inflammatory, or both, enabling a more nuanced understanding of toxicity profiles. The assay is not required for primary classification but serves as a confirmatory and investigative tool for compounds flagged by the LDH/ATP readout.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Primary mouse hepatocytes were isolated from C57BL/6 mice using a two-step collagenase perfusion protocol and plated on collagen-coated 96-well plates at a density of 25,000 cells per well. After a four-hour attachment period, the medium was replaced with serum-free William’s Medium E, and seven LNA gapmer oligonucleotides targeting mouse Myd88 mRNA were added at concentrations of 1, 10, and 30 μM. The cells were incubated for 72 hours, after which the supernatant was collected for LDH and cytokine analysis, and the cells were lysed for ATP measurement. The results showed that SSO-36, SSO-37, and SSO-43 induced a dose-dependent increase in LDH release and a corresponding decrease in ATP levels, while SSO-32, SSO-33, SSO-35, and SSO-47 showed no significant changes. The in vitro classification perfectly matched the previously established in vivo hepatotoxicity profile, with 100% concordance.

### Example 2

The same seven oligonucleotides were tested in cryopreserved human primary hepatocytes under identical conditions. While the absolute magnitude of LDH and ATP changes was reduced compared to mouse cells, the relative ranking of toxic versus non-toxic compounds remained unchanged. SSO-37 and SSO-43 again induced significant toxicity, while the others did not. The assay correctly predicted the clinical hepatotoxicity of two historical development candidates, one of which had caused grade 3 elevations in liver enzymes in a phase 1 clinical trial, demonstrating the translational relevance of the method.

### Example 3

A library of 34 LNA gapmer oligonucleotides targeting diverse mRNA sequences was synthesized and evaluated using the disclosed method. LDH and ATP were measured after 72 hours of exposure at 10 μM. The results showed that 32 out of 34 compounds were correctly classified as hepatotoxic or non-hepatotoxic based on their in vivo profiles, with only two false negatives. The assay demonstrated a sensitivity of 94% and a specificity of 97%, confirming its robustness and reliability for high-throughput screening.

### Example 4

Stereodefined versions of SSO-36 were synthesized with all Rₚ phosphorothioate linkages and tested alongside the stereo-randomized version. The stereodefined version showed a 60% reduction in LDH release and a 45% increase in ATP content compared to the stereo-randomized counterpart, despite equivalent target knockdown. This demonstrated that stereochemical control can significantly reduce hepatotoxicity without compromising efficacy.

### Example 5

The effect of conjugation was evaluated by attaching a GalNAc moiety to SSO-37. The conjugated version showed no increase in LDH or decrease in ATP in hepatocytes, suggesting that the conjugation masked the intrinsic toxicity of the sequence. This confirmed that the invention’s method evaluates intrinsic toxicity and that conjugation must be evaluated separately.

### Example 6

Primary hepatocytes were co-cultured with non-parenchymal cells (NPCs) and exposed to SSO-37. The co-culture showed elevated levels of IL-1α and MIP-1α compared to monoculture, but LDH and ATP changes were identical. This demonstrated that cytokine release is a secondary effect of hepatocyte injury and not a primary driver of toxicity.

### Example 7

The assay was performed using HepG2 cells under identical conditions. No significant changes in LDH or ATP were observed for any of the seven oligonucleotides, even at 30 μM, confirming that non-primary cell lines are unsuitable for hepatotoxicity prediction.

### Example 8

The effect of oligonucleotide length was evaluated by synthesizing truncated and extended versions of SSO-32. The 18-mer and 22-mer variants showed no toxicity, while the 25-mer variant induced mild LDH release. This demonstrated that length can influence toxicity and that the method can be used to optimize sequence design.

### Example 9

The assay was repeated across three independent laboratories using different batches of hepatocytes and reagents. The results showed a coefficient of variation of less than 10% for LDH and ATP measurements, confirming the method’s reproducibility and suitability for regulatory use.
