## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of pharmaceutical compositions and therapeutic methods for the treatment of cystinosis, a rare inherited lysosomal storage disorder characterized by the pathological accumulation of cystine within lysosomes of various tissues, particularly the kidneys, cornea, liver, and other organs. More specifically, the invention relates to novel amide-based thiol-containing compounds and their use as cystine-depleting agents for the treatment of both nephropathic and ocular forms of cystinosis. The invention further encompasses the formulation and delivery of these compounds in ophthalmic dosage forms, including inserts, solutions, and sustained-release devices, for the targeted reduction of corneal cystine crystals, which are a hallmark of the disease and a primary source of visual morbidity. The compounds disclosed herein, namely N-acetylcysteine amide (NPI-001) and its dimeric derivative (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (NPI-002), represent a new class of therapeutic agents that demonstrate superior or comparable efficacy to existing cysteamine-based therapies while exhibiting significantly improved tolerability, reduced cytotoxicity, and enhanced cellular penetration. The invention further includes methods for the preparation, stabilization, and administration of these compounds in ocular formulations that enable prolonged contact with corneal tissues, overcoming the limitations of conventional eye drops that require frequent dosing and cause ocular irritation. The invention is particularly relevant to the field of ophthalmology, rare disease therapeutics, lysosomal storage disorder management, and drug delivery systems designed for avascular tissues such as the cornea.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cystinosis is a severe autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutations in the CTNS gene, which encodes the lysosomal membrane transporter cystinosin. The absence or dysfunction of this transporter results in the inability of lysosomes to export cystine, leading to its progressive accumulation as insoluble crystals within the lysosomal compartment of nearly all cell types. This intralysosomal cystine buildup initiates a cascade of cellular dysfunction, oxidative stress, mitochondrial damage, and ultimately apoptosis, which manifests clinically in multiple organ systems. The most severe form, nephropathic cystinosis, presents in infancy with renal Fanconi syndrome, characterized by polyuria, polydipsia, dehydration, acidosis, and electrolyte wasting, and if left untreated, progresses to end-stage renal disease by the age of ten. Even with the advent of renal transplantation, patients continue to suffer from extra-renal complications, including photophobia, corneal opacification, retinal degeneration, muscle wasting, hypothyroidism, and neurocognitive decline. The cornea, being an avascular tissue, is especially vulnerable to cystine crystal deposition, which begins as early as six months of age and leads to debilitating photophobia, recurrent corneal erosions, and progressive visual impairment. Despite the availability of oral cysteamine therapy, which remains the only approved treatment for systemic cystinosis, its use is severely limited by poor patient adherence due to its intensely unpleasant odor, gastrointestinal side effects such as nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain, and the necessity for dosing every six hours to maintain therapeutic plasma concentrations. Furthermore, oral cysteamine does not achieve sufficient concentrations in the cornea to effectively reduce intracellular cystine levels, rendering it ineffective for treating the ocular manifestations of the disease. Topical ophthalmic formulations of cysteamine hydrochloride, such as CYSTARAN® and CYSTADROPS®, have been developed to address this limitation, but they require administration every hour while awake, resulting in poor compliance, persistent ocular discomfort, burning, stinging, and blurred vision, which further discourages consistent use. The chronic nature of cystinosis demands a therapeutic approach that is not only effective in reducing lysosomal cystine but also safe for long-term use, minimally irritating to ocular surfaces, and capable of sustained delivery without frequent reapplication. Existing therapies fail to meet these criteria, creating a substantial unmet medical need for a novel class of cystine-depleting agents that can be delivered topically to the eye with improved tolerability, longer duration of action, and greater efficacy than current options. While N-acetylcysteine has been investigated as an adjunctive antioxidant in cystinosis, its poor membrane permeability and rapid metabolism limit its utility. The amide derivatives of N-acetylcysteine, particularly N-acetylcysteine amide and its dimeric form, have emerged as promising candidates due to their enhanced lipophilicity, improved cellular uptake, resistance to enzymatic degradation, and ability to penetrate lysosomal membranes more effectively than their parent compounds. These compounds have demonstrated antioxidant properties in other disease models and have shown potential for reducing oxidative stress in cystinotic cells, but their direct cystine-depleting activity and suitability for ophthalmic delivery have not been previously explored or disclosed. There is therefore a critical gap in the art for a safe, well-tolerated, and highly effective ophthalmic formulation capable of achieving sustained cystine depletion in the cornea without the drawbacks associated with current therapies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious therapeutic approach for the treatment of cystinosis, particularly the ocular manifestations thereof, through the use of specific amide derivatives of N-acetylcysteine, namely N-acetylcysteine amide (NPI-001) and its dimeric form, (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (NPI-002), as potent and well-tolerated cystine-depleting agents. The invention demonstrates, for the first time, that these compounds are capable of significantly reducing intralysosomal cystine concentrations in human cystinotic fibroblasts without inducing cytotoxicity, and in fact enhance cellular viability at therapeutic concentrations. Unlike cysteamine, which exhibits dose-dependent cytotoxicity at higher concentrations and causes significant ocular irritation upon topical application, both NPI-001 and NPI-002 show no signs of cellular toxicity even at concentrations exceeding those required for maximal cystine depletion, and demonstrate superior ophthalmic tolerability in preclinical models. The invention further establishes that NPI-001 achieves more rapid and sustained cystine reduction than cysteamine, with statistically significant depletion observed as early as six hours post-treatment and maintained over a 72-hour period. NPI-002, while slightly less potent than NPI-001, still outperforms cysteamine at multiple time points and exhibits a more favorable safety profile. The invention further encompasses ophthalmic dosage forms comprising NPI-001 or NPI-002 at concentrations ranging from 0.1% to 2.0% by weight, formulated in aqueous or semi-solid matrices that provide extended contact time with the corneal surface, thereby enabling prolonged cystine depletion without the need for frequent dosing. These formulations include inserts, gels, suspensions, and controlled-release devices that are designed to release the active ingredient over a period of 8 to 24 hours, significantly improving patient compliance compared to hourly eye drops. The invention further includes methods of treating cystinosis by administering such formulations topically to the eye, thereby reducing corneal cystine crystal burden, alleviating photophobia, preventing corneal erosion, and preserving visual function. The invention further provides methods of preparing and stabilizing these compounds in pharmaceutical compositions that maintain chemical integrity during storage and upon administration, including the use of antioxidants, chelating agents, and pH buffers to prevent oxidation and hydrolysis. The invention further demonstrates that the therapeutic efficacy of these compounds is not dependent on the formation of mixed disulfides with cystine, as their activity persists even under conditions where such reactions are chemically inhibited, suggesting an alternative or complementary mechanism of action involving direct lysosomal penetration and intralysosomal cystine reduction. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of ocular cystinosis by introducing a new class of compounds that are not only more effective than existing therapies but also fundamentally safer and more suitable for chronic, lifelong administration.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Cytotoxicity of NACA and diNACA in Cystinotic Fibroblast Cell Culture

The cytotoxic potential of N-acetylcysteine amide (NPI-001) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (NPI-002) was rigorously evaluated in human cystinotic fibroblasts using a standardized colorimetric MTT assay, which measures mitochondrial reductase activity as a surrogate for cellular metabolic health and viability. Cultures of human cystinotic fibroblasts were maintained under standard conditions in Eagle’s minimum essential media supplemented with 15% fetal bovine serum, penicillin, streptomycin, and glutamine, and were exposed to concentrations of 25, 50, and 75 micromolar of either NPI-001, NPI-002, or cysteamine hydrochloride for periods of 48 and 72 hours. The choice of these concentrations was based on pharmacokinetic data from clinical studies of oral cysteamine bitartrate, which demonstrated peak plasma concentrations ranging from 30 to 70 micromolar following a therapeutic dose of 15 mg/kg, thereby ensuring that the in vitro conditions reflected physiologically relevant exposure levels. Following incubation, the culture media was removed and replaced with a solution of 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) at a concentration of 0.5 mg/mL, and cells were incubated for an additional four hours at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% carbon dioxide. During this time, viable cells metabolized the yellow MTT tetrazolium salt into insoluble purple formazan crystals, which accumulated intracellularly in proportion to mitochondrial activity. The MTT solution was then aspirated, dimethyl sulfoxide was added to each well to dissolve the formazan crystals, and absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a multiwell plate reader. The absorbance values were normalized to untreated control cells, and percentage viability was calculated for each treatment group. The results revealed a striking and unexpected finding: neither NPI-001 nor NPI-002 exhibited any cytotoxic effects at any of the tested concentrations or time points. In fact, both compounds demonstrated a statistically significant enhancement of cell viability compared to untreated controls. At 48 hours, treatment with 25 micromolar NPI-001 increased viability by approximately 12%, while 50 and 75 micromolar concentrations increased viability by 18% and 21%, respectively. Similar trends were observed at 72 hours, with viability increases of 15%, 23%, and 26% for the respective concentrations. NPI-002 produced comparable effects, with viability increases ranging from 10% to 24% across the concentration gradient. In contrast, cysteamine hydrochloride exhibited a concentration-dependent cytotoxic profile. While 50 and 75 micromolar cysteamine achieved statistically significant increases in viability at both 48 and 72 hours, the 25 micromolar concentration failed to produce any significant effect, suggesting a narrow therapeutic window. Moreover, at the highest concentration tested, cysteamine caused a measurable decline in viability compared to the NPI-001 and NPI-002 groups, indicating that even at concentrations considered therapeutic, cysteamine imposes a metabolic burden on cystinotic cells. The absence of cytotoxicity with NPI-001 and NPI-002, coupled with their ability to enhance cellular viability, suggests that these compounds not only lack the toxic liabilities associated with cysteamine but may actively support cellular homeostasis in the context of cystinosis. This protective effect may be attributable to their antioxidant properties, their ability to replenish intracellular glutathione, or their capacity to mitigate oxidative stress induced by lysosomal cystine accumulation. Importantly, the viability enhancement was observed consistently across multiple independent experiments, with no variability attributable to cell passage number or batch differences in media composition, confirming the robustness of the findings. The implications of this observation are profound: whereas cysteamine therapy, despite its efficacy, contributes to cellular stress and may accelerate long-term tissue damage, NPI-001 and NPI-002 appear to offer a dual benefit—cystine depletion combined with cytoprotection. This makes them uniquely suited for chronic administration, particularly in pediatric patients who require lifelong treatment, and for use in sensitive tissues such as the cornea, where cellular integrity is paramount to maintaining visual function. The data further suggest that the amide moiety of these compounds confers a protective advantage over the free thiol of cysteamine, possibly by reducing the formation of reactive oxygen species during thiol oxidation or by facilitating more efficient intracellular trafficking without triggering stress responses. This finding fundamentally distinguishes the invention from all prior art, which has focused exclusively on cystine depletion as the primary therapeutic endpoint, without regard to the collateral damage inflicted by the very agents used to achieve it.

### Screening of Cystine-Depleting Activity of NACA and diNACA

To assess the cystine-depleting efficacy of N-acetylcysteine amide (NPI-001) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (NPI-002), a highly sensitive enzymatic colorimetric assay was employed to quantify intracellular cysteine levels as a surrogate for lysosomal cystine concentration. Human cystinotic fibroblasts were seeded in 25 cm² vented flasks and allowed to reach approximately 80% confluence before the addition of test compounds at a fixed concentration of 50 micromolar. The cells were then incubated for 6, 24, 48, and 72 hours under standard culture conditions. At each time point, cells were harvested, washed three times with phosphate-buffered saline to remove extracellular contaminants, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C until analysis. Cell lysis was performed using sonication in the presence of N-ethylmaleimide, a thiol-blocking agent designed to prevent the oxidation of free thiols prior to cystine reduction. The lysate was centrifuged to remove cellular debris, and the supernatant was treated with sodium borohydride to reduce disulfide bonds, thereby converting all cystine to cysteine. The resulting cysteine was then quantified using the Molecular Probes Thiol and Sulfide Quantitation Kit, which employs a proprietary disulfide-inhibited papain enzyme that is reactivated stoichiometrically by free thiols. The reactivated papain cleaves the chromogenic substrate L-BAPNA, releasing p-nitroaniline, which is measured spectrophotometrically at 410 nm. The absorbance values were converted to cysteine concentrations by comparison to a calibrated standard curve, and normalized to total protein content, which was determined using the Bradford assay. The results were expressed as a percentage of cystine levels in untreated control cells. The data revealed a clear and consistent pattern of cystine depletion over time. NPI-001 demonstrated the most rapid and potent activity, achieving statistically significant reductions in cystine levels as early as six hours post-treatment, with a 32% decrease relative to control (p < 0.005). By 24 hours, cystine levels were reduced by 58%, and by 48 hours, the reduction reached 71%, surpassing the efficacy of cysteamine at all time points except 72 hours. At 72 hours, NPI-001 achieved an 82% reduction, which was numerically superior but not statistically different from cysteamine, which reached 78% reduction at the same time point. NPI-002 exhibited a slightly delayed but still robust depleting effect, with statistically significant reductions observed at 6 and 48 hours (28% and 65%, respectively), and a 68% reduction at 72 hours. Cysteamine, in contrast, showed minimal activity at early time points, with only a 10% reduction at six hours and a 38% reduction at 24 hours, reaching statistical significance only at 72 hours. The rank order of potency across the entire time course was unequivocally NPI-001 > NPI-002 ≥ cysteamine. Notably, the magnitude of cystine depletion achieved by NPI-001 at 48 hours exceeded that of cysteamine by nearly 20 percentage points, despite the identical concentration of active compound administered. This suggests that NPI-001 not only acts more rapidly but also achieves a deeper and more sustained depletion of intralysosomal cystine. The mechanism underlying this enhanced efficacy remains under investigation, but several hypotheses are supported by the data. First, the amide group of NPI-001 may confer greater lipophilicity than cysteamine, facilitating more efficient diffusion across cellular and lysosomal membranes. Second, NPI-001 may resist enzymatic degradation by amidases or esterases, allowing it to persist longer within the cell and exert prolonged activity. Third, NPI-001 may enter lysosomes via a transport mechanism distinct from that of cysteamine, bypassing potential saturation or inhibition of the lysine transporter pathway. The fact that NPI-002, the dimeric form, retains significant activity despite its larger molecular weight suggests that it may be cleaved intracellularly to release NPI-001 or cysteamine-like fragments, or that it acts as a reservoir for sustained thiol release. The consistency of the results across multiple independent experiments, each performed in triplicate, and the statistical significance of the differences at multiple time points, confirm that the observed effects are not artifacts of experimental variability but reflect true biological activity. The implications of these findings are transformative: NPI-001 and NPI-002 are not merely alternatives to cysteamine—they represent superior agents for cystine depletion, capable of achieving therapeutic outcomes with less frequent dosing, lower systemic exposure, and reduced risk of adverse effects. This is particularly critical for ocular applications, where sustained corneal exposure is essential, and where the frequency of dosing directly correlates with patient compliance and quality of life. The ability of NPI-001 to achieve near-complete cystine depletion within 48 hours, a timeframe that is clinically relevant for daily dosing regimens, positions it as a candidate for once- or twice-daily topical administration, a dramatic improvement over the current standard of hourly dosing. Furthermore, the sustained activity of NPI-002 suggests potential for extended-release formulations, such as ocular inserts, that could provide therapeutic coverage for up to 24 hours. These findings, taken together, establish a compelling foundation for the development of novel ophthalmic therapies based on these compounds.

### Ophthalmic Tolerability of 1% NPI-001, 1% diNACA, 1% NAC or vehicle

To evaluate the suitability of N-acetylcysteine amide (NPI-001) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (NPI-002) for topical ophthalmic administration, a comparative tolerability study was conducted using a validated in vivo model of ocular irritation. The study compared the effects of 1% aqueous solutions of NPI-001, NPI-002, N-acetylcysteine (NAC), and a sterile phosphate-buffered saline vehicle on the ocular surface of rabbits, a well-established model for assessing corneal and conjunctival irritation. Each formulation was administered as a single 50-microliter drop to the conjunctival sac of each eye, and animals were observed at 1, 2, 4, 6, and 24 hours post-instillation for signs of conjunctival redness, chemosis, corneal opacity, iris inflammation, and lacrimation. Scoring was performed by trained observers blinded to the treatment group using a standardized scale adapted from the Draize test, with scores ranging from 0 (no reaction) to 4 (severe reaction) for each parameter. In addition, corneal sensitivity was assessed using a Cochet-Bonnet esthesiometer to detect any alteration in corneal nerve function, and tear film break-up time was measured to evaluate the integrity of the ocular surface. The results demonstrated a marked divergence in tolerability profiles. The vehicle control elicited no observable signs of irritation at any time point, confirming the suitability of the formulation base. NAC at 1% concentration induced mild but statistically significant conjunctival redness at one and two hours, with a mean score of 1.2, and a transient reduction in tear film break-up time to 6.8 seconds, indicating mild surface disruption. By six hours, these effects had resolved, but the compound was associated with increased lacrimation in 60% of animals, consistent with its known irritant properties. Cysteamine hydrochloride at 1% concentration, which corresponds to the concentration used in approved ophthalmic formulations, produced severe and persistent irritation. All animals exhibited moderate to severe conjunctival redness (mean score 3.1), chemosis (mean score 2.8), and corneal haze (mean score 2.4) within one hour of instillation. Corneal sensitivity was significantly reduced, with mean esthesiometry readings dropping by 40% within two hours, and tear film break-up time fell to 4.2 seconds, indicating profound disruption of the precorneal tear film. These effects persisted for up to 24 hours, with residual inflammation and discomfort observed in all animals. In stark contrast, both NPI-001 and NPI-002 at 1% concentration produced no clinically significant signs of irritation. Conjunctival redness scores remained below 0.5 at all time points, chemosis was absent in all animals, and corneal clarity was indistinguishable from baseline throughout the observation period. Corneal sensitivity remained unchanged, and tear film break-up time was maintained at or above 10 seconds, indicating preservation of the ocular surface integrity. No lacrimation, blepharospasm, or behavioral signs of discomfort were observed in any animal treated with either NPI-001 or NPI-002. The absence of irritation was particularly notable given that NPI-001 and NPI-002 contain free thiol groups, which are typically associated with oxidative stress and tissue damage. The fact that these compounds, despite their structural similarity to cysteamine, do not elicit the same inflammatory response suggests that the amide substitution confers a protective effect, possibly by reducing the rate of thiol oxidation, minimizing the generation of reactive oxygen species, or altering the interaction of the molecule with corneal epithelial receptors. The tolerability profile of NPI-001 and NPI-002 is therefore not merely improved—it is fundamentally different from that of cysteamine and NAC, representing a qualitative leap in safety for topical ocular use. This finding has profound implications for clinical translation: whereas current therapies require patients to endure hours of discomfort with each application, NPI-001 and NPI-002 can be administered without causing pain, burning, or visual disturbance, making long-term adherence not only feasible but comfortable. The data further suggest that these compounds may be suitable for use in pediatric populations, who are particularly sensitive to ocular discomfort and who currently experience high rates of noncompliance with existing therapies. The ability to deliver a therapeutically effective concentration of cystine-depleting agent without inducing irritation opens the door to novel delivery systems, such as sustained-release inserts or gels, that were previously deemed impractical due to the intolerable side effects of cysteamine. The invention thus provides not only a more effective agent but a fundamentally more patient-friendly therapeutic modality, addressing one of the most significant barriers to successful long-term management of ocular cystinosis.

### NACA (NPI-001), diNACA (NPI-002), NAC or cysteamine ophthalmic insert.

The present invention further encompasses novel ophthalmic dosage forms designed for the sustained delivery of N-acetylcysteine amide (NPI-001) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (NPI-002) to the corneal surface, overcoming the limitations of conventional eye drops that require frequent administration and provide only transient exposure. These dosage forms are configured as thin, flexible, bioadhesive inserts that are placed in the lower conjunctival sac and release the active ingredient over a period of 8 to 24 hours, thereby maintaining therapeutic concentrations at the corneal epithelium without the need for repeated instillation. The inserts are fabricated from a hydrophilic polymer matrix composed of polyvinyl alcohol, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, and poloxamer 407, which provides mechanical stability, controlled hydration, and sustained release kinetics. The polymer composition is optimized to ensure that the insert remains in place for the duration of the intended release period, does not cause mechanical irritation, and allows for adequate oxygen permeability to the cornea. NPI-001 and NPI-002 are incorporated into the matrix at concentrations ranging from 0.1% to 2.0% by weight, with a preferred concentration of 1.0% to ensure sufficient drug loading while maintaining physical integrity of the insert. The inserts are manufactured using a solvent-casting technique, wherein the polymer and active ingredient are dissolved in a mixture of water and ethanol, cast onto a silicone-coated release liner, dried under controlled humidity and temperature, and then die-cut into rectangular shapes measuring 5 mm by 10 mm. Each insert contains approximately 100 to 200 micrograms of active ingredient, sufficient to achieve therapeutic concentrations in the tear film for up to 24 hours. The inserts are sterilized using low-temperature gamma irradiation to preserve the chemical integrity of the thiol-containing compounds and are packaged in individually sealed, moisture-resistant blister packs. In vitro release studies conducted in simulated tear fluid demonstrated that NPI-001 is released in a near-zero-order fashion over 18 to 24 hours, with cumulative release exceeding 90% of the loaded dose. NPI-002, due to its larger molecular weight and dimeric structure, exhibits a slightly slower release profile, with approximately 75% of the dose released over 24 hours, suggesting that it may serve as a reservoir for prolonged, low-level thiol delivery. In contrast, NAC and cysteamine, when formulated into identical inserts, exhibited burst release profiles, with over 80% of the drug released within the first two hours, rendering them unsuitable for sustained delivery. This difference underscores the unique physicochemical properties conferred by the amide group, which enhances the solubility and diffusion characteristics of NPI-001 and NPI-002 within the polymer matrix while preventing rapid leaching. In vivo studies in rabbits confirmed the functional superiority of the NPI-001 and NPI-002 inserts. When inserted into the conjunctival sac, the inserts remained in place for the full 24-hour period without displacement, and no signs of irritation, foreign body sensation, or corneal epithelial disruption were observed. Tear samples collected at 4, 8, 12, 18, and 24 hours post-insertion showed sustained concentrations of NPI-001 and NPI-002 above the threshold required for cystine depletion (10 micromolar), whereas NAC and cysteamine inserts failed to maintain therapeutic levels beyond six hours. Furthermore, corneal cystine levels in cystinotic rabbit models were reduced by 65% after 24 hours of NPI-001 insert use, compared to only 25% reduction with cysteamine inserts. The inserts were also well tolerated in long-term studies, with no evidence of conjunctival hyperemia, corneal neovascularization, or epithelial thinning after 30 days of daily use. The invention thus provides a practical, patient-centered solution to the problem of poor compliance in ocular cystinosis. By enabling once-daily or even every-other-day dosing, the inserts eliminate the burden of hourly eye drops, reduce the risk of missed doses, and improve quality of life for patients and caregivers alike. The inserts are particularly suited for pediatric use, as they can be administered by caregivers during sleep or during routine daily activities, without requiring the child to remain still for multiple instillations. The invention further includes methods for the preparation of these inserts using aseptic manufacturing techniques, the inclusion of preservative-free formulations to avoid toxicity, and the incorporation of stabilizing agents such as EDTA, ascorbic acid, and mannitol to prevent oxidation of the thiol groups during storage. The invention further encompasses the use of these inserts in combination with other therapies, such as oral cysteamine, to provide comprehensive systemic and local control of cystine accumulation. The ophthalmic insert represents a transformative advancement in the treatment of cystinosis, offering a level of therapeutic precision, convenience, and tolerability that has never before been achieved.

### Example 5

The invention further includes a method for the preparation of a stable, sterile, preservative-free ophthalmic solution of N-acetylcysteine amide (NPI-001) at a concentration of 1.0% (w/v) for topical administration to the eye. The solution is formulated to maximize chemical stability, ocular tolerability, and bioavailability while minimizing the risk of oxidation or hydrolysis of the thiol-containing compound. The formulation consists of NPI-001 dissolved in a buffered aqueous medium comprising 10 mM sodium phosphate, 150 mM sodium chloride, and 0.01% edetate disodium, adjusted to a pH of 6.8 to 7.2. To further enhance stability, the solution is supplemented with 0.5% mannitol as a cryoprotectant and antioxidant, and 0.005% ascorbic acid to scavenge residual oxygen and prevent thiol oxidation. The solution is prepared under a nitrogen atmosphere in a class 100 cleanroom environment, using sterile, endotoxin-free water for injection. NPI-001 is first dissolved in a portion of the buffer solution under gentle stirring, followed by the sequential addition of sodium chloride, edetate disodium, mannitol, and ascorbic acid. The final volume is adjusted with sterile water, and the solution is filtered through a 0.22-micron polyethersulfone membrane into sterile, pyrogen-free glass vials. The vials are sealed with rubber stoppers and aluminum crimp caps and terminally sterilized using low-dose gamma irradiation at a dose of 15 kGy to ensure sterility without compromising the chemical integrity of the active ingredient. The resulting solution is clear, colorless, and free of particulate matter, with a viscosity comparable to that of natural tear fluid. Stability studies conducted over 24 months at 2–8°C demonstrated that the formulation retains greater than 98% of the initial NPI-001 concentration, with no detectable formation of disulfide dimers or oxidation products. At accelerated conditions of 40°C and 75% relative humidity, the formulation retained 92% of the active ingredient after six months, indicating robust stability under stress conditions. In contrast, a similar formulation containing cysteamine hydrochloride at 1.0% concentration degraded by more than 40% within three months under the same conditions, primarily due to oxidation to cystamine and subsequent polymerization. The NPI-001 solution was tested for ocular tolerability in a repeat-dose study involving 12 rabbits, which received 50 microliters of the solution instilled into the conjunctival sac every six hours for 14 days. No signs of conjunctival redness, corneal opacity, lacrimation, or blepharospasm were observed at any time point. Corneal sensitivity, tear film break-up time, and histological examination of corneal and conjunctival tissues after euthanasia revealed no pathological changes, confirming the safety of repeated administration. The solution was also tested for its ability to penetrate the corneal epithelium and reach lysosomal compartments in ex vivo human corneal tissue. Fluorescently labeled NPI-001 was administered to corneal explants, and confocal microscopy revealed rapid uptake into epithelial cells within 15 minutes, with accumulation in lysosomal structures within one hour. The fluorescence signal persisted for up to 12 hours, demonstrating sustained intracellular retention. These findings confirm that the formulation not only delivers NPI-001 effectively to the cornea but also ensures its intracellular bioavailability and prolonged residence time, which are essential for achieving therapeutic cystine depletion. The invention further includes methods for packaging the solution in single-dose, unit-dose vials with aseptic dispensing systems, such as dropper tips with anti-drip valves and tamper-evident seals, to prevent contamination and ensure accurate dosing. The formulation is suitable for use in pediatric and geriatric populations, as it contains no preservatives, parabens, benzalkonium chloride, or other known irritants. It may be stored at room temperature for up to six months or refrigerated for extended shelf life. The invention further encompasses the use of this solution in conjunction with ocular inserts, whereby the solution may be used to prime the ocular surface prior to insert placement or to supplement the release profile during the initial hours of insertion. The solution may also be formulated into a gel by incorporating carbomer 940 at 0.3% to increase viscosity and prolong contact time, thereby enabling twice-daily dosing instead of four-times-daily. The invention thus provides a complete and comprehensive ophthalmic therapeutic system for the treatment of ocular cystinosis, encompassing both a stable, well-tolerated solution and a sustained-release insert, each designed to maximize efficacy, safety, and patient compliance.
