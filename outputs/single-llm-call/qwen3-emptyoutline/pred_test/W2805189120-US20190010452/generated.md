# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel method for the in vitro differentiation of functional hypothalamic neurons, particularly vasopressin-secreting magnocellular neurons, from human pluripotent stem cells. More specifically, the invention provides a precisely controlled, stepwise protocol for the generation of hypothalamic tissue with high efficiency and functional maturity, enabling the production of neurons capable of synthesizing, storing, and physiologically releasing vasopressin in response to depolarizing stimuli. The invention further encompasses methods for the simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues in a co-culture system, thereby recapitulating the neuroendocrine axis in vitro. This technology finds critical application in the modeling of neuroendocrine disorders such as central diabetes insipidus, the screening of pharmacological agents targeting hypothalamic function, the study of human embryonic development, and the future development of cell-based regenerative therapies for patients with hypothalamic dysfunction. The method is distinguished by its ability to recapitulate the spatiotemporal signaling environment of early neural development, including the precise modulation of bone morphogenetic protein, sonic hedgehog, and Akt signaling pathways to direct progenitor cells toward a dorsal hypothalamic fate, followed by specialized culture conditions that promote terminal neuronal maturation and hormone secretion.

## BACKGROUND ART

The hypothalamus is a central component of the neuroendocrine system, orchestrating vital homeostatic functions including fluid balance, thermoregulation, appetite, circadian rhythms, and reproductive behavior through the integration of neural and hormonal signals. Among its most critical outputs is the secretion of arginine vasopressin (AVP), a nine-amino-acid peptide hormone synthesized primarily in the magnocellular neurons of the paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei. AVP is transported along axons to the posterior pituitary, where it is released into systemic circulation to act on renal V2 receptors, promoting water reabsorption and maintaining plasma osmolality. Deficiencies in AVP production or release result in central diabetes insipidus, a condition characterized by excessive urine output, profound polydipsia, and, in severe cases, life-threatening dehydration due to the loss of thirst sensation. Current therapeutic interventions rely on synthetic analogs such as desmopressin; however, these treatments are associated with significant risks, including hyponatremia, poor pharmacokinetic control, and an inability to replicate the pulsatile, osmotically regulated secretion patterns of native AVP neurons. Moreover, in patients with concomitant osmoreceptor dysfunction, pharmacological substitution becomes inadequate, underscoring the urgent need for regenerative strategies that restore endogenous neuroendocrine function.

Prior efforts to generate hypothalamic neurons from human pluripotent stem cells have been limited by low efficiency, incomplete maturation, and the absence of demonstrable hormone secretion. One approach, based on murine embryological models, utilized serum-free floating culture of embryoid body-like aggregates (SFEBq) to induce hypothalamic progenitors; however, when applied to human cells, this method yielded minimal expression of AVP lineage markers and no detectable hormone release. Other protocols relied on monolayer differentiation or unoptimized growth factor cocktails that failed to recapitulate the complex signaling gradients required for regional specification within the diencephalon. These methods often resulted in heterogeneous cell populations containing telencephalic, retinal, or non-neural contaminants, with AVP-positive cells appearing at frequencies below 1% of the total population. Furthermore, the transition from progenitor to mature, secretory neuron remained inefficient, with cells failing to develop the morphological and electrophysiological characteristics of native magnocellular neurons, including large somata, axonal projections, and vesicular storage machinery. The absence of a reliable in vitro model has hindered the mechanistic study of human hypothalamic development, the identification of disease-causing mutations in genetic forms of central diabetes insipidus, and the high-throughput screening of novel therapeutics that modulate AVP synthesis or release.

Existing technologies for neural differentiation from pluripotent stem cells have largely focused on cortical, dopaminergic, or motor neuron lineages, with little attention paid to the intricate patterning required for hypothalamic specification. The hypothalamus arises from the rostral diencephalon during early embryogenesis, a region whose identity is determined by a precise balance of morphogen gradients, including low levels of Wnt and retinoic acid, moderate BMP signaling from the dorsal ectoderm, and ventralizing signals from the notochord and prechordal plate, primarily mediated by sonic hedgehog. In vivo, the dorsal hypothalamus, which gives rise to AVP neurons, is specified by a unique combination of transcription factors including RAX, PAX6, OTP, and BRN2, whereas ventral hypothalamic fates are governed by NKX2.1 and SHH. No prior method has successfully isolated and expanded these dorsal hypothalamic progenitors from human pluripotent stem cells with sufficient purity and fidelity to permit their subsequent differentiation into functional, hormone-secreting neurons. Additionally, the transition from three-dimensional aggregate culture to a two-dimensional environment conducive to neuronal maturation has not been systematically optimized, and the role of oxygen tension, extracellular matrix composition, and neurotrophic support in promoting terminal differentiation has remained largely unexplored. Consequently, the field has lacked a robust, reproducible, and scalable platform for generating human hypothalamic neurons capable of physiological hormone secretion, thereby impeding progress in both basic science and translational applications.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the absence of a reliable, efficient, and physiologically relevant method for generating functional vasopressin-secreting neurons from human pluripotent stem cells. Despite decades of research into neural differentiation, no prior protocol has succeeded in consistently producing human hypothalamic neurons that not only express the full complement of molecular markers associated with mature AVP magnocellular neurons—including RAX, PAX6, OTP, BRN2, neurophysin II, and copeptin—but also demonstrate the capacity to synthesize, store, and release vasopressin in a regulated, stimulus-dependent manner. Existing methods suffer from multiple critical limitations: first, they fail to generate a sufficient proportion of dorsal hypothalamic progenitors, often yielding mixed populations contaminated with telencephalic or retinal lineages; second, they do not provide the necessary microenvironmental cues—such as oxygen tension, extracellular matrix composition, and neurotrophic support—to enable terminal neuronal maturation; third, they lack the ability to recapitulate the physiological secretory response to depolarizing stimuli, such as elevated extracellular potassium, which is essential for validating functional maturity; and fourth, they do not permit the coordinated development of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues, thereby precluding the study of neuroendocrine axis formation in vitro. These shortcomings have rendered prior approaches unsuitable for modeling human neuroendocrine diseases, screening drug candidates, or advancing cell replacement therapies for conditions such as central diabetes insipidus. There exists, therefore, a compelling and unmet need for a method that overcomes these barriers by precisely controlling developmental signaling, optimizing culture conditions for neuronal maturation, and enabling the production of functional, hormone-secreting AVP neurons with high efficiency and reproducibility.

### Solution to Problem

The solution to the aforementioned technical problem is provided by a novel, multi-stage method for the in vitro differentiation of functional vasopressin-secreting hypothalamic neurons from human pluripotent stem cells. The method comprises a series of precisely timed and chemically defined steps that recapitulate the key developmental events of hypothalamic specification and neuronal maturation. The first stage involves the initiation of three-dimensional aggregate culture using a serum-free, chemically defined medium supplemented with a low concentration of knock-out serum replacement to promote stable aggregation without inducing telencephalic fate. Within this system, the addition of bone morphogenetic protein 4 (BMP4) and smoothened agonist (SAG) during a specific developmental window—between days 6 and 12—induces a highly enriched population of RAX-positive hypothalamic progenitors, with over 80% of cells expressing this key marker. Critically, the concentration of BMP4 is optimized between 0.5 and 2.0 nM, while SAG is maintained at 0.5 to 1.0 μM, to selectively promote dorsal hypothalamic identity characterized by co-expression of PAX6 and absence of NKX2.1. The inclusion of an Akt inhibitor during this phase further enhances the rostral identity of the progenitors without compromising RAX induction, thereby ensuring the generation of cells with the correct positional information for AVP neuron specification. In the second stage, aggregates are transitioned to a gas-liquid interphase culture using a Transwell system, which improves oxygenation and nutrient diffusion, leading to a tenfold increase in the expression of AVP lineage markers OTP and BRN2. In the third stage, aggregates are dissociated into single cells and plated onto a substrate coated with Matrigel, poly-D-lysine, and laminin, providing the necessary extracellular matrix for neurite outgrowth and neuronal maturation. This two-dimensional culture, maintained in a medium supplemented with brain-derived neurotrophic factor, neurotrophin-3, and LM22A-4, supports the long-term survival and terminal differentiation of neurons over 150 days. By day 150, a significant proportion of cells express vasopressin, neurophysin II, and copeptin, display morphological features consistent with magnocellular neurons—including large somata exceeding 40 micrometers in length—and, most importantly, demonstrate regulated secretion of vasopressin in response to high-potassium depolarization, as confirmed by radioimmunoassay. Furthermore, the method enables the parallel generation of ventral hypothalamic neurons, such as those producing melanin-concentrating hormone, and permits the simultaneous maturation of pituitary tissue when co-cultured with hypothalamic aggregates, thereby establishing a functional neuroendocrine axis in vitro. This comprehensive approach represents the first method capable of generating human hypothalamic neurons that are not only molecularly and morphologically authentic but also physiologically functional, thereby solving the long-standing problem of producing viable, hormone-secreting AVP neurons from pluripotent stem cells.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### 1. Method for Producing Cellular Structure Including Hypothalamic Tissue

The method for producing a cellular structure comprising hypothalamic tissue begins with the maintenance of human pluripotent stem cells under feeder-dependent conditions using a medium composed of DMEM/F12 supplemented with 20% knock-out serum replacement, non-essential amino acids, L-glutamine, basic fibroblast growth factor, and 2-mercaptoethanol. Prior to differentiation, the stem cell colonies are detached using a combination of trypsin and collagenase IV and subjected to a 90-minute pre-plating step on gelatin-coated plates to eliminate residual feeder cells. The resulting cell clumps are then dissociated into single cells using TrypLE Express in the presence of DNase I and the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 to prevent anoikis. These single cells are rapidly reaggregated in low-cell-adhesion, V-bottomed 96-well plates at a density of 10,000 cells per well in a chemically defined medium consisting of serum-free, growth factor-free culture medium supplemented with 5% knock-out serum replacement, non-essential amino acids, pyruvate, 2-mercaptoethanol, and 20 mM Y-27632. The initial culture is maintained for a period of 24 hours, after which the medium is partially replaced with fresh medium lacking Y-27632, and this stepwise reduction is repeated over the next three days to allow for the stabilization of the aggregates. From day 6 of culture, the medium is supplemented with bone morphogenetic protein 4 at a concentration of 0.5 to 2.0 nM and smoothened agonist at a concentration of 0.5 to 1.0 μM, and these factors are maintained until day 12. Concurrently, an Akt inhibitor is introduced at a concentration of 0.5 μM to enhance the rostral identity of the developing progenitors. The aggregates are cultured under these conditions for a total of 30 days, during which time they develop into spherical structures exhibiting robust expression of RAX, a marker of rostral hypothalamic progenitors, with over 80% of cells in the aggregate expressing this transcription factor. Immunohistochemical analysis confirms the absence of telencephalic markers such as FOXG1 and neural retinal markers such as VSX2, indicating a high degree of specificity for hypothalamic lineage commitment. At day 30, the aggregates are assessed for the co-expression of dorsal hypothalamic markers PAX6 and NKX2.1, with the optimal condition for dorsal hypothalamic specification identified as 1.5 nM BMP4 and 0.5 μM SAG, resulting in a population predominantly positive for PAX6 and negative for NKX2.1. These dorsal hypothalamic aggregates are then transferred to a Transwell culture system, wherein the aggregates are placed on a porous membrane suspended above a reservoir of differentiation medium composed of DMEM/F12 supplemented with N2, B27, glucose, and ciliary neurotrophic factor. This gas-liquid interphase culture is maintained for 30 additional days, during which time the expression of AVP lineage markers OTP and BRN2 increases markedly, with over 30% of cells in the aggregate expressing these transcription factors. The improved oxygenation and reduced diffusion barriers inherent in the Transwell system are believed to mitigate central necrosis and promote the survival and maturation of progenitor cells. Following this stage, the aggregates are enzymatically dissociated into single cells using Accumax in the presence of DNase I and Y-27632, and the resulting cell suspension is plated onto glass coverslips previously coated with a layer of Matrigel, poly-D-lysine, and laminin. The plated cells are maintained in a specialized neuronal maturation medium containing DMEM/F12, N2, B27, 10% fetal bovine serum, brain-derived neurotrophic factor, neurotrophin-3, LM22A-4, and 10 μM Y-27632, with half of the medium replaced every other day. Under these conditions, the cells undergo extensive neurite outgrowth and morphological maturation over a period of 120 to 180 days. By day 150, a significant proportion of cells exhibit large, rounded somata measuring approximately 40 by 20 micrometers, consistent with the morphology of magnocellular neurons in vivo. Immunofluorescence staining confirms the expression of vasopressin, neurophysin II, and copeptin in these cells, with minimal co-expression of markers for other neuronal subtypes such as corticotropin-releasing hormone or oxytocin, indicating a high degree of lineage fidelity. The absence of glial markers such as GFAP, IBA1, and OLIG2 confirms the neuronal specificity of the differentiated population. This method, therefore, provides a robust and reproducible means of generating a cellular structure comprising functional hypothalamic tissue with a high degree of anatomical and physiological authenticity.

### EXAMPLES

In a first example, human embryonic stem cell line VA22-N37, which carries a RAX::Venus reporter construct, was subjected to the differentiation protocol described above. On day 20, fluorescence-activated cell sorting revealed that 82% of cells expressed RAX::Venus, confirming the high efficiency of hypothalamic progenitor induction. Immunostaining of day 30 aggregates demonstrated that 78% of RAX-positive cells co-expressed PAX6 and were negative for NKX2.1, indicating a dorsal hypothalamic identity. Following transfer to Transwell culture, the number of OTP-positive cells increased from 3% to 32% of the total population, and BRN2 expression rose from 2% to 28%. Upon dissociation and two-dimensional culture, vasopressin-positive neurons were observed at a frequency of 15% of all TUJ1-positive neurons by day 150. These neurons exhibited extensive axonal processes extending over 200 micrometers and displayed large somata with a mean diameter of 42 micrometers. In a second example, the same protocol was applied to two additional human embryonic stem cell lines, KhES-1 and KhES-3, with comparable results: RAX induction rates exceeded 80% in both lines, and vasopressin-positive neurons were generated at frequencies of 13% and 14%, respectively, confirming the robustness and reproducibility of the method across different genetic backgrounds. In a third example, the effect of varying concentrations of BMP4 and SAG was systematically evaluated. When BMP4 was applied at 0.2 nM or 4.0 nM, RAX induction dropped below 40%, and the proportion of PAX6-positive cells decreased significantly, while NKX2.1 expression increased, indicating a shift toward ventral fate. Similarly, SAG concentrations below 0.2 μM or above 2.0 μM resulted in either insufficient dorsal specification or the emergence of neural retinal markers. The optimal concentration window for dorsal hypothalamic induction was therefore established as 0.5 to 2.0 nM BMP4 and 0.5 to 1.0 μM SAG. In a fourth example, the omission of Akt inhibitor resulted in a 40% reduction in the number of vasopressin-positive neurons, despite unchanged RAX and PAX6 expression, suggesting that Akt inhibition is critical for terminal neuronal maturation rather than progenitor specification. In a fifth example, the Transwell culture was replaced with continuous three-dimensional culture in V-bottom wells, resulting in a 90% reduction in OTP and BRN2 expression and no detectable vasopressin secretion, demonstrating the necessity of the gas-liquid interphase for progenitor maturation. In a sixth example, the use of uncoated coverslips for two-dimensional culture led to poor cell adhesion, extensive cell death, and negligible neuronal maturation, whereas the inclusion of Matrigel, poly-D-lysine, and laminin in combination was essential for neurite extension and survival. In a seventh example, radioimmunoassay of conditioned medium from day 150 cultures revealed basal vasopressin concentrations of 12.4 ± 1.8 pg/mL, which increased to 48.7 ± 3.2 pg/mL following stimulation with 100 mM KCl, demonstrating regulated, stimulus-dependent hormone release. In an eighth example, quantitative PCR analysis of MCH expression in ventral hypothalamic cultures showed a 6.5-fold increase following stimulation with nerve growth factor, dexamethasone, and lithium chloride, confirming the functional responsiveness of the generated neurons to physiological modulators. These examples collectively validate the efficacy, specificity, and physiological relevance of the method for generating functional hypothalamic tissue.

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary

An additional embodiment of the invention provides a method for the simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues in a co-culture system, thereby recapitulating the neuroendocrine axis in vitro. This method begins with the generation of hypothalamic progenitors as described in the preceding embodiment, followed by the concurrent differentiation of pituitary tissue from the same pluripotent stem cell population. On day 15 of the initial SFEBq culture, a subset of aggregates is separated and placed into a separate differentiation medium containing activin A, fibroblast growth factor 8, and bone morphogenetic protein 4 at concentrations optimized for anterior pituitary specification. These aggregates are maintained in suspension for an additional 20 days, during which they develop into structures expressing key pituitary transcription factors including PROP1, POU1F1, and TPIT, indicative of progenitor commitment to corticotroph, somatotroph, and thyrotroph lineages. At day 35, the hypothalamic aggregates and pituitary aggregates are co-cultured in a Transwell system, wherein the hypothalamic aggregates are placed on the upper membrane and the pituitary aggregates are suspended in the lower chamber. The medium is supplemented with neurotrophic factors and maintained under conditions that support both neuronal and endocrine maturation. Over the subsequent 60 to 90 days, axonal projections from the hypothalamic aggregates are observed to extend toward and make contact with the pituitary aggregates, forming structures reminiscent of the hypothalamic-hypophyseal tract. Immunohistochemical analysis reveals the presence of vasopressin-positive axon terminals in close proximity to pituitary cells expressing adrenocorticotropic hormone, thyroid-stimulating hormone, and growth hormone. Furthermore, stimulation of the hypothalamic compartment with high potassium results in a measurable increase in the secretion of these pituitary hormones, as detected by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, indicating functional neuroendocrine coupling. This co-culture system enables the study of hypothalamic control over pituitary hormone release in a human-specific context, facilitates the screening of drugs that modulate neuroendocrine signaling, and provides a platform for the reconstruction of a complete neuroendocrine axis for potential transplantation. The method eliminates the need for exogenous hormone addition and instead relies on endogenous, physiologically regulated communication between the two tissues, thereby offering a superior model for studying diseases of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis such as hypopituitarism, Cushing’s disease, and disorders of water balance.

### 3. Conclusion

The method described herein represents a transformative advance in the field of human neuroendocrine biology and regenerative medicine. By systematically recapitulating the developmental signaling events that govern hypothalamic specification, the invention provides a reproducible, scalable, and highly efficient protocol for generating functional vasopressin-secreting neurons from human pluripotent stem cells. The integration of precise morphogen modulation, oxygen-sensitive culture conditions, extracellular matrix optimization, and neurotrophic support ensures the generation of neurons that are not only molecularly and morphologically authentic but also physiologically responsive to depolarizing stimuli. The ability to simultaneously mature hypothalamic and pituitary tissues further extends the utility of the method to the study of neuroendocrine axis formation and dysfunction. This technology enables the creation of patient-specific disease models for central diabetes insipidus and other hypothalamic disorders, facilitates high-throughput drug screening for novel therapeutics that modulate AVP secretion, and lays the foundation for future cell replacement therapies aimed at restoring endogenous neuroendocrine function. The method is applicable to both embryonic and induced pluripotent stem cells, allowing for the generation of genetically matched tissues for personalized medicine. The absence of feeder cells, the use of chemically defined components, and the scalability of the protocol render it suitable for clinical translation under Good Manufacturing Practice conditions. The invention therefore fulfills a long-standing need in biomedical research and opens new avenues for understanding, diagnosing, and treating disorders of the neuroendocrine system.

### 1. Appearance of Hypothalamic AVP Neuron

The appearance of vasopressin-secreting neurons derived from human pluripotent stem cells under the conditions of the present invention is characterized by a distinct and highly reproducible set of morphological, molecular, and functional features that closely mirror those of native magnocellular neurons in the human hypothalamus. By day 150 of differentiation, these neurons exhibit a large, rounded cell body with a mean diameter of approximately 40 micrometers in the longitudinal axis and 20 micrometers in the transverse axis, dimensions that are consistent with the size of magnocellular neurons observed in postmortem human hypothalamic tissue. The cytoplasm is abundant and contains numerous organelles, including a prominent Golgi apparatus and multiple vesicles that stain positively for vasopressin, neurophysin II, and copeptin, indicating the presence of mature secretory granules. The nucleus is large and centrally located, with a distinct nucleolus, and the chromatin is arranged in a pattern typical of metabolically active neurons. The neurons extend long, branched neurites that can reach lengths exceeding 200 micrometers, with varicosities along their length that correspond to sites of neurotransmitter release. These processes are positive for microtubule-associated protein 2 and tau, confirming their neuronal identity, and are devoid of glial markers such as glial fibrillary acidic protein or ionized calcium-binding adapter molecule 1. Immunofluorescence staining reveals that vasopressin is localized in a punctate pattern throughout the soma and along the proximal axon, consistent with vesicular storage, and is absent from the distal terminals, suggesting active transport to synaptic sites. Co-staining with neurophysin II and copeptin demonstrates co-expression in over 95% of vasopressin-positive cells, confirming the authenticity of the neurosecretory phenotype. Electrophysiological recordings, though not explicitly detailed in the present disclosure, are anticipated to reveal the presence of voltage-gated sodium and potassium channels, spontaneous firing activity, and depolarization-induced calcium influx, all hallmarks of mature neuroendocrine neurons. The absence of co-expression with corticotropin-releasing hormone, oxytocin, or other hypothalamic neuropeptides further confirms the specificity of the differentiation protocol for the AVP lineage. The neurons are not observed to form synapses with other hypothalamic neurons in vitro, consistent with the known lack of local circuitry in magnocellular populations, which instead project axons to the posterior pituitary. The overall architecture of the differentiated population, when viewed in three-dimensional reconstruction, resembles the compact, clustered organization of the paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei, with neurons arranged in small clusters separated by minimal extracellular space. This level of structural and molecular fidelity, achieved without the use of exogenous transcription factors or genetic manipulation, represents a significant advancement over prior methods and confirms the physiological relevance of the generated neurons.

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary

The simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues, as described in the present invention, results in the formation of a structurally and functionally integrated neuroendocrine unit that recapitulates the fundamental architecture of the hypothalamic-hypophyseal axis. When hypothalamic and pituitary aggregates are co-cultured under the specified conditions, the hypothalamic neurons extend long, unmyelinated axons that traverse the interstitial space between the two aggregates and terminate in close apposition to the pituitary parenchyma. These axons are positive for vasopressin, neurophysin II, and synaptic vesicle proteins such as synaptophysin and vesicular monoamine transporter 2, confirming their neurosecretory nature. The pituitary tissue, derived from the same pluripotent stem cell line, differentiates into distinct endocrine cell types, including corticotrophs expressing pro-opiomelanocortin and adrenocorticotropic hormone, thyrotrophs expressing thyrotropin-releasing hormone receptor and thyroid-stimulating hormone, and somatotrophs expressing growth hormone and prolactin. These cells are organized into clusters that resemble the anterior pituitary lobule structure, with a central vascular-like network formed by endothelial cells that spontaneously emerge from the culture. The proximity of vasopressin-positive axon terminals to the pituitary cells is consistently observed, with synaptic-like junctions forming between the axonal varicosities and the plasma membrane of the endocrine cells. Functional coupling is demonstrated by the release of adrenocorticotropic hormone, thyroid-stimulating hormone, and growth hormone in response to depolarization of the hypothalamic compartment with high potassium, with a latency of 5 to 10 minutes and a magnitude of secretion proportional to the duration and intensity of stimulation. This response is abolished when the axonal connection between the two compartments is physically disrupted, confirming that the hormone release is mediated by neural input rather than paracrine diffusion. The pituitary cells also exhibit increased expression of receptors for hypothalamic releasing factors, including corticotropin-releasing hormone receptor and thyrotropin-releasing hormone receptor, indicating their maturation into fully responsive endocrine cells. The co-culture system remains viable for over six months, with sustained hormone secretion and axonal integrity, suggesting long-term stability of the neuroendocrine unit. This embodiment enables the study of human-specific neuroendocrine feedback loops, the identification of pathogenic mechanisms in disorders such as Sheehan’s syndrome or hypothalamic amenorrhea, and the testing of novel therapeutics that modulate the axis at the level of neural input, pituitary response, or both. The ability to generate this integrated system from a single pluripotent stem cell source eliminates the need for xenogeneic components and provides a fully human model for neuroendocrine research and regenerative applications.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The method disclosed herein possesses significant industrial applicability across multiple sectors of biomedical research, pharmaceutical development, and regenerative medicine. In the field of drug discovery, the ability to generate large quantities of functional human vasopressin-secreting neurons enables high-throughput screening of compound libraries for agents that modulate AVP synthesis, storage, or release, thereby accelerating the identification of novel therapeutics for central diabetes insipidus, syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion, and other disorders of water balance. Pharmaceutical companies can utilize this system to evaluate the efficacy, toxicity, and pharmacokinetic profile of candidate drugs in a human-relevant context, reducing reliance on animal models that often fail to predict human responses. In the realm of personalized medicine, the method is compatible with induced pluripotent stem cells derived from patients with genetic forms of central diabetes insipidus, allowing for the creation of patient-specific disease models to study the molecular basis of their condition and to test individualized therapeutic interventions. In regenerative medicine, the protocol provides a scalable and clinically compliant pathway for the generation of transplantable hypothalamic tissue, which may be used to restore endogenous hormone production in patients with irreversible hypothalamic damage due to trauma, tumor resection, or autoimmune destruction. The absence of feeder cells, the use of chemically defined media, and the modular nature of the culture system render it suitable for Good Manufacturing Practice-compliant production, facilitating regulatory approval for clinical trials. Furthermore, the simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues opens new avenues for bioengineered endocrine organoids that could be implanted to restore the entire hypothalamic-pituitary axis, offering a potential cure for complex endocrine deficiencies. The technology also holds value in academic and contract research organizations for the study of human embryonic development, neuroendocrine evolution, and the impact of environmental toxins on hypothalamic function. The scalability, reproducibility, and physiological fidelity of the method ensure its broad utility across industry, academia, and clinical settings, establishing it as a foundational platform for the next generation of neuroendocrine therapeutics and regenerative therapies.
