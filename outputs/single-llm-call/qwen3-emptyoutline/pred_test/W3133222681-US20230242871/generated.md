## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The generation of primordial germ cell-like cells from human pluripotent stem cells has emerged as a critical frontier in reproductive biology, developmental medicine, and regenerative therapeutics. Primordial germ cells serve as the sole cellular precursors to gametes—eggs and sperm—and are responsible for the transmission of genetic and epigenetic information across generations. Their specification during early embryogenesis is a tightly regulated, evolutionarily conserved process that remains poorly understood in humans due to the ethical and technical limitations surrounding the study of post-implantation embryos. While mouse models have provided foundational insights into the molecular and cellular mechanisms of germ cell fate determination, significant differences in developmental timing, signaling requirements, and gene regulatory networks between rodents and primates necessitate direct investigation of human germline development. Prior efforts to recapitulate human primordial germ cell specification in vitro have relied heavily on three-dimensional aggregate cultures, which, while capable of generating germ cell-like populations, suffer from inherent heterogeneity, poor reproducibility, and limited accessibility for real-time molecular interrogation. These 3D systems introduce uncontrolled variables such as uneven diffusion of signaling molecules, variable cell-cell contact dynamics, and spatial gradients of growth factors that obscure the precise temporal and quantitative requirements of key developmental cues. As a result, the efficiency of primordial germ cell-like cell generation has remained low, typically ranging from 5% to 20% across diverse stem cell lines, and the identity of the resulting cells has been difficult to validate with high confidence due to the co-existence of non-germline lineages such as mesoderm and endoderm within the same culture. Furthermore, the absence of reliable, lineage-specific cell surface markers has precluded the purification of homogeneous primordial germ cell-like cell populations, thereby hindering downstream functional studies, transcriptomic profiling, and potential clinical applications. Although several surface markers—including EpCAM, ITGA6, PDPN, and alkaline phosphatase—have been proposed to enrich for primordial germ cell-like cells, these markers are also expressed on undifferentiated pluripotent stem cells, rendering them unsuitable for definitive isolation. The transcriptional trajectory of human primordial germ cell specification has also been a subject of debate. A prevailing model, largely extrapolated from murine studies, posits that pluripotency factors such as OCT4 and NANOG are transiently silenced during early differentiation and subsequently reactivated exclusively in cells committed to the germline lineage. However, recent observations in non-human primates suggest an alternative paradigm in which pluripotency genes remain continuously expressed throughout the transition from epiblast to primordial germ cells, implying a more direct molecular continuum between pluripotency and germline states. This hypothesis has remained untested in human systems due to the lack of a scalable, controllable, and analyzable in vitro model. Additionally, the role of WNT signaling in human primordial germ cell specification has been incompletely characterized; while its initial activation is known to be necessary for posterior epiblast formation, the requirement for its subsequent inhibition has not been systematically demonstrated or mechanistically explained. The absence of a robust, two-dimensional differentiation platform capable of recapitulating the dynamic signaling environment of early human embryogenesis has thus impeded progress in defining the precise molecular logic governing human germline commitment. Consequently, there exists a critical and unmet need for a simplified, reproducible, and high-efficiency method to generate human primordial germ cell-like cells in monolayer culture, coupled with a defined set of cell surface markers enabling their precise isolation and a clarified understanding of the transcriptional continuity between pluripotency and germline states. Such a platform would not only advance fundamental knowledge of human development but also enable the scalable production of purified germ cell precursors for applications in infertility modeling, genetic disease screening, and the eventual in vitro derivation of functional gametes.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel, simplified, and highly efficient method for the generation of human primordial germ cell-like cells in monolayer culture, wherein the temporal dynamics of WNT signaling are precisely controlled to enhance specification efficiency and fidelity. The method comprises two sequential phases: a first phase involving a brief, 12-hour exposure of human pluripotent stem cells to posteriorizing signals, including WNT agonists and TGFβ ligands, to generate a population of posterior epiblast-like intermediate cells that co-express pluripotency factors OCT4 and NANOG alongside primitive streak markers such as BRACHYURY and MIXL1; and a second phase in which WNT signaling is actively inhibited through the addition of a small molecule antagonist, while simultaneously providing BMP, SCF, and EGF ligands to drive commitment to the germline lineage. This approach results in the robust generation of human primordial germ cell-like cells within 3.5 days, achieving purities of 20% to 73% across multiple human embryonic and induced pluripotent stem cell lines without the need for transgenic reporters. The invention further discloses a novel combination of cell surface markers—CXCR4 as a positive marker and PDGFRα and GARP as negative markers—that enables the specific isolation of human primordial germ cell-like cells from heterogeneous differentiation cultures by fluorescence-activated cell sorting. This surface marker signature distinguishes primordial germ cell-like cells from undifferentiated pluripotent stem cells, mesodermal derivatives, and endodermal lineages, thereby permitting the purification of a nearly homogeneous population of cells with transcriptional and proteomic profiles that closely mirror those of early human fetal primordial germ cells. Single-cell RNA sequencing of these purified cells reveals that pluripotency transcription factors OCT4 and NANOG are not downregulated and subsequently re-expressed during germline specification, but rather are continuously expressed throughout the transition from pluripotent stem cells to posterior epiblast intermediates to mature primordial germ cell-like cells, establishing a direct molecular bridge between pluripotency and germline states. This finding contradicts the previously accepted model of pluripotency silencing and reactivation and provides a new paradigm for understanding how cell fate is maintained across lineage transitions. The invention further demonstrates that the monolayer-derived primordial germ cell-like cells exhibit a transcriptome-wide similarity to early-stage fetal germ cells isolated from human embryos between five and six weeks of gestation, validating their biological authenticity and developmental relevance. The method is scalable, serum-free, and compatible with a broad range of human pluripotent stem cell lines, including both male and female lines, and does not require genetic modification or reporter systems for efficient cell isolation. The invention thus provides a transformative platform for studying human germline development, modeling genetic causes of infertility, screening for germline mutations, and ultimately enabling the in vitro production of human gametes for reproductive medicine.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “human pluripotent stem cell” refers to any cell derived from the inner cell mass of a human blastocyst or reprogrammed from a somatic cell that retains the capacity to differentiate into all three embryonic germ layers—ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm—and to self-renew indefinitely under defined culture conditions. Human pluripotent stem cells include both human embryonic stem cells and human induced pluripotent stem cells. The term “primordial germ cell-like cell” refers to a cell generated in vitro from human pluripotent stem cells that exhibits molecular, phenotypic, and transcriptional characteristics consistent with those of native primordial germ cells as they exist during early human embryogenesis, including the expression of key germline markers such as NANOS3, PRDM1, TFAP2C, SOX17, and POU5F1, the absence of somatic lineage markers such as FOXA2, CDX2, HAND1, and PDGFRα, and the capacity to undergo further maturation toward meiotic competence when provided with appropriate environmental cues. The term “posterior epiblast” refers to a transient, developmentally intermediate cell population generated during the early stages of human pluripotent stem cell differentiation, characterized by the co-expression of pluripotency transcription factors OCT4 and NANOG with posterior epiblast and primitive streak-associated genes such as BRACHYURY, MIXL1, NODAL, and FGF8, and which serves as the direct precursor to primordial germ cell-like cells under the conditions described herein. The term “temporally dynamic WNT signaling” refers to the sequential, precisely timed activation of WNT signaling followed by its active inhibition during in vitro differentiation, wherein the initial activation is achieved through the addition of a WNT agonist such as CHIR99021 for a duration of 12 hours, and the subsequent inhibition is achieved through the addition of a WNT antagonist such as XAV939, thereby creating a signaling environment that mimics the endogenous regulatory logic of early human embryogenesis. The term “cell surface marker signature” refers to a combination of cell surface antigens whose expression pattern uniquely identifies a specific cell type within a heterogeneous population; in this invention, the signature CXCR4+PDGFRα−GARP− defines human primordial germ cell-like cells with high specificity and purity, distinguishing them from undifferentiated pluripotent stem cells and mis-differentiated mesodermal cells. The term “fluorescence-activated cell sorting” refers to a method of cell separation based on the detection of fluorescently labeled antibodies bound to specific cell surface antigens, enabling the isolation of a pure population of cells based on their surface marker expression profile. The term “transcriptional similarity” refers to the degree of overlap in gene expression patterns between two cell populations, as determined by high-resolution single-cell RNA sequencing, wherein the expression levels of hundreds to thousands of genes are compared to establish developmental relatedness. The term “continuous expression” refers to the sustained presence of a gene product, such as the transcription factor NANOG, throughout a developmental transition without detectable downregulation or interruption, indicating that the gene is not silenced and reactivated but rather remains active as a molecular bridge between cell states. The term “fetal primordial germ cell” refers to a native germ cell isolated from a human fetus during the early stages of gonadal development, typically between 5 and 8 weeks of gestation, which represents the in vivo counterpart of in vitro-derived primordial germ cell-like cells. The term “monolayer culture” refers to a two-dimensional cell growth system in which cells are adherent to a flat, planar substrate and are not aggregated into three-dimensional structures, thereby enabling uniform exposure to soluble factors and facilitating high-resolution imaging, molecular analysis, and scalable production. The term “serum-free medium” refers to a defined culture medium that does not contain animal-derived serum or undefined components, ensuring reproducibility, reducing batch-to-batch variability, and enhancing the safety profile for potential clinical applications. The term “differentiation efficiency” refers to the proportion of cells within a culture that acquire the desired cell fate, as quantified by the percentage of cells expressing specific germline markers such as NANOS3 or SOX17 following a defined differentiation protocol. The term “germline specification” refers to the process by which a pluripotent cell commits to the primordial germ cell lineage, involving the activation of germline-specific genes, the repression of somatic lineage genes, and the establishment of a transcriptional and epigenetic state distinct from that of neighboring somatic cells.

### Methods

The method of generating human primordial germ cell-like cells comprises a two-phase differentiation protocol conducted in a serum-free, monolayer culture system. In the first phase, undifferentiated human pluripotent stem cells are cultured in a basal medium comprising Advanced RPMI 1640 supplemented with B27, non-essential amino acids, penicillin, streptomycin, and L-glutamine. The cells are maintained in this medium for a period of 12 hours in the presence of Activin A at a concentration of 100 ng/mL and the WNT agonist CHIR99021 at a concentration of 3 μM, along with the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 at 10 μM to enhance cell survival during dissociation and plating. This combination of signals induces the transition of pluripotent stem cells into a posterior epiblast-like intermediate state, characterized by the co-expression of pluripotency factors OCT4 and NANOG with posterior epiblast markers such as BRACHYURY, MIXL1, and NODAL. Following this 12-hour exposure, the medium is removed, the cells are washed once with phosphate-buffered saline, and the second phase of differentiation is initiated. In the second phase, the cells are cultured for an additional 72 hours in the same basal medium supplemented with BMP4 at a concentration of 40 ng/mL, the WNT inhibitor XAV939 at 1 μM, and the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 at 10 μM for the first 24 hours. Subsequently, the medium is replaced with a formulation containing 100 ng/mL stem cell factor, 50 ng/mL epidermal growth factor, 1 μM XAV939, and 10 μM Y-27632 for an additional 24 hours, followed by a final 24-hour incubation with 40 ng/mL BMP4, 100 ng/mL stem cell factor, 50 ng/mL epidermal growth factor, 1 μM XAV939, and 10 μM Y-27632. Throughout the entire differentiation period, cells remain in monolayer culture and are not aggregated into three-dimensional structures. The use of XAV939, a tankyrase inhibitor that stabilizes AXIN and promotes β-catenin degradation, is critical for the inhibition of endogenous WNT signaling, which is otherwise produced by the differentiating cells and acts as a potent repressor of germline specification. The concentration of BMP4 is significantly lower than that used in prior 3D protocols—approximately 25-fold lower—demonstrating that monolayer culture enhances signaling efficiency and reduces the need for supraphysiological ligand concentrations. The ROCK inhibitor is included throughout the second phase to maintain cell viability, but its removal does not significantly impair differentiation efficiency, indicating that its primary role is supportive rather than instructive. The entire process is conducted under standard cell culture conditions at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2, and the medium is changed daily without disturbing the monolayer. Differentiation efficiency is quantified by flow cytometry using reporter lines such as NANOS3-mCherry or SOX17-GFP, or by immunostaining for germline-specific proteins such as PRDM1, TFAP2C, and NANOG. The method is reproducible across multiple human embryonic and induced pluripotent stem cell lines, including H1, H9, and multiple patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cell lines, regardless of sex or genetic background. The method does not require feeder cells, extracellular matrix coatings beyond initial Matrigel seeding, or genetic modification of the parental stem cell line, making it broadly applicable for research and potential clinical translation.

### Cell Compositions

The cell composition generated by the method of this invention comprises a heterogeneous population of cells at day 3.5 of differentiation, of which 20% to 73% are primordial germ cell-like cells, as defined by the co-expression of the germline-specific transcription factors NANOS3, PRDM1, TFAP2C, SOX17, and POU5F1, and the absence of markers associated with somatic lineages such as FOXA2, CDX2, HAND1, and PDGFRα. The remaining cells in the culture are predominantly mesoderm-like cells, as evidenced by the expression of cardiac and lateral mesoderm markers including HAND1, TMEM88, MYL4, and ACTC1, and to a lesser extent, cells with endodermal or residual pluripotent characteristics. The primordial germ cell-like cells exhibit a uniform morphology characterized by a rounded, refractile appearance, reduced cell size compared to undifferentiated pluripotent stem cells, and high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio. At the transcriptomic level, these cells express a core set of germline-defining genes including NANOS3, NANOG, PRDM1, TFAP2C, KLF4, TCL1B, and DAZL, while downregulating genes associated with pluripotency maintenance such as SOX2 and REX1, and suppressing genes involved in epithelial-mesenchymal transition and somatic differentiation. The cells do not express markers of extraembryonic lineages such as CDX2 or GATA6, nor do they express markers of definitive endoderm such as HHEX or SOX17 in combination with FOXA2, thereby confirming their lineage specificity. The primordial germ cell-like cells are capable of being isolated to greater than 97% purity using fluorescence-activated cell sorting based on the surface marker signature CXCR4+PDGFRα−GARP−. This purified population exhibits a transcriptional profile that is nearly identical to that of the bulk primordial germ cell-like cell population, with minimal contamination from non-germline cells. The purified cells retain their identity upon re-plating and can be maintained in culture for up to seven days without loss of germline marker expression, indicating a stable commitment to the germline fate. The cells do not spontaneously differentiate into somatic lineages under standard culture conditions, and their transcriptome is distinct from that of neural progenitors, cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, or endodermal cells. The cell composition generated by this method is free of residual undifferentiated pluripotent stem cells, as confirmed by the absence of OCT4 and NANOG expression in the CXCR4−PDGFRα+GARP+ fraction, and the lack of teratoma formation when transplanted into immunodeficient mice, although such in vivo assays are not required for the validation of the invention. The cell composition may be cryopreserved in standard freezing media containing dimethyl sulfoxide and serum replacement, and thawed with high viability and retention of germline marker expression, enabling long-term storage and distribution. The cell composition may also be used directly for downstream applications including single-cell RNA sequencing, chromatin accessibility assays, epigenetic profiling, and in vitro maturation protocols aimed at generating meiosis-competent gametes.

### METHODS OF USE

The method of this invention has multiple applications in basic research, disease modeling, and therapeutic development. One primary use is in the study of human germline development, wherein the monolayer platform enables high-resolution, real-time interrogation of the transcriptional, epigenetic, and signaling events that govern the transition from pluripotency to germline fate. The ability to isolate a pure population of primordial germ cell-like cells using the CXCR4+PDGFRα−GARP− signature allows for the performance of single-cell omics analyses, including RNA sequencing, ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq, and methylation profiling, to define the regulatory networks that control germline specification without confounding signals from contaminating lineages. Another use is in the modeling of genetic causes of infertility, wherein patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cells carrying mutations in genes such as DAZL, BOULE, or SYCP3 can be differentiated into primordial germ cell-like cells to assess the impact of these mutations on germ cell survival, proliferation, and gene expression. The method enables the comparison of germ cell development between fertile and infertile individuals, providing a platform for identifying molecular biomarkers of germline dysfunction. The invention may also be used for high-throughput drug screening to identify small molecules or growth factors that enhance the efficiency of primordial germ cell specification, or that rescue defects in germline development caused by genetic mutations. The purified primordial germ cell-like cells may be used as a source of material for in vitro maturation into oogonia or spermatogonia, with the ultimate goal of generating functional gametes for reproductive medicine. The method may be adapted to generate primordial germ cell-like cells from frozen or archived stem cell lines, enabling the creation of biobanks of germline precursors for future therapeutic use. The invention may also be employed in the development of artificial gametogenesis systems, wherein the primordial germ cell-like cells are co-cultured with somatic support cells derived from ovarian or testicular tissue to recapitulate the niche environment required for meiotic entry and gamete maturation. The method may be integrated with gene editing technologies such as CRISPR-Cas9 to correct disease-causing mutations in primordial germ cell-like cells prior to their use in assisted reproduction. The purified cells may also be used for the generation of chimeric embryos in non-human primate models to assess their developmental potential in vivo, although such experiments are subject to ethical and regulatory oversight. The invention further enables the study of epigenetic reprogramming during germline specification, including the erasure of DNA methylation and histone modifications, by providing a homogeneous population of cells at a defined developmental stage. The method may be used to investigate the effects of environmental toxins, endocrine disruptors, or nutritional factors on human germline development, providing insights into transgenerational inheritance of epigenetic states. The invention may also be applied in the field of synthetic biology, wherein the transcriptional network of primordial germ cell-like cells is reconstructed to design synthetic gene circuits that direct germ cell fate with enhanced precision. The method may be scaled to industrial bioreactor systems for the large-scale production of primordial germ cell-like cells for clinical-grade applications, including cell therapy for premature ovarian insufficiency or azoospermia. The invention thus provides a foundational platform for the study, manipulation, and potential clinical application of human germ cells.

### EXAMPLES

Example 1: Generation of human primordial germ cell-like cells using the monolayer differentiation protocol. Undifferentiated H9 human embryonic stem cells were maintained in mTeSR1 medium on Matrigel-coated plates and passaged every four to five days using Accutase. On the day of differentiation, cells were dissociated and plated at a density of 1.5 × 10^5 cells per cm² in aRB27 basal medium supplemented with 100 ng/mL Activin A, 3 μM CHIR99021, and 10 μM Y-27632. After 12 hours, the medium was removed, the cells were washed once with phosphate-buffered saline, and fresh medium containing 40 ng/mL BMP4, 1 μM XAV939, and 10 μM Y-27632 was added. After 24 hours, the medium was replaced with 100 ng/mL SCF, 50 ng/mL EGF, 1 μM XAV939, and 10 μM Y-27632. After an additional 24 hours, the medium was replaced with 40 ng/mL BMP4, 100 ng/mL SCF, 50 ng/mL EGF, 1 μM XAV939, and 10 μM Y-27632. On day 3.5, cells were harvested and analyzed by flow cytometry using an anti-NANOS3-mCherry antibody. The percentage of NANOS3-mCherry-positive cells was determined to be 32.7%. Immunostaining confirmed the co-expression of PRDM1, TFAP2C, and SOX17 in the NANOS3-positive population, while the absence of FOXA2 and CDX2 confirmed lineage specificity. The same protocol was applied to five additional human pluripotent stem cell lines, including H1, two male hiPSC lines, and two female hiPSC lines, yielding an average differentiation efficiency of 36.7% with a standard deviation of 22.6%, demonstrating broad applicability.

Example 2: Identification of the CXCR4+PDGFRα−GARP− surface marker signature. A high-throughput screen was performed using a panel of 369 cell surface antibodies conjugated to phycoerythrin, applied to SOX17-GFP human embryonic stem cells differentiated into primordial germ cell-like cells at day 3.5. Cells were analyzed by flow cytometry using a CytoFLEX instrument. The GFP-positive population was gated, and the mean fluorescence intensity of each antibody was compared between GFP-positive and GFP-negative cells. CXCR4 showed the highest specificity for the GFP-positive population, with a 12-fold increase in mean fluorescence intensity compared to negative cells. PDGFRα and GARP were found to be highly expressed in the GFP-negative population, with 95% and 93% of negative cells expressing these markers, respectively. A combination of CXCR4 positivity with PDGFRα and GARP negativity was found to isolate 97.2% of the SOX17-GFP-positive population, with a purity of 98.1% as confirmed by single-cell RNA sequencing. This signature was validated across five independent stem cell lines and was shown to be superior to previously reported markers such as EpCAM, ITGA6, and PDPN, which were expressed on undifferentiated stem cells and thus unsuitable for purification.

Example 3: Demonstration of continuous NANOG expression during germline specification. A NANOG-2A-YFP reporter human embryonic stem cell line was generated using CRISPR-Cas9 and AAV6-mediated genome editing to insert a 2A-YFP cassette immediately downstream of the NANOG stop codon without disrupting the coding sequence. Differentiation was performed as described in Example 1, and cells were analyzed daily by flow cytometry for NANOG-YFP and CXCR4 expression. Undifferentiated stem cells were NANOG-YFP+CXCR4−. By day 0.5, a majority of cells remained NANOG-YFP+ but began to express low levels of CXCR4. By day 2.5, two distinct populations emerged: one that was NANOG-YFP+CXCR4+ (primordial germ cell-like cells) and another that was NANOG-YFP−CXCR4− (non-germline cells). Intracellular staining for NANOG protein confirmed the continuous expression of NANOG in the germline lineage, with no detectable downregulation between day 0 and day 3.5. Pseudotime analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing data confirmed that NANOG expression remained stable throughout the trajectory from pluripotent stem cells to primordial germ cell-like cells, with no dip in expression corresponding to a hypothetical silencing phase.

Example 4: Transcriptomic comparison of in vitro-derived primordial germ cell-like cells with human fetal primordial germ cells. Single-cell RNA sequencing was performed on purified CXCR4+PDGFRα−GARP− primordial germ cell-like cells generated by the method of this invention and compared to a published dataset of human fetal germ cells from 5 to 26 weeks of gestation. The in vitro-derived cells were found to cluster most closely with the FGC1 population, which represents early, pre-migratory primordial germ cells. The expression of key germline genes including NANOS3, PRDM1, TFAP2C, and KLF4 was highly correlated between the two populations, with Pearson correlation coefficients exceeding 0.85. Genes associated with later stages of germ cell development, such as those involved in retinoid signaling and meiosis, were absent in the in vitro-derived cells, confirming their similarity to early fetal germ cells. Hierarchical clustering and principal component analysis confirmed that the in vitro-derived cells formed a distinct cluster separate from somatic cells and endodermal lineages, and were more similar to fetal germ cells than to any other cell type in the dataset.

Example 5: Scalability and reproducibility across diverse stem cell lines. The monolayer differentiation protocol was applied to five additional human pluripotent stem cell lines, including three male and two female lines derived from individuals with diverse genetic backgrounds. Each line was differentiated independently in triplicate, and the percentage of CXCR4+PDGFRα−GARP− cells was quantified by flow cytometry. The average efficiency across all lines was 36.7% ± 22.6%, with individual lines achieving efficiencies ranging from 14.2% to 73.2%. Immunostaining confirmed the expression of PRDM1, TFAP2C, and SOX17 in all lines, and qPCR analysis confirmed the upregulation of germline-specific transcripts and the downregulation of somatic markers. No significant differences in efficiency were observed between male and female lines, indicating that the method is sex-neutral and robust across genetic variation.
