# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of lipid signaling has undergone significant transformation over the past two decades, particularly with the recognition of sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) as a critical bioactive lipid mediator that governs a wide array of cellular processes essential to both physiological homeostasis and pathological states. S1P is generated through the phosphorylation of sphingosine by two closely related but functionally distinct enzymes known as sphingosine kinase 1 (SphK1) and sphingosine kinase 2 (SphK2). These enzymes catalyze the conversion of sphingosine into S1P, thereby shifting the balance of the sphingolipid rheostat—a dynamic equilibrium between pro-apoptotic ceramide and sphingosine and the pro-survival S1P—that dictates cell fate decisions including proliferation, migration, survival, and programmed cell death. While SphK1 has been extensively studied and is widely recognized for its role in promoting oncogenic signaling, particularly in contexts such as tumor angiogenesis, metastasis, and resistance to chemotherapy, the biological functions of SphK2 have remained more enigmatic and, at times, contradictory. Early studies suggested that SphK2 exerted pro-apoptotic effects, particularly when overexpressed, leading to the hypothesis that it functioned as a counterbalance to SphK1. However, subsequent investigations have revealed that SphK2 plays a more nuanced and context-dependent role, with evidence now indicating that its activity supports tumor cell proliferation, migration, and survival in multiple cancer types, including glioblastoma, breast cancer, and leukemia. The subcellular localization of SphK2, which includes the nucleus, endoplasmic reticulum, and mitochondria, further distinguishes it from the predominantly cytosolic SphK1 and suggests unique intracellular signaling functions. Notably, SphK2 has been implicated in the regulation of histone deacetylase activity through the nuclear production of S1P, thereby influencing epigenetic modifications and gene expression patterns that contribute to malignant transformation. Additionally, SphK2 has been shown to modulate mitochondrial function, impacting energy metabolism and apoptosis sensitivity in ways that are distinct from SphK1-mediated pathways. Despite these advances, the pharmacological tools available to interrogate SphK2 function remain severely limited. While several potent and selective inhibitors of SphK1 have been developed and are now in preclinical and clinical evaluation, the number of compounds that selectively target SphK2 with high potency is exceedingly small. Existing SphK2 inhibitors such as ABC 294640, SG-12, R-FTY720-OMe, and trans-12 exhibit moderate inhibitory activity, often accompanied by off-target effects, poor pharmacokinetic profiles, or insufficient selectivity over SphK1, which limits their utility as mechanistic probes or therapeutic candidates. The lack of chemically diverse, highly selective, and potent SphK2 inhibitors has hindered the ability of researchers to fully elucidate the distinct roles of SphK2 in cancer biology, immune regulation, and other disease contexts. Moreover, the absence of a crystal structure for either SphK1 or SphK2 has impeded structure-based drug design efforts, leaving medicinal chemistry initiatives largely reliant on iterative screening and empirical optimization. In this context, there exists a compelling and unmet need for novel chemical scaffolds capable of selectively inhibiting SphK2 with high potency, minimal off-target activity, and favorable drug-like properties. Such compounds would not only serve as invaluable tools for dissecting the biological functions of SphK2 but could also form the foundation for the development of next-generation therapeutics targeting cancers and inflammatory conditions where SphK2 activity is pathologically elevated. The present invention addresses this critical gap by introducing a new class of thiazolidine-2,4-dione derivatives that demonstrate unprecedented selectivity for SphK2 over SphK1, along with potent cellular and in vivo activity, thereby establishing a novel pharmacological platform for the targeted modulation of S1P signaling.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of compounds that selectively inhibit sphingosine kinase 2 (SphK2) with high potency and minimal activity against sphingosine kinase 1 (SphK1), thereby enabling the precise pharmacological modulation of S1P signaling pathways that are critically involved in cancer progression, immune dysregulation, and other pathological conditions. The compounds of the invention are characterized by a core structure comprising a thiazolidine-2,4-dione ring substituted at the 3-position with a 2-aminoethyl group and at the 5-position with an alkylidene linker terminating in an aromatic ring bearing an alkoxy substituent. The specific spatial arrangement of these moieties, particularly the length and flexibility of the alkyl chain connecting the thiazolidine-2,4-dione core to the aromatic ring, along with the electronic and steric properties of the alkoxy group, confers exceptional selectivity for SphK2 over SphK1, a distinction that has eluded prior art compounds. The lead compound of the invention, designated as K145, is exemplified by the chemical structure of 3-(2-aminoethyl)-5-[3-(4-butoxyphenyl)propylidene]thiazolidine-2,4-dione, which demonstrates an IC50 value of approximately 4.3 µM against recombinant human SphK2 while showing no measurable inhibition of SphK1 at concentrations up to 10 µM. This selectivity profile is further corroborated by molecular modeling studies, which reveal that the compound binds preferentially within the sphingosine-binding pocket of SphK2 through a network of hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions that are not optimally accommodated in the homologous region of SphK1, primarily due to a single amino acid substitution—Gln346 in SphK2 versus Glu180 in SphK1—that disrupts a key polar interaction with the carbonyl oxygen of the thiazolidine-2,4-dione ring. The compounds of the invention are not only potent inhibitors of SphK2 enzymatic activity in vitro but also effectively penetrate cells, reduce intracellular S1P levels without altering ceramide or ceramide-1-phosphate concentrations, and inhibit the phosphorylation of FTY720, a known substrate of SphK2, thereby confirming target engagement in a cellular context. In human leukemia U937 cells, the compounds induce concentration-dependent suppression of proliferation and promote late-stage apoptosis, concomitant with the downregulation of phosphorylated ERK and Akt, two key downstream effectors of S1P-mediated survival signaling. These cellular effects are mirrored in vivo, where administration of the compounds via intraperitoneal or oral routes significantly inhibits the growth of human leukemia U937 xenografts in immunodeficient mice and murine JC mammary adenocarcinoma tumors in immunocompetent BALB/c mice, with tumor growth inhibition rates exceeding 40% at well-tolerated doses. Importantly, no significant body weight loss or histopathological signs of toxicity in major organs are observed following treatment, indicating a favorable safety profile. The compounds are orally bioavailable, as demonstrated by their efficacy following oral gavage administration, and are detectable in tumor tissue at pharmacologically relevant concentrations. Furthermore, the compounds do not inhibit other lipid kinases, including ceramide kinase, nor do they exhibit significant off-target activity against a panel of eleven additional protein kinases, underscoring their high degree of selectivity. The invention thus encompasses a broad genus of compounds defined by Formula I, wherein the substituents on the thiazolidine-2,4-dione scaffold are optimized to enhance potency, selectivity, metabolic stability, and pharmacokinetic properties. The compounds of the invention represent the first chemically distinct class of SphK2-selective inhibitors derived from a thiazolidine-2,4-dione scaffold, and they provide a novel platform for the development of therapeutics targeting diseases driven by aberrant SphK2 activity, including hematological malignancies, solid tumors, autoimmune disorders, and inflammatory conditions. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the compounds of Formula I in association with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, methods of treating diseases characterized by elevated SphK2 activity by administering an effective amount of the compound, and methods of inhibiting SphK2 in a biological sample by contacting the sample with the compound. The invention also includes diagnostic and research kits containing the compounds for the detection and modulation of SphK2 activity in cells or tissues.
