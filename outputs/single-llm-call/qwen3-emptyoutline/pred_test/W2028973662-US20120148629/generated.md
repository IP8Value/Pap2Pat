# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunomodulatory compositions and therapeutic interventions for the management of allergic diseases, particularly food allergies. More specifically, the invention concerns a novel probiotic strain, Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, and its use in the reduction of allergic symptoms in individuals who have already undergone sensitization to an allergen. The invention further encompasses the use of this strain in the formulation of pharmaceutical, nutraceutical, or dietary compositions intended for oral administration to modulate localized immune responses in the gastrointestinal tract, particularly within the ileum, by suppressing the expression of key Th2-associated cytokines and chemokines without altering systemic humoral immunity. The invention is particularly directed toward the management phase of allergic disease progression, wherein sensitization has already occurred and clinical manifestations are present or imminent upon allergen re-exposure. This distinguishes the invention from prior approaches that focus on prevention of initial sensitization, as the present composition demonstrates no preventive effect but exerts a potent and specific therapeutic effect during the symptomatic phase of allergy. The invention further provides a method for identifying individuals who would benefit from such intervention based on the presence of elevated Th2 immune markers in the intestinal mucosa, thereby enabling targeted, mechanism-based therapeutic strategies for food allergy management.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Allergic diseases, including food allergies, have experienced a dramatic global increase in prevalence over the past several decades, particularly in industrialized nations, with childhood onset being the most common presentation. The pathogenesis of food allergy involves a dysregulated adaptive immune response characterized by the aberrant activation of CD4+ T helper 2 (Th2) cells, which secrete a constellation of cytokines—most notably interleukin-4 (IL-4), interleukin-5 (IL-5), and interleukin-13 (IL-13)—that drive IgE production, eosinophil recruitment, mast cell activation, and epithelial barrier dysfunction. Sensitization to food allergens such as ovalbumin, milk proteins, or egg components typically occurs during early life through oral or mucosal exposure, often in the context of disrupted gut microbiota or impaired intestinal barrier integrity. Once sensitization is established, subsequent exposure to the allergen triggers a cascade of inflammatory events that culminate in clinical symptoms ranging from mild pruritus and gastrointestinal distress to life-threatening anaphylaxis. 

Current therapeutic strategies for food allergy are largely limited to allergen avoidance, emergency treatment with epinephrine, and, in some cases, oral immunotherapy (OIT), which aims to induce desensitization through controlled, incremental exposure. However, OIT carries significant risks of adverse reactions, does not reliably induce long-term tolerance, and is not effective in all patients. Moreover, no approved therapies exist that specifically target the underlying Th2-driven inflammatory cascade at the mucosal level without inducing systemic immunosuppression or altering humoral immunity. Probiotics have been extensively investigated as potential modulators of allergic responses due to their capacity to interact with gut-associated lymphoid tissue and influence dendritic cell maturation, T-cell polarization, and cytokine secretion. Numerous studies have explored the use of Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains in both prevention and management of atopic diseases, yet results have been inconsistent, often attributable to strain-specific effects, timing of administration, and variability in clinical models. 

Critically, the distinction between prevention—intervention prior to or during the sensitization phase—and management—intervention after sensitization has occurred and symptoms are present—has been inadequately addressed in prior art. Most probiotic studies have focused on prophylactic administration in neonates or infants at high risk of allergy, with limited exploration of therapeutic utility in already sensitized individuals. Furthermore, many studies have relied on systemic immunological endpoints such as serum IgE levels or mast cell protease concentrations as primary indicators of efficacy, despite evidence that these markers may not correlate with mucosal inflammation or clinical symptom severity. The present invention addresses these gaps by identifying a specific probiotic strain, Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, that lacks preventive activity but demonstrates a robust and reproducible ability to reduce clinical symptoms of food allergy when administered during the management phase. Importantly, this effect is mediated not through modulation of systemic antibody titers or mast cell degranulation, but through localized suppression of IL-13 expression and downstream Th2 chemokines—CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL17 (TARC)—specifically within the ileal mucosa. This site-specific immunomodulatory action represents a novel mechanism of therapeutic intervention that has not been previously described or exploited in the context of food allergy management. The invention therefore provides a paradigm shift in the approach to probiotic therapy, moving from broad, non-specific immune stimulation toward precision modulation of localized inflammatory pathways in the gut.

## Prevention of Allergies can be Achieved on Different Levels:

Allergic disease can be conceptualized as a multi-phase process that unfolds over time, beginning with initial exposure to an allergen, followed by immune sensitization, and culminating in the manifestation of clinical symptoms upon re-exposure. These phases represent distinct biological windows during which therapeutic intervention may be applied, each requiring a fundamentally different mechanistic approach. The first level of intervention, referred to as prevention, aims to inhibit the initial development of allergen-specific immune memory. This phase typically occurs during early childhood, when the immune system is still maturing and the gut microbiota is being established. Interventions at this level often involve the administration of immunomodulatory agents—such as probiotics, prebiotics, or dietary components—prior to or concurrent with the first significant exposure to the allergen. The goal is to promote immune tolerance by skewing dendritic cell function toward regulatory phenotypes, enhancing epithelial barrier integrity, and suppressing the Th2 polarization that leads to IgE class switching and mast cell priming. However, prevention strategies are inherently limited by the need for early identification of at-risk individuals and the challenge of predicting which allergens will trigger sensitization. Moreover, as demonstrated in the present invention, not all immunomodulatory agents are effective at this stage; Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, for instance, showed no significant impact on the development of sensitization or the subsequent allergic response when administered prior to allergen exposure.

The second level of intervention, termed management, occurs after sensitization has been established and the immune system has developed a memory response to the allergen. At this stage, the individual may remain asymptomatic until re-exposure triggers an effector response mediated by allergen-specific IgE, mast cells, eosinophils, and Th2 cytokines. Management strategies are therefore designed to interrupt or dampen this effector phase, reducing symptom severity without necessarily eradicating the underlying immune memory. This is the critical phase targeted by the present invention. Unlike prevention, which seeks to alter immune development, management focuses on modulating the inflammatory milieu at the site of allergen encounter—in this case, the ileal mucosa. The invention demonstrates that Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, when administered during this window, does not affect systemic IgE, IgG1, IgG2a, or mast cell-derived MMCP-1 levels, indicating that its mechanism is independent of classical humoral or mast cell pathways. Instead, it exerts a highly localized effect by suppressing the expression of IL-13, a central cytokine in the chronic phase of allergic inflammation, and its downstream chemokine effectors CCL11 and CCL17, which are responsible for eosinophil recruitment and T-cell trafficking to the intestinal mucosa. This distinction is of profound clinical significance: it suggests that a therapeutic agent can be effective in alleviating symptoms without altering the systemic immune profile, thereby avoiding the risks associated with broad immunosuppression. Furthermore, it implies that the efficacy of such an agent is contingent upon its ability to reach and persist in the specific anatomical site where the inflammatory cascade is most active—in this case, the ileum. This level of spatial specificity has not been previously documented in probiotic-based allergy therapies and represents a novel paradigm in mucosal immunotherapy.

A third, less commonly considered level of intervention involves long-term immune reprogramming or tolerance induction, which may follow repeated administration of a management agent over an extended period. While the present invention does not explicitly address this phase, the observed suppression of IL-13 and chemokine expression suggests that sustained administration of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 could potentially lead to epigenetic or transcriptional reprogramming of local T cells or dendritic cells, shifting the balance from a Th2-dominant to a more regulatory or balanced state. This possibility opens the door for future investigations into whether this strain can be used not only for acute symptom control but also as part of a longer-term strategy to modify the natural history of food allergy. Importantly, the invention establishes that the timing of intervention—whether during prevention or management—is not merely a logistical consideration but a fundamental determinant of biological outcome. The failure of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 to prevent sensitization, coupled with its potent efficacy in managing symptoms, underscores that probiotic effects are not universal but are instead context-dependent, strain-specific, and phase-specific. This insight fundamentally alters the way probiotics should be evaluated and deployed in clinical practice, moving away from a one-size-fits-all approach toward a precision medicine model in which the choice of strain and timing of administration are determined by the phase of disease progression.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and unexpected therapeutic approach for the management of food allergy symptoms in sensitized individuals through the oral administration of a specific probiotic strain, Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, which demonstrates no preventive effect but exerts a potent and localized immunomodulatory action during the symptomatic phase of allergy. The invention is based on the discovery that this strain, when administered to individuals who have already undergone allergen sensitization, significantly reduces clinical manifestations of food allergy without altering systemic markers of humoral immunity such as allergen-specific IgE, IgG1, or IgG2a, or mast cell activation as measured by serum MMCP-1 levels. Instead, the therapeutic effect is mediated through the selective suppression of interleukin-13 (IL-13) gene expression and the downregulation of Th2-associated chemokines CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL17 (TARC) specifically within the ileal mucosa, a site identified as the primary locus of inflammatory activity during food allergen challenge. This site-specific modulation occurs without affecting gene expression in the jejunum, demonstrating a remarkable anatomical precision in the strain’s mechanism of action. The invention further provides a method for identifying candidates for this therapy based on the presence of elevated IL-13 and chemokine expression in intestinal tissue, thereby enabling personalized, mechanism-driven intervention.

The invention encompasses a composition comprising Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 in a viable, non-replicating, or lyophilized form, optionally combined with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, for use in the manufacture of a medicament intended for the management of food allergy symptoms in sensitized individuals. The composition is administered orally, preferably in the form of a liquid, capsule, tablet, or food supplement, and is administered during the management phase, defined as the period following allergen sensitization and prior to or during allergen re-exposure. The invention further provides a method of treating food allergy in a subject who has been previously sensitized to an allergen, comprising the step of orally administering an effective amount of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 to reduce clinical symptoms associated with allergen challenge. The method is characterized by the absence of effect on systemic IgE, IgG1, IgG2a, or MMCP-1 levels, and the presence of a statistically significant reduction in IL-13, CCL11, and CCL17 gene expression in the ileal mucosa. The invention further provides diagnostic criteria for selecting subjects who are likely to respond to this therapy, including the detection of elevated IL-13 mRNA levels in intestinal biopsies or fecal samples, or the presence of clinical symptoms following allergen exposure in the absence of complete desensitization. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in probiotic-based allergy therapy, moving from broad immunomodulation to targeted, site-specific suppression of mucosal inflammation, and provides the first evidence that a probiotic strain can exert therapeutic efficacy in food allergy by modulating a single cytokine pathway in a specific intestinal segment without altering systemic immunity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Effect of the Composition:

The composition comprising Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 exerts a profound and specific effect on the clinical and immunological manifestations of food allergy when administered during the management phase, but not during the prevention phase. In a murine model of ovalbumin-induced food allergy, oral administration of the strain at a dose of 5 × 10⁸ colony-forming units per milliliter in drinking water during the period immediately following the final sensitization and prior to allergen challenge resulted in a statistically significant reduction in clinical symptom scores compared to sensitized control animals. This reduction was evident across multiple parameters, including decreased scratching behavior, reduced incidence of diarrhea, and avoidance of severe symptoms such as labored respiration or cyanosis. Notably, this therapeutic effect was not accompanied by any significant change in serum levels of allergen-specific immunoglobulins, including IgE, IgG1, or IgG2a, nor was there a reduction in mast cell degranulation as measured by mouse mast cell protease-1 (MMCP-1). These findings indicate that the mechanism of action is independent of the classical IgE-mediated mast cell activation pathway, which is typically targeted by antihistamines or anti-IgE biologics. Instead, the beneficial effect is mediated through a localized immunomodulatory event within the intestinal mucosa, specifically in the ileum, where the relative expression levels of IL-13, CCL11, and CCL17 were markedly reduced following administration of the strain. The suppression of IL-13, a central cytokine in the maintenance of chronic Th2 inflammation, is particularly significant because it drives the expression of chemokines that recruit eosinophils and other inflammatory cells to the site of allergen exposure. The reduction in CCL11 and CCL17 further confirms the downstream impact of IL-13 inhibition, as these chemokines are known to be transcriptionally regulated by IL-13 signaling through the IL-4Rα receptor. The absence of any effect on IL-4, IL-5, or IL-10 expression, as well as the lack of impact on gene expression in the jejunum, underscores the precision of this mechanism. This suggests that Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 does not act as a general anti-inflammatory agent but rather as a targeted modulator of a specific cytokine-chemokine axis in a defined anatomical location. The strain’s ability to selectively suppress this pathway without altering systemic immunity makes it uniquely suited for long-term management of food allergy, as it avoids the risks of broad immunosuppression while effectively mitigating symptoms. Furthermore, the fact that the same strain had no effect when administered during the prevention phase indicates that its activity is contingent upon the presence of an established inflammatory environment, suggesting that it may interact with activated dendritic cells, epithelial cells, or regulatory T cells that are only present or functionally altered following sensitization. This phase-specificity is a critical feature of the invention, distinguishing it from all prior probiotic interventions that have sought to prevent sensitization rather than treat established disease.

### Composition of the Invention or for Use in the Invention:

The composition of the invention comprises Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, a lactic acid bacterial strain originally isolated from a dairy starter culture and deposited at the Collection Nationale de Cultures de Microorganismes at Institut Pasteur, Paris, France under accession number CNCM I-4154. The strain is maintained in a viable, metabolically active state and may be formulated in a variety of delivery systems suitable for oral administration, including liquid suspensions, freeze-dried powders, enteric-coated capsules, tablets, or food matrices such as fermented dairy products, infant formulas, or nutritional supplements. The composition may contain a minimum effective dose of 1 × 10⁸ colony-forming units per day, with optimal efficacy observed at 5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL in drinking water, corresponding to an approximate daily intake of 1 × 10⁹ to 5 × 10⁹ CFU in a human equivalent dose. The strain may be administered in its live form, or in a non-viable, heat-killed, or lyophilized state, provided that the immunomodulatory properties are preserved, as the active components may include cell wall structures, secreted metabolites, or extracellular vesicles that retain biological activity even in the absence of viability. The composition may further include pharmaceutically acceptable excipients such as stabilizers, cryoprotectants, prebiotic fibers, or buffering agents to enhance survival through the gastrointestinal tract and ensure consistent delivery to the ileum. The composition is intended for use in the manufacture of a medicament for the management of food allergy symptoms in individuals who have already undergone allergen sensitization, as defined by the presence of allergen-specific IgE, a history of clinical reaction upon exposure, or elevated mucosal expression of IL-13 and Th2-associated chemokines. The composition is not intended for use in the prevention of sensitization, as it has been demonstrated to be ineffective in this context. The formulation may be designed for daily administration over a period of at least seven days, with optimal results observed after a minimum of ten days of continuous dosing prior to allergen challenge. The composition may be co-administered with other therapeutic agents, including antihistamines, corticosteroids, or probiotics of different strains, provided that such combinations do not interfere with the strain’s ability to suppress IL-13 and chemokine expression in the ileum. The composition may also be packaged with diagnostic tools for the identification of eligible subjects, such as assays for fecal or serum biomarkers of Th2 inflammation, thereby enabling a precision medicine approach to food allergy management.

### Target Group:

The target group for the invention consists of individuals who have been previously sensitized to a food allergen and who experience recurrent clinical symptoms upon re-exposure, but who have not achieved sustained unresponsiveness through other forms of immunotherapy. This includes children and adults with documented IgE-mediated food allergies to common allergens such as cow’s milk, hen’s egg, wheat, soy, or peanut, who continue to experience gastrointestinal, cutaneous, or respiratory symptoms despite avoidance strategies or partial desensitization through oral immunotherapy. The invention is particularly suited for individuals who exhibit elevated levels of Th2 cytokines or chemokines in the intestinal mucosa, as detected by biopsy, stool-based RNA analysis, or non-invasive biomarker panels. The target population may also include individuals who are intolerant to or have contraindications for conventional immunotherapy due to the risk of anaphylaxis, or those who have failed to achieve clinical tolerance despite prolonged treatment. The invention is not intended for use in individuals who have not been sensitized, as administration during the prevention phase has been shown to be ineffective. It is also not intended for individuals with non-IgE-mediated food intolerances, such as lactose intolerance or celiac disease, as the mechanism of action is specific to Th2-driven allergic inflammation. The invention may be particularly beneficial for pediatric populations, given the high prevalence of food allergies in early childhood and the potential for long-term management to alter disease trajectory. The target group may be further refined by clinical criteria, including the presence of eosinophilic infiltration in the ileum, elevated serum IL-13 levels, or a history of recurrent abdominal pain, vomiting, or diarrhea following allergen exposure. Diagnostic tools may be employed to confirm eligibility, ensuring that therapy is directed only to those individuals whose immune profile is consistent with the mechanism of action of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287. This precision approach minimizes unnecessary treatment, reduces healthcare costs, and enhances therapeutic efficacy by aligning the intervention with the underlying biology of the disease.

## Experimental Results, Section 1:

In a series of controlled in vivo experiments using a well-established murine model of ovalbumin-induced food allergy, the therapeutic potential of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 was evaluated under two distinct administration paradigms: prevention and management. In the prevention paradigm, the strain was administered ad libitum in drinking water at a concentration of 5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL beginning five days prior to the first oral sensitization with ovalbumin and cholera toxin, and continued throughout the seven-week sensitization period and subsequent challenge. In the management paradigm, administration was initiated only after the final sensitization, on day 43, and continued for eight days until the time of allergen challenge on day 50. Clinical symptoms were assessed for 30 minutes following challenge using a validated scoring system that evaluated scratching behavior, fur condition, stool consistency, respiratory distress, and signs of anaphylaxis. Mice in the positive control group, sensitized but not treated, exhibited a mean clinical score of 1.9 ± 0.3 in the exploratory experiment and 2.5 ± 0.31 in the confirmatory experiment, both significantly higher than the negative control group (0.5 ± 0.17 and 0.4 ± 0.16, respectively; P < 0.01). In contrast, mice receiving Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 during the prevention phase displayed mean clinical scores of 2.4 ± 0.37 and 1.7 ± 0.42, which were not statistically different from the positive control group (P = 0.97 and P = 0.18, respectively), indicating no preventive effect. However, mice treated during the management phase exhibited mean scores of 0.9 ± 0.26 and 1.3 ± 0.37, representing a statistically significant reduction compared to the positive control in the confirmatory experiment (P = 0.038) and a strong trend toward significance in the exploratory experiment (P = 0.08). These results demonstrate that the strain’s efficacy is strictly confined to the management phase and is not attributable to general immune enhancement or altered gut microbiota composition prior to sensitization. Serum levels of MMCP-1, a marker of mast cell activation, were elevated in the positive control group compared to the negative control (1107 ± 322 pg/mL vs. 3.8 ± 2.2 pg/mL; P ≤ 0.001), but remained unchanged in both the prevention (2113 ± 521 pg/mL; P = 0.17) and management (773 ± 298 pg/mL; P = 0.48) groups, confirming that the strain does not inhibit mast cell degranulation. Similarly, allergen-specific IgE, IgG1, and IgG2a levels were significantly elevated in the positive control group compared to the negative control, but were unaffected by administration of the strain in either phase, with no statistically significant differences observed between treated and untreated sensitized groups (P ≥ 0.1 for IgE, P ≥ 0.3 for IgG1 and IgG2a). These findings collectively establish that the therapeutic effect of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 is independent of classical humoral or mast cell-mediated pathways and must therefore be mediated through an alternative, mucosa-specific mechanism.

## Experimental Results, Section 2:

To investigate the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying the observed clinical improvement in the management phase, ex vivo cytokine production by mesenteric lymph node (MLN) cells was assessed following antigen restimulation. MLN cells were harvested from mice in all experimental groups, cultured in the presence or absence of ovalbumin, and supernatants were analyzed for Th2 cytokines using multiplex ELISA. Cells from the negative control group secreted minimal levels of IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, and IL-13, consistent with a non-sensitized state. In contrast, MLN cells from the positive control group exhibited significantly elevated secretion of all four cytokines, reflecting a robust Th2-polarized response to allergen re-exposure. Administration of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 during the prevention phase did not alter this cytokine profile; levels of IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, and IL-13 were comparable to those in the positive control group. However, in the management group, a marked reduction in IL-13 secretion was observed, with mean concentrations decreasing by approximately 40% compared to the positive control (P = 0.08 in the exploratory experiment and trending toward significance in the confirmatory experiment). No significant changes were observed in the secretion of IL-4, IL-5, or IL-10, indicating a selective suppression of IL-13 production. This finding was corroborated by quantitative gene expression analysis of ileal and jejunal tissue. In the ileum, IL-13 mRNA expression was increased by more than 100-fold in the positive control group compared to the negative control (992.6 ± 360.2 vs. 7.3 ± 1.2; P ≤ 0.01), while administration of the strain during the management phase reduced this expression by nearly 90% (124 ± 62; P = 0.015). In contrast, no significant difference in IL-13 expression was observed in the jejunum across any group, confirming the site-specific nature of the effect. Similar results were obtained for IL-5, with a non-significant trend toward reduction in the ileum (177 ± 95 vs. 726 ± 403; P = 0.1) but no change in the jejunum. The expression of Th2-associated chemokines CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL17 (TARC) was also analyzed. In the ileum, CCL11 expression was elevated 3.2-fold in the positive control group compared to the negative control (24,904 ± 6,797 vs. 7,809 ± 3,286; P = 0.006), and was reduced by over 79% in the management group (5,172 ± 1,341; P = 0.002). CCL17 expression was increased nearly tenfold in the positive control group (3,943 ± 888 vs. 400 ± 177; P = 0.001) and reduced by more than 56% in the management group (1,716 ± 521; P = 0.04). Again, no significant changes were observed in either chemokine in the jejunum. These results demonstrate that Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 exerts a highly localized effect on the ileal mucosa, selectively suppressing the IL-13–CCL11/CCL17 axis without affecting systemic immunity or other intestinal segments. The precision of this effect suggests a direct interaction between the bacterial strain and immune cells or epithelial structures within the ileum, potentially mediated by microbial metabolites, surface molecules, or secreted factors that modulate local gene transcription.

## Analytical Methods:

### Reagents and Bacterial Biomass:

Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 was obtained from the Nestlé Culture Collection (NCC) and deposited at the Collection Nationale de Cultures de Microorganismes at Institut Pasteur, Paris, France under accession number CNCM I-4154. The strain was propagated in M17 broth (Oxoid, Basingstoke, UK) supplemented with 0.5% glucose under aerobic conditions at 30°C with agitation. Growth kinetics were determined by measuring optical density at 600 nm over a 24-hour period, and bacterial cells were harvested during the early stationary phase, approximately three hours after the transition from exponential growth, to ensure maximal metabolic activity and immunomodulatory potency. Harvested cells were centrifuged at 5,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4°C, washed twice in sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and resuspended in PBS containing 20% glycerol. Aliquots were frozen at −80°C and stored until use. Viability was confirmed by plate counting on M17 agar after serial dilution and incubation for 48 hours at 30°C. For in vivo administration, frozen biomass was thawed, diluted in sterile water, and administered ad libitum in drinking water at a concentration of 5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL. Control groups received sterile water or PBS alone. All reagents used for in vitro and in vivo analyses were of molecular biology grade and free of endotoxin contamination, as verified by Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) assay. All bacterial manipulations were performed under aseptic conditions in a laminar flow cabinet, and all culture media were sterilized by autoclaving at 121°C for 15 minutes prior to use.

### Isolation and Culture of Th2-Skewed Human PBMC:

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from healthy adult donors with no history of allergic disease using density gradient centrifugation over Ficoll-Paque Premium (GE Healthcare, Uppsala, Sweden). Cells were washed three times in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1% L-glutamine, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, and 50 μM β-mercaptoethanol. PBMCs were seeded at a density of 1 × 10⁶ cells per well in 24-well plates and stimulated with phytohemagglutinin (PHA, 5 μg/mL; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) and recombinant human IL-4 (10 ng/mL; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) for 72 hours to induce a Th2-skewed phenotype. After stimulation, cells were washed and resuspended in fresh medium containing Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 10:1. Co-cultures were maintained for an additional 24 hours, after which supernatants were collected for cytokine analysis and cells were harvested for RNA extraction. Control cultures received medium alone or heat-killed bacteria. Cytokine levels in supernatants were quantified by ELISA for IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, and IL-10. Gene expression analysis was performed using real-time PCR to assess the transcriptional profile of Th2-associated genes. The results demonstrated a significant reduction in IL-13 production and mRNA expression in PBMCs co-cultured with live Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, but not with heat-killed bacteria, indicating that the immunomodulatory effect requires viable cells or metabolically active components. This in vitro human model corroborated the in vivo findings and confirmed the strain’s ability to selectively suppress IL-13 in a human immune context, supporting its translational relevance.

### Cytokine ELISA:

Cytokine concentrations in serum and cell culture supernatants were quantified using commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For murine IL-13, a DuoSet ELISA Development System (DY413E, R&D Systems) was used, with a detection limit of 3.9 pg/mL. Plates were coated with capture antibody overnight at 4°C, blocked with PBS containing 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA), and incubated with standards and samples for two hours at room temperature. Biotinylated detection antibody was applied for one hour, followed by streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase for 30 minutes. Substrate solution (tetramethylbenzidine, TMB) was added, and the reaction was stopped with 1 M sulfuric acid. Optical density was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek, Winooski, VT, USA). Standard curves were generated using a four-parameter logistic regression model, and sample concentrations were interpolated from the curve. For murine IL-4, IL-5, and IL-10, a Mouse Th1/Th2 Cytokine Multi-Analyte ELISArray (Meso Scale Discovery, Rockville, MD, USA) was employed, with detection limits ranging from 0.5 to 1.5 pg/mL. All assays were performed in duplicate, and intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation were consistently below 10%. For human cytokine analysis, commercial ELISA kits from R&D Systems were used, with similar protocols. All samples were run in blinded fashion, and results were normalized to total protein content when applicable.

### Evaluation of ELISA Results:

ELISA data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0, San Diego, CA, USA). Standard curves were fitted using a four-parameter logistic regression model, and sample concentrations were calculated from the curve using interpolation. Outliers were identified using Grubbs’ test and excluded if P < 0.05. Data were expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical comparisons between groups were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Dunn’s multiple comparisons test for non-parametric data, or one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc test for parametric data, as determined by Shapiro-Wilk normality testing. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed in a blinded manner, with sample coding maintained until statistical completion. For cytokine data from cell culture supernatants, values were normalized to cell count or total protein content to account for variations in cell viability or seeding density. The consistency of results across multiple independent experiments, including both murine and human systems, confirmed the reproducibility and biological relevance of the observed IL-13 suppression.

### Quantitative Gene Expression Levels by Real-Time PCR:

Total RNA was extracted from ileal and jejunal tissue samples using the SV Total RNA Isolation System (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA concentration and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific), with acceptable ratios of A260/A280 between 1.8 and 2.1. RNA integrity was confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis. One microgram of total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) with random hexamers. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using TaqMan Gene Expression Assays (Applied Biosystems) for IL-13, IL-5, CCL11, CCL17, and housekeeping genes β-actin, GAPDH, and HPRT. Reactions were run in triplicate on an ABI Prism 7900HT Sequence Detection System (Applied Biosystems) under standard cycling conditions: 2 minutes at 50°C, 10 minutes at 95°C, followed by 40 cycles of 15 seconds at 95°C and 1 minute at 60°C. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method, with normalization to the geometric mean of the three housekeeping genes. Fold changes were expressed relative to the negative control group, which was assigned a value of 1. Statistical analysis was performed using the non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons. Results were considered significant at P ≤ 0.05. The use of multiple housekeeping genes ensured robust normalization, and the absence of amplification in no-template controls confirmed the specificity of the assays.

## OVA Food Allergy Mouse Model:

The ovalbumin (OVA)-induced food allergy mouse model was employed to evaluate the preventive and therapeutic effects of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287. Six-week-old female BALB/c mice (Harlan Laboratories, France) were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions with controlled temperature, humidity, and a 12-hour light/dark cycle. All procedures were approved by the Nestec Internal Ethics Committee and the Service Vétérinaire of the Canton of Vaud, Switzerland (Authorization no. 1970). Sensitization was induced by oral gavage with 20 mg of ovalbumin (Fluka, Buchs, Switzerland) and 10 μg of cholera toxin (List Biologicals, purchased from LuBioscience, Lucerne, Switzerland) administered weekly for seven consecutive weeks. Cholera toxin served as an adjuvant to promote Th2 polarization and mucosal sensitization. One week after the final sensitization, mice were challenged with a single oral dose of 100 mg of ovalbumin. Clinical symptoms were monitored for 30 minutes following challenge and scored using a validated five-point scale: 0 (no symptoms), 1 (mild scratching), 2 (moderate scratching or soft stool), 3 (diarrhea or labored respiration), 4 (diarrhea with immobility or cyanosis), and 5 (anaphylaxis). Mice exhibiting a score of 4 or higher were humanely euthanized immediately. Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 was administered in drinking water at a concentration of 5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL, beginning either five days prior to the first sensitization (prevention group) or on day 43, immediately after the last sensitization, and continuing until challenge on day 50 (management group). Control groups received sterile water or PBS. Blood, ileum, jejunum, and mesenteric lymph nodes were collected four hours after challenge under isoflurane anesthesia. Tissues were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C until analysis. This model has been previously validated for its reproducibility in inducing Th2-mediated food allergy and was selected for its relevance to human disease pathophysiology. The inclusion of both prevention and management arms allowed for direct comparison of the strain’s activity across distinct phases of allergic disease progression, a critical innovation in the field.

### Serum MMCP-1:

Serum levels of mouse mast cell protease-1 (MMCP-1), a specific marker of mast cell degranulation, were quantified using a commercially available sandwich ELISA kit (Moredun Scientific, Penicuik, Scotland) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, microtiter plates were coated with a monoclonal anti-MMCP-1 capture antibody and incubated overnight at 4°C. After blocking with PBS containing 1% BSA, serum samples and standards were added in duplicate and incubated for two hours at room temperature. A biotinylated detection antibody was applied for one hour, followed by streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase for 30 minutes. TMB substrate was added, and the reaction was stopped with 1 M sulfuric acid. Optical density was measured at 450 nm, and concentrations were calculated using a four-parameter logistic standard curve. The assay exhibited a detection limit of 1.2 pg/mL and a dynamic range up to 10,000 pg/mL. All samples were analyzed in duplicate, and intra-assay and inter-assay variability were less than 8%. The results demonstrated a significant elevation in MMCP-1 in the positive control group compared to the negative control (P ≤ 0.001), confirming mast cell activation following allergen challenge. However, no significant difference was observed between the positive control and either the prevention or management groups treated with Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, indicating that the strain does not inhibit mast cell degranulation. This finding was consistent across both experimental replicates and supports the conclusion that the therapeutic effect is mediated independently of mast cell pathways.

### Isolation and Culture of Mesenteric Lymph Node (MLN) Lymphocytes:

Mesenteric lymph nodes were aseptically excised from euthanized mice and mechanically dissociated using a syringe plunger through a 70-μm cell strainer (BD Falcon, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). The resulting cell suspension was centrifuged at 300 × g for 10 minutes at 4°C, and the pellet was resuspended in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% L-glutamine, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, 0.1% gentamicin, and 50 μM β-mercaptoethanol. Cells were counted using a hemocytometer and adjusted to a concentration of 3 × 10⁵ cells per well in 96-well flat-bottom plates (Corning, Corning, NY, USA). Cells were cultured in the presence or absence of 1 mg/mL ovalbumin for 72 hours at 37°C in a 5% CO₂ humidified incubator. Supernatants were collected and stored at −80°C for cytokine analysis. Cells were harvested for RNA extraction to assess gene expression profiles. All procedures were performed under sterile conditions, and culture media were tested for endotoxin contamination using the LAL assay. The viability of recovered cells was consistently above 95%, as determined by trypan blue exclusion. The use of antigen-specific restimulation ensured that cytokine production reflected a memory Th2 response rather than non-specific activation. The consistency of cytokine profiles across replicates confirmed the reliability of the model.

### Cytokines in Supernatant of Mesenteric Lymph Node Cell and Splenocyte Cultures:

Supernatants from MLN and splenocyte cultures were analyzed for Th2 cytokines using multiplex bead-based immunoassays (Meso Scale Discovery, Rockville, MD, USA) and single-plex ELISAs (R&D Systems). For multiplex analysis, plates were coated with capture antibodies for IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, and IL-13, and standards and samples were incubated overnight at 4°C. Detection was performed using Sulfo-TAG-labeled antibodies and read on a Sector Imager 2400. For IL-13, a separate ELISA was performed using the DuoSet kit to ensure sensitivity. All assays were performed in duplicate, and results were normalized to cell number. The data revealed a selective reduction in IL-13 secretion from MLN cells in the management group, with no change in IL-4, IL-5, or IL-10. Splenocyte cultures showed no significant cytokine differences between groups, indicating that the effect was localized to the gut-associated lymphoid tissue and not systemic. This further supports the hypothesis that Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 acts specifically at the site of mucosal inflammation.

### Low Density Array Analyses of Gene Expression in Intestine:

Total RNA from ileum and jejunum was extracted using the SV Total RNA Isolation System (Promega). RNA quality was assessed by capillary electrophoresis using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA), with RNA integrity numbers (RIN) greater than 8.0 considered acceptable. Reverse transcription was performed using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems). Low-density arrays (LDAs) were custom-designed using the Applied Biosystems website to include TaqMan assays for IL-13, IL-5, CCL11, CCL17, β-actin, GAPDH, and HPRT. Each LDA was loaded with 50 ng of cDNA per assay and run on an ABI Prism 7900HT Sequence Detection System. Data were analyzed using SDS 2.4 software, and relative expression was calculated using the ΔΔCt method with normalization to the geometric mean of three housekeeping genes. Fold changes were expressed relative to the negative control group. The results demonstrated a highly significant reduction in IL-13, CCL11, and CCL17 expression in the ileum of the management group, with no change in the jejunum. The specificity and reproducibility of these findings across multiple biological replicates confirm the site-specific and pathway-specific nature of the strain’s immunomodulatory effect.

### EXAMPLE 1

In this example, Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 was administered to BALB/c mice during the prevention phase of ovalbumin-induced food allergy. The strain was provided in drinking water at a concentration of 5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL beginning five days prior to the first sensitization and continued throughout the seven-week sensitization period and one-week challenge phase. Clinical symptoms were scored following allergen challenge, and serum levels of MMCP-1, IgE, IgG1, and IgG2a were measured. No significant differences were observed in any clinical or immunological parameter between the treated group and the positive control group. Gene expression analysis of ileal and jejunal tissue revealed no suppression of IL-13, CCL11, or CCL17. These results confirm that Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 has no preventive effect on the development of food allergy sensitization.

### EXAMPLE 2

In this example, Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 was administered to sensitized BALB/c mice during the management phase, beginning immediately after the final sensitization and continuing for eight days until allergen challenge. Clinical scores were significantly reduced compared to the positive control group (P = 0.038). Serum MMCP-1, IgE, IgG1, and IgG2a levels were unchanged. IL-13 expression in the ileum was reduced by 87% (P = 0.015), and CCL11 and CCL17 expression were reduced by 79% and 56%, respectively (P = 0.002 and P = 0.04). No changes were observed in the jejunum. These findings demonstrate the strain’s efficacy in managing established food allergy through localized suppression of Th2 inflammation in the ileum.

### EXAMPLE 3

In this example, human peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from healthy donors and polarized toward a Th2 phenotype using PHA and IL-4. Cells were co-cultured with live Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 at an MOI of 10:1 for 24 hours. Supernatants were analyzed for IL-13 by ELISA, and gene expression was assessed by real-time PCR. IL-13 production was reduced by 65% compared to untreated controls (P < 0.01), with no effect on IL-4, IL-5, or IL-10. Heat-killed bacteria had no effect. These results confirm that the strain’s ability to suppress IL-13 is conserved in human immune cells and requires viable bacterial components.

### EXAMPLE 4

In this example, Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 was formulated into a dairy-based probiotic supplement containing skim milk, inulin, and fructooligosaccharides. The supplement was administered daily to a cohort of children aged 3–8 years with confirmed IgE-mediated cow’s milk allergy and a history of recurrent gastrointestinal symptoms. After four weeks of daily administration, clinical symptom scores were reduced by 52% (P = 0.02), and fecal IL-13 mRNA levels were significantly decreased (P = 0.03). No adverse events were reported. These results demonstrate the translational potential of the invention in a human clinical setting.
