# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel class of hybrid small molecules designed for the prevention and treatment of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)-induced gastric mucosal injury, commonly referred to as gastropathy. Specifically, the invention encompasses a chemically engineered compound formed by the covalent conjugation of a mitochondriotropic amine moiety with a polyphenolic antioxidant bearing iron-chelating functionality. The resulting molecule exhibits a unique combination of physicochemical and biological properties that enable it to selectively penetrate mitochondrial membranes, scavenge intramitochondrial reactive oxygen species, chelate labile iron ions, and thereby inhibit mitochondrial oxidative stress, preserve mitochondrial respiratory function, and prevent the activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway in gastric epithelial cells. This invention finds particular utility in the prophylactic and therapeutic management of gastrointestinal damage caused by NSAIDs such as indomethacin, aspirin, ibuprofen, and naproxen, which are widely prescribed for their analgesic, antipyretic, and anti-inflammatory effects but are notorious for inducing mucosal ulceration, hemorrhage, and apoptosis in the gastric lining. The compound of the invention is distinguished from prior art antioxidants and iron chelators by its ability to localize specifically within mitochondria, where the primary pathological events of NSAID-induced gastropathy originate, thereby achieving a targeted, multi-mechanistic intervention that is both more potent and more physiologically rational than conventional systemic approaches. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the compound, methods of administration, and diagnostic applications utilizing radiolabeled derivatives for tracking mitochondrial uptake and biodistribution.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) represent one of the most commonly consumed classes of medications worldwide, utilized for the management of pain, fever, and inflammation associated with conditions ranging from arthritis to cardiovascular prophylaxis. Despite their therapeutic benefits, their clinical utility is severely limited by a high incidence of gastrointestinal toxicity, particularly in the gastric mucosa. NSAID-induced gastropathy manifests as erosions, ulcers, bleeding, and perforation, and is responsible for significant morbidity, hospitalization, and mortality, especially among elderly and chronically medicated populations. The pathogenesis of this injury has been traditionally attributed to the inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX)-1, which reduces the synthesis of cytoprotective prostaglandins. However, accumulating evidence over the past two decades has established that COX-independent mechanisms play an equally, if not more, critical role in the initiation and progression of mucosal damage. Among these, mitochondrial dysfunction has emerged as a central and unifying pathological event.

NSAIDs, particularly those with acidic carboxyl groups such as indomethacin, are known to accumulate within mitochondria due to their lipophilic nature and the negative mitochondrial membrane potential. Once inside, they directly interfere with the electron transport chain, primarily at complex I, leading to electron leakage and the excessive production of superoxide anion radicals. This surge in mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) triggers a cascade of oxidative damage, including lipid peroxidation of mitochondrial membranes, oxidation of critical protein thiols, and carbonylation of structural and enzymatic proteins. Concurrently, ROS induce the release of free iron from iron-sulfur cluster-containing proteins such as aconitase, which, in turn, catalyzes the Fenton reaction to generate highly reactive hydroxyl radicals. This self-amplifying cycle of oxidative stress—termed mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS)—results in the collapse of the mitochondrial transmembrane potential, inhibition of respiratory chain activity, depletion of cellular ATP, and ultimately, the opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore. These events culminate in the activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway, characterized by cytochrome c release, caspase-9 and caspase-3 activation, DNA fragmentation, and programmed cell death of gastric epithelial cells. The loss of epithelial integrity leads to increased permeability, exposure of underlying tissues to gastric acid and pepsin, and the development of visible mucosal lesions.

Existing therapeutic strategies for NSAID-induced gastropathy have largely focused on either suppressing gastric acid secretion through proton pump inhibitors or histamine H2 receptor antagonists, or on co-administering exogenous prostaglandin analogs. While these approaches offer partial protection, they do not address the root cause of the injury—the mitochondrial oxidative stress—and are associated with significant limitations. Proton pump inhibitors, for instance, are linked to an increased risk of bacterial overgrowth, enteric infections, acute interstitial nephritis, and even micronutrient malabsorption. Moreover, they have been shown to exacerbate NSAID-induced small intestinal injury by altering gut microbiota composition. Conventional antioxidants such as vitamin E, quercetin, and gallic acid, while possessing free radical-scavenging capacity in vitro, suffer from poor bioavailability, rapid metabolism, and inability to penetrate mitochondria in therapeutically relevant concentrations. Similarly, iron chelators like desferrioxamine, though effective in binding free iron, are large, hydrophilic molecules that cannot cross biological membranes efficiently and require parenteral administration, rendering them unsuitable for chronic oral use. Furthermore, none of these agents have demonstrated the ability to simultaneously target both mitochondrial ROS and labile iron pools within the organelle, which are interdependent drivers of the pathological cascade.

Prior art has attempted to improve mitochondrial targeting by attaching antioxidants to lipophilic cations such as triphenylphosphonium, yielding compounds like MitoQ and MitoTEMPO. While these molecules show enhanced mitochondrial accumulation, they lack intrinsic iron-chelating capacity and are limited in their ability to neutralize the iron-mediated amplification of oxidative damage. Other hybrid molecules combining antioxidant and chelating moieties have been explored, but none have successfully integrated the critical triad of properties required for effective intervention: (1) mitochondrial targeting via lipophilicity and charge distribution, (2) potent dual antioxidant activity against both superoxide and hydroxyl radicals, and (3) high-affinity iron chelation capable of sequestering labile iron within the mitochondrial matrix. Consequently, there remains a critical unmet need for a small, orally bioavailable molecule that can penetrate mitochondria, simultaneously quench ROS and chelate iron, and thereby halt the progression of NSAID-induced gastropathy at its source. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing a novel class of hybrid molecules derived from the covalent linkage of a mitochondriotropic tryptamine scaffold with a polyphenolic antioxidant possessing intrinsic iron-binding sites, thereby achieving a synergistic, multi-targeted therapeutic effect that is unprecedented in the field.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

The primary objective of the present invention is to provide a novel compound capable of preventing and treating NSAID-induced gastric mucosal injury by directly targeting the mitochondrial origin of oxidative stress, thereby overcoming the limitations of existing therapies. A further objective is to deliver a molecule that can selectively accumulate within the mitochondria of gastric epithelial cells, where NSAIDs induce the generation of superoxide anions and the release of labile iron, and exert a dual mechanism of action by simultaneously scavenging reactive oxygen species and chelating free iron ions to disrupt the self-propagating cycle of mitochondrial oxidative stress.

Another objective is to provide a compound that preserves mitochondrial structural and functional integrity by preventing the collapse of the mitochondrial transmembrane potential, maintaining respiratory control ratio, sustaining dehydrogenase activity, and inhibiting lipid peroxidation, protein carbonylation, and thiol depletion—all hallmarks of mitochondrial dysfunction induced by NSAIDs. A related objective is to prevent the activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway by inhibiting the upregulation of pro-apoptotic proteins such as Bax, suppressing the downregulation of anti-apoptotic proteins such as Bcl-2, and blocking the cleavage and activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3, thereby reducing epithelial cell death and promoting mucosal integrity.

A further objective is to accelerate the healing of pre-existing NSAID-induced gastric lesions by promoting the restoration of mucosal architecture, re-epithelialization, and tissue regeneration, thereby offering not only prophylactic but also therapeutic utility. Another objective is to provide a compound with superior efficacy compared to conventional antioxidants, iron chelators, and standard gastroprotective agents such as proton pump inhibitors, while avoiding their associated adverse effects, including dysbiosis, renal toxicity, and increased infection risk.

An additional objective is to enable the radiolabeling of the compound with technetium-99m for non-invasive imaging of mitochondrial uptake and biodistribution in vivo, thereby facilitating pharmacokinetic studies, dose optimization, and diagnostic applications in clinical settings. A further objective is to provide pharmaceutical compositions comprising the compound in various dosage forms suitable for oral, intraperitoneal, or intravenous administration, with enhanced stability, bioavailability, and shelf life. A final objective is to establish a structure-activity relationship demonstrating that the amide linkage between the tryptamine and polyphenolic moieties confers significantly greater biological activity than ester or other linkages, and that the presence of a 5-hydroxy group on the indole ring of the tryptamine component is essential for optimal mitochondrial targeting and protective efficacy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel hybrid small molecule characterized by a covalent linkage between a mitochondriotropic amine moiety and a polyphenolic antioxidant bearing iron-chelating functionality, wherein the molecule is capable of penetrating mitochondrial membranes, scavenging intramitochondrial reactive oxygen species, chelating labile iron ions, and thereby preventing mitochondrial oxidative stress, preserving mitochondrial function, and inhibiting the intrinsic apoptotic pathway in gastric epithelial cells exposed to nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. The compound of the invention is represented by the chemical structure of N-(5-hydroxytryptaminyl)-3,4,5-trihydroxybenzamide, designated herein as SEGA (3a), which is synthesized through an amide bond formation between the primary amine of 5-hydroxytryptamine and the carboxylic acid group of gallic acid. This specific molecular architecture enables the compound to simultaneously fulfill three critical therapeutic functions: first, the 5-hydroxytryptamine moiety facilitates mitochondrial uptake due to its lipophilic character and structural similarity to endogenous mitochondrial substrates; second, the gallic acid moiety provides potent antioxidant activity through its three phenolic hydroxyl groups, enabling efficient scavenging of superoxide and hydroxyl radicals; and third, the ortho-trihydroxy arrangement of the galloyl group confers high-affinity iron chelation, effectively sequestering free iron within the mitochondrial matrix and inhibiting Fenton chemistry.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the compound in association with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent, formulated for oral, intraperitoneal, or intravenous administration. The compound demonstrates a dose-dependent protective effect against NSAID-induced gastric mucosal injury in vivo, with an ED50 value of approximately 6.9 mg/kg body weight in a rat model of indomethacin-induced gastropathy, significantly outperforming conventional antioxidants and iron chelators such as gallic acid, vitamin E, and desferrioxamine. The compound not only prevents the formation of new lesions but also accelerates the healing of pre-existing mucosal damage, restoring normal gastric architecture within 20 hours of administration. Mechanistically, the compound enters mitochondria in vivo, as confirmed by radiolabeling with technetium-99m, where it reduces intramitochondrial superoxide levels, suppresses free iron accumulation, prevents lipid peroxidation and protein carbonylation, maintains mitochondrial membrane potential, sustains respiratory control ratio, and inhibits the activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3. The compound further modulates the expression of apoptosis-regulating genes, upregulating Bcl-2 and downregulating Bax, thereby shifting the cellular balance toward survival. The amide linkage between the two pharmacophores is essential for biological activity, as ester-linked analogs exhibit significantly reduced efficacy. The presence of the 5-hydroxy group on the indole ring of the tryptamine moiety is likewise critical, as substitution with methoxy or hydrogen results in diminished mitochondrial targeting and protective potency. The invention further includes methods of use for the prevention and treatment of NSAID-induced gastropathy in mammals, including humans, by administering an effective amount of the compound prior to or following NSAID exposure. Additionally, the invention provides a diagnostic method for monitoring mitochondrial uptake and biodistribution of the compound using technetium-99m-labeled derivatives, enabling non-invasive assessment of drug delivery and therapeutic response.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The compound of the invention, designated SEGA (3a), is a synthetic hybrid molecule formed by the amide bond conjugation of 5-hydroxytryptamine and gallic acid, resulting in the chemical structure of N-(5-hydroxytryptaminyl)-3,4,5-trihydroxybenzamide. This molecular architecture is the product of deliberate design, integrating two distinct pharmacophoric elements into a single, low-molecular-weight entity that overcomes the pharmacokinetic and mechanistic limitations of their individual components. The 5-hydroxytryptamine moiety, derived from the endogenous neurotransmitter serotonin, possesses inherent lipophilicity and structural features that facilitate its passage across biological membranes, including the mitochondrial outer and inner membranes. The presence of the indole ring and the 5-hydroxy substituent enhances its affinity for mitochondrial compartments, likely through interactions with membrane transporters or lipid bilayer partitioning driven by the molecule’s overall charge distribution and hydrophobic surface area. The gallic acid moiety, a naturally occurring trihydroxybenzoic acid, contributes three phenolic hydroxyl groups arranged in an ortho-trihydroxy configuration, a structural motif known to confer exceptional free radical-scavenging capacity and high-affinity iron chelation. The amide linkage between these two moieties is not merely a passive connector but a strategically chosen chemical bond that enhances metabolic stability, prevents rapid hydrolysis in the gastrointestinal tract, and maintains the spatial orientation necessary for both mitochondrial targeting and dual antioxidant-chelating activity.

The synthesis of SEGA (3a) is achieved through a straightforward, scalable, and reproducible chemical process involving the activation of the carboxylic acid group of gallic acid using 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide hydrochloride (EDC) in the presence of 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HOBt) and N,N-dimethylaminopyridine (DMAP) as catalysts, followed by nucleophilic attack by the primary amine of 5-hydroxytryptamine in anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide under ambient conditions. The reaction proceeds with high yield and selectivity, producing the desired amide without significant side products. The resulting compound is purified by silica gel chromatography and characterized by nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, mass spectrometry, and high-performance liquid chromatography to confirm identity and purity. The compound is obtained as a crystalline solid with a melting point consistent with its molecular structure and demonstrates solubility in dimethyl sulfoxide and aqueous ethanol, facilitating formulation for biological testing. The molecular weight of SEGA (3a) is approximately 316.3 g/mol, placing it well within the range suitable for oral absorption and mitochondrial penetration. The compound is stable under physiological conditions, as demonstrated by its resistance to hydrolysis in serum and saline over 24 hours, and retains its antioxidant and iron-chelating properties in the presence of biological matrices.

The biological activity of SEGA (3a) is predicated on its ability to localize within mitochondria, where it intercepts the primary drivers of NSAID-induced gastropathy. Upon administration, the compound is absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract and distributed systemically, with preferential accumulation in the gastric mucosa, as confirmed by radiolabeling studies using technetium-99m. The mitochondrial uptake of the compound is not passive but likely involves active transport mechanisms or facilitated diffusion driven by the mitochondrial membrane potential, which is maintained even under conditions of NSAID-induced stress. Once inside the mitochondrial matrix, SEGA (3a) exerts its protective effects through two synergistic mechanisms. First, the phenolic hydroxyl groups of the galloyl moiety donate hydrogen atoms to neutralize superoxide anions, hydroxyl radicals, and other reactive oxygen species generated by the disruption of the electron transport chain by NSAIDs. This direct scavenging prevents the oxidation of mitochondrial lipids, proteins, and DNA, thereby preserving membrane integrity and enzymatic function. Second, the ortho-trihydroxy arrangement of the gallic acid unit forms a stable, hexadentate complex with free ferrous and ferric ions, effectively sequestering them and preventing their participation in the Fenton reaction, which would otherwise convert hydrogen peroxide into the highly destructive hydroxyl radical. By simultaneously quenching ROS and chelating iron, SEGA (3a) disrupts the vicious cycle of oxidative stress that amplifies mitochondrial damage and triggers apoptosis.

The consequences of this dual action are profound and multifaceted. Mitochondrial lipid peroxidation, as measured by thiobarbituric acid-reactive substances, is significantly reduced in the presence of SEGA (3a), indicating preservation of cardiolipin and other phospholipids essential for respiratory complex assembly. Protein carbonylation, a marker of irreversible oxidative damage to amino acid side chains, is markedly diminished, preserving the function of key enzymes involved in energy metabolism. Thiol depletion, which compromises the antioxidant capacity of glutathione and thioredoxin systems, is prevented, maintaining the redox balance within the organelle. The mitochondrial transmembrane potential, a critical determinant of ATP synthesis and ion homeostasis, is stabilized, as evidenced by the maintenance of the JC-1 aggregate-to-monomer fluorescence ratio. Respiratory control ratio, which reflects the coupling efficiency of oxidative phosphorylation, is preserved, indicating that SEGA (3a) prevents the uncoupling of electron transport from ATP production. Mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity, as measured by the reduction of MTT to formazan, remains near normal levels, confirming the preservation of metabolic function. These biochemical and biophysical improvements translate directly into the inhibition of apoptosis. The activation of caspase-9, the initiator caspase of the mitochondrial pathway, is suppressed, as is the downstream cleavage of caspase-3, the executioner protease responsible for DNA fragmentation and cellular dismantling. The expression of pro-apoptotic Bax is downregulated, while the anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 is upregulated, shifting the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio toward cell survival. DNA fragmentation, as detected by TUNEL staining, is dramatically reduced, and the number of cells exhibiting morphological features of apoptosis—such as chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, and membrane blebbing—is significantly diminished. Histological examination of gastric mucosa reveals that the normal architecture of the epithelial layer, including the preservation of surface mucus-secreting cells, gastric pits, and glandular integrity, is maintained in the presence of SEGA (3a), even following exposure to high doses of indomethacin.

The therapeutic utility of SEGA (3a) extends beyond prevention to active healing. When administered after the induction of gastric injury, the compound accelerates the repair of mucosal defects, promoting re-epithelialization, restoration of the mucus barrier, and regeneration of damaged tissue. This healing effect is evident within hours of administration and is complete within 20 hours, far outpacing the natural autohealing process observed in untreated animals. This suggests that SEGA (3a) not only halts ongoing damage but also stimulates endogenous repair mechanisms, possibly by reducing the inflammatory milieu, enhancing cellular proliferation, and improving mitochondrial bioenergetics in regenerating epithelial cells. The compound’s efficacy is not limited to indomethacin; given its mechanism of action, it is expected to be effective against other NSAIDs that induce mitochondrial oxidative stress, including aspirin, diclofenac, and naproxen. Furthermore, the compound does not interfere with the anti-inflammatory or analgesic properties of NSAIDs, making it an ideal co-therapeutic agent that can be co-administered without compromising the primary indication for NSAID use.

The invention further encompasses the radiolabeling of SEGA (3a) with technetium-99m via a stannous chloride-mediated reduction method, resulting in a stable, high-yield radiopharmaceutical suitable for in vivo imaging. The labeled compound demonstrates minimal transchelation when challenged with diethylene triamine pentaacetic acid, confirming the strength of the technetium binding. The radiolabeled derivative retains its biological activity and mitochondrial targeting properties, allowing for non-invasive quantification of its biodistribution, organ uptake, and mitochondrial accumulation in real time. This feature enables the development of diagnostic protocols for patient stratification, dose optimization, and monitoring of therapeutic response in clinical settings. The stability of the compound in physiological fluids, its oral bioavailability, and its lack of acute toxicity further support its translation into clinical applications.

### Biological Effects

The biological effects of the compound of the invention are mediated through a cascade of interdependent molecular and cellular events that converge to preserve mitochondrial integrity and prevent epithelial cell death in the gastric mucosa. Upon administration, SEGA (3a) is rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal lumen and enters the systemic circulation, where it demonstrates favorable pharmacokinetic properties, including rapid distribution to target tissues, low plasma protein binding, and minimal metabolism by hepatic enzymes. The compound achieves peak concentrations in the gastric mucosa within 30 to 60 minutes following oral or intraperitoneal administration, coinciding with the onset of NSAID-induced mitochondrial stress. The mitochondrial accumulation of SEGA (3a) is quantified at approximately 0.05% of the administered dose per milligram of mitochondrial protein, a level sufficient to exert profound biological effects despite the relatively low absolute concentration. This selective accumulation is attributed to the compound’s lipophilicity, molecular weight, and structural resemblance to endogenous mitochondrial substrates, which likely facilitate its transport through the outer mitochondrial membrane via voltage-dependent anion channels and its subsequent retention within the matrix due to the negative membrane potential.

Once inside the mitochondria, SEGA (3a) exerts its primary effect by intercepting reactive oxygen species generated at complex I of the electron transport chain. NSAIDs such as indomethacin bind to and inhibit complex I, leading to electron leakage and the one-electron reduction of molecular oxygen to form superoxide anion. The phenolic hydroxyl groups of the galloyl moiety in SEGA (3a) act as hydrogen donors, reducing superoxide to hydrogen peroxide and subsequently neutralizing hydroxyl radicals through radical adduct formation. This direct scavenging activity is confirmed by in vitro assays demonstrating concentration-dependent quenching of DPPH radicals and enhancement of ferric reducing antioxidant power, with activity exceeding that of gallic acid alone. The compound’s ability to reduce the oxidation of MitoSOX, a mitochondria-targeted fluorescent probe for superoxide, confirms its intramitochondrial activity in intact cells. The reduction in superoxide levels prevents the oxidative damage of mitochondrial components, including the lipid bilayer, respiratory complexes, and matrix enzymes. Lipid peroxidation, measured by the formation of thiobarbituric acid-reactive substances such as malondialdehyde, is significantly suppressed in the presence of SEGA (3a), preserving the fluidity and integrity of mitochondrial membranes. Protein carbonylation, a marker of irreversible oxidative modification of lysine, arginine, proline, and threonine residues, is markedly reduced, maintaining the structural and functional integrity of enzymes involved in the tricarboxylic acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation. Thiol depletion, which compromises the reducing capacity of glutathione and thioredoxin systems, is prevented, thereby sustaining the mitochondrial antioxidant defense network.

A second, equally critical mechanism of action is the chelation of labile iron. Mitochondrial oxidative stress induces the release of iron from iron-sulfur cluster-containing proteins such as aconitase and succinate dehydrogenase. This free iron, in its ferrous state, reacts with hydrogen peroxide via the Fenton reaction to generate hydroxyl radicals, which are far more reactive and destructive than superoxide. SEGA (3a) binds free iron with high affinity, as demonstrated by the inhibition of the Fe(II)-TPTZ complex formation in spectrophotometric assays and by the quenching of Phen Green SK fluorescence in live cells. The ortho-trihydroxy configuration of the galloyl group forms a stable, octahedral complex with iron, effectively sequestering it and preventing its participation in radical generation. This chelation is specific and reversible, allowing for the regulation of iron homeostasis without inducing systemic iron deficiency. The dual action of ROS scavenging and iron chelation creates a synergistic effect: by reducing superoxide, SEGA (3a) limits the substrate for hydroxyl radical formation, and by chelating iron, it removes the catalyst for the Fenton reaction. This dual blockade effectively breaks the self-propagating cycle of mitochondrial oxidative stress, which is the central driver of NSAID-induced gastropathy.

The preservation of mitochondrial function is a direct consequence of these mechanisms. The mitochondrial transmembrane potential, which is essential for ATP synthesis and calcium buffering, is maintained in the presence of SEGA (3a), as evidenced by the retention of the JC-1 aggregate-to-monomer fluorescence ratio. The respiratory control ratio, which reflects the coupling efficiency between electron transport and oxidative phosphorylation, remains near baseline levels, indicating that the compound prevents the uncoupling of respiration that typically occurs during NSAID exposure. Mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity, as measured by the reduction of MTT to formazan, is preserved, confirming that the metabolic machinery remains functional. The compound also prevents the opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore, a key event in apoptosis, by maintaining calcium homeostasis and reducing oxidative stress. These functional improvements translate into the inhibition of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. The release of cytochrome c from the mitochondrial intermembrane space into the cytosol is suppressed, preventing the formation of the apoptosome and the subsequent activation of caspase-9. The cleavage and activation of caspase-3, the executioner protease responsible for DNA fragmentation and cellular dismantling, are significantly attenuated, as confirmed by both enzymatic assays and immunohistochemical detection of active caspase-3. The expression of pro-apoptotic Bax is downregulated, while the expression of anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 is upregulated, shifting the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio toward cell survival. These molecular changes are corroborated by the marked reduction in TUNEL-positive cells, indicating a significant decrease in DNA fragmentation and apoptotic cell death.

The compound’s effects are not limited to the prevention of injury but extend to the acceleration of healing. In models where gastric mucosal damage is first induced by indomethacin and SEGA (3a) is administered thereafter, the rate of mucosal repair is significantly enhanced. Within four hours of treatment, the injury index is reduced by more than 70% compared to vehicle-treated controls, and by 20 hours, the mucosal architecture is fully restored, with complete re-epithelialization, regeneration of gastric glands, and restoration of the mucus barrier. This healing effect is likely mediated by multiple mechanisms, including the reduction of inflammatory cytokines, the promotion of epithelial cell proliferation, and the restoration of mitochondrial bioenergetics in regenerating cells. The compound does not interfere with the normal physiological turnover of gastric epithelial cells, nor does it induce hyperplasia or aberrant proliferation, indicating a highly targeted and physiologically compatible mode of action.

The biological effects of SEGA (3a) are highly specific to its molecular structure. Analogues in which the amide linkage is replaced by an ester bond, such as GASE (4d), exhibit significantly reduced antioxidant and iron-chelating activity, as well as diminished mitochondrial accumulation and protective efficacy. Similarly, derivatives in which the 5-hydroxy group on the indole ring is substituted with methoxy or hydrogen, such as MEGA (3c) and TRGA (3b), show markedly reduced potency, confirming the critical role of this substituent in mitochondrial targeting. The compound’s efficacy is superior to that of conventional antioxidants, iron chelators, and proton pump inhibitors, with an ED50 value of 6.9 mg/kg body weight in rats, making it approximately 2.7-fold more potent than gallic acid and over 14-fold more potent than desferrioxamine. Importantly, the compound does not inhibit cyclooxygenase activity, preserving the anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects of co-administered NSAIDs. Its low toxicity profile, absence of gastrointestinal irritation, and lack of systemic side effects further distinguish it from existing therapies. The compound’s ability to penetrate mitochondria, simultaneously neutralize two key drivers of oxidative stress, and promote tissue repair represents a paradigm shift in the management of NSAID-induced gastropathy, offering a mechanistically rational, highly effective, and clinically translatable solution to a longstanding therapeutic challenge.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The compound designated SEGA (3a), N-(5-hydroxytryptaminyl)-3,4,5-trihydroxybenzamide, was synthesized by the amide coupling of gallic acid and 5-hydroxytryptamine using 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide hydrochloride (EDC) and 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HOBt) as coupling agents in anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide. Gallic acid (12 mmol) was dissolved in 10 mL of N,N-dimethylformamide under nitrogen atmosphere, followed by the addition of HOBt (12 mmol), 5-hydroxytryptamine hydrochloride (12 mmol), and triethylamine (72 mmol). EDC hydrochloride (14.4 mmol) was then added portion-wise at 0°C, and the reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature for 18 hours. The reaction progress was monitored by thin layer chromatography using ethyl acetate:methanol (8:2) as the mobile phase, with visualization under UV light and ninhydrin staining. Upon completion, the reaction was quenched by the addition of 50 mL of ice-cold distilled water, and the resulting precipitate was extracted with ethyl acetate (3 × 50 mL). The combined organic layers were washed with saturated sodium chloride solution (2 × 50 mL), dried over anhydrous sodium sulfate, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by column chromatography over silica gel using a gradient elution of dichloromethane:methanol (95:5 to 85:15) to afford SEGA (3a) as a white crystalline solid. The yield was 82%. The compound was characterized by ¹H NMR (400 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ 12.15 (s, 1H, OH), 10.85 (s, 1H, NH), 9.45 (s, 1H, OH), 8.90 (s, 1H, OH), 8.05 (d, J = 8.0 Hz, 1H, Ar-H), 7.85 (d, J = 8.0 Hz, 1H, Ar-H), 7.65 (t, J = 7.5 Hz, 1H, Ar-H), 7.45 (t, J = 7.5 Hz, 1H, Ar-H), 7.25 (s, 1H, indole-H), 6.95 (d, J = 8.0 Hz, 1H, indole-H), 6.75 (d, J = 8.0 Hz, 1H, indole-H), 4.25 (t, J = 6.5 Hz, 2H, CH2), 3.05 (t, J = 6.5 Hz, 2H, CH2), 2.85 (s, 1H, indole-NH), 2.55 (s, 1H, OH), 2.25 (s, 1H, OH); ¹³C NMR (100 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ 168.5 (C=O), 158.2, 155.7, 152.3, 147.8, 145.2, 138.5, 132.7, 128.9, 125.4, 122.8, 118.7, 116.5, 114.2, 110.8, 108.5, 105.2, 39.5 (CH2), 32.8 (CH2); ESI-MS: m/z 317.1 [M+H]+; elemental analysis: calculated for C17H18N2O5: C, 64.49; H, 5.73; N, 8.84; found: C, 64.37; H, 5.68; N, 8.91. The compound was stored at -20°C under desiccated conditions and dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide for biological testing.

### Example 2

The antioxidant activity of SEGA (3a) was evaluated using the ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) assay. A FRAP reagent was prepared by mixing 10 mL of 200 mM acetate buffer (pH 3.6), 1 mL of 10 mM 2,4,6-tris(2-pyridyl)-s-triazine (TPTZ) in 40 mM HCl, and 1 mL of 20 mM ferric chloride solution in distilled water. The reagent was equilibrated at 37°C for one hour. SEGA (3a) was dissolved in methanol at concentrations ranging from 1 to 100 μM, and 25 μL of each solution was added in triplicate to a 96-well microplate. Freshly prepared FRAP reagent (175 μL) was added to each well, and the absorbance was measured at 595 nm at 0, 6, 12, 30, 60, and 150 minutes using a microplate reader. The change in absorbance relative to a blank (methanol + FRAP reagent) was calculated, and the FRAP value at 6 minutes was determined using the formula ΔA_t = A_sample - A_blank. The FRAP value was expressed as micromoles of ferrous sulfate equivalents per micromole of compound. SEGA (3a) exhibited a FRAP value of 4.3 ± 0.2 μmol Fe²⁺/μmol at 6 minutes, significantly higher than gallic acid (3.1 ± 0.1), 5-hydroxytryptamine (0.4 ± 0.05), and the ester-linked analogue GASE (4d) (1.8 ± 0.1). The concentration-dependent increase in FRAP activity confirmed the potent reducing capacity of SEGA (3a).

### Example 3

The free radical-scavenging activity of SEGA (3a) was assessed using the 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) assay. A 0.15 mM solution of DPPH in methanol (80% v/v) was prepared and kept in the dark at room temperature. SEGA (3a) was dissolved in methanol at concentrations ranging from 10 to 100 μM, and 1 mL of each solution was mixed with 4 mL of DPPH solution. The mixture was incubated at room temperature in the dark for 30 minutes, and the absorbance was measured at 517 nm using a UV-visible spectrophotometer. The percentage inhibition of DPPH radical was calculated as [(A_control - A_sample)/A_control] × 100, where A_control is the absorbance of DPPH without compound and A_sample is the absorbance in the presence of SEGA (3a). SEGA (3a) demonstrated a concentration-dependent inhibition of DPPH radicals, with an IC50 value of 12.5 ± 1.2 μM, compared to gallic acid (IC50 = 18.7 ± 1.5 μM), 5-hydroxytryptamine (IC50 > 100 μM), and GASE (4d) (IC50 = 35.2 ± 2.1 μM). The results confirmed that SEGA (3a) possesses superior free radical-scavenging activity compared to its individual components and structural analogues.

### Example 4

The iron-chelating activity of SEGA (3a) was evaluated using a spectrophotometric assay based on the formation of the Fe(II)-TPTZ complex. A 10 μM solution of ferrous sulfate in 20 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) was prepared. SEGA (3a) was added at concentrations ranging from 500 nM to 100 μM, followed by the addition of 20 μM TPTZ. The absorbance of the Fe(II)-TPTZ complex was measured immediately at 595 nm using a PerkinElmer Lambda 15 UV-visible spectrophotometer with a 1-cm quartz cell. Desferrioxamine was used as a positive control. The inhibition of complex formation was calculated as [(A_control - A_sample)/A_control] × 100. SEGA (3a) inhibited Fe(II)-TPTZ complex formation by 89.5 ± 2.1% at 100 μM, compared to desferrioxamine (92.3 ± 1.8%) and gallic acid (75.6 ± 3.2%). The absence of the characteristic absorbance peak at 595 nm in the presence of SEGA (3a) confirmed its ability to chelate free iron. The binding affinity was further validated by the quenching of Phen Green SK fluorescence in gastric mucosal cells, indicating intracellular iron sequestration.

### Example 5

The gastroprotective efficacy of SEGA (3a) was evaluated in a rat model of indomethacin-induced gastropathy. Sprague-Dawley rats (n = 6 per group, 180–220 g) were fasted for 24 hours with free access to water. Indomethacin (48 mg/kg body weight) was administered orally to induce gastric injury. SEGA (3a) was administered intraperitoneally at doses of 1, 3, 5, 10, 20, and 50 mg/kg body weight 30 minutes prior to indomethacin. Control animals received vehicle only. Four hours after indomethacin administration, the animals were euthanized, and the stomachs were excised, opened along the greater curvature, and rinsed with phosphate-buffered saline. The severity of mucosal lesions was scored using a standardized injury index: 0 = no lesion; 1 = 1–2 mm lesion; 2 = 3–4 mm lesion; 4 = 5–6 mm lesion; 8 = >6 mm lesion. The mean injury index was calculated as the sum of individual scores divided by the number of animals. SEGA (3a) demonstrated a dose-dependent reduction in injury index, with an ED50 value of 6.9 ± 0.8 mg/kg. At 50 mg/kg, the injury index was reduced from 7.2 ± 0.5 in the indomethacin-only group to 1.1 ± 0.3 (p < 0.001). Histological examination revealed preservation of gastric mucosal architecture, with minimal epithelial shedding and no hemorrhage in SEGA (3a)-pretreated animals, in contrast to extensive erosion and bleeding in the control group.

### Example 6

The effect of SEGA (3a) on mitochondrial oxidative stress was evaluated in primary cultures of rat gastric mucosal cells. Cells were isolated from gastric mucosa using collagenase and hyaluronidase digestion and cultured in Ham’s F-12 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. Cells were divided into three groups: control (vehicle), indomethacin-treated (5 mM), and SEGA (3a)-pretreated (10 μM for 30 minutes followed by 5 mM indomethacin). Intramitochondrial superoxide was detected using MitoSOX Red (5 μM, 15 minutes, 37°C), and fluorescence was visualized by confocal microscopy. Indomethacin induced a 4.5-fold increase in MitoSOX fluorescence, which was reduced by 85% in the SEGA (3a)-pretreated group. Intramitochondrial free iron was measured using Phen Green SK (20 μM, 15 minutes, 37°C). Indomethacin increased Phen Green SK fluorescence by 3.8-fold, indicating iron accumulation, which was suppressed by 81% in the SEGA (3a)-pretreated group. Mitochondrial lipid peroxidation, protein carbonylation, and thiol depletion were quantified in isolated mitochondria. SEGA (3a) reduced lipid peroxidation by 78%, protein carbonylation by 72%, and prevented thiol depletion by 89% compared to indomethacin alone. The compound also restored mitochondrial membrane potential, as measured by JC-1 fluorescence ratio (590/530 nm), from 0.6 ± 0.05 in the indomethacin group to 1.9 ± 0.1 in the SEGA (3a)-pretreated group, approaching control levels (2.1 ± 0.1).

### Example 7

The impact of SEGA (3a) on mitochondrial respiratory function was assessed using a Clark-type oxygen electrode. Mitochondria were isolated from gastric mucosa of rats pretreated with SEGA (3a) (50 mg/kg) or vehicle, followed by indomethacin (48 mg/kg). State 2 respiration (basal) and state 3 respiration (ADP-stimulated) were measured in a respiratory medium containing glutamate and malate. The respiratory control ratio (RCR) was calculated as the ratio of state 3 to state 4 respiration. Indomethacin reduced RCR from 4.2 ± 0.3 in controls to 1.8 ± 0.2, indicating uncoupling. SEGA (3a) pretreatment restored RCR to 3.9 ± 0.4, comparable to control values. Mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity, assessed by MTT reduction, was reduced by 65% in the indomethacin group but was preserved at 92% of control levels in the SEGA (3a)-pretreated group. Caspase-9 and caspase-3 activities, measured using colorimetric kits, were increased by 2.1-fold and 2.4-fold, respectively, in the indomethacin group. SEGA (3a) reduced caspase-9 activity by 88% and caspase-3 activity by 85%. RT-PCR analysis showed that SEGA (3a) reversed the indomethacin-induced downregulation of Bcl-2 mRNA and upregulation of Bax mRNA, resulting in a Bax/Bcl-2 ratio of 0.8 ± 0.1 compared to 3.2 ± 0.3 in the indomethacin group.

### Example 8

The healing efficacy of SEGA (3a) was evaluated in a post-injury model. Gastric mucosal injury was induced in rats by oral administration of indomethacin (48 mg/kg). Four hours later, animals were divided into two groups: autohealing (vehicle) and SEGA (3a)-treated (50 mg/kg intraperitoneally). Stomachs were harvested at 2, 4, 8, and 24 hours post-treatment. The injury index was measured at each time point. In the autohealing group, the injury index decreased from 52 ± 3 at 4 hours to 28 ± 2 at 8 hours, 20 ± 2 at 16 hours, and 12 ± 1 at 24 hours. In the SEGA (3a)-treated group, the injury index was reduced to 14 ± 2 at 4 hours, 8 ± 1 at 8 hours, 3 ± 0.5 at 16 hours, and 0 at 24 hours. Histological analysis confirmed complete restoration of mucosal architecture in the SEGA (3a)-treated group by 20 hours, whereas the autohealing group exhibited persistent epithelial discontinuity and glandular distortion. TUNEL staining revealed a 90% reduction in apoptotic cells in the SEGA (3a)-treated group at 8 hours compared to controls.

### Example 9

SEGA (3a) was radiolabeled with technetium-99m using a stannous chloride reduction method. Aqueous sodium pertechnetate (2 mCi/mL) was mixed with freshly prepared stannous chloride (1 mg/mL, 0.03 mL) at pH 3.2, followed by the addition of SEGA (3a) solution (3 mg/mL, 1 mL). The mixture was incubated at 30°C for 20 minutes. Radiolabeling efficiency was determined by ascending thin-layer chromatography using acetone as the mobile phase. The radiochemical purity was 96.5 ± 1.2%, with no detectable free pertechnetate or radiocolloid. Stability in saline and rat serum was assessed over 24 hours at 37°C, with no significant degradation observed. Transchelation studies using diethylene triamine pentaacetic acid (50 mM) showed less than 3% release of 99mTc after 2 hours. In vivo biodistribution in rats showed 0.05% of the injected dose per mg of mitochondrial protein in gastric mucosa, confirming mitochondrial targeting. The radiolabeled compound enabled non-invasive imaging of mitochondrial uptake using gamma scintigraphy, demonstrating rapid accumulation in the gastric mucosa within 30 minutes of administration.

## ADVANTAGES OF THE INVENTION

The compound of the present invention offers a multitude of advantages over existing therapeutic and prophylactic agents for NSAID-induced gastropathy. Unlike proton pump inhibitors, which suppress acid secretion but fail to address the root cause of mitochondrial oxidative stress and are associated with increased risk of infections, renal injury, and dysbiosis, the compound of the invention directly targets the primary pathological mechanism—mitochondrial dysfunction—without interfering with gastric acid physiology. Unlike conventional antioxidants such as vitamin E, quercetin, or gallic acid, which are poorly absorbed, rapidly metabolized, and unable to penetrate mitochondria in therapeutically relevant concentrations, the compound of the invention is specifically engineered to accumulate within mitochondria, where it exerts a dual mechanism of action by simultaneously scavenging reactive oxygen species and chelating labile iron. This targeted, multi-functional approach results in a potency that is significantly greater than that of any single-agent antioxidant or iron chelator, as demonstrated by its ED50 value of 6.9 mg/kg, which is over 14-fold lower than desferrioxamine and nearly three-fold lower than gallic acid. The compound’s amide linkage confers metabolic stability, preventing hydrolysis in the gastrointestinal tract and ensuring systemic bioavailability, while its low molecular weight and lipophilicity facilitate efficient absorption and mitochondrial uptake. The compound does not inhibit cyclooxygenase activity, preserving the anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects of co-administered NSAIDs, thereby enabling its use as a safe and effective adjunct therapy. The compound’s ability to accelerate the healing of pre-existing mucosal lesions distinguishes it from purely prophylactic agents, offering true therapeutic utility in patients who have already sustained injury. The radiolabeling capability of the compound with technetium-99m provides a unique diagnostic advantage, allowing for non-invasive monitoring of mitochondrial delivery and biodistribution, which can be leveraged for personalized dosing, patient stratification, and real-time assessment of therapeutic response in clinical settings. The compound exhibits no acute toxicity, no gastrointestinal irritation, and no systemic side effects in preclinical models, supporting its potential for long-term use. Its synthesis is straightforward, scalable, and cost-effective, using commercially available starting materials and standard organic chemistry techniques, making it amenable to industrial production. The structure-activity relationship established in this invention provides a rational framework for the development of next-generation derivatives with enhanced potency, selectivity, and pharmacokinetic profiles. Collectively, these advantages position the compound of the invention as a first-in-class therapeutic agent that addresses the fundamental biology of NSAID-induced gastropathy, offering a safe, effective, and mechanistically superior alternative to current standard-of-care therapies.
