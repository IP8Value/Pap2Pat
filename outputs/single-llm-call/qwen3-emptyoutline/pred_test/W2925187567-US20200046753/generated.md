# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel nonviral, hybrid mRNA/DNA system for the sustained cytoplasmic expression of short hairpin RNA (shRNA) and the subsequent long-term silencing of target genes in eukaryotic cells, both in vitro and in vivo. More specifically, the invention encompasses a self-amplifying, T7 bacteriophage RNA polymerase (T7pol)-based autogene system that enables continuous production of shRNA without requiring nuclear entry of genetic material, thereby overcoming the limitations of conventional nuclear-dependent shRNA expression systems and transient siRNA delivery methods. The invention further includes a liposomal delivery vehicle composed of 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphatidylcholine (DOPC) that efficiently encapsulates and protects the hybrid mRNA/DNA components, facilitating systemic administration and tumor-specific accumulation via the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect. This system is particularly suited for therapeutic applications requiring prolonged gene silencing, such as the treatment of cancer, chronic viral infections, and other diseases driven by persistent expression of pathogenic genes, without the need for repeated dosing. The invention represents a significant advancement in nonviral RNA interference (RNAi) therapeutics by providing a platform that achieves sustained target gene suppression for over thirteen days in vitro and for at least six days in vivo following a single administration, while maintaining low immunogenicity and minimal cytotoxicity.

## BACKGROUND ART

RNA interference (RNAi) has emerged as a powerful molecular tool for the sequence-specific suppression of gene expression, with broad applications in functional genomics and therapeutic development. The introduction of synthetic small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), typically 21–23 nucleotides in length, enabled the targeted degradation of complementary mRNA transcripts through the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC). However, the clinical utility of synthetic siRNAs has been severely constrained by their transient nature, with gene silencing effects typically lasting only two to four days in cell culture and even less in vivo due to rapid degradation, dilution during cell division, and inefficient cellular uptake. Consequently, repeated administrations are required to maintain therapeutic efficacy, which poses significant logistical, economic, and safety challenges for chronic disease management.

To extend the duration of gene silencing, recombinant viral vectors such as adenoviruses, lentiviruses, and adeno-associated viruses have been employed to deliver DNA constructs encoding short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) under the control of RNA polymerase III promoters, such as U6 or H1. These systems enable intranuclear transcription of shRNA, which is subsequently processed by Dicer into mature siRNAs. While effective in achieving long-term silencing, viral vectors carry inherent risks, including insertional mutagenesis, immunogenicity, and the potential for oncogenic transformation. Furthermore, large-scale manufacturing of high-titer, clinical-grade viral vectors remains costly and technically demanding, limiting their broad applicability in routine therapeutic settings.

Nonviral plasmid-based systems have been developed as safer alternatives to viral vectors. These systems typically rely on the delivery of plasmid DNA encoding shRNA under the control of a U6 or H1 promoter, followed by nuclear translocation and transcription. However, a fundamental limitation of these approaches is the extremely low efficiency of nuclear entry of exogenous plasmid DNA in non-dividing or post-mitotic cells, with less than 1% of internalized plasmids reaching the nucleus. This bottleneck severely restricts the level and duration of shRNA expression, particularly in vivo, where many target tissues, including solid tumors and neurons, consist predominantly of non-dividing cells. Moreover, even when nuclear entry is achieved, the sustained expression of shRNA from plasmid templates is often limited by promoter silencing, epigenetic modifications, and saturation of the nuclear export machinery, particularly through competition with endogenous microRNAs for Exportin-5.

An alternative strategy to bypass nuclear dependence involves the use of bacteriophage T7 RNA polymerase to drive cytoplasmic transcription of shRNA. The T7 polymerase is a single-subunit enzyme that recognizes a specific promoter sequence and transcribes RNA without requiring host cell cofactors, making it uniquely suited for cytoplasmic expression systems. Previous attempts to exploit this property have employed dual-promoter systems, such as the pCMV/pT7 system, in which the cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter drives expression of T7 polymerase in the nucleus, which then transcribes shRNA from a co-delivered pT7 promoter-containing plasmid in the cytoplasm. However, these systems still require nuclear entry of the CMV-driven T7 polymerase expression plasmid, thereby inheriting the same limitations of inefficient nuclear translocation and transient expression. Furthermore, the reliance on CMV promoter activity results in delayed onset of T7 polymerase expression, limiting the speed and magnitude of the autoregulatory feedback loop necessary for sustained shRNA production.

The concept of T7-based autogene systems—wherein T7 polymerase is expressed from a T7 promoter-containing plasmid, enabling autocatalytic amplification of its own production—has been demonstrated in the context of protein expression. These systems rely on an initial trigger of T7 polymerase expression, followed by iterative transcription and translation that results in exponential accumulation of the enzyme and, consequently, high-level expression of a downstream transgene. However, prior applications of T7 autogenes have focused exclusively on protein production and have not been adapted for the sustained expression of RNA molecules such as shRNA. Moreover, existing T7 autogene systems have relied on nuclear transcription of T7 polymerase mRNA via eukaryotic promoters, thereby reintroducing the nuclear entry barrier that undermines the entire rationale for a cytoplasmic expression platform.

Liposomal delivery systems have been widely explored for the systemic delivery of nucleic acids. Cationic lipids, while effective at complexing negatively charged oligonucleotides, often induce cytotoxicity, nonspecific organ accumulation, and immune activation due to strong electrostatic interactions with cell membranes and serum proteins. Neutral lipids, such as 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphatidylcholine (DOPC), have shown promise in reducing immunogenicity and improving biodistribution, particularly in tumor models where the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect allows nano-sized particles to accumulate preferentially in leaky tumor vasculature. However, prior formulations using DOPC liposomes have been limited to the delivery of chemically synthesized siRNAs or antisense oligonucleotides and have not been successfully adapted for the co-delivery of multiple nucleic acid components—including mRNA, DNA fragments, and plasmids—in a stable, functional, and scalable manner.

Thus, despite decades of research into RNAi delivery, a critical unmet need remains for a nonviral, non-immunogenic, and nuclear-independent system capable of inducing sustained, high-level shRNA expression in the cytoplasm following a single administration, with the ability to achieve long-term gene silencing in vivo without repeated dosing. No prior art discloses or suggests a hybrid mRNA/DNA system composed of T7 polymerase mRNA, a pT7-driven shRNA-encoding DNA fragment, and a T7 autogene plasmid containing an internal ribosomal entry site (IRES), all encapsulated within a DOPC liposome, that collectively enable autocatalytic, cytoplasmic, and sustained production of shRNA leading to prolonged siRNA generation and gene silencing for more than thirteen days in vitro and six days in vivo following a single systemic injection. There is also no prior disclosure of a liposomal formulation that simultaneously protects and delivers these three distinct nucleic acid components with high complexing efficiency, maintains nano-scale size and neutral zeta potential, avoids immune activation, and enables tumor-specific accumulation without renal clearance or nonspecific organ uptake.

## DISCLOSURE

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the inability of existing RNA interference (RNAi) delivery systems to achieve prolonged, sustained, and therapeutically meaningful gene silencing in vivo following a single administration, particularly in non-dividing or poorly transfectable cells such as those found in solid tumors, neurons, and primary immune cells. Conventional synthetic siRNA formulations are rapidly degraded and diluted, necessitating frequent re-administration, which is impractical for chronic disease management and increases the risk of off-target effects and immune activation. Viral vector-based shRNA expression systems, while capable of long-term silencing, are limited by safety concerns, high production costs, and the requirement for nuclear entry of genetic material, which is inefficient in non-dividing cells. Nonviral plasmid-based shRNA systems are similarly constrained by poor nuclear translocation, promoter silencing, and saturation of nuclear export machinery. Furthermore, existing cytoplasmic expression systems that utilize T7 polymerase still depend on nuclear transcription of the polymerase gene, thereby failing to fully exploit the advantages of cytoplasmic transcription. There is a critical need for a delivery platform that eliminates the nuclear barrier entirely, enables continuous replenishment of the transcriptional machinery, and produces shRNA at levels sufficient to generate mature siRNAs for over ten days without requiring repeated dosing, while simultaneously avoiding immunogenicity, cytotoxicity, and nonspecific biodistribution. The present invention solves this problem by introducing a novel hybrid mRNA/DNA system that operates entirely within the cytoplasm and is delivered via a biocompatible, neutrally charged liposomal vehicle optimized for systemic administration and tumor targeting.

### Technical Solution

The technical solution provided by the present invention is a hybrid mRNA/DNA system comprising three essential components: (1) a 5′-capped messenger RNA encoding bacteriophage T7 RNA polymerase (T7pol); (2) a synthetic, double-stranded DNA fragment encoding a short hairpin RNA (shRNA) under the transcriptional control of a T7 promoter; and (3) a plasmid DNA encoding a T7 autogene, wherein the T7pol gene is positioned downstream of a T7 promoter and an internal ribosomal entry site (IRES), and upstream of a polyadenylation signal and a T7 transcriptional terminator. These components are co-encapsulated within liposomes composed of 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphatidylcholine (DOPC), forming a stable, nano-sized, neutrally charged lipoplex designated as auto_shRNA@LS. Upon cellular uptake, the 5′-capped T7pol mRNA is immediately translated by cytoplasmic ribosomes to produce initial T7 polymerase protein. This enzyme binds to the T7 promoter on the autogene plasmid, initiating transcription of an IRES-containing T7pol mRNA transcript, which is then efficiently translated into additional T7 polymerase, thereby establishing a positive feedback loop that amplifies T7 polymerase levels exponentially over time. A fraction of the accumulating T7 polymerase molecules bind to the T7 promoter on the pT7/shRNA DNA fragment, driving continuous transcription of shRNA in the cytoplasm. The resulting shRNA hairpins are processed by the endogenous cellular RNAi machinery, including Dicer and RISC, into mature siRNAs that mediate sequence-specific degradation of the target mRNA. The use of a 5′-cap on the T7pol mRNA ensures efficient translation initiation without nuclear involvement, while the inclusion of the IRES element in the autogene plasmid ensures robust translation of the T7pol transcript generated from the autogene, thereby maximizing the autocatalytic amplification. The DOPC liposome vehicle protects the nucleic acid components from degradation, facilitates cellular internalization via endocytosis, and enables preferential accumulation in tumor tissues through the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect due to its nano-scale size and neutral surface charge. This system operates entirely within the cytoplasm, eliminating the need for nuclear entry of any genetic component, and achieves sustained shRNA expression and siRNA generation for over thirteen days in vitro and six days in vivo following a single systemic administration.

### Advantageous Effects of the Invention

The invention provides several significant and unexpected advantages over prior art systems. First, by eliminating the requirement for nuclear entry of any genetic material, the invention enables efficient and sustained gene silencing in non-dividing and post-mitotic cells, which constitute the majority of cells in many therapeutically relevant tissues, including solid tumors, neurons, and cardiac myocytes. Second, the co-delivery of pre-formed, 5′-capped T7pol mRNA with the T7 autogene plasmid results in a 4.0-fold increase in T7 polymerase expression compared to systems relying on nuclear transcription of T7 polymerase via the CMV promoter, leading to significantly higher and more prolonged shRNA production. Third, the system generates mature siRNAs continuously for over thirteen days in vitro, far exceeding the duration of silencing achieved by synthetic siRNAs (typically 2–4 days) or nuclear shRNA expression systems (typically 3–5 days). Fourth, the DOPC liposomal vehicle enables systemic administration with minimal immunogenicity, as demonstrated by the absence of TNF-α or IFN-α induction in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells and in vivo mouse models, in contrast to conventional siRNA-lipofectamine complexes, which trigger robust innate immune responses. Fifth, the neutral charge and nano-scale size (~31 nm) of the lipoplexes prevent renal clearance and promote tumor-specific accumulation via the EPR effect, resulting in high local concentrations of the therapeutic payload at the disease site without significant uptake by the liver or spleen. Sixth, the system achieves long-term gene silencing in vivo after a single intravenous injection, whereas conventional siRNA therapies require repeated dosing every two to three days to maintain efficacy. Seventh, the hybrid system avoids the use of cationic lipids or polycationic polymers, thereby minimizing cytotoxicity and nonspecific tissue binding. Eighth, the modular design of the system allows for easy substitution of the shRNA sequence to target any gene of interest, rendering it a versatile platform technology applicable to a broad range of diseases. Ninth, the system does not integrate into the host genome, eliminating the risk of insertional mutagenesis associated with viral vectors. Tenth, the components are chemically defined and can be manufactured under GMP conditions using scalable in vitro transcription and chemical synthesis methods, enabling cost-effective and reproducible production for clinical translation. Collectively, these advantages represent a paradigm shift in RNAi therapeutics, transforming the field from one reliant on frequent, short-lived interventions to one capable of achieving durable, single-dose gene silencing with an excellent safety profile.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Example 1. Design and Preparation of pT7/T7 Polymerase System Capable of Sustained Expression of shRNA in Cytoplasm

The invention comprises a hybrid mRNA/DNA system designed for sustained cytoplasmic expression of short hairpin RNA (shRNA) without requiring nuclear translocation of any genetic component. The system is composed of three distinct nucleic acid elements: a 5′-capped mRNA encoding bacteriophage T7 RNA polymerase (T7pol), a synthetic double-stranded DNA fragment encoding an shRNA under the control of a T7 promoter, and a plasmid DNA encoding a T7 autogene. The T7pol mRNA is synthesized in vitro using a DNA template containing a T7 promoter sequence upstream of the T7pol coding region, with the inclusion of an anti-reverse cap analog (ARCA) during transcription to ensure incorporation of a 5′-cap structure. This cap structure is essential for efficient ribosomal binding and translation initiation in the cytoplasm, as uncapped mRNAs are rapidly degraded and poorly translated in mammalian cells. The T7pol coding sequence is derived from the bacteriophage T7 genome and encodes a single-subunit RNA polymerase capable of recognizing the T7 promoter and transcribing downstream sequences without the need for host cell transcription factors. The T7pol mRNA is purified by LiCl precipitation and ethanol precipitation to remove residual DNA templates and unincorporated nucleotides, and its concentration and integrity are verified by spectrophotometry and agarose gel electrophoresis. The pT7/shRNA DNA fragment is synthesized using an assembly PCR method, wherein overlapping oligonucleotides are designed to encode a 19-nucleotide sequence complementary to the target mRNA, flanked by a 9-nucleotide loop region and terminated by a T7 promoter sequence at the 5′ end and a T7 terminator at the 3′ end. The first nucleotide of the transcribed RNA is designed to be guanosine, as T7 polymerase initiates transcription most efficiently with a G residue. The DNA fragment is purified by gel extraction and verified by sequencing. The T7 autogene plasmid is constructed by cloning the T7pol coding sequence downstream of a T7 promoter and an internal ribosomal entry site (IRES) derived from encephalomyocarditis virus (EMCV), followed by a polyadenylation signal and a T7 transcriptional terminator. The IRES element is critical for enabling efficient translation of the T7pol mRNA transcribed from the autogene plasmid, as this transcript lacks a 5′ cap and would otherwise be poorly translated. The plasmid backbone is derived from a standard bacterial cloning vector and contains an origin of replication and an antibiotic resistance gene for propagation in E. coli. The plasmid is amplified in bacterial culture, purified by alkaline lysis and endotoxin removal, and verified by restriction digestion and sequencing. The three components are mixed in a defined molar ratio of pT7/shRNA DNA fragment : T7pol mRNA : T7 autogene plasmid of 0.4 : 1 : 1 to ensure optimal balance between initial trigger, amplification, and effector output. This precise stoichiometry is determined empirically to maximize T7 polymerase accumulation without saturating the cellular translation machinery or inducing cytotoxicity. The system is designed to function entirely within the cytoplasm: upon delivery into the cell, the 5′-capped T7pol mRNA is immediately translated into functional T7 polymerase protein, which binds to the T7 promoter on the autogene plasmid and initiates transcription of a new T7pol mRNA bearing the IRES. This new transcript is efficiently translated, leading to exponential amplification of T7 polymerase levels. A portion of the accumulating T7 polymerase binds to the pT7/shRNA DNA fragment, initiating continuous transcription of shRNA hairpins. The shRNA is processed by Dicer into mature siRNAs, which load into RISC to mediate degradation of the target mRNA. This entire cascade occurs without any component entering the nucleus, thereby bypassing the major bottleneck of nuclear import that plagues conventional plasmid-based systems. The system is capable of sustaining T7 polymerase expression for at least nine days and shRNA production for over thirteen days, as demonstrated by quantitative Western blotting and TaqMan assays, respectively.

### Example 2. Preparation of DOPC Liposome Transporter Loaded with Sustained Cytoplasmic Expression System for shRNA

The invention further comprises a liposomal transporter composed of 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphatidylcholine (DOPC) that encapsulates the hybrid mRNA/DNA system described in Example 1. DOPC is selected as the lipid component due to its neutral charge, biocompatibility, and proven ability to facilitate systemic delivery of nucleic acids via the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect. The liposomes are prepared using a solvent evaporation method: DOPC is dissolved in t-butanol at a concentration of 10 mg/mL, and the three nucleic acid components—the pT7/shRNA DNA fragment, the 5′-capped T7pol mRNA, and the T7 autogene plasmid—are added at the previously defined molar ratio of 0.4 : 1 : 1. Tween 20 is incorporated at a weight ratio of 1:19 relative to the total nucleic acid and lipid content to stabilize the lipoplex structure and prevent aggregation during lyophilization. The mixture is then rapidly frozen in an acetone/dry ice bath and lyophilized to remove the organic solvent, resulting in a dry lipid-nucleic acid matrix. The lyophilized cake is rehydrated with sterile 0.9% saline solution to a final concentration of 150 μg/mL of total nucleic acids. The resulting lipoplexes are purified by ultrafiltration using a 30 kDa molecular weight cutoff (MWCO) centrifugal filter to remove unencapsulated nucleic acids, achieving a complexation efficiency of greater than 90%. The lipoplexes are characterized for size and surface charge using dynamic light scattering and zeta potential analysis, revealing a hydrodynamic diameter of 31.4 ± 1.2 nm and a zeta potential of 1.1 mV, confirming their nano-scale size and neutral surface charge. The neutral charge is critical for avoiding opsonization, nonspecific binding to endothelial cells, and rapid clearance by the reticuloendothelial system. The lipoplexes remain stable for at least 72 hours at 4°C and for 24 hours at 37°C in serum-containing media, as confirmed by gel electrophoresis and nanoparticle tracking analysis. The liposomes are capable of encapsulating all three components simultaneously without compromising the structural integrity or biological activity of any component. The encapsulation protects the mRNA from RNase degradation, the DNA fragments from nuclease activity, and the plasmid from shearing forces during circulation. The lipoplexes are formulated to be injectable via intravenous administration, with no visible precipitation or aggregation upon dilution in physiological buffers. The final product, designated auto_shRNA@LS, is sterile-filtered, endotoxin-tested, and stored at 4°C until use. This formulation enables systemic delivery of the entire hybrid system in a single, stable, and scalable vehicle, overcoming the historical challenge of co-delivering multiple nucleic acid species with disparate physicochemical properties.

### Example 3. Examination of Biophysicochemical Properties of DOPC Liposome Transporter Loaded with Sustained Cytoplasmic Expression System for shRNA

The biophysicochemical properties of the DOPC liposome transporter loaded with the sustained cytoplasmic expression system for shRNA (auto_shRNA@LS) were rigorously characterized to ensure its suitability for in vivo delivery. Gel retardation assays were performed to assess the efficiency of nucleic acid complexation with DOPC liposomes. When increasing amounts of DOPC were mixed with a fixed amount of the three nucleic acid components, complete retardation of all components was observed at a weight ratio of oligonucleotides to DOPC of 1:10, indicating that all mRNA, DNA fragment, and plasmid components were fully encapsulated within the liposomes. At lower ratios, partial migration of nucleic acids was observed, confirming that the 1:10 ratio is optimal for complete complexation. Dynamic light scattering analysis revealed that the auto_shRNA@LS lipoplexes had a mean hydrodynamic diameter of 31.4 ± 1.2 nm, with a polydispersity index of less than 0.15, indicating a highly monodisperse population suitable for renal avoidance and EPR-mediated tumor accumulation. The zeta potential was measured at 1.1 ± 0.3 mV, confirming a nearly neutral surface charge, which minimizes interactions with serum proteins and reduces non-specific uptake by the liver and spleen. The lipoplexes were stable in 10% fetal bovine serum for up to 24 hours, with no significant change in size or charge, demonstrating resistance to serum-induced aggregation. Cryo-electron microscopy confirmed the spherical morphology of the lipoplexes, with a lipid bilayer surrounding a core containing the nucleic acid components. The encapsulation efficiency for each component was quantified by fluorescence-based assays: when Cy5.5-labeled pT7/shRNA DNA fragments were incorporated into the formulation, over 92% of the label was retained within the liposomes after purification, and the fluorescence signal remained intact after 48 hours of incubation in serum. The lipoplexes were tested for cytotoxicity using the MTT assay in B16F10/RFP melanoma cells across a concentration range of 0.1 to 60 μg/mL. No significant reduction in cell viability was observed at any concentration after 24 or 48 hours of exposure, confirming the biocompatibility of the formulation. Cellular uptake studies using confocal microscopy and flow cytometry demonstrated that over 98% of cells internalized the Cy5.5-labeled lipoplexes within one hour of incubation, and the fluorescence signal was localized exclusively in the cytoplasm, with no detectable signal in the nucleus, confirming the cytoplasmic delivery mechanism. The lipoplexes did not induce any morphological changes in cells, and no signs of apoptosis or necrosis were observed under light microscopy. These data collectively demonstrate that the DOPC liposome transporter is a highly efficient, stable, non-toxic, and cytoplasm-specific delivery vehicle for the hybrid mRNA/DNA system, enabling systemic administration with minimal off-target effects.

### Example 4. Experiment on Sustained Expression of T7 Polymerase Using Modified pT7/T7 Polymerase System

To evaluate the capacity of the hybrid system to sustain T7 polymerase expression, a series of comparative experiments were conducted in B16F10/RFP cells. Cells were transfected with lipoplexes containing either the full hybrid system (T7pol mRNA + pT7/shRNA DNA fragment + T7 autogene plasmid), a control system lacking the autogene plasmid (T7pol mRNA + pT7/shRNA DNA fragment), or a conventional system using a pCMV-T7pol plasmid to drive T7 polymerase expression from the nucleus. Western blot analysis using a polyclonal antibody specific for T7 polymerase was performed at days 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 post-transfection. In cells transfected with the hybrid system, T7 polymerase levels increased rapidly within the first 24 hours, peaked at day 5, and remained elevated at detectable levels through day 9, with a 4.0-fold higher expression compared to the pCMV-T7pol control at day 7. In contrast, cells transfected with the pCMV-T7pol plasmid showed a modest increase in T7 polymerase at day 1, followed by a sharp decline by day 3, with negligible expression by day 5, consistent with the known silencing of the CMV promoter in mammalian cells. Cells transfected with the system lacking the autogene plasmid exhibited a transient increase in T7 polymerase at day 1, followed by a rapid decline by day 3, confirming that the autogene plasmid is essential for sustained amplification. Quantitative densitometry analysis revealed that the hybrid system produced an average of 12.7 ng of T7 polymerase per million cells at day 7, whereas the pCMV-T7pol system produced only 3.2 ng. The persistence of T7 polymerase expression beyond day 7 was confirmed by immunofluorescence staining, which showed continuous nuclear exclusion of the enzyme and its accumulation in the cytoplasmic compartment. The absence of T7 polymerase in untransfected cells confirmed the specificity of the detection. These results demonstrate that the co-delivery of T7pol mRNA with the IRES-containing autogene plasmid creates a self-sustaining amplification loop that is independent of nuclear transcription and capable of maintaining high levels of the transcriptional enzyme for over nine days. This prolonged expression is critical for the continuous transcription of shRNA and the subsequent generation of mature siRNAs over an extended period, a feature not achievable by any prior nonviral RNAi delivery system.

### Example 5. Examination of Long-Term Cytoplasmic Expression of shRNAs by Auto_shRFP@LS and their Conversion to siRNAs

The long-term cytoplasmic expression of shRNA and its conversion into mature siRNA by the auto_shRFP@LS system was quantitatively assessed using a TaqMan-based small RNA assay. B16F10/RFP cells were transfected with auto_shRFP@LS or a control system lacking the T7 autogene plasmid (auto(−)_shRFP@LS), and total RNA was extracted at days 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, and 13 post-transfection. A custom TaqMan assay was designed to specifically detect the guide strand of the mature RFP siRNA (siRFP), using a stem-loop reverse transcription primer that distinguishes between the precursor shRNA hairpin and the processed siRNA duplex. A standard curve was established using serial dilutions of synthetic siRFP oligonucleotides, enabling absolute quantification of siRFP molecules per cell. In cells treated with auto_shRFP@LS, siRFP levels increased exponentially from day 1 to day 10, reaching a peak of approximately 12,500 molecules per cell, and remained elevated at 8,200 molecules per cell even at day 13. In contrast, cells treated with auto(−)_shRFP@LS showed a linear increase in siRFP, peaking at only 1,800 molecules per cell at day 7 and declining to baseline by day 10. The difference in siRNA production between the two systems was statistically significant (p < 0.0001) at all time points beyond day 5. The absence of amplification in negative controls, including samples treated with scrambled shRNA or untreated cells, confirmed the specificity of the assay. The persistence of siRNA levels beyond day 10 indicates that the T7 polymerase produced by the autogene system continues to transcribe shRNA for over thirteen days, and that the cellular RNAi machinery remains active and capable of processing the hairpins into functional siRNAs throughout this period. This is the first demonstration of sustained siRNA production in mammalian cells without viral vectors or genomic integration, and represents a fundamental advancement in RNAi therapeutics. The continuous production of siRNA ensures that target mRNA degradation occurs over an extended time window, preventing rebound of gene expression that is commonly observed with transient siRNA delivery.

### Example 6. Examination of In Vitro Gene Silencing by Auto shRNA @LS Nanoparticles

The functional consequence of sustained siRNA production was evaluated by measuring the silencing of the red fluorescent protein (RFP) gene in B16F10/RFP cells. Cells were transfected with auto_shRFP@LS, a control system lacking the autogene plasmid (auto(−)_shRFP@LS), synthetic siRFP@LS, or scrambled auto_shRFP@LS, and RFP mRNA and protein levels were monitored over 14 days. Quantitative RT-PCR analysis revealed that RFP mRNA levels in auto_shRFP@LS-treated cells decreased progressively, reaching a nadir of 54% of control levels at day 7 and remaining suppressed at 61% at day 13. In contrast, synthetic siRFP@LS-treated cells showed a transient reduction to 75% at day 3, followed by complete recovery to baseline levels by day 7. The auto(−)_shRFP@LS system showed moderate suppression, reaching 78% at day 5 and returning to baseline by day 10, confirming that the autogene is essential for prolonged silencing. Western blot analysis confirmed these findings at the protein level: RFP protein was reduced by 82% at day 7 in auto_shRFP@LS-treated cells and remained 58% suppressed at day 10, whereas siRFP@LS-treated cells showed only 35% suppression at day 3 and full recovery by day 5. Flow cytometry analysis of RFP fluorescence intensity showed that 49% of cells remained RFP-negative at day 7 in the auto_shRFP@LS group, compared to 12% in the siRFP@LS group. By day 10, 33% of cells in the auto_shRFP@LS group remained RFP-negative, while all cells in the siRFP@LS group had fully recovered fluorescence. These data demonstrate that the auto_shRNA@LS system achieves a level of silencing duration and depth that is unattainable by any conventional siRNA delivery method. The persistence of silencing correlates directly with the sustained production of siRNA, as demonstrated in Example 5, and confirms that the system is not merely delaying degradation but actively replenishing the silencing agent over time. The lack of silencing in the scrambled control confirms sequence specificity, and the absence of off-target effects on housekeeping genes such as β-actin confirms the fidelity of the RNAi mechanism.

### Example 7. Examination of Cancer Accumulation Property of Auto_shRNA@LS Nanoparticles in Mouse Xenograft Model

To evaluate the in vivo biodistribution and tumor-targeting capability of the auto_shRNA@LS nanoparticles, a B16F10 melanoma xenograft model was established in BALB/c nude mice. Cy5.5-labeled auto_shRNA@LS lipoplexes or free Cy5.5-siRFP were administered intravenously via tail vein, and mice were sacrificed 24 hours later for ex vivo imaging of organs and tumors. Fluorescence imaging using the IVIS Spectrum system revealed that free Cy5.5-siRFP accumulated predominantly in the kidneys, with no detectable signal in the tumor tissue, indicating rapid renal clearance and lack of tumor retention. In stark contrast, Cy5.5-labeled auto_shRNA@LS lipoplexes exhibited intense fluorescence in the tumor tissue, with a 7.3-fold higher signal intensity compared to free siRFP. Moderate fluorescence was also observed in the liver and spleen, consistent with some degree of reticuloendothelial system uptake, but the tumor-to-liver and tumor-to-spleen ratios were 3.8 and 4.1, respectively, indicating preferential accumulation at the tumor site. Histological analysis of tumor sections confirmed the presence of lipoplexes within the tumor interstitium, with no evidence of extravasation into surrounding healthy tissue. The particle size of 31.4 nm is below the renal filtration threshold of approximately 5–6 nm, explaining the prolonged circulation time and enabling passive targeting via the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect. The neutral charge of the lipoplexes minimizes opsonization and macrophage uptake, further enhancing tumor accumulation. These findings demonstrate that the DOPC liposome transporter enables efficient systemic delivery of the hybrid mRNA/DNA system to solid tumors, overcoming the major limitation of conventional nucleic acid delivery systems that are rapidly cleared or sequestered in non-target organs. The ability to achieve high local concentrations of the therapeutic payload at the tumor site without repeated dosing is a critical advantage for clinical translation.

### Example 8. Examination of Gene Silencing of Auto_shRNA@LS Nanoparticles in Mouse Xenograft Model

The therapeutic efficacy of the auto_shRNA@LS nanoparticles was evaluated in a B16F10/RFP tumor xenograft model using noninvasive fluorescence imaging. Mice bearing established RFP-expressing tumors were administered a single intravenous injection of auto_shRNA@LS, scrambled auto_shRNA@LS, synthetic siRFP@LS, or PBS. RFP fluorescence intensity was measured at days 0, 3, 4, 5, and 6 post-injection. In the PBS and scrambled control groups, RFP fluorescence increased proportionally with tumor growth, as expected. In the siRFP@LS group, a transient suppression of fluorescence was observed at day 3, with a 42% reduction compared to baseline, but fluorescence rebounded to control levels by day 5. In contrast, mice treated with auto_shRNA@LS exhibited a progressive and sustained reduction in RFP fluorescence, with a 68% decrease at day 3 and a 74% decrease at day 6, despite continued tumor growth. Ex vivo imaging of excised tumors confirmed these findings, with auto_shRNA@LS-treated tumors showing a 2.7-fold lower RFP signal intensity compared to PBS-treated tumors. Immunohistochemical staining using anti-RFP antibodies revealed abundant RFP protein expression in tumors from control groups, while tumors from auto_shRNA@LS-treated mice showed near-complete absence of RFP staining. Quantitative RT-PCR analysis of tumor tissue RNA confirmed a 43% reduction in RFP mRNA levels in the auto_shRNA@LS group compared to PBS controls, whereas no significant reduction was observed in the siRFP@LS or scrambled groups. Plasma cytokine analysis showed no elevation of TNF-α or IFN-α at 1 or 24 hours post-injection in the auto_shRNA@LS group, whereas siRFP@Lipofectamine induced a significant spike in both cytokines at 1 hour, confirming the low immunogenicity of the formulation. These results demonstrate that a single administration of auto_shRNA@LS nanoparticles is sufficient to achieve sustained, sequence-specific gene silencing in vivo for at least six days, a duration unprecedented in nonviral RNAi therapeutics. The combination of tumor-specific delivery, sustained intracellular siRNA production, and lack of immune activation establishes this system as a transformative platform for cancer therapy and other chronic diseases requiring long-term gene suppression.
