# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biopharmaceuticals and ocular therapeutics, specifically to novel engineered protein inhibitors of vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) for the treatment of angiogenic and vascular permeability-related diseases of the eye. The invention encompasses a class of highly potent, stable, and soluble designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins) that bind VEGF-A with picomolar affinity, effectively neutralizing its biological activity in both anterior and posterior segments of the eye. These DARPins are suitable for administration via intravitreal injection or topical application as ocular drops, offering a transformative approach to the management of sight-threatening conditions such as wet age-related macular degeneration, diabetic macular edema, retinal vein occlusion, corneal neovascularization, and choroidal neovascularization. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising these DARPins in stable, high-concentration liquid formulations, methods of manufacturing such compositions, and therapeutic regimens utilizing them to achieve prolonged intraocular efficacy with reduced dosing frequency compared to existing anti-VEGF biologics.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) is a central mediator of pathological angiogenesis and vascular hyperpermeability in a variety of ocular diseases. Its overexpression in the retina and choroid drives the formation of abnormal, leaky blood vessels that compromise visual acuity and lead to irreversible structural damage. In wet age-related macular degeneration, for example, VEGF-A promotes the growth of choroidal neovascular membranes that invade the subretinal space, causing hemorrhage, fluid accumulation, and photoreceptor degeneration. Similarly, in diabetic retinopathy and retinal vein occlusion, elevated VEGF-A levels induce breakdown of the blood-retinal barrier, resulting in macular edema and progressive vision loss. The clinical success of VEGF-A inhibition has been well established, with monoclonal antibody fragments such as ranibizumab and bevacizumab, as well as aptamers like pegaptanib, becoming standard-of-care therapies for these conditions. These agents have demonstrated significant efficacy in halting disease progression and, in many cases, restoring vision.

Despite their therapeutic value, current anti-VEGF treatments are burdened by substantial limitations. The most critical drawback is the necessity for frequent intravitreal injections, typically administered every four to six weeks, to maintain therapeutic concentrations in the vitreous humor. This regimen places considerable physical, psychological, and financial strain on patients, who must endure repeated invasive procedures associated with risks such as endophthalmitis, retinal detachment, cataract progression, and intraocular pressure spikes. The cumulative incidence of these complications increases with each injection, and the economic burden on healthcare systems is immense, driven by the cost of drug procurement, physician time, sterile procedural infrastructure, and post-injection monitoring. Moreover, patient adherence to these demanding schedules remains suboptimal, leading to undertreatment and preventable vision loss.

The pharmacokinetic profile of existing agents further exacerbates this challenge. Ranibizumab, a Fab fragment derived from a humanized monoclonal antibody, has a vitreous half-life of approximately five to seven days, necessitating frequent re-administration. Bevacizumab, though used off-label, exhibits similar clearance kinetics and lacks formal regulatory approval for intraocular use. Pegaptanib, an RNA aptamer, binds only a specific isoform of VEGF-A and demonstrates lower potency, requiring higher dosing concentrations and offering less comprehensive inhibition. These limitations have spurred intense research into next-generation VEGF inhibitors with enhanced binding affinity, prolonged intraocular persistence, and alternative routes of delivery.

Topical administration of biologics via eye drops represents a compelling alternative to intravitreal injection, as it eliminates procedural risks, reduces patient burden, and lowers healthcare costs. However, the ocular barriers—including the corneal epithelium, tear turnover, and nasolacrimal drainage—have historically rendered systemic delivery of large proteins ineffective for posterior segment diseases. Conventional antibodies and even smaller fragments have shown negligible penetration into the retina following topical application, limiting their utility in this context. Consequently, the development of a molecule capable of achieving sufficient intraocular bioavailability after topical instillation, while maintaining high potency against VEGF-A, has remained an unmet need.

The field has also sought agents with improved biophysical properties to facilitate high-concentration formulation, long-term stability, and ease of manufacturing. Antibody-based therapeutics often suffer from aggregation, low solubility at high concentrations, and sensitivity to thermal stress, complicating storage, transport, and clinical deployment. In contrast, engineered non-antibody scaffolds such as designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins) have emerged as promising alternatives due to their small size, high thermal and thermodynamic stability, exceptional solubility, and lack of disulfide bonds or glycosylation requirements. DARPins are derived from naturally occurring ankyrin repeat domains and can be selected from large synthetic libraries using in vitro display technologies such as ribosome display, enabling the rapid isolation of high-affinity binders without the need for immunization or affinity maturation. Their single-domain architecture allows for straightforward recombinant expression in bacterial systems, yielding high titers of pure, endotoxin-free protein suitable for direct formulation.

Despite these advantages, no DARPin-based VEGF-A inhibitor has previously been demonstrated to achieve both picomolar binding affinity and functional efficacy in ocular disease models via either intravitreal or topical routes. Moreover, the ability of DARPins to penetrate retinal tissues following intravitreal injection and to retain biological activity after topical application to the cornea—thereby reaching the posterior segment—has not been established. The present invention addresses these gaps by disclosing a novel family of VEGF-A-targeting DARPins with unprecedented binding potency, demonstrated efficacy in multiple preclinical models of ocular neovascularization and vascular leakage, and compatibility with both intravitreal and topical delivery modalities. These molecules represent a paradigm shift in ocular biologics, offering the potential to replace frequent injections with infrequent dosing or even non-invasive topical therapy, thereby transforming the standard of care for millions of patients worldwide.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a class of novel designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins) that bind vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) with high specificity and picomolar affinity, effectively inhibiting its interaction with its primary receptor, VEGFR-2, and thereby suppressing downstream angiogenic and vascular permeability signaling pathways. These DARPins are engineered to be stable, soluble, and non-immunogenic, enabling their formulation into high-concentration liquid preparations suitable for intravitreal injection or topical ocular administration. The invention encompasses isolated and purified DARPins comprising amino acid sequences derived from ankyrin repeat scaffolds, specifically selected for their ability to bind VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121 isoforms with dissociation constants below 100 pM, as confirmed by surface plasmon resonance and competitive enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays. These DARPins exhibit no cross-reactivity with other members of the VEGF family, including VEGF-C, and demonstrate potent inhibition of VEGF-A-induced phosphorylation of VEGFR-2 in human endothelial cells, as well as suppression of endothelial sprouting in three-dimensional spheroid models of angiogenesis.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a DARPin designated as DARPin #4, which binds VEGF-A with an apparent IC50 of less than 10 pM in a Quantikine sandwich ELISA and inhibits VEGF-A-induced vascular leakage in rabbit eyes following intravitreal administration at a dose of 50 μg, achieving a fluorescence leakage ratio of 0.8 ± 0.1 compared to 6.9 ± 3.1 in vehicle-treated controls. In another embodiment, the invention provides DARPin #6, which, when administered topically as eye drops at a concentration of 10 mg/mL four times daily, significantly reduces the size of laser-induced choroidal neovascularization lesions in rats to a degree comparable to intravitreal triamcinolone, with a mean lesion reduction of 43% in vessel density and 31% in maximum vessel length in a rabbit corneal suture model. These DARPins are capable of penetrating the retinal layers following intravitreal injection, as demonstrated by fluorescent labeling and confocal microscopy in murine models, and are retained in ocular tissues for extended periods without evidence of inflammatory response or tissue damage.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more of these DARPins in a pharmaceutically acceptable aqueous buffer, wherein the concentration of the DARPin is at least 10 mg/mL, preferably at least 20 mg/mL, and most preferably at least 30 mg/mL, with minimal excipients to preserve stability and solubility. The compositions are formulated without preservatives, adjuvants, or chemical modifications such as PEGylation, and are stable for at least six months when stored at 2–8°C. The DARPins are produced by recombinant expression in Escherichia coli, purified from the soluble fraction using affinity and size-exclusion chromatography, and endotoxin-depleted to levels below 0.1 EU/mg, ensuring compatibility with intraocular administration. The invention also encompasses methods of treating ocular diseases characterized by pathological angiogenesis or vascular leakage, comprising administering an effective amount of the disclosed DARPins to a patient in need thereof, either by intravitreal injection or topical instillation, with dosing intervals of at least seven days, preferably fourteen days, and most preferably twenty-one days or longer, thereby reducing the frequency of intervention compared to existing therapies.

The invention further includes diagnostic kits for detecting VEGF-A levels in ocular fluids, comprising immobilized DARPins of the invention as capture agents, and methods of screening for additional VEGF-A inhibitors using the disclosed DARPins as reference standards. The DARPins of the invention are distinguished from prior art by their combination of ultra-high affinity, lack of effector functions, resistance to aggregation, and demonstrated efficacy via both intravitreal and topical routes, enabling a new class of ocular therapeutics that are safer, more convenient, and more cost-effective than current biologics. The invention thus provides a transformative solution to the longstanding clinical challenge of frequent intraocular injections, opening the door to non-invasive, patient-friendly treatment regimens for chronic retinal diseases.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a novel class of engineered protein therapeutics based on designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins) that function as high-affinity, high-potency antagonists of vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) for the treatment of ocular diseases involving pathological angiogenesis and vascular permeability. These DARPins are derived from a non-immunoglobulin scaffold that confers exceptional biophysical stability, solubility, and manufacturability, distinguishing them from conventional antibody-based anti-VEGF agents. The invention encompasses the isolation, characterization, and therapeutic application of specific DARPin variants that exhibit picomolar binding affinity for VEGF-A, effectively blocking its interaction with VEGFR-2 and suppressing downstream signaling cascades responsible for endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and vascular leakage. The DARPins of the invention are produced through recombinant expression in bacterial systems, purified to homogeneity without chemical modification, and formulated into stable, high-concentration liquid compositions suitable for direct administration to the eye via either intravitreal injection or topical instillation.

The core structural framework of the DARPins described herein is based on the consensus ankyrin repeat motif, which consists of approximately 33 amino acids arranged in a helix-turn-helix conformation that stacks to form a solenoid-like structure with a large, continuous binding surface. Unlike antibodies, which rely on hypervariable loops for antigen recognition, DARPins utilize a combination of conserved framework residues and strategically positioned variable residues at the N-terminal and C-terminal cap regions to achieve high specificity and affinity. The DARPins of the invention were generated through ribosome display selection from naïve libraries containing multiple repeat units, without the need for subsequent affinity maturation or randomization. This approach enabled the rapid identification of binders with intrinsic picomolar affinity, a feat previously unattainable with in vitro selection from unmodified libraries. The selected DARPins bind VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121 with equal potency, indicating that they target a conserved epitope common to both major splice variants, while showing no detectable binding to VEGF-C, VEGF-B, or placental growth factor, thereby minimizing the risk of off-target effects.

The binding affinity of the DARPins was rigorously quantified using multiple orthogonal methods. In a Quantikine sandwich ELISA, where VEGF-A is captured by a monoclonal antibody and detected via a polyclonal HRP-conjugated antibody, the DARPins demonstrated dose-dependent inhibition of signal with IC50 values below 10 pM when VEGF-A was present at limiting concentrations. This level of inhibition was significantly greater than that observed with ranibizumab or bevacizumab under identical conditions. Surface plasmon resonance analysis confirmed these findings, revealing association rate constants (kon) in the range of 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹ and dissociation rate constants (koff) below 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, corresponding to equilibrium dissociation constants (KD) in the low picomolar range. The slow off-rates suggest that the DARPins form long-lived complexes with VEGF-A, effectively sequestering the ligand and preventing its interaction with VEGFR-2 for extended durations. This kinetic profile is critical for sustained pharmacological activity in the vitreous humor, where clearance mechanisms are relatively slow but still capable of reducing drug concentration over time.

Functional antagonism of VEGF-A signaling was confirmed in two complementary cellular assays. In a VEGFR-2 phosphorylation assay using immortalized human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs), pre-incubation of VEGF-A with the DARPins completely abolished receptor phosphorylation at concentrations as low as 1 nM, whereas isotype control DARPins had no effect. The potency of inhibition was comparable to or exceeded that of ranibizumab, even though the DARPins were monovalent and ranibizumab is bivalent. In a three-dimensional spheroid sprouting assay, where HUVECs aggregate into spheroids and extend capillary-like projections in response to VEGF-A stimulation, the DARPins suppressed sprout formation with near-complete efficacy at 1 nM, reducing total sprout length by over 80% compared to vehicle controls. These results confirm that the DARPins not only bind VEGF-A with high affinity but also functionally neutralize its biological activity in physiologically relevant cellular contexts.

A critical feature of the DARPins of the invention is their exceptional biophysical stability. Unlike antibodies, which are prone to aggregation, denaturation, and conformational instability at high concentrations or elevated temperatures, the DARPins remain fully soluble and monomeric even at concentrations exceeding 50 mg/mL. This property is attributable to their lack of disulfide bonds, absence of glycosylation, and highly compact, rigid structure. Thermal denaturation studies using circular dichroism spectroscopy revealed melting temperatures (Tm) above 85°C, far exceeding those of conventional antibodies, which typically denature between 60°C and 70°C. The DARPins also exhibited no loss of binding activity after prolonged storage at 4°C for over six months or after multiple freeze-thaw cycles, indicating exceptional formulation stability. These characteristics enable the development of concentrated, preservative-free liquid formulations that can be administered in small volumes, minimizing mechanical stress on ocular tissues during injection and reducing the risk of post-injection inflammation.

The DARPins were produced through recombinant expression in Escherichia coli XL-1 Blue cells, using a plasmid-based system that directs cytoplasmic expression without fusion tags or signal peptides. The proteins were recovered from the soluble fraction following cell lysis and purified using a two-step process involving immobilized metal affinity chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography. The final product was free of aggregates, as confirmed by analytical ultracentrifugation and dynamic light scattering, and contained less than 0.1 endotoxin units per milligram, as determined by Limulus amebocyte lysate assay. The purified DARPins were then dialyzed into phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and concentrated to the desired formulation strength. No chemical modifications, such as PEGylation, glycosylation, or fusion to albumin or Fc domains, were applied, preserving their small size, rapid tissue penetration, and lack of Fc-mediated effector functions.

The ability of the DARPins to penetrate ocular tissues following intravitreal injection was demonstrated using fluorescently labeled variants. DARPin #4 and an isotype control, each engineered with a single C-terminal cysteine for site-specific Alexa Fluor 555 conjugation, were injected into the vitreous cavity of mice. At 24, 48, and 72 hours post-injection, whole-eye cross-sections were examined by confocal microscopy. The labeled DARPin was detected throughout all retinal layers, including the inner nuclear layer, outer nuclear layer, and photoreceptor layer, with no evidence of preferential retention in the vitreous or anterior segment. The isotype control exhibited identical distribution, confirming that penetration was not dependent on VEGF-A binding but rather on the small size and hydrophilic nature of the DARPin scaffold. No autofluorescence or nonspecific signal was observed in control eyes injected with PBS alone, validating the specificity of the detection. These findings indicate that the DARPins are capable of reaching the site of pathological VEGF-A production in the retina and choroid, a prerequisite for effective treatment of posterior segment diseases.

The therapeutic efficacy of the DARPins was evaluated in multiple preclinical models of ocular neovascularization and vascular leakage. In a rabbit model of VEGF-A-induced vascular permeability, intravitreal injection of 50 μg of DARPin #4 completely suppressed fluorescein leakage into the vitreous cavity following subsequent intravitreal administration of recombinant human VEGF-A165. The fluorescence intensity ratio between the treated and untreated contralateral eye was 0.8 ± 0.1, indicating near-complete inhibition of vascular leakage, whereas vehicle-treated eyes exhibited a ratio of 6.9 ± 3.1, reflecting unimpeded permeability. This effect was achieved at a dose less than one-tenth of the clinically used dose of ranibizumab, underscoring the superior potency of the DARPins. Histological examination of the retinas and choroids revealed no signs of inflammation, edema, or tissue damage, and no systemic adverse events were observed.

In a separate study, the DARPins were administered topically as eye drops in a rabbit model of corneal neovascularization induced by suture placement. Eight daily instillations of a 20 mg/mL solution of DARPin #4 for 20 consecutive days resulted in a 43% reduction in vessel density and a 31% reduction in maximum vessel length compared to vehicle-treated controls. The effect was dose-dependent and sustained throughout the treatment period, with no evidence of corneal toxicity, epithelial erosion, or conjunctival irritation beyond that attributable to the suture procedure itself. These results demonstrate that the DARPins are capable of penetrating the corneal epithelium, traversing the anterior chamber, and reaching the posterior segment in sufficient concentrations to exert a therapeutic effect.

Further validation was obtained in a rat model of laser-induced choroidal neovascularization (CNV), a well-established surrogate for wet age-related macular degeneration. Topical administration of DARPin #6 at 10 mg/mL four times daily for 14 days reduced the mean CNV lesion volume by 40% compared to PBS-treated controls, with statistical significance comparable to that achieved by intravitreal triamcinolone. Fluorescein angiography and histological analysis of flat-mounted choroids confirmed a marked reduction in the number and size of neovascular complexes. The DARPins were well tolerated, with no signs of ocular irritation, intraocular inflammation, or systemic toxicity. Body weight remained stable throughout the study, and no behavioral abnormalities were observed.

The DARPins of the invention are distinct from all previously described anti-VEGF agents in several key respects. First, they are the first non-antibody biologics to demonstrate picomolar affinity for VEGF-A without affinity maturation. Second, they are the first to show functional efficacy in both intravitreal and topical delivery routes, overcoming the historical barrier to non-invasive posterior segment therapy. Third, they are formulated without excipients, preservatives, or chemical modifications, simplifying manufacturing, reducing immunogenicity risk, and enhancing stability. Fourth, their small size and high solubility enable high-concentration formulations that allow for sub-10 μL intravitreal injections, minimizing mechanical trauma. Fifth, their thermal stability permits storage at refrigerated temperatures without lyophilization, reducing cold-chain requirements and cost. Finally, their lack of Fc domains eliminates the risk of complement activation or antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, making them ideal antagonists for cytokine blockade without unintended immune consequences.

The invention further encompasses methods of manufacturing the DARPins at scale, including fermentation in bioreactors, purification using multi-step chromatography, sterile filtration, and fill-finish operations under aseptic conditions. The resulting product is a clear, colorless, sterile solution in PBS, pH 7.4, containing 10–50 mg/mL of DARPin, with no detectable endotoxin, aggregates, or host cell proteins. The composition may be packaged in single-dose vials, prefilled syringes, or multidose containers for intravitreal use, or in dropper bottles for topical application. The invention also includes methods of treatment comprising the administration of the DARPin to a human patient suffering from wet age-related macular degeneration, diabetic macular edema, retinal vein occlusion, corneal neovascularization, or choroidal neovascularization, wherein the DARPin is administered either by intravitreal injection at a dose of 10–100 μg every 4 to 12 weeks, or by topical instillation of 1–5 drops of a 10–30 mg/mL solution four to eight times daily. The treatment may be initiated as monotherapy or in combination with other agents, such as corticosteroids or anti-inflammatory drugs, depending on the clinical presentation.

The DARPins of the invention represent a fundamental advancement in ocular biologics, offering a pathway to durable, non-invasive, and cost-effective therapy for chronic retinal diseases. Their unique combination of ultra-high affinity, broad tissue penetration, exceptional stability, and compatibility with multiple delivery routes positions them as the next generation of anti-VEGF therapeutics, capable of transforming clinical practice and improving outcomes for millions of patients worldwide.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The generation of a panel of high-affinity vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A)-binding designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins) was accomplished through ribosome display selection from naïve DARPin libraries without the use of affinity maturation or additional randomization. Two distinct libraries, designated N2C and N3C, were constructed using synthetic DNA sequences encoding ankyrin repeat modules with variable residues at positions known to mediate target interaction. The libraries were subjected to seven rounds of selection, beginning with three standard rounds on immobilized human VEGF-A165 coated onto Maxisorp microtiter plates. Following these rounds, three consecutive off-rate selection rounds were performed using decreasing concentrations of biotinylated VEGF-A (ranging from 1 nM to 10 pM) in the presence of a 1 μM excess of unlabeled VEGF-A to competitively displace low-affinity binders. A final enrichment round was conducted to consolidate the selected pool. DNA from the final pool was amplified using a proofreading DNA polymerase to minimize mutational error, ligated into a bacterial expression vector, and transformed into Escherichia coli XL-1 Blue cells. Individual colonies were picked and grown in deep-well plates, and crude lysates were screened for VEGF-A binding using a plate-based ELISA format. Binding DARPins were detected using an anti-RGSHis antibody conjugated to alkaline phosphatase, with 4-nitrophenyl phosphate serving as the chromogenic substrate. DARPins yielding strong signals above background were selected for further characterization. The DNA sequences of the top binders were determined by Sanger sequencing, and the corresponding amino acid sequences were deduced. Ten distinct DARPins were identified, each exhibiting unique amino acid sequences in their variable repeat regions while maintaining the conserved ankyrin repeat scaffold. These DARPins were designated DARPin #1 through DARPin #10. All ten DARPins were expressed in shake-flask cultures using LB-Lennox medium supplemented with 1% glucose and harvested at mid-log phase. Expression levels ranged from 180 to 220 mg of soluble protein per liter of culture, consistent with previously reported yields for unmodified DARPins. The proteins were purified from the soluble fraction using nickel-nitrilotriacetic acid affinity chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography on a Superdex 200 column. Purity was confirmed by SDS-PAGE, which revealed a single band at approximately 18 kDa, corresponding to the expected molecular weight of the DARPin monomer. Mass spectrometry analysis confirmed the identity and homogeneity of the purified proteins, with no detectable modifications or degradation products. The purified DARPins were then re-buffered into phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) by dialysis using a 3 kDa molecular weight cutoff membrane and concentrated to 1–2 mg/mL. These DARPins formed the foundation for subsequent functional and binding analyses.

### Example 2

The binding affinity and specificity of the ten VEGF-A-binding DARPins were rigorously characterized using a Quantikine sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) designed to measure the ability of test compounds to inhibit the binding of VEGF-A to a capture antibody. In this assay, VEGF-A at a concentration of 25 pM was pre-incubated with each DARPin at 250 pM, or with control agents including ranibizumab (Lucentis) at 125 pM, bevacizumab (Avastin) at 125 pM, and two non-binding isotype control DARPins (I1 and I2) at 250 pM. The mixtures were then transferred to microtiter plates pre-coated with a monoclonal anti-VEGF-A antibody. After incubation and washing, bound VEGF-A was detected using a polyclonal anti-VEGF-A antibody conjugated to horseradish peroxidase, followed by addition of a chromogenic substrate. The optical density was measured at 450 nm, and the percentage of inhibition was calculated relative to a control containing VEGF-A without inhibitor. All ten DARPins demonstrated potent inhibition of VEGF-A binding, with signal suppression exceeding 50% in all cases, and seven of the ten achieving inhibition levels approaching the background signal of the assay, indicating near-complete neutralization. In contrast, the isotype control DARPins showed no significant inhibition, confirming that the observed effects were specific to VEGF-A binding. Ranibizumab and bevacizumab also inhibited the signal, but to a lesser extent than the top-performing DARPins. To determine the potency of the most effective candidate, DARPin #4, a dose-response curve was generated using VEGF-A at a fixed concentration of 20 pM and DARPin #4 concentrations ranging from 0.1 pM to 100 pM. The data were fitted using a four-parameter logistic model, yielding an apparent IC50 value of 10 pM. Given that the VEGF-A concentration was limiting, this value represents an underestimate of the true affinity, and the actual dissociation constant is expected to be below 10 pM. To confirm these findings, surface plasmon resonance (SPR) was employed using a Biacore T200 instrument. VEGF-A165 was immobilized on a CM5 sensor chip, and DARPin #4 was flowed over the surface at concentrations ranging from 0.1 nM to 10 nM. The association and dissociation phases were monitored in real time, and kinetic parameters were calculated using a 1:1 binding model. The association rate constant (kon) was determined to be 1.2 × 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹, and the dissociation rate constant (koff) was 4.8 × 10⁻⁶ s⁻¹, yielding a calculated equilibrium dissociation constant (KD) of 4.0 pM. Similar results were obtained for DARPin #6, with a KD of 7.2 pM. These values represent some of the highest affinities ever reported for a protein binder isolated from a naïve library without affinity maturation. Cross-reactivity studies were performed using VEGF-A121, VEGF-A165, and VEGF-C. All DARPins bound VEGF-A121 and VEGF-A165 with comparable affinity, while no binding to VEGF-C was detected, even at concentrations up to 100 nM, confirming their specificity for the VEGF-A isoforms.

### Example 3

The functional antagonism of VEGF-A signaling by the selected DARPins was evaluated using two complementary in vitro models of endothelial cell activation. The first model assessed the inhibition of VEGF receptor 2 (VEGFR-2) phosphorylation in immortalized human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs). Cells were plated in 48-well plates and allowed to adhere overnight in endothelial cell growth medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. The medium was then replaced with serum-free medium, and cells were incubated for 7 minutes at room temperature with either 1.4 nM VEGF-A alone, 1.4 nM VEGF-A pre-incubated with 1.4 nM of each DARPin, or buffer controls. Following incubation, cells were lysed in a buffer containing phosphatase inhibitors, and the lysates were analyzed using a sandwich ELISA specific for phosphorylated VEGFR-2. The assay utilized a capture antibody directed against the VEGFR-2 extracellular domain and a detection antibody specific for phosphotyrosine residues. Signal was quantified by absorbance at 450 nm. All ten DARPins completely suppressed VEGF-A-induced phosphorylation of VEGFR-2, reducing the signal to baseline levels observed in the absence of VEGF-A. The isotype control DARPins had no effect, demonstrating that inhibition was dependent on VEGF-A binding. Ranibizumab and bevacizumab also inhibited phosphorylation, but the DARPins achieved equivalent or superior inhibition at the same concentration, despite being monovalent. The second model employed a three-dimensional spheroid sprouting assay to evaluate the inhibition of angiogenic sprouting in a more physiologically relevant context. HUVECs were seeded in hanging drops of medium containing carboxymethylcellulose to form spheroids over 24 hours. The spheroids were then embedded in a collagen type I matrix and cultured in serum-free medium supplemented with 3 ng/mL VEGF-A and either 1 nM DARPin, 1 nM ranibizumab, or vehicle control. After 24 hours, the spheroids were imaged using phase-contrast microscopy, and sprout length was quantified using automated image analysis software. The total sprout length per spheroid was calculated for each condition. DARPins #2 and #4, selected for their high binding affinity, reduced sprout length by 85% and 88%, respectively, compared to the vehicle control. Ranibizumab achieved a 79% reduction, while the isotype control DARPin I2 showed no inhibition, with sprout lengths equivalent to the VEGF-A-only control. Statistical analysis using one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test confirmed that all active treatments were significantly different from the control (p < 0.001). These results demonstrate that the DARPins not only bind VEGF-A with high affinity but also effectively neutralize its biological function in endothelial cell activation, thereby inhibiting key steps in the angiogenic cascade.

### Example 4

To determine whether the DARPins could penetrate ocular tissues following intravitreal injection, fluorescently labeled versions of DARPin #4 and an isotype control DARPin (I1) were generated and administered to mice. Both DARPins were engineered to contain a single C-terminal cysteine residue to enable site-specific conjugation with Alexa Fluor 555, a fluorescent dye with excitation and emission maxima of 555 nm and 580 nm, respectively. The purified DARPins were re-buffered into PBS, and the cysteine residue was reacted with the dye according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Unconjugated dye was removed using Nap-5 desalting columns, followed by extensive dialysis against PBS. UV spectrophotometry confirmed labeling efficiencies of 32% and 73% for DARPin #4 and I1, respectively. Endotoxin levels were reduced to below 0.1 EU/mg using EndoTrap columns, and the final concentration of each labeled DARPin was adjusted to 34 μM and 130 μM, respectively. Three-week-old C57BL/6 mice were anesthetized with isoflurane, pupils were dilated with phenylephrine and tropicamide, and 1 μL of the labeled DARPin or PBS control was injected into the vitreous cavity using a 33-gauge needle under an operating microscope. Eyes were enucleated at 24, 48, and 72 hours post-injection, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, cryosectioned at 10 μm thickness, and mounted on glass slides. Fluorescence imaging was performed using a confocal laser scanning microscope with identical exposure settings across all samples. Fluorescent signal from the labeled DARPins was clearly detectable throughout the retinal layers, including the ganglion cell layer, inner plexiform layer, inner nuclear layer, outer plexiform layer, and photoreceptor layer. The signal intensity remained stable over the 72-hour observation period, with no evidence of diffusion into the anterior chamber or optic nerve. Control eyes injected with PBS alone showed no fluorescence, confirming the absence of autofluorescence artifacts. The isotype control DARPin I1 exhibited identical distribution patterns, indicating that penetration was independent of VEGF-A binding and attributable to the small size and hydrophilic nature of the DARPin scaffold. No signs of retinal thinning, cellular infiltration, or structural disruption were observed in histological sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin. These findings demonstrate that the DARPins are capable of diffusing through the vitreous and reaching the site of pathological VEGF-A expression in the retina, a critical requirement for the treatment of posterior segment diseases.

### Example 5

The therapeutic efficacy of the DARPins was evaluated in two in vivo models of ocular neovascularization and vascular leakage. The first model assessed the inhibition of VEGF-A-induced vascular permeability in pigmented rabbits. Two groups of five rabbits, each weighing between 2 and 2.5 kg, were used. On day 1, one eye of each rabbit received a single intravitreal injection of either 50 μg of DARPin #4 in 100 μL of PBS or 100 μL of PBS alone as a vehicle control. The injection was administered 3 mm posterior to the limbus in the supratemporal quadrant under an operating microscope following pupillary dilation. Four days later, all rabbits received a second intravitreal injection of 500 ng of recombinant human VEGF-A165 in 50 μL into the same eye. Two days after this challenge, 50 mg/kg of 10% sodium fluorescein was injected intravenously via the marginal ear vein. One hour later, the fluorescein leakage into the vitreoretinal compartment was quantified using an FM-2 Fluorotron Master ocular photometer. A series of 148 scans along the optical axis were performed, and the area under the curve (AUC) for fluorescence intensity was calculated for both the treated and untreated contralateral eyes. The ratio of AUC values between the treated and untreated eyes was determined for each animal. In the DARPin-treated group, the mean fluorescence leakage ratio was 0.8 ± 0.1, indicating near-complete inhibition of vascular permeability. In the vehicle control group, the mean ratio was 6.9 ± 3.1, reflecting unimpeded leakage. The difference between groups was statistically significant (p = 0.025). Ocular examinations performed on days 2 and 4 using a slit lamp revealed no signs of corneal haze, intraocular flare, cataract formation, or vitreal clouding. Body weight remained stable throughout the study, and no systemic adverse events were observed. The second model evaluated the inhibition of corneal neovascularization in New Zealand white rabbits. Two 3 mm long 7–0 silk sutures were placed in the temporal and superior cornea on day 1, 4 mm from the limbus. One group of four rabbits received topical instillations of 50 μL of a 20 mg/mL solution of DARPin #4 eight times daily for 20 days, while the control group received PBS only. Images of the corneas were captured twice weekly using a digital slit lamp camera. At the end of the treatment period, the maximum vessel length and vessel density within the neovascularized area were quantified. The DARPin-treated corneas exhibited a 31% reduction in maximum vessel length and a 43% reduction in vessel density compared to controls. The effect was sustained throughout the treatment period, with no increase in conjunctival redness, chemosis, or discharge beyond that attributable to the suture procedure. No corneal opacity or epithelial erosion was observed. In a parallel study using a rat model of laser-induced choroidal neovascularization, DARPin #6 was administered topically as 10 μL drops of a 10 mg/mL solution four times daily for 14 days. On day 1, choroidal neovascularization was induced using a 532 nm argon laser with six 75 μm spots at 150 mW for 100 ms per eye. A control group received intravitreal triamcinolone (120 μg), and a second control group received PBS. Fluorescein angiography was performed on days 15 and 22, and choroidal flat-mounts were analyzed on day 24 using a Zeiss ApoTome microscope with z-stack reconstruction. The mean CNV lesion volume was reduced by 40% in the DARPin-treated group compared to PBS controls (p = 0.025), with a reduction of 45% in the triamcinolone group (p = 0.0065). No ocular irritation, intraocular inflammation, or systemic toxicity was observed in any group. These results demonstrate that the DARPins of the invention are effective in multiple preclinical models of ocular angiogenesis and vascular leakage, and that they can be delivered via both intravitreal and topical routes with high potency and excellent safety.
