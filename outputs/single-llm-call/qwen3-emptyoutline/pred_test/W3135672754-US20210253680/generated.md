## BACKGROUND

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of neurotrauma therapeutics, specifically to the treatment of acute spinal cord injury through targeted modulation of intercellular communication pathways. More particularly, the invention concerns the use of a novel monoclonal antibody, designated MHC1, that selectively inhibits the opening of connexin 43 (Cx43) hemichannels without affecting the function of Cx43-containing gap junctions. This selective inhibition mitigates the secondary pathological cascade that follows spinal cord injury, including the release of cytotoxic molecules, neuroinflammation, glial scar formation, and neuronal death. The invention further encompasses methods of administering the antibody within a defined therapeutic window following injury, kits for diagnostic and therapeutic use, and compositions for intravenous, intraperitoneal, or intracerebroventricular delivery. The invention is particularly directed to the treatment of incomplete spinal cord injuries in humans, where preservation of residual neural function is critical to long-term neurological recovery. The antibody described herein represents a first-in-class biologic agent that distinguishes between two structurally and functionally distinct conformations of the same protein—hemichannels and gap junctions—thereby achieving a level of precision previously unattainable with small molecule inhibitors or nonselective peptides. This specificity allows for the suppression of pathological signaling while preserving essential physiological communication between astrocytes and other neural cells, a critical advantage over prior therapeutic approaches. The invention is applicable to both traumatic and ischemic forms of spinal cord injury, and its mechanism of action is independent of axonal regeneration, making it uniquely suited for acute intervention in the critical hours following injury when secondary damage is most active.

### 2. Description of Related Art

Spinal cord injury remains one of the most devastating neurological conditions, with limited therapeutic options available to halt or reverse the progression of secondary damage that follows the initial mechanical trauma. The primary injury, caused by direct mechanical disruption of neural tissue, is often followed by a complex and self-propagating cascade of secondary events, including ischemia, excitotoxicity, oxidative stress, inflammation, and the formation of a glial scar. These processes collectively expand the area of tissue destruction and lead to permanent functional deficits. A central component of this secondary injury is the dysregulation of ion and metabolite homeostasis, particularly in astrocytes, which are the most abundant glial cells in the central nervous system and play a pivotal role in maintaining the extracellular environment. Connexin 43, a transmembrane protein predominantly expressed in astrocytes, forms two distinct types of intercellular channels: gap junctions, which directly connect the cytoplasm of adjacent cells, and hemichannels, which open to the extracellular space and permit the release of signaling molecules such as ATP, glutamate, and ions. While gap junctions are essential for metabolic coupling and coordinated astrocyte responses under physiological conditions, the pathological opening of hemichannels has been implicated in the propagation of neurotoxic signals following injury. Previous studies have demonstrated that Cx43 expression is upregulated in astrocytes after spinal cord injury, and that global deletion of Cx43 in astrocytes reduces lesion size and improves functional outcomes in rodent models. However, these genetic approaches do not differentiate between the roles of gap junctions and hemichannels, and the loss of gap junction function may itself be detrimental to tissue repair and homeostasis. Consequently, the development of a selective inhibitor of Cx43 hemichannels has been a long-standing goal in the field of neurotrauma research. Prior pharmacological agents, such as carbenoxolone and 18β-glycyrrhetinic acid, have been shown to block both hemichannels and gap junctions, leading to off-target effects that compromise their therapeutic utility. Similarly, peptide-based inhibitors such as Peptide5, derived from the extracellular loop of Cx43, have demonstrated efficacy in reducing inflammation and improving recovery after spinal cord injury, but they lack specificity and are known to interfere with gap junctional communication, thereby potentially disrupting beneficial intercellular signaling. Furthermore, these peptides are susceptible to rapid proteolytic degradation, limiting their bioavailability and duration of action. Antibody-based therapies have emerged as a promising modality for targeting extracellular domains of membrane proteins with high specificity and prolonged half-life. However, no prior antibody has been reported to selectively inhibit Cx43 hemichannels while leaving gap junctions intact. The blood-spinal cord barrier, which normally restricts the passage of large molecules such as antibodies into the spinal cord parenchyma, becomes transiently permeable following acute injury, creating a narrow therapeutic window during which systemically administered biologics can reach the lesion site. Prior attempts to deliver therapeutic antibodies to the injured spinal cord have focused on neuroprotective agents targeting cytokines, growth factors, or apoptotic pathways, but none have addressed the specific role of Cx43 hemichannels. Moreover, the timing of intervention has been poorly defined, with most studies administering treatments either prophylactically or in the chronic phase, when the pathological drivers of secondary injury have already subsided. The discovery that Cx43 hemichannels are activated within minutes to hours after injury by mechanical stress and inflammatory mediators such as interleukin-1β has provided a compelling rationale for acute intervention. However, the absence of a selective inhibitor has prevented the validation of this hypothesis in vivo. The present invention fills this critical gap by providing a monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to a specific epitope on the second extracellular loop of Cx43, a region critical for hemichannel gating but inaccessible within the tightly apposed membranes of gap junctions. This antibody does not bind to pannexin channels or other connexin isoforms, demonstrating remarkable specificity. The functional validation of this antibody in multiple models of spinal cord injury, including contusion and compression, has shown consistent and significant improvements in functional recovery, tissue preservation, and reduction of gliosis, without any observable adverse effects on baseline neural communication. The invention therefore represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of spinal cord injury, moving from broad-spectrum anti-inflammatory or neuroprotective strategies to a precision approach that targets a single, well-defined pathological mechanism with minimal disruption to physiological processes.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel monoclonal antibody, designated MHC1, that selectively inhibits the opening of connexin 43 (Cx43) hemichannels without affecting the function of Cx43-containing gap junctions. The antibody is a mouse-human chimeric immunoglobulin comprising murine variable regions derived from a hybridoma clone and human constant regions of the IgG1 isotype and kappa light chain. The antibody binds with high affinity to an extracellular epitope of Cx43 located within the second extracellular loop, a region critical for hemichannel gating but sterically inaccessible in the context of gap junctions formed between adjacent cells. The binding of MHC1 to Cx43 prevents the pathological opening of hemichannels in response to injury-associated stimuli, including mechanical stress, low extracellular calcium and magnesium concentrations, and proinflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-1β. This inhibition results in a significant reduction in the release of cytotoxic molecules such as ATP, glutamate, and reactive oxygen species, thereby attenuating the secondary injury cascade that follows spinal cord trauma. The invention further encompasses methods of treating spinal cord injury in a mammal, particularly a human, by administering an effective amount of the MHC1 antibody within 30 minutes to 2 hours after the occurrence of the injury. The antibody may be administered via intraperitoneal, intravenous, or intracerebroventricular routes, with intraperitoneal administration being preferred for its practicality and efficacy in preclinical models. The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the MHC1 antibody in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for storage, transport, and clinical administration. The antibody is stable in aqueous formulations at physiological pH and temperature, and retains its binding affinity and functional activity after lyophilization and reconstitution. The invention further provides diagnostic kits for the detection of Cx43 hemichannel activity in biological samples, comprising the MHC1 antibody conjugated to a detectable label, such as a fluorophore, enzyme, or radioisotope, for use in imaging or in vitro assays. The invention further provides methods for assessing the efficacy of treatment in patients with spinal cord injury by measuring changes in biomarkers of secondary injury, including levels of extracellular ATP, glutamate, glial fibrillary acidic protein, and lesion volume on magnetic resonance imaging, wherein a reduction in these markers following administration of the MHC1 antibody correlates with improved neurological outcomes. The invention further provides methods for identifying patients who are likely to benefit from treatment with the MHC1 antibody, based on the presence of incomplete spinal cord injury, preservation of some motor or sensory function, and evidence of acute Cx43 hemichannel activation in the perilesional tissue. The invention further provides methods for determining the therapeutic window for administration of the MHC1 antibody, which is limited to the acute phase of injury, prior to the resealing of the blood-spinal cord barrier and the resolution of inflammatory stimuli that drive hemichannel opening. Administration of the antibody beyond 24 hours after injury, particularly after the establishment of chronic pathology, does not confer significant functional benefit, despite the presence of the antibody at the injury site. The invention further provides methods for combining the MHC1 antibody with other therapeutic modalities, including hypothermia, rehabilitation, or neurotrophic factor delivery, to enhance recovery outcomes. The invention further provides methods for manufacturing the MHC1 antibody using recombinant expression systems, including transient transfection of mammalian cell lines such as HEK293F or stable cell lines derived from CHO cells, followed by purification by protein A affinity chromatography. The antibody is produced in a form free of endotoxin, host cell proteins, and aggregates, and is formulated for intravenous infusion or subcutaneous injection. The invention further provides methods for determining the optimal dosing regimen, which includes a single administration of 10 to 50 mg/kg body weight, with a preferred dose of 25 mg/kg, delivered within 30 minutes of injury. The invention further provides methods for monitoring the pharmacokinetics and biodistribution of the MHC1 antibody in vivo, using anti-human IgG immunohistochemistry or flow cytometry to confirm delivery to the lesion site and to exclude systemic toxicity. The invention further provides methods for preventing or reducing the formation of glial scars, preserving white and gray matter, and protecting neurons from death following spinal cord injury, through the selective inhibition of Cx43 hemichannels by the MHC1 antibody. The invention further provides methods for improving locomotor function in mammals with spinal cord injury, as measured by standardized behavioral scales such as the Basso Mouse Scale or the Basso, Beattie, and Bresnahan scale, wherein treatment with the MHC1 antibody results in significantly greater recovery of hind limb coordination, weight support, and stepping ability compared to control treatments.
