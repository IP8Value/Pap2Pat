# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic methods and compositions for the selective enhancement of skeletal muscle mass and function without inducing adverse growth effects in androgen-sensitive tissues, particularly the prostate. More specifically, the invention encompasses the use of testosterone or its biologically active analogues or derivatives in combination with an ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) inhibitor to achieve anabolic effects on striated muscle tissue while simultaneously preventing or substantially reducing prostate enlargement, hyperplasia, or other androgen-mediated proliferative responses in the prostate gland. The invention further encompasses the use of follistatin or its functional fragments, analogues, or mimetics as standalone agents for the selective promotion of lean body mass and reduction of adipose tissue without stimulating prostate growth. These methods and compositions are particularly useful in the treatment of hypogonadism, age-related muscle wasting, sarcopenia, cachexia associated with chronic illness, and other conditions characterized by low circulating androgen levels and concomitant loss of muscle mass, wherein traditional testosterone replacement therapy is contraindicated due to the risk of exacerbating benign prostatic hyperplasia, prostatic intraepithelial neoplasia, or prostate cancer. The invention also includes pharmaceutical formulations, dosing regimens, and diagnostic kits designed to facilitate the safe and effective administration of these therapeutic combinations in human and veterinary subjects.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Testosterone is a naturally occurring androgenic steroid hormone that plays a central role in the development and maintenance of male secondary sexual characteristics, as well as in the regulation of skeletal muscle mass, bone density, erythropoiesis, and sexual function. In men, circulating testosterone levels exhibit a well-documented positive correlation with lean body mass, muscular strength, and physical performance. Clinical studies have demonstrated that exogenous administration of testosterone to hypogonadal men results in significant increases in lean body mass, maximal voluntary strength, and overall physical function. These anabolic effects have led to considerable interest in testosterone as a therapeutic agent for the treatment of age-related muscle decline, cancer-associated cachexia, and other catabolic states. However, the clinical utility of testosterone replacement therapy is severely limited by its well-established proliferative effects on the prostate gland. Testosterone, either directly or following its intracellular conversion to dihydrotestosterone by 5α-reductase, activates the androgen receptor in prostatic epithelial and stromal cells, triggering transcriptional programs that promote cell proliferation, inhibit apoptosis, and increase glandular volume. This effect is particularly concerning in older men, who are at elevated risk for benign prostatic hyperplasia and prostate cancer, and in whom testosterone therapy may accelerate the progression of undiagnosed or subclinical malignancies. Consequently, despite the clear benefits of testosterone on muscle anabolism, its use in populations with intact or at-risk prostates has been met with substantial caution, regulatory restrictions, and clinical reluctance.

Alternative strategies to circumvent this limitation have focused on the development of selective androgen receptor modulators (SARMs), which aim to dissociate the anabolic effects of androgens on skeletal muscle from their proliferative effects on the prostate and other androgen-responsive tissues. While some SARMs have demonstrated promising tissue selectivity in preclinical models, many have failed to translate into clinically viable therapies due to insufficient potency, unpredictable off-target effects, hepatotoxicity, or incomplete dissociation of anabolic from androgenic activities. Furthermore, SARMs often lack the full spectrum of physiological benefits conferred by testosterone, including effects on libido, mood, bone mineral density, and erythropoiesis, which are critical for comprehensive health restoration in hypogonadal individuals. Thus, there remains a significant unmet medical need for a therapeutic approach that preserves the full anabolic and physiological profile of testosterone while selectively ablating its adverse effects on the prostate.

The biological basis for this differential tissue response has not been fully elucidated. Although testosterone is known to activate the androgen receptor in both skeletal muscle and prostate tissue, the downstream transcriptional and metabolic consequences of androgen receptor signaling vary significantly between these two organ systems. In skeletal muscle, testosterone promotes myogenic differentiation of satellite cells and muscle progenitor cells through the activation of the androgen receptor–β-catenin signaling axis, leading to the upregulation of key myogenic regulators such as follistatin. Follistatin, a secreted glycoprotein that neutralizes members of the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) superfamily—including activins, myostatin, and certain bone morphogenetic proteins—has been shown to be both necessary and sufficient for mediating testosterone-induced muscle hypertrophy. Administration of recombinant follistatin alone has been demonstrated to increase skeletal muscle mass in multiple animal models, and transgenic overexpression of follistatin leads to dramatic muscle hyperplasia and hypertrophy without overt systemic toxicity. However, the effects of follistatin on the prostate have been inconsistent and poorly characterized. Some studies suggest that follistatin may exert anti-proliferative effects by neutralizing activin-mediated growth suppression, while others indicate potential pro-tumorigenic roles in advanced prostate cancer. The precise mechanisms underlying the differential response of muscle and prostate to follistatin, and whether this differential response could be exploited therapeutically, remained unresolved.

Recent investigations into the transcriptional responses of androgen-sensitive tissues to testosterone and follistatin have revealed a striking divergence in gene expression profiles. Microarray analyses comparing the transcriptomes of skeletal muscle and prostate tissue from castrated mice treated with testosterone versus recombinant follistatin identified a highly selective set of genes and biological pathways that were activated by testosterone but not by follistatin in the prostate. Among these, the polyamine biosynthetic pathway emerged as the most significantly and specifically upregulated pathway in the prostate in response to testosterone, with no corresponding induction observed in muscle or in response to follistatin. Within this pathway, ornithine decarboxylase (ODC1), the rate-limiting enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of ornithine to putrescine, was found to be robustly induced by testosterone in the prostate but remained unchanged in both muscle and in response to follistatin treatment. This finding was corroborated by time-course experiments demonstrating a direct temporal correlation between the recovery of prostate mass following testosterone replacement and the upregulation of ODC1 mRNA and protein expression. Moreover, in vitro studies using androgen-sensitive prostate epithelial cell lines confirmed that testosterone, but not follistatin, induces ODC1 expression and stimulates cell cycle progression into S-phase, as measured by bromodeoxyuridine incorporation and flow cytometric analysis. These observations suggested that ODC1 and its downstream polyamine products—putrescine, spermidine, and spermine—may serve as essential mediators of testosterone-induced prostate growth, while being dispensable for its anabolic effects on muscle.

Ornithine decarboxylase has long been recognized as a key regulator of cellular proliferation, particularly in rapidly dividing tissues such as the prostate, intestinal mucosa, and certain tumors. Its expression is tightly regulated by growth factors, hormones, and cellular stress, and its activity is essential for the synthesis of polyamines, which are required for DNA replication, RNA translation, and stabilization of chromatin structure. In the prostate, ODC1 expression is androgen-responsive, and its inhibition has been shown to suppress prostate growth in multiple preclinical models. The irreversible ODC inhibitor α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO), originally developed as an antitrypanosomal agent and later investigated for the prevention of colorectal and skin cancers, has demonstrated potent inhibitory activity against ODC in vivo, with minimal systemic toxicity. Given the differential dependence of prostate and muscle on ODC1 for testosterone-mediated growth, it was hypothesized that co-administration of testosterone with DFMO might selectively block the proliferative effects of testosterone on the prostate while preserving its anabolic effects on skeletal muscle. Experimental validation of this hypothesis revealed that in castrated mice, testosterone administration fully restored both prostate and muscle mass to levels comparable to those in intact controls. However, when testosterone was co-administered with DFMO, prostate mass remained suppressed to the level observed in castrated controls, while muscle mass was restored to the same extent as with testosterone alone. Histological examination confirmed that DFMO completely abrogated the testosterone-induced epithelial hyperplasia and luminal expansion of the prostate, without affecting the hypertrophy of muscle fibers. These findings established, for the first time, a mechanistic basis for the tissue-selective actions of testosterone and provided a novel therapeutic paradigm: the pharmacological uncoupling of androgenic and anabolic effects through the targeted inhibition of a single enzyme downstream of the androgen receptor.

The clinical implications of this discovery are profound. Testosterone and DFMO are both FDA-approved agents with well-characterized safety profiles in humans. Testosterone has been used for decades in the treatment of male hypogonadism, while DFMO has been approved for the treatment of human African trypanosomiasis and has been investigated in clinical trials for hirsutism, neuroblastoma, and chemoprevention of colorectal adenomas. The combination of these two agents, therefore, presents a uniquely attractive opportunity for rapid clinical translation. Unlike novel chemical entities requiring extensive preclinical toxicology and pharmacokinetic profiling, this combination leverages existing regulatory data, manufacturing infrastructure, and clinical experience, potentially enabling accelerated development pathways. Furthermore, because the mechanism of action is based on a physiological pathway—polyamine biosynthesis—that is not directly targeted by existing androgen receptor antagonists or SARMs, this approach offers a fundamentally new strategy for achieving tissue selectivity in androgen therapy. The invention disclosed herein encompasses not only the combination of testosterone and ODC inhibitors for the treatment of muscle wasting disorders, but also the use of follistatin as a standalone anabolic agent that naturally bypasses the prostate-stimulating effects of testosterone, thereby providing two distinct, complementary therapeutic avenues for the selective enhancement of muscle mass without prostate risk.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for selectively enhancing skeletal muscle mass and function in a subject while avoiding or substantially reducing the proliferative effects of androgens on the prostate gland. This method comprises the concurrent administration of testosterone or a biologically active analogue or derivative thereof, in combination with an ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) inhibitor, wherein the ODC inhibitor is capable of suppressing the synthesis of polyamines in the prostate without interfering with the anabolic signaling pathways in skeletal muscle. The invention further provides a method for increasing lean body mass and reducing adipose tissue in a subject by administering follistatin or a functional fragment, analogue, or mimetic thereof, wherein such administration results in significant increases in skeletal muscle mass without concomitant stimulation of prostate growth. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that testosterone promotes muscle anabolism through a pathway that is independent of ODC1 activity, whereas its proliferative effects on the prostate are critically dependent on ODC1-mediated polyamine biosynthesis. This mechanistic insight enables the selective targeting of prostate growth without compromising the beneficial anabolic effects of testosterone on muscle, bone, and other tissues. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising testosterone or its analogues in combination with an ODC inhibitor, formulated for oral, parenteral, transdermal, or implantable delivery, and designed to maintain therapeutic concentrations of both agents in systemic circulation for sustained anabolic and anti-proliferative effects. The invention also includes dosing regimens optimized to achieve maximal muscle anabolism while maintaining complete suppression of prostate growth, as well as diagnostic kits for identifying subjects who are most likely to benefit from these therapies based on baseline androgen levels, prostate-specific antigen concentrations, and genetic markers of ODC1 expression or polyamine metabolism. The invention further provides for the use of these methods and compositions in the treatment of hypogonadism, age-related sarcopenia, cachexia associated with cancer or chronic disease, frailty in the elderly, and other conditions characterized by low muscle mass and androgen deficiency, wherein traditional testosterone replacement therapy is contraindicated due to the risk of prostate complications. The invention further provides for the use of follistatin or its functional derivatives as a standalone anabolic agent that naturally avoids prostate stimulation, thereby offering an alternative therapeutic strategy for patients who cannot tolerate exogenous androgens or who seek to avoid androgen-related side effects entirely. The methods and compositions of the invention are distinguished from prior art by their reliance on a specific biochemical pathway—polyamine biosynthesis—as the point of intervention for achieving tissue selectivity, a mechanism not previously recognized, exploited, or suggested in the context of androgen therapy.

### Testosterone

Testosterone is a C19 steroid hormone derived from cholesterol through a series of enzymatic transformations involving the enzymes P450scc, 3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase, 17α-hydroxylase, and 17β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase. In males, testosterone is primarily synthesized in the Leydig cells of the testes under the regulation of luteinizing hormone, although smaller amounts are produced in the adrenal cortex and, in females, in the ovaries. Testosterone exerts its physiological effects by binding to the intracellular androgen receptor, a ligand-dependent transcription factor that, upon activation, translocates to the nucleus and modulates the expression of hundreds of target genes involved in anabolism, metabolism, and cell proliferation. In skeletal muscle, testosterone promotes the differentiation and fusion of satellite cells into mature myofibers, increases protein synthesis through activation of the mTOR pathway, and suppresses protein degradation by inhibiting the ubiquitin–proteasome system. These effects are mediated, in part, through the induction of follistatin, a secreted glycoprotein that neutralizes the muscle growth inhibitors activin and myostatin. The resulting increase in myogenic regulatory factor expression leads to enhanced muscle fiber hypertrophy and hyperplasia. In the prostate, testosterone is converted intracellularly to the more potent androgen dihydrotestosterone by the enzyme 5α-reductase, which then binds to the androgen receptor with higher affinity and duration, triggering a distinct transcriptional program that includes the upregulation of genes involved in cell cycle progression, extracellular matrix remodeling, and polyamine biosynthesis. The androgen receptor in prostate epithelial cells directly binds to androgen response elements in the promoter region of the ornithine decarboxylase gene, leading to a robust increase in ODC1 mRNA and protein expression. This upregulation is absent in muscle tissue, where testosterone does not significantly alter ODC1 levels, even under conditions of maximal anabolic stimulation. The differential regulation of ODC1 by testosterone in these two tissues forms the mechanistic foundation of the present invention. Testosterone analogues and derivatives, including esterified forms such as testosterone enanthate and testosterone undecanoate, are widely used in clinical practice for the treatment of hypogonadism. These compounds are designed to prolong the half-life of testosterone in circulation by slowing its release from intramuscular or subcutaneous depots. Testosterone undecanoate, for example, is administered orally in capsule form and absorbed via the lymphatic system, bypassing first-pass hepatic metabolism. Other derivatives, such as oxandrolone and stanozolol, are 17α-alkylated to enhance oral bioavailability, although these modifications are associated with increased hepatotoxicity. The present invention does not require the use of any specific testosterone derivative; rather, it encompasses any compound that retains the ability to activate the androgen receptor in muscle and induce follistatin expression, while permitting the selective inhibition of its prostate-stimulating effects through co-administration with an ODC inhibitor. The therapeutic efficacy of testosterone in increasing muscle mass has been demonstrated in numerous clinical trials involving hypogonadal men, elderly men with low testosterone, and patients with chronic disease. In these studies, testosterone administration consistently results in increases in lean body mass ranging from 1.5 to 3.5 kg over a period of 3 to 6 months, accompanied by improvements in strength, mobility, and quality of life. However, these benefits are often accompanied by increases in prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, prostate volume, and symptoms of lower urinary tract obstruction, which limit long-term use. The present invention resolves this clinical dilemma by demonstrating that the anabolic effects of testosterone on muscle can be preserved while its proliferative effects on the prostate are completely blocked by the addition of an ODC inhibitor. This represents a paradigm shift in androgen therapy, moving away from the search for tissue-selective androgen receptor ligands toward the pharmacological uncoupling of downstream signaling pathways that diverge between tissues.

### Method for Increasing Testosterone Levels in a Subject

The present invention provides a method for increasing testosterone levels in a subject who is suffering from a low testosterone disorder, wherein the method comprises the administration of testosterone or a biologically active analogue or derivative thereof in combination with an ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) inhibitor, such that the testosterone levels in the subject are elevated to within the normal physiological range for age and sex, while the proliferative effects of testosterone on the prostate are substantially inhibited. The method is particularly useful in subjects with primary or secondary hypogonadism, age-related decline in testosterone production, or androgen deficiency due to chronic illness, wherein traditional testosterone replacement therapy is contraindicated due to the risk of prostate enlargement or cancer progression. The method involves the determination of baseline serum testosterone levels, prostate volume, and prostate-specific antigen (PSA) concentration in the subject, followed by the initiation of daily administration of testosterone and an ODC inhibitor. The testosterone may be administered in any pharmaceutically acceptable form, including oral capsules, transdermal patches, intramuscular injections, subcutaneous implants, or nasal sprays, and the ODC inhibitor may be administered orally, intravenously, subcutaneously, or via implant. The dose of testosterone is selected to achieve a serum concentration of at least 300 ng/dL, preferably between 400 and 800 ng/dL, and the dose of the ODC inhibitor is selected to achieve a serum concentration sufficient to inhibit at least 90% of ODC1 enzymatic activity in prostate tissue. In one embodiment, the method comprises the daily oral administration of 120 mg of testosterone undecanoate and 500 mg of α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO) to a male subject with serum testosterone levels below 300 ng/dL. In another embodiment, the method comprises the weekly intramuscular injection of 1000 mg of testosterone enanthate and the daily oral administration of 500 mg of DFMO. In a further embodiment, the method comprises the daily application of a transdermal patch delivering 10 mg of testosterone and 15 mg of DFMO to the skin of the abdomen. The method may be continued for a period of at least 14 days, preferably for 28 days or longer, and may be maintained indefinitely under medical supervision. The method further includes periodic monitoring of serum testosterone levels, PSA concentration, prostate volume by transrectal ultrasound, and muscle mass by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry or magnetic resonance imaging. The method is designed to achieve a sustained increase in lean body mass, muscle strength, and physical function, while maintaining prostate volume and PSA levels within the normal range for age-matched controls. The method is distinguished from prior art by its use of an ODC inhibitor to selectively block the proliferative effects of testosterone on the prostate, while preserving its anabolic effects on muscle, bone, and other tissues. The method does not require the use of androgen receptor antagonists, 5α-reductase inhibitors, or other agents that interfere with androgen signaling, and does not result in the suppression of libido, erectile function, or spermatogenesis, which are common side effects of such agents. The method is particularly advantageous in elderly men, who are at high risk for both sarcopenia and prostate cancer, and in men who have undergone radical prostatectomy for localized prostate cancer and are at low risk of recurrence but require androgen replacement for quality of life and muscle preservation. The method may be combined with resistance training, nutritional supplementation, or other interventions to further enhance muscle anabolism. The method may also be used in women with low testosterone levels and muscle wasting due to adrenal insufficiency, chronic illness, or postmenopausal androgen decline, provided that the dose of testosterone is adjusted to avoid virilization. The method is safe, well-tolerated, and does not result in significant hepatotoxicity, renal toxicity, hematologic abnormalities, or cardiovascular events when administered according to the prescribed regimens.

### Oral Formulations

The present invention provides novel oral formulations for the concurrent delivery of testosterone and an ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) inhibitor, wherein the formulations are designed to overcome the pharmacokinetic challenges associated with the oral bioavailability of testosterone and the rapid renal clearance of ODC inhibitors. Testosterone, in its native form, is subject to extensive first-pass metabolism in the liver following oral administration, resulting in low and variable systemic exposure. To circumvent this limitation, testosterone is typically administered as an esterified prodrug, such as testosterone undecanoate, which is absorbed via the lymphatic system and bypasses hepatic degradation. The present invention incorporates testosterone undecanoate into an oral capsule formulation that includes a lipid-based excipient system designed to enhance lymphatic uptake and prolong systemic circulation. The formulation comprises testosterone undecanoate at a concentration of 100 to 200 mg per capsule, suspended in a mixture of medium-chain triglycerides, oleic acid, and polyethylene glycol, and encapsulated in a gelatin shell with a delayed-release coating to ensure release in the distal small intestine. The formulation further includes an ODC inhibitor, such as α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO), at a concentration of 400 to 800 mg per capsule, formulated as a fine crystalline powder to ensure rapid dissolution in gastric fluid. The DFMO is not co-encapsulated with the testosterone undecanoate to prevent potential chemical interactions, but is instead administered as a separate capsule taken simultaneously with the testosterone capsule. In an alternative embodiment, the formulation comprises a single capsule containing both agents in a dual-layer tablet, wherein the inner layer contains testosterone undecanoate coated with an enteric polymer that resists dissolution in the acidic environment of the stomach and dissolves only at a pH above 6.0, and the outer layer contains DFMO in a rapidly dissolving matrix that releases the compound within minutes of ingestion. The formulation may further include a disintegrant such as croscarmellose sodium, a lubricant such as magnesium stearate, and a stabilizer such as ascorbic acid to prevent oxidation of the testosterone ester. The formulation is designed to be taken once daily, preferably with a high-fat meal to enhance lymphatic absorption of testosterone undecanoate. The formulation results in a peak serum concentration of testosterone within 6 to 8 hours after administration, with a half-life of approximately 20 to 30 hours, and a sustained concentration above 300 ng/dL for at least 24 hours. The serum concentration of DFMO reaches a peak within 1 to 2 hours after ingestion, with a half-life of approximately 3 to 5 hours, and maintains concentrations above 10 μM for at least 8 hours, sufficient to inhibit ODC1 activity in the prostate throughout the day. The formulation is stable for at least 24 months when stored at room temperature in a dry, light-resistant container. The formulation is free of any component that would interfere with the absorption of testosterone or the bioavailability of DFMO, and does not contain any substances known to induce or inhibit cytochrome P450 enzymes, thereby minimizing the risk of drug–drug interactions. The formulation may be packaged in blister packs containing 30 daily doses, with each blister labeled with the day of the week and instructions for co-administration. The formulation may also include a printed insert detailing the mechanism of action, expected benefits, monitoring parameters, and potential side effects. The formulation is suitable for use in elderly patients, including those with dysphagia, as the capsules are small and easily swallowed. The formulation may be modified to include a flavoring agent to improve palatability, or may be formulated as a chewable tablet for patients who have difficulty swallowing capsules. The formulation is designed to be compatible with other medications commonly taken by hypogonadal patients, including antihypertensives, statins, and antidiabetic agents.

### Doses and Administration Regimens

The present invention provides optimized doses and administration regimens for the co-administration of testosterone or its analogues with an ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) inhibitor, wherein the doses are selected to achieve maximal anabolic effects on skeletal muscle while completely suppressing proliferative effects on the prostate. The optimal dose of testosterone depends on the route of administration, the specific analogue used, and the baseline androgen status of the subject. For oral administration of testosterone undecanoate, the preferred daily dose is 120 mg, which results in serum testosterone concentrations of 400 to 700 ng/dL, within the normal physiological range for young adult males. For intramuscular administration of testosterone enanthate, the preferred dose is 1000 mg administered once every 10 to 14 days, which maintains serum testosterone levels above 300 ng/dL throughout the dosing interval. For transdermal administration, the preferred daily dose is 10 mg delivered via a patch applied to the skin of the abdomen or upper arm. For subcutaneous implantation, the preferred dose is 1000 mg of testosterone propionate implanted once every 6 months. The optimal dose of the ODC inhibitor, α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO), is 500 mg administered orally once daily, which results in serum concentrations of 15 to 25 μM, sufficient to inhibit greater than 90% of ODC1 activity in the prostate. In alternative embodiments, the dose of DFMO may be adjusted based on renal function, with a reduction to 250 mg daily in patients with moderate to severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <50 mL/min). The administration regimen is designed to ensure that both agents are present in systemic circulation simultaneously for a sufficient duration to exert their combined effects. ### Low Testosterone Disorders and Subjects Amenable to Treatment

The present invention provides methods for the treatment of low testosterone disorders in subjects who are amenable to therapy with testosterone in combination with an ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) inhibitor. Low testosterone disorders include primary hypogonadism, secondary hypogonadism, late-onset hypogonadism, androgen deficiency due to chronic illness, and age-related decline in testosterone production. Primary hypogonadism is characterized by testicular failure, resulting in low testosterone levels and elevated luteinizing hormone levels, and may be caused by genetic disorders such as Klinefelter syndrome, cryptorchidism, or testicular injury. Secondary hypogonadism is characterized by impaired hypothalamic or pituitary function, resulting in low testosterone levels and low or normal luteinizing hormone levels, and may be caused by obesity, chronic stress, opioid use, or pituitary tumors. Late-onset hypogonadism is a condition characterized by a gradual decline in testosterone levels with advancing age, typically beginning after the age of 40, and is associated with symptoms of fatigue, decreased muscle mass, reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, and depression. Androgen deficiency due to chronic illness occurs in patients with conditions such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, congestive heart failure, end-stage renal disease, HIV/AIDS, and cancer, and is associated with cachexia, muscle wasting, and poor prognosis. Age-related decline in testosterone production is a universal phenomenon in aging men, with serum testosterone levels decreasing by approximately 1% per year after the age of 30. The present invention is particularly useful in subjects with these conditions who are at increased risk for prostate complications, including those with a family history of prostate cancer, elevated prostate-specific antigen levels, benign prostatic hyperplasia, or a history of prostate biopsy with high-grade prostatic intraepithelial neoplasia. The invention is also useful in subjects who have undergone radical prostatectomy for localized prostate cancer and are at low risk of recurrence but require androgen replacement to preserve muscle mass, bone density, and quality of life. The invention is further useful in subjects with cachexia due to cancer or chronic illness, who are unable to tolerate traditional testosterone therapy due to the risk of tumor progression. The invention is also useful in women with low testosterone levels and muscle wasting due to adrenal insufficiency, postmenopausal androgen decline, or chronic illness, provided that the dose of testosterone is adjusted to avoid virilization. The invention is not intended for use in subjects with untreated prostate cancer, metastatic prostate cancer, or a history of breast cancer, as the safety of testosterone in these populations has not been established. The invention is also not intended for use in subjects with severe liver disease, uncontrolled heart failure, or polycythemia vera, as these conditions may be exacerbated by testosterone administration. The invention is suitable for use in subjects of any age, from young adults with hypogonadism to elderly men with sarcopenia, and may be initiated at any time following diagnosis of low testosterone, provided that prostate cancer has been ruled out by appropriate screening. The invention allows for the safe and effective restoration of testosterone levels in these populations, with the added benefit of prostate protection, thereby expanding the therapeutic window for androgen replacement therapy.
