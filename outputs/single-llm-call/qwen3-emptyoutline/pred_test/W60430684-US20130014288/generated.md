## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular biology and pharmacology, and more specifically to novel methods and compositions for the detection, quantification, and screening of membrane protein trafficking defects and their pharmacological correction. The invention provides a genetically encoded, fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) fusion system that enables selective, sensitive, and high-throughput visualization of cell surface expression of misfolded membrane proteins, particularly the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) bearing the F508del mutation. This system allows for the direct, real-time, and wash-free detection of rescued mutant protein at the plasma membrane without reliance on functional assays, antibody labeling, or biochemical extraction techniques. The invention further encompasses the use of this platform to evaluate and compare the efficacy of small molecule correctors that restore trafficking of misfolded CFTR, as well as to identify novel therapeutic agents capable of rescuing protein misfolding in CFTR and other disease-associated membrane proteins. The platform is broadly applicable to any transmembrane protein whose trafficking to the cell surface is impaired due to mutation-induced misfolding, thereby offering a transformative tool for drug discovery in a wide range of protein conformational diseases.

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is a life-limiting autosomal recessive genetic disorder caused by mutations in the CFTR gene, which encodes a cAMP-regulated chloride and bicarbonate ion channel expressed primarily in epithelial cells of the lungs, pancreas, liver, intestines, and reproductive tract. The most common disease-causing mutation, deletion of phenylalanine at position 508 (F508del), occurs in approximately 90% of CF patients and results in a severe folding defect that prevents the protein from exiting the endoplasmic reticulum. Consequently, the mutant protein is recognized by the endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation (ERAD) machinery, ubiquitinated, and targeted for proteasomal destruction before reaching the plasma membrane. Even when small amounts of F508del-CFTR escape degradation and reach the cell surface, its channel function remains significantly impaired due to intrinsic instability and reduced open probability. Despite decades of research, therapeutic strategies aimed at correcting the underlying molecular defect have been limited by the inability to directly and quantitatively measure the abundance of rescued mutant protein at the plasma membrane in live cells under physiologically relevant conditions.

Traditional methods for assessing CFTR trafficking have relied on indirect functional readouts, such as halide-sensitive fluorescent dyes like SPQ, which measure anion efflux as a proxy for channel activity, or on labor-intensive biochemical techniques such as cell surface biotinylation followed by immunoblotting. These approaches suffer from significant limitations: functional assays are susceptible to signal saturation, require multiple wash steps that may perturb membrane integrity, and cannot distinguish between rescued protein that is present at the membrane but nonfunctional and protein that is both correctly trafficked and fully active. Biotinylation and immunofluorescence techniques, while capable of detecting surface-localized protein, necessitate fixation, permeabilization, antibody incubation, and extensive washing, which introduce variability, reduce throughput, and risk artifacts from non-specific binding or epitope masking. Moreover, these methods are incompatible with real-time, longitudinal monitoring of trafficking dynamics in living cells and are poorly suited for high-throughput screening of compound libraries.

Recent advances in chemical biology have led to the development of genetically encoded fluorogen-activating proteins (FAPs), which are engineered single-chain antibody fragments that bind and activate the fluorescence of otherwise non-fluorescent small-molecule dyes called fluorogens. When fused to a target protein, FAPs confer the ability to selectively label the protein with high signal-to-noise ratio using cell-impermeant fluorogens that remain dark in solution but become highly fluorescent upon binding to the FAP. Crucially, because the fluorogen cannot cross the plasma membrane, fluorescence is restricted exclusively to FAP-tagged proteins that have been successfully trafficked to and inserted into the cell surface. This feature eliminates background signal from intracellular pools of misfolded or retained protein and provides a direct, quantitative, and dynamic readout of plasma membrane localization without requiring fixation, permeabilization, or antibody-based detection.

Despite the promise of this technology, its application to complex, multi-domain membrane proteins such as CFTR has not been previously demonstrated, nor has it been validated in native human epithelial cell systems that express endogenous, untagged mutant CFTR. Furthermore, no prior system has enabled the direct comparison of corrector efficacy across multiple compounds and combinations using a single, standardized, high-throughput, and quantitative platform that correlates precisely with both biochemical maturation and functional rescue in physiologically relevant models. The present invention overcomes these longstanding technical barriers by providing a robust, modular, and universally applicable FAP-CFTR fusion system that faithfully recapitulates the trafficking behavior of untagged CFTR, enables sensitive detection of rescued mutant protein at the plasma membrane, and serves as a powerful new tool for the discovery and optimization of pharmacological correctors for CF and other protein misfolding disorders.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious platform for the detection and quantification of cell surface expression of misfolded membrane proteins, particularly the F508del mutant of the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR), through the use of genetically encoded fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) fusions. The invention comprises a series of recombinant constructs in which a fluorogen-activating protein is fused either to the N-terminus of CFTR, incorporating an additional transmembrane segment derived from the platelet-derived growth factor receptor to ensure proper membrane anchoring, or inserted into the fourth extracellular loop (EL4) of CFTR, preserving the native glycosylation sites at asparagine residues 894 and 900. These FAP-CFTR fusion proteins, when expressed in mammalian cells, retain the trafficking, maturation, and functional properties of wild-type and F508del-CFTR, as confirmed by immunoblot analysis, iodide efflux assays, and Ussing chamber measurements. The key innovation lies in the use of a cell-impermeant fluorogen, MG-11p, which binds selectively to the extracellularly exposed FAP domain and becomes highly fluorescent only when the fusion protein has reached the plasma membrane. This enables the direct, real-time, wash-free visualization and quantification of rescued F508del-CFTR at the cell surface using fluorescence microscopy or flow cytometry, without interference from intracellular pools of misfolded protein.

The invention further provides a method for evaluating the efficacy of small molecule correctors that promote the trafficking of F508del-CFTR to the plasma membrane. By treating cells expressing FAP-F508del-CFTR with candidate compounds and measuring the resulting increase in MG-11p fluorescence, the invention enables a highly sensitive, quantitative, and high-throughput assessment of corrector potency that correlates precisely with functional rescue measured by anion transport and with biochemical maturation as observed by the appearance of the mature, complex-glycosylated C-band form of CFTR on immunoblots. The system has been validated across multiple cell types, including HEK293 cells and primary human bronchial epithelial (HBE) cells derived from CF patients homozygous for F508del, demonstrating that the FAP-based readout accurately reflects the behavior of endogenous, untagged CFTR. The invention further demonstrates that certain corrector combinations, notably C4 with C18 or CFFT-002 with C18, produce synergistic improvements in surface expression and function that are significantly greater than those achieved by any single agent, and that these synergistic effects are reliably detected by the FAP platform.

The invention further encompasses a modular design principle that allows for the rapid generation of FAP fusions with other disease-relevant transmembrane proteins, thereby extending the utility of the platform beyond CFTR to other conditions characterized by protein misfolding and defective trafficking, including but not limited to long QT syndrome (hERG), familial hypercholesterolemia (LDL receptor), type 2 diabetes (GLUT4), diabetes insipidus (aquaporin-2), and retinal degeneration (BEST1). The system eliminates the need for antibodies, fixation, permeabilization, or functional readouts that are indirect or subject to saturation, and instead provides a direct, dynamic, and quantitative measure of plasma membrane localization that is scalable for high-throughput screening. The invention thus represents a transformative advancement in the field of pharmacological chaperone discovery, offering a standardized, sensitive, and broadly applicable method to accelerate the identification and optimization of therapeutics for protein conformational diseases.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention describes a novel and highly innovative system for the detection, quantification, and screening of pharmacological correctors that rescue the trafficking of misfolded membrane proteins, with particular application to the F508del mutant of the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR). The invention is based on the fusion of a genetically encoded fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) to CFTR at two distinct locations: the N-terminus, with an additional transmembrane segment to ensure proper membrane integration, and the fourth extracellular loop (EL4), a region previously shown to tolerate insertions without disrupting channel function. These fusion constructs, designated FAP-CFTR and CFTR EL4-FAP, respectively, are expressed in mammalian cells and are capable of being selectively labeled with a cell-impermeant fluorogen, MG-11p, which remains non-fluorescent in solution but undergoes a dramatic increase in quantum yield—exceeding 15,000-fold—upon binding to the FAP domain. Because MG-11p cannot penetrate the plasma membrane, fluorescence is observed exclusively when the FAP tag is exposed on the extracellular surface, thereby providing a direct, unambiguous, and quantitative measure of the amount of CFTR that has successfully trafficked to the plasma membrane. This fundamental principle eliminates the need for fixation, permeabilization, antibody incubation, or wash steps that are inherent to traditional immunofluorescence or biotinylation methods, and avoids the indirect, saturation-prone nature of functional assays such as SPQ-based iodide efflux measurements.

The construction of the FAP-CFTR fusion protein involved the insertion of a Malachite Green-binding FAP variant, dNP138, into the pBabeSacLac2 retroviral expression vector, which was subsequently used to generate stable cell lines expressing either wild-type or F508del-mutant CFTR fused to the FAP at the N-terminus. To ensure that the FAP was properly oriented and accessible on the extracellular side of the membrane, an additional transmembrane segment derived from the platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR) was included between the FAP and the first transmembrane domain of CFTR. This design was based on prior work demonstrating that such an arrangement allows for proper membrane insertion and prevents misfolding or retention of the fusion protein in the endoplasmic reticulum. The CFTR EL4-FAP construct was generated by introducing KpnI and EcoRV restriction sites into the fourth extracellular loop of CFTR, between the two conserved asparagine residues (N894 and N900) that serve as sites for N-linked glycosylation. The FAP sequence was inserted between the amino acid residues KGNSTHS and RNNSY, thereby preserving the glycosylation consensus motifs and maintaining the structural integrity of the extracellular domain. Both constructs were cloned into retroviral vectors and transduced into HEK293 cells, which were then selected for stable expression and used in all subsequent experiments.

The behavior of the FAP-CFTR and CFTR EL4-FAP fusion proteins was rigorously characterized to confirm that the addition of the FAP tag did not alter the fundamental trafficking, maturation, or functional properties of CFTR. Confocal microscopy using the cell-impermeant fluorogen MG-11p revealed that wild-type CFTR fused to the FAP was efficiently trafficked to the plasma membrane, with bright, well-defined labeling at the cell periphery and minimal intracellular signal. In contrast, when the same constructs were expressed with the F508del mutation, no surface fluorescence was detected under control conditions, consistent with the known trafficking defect of this mutant. However, when cells were treated with the cell-permeant fluorogen MG-ester, a strong intracellular signal was observed, confirming that the fusion protein was expressed and localized to intracellular compartments, primarily the endoplasmic reticulum, but was not accessible to the extracellular environment. This observation mirrored the behavior of untagged F508del-CFTR and confirmed that the FAP tag did not mask or alter the intrinsic folding defect of the mutant protein. Upon treatment with known corrector compounds such as C4 and C18, robust surface fluorescence was detected in both FAP-CFTR and CFTR EL4-FAP F508del-expressing cells, demonstrating that the FAP system could detect pharmacological rescue of trafficking with high sensitivity and specificity.

To validate that the FAP-CFTR fusion proteins retained functional activity, iodide efflux assays were performed using the halide-sensitive dye SPQ. Cells expressing wild-type FAP-CFTR exhibited a rapid and robust increase in fluorescence upon replacement of iodide with nitrate in the presence of forskolin and the potentiator P2, indicating that the channel was fully functional and responsive to cAMP-dependent activation. In contrast, F508del-CFTR FAP constructs showed no significant efflux under control conditions, but exhibited substantial and statistically significant increases in anion transport following treatment with corrector combinations such as C4 + C18 or CFFT-002 + C18. These functional results were corroborated by Ussing chamber experiments performed on primary human bronchial epithelial (HBE) cells derived from CF patients homozygous for F508del. In these physiologically relevant cells, which express endogenous, untagged CFTR, the combination of CFFT-002 and C18 produced a significant increase in transepithelial short-circuit current, whereas C4 alone had no measurable effect. The magnitude and pattern of functional rescue observed in HBE cells were strikingly similar to those detected by the FAP platform in HEK293 cells, demonstrating that the FAP-CFTR system faithfully recapitulates the behavior of endogenous CFTR in a native epithelial context.

Biochemical analysis of the fusion proteins by immunoblotting further confirmed that the FAP-CFTR constructs underwent the same post-translational modifications as wild-type and F508del-CFTR. Wild-type FAP-CFTR migrated as two distinct bands on SDS-PAGE gels: a core-glycosylated B-band and a mature, complex-glycosylated C-band, corresponding to the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi-modified forms, respectively. The CFTR EL4-FAP construct, however, exhibited a less distinct C-band, suggesting that the presence of the FAP tag in the fourth extracellular loop may partially interfere with the efficiency of glycosylation or glycan processing, possibly due to steric hindrance or altered accessibility of the glycosylation sites. Nevertheless, both constructs showed clear evidence of pharmacological rescue: treatment with corrector combinations such as C4 + C18 or CFFT-002 + C18 resulted in the appearance of a higher molecular weight band corresponding to the mature C-form of CFTR in both FAP-F508del-CFTR and CFTR EL4-FAP constructs. In HBE cells expressing endogenous F508del-CFTR, the same corrector combinations induced the appearance of the mature C-band, confirming that the biochemical signature of rescue observed in the FAP system is directly translatable to the native protein.

The most significant innovation of the invention lies in its ability to quantify corrector efficacy with unprecedented precision and dynamic range using flow cytometry. By treating cells expressing FAP-F508del-CFTR with individual correctors or combinations and then measuring MG-11p fluorescence in a population of 10,000 cells per condition, the invention provides a highly reproducible, quantitative, and statistically robust metric of surface expression. This approach eliminates the variability introduced by manual image analysis, cell-to-cell heterogeneity in expression levels, and the confounding effects of enzymatic detachment methods such as trypsinization, which can cleave extracellular domains and disrupt surface protein integrity. The use of a non-enzymatic cell detachment reagent ensured that surface-exposed FAP tags remained intact during sample preparation. The results revealed that C18 was the most potent single-agent corrector, increasing surface expression by more than threefold compared to C4, and that combinations of C4 + C18 or CFFT-002 + C18 produced synergistic increases in surface expression that were eight- to ninefold greater than C4 alone. Importantly, these quantitative differences in surface expression directly correlated with the magnitude of functional rescue observed in both iodide efflux and Ussing chamber assays, validating the FAP platform as a predictive and reliable surrogate for both trafficking and function.

The modular nature of the FAP system allows for its rapid adaptation to other transmembrane proteins. The FAP sequence can be fused to the N-terminus of any membrane protein with a known extracellular domain, provided that an appropriate transmembrane anchor is included, or inserted into a permissive extracellular loop, as demonstrated here with CFTR. The system has been validated in two distinct cell types—HEK293 cells and primary human airway epithelial cells—and performs equally well in both, indicating that the platform is not dependent on a specific cellular background. The use of a cell-impermeant fluorogen ensures that the signal is strictly limited to the plasma membrane, making it uniquely suited for detecting subtle changes in trafficking efficiency that may be missed by other methods. The invention further enables longitudinal, real-time monitoring of trafficking dynamics in live cells, allowing researchers to study the kinetics of rescue, the stability of rescued protein at the membrane, and the effects of prolonged compound exposure—all of which are critical for drug development but are currently inaccessible with existing technologies.

The invention also provides a framework for high-throughput screening of compound libraries. The FAP-CFTR system can be readily adapted to multi-well plate formats, enabling the simultaneous testing of hundreds or thousands of small molecules for their ability to enhance surface expression of F508del-CFTR. Because the readout is fluorescence-based and requires no wash steps, the assay can be performed in a homogeneous format, significantly reducing assay time, cost, and complexity. The high signal-to-noise ratio and large dynamic range of the FAP-fluorogen system allow for the detection of even marginal improvements in trafficking, making it possible to identify weak correctors that might be overlooked by functional assays with limited sensitivity. Furthermore, because the platform measures trafficking directly rather than functionally, it can distinguish between compounds that enhance surface delivery and those that merely potentiate channel activity, enabling the discovery of novel classes of correctors with distinct mechanisms of action.

The invention further enables the rational design of combination therapies. The observation that certain corrector pairs, such as CFFT-002 and C18, produce synergistic effects that are greater than the sum of their individual contributions suggests that these compounds act on distinct structural elements or cellular pathways involved in CFTR folding and trafficking. The FAP platform allows for the systematic testing of all possible combinations of compounds, enabling the identification of optimal synergistic pairs that maximize surface expression and functional rescue. This capability is particularly valuable in the context of cystic fibrosis, where combination therapies are now the standard of care, and where the ability to predict and optimize synergistic interactions is critical for clinical success.

The invention has been validated in multiple independent assays—confocal microscopy, flow cytometry, iodide efflux, Ussing chambers, and immunoblotting—and consistently demonstrates that the FAP-CFTR system provides a direct, quantitative, and highly sensitive measure of F508del-CFTR trafficking that correlates precisely with functional and biochemical outcomes. This level of concordance across diverse methodologies and biological systems has not been achieved by any prior technology and represents a major advance in the field. The invention thus provides not only a new tool for studying CFTR biology but also a transformative platform for accelerating the discovery and development of therapeutics for cystic fibrosis and a broad range of other protein misfolding diseases.

## EXPERIMENTAL

### EXAMPLE 1

The generation of stable mammalian cell lines expressing fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) fusions with either wild-type or F508del-mutant cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) was accomplished through retroviral transduction of HEK293 cells using a modified pBabeSacLac2 expression vector. The FAP sequence, corresponding to the Malachite Green-binding variant dNP138, was first inserted into the Sac module of the pBabeSacLac2 plasmid via SfiI restriction digestion and ligation. Subsequently, the coding sequence for either wild-type or F508del-CFTR was amplified by polymerase chain reaction using primers that introduced SfiI restriction sites at both the 5′ and 3′ ends. The amplified CFTR fragment was digested with SfiI and ligated into the Lac module of the modified pBabeSacLac2 vector, resulting in the construction of pBabe dNP138-CFTR (WT) and pBabe dNP138-CFTR (F508del). For the N-terminal fusion, an additional transmembrane segment derived from the platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR) was included between the FAP and the first transmembrane domain of CFTR to ensure proper membrane anchoring and orientation of the FAP domain on the extracellular face of the plasma membrane. This design was based on prior structural and functional studies demonstrating that such an arrangement prevents misfolding and intracellular retention of the fusion protein while maintaining the native trafficking behavior of CFTR.

For the fourth extracellular loop (EL4) fusion, a modified CFTR plasmid containing KpnI and EcoRV restriction sites flanking the EL4 region was obtained from a collaborator. The FAP sequence was amplified using primers containing KpnI and EcoRV restriction sites and ligated into the EL4 region between the amino acid residues KGNSTHS and RNNSY, thereby preserving the two conserved asparagine residues at positions 894 and 900 that serve as sites for N-linked glycosylation. The resulting CFTR EL4-FAP construct was then transferred into the pBabe retroviral vector using BamHI and XhoI restriction sites to facilitate stable expression in mammalian cells. Both the N-terminal and EL4 fusion constructs were verified by DNA sequencing to ensure the fidelity of the inserted sequences and the absence of unintended mutations.

Retroviral particles were generated using the Phoenix Ecotropic Packaging System. Phoenix-ECO cells were transfected with the pBabe dNP138-CFTR plasmids using standard calcium phosphate precipitation methods. After 24 hours, the transfection medium was replaced with fresh Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium supplemented with calf serum, and the cells were incubated at 32°C under 5% CO₂ for an additional 48 hours to allow for viral particle production. The viral supernatant was then collected, filtered through a 0.45-μm syringe filter to remove cellular debris, and flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen for storage. Recipient HEK293 cells were plated at a density of 2 × 10⁵ cells per 35-mm dish and transduced the following day by incubation with the viral supernatant in the presence of 6 μg/mL hexadimethrine bromide to enhance transduction efficiency. After 24 hours of infection, the viral medium was replaced with fresh growth medium, and cells were allowed to recover for an additional 48 hours before selection with puromycin to establish stable polyclonal cell lines. Expression of the FAP-CFTR fusion proteins was confirmed by fluorescence microscopy following incubation with the cell-permeant fluorogen MG-ester, which revealed intracellular fluorescence consistent with endoplasmic reticulum localization in F508del-expressing cells and plasma membrane localization in wild-type-expressing cells.

To assess the trafficking defect of F508del-CFTR, cells were treated with the cell-impermeant fluorogen MG-11p at a concentration of 50 nM for 30 minutes at 37°C and imaged using a Nikon Ti Eclipse confocal microscope equipped with a 640-nm laser and a 700/75-nm emission filter. Under control conditions, no fluorescence signal was detected at the plasma membrane in F508del-CFTR-expressing cells, whereas wild-type FAP-CFTR exhibited bright, well-defined labeling at the cell periphery. This absence of surface signal in the F508del mutant confirmed that the FAP tag did not bypass the intrinsic trafficking defect of the mutation. To verify that the fusion protein was expressed and retained intracellularly, cells were incubated with MG-ester, which diffused across the plasma membrane and activated fluorescence in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi compartments, producing a strong intracellular signal that was absent in untransduced cells. These results demonstrated that the FAP-CFTR fusion proteins faithfully recapitulated the trafficking behavior of untagged CFTR and that the FAP tag did not alter the fundamental folding defect of F508del-CFTR.

### EXAMPLE 2

To evaluate the pharmacological rescue of F508del-CFTR trafficking, stable HEK293 cell lines expressing either FAP-F508del-CFTR or CFTR EL4-FAP were treated with a panel of small molecule correctors, including C4, CFFT-002, and C18, either individually or in combination. Correctors were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and added to the culture medium at final concentrations of 10 μM for C4, 5 μM for CFFT-002, and 5 μM for C18. Vehicle-treated control cells received an equivalent volume of DMSO. Cells were incubated with the compounds for 24 hours at 37°C under 5% CO₂, after which they were washed once with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and incubated with the cell-impermeant fluorogen MG-11p at a concentration of 50 nM for 30 minutes at 37°C. Fluorescence was then visualized using confocal microscopy with a 640-nm excitation laser and a 700/75-nm emission filter. In vehicle-treated controls, no surface fluorescence was observed, confirming the absence of rescued protein at the plasma membrane. In contrast, cells treated with C4, CFFT-002, or C18 individually exhibited detectable surface fluorescence, with the strongest signal observed in cells treated with C18. The most robust rescue was achieved with combination treatments: C4 + C18 and CFFT-002 + C18 produced intense, uniform labeling of the plasma membrane, indicating a dramatic increase in the abundance of F508del-CFTR at the cell surface.

To quantify the extent of rescue, cells were detached from the culture dish using Cellstripper™, a non-enzymatic cell dissociation reagent, to preserve the integrity of surface-exposed FAP tags. Cells were centrifuged at 500 × g for 5 minutes at 4°C, resuspended in PBS, and analyzed by flow cytometry using a 640-nm laser for excitation and a 685/35-nm bandpass filter for emission. A total of 10,000 events were recorded per sample, and the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) was calculated for each condition. The MFI for each corrector condition was normalized to the MFI of the C18-treated group to account for inter-experimental variability. The results revealed that C18 was the most potent single-agent corrector, increasing surface expression by 3.5-fold compared to vehicle-treated controls. C4 and CFFT-002 each produced modest increases in surface expression, with MFI values of approximately 0.53 and 0.55, respectively, relative to C18. Combination treatments yielded significantly greater effects: C4 + C18 increased surface expression by 9.3-fold relative to C4 alone, while CFFT-002 + C18 increased expression by 8.6-fold. Notably, the combination of C4 and CFFT-002 did not produce a statistically significant improvement over either compound alone, suggesting that these two correctors may act on overlapping or redundant targets. In contrast, the synergy observed with C18 indicated that it likely acts through a distinct mechanism, possibly by stabilizing a different conformational state of F508del-CFTR or by modulating a separate quality control pathway.

To confirm that the increased surface fluorescence corresponded to genuine trafficking rescue and not merely enhanced fluorogen binding, parallel experiments were performed using the cell-permeant fluorogen MG-ester. In these experiments, the total intracellular fluorescence of F508del-CFTR was measured before and after corrector treatment. No significant increase in intracellular fluorescence was observed in any condition, indicating that the total pool of F508del-CFTR protein was not substantially altered by corrector treatment. This finding demonstrated that the increase in MG-11p fluorescence was due to enhanced trafficking of existing protein to the plasma membrane, rather than increased total expression or stabilization of intracellular pools. Furthermore, the lack of change in intracellular signal confirmed that the FAP tag did not alter the intrinsic degradation kinetics of F508del-CFTR and that the rescue observed was due to improved folding and export from the endoplasmic reticulum.

### Summary of Corrector Efficacy from FAP Labeling and Functional Assays

The efficacy of each corrector and combination was systematically evaluated across multiple independent assays to determine the concordance between FAP-based surface detection and established functional and biochemical readouts. In HEK293 cells expressing FAP-F508del-CFTR, the magnitude of surface fluorescence measured by flow cytometry was directly correlated with the degree of anion transport activity measured by SPQ iodide efflux assays. Cells treated with C18 exhibited a 3.2-fold increase in the rate of iodide efflux compared to vehicle controls, while the combination of C4 + C18 produced a 7.8-fold increase, consistent with the 9.3-fold increase in surface expression observed by flow cytometry. Similarly, in CFTR EL4-FAP-expressing cells, the correlation between surface fluorescence and anion transport was maintained, albeit with lower absolute values, reflecting the reduced trafficking efficiency of the EL4 fusion construct. In both constructs, the combination of CFFT-002 and C18 produced the most robust functional rescue, with iodide efflux rates approaching 60% of those observed in wild-type CFTR-expressing cells.

Immunoblot analysis of whole-cell lysates confirmed that the increase in surface expression was accompanied by the acquisition of mature, complex-glycosylated CFTR. In FAP-F508del-CFTR-expressing cells, treatment with C4 + C18 or CFFT-002 + C18 resulted in the appearance of a distinct C-band on SDS-PAGE gels, corresponding to the fully glycosylated form of CFTR that has passed through the Golgi apparatus. This band was absent in vehicle-treated controls and was of comparable molecular weight to the C-band observed in wild-type FAP-CFTR. In contrast, the CFTR EL4-FAP construct exhibited a less distinct C-band, suggesting that the FAP insertion in the fourth extracellular loop partially impaired glycosylation efficiency, consistent with prior observations of altered glycan processing in EL4-tagged CFTR. Nevertheless, the presence of a higher molecular weight band in corrected samples confirmed that pharmacological rescue led to improved folding and trafficking beyond the endoplasmic reticulum.

The most compelling validation of the FAP platform came from experiments performed in primary human bronchial epithelial (HBE) cells derived from CF patients homozygous for the F508del mutation. These cells express endogenous, untagged CFTR and are considered the gold standard for preclinical evaluation of CF therapeutics. When treated with C18 alone, HBE cells exhibited a statistically significant increase in transepithelial short-circuit current in Ussing chamber experiments, whereas C4 alone had no measurable effect. The combination of CFFT-002 and C18 produced the most robust functional rescue, increasing short-circuit current by more than 2.5-fold compared to vehicle-treated controls. These results mirrored the relative efficacy observed in the FAP-based assays, with C18 emerging as the most potent single agent and the combination of CFFT-002 and C18 producing the greatest synergistic effect. Importantly, the pattern of corrector efficacy was identical in both the FAP-expressing HEK293 cells and the endogenous CFTR-expressing HBE cells, demonstrating that the FAP platform accurately recapitulates the behavior of the native protein in a physiologically relevant context.

Densitometry analysis of immunoblots from HBE cells further confirmed that the combination of CFFT-002 and C18 induced the appearance of the mature C-band of CFTR, whereas C4 alone did not. This biochemical signature of rescue was consistent with the functional and trafficking data obtained from the FAP system, providing a multi-layered validation of the platform’s predictive power. Statistical analysis using a two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test confirmed that all observed differences between corrector-treated and vehicle-treated groups were significant, with p-values ranging from <0.0001 to 0.01 depending on the condition and assay. The high degree of concordance across all assays—flow cytometry, iodide efflux, Ussing chambers, and immunoblotting—demonstrates that the FAP-CFTR system is not merely a surrogate readout but a faithful and quantitative representation of the biological behavior of F508del-CFTR. This level of consistency across disparate methodologies and biological systems has not been achieved by any prior technology and establishes the FAP platform as a transformative tool for drug discovery in cystic fibrosis and other protein misfolding diseases.
