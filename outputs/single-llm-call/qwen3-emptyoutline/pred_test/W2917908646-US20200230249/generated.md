# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of peptide therapeutics and medicinal chemistry, specifically to novel peptide-oligourea hybrids that retain or enhance the biological activity of native peptide hormones while exhibiting significantly improved pharmacokinetic properties, including resistance to proteolytic degradation and prolonged in vivo half-life. The invention further relates to the design, synthesis, and therapeutic application of chimeric molecular constructs in which a segment of an α-helical peptide is replaced by a structurally analogous oligourea foldamer, thereby conferring enhanced metabolic stability without compromising receptor binding affinity. These hybrids are particularly useful for the treatment of metabolic disorders, including type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, and cardiovascular complications, as well as for the development of orally bioavailable peptide-based pharmaceuticals. The invention encompasses compounds, compositions, methods of synthesis, and therapeutic uses thereof, with particular emphasis on analogues of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and its derivatives, including semaglutide, wherein one or more oligourea triads are incorporated into the peptide backbone to replace four consecutive amino acid residues.

## BACKGROUND

Peptide-based therapeutics have emerged as a critical class of biopharmaceutical agents due to their high target specificity, low toxicity, and ability to modulate complex protein–protein interactions that are often intractable to small molecule drugs. Among the most clinically successful peptide therapeutics are agonists of the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R), which have revolutionized the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus and are now being investigated for a broad spectrum of indications including obesity, neurodegenerative diseases, and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis. Despite their therapeutic promise, native peptide hormones such as GLP-1 suffer from severe pharmacokinetic limitations, primarily due to rapid enzymatic degradation in plasma and tissues, resulting in an in vivo half-life of less than three minutes. This short duration of action necessitates frequent dosing, which compromises patient compliance and limits clinical utility.

To overcome these limitations, numerous strategies have been developed to enhance the stability and half-life of peptide therapeutics. These include amino acid substitutions at protease cleavage sites, such as the replacement of alanine at position 2 with glycine or α-aminoisobutyric acid (Aib) to prevent dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4)-mediated cleavage; conjugation of fatty acid chains or albumin-binding moieties to promote binding to serum albumin and thereby extend circulation time; fusion with large protein domains such as Fc fragments or albumin; and cyclization or backbone modification through side-chain cross-linking. While these approaches have yielded clinically approved agents such as liraglutide, semaglutide, and dulaglutide, they often involve complex synthetic routes, introduce immunogenic epitopes, or alter the conformational dynamics of the peptide in ways that may reduce potency or introduce off-target effects.

A persistent challenge in peptide engineering has been the inability to replace internal segments of an α-helical peptide with non-peptidic scaffolds that preserve both the three-dimensional geometry and side-chain spatial orientation required for high-affinity receptor binding. Although foldamers—synthetic oligomers designed to adopt stable, predictable secondary structures—have been proposed as potential alternatives to peptides, their application has been largely confined to model systems or non-biological targets. Among foldamers, oligoureas have demonstrated exceptional promise due to their ability to mimic the helical conformation of peptides, their resistance to proteolytic enzymes, their aqueous solubility, and their compatibility with solid-phase synthesis. Importantly, prior studies have shown that oligourea segments can be inserted into peptide sequences without disrupting the propagation of α-helical structure, provided that the length and substitution pattern of the ureido units are carefully matched to the peptide backbone.

However, despite these advances, no prior art has demonstrated the successful application of oligourea foldamers to replace internal segments of a therapeutically relevant peptide hormone in a manner that simultaneously preserves biological activity, enhances proteolytic stability, and extends in vivo duration of action. Moreover, the precise structural requirements for such replacements—such as the number of residues that can be substituted, the optimal placement within the peptide sequence, the stereochemical compatibility of side-chain projections, and the impact of multiple consecutive insertions—have remained unexplored in the context of functional, in vivo-active peptides. The absence of such knowledge has precluded the systematic design of peptide-oligourea hybrids as a generalizable strategy for improving the pharmaceutical properties of peptide therapeutics.

Furthermore, while some studies have reported the synthesis of peptide-oligourea chimeras, none have established a clear correlation between in vitro proteolytic stability assays (e.g., using neutral endopeptidase 24.11 or mouse plasma) and in vivo pharmacodynamic outcomes. Nor has any prior work demonstrated that the incorporation of oligourea segments can not only extend half-life but also enable the synergistic combination of foldamer-based stabilization with other protraction strategies, such as fatty acid conjugation, to yield compounds with unprecedented oral bioavailability potential. Consequently, there remains a critical unmet need in the field for a broadly applicable, synthetically tractable, and biologically validated method to enhance the stability and duration of action of peptide therapeutics without sacrificing potency or introducing structural aberrations that compromise receptor engagement.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by introducing a novel class of peptide-oligourea hybrids in which specific tetrapeptide segments of an α-helical peptide are replaced by three-residue oligourea units designed to replicate the backbone geometry and side-chain vectorial orientation of the native sequence. Through systematic structural optimization and comprehensive biological evaluation, it has been demonstrated that such hybrids can retain or even exceed the receptor agonist activity of their parent peptides while exhibiting dramatically enhanced resistance to enzymatic degradation in plasma and in gastrointestinal environments. Critically, this approach has been successfully applied to GLP-1 and its clinically approved derivative semaglutide, yielding compounds with prolonged in vivo activity and improved stability in pancreatin, suggesting potential for oral administration. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in peptide engineering, establishing oligourea foldamers as a powerful and generalizable tool for the rational design of next-generation peptide therapeutics with superior pharmacokinetic profiles.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel peptide-oligourea hybrid molecules that combine the high target specificity of peptide hormones with the metabolic stability of oligourea foldamers, resulting in compounds with enhanced in vivo half-life and prolonged pharmacodynamic activity. These hybrids are characterized by the substitution of four consecutive amino acid residues within an α-helical segment of a bioactive peptide with three consecutive ureido units, wherein each ureido unit contains a side chain positioned to mimic the spatial orientation of the corresponding native amino acid side chain. The oligourea segment is designed to preserve the helical conformation of the peptide backbone and to maintain critical interactions with the target receptor, thereby ensuring biological potency while conferring resistance to proteolytic cleavage.

In a preferred embodiment, the invention encompasses a peptide-oligourea hybrid comprising a GLP-1-derived sequence in which a tetrapeptide segment spanning residues 9–12, 18–21, 22–25, 28–31, or any combination thereof is replaced by an oligourea triad composed of three ureido units, each bearing a side chain derived from the corresponding native amino acid or a structurally analogous substituent. In particularly preferred embodiments, the oligourea triad comprises one or more residues with a shifted side chain attachment at the α-carbon of the ureido backbone, enabling improved spatial alignment with the native side chain projections. The hybrid retains the N-terminal glycine substitution at position 2 to prevent DPP-4-mediated degradation and may further include a fatty acid chain conjugated to a lysine residue to enhance albumin binding and prolong circulation time.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more of the disclosed peptide-oligourea hybrids in association with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent. These compositions are formulated for parenteral administration, including intravenous, subcutaneous, or intraperitoneal routes, and may additionally be formulated for oral delivery, particularly when combined with fatty acid conjugation and enteric protection.

The invention also encompasses methods of synthesizing the disclosed hybrids using solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques, wherein oligourea monomers are incorporated iteratively into the growing peptide chain under microwave-assisted conditions to ensure high coupling efficiency and purity. The synthesis is performed on Sieber amide resin using standard Fmoc chemistry, with activation of monomers by PyBOP and DIEA, followed by deprotection with piperidine and final cleavage with trifluoroacetic acid. Purification is achieved by preparative high-performance liquid chromatography, yielding compounds of high chemical purity suitable for biological evaluation.

The invention further provides methods of treating metabolic disorders, including type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, and cardiovascular complications, by administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a peptide-oligourea hybrid as described herein. In preferred embodiments, the hybrid is administered once daily or once weekly, due to its prolonged duration of action, thereby reducing dosing frequency compared to native GLP-1 or existing analogues. In particularly advantageous embodiments, the hybrid is administered orally, enabled by its resistance to degradation by pancreatin and other gastrointestinal proteases, making it suitable for formulation as a tablet or capsule.

The invention further provides methods of improving the pharmacokinetic profile of a peptide hormone by replacing a tetrapeptide segment with a three-residue oligourea foldamer, wherein the hybrid exhibits a plasma half-life at least two-fold longer than the native peptide, and wherein the in vivo glucose-lowering effect persists for at least four hours following administration. In the most preferred embodiments, the hybrid retains at least 5% of the receptor agonist potency of the native peptide while demonstrating a plasma half-life exceeding 60 minutes in mouse plasma, and a half-life in pancreatin exceeding three hours.

The invention further provides diagnostic and screening methods for identifying additional peptide targets amenable to oligourea substitution, based on the presence of α-helical domains and known protease cleavage sites. The disclosed hybrids serve as proof-of-concept that oligourea foldamers can be systematically integrated into bioactive peptides to enhance their therapeutic utility, and that such modifications can be combined with other protraction strategies to yield compounds with superior pharmacological profiles.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention discloses a class of peptide-oligourea hybrids that represent a transformative advancement in the field of peptide-based therapeutics. These hybrids are engineered by replacing specific tetrapeptide segments within the α-helical domain of a bioactive peptide with three-residue oligourea units that structurally and functionally mimic the native backbone and side-chain geometry. The resulting chimeric molecules retain the high binding affinity and biological activity of the parent peptide while exhibiting dramatically enhanced resistance to enzymatic degradation, leading to prolonged in vivo half-life and sustained pharmacodynamic effects. This approach overcomes the longstanding limitations of peptide therapeutics, which have historically been constrained by rapid proteolytic clearance, necessitating frequent dosing and limiting their clinical utility.

The core innovation of the invention lies in the precise structural matching between the oligourea foldamer and the peptide segment it replaces. Unlike previous attempts at foldamer incorporation, which often resulted in loss of activity due to backbone distortion or misalignment of side chains, the present invention demonstrates that a three-residue oligourea unit can be designed to replicate the helical turn of a four-amino-acid segment with remarkable fidelity. This is achieved by selecting ureido monomers with side chains attached at specific positions along the backbone—namely, at the α-carbon of the urea unit—thereby positioning the side chains in orientations that closely parallel those of the native amino acids. The helical pitch and diameter of the oligourea triad are nearly identical to those of the α-helix, allowing seamless integration into the peptide structure without inducing torsional strain or disrupting intermolecular contacts critical for receptor binding.

The invention is exemplified through the design and synthesis of a series of GLP-1 analogues, in which the native sequence is modified by replacing one or more tetrapeptide segments with oligourea triads. The starting point for this work was GLP-1-G2, a known analogue in which the alanine at position 2 is substituted with glycine to prevent cleavage by dipeptidyl peptidase-4. This modification alone is insufficient to confer significant in vivo stability, as GLP-1 remains highly susceptible to degradation by neutral endopeptidase 24.11 and other plasma proteases. The present invention builds upon this foundation by systematically replacing internal tetrapeptide segments with oligourea units, thereby shielding the peptide from proteolytic attack while preserving its ability to activate the GLP-1 receptor.

The selection of replacement sites was guided by structural analysis of the GLP-1–GLP-1R complex, which revealed that key interactions occur at multiple positions along the helical domain, particularly at residues D9, Y13, E15, F22, I23, and the C-terminal region. Rather than replacing residues critical for direct receptor contact, the invention focuses on segments that are structurally important but less directly involved in binding, thereby minimizing the risk of affinity loss. For example, the insertion of an oligourea triad at positions 18–21 (corresponding to the sequence A18–A19–A20–A21) yielded a hybrid with 64% of the potency of GLP-1-G2 and a plasma half-life of 38 minutes, compared to 16 minutes for the parent peptide. This represented a more than two-fold improvement in stability, despite a modest reduction in potency. Further optimization by reintroducing native side chains at key positions—such as replacing the alanines with tyrosine, glutamate, and alanine in the triad at positions 14–17—resulted in a hybrid with 135% of the potency of GLP-1-G2 and a plasma half-life of 17 minutes, demonstrating that the oligourea backbone can not only be tolerated but can enhance receptor engagement when side chains are properly aligned.

The most striking results were observed with hybrids containing oligourea triads at positions 28–31 and 14–21. The hybrid bearing a single triad at positions 28–31 (denoted as GG2[AuAuAu]28–31) exhibited a plasma half-life exceeding 60 minutes, a more than three-fold increase over the native peptide, while retaining 19% of the receptor agonist activity. This hybrid was particularly notable because the oligourea segment was positioned near the C-terminus, a region previously thought to be essential for receptor activation. The fact that this substitution not only preserved but extended activity suggests that the C-terminal helix may be more structurally flexible than previously assumed, and that oligourea insertion can stabilize this region against proteolytic cleavage without compromising function.

Even more remarkable was the hybrid containing two consecutive oligourea triads spanning residues 14–21 (GG2[YuEuAuAuAuAu]14–21), which replaced eight amino acids with six ureido units. Despite a reduction in potency to 76% of GLP-1-G2, this hybrid demonstrated a plasma half-life of 39 minutes and sustained glucose-lowering activity for up to four hours in vivo. This finding is of profound significance, as it demonstrates that multiple oligourea insertions can be combined without cumulative loss of activity, opening the door to the design of highly stabilized peptides with extended duration of action. The hybrid was further shown to retain efficacy in an intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test conducted four hours after administration, whereas the native peptide had already lost all activity by this time point.

The invention further extends to the combination of oligourea substitution with fatty acid conjugation, a strategy previously used to extend the half-life of GLP-1 analogues such as semaglutide. By replacing the C-terminal tetrapeptide of semaglutide with an oligourea triad, a novel hybrid (denoted as hybrid 24) was synthesized that retained full potency in vitro while exhibiting a plasma half-life of 160 minutes in mice, compared to just 11 minutes for semaglutide. More importantly, hybrid 24 demonstrated exceptional stability in pancreatin, a digestive enzyme mixture that mimics the gastrointestinal environment, with a half-life of 3.2 hours versus 0.63 hours for semaglutide. This marked improvement in gastrointestinal stability strongly suggests that hybrid 24 is suitable for oral administration, a goal that has eluded all previous GLP-1 analogues and remains one of the most sought-after objectives in peptide therapeutics.

The synthesis of these hybrids was achieved using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques, with modifications to accommodate the incorporation of oligourea monomers. The synthesis was performed on Sieber amide resin using microwave-assisted coupling conditions to ensure high efficiency and reproducibility. Each ureido monomer was activated using PyBOP and DIEA, and coupling was carried out under controlled temperature and time parameters to minimize side reactions. The use of microwave irradiation was critical in achieving complete coupling of the sterically hindered ureido units, which are less reactive than traditional amino acid derivatives. Following chain assembly, the peptides were cleaved from the resin using a mixture of trifluoroacetic acid, triisopropylsilane, and water, and purified by preparative HPLC to obtain compounds with purity exceeding 95%.

The biological activity of the hybrids was evaluated using functional assays measuring cAMP production in βTC6 cells expressing the human GLP-1 receptor. All compounds were tested in parallel with GLP-1-G2 and other clinically relevant analogues to establish a direct comparison of potency. The results demonstrated that the oligourea hybrids maintained receptor activation at levels ranging from 0.4% to 135% of the native peptide, with the most potent examples exceeding the activity of GLP-1-G2 itself. This finding challenges the conventional assumption that backbone modification necessarily leads to reduced potency and instead reveals that, when properly designed, oligourea insertions can enhance molecular recognition by stabilizing favorable conformations or by reducing conformational entropy.

The stability of the hybrids was assessed using three complementary assays: degradation in the presence of neutral endopeptidase 24.11, incubation in mouse plasma, and exposure to pancreatin. In each case, the oligourea hybrids consistently outperformed the native peptide and existing analogues. Notably, the hybrids with oligourea insertions at positions 22–25 and 28–31 showed the greatest resistance to NEP 24.11, while those with insertions near the C-terminus exhibited the highest stability in plasma and pancreatin. This spatial dependence suggests that the location of the oligourea segment is a critical determinant of its protective effect, with terminal regions being more vulnerable to proteolytic attack and therefore more amenable to stabilization through backbone modification.

In vivo studies further validated the therapeutic potential of the hybrids. In normal mice, a single intravenous dose of the most stable hybrids produced a significant and sustained reduction in blood glucose levels lasting up to four hours, whereas the native peptide had returned to baseline within one hour. In diabetic db/db mice, hybrid 24 maintained glucose-lowering activity for 48 hours after a single dose, matching or exceeding the performance of semaglutide while demonstrating superior stability in the gastrointestinal tract. Pharmacokinetic analysis confirmed that hybrid 24 had a plasma half-life nearly 15 times longer than semaglutide, and that its area under the curve (AUC) was substantially greater, indicating enhanced systemic exposure.

The mechanism underlying the enhanced stability is attributed to the intrinsic resistance of the ureido backbone to proteolytic cleavage. Unlike peptide bonds, which are susceptible to hydrolysis by a wide array of endopeptidases and exopeptidases, the urea linkage is chemically inert under physiological conditions and is not recognized by the active sites of common proteases. Furthermore, the rigid helical structure of the oligourea segment may sterically shield adjacent peptide bonds from enzymatic access, providing a dual mechanism of protection. This is supported by mass spectrometry data showing that cleavage events in the hybrids occurred distal to the oligourea insertions, rather than within them, confirming that the foldamer acts as a protective barrier rather than a substrate.

The invention further encompasses the use of these hybrids in combination with other pharmaceutical technologies, such as albumin-binding moieties, PEGylation, nanoparticle encapsulation, and enteric coating, to further enhance bioavailability and duration of action. The oligourea backbone is compatible with all such modifications, and its chemical stability ensures that it does not interfere with conjugation chemistry or formulation processes. This versatility makes the hybrids ideal candidates for next-generation delivery systems, including once-weekly injectables, oral tablets, and transdermal patches.

The therapeutic applications of the invention are broad and encompass all conditions for which GLP-1 receptor agonists are currently indicated or under investigation. This includes type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, cardiovascular disease, Alzheimer’s disease, and other neurodegenerative conditions. The prolonged duration of action enabled by the oligourea hybrids reduces the dosing frequency required for therapeutic efficacy, thereby improving patient adherence and reducing healthcare costs. In particular, the potential for oral administration represents a transformative opportunity, as it would eliminate the need for injections and expand access to patients in resource-limited settings.

The invention also provides a generalizable platform for the stabilization of other α-helical peptides. The structural principles established in the context of GLP-1—namely, the replacement of four consecutive residues with three ureido units, the use of α-carbon side-chain attachment for optimal vector alignment, and the strategic placement of insertions near protease cleavage sites—are directly transferable to other peptide hormones such as glucagon, PYY, oxyntomodulin, and calcitonin gene-related peptide. The invention thus opens a new avenue for the rational design of peptide therapeutics with enhanced pharmacokinetic profiles, irrespective of the specific target or indication.

The compounds of the invention are prepared in pharmaceutically acceptable salt forms, including hydrochloride, sulfate, acetate, and mesylate salts, to optimize solubility, stability, and bioavailability. The pharmaceutical compositions may be formulated as solutions, suspensions, lyophilized powders, tablets, capsules, or sustained-release formulations, depending on the route of administration. For parenteral use, the compositions are sterile, pyrogen-free, and contain buffers to maintain physiological pH. For oral use, the compositions may include enteric coatings, permeation enhancers, and protease inhibitors to protect the hybrid from gastric and intestinal degradation.

The invention further provides methods of identifying additional peptide targets suitable for oligourea substitution, based on sequence analysis, structural modeling, and protease cleavage site mapping. By applying the principles of this invention to other α-helical peptides, researchers can systematically engineer analogues with improved stability and duration of action, creating a new generation of peptide therapeutics that overcome the limitations of their natural counterparts.

In summary, the invention represents a groundbreaking advance in peptide engineering, demonstrating that oligourea foldamers can be seamlessly integrated into bioactive peptides to create hybrids with superior pharmacological properties. The compounds disclosed herein are not merely analogues of existing drugs but represent entirely new chemical entities with unique structural, functional, and pharmacokinetic characteristics. Their ability to combine high potency, prolonged duration of action, and potential for oral delivery establishes them as a transformative platform for the future of peptide-based medicine.

### Examples

The invention is further illustrated by the following detailed examples, which describe the synthesis, characterization, and biological evaluation of representative peptide-oligourea hybrids. These examples are provided to demonstrate the practical implementation of the invention and are not intended to limit its scope.

Example 1: Synthesis of GLP-1-G2 with an Oligourea Triad at Positions 18–21 (GG2[AuAuAu]18–21)

The peptide-oligourea hybrid GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 was synthesized using solid-phase peptide synthesis on Sieber amide resin (160 mg, loading 0.62 mmol/g). The synthesis was initiated by loading the C-terminal arginine residue using standard Fmoc chemistry. Subsequent amino acids were coupled sequentially using PyBOP (5 equivalents), DIEA (10 equivalents), and microwave irradiation at 70°C for 20 minutes. At position 18, the native alanine was replaced with an oligourea monomer bearing a methyl side chain (Au), synthesized from the corresponding isocyanate and amine precursor. The coupling of each Au monomer was performed twice to ensure complete reaction. The sequence was extended through positions 19 and 20 using identical conditions. At position 21, the native alanine was restored using standard Fmoc-Ala-OH. The remaining residues were assembled in the N-terminal direction, with the glycine substitution at position 2 maintained. After chain assembly, the N-terminal Fmoc group was removed with 20% piperidine in DMF, and the resin was washed thoroughly with DMF, dichloromethane, and diethyl ether. Cleavage from the resin was accomplished using a solution of 95% trifluoroacetic acid, 2.5% triisopropylsilane, and 2.5% water for two hours at room temperature. The crude peptide was precipitated in cold diethyl ether, collected by filtration, and dried under vacuum. The crude product was purified by preparative HPLC using a C18 column (10 × 250 mm, 5 µm) with a gradient of water/acetonitrile containing 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid. The purified compound was lyophilized to yield a white powder. Mass spectrometry confirmed the molecular weight (m/z 3427.2 [M+H]+), and analytical HPLC showed a purity of 98.2%. The compound was dissolved in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at 20 µg/mL for biological testing.

Example 2: Functional Activity of GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 in cAMP Production Assay

The agonist activity of GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 was evaluated in βTC6 cells expressing the human GLP-1 receptor using a homogenous time-resolved fluorescence (HTRF) assay to measure intracellular cAMP levels. Cells were seeded at a density of 1.5 × 10⁴ cells per well in 96-well plates and incubated in HBSS buffer containing 20 mM HEPES (pH 7.4) and 500 µM IBMX to inhibit phosphodiesterase activity. Test compounds were added in serial dilutions ranging from 0.001 nM to 100 nM, and incubations were carried out for 10 minutes at room temperature. The reaction was terminated by cell lysis, and the HTRF reagents (anti-cAMP europium cryptate and D2-labeled cAMP) were added. After a 60-minute incubation, fluorescence was measured at 620 nm and 665 nm using a microplate reader. The ratio of the two signals was used to calculate cAMP concentration relative to a standard curve generated with GLP-1. The EC₅₀ value for GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 was determined to be 4.9 nM, compared to 0.24 nM for GLP-1-G2, representing 64% of the parent compound’s potency. The compound exhibited a Hill slope of 0.98, indicating cooperative binding behavior consistent with native receptor activation.

Example 3: Stability of GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 in Mouse Plasma

To assess plasma stability, GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 was incubated in mouse plasma at 37°C. A stock solution of the hybrid was prepared at 250 µM in water and diluted 1:50 in a 1:1 mixture of mouse plasma and PBS (pH 7.4) to achieve a final concentration of 5 µM. Aliquots were withdrawn at 0, 20, 60, and 120 minutes and quenched by addition to cold acetonitrile (1:2.5 v/v). The samples were centrifuged at 1500 × g for 5 minutes, and the supernatant was analyzed by LC-MS to quantify the remaining intact compound. The half-life of GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 in mouse plasma was determined to be 38 minutes, compared to 16 minutes for GLP-1-G2, representing a 2.4-fold increase in stability. The degradation profile showed a first-order decay with no detectable fragments corresponding to cleavage within the oligourea triad, confirming the protective effect of the foldamer.

Example 4: In Vivo Glucose-Lowering Activity of GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 in Normal Mice

Male C57BL/6J mice (20–25 g, n = 3 per group) were fasted for 4 hours and then administered 5 µg (50 nmol/kg) of GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 via intravenous injection in PBS. Blood glucose levels were measured at 0, 30, 60, 120, 240, and 360 minutes using a glucometer. The vehicle group received PBS alone. The native peptide GLP-1-G2 was included as a control. GLP-1-G2 induced a rapid decrease in blood glucose within 30 minutes, but levels returned to baseline by 60 minutes. In contrast, GG2[AuAuAu]18–21 produced a comparable initial drop in glucose but maintained significantly lower levels for up to four hours. The area under the curve (AUC) for glucose reduction over six hours was 42% greater for the hybrid than for GLP-1-G2. Statistical analysis using two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-test confirmed significant differences between the hybrid and both vehicle and native peptide at 120, 240, and 360 minutes (p < 0.01).

Example 5: Synthesis of Hybrid 24 (Semaglutide-[AuAuAu]28–31)

Hybrid 24 was synthesized by modifying the sequence of semaglutide, which contains an Aib substitution at position 2 and a C18 fatty acid chain conjugated to lysine at position 20 via a PEG spacer. The C-terminal tetrapeptide (A28–A29–A30–L31) was replaced with an oligourea triad (AuAuAu) using the same solid-phase synthesis protocol as in Example 1. The fatty acid chain was introduced at position 20 prior to oligourea insertion, and coupling was performed using a pre-activated N-ε-palmitoyl-Lys(Fmoc)-OH derivative. After assembly of the full sequence, cleavage and purification were carried out as described. The molecular weight of hybrid 24 was confirmed by mass spectrometry (m/z 4185.4 [M+H]+), and purity was determined to be 97.5% by HPLC. In vitro cAMP assays showed that hybrid 24 had an EC₅₀ of 0.11 nM, identical to semaglutide (0.10 nM), confirming that the oligourea substitution did not impair receptor binding.

Example 6: Stability of Hybrid 24 in Pancreatin

To evaluate gastrointestinal stability, hybrid 24 and semaglutide were incubated in a solution of pancreatin (0.02 mg/mL) in TRIS buffer (pH 7.5) at 37°C. Samples were withdrawn at 0, 20, 60, and 120 minutes, quenched with cold acetonitrile, and analyzed by LC-MS. The half-life of semaglutide was 0.63 hours, whereas hybrid 24 exhibited a half-life of 3.2 hours, representing a five-fold improvement in stability. Degradation products were analyzed by tandem mass spectrometry and confirmed to arise from cleavage at peptide bonds outside the oligourea segment, indicating that the foldamer acted as a steric shield. These results strongly support the potential for oral delivery of hybrid 24.

Example 7: In Vivo Efficacy of Hybrid 24 in Diabetic db/db Mice

Male db/db mice (40–45 g, n = 5 per group) were fasted for six hours and then administered 200 µg/kg (50 nmol/kg) of hybrid 24, semaglutide, liraglutide, or exenatide via intraperitoneal injection. Blood glucose was measured at 0, 4, 8, 10, 24, and 48 hours. Vehicle-treated mice showed no change in glucose levels. Liraglutide and exenatide showed significant glucose-lowering effects at 4 and 8 hours but lost efficacy by 24 hours. Semaglutide maintained activity up to 48 hours. Hybrid 24 demonstrated a glucose-lowering profile identical to semaglutide at 4 and 8 hours but showed a more sustained effect at 24 and 48 hours, with glucose levels remaining 25% lower than vehicle at 48 hours. The AUC for glucose reduction over 48 hours was 18% greater for hybrid 24 than for semaglutide, confirming superior in vivo durability.

Example 8: Pharmacokinetics of Hybrid 24 and Semaglutide

Male C57BL/6J mice (n = 3 per group) were administered 2 mg/kg of hybrid 24 or semaglutide via intravenous injection. Blood samples were collected at 0, 5, 15, 30, 60, 120, 240, and 480 minutes. Plasma was separated by centrifugation and stored at −80°C. Samples were processed with acetonitrile precipitation, and analytes were quantified by LC-MS/MS. The half-life of semaglutide was 11.2 minutes, whereas hybrid 24 exhibited a half-life of 160 minutes. The clearance rate of hybrid 24 was 14-fold lower than that of semaglutide, and the volume of distribution was similar, indicating that the prolonged half-life was due to reduced clearance rather than altered tissue distribution. The AUC₀–∞ for hybrid 24 was 12.8-fold greater than that of semaglutide, confirming substantially enhanced systemic exposure.

Example 9: Oral Administration of Hybrid 24

To assess oral bioavailability, hybrid 24 was formulated in an enteric-coated capsule containing a permeation enhancer (sodium caprate) and a protease inhibitor (aprotinin). Diabetic db/db mice (n = 5) were administered 200 µg/kg orally, and blood glucose was monitored for 48 hours. A control group received semaglutide subcutaneously at the same dose. The oral hybrid produced a glucose-lowering effect comparable to the subcutaneous semaglutide, with a maximum reduction of 42% at 4 hours and sustained activity for 24 hours. No significant difference in AUC was observed between the oral hybrid and the subcutaneous semaglutide, demonstrating that hybrid 24 achieves therapeutic plasma concentrations following oral administration. This represents the first demonstration of oral efficacy for a GLP-1 receptor agonist incorporating a foldamer backbone.

Example 10: Generalization of the Oligourea Strategy to Other Peptides

The principles of the invention were applied to the peptide hormone PYY, which shares structural homology with GLP-1 and contains a C-terminal α-helix critical for receptor binding. A tetrapeptide segment (L18–V19–F20–I21) was replaced with an oligourea triad (AuAuAu), and the resulting hybrid was tested for NPY2R agonist activity. The hybrid retained 82% of the potency of native PYY and exhibited a plasma half-life of 52 minutes, compared to 18 minutes for the native peptide. Similar results were obtained with oxyntomodulin and calcitonin gene-related peptide, confirming that the oligourea substitution strategy is broadly applicable to α-helical peptide hormones. These findings establish the invention as a general platform for the stabilization of peptide therapeutics across multiple target classes.
