# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to wireless communication systems operating in the millimeter wave frequency band, and more specifically to methods and apparatuses for dynamically managing antenna array configurations in user equipment to reduce power consumption and thermal load while preserving beam correspondence between downlink and uplink communication channels. The invention addresses the challenge of maintaining spatial beam alignment between transmit and receive paths when selectively deactivating portions of a multi-element phased antenna array during uplink transmission, without requiring re-sweeping of beam indices or fallback to lower-frequency bands. The disclosed techniques enable user equipment to transition seamlessly between full-array and sub-array beamforming modes—hereinafter termed “full-chain” and “sub-chain” beam operations—by constructing a structured codebook of beamforming vectors that preserve angular correspondence across varying levels of antenna activation. This is particularly critical in fifth-generation (5G) and beyond mobile devices where high-frequency millimeter wave operation leads to elevated power amplifier utilization, rapid thermal accumulation, and battery life degradation. The invention provides a systematic framework for designing sub-chain beam codebooks that maintain directional consistency with full-chain beams, thereby eliminating the need for additional beam management procedures during mode transitions, reducing latency, conserving energy, and enhancing user experience through uninterrupted high-data-rate connectivity.

## BACKGROUND

In modern wireless communication systems, especially those operating in the millimeter wave spectrum (typically 24 GHz to 100 GHz), user equipment such as smartphones and mobile terminals rely on phased antenna arrays to achieve sufficient directional gain to overcome severe path loss and atmospheric attenuation. Unlike sub-6 GHz systems where single-antenna or small MIMO configurations suffice, millimeter wave systems require large-scale antenna arrays—often comprising dozens of elements—to generate narrow, high-gain beams capable of sustaining gigabit-per-second data rates. These arrays are typically implemented using a limited number of radio frequency (RF) chains, each connected to multiple antenna elements via phase shifters and power amplifiers, forming what is commonly referred to as a hybrid beamforming architecture. In such architectures, the beamforming process is divided into analog beamforming, performed by phase shifters at the antenna level, and digital beamforming, performed at the baseband using signal processing algorithms. The number of RF chains is often constrained by cost, power, and thermal considerations, leading to designs where the number of active antenna elements is less than the total number of available elements.

One of the most significant challenges in millimeter wave mobile devices is the excessive power consumption and associated thermal rise caused by the simultaneous activation of multiple power amplifiers. Each power amplifier requires a fixed bias current and standby power, regardless of the transmitted signal amplitude. Consequently, even when transmitting at low power levels, the total power draw remains high if all amplifiers are active. This leads to rapid battery depletion and elevated device temperatures, which can trigger thermal throttling mechanisms, force fallback to lower-frequency sub-6 GHz bands, or even cause user discomfort and safety concerns. While fallback to LTE or 4G networks is a common mitigation strategy, it results in a substantial reduction in data throughput—from multi-gigabit speeds to a few hundred megabits per second—and introduces additional latency due to protocol switching, reconnection delays, and potential service interruption. Moreover, frequent toggling between millimeter wave and sub-6 GHz modes increases signaling overhead and degrades the user’s perception of network reliability.

An alternative approach to mitigating power and thermal issues is to deactivate a subset of antenna elements during uplink transmission, thereby reducing the number of active power amplifiers and lowering both the transmitted power and the base power consumption. This technique, referred to herein as “sub-chain beam operation,” involves activating only a portion of the full antenna array while retaining the full array for downlink reception. Since uplink transmission typically consumes more power than downlink reception—due to lower transmit power budgets and the absence of base station-level power amplification—this asymmetric operation offers a compelling trade-off: maintaining high downlink performance while reducing uplink power consumption. However, this approach introduces a critical problem: the disruption of downlink-uplink beam correspondence.

In 5G New Radio (NR) standards, beam management protocols assume that the optimal downlink beam for channel estimation and reference signal reception is also the optimal uplink beam for transmission. This assumption, known as DL-UL beam reciprocity or beam correspondence, enables the network to infer uplink channel conditions from downlink measurements, thereby eliminating the need for separate uplink beam sweeping procedures. When the user equipment transitions from a full-array transmission mode to a sub-array mode, the spatial characteristics of the transmit beam change, potentially rendering the previously identified best downlink beam suboptimal or entirely unsuitable for uplink transmission. Without a mechanism to preserve this correspondence, the device must initiate a new beam search, increasing latency, consuming additional power, and degrading the overall system efficiency.

Prior art has explored methods to reduce power consumption in millimeter wave systems, including power scaling of all active elements, adaptive modulation and coding, and dynamic antenna switching. However, these approaches either fail to address the base power consumption of inactive power amplifiers or do not preserve spatial beam alignment. Some prior work has considered hierarchical codebook designs that generate wide beams by deactivating elements, but these were intended for beam broadening in initial access scenarios, not for sustained power-saving operations during active data transmission. Other approaches have proposed using machine learning to predict optimal beam pairs, but these require extensive training data, computational overhead, and real-time feedback loops that are impractical for mobile devices with limited processing capabilities.

Furthermore, existing beam codebooks are typically designed under the assumption that the same number of antenna elements are used for both transmission and reception. There is no standardized methodology for generating a set of sub-chain beamforming vectors that maintain directional similarity to a pre-defined full-chain codebook while simultaneously ensuring adequate angular coverage and low mutual interference. The absence of such a framework has led to ad hoc implementations that either sacrifice coverage performance or necessitate frequent beam reselection, undermining the very benefits sought by sub-chain operation.

This invention resolves these shortcomings by introducing three novel methods for designing sub-chain beam codebooks that explicitly preserve beam correspondence between full-chain and sub-chain modes. The methods are grounded in a rigorous mathematical formulation that accounts for the physical constraints of practical millimeter wave hardware, including discrete phase shifter resolution, polarization symmetry, and fixed antenna element activation. By optimizing for similarity, spherical coverage, or a balanced combination thereof, the invention enables user equipment to switch between different levels of antenna activation without triggering beam management procedures, thereby reducing latency, conserving energy, and maintaining uninterrupted high-throughput connectivity. The disclosed techniques are applicable to any millimeter wave device employing hybrid beamforming architectures with multiple polarizations and limited RF chains, and are particularly suited for smartphones, tablets, and other mobile terminals operating in dense urban environments where thermal management and battery life are paramount.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel method for designing a codebook of sub-chain beamforming vectors for use in millimeter wave wireless communication systems, wherein the codebook is constructed to preserve directional correspondence between full-chain and sub-chain beam operations, thereby eliminating the need for re-sweeping beam indices during transitions between modes of antenna activation. The invention enables user equipment to reduce power consumption and thermal load during uplink transmission by deactivating a subset of antenna elements while maintaining compatibility with the downlink beam management procedures defined by the communication standard. The core innovation lies in the systematic generation of a set of sub-chain beamforming vectors that are optimized to maintain spatial alignment with a pre-defined set of full-chain beams, even when the number of active antenna elements is reduced.

The invention comprises three distinct design methodologies for constructing the sub-chain beam codebook: Similarity Score Maximization (Sim-Max), Spherical Coverage Maximization (SC-Max), and Beam Correspondence Spherical Coverage Maximization (BC-SC-Max). Each method is tailored to specific operational priorities—beam mapping accuracy, angular coverage efficiency, or a balanced compromise between the two—and is implemented using constrained optimization techniques that respect the physical limitations of real-world hardware, including discrete phase quantization, polarization symmetry, and binary antenna activation.

In the first method, Sim-Max, each sub-chain beam is designed to closely resemble its corresponding full-chain beam in terms of radiation pattern shape and angular orientation. This is achieved by maximizing a similarity score that quantifies the overlap between the beam patterns over the entire sphere, normalized to account for differences in total radiated power. The optimization is performed under the constraint that the number of active antenna elements per polarization is fixed and that phase values are restricted to a finite set determined by the resolution of the phase shifters. The resulting sub-chain beams exhibit high fidelity to their full-chain counterparts, ensuring that the best downlink beam is highly likely to correspond to the best uplink beam even after antenna deactivation.

In the second method, SC-Max, the sub-chain codebook is optimized to maximize the average beam gain over the entire spherical space, independent of any correspondence with the full-chain beams. This approach prioritizes uniform spatial coverage and is suitable for scenarios where the user equipment operates symmetrically in both uplink and downlink with the same number of active elements. Although this method does not inherently preserve beam correspondence, it achieves superior spherical coverage compared to the Sim-Max method, particularly in low percentile regions of the cumulative distribution function. However, without additional alignment procedures, transitions between full-chain and sub-chain modes require full beam sweeping, which introduces latency and power overhead.

In the third method, BC-SC-Max, the invention introduces a hybrid optimization strategy that combines the strengths of the first two methods. The unit sphere is first partitioned into disjoint angular regions, each corresponding to the main lobe region of a full-chain beam. For each such region, a sub-chain beam is independently optimized to maximize the average gain within that region, while maintaining the same number of active elements per polarization and adhering to discrete phase constraints. This ensures that each sub-chain beam is tailored to cover the same angular sector as its corresponding full-chain beam, thereby preserving the beam correspondence while simultaneously achieving near-optimal spherical coverage. The result is a codebook that enables seamless transitions between full-chain and sub-chain modes with a beam matching rate exceeding 90% for moderate levels of antenna deactivation and remaining above 60% even for extreme cases where only a single antenna element is activated.

The invention further incorporates a hardware-aware constraint that the number of activated antennas in each polarization must be equal, reflecting the practical design of dual-polarized patch arrays commonly found in mobile devices. This constraint ensures balanced radiation patterns and avoids polarization imbalance that could lead to signal distortion or reduced diversity gain. The optimization problems underlying each method are solved using exhaustive search for small array sizes or iterative phase optimization algorithms for larger configurations, with multiple random initializations to avoid local optima. The resulting codebooks are stored in non-volatile memory within the user equipment and are selected dynamically based on thermal conditions, battery status, and network requirements.

The invention eliminates the need for additional beam management signaling during mode transitions, reduces power consumption by deactivating unnecessary power amplifiers, and prevents thermal throttling that would otherwise force fallback to sub-6 GHz bands. It enables user equipment to operate continuously in the millimeter wave band under varying power constraints, thereby maximizing data throughput, minimizing latency, and enhancing the overall user experience. The disclosed methods are compatible with existing 5G NR beam management protocols and require no modifications to the network infrastructure, making them immediately deployable in current and future mobile communication systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The detailed implementation of the invention encompasses a comprehensive framework for the design, generation, and operational deployment of sub-chain beam codebooks in millimeter wave user equipment, with the explicit objective of preserving beam correspondence between downlink and uplink transmission modes while minimizing power consumption and thermal load. The system architecture is predicated on a dual-polarized antenna array configuration, wherein each array consists of a plurality of patch antenna elements arranged along the edges of a mobile device, with each element capable of being independently activated or deactivated through a binary control signal. The antenna elements are grouped into vertical and horizontal polarizations, with each polarization forming a linear array of L elements, resulting in a total of 2L antenna elements per array. The beamforming vector, denoted as w, is a complex-valued vector of dimension 2L, where each element corresponds to the complex weight applied to a specific antenna element. The magnitude of each weight is constrained to be either zero or one, indicating whether the corresponding antenna element is deactivated or activated, respectively. The phase of each non-zero weight is restricted to a finite set of discrete values determined by the resolution of the phase shifters, such that for a b-bit phase shifter, the phase of each active element must satisfy the constraint that the complex weight lies on the b-bit phase constellation, i.e., w_k ∈ {e^(j2πm/2^b) | m ∈ {0, 1, ..., 2^b - 1}} for each k where |w_k| = 1.

The radiation pattern of the antenna array is computed from the electromagnetic field response of each individual antenna element, which is obtained through high-fidelity electromagnetic simulations or empirical measurements that incorporate the influence of the device housing, user grip, and surrounding materials. These effects are critical, as the presence of the human hand, plastic casing, and internal components significantly distorts the ideal free-space radiation pattern, particularly in the near-field region. The composite beam pattern for a given beamforming vector w is calculated as the magnitude-squared of the weighted sum of the E-field responses across all elements, expressed as B(θ, φ) = |M(θ, φ) · w|^2, where M(θ, φ) is a matrix of size N × 2L, with N representing the number of spatial sampling points on the unit sphere, and each column of M corresponds to the E-field response of an antenna element in the direction (θ, φ). The matrix M is precomputed and stored in the device’s memory, enabling rapid evaluation of beam patterns without requiring real-time electromagnetic computation.

The invention defines three distinct optimization objectives for generating the sub-chain beam codebook, each corresponding to a different operational priority. The first objective, Similarity Score Maximization (Sim-Max), seeks to ensure that each sub-chain beam closely mirrors the spatial characteristics of its corresponding full-chain beam. This is formalized by defining a similarity score between a full-chain beam G_i and a candidate sub-chain beam B_j as the normalized inner product of their radiation patterns over a dense set of N_p sampling points uniformly distributed on the unit sphere, such as those generated by a Fibonacci lattice. The similarity score is computed as S(G_i, B_j) = [∑_{n=1}^{N_p} G_i(θ_n, φ_n) · B_j(θ_n, φ_n)] / [∑_{n=1}^{N_p} G_i^2(θ_n, φ_n)], where the denominator serves as a normalization factor to ensure that the score equals unity when B_j is identical to G_i. The candidate sub-chain beam with the highest similarity score to G_i is selected as the corresponding sub-chain beam for the i-th full-chain beam. To generate the sub-chain beam, the optimization problem P1 is solved, which seeks to maximize the similarity score subject to the constraints that the number of active elements per polarization is fixed at L_A, that the phase values are quantized to b-bit resolution, and that the activation pattern satisfies the polarization symmetry constraint [w]_1:L_0 = [w]_{L+1:2L}_0. Due to the non-convex nature of the L0 norm and discrete phase constraints, an exhaustive search over all possible activation patterns is performed for small values of L and L_A, and for each activation pattern, an iterative phase optimization algorithm is applied to determine the optimal phase weights. This algorithm, derived from cyclic coordinate descent, updates the phase of each active element in sequence while holding the others fixed, repeating until convergence. Multiple random initializations are employed to mitigate the risk of converging to a local optimum, and the solution yielding the highest similarity score is retained. The resulting codebook ensures that the radiation pattern of each sub-chain beam is spatially aligned with its corresponding full-chain beam, thereby preserving the angular correspondence critical for beam management.

The second objective, Spherical Coverage Maximization (SC-Max), is designed to optimize the overall angular coverage of the sub-chain codebook without regard to correspondence with the full-chain beams. This method is appropriate for scenarios where the user equipment operates symmetrically in both uplink and downlink using the same number of active elements, and where the primary concern is maximizing the minimum beam gain across all directions. The optimization problem P2 is formulated to maximize the average beam gain over the entire unit sphere, subject to the same constraints on activation and phase quantization. The solution is obtained using a variant of the K-Means clustering algorithm, wherein the unit sphere is partitioned into K clusters, each associated with one beam in the codebook. In each iteration, every sampling point is assigned to the beam that provides the highest gain at that location, and then each beam is re-optimized to maximize the sum of gains over its assigned cluster. The beam optimization step involves solving L × L_A^2 × K separate sub-problems, each corresponding to a unique combination of activation pattern and beam index, and selecting the one that yields the highest gain for the assigned cluster. The resulting codebook exhibits superior spherical coverage, particularly in the lower percentiles of the cumulative distribution function, meaning that even in directions where the beam gain is weakest, the performance remains relatively high. However, because the sub-chain beams are not designed with reference to the full-chain beams, the best beam index in the sub-chain codebook may not correspond to the best beam index in the full-chain codebook, necessitating a full beam sweep upon mode transition.

The third and most innovative method, Beam Correspondence Spherical Coverage Maximization (BC-SC-Max), integrates the strengths of the first two approaches by introducing a targeted optimization strategy that preserves beam correspondence while achieving near-optimal spherical coverage. The procedure begins by partitioning the unit sphere into K disjoint angular regions, each corresponding to the main lobe region of a full-chain beam. This partitioning is performed by assigning each sampling point to the full-chain beam that yields the highest gain at that point, thereby defining a Voronoi-like tessellation of the sphere. For each region D_k associated with the k-th full-chain beam, a sub-chain beam is then designed by solving an optimization problem P3, which seeks to maximize the average gain over the angular region D_k, subject to the same constraints on activation, phase quantization, and polarization symmetry as in P1 and P2. Unlike P1, which optimizes over the entire sphere, P3 restricts the summation to the region D_k, and unlike P2, which seeks uniform coverage, P3 focuses the optimization on the angular sector where the full-chain beam is dominant. This ensures that the sub-chain beam is tailored to serve the same angular domain as its corresponding full-chain beam, thereby preserving the beam correspondence. The optimization is again performed using the iterative phase algorithm, with multiple initializations to ensure global optimality. The resulting codebook exhibits a remarkable balance: the beam correspondence is preserved with a matching rate exceeding 90% when transitioning between full-chain, 4-element, and 3-element modes, and remains above 60% even when transitioning to a 1-element mode, while the spherical coverage is nearly indistinguishable from that of the SC-Max method. This dual optimization capability is the key innovation of the invention, as it enables seamless, low-latency mode transitions without requiring additional beam management signaling.

The invention further incorporates a hardware-aware constraint that the number of activated antennas in the vertical and horizontal polarizations must be equal. This constraint is motivated by the physical design of dual-polarized patch arrays, where the vertical and horizontal elements are symmetrically arranged and must be activated in pairs to maintain balanced radiation patterns and avoid polarization mismatch. The constraint is enforced by requiring that the L0 norm of the first L elements of the beamforming vector equals the L0 norm of the last L elements, i.e., [w]_1:L_0 = [w]_{L+1:2L}_0. This ensures that the beam pattern remains symmetric in polarization, which is critical for maintaining polarization diversity and avoiding signal degradation due to cross-polarization interference. The constraint is incorporated directly into the optimization problems P1, P2, and P3, and is handled during the exhaustive search by only considering activation patterns that satisfy this condition.

The codebooks generated by the three methods are stored in the user equipment’s memory as a set of precomputed beamforming vectors, indexed by the number of active elements and the beam index. During operation, the device continuously monitors its internal temperature, battery level, and network requirements. When the device detects that the temperature is rising above a predefined threshold, or that the battery level is below a certain percentage, it initiates a transition from full-chain to sub-chain mode. The transition is seamless because the best downlink beam index is directly mapped to the corresponding sub-chain beam index in the codebook, eliminating the need for beam sweeping. The device selects the sub-chain beam with the same index as the best downlink beam, and transmits using the reduced number of active elements. Upon returning to full-chain mode, the device reactivates all elements and reuses the same beam index, confident that the spatial alignment has been preserved. This process is repeated dynamically as conditions change, enabling continuous millimeter wave operation without fallback to sub-6 GHz bands.

The invention further includes a mechanism for evaluating the beam correspondence performance across different codebooks. The best beam matching rate is defined as the probability that the index of the best beam in the sub-chain codebook matches the index of the best beam in the full-chain codebook over the entire unit sphere, assuming uniform distribution of channel directions. This metric is computed by comparing the argmax of the beam gain over the sampling points for both codebooks and calculating the fraction of points where the indices coincide. The results demonstrate that the BC-SC-Max method achieves a matching rate of over 90% for moderate deactivations and above 60% for extreme deactivations, far surpassing the SC-Max method, which achieves less than 25% without repair, and even the repaired SC-Max method, which, despite applying the Hungarian algorithm to optimally pair beams across codebooks, still falls short of the BC-SC-Max performance. This confirms that the BC-SC-Max method is not merely an improvement in beam pairing, but a fundamental advancement in codebook design that embeds correspondence into the optimization objective itself.

The invention also provides a comparative analysis of the spherical coverage performance of the three methods. The cumulative distribution function of the composite beam gain, defined as the maximum gain achieved by any beam in the codebook at each sampling point, is evaluated for all codebooks. The results show that the Sim-Max method, while achieving high correspondence, suffers from inferior coverage in the lower percentiles, as its beams are constrained to mimic the full-chain beams and thus lack the diversity needed to cover angular gaps. The SC-Max method achieves the highest coverage, as expected, but at the cost of correspondence. The BC-SC-Max method, however, achieves coverage performance nearly identical to SC-Max, demonstrating that it is possible to simultaneously maximize both correspondence and coverage. This is a critical breakthrough, as prior art has assumed a trade-off between these two objectives, and has therefore been forced to choose one at the expense of the other.

The invention is implemented entirely within the user equipment and requires no changes to the network infrastructure, base station protocols, or air interface specifications. It is fully compatible with 5G New Radio beam management procedures, including beam reporting, beam refinement, and beam failure recovery. The codebooks are generated offline during device manufacturing or firmware updates and are stored in non-volatile memory. The device’s baseband processor selects the appropriate codebook based on real-time sensor inputs and transmits using the corresponding beamforming vector. The entire process requires minimal computational resources and can be executed in real time on low-power embedded processors. The invention thus provides a practical, scalable, and immediately deployable solution to the critical problem of power and thermal management in millimeter wave mobile devices, enabling the sustained utilization of high-bandwidth spectrum without compromising user experience, battery life, or device safety.
