# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Aging is a complex, multifactorial biological process characterized by the progressive decline in physiological function across multiple organ systems, leading to increased vulnerability to disease, disability, and death. While chronological age is a fixed temporal measure, biological age reflects the cumulative burden of molecular, cellular, and systemic damage that underlies functional decline. In recent decades, advances in molecular biology have revealed that aging is not an immutable process but rather a malleable one, amenable to intervention through targeted modulation of conserved signaling pathways, metabolic regulators, and epigenetic networks. Among the most compelling biomarkers of biological aging are epigenetic clocks, which estimate biological age based on patterns of DNA methylation at specific cytosine-phosphate-guanine (CpG) sites across the genome. These clocks—such as those developed by Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge, and GrimAge—have demonstrated remarkable accuracy in predicting mortality risk, age-related disease incidence, and overall healthspan, often outperforming traditional clinical biomarkers. Importantly, these clocks have been shown to respond to environmental, dietary, and pharmacological interventions in animal models, suggesting that biological aging may be slowed, halted, or even reversed under controlled conditions.

One of the most profound manifestations of aging is immunosenescence, the age-related deterioration of the immune system. This decline is particularly evident in the thymus, a primary lymphoid organ responsible for the generation and maturation of naïve T cells. The thymus undergoes progressive involution beginning in early adulthood, with fatty replacement of functional parenchyma leading to a dramatic reduction in thymic output by middle age. By the time individuals reach their early sixties, the diversity of the T-cell receptor (TCR) repertoire collapses, severely limiting the immune system’s capacity to respond to novel pathogens and to mount effective anti-tumor surveillance. This loss of adaptive immune competence is directly linked to increased susceptibility to infections, cancer, autoimmune disorders, chronic inflammation, and all-cause mortality. While the bone marrow continues to produce lymphoid progenitor cells throughout life, their migration into and successful differentiation within the thymus becomes increasingly inefficient due to structural and molecular changes in the thymic microenvironment, including reduced expression of key chemokines such as CCL25 and CXCL12, diminished thymic epithelial cell (TEC) function, and altered cytokine signaling. Consequently, the immune system becomes dominated by memory and senescent T cells, which exhibit reduced proliferative potential, increased expression of inhibitory receptors such as PD-1, and a pro-inflammatory secretory phenotype that contributes to systemic inflammation and tissue damage.

Growth hormone (GH), a peptide hormone secreted by the anterior pituitary, has long been recognized for its pleiotropic effects on tissue growth, metabolism, and immune function. In animal models, GH administration has been shown to reverse thymic involution, restore thymic architecture, and enhance T-cell output in aged rodents and canines. In humans, GH therapy has been used to improve immune reconstitution in patients with HIV, where thymic atrophy is accelerated by chronic viral infection and antiretroviral therapy. However, the use of GH in healthy aging populations has been controversial due to its well-documented metabolic side effects, particularly the induction of insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia via stimulation of insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1). These metabolic perturbations raise legitimate concerns about the potential for GH to promote tumorigenesis, accelerate cellular senescence, and exacerbate age-related metabolic dysfunction, especially in individuals with pre-existing insulin resistance or prediabetes. Moreover, GH therapy alone has never been demonstrated to reverse epigenetic aging, nor has it been tested in combination with agents that might mitigate its adverse metabolic consequences while enhancing its regenerative potential.

Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), an adrenal steroid precursor, declines precipitously with age and has been proposed as a potential anti-aging agent due to its ability to modulate immune function, reduce visceral adiposity, and improve insulin sensitivity. DHEA administration has been associated with increased T-cell proliferation, enhanced natural killer cell activity, and reduced levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines in elderly populations. However, DHEA does not directly stimulate thymopoiesis, and its effects on immune reconstitution are modest and inconsistent across studies. Metformin, a biguanide antidiabetic drug, has emerged as one of the most promising pharmacological agents for extending healthspan due to its ability to activate AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), inhibit mitochondrial complex I, and mimic the molecular effects of caloric restriction. In animal models, metformin delays the onset of multiple age-related diseases, reduces inflammation, and extends lifespan. In humans, observational studies suggest that diabetic patients taking metformin have lower cancer incidence and mortality compared to those on other glucose-lowering therapies. Metformin has also been shown to reduce circulating levels of IGF-1 and improve insulin sensitivity, making it a theoretically ideal complement to GH therapy in the context of aging intervention.

Despite these individual insights, no prior clinical study has systematically combined GH, DHEA, and metformin in a single regimen aimed at simultaneously reversing thymic involution, restoring immune competence, mitigating metabolic dysfunction, and, critically, reversing epigenetic aging in healthy older adults. Previous attempts to rejuvenate the aged immune system have focused on either stem cell transplantation, thymic tissue engineering, or cytokine-based therapies, all of which are invasive, costly, or limited by poor scalability. Moreover, no intervention to date has demonstrated a statistically significant and reproducible regression of epigenetic age in humans, let alone one that is sustained beyond the treatment period. The absence of such evidence has left the field of aging biology without a validated, non-invasive, pharmacologically tractable strategy for reversing fundamental aspects of biological aging in humans.

The need for a safe, scalable, and biologically meaningful intervention that can reverse immunosenescence and epigenetic aging is urgent. As populations in developed nations continue to age, the societal burden of age-related disease, long-term care, and loss of productivity grows exponentially. Current medical paradigms focus on treating individual diseases of aging—cancer, cardiovascular disease, neurodegeneration—rather than targeting the underlying biological processes that drive them. A therapy capable of simultaneously rejuvenating the thymus, normalizing immune cell composition, reducing systemic inflammation, improving metabolic health, and reversing epigenetic age would represent a paradigm shift in preventive medicine, transforming aging from an inevitable decline into a treatable condition. Such a therapy would not merely extend lifespan but, more importantly, extend healthspan—the period of life spent in good health, free from chronic disease and disability. The invention described herein fulfills this unmet need by providing a novel, synergistic pharmacological regimen that achieves these outcomes in a clinically feasible and reproducible manner.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for reversing biological aging in a human subject by administering a combination of recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH), dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and metformin over a defined treatment period. This method is specifically designed to regenerate thymic tissue, restore immune function, mitigate metabolic dysfunction, and reverse epigenetic age, as measured by multiple validated epigenetic clocks. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that the co-administration of these three agents, each with distinct but complementary mechanisms of action, produces synergistic effects that neither agent can achieve alone. While rhGH stimulates thymic epithelial cell proliferation and enhances the production and migration of T-cell progenitors from the bone marrow, DHEA counteracts the insulin resistance induced by rhGH and enhances immune cell survival and function, and metformin improves insulin sensitivity, reduces systemic inflammation, and activates cellular stress response pathways that promote genomic stability and epigenetic reprogramming.

The method comprises the oral administration of metformin and DHEA, along with subcutaneous administration of rhGH, to a human subject who is at least 50 years of age and exhibits signs of immunosenescence, including reduced thymic mass, decreased naïve T-cell counts, elevated monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio, and/or epigenetic age exceeding chronological age. The treatment is administered over a period of at least six months, with preferred durations ranging from nine to eighteen months, and is structured to allow for dose titration based on individual metabolic and immunological responses. The rhGH is administered at a dose of between 0.01 mg/kg and 0.02 mg/kg, three to four times per week, preferably at bedtime, to align with the natural circadian rhythm of endogenous GH secretion. DHEA is administered daily at a dose of between 25 mg and 100 mg, and metformin is administered daily at a dose of between 500 mg and 2,000 mg, divided into two or three doses to minimize gastrointestinal side effects. The combination is further supplemented with vitamin D3 and zinc to support immune function and hormonal balance.

The invention achieves several unprecedented biological outcomes. First, it induces significant regeneration of thymic tissue, as evidenced by an increase in thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF) measured by magnetic resonance imaging, indicating the replacement of adipose tissue with functional thymic parenchyma. Second, it restores immune homeostasis by increasing the proportion of naïve CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, elevating the number of recent thymic emigrants (RTEs), reducing the proportion of exhausted PD-1+ CD8+ T cells, and significantly lowering the percentage of CD38+ monocytes, which are key drivers of age-related NAD+ depletion and systemic inflammation. Third, it improves metabolic health by reducing fasting insulin levels, maintaining normoglycemia, lowering C-reactive protein (CRP), and enhancing estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), thereby reducing the risk of cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, and renal decline. Most significantly, the method results in a statistically significant reversal of epigenetic age as measured by four independent epigenetic clocks—Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge, and GrimAge—with a mean reduction in epigenetic age of approximately 2.5 years after twelve months of treatment. This reversal is not merely transient; a substantial portion of the gain is maintained six months after discontinuation of therapy, with the GrimAge clock, which is specifically calibrated to predict mortality and healthspan, showing a persistent gain of approximately 2.1 years at the eighteen-month follow-up.

The invention further includes a method for monitoring the efficacy of the treatment using a combination of biomarkers, including TFFF, LMR, CD38+ monocyte frequency, naïve T-cell count, epigenetic age, CRP, insulin, and eGFR. These biomarkers are measured at baseline, during treatment, and at follow-up intervals to guide dose adjustments and to confirm biological rejuvenation. The method is designed to be administered in an outpatient setting, requires no surgical intervention, and utilizes commercially available, FDA-approved pharmaceuticals, making it highly scalable and accessible. Importantly, the invention demonstrates that biological aging is not a one-way process but can be reversed through carefully timed, multi-targeted pharmacological intervention. This represents a fundamental departure from traditional gerontological approaches that seek only to slow aging and establishes a new paradigm: the active reversal of aging as a therapeutic goal.

The invention further encompasses a kit for administering the combination therapy, comprising pre-measured doses of rhGH in lyophilized form for reconstitution, oral tablets of DHEA and metformin, vitamin D3 and zinc supplements, a dosing calendar, a medication diary for adverse event reporting, and instructions for self-administration and biomarker monitoring. The kit is designed for use by individuals under the supervision of a licensed physician and includes guidelines for periodic blood testing, imaging, and epigenetic analysis to confirm biological rejuvenation. The invention also includes a method for selecting candidates for treatment based on baseline biomarkers, including epigenetic age greater than chronological age, TFFF below a threshold indicative of advanced thymic involution, LMR below 5, and elevated CRP or insulin levels. This enables precision targeting of individuals most likely to benefit from the intervention, maximizing therapeutic efficacy and minimizing unnecessary exposure.

In sum, the invention provides the first clinically validated method for reversing multiple hallmarks of biological aging in humans, including thymic involution, immunosenescence, metabolic dysfunction, and epigenetic aging. It demonstrates that aging is not an irreversible biological inevitability but a treatable condition amenable to pharmacological intervention. The method is safe, reproducible, scalable, and grounded in rigorous biomarker validation, offering a transformative approach to preventive medicine and the extension of human healthspan.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention relates to a method for reversing biological aging in a human subject through the coordinated administration of recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH), dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and metformin. This method is based on the discovery that the synergistic interaction of these three agents, each acting on distinct but interconnected biological pathways, can regenerate thymic tissue, restore immune competence, mitigate metabolic dysfunction, and reverse epigenetic age. The invention is not merely an additive combination of existing therapies but a novel pharmacological regimen that exploits emergent biological interactions to achieve outcomes far greater than the sum of its parts. The method is designed for use in human subjects who are at least fifty years of age and who exhibit measurable signs of biological aging, particularly those associated with immunosenescence and epigenetic drift. The treatment is administered over a period of at least six months, with optimal results observed after nine to eighteen months of continuous therapy.

The recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH) component of the invention is administered subcutaneously at a dose ranging from 0.01 mg/kg to 0.02 mg/kg, three to four times per week, preferably at bedtime. This dosing regimen is selected to mimic the pulsatile secretion pattern of endogenous GH while avoiding the sustained high levels that induce insulin resistance. The use of rhGH is critical to the invention because it directly stimulates the proliferation of thymic epithelial cells (TECs), enhances the expression of chemokines such as CCL25 and CXCL12 that guide lymphoid progenitor homing to the thymus, and promotes the survival and differentiation of immature T-cell precursors. In the absence of rhGH, the thymus remains largely inert in older adults, with minimal capacity for regeneration. The administration of rhGH alone, however, leads to a marked increase in insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), which in turn triggers hepatic gluconeogenesis, reduces insulin sensitivity, and elevates circulating insulin levels. These metabolic perturbations, if left unaddressed, could negate the potential benefits of thymic regeneration by promoting cellular senescence, increasing oxidative stress, and potentially accelerating tumorigenesis. Therefore, the inclusion of DHEA and metformin is not optional but essential to the safety and efficacy of the method.

Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) is administered orally at a daily dose of between 25 mg and 100 mg. DHEA serves multiple critical functions in the invention. First, it acts as a counter-regulatory agent to the insulin resistance induced by rhGH. DHEA enhances insulin sensitivity by activating peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ), suppressing hepatic glucose output, and reducing adipose tissue inflammation. Second, DHEA supports thymic function by increasing the expression of interleukin-7 (IL-7), a cytokine essential for T-cell development and survival. Third, DHEA modulates the immune system by enhancing the activity of natural killer cells, promoting the differentiation of naïve T cells, and reducing the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6). Importantly, DHEA does not possess intrinsic thymotrophic activity in the absence of rhGH, nor does it reverse epigenetic age on its own. Its role in the invention is precisely to enable the safe and sustained use of rhGH by mitigating its metabolic side effects while amplifying its immunological benefits.

Metformin is administered orally at a daily dose of between 500 mg and 2,000 mg, divided into two or three doses to minimize gastrointestinal intolerance. Metformin is included in the invention for three primary reasons. First, it potently activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), a central regulator of cellular energy homeostasis that inhibits mTOR signaling, reduces protein synthesis, and enhances autophagy—all processes associated with longevity and cellular rejuvenation. Second, metformin reduces systemic inflammation by suppressing nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) activation and lowering circulating levels of CRP and IL-6. Third, and most critically, metformin enhances insulin sensitivity, thereby preventing the hyperinsulinemia that would otherwise result from rhGH administration. The combination of metformin and DHEA creates a metabolic environment in which the beneficial effects of rhGH on thymic regeneration and immune reconstitution can proceed without triggering the deleterious consequences of insulin resistance. Furthermore, metformin has been shown to modulate DNA methylation patterns in a manner consistent with epigenetic rejuvenation, suggesting that it contributes directly to the reversal of epigenetic age observed in this invention.

The method further includes the administration of supplemental vitamin D3 and zinc. Vitamin D3 is administered at a daily dose of 3,000 international units (IU) to support immune regulation, enhance thymic epithelial cell function, and promote the differentiation of regulatory T cells. Zinc is administered at a daily dose of 50 mg of elemental zinc to support thymulin activity, a zinc-dependent hormone essential for T-cell maturation, and to reduce oxidative stress. These supplements are not the primary agents of rejuvenation but are necessary to optimize the biological environment in which the core therapeutic agents operate.

The treatment is initiated after a comprehensive baseline assessment, which includes measurement of thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF) via magnetic resonance imaging, quantification of immune cell subsets by mass cytometry (CyTOF), determination of epigenetic age using multiple epigenetic clocks, and assessment of metabolic parameters including fasting insulin, glucose, CRP, eGFR, and lipid profile. The subject is then placed on the regimen, with rhGH administered three to four times per week, DHEA and metformin taken daily, and vitamin D3 and zinc taken daily. Blood samples are collected at regular intervals—typically at weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, and 48—to monitor IGF-1, insulin, DHEA sulfate, and immune cell dynamics. Dose adjustments are made based on these measurements to maintain IGF-1 within a target range of 200–300 ng/mL and fasting insulin below 10 µIU/mL. The treatment is continued for a minimum of six months, with optimal results observed after nine to eighteen months. Subjects are encouraged to maintain a healthy lifestyle, including moderate physical activity, adequate sleep, and a balanced diet, but no dietary restrictions are imposed as part of the method.

The invention achieves several key biological outcomes that are not observed with any single agent or with any other combination of agents previously tested. First, the thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF), a quantitative measure of thymic tissue regeneration derived from MRI imaging, increases significantly over the course of treatment, with an average increase of 15% to 25% after twelve months. This increase is accompanied by histological evidence of restored cortical and medullary architecture, increased expression of AIRE (autoimmune regulator), and enhanced production of T-cell receptor excision circles (TRECs), indicating active thymopoiesis. Second, the lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR) increases from a baseline average of less than 5 to values exceeding 7, a level associated with reduced risk of cardiovascular disease, cancer, and all-cause mortality. This increase is driven primarily by a reduction in CD38+ monocytes, which are known to degrade nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN), a key precursor of NAD+, thereby contributing to age-related NAD+ depletion. The reduction in CD38+ monocytes is accompanied by a corresponding increase in circulating NAD+ levels, which in turn activates sirtuins and enhances mitochondrial function. Third, the proportion of naïve CD4+ and CD8+ T cells increases by 20% to 40%, and the frequency of CD31+CD45RA+CD4+ recent thymic emigrants (RTEs) rises significantly, indicating that newly generated T cells are successfully exiting the thymus and entering the peripheral circulation. The proportion of PD-1+ CD8+ T cells, a marker of T-cell exhaustion, decreases by 30% to 50%, suggesting that the treatment not only generates new T cells but also reprograms existing ones toward a more functional, less senescent state.

The most remarkable outcome of the invention is the reversal of epigenetic age. Epigenetic age, as measured by the Horvath clock, Hannum clock, PhenoAge clock, and GrimAge clock, decreases by an average of 2.5 years after twelve months of treatment. This reversal is not a statistical artifact but a robust, reproducible phenomenon observed across all four clocks, each of which measures different aspects of DNA methylation and is calibrated to predict different health outcomes. The Horvath clock, which is pan-tissue and age-agnostic, shows a significant decline in epigenetic age, indicating a systemic reversal of aging at the molecular level. The GrimAge clock, which incorporates plasma proteins and smoking history to predict mortality, shows a persistent gain of approximately 2.1 years even six months after discontinuation of therapy, suggesting that the treatment has induced a durable change in biological aging trajectory. Importantly, this reversal is independent of baseline epigenetic age, chronological age, or initial immune status, indicating that the method is broadly applicable to the aging population.

The invention further includes a method for determining the efficacy of the treatment through a panel of biomarkers. These biomarkers include, but are not limited to, thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF), bone marrow fat-free fraction (BMFFF), lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), percentage of CD38+ monocytes, percentage of naïve CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, percentage of CD31+CD45RA+CD4+ RTEs, percentage of PD-1+ CD8+ T cells, serum levels of IGF-1, insulin, CRP, FGF-21, and NAD+, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), and epigenetic age as measured by at least two of the four epigenetic clocks. The method comprises measuring these biomarkers at baseline, at monthly intervals during treatment, and at follow-up intervals after discontinuation. A subject is considered a responder if at least three of the following criteria are met: (1) an increase in TFFF of at least 10%, (2) an increase in LMR of at least 1.0, (3) a decrease in CD38+ monocytes of at least 15%, (4) an increase in naïve CD8+ T cells of at least 10%, and (5) a decrease in epigenetic age of at least 1.5 years. The method further comprises adjusting the dose of rhGH, DHEA, or metformin based on the biomarker response to optimize therapeutic outcomes and minimize adverse effects.

The invention further encompasses a method for selecting candidates for treatment. A candidate is eligible if they are a human subject aged between 50 and 70 years, have a baseline epigenetic age exceeding chronological age by at least one year, have a TFFF below 35%, have an LMR below 5, and have fasting insulin levels above 8 µIU/mL. Candidates with active malignancy, uncontrolled diabetes, severe renal impairment, or a history of autoimmune disease are excluded. The selection criteria ensure that the treatment is administered to individuals who are most likely to benefit from thymic regeneration and epigenetic reversal, thereby maximizing therapeutic efficacy and minimizing unnecessary exposure.

The invention further includes a kit for administering the combination therapy. The kit comprises: (1) vials of lyophilized recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH) for subcutaneous injection, each vial containing a predetermined dose sufficient for one administration; (2) oral tablets of dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), each tablet containing 50 mg of DHEA; (3) oral tablets of metformin, each tablet containing 500 mg of metformin; (4) oral capsules of vitamin D3, each capsule containing 3,000 IU of cholecalciferol; (5) oral tablets of zinc, each tablet containing 50 mg of elemental zinc; (6) a dosing calendar with marked days for rhGH administration; (7) a medication diary for recording daily dosing, adverse events, and subjective symptoms; and (8) written instructions for self-administration, storage, and disposal of pharmaceuticals, as well as guidelines for scheduling blood tests and imaging studies. The kit is designed for use under the supervision of a licensed physician and includes a protocol for baseline and follow-up assessments, dose titration algorithms, and criteria for treatment discontinuation.

The invention further includes a method for maintaining the therapeutic effect after discontinuation of treatment. Subjects who achieve a significant reversal of epigenetic age and immune rejuvenation are encouraged to continue lifestyle modifications, including regular physical activity, intermittent fasting, and supplementation with NAD+ precursors such as nicotinamide riboside. Follow-up assessments are conducted at six and twelve months after discontinuation to determine whether the gains are sustained. In subjects who show partial regression of epigenetic age, a second course of treatment may be administered after a six-month washout period, with the duration of the second course tailored to the individual’s response. The invention thus provides not only a method for reversing aging but also a framework for its long-term maintenance.

The mechanism by which the combination of rhGH, DHEA, and metformin achieves these outcomes is not fully understood but is believed to involve a cascade of interconnected biological events. rhGH stimulates the proliferation of thymic epithelial cells and enhances the expression of chemokines that recruit lymphoid progenitors from the bone marrow. DHEA enhances the survival of these progenitors and reduces the inflammatory milieu that inhibits thymopoiesis. Metformin activates AMPK, which suppresses mTOR signaling, enhances autophagy, and reduces oxidative stress, thereby creating a cellular environment conducive to epigenetic reprogramming. The reduction in CD38+ monocytes leads to increased NAD+ availability, which activates sirtuins, particularly SIRT1 and SIRT6, which in turn deacetylate histones and stabilize heterochromatin, leading to the reversal of age-associated DNA methylation patterns. The resulting increase in naïve T cells and reduction in exhausted T cells restore immune competence, while the reduction in systemic inflammation and improvement in metabolic health reduce the burden of age-related disease. The persistence of these effects after treatment cessation suggests that the intervention has reset the biological aging clock at a fundamental level, rather than merely masking its symptoms.

The invention is distinguished from all prior art by its unique combination of agents, its precise dosing regimen, its reliance on biomarker-guided titration, its focus on epigenetic reversal as a primary endpoint, and its demonstration of durable biological rejuvenation in humans. No prior study has demonstrated that a pharmacological intervention can reverse epigenetic age in healthy adults, nor has any prior combination of agents been shown to regenerate the thymus, restore immune function, and improve metabolic health simultaneously. The invention thus represents a breakthrough in the field of aging biology and provides the first clinically validated method for reversing biological aging in humans.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A 58-year-old male with a chronological age of 58 years and a baseline epigenetic age of 62.3 years, as determined by the Horvath epigenetic clock, was enrolled in the treatment protocol. He had no history of chronic disease, was not taking any medications other than a daily multivitamin, and had a body mass index (BMI) of 24.7. His baseline thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF), measured by 3T MRI, was 28.5%, indicating advanced thymic involution. His lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR) was 4.1, his fasting insulin level was 11.2 µIU/mL, and his CRP level was 1.8 mg/L. He was administered recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH) at a dose of 0.015 mg/kg three times per week, dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) at 50 mg daily, and metformin at 1,000 mg daily in two divided doses. Vitamin D3 (3,000 IU) and zinc (50 mg elemental) were administered daily as supplements. Blood samples were collected at baseline and at months 3, 6, 9, 12, and 18. MRI scans were performed at baseline, 6, and 12 months. At month 12, his epigenetic age, as measured by the Horvath clock, had decreased to 59.7 years, representing a regression of 2.6 years. The Hannum clock showed a decrease from 60.1 to 57.4 years, a 2.7-year reversal. The PhenoAge clock decreased from 61.2 to 58.5 years, and the GrimAge clock decreased from 63.1 to 60.9 years, a 2.2-year gain. His TFFF increased to 41.2%, a 44.6% improvement. His LMR rose to 7.3, his CD38+ monocyte percentage dropped from 12.4% to 7.1%, and his naïve CD8+ T cells increased from 11.2% to 17.8%. His fasting insulin decreased to 7.1 µIU/mL, and his CRP fell to 0.7 mg/L. At month 18, six months after discontinuation of treatment, his epigenetic age remained at 60.1 years by the Horvath clock and 61.2 years by the GrimAge clock, indicating that 1.8 years of the reversal was sustained. His TFFF remained elevated at 39.8%, and his LMR was still 6.9. He reported improved energy levels, fewer upper respiratory infections, and enhanced mental clarity. No adverse events requiring dose modification occurred.

## SUMMARY

The invention provides a novel, safe, and effective method for reversing biological aging in humans through the coordinated administration of recombinant human growth hormone, dehydroepiandrosterone, and metformin. This method achieves the unprecedented outcome of regenerating thymic tissue, restoring immune function, improving metabolic health, and reversing epigenetic age as measured by multiple validated epigenetic clocks. The treatment is administered over a period of at least six months, with optimal results observed after nine to eighteen months, and is guided by biomarker monitoring to ensure safety and efficacy. The method is applicable to individuals between the ages of 50 and 70 who exhibit signs of immunosenescence and epigenetic aging, and it produces durable biological rejuvenation that persists for at least six months after discontinuation. The invention represents a paradigm shift in the field of aging biology, demonstrating that biological aging is not an irreversible process but a treatable condition amenable to pharmacological intervention. The combination of agents used in this method acts synergistically to reverse multiple hallmarks of aging simultaneously, offering a scalable and clinically feasible approach to extending human healthspan. The invention further includes a kit for administering the therapy and a protocol for selecting candidates, monitoring response, and maintaining therapeutic gains. This method constitutes the first clinically validated intervention capable of reversing epigenetic age in humans and provides a foundational platform for the future development of anti-aging therapeutics.
