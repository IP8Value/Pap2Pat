# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of computer vision and artificial intelligence, specifically to unsupervised video anomaly detection systems capable of operating in cross-domain environments without requiring access to training data from the target domain. The invention introduces a novel framework for detecting anomalous events in video sequences by leveraging relative normalcy learning through the synergistic integration of a generative future-frame prediction model, a normalcy classifier module, and an untrained convolutional neural network-based anomaly synthesis mechanism. This framework enables robust anomaly detection in previously unseen environments—such as surveillance cameras in new urban settings, industrial facilities, or public transit systems—without the need for retraining, fine-tuning, or any form of domain adaptation. The system is particularly suited for real-world deployment scenarios where access to labeled or even unlabeled video data from the target domain is restricted due to privacy regulations, intellectual property constraints, logistical limitations, or operational secrecy. By eliminating dependency on target-domain training data and avoiding reliance on pre-trained object detectors or domain-specific annotations, the invention provides a scalable, computationally efficient, and privacy-preserving solution for video anomaly detection that can be deployed “out-of-the-box” across diverse and dynamically changing environments. The invention further enhances generalization by incorporating task-irrelevant video datasets—originally collected for unrelated computer vision tasks such as action recognition or gesture classification—as sources for generating diverse pseudo-anomalies, thereby significantly broadening the spectrum of abnormal patterns the system can recognize without prior exposure to target-domain anomalies. The technical innovations described herein are applicable to security monitoring, smart city infrastructure, autonomous systems, industrial quality control, and healthcare surveillance, where adaptability, low computational overhead, and zero-shot generalization are critical performance requirements.

## BACKGROUND

Traditional approaches to video anomaly detection have long relied on supervised or weakly-supervised learning paradigms, which require extensive manual annotation of abnormal events within training datasets. Such methods are inherently limited in practical deployment due to the extreme rarity of anomalous events in real-world video streams, the high cost and time required to label such events, and the ethical and legal challenges associated with collecting and storing sensitive surveillance footage. As a result, unsupervised methods have emerged as the dominant paradigm, wherein models are trained exclusively on videos containing normal activities and are expected to identify deviations from the learned normal patterns as anomalies during inference. These methods typically employ autoencoders, generative adversarial networks, or memory-augmented architectures to model the statistical distribution of normal video frames and flag frames with high reconstruction error or low discriminator confidence as anomalous. However, these approaches suffer from a critical limitation: they are trained and evaluated under the assumption that the training and testing environments are drawn from the same domain. When deployed in a new environment—such as a different camera angle, lighting condition, background scene, or behavioral context—these models exhibit severe performance degradation due to domain shift, a phenomenon wherein the statistical properties of normal activities differ significantly between source and target domains.

Recent efforts to address this limitation have led to the development of cross-domain video anomaly detection (xVAD) frameworks, which aim to transfer knowledge from a source domain with abundant normal video data to a target domain with no labeled anomalies. These methods, however, still require access to at least some unlabeled or minimally labeled video data from the target domain during training, either to perform domain adaptation, fine-tuning, or to calibrate model parameters. This requirement renders them impractical for many real-world applications where target-domain data cannot be collected due to privacy laws, corporate confidentiality, or physical inaccessibility. Furthermore, several state-of-the-art xVAD systems depend on external object detection models—such as YOLOv3 or CenterNet—to localize and manipulate foreground objects in video frames to synthesize pseudo-anomalies. These methods introduce significant computational overhead, require additional training of object detectors, and are brittle to variations in object appearance, occlusion, or background complexity. The reliance on pre-trained detectors also ties the system’s performance to the quality and coverage of the detector’s training data, limiting its ability to generalize to novel or unseen anomaly types.

Moreover, existing approaches are constrained by the limited scale and diversity of publicly available video anomaly detection datasets, which typically contain fewer than one hundred training videos per domain and focus on narrow, repetitive scenarios such as pedestrian movement in hallways or shopping malls. This scarcity restricts the model’s capacity to learn rich, contextually varied representations of normalcy, leading to overfitting and poor generalization. The absence of a mechanism to leverage the vast reservoir of unlabeled, task-irrelevant video data—such as those from action recognition, video classification, or social media platforms—represents a missed opportunity to enhance the robustness and scalability of anomaly detection systems. No prior work has demonstrated the feasibility of training a video anomaly detection model entirely from task-irrelevant data without any access to source-domain VAD datasets, nor has any method successfully decoupled the learning of normalcy from the explicit modeling of normal patterns alone. Instead, all existing methods implicitly assume that normalcy can be fully captured by modeling the statistical regularities of normal frames, neglecting the fundamental insight that normalcy is a relative concept—defined not by what is present, but by what is absent or incongruent. This oversight has led to models that are overly sensitive to domain-specific textures, lighting, or motion patterns, and incapable of recognizing anomalies that deviate from the source domain’s notion of normality. There exists, therefore, a critical and unmet need for a video anomaly detection system that operates in a zero-shot cross-domain setting—requiring no target-domain training data, no pre-trained object detectors, no domain adaptation, and no manual labeling—while simultaneously achieving superior detection accuracy, computational efficiency, and generalization capability across diverse and unknown environments.

## SUMMARY

The present invention discloses a novel framework for zero-shot cross-domain video anomaly detection, referred to as zxVAD, which enables the detection of anomalous events in video sequences from a target domain without any access to training data from that domain and without prior knowledge of the nature or type of anomalies present. The system operates under the assumption that only normal video data from a source domain—or even entirely task-irrelevant video data from unrelated domains—is available for training, and that the target domain may contain entirely novel types of anomalies never encountered during training. The zxVAD framework comprises three core components: a generative future-frame prediction network, a normalcy classifier module, and an untrained convolutional neural network-based anomaly synthesis module. Unlike prior methods that learn to reconstruct or predict normal frames based solely on their statistical distribution, zxVAD learns to distinguish normal frames by contrasting them with synthetically generated pseudo-abnormal frames, thereby encoding normalcy as a relative property defined by deviation rather than by direct modeling. The anomaly synthesis module employs a randomly initialized, untrained convolutional neural network to localize foreground objects in input video frames—whether from the source domain or from task-irrelevant datasets—without any prior training or parameter optimization. These localized objects are then randomly pasted onto normal video frames to create diverse, contextually plausible pseudo-anomalies, effectively simulating a wide spectrum of potential abnormal events without requiring domain-specific knowledge or object detection models. The normalcy classifier is trained using four novel loss functions that jointly enforce the discrimination between normal and pseudo-abnormal frames, while simultaneously ensuring that the classifier’s decision is grounded in spatially coherent attention patterns that reflect the presence or absence of foreign objects. These losses include a normalcy loss that distinguishes between normal and abnormal labels, a relative normalcy loss that maximizes the differential probability of normality between normal and pseudo-abnormal frames, an attention affirmation loss that ensures the classifier attends to the entire scene for normal frames and to the inserted object for pseudo-abnormal frames, and a relative attention affirmation loss that enforces a margin-based separation between the attention maps of normal frames and their augmented versions versus those of pseudo-abnormal frames. The generative network, trained to predict future video frames, is regularized by the normalcy classifier’s feedback, enabling it to learn representations that are not only temporally coherent but also semantically aligned with the relative normalcy signal. The entire system is trained end-to-end using only source-domain or task-irrelevant data, and at inference time, it requires no adaptation, fine-tuning, or domain-specific calibration. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmark datasets in zero-shot cross-domain settings, outperforming existing methods in detection accuracy while reducing model size, computational cost, and energy consumption by significant margins. Furthermore, the system demonstrates the remarkable capability of achieving competitive anomaly detection performance even when trained exclusively on task-irrelevant video datasets, such as those used for action recognition or gesture classification, thereby proving that the concept of normalcy can be effectively learned from data that is semantically unrelated to the anomaly detection task. This breakthrough enables the deployment of robust, privacy-preserving, and scalable video surveillance systems in environments where access to target-domain training data is prohibited, impractical, or impossible.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### System Overview

The zxVAD system is a fully unsupervised, end-to-end trainable framework designed for zero-shot cross-domain video anomaly detection. It operates under the stringent constraint that no video data from the target domain—neither normal nor anomalous—is available during training, and that the system must generalize to entirely novel anomaly types not present in the training data. The architecture is composed of three interdependent modules: a generative future-frame prediction network, a normalcy classifier, and an untrained convolutional neural network-based anomaly synthesis module. These components are trained jointly in a single optimization loop, with the generator producing future frames from a sequence of past frames, the anomaly synthesis module generating pseudo-abnormal variants of those frames, and the normalcy classifier providing a relative normalcy signal that guides the generator to learn representations that are inherently sensitive to deviations from normalcy. The system does not rely on any pre-trained object detectors, semantic segmenters, or domain-specific annotations. Instead, it leverages the intrinsic ability of randomly initialized convolutional neural networks to localize semantically meaningful objects in video frames, even without parameter updates, by exploiting the structural contrast between textured foreground regions and relatively uniform background regions. This property allows the system to generate diverse and realistic pseudo-anomalies at zero additional training cost, making it both computationally efficient and highly scalable. The generator is implemented as a U-Net architecture with a memory module at its bottleneck, enabling it to retain and recall long-term patterns of normal activity across video sequences. The memory module is regularized through a sparsity constraint that ensures only the most relevant memory slots are activated for each input, thereby preventing overfitting and promoting generalization. The discriminator, a Patch-GAN variant, provides adversarial feedback to the generator to ensure that the predicted future frames are visually plausible and temporally consistent with the input sequence. However, unlike traditional GAN-based anomaly detection methods, the generator’s learning is not governed solely by adversarial and reconstruction losses. Instead, it is explicitly regularized by the normalcy classifier, which introduces a relative normalcy signal derived from the contrast between normal frames and their pseudo-abnormal counterparts. The normalcy classifier itself is a lightweight convolutional network that processes both the predicted future frame and the corresponding pseudo-abnormal frame, outputting logits that indicate the likelihood of each frame being normal. The classifier is trained using four complementary loss functions that collectively enforce a hierarchical understanding of normalcy: first, by distinguishing between normal and abnormal labels; second, by maximizing the relative difference in normalcy probability between normal and pseudo-abnormal frames; third, by ensuring that the classifier’s attention is spatially aligned with the semantic content of the frames; and fourth, by enforcing a margin-based separation between the attention patterns of normal frames and pseudo-abnormal frames. This multi-level regularization ensures that the generator does not merely learn to reconstruct typical patterns but learns to recognize what constitutes a deviation from normalcy in a contextually aware and semantically grounded manner. At inference time, the system receives a sequence of consecutive video frames from the target domain and predicts the next frame using the generator. The predicted frame is then compared to the actual next frame using a pixel-wise reconstruction error metric, such as the structural similarity index or peak signal-to-noise ratio. A high reconstruction error indicates a deviation from the learned normalcy model and is interpreted as an anomaly. Crucially, because the model has been trained to recognize normalcy through relative contrast rather than absolute reconstruction, it is capable of identifying anomalies that are visually and semantically dissimilar to any training example, including those drawn from entirely different domains. The system’s ability to operate without target-domain data, without object detectors, and without domain adaptation makes it uniquely suited for deployment in real-world surveillance scenarios where privacy, scalability, and adaptability are paramount. The entire system can be trained on commodity hardware using standard optimization procedures and requires no specialized infrastructure, making it accessible to a wide range of users—from municipal security agencies to private enterprises—without requiring access to proprietary datasets or expert-level machine learning knowledge. The framework’s modular design allows for easy substitution of components—for example, different backbone architectures for the generator or classifier—without compromising performance, ensuring long-term compatibility with evolving hardware and software ecosystems. Furthermore, the use of task-irrelevant datasets as training sources opens the door to a new paradigm in unsupervised learning, where the abundance of unlabeled video data from unrelated domains can be harnessed to solve tasks for which labeled data is scarce or unavailable. This innovation fundamentally redefines the boundaries of what is possible in zero-shot video anomaly detection and establishes a new standard for robust, generalizable, and privacy-preserving surveillance systems.
