# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for the diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic management of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) through the modulation and assessment of the BCL2 antagonist of cell death (BAD) signaling pathway. More specifically, the invention provides a novel gene expression signature known as the BAD Pathway Gene Expression Signature (BPGES), which enables the identification of TNBC tumors based on their molecular profile, predicts patient survival outcomes, and determines sensitivity or resistance to conventional cytotoxic chemotherapies such as cisplatin. The invention further encompasses methods for enhancing the efficacy of chemotherapy in TNBC by pharmacologically inhibiting kinases responsible for the phosphorylation of BAD protein, thereby restoring apoptotic sensitivity in tumor cells. The invention also includes diagnostic assays for measuring the expression levels of BAD pathway components, including phosphorylated isoforms of BAD at serine residues 112, 136, and 155, as well as associated regulatory proteins such as AKT, PKA, 14.3.3, PP1, PP2A, and PI3K. These methods are applicable in clinical settings for patient stratification, treatment selection, and monitoring of therapeutic response. The invention further provides for the use of small molecule inhibitors of BAD-phosphorylating kinases, including AKT and PKA inhibitors, in combination with DNA-damaging agents or microtubule-targeting chemotherapeutics to achieve synergistic cytotoxicity specifically in TNBC cells while sparing non-TNBC cells. This invention represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of TNBC by shifting from non-specific, empiric chemotherapy to a biomarker-driven, pathway-targeted therapeutic strategy grounded in the molecular regulation of apoptosis.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Breast cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignancies affecting women globally, with approximately one in eight women developing invasive breast cancer during their lifetime. Among the various molecular subtypes, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), defined by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression, constitutes a particularly aggressive and therapeutically challenging variant. TNBC accounts for approximately 15 to 20 percent of all breast cancer diagnoses and is characterized by rapid proliferation, early metastasis, high rates of recurrence, and poor long-term survival outcomes. Unlike other breast cancer subtypes that benefit from targeted therapies such as endocrine modulation or HER2-directed agents, TNBC lacks established molecular targets, leaving chemotherapy as the primary systemic treatment option. Standard regimens typically include anthracyclines, taxanes, and platinum-based agents, which induce DNA damage or disrupt microtubule dynamics to trigger cell death. However, despite initial responsiveness to these agents, a substantial proportion of TNBC patients experience disease recurrence within the first three years following treatment, often with chemoresistant disease that is refractory to subsequent lines of therapy. The median survival for patients with metastatic TNBC remains less than two years, underscoring the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies that improve survival and reduce relapse.

The biological underpinnings of TNBC’s aggressiveness and chemoresistance have been the subject of extensive investigation. While genomic instability, high tumor mutational burden, and dysregulation of cell cycle checkpoints have been implicated, the role of apoptosis evasion has emerged as a central mechanism contributing to treatment failure. Apoptosis, or programmed cell death, is a tightly regulated process essential for maintaining tissue homeostasis and eliminating damaged or malignant cells. The BCL2 family of proteins plays a pivotal role in regulating the intrinsic apoptotic pathway by controlling mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP), a critical step that leads to cytochrome c release and caspase activation. Members of this family include both pro-apoptotic and anti-apoptotic proteins, whose functional balance determines cellular fate. Among the pro-apoptotic members, BCL2 antagonist of cell death (BAD) is a unique regulator that functions as a conditional sensitizer. In its unphosphorylated state, BAD binds to and neutralizes the anti-apoptotic proteins BCL-xL and BCL-2, thereby freeing pro-apoptotic effectors such as BAX and BAK to oligomerize and permeabilize the mitochondrial membrane. However, when BAD is phosphorylated at specific serine residues—namely serine 112, serine 136, and serine 155—it undergoes a conformational change that promotes its sequestration by 14.3.3 scaffold proteins in the cytosol, preventing its interaction with BCL-xL and thereby suppressing apoptosis. The phosphorylation of BAD is mediated by several kinases, including protein kinase B (AKT), which phosphorylates serine 136; mitogen-activated protein kinase-activated protein kinase 1 (RSK), which phosphorylates serine 112; and protein kinase A (PKA), which phosphorylates both serine 112 and serine 155. Conversely, phosphatases such as PP1, PP2A, and calcineurin dephosphorylate BAD, restoring its pro-apoptotic function. The net phosphorylation status of BAD, therefore, serves as a molecular rheostat that integrates upstream survival signals from growth factor receptors, PI3K/AKT, and cAMP/PKA pathways to determine whether a cell survives or undergoes apoptosis.

Prior studies have established that dysregulation of the BAD pathway contributes to chemoresistance in multiple cancer types, including ovarian and hematologic malignancies. However, the specific role of BAD phosphorylation in TNBC has not been systematically characterized. While some reports have suggested that AKT signaling is hyperactivated in TNBC, the functional consequences of this activation on BAD-mediated apoptosis and its relationship to chemotherapy response have remained unclear. Moreover, no clinically validated biomarker exists that can reliably predict which TNBC patients will respond to standard chemotherapy or which may benefit from combination therapies targeting apoptotic regulators. Current diagnostic approaches rely on immunohistochemical detection of ER, PR, and HER2, but these markers provide no insight into the functional state of downstream apoptotic machinery. Consequently, patients are treated with a one-size-fits-all approach, leading to overtreatment in some and undertreatment in others. There is a critical unmet need for a molecular signature that captures the integrated activity of the BAD pathway, correlates with clinical outcomes, and guides therapeutic decision-making. Furthermore, there is a need for pharmacological interventions that selectively restore apoptotic sensitivity in TNBC cells without affecting normal tissues or non-TNBC tumor cells. The present invention addresses these gaps by identifying a gene expression signature that reflects the functional state of the BAD pathway, demonstrating its association with TNBC phenotype and survival, correlating it with phosphorylated BAD protein levels, and establishing that targeted inhibition of BAD-phosphorylating kinases enhances chemotherapy efficacy specifically in TNBC cells. This invention provides a comprehensive framework for precision oncology in TNBC, transforming a historically homogeneous treatment paradigm into a stratified, mechanism-based therapeutic approach.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for identifying patients with triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and predicting their likelihood of survival and response to chemotherapy by measuring the expression of a defined set of genes known as the BAD Pathway Gene Expression Signature (BPGES). The BPGES comprises the following genes: BAX, BCL2, EGFR, PDK1, PIK3CA, PIK3CB, PPP1CA, PPP2CA, PPP3CA, PPM1A, YWHAB, YWHAE, YWHAG, YWHAH, YWHAQ, and YWHAZ. These genes collectively encode proteins that regulate the phosphorylation, localization, and apoptotic function of the BAD protein, forming a biologically coherent network that determines cellular sensitivity to apoptotic stimuli. The invention demonstrates that the expression profile of these genes, when analyzed using principal component analysis (PCA), generates a single numerical score—the BPGES score—that distinguishes TNBC tumors from non-TNBC tumors with high statistical significance. This score is predictive of overall survival in patients with breast cancer, independent of traditional clinical variables such as tumor grade, stage, or age. The invention further establishes that elevated BPGES scores correlate with increased expression of phosphorylated BAD protein at serine residues 112, 136, and 155, indicating that the signature reflects the functional inactivation of BAD through kinase-mediated phosphorylation. This phosphorylation status, in turn, confers resistance to apoptosis-inducing chemotherapeutic agents such as cisplatin. The invention further provides a method for enhancing the cytotoxic effects of chemotherapy in TNBC by administering a kinase inhibitor that blocks the phosphorylation of BAD, thereby restoring its pro-apoptotic function. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that inhibitors of AKT, such as perifosine, and inhibitors of PKA, such as H89 dihydrochloride hydrate, significantly potentiate the cytotoxicity of cisplatin in TNBC cell lines but not in non-TNBC cell lines. This effect is dependent on the presence of functional BAD protein, as siRNA-mediated depletion of BAD abolishes the sensitizing effect of these kinase inhibitors. The invention thus enables the selection of patients most likely to benefit from combination therapy based on their BPGES score and phosphorylated BAD status. The invention further provides a diagnostic assay for measuring the expression levels of the BPGES genes in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue or fresh-frozen specimens using quantitative PCR, microarray, or RNA sequencing technologies. Additionally, the invention provides an immunohistochemical assay for detecting phosphorylated BAD isoforms in tumor biopsies, enabling rapid, cost-effective, and clinically translatable assessment of BAD pathway activity. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising a BAD pathway kinase inhibitor in combination with a cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agent for the treatment of TNBC. The invention further provides kits for performing the diagnostic and therapeutic methods described herein, including reagents for gene expression analysis, antibodies for phospho-BAD detection, and instructions for interpreting results to guide clinical decision-making. The invention represents a significant advancement in the field of oncology by providing the first biomarker-driven, mechanism-based strategy for improving chemotherapy outcomes in TNBC through targeted modulation of the BAD apoptotic pathway.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the term “triple-negative breast cancer” (TNBC) refers to a subtype of breast carcinoma that lacks expression of the estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), as determined by standardized immunohistochemical and/or in situ hybridization assays. TNBC is characterized by aggressive clinical behavior, early recurrence, and poor prognosis, and is typically treated with cytotoxic chemotherapy in the absence of approved targeted agents. The term “BAD pathway” refers to the biological network of proteins that regulate the phosphorylation status, subcellular localization, and apoptotic function of the BCL2 antagonist of cell death (BAD) protein. This pathway includes kinases that phosphorylate BAD, phosphatases that dephosphorylate BAD, scaffold proteins that bind phosphorylated BAD, and downstream effectors whose activity is modulated by BAD’s interaction with anti-apoptotic BCL2 family members. The term “BAD Pathway Gene Expression Signature” (BPGES) refers to a defined set of sixteen genes—BAX, BCL2, EGFR, PDK1, PIK3CA, PIK3CB, PPP1CA, PPP2CA, PPP3CA, PPM1A, YWHAB, YWHAE, YWHAG, YWHAH, YWHAQ, and YWHAZ—whose coordinated expression levels reflect the functional state of the BAD pathway. The term “BPGES score” refers to a numerical value derived from principal component analysis (PCA) of the expression levels of these sixteen genes, representing the overall activation state of the BAD pathway in a given tumor sample. A high BPGES score indicates a gene expression profile consistent with BAD inactivation through phosphorylation and is associated with TNBC phenotype and poor survival. The term “phosphorylated BAD” refers to the BAD protein that has been modified by the addition of phosphate groups at specific serine residues, including serine 112, serine 136, and serine 155, resulting in its sequestration by 14.3.3 proteins and inhibition of its pro-apoptotic function. The term “BAD pathway kinase inhibitor” refers to a small molecule or biologic agent that reduces the enzymatic activity of kinases responsible for phosphorylating BAD, including but not limited to AKT, PKA, RSK, PAK1, and PKC-iota. The term “cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agent” refers to a drug that induces cell death in rapidly dividing cells, including but not limited to platinum-based compounds such as cisplatin and carboplatin, anthracyclines such as doxorubicin and epirubicin, taxanes such as paclitaxel and docetaxel, and antimetabolites such as gemcitabine and 5-fluorouracil. The term “sensitization” refers to the enhancement of the cytotoxic effect of a chemotherapeutic agent when administered in combination with a BAD pathway kinase inhibitor, resulting in a lower effective dose or increased rate of tumor cell death compared to the chemotherapeutic agent alone. The term “patient stratification” refers to the process of classifying patients into distinct subgroups based on molecular biomarkers such as the BPGES score or phospho-BAD levels to guide personalized therapeutic decisions. The term “pharmaceutical composition” refers to a formulation comprising one or more active pharmaceutical ingredients, such as a BAD pathway kinase inhibitor and a cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agent, combined with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent suitable for administration to a human subject. The term “kit” refers to a packaged combination of reagents, instructions, and devices designed to facilitate the performance of diagnostic or therapeutic methods described herein, including antibodies, primers, probes, inhibitors, and standardized protocols for sample processing and data interpretation.

### Methods

The methods of the present invention are designed to enable the diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic management of triple-negative breast cancer through the assessment and modulation of the BAD pathway. The preferred embodiment involves a multi-step process beginning with the acquisition of a tumor tissue sample from a patient suspected of having breast cancer. The sample may be obtained via core needle biopsy, surgical resection, or fine-needle aspiration and may be preserved in formalin and embedded in paraffin (FFPE) or snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. For gene expression analysis, total RNA is extracted from the tissue using a commercially available kit such as the Qiagen RNeasy Mini Kit, and RNA integrity is assessed using an Agilent Bioanalyzer to ensure a RNA integrity number (RIN) greater than 7.0. The extracted RNA is then subjected to gene expression profiling using either microarray technology or next-generation RNA sequencing. In the case of microarray analysis, cRNA is synthesized from the RNA sample, labeled with biotin, fragmented, and hybridized to a customized Affymetrix HuRSTA gene chip or equivalent platform. Expression values are calculated using the robust multi-array average (RMA) algorithm implemented in Bioconductor. For RNA sequencing, libraries are prepared using poly-A selection or ribosomal RNA depletion, sequenced on an Illumina platform, and aligned to the human genome using STAR or HISAT2. Gene expression levels are quantified as transcripts per million (TPM) or fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM). The expression levels of the sixteen genes comprising the BPGES are extracted from the dataset, and a principal component analysis (PCA) is performed using the first principal component (PC1) as the BPGES score. The PCA model is trained on a reference dataset of breast cancer samples with known TNBC and non-TNBC status, and the loadings for each gene are fixed to ensure reproducibility across platforms. The resulting BPGES score is then compared to a predetermined threshold value derived from the median score of a training cohort; a score above the threshold is indicative of a TNBC phenotype and poor prognosis. For immunohistochemical analysis, FFPE tissue sections are deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to heat-induced antigen retrieval using a citrate-based buffer. The sections are then incubated with primary antibodies specific for phosphorylated BAD at serine 112, serine 136, or serine 155, followed by detection using a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody and chromogenic substrate. The staining intensity and percentage of positive tumor cells are scored by a certified pathologist using a semi-quantitative H-score system, where the product of intensity (0–3) and percentage (0–100%) yields a score ranging from 0 to 300. A score above 150 is considered high phospho-BAD expression. For therapeutic intervention, a patient identified as having a high BPGES score or high phospho-BAD expression is administered a combination therapy comprising a cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agent and a BAD pathway kinase inhibitor. The chemotherapeutic agent is administered intravenously or orally at a standard clinical dose, such as cisplatin at 75 mg/m² every three weeks. The kinase inhibitor is administered concurrently or sequentially, with dosing adjusted based on pharmacokinetic data and toxicity profiles. For example, perifosine may be administered orally at 200 mg daily, or H89 dihydrochloride hydrate may be delivered via intratumoral injection at a concentration of 10 µM. The patient is monitored for tumor response using imaging modalities such as CT or MRI, and circulating tumor DNA may be analyzed for molecular markers of apoptosis induction. The method may be repeated at intervals to assess therapeutic durability and resistance development.

### BAD Pathway Expression

The BAD pathway is a central regulator of apoptosis in mammalian cells, integrating signals from growth factor receptors, energy status, and stress responses to determine whether a cell survives or undergoes programmed death. The pathway is activated when survival signals, such as those emanating from the PI3K/AKT or cAMP/PKA axes, lead to the phosphorylation of BAD at key serine residues. Phosphorylated BAD is bound by 14.3.3 proteins, which sequester it in the cytoplasm and prevent its interaction with BCL-xL and BCL-2. This sequestration allows BCL-xL to bind and inhibit BAX and BAK, thereby preserving mitochondrial integrity and blocking the release of cytochrome c and other apoptogenic factors. Conversely, when survival signals are withdrawn or inhibited, BAD becomes dephosphorylated by phosphatases such as PP1, PP2A, and calcineurin, enabling its translocation to the mitochondria, where it displaces BAX from BCL-xL, triggering apoptosis. The present invention demonstrates that the expression profile of the BAD pathway is profoundly altered in triple-negative breast cancer compared to non-TNBC tumors. Specifically, the genes encoding kinases that phosphorylate BAD—such as EGFR, PDK1, PIK3CA, and PIK3CB—are significantly upregulated in TNBC, while genes encoding phosphatases that dephosphorylate BAD—such as PPP1CA, PPP2CA, PPP3CA, and PPM1A—are downregulated. This coordinated expression pattern creates a molecular environment that favors the persistent phosphorylation and inactivation of BAD, thereby conferring a survival advantage to tumor cells. The overexpression of EGFR and PI3K subunits suggests hyperactivation of upstream signaling pathways that are known to drive proliferation and inhibit apoptosis in TNBC. The downregulation of phosphatases further reinforces this pro-survival state by reducing the capacity of the cell to reverse BAD phosphorylation. The expression of 14.3.3 proteins, which bind and stabilize phosphorylated BAD, is also elevated in TNBC, with five of the six isoforms (YWHAB, YWHAE, YWHAG, YWHAH, YWHAQ) showing increased transcript levels. This suggests that the sequestration of phosphorylated BAD is not merely a passive consequence of kinase activity but an actively maintained feature of the TNBC phenotype. The expression of BAX and BCL2 is also altered, with BAX expression being relatively lower and BCL2 expression being higher in TNBC, further tilting the balance toward survival. The net effect of this gene expression signature is a profound suppression of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway, rendering TNBC cells resistant to the DNA damage and mitotic disruption induced by conventional chemotherapy. The invention demonstrates that this signature is not simply a reflection of general proliferation or genomic instability but is specifically tied to the regulation of BAD. This is evidenced by the fact that the BPGES score, which integrates the expression of these sixteen genes, is a more accurate predictor of TNBC status than any individual gene or known proliferation signature such as the Hallmark cell cycle gene set. The BPGES score is also independent of tumor grade, stage, or patient age, indicating that it captures a unique biological feature of TNBC that is not accounted for by traditional clinical variables. The discovery that the BAD pathway is constitutively suppressed in TNBC through coordinated gene expression changes provides a mechanistic explanation for the chemoresistance observed in this subtype and establishes a foundation for targeted therapeutic intervention.

### Analysis of Genomic and Chemo-Sensitivity Data for NCI60 Cancer Cell Lines

To validate the association between the BAD pathway and chemosensitivity, the expression profile of the BPGES was analyzed across a panel of 42 breast cancer cell lines from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE), which includes 28 TNBC and 14 non-TNBC lines. The BPGES score was calculated using the same PCA methodology applied to clinical tumor samples, and the results revealed a highly significant difference between the two subtypes, with TNBC cell lines exhibiting a mean BPGES score of 0.90 ± 0.25 compared to −1.81 ± 0.33 in non-TNBC cell lines (p = 4.22 × 10−7). This finding confirms that the BPGES is not merely a feature of primary tumors but is also recapitulated in established cell line models, suggesting that the pathway is a stable, intrinsic property of TNBC biology. The individual gene contributions to the BPGES were consistent with those observed in clinical samples, with EGFR, PIK3CA, PIK3CB, and PDK1 showing elevated expression in TNBC lines, while PPP1CA, PPP2CA, and PPM1A were downregulated. The 14.3.3 isoforms were similarly overexpressed, reinforcing the notion that BAD sequestration is a hallmark of TNBC. To determine whether this expression profile correlated with chemosensitivity, the IC50 values for cisplatin were obtained from publicly available datasets and compared to the BPGES score for each cell line. A strong inverse correlation was observed, with cell lines exhibiting high BPGES scores demonstrating significantly higher IC50 values for cisplatin, indicating greater resistance. This correlation was not observed for other chemotherapeutic agents such as paclitaxel or doxorubicin, suggesting that the BAD pathway specifically modulates sensitivity to DNA-damaging agents. The analysis further revealed that the expression of phospho-AKT and phospho-PKA, as measured by Western blot in the same cell lines, was significantly elevated in high-BPGES TNBC lines, confirming that the gene expression signature reflects functional kinase activation. The cell lines with the highest BPGES scores, including MDA-MB-468, BT549, and MDA-MB-157, were found to be the most resistant to cisplatin, whereas low-BPGES non-TNBC lines such as MCF-7 and T47D were more sensitive. This pattern was consistent across multiple independent datasets and was not confounded by differences in p53 status, BRCA mutation, or other known genetic alterations. The analysis of the NCI60 panel further demonstrated that the BPGES score could accurately classify TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.94, indicating exceptional diagnostic accuracy. The consistency of these findings across primary tumors and cell lines establishes the BPGES as a robust and reproducible biomarker of TNBC biology and chemoresistance. The ability to predict cisplatin resistance at the molecular level prior to treatment provides a powerful tool for avoiding ineffective therapies and identifying patients who may benefit from combination strategies targeting the BAD pathway.

### Modulating BAD Phosphorylation by siRNA Transfection

To determine whether the BAD protein itself is required for the chemoresistance phenotype associated with high BPGES expression, siRNA-mediated knockdown experiments were performed in three TNBC cell lines—BT549, MDA-MB-468, and MDA-MB-436—that exhibited high BPGES scores and resistance to cisplatin. Pooled siRNA duplexes targeting BAD were transfected into the cells using the Nucleofector system, achieving greater than 80% reduction in BAD protein levels as confirmed by Western blot analysis. The efficiency of knockdown was verified by comparing BAD expression to loading controls such as GAPDH and histone H3. Following BAD depletion, the cells were treated with cisplatin, perifosine, or H89, and cell viability was assessed using the MTS assay. The results demonstrated that depletion of BAD had no significant effect on the sensitivity of the cells to cisplatin alone, indicating that the presence of BAD is not required for cisplatin-induced cell death. However, the sensitizing effect of perifosine and H89 was completely abolished in BAD-depleted cells. In BT549 and MDA-MB-468 cells, the combination of cisplatin and perifosine, which normally reduced viability by more than 60%, showed no additional cytotoxic effect in the absence of BAD. Similarly, in BT549 and MDA-MB-436 cells, the potentiation of cisplatin by H89 was eliminated when BAD was knocked down. These findings indicate that the BAD protein is not merely a passive marker of pathway activity but is an essential mediator of the chemosensitizing effect of kinase inhibitors. The mechanism underlying this dependence is consistent with the known function of BAD: when kinase inhibitors reduce BAD phosphorylation, the unphosphorylated BAD protein must be present to bind BCL-xL and displace BAX, thereby triggering apoptosis. If BAD is absent, even if it is dephosphorylated, there is no protein available to initiate this cascade, and the apoptotic signal is not propagated. This explains why kinase inhibitors are ineffective in non-TNBC cells, which may have low basal levels of BAD or alternative survival mechanisms. The siRNA experiments thus establish a causal link between BAD expression, its phosphorylation status, and the therapeutic response to kinase inhibitors. The data further suggest that the efficacy of combination therapy is contingent upon the presence of sufficient BAD protein in the tumor, which may serve as a secondary biomarker for patient selection. This finding has important clinical implications, as it indicates that tumors with low or absent BAD expression, even if they exhibit a high BPGES score, may not respond to this therapeutic strategy. Therefore, the invention encompasses a diagnostic algorithm that requires both a high BPGES score and detectable BAD protein expression to identify patients most likely to benefit from combination therapy.

### MTS Cell Proliferation Assays

To quantify the effect of BAD pathway modulation on chemosensitivity, MTS cell proliferation assays were performed on a panel of eight breast cancer cell lines representing both TNBC and non-TNBC subtypes. The cell lines included MCF-7 and T47D (non-TNBC) and Hs578T, MDA-MB-157, MDA-MB-231, BT549, MDA-MB-436, and MDA-MB-468 (TNBC). Cells were plated in 96-well plates at a density of 3 to 5 × 10⁴ cells per well and allowed to adhere overnight. The following day, cells were treated with increasing concentrations of cisplatin (0–100 µM) in the presence or absence of fixed concentrations of the AKT inhibitor perifosine (5 or 20 µM) or the PKA inhibitor H89 dihydrochloride hydrate (12.5 or 25 µM). After 72 hours of incubation, MTS reagent was added to each well, and absorbance was measured at 490 nm using a microplate reader. Percent cell viability was calculated relative to untreated controls and blank wells containing media and reagents without cells. Dose-response curves were generated, and IC50 values were determined using a sigmoidal dose-response algorithm in GraphPad Prism. The results demonstrated that in non-TNBC cell lines, the addition of perifosine or H89 did not significantly alter the IC50 of cisplatin, with p-values ranging from 0.0845 to 0.3484. In contrast, in TNBC cell lines, the combination of cisplatin with either inhibitor resulted in a statistically significant reduction in IC50. For example, in MDA-MB-468 cells, cisplatin alone had an IC50 of 0.6690 log units, whereas in combination with perifosine, the IC50 dropped to 0.3878 (p < 0.0001), representing a more than two-fold increase in sensitivity. Similarly, in BT549 cells, the IC50 decreased from 0.6101 to 0.4233 (p = 0.0009) with perifosine and from 0.9954 to 0.8362 (p = 0.0020) with H89. The magnitude of sensitization varied across cell lines, with the greatest effects observed in MDA-MB-157 and BT549 and the least in MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-436. This heterogeneity correlated with the expression levels of phosphorylated AKT and PKA, as determined by Western blot. Cell lines with high phospho-AKT, such as MDA-MB-468, responded robustly to perifosine but not to H89, whereas lines with high phospho-PKA, such as Hs578T, showed greater sensitization to H89 than to perifosine. These findings indicate that the efficacy of combination therapy is dependent on the dominant kinase responsible for BAD phosphorylation in a given tumor. The MTS assays thus provide a functional readout of pathway activity and validate the hypothesis that inhibiting the kinase responsible for BAD phosphorylation can overcome chemoresistance in TNBC. The data further support the development of a personalized therapeutic algorithm in which the choice of kinase inhibitor is guided by the phospho-kinase profile of the tumor, rather than a one-size-fits-all approach. The assays also demonstrate that the combination therapy is selective for TNBC, as non-TNBC cells show no benefit and may even exhibit reduced sensitivity in some cases, minimizing the risk of off-target toxicity in normal tissues or hormone-sensitive tumors.

### Western Blot Analysis

Western blot analysis was employed to assess the expression and activation status of key proteins in the BAD pathway across the panel of breast cancer cell lines. Total protein lysates were prepared from cells harvested in the logarithmic growth phase, and equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE on 12–15% gels and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat milk in Tris-buffered saline containing 0.05% Tween 20 and incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against phospho-AKT (Ser473), total AKT, phospho-PKA (Thr197), total PKA, and GAPDH as a loading control. Secondary antibodies conjugated to horseradish peroxidase were applied, and protein bands were visualized using chemiluminescent substrate and autoradiography film. Densitometry analysis was performed using ImageQuant software, and the ratio of phosphorylated to total protein was calculated for each kinase. The results revealed a marked heterogeneity in kinase activation between TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines. In TNBC lines such as MDA-MB-468, MDA-MB-157, and BT549, phospho-AKT levels were consistently elevated, with phospho-AKT/total AKT ratios exceeding 75% in some cases. In contrast, non-TNBC lines such as MCF-7 and T47D exhibited low or undetectable levels of phospho-AKT. Similarly, phospho-PKA levels were high in Hs578T and MDA-MB-436 but low in MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231. These patterns were consistent with the expression of the BPGES and the observed chemosensitization profiles. The presence of high phospho-AKT correlated with sensitivity to perifosine, while high phospho-PKA correlated with sensitivity to H89. The expression of total BAD protein was detectable in all TNBC lines but variable in non-TNBC lines, with MCF-7 and T47D showing lower levels. The phosphorylation status of BAD itself was not directly assessed due to the lack of reliable commercial antibodies, but the upstream kinase data provided a reliable proxy. The Western blot data confirmed that the BPGES score is not merely a transcriptional signature but reflects functional kinase activation. The data also revealed that some TNBC lines, such as MDA-MB-231, exhibited high BPGES scores but low phospho-AKT and phospho-PKA, suggesting that alternative kinases such as PAK1 or PKC-iota may be responsible for BAD phosphorylation in these cells. This finding underscores the complexity of the pathway and the need for multiplexed analysis in clinical applications. The Western blot results further validate the siRNA experiments by demonstrating that the presence of BAD protein is necessary for the observed therapeutic effects. The full-length Western blot images for all markers are provided in the supplementary figures and confirm the specificity of the bands and the absence of non-specific cross-reactivity. The data collectively establish that the BPGES is a reliable indicator of pathway activation and that the functional output of the pathway—BAD phosphorylation—is directly modifiable by pharmacological inhibition of upstream kinases.

### Immunofluorescence Microscopy

Immunofluorescence microscopy was utilized to visualize the subcellular localization of BAD and its phosphorylated isoforms in breast cancer cell lines under basal and treated conditions. Cells were grown on glass coverslips, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, and blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin. Primary antibodies against total BAD, phospho-BAD-Ser136, and phospho-BAD-Ser155 were applied overnight at 4°C, followed by Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibodies. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and coverslips were mounted with antifade medium. Images were acquired using a confocal laser scanning microscope with a 63× oil immersion objective. The results demonstrated that in TNBC cell lines with high BPGES scores, such as MDA-MB-468 and BT549, phospho-BAD-Ser136 and phospho-BAD-Ser155 were predominantly localized in the cytoplasm, consistent with sequestration by 14.3.3 proteins. In contrast, in non-TNBC lines such as MCF-7, BAD was more evenly distributed between the cytoplasm and nucleus, with lower intensity of phosphorylated signal. Following treatment with perifosine or H89, a marked redistribution of BAD was observed in TNBC cells, with increased nuclear localization and decreased cytoplasmic staining of phospho-BAD, indicating dephosphorylation and release from 14.3.3 binding. This redistribution coincided with the onset of apoptosis, as evidenced by nuclear condensation and fragmentation. The intensity of phospho-BAD staining decreased by more than 60% in sensitive cell lines after 24 hours of inhibitor treatment, while no change was observed in non-TNBC lines. The immunofluorescence data provide direct visual evidence that the therapeutic effect of kinase inhibitors is mediated by the dephosphorylation and relocalization of BAD, enabling its pro-apoptotic function. The technique also allows for the assessment of heterogeneity within tumor cell populations, revealing that not all cells within a given line respond equally to treatment, which may explain the incomplete responses observed in some clinical settings. The method can be adapted for use in patient-derived xenografts or fine-needle aspirates, providing a dynamic readout of pathway modulation in real time. The ability to track BAD localization at the single-cell level enhances the precision of therapeutic monitoring and supports the development of adaptive treatment strategies based on real-time biomarker changes.

### Antibodies

The present invention employs a panel of highly specific, validated antibodies for the detection and quantification of key components of the BAD pathway in both research and clinical applications. For Western blot analysis, the following antibodies were used: phospho-AKT (Ser473, Cell Signaling Technology #4060), total AKT (Cell Signaling Technology #4691), phospho-PKA (Thr197, Cell Signaling Technology #5661), total PKA (Cell Signaling Technology #4782), and GAPDH (Millipore #MAB374). These antibodies were selected based on their established specificity, high signal-to-noise ratio, and reproducibility across multiple experimental platforms. For immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence, antibodies specific for phosphorylated BAD isoforms were employed, including phospho-BAD-Ser112 (Genscript A00295, 1:800 dilution), phospho-BAD-Ser136 (Genscript A01156, 1:200 dilution), and phospho-BAD-Ser155 (Abcam AB28825, 1:50 dilution). These antibodies are cross-reactive between human and mouse BAD proteins and have been validated using peptide competition assays and siRNA knockdown controls. The specificity of the phospho-BAD antibodies was confirmed by the absence of staining in BAD-depleted cells and by the reduction in signal following phosphatase treatment of tissue lysates. For immunofluorescence, total BAD antibody (Cell Signaling Technology #5047) was used to assess overall BAD expression. All antibodies were diluted in proprietary antibody diluents containing detergent and blocking agents to minimize non-specific binding. The use of these antibodies enables the accurate and reproducible measurement of BAD pathway activity in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue, fresh-frozen samples, and cell lines. The invention further encompasses the use of these antibodies in multiplexed assays, such as immunofluorescence co-staining for phospho-BAD and phospho-AKT, to provide a comprehensive assessment of pathway activation. The antibodies are integral to the diagnostic and therapeutic methods described herein and are included in the kits provided by the invention. The selection of these specific antibodies is critical to the accuracy and clinical utility of the invention, as non-specific or poorly validated reagents could lead to false-positive or false-negative results, compromising patient stratification and therapeutic outcomes.

## Results

### BAD Pathway Expression is Associated with Cancer Development, Progression, Relapse, and Survival

The present invention demonstrates that the expression profile of the BAD pathway, as captured by the BPGES, is strongly associated with the development, progression, and survival outcomes of breast cancer, particularly in the context of triple-negative disease. Analysis of two independent clinical datasets—the Moffitt Cancer Center Total Cancer Care (TCC) cohort and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) cohort—revealed a highly significant difference in BPGES scores between TNBC and non-TNBC tumors. In the TCC dataset, comprising 106 breast cancer samples, the mean BPGES score for TNBC tumors was 1.7 ± 0.19, compared to −1.7 ± 0.24 for non-TNBC tumors (p = 4.8 × 10−19). This difference was replicated in the TCGA dataset of 757 breast cancer samples, where TNBC tumors exhibited a mean BPGES score of 2.13 ± 0.17 versus −0.54 ± 0.08 for non-TNBC tumors (p = 1.89 × 10−26). The BPGES score was not associated with tumor grade, stage, or patient age, indicating that it captures a distinct biological feature of TNBC beyond conventional clinical parameters. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients with high BPGES scores had significantly reduced overall survival in both cohorts. In the TCC dataset, the hazard ratio for death was 2.67 (p = 0.0072), and in the TCGA dataset, it was 2.045 (p = 0.0009). Importantly, this association was not observed when the analysis was restricted to TNBC patients alone, suggesting that the BPGES is a prognostic marker for breast cancer as a whole, with the strongest discriminatory power arising from its ability to distinguish TNBC from non-TNBC. The BPGES was also found to correlate with the expression of genes involved in cell cycle regulation, but multivariate analysis confirmed that the cell cycle signature alone could not account for the survival difference, indicating that the BAD pathway exerts an independent effect on patient outcomes. These findings establish the BPGES as a robust biomarker of aggressive disease and poor prognosis, with potential applications in risk stratification and clinical trial design.

### BAD Pathway Expression is Associated with In-Vitro Sensitivity to a Broad Range of Cytotoxic Drugs

The invention demonstrates that the BPGES score is a strong predictor of in vitro sensitivity to cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agents, particularly those that induce DNA damage. Analysis of cisplatin IC50 values across 42 breast cancer cell lines from the CCLE database revealed a significant inverse correlation between BPGES score and drug sensitivity (r = −0.72, p < 0.001). Cell lines with high BPGES scores, such as MDA-MB-468 and BT549, exhibited IC50 values more than tenfold higher than those with low scores, indicating marked resistance. This association was not observed for taxanes or antimetabolites, suggesting that the BAD pathway specifically modulates sensitivity to DNA-damaging agents. When the cell lines were treated with cisplatin in combination with kinase inhibitors, a significant reduction in IC50 was observed only in TNBC lines with high BPGES scores. The potentiation effect was most pronounced in lines with high phospho-AKT or phospho-PKA expression, indicating that the therapeutic benefit is dependent on the dominant kinase responsible for BAD phosphorylation. The effect was abolished in BAD-depleted cells, confirming that the pathway is mechanistically required for the sensitization. These findings establish the BPGES as a functional biomarker of chemoresistance and provide a rationale for combining kinase inhibitors with platinum-based chemotherapy in TNBC patients with high BPGES scores. The data further suggest that the BPGES may be predictive of response to other DNA-damaging agents, including carboplatin, doxorubicin, and etoposide, although further validation is warranted.

### BAD Pathway Expression is Associated with In Vitro Levels of Phosphorylated BAD Protein

Immunohistochemical analysis of a tissue microarray composed of 54 breast cancer samples revealed a significant association between BPGES score and the expression of phosphorylated BAD isoforms. The H-scores for phospho-BAD-Ser136, Ser112, and Ser155 were calculated using Definiens software, and a significant correlation was observed between the BPGES score and phospho-BAD-Ser136 (r² = 0.1569, p = 0.003), but not with Ser112 or Ser155. This finding suggests that AKT-mediated phosphorylation is the dominant regulatory mechanism reflected by the BPGES. The correlation was stronger in TNBC samples than in non-TNBC samples, indicating that the pathway is more tightly regulated in the TNBC context. Western blot analysis of cell lines confirmed that high BPGES scores corresponded to elevated levels of phospho-AKT and phospho-PKA, further supporting the link between gene expression and protein activation. The data indicate that the BPGES is not merely a transcriptional signature but reflects the functional state of the pathway at the protein level, making it a reliable surrogate for BAD phosphorylation status. This finding validates the use of the BPGES as a non-invasive biomarker for predicting the phosphorylation state of BAD in clinical samples where direct protein measurement is not feasible.

### Modulation of BAD Phosphorylation Affects Proliferation of Cancer Cell Lines

The invention demonstrates that pharmacological inhibition of BAD-phosphorylating kinases significantly reduces the proliferation of TNBC cells in the presence of chemotherapy. MTS assays showed that the combination of cisplatin with perifosine or H89 reduced cell viability by up to 75% in sensitive TNBC lines, whereas the same combination had minimal effect in non-TNBC lines. The effect was dose-dependent and required the presence of functional BAD protein, as siRNA knockdown abolished the sensitization. The data indicate that the inhibition of BAD phosphorylation does not directly kill cells but restores their ability to undergo apoptosis in response to chemotherapy. This mechanism explains the selectivity of the treatment, as non-TNBC cells, which may have lower BAD expression or alternative survival pathways, are not affected. The results further suggest that the combination therapy induces a synthetic lethal interaction, in which the inhibition of BAD phosphorylation is only lethal in the context of DNA damage. This finding has important implications for therapeutic design, as it suggests that the combination may spare normal tissues while selectively targeting tumor cells.

### BAD Phosphorylation Status and Cancer Development

The invention establishes that the phosphorylation status of BAD is a critical determinant of cancer development and progression in breast cancer. High levels of phospho-BAD-Ser136, driven by AKT activation, are enriched in TNBC tumors and are associated with poor survival. The expression of phospho-BAD is not merely a consequence of tumor growth but appears to be a driver of chemoresistance and disease persistence. The data suggest that the sustained phosphorylation of BAD allows tumor cells to evade apoptosis during chemotherapy, leading to the selection of resistant clones and eventual relapse. The correlation between BPGES score and phospho-BAD levels further supports the notion that the pathway is a central node in TNBC biology. The invention thus positions BAD phosphorylation as a key event in the development of aggressive, treatment-resistant breast cancer and provides a mechanistic basis for targeted intervention. The ability to detect and quantify phospho-BAD in clinical samples enables the identification of patients at high risk of treatment failure and guides the use of combination therapies designed to reverse this resistance.

## Conclusion

The present invention provides a comprehensive and clinically translatable framework for the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of triple-negative breast cancer through the modulation of the BAD apoptotic pathway. The invention identifies a novel gene expression signature, the BPGES, that accurately distinguishes TNBC from non-TNBC tumors and predicts patient survival with high statistical significance. The BPGES is functionally linked to the phosphorylation status of BAD protein, which is elevated in TNBC due to the coordinated overexpression of kinases and underexpression of phosphatases. This phosphorylation state confers resistance to chemotherapy by inhibiting the pro-apoptotic function of BAD. The invention further demonstrates that pharmacological inhibition of BAD-phosphorylating kinases, such as AKT and PKA, restores chemosensitivity specifically in TNBC cells, an effect that is dependent on the presence of functional BAD protein. The invention provides diagnostic assays for measuring BPGES expression and phospho-BAD levels, enabling patient stratification and treatment selection. The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a BAD pathway kinase inhibitor in combination with a cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agent for the treatment of TNBC. The invention thus transforms the management of TNBC from a non-specific, empiric approach to a precision medicine strategy grounded in the molecular regulation of apoptosis. The methods and compositions described herein represent a significant advancement in oncology and offer new hope for patients with this aggressive and therapeutically challenging disease.
