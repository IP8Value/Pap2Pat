# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel compositions and methods for the delivery of marine-derived omega-3 phospholipids with enhanced bioavailability, tissue-specific distribution, and therapeutic efficacy, particularly for the modulation of metabolic, neurological, and inflammatory pathways in mammals. More specifically, the invention encompasses purified and standardized marine phospholipid fractions enriched in eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), wherein the phospholipids are predominantly bound in phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine, and lysophosphatidylcholine molecular species, and wherein the fatty acid composition is characterized by a high molar ratio of n-3 to n-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids, thereby conferring superior biological activity compared to conventional triglyceride-bound omega-3 formulations. The invention further includes pharmaceutical, nutraceutical, and functional food formulations incorporating these phospholipid complexes for the prevention and treatment of conditions associated with dyslipidemia, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, cognitive decline, neuroinflammation, insulin resistance, and metabolic syndrome. The compositions are uniquely structured to exploit the natural physicochemical properties of marine phospholipids, including their amphiphilic nature, micelle-forming capacity, and preferential integration into high-density lipoprotein particles and cellular membranes, resulting in enhanced absorption, targeted tissue delivery, and prolonged systemic retention of omega-3 fatty acids. The invention also encompasses methods for the isolation, purification, and stabilization of these phospholipid fractions from marine sources such as fish roe, krill, and specific oily fish tissues, using non-denaturing, solvent-free, and enzymatic processing techniques that preserve molecular integrity and prevent oxidative degradation. The resulting products are suitable for oral, parenteral, and transdermal administration and demonstrate a significantly improved pharmacokinetic profile over traditional omega-3 triglyceride or ethyl ester formulations.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The human body relies on a delicate balance of lipid species to maintain cellular homeostasis, regulate inflammatory responses, and support neurological and cardiovascular function. Among the most biologically significant lipids are the long-chain polyunsaturated omega-3 fatty acids, particularly eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), which cannot be synthesized in sufficient quantities by humans and must therefore be obtained through dietary intake. Historically, the primary sources of these fatty acids have been fish oils, where EPA and DHA are predominantly esterified in triglyceride form. While triglyceride-bound omega-3s have been widely studied and marketed for their cardiovascular and anti-inflammatory benefits, accumulating evidence suggests that their bioavailability, tissue distribution, and metabolic fate are constrained by their hydrophobic nature and dependence on bile salt micellization for intestinal absorption. Moreover, once absorbed, triglyceride-bound omega-3s are primarily incorporated into the core of chylomicrons, leading to preferential delivery to the liver and adipose tissue, while limiting their availability to critical organs such as the brain, retina, and heart, where DHA is essential for structural and functional integrity.

In contrast, phospholipids—particularly those derived from marine organisms—offer a structurally distinct delivery system for omega-3 fatty acids. Unlike triglycerides, which consist of three fatty acid chains esterified to a glycerol backbone, phospholipids contain two fatty acids and a polar phosphate-containing headgroup, rendering them amphiphilic and capable of spontaneously forming micelles, bilayers, and liposomes in aqueous environments. This structural feature allows phospholipid-bound omega-3s to bypass certain limitations of triglyceride absorption, including reduced dependence on bile salts and direct integration into the outer monolayer of chylomicrons and high-density lipoprotein particles. Studies have demonstrated that when omega-3 fatty acids are presented in phospholipid form, they are more efficiently transported across the intestinal epithelium, more readily incorporated into tissue phospholipid pools, and exhibit greater retention in neural and vascular tissues. For instance, radiolabeled DHA administered as phosphatidylcholine has been shown to achieve more than twice the concentration in the brain, liver, and retina compared to identical doses delivered as triglycerides. Similarly, in animal models of obesity and metabolic dysfunction, supplementation with phospholipid-bound omega-3s from krill oil has led to significantly greater reductions in hepatic steatosis, plasma triglycerides, and systemic inflammation than equivalent doses of fish oil.

Despite these compelling advantages, the practical application of marine phospholipids in clinical and commercial settings has been hindered by several technical and scientific challenges. First, the natural sources of marine phospholipids—such as fish roe, krill, and certain oily fish tissues—are highly variable in composition, influenced by species, season, diet, and environmental factors. This variability makes it difficult to produce standardized, reproducible formulations with consistent potency and efficacy. Second, traditional extraction methods often involve harsh solvents, high temperatures, or prolonged processing times that lead to oxidative degradation of the highly unsaturated fatty acids, resulting in rancidity, loss of bioactivity, and formation of harmful oxidation byproducts. Third, the complex molecular diversity of phospholipid species—ranging from phosphatidylcholine and phosphatidylethanolamine to lysophosphatidylcholine and sphingomyelin—has not been systematically characterized or optimized for specific therapeutic outcomes. Most commercial products, including krill oil supplements, contain undefined mixtures of phospholipids with inconsistent ratios of EPA and DHA, and lack precise quantification of key molecular species such as those containing DHA at the sn-1 position of lysophosphatidylcholine, which has been shown to be particularly efficient in crossing the blood-brain barrier.

Furthermore, while some studies have suggested that phospholipid-bound omega-3s may exert unique effects on lipid metabolism, neurotransmitter synthesis, and endocannabinoid signaling, the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. There is a critical gap in the scientific literature regarding the precise molecular pathways through which marine phospholipids modulate gene expression, membrane fluidity, and intracellular signaling cascades in a manner distinct from triglyceride-bound forms. Additionally, the role of the phospholipid headgroup—particularly choline—as a methyl donor and precursor to acetylcholine has been largely overlooked in the context of omega-3 delivery, despite evidence that choline deficiency is associated with cognitive decline and hepatic dysfunction. The synergistic interaction between the phospholipid backbone, the omega-3 fatty acid chain, and the choline moiety has not been harnessed in any existing commercial formulation.

Another significant limitation of current approaches is the lack of formulations designed for targeted delivery to specific tissues. While triglyceride-bound omega-3s are predominantly metabolized in the liver and distributed to adipose tissue, phospholipid-bound forms have demonstrated a remarkable propensity to accumulate in the brain, retina, and cardiac muscle. However, no existing product has been engineered to exploit this differential distribution by optimizing phospholipid species composition, fatty acid positioning, or particle size to enhance delivery to these critical sites. Moreover, the stability of phospholipid formulations under gastrointestinal conditions, during storage, and in the presence of oxygen and light has not been adequately addressed, leading to inconsistent clinical outcomes and consumer skepticism regarding efficacy.

In the field of functional foods and nutraceuticals, marine phospholipids have been incorporated into products such as infant formula, energy bars, and dietary supplements, but typically at low concentrations and without standardization of key bioactive components. The emulsifying properties of phospholipids have been utilized for texture and shelf-life improvement, but their intrinsic therapeutic potential as bioactive lipid mediators has not been fully leveraged. Similarly, in pharmaceutical applications, phospholipids have been used as excipients in liposomal drug delivery systems, but rarely as the primary active ingredient with defined pharmacological activity. There is therefore a substantial unmet need for a novel class of marine phospholipid-based therapeutics that are not merely supplements, but precisely engineered molecular entities capable of modulating disease pathways with high specificity and potency.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by providing a novel, standardized, and clinically validated composition of marine omega-3 phospholipids that is optimized for maximum bioavailability, tissue-specific delivery, and therapeutic efficacy. The invention is based on the discovery that specific phospholipid molecular species—particularly those enriched in DHA at the sn-1 position of lysophosphatidylcholine and in EPA-DHA combinations within phosphatidylcholine—exhibit superior biological activity compared to other forms. The invention further provides a novel extraction and purification process that preserves molecular integrity, minimizes oxidation, and enables scalable production of high-purity phospholipid fractions. The resulting compositions are formulated for oral, sublingual, or parenteral administration and demonstrate unprecedented pharmacokinetic profiles, including rapid absorption, prolonged circulation, and enhanced accumulation in the central nervous system and cardiovascular tissues. The invention also includes methods for identifying biomarkers of phospholipid efficacy, such as changes in plasma lysophosphatidylcholine levels, erythrocyte DHA content, and hepatic phospholipid remodeling, which can be used to monitor therapeutic response and personalize dosing. This represents a paradigm shift in the field of omega-3 therapeutics, moving beyond generic fish oil supplements to precision lipid medicine grounded in molecular structure, biological mechanism, and clinical outcome.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious composition of marine-derived omega-3 phospholipids that are uniquely structured to maximize bioavailability, tissue targeting, and therapeutic efficacy in mammals, particularly humans. The composition comprises a purified fraction of phospholipids isolated from marine sources, wherein at least 70% of the total phospholipid content is composed of phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine, and lysophosphatidylcholine species, and wherein the fatty acid profile is characterized by a minimum molar ratio of eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) to docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) of 1:1.5, and wherein at least 40% of the total EPA and DHA content is esterified at the sn-2 position of the glycerol backbone, and wherein the total concentration of omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids exceeds 35% by weight of the total phospholipid fraction. The composition further contains less than 5% triglycerides, less than 2% free fatty acids, and less than 0.1% cholesterol by weight, ensuring a high degree of purity and minimizing confounding lipid components that may interfere with biological activity. The phospholipid molecules are substantially free from oxidation products, as confirmed by a peroxide value below 2 meq/kg and a thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS) value below 0.5 μM malondialdehyde equivalents, thereby preserving their structural integrity and biological potency.

The invention further encompasses a method for producing the composition, comprising the steps of: (a) obtaining a lipid-rich extract from marine tissue selected from the group consisting of fish roe, krill, and cold-water oily fish; (b) subjecting the extract to a non-denaturing, solvent-free enzymatic hydrolysis using phospholipase A1 or A2 to selectively remove non-omega-3 fatty acids from the sn-1 or sn-2 positions; (c) separating the resulting phospholipid fraction using countercurrent chromatography or membrane ultrafiltration to isolate phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine, and lysophosphatidylcholine species; (d) concentrating the phospholipid fraction under reduced pressure and low temperature to prevent oxidation; (e) stabilizing the fraction with a natural antioxidant system comprising tocopherols, ascorbyl palmitate, and rosemary extract; and (f) formulating the stabilized phospholipid fraction into a solid, semi-solid, or liquid dosage form suitable for oral, sublingual, or parenteral administration. The method ensures that the final composition retains a minimum of 85% of the original phospholipid molecular species and achieves a recovery yield of at least 60% of the total omega-3 phospholipids from the starting material.

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical or nutraceutical formulation comprising the composition described above, wherein the formulation is selected from the group consisting of capsules, softgels, tablets, powders, emulsions, liposomes, injectable suspensions, and transdermal patches. The formulation may further comprise a carrier selected from the group consisting of medium-chain triglycerides, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, lecithin, alginate, and chitosan, and may be designed for delayed, sustained, or targeted release in the gastrointestinal tract. In a preferred embodiment, the formulation is encapsulated in an enteric-coated capsule to protect the phospholipids from gastric acid degradation and to ensure release in the duodenum, where phospholipid absorption is most efficient.

The invention further provides a method for enhancing the bioavailability of omega-3 fatty acids in a mammal, comprising administering to the mammal an effective amount of the composition described above, wherein the administration results in a statistically significant increase in the concentration of EPA and DHA in erythrocytes, plasma phospholipids, or brain tissue compared to administration of an equivalent amount of omega-3 fatty acids in triglyceride or ethyl ester form. The method further includes monitoring biomarkers of efficacy, including plasma lysophosphatidylcholine levels, erythrocyte omega-3 index, hepatic phospholipid composition, and serum adiponectin concentration, to assess therapeutic response and adjust dosage.

The invention further provides a method for treating or preventing a condition selected from the group consisting of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance, cognitive impairment, mild cognitive impairment, Alzheimer’s disease, depression, anxiety, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, atherosclerosis, hypertriglyceridemia, and retinal degeneration, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of the composition described above. The treatment results in a reduction in hepatic triglyceride accumulation, an increase in high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, a decrease in pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, an increase in brain DHA content, and an improvement in cognitive performance as measured by standardized neuropsychological tests.

The invention further provides a method for improving membrane fluidity and signal transduction in neuronal and vascular cells, comprising contacting the cells with the composition described above, wherein the phospholipids are incorporated into the cell membrane, resulting in increased membrane microdomain organization, enhanced receptor mobility, and improved responsiveness to neurotransmitters and growth factors. The incorporation of phospholipids is confirmed by mass spectrometry analysis of membrane lipid extracts, demonstrating enrichment of DHA-containing phosphatidylcholine and phosphatidylethanolamine species.

The invention further provides a method for modulating the endocannabinoid system in adipose tissue and the brain, comprising administering the composition described above, wherein the phospholipid-bound EPA and DHA serve as precursors for the synthesis of specialized pro-resolving mediators such as resolvins and protectins, and wherein the resulting decrease in endocannabinoid tone leads to reduced adipocyte hypertrophy, improved insulin sensitivity, and decreased neuroinflammation. The modulation is quantified by measuring plasma levels of anandamide and 2-arachidonoylglycerol, which are shown to decrease by at least 30% following treatment.

The invention further provides a method for enhancing the delivery of DHA to the developing brain, comprising administering the composition to a pregnant or lactating female, wherein the lysophosphatidylcholine species containing DHA at the sn-1 position are efficiently transported across the placental and blood-brain barriers, resulting in increased DHA content in fetal and neonatal brain tissue, improved synaptic density, and enhanced learning and memory performance in offspring. The method is supported by in vivo imaging and radiolabeling studies demonstrating preferential uptake of sn-1 DHA-lysoPC over sn-2 DHA-PC or DHA-triglyceride.

The invention further provides a method for reducing oxidative stress in the liver and brain, comprising administering the composition described above, wherein the phospholipids act as endogenous antioxidants by scavenging lipid peroxyl radicals and inhibiting the propagation of lipid peroxidation chains, thereby preserving membrane integrity and preventing the formation of toxic aldehydes such as 4-hydroxynonenal. The protective effect is confirmed by decreased levels of malondialdehyde and protein carbonyls in tissue homogenates and increased activity of glutathione peroxidase and superoxide dismutase.

The invention further provides a method for improving the emulsifying properties of food and cosmetic formulations, comprising incorporating the composition described above as a natural emulsifier, wherein the phospholipids form stable oil-in-water or water-in-oil emulsions without the need for synthetic surfactants, and wherein the resulting product exhibits enhanced stability, improved texture, and added nutritional value due to the presence of bioactive omega-3 phospholipids.
