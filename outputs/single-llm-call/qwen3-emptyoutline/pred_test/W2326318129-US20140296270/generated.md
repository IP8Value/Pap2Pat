# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to novel small-molecule compounds and their use in the inhibition of signal transducer and activator of transcription proteins, particularly STAT3 and STAT1, for the treatment of cancers characterized by aberrant activation of these transcription factors. More specifically, the invention encompasses a class of N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide derivatives that bind with high affinity to the Src homology 2 (SH2) domain of STAT3 and STAT1, thereby preventing their phosphorylation, dimerization, nuclear translocation, and subsequent transcriptional activation of oncogenic and radioresistance-associated gene networks. The compounds of the invention are particularly effective in the treatment of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), as well as other malignancies exhibiting constitutive or ligand-induced activation of STAT3 and/or STAT1 signaling pathways. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising these compounds, methods of administering them, and kits for their use in combination with ionizing radiation or chemotherapeutic agents to overcome treatment resistance and improve clinical outcomes in patients with STAT-activated tumors.

The compounds disclosed herein represent a significant advancement over previously described STAT3 inhibitors, which have been limited by poor potency, lack of selectivity, covalent mechanisms of action leading to off-target toxicity, or inability to penetrate tumors effectively. Unlike earlier agents such as Stattic, S3I-201, or STA-21, which exhibit IC50 values in the micromolar range and often fail to achieve sufficient bioavailability or tumor concentration for therapeutic efficacy, the compounds of the present invention, particularly the lead compound designated C188-9, demonstrate sub-micromolar inhibitory activity against both STAT3 and STAT1, high binding affinity as measured by microscale thermophoresis, oral bioavailability, favorable pharmacokinetic profiles, and a wide therapeutic window in preclinical models. The dual inhibition of STAT3 and STAT1 is a distinguishing feature of the invention, as it simultaneously targets both the pro-proliferative and pro-survival functions of STAT3 and the radioresistance and immune-evasion pathways mediated by STAT1, thereby addressing two critical mechanisms of treatment failure in HNSCC and other solid tumors. The invention therefore provides a novel, clinically viable strategy for the targeted disruption of oncogenic transcription factor signaling in cancers where conventional therapies have proven inadequate.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) is a member of the STAT family of cytoplasmic transcription factors that mediate cellular responses to cytokines, growth factors, and other extracellular stimuli. Upon activation by receptor-associated tyrosine kinases, STAT3 becomes phosphorylated at a conserved tyrosine residue (Y705), leading to its dimerization via reciprocal interaction between the SH2 domain of one monomer and the phosphotyrosine motif of another. These homodimers translocate to the nucleus, where they bind to specific DNA response elements and activate the transcription of genes involved in cell proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, immune evasion, and metabolic reprogramming—all of which are hallmarks of cancer. Constitutive activation of STAT3 has been documented in approximately half of all human malignancies, including breast, lung, prostate, pancreatic, and hematologic cancers, but is particularly prevalent and clinically significant in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), where it is associated with poor prognosis, resistance to radiotherapy, and tumor recurrence.

In HNSCC, STAT3 activation frequently occurs through autocrine loops involving interleukin-6 (IL-6) and its receptor gp130, or through upstream signaling from receptor tyrosine kinases such as EGFR, even in cases where EGFR itself is not mutated or overexpressed. This redundancy in activation pathways renders therapies targeting single upstream nodes, such as EGFR inhibitors, ineffective in a substantial subset of patients. Moreover, direct inhibition of STAT3 has been shown to induce apoptosis and suppress tumor growth in HNSCC xenograft models, validating it as a high-value therapeutic target. However, the development of clinically effective STAT3 inhibitors has been hindered by several challenges. First, many early-generation inhibitors, such as Stattic, rely on covalent modification of cysteine residues within the SH2 domain, which leads to non-specific reactivity and dose-limiting toxicity. Second, compounds such as S3I-201 and STA-21 exhibit weak binding affinity and poor cellular permeability, resulting in high IC50 values that are not therapeutically achievable in vivo. Third, most inhibitors have been designed to selectively target STAT3 while sparing STAT1, based on the assumption that STAT1 functions primarily as a tumor suppressor. However, emerging evidence has revealed that STAT1, far from being universally anti-tumorigenic, plays a critical role in mediating resistance to ionizing radiation and certain chemotherapeutic agents through the upregulation of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) and DNA repair pathways.

Recent studies have demonstrated that tumors resistant to radiotherapy, including HNSCC, exhibit elevated levels of phosphorylated STAT1 and a distinct gene expression signature known as the interferon-related damage signature (IRDS), which includes genes such as OASL, IFIT3, MX2, and IRF7. Silencing STAT1 in radioresistant HNSCC cell lines restores sensitivity to radiation, indicating that STAT1 is not merely a bystander but a key driver of treatment failure. This dual role of STAT3 in promoting tumor growth and STAT1 in promoting therapy resistance has created a compelling rationale for the development of a single agent capable of inhibiting both transcription factors. However, no such agent has been clinically validated to date. Existing small-molecule inhibitors either lack sufficient potency against STAT3, fail to inhibit STAT1, or exhibit unacceptable pharmacokinetic properties. Furthermore, the high degree of structural homology between the SH2 domains of STAT3 and STAT1—particularly in the phosphotyrosine-binding pocket—has made the design of dual inhibitors a formidable challenge, as most compounds that bind one isoform show minimal activity against the other.

The need for a potent, selective, orally bioavailable, and safe inhibitor of both STAT3 and STAT1 is therefore acute, particularly in the context of HNSCC, where the five-year survival rate remains below 50% despite multimodal therapy, and where recurrent or metastatic disease is largely incurable. The current standard of care, which combines surgery, radiation, and platinum-based chemotherapy, often leads to severe toxicity and fails to eradicate cancer stem cells responsible for relapse. The development of a molecularly targeted agent that can penetrate tumors, inhibit oncogenic transcription, reverse radioresistance, and spare normal tissues would represent a transformative advance in oncology. The present invention fulfills this unmet medical need by providing a novel class of compounds that bind with nanomolar affinity to the SH2 domains of both STAT3 and STAT1, effectively block their activation, suppress downstream oncogenic transcription, and demonstrate robust anti-tumor activity in vivo without the toxic liabilities of earlier compounds.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide derivatives that function as potent, reversible, and selective inhibitors of the Src homology 2 (SH2) domains of both signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) and signal transducer and activator of transcription 1 (STAT1). These compounds prevent the phosphorylation-dependent dimerization of STAT3 and STAT1, thereby blocking their nuclear translocation and transcriptional activation of genes involved in tumor cell proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, immune evasion, and resistance to ionizing radiation and chemotherapy. The lead compound of the invention, designated C188-9, exhibits high binding affinity for STAT3 (KD = 4.7 ± 0.4 nM) and STAT1 (KD = 5.2 ± 0.6 nM), as determined by microscale thermophoresis, and demonstrates sub-micromolar inhibitory activity in cellular assays of phospho-STAT3 and phospho-STAT1 suppression, with IC50 values ranging from 3.7 to 9.5 μM depending on the stimulus and cell type. C188-9 is orally bioavailable, achieves high concentrations in tumor tissue relative to plasma, and is well tolerated in preclinical species at doses up to 200 mg/kg/day for 28 days without evidence of clinical, hematological, or histopathological toxicity.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising C188-9 or its pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, or prodrugs, formulated for oral, intravenous, intraperitoneal, or topical administration, and optionally combined with ionizing radiation or chemotherapeutic agents such as cisplatin, 5-fluorouracil, or paclitaxel. The compounds of the invention are particularly useful in the treatment of cancers characterized by constitutive or ligand-induced activation of STAT3 and/or STAT1, including head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, glioblastoma, triple-negative breast cancer, pancreatic adenocarcinoma, and certain hematologic malignancies. The invention also provides methods of treating such cancers by administering an effective amount of the compound to a subject in need thereof, thereby reducing tumor burden, inhibiting metastasis, reversing radioresistance, and prolonging survival. The compounds of the invention are distinguished from prior art inhibitors by their dual inhibition of STAT3 and STAT1, their non-covalent mechanism of action, their high binding affinity, their favorable pharmacokinetic profile, and their demonstrated efficacy in suppressing both oncogenic and radioresistance-associated gene networks in vivo.

In addition to the lead compound C188-9, the invention includes structurally related derivatives that retain the core N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide scaffold and possess substituents at the 3-position of the naphthyl ring that enhance binding affinity and cellular potency, including but not limited to triazole-3-yl-mercapto, binaphthyl, substituted phenyl, heterocyclic, and alkyl-aryl moieties. These derivatives are identified through virtual ligand screening, two-dimensional fingerprint similarity analysis, and three-dimensional pharmacophore modeling, and are validated for STAT3 and STAT1 inhibitory activity using surface plasmon resonance, phosphoflow cytometry, Luminex bead-based assays, and xenograft tumor models. The invention further includes kits for the diagnosis and treatment of STAT-activated cancers, comprising a compound of the invention, instructions for use, and optionally, reagents for detecting phospho-STAT3 or phospho-STAT1 levels in tumor biopsies or circulating tumor cells to identify patients most likely to benefit from therapy. The compounds of the invention represent a breakthrough in targeted cancer therapy by simultaneously disabling two critical transcription factors that drive tumor progression and treatment resistance, thereby offering a novel and highly effective therapeutic strategy for patients with aggressive, therapy-refractory malignancies.
