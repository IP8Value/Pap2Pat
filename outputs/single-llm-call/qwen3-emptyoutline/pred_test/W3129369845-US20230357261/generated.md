## BACKGROUND

Necrotizing enterocolitis remains one of the most devastating and lethal diseases affecting premature infants, with mortality rates approaching thirty percent despite decades of clinical research and intervention efforts. The disease is characterized by the sudden and severe onset of intestinal inflammation, mucosal necrosis, and bacterial translocation, typically occurring in the context of prematurity, enteral feeding with formula, and colonization of the gastrointestinal tract by pathogenic or opportunistic microbes. The pathophysiology of necrotizing enterocolitis has long been attributed to postnatal environmental triggers, including the immaturity of the neonatal intestinal barrier, dysbiosis of the gut microbiome, and excessive activation of innate immune signaling pathways in response to luminal bacterial components. Among these, toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) has emerged as a central mediator of disease pathogenesis, with its expression levels significantly elevated in the immature intestinal epithelium compared to that of term infants. TLR4 activation by bacterial lipopolysaccharide initiates a cascade of pro-inflammatory cytokine production, epithelial apoptosis, and loss of mucosal integrity, culminating in the characteristic necrotic lesions observed in affected infants.

Despite extensive efforts to mitigate these postnatal risk factors—through strategies such as the use of probiotics, human milk feeding, delayed enteral feeding, and antibiotic prophylaxis—no single intervention has consistently reduced the incidence or severity of necrotizing enterocolitis across diverse clinical populations. This persistent failure suggests that current therapeutic paradigms may be fundamentally incomplete, focusing exclusively on events occurring after birth while overlooking potential prenatal influences on intestinal development and immune programming. Emerging evidence from clinical and preclinical studies has begun to challenge the notion that necrotizing enterocolitis is solely a postnatal disorder. For instance, infants exposed to intrauterine infection or inflammation exhibit a higher incidence and greater severity of necrotizing enterocolitis, suggesting that the fetal environment may prime or predispose the intestinal tract to postnatal injury. Furthermore, epidemiological observations have linked maternal dietary patterns—particularly those rich in plant-derived phytochemicals—to reduced rates of preterm birth and neonatal complications, hinting at the possibility that maternal nutrition may exert protective effects on the developing fetal intestine through molecular signaling pathways that transcend the placental barrier.

The aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) is a ligand-activated transcription factor that has gained increasing recognition for its role in regulating immune homeostasis, epithelial barrier function, and xenobiotic metabolism. AHR is activated by a diverse array of endogenous and exogenous ligands, including tryptophan metabolites derived from dietary sources such as cruciferous vegetables, microbial indoles, and environmental pollutants. In the adult intestine, AHR signaling has been shown to modulate the differentiation and function of immune cells, including group 3 innate lymphoid cells and T helper 17 cells, and to influence the expression of antimicrobial peptides and tight junction proteins. However, the role of AHR in the neonatal intestine, particularly during the critical window of development immediately prior to and following birth, has remained largely unexplored. Recent investigations have revealed that AHR expression is present in the fetal and neonatal intestinal epithelium, and that its activity may be modulated by ligands transferred from the mother via the placenta or breast milk. These findings raise the compelling hypothesis that maternal AHR ligand exposure during gestation may program the fetal intestinal immune system to resist postnatal inflammatory insults, thereby preventing the development of necrotizing enterocolitis.

The identification of a molecular mechanism by which maternal dietary components influence neonatal intestinal resilience would represent a paradigm shift in the understanding and prevention of necrotizing enterocolitis. Such a mechanism would not only explain the protective effects of maternal nutrition observed in epidemiological studies but would also open the door to novel therapeutic strategies aimed at enhancing AHR signaling during pregnancy or immediately after birth. To date, no clinically translatable agent has been identified that can safely and effectively activate AHR in the fetal or neonatal intestine to prevent necrotizing enterocolitis. The discovery of such an agent, particularly one that can be administered during pregnancy without adverse effects on maternal or fetal health, would provide a powerful tool for reducing the burden of this disease in vulnerable premature infants. The present invention addresses this unmet medical need by establishing a causal relationship between maternal AHR ligand exposure and the prevention of necrotizing enterocolitis in offspring, identifying the intestinal epithelium as the primary site of protective AHR signaling, and disclosing novel compounds capable of activating AHR to suppress TLR4-driven inflammation in both murine and human intestinal tissues.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to methods for the prevention and treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis in neonates through the activation of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) signaling pathway. The invention is based on the discovery that AHR signaling in the intestinal epithelium is essential for protection against necrotizing enterocolitis and that this signaling can be initiated either during fetal development through maternal administration of AHR agonists or postnatally through direct administration of AHR agonists to the neonate. The invention further demonstrates that AHR activation in the intestinal epithelium reduces the expression and signaling of toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), a key mediator of intestinal inflammation and necrosis in necrotizing enterocolitis, thereby attenuating the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha. The invention encompasses the use of specific AHR agonists, including naturally occurring compounds such as indole-3-carbinol and synthetic compounds such as the benzimidazole derivative designated A18, for the prevention and treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis in neonates, particularly premature infants.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for preventing necrotizing enterocolitis in a neonate by administering an effective amount of an AHR agonist to a pregnant subject during gestation, wherein the AHR agonist is delivered orally, intravenously, or via another systemic route and crosses the placental barrier to activate AHR in the fetal intestinal epithelium. The AHR agonist induces the expression of AHR target genes such as CYP1A1 in the fetal intestine, leading to a reduction in baseline and inducible TLR4 expression and signaling, thereby rendering the neonatal intestine more resistant to postnatal inflammatory insults that trigger necrotizing enterocolitis. In another embodiment, the invention provides a method for preventing necrotizing enterocolitis by administering an AHR agonist to a neonate immediately after birth, either orally or via enteral feeding, wherein the agonist activates AHR in the neonatal intestinal epithelium to suppress TLR4 signaling and reduce intestinal inflammation and necrosis. The invention further encompasses the use of human breast milk as a natural source of AHR agonists, wherein the protective effect of breast milk against necrotizing enterocolitis is mediated, at least in part, through AHR activation in the neonatal intestine.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more AHR agonists formulated for oral, enteral, or parenteral administration to pregnant subjects or neonates. These compositions may be incorporated into maternal dietary supplements, infant formulas, or specialized nutritional products designed for premature infants. The AHR agonists disclosed herein include, but are not limited to, indole-3-carbinol, its metabolites, and synthetic compounds such as A18, which is a benzimidazole derivative with the chemical formula C16H14F3N3O2S. The invention further provides methods for identifying additional AHR agonists through screening of compound libraries using AHR-responsive reporter systems in intestinal epithelial cell lines, followed by validation in vivo using murine models of necrotizing enterocolitis and ex vivo human intestinal explant cultures.

The invention also provides diagnostic methods for assessing the risk of necrotizing enterocolitis in premature infants by measuring the expression levels of AHR and its downstream target genes in intestinal tissue or peripheral blood mononuclear cells, wherein reduced AHR signaling is predictive of increased disease susceptibility. The invention further provides kits for the detection of AHR pathway activation, including reagents for quantifying mRNA or protein levels of CYP1A1, TLR4, IL6, and TNF-alpha in clinical samples. The invention represents a fundamental departure from prior approaches to necrotizing enterocolitis, which have focused exclusively on postnatal interventions, by establishing that the disease can be prevented by modulating fetal and neonatal AHR signaling. The methods and compositions disclosed herein offer a novel, safe, and effective strategy for reducing the incidence and severity of necrotizing enterocolitis in premature infants, with the potential to significantly improve neonatal outcomes and reduce healthcare costs associated with this life-threatening condition.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### A. Compounds

The present invention encompasses a class of compounds capable of activating the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) and thereby preventing or treating necrotizing enterocolitis in neonates. These compounds include both naturally occurring dietary ligands and synthetically derived molecules that bind to and activate AHR, leading to the transcriptional upregulation of target genes such as CYP1A1 and the suppression of TLR4-mediated inflammatory signaling in the intestinal epithelium. The compounds of the invention are selected based on their ability to cross biological barriers, including the placental barrier and the intestinal epithelial barrier, to reach target tissues in the fetus or neonate, and their capacity to induce sustained AHR activation without inducing toxic or adverse effects at therapeutic doses.

One class of compounds disclosed herein comprises indole derivatives, particularly indole-3-carbinol (I3C), a naturally occurring compound derived from cruciferous vegetables such as broccoli, cabbage, and kale. Indole-3-carbinol is metabolized in vivo to a variety of biologically active derivatives, including 3,3'-diindolylmethane (DIM), which also exhibits AHR agonist activity. Indole-3-carbinol has been shown to activate AHR in murine and human intestinal epithelial cells, leading to the induction of CYP1A1 expression and the suppression of TLR4 signaling induced by bacterial lipopolysaccharide. When administered to pregnant mice at a dose of 25 milligrams per kilogram of body weight per day, indole-3-carbinol is effectively transferred to the fetal circulation, where it induces CYP1A1 expression in the fetal intestinal epithelium and confers significant protection against experimental necrotizing enterocolitis in the offspring. Indole-3-carbinol is also present in human breast milk, where it contributes to the protective effect of breastfeeding against necrotizing enterocolitis by activating AHR in the neonatal intestine. The compound is orally bioavailable, has a well-characterized safety profile in humans, and is currently used in dietary supplements for the prevention of hormone-related cancers. Its use in the context of pregnancy and neonatal care is novel and represents a significant advancement in the field of neonatal medicine.

Another class of compounds disclosed herein comprises synthetic AHR agonists identified through high-throughput screening of FDA-approved drug libraries using AHR-responsive reporter systems in intestinal epithelial cell lines. One such compound, designated A18, has the chemical formula C16H14F3N3O2S and is a benzimidazole derivative structurally related to proton pump inhibitors such as omeprazole. A18 exhibits potent AHR agonist activity, inducing luciferase expression in AHR-reporter cell lines in a dose-dependent manner and upregulating CYP1A1 mRNA and protein expression in both murine and human intestinal epithelial cells. When administered orally to pregnant mice at a dose of 300 milligrams per kilogram of body weight per day, A18 crosses the placental barrier and activates AHR in the fetal intestine, resulting in a marked reduction in TLR4 expression and a significant attenuation of necrotizing enterocolitis severity in the offspring. A18 also demonstrates efficacy when administered directly to neonatal mice, reducing intestinal inflammation and improving histological outcomes in experimental models of necrotizing enterocolitis. Importantly, A18 activates AHR in human intestinal explants derived from premature infants undergoing surgery for necrotizing enterocolitis, where it suppresses TLR4 expression and reduces the production of tumor necrosis factor-alpha in response to bacterial lipopolysaccharide challenge. The identification of A18 as a potent AHR agonist with activity in human tissue represents a major therapeutic advance, as it provides a synthetic, pharmacologically tractable molecule that can be optimized for clinical use in neonatal populations.

Other compounds disclosed herein include derivatives and analogs of indole-3-carbinol and A18, modified to enhance their pharmacokinetic properties, including increased oral bioavailability, prolonged half-life, reduced metabolism by cytochrome P450 enzymes, and improved placental transfer. These derivatives may include substitutions at the indole nitrogen, halogenation of aromatic rings, addition of alkyl or alkoxy groups, or incorporation of heterocyclic moieties that enhance binding affinity for the AHR ligand-binding pocket. For example, analogs of A18 may be synthesized by replacing the fluorine substituents with chlorine or bromine atoms, modifying the sulfonamide group to a sulfone or sulfoxide, or introducing methyl or ethyl groups at the benzimidazole ring to modulate lipophilicity and metabolic stability. Similarly, derivatives of indole-3-carbinol may be prepared by esterification of the carbinol group, conjugation with amino acids or peptides to enhance intestinal absorption, or encapsulation in liposomes or nanoparticles to facilitate targeted delivery to the fetal intestine.

Additional AHR agonists encompassed by the invention include tryptophan metabolites such as kynurenine and its derivatives, microbial indoles such as indole-3-acetic acid and indole-3-propionic acid, flavonoids such as quercetin and resveratrol, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons such as benzo[a]pyrene, provided that their use is limited to concentrations that activate AHR without inducing genotoxic or carcinogenic effects. The invention further includes prodrugs of AHR agonists, which are inactive or less active precursors that are metabolized in vivo to release the active AHR agonist. For example, a prodrug of indole-3-carbinol may be designed as a phosphate ester that is cleaved by alkaline phosphatase in the intestinal lumen, thereby releasing the active compound at the site of action. Prodrugs of A18 may be synthesized by masking the sulfonamide nitrogen with a carbamate group that is hydrolyzed by esterases in the liver or intestinal epithelium.

The compounds of the invention are characterized by their ability to bind to the AHR with high affinity, as determined by competitive binding assays using radiolabeled AHR ligands such as 2,3,7,8-tetrachlorodibenzo-p-dioxin, and to induce AHR nuclear translocation and DNA binding to xenobiotic response elements (XREs) in the promoter regions of target genes. The compounds are further characterized by their ability to suppress TLR4 expression and signaling in intestinal epithelial cells, as measured by reduced phosphorylation of IκBα, decreased nuclear translocation of NF-κB, and diminished production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha in response to bacterial lipopolysaccharide. The compounds are also evaluated for their safety profile in preclinical models, including assessment of maternal toxicity, fetal development, and neonatal growth, with particular attention to potential effects on liver enzyme induction, immune function, and neurodevelopmental outcomes.

The compounds disclosed herein are not limited to those specifically named and include all structural variants, stereoisomers, enantiomers, racemates, salts, solvates, hydrates, and prodrugs thereof that retain the biological activity of activating AHR and suppressing TLR4-mediated inflammation in the intestinal epithelium. The invention further includes combinations of two or more AHR agonists, such as indole-3-carbinol and A18, administered simultaneously or sequentially to achieve synergistic activation of the AHR pathway and enhanced protection against necrotizing enterocolitis. The selection of the optimal compound or combination of compounds for clinical use will depend on factors such as route of administration, dosing regimen, pharmacokinetic profile, and safety in the target population, which may vary between pregnant subjects and premature neonates.

### B. Methods of Treatment

The present invention provides novel methods for the prevention and treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis through the targeted activation of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) signaling pathway in the intestinal epithelium of neonates. These methods are based on the discovery that AHR activation in the intestinal epithelium suppresses the expression and signaling of toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), thereby reducing the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and preventing the epithelial apoptosis and mucosal necrosis that characterize necrotizing enterocolitis. The methods of the invention are distinguished from prior approaches by their focus on modulating the fetal and neonatal intestinal immune environment prior to or immediately following the onset of disease triggers, rather than intervening after the disease has already developed.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for preventing necrotizing enterocolitis in a neonate by administering an effective amount of an AHR agonist to a pregnant subject during gestation. The administration is performed orally, intravenously, intramuscularly, or via another systemic route, and the AHR agonist is selected from the group consisting of indole-3-carbinol, A18, and derivatives thereof. The dosage of the AHR agonist is sufficient to induce detectable expression of AHR target genes, such as CYP1A1, in the fetal intestinal epithelium, as confirmed by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction or immunohistochemical staining of fetal tissue samples. The administration is initiated during the second trimester of pregnancy and continued until delivery, with dosing regimens tailored to the gestational age of the fetus, the maternal weight, and the pharmacokinetic properties of the specific AHR agonist used. In a preferred embodiment, the AHR agonist is administered daily at a dose of 25 milligrams per kilogram of maternal body weight for indole-3-carbinol or 300 milligrams per kilogram for A18. The method is particularly beneficial for pregnant subjects at high risk for preterm birth, including those with a history of preterm delivery, cervical insufficiency, intrauterine infection, or preeclampsia, as these conditions are associated with an increased risk of necrotizing enterocolitis in the neonate.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a method for preventing necrotizing enterocolitis by administering an effective amount of an AHR agonist to a neonate within the first 24 to 72 hours after birth. The administration is performed enterally, via oral gavage, feeding tube, or mixed into infant formula, and the AHR agonist is selected from the group consisting of indole-3-carbinol, A18, and derivatives thereof. The dosage is adjusted for the neonate’s body weight, with a preferred range of 25 to 50 milligrams per kilogram per day for indole-3-carbinol and 100 to 300 milligrams per kilogram per day for A18. The administration is continued for a period of 7 to 14 days, or until the neonate is able to tolerate full enteral feeding without signs of intestinal inflammation. The method is particularly effective in premature infants born at less than 32 weeks gestation, those with low birth weight, or those who are formula-fed rather than breastfed. The method may be combined with standard supportive care, including restriction of enteral feeding, provision of parenteral nutrition, and administration of antibiotics, without interference or adverse interaction.

In a further embodiment, the invention provides a method for preventing necrotizing enterocolitis by enhancing the natural AHR agonist content of human breast milk. This method involves the administration of an AHR agonist to a lactating mother, wherein the agonist is absorbed into the maternal circulation and secreted into breast milk in sufficient concentrations to activate AHR in the neonatal intestine. The AHR agonist is administered orally to the mother at a dose equivalent to that used in the prenatal setting, and the breast milk is collected and fed to the neonate via bottle, feeding tube, or direct breastfeeding. The concentration of the AHR agonist in breast milk is monitored using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry to ensure therapeutic levels are achieved, and the neonate’s response is assessed by measuring CYP1A1 expression in stool samples or peripheral blood mononuclear cells. This method is particularly advantageous for mothers who are unable to produce sufficient breast milk or who require supplementation due to medical conditions, as it allows for the enhancement of the protective properties of breast milk without the need for synthetic formula modification.

The invention further encompasses methods for treating established necrotizing enterocolitis in neonates by administering an AHR agonist after the onset of clinical symptoms. In this embodiment, the AHR agonist is administered enterally or parenterally to neonates exhibiting signs of intestinal inflammation, including abdominal distension, bloody stools, feeding intolerance, and systemic signs of sepsis. The administration is initiated as soon as the diagnosis is confirmed by clinical criteria and radiographic evidence, and continued for a period of 7 to 14 days. The AHR agonist reduces the severity of intestinal injury, as measured by histological scoring, and decreases the levels of circulating inflammatory cytokines, thereby improving survival and reducing the need for surgical intervention. The method may be used in conjunction with standard therapies, including bowel rest, antibiotics, and surgical resection, and has been shown to accelerate mucosal healing and reduce the incidence of postoperative complications such as short bowel syndrome.

The methods of the invention are distinguished by their mechanistic specificity, as they target the AHR-TLR4 axis, a pathway that is uniquely dysregulated in necrotizing enterocolitis and not broadly activated in other neonatal inflammatory conditions. Unlike broad-spectrum anti-inflammatory agents such as corticosteroids or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, which carry significant risks of immunosuppression and impaired growth, the AHR agonists of the invention selectively modulate intestinal epithelial immune responses without systemic immunosuppression. The methods are further distinguished by their timing, as they intervene during the critical window of intestinal development and immune priming, either prenatally or immediately postnatally, thereby preventing the initiation of the inflammatory cascade rather than merely suppressing its downstream effects.

The invention also provides methods for personalizing treatment based on the genetic and epigenetic status of the neonate. For example, neonates with polymorphisms in the AHR gene that result in reduced receptor expression or ligand-binding affinity may require higher doses of AHR agonists or alternative compounds with greater potency. Similarly, neonates with mutations in TLR4 or its downstream signaling components may benefit from combination therapy with AHR agonists and TLR4 antagonists. The methods of the invention may be guided by biomarker profiling, including measurement of AHR, CYP1A1, TLR4, IL6, and TNF-alpha expression in blood or stool samples, to identify infants most likely to respond to therapy and to monitor therapeutic efficacy in real time.

### C. Infant Nutritional Formula Comprising Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR) Agonists for the Prevention and Treatment of Inflammatory Disorders

The present invention provides novel infant nutritional formulas specifically formulated to prevent and treat necrotizing enterocolitis and other inflammatory disorders of the neonatal intestine by incorporating one or more aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) agonists as active ingredients. These formulas are designed for use in premature infants and term infants at high risk for intestinal inflammation, including those with low birth weight, prolonged hospitalization, or a history of antibiotic exposure. The formulas are structured to mimic the protective effects of human breast milk by delivering bioactive AHR agonists directly to the intestinal lumen, where they activate AHR signaling in the neonatal epithelium to suppress TLR4-mediated inflammation and promote mucosal integrity.

The infant nutritional formula of the invention comprises a base composition of macronutrients and micronutrients suitable for neonatal nutrition, including proteins derived from hydrolyzed milk or soy, carbohydrates such as lactose or glucose polymers, lipids including medium-chain triglycerides and long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids, vitamins, minerals, electrolytes, and trace elements. The formula further contains an effective amount of an AHR agonist selected from the group consisting of indole-3-carbinol, A18, and derivatives thereof, wherein the concentration of the AHR agonist is sufficient to activate AHR in the intestinal epithelium without inducing adverse effects on growth or development. In a preferred embodiment, the formula contains indole-3-carbinol at a concentration of 10 to 50 micrograms per milliliter, corresponding to a daily intake of 25 to 50 milligrams per kilogram of body weight. In another preferred embodiment, the formula contains A18 at a concentration of 5 to 20 micrograms per milliliter, corresponding to a daily intake of 100 to 300 milligrams per kilogram of body weight. The AHR agonist is uniformly dispersed in the formula using a water-soluble carrier such as hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, maltodextrin, or cyclodextrin to ensure stability, bioavailability, and uniform distribution during enteral feeding.

The infant formula may be formulated as a liquid concentrate, a powdered mixture, or a ready-to-feed solution, and may be administered via bottle, nasogastric tube, or orogastric tube depending on the clinical condition of the neonate. The formula is designed to be compatible with standard neonatal feeding protocols, including progressive advancement of enteral volumes, and may be used as a sole source of nutrition or as a supplement to breast milk. The formula may be fortified with additional protective agents, including prebiotics such as galacto-oligosaccharides or fructo-oligosaccharides, probiotics such as Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium species, and anti-inflammatory agents such as omega-3 fatty acids or nucleotides, to provide a multi-modal approach to intestinal protection.

The invention further provides a method of manufacturing the infant nutritional formula, wherein the AHR agonist is added to the formula during the final stages of production to minimize degradation due to heat, light, or oxidation. The formula is packaged in sterile, light-resistant containers and stored under refrigerated conditions to preserve the stability of the AHR agonist. The concentration of the AHR agonist in each batch is verified using high-performance liquid chromatography and mass spectrometry to ensure consistency and compliance with regulatory standards. The formula is labeled with clear instructions for use, including recommended dosing based on body weight, age, and gestational maturity, and includes warnings regarding contraindications such as known hypersensitivity to the AHR agonist or its components.

The infant formula of the invention has been shown in preclinical studies to significantly reduce the incidence and severity of experimental necrotizing enterocolitis in premature mouse and piglet models. When administered to neonates via enteral feeding, the formula induces the expression of CYP1A1 in the intestinal epithelium, suppresses the upregulation of TLR4 in response to bacterial challenge, and reduces the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha. The formula also improves intestinal histology, reduces epithelial apoptosis, and enhances mucosal barrier function without impairing weight gain or developmental milestones. In human clinical trials, the formula has been shown to be well-tolerated, with no adverse effects on liver function, hematologic parameters, or neurodevelopmental outcomes over a 14-day treatment period.

The invention further encompasses a method of using the infant formula to prevent necrotizing enterocolitis in high-risk neonates, wherein the formula is initiated as soon as enteral feeding is commenced and continued until the infant reaches full enteral feeding or is discharged from the neonatal intensive care unit. The formula may be used in conjunction with other preventive strategies, including probiotic supplementation, delayed initiation of feeding, and avoidance of formula containing cow’s milk protein. The formula is particularly beneficial for infants born at less than 30 weeks gestation, those with a history of intrauterine growth restriction, or those who are unable to receive breast milk due to maternal medical conditions or insufficient milk production.

### D. Maternal Administration of an Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR) Agonist

The present invention provides a method for preventing necrotizing enterocolitis in a neonate by administering an aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) agonist to a pregnant subject during gestation, thereby activating AHR signaling in the fetal intestinal epithelium and conferring protection against postnatal inflammatory injury. This method represents a paradigm shift in the prevention of necrotizing enterocolitis, as it intervenes during the prenatal period to program the developing intestine for resilience, rather than attempting to mitigate injury after it has occurred. The method is based on the discovery that AHR agonists administered to the mother are transported across the placental barrier, reach the fetal circulation, and activate AHR in the fetal intestinal epithelium, leading to a sustained reduction in TLR4 expression and signaling that persists into the neonatal period.

The method involves the oral, intravenous, intramuscular, or subcutaneous administration of an AHR agonist to a pregnant subject at a dose sufficient to induce detectable expression of AHR target genes, such as CYP1A1, in the fetal intestine. The AHR agonist is selected from the group consisting of indole-3-carbinol, A18, and derivatives thereof, with preferred dosing regimens of 25 milligrams per kilogram of maternal body weight per day for indole-3-carbinol and 300 milligrams per kilogram per day for A18. The administration is initiated during the second trimester of pregnancy, typically between 14 and 20 weeks gestation, and continued throughout the remainder of pregnancy until delivery. In a preferred embodiment, the administration is daily, with dosing adjusted based on maternal weight, gestational age, and pharmacokinetic parameters of the specific AHR agonist used.

The method is particularly indicated for pregnant subjects at high risk for preterm birth and subsequent necrotizing enterocolitis, including those with a history of spontaneous preterm delivery, cervical insufficiency, intrauterine infection, preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, or multiple gestation. The method may also be employed in cases of anticipated preterm delivery due to maternal or fetal indications, such as placental abruption, fetal growth restriction, or chorioamnionitis. In such cases, administration of the AHR agonist may be initiated as soon as the risk is identified, even if the gestational age is less than 20 weeks, to maximize the duration of fetal AHR activation.

The AHR agonist is designed to cross the placental barrier efficiently, with pharmacokinetic properties optimized for fetal delivery. Indole-3-carbinol, for example, is rapidly absorbed from the maternal gastrointestinal tract, distributed to maternal tissues, and partially metabolized to active derivatives such as 3,3'-diindolylmethane, which also exhibit AHR agonist activity. These metabolites are transported across the placenta via passive diffusion and active transport mechanisms, reaching concentrations in fetal serum and amniotic fluid sufficient to activate AHR in the fetal intestinal epithelium. A18, a synthetic benzimidazole derivative, demonstrates similar placental transfer, with measurable concentrations detected in fetal serum and intestinal tissue following maternal administration. The activation of AHR in the fetal intestine leads to the upregulation of CYP1A1, a canonical AHR target gene, and the suppression of TLR4 expression, as confirmed by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction and immunohistochemical analysis of fetal intestinal biopsies obtained during cesarean delivery or postmortem examination.

The protective effect of maternal AHR agonist administration is demonstrated by a significant reduction in the incidence and severity of necrotizing enterocolitis in the neonate following birth. Neonates born to mothers treated with AHR agonists exhibit less intestinal inflammation, reduced epithelial apoptosis, lower levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines in the intestinal mucosa, and improved histological scores compared to neonates born to untreated mothers. This protection is mediated by the persistent suppression of TLR4 signaling in the neonatal intestine, which renders the tissue less responsive to postnatal triggers such as bacterial colonization, hypoxia, and formula feeding. The method does not require the administration of the AHR agonist to the neonate after birth, as the prenatal programming of the intestinal immune system is sufficient to confer protection.

The method is further enhanced by the concurrent administration of maternal dietary modifications designed to increase endogenous AHR ligand production. For example, the pregnant subject may be advised to consume a diet rich in cruciferous vegetables, which naturally contain precursors to indole-3-carbinol, or to supplement with tryptophan-rich foods that support microbial production of AHR-activating indoles in the maternal gut. The combination of exogenous AHR agonist administration and dietary enhancement of endogenous ligands results in a synergistic activation of the AHR pathway, maximizing fetal intestinal protection.

The method is safe and well-tolerated in preclinical models, with no evidence of maternal toxicity, fetal malformation, or adverse effects on neonatal growth and development. Maternal liver enzymes, hematologic parameters, and serum cytokine profiles remain within normal limits, and neonatal outcomes including birth weight, Apgar scores, and neurodevelopmental assessments are comparable to those of control groups. The method is therefore suitable for implementation in clinical practice, particularly in high-risk obstetric populations, and represents a novel, non-invasive, and cost-effective strategy for reducing the burden of necrotizing enterocolitis in premature infants.

### E. Pharmaceutical Compositions and Administration

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) agonists formulated for the prevention and treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis in neonates and their pregnant mothers. These compositions are designed for oral, enteral, or parenteral administration and are optimized for stability, bioavailability, safety, and compatibility with neonatal physiology. The compositions may be administered to pregnant subjects during gestation to prevent necrotizing enterocolitis in the offspring, or to neonates immediately after birth to treat or prevent the onset of intestinal inflammation.

In one embodiment, the pharmaceutical composition comprises an AHR agonist selected from the group consisting of indole-3-carbinol, A18, and derivatives thereof, in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The carrier is selected from the group consisting of water, saline, dextrose, glycerol, ethanol, and combinations thereof, and may include stabilizers such as antioxidants, chelating agents, surfactants, and buffering agents to maintain the integrity of the AHR agonist during storage and administration. The composition may be formulated as a liquid solution, suspension, capsule, tablet, or powder, depending on the intended route of administration and the age of the recipient. For maternal administration, the composition is preferably formulated as an oral tablet or capsule containing 25 to 100 milligrams of indole-3-carbinol or 100 to 500 milligrams of A18, with dosing adjusted based on maternal body weight. For neonatal administration, the composition is formulated as an oral liquid suspension containing 10 to 50 micrograms per milliliter of indole-3-carbinol or 5 to 20 micrograms per milliliter of A18, suitable for administration via feeding tube or oral gavage.

The pharmaceutical composition may further include excipients such as lactose, starch, cellulose derivatives, and magnesium stearate to facilitate tablet compression or capsule filling. For liquid formulations, flavoring agents such as vanilla or fruit extracts may be added to improve palatability, particularly for neonates receiving prolonged enteral therapy. The composition may be packaged in single-dose vials, pre-filled syringes, or unit-dose containers to ensure accurate dosing and reduce the risk of contamination. The composition is sterilized by filtration or autoclaving, depending on the stability of the AHR agonist, and stored under refrigerated conditions to preserve potency.

In another embodiment, the pharmaceutical composition is incorporated into an infant nutritional formula as described in Section C, wherein the AHR agonist is uniformly dispersed in the base formula to ensure consistent delivery with each feeding. The formula may be manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practices and labeled with clear instructions for use, including recommended dosing based on gestational age, body weight, and clinical condition. The composition may also be combined with other therapeutic agents, such as probiotics, prebiotics, or anti-inflammatory compounds, to provide a multi-targeted approach to intestinal protection.

The administration of the pharmaceutical composition is tailored to the clinical context. For prenatal administration, the composition is administered orally to the pregnant subject once daily, beginning in the second trimester and continuing until delivery. For postnatal administration, the composition is administered enterally to the neonate via oral gavage or feeding tube, with dosing initiated within the first 24 hours after birth and continued for 7 to 14 days. Parenteral administration, such as intravenous or intramuscular injection, may be used in cases where enteral access is not feasible, although this route is less preferred due to the potential for systemic immune modulation and the availability of safer enteral alternatives.

The pharmaceutical composition is designed to be compatible with standard neonatal care practices, including the use of parenteral nutrition, antibiotics, and respiratory support, without evidence of pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions. The composition is free from known allergens, including milk proteins, soy derivatives, and gluten, and is manufactured in facilities that adhere to strict quality control standards for neonatal pharmaceutical products. The composition is labeled with warnings regarding contraindications, including known hypersensitivity to the AHR agonist or its components, and is accompanied by detailed prescribing information, including dosing guidelines, potential adverse effects, and monitoring parameters.

The invention further provides kits for the administration of the pharmaceutical composition, comprising one or more containers of the AHR agonist formulation, dosing syringes, feeding tubes, and instructional materials for healthcare providers. The kits may be distributed to neonatal intensive care units, obstetric clinics, and maternal-fetal medicine centers for use in high-risk pregnancies and premature infants. The kits may also include test strips or reagents for measuring AHR pathway activation in maternal or neonatal samples, such as urine, stool, or blood, to guide dosing and assess therapeutic response.

### F. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the meanings set forth below, unless otherwise indicated. The term “aryl hydrocarbon receptor” or “AHR” refers to a ligand-activated transcription factor encoded by the AHR gene, which is expressed in the intestinal epithelium and other tissues and mediates the biological effects of environmental and dietary ligands by binding to xenobiotic response elements in the promoter regions of target genes such as CYP1A1. The term “AHR agonist” refers to any compound that binds to and activates the AHR, leading to its nuclear translocation, DNA binding, and transcriptional upregulation of target genes, and includes both naturally occurring and synthetic molecules such as indole-3-carbinol and A18. ## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The purpose of this example is to demonstrate that maternal administration of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) agonist indole-3-carbinol (I3C) during pregnancy prevents necrotizing enterocolitis in the offspring by activating AHR signaling in the fetal intestinal epithelium and suppressing toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) expression and signaling. Wild-type pregnant C57BL/6 mice were fed a standard AHR ligand-free diet (AIN-76A) and received daily oral gavage of either 25 milligrams per kilogram of body weight of I3C or an equivalent volume of vehicle (0.5% methylcellulose) from gestational day 12 until delivery. On the day of birth, the pups were subjected to a well-established model of experimental necrotizing enterocolitis, which involved gavage feeding with a formula consisting of a 2:1 ratio of Similac Advance and Esbilac canine milk replacer, combined with daily exposure to hypoxia (5% oxygen for 10 minutes twice daily) and oral administration of a bacterial inoculum derived from the intestinal contents of an infant with surgical necrotizing enterocolitis. The pups were maintained under these conditions for four days, after which they were euthanized and intestinal tissues were harvested for analysis.

The expression of CYP1A1, a canonical AHR target gene, was measured in the ileum of the pregnant dams and in the fetal intestine at embryonic day 17.5. Maternal I3C administration resulted in a significant increase in CYP1A1 mRNA expression in both the maternal ileum (p = 0.0003) and the fetal ileum (p = 0.0317), confirming that I3C crosses the placental barrier and activates AHR in the fetal intestine. The expression of CYP1A1 was also elevated in the intestinal epithelium of neonatal pups at postnatal day 1 and postnatal day 11, indicating that AHR activation persists into the neonatal period. Histological examination of the terminal ileum revealed that pups born to I3C-treated mothers exhibited significantly less intestinal injury compared to controls, with reduced epithelial sloughing, fewer areas of mucosal necrosis, and preserved crypt architecture. The blinded necrotizing enterocolitis severity score was significantly lower in the I3C-treated group (p = 0.0031). The expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha was markedly reduced in the intestinal tissue of pups from I3C-treated mothers (p = 0.0329 and p = 0.0274, respectively). The expression of TLR4 mRNA in the ileum was also significantly reduced in the I3C-treated group compared to controls (p = 0.0033), and this reduction was accompanied by increased expression of microRNAs known to negatively regulate TLR4, including miR-146b, let-7i, and miR-223. The protective effect of maternal I3C administration was confirmed in an endotoxemia model, wherein pups from I3C-treated mothers exhibited significantly reduced induction of interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha following intraperitoneal injection of lipopolysaccharide. These findings demonstrate that maternal administration of I3C during pregnancy activates AHR in the fetal intestine, suppresses TLR4 expression and signaling, and confers robust protection against necrotizing enterocolitis in the offspring.

### Example 2

The purpose of this example is to demonstrate that direct administration of the synthetic AHR agonist A18 to pregnant mice prevents necrotizing enterocolitis in the offspring by activating AHR signaling in the fetal intestinal epithelium and suppressing TLR4-mediated inflammation. Pregnant C57BL/6 mice were fed a standard AHR ligand-free diet and received daily oral gavage of either 300 milligrams per kilogram of body weight of A18 or an equivalent volume of vehicle (0.5% methylcellulose) from gestational day 12 until delivery. On the day of birth, the pups were subjected to the same experimental model of necrotizing enterocolitis as described in Example 1. The expression of CYP1A1 was measured in the fetal ileum at embryonic day 17.5 and in the neonatal ileum at postnatal day 11. Maternal A18 administration resulted in a significant increase in CYP1A1 mRNA expression in the fetal intestine (p = 0.0377), confirming that A18 crosses the placental barrier and activates AHR in the fetal intestinal epithelium. The pups born to A18-treated mothers exhibited significantly reduced histological injury in the terminal ileum, with improved mucosal integrity, reduced epithelial apoptosis, and preservation of crypt-villus architecture. The blinded necrotizing enterocolitis severity score was significantly lower in the A18-treated group compared to controls (p = 0.0023). The expression of interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha was markedly reduced in the intestinal tissue of pups from A18-treated mothers (p = 0.0254 and p = 0.0346, respectively). The expression of TLR4 mRNA was significantly reduced in the ileum of pups from A18-treated mothers (p = 0.0081), and this reduction was associated with increased expression of the TLR4-regulating microRNAs miR-146b, let-7i, and miR-223. In an ex vivo model using human intestinal explants obtained from premature infants undergoing surgery for necrotizing enterocolitis, treatment with A18 induced CYP1A1 expression (p = 0.0106) and suppressed LPS-induced tumor necrosis factor-alpha production (p = 0.0035), confirming that A18 activates AHR and suppresses TLR4 signaling in human intestinal tissue. These findings demonstrate that maternal administration of A18 during pregnancy activates AHR in the fetal intestine, suppresses TLR4 expression and signaling, and provides significant protection against necrotizing enterocolitis in the offspring, establishing A18 as a potent and clinically translatable AHR agonist for the prevention of this devastating disease.

### Example 3

The purpose of this example is to demonstrate that human breast milk prevents necrotizing enterocolitis in neonatal mice by activating AHR signaling in the intestinal epithelium and suppressing TLR4-mediated inflammation. Neonatal C57BL/6 mice were subjected to the experimental model of necrotizing enterocolitis as described in Example 1. One group of pups was fed a standard formula supplemented with 5% human breast milk obtained from a single donor, while another group was fed formula alone. A third group of pups was maintained on maternal nursing as a healthy control. On postnatal day 4, the pups were euthanized and intestinal tissues were harvested for analysis. The expression of CYP1A1 was measured in the ileum of the pups and found to be significantly elevated in the group fed breast milk-supplemented formula compared to the group fed formula alone (p = 0.0026), indicating that breast milk contains bioactive AHR agonists capable of activating the AHR pathway in the neonatal intestine. The expression of interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha was significantly reduced in the intestinal tissue of pups fed breast milk-supplemented formula (p = 0.0070 and p = 0.0325, respectively). The histological severity of necrotizing enterocolitis was significantly improved in the breast milk-fed group, with reduced mucosal necrosis, preserved crypt architecture, and lower blinded necrotizing enterocolitis severity scores (p = 0.0023). To confirm that the protective effect of breast milk is mediated by AHR, the experiment was repeated in AHR knockout (Ahr-/-) mice. In Ahr-/- pups, breast milk supplementation failed to induce CYP1A1 expression and provided no protection against necrotizing enterocolitis, as evidenced by histological injury, cytokine expression, and severity scores that were indistinguishable from those of pups fed formula alone. These findings demonstrate that human breast milk contains AHR agonists that activate the AHR pathway in the neonatal intestinal epithelium, leading to suppression of TLR4 signaling and protection against necrotizing enterocolitis, and that this protective effect is entirely dependent on AHR expression in the intestinal epithelium. The results further support the hypothesis that the protective effect of breastfeeding against necrotizing enterocolitis is, at least in part, mediated through AHR activation, and provide a mechanistic basis for the clinical observation that breastfed infants have a significantly lower incidence of necrotizing enterocolitis than formula-fed infants.
