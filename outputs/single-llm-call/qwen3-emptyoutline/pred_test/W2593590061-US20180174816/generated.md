# DESCRIPTION

## MR-TOF with Frequent Pulsing

The invention pertains to a multi-reflecting time-of-flight mass spectrometer configured for high-resolution mass analysis through the application of encoded frequent pulsing of an orthogonal accelerator. This configuration fundamentally alters the duty cycle and dynamic range of the mass spectrometer by systematically sampling a continuous ion beam at precisely controlled, non-repeating time intervals, thereby enabling the acquisition of multiplexed mass spectra that can be uniquely decoded without spectral ambiguity. Unlike conventional orthogonal acceleration schemes that operate at fixed, low-frequency pulses to avoid spectral overlap, this system deliberately introduces a sequence of pulses with time intervals that are mathematically distinct from one another, ensuring that any overlap between ion signals from different m/z species occurs only by chance and not systematically. This approach transforms what would otherwise be an unresolvable spectral interference into a recoverable multiplexed signal, analogous to the principles of Hadamard encoding in spectroscopy but adapted to the unique constraints of high-resolution time-of-flight mass spectrometry.

The core of the invention lies in the implementation of a pulsed orthogonal accelerator positioned upstream of a multi-reflecting time-of-flight analyzer, where the accelerator is driven by a programmable high-voltage waveform generator capable of producing pulse sequences with intervals defined by a non-linear, non-repeating mathematical function. In a preferred embodiment, the time intervals between successive pulses are determined by the formula T_i = T_D × i × (i−1)/2, where T_D is a base temporal unit and i represents the pulse number in the sequence. This quadratic progression ensures that the difference between any two pulse intervals is unique across the entire sequence, thereby eliminating the possibility of systematic aliasing between ion packets originating from different mass species. The resulting multiplexed time-of-flight spectrum contains overlapping ion arrival events, but because the temporal structure of the pulsing is known a priori, a decoding algorithm can reconstruct the original mass spectrum by testing all possible combinations of ion peaks that are consistent with the known pulse timing and the expected mass-to-charge relationships.

The multi-reflecting time-of-flight analyzer itself comprises a pair of planar, gridless electrostatic ion mirrors separated by a drift region, with the ion trajectory folded through multiple reflections to extend the effective flight path to tens of meters within a compact instrument housing. The ion mirrors are designed to provide third-order isochronicity with respect to ion kinetic energy and spatial focusing in the transverse plane, minimizing aberrations that would otherwise broaden ion peaks and reduce mass resolution. The drift region is further equipped with a periodic lens array that confines the ion packet along its zigzag trajectory, ensuring that each ion packet completes a predetermined number of reflections before reaching the detector. However, in this invention, the periodic lens array is not used to fix the number of reflections per ion packet, but rather to stabilize the ion packet’s spatial profile during transit, allowing the system to maintain high resolution even as the ion packet density increases due to the higher duty cycle enabled by frequent pulsing.

The orthogonal accelerator is positioned such that the continuous ion beam from the ion source is directed perpendicular to the plane of the ion mirrors, thereby minimizing the initial spatial and angular divergence of ions entering the analyzer. This geometry, known as double orthogonal acceleration, ensures that the ion packet is launched with minimal emittance in the direction of the drift axis, reducing the contribution of initial time spread to peak broadening. The ion source may be any continuous source, including electrospray, atmospheric pressure chemical ionization, or conditioned glow discharge, and is operated under conditions that produce a steady flux of ions with minimal fragmentation. The orthogonal accelerator applies a short-duration, high-voltage pulse to a set of electrostatic lenses, accelerating a slice of the continuous ion beam into the analyzer at precisely controlled intervals. The duration of each pulse is kept short—on the order of nanoseconds—to ensure that the ion packet remains spatially compact as it traverses the analyzer, preserving the high mass resolution inherent to the multi-reflecting design.

The detector is a microchannel plate-based time-to-digital converter capable of recording the arrival time of individual ions with sub-nanosecond precision and a dynamic range sufficient to accommodate the increased signal intensity resulting from the higher duty cycle. The data acquisition system is synchronized with the pulse generator and records the full time-of-flight trace for each pulse sequence, storing the data in a format that preserves the temporal relationship between ion arrivals and the corresponding pulse number. The decoding algorithm, implemented in software, operates by constructing a hypothesis matrix that tests every possible combination of ion peaks that could have contributed to the observed multiplexed signal. For each candidate mass-to-charge ratio, the algorithm evaluates whether the observed signal intensities at various time points are consistent with the known pulse timing sequence and whether the intensities follow a physically plausible distribution consistent with the expected ion abundance and detector response. The algorithm further checks for consistency across multiple pulse sequences, rejecting hypotheses that produce conflicting intensity patterns or that violate the uniqueness constraint of the pulse interval function.

The result of this process is a reconstructed mass spectrum with a signal-to-noise ratio that is proportional to the square root of the number of pulses in the sequence, effectively increasing the sensitivity of the instrument by up to two orders of magnitude compared to conventional single-pulse operation. This sensitivity enhancement is not merely a function of increased ion sampling but is directly attributable to the elimination of spectral overlap artifacts through the use of non-repeating pulse intervals. The method is particularly effective for analyzing complex mixtures where the number of detected species is large and the relative abundance of minor components is low, as the high mass resolution of the multi-reflecting analyzer ensures that even closely spaced isobaric species are resolved, reducing the likelihood of false overlaps during decoding. Furthermore, because the pulse sequence is encoded and known, the system can be operated in a mode where the pulse frequency is increased beyond the point where spectral overlap would be unresolvable in conventional systems, thereby maximizing the utilization of the continuous ion beam and achieving duty cycles approaching 25% or higher, compared to the typical 0.1–0.5% of conventional orthogonal acceleration systems.

The invention also enables the tracking of ion flow dynamics with unprecedented temporal resolution. Because each ion packet is tagged with a unique pulse identifier, the intensity profile of any given m/z species can be reconstructed as a function of time with a time resolution equal to the pulse interval, which may be as short as 10 microseconds. This capability allows for the real-time monitoring of chromatographic elution profiles, surface desorption events, or transient ionization phenomena without the need for external time-stamping or synchronization. In applications such as imaging mass spectrometry or rapid screening of biological samples, this feature provides a direct link between spatial or temporal variation in ion abundance and the corresponding mass spectral signature, enabling the construction of high-fidelity 2D or 3D chemical maps with sub-second temporal resolution.

The system is further enhanced by the ability to switch between single-pulse and encoded frequent pulsing modes based on the complexity of the sample and the desired analytical outcome. For samples with low ion abundance or when high dynamic range is not required, the system may operate in single-pulse mode to produce a conventional, non-multiplexed spectrum. For complex mixtures or when detecting trace analytes in a high-background matrix, the system automatically engages the encoded frequent pulsing mode, thereby optimizing the trade-off between sensitivity, dynamic range, and data acquisition time. The transition between modes is seamless and controlled by the data system, which evaluates the total ion current and peak density in real time to determine the optimal acquisition strategy.

The invention also extends to the use of cylindrical or quasi-planar ion mirror geometries, where the extended flight path further enhances mass resolution without compromising the benefits of frequent pulsing. In such configurations, the ion packet may undergo over 100 reflections, achieving resolving powers exceeding 500,000, and the encoded pulsing scheme ensures that the increased signal intensity does not lead to saturation or loss of resolution due to space charge effects. The system is further compatible with open trap configurations, where the absence of periodic lenses allows ion packets to undergo variable numbers of reflections, producing a broader, multiplexed signal envelope that can be decoded using similar algorithms. The combination of encoded frequent pulsing with these advanced ion mirror designs represents a fundamental advancement in the field of mass spectrometry, enabling the simultaneous achievement of high resolution, high sensitivity, high dynamic range, and high temporal resolution—all previously considered mutually exclusive objectives.

The practical implementation of this invention requires the integration of high-speed digital electronics, precision high-voltage pulse generation, and sophisticated signal processing algorithms, all of which are now commercially feasible. The system can be manufactured as a standalone mass spectrometer or retrofitted into existing multi-reflecting time-of-flight platforms with minimal modification to the ion optics. The decoding algorithm is implemented in software that runs on standard computing hardware and is capable of processing large datasets in real time, making the system suitable for high-throughput applications in proteomics, metabolomics, environmental analysis, and clinical diagnostics. The invention thus provides a novel and non-obvious solution to the long-standing limitations of orthogonal acceleration in high-resolution time-of-flight mass spectrometry, enabling a new paradigm of mass spectral acquisition that is both more efficient and more informative than any prior art.

## Comprehensive MS-MS (C-MS-MS)

The invention introduces a comprehensive tandem mass spectrometry system that enables the simultaneous fragmentation and detection of all precursor ions within a broad mass range, without the need for sequential scanning or selective isolation of individual parent species. This system, referred to as Comprehensive MS-MS or C-MS-MS, integrates a high-resolution multi-reflecting time-of-flight mass analyzer with a novel ion fragmentation and detection architecture that preserves the integrity of precursor ion information while enabling the parallel acquisition of fragment ion spectra for every ion species present in the sample. Unlike conventional tandem mass spectrometers that isolate one precursor ion at a time and sequentially fragment and detect its products, this system operates in a fully parallel mode, where all ions are fragmented simultaneously and their fragment ions are detected in a single, multiplexed time-of-flight event, with the identity of each fragment spectrum recovered through advanced decoding algorithms that correlate fragment ion arrival times with the known timing of precursor ion ejection.

The system comprises a first stage ion separator, which may be a quadrupole filter, an ion mobility separator, or a linear ion trap array, configured to transmit a broad mass window of precursor ions without selective isolation. These ions are then directed into a fragmentation cell that operates in a vacuum environment, eliminating the need for collisional gas and instead employing surface-induced dissociation on a chemically modified surface coated with non-volatile fluorinated polymers. The surface is maintained at a controlled potential and is pulsed synchronously with the ejection of precursor ions from the first stage separator, ensuring that each ion packet impinges upon the surface at a precisely defined time and energy, thereby inducing fragmentation with high efficiency and reproducibility. The fragmentation process is non-thermal and produces fragment ions with minimal secondary fragmentation, preserving the structural information of the parent ions. The resulting fragment ions are then extracted from the fragmentation cell by a pulsed electrostatic field and injected into a second multi-reflecting time-of-flight analyzer, which operates under the same principles of high-resolution ion reflection and encoded frequent pulsing as described in the previous section.

The key innovation of the C-MS-MS system lies in the synchronization and encoding of the fragmentation event with the ion ejection sequence. The first stage separator does not isolate individual ions but instead releases a continuous stream of precursor ions over a defined time window, during which the orthogonal accelerator of the second stage analyzer is pulsed at a high frequency with a unique, non-repeating time interval sequence. The fragmentation cell is triggered by a pulse generator that is phase-locked to the same timing source, ensuring that the surface-induced dissociation event occurs at a fixed delay after each precursor ion packet is released. This delay is calibrated to ensure that all fragment ions from a given precursor are generated within a narrow temporal window, allowing their time-of-flight to be accurately correlated with the identity of the parent ion. The fragment ions are then detected by a high-speed microchannel plate detector, and the resulting time-of-flight trace is recorded as a multiplexed signal containing contributions from all precursor ions and their respective fragments.

The decoding algorithm operates by first reconstructing the mass spectrum of the precursor ions from the multiplexed signal using the same principles of encoded frequent pulsing described earlier. For each identified precursor ion, the algorithm then examines the time-of-flight trace for the presence of fragment ions that appear at a consistent time delay relative to the arrival time of the precursor ion. Because the fragmentation delay is fixed and known, the expected arrival time of each fragment ion can be calculated based on its mass-to-charge ratio and the known kinetic energy imparted during the dissociation event. The algorithm then searches for all possible fragment ion combinations that are consistent with the known precursor mass, the expected fragmentation pathways, and the measured time delays. This process is repeated for every precursor ion in the spectrum, generating a complete set of fragment ion spectra for all detected parent ions in a single acquisition cycle.

The system is capable of operating with precursor ion mass windows as wide as 20 to 30 atomic mass units, yet still achieves a parent ion mass resolution of better than 1 amu in the reconstructed fragment spectra, due to the high temporal precision of the time-of-flight measurement and the high mass accuracy of the multi-reflecting analyzer. This capability eliminates the need for narrow, sequential precursor isolation, thereby increasing the duty cycle of the MS-MS analysis by more than an order of magnitude compared to conventional Q-TOF or triple quadrupole systems. The system is particularly effective for the analysis of complex mixtures such as protein digests, natural products, or environmental samples, where the number of potential precursor ions is large and the abundance of individual species is low. In such cases, the C-MS-MS system can acquire comprehensive fragmentation data for thousands of ions in a single scan, enabling the identification of low-abundance species that would be missed by conventional methods due to insufficient sampling time.

The fragmentation efficiency of the surface-induced dissociation cell is enhanced by the use of a dual-sided, elongated detection surface that maximizes the geometric acceptance of incoming ions and minimizes losses due to angular divergence. The surface is coated with a thin layer of fluorinated polymer that has been engineered to promote efficient energy transfer upon ion impact while minimizing adsorption or chemical modification of the fragment ions. The coating is maintained under ultra-high vacuum conditions to prevent contamination and is periodically regenerated through in situ plasma cleaning to ensure long-term stability. The absence of collisional gas in the fragmentation cell eliminates the need for pressure control, reduces the risk of ion-molecule reactions, and prevents the dilution of the ion flux, thereby preserving the sensitivity and dynamic range of the system.

The C-MS-MS system is further enhanced by the integration of a data-dependent acquisition protocol that automatically adjusts the width of the precursor ion window and the frequency of the orthogonal accelerator pulses based on the complexity of the sample. For samples with low ion abundance, the system operates with a narrow mass window and a lower pulse frequency to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio. For complex samples, the system expands the mass window and increases the pulse frequency to capture as many precursor ions as possible, while the decoding algorithm compensates for the increased spectral density by applying more stringent filtering criteria based on ion intensity, mass accuracy, and fragmentation consistency. The system also includes a machine learning module that learns the fragmentation patterns of common compound classes over time, improving the accuracy of spectral assignment and reducing the rate of false positives.

The practical implementation of the C-MS-MS system requires the precise synchronization of multiple high-speed electronic systems, including the ion separator, the fragmentation cell trigger, the orthogonal accelerator, and the data acquisition system. All components are controlled by a central timing unit that generates a master clock signal distributed with sub-nanosecond precision to each subsystem, ensuring that the temporal relationships between ion ejection, fragmentation, and detection are maintained with the required accuracy. The system is housed in a single vacuum chamber to minimize ion losses during transfer and is equipped with a multi-stage ion guide system that maintains the ion flux from the source to the detector with minimal diffusion or scattering.

The invention provides a transformative advancement in tandem mass spectrometry by enabling the acquisition of comprehensive fragmentation data for all ions in a sample in a single, rapid measurement. This capability fundamentally changes the paradigm of MS-MS analysis, shifting from a targeted, sequential approach to a global, all-mass approach that is both more sensitive and more informative. The system is particularly well-suited for applications in proteomics, metabolomics, lipidomics, and forensic analysis, where the identification of unknown compounds and the detection of trace analytes in complex matrices are critical. The ability to recover fragment spectra for ions present at femtogram levels, without the need for prior knowledge of their identity, represents a significant leap forward in the field of analytical chemistry and opens new possibilities for discovery-driven research.

## Parallel Mass Separators

The invention introduces a novel architecture for mass spectrometry that employs multiple, simultaneously operating mass separators arranged in parallel to increase the overall throughput, sensitivity, and dynamic range of the system. Unlike conventional tandem mass spectrometers that rely on sequential isolation and fragmentation of precursor ions, this system utilizes an array of independent mass separation stages, each capable of processing a distinct subset of the incoming ion population, thereby enabling the concurrent analysis of multiple mass ranges or ion populations without interference or loss of signal. The parallel mass separators are integrated into a single instrument platform, sharing common ion sources, vacuum systems, and data acquisition electronics, while maintaining independent ion optical paths to prevent cross-talk and ensure the integrity of each separation channel.

Each mass separator in the array is configured as a linear ion trap, a quadrupole filter, or an ion mobility drift cell, depending on the desired separation mechanism. In a preferred embodiment, the array consists of thirty to fifty linear ion traps arranged coaxially within a single vacuum chamber, each trap having a length of 10 to 30 centimeters and operated under conditions that allow for the accumulation and rapid ejection of ions with a mass resolution of 30 to 50. The traps are sequentially loaded with ions from a continuous ion source via a common ion funnel and RF ion guide system, and each trap is independently controlled by a dedicated set of DC and RF electrodes that enable mass-selective ejection based on opposing quadrupolar fields. The ejection timing of each trap is staggered in time to prevent overlap of ion packets, and the ejection sequence is synchronized with the pulsing of a downstream multi-reflecting time-of-flight analyzer operating under the encoded frequent pulsing scheme described in prior sections.

The ion traps are arranged such that their ejection axes are aligned with the entrance of a single multi-reflecting time-of-flight analyzer, which serves as the common detector for all separation channels. The time-of-flight analyzer is configured to receive ion packets from each trap in rapid succession, with each packet arriving at a precisely defined time interval determined by the ejection sequence of the trap array. The orthogonal accelerator of the time-of-flight analyzer is pulsed at a high frequency with a unique, non-repeating time interval sequence, ensuring that the ion packets from each trap are tagged with a unique temporal signature. The resulting multiplexed time-of-flight signal contains contributions from all traps, but because the ejection sequence is known and the pulse timing is encoded, a decoding algorithm can separate the signal into individual mass spectra corresponding to each trap.

The system is capable of handling ion fluxes approaching 10^9 ions per second, a level that would overwhelm a single ion trap or quadrupole filter due to space charge effects and detector saturation. By distributing the ion load across multiple traps, each trap operates well below its space charge limit, allowing for the accumulation of large numbers of ions without compromising resolution or dynamic range. The ejection of ions from each trap occurs on a millisecond timescale, with each trap releasing its stored ions in a single, high-intensity packet that is rapidly accelerated into the time-of-flight analyzer. The combination of high-capacity trapping and rapid ejection enables the system to analyze a broad mass range in a fraction of the time required by conventional sequential scanning methods.

In an alternative embodiment, the parallel mass separators are configured as ion mobility drift cells, each operating under a different electric field gradient or buffer gas composition to separate ions based on their collisional cross-section. The drift cells are arranged in parallel, with each cell having a length of 10 to 50 centimeters and a drift time of 1 to 10 milliseconds. Ions are introduced into each cell via a common ion funnel and are pulsed out in synchronized packets that are directed into the same multi-reflecting time-of-flight analyzer. The mobility separation is encoded with a non-uniform pulse sequence, similar to the encoded frequent pulsing scheme, allowing the system to resolve multiple mobility states for each mass-to-charge ratio. The resulting data provides a two-dimensional separation space of mass and mobility, enabling the identification of isomeric species that cannot be distinguished by mass alone.

The system further includes a data acquisition and processing unit that correlates the arrival time of each ion packet with the identity of the separator that produced it, reconstructing the mass spectrum for each channel independently. The algorithm accounts for variations in ion transmission efficiency between channels, applies calibration factors based on internal standards, and corrects for any residual temporal overlap between adjacent ejection events. The output of the system is a set of parallel mass spectra, each corresponding to a specific separator, that can be analyzed individually or combined into a composite spectrum for comprehensive analysis.

The invention provides a significant advantage over prior art systems by eliminating the sequential bottleneck inherent in conventional tandem mass spectrometers. Instead of spending time isolating one precursor ion at a time, the system simultaneously processes hundreds of ions across multiple channels, dramatically increasing the speed and efficiency of analysis. The parallel architecture also enhances the dynamic range of the system, as the ion load is distributed across multiple detectors, preventing saturation and allowing for the detection of both high-abundance and low-abundance species in the same analysis. The system is particularly well-suited for applications requiring high-throughput analysis, such as clinical diagnostics, proteomics, and environmental monitoring, where the ability to analyze large numbers of samples rapidly and accurately is essential.

The integration of the parallel mass separators with the encoded frequent pulsing scheme of the time-of-flight analyzer further enhances the system’s capabilities, enabling the acquisition of high-resolution fragment ion spectra for each precursor ion in each channel. The system can be operated in a variety of modes, including full-scan MS, MS-MS, and IMS-MS, and can be configured to switch between modes automatically based on the characteristics of the sample. The invention thus represents a fundamental advancement in mass spectrometry by enabling the simultaneous, parallel analysis of multiple ion populations with high resolution, high sensitivity, and high throughput, all within a single, integrated instrument platform.

## Resistive Ion Guides

The invention introduces a novel class of ion transport devices known as resistive ion guides, which are designed to efficiently transmit, focus, and stabilize ion beams over extended distances without the use of radio-frequency fields, thereby eliminating the limitations associated with traditional RF quadrupole ion guides. Unlike conventional RF ion guides, which rely on dynamic electric fields to confine ions radially and prevent their loss to the walls of the transport channel, resistive ion guides utilize a combination of static electric fields and controlled surface conductivity to achieve ion confinement and guidance. This approach enables the transport of high ion fluxes with minimal space charge expansion, reduced ion-molecule reactions, and improved transmission efficiency, particularly in environments where RF fields are undesirable or impractical.

The resistive ion guide is constructed as a cylindrical or planar tube composed of a dielectric material with a thin, semi-conductive coating applied to its inner surface. The coating is made from a material such as doped diamond-like carbon, indium tin oxide, or a polymer-based conductive composite, chosen for its ability to maintain a stable surface conductivity under ultra-high vacuum conditions while resisting contamination and degradation over prolonged operation. The ends of the guide are connected to high-voltage power supplies that establish a longitudinal electric field gradient along the length of the guide, driving the ions from the ion source toward the mass analyzer. The surface conductivity of the coating allows for the redistribution of charge along the inner wall, creating a self-regulating electric field that counteracts the space charge repulsion of the ion beam and prevents its radial expansion.

The key innovation of the resistive ion guide lies in its ability to maintain a stable ion beam profile over distances exceeding one meter, even when transporting ion fluxes on the order of 10^9 ions per second. In conventional RF ion guides, the space charge of dense ion beams causes radial expansion, leading to ion loss and reduced transmission efficiency. This effect is exacerbated at higher pressures, where ion-molecule collisions further disrupt the beam. In contrast, the resistive ion guide actively compensates for space charge effects by allowing the surface to accumulate and redistribute charge in response to the local ion density. As the ion beam passes through a region of high ion concentration, the surface charge density increases locally, creating a repulsive electric field that pushes the ions back toward the center of the guide. Conversely, in regions of low ion density, the surface charge density decreases, allowing the ions to spread slightly and maintain a uniform beam profile. This feedback mechanism is entirely passive and does not require external control or feedback loops, making the system inherently stable and robust.

The resistive ion guide is particularly effective in applications where the ion source produces a continuous, high-flux beam, such as electrospray, conditioned glow discharge, or atmospheric pressure chemical ionization. The guide is positioned between the ion source and the first stage of mass analysis, such as a quadrupole filter, ion mobility separator, or orthogonal accelerator, and is designed to minimize the time ions spend in the transport region to reduce the likelihood of ion-molecule reactions or chemical degradation. The length of the guide is optimized based on the ion mobility and the desired transmission efficiency, with typical lengths ranging from 30 centimeters to 1.5 meters. The inner diameter of the guide is selected to balance the need for efficient ion confinement with the requirement for minimal ion-wall interactions, with diameters ranging from 2 to 10 millimeters.

The resistive ion guide is further enhanced by the incorporation of segmented electrodes along its length, which allow for fine-tuning of the electric field profile to compensate for variations in ion flux or to focus the beam at specific points. These electrodes are connected to independent voltage sources and are controlled by a feedback system that monitors the ion current at the output of the guide and adjusts the voltage on each segment to maintain optimal transmission. The system can be operated in either a constant voltage mode, where the field gradient is fixed, or in a dynamic mode, where the field is modulated in response to changes in ion flux, enabling the guide to adapt to varying sample conditions.

The invention provides a significant advantage over conventional RF ion guides by eliminating the need for radio-frequency power supplies, which are bulky, expensive, and prone to electromagnetic interference. The resistive ion guide operates with low power consumption, generates no RF noise, and is compatible with high-resolution mass spectrometers that require a quiet electrical environment. The absence of RF fields also eliminates the risk of ion fragmentation due to resonant excitation, preserving the integrity of fragile molecular ions. Additionally, the resistive guide is less sensitive to variations in gas pressure, making it suitable for operation across a wide range of pressures, from atmospheric to ultra-high vacuum.

The resistive ion guide is particularly well-suited for integration into multi-channel mass spectrometry systems, where multiple ion sources feed into a single analyzer. In such systems, the resistive guides can be arranged in parallel, each serving a separate ion channel, and can be operated independently to optimize transmission for each source. The guides can also be combined with the encoded frequent pulsing scheme and the parallel mass separator architecture described in prior sections, providing a high-efficiency, low-noise ion transport pathway that enhances the overall sensitivity and dynamic range of the system.

The practical implementation of the resistive ion guide requires the precise control of surface conductivity, which is achieved through careful selection of coating materials and deposition techniques. The coating is applied using physical vapor deposition, chemical vapor deposition, or spray-coating methods, and its conductivity is calibrated to a target value of 10^−6 to 10^−4 siemens per centimeter, depending on the operating pressure and ion species. The guide is housed in a vacuum-compatible enclosure and is baked out prior to operation to remove adsorbed contaminants that could alter the surface properties. The system is further equipped with a monitoring system that measures the ion current at the input and output of the guide, providing real-time feedback on transmission efficiency and enabling predictive maintenance.

The invention represents a fundamental advancement in ion optics by providing a novel, passive method for ion confinement and transport that is simple, reliable, and scalable. The resistive ion guide enables the efficient transmission of high-flux ion beams over long distances without the complexity and limitations of RF fields, making it ideal for next-generation mass spectrometers that demand high sensitivity, high throughput, and low noise. Its integration into multi-channel, high-resolution systems represents a significant step forward in the field of analytical chemistry, enabling the analysis of complex samples with unprecedented speed and accuracy.

## TOF Detectors

The invention introduces a novel time-of-flight ion detector system that combines high temporal resolution, extended dynamic range, and multi-channel detection capability to meet the demanding requirements of high-throughput, high-resolution mass spectrometry. Unlike conventional microchannel plate detectors or Faraday cups, which are limited in their ability to resolve closely spaced ion arrivals or to handle high ion fluxes without saturation, this detector system employs a hybrid architecture that integrates an array of high-speed, low-noise ion-to-electron converters with a distributed charge collection and time-stamping circuitry, enabling the simultaneous detection of multiple ion packets with sub-nanosecond precision and a dynamic range exceeding six orders of magnitude.

The detector is constructed as a planar or cylindrical array of microchannel plates, each with a diameter of 1 to 5 millimeters and a gain of 10^6 to 10^7, arranged in a grid pattern that covers the entire exit plane of the time-of-flight analyzer. Each microchannel plate is coupled to a dedicated charge-sensitive amplifier and a time-to-digital converter, which records the arrival time of each ion event with a resolution of less than 500 picoseconds. The amplifiers are designed with low input impedance and high bandwidth to minimize signal distortion and to ensure that the temporal profile of the ion arrival is preserved. The time-to-digital converters are synchronized with a master clock that is derived from the same timing source that drives the orthogonal accelerator and the encoded frequent pulsing sequence, ensuring that the arrival time of each ion is accurately tagged with the corresponding pulse identifier.

The key innovation of the detector system lies in its distributed architecture, which allows each microchannel plate to operate independently, thereby preventing the saturation and pile-up effects that occur in conventional single-channel detectors when exposed to high ion fluxes. In conventional systems, the entire ion beam is focused onto a single detector, and when the ion arrival rate exceeds the recovery time of the detector, multiple ion events are recorded as a single, unresolved pulse, leading to loss of information and reduced dynamic range. In this invention, the ion beam is spread across the array of detectors, with each detector receiving only a fraction of the total ion flux. This spatial distribution ensures that no single detector is overwhelmed, even when the total ion flux exceeds 10^8 ions per second. The system further employs a weighting algorithm that reconstructs the total ion current by summing the signals from all detectors, applying correction factors based on the relative sensitivity of each detector and its position relative to the ion beam center.

The detector array is further enhanced by the inclusion of a secondary, low-gain detector that operates in a linear mode to measure the total ion current over extended time periods. This detector is constructed from a conductive mesh or a segmented Faraday cup and is positioned behind the array of microchannel plates, where it collects the residual charge from ions that have passed through the primary detectors. The signal from this secondary detector is used to calibrate the gain of the primary detectors and to provide a continuous, real-time measurement of the total ion flux, enabling the system to adjust its acquisition parameters dynamically in response to changes in sample concentration.

The time-stamping circuitry is designed to handle the high data rate generated by the detector array, with each microchannel plate capable of producing up to 10^6 events per second. The system employs a field-programmable gate array to process the incoming signals in real time, applying a peak-finding algorithm to identify ion arrival events and to assign them to the correct mass-to-charge ratio based on the known pulse timing sequence. The algorithm also filters out noise events, such as dark current pulses or secondary electron emissions, by requiring that each detected event be accompanied by a signal above a predefined threshold and that it occur within a narrow time window corresponding to the expected arrival time of the ion packet.

The detector system is further equipped with a multi-layer shielding structure that minimizes electromagnetic interference and prevents the ingress of stray electrons or ions from external sources. The shielding is constructed from a combination of mu-metal, copper, and conductive polymer layers, and is grounded to the instrument chassis to ensure a stable reference potential. The entire detector assembly is housed in a vacuum-compatible enclosure and is cooled to reduce thermal noise and to maintain the stability of the microchannel plates over prolonged operation.

The invention provides a significant advantage over prior art detectors by enabling the detection of high-flux, high-resolution ion beams without saturation, pile-up, or loss of temporal resolution. The distributed architecture allows the system to handle ion fluxes that are orders of magnitude higher than those compatible with conventional detectors, while maintaining the sub-nanosecond timing precision required for high-resolution time-of-flight mass spectrometry. The system is particularly well-suited for use with the encoded frequent pulsing scheme and the parallel mass separator architecture described in prior sections, where the high ion flux and complex multiplexed signals demand a detector capable of resolving individual ion events with high fidelity.

The practical implementation of the detector system requires the precise alignment of the microchannel plate array with the ion beam exit plane, as well as the calibration of each detector’s gain and timing response. The system is equipped with an automated calibration routine that uses a reference ion species with a known mass-to-charge ratio to adjust the gain and offset of each detector, ensuring that the entire array responds uniformly to the same ion signal. The calibration is performed automatically at regular intervals and can be triggered manually in response to changes in operating conditions.

The invention represents a fundamental advancement in ion detection technology by providing a scalable, high-performance detector system that is capable of meeting the demands of next-generation mass spectrometers. Its integration into the overall system enables the full realization of the sensitivity, dynamic range, and throughput benefits of the encoded frequent pulsing and parallel mass separation architectures, making it possible to analyze complex samples with unprecedented speed and accuracy.

## Data System

The invention introduces a comprehensive data acquisition and processing system specifically designed to manage the high-volume, high-dimensional data streams generated by the multi-reflecting time-of-flight mass spectrometer operating under the encoded frequent pulsing, comprehensive tandem mass spectrometry, parallel mass separator, resistive ion guide, and high-resolution detector architectures described herein. Unlike conventional mass spectrometry data systems, which are optimized for low-throughput, single-channel acquisition and rely on simple peak detection and integration algorithms, this system is built upon a distributed, real-time processing architecture that enables the simultaneous acquisition, decoding, correlation, and analysis of multiplexed mass spectra across multiple channels, with full temporal and mass resolution preserved throughout the data pipeline.

The data system is composed of three primary components: a high-speed data acquisition module, a parallel processing engine, and an intelligent analysis and reporting module. The data acquisition module is implemented as a field-programmable gate array that receives the raw time-stamped ion events from the detector array at a rate exceeding 10^9 events per second. Each event is recorded as a 64-bit data packet containing the arrival time, the detector identifier, the pulse sequence number, and a timestamp synchronized to the master clock of the instrument. The data packets are buffered in a high-speed memory array and streamed to the processing engine via a high-bandwidth data bus, ensuring that no data is lost during acquisition.

The parallel processing engine is constructed from a cluster of multi-core processors and graphics processing units, each dedicated to a specific task in the data decoding pipeline. The first stage of processing involves the demultiplexing of the encoded frequent pulsing data, where the algorithm reconstructs the individual mass spectra for each pulse sequence by testing all possible combinations of ion peaks that are consistent with the known pulse timing function and the expected mass-to-charge relationships. This process is performed in parallel across multiple processors, with each processor assigned a subset of the pulse sequences to decode. The algorithm applies statistical filtering to reject false positives, using criteria such as peak intensity consistency, mass accuracy, and temporal coherence across multiple pulse sequences.

The second stage of processing involves the correlation of fragment ion spectra with their parent ions in the comprehensive MS-MS mode. The algorithm identifies all fragment ion peaks that appear at a consistent time delay relative to a candidate parent ion and tests whether the mass difference between the parent and fragment is consistent with known fragmentation pathways. The system includes a library of common fragmentation patterns for peptides, lipids, metabolites, and small molecules, which is used to guide the assignment of fragment spectra and to reduce the computational burden of the search. The algorithm also applies machine learning techniques to identify novel fragmentation patterns and to improve the accuracy of spectral assignment over time.

The third stage of processing involves the deconvolution of the parallel mass separator data, where the system separates the multiplexed signals from each trap or mobility cell into individual mass spectra based on the known ejection sequence and the encoded pulse timing. The algorithm accounts for variations in transmission efficiency between channels and applies calibration factors to correct for differences in detector response and ion transmission. The output of this stage is a set of independent mass spectra for each separator, which are then combined into a composite spectrum for comprehensive analysis.

The intelligent analysis and reporting module is responsible for the final interpretation of the data and the generation of analytical reports. The module includes a cheminformatics engine that assigns elemental compositions to detected ions based on their mass-to-charge ratio and isotopic pattern, using a database of known chemical formulas and a probabilistic model of isotopic abundance. The system also performs structural inference by matching the fragment spectra to a library of reference spectra and by applying de novo sequencing algorithms to identify unknown peptides and oligomers. The results are presented in a user-friendly interface that displays the mass spectra, chromatographic profiles, mobility plots, and 3D chemical maps in real time, with interactive tools for zooming, filtering, and annotating features of interest.

The system is further equipped with a data compression and storage module that reduces the size of the raw data by a factor of 10 to 100 without loss of analytical information. The compression algorithm identifies redundant or low-signal events and replaces them with statistical summaries, while preserving the high-resolution temporal and mass information required for accurate decoding. The compressed data is stored in a proprietary format that is optimized for rapid retrieval and analysis, and is compatible with standard data exchange protocols such as mzML and mzXML.

The data system is designed to operate in a closed-loop mode, where the analysis results are fed back to the instrument control system to enable adaptive acquisition. For example, if the system detects the presence of a low-abundance species of interest, it can automatically increase the pulse frequency or extend the acquisition time to enhance the signal. If the system detects a saturated detector or a high background, it can switch to a different acquisition mode or trigger a cleaning cycle. The system also includes a remote monitoring and diagnostic module that allows operators to control the instrument and view the data in real time from any location, using standard web browsers or mobile applications.

## SUMMARY

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention provides a comprehensive, integrated mass spectrometry system that achieves unprecedented performance in sensitivity, resolution, dynamic range, and throughput by synergistically combining multiple novel technologies into a single analytical platform. The system is built upon a multi-reflecting time-of-flight mass analyzer that employs planar, gridless electrostatic ion mirrors to fold the ion trajectory over tens of meters, achieving mass resolving powers exceeding 500,000 and mass accuracy at the sub-ppm level. The analyzer is coupled to a programmable orthogonal accelerator that operates under an encoded frequent pulsing scheme, where ion packets are ejected at non-repeating, mathematically unique time intervals to enable the acquisition of multiplexed mass spectra that can be uniquely decoded without spectral ambiguity. This approach increases the duty cycle of the instrument by up to two orders of magnitude compared to conventional systems, dramatically enhancing sensitivity and dynamic range while preserving high mass resolution.

The system further incorporates a comprehensive tandem mass spectrometry module that enables the simultaneous fragmentation of all precursor ions in a broad mass window, using a surface-induced dissociation cell that operates under vacuum conditions to avoid gas-phase reactions and ion dilution. The fragmentation event is synchronized with the ejection of precursor ions from a parallel array of linear ion traps or ion mobility separators, each of which processes a distinct subset of the ion population to prevent space charge overload and to enable high-flux analysis. The resulting fragment ions are detected by a high-resolution, multi-channel time-of-flight detector array that captures every ion event with sub-nanosecond precision and a dynamic range exceeding six orders of magnitude. The entire system is supported by a resistive ion guide that transports ions over extended distances without RF fields, minimizing ion loss, chemical degradation, and electromagnetic interference.

A distributed data acquisition and processing system is integrated into the platform to manage the high-volume, high-dimensional data streams generated by the instrument. The system employs a real-time decoding algorithm that reconstructs individual mass spectra from multiplexed signals, correlates fragment ions with their parent species, and assigns elemental compositions and structural identities to detected ions using cheminformatics and machine learning techniques. The system is capable of operating in multiple modes, including full-scan MS, MS-MS, IMS-MS, and 3D-tag analysis, and can adapt its acquisition parameters automatically based on sample complexity. The instrument is designed for high-throughput applications, with modular components that can be scaled to meet the demands of clinical, proteomic, or industrial analysis, and is supported by automated calibration, remote monitoring, and predictive maintenance features that ensure long-term reliability and minimal operator intervention.

The invention thus provides a complete and novel solution to the longstanding challenges of mass spectrometry, enabling the rapid, accurate, and comprehensive analysis of complex molecular mixtures with a level of performance that surpasses all prior art.
