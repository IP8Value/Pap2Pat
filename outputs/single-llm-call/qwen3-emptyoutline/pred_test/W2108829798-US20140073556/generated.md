# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular therapeutics for the treatment of inflammatory and neuroinflammatory disorders. More specifically, it concerns novel synthetic peptide compounds derived from the N-terminal domain of the human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra), which are capable of selectively binding to the interleukin-1 receptor type I (IL-1RI) and thereby inhibiting downstream pro-inflammatory signaling pathways without eliciting agonist activity. These compounds are designed to function as potent, low-molecular-weight antagonists of interleukin-1-mediated inflammation, with demonstrated ability to cross the blood-brain barrier and exert therapeutic effects in both peripheral and central nervous system inflammatory conditions. The invention further encompasses multimeric forms of these peptides, pharmaceutical formulations containing them, and methods of their administration for the treatment of diseases characterized by dysregulated interleukin-1 signaling, including but not limited to rheumatoid arthritis, type 2 diabetes mellitus, Alzheimer’s disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, lipopolysaccharide-induced neuroinflammation, and other conditions involving chronic or acute neuroinflammation. The compounds disclosed herein represent a significant advancement over existing biologic IL-1 antagonists due to their enhanced stability, reduced immunogenicity, improved pharmacokinetic profile, and superior penetration into the central nervous system, enabling systemic administration to achieve therapeutic concentrations in the brain parenchyma and cerebrospinal fluid.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

Inflammatory processes are fundamental to the body’s defense against infection and tissue injury; however, when dysregulated, they contribute to the pathogenesis of a wide array of chronic and degenerative diseases. Among the most potent mediators of inflammation is interleukin-1, a cytokine family composed primarily of IL-1α and IL-1β, which act through a common receptor complex consisting of IL-1 receptor type I (IL-1RI) and the interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein (IL-1RAcP). Upon ligand binding, this complex initiates intracellular signaling cascades involving nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways, leading to the transcriptional upregulation of numerous pro-inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6), chemokines, and adhesion molecules. This cascade is central to the development of acute inflammatory responses and, when sustained, to the tissue destruction observed in autoimmune and neurodegenerative disorders.

The critical role of IL-1 in disease pathogenesis has been extensively documented in conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis, where elevated levels of IL-1β in synovial fluid correlate with joint erosion and cartilage degradation; in type 2 diabetes, where IL-1β contributes to pancreatic β-cell apoptosis and insulin resistance; and in neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease and multiple sclerosis, where microglial-derived IL-1β drives chronic neuroinflammation, synaptic dysfunction, and neuronal loss. Despite the clear therapeutic rationale for inhibiting IL-1 signaling, clinical translation has been hampered by the limitations of existing biologic agents. The most widely used therapeutic, anakinra, is a recombinant form of the naturally occurring IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra), which acts as a competitive inhibitor by binding to IL-1RI without recruiting IL-1RAcP, thus preventing signal transduction. While anakinra has received regulatory approval for several autoinflammatory conditions, its clinical utility is constrained by a short plasma half-life requiring daily subcutaneous injections, poor penetration across the blood-brain barrier, high manufacturing costs, batch-to-batch variability, and the potential for immunogenic reactions due to its proteinaceous nature.

Moreover, the inability of anakinra and other large-molecule IL-1 inhibitors to effectively reach the central nervous system has precluded their use in neuroinflammatory disorders, despite compelling preclinical evidence that IL-1 blockade can mitigate cognitive decline, neuronal apoptosis, and behavioral deficits in animal models of Alzheimer’s disease, experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, and sepsis-associated encephalopathy. The blood-brain barrier remains a formidable obstacle for biologics, limiting their distribution to the brain parenchyma to less than 0.1% of the systemic concentration. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet medical need for a small-molecule, non-immunogenic, orally bioavailable, or systemically administered antagonist capable of achieving therapeutic concentrations in the central nervous system while maintaining high specificity and potency for IL-1RI. Previous attempts to develop small-molecule IL-1 inhibitors have yielded compounds with low affinity, poor selectivity, or off-target effects, and none have demonstrated the capacity to cross the blood-brain barrier in therapeutically relevant amounts. The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing a novel class of synthetic peptides derived from a precisely defined structural motif within the N-terminal region of IL-1Ra that mediates its interaction with the third immunoglobulin-like domain of IL-1RI. These peptides, termed Ilantide and its multimeric derivatives, exhibit nanomolar binding affinity for IL-1RI, effectively compete with IL-1β for receptor occupancy, inhibit downstream inflammatory signaling, protect neurons and pancreatic β-cells from cytokine-induced damage, reduce clinical severity in models of arthritis and neuroinflammation, and, most significantly, demonstrate robust penetration of the blood-brain barrier following systemic administration, thereby enabling the treatment of both peripheral and central inflammatory pathologies with a single, well-tolerated therapeutic agent.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of synthetic peptide compounds that function as high-affinity, selective, and non-agonistic antagonists of the interleukin-1 receptor type I (IL-1RI), thereby inhibiting the pro-inflammatory signaling cascade initiated by interleukin-1α and interleukin-1β. The core invention comprises a ten-amino acid peptide sequence, designated Ilantide, having the primary structure Ser-Gly-Arg-Lys-Ser-Ser-Lys-Met-Gln-Ala (SEQ ID NO: 1), which is derived from the N-terminal region of human IL-1Ra and is capable of binding to IL-1RI with an affinity comparable to that of native IL-1Ra and superior to that of IL-1β itself. This peptide, in its monomeric form (Ilantide-m), and in its multimeric form (Ilantide-t), composed of four Ilantide units linked to a lysine core, effectively competes with IL-1β for binding to IL-1RI, prevents the recruitment of IL-1RAcP, and consequently inhibits the activation of NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways, leading to the suppression of downstream inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α, IL-6, and nitric oxide. Importantly, the peptide does not induce any detectable agonist activity, even at supraphysiological concentrations, and exhibits no significant binding to unrelated immunoglobulin-like domains, confirming its specificity for IL-1RI.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the Ilantide peptide or its multimeric variants, formulated for systemic administration via subcutaneous, intravenous, intranasal, or oral routes, with enhanced stability conferred by chemical modifications such as N-terminal acetylation, C-terminal amidation, or incorporation of non-natural amino acids to resist proteolytic degradation. The multimeric form, Ilantide-t, demonstrates significantly enhanced potency in inhibiting IL-1β-induced apoptosis in pancreatic islets and in reducing clinical severity in models of collagen-induced arthritis and experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, without increasing toxicity or immunogenicity. Crucially, both monomeric and multimeric forms of Ilantide demonstrate the ability to cross the blood-brain barrier following systemic administration, achieving measurable concentrations in cerebrospinal fluid at levels sufficient to exert neuroprotective effects, as demonstrated by the amelioration of social memory deficits in amyloid-β-treated rodents, the attenuation of lipopolysaccharide-induced sickness behavior, and the delay in the onset and progression of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis. These effects are achieved without altering baseline social behavior or inducing systemic immunosuppression, indicating a targeted modulation of pathological inflammation rather than global immune suppression.

The invention further includes methods of treatment for a variety of inflammatory and neuroinflammatory conditions, including rheumatoid arthritis, type 2 diabetes mellitus, Alzheimer’s disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, multiple sclerosis, sepsis-associated encephalopathy, and other conditions characterized by elevated IL-1 signaling, wherein the method comprises administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of the Ilantide peptide or its multimeric variant. The invention further provides methods for the synthesis of these peptides using solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques, purification by gel filtration chromatography, and characterization by mass spectrometry and surface plasmon resonance, ensuring reproducible production of high-purity, well-characterized therapeutic agents. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in the therapeutic targeting of IL-1-mediated inflammation by providing a small, stable, brain-penetrant, non-immunogenic, and highly specific antagonist that overcomes the fundamental limitations of existing biologic therapies, enabling the effective treatment of both systemic and central nervous system inflammatory disorders with a single, scalable, and cost-effective agent.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Inflammation

Inflammation is a complex, evolutionarily conserved biological response orchestrated by the immune system to eliminate harmful stimuli, such as pathogens, damaged cells, or irritants, and to initiate tissue repair. While acute inflammation is a protective and self-limiting process, chronic inflammation arises when the initiating stimulus persists or when regulatory mechanisms fail, leading to sustained immune activation, tissue destruction, and functional impairment. The pathophysiology of chronic inflammation involves the persistent release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and reactive oxygen species, which recruit and activate immune cells, alter cellular metabolism, and disrupt tissue homeostasis. Among the most potent and pleiotropic mediators of this process is interleukin-1, a cytokine that acts as a master regulator of inflammation across multiple organ systems. IL-1β, in particular, is synthesized as an inactive precursor that requires cleavage by the inflammasome to become biologically active, and its release is tightly regulated under physiological conditions. However, in chronic inflammatory diseases, dysregulated inflammasome activation leads to excessive IL-1β production, which perpetuates a self-amplifying cycle of inflammation through the induction of secondary cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6, the upregulation of adhesion molecules on endothelial cells, and the recruitment of neutrophils and monocytes to sites of tissue injury.

In peripheral tissues, IL-1β contributes to the destruction of articular cartilage in rheumatoid arthritis, the apoptosis of pancreatic β-cells in type 2 diabetes, and the fibrotic remodeling of lung and liver tissue in chronic inflammatory diseases. In the central nervous system, IL-1β is primarily produced by activated microglia and astrocytes in response to neuronal damage, protein aggregates, or systemic inflammatory signals, and its sustained presence is associated with synaptic loss, impaired neurogenesis, and cognitive decline. The neuroinflammatory cascade initiated by IL-1β activates NF-κB and p38 MAPK pathways in neurons and glial cells, leading to the expression of inducible nitric oxide synthase, cyclooxygenase-2, and other enzymes that generate neurotoxic mediators. This process is implicated in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer’s disease, where amyloid-β plaques trigger microglial activation and IL-1β release, which in turn accelerates tau hyperphosphorylation and neuronal death. Similarly, in multiple sclerosis, IL-1β promotes T-cell infiltration across the blood-brain barrier, enhances demyelination, and inhibits remyelination by oligodendrocyte precursor cells. The therapeutic targeting of IL-1 has therefore emerged as a rational strategy for interrupting this self-sustaining inflammatory loop in both peripheral and central diseases. However, the clinical success of biologic IL-1 inhibitors has been limited by their inability to penetrate the blood-brain barrier, their short half-life, and their high cost of production. The present invention overcomes these limitations by introducing a synthetic peptide that retains the high specificity and antagonistic activity of IL-1Ra while possessing the physicochemical properties necessary for systemic delivery and central nervous system access, thereby enabling a unified therapeutic approach to diverse inflammatory conditions that were previously treated with disparate and often ineffective agents.

### Interleukins

Interleukins are a group of cytokines originally identified as mediators of communication between leukocytes, but now recognized as key regulators of immune responses, inflammation, hematopoiesis, and tissue repair across a broad spectrum of cell types. The interleukin-1 family, which includes IL-1α, IL-1β, IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra), IL-18, IL-33, and several other members, plays a central role in initiating and amplifying inflammatory responses. IL-1α and IL-1β are the primary pro-inflammatory ligands of this family, sharing approximately 26% amino acid sequence identity and exhibiting overlapping biological activities. Both isoforms bind with high affinity to the interleukin-1 receptor type I (IL-1RI), a transmembrane protein composed of three immunoglobulin-like domains, which upon ligand binding recruits the interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein (IL-1RAcP) to form a ternary signaling complex. This complex triggers the intracellular recruitment of adaptor proteins such as MyD88, leading to the activation of downstream signaling cascades involving interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinases, tumor necrosis factor receptor-associated factor 6, and ultimately the nuclear translocation of transcription factors such as NF-κB and AP-1, which drive the expression of hundreds of pro-inflammatory genes.

The biological effects of IL-1β are extensive and include the induction of fever, the stimulation of acute-phase protein synthesis in the liver, the promotion of T-cell activation and differentiation, the enhancement of endothelial permeability, and the sensitization of nociceptors to pain. In the central nervous system, IL-1β modulates synaptic plasticity, alters neurotransmitter release, suppresses neurogenesis in the hippocampus, and induces behavioral changes such as anorexia, lethargy, and social withdrawal, collectively termed “sickness behavior.” The physiological role of IL-1β is tightly regulated by endogenous inhibitors, the most prominent of which is IL-1Ra, a naturally occurring protein that binds to IL-1RI with an affinity similar to that of IL-1β but fails to recruit IL-1RAcP, thereby acting as a competitive antagonist. The balance between IL-1β and IL-1Ra is critical for maintaining immune homeostasis; an excess of IL-1β relative to IL-1Ra is a hallmark of numerous autoimmune and neurodegenerative diseases. Therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring this balance have traditionally relied on recombinant IL-1Ra (anakinra), monoclonal antibodies against IL-1β (canakinumab), or soluble decoy receptors (rilonacept), all of which are large-molecule biologics with significant limitations in terms of pharmacokinetics, immunogenicity, and tissue penetration.

The present invention introduces a novel class of synthetic peptides derived from the N-terminal region of IL-1Ra that function as high-affinity, non-agonistic antagonists of IL-1RI. Unlike full-length IL-1Ra, which interacts with multiple domains of IL-1RI and may sterically hinder the formation of the IL-1RI/IL-1RAcP complex through conformational changes, the Ilantide peptide targets a specific binding epitope within the third immunoglobulin-like domain of IL-1RI, allowing for a more precise and potentially more efficient blockade of IL-1β binding. This targeted interaction enables the peptide to achieve potent inhibition of IL-1 signaling without the need for extensive structural mimicry, resulting in a smaller, more stable molecule that retains high specificity and avoids off-target interactions. Furthermore, the synthetic nature of the peptide allows for chemical modifications that enhance its resistance to proteolytic degradation, increase its half-life in circulation, and improve its ability to cross biological barriers such as the blood-brain barrier. The discovery that a ten-amino acid sequence derived from IL-1Ra can recapitulate the full antagonistic function of the entire protein represents a paradigm shift in the design of cytokine inhibitors, demonstrating that high-affinity, functionally complete antagonists can be derived from minimal structural motifs rather than full-length proteins. This insight not only provides a new therapeutic agent but also opens the door to the development of a new generation of peptide-based inhibitors targeting other members of the interleukin family, including IL-18, IL-33, and IL-36, which share structural homology with IL-1β and utilize similar receptor complexes.

### Peptide Fragments of the Present Invention

The peptide fragments of the present invention are defined by their specific amino acid sequence, structural configuration, and functional activity as high-affinity, non-agonistic antagonists of the interleukin-1 receptor type I. The core peptide, designated Ilantide, comprises the sequence Ser-Gly-Arg-Lys-Ser-Ser-Lys-Met-Gln-Ala (SEQ ID NO: 1), which corresponds to residues 1 to 10 of the mature human IL-1Ra protein, as determined by structural modeling of the IL-1Ra–IL-1RI complex. This sequence was selected based on its location within the N-terminal region of IL-1Ra that makes direct contact with the Ig3 domain of IL-1RI, as evidenced by crystallographic studies of the ternary complex. The peptide is synthesized as a linear, unmodified chain, but may be chemically modified at the N-terminus, C-terminus, or side chains to enhance stability, bioavailability, or pharmacokinetic properties. Such modifications may include N-terminal acetylation, C-terminal amidation, substitution of methionine with norleucine to prevent oxidation, or replacement of lysine residues with arginine to reduce susceptibility to proteases. The peptide may also be cyclized via disulfide bridges or lactam linkages to further stabilize its conformation and increase resistance to enzymatic degradation.

In addition to the monomeric form, the invention encompasses multimeric constructs in which multiple copies of the Ilantide sequence are covalently linked to a polyvalent scaffold. The most preferred multimeric form, Ilantide-t, consists of four Ilantide units attached to a lysine core via amide bonds, resulting in a dendrimeric structure with a molecular weight of approximately 4,500 Daltons. This multimeric architecture enhances binding avidity through multivalent interactions with IL-1RI, leading to a significant increase in functional potency compared to the monomeric form, as demonstrated by its superior ability to inhibit IL-1β-induced apoptosis in pancreatic islets and to suppress clinical severity in models of arthritis and neuroinflammation. The multimeric form retains the specificity of the monomeric peptide, as confirmed by its lack of binding to unrelated immunoglobulin-like domains such as NCAM Ig1–Ig2, and does not induce any detectable agonist activity, even at concentrations exceeding 100-fold the Kd value for IL-1RI binding. Alternative multimeric forms may be constructed using scaffolds such as glutamic acid, ornithine, or synthetic polymers such as polylysine or dendritic polyglycerol, provided that the resulting construct maintains the spatial orientation necessary for IL-1RI engagement and does not introduce immunogenic epitopes.

The invention further includes analogs of Ilantide in which one or more amino acids are substituted with non-natural or D-amino acids to enhance metabolic stability. For example, substitution of L-lysine at position 4 or 7 with D-lysine may confer resistance to trypsin-like proteases, while substitution of methionine at position 8 with norleucine eliminates oxidative degradation. Substitution of serine residues with phosphoserine or hydroxyproline may modulate hydrogen bonding and receptor interaction kinetics. The invention also encompasses truncated variants of Ilantide, such as the eight-amino acid sequence Gly-Arg-Lys-Ser-Ser-Lys-Met-Gln (SEQ ID NO: 2), and extended variants such as the twelve-amino acid sequence Ser-Gly-Arg-Lys-Ser-Ser-Lys-Met-Gln-Ala-Pro-Thr (SEQ ID NO: 3), provided that these variants retain the ability to bind IL-1RI with a Kd of less than 100 nM and inhibit IL-1β-induced NF-κB activation by at least 50% at a concentration of 1 μM. The scrambled and reversed control peptides described in the experimental section, such as KQSAGKRSMS and AQMKSSKRGS, serve as negative controls and are explicitly excluded from the scope of the invention, as they lack biological activity and do not bind IL-1RI with measurable affinity. The invention further includes salts, solvates, hydrates, and prodrugs of the Ilantide peptide, such as acetate, hydrochloride, or phosphate salts, or esterified derivatives designed to enhance oral absorption, which are hydrolyzed in vivo to release the active peptide. All such variants, analogs, and derivatives are encompassed within the scope of the invention provided they retain the core functional properties of IL-1RI antagonism, non-agonism, and blood-brain barrier penetration.

### Synthetic Preparation

The synthetic preparation of the Ilantide peptide and its multimeric variants is accomplished through established solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques using the fluorenylmethyloxycarbonyl (Fmoc) protection strategy, which allows for high-yield, high-purity production under controlled conditions. The synthesis is performed on TentaGel resin or similar polyethylene glycol-polystyrene copolymer supports, which provide optimal swelling properties in organic solvents and facilitate efficient coupling and deprotection steps. The amino acid sequence is assembled from the C-terminus to the N-terminus, with each amino acid added in excess (3–5 equivalents) in the presence of activating agents such as HBTU or HATU and a base such as diisopropylethylamine to ensure complete coupling. The Fmoc group is removed after each coupling step using 20% piperidine in dimethylformamide, and the resin is washed thoroughly with dimethylformamide, dichloromethane, and methanol between steps to remove residual reagents and byproducts. After completion of the linear sequence, the peptide is cleaved from the resin using a cocktail of trifluoroacetic acid, water, and triisopropylsilane, which simultaneously removes side-chain protecting groups and releases the free peptide into solution.

For the preparation of the multimeric form, Ilantide-t, a lysine backbone is incorporated at the C-terminus of the synthesis, with the ε-amino group of lysine left unprotected during the final coupling steps. After cleavage and purification, four equivalents of the Ilantide monomer are coupled to the lysine core using standard peptide coupling chemistry, resulting in a tetrameric dendrimer with four identical peptide arms. The crude product is purified by gel filtration chromatography using Sephadex G-10 or similar size-exclusion resins, which separate the multimeric form from unreacted monomers, truncated sequences, and other impurities based on molecular size. The purified fractions are lyophilized and analyzed by high-performance liquid chromatography to confirm purity, which is consistently greater than 85%, and by mass spectrometry to verify molecular weight and sequence integrity. The monomeric form is similarly purified and characterized, with purity confirmed by analytical HPLC and tandem mass spectrometry. The final products are sterile-filtered, lyophilized, and stored under desiccated conditions at -20°C until formulation.

The synthetic process is scalable and amenable to Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards, allowing for the production of clinical-grade material in quantities sufficient for human trials. The use of synthetic methods, as opposed to recombinant expression systems, eliminates the risk of endotoxin contamination, batch-to-batch variability, and immunogenic modifications such as glycosylation or incorrect disulfide pairing that are common in biologics. Furthermore, the synthetic route allows for the incorporation of non-natural amino acids, isotopic labels, or fluorescent tags for pharmacokinetic studies without altering the core biological activity. The entire process is reproducible, cost-effective, and does not require the use of animal-derived components, making it suitable for large-scale manufacturing and long-term commercial production. The resulting peptide is chemically defined, structurally homogeneous, and free of impurities that could trigger immune responses, distinguishing it fundamentally from recombinant proteins such as anakinra and offering a significant advantage in terms of safety, consistency, and regulatory compliance.

### Compound of the Present Invention

The compound of the present invention is a synthetic peptide that functions as a selective, high-affinity, non-agonistic antagonist of the interleukin-1 receptor type I, and is characterized by its unique amino acid sequence, structural properties, and biological activity profile. The compound, designated Ilantide, has the primary structure Ser-Gly-Arg-Lys-Ser-Ser-Lys-Met-Gln-Ala (SEQ ID NO: 1), which is derived from the N-terminal region of human IL-1Ra and is capable of binding to IL-1RI with an equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd) of approximately 2.75 × 10⁻⁸ M, as determined by surface plasmon resonance analysis. This binding affinity is comparable to that of native IL-1Ra (Kd = 4.8 × 10⁻⁸ M) and significantly higher than that of IL-1β (Kd = 1.71 × 10⁻⁷ M), demonstrating that the peptide is not merely a weak mimic but a potent competitor for receptor occupancy. The compound does not induce any detectable intracellular signaling in cells expressing IL-1RI, including activation of NF-κB, phosphorylation of MAPKs, or secretion of TNF-α, IL-6, or nitric oxide, confirming its complete lack of agonist activity. The compound exhibits no binding to unrelated immunoglobulin-like domains such as NCAM Ig1–Ig2, confirming its specificity for IL-1RI and minimizing the risk of off-target effects.

The compound is synthesized chemically using solid-phase peptide synthesis and purified to a homogeneity of at least 85% by high-performance liquid chromatography, with identity confirmed by mass spectrometry and amino acid analysis. The compound is stable under physiological conditions for a period of at least 2 hours in plasma, as demonstrated by pharmacokinetic studies in rodents, and retains its biological activity after storage at -20°C for periods exceeding 12 months. The compound is capable of crossing the blood-brain barrier following systemic administration, achieving cerebrospinal fluid concentrations of approximately 125 ng/mL within 30 minutes of subcutaneous injection at a dose of 10 mg/kg, with a plasma-to-CSF concentration ratio of approximately 55. This level of penetration is unprecedented among peptide-based IL-1 antagonists and enables the compound to exert therapeutic effects in the central nervous system, including the amelioration of cognitive deficits in amyloid-β-treated rodents, the suppression of neuroinflammation in experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, and the attenuation of lipopolysaccharide-induced sickness behavior.

The compound is not a naturally occurring protein, nor is it derived from recombinant expression systems. It is a fully synthetic molecule with a defined chemical structure, free of post-translational modifications, and devoid of immunogenic epitopes that could trigger antibody formation in treated subjects. The compound is not a fusion protein, antibody, or biologic, and does not contain any non-peptide components such as Fc domains, albumin-binding moieties, or PEG chains. Its small size, lack of disulfide bonds, and absence of glycosylation distinguish it fundamentally from existing IL-1 inhibitors such as anakinra, canakinumab, or rilonacept. The compound is suitable for formulation in aqueous solutions, lyophilized powders, nasal sprays, or transdermal patches, and may be administered subcutaneously, intravenously, intranasally, or orally in combination with absorption enhancers. The compound demonstrates a favorable safety profile in preclinical studies, with no evidence of toxicity, organ damage, or immune activation at doses up to 100 mg/kg. The compound represents a novel chemical entity with no prior art disclosure, and its use as a therapeutic agent for the treatment of inflammatory and neuroinflammatory diseases constitutes a non-obvious and inventive advancement over existing therapies.

### Multimeric Compound

The multimeric compound of the present invention is a dendrimeric structure comprising four copies of the Ilantide peptide covalently attached to a central lysine scaffold, designated Ilantide-t, with the molecular formula C₄₆H₈₈N₁₈O₂₀ and a molecular weight of approximately 4,500 Daltons. This multimeric form is synthesized by coupling four equivalents of the Ilantide monomer to the ε-amino group of lysine using standard peptide coupling reagents, resulting in a symmetric, branched architecture with four identical peptide arms extending from a central core. The multimeric compound exhibits enhanced binding avidity for IL-1RI compared to the monomeric form, as demonstrated by surface plasmon resonance, where the association rate constant (kₐ) is increased and the dissociation rate constant (k𝒹) is decreased, resulting in a lower apparent Kd and prolonged receptor occupancy. This increased avidity translates into superior functional potency in biological assays, with the multimeric compound inhibiting IL-1β-induced apoptosis in pancreatic islets by 93% at a concentration of 8.68 × 10⁶ nM, compared to 49% inhibition by the monomeric form at a concentration of 8.68 × 10³ nM, indicating a 1,000-fold increase in potency.

The multimeric compound retains the specificity of the monomeric peptide, showing no binding to unrelated immunoglobulin-like domains such as NCAM Ig1–Ig2, and does not induce any agonist activity, even at concentrations exceeding 100-fold the Kd value for IL-1RI binding. The compound is stable in plasma for a period of at least 3 hours, as demonstrated by pharmacokinetic studies, and achieves cerebrospinal fluid concentrations sufficient to exert neuroprotective effects following systemic administration. The multimeric structure does not increase immunogenicity, as confirmed by the absence of anti-peptide antibody formation in treated animals over a 21-day treatment period, and does not induce complement activation, histamine release, or cytokine storm in vitro or in vivo. The compound is purified by gel filtration chromatography to remove unreacted monomers and truncated sequences, and is characterized by mass spectrometry, HPLC, and circular dichroism spectroscopy to confirm structural homogeneity and conformational stability.

The multimeric compound is particularly advantageous for the treatment of conditions requiring sustained receptor blockade, such as chronic arthritis or neurodegenerative diseases, where frequent dosing of the monomeric form may be impractical. The increased half-life and potency of the multimeric form allow for less frequent administration, improved patient compliance, and reduced manufacturing costs per dose. The compound may be further modified by conjugation to polyethylene glycol, albumin-binding peptides, or lipid moieties to further extend its circulation time, or by incorporation of cell-penetrating sequences to enhance uptake into specific cell types such as microglia or astrocytes. Alternative multimeric scaffolds, such as glutamic acid, ornithine, or synthetic dendrimers based on polylysine or polyglycerol, may be employed to generate variants with altered valency, spacing, or flexibility, provided that the resulting construct maintains the ability to bind IL-1RI with an affinity of less than 100 nM and inhibit IL-1β-induced signaling by at least 70% at a concentration of 1 μM. The multimeric compound represents a novel class of therapeutic agents that leverage multivalency to enhance potency without compromising specificity or safety, and its use in the treatment of inflammatory and neuroinflammatory diseases constitutes a significant advancement over existing monomeric peptide inhibitors and biologic antagonists.

### Methods

The methods of the present invention encompass the use of the Ilantide peptide and its multimeric variants for the inhibition of interleukin-1-mediated inflammation in a variety of pathological contexts, both peripheral and central. The method comprises the administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the compound to a mammalian subject in need thereof, wherein the compound is selected from the group consisting of the monomeric Ilantide peptide (SEQ ID NO: 1), the multimeric Ilantide-t dendrimer, and pharmaceutically acceptable salts or derivatives thereof. The administration may be performed via subcutaneous, intravenous, intranasal, intraperitoneal, or oral routes, with subcutaneous administration being the preferred mode due to its ease of use, predictable pharmacokinetics, and suitability for chronic dosing regimens. The compound may be administered as a single dose or as a series of doses over a period of days, weeks, or months, depending on the severity and chronicity of the condition being treated.

The method is applicable to the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis, wherein the compound is administered to a subject diagnosed with active disease, as evidenced by elevated C-reactive protein, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, or joint swelling, and results in a reduction in clinical arthritis score, synovial inflammation, and cartilage degradation. The method is also applicable to the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus, wherein the compound is administered to a subject with impaired glucose tolerance or insulin resistance, and results in improved β-cell survival, increased insulin secretion, and reduced fasting glucose levels. The method is further applicable to the treatment of neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease, wherein the compound is administered to a subject exhibiting cognitive decline, memory deficits, or elevated cerebrospinal fluid levels of IL-1β, and results in amelioration of social recognition deficits, reduction in microglial activation, and preservation of synaptic density. The method is additionally applicable to the treatment of multiple sclerosis, wherein the compound is administered to a subject with relapsing-remitting or progressive disease, and results in delayed onset of neurological symptoms, reduced demyelination, and improved motor function.

The method may be practiced in combination with other therapeutic agents, including corticosteroids, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs, insulin-sensitizing agents, or neuroprotective compounds, provided that the combination does not diminish the efficacy or safety of the Ilantide compound. The method may also be practiced in conjunction with diagnostic biomarkers, such as measurement of IL-1β levels in serum or cerebrospinal fluid, to identify subjects most likely to respond to treatment. The method is suitable for use in adult and pediatric populations, and may be adapted for use in veterinary medicine for the treatment of inflammatory conditions in companion animals. The method is not limited to the treatment of established disease but may also be employed prophylactically in high-risk individuals, such as those with a genetic predisposition to autoimmunity or those undergoing systemic inflammatory insults such as sepsis or major surgery. The method is distinguished from prior art by its use of a synthetic, small-molecule, brain-penetrant IL-1RI antagonist that achieves therapeutic concentrations in both peripheral tissues and the central nervous system, thereby enabling a unified therapeutic approach to a broad spectrum of inflammatory conditions that were previously treated with disparate and often ineffective agents.

For nasal formulations, the peptide is formulated as a spray containing 0.5 to 5 mg/mL of Ilantide in a buffered saline solution with penetration enhancers such as chitosan, cyclodextrin, or caprylocaproyl macrogol-8 glycerides to facilitate absorption across the nasal mucosa. The formulation may further include controlled-release matrices such as microspheres, liposomes, or hydrogels that release the peptide gradually over a period of 24 to 72 hours, reducing the frequency of administration. The formulation is free of preservatives such as benzyl alcohol or parabens, which may be toxic or immunogenic, and is manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practice conditions to ensure sterility, purity, and consistency. The formulation is suitable for single-dose or multi-dose vials, auto-injectors, or pre-filled syringes, and is packaged in light-resistant, airtight containers to protect the peptide from degradation. The formulation is stable for at least 12 months when stored at 2–8°C and for at least 6 months at room temperature, making it suitable for widespread clinical use. The formulation does not contain any animal-derived components, endotoxins, or immunogenic impurities, and is compatible with standard pharmaceutical handling and administration protocols.

### Dosage

The dosage of the Ilantide peptide or its multimeric variant for therapeutic use is determined based on the route of administration, the severity of the condition, the patient’s body weight, and the pharmacokinetic profile of the compound. For subcutaneous administration, the preferred dosage range is 0.1 to 10 mg/kg of body weight per day, with a more preferred range of 1 to 5 mg/kg per day, and the most preferred dosage of 10 mg/kg administered once daily. For intravenous administration, the dosage range is 0.05 to 5 mg/kg per day, administered as a single bolus or continuous infusion over 1 to 24 hours. For intranasal administration, the dosage range is 0.5 to 20 mg per dose, administered once or twice daily. For oral administration, the dosage range is 5 to 50 mg per dose, administered once or twice daily, with the compound formulated in enteric-coated capsules or nanoparticles to enhance absorption. The dosage may be adjusted based on clinical response, with higher doses used for acute conditions such as sepsis or experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, and lower maintenance doses used for chronic conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis or Alzheimer’s disease.

The dosage regimen may consist of a loading dose followed by maintenance doses, with the loading dose being 1.5 to 2 times the maintenance dose to achieve therapeutic concentrations more rapidly. For chronic conditions, the compound may be administered daily for a period of 7 to 28 days, followed by a maintenance phase of administration every other day or weekly, depending on the duration of pharmacological effect. The compound may be administered continuously for a period of weeks, months, or years, with periodic monitoring of inflammatory biomarkers such as C-reactive protein, IL-6, or cerebrospinal fluid IL-1β levels to guide dose adjustments. The compound is well-tolerated at all tested doses, with no evidence of toxicity, organ damage, or immune activation in preclinical models, and is suitable for long-term use. The dosage is not dependent on renal or hepatic function, as the compound is primarily cleared by proteolytic degradation and renal excretion of small fragments, and does not require dose adjustment in patients with mild to moderate renal or hepatic impairment. The dosage is suitable for use in adult and pediatric populations, with pediatric dosing calculated on a weight-based scale. The dosage is optimized to achieve cerebrospinal fluid concentrations of at least 50 ng/mL, which has been demonstrated to be sufficient to exert neuroprotective effects in animal models of neuroinflammation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The binding affinity of the Ilantide peptide for the interleukin-1 receptor type I was assessed using surface plasmon resonance analysis on a Biacore 2000 instrument. A CM4 sensor chip was activated with a mixture of N-hydroxysuccinimide and N-ethyl-N’-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide, and the Ilantide peptide, IL-1β, or IL-1Ra was immobilized on the chip surface by amine coupling at a density of approximately 500 resonance units. The recombinant ectodomain of IL-1RI, purchased from R&D Systems, was then injected over the sensor surface at concentrations ranging from 1 to 100 nM in running buffer containing 150 mM NaCl and 10 mM sodium phosphate at pH 7.4, with a flow rate of 5 μL/min and a temperature of 25°C. The association phase was monitored for 180 seconds, followed by a 600-second dissociation phase. The sensor surface was regenerated between cycles using a 10 mM glycine-HCl buffer at pH 1.5. The binding kinetics were analyzed using BIAevaluation software, and the equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd) was calculated from the ratio of the dissociation rate constant (k𝒹) to the association rate constant (kₐ). The Ilantide peptide bound to IL-1RI with a Kd of 2.75 × 10⁻⁸ M, which was comparable to the Kd of IL-1Ra (4.8 × 10⁻⁸ M) and significantly lower than the Kd of IL-1β (1.71 × 10⁻⁷ M). As a negative control, the neural cell adhesion molecule Ig1–Ig2 domain was injected over the Ilantide-coated surface and showed no detectable binding, confirming the specificity of the interaction. These results demonstrate that the Ilantide peptide is a high-affinity ligand for IL-1RI and is capable of competing with IL-1β for receptor occupancy.

### Example 2

The ability of the Ilantide peptide to inhibit IL-1β-induced activation of the nuclear factor κB pathway was evaluated using HEK-Blue IL-1β cells, a reporter cell line engineered to express a secreted embryonic alkaline phosphatase (SEAP) gene under the control of an NF-κB-responsive promoter. Cells were seeded at a density of 3.5 × 10⁵ cells/mL in 96-well plates and incubated overnight at 37°C in 5% CO₂. The next day, cells were treated with 1.2 pM IL-1β alone or in combination with increasing concentrations of Ilantide-m, Ilantide-t, IL-1Ra, sIL-1RI, or scrambled control peptides. After 24 hours of incubation, 150 μL of supernatant was transferred to a new 96-well plate and mixed with 50 μL of QUANTI-Blue reagent (InvivoGen). The plates were incubated at 37°C for 40 minutes, and absorbance was measured at 600 nm using a microplate reader. The concentration of SEAP was calculated from a standard curve generated using known concentrations of purified SEAP. Ilantide-m and Ilantide-t dose-dependently inhibited IL-1β-induced SEAP expression, with IC₅₀ values of 1.2 nM and 0.8 nM, respectively. IL-1Ra and sIL-1RI showed similar inhibition, with IC₅₀ values of 0.9 nM and 1.1 nM, respectively. Scrambled peptides and the reversed sequence of Ilantide had no significant effect on SEAP expression at concentrations up to 100 nM. These results demonstrate that the Ilantide peptide is a potent and specific inhibitor of IL-1β-induced NF-κB signaling.

### Example 3

The inhibition of IL-1β-induced tumor necrosis factor-alpha secretion by macrophages was assessed using the murine alveolar macrophage cell line AMJ2-C8. Cells were seeded at a density of 2.5 × 10⁵ cells per well in six-well plates and incubated overnight. The next day, cells were pre-treated with Ilantide-m, Ilantide-t, IL-1Ra, sIL-1RI, hydrocortisone, or vehicle for 10 minutes, followed by stimulation with 1.2 pM IL-1β or IFN-γ for 24 hours. Conditioned medium was collected and added to L929 fibroblasts seeded at a density of 0.2 × 10² cells/mL in 96-well plates, along with 0.6 μg/mL actinomycin D to sensitize the cells to TNF-α-induced apoptosis. After 24 hours, 20 μL of MTT reagent was added to each well and incubated for 45 minutes at 37°C. The formazan crystals were solubilized, and absorbance was measured at 490 nm. The amount of TNF-α in the conditioned medium was calibrated against a standard curve generated using recombinant TNF-α. Ilantide-m and Ilantide-t significantly reduced TNF-α-induced cytotoxicity in L929 cells, with inhibition rates of 78% and 89%, respectively, at a concentration of 10 nM. IL-1Ra and sIL-1RI showed similar inhibition, with rates of 82% and 85%, respectively. Scrambled peptides had no significant effect. These results demonstrate that the Ilantide peptide effectively inhibits IL-1β-induced TNF-α production in macrophages, a key mediator of inflammatory tissue damage.

### Example 4

The neuroprotective effects of the Ilantide peptide were evaluated in primary cultures of cerebellar granule neurons isolated from 7-day-old Wistar rats. Neurons were plated on poly-L-lysine-coated chamber slides at a density of 1 × 10⁵ cells/cm² and maintained in Neurobasal-A medium supplemented with B27 and GlutaMAX for 7 days in high-potassium medium to promote survival. The medium was then switched to low-potassium medium to induce apoptosis, and cells were treated with Ilantide-m, Ilantide-t, IL-1Ra, IL-1β, or insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) as a positive control. After 48 hours, cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay, and neurite outgrowth was quantified by immunofluorescence staining with anti-β-tubulin III antibody and image analysis software. Ilantide-m and Ilantide-t significantly increased neuronal survival compared to untreated controls, with survival rates of 72% and 81%, respectively, compared to 45% in the untreated group. IGF-1 increased survival to 85%. IL-1β had no protective effect and induced further cell death. Ilantide-t and IL-1Ra also significantly increased the length and branching of neurites, with average neurite length increasing by 68% and 75%, respectively, compared to controls. These results demonstrate that the Ilantide peptide promotes neuronal survival and neuritogenesis, likely by blocking endogenous IL-1 signaling in the culture system.

### Example 5

The protective effect of the Ilantide peptide against IL-1β-induced apoptosis in rat pancreatic islets was evaluated by isolating neonatal islets from 3- to 6-day-old Wistar rats and culturing them in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 0.5% newborn calf serum. Islets were exposed to 150 pg/mL IL-1β in the presence or absence of Ilantide-m, Ilantide-t, or anakinra (Ana) for 24 hours. Apoptosis was quantified using a cell death detection ELISA, and nitric oxide production was measured using the Griess reaction. Ilantide-t inhibited IL-1β-induced apoptosis by 93% at a concentration of 8.68 × 10⁶ nM, surpassing the maximal inhibition achieved by anakinra (64% at 8.68 × 10⁴ nM). Ilantide-m inhibited apoptosis by 49% at 8.68 × 10³ nM. Neither Ilantide-m nor Ilantide-t significantly reduced nitric oxide production, whereas anakinra reduced nitrite levels by 78%. These results demonstrate that the Ilantide peptide protects pancreatic β-cells from IL-1β-induced apoptosis with greater efficacy than anakinra, but through a mechanism that does not involve inhibition of nitric oxide synthesis, suggesting a distinct mode of action.

### Example 6

The ability of the Ilantide peptide to cross the blood-brain barrier was assessed in adult male Wistar rats following subcutaneous administration of biotinylated Ilantide-t at a dose of 10 mg/kg. Blood samples were collected at 20, 60, 140, 300, and 1,800 minutes after injection, and cerebrospinal fluid was collected from the cisterna magna 30 minutes after injection. Plasma and CSF samples were analyzed using a competitive ELISA with biotinylated bovine serum albumin as the capture agent and streptavidin-peroxidase as the detection reagent. Ilantide-t was detected in plasma at 20 minutes at a concentration of 5,816 ± 44 ng/mL, with a half-life of approximately 2 hours. The concentration in CSF was 125 ± 13 ng/mL at 30 minutes, resulting in a plasma-to-CSF concentration ratio of 55. No Ilantide-t was detected in CSF samples from control animals injected with saline. These results demonstrate that the Ilantide peptide, in its multimeric form, efficiently crosses the blood-brain barrier following systemic administration, achieving concentrations in the central nervous system that are sufficient to exert biological effects.

### Example 7

The therapeutic efficacy of the Ilantide peptide was evaluated in a rat model of collagen-induced arthritis. Bovine type II collagen was emulsified in complete Freund’s adjuvant and injected intradermally at the tail base of male Wistar rats to induce arthritis. On day 8, before the onset of clinical signs, rats were randomized into two groups and treated daily with either Ilantide-t (10 mg/kg, subcutaneous) or vehicle (PBS, subcutaneous) for 8 days. Arthritis severity was scored daily using a standardized scale ranging from 0 to 4 per limb, with a maximum cumulative score of 8. The mean clinical score in the vehicle group increased from 0.8 on day 8 to 5.2 on day 16, whereas the Ilantide-t-treated group showed a significantly lower mean score of 2.9 on day 16 (p < 0.05). The area under the clinical score curve was reduced by 58% in the Ilantide-t group compared to the vehicle group. Histological analysis of joint sections revealed reduced synovial inflammation, cartilage erosion, and bone destruction in the Ilantide-t-treated animals. These results demonstrate that the Ilantide peptide significantly attenuates the progression of collagen-induced arthritis in vivo.

### Example 8

The effect of the Ilantide peptide on lipopolysaccharide-induced behavioral depression was assessed in rats. Animals were injected intraperitoneally with 250 μg/kg LPS or saline, followed immediately by subcutaneous injection of Ilantide-t (10 mg/kg), anakinra (100 mg/kg), or vehicle. Social interaction was measured over 4-minute sessions at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, and 24 hours after injection, with a juvenile rat introduced into the test cage. Social interaction time was recorded as the percentage of baseline values. LPS significantly reduced social interaction to 42% of baseline at 4 hours, whereas Ilantide-t restored interaction to 78% of baseline (p < 0.01). Anakinra had no significant effect. Ilantide-t also attenuated LPS-induced weight loss, with treated animals losing only 3.2% of body weight compared to 8.1% in the LPS/vehicle group. Plasma IL-10 levels were elevated in Ilantide-t-treated animals at 2 hours, while IL-6 and IFN-γ levels showed a trend toward reduction. These results demonstrate that the Ilantide peptide ameliorates neuroinflammation-induced behavioral deficits and promotes an anti-inflammatory cytokine shift in vivo.

### Example 9

The effect of the Ilantide peptide on amyloid-β-induced social memory deficits was evaluated in a rat model of Alzheimer’s disease. Aggregated Aβ25–35 peptide was injected intracerebroventricularly into rats, followed by daily subcutaneous injections of Ilantide-t (10 mg/kg) or vehicle for 19 days. On day 20, social recognition was tested using a three-trial paradigm with a 30-minute intertrial interval. The recognition ratio (RRfamiliar) was calculated as the time spent investigating the familiar juvenile during trial 2 divided by the total time spent investigating during trials 1 and 2. Control rats exhibited a RRfamiliar of 0.32, indicating intact social memory. Aβ/vehicle-treated rats had a RRfamiliar of 0.51, indicating memory impairment. Aβ/Ilantide-t-treated rats had a RRfamiliar of 0.38, significantly different from the Aβ/vehicle group (p < 0.05) and not different from controls. The RRnew for a novel juvenile was not different from 0.5 in any group, confirming that the effect was specific to memory and not due to changes in general sociability. These results demonstrate that the Ilantide peptide rescues social memory deficits induced by amyloid-β, likely by inhibiting neuroinflammation and preserving synaptic function in the hippocampus and related brain regions.
