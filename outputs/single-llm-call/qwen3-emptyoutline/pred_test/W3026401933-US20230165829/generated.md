# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of molecular oncology and targeted cancer therapeutics, specifically to novel compounds and methods for the inhibition of RAS GTPase activation in mammalian cells. More particularly, the invention provides a class of flavagline derivatives that selectively disrupt the interaction between prohibitin proteins and phospholipids at the plasma membrane, thereby preventing the guanine nucleotide loading of RAS isoforms, including KRAS, NRAS, and HRAS, regardless of oncogenic mutation status. The compounds of the invention are effective at nanomolar concentrations and exhibit potent anti-tumorigenic activity in both in vitro and in vivo models of RAS-driven cancers, including non-small cell lung carcinoma, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and colorectal carcinoma. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these compounds, methods of manufacturing them, and their use in the treatment of cancers characterized by aberrant RAS signaling, particularly those resistant to conventional kinase inhibitors or currently deemed undruggable due to the lack of accessible binding pockets on the RAS protein surface. The compounds of the invention operate through a novel mechanism of action distinct from existing RAS inhibitors, which typically target nucleotide-binding pockets or covalent modifications of specific mutant alleles, and instead modulate the spatial organization of RAS at the plasma membrane by interfering with prohibitin-mediated lipid scaffolding.

## BACKGROUND ART

RAS proteins are central regulators of intracellular signal transduction pathways that govern cell proliferation, survival, differentiation, and metabolism. As small GTPases, they function as molecular switches cycling between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. This cycle is tightly regulated by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs), which promote the exchange of GDP for GTP, and GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs), which accelerate the intrinsic GTP hydrolysis activity of RAS. Mutations in RAS genes, particularly at codons 12, 13, and 61, impair GAP-mediated GTP hydrolysis, resulting in constitutive activation of RAS and persistent downstream signaling through effectors such as RAF, PI3K, and RALGDS. These mutations are among the most frequent genetic alterations in human cancers, occurring in approximately 30% of all malignancies, with KRAS mutations dominating in pancreatic (over 90%), colorectal (40–50%), and non-small cell lung cancers (25–30%). Despite decades of research, RAS proteins have been historically considered undruggable due to their smooth, shallow surface topology and picomolar affinity for GTP/GDP, which renders traditional small-molecule inhibition strategies ineffective.

Recent breakthroughs have led to the development of covalent inhibitors targeting the KRAS G12C mutant, such as sotorasib and adagrasib, which exploit a unique cysteine residue introduced by the G12C substitution to form an irreversible bond in the switch-II pocket. These agents have demonstrated clinical efficacy in subsets of non-small cell lung cancer patients; however, their utility is severely limited by the narrow spectrum of targetable mutations, acquired resistance mechanisms, and feedback reactivation of wild-type RAS isoforms through receptor tyrosine kinase signaling. Moreover, these inhibitors are ineffective against other prevalent KRAS mutants such as G12D, G12V, and Q61H, as well as against NRAS and HRAS mutations, which are common in melanoma and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, respectively. Consequently, there remains a critical unmet medical need for broad-spectrum RAS inhibitors capable of suppressing all major oncogenic RAS variants irrespective of mutation type or isoform.

Several alternative strategies have been explored to circumvent the challenges of direct RAS inhibition. These include targeting upstream regulators such as receptor tyrosine kinases, downstream effectors such as MEK or ERK, synthetic lethal partners, or RAS membrane localization mechanisms. Among the latter, disruption of RAS nanoclustering at the plasma membrane has emerged as a promising avenue. RAS isoforms are post-translationally modified by farnesylation and, in some cases, palmitoylation, which facilitate their association with the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane. Within this membrane environment, RAS proteins organize into dynamic nanoscale signaling platforms known as nanoclusters, whose formation is critically dependent on specific phospholipids such as phosphatidylserine and phosphatidic acid. These lipid microdomains serve as essential platforms for the recruitment and activation of effector proteins, including CRAF and BRAF. Disruption of these lipid-RAS interactions has been shown to impair RAS signaling without altering total RAS expression or nucleotide content, offering a novel therapeutic window.

Prohibitins, specifically prohibitin 1 (PHB1) and prohibitin 2 (PHB2), are evolutionarily conserved, ubiquitously expressed proteins that form ring-like oligomeric complexes in the inner mitochondrial membrane, where they regulate cristae morphology and mitochondrial function. However, a significant fraction of prohibitins localizes to the plasma membrane, where they function as scaffolds for phospholipids and signaling proteins. PHB1 has been shown to directly bind to CRAF and is required for its full activation in response to RAS signaling. Moreover, prohibitins have been demonstrated to interact with phosphatidylserine and phosphatidic acid, suggesting a role in organizing lipid microdomains that facilitate RAS nanoclustering. Previous studies have identified small molecules such as fluorizoline and rocaglamide as inhibitors of the PHB1-CRAF interaction, which suppress RAS-driven tumorigenesis in preclinical models. However, these compounds were found to be ineffective against BRAF-mutant cancers, and their precise molecular targets and mechanisms of action remained incompletely defined. Furthermore, rocaglamide, a naturally occurring flavagline, was previously reported to also inhibit the eukaryotic initiation factor 4A (eIF4A), a key component of the mRNA translation machinery, raising concerns about off-target effects and complicating the interpretation of its anti-tumorigenic activity.

Despite these advances, no compound has been identified that selectively and potently inhibits RAS GTP loading across all major isoforms and mutants by specifically disrupting the prohibitin-phospholipid-RAS axis at the plasma membrane without relying on eIF4A inhibition or covalent modification of mutant residues. There remains a significant gap in the art for a class of compounds that can achieve broad-spectrum RAS inhibition through a novel, non-covalent, non-mutant-dependent mechanism that is effective in vivo, exhibits low toxicity, and is amenable to chemical optimization for clinical development.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of flavagline derivatives that inhibit RAS activation by disrupting the interaction between prohibitin proteins and phospholipids at the plasma membrane, thereby preventing the GTP loading of RAS isoforms. The compounds of the invention are structurally characterized by a cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core with specific substituents at positions 1, 3′, 4′, and 8 that confer potent and selective inhibition of RAS GTP loading across KRAS, NRAS, and HRAS isoforms, regardless of oncogenic mutation status. These compounds do not directly bind to RAS proteins, nor do they inhibit eIF4A, but instead stabilize a prohibitin-phospholipid complex in a conformation that is incompatible with RAS recruitment and activation. The invention demonstrates that the inhibition of RAS activation is directly correlated with the suppression of RAS nanoclustering at the plasma membrane, as evidenced by fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy (FLIM-FRET) and immunogold electron microscopy, and is reversed upon genetic depletion of prohibitin 1. The compounds of the invention exhibit an IC50 in the low nanomolar range (10–50 nM) in cellular assays measuring RAS-GTP loading, suppress anchorage-independent growth in multiple RAS-mutant cancer cell lines, and significantly reduce tumor burden in autochthonous mouse models of KRAS-driven non-small cell lung carcinoma without inducing detectable hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity at therapeutic doses.

The compounds of the invention are defined by the general structure of a cyclopenta[b]benzofuran scaffold substituted at position 8 with a hydrogen atom, a methoxy group, a bromine atom, a fluorine atom, or a chlorine atom. In a preferred embodiment, the compound is rocaglamide, wherein R8 is —H. In another preferred embodiment, the compound is a derivative wherein R8 is —OCH3. In a further preferred embodiment, the compound is a derivative wherein R8 is —Br. In yet another preferred embodiment, the compound is a derivative wherein R8 is —F. In a still further preferred embodiment, the compound is a derivative wherein R8 is —Cl. Each of these substituents at position 8 is associated with a distinct pharmacological profile, with —H and —OCH3 conferring the highest potency in inhibiting RAS GTP loading, while —Br, —F, and —Cl demonstrate enhanced metabolic stability and bioavailability in vivo. The compounds of the invention retain their activity against all major KRAS mutants, including G12D, G12V, G13D, and Q61H, as well as against NRAS Q61K and HRAS G12V, and are effective in cell lines harboring BRAF mutations, indicating a mechanism independent of downstream pathway activation status. The inhibition of RAS activation by these compounds is reversible, as demonstrated by the restoration of GTP loading upon exogenous addition of GTPγS to cell lysates, and is not associated with changes in total RAS protein levels, phosphorylation of upstream receptors such as EGFR, or alterations in the expression of eIF4A. The compounds of the invention do not induce apoptosis or cell cycle arrest at concentrations that fully inhibit RAS activation, suggesting that their primary mechanism of action is the suppression of oncogenic signaling rather than cytotoxicity.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more of the flavagline derivatives described herein, in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent. These compositions may be formulated for oral, intravenous, intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, or inhalation administration and may include additional therapeutic agents such as MEK inhibitors, PI3K inhibitors, or immune checkpoint inhibitors to enhance efficacy or overcome resistance. The invention also provides methods for the synthesis of the flavagline derivatives, including the selective modification of the cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core at position 8 through halogenation, methoxylation, or deoxygenation reactions, as well as methods for purifying and characterizing these compounds using high-performance liquid chromatography, mass spectrometry, and nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy. The invention further provides methods of treating a subject with a RAS-driven cancer by administering an effective amount of a compound of the invention, wherein the cancer is selected from the group consisting of non-small cell lung carcinoma, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, colorectal carcinoma, melanoma, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, ovarian carcinoma, and acute myeloid leukemia. The invention further provides methods for identifying subjects likely to respond to treatment with the compounds of the invention by detecting the presence of RAS mutations in tumor tissue or circulating tumor DNA, or by assessing the expression levels of prohibitin 1 and prohibitin 2 in tumor biopsies.

The invention further provides kits for the diagnosis or monitoring of RAS-driven cancers, comprising one or more of the compounds of the invention, instructions for use, and reagents for detecting RAS activation status, prohibitin expression, or phospholipid composition in biological samples. The invention further provides uses of the compounds of the invention in the manufacture of a medicament for the treatment of RAS-driven cancers. The invention further provides methods for screening for additional compounds that modulate the prohibitin-phospholipid-RAS axis by employing cellular assays that measure RAS-GTP loading, nanoclustering, or prohibitin-lipid binding in the presence of test compounds. The invention further provides methods for optimizing the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of the flavagline derivatives through structural modification of substituents at positions 1, 3′, 4′, and 8, including the introduction of alkyl, aryl, heteroaryl, amide, ester, or carbamate groups that enhance membrane permeability, metabolic stability, or target affinity without compromising selectivity for the prohibitin-phospholipid complex.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The compounds of the invention are synthesized through a multi-step organic synthesis protocol beginning with the preparation of the cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core via a Diels-Alder cycloaddition reaction between a substituted benzene derivative and a diene moiety, followed by regioselective functionalization at position 8 using electrophilic halogenation, Friedel-Crafts alkylation, or oxidative demethylation techniques. The substituent at position 8 is introduced either during the initial ring formation or through late-stage modification of a pre-formed flavagline scaffold. For example, when R8 is —H, the precursor compound is subjected to catalytic hydrogenation under controlled conditions to remove the methoxy group, yielding the deoxygenated flavagline derivative. When R8 is —OCH3, the compound is synthesized by methylation of a phenolic precursor using dimethyl sulfate or methyl iodide in the presence of a base such as potassium carbonate. When R8 is —Br, bromination is achieved using N-bromosuccinimide in acetic acid or bromine in carbon tetrachloride under light-controlled conditions to ensure monobromination at the desired position. When R8 is —F, fluorination is performed using Selectfluor or a Balz-Schiemann reaction sequence involving diazotization followed by thermal decomposition of the diazonium salt. When R8 is —Cl, chlorination is carried out using chlorine gas in the presence of a Lewis acid catalyst such as aluminum chloride or via electrophilic substitution using sulfuryl chloride. Each synthetic route is optimized to yield the target compound with a purity exceeding 98% as determined by HPLC and characterized by mass spectrometry and 1H/13C NMR spectroscopy to confirm the identity and stereochemistry of the substituent at position 8.

The compounds of the invention are formulated into pharmaceutical compositions suitable for administration to mammals, including humans, for the treatment of RAS-driven cancers. For oral administration, the compounds may be formulated as tablets, capsules, or suspensions containing excipients such as microcrystalline cellulose, lactose, starch, magnesium stearate, or hydroxypropyl methylcellulose. For parenteral administration, the compounds may be dissolved in sterile water, saline, or dextrose solution, optionally with the addition of solubilizing agents such as cyclodextrin, polysorbate 80, or ethanol, and adjusted to a pH between 5.0 and 7.4. The compounds may also be encapsulated in liposomes, nanoparticles, or polymeric micelles to enhance bioavailability, reduce systemic toxicity, and promote tumor targeting through enhanced permeability and retention effects. The dosage range for administration is typically between 0.1 mg/kg and 10 mg/kg body weight per day, administered once daily or in divided doses, depending on the route of administration and the severity of the disease. In preclinical models, a dose of 2.5 mg/kg administered intraperitoneally three times per week has been shown to achieve plasma concentrations exceeding the IC50 for RAS inhibition while maintaining a wide therapeutic window, with no evidence of weight loss, organ toxicity, or behavioral abnormalities in treated animals.

The compounds of the invention are evaluated for their biological activity using a variety of in vitro and in vivo assays. In cellular assays, the inhibition of RAS GTP loading is measured using a NanoBiT complementation assay in which the large fragment of NanoLuc luciferase (LgBit) is fused to full-length RAS and the small fragment (SmBit) is fused to the RAS-binding domain of CRAF. Upon GTP binding, RAS recruits CRAF-RBD, resulting in luciferase reconstitution and luminescence, which is quantified using a luminometer. Treatment with the compounds of the invention results in a dose-dependent reduction in luminescence, with an IC50 ranging from 10 to 50 nM depending on the substituent at position 8. The specificity of inhibition is confirmed by demonstrating that the compounds do not affect the interaction between other GTPases and their effectors, such as RhoA and ROCK, or between unrelated protein-protein interactions such as p53 and MDM2. The compounds are further tested for their ability to inhibit downstream signaling by western blot analysis of phosphorylated MEK and ERK, which are consistently suppressed in a dose-dependent manner following compound treatment. The compounds are also evaluated for their effects on cell proliferation using MTT and colony formation assays in a panel of RAS-mutant cancer cell lines, including HCT-116 (KRAS G13D), ASPC-1 (KRAS G12D), Calu-1 (KRAS G12C), A375 (BRAF V600E), and SK-MEL-28 (NRAS Q61K). All compounds exhibit potent growth inhibition with IC50 values between 5 and 20 nM, with the —H and —OCH3 derivatives showing the highest potency. The compounds are further tested in soft agar colony formation assays, where they completely suppress anchorage-independent growth at concentrations as low as 10 nM, confirming their ability to inhibit tumorigenic transformation.

The mechanism of action of the compounds is investigated using a combination of biochemical, biophysical, and imaging techniques. Cellular thermal shift assays demonstrate that the compounds stabilize the prohibitin 1-RAS complex at elevated temperatures, indicating direct or indirect binding to the prohibitin complex. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments confirm that the compounds enhance the interaction between prohibitin 1 and phosphatidylserine and phosphatidic acid, as detected by lipid strip binding assays, while simultaneously reducing the association of RAS with prohibitin 1. Fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy (FLIM-FRET) using mGFP-tagged RAS and mCherry-tagged phospholipid biosensors reveals that the compounds abolish the FRET signal between KRAS and phosphatidylserine or phosphatidic acid, indicating disruption of nanocluster formation, while leaving HRAS and NRAS nanoclustering unaffected in certain contexts. Immunogold electron microscopy of plasma membrane sheets shows a significant reduction in the density and clustering of gold-labeled KRAS particles following compound treatment, as quantified by Ripley’s K-function analysis, whereas the distribution of HRAS and NRAS remains unchanged. Genetic knockdown of prohibitin 1 using siRNA abolishes RAS activation upon EGF stimulation and renders the cells resistant to the inhibitory effects of the compounds, confirming that prohibitin 1 is essential for their mechanism of action. The compounds do not affect the expression or phosphorylation of EGFR, MEK, or ERK in the absence of RAS activation, indicating that their effects are upstream of the MAPK pathway and are not due to general kinase inhibition.

The compounds of the invention are evaluated for their in vivo efficacy using an autochthonous mouse model of KRAS G12D-driven non-small cell lung carcinoma. Transgenic mice expressing doxycycline-inducible KRAS G12D under the control of the SP-C promoter are treated with the compounds via intraperitoneal injection at a dose of 2.5 mg/kg three times per week for six weeks. Control mice receive vehicle alone. After six weeks, the lungs are harvested and analyzed for tumor burden by histopathological examination and micro-CT imaging. Mice treated with the compounds exhibit a 70–85% reduction in the number and size of lung nodules compared to vehicle-treated controls, with no evidence of weight loss, liver enzyme elevation, or renal dysfunction. Histological analysis reveals a marked reduction in Ki67 staining and an increase in cleaved caspase-3 in tumor tissues, indicating suppression of proliferation and induction of apoptosis. The compounds are also tested in xenograft models using human RAS-mutant cancer cell lines implanted subcutaneously in immunodeficient mice, where they similarly suppress tumor growth at low doses. Pharmacokinetic studies in mice demonstrate that the compounds achieve plasma concentrations exceeding 100 nM for up to 8 hours following intraperitoneal administration, with a half-life of approximately 4–6 hours and low clearance, indicating favorable bioavailability. The compounds are also tested for their ability to cross the blood-brain barrier and inhibit RAS-driven glioblastoma in orthotopic models, where they demonstrate moderate penetration and significant tumor growth inhibition, suggesting potential utility in central nervous system malignancies.

The compounds of the invention are further optimized for clinical development through structure-activity relationship studies. Substituents at positions 1, 3′, and 4′ of the cyclopenta[b]benzofuran scaffold are systematically varied to improve potency, solubility, and metabolic stability. For example, replacement of the methoxy group at position 1 with a formamide or dimethylurea group enhances binding affinity to the prohibitin complex, while substitution of the methoxy group at position 4′ with bromine or chlorine increases plasma protein binding and extends half-life. Introduction of a fluorine atom at position 3′ modulates electron density and enhances membrane permeability, leading to improved cellular uptake. The most potent derivatives are selected for further preclinical development, including toxicology studies in rodents and non-human primates, formulation optimization, and scale-up synthesis under Good Manufacturing Practice conditions. The compounds are also tested for their potential to overcome resistance to existing RAS inhibitors, such as sotorasib and adagrasib, in cell lines that have acquired resistance through secondary mutations or pathway reactivation. In all cases, the compounds of the invention retain full activity, demonstrating their potential as next-generation RAS inhibitors that act through a novel, non-mutant-dependent mechanism.

The invention further encompasses methods for identifying patients who are most likely to benefit from treatment with the compounds of the invention. Biomarkers for patient selection include the presence of RAS mutations in tumor tissue or circulating tumor DNA, elevated expression of prohibitin 1 and prohibitin 2 in tumor biopsies, and a signature of phosphatidylserine and phosphatidic acid enrichment in the plasma membrane, as detected by lipidomics or fluorescent lipid probes. Patients whose tumors exhibit low prohibitin expression or mutations in prohibitin genes are predicted to be non-responders. The invention further provides diagnostic kits for detecting these biomarkers, comprising antibodies against prohibitin 1 and prohibitin 2, lipid-binding probes, and primers for RAS mutation detection, along with instructions for interpreting the results in the context of compound therapy.

The invention further provides methods for combination therapy, wherein the compounds of the invention are administered concurrently or sequentially with other anti-cancer agents, including MEK inhibitors such as trametinib or cobimetinib, PI3K inhibitors such as alpelisib, mTOR inhibitors such as everolimus, or immune checkpoint inhibitors such as pembrolizumab or nivolumab. In preclinical models, combination with MEK inhibitors results in synergistic suppression of tumor growth and delays the emergence of resistance, while combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors enhances T-cell infiltration and tumor cell killing, suggesting that the compounds may remodel the tumor microenvironment to enhance immunogenicity. The invention further provides methods for monitoring treatment response using liquid biopsy techniques to track changes in RAS activation status, prohibitin-lipid interactions, or circulating tumor DNA levels over time, enabling adaptive therapy strategies.

The compounds of the invention represent a paradigm shift in the targeting of RAS-driven cancers, moving away from direct inhibition of the RAS protein toward modulation of its membrane microenvironment through prohibitin-mediated lipid scaffolding. This novel mechanism allows for the inhibition of all major RAS isoforms and mutants with a single agent, overcoming the limitations of allele-specific inhibitors and providing a broad-spectrum therapeutic option for patients with RAS-driven malignancies. The compounds are highly selective, potent, and effective in vivo, with a favorable safety profile, making them ideal candidates for clinical development. The invention provides a comprehensive foundation for the development of a new class of anti-cancer drugs that address one of the most challenging and prevalent targets in oncology.
