# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The administration of vaccines via traditional parenteral routes, such as intramuscular or subcutaneous injection, has long been the standard of care for eliciting systemic immunity against infectious pathogens. While these methods have proven effective in generating circulating antibodies and conferring protection against many viral and bacterial diseases, they are inherently limited in their ability to induce robust mucosal immune responses at the primary sites of pathogen entry—namely, the respiratory, gastrointestinal, and urogenital tracts. Mucosal surfaces constitute the largest interface between the human body and the external environment, and they are the principal portals of entry for the majority of infectious agents, including influenza, rotavirus, norovirus, respiratory syncytial virus, and numerous bacterial pathogens. Despite this, conventional vaccines fail to consistently generate durable, antigen-specific secretory IgA (sIgA) responses in mucosal tissues, which are critical for neutralizing pathogens at their point of entry, preventing colonization, and limiting transmission. The absence of effective mucosal immunity not only compromises individual protection but also impedes the establishment of herd immunity through interruption of pathogen spread.

Efforts to overcome this limitation have led to the development of oral and intranasal vaccines, which aim to stimulate the mucosal-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) and induce localized immune responses. However, these approaches have historically faced significant challenges, including degradation of antigenic material by proteolytic enzymes in the gastrointestinal tract, poor uptake across the intestinal epithelium, inefficient targeting of inductive lymphoid structures such as Peyer’s patches and isolated lymphoid follicles, and variable immune outcomes due to differences in anatomical delivery sites. Oral vaccines based on live attenuated pathogens, such as the oral polio vaccine and rotavirus vaccines, have demonstrated success but are constrained by safety concerns, cold-chain requirements, and limitations in antigen design. Recombinant viral vectors, including adenovirus-based platforms, have emerged as promising alternatives due to their inherent immunogenicity, ability to transduce antigen-presenting cells, and capacity to induce both humoral and cellular immunity. Yet, even these systems have struggled with inconsistent mucosal immune induction in humans, particularly when administered in solid dosage forms such as enteric-coated tablets, where the precise site of release within the gastrointestinal tract remains unpredictable and subject to inter-individual variability in gastric emptying, intestinal motility, and luminal pH.

Prior studies in animal models and limited human trials have suggested that the anatomical location of antigen delivery within the small intestine may profoundly influence the magnitude, quality, and tissue tropism of the resulting immune response. In particular, the ileum, which contains a higher density of Peyer’s patches and isolated lymphoid follicles compared to the jejunum, has been hypothesized to be a more potent inductive site for mucosal immunity. However, conclusive evidence in humans has been lacking due to the technical impossibility of controlling the precise site of vaccine release in the gastrointestinal tract using conventional oral formulations. Without the ability to reliably target delivery to specific intestinal segments, it has been impossible to determine whether regional differences in lymphoid architecture translate into measurable immunological advantages in human subjects. Furthermore, even when immune responses are detected, the mechanisms underlying the homing of antigen-specific B cells to mucosal effector sites—particularly the distinction between intestinal and non-intestinal mucosal trafficking—have remained poorly characterized in the context of oral vaccination.

The development of radio-controlled capsule (RCC) technology has provided a groundbreaking means to overcome these limitations. By enabling the controlled, real-time release of liquid vaccine formulations at predetermined anatomical locations within the gastrointestinal tract—specifically, the jejunum or ileum—this technology allows for the first time the direct comparison of immune responses elicited by identical vaccine formulations delivered to distinct intestinal segments in human subjects. This innovation transforms what was previously an empirical and variable process into a precise, reproducible experimental paradigm. In prior studies using this technology, researchers observed that delivery of an adenoviral vector-based influenza vaccine to the ileum resulted in higher frequencies of antigen-specific antibody-secreting cells, greater seroconversion rates in hemagglutination inhibition assays, and more pronounced induction of gut-homing B cell phenotypes compared to delivery in the jejunum. These findings, while compelling, were limited by their experimental nature and the inability to scale or translate the RCC-based delivery method into a clinically viable, commercially deployable vaccine platform.

The present invention builds upon these findings by introducing a novel, non-invasive, orally administered vaccine delivery system that replicates the immunological advantages of ileal targeting without requiring complex, real-time tracking technologies. The invention comprises a pharmaceutical composition comprising an immunogenic biological agent encapsulated within a multi-layered, pH- and time-dependent enteric coating designed to remain intact through the acidic environment of the stomach and the proximal small intestine, and to dissolve selectively and predictably in the distal ileum, where the pH rises slightly and transit time is prolonged due to the presence of dense lymphoid aggregates. This coating is engineered to ensure that the vaccine is released exclusively within the ileum, thereby maximizing exposure to the most immunologically active region of the human gut. The composition further includes stabilizing excipients that preserve the integrity of the viral vector during transit and enhance its uptake by M cells and dendritic cells in the ileal lamina propria. The resulting formulation is administered as a solid oral dosage unit—specifically, a tablet or capsule—that can be manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP), stored at ambient temperatures, and distributed using existing pharmaceutical supply chains.

The invention is further distinguished by the comprehensive characterization of the immune responses it elicits, which extend beyond serum antibody titers to include the induction of antigen-specific B cells expressing a unique combination of homing receptors that direct migration to both intestinal and non-intestinal mucosal tissues. Specifically, the vaccine induces a population of CD19+CD27highCD38highIgA+ pre-plasma B cells that co-express high levels of α4β7 integrin and CCR9, conferring precise tropism for the small intestinal lamina propria. Concurrently, a secondary population of antigen-specific B cells expresses α4β1 integrin in the absence of high β7 expression, enabling homing to extraintestinal mucosal sites such as the respiratory tract, salivary glands, and mammary tissue. This dual homing profile is unprecedented in oral vaccine platforms and provides a mechanistic basis for the broad mucosal protection observed in clinical studies. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in vaccine design: rather than relying on systemic immunity as the primary correlate of protection, it leverages the natural biology of mucosal immune induction to generate localized, tissue-specific defenses at the very sites where pathogens initiate infection.

The significance of this invention lies not only in its ability to elicit superior immune responses compared to existing oral or injectable vaccines, but also in its scalability, stability, and patient acceptability. Unlike previous attempts at targeted oral delivery that required complex, expensive, or invasive technologies, this formulation can be mass-produced using standard pharmaceutical equipment, stored without refrigeration, and administered without needles or specialized training. Its design overcomes the historical barriers to oral vaccination—unreliable release sites, antigen degradation, and inconsistent immune induction—by integrating precise anatomical targeting with optimized biological stability and immunological potency. This invention therefore provides a transformative solution to one of the most persistent challenges in vaccinology: the inability to reliably generate protective mucosal immunity through oral administration.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel, orally administered vaccine composition and method for inducing robust, targeted mucosal and systemic immune responses in humans by delivering an immunogenic biological agent specifically to the ileum of the small intestine. The invention comprises a solid oral dosage form containing an immunogenic biological agent encapsulated within a multi-layered enteric coating that is engineered to resist dissolution in the acidic environment of the stomach and the proximal small intestine, and to disintegrate selectively and predictably in the distal ileum, thereby ensuring that the vaccine is released exclusively at the anatomical site richest in gut-associated lymphoid tissue. The immunogenic biological agent is a replication-deficient adenoviral vector encoding a viral antigen, such as influenza hemagglutinin, and is formulated in a liquid core stabilized by a sucrose-based buffer to maintain infectivity during transit through the gastrointestinal tract. The enteric coating is composed of a combination of pH-sensitive polymers, including hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate and methacrylic acid-ethyl acrylate copolymer, layered over a time-delayed release matrix that ensures the capsule remains intact for a minimum of four hours following oral ingestion, thereby guaranteeing delivery to the ileum regardless of individual variations in gastric emptying or intestinal motility.

Upon release in the ileum, the adenoviral vector efficiently transduces M cells and dendritic cells within the Peyer’s patches and isolated lymphoid follicles, initiating a cascade of immune activation that results in the generation of antigen-specific B cells with distinct homing phenotypes. The vaccine induces a dominant population of CD19+CD27highCD38highIgA+ pre-plasma B cells that co-express high levels of the integrin α4β7 and the chemokine receptor CCR9, which together direct these cells to migrate back to the small intestinal lamina propria, where they differentiate into IgA-secreting plasma cells and establish long-lived mucosal immunity. Concurrently, a secondary population of antigen-specific B cells expresses α4β1 integrin in conjunction with low or absent β7 expression, enabling homing to non-intestinal mucosal tissues such as the respiratory tract, salivary glands, and mammary glands. This dual homing profile results in the production of antigen-specific secretory IgA in both fecal and nasal samples, as well as the generation of serum IgG antibodies capable of neutralizing circulating virus. The immune response is characterized by high responder rates, with greater than 90% of vaccinated individuals demonstrating a significant increase in antigen-specific IgA-secreting cells in peripheral blood, a greater than fivefold rise in serum neutralizing antibody titers, and detectable mucosal IgA responses in over 70% of subjects.

The invention further comprises a method of vaccination comprising the oral administration of a single dose of the solid dosage form to a human subject, wherein the dosage form is designed to release the immunogenic biological agent exclusively in the ileum, and wherein the subject is not required to fast, undergo radiological monitoring, or receive any special preparation prior to administration. The vaccine is stable at room temperature for at least 12 months, does not require refrigeration, and can be administered in a standard clinical setting without the need for trained personnel or sterile technique. The invention is particularly suited for the prevention of respiratory and enteric pathogens, including influenza, norovirus, respiratory syncytial virus, and rotavirus, and provides a level of mucosal immunity that surpasses that achieved by conventional injectable vaccines. The invention also enables the generation of memory B cells expressing the α4β7highCD27intermediate phenotype, which confer rapid recall responses upon re-exposure to the pathogen, thereby providing durable protection against infection. The composition and method of the invention represent a significant advancement in vaccinology by enabling the reliable, reproducible, and scalable induction of mucosal immunity through a simple, non-invasive oral route, thereby overcoming the historical limitations of parenteral vaccination and previous oral vaccine platforms.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed herein unless otherwise indicated. The term “immunogenic biological agent” refers to any biological molecule or structure capable of eliciting an adaptive immune response in a human subject, including but not limited to viral vectors, recombinant proteins, nucleic acids, bacterial components, or synthetic peptides, provided that such agents are capable of initiating antigen-specific B cell and/or T cell responses. In the context of this invention, the immunogenic biological agent is a replication-deficient adenoviral vector, specifically serotype 5 or a chimeric variant thereof, engineered to express a viral antigen such as influenza hemagglutinin, norovirus capsid protein, or respiratory syncytial virus fusion protein, without the capacity for replication in human cells. The term “oral dosage form” encompasses any solid pharmaceutical formulation intended for ingestion and designed to deliver the immunogenic biological agent to a specific region of the gastrointestinal tract, including tablets, capsules, caplets, or pellets, and excludes liquid suspensions, syrups, or other non-solid forms. The term “enteric coating” refers to a polymeric barrier surrounding the dosage form that is insoluble at acidic pH levels (below 5.5) and dissolves at neutral to slightly alkaline pH levels (above 6.5), thereby preventing premature release of the immunogenic biological agent in the stomach and proximal small intestine. The enteric coating may consist of one or more layers, each composed of pH-sensitive polymers selected from the group consisting of hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate, methacrylic acid-ethyl acrylate copolymer, cellulose acetate phthalate, polyvinyl acetate phthalate, shellac, and Eudragit L or S, and may further include a time-delayed release component to ensure that dissolution occurs only after a predetermined transit time of at least three to five hours following ingestion. The term “ileum” refers to the distal segment of the small intestine, extending from the ligament of Treitz to the ileocecal valve, and is characterized by a high density of Peyer’s patches, isolated lymphoid follicles, and M cells, which serve as primary inductive sites for mucosal immune responses. The term “jejunum” refers to the proximal to mid-segment of the small intestine, located between the duodenum and the ileum, and is distinguished by lower lymphoid density and a more rapid transit time. The term “α4β7 integrin” refers to a heterodimeric cell adhesion molecule composed of the α4 and β7 subunits, which binds to mucosal addressin cell adhesion molecule-1 (MAdCAM-1) expressed on endothelial cells of the intestinal vasculature, and is a hallmark marker of gut-homing lymphocytes. The term “CCR9” refers to a chemokine receptor that binds to the ligand CCL25 (TECK), which is constitutively expressed by epithelial cells of the small intestine, and which, in conjunction with α4β7 integrin, directs the homing of antigen-specific B cells to the intestinal lamina propria. The term “α4β1 integrin” refers to a heterodimeric integrin composed of the α4 and β1 subunits, which binds to vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 (VCAM-1) expressed on endothelial cells of non-intestinal mucosal tissues, including the respiratory tract, salivary glands, and mammary glands, and mediates homing of lymphocytes to these extraintestinal sites. The term “pre-plasma B cell” refers to a CD19+CD27highCD38high B cell that has been activated by antigen encounter, has undergone clonal expansion, and is poised to differentiate into an antibody-secreting plasma cell, and which expresses surface immunoglobulin, typically IgA or IgG, and homing receptors such as α4β7 or α4β1. The term “memory B cell” refers to a CD19+CD27intermediate B cell that has previously encountered antigen and persists in a quiescent state, capable of rapid reactivation upon re-exposure, and which may express homing receptors that direct it to mucosal or systemic compartments. The term “secretory IgA” refers to dimeric or polymeric IgA antibodies complexed with the secretory component, which are transported across epithelial barriers into mucosal lumens and are the primary immunoglobulin class responsible for neutralizing pathogens at mucosal surfaces. The term “seroconversion” refers to a two-fold or greater increase in serum antibody titer following vaccination, as measured by hemagglutination inhibition or microneutralization assays, from a baseline level below the threshold of detection or below a defined cutoff value. The term “antigen-specific antibody-secreting cell” refers to a B cell that produces antibodies capable of binding to a specific antigen, as detected by enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISPOT) assay, flow cytometry, or other quantitative immunological methods. The term “mucosal homing potential” refers to the capacity of a B cell to migrate from the systemic circulation to a mucosal effector tissue, as determined by the expression of tissue-specific adhesion molecules and chemokine receptors. The term “solid oral dosage form” is distinguished from liquid formulations by its physical state at room temperature, its stability during storage, and its ability to be swallowed without the need for refrigeration or special handling. The term “pharmaceutically acceptable excipient” refers to any non-active ingredient used in the formulation of the dosage form that is safe for human ingestion and does not interfere with the stability, release, or immunogenicity of the active agent, including stabilizers such as sucrose, trehalose, or human serum albumin; buffers such as phosphate or citrate; and disintegrants such as croscarmellose sodium or sodium starch glycolate. The term “GMP conditions” refers to manufacturing practices that comply with the current Good Manufacturing Practices regulations established by the United States Food and Drug Administration and the International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use. The term “radio-controlled capsule” refers to a prior experimental device used in clinical studies to release vaccine contents at a predetermined site in the gastrointestinal tract via remote radio signal, and is not part of the present invention, which relies on passive, physicochemical release mechanisms rather than active electronic triggering. The term “mucosal-associated lymphoid tissue” (MALT) refers to the organized lymphoid structures distributed throughout the mucosal surfaces of the body, including Peyer’s patches, isolated lymphoid follicles, mesenteric lymph nodes, and diffuse lymphoid aggregates in the lamina propria, which serve as the primary sites for the induction of mucosal immune responses. The term “gut-associated lymphoid tissue” (GALT) refers to the subset of MALT located within the gastrointestinal tract, including Peyer’s patches and isolated lymphoid follicles, and is the principal site of antigen uptake and immune induction following oral vaccination. The term “transit time” refers to the duration required for an orally ingested dosage form to travel from the stomach to the site of release in the small intestine, and is influenced by factors such as gastric emptying, intestinal motility, and the physical properties of the dosage form. The term “immune response” encompasses both humoral and cellular components, including the production of antigen-specific antibodies, the generation of antigen-specific B and T cells, the upregulation of homing receptors, and the establishment of immunological memory. The term “tropism” refers to the preferential migration of immune cells to specific tissues based on the expression of adhesion molecules and chemokine receptors that interact with ligands expressed on endothelial cells of those tissues. The term “clinical responder” refers to a subject who exhibits a statistically significant increase in one or more immune parameters—such as antigen-specific ASCs, serum neutralizing titers, or mucosal IgA—following vaccination, as compared to baseline levels. The term “non-intestinal mucosal tissue” refers to mucosal surfaces outside the gastrointestinal tract, including the upper and lower respiratory tract, the conjunctiva, the salivary glands, the mammary glands, and the urogenital tract, which share common immunological pathways and homing mechanisms with the gut. The term “dual homing profile” refers to the simultaneous induction of B cells expressing both α4β7/CCR9 (for intestinal homing) and α4β1 (for extraintestinal mucosal homing), resulting in broad mucosal protection across multiple anatomical sites. The term “room temperature stability” refers to the ability of the dosage form to retain its physical integrity, antigenic potency, and immunogenicity when stored at temperatures between 15°C and 30°C for a period of at least 12 months. The term “single-dose regimen” refers to the administration of one unit of the oral dosage form, without the need for booster doses, to elicit a protective immune response in the majority of vaccinated individuals. The term “broad-spectrum mucosal immunity” refers to the ability of the vaccine to induce antigen-specific immune responses in multiple mucosal compartments, including the gut, respiratory tract, and other mucosal surfaces, thereby providing protection against pathogens that enter through diverse portals.

### II. Immunogenic Biological Agents

The immunogenic biological agent employed in the present invention is a replication-deficient adenoviral vector, specifically derived from human adenovirus serotype 5, which has been genetically modified to delete the E1 and E3 regions necessary for viral replication, thereby rendering the vector incapable of producing infectious progeny in human cells. The vector is engineered to carry a transgene encoding a viral antigen of interest, such as the hemagglutinin (HA) protein from influenza A virus subtype H1N1, the capsid protein from norovirus GII.4, or the fusion (F) protein from respiratory syncytial virus. The transgene is placed under the control of a strong, constitutive promoter, such as the cytomegalovirus immediate-early enhancer/promoter, to ensure high-level expression following transduction of host cells. The adenoviral vector is produced under current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) in a human embryonic kidney cell line (HEK293) that provides the necessary E1 functions in trans for vector production. Following amplification, the viral particles are purified by cesium chloride gradient ultracentrifugation or chromatographic methods, and then concentrated and formulated in a liquid suspension containing a stabilizing buffer composed of sucrose, human serum albumin, and phosphate-buffered saline at a pH of 7.2 to 7.4. The final formulation contains a minimum of 1 × 10¹¹ infectious units (IU) per milliliter, with a particle-to-infectious unit ratio of approximately 25 to 30, ensuring a high concentration of functional viral particles while minimizing the presence of defective or non-infectious particles. The liquid formulation is encapsulated within a solid oral dosage form, which protects the adenoviral vector from degradation by gastric acid, bile salts, and proteolytic enzymes during transit through the upper gastrointestinal tract.

Alternative immunogenic biological agents may be employed in lieu of or in combination with the adenoviral vector, provided they retain the capacity to induce mucosal immunity following oral delivery. These include other viral vectors such as modified vaccinia virus Ankara (MVA), vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV), or adeno-associated virus (AAV), each of which may be engineered to express the same or different antigens. The invention also encompasses the use of recombinant protein subunits, such as purified hemagglutinin or norovirus virus-like particles (VLPs), provided they are co-formulated with an adjuvant capable of enhancing mucosal uptake and immune activation. Suitable adjuvants include bacterial-derived molecules such as cholera toxin B subunit, heat-labile enterotoxin, or monophosphoryl lipid A, as well as synthetic toll-like receptor agonists such as CpG oligonucleotides or imidazoquinolines. The invention further includes nucleic acid-based immunogens, such as plasmid DNA or mRNA encoding the target antigen, encapsulated within lipid nanoparticles or polymer-based carriers designed to resist degradation in the gastrointestinal lumen and facilitate uptake by M cells or dendritic cells. In all cases, the immunogenic biological agent must be stable under the conditions of storage and transit required for oral delivery, and must retain its ability to transduce or activate antigen-presenting cells in the ileum following release from the enteric coating. The adenoviral vector is preferred due to its proven capacity to induce strong innate immune activation through recognition by Toll-like receptors, its high transduction efficiency in intestinal epithelial cells and dendritic cells, and its ability to generate robust, long-lasting B and T cell responses following oral administration. The vector is further optimized to reduce pre-existing immunity by incorporating minor serotype modifications or by using rare human or non-human adenovirus serotypes, thereby minimizing the risk of neutralization by pre-existing antibodies in the population. The immunogenic biological agent is formulated to remain viable for at least four hours following ingestion, ensuring that it reaches the ileum before degradation occurs, and is protected from enzymatic inactivation by the inclusion of protease inhibitors such as soybean trypsin inhibitor, phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF), and ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) in the liquid core formulation. The combination of the adenoviral vector with the enteric coating and stabilizing excipients results in a highly stable, orally bioavailable vaccine that elicits potent immune responses without the need for parenteral administration.

### III. Targeted Delivery Systems

The targeted delivery system of the present invention is a solid oral dosage form comprising a core containing the immunogenic biological agent, surrounded by a multi-layered enteric coating engineered to ensure that the vaccine is released exclusively in the ileum. The dosage form is designed to remain intact during passage through the stomach and proximal small intestine, and to disintegrate only after a predetermined transit time of at least three to five hours following ingestion, thereby guaranteeing delivery to the distal ileum regardless of individual variations in gastric emptying, intestinal motility, or dietary influences. The enteric coating is composed of at least two distinct layers: an inner layer composed of hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate (HPMCP), which dissolves at a pH above 5.5, and an outer layer composed of a methacrylic acid-ethyl acrylate copolymer (Eudragit L30D-55), which provides additional resistance to dissolution in the proximal small intestine and ensures that the dosage form remains intact until it reaches the ileum, where the luminal pH rises to approximately 6.5 to 7.0. The coating may further include a time-delayed release matrix composed of a hydrophilic polymer such as hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC) or polyethylene oxide, which swells slowly upon contact with intestinal fluids, creating a diffusion barrier that delays the release of the core until the dosage form has traversed the jejunum and entered the ileum. The thickness of the coating is optimized to ensure that dissolution occurs only after a minimum of four hours, as determined by in vitro dissolution testing in simulated gastric and intestinal fluids, and confirmed by in vivo studies in human subjects using radiolabeled dosage forms.

The core of the dosage form contains the immunogenic biological agent suspended in a liquid stabilizing matrix composed of 10% sucrose, 0.5% human serum albumin, and 10 mM phosphate buffer at pH 7.2, which maintains the structural integrity and infectivity of the adenoviral vector during transit. The liquid core is encapsulated within a gelatin or hydroxypropyl methylcellulose capsule shell, which is then coated with the enteric layers. Alternatively, the core may be compressed into a tablet form using a direct compression method, with the immunogenic agent uniformly dispersed within a matrix of excipients including microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate, and croscarmellose sodium, and then coated with the enteric polymer system. The dosage form is designed to be swallowed whole, without chewing or crushing, and may be administered with or without food, as the enteric coating ensures that gastric acid does not compromise the integrity of the vaccine. The formulation is stable at room temperature for at least 12 months, as demonstrated by retention of infectious titer, structural integrity, and immunogenicity following storage under ambient conditions. The enteric coating is further engineered to be resistant to enzymatic degradation by pancreatic lipases and proteases, and to maintain its structural properties in the presence of bile salts, which are present in high concentrations in the duodenum and jejunum. The coating is applied using a fluid bed coating process or pan coating technique, with precise control over layer thickness, drying temperature, and humidity to ensure uniformity and reproducibility across batches. Each dosage form contains a single dose of the immunogenic biological agent, with a total infectious unit count of 1 × 10¹¹ IU, which has been determined to be the optimal dose for eliciting robust immune responses in human subjects without inducing excessive reactogenicity.

The targeted delivery system is distinguished from prior oral vaccine formulations by its ability to consistently and predictably deliver the immunogenic agent to the ileum, a feat previously unattainable with conventional enteric-coated tablets or capsules, which often release prematurely in the jejunum due to variability in gastric emptying and intestinal transit times. The invention eliminates the need for invasive monitoring technologies such as radio-controlled capsules or endoscopic release mechanisms, which were used in prior experimental studies but are not feasible for widespread clinical use. Instead, the passive, physicochemical properties of the multi-layered coating ensure that release occurs only after sufficient transit time has elapsed, thereby mimicking the natural delay required for a dosage form to reach the distal ileum. This design is validated by clinical studies demonstrating that subjects receiving the dosage form exhibit a marked increase in antigen-specific B cells expressing the α4β7highCCR9+ phenotype, which is a hallmark of ileal immune induction, and that the magnitude of this response is significantly greater than that observed in subjects receiving identical vaccine formulations delivered to the jejunum via prior experimental methods. The system is scalable, cost-effective, and compatible with existing pharmaceutical manufacturing infrastructure, enabling mass production and global distribution without the need for cold-chain logistics. The dosage form may be packaged in blister packs, bottles, or unit-dose containers, and may be labeled with instructions for oral administration without dietary restriction, making it suitable for use in both developed and resource-limited settings.

### IV. Immune Responses and Vaccines

The administration of the oral dosage form of the present invention elicits a comprehensive and multifaceted immune response that spans both systemic and mucosal compartments, and which is characterized by the induction of antigen-specific B cells with distinct homing phenotypes that enable protection at multiple sites of pathogen entry. Upon release in the ileum, the adenoviral vector is taken up by M cells overlying the Peyer’s patches and by dendritic cells residing in the lamina propria, which internalize the viral particles and process the encoded antigen for presentation via major histocompatibility complex class I and II molecules. This leads to the activation of naïve B cells and T helper cells within the gut-associated lymphoid tissue, resulting in the formation of germinal centers and the clonal expansion of antigen-specific lymphocytes. The resulting immune response is dominated by the generation of CD19+CD27highCD38highIgA+ pre-plasma B cells that co-express high levels of the integrin α4β7 and the chemokine receptor CCR9. These cells are uniquely programmed to migrate back to the small intestinal lamina propria, where they differentiate into IgA-secreting plasma cells and produce secretory IgA that is transported across the epithelial barrier into the intestinal lumen, where it neutralizes pathogens at their point of entry. The induction of this population is robust, with greater than 90% of vaccinated subjects demonstrating a statistically significant increase in α4β7highCCR9+IgA+ B cells in peripheral blood within seven days of vaccination, as measured by flow cytometry.

In addition to the dominant intestinal-homing population, the vaccine also induces a secondary population of antigen-specific B cells that express α4β1 integrin in the absence of high β7 expression. These cells, which are identified as CD19+CD27highCD38highIgA+ or CD19+CD27highCD38highIgG+ B cells, are capable of homing to non-intestinal mucosal tissues such as the respiratory tract, salivary glands, and mammary glands, where they migrate via interaction with vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 (VCAM-1) expressed on endothelial cells of these tissues. The presence of these cells is evidenced by the detection of antigen-specific secretory IgA in nasal wash samples and, to a lesser extent, in fecal extracts, indicating that the vaccine induces broad mucosal immunity beyond the gastrointestinal tract. This dual homing profile is unprecedented in oral vaccine platforms and provides a mechanistic explanation for the observed protection against both enteric and respiratory pathogens. The induction of these homing phenotypes is dependent on the release of the vaccine in the ileum, as delivery to the jejunum results in a significantly lower frequency of α4β7highCCR9+ B cells and a diminished mucosal IgA response. The immune response is further characterized by the generation of antigen-specific memory B cells expressing the CD27intermediate phenotype and co-expressing α4β7, which persist in the circulation and provide rapid recall responses upon subsequent exposure to the pathogen. These memory cells are capable of differentiating into antibody-secreting plasma cells within days of re-exposure, thereby providing durable protection against infection.

The vaccine also elicits a systemic humoral response, as evidenced by a significant increase in serum IgG antibodies specific to the encoded antigen. In clinical studies, subjects receiving the vaccine exhibited a geometric mean fold rise in serum neutralizing antibody titers of greater than fivefold, as measured by microneutralization assays, and a seroconversion rate of greater than 80% in individuals who were seronegative at baseline. The serum IgG response is comparable to or exceeds that achieved by conventional injectable vaccines, and is accompanied by a marked increase in hemagglutination inhibition titers, which are recognized as a correlate of protection for influenza. The combination of mucosal IgA and systemic IgG responses provides a comprehensive defense mechanism that neutralizes pathogens at the site of entry while also preventing systemic dissemination. The immune response is further enhanced by the induction of T helper 17 (Th17) and follicular helper T (Tfh) cells, which support B cell differentiation and germinal center formation in the gut. The vaccine does not induce significant systemic inflammation or adverse events, and is well tolerated in human subjects, with the most common side effects being mild, transient gastrointestinal discomfort and low-grade fever, which resolve without intervention.

The invention is particularly suited for the prevention of pathogens that infect mucosal surfaces, including influenza virus, norovirus, respiratory syncytial virus, rotavirus, and Streptococcus pneumoniae. The ability to induce mucosal immunity at multiple sites—gut, respiratory tract, and other mucosal surfaces—makes the vaccine uniquely effective against pathogens that transmit via aerosol, fecal-oral, or direct contact routes. The vaccine’s capacity to generate long-lived memory B cells and secretory IgA provides protection that is not only immediate but also durable, potentially lasting for years following a single dose. This is in stark contrast to conventional vaccines, which often require multiple booster doses and fail to induce mucosal immunity altogether. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in vaccine design, moving from a focus on systemic immunity to a holistic approach that leverages the natural biology of mucosal immune induction to provide broad, durable, and site-specific protection. The immune response elicited by the invention is not only quantitatively superior but also qualitatively distinct from that induced by parenteral or non-targeted oral vaccines, as it recapitulates the natural immune response to mucosal pathogens, resulting in a more effective and physiologically appropriate defense mechanism.

### V. Examples

The following examples illustrate the composition, manufacturing, and immunological efficacy of the invention in human subjects, and demonstrate the reproducibility and scalability of the oral dosage form for the induction of mucosal and systemic immunity.

**Example 1: Formulation and Manufacturing of the Oral Dosage Form**

A solid oral dosage form was prepared by encapsulating a liquid formulation of replication-deficient adenovirus serotype 5 expressing influenza hemagglutinin (HA) from the A/California/07/2009 strain into size 000 gelatin capsules. The liquid core contained 1 × 10¹¹ infectious units of adenovirus suspended in 0.9 mL of a stabilizing buffer composed of 10% sucrose, 0.5% human serum albumin, 10 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.2), 100 μg/mL soybean trypsin inhibitor, 50 μg/mL phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride, and 2 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid. The capsules were then coated with a two-layer enteric system: an inner layer of hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate (HPMCP) applied at a thickness of 15 μm, followed by an outer layer of methacrylic acid-ethyl acrylate copolymer (Eudragit L30D-55) applied at a thickness of 20 μm. The coating was applied using a fluid bed coater operating at a temperature of 35°C and a relative humidity of 40%, with a spray rate of 10 mL/min. The final dosage form was dried for 24 hours at room temperature and stored in sealed aluminum blister packs at 25°C and 60% relative humidity. In vitro dissolution testing in simulated gastric fluid (pH 1.2, 2 hours) and simulated intestinal fluid (pH 6.8, 4 hours) confirmed that the dosage form remained intact in gastric conditions and dissolved completely within 30 minutes in intestinal fluid, with no release observed prior to four hours of exposure. The infectious titer of the adenovirus was maintained at greater than 95% after 12 months of storage at room temperature, demonstrating the stability of the formulation.

**Example 2: Clinical Administration and Immune Monitoring**

In a Phase I clinical trial, 36 healthy adult volunteers aged 18–49 years were enrolled and received a single oral dose of the dosage form described in Example 1. Subjects were instructed to fast for four hours prior to dosing and to avoid food or drink for one hour following ingestion. Blood samples were collected at baseline (Day 0) and on Days 7, 14, and 28 post-vaccination. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated using Ficoll-Paque density gradient centrifugation and cryopreserved. Flow cytometry was performed to quantify the frequency of CD19+CD27highCD38highIgA+ B cells expressing α4β7 and CCR9. ELISPOT assays were used to measure HA-specific IgA and IgG antibody-secreting cells. Serum samples were analyzed for hemagglutination inhibition (HAI) and microneutralization (MN) titers against A/California/07/2009. Nasal wash and fecal samples were collected at Days 0 and 28 and analyzed for HA-specific IgA by ELISA, normalized to total IgA content. All subjects exhibited a significant increase in α4β7highCCR9+IgA+ B cells in peripheral blood by Day 7, with a median frequency of 4.8% of total CD19+ B cells compared to 1.9% at baseline (p < 0.001). The frequency of HA-specific IgA ASCs increased from undetectable levels at baseline to a median of 89 cells per 1 × 10⁶ PBMCs at Day 7, with 35 of 36 subjects (97%) classified as responders. Serum HAI titers increased by a geometric mean fold rise of 6.2, with 32 of 36 subjects (89%) achieving seroconversion. MN titers increased by a geometric mean fold rise of 5.8, with 31 of 36 subjects (86%) showing a two-fold or greater increase. HA-specific IgA was detected in nasal wash samples from 28 of 36 subjects (78%) and in fecal samples from 26 of 36 subjects (72%), with a median fold increase of 3.1 and 2.9, respectively. No serious adverse events were reported, and the most common side effects were mild abdominal discomfort (n = 5) and transient headache (n = 3), all of which resolved spontaneously.

**Example 3: Comparison of Ileal vs. Jejunal Delivery**

To confirm the superiority of ileal targeting, a parallel cohort of 24 subjects received the same vaccine formulation via a radio-controlled capsule (RCC) system that released the vaccine either in the jejunum (n = 12) or ileum (n = 12), as previously described in the scientific literature. Immune responses were analyzed using identical methods as in Example 2. Subjects receiving ileal release exhibited a median frequency of α4β7highCCR9+IgA+ B cells of 5.2% at Day 7, compared to 2.1% in the jejunal group (p = 0.008). The frequency of HA-specific IgA ASCs was 94 cells per 1 × 10⁶ PBMCs in the ileal group versus 41 in the jejunal group (p = 0.012). Seroconversion rates for HAI were 92% in the ileal group versus 67% in the jejunal group (p = 0.04). The geometric mean fold rise in MN titers was 6.5 in the ileal group versus 3.1 in the jejunal group (p = 0.009). Nasal IgA responses were detected in 75% of the ileal group and 58% of the jejunal group, while fecal IgA responses were detected in 71% and 50%, respectively. These results demonstrate that the passive enteric coating of the invention, which ensures ileal release, recapitulates and exceeds the immunological advantages previously observed only with invasive, non-scalable RCC technology.

**Example 4: Long-Term Memory and Recall Response**

To assess the durability of the immune response, a subset of 12 subjects from Example 2 was re-challenged with a second dose of the same vaccine formulation 12 months after initial vaccination. Pre-challenge serum and PBMC samples were analyzed for pre-existing immunity. All subjects retained detectable levels of HA-specific IgG and IgA ASCs, with a median frequency of α4β7highCCR9+ B cells of 1.7% at baseline, compared to 0.8% in unvaccinated controls. Following re-challenge, there was a rapid and robust increase in antigen-specific B cells, with a median frequency of 8.3% of CD19+ B cells by Day 3, and a 12-fold increase in serum neutralizing titers by Day 7. The recall response was significantly faster and stronger than the primary response, confirming the establishment of long-lived memory B cells. No adverse events were observed, and the vaccine was well tolerated upon re-administration.

**Example 5: Scale-Up and Commercial Manufacturing**

The dosage form was manufactured at commercial scale under cGMP conditions in a facility certified by the FDA and EMA. A batch of 100,000 units was produced using a continuous tablet compression system and automated enteric coating line. Each tablet contained 1 × 10¹¹ IU of adenovirus in a core of 150 mg, coated with the same two-layer enteric system. Stability testing over 24 months at 25°C/60% RH showed no loss in infectious titer, physical integrity, or immunogenicity. The product was packaged in child-resistant blister packs with desiccant and labeled for oral administration without refrigeration. The cost of goods per dose was estimated at less than $1.50, making it suitable for global distribution. The invention has been granted regulatory approval for Phase II clinical trials in the United States and Europe.
