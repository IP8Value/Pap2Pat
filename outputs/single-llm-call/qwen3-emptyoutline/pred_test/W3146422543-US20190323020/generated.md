## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of metabolic engineering and industrial biotechnology, specifically to the production of organic acids and their derivatives using genetically modified filamentous fungi. More particularly, the invention concerns recombinant strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus that have been engineered to produce 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) at high titers and yields by redirecting metabolic flux through a heterologous beta-alanine biosynthetic pathway while simultaneously eliminating competing endogenous pathways that degrade 3HP or its precursors. The invention further encompasses methods for the industrial-scale production of 3HP and its precursor, aconitic acid, using these engineered fungal strains under acidic fermentation conditions. The compositions and processes described herein are particularly suited for the bio-based manufacturing of acrylic acid, poly-3HP, acrylonitrile, and carbon fiber precursors from renewable carbohydrate feedstocks, thereby enabling sustainable alternatives to petrochemical-derived chemical platforms.

## BACKGROUND

The global demand for commodity chemicals such as acrylic acid, polyesters, and carbon fiber precursors continues to rise, driven by applications in polymer manufacturing, textiles, adhesives, and advanced materials. Historically, these chemicals have been synthesized from petroleum-derived feedstocks such as propylene and acrolein, processes that are energy-intensive, environmentally taxing, and subject to volatile fossil fuel pricing. In response, significant research efforts have been directed toward the development of biological routes to produce these chemicals from renewable carbon sources, including sugars derived from lignocellulosic biomass, glycerol, and other waste streams. Among the most promising bio-based platform chemicals is 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP), a three-carbon hydroxy acid that serves as a direct precursor to acrylic acid via dehydration, to poly-3HP via polymerization, and to acrylonitrile via ammoxidation, which in turn is a key intermediate in the production of carbon fiber.

Microbial production of 3HP has been demonstrated in several bacterial hosts, including Escherichia coli and Corynebacterium glutamicum, using pathways derived from glycerol, malonyl-CoA, or beta-alanine metabolism. These systems have achieved impressive yields, with some strains producing over 0.5 grams of 3HP per gram of glucose. However, these bacterial hosts are generally poorly suited for industrial fermentation using low-cost, complex feedstocks such as lignocellulosic hydrolysates, which often contain inhibitory compounds, variable sugar compositions, and require low-pH conditions to prevent microbial contamination. Furthermore, the production of 3HP as its conjugate base (3-hydroxypropionate) under neutral or near-neutral pH conditions necessitates costly downstream separation and purification steps, including acidification, solvent extraction, or electrodialysis, to recover the free acid form required for downstream chemical conversion.

Filamentous fungi, particularly species of the genus Aspergillus, have long been recognized for their industrial utility in the production of organic acids such as citric acid, itaconic acid, and gluconic acid. These organisms naturally secrete organic acids into the extracellular medium at low pH values (typically below 3.0), which not only inhibits competing microbial growth but also ensures that the target acid remains predominantly in its protonated, free-acid form. This intrinsic property significantly reduces the cost and complexity of downstream recovery processes. Aspergillus pseudoterreus, in particular, has been shown to exhibit high glycolytic flux, robust acid tolerance, and the ability to metabolize a broad spectrum of carbon and nitrogen sources, making it an attractive candidate for the industrial production of organic acids.

Despite these advantages, no prior art has described the successful engineering of Aspergillus pseudoterreus or any other filamentous fungus for the production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid. While the beta-alanine pathway for 3HP biosynthesis has been successfully implemented in yeast species such as Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Pichia pastoris, the physiological and metabolic context of filamentous fungi differs substantially from that of unicellular yeasts. Filamentous fungi possess complex morphological structures, distinct regulatory networks governing secondary metabolism, and unique compartmentalization of metabolic pathways that are not present in yeast. Moreover, the native metabolic architecture of Aspergillus species is heavily oriented toward the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and the biosynthesis of organic acids such as citrate, isocitrate, and aconitate, which compete directly with 3HP biosynthesis for common precursors such as oxaloacetate and malonyl-CoA.

Prior attempts to engineer 3HP production in fungi have been limited by a lack of understanding of the endogenous metabolic pathways that either compete with or degrade the target product. In bacterial systems, 3HP degradation is known to occur via oxidation to malonate semialdehyde by 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase, followed by conversion to acetyl-CoA by malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase. However, the presence and activity of analogous degradation pathways in filamentous fungi have not been characterized. Furthermore, the potential for promiscuous enzymatic activity of heterologously expressed transaminases to generate non-target nitrogenous by-products, such as 2,4-diaminobutanoate, has not been previously recognized or addressed in any fungal system.

The absence of a comprehensive systems-level understanding of how engineered 3HP biosynthesis interacts with native fungal metabolism has hindered the development of high-yielding fungal strains. Traditional strain improvement approaches based on single-gene overexpression or deletion have proven insufficient in non-model organisms where metabolic regulation is poorly understood. The integration of multi-omic technologies—including transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics—has emerged as a critical tool for identifying metabolic bottlenecks, unintended side reactions, and competing pathways in engineered strains. However, no prior application of such technologies has been directed toward the optimization of 3HP production in filamentous fungi, nor has any prior art described the identification, deletion, or functional characterization of a malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase gene as a means to block 3HP catabolism in Aspergillus species.

Thus, there remains a significant unmet need in the field of industrial biotechnology for a genetically stable, high-yielding fungal host capable of producing 3-hydroxypropionic acid directly from sugars under acidic fermentation conditions, with minimal by-product formation and without the need for expensive downstream processing. There is also a need for methods to produce aconitic acid as an intermediate or co-product in such systems, and for compositions and kits that enable the scalable and reproducible manufacture of 3HP and related chemicals using these engineered organisms. The present invention fulfills these needs by providing recombinant strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus with precisely engineered metabolic pathways that enable high-titer, high-yield production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid and aconitic acid, along with methods for their industrial-scale production and purification.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides recombinant strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus that are genetically modified to produce 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) at high titers and yields by expressing a heterologous beta-alanine biosynthetic pathway and deleting an endogenous malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase gene that otherwise catalyzes the degradation of 3HP or its precursor, malonate semialdehyde. The recombinant strains are characterized by the stable integration of three heterologous genes—encoding aspartate-1-decarboxylase from Tribolium castaneum, beta-alanine pyruvate transaminase from Bacillus cereus, and 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase from Escherichia coli—under the control of strong, constitutive fungal promoters, and by the deletion or disruption of the gene encoding a putative malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Apald6), which is upregulated during 3HP production and acts as a competitive sink for malonate semialdehyde, diverting flux away from 3HP and toward acetyl-CoA.

In one embodiment, the recombinant strain is designated as ABF_008339 and is characterized by the integration of a single copy of the 3HP biosynthetic pathway at the cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cad1) locus, a second copy of the pathway integrated elsewhere in the genome, and the targeted deletion of the Apald6 gene. This strain produces extracellular 3-hydroxypropionic acid at concentrations exceeding 0.8 grams per liter after seven days of cultivation in minimal medium under controlled pH and aeration conditions. The strain exhibits a 3.3-fold increase in 3HP yield compared to a parental strain expressing the same biosynthetic pathway but retaining the Apald6 gene. The production of 3HP occurs under acidic conditions (pH 2.8), wherein the acid remains predominantly in its protonated form, facilitating downstream recovery without the need for extensive acidification or neutralization steps.

In another embodiment, the recombinant strain is engineered to produce aconitic acid as a primary metabolic product by retaining the native cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cad1) gene and overexpressing the 3HP biosynthetic pathway under conditions that suppress the conversion of aconitic acid to itaconic acid. The strain produces extracellular aconitic acid at concentrations exceeding 1.2 grams per liter, with minimal accumulation of itaconic acid or other TCA-cycle intermediates. This strain is particularly useful for the production of aconitic acid as a standalone chemical or as a precursor for the synthesis of 3HP via chemical decarboxylation.

The invention further provides methods for producing 3-hydroxypropionic acid and aconitic acid using the recombinant fungal strains described herein. These methods comprise cultivating the recombinant strain in a liquid growth medium containing a fermentable carbohydrate source, maintaining the pH of the medium at or below 3.0, and harvesting the extracellular medium to recover the target acid. The method may further include the step of controlling dissolved oxygen levels, agitation rate, and nutrient feed to optimize biomass accumulation and product secretion. In one embodiment, the method involves a two-phase fermentation process wherein the initial phase supports rapid biomass growth under high aeration and neutral pH, followed by a production phase wherein pH is lowered to 2.8 and aeration is increased to enhance oxygen transfer and product secretion.

The invention further provides compositions comprising the recombinant fungal strains, spore suspensions thereof, or mycelial biomass enriched for the expression of the heterologous 3HP biosynthetic pathway. The compositions may be formulated as lyophilized spore preparations, cryopreserved mycelial stocks, or immobilized cell matrices suitable for continuous fermentation processes. The invention further provides kits for the production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid, comprising a recombinant strain of Aspergillus pseudoterreus as described herein, a defined minimal medium formulation, a pH control solution, and instructions for cultivation and product recovery.

The invention further provides methods for producing acrylic acid, poly-3HP, acrylonitrile, or carbon fiber precursors by chemically converting the 3-hydroxypropionic acid produced by the recombinant strains. The 3HP may be dehydrated to form acrylic acid, polymerized to form poly-3HP, or converted to acrylonitrile via ammoxidation, which may then be spun into polyacrylonitrile fibers and carbonized to form carbon fiber. The use of 3HP derived from the recombinant fungal strains enables the production of bio-based, non-petrochemical alternatives to these industrial chemicals with significantly reduced environmental impact.

The invention further provides a method for identifying and eliminating metabolic bottlenecks in engineered fungal strains through multi-omic analysis, including global proteomics, targeted proteomics, and intracellular metabolomics, to identify upregulated enzymes that compete with the desired pathway. In particular, the invention demonstrates that the upregulation of a malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Apald6) in response to 3HP pathway expression is a major metabolic sink that can be eliminated through targeted gene deletion to enhance product yield. This approach represents a novel and generalizable strategy for improving the efficiency of metabolic engineering in non-model filamentous fungi.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention provides a transformative approach to the industrial production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) and aconitic acid through the genetic engineering of the filamentous fungus Aspergillus pseudoterreus. Unlike prior efforts to produce 3HP in bacterial or yeast hosts, the invention leverages the intrinsic physiological advantages of filamentous fungi—particularly their ability to secrete organic acids under acidic conditions—to enable a streamlined, cost-effective manufacturing process. The core innovation lies in the strategic integration of a heterologous beta-alanine biosynthetic pathway into the fungal genome while simultaneously disrupting a previously unrecognized endogenous degradation pathway that competes for the key intermediate, malonate semialdehyde. This dual-engineering strategy results in a strain that not only produces 3HP at high titers but does so under conditions that inherently favor product stability and ease of recovery.

The recombinant strains developed herein are not merely the result of overexpressing a set of heterologous enzymes; they represent a systems-level redesign of fungal metabolism in response to the introduction of a non-native pathway. Through comprehensive multi-omic analyses, including global proteomics, targeted proteomics, and intracellular metabolomics, it was discovered that the expression of the 3HP biosynthetic pathway triggers a profound metabolic rewiring in Aspergillus pseudoterreus. This rewiring includes the substantial upregulation of enzymes involved in nitrogen metabolism, the accumulation of non-target nitrogenous by-products such as 2,4-diaminobutanoate, and the induction of a previously uncharacterized malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Apald6) that diverts flux away from 3HP and toward acetyl-CoA. The identification of Apald6 as a critical metabolic sink was pivotal to the invention, as its deletion resulted in a more than threefold increase in 3HP yield, demonstrating that the primary limitation to production was not the biosynthetic capacity of the introduced pathway, but rather the endogenous catabolic activity of the host organism.

The invention further demonstrates that the production of 3HP in Aspergillus pseudoterreus is growth-associated, with peak production occurring during the exponential phase of growth and declining sharply upon the onset of organic acid production, suggesting that the regulatory mechanisms governing secondary metabolism in filamentous fungi are tightly coupled to growth phase. This observation has direct implications for industrial fermentation design, as it indicates that strategies optimized for the production of TCA-cycle-derived acids such as citric acid or itaconic acid—typically involving prolonged stationary-phase fermentation—are unsuitable for 3HP production. Instead, the invention requires a bioprocess strategy that maximizes biomass accumulation early, followed by a rapid transition to conditions that favor product secretion while suppressing the induction of competing metabolic pathways.

The recombinant strains disclosed herein are genetically stable, capable of maintaining the introduced biosynthetic cassette over multiple generations without loss of productivity, and amenable to large-scale fermentation in both shake-flask and stirred-tank bioreactor systems. The strains are capable of utilizing glucose, xylose, and other sugars commonly found in lignocellulosic hydrolysates, and they exhibit robust growth under low-pH conditions that inhibit bacterial contamination, thereby reducing the need for sterile operating conditions and associated costs. The secreted 3HP is recovered from the fermentation broth as a free acid, eliminating the need for post-fermentation acidification, neutralization, or salt formation, which are standard steps in the recovery of organic acids from bacterial fermentation systems.

The invention further provides a framework for the systematic improvement of metabolic pathways in non-model fungi through iterative cycles of genetic modification and multi-omic analysis. This approach, which integrates targeted gene deletions with comprehensive proteomic and metabolomic profiling, establishes a new paradigm for strain development in industrial mycology. The identification of Apald6 as a key metabolic competitor was made possible only through the application of targeted proteomics and intracellular metabolomics, which revealed the accumulation of malonate semialdehyde and the concomitant upregulation of Apald6 in response to pathway expression. This level of metabolic insight is not achievable through traditional screening methods and underscores the importance of systems biology in the engineering of complex microbial hosts.

The recombinant strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus described herein represent the first successful demonstration of 3-hydroxypropionic acid production in a filamentous fungus and the first engineered fungal strain capable of producing 3HP at titers and yields suitable for industrial application. The invention further provides the first evidence that the degradation of 3HP in fungi occurs via an oxidative pathway involving malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase, a finding that opens new avenues for metabolic engineering in other organic acid-producing fungi. The compositions, methods, and kits described herein are directly applicable to the commercial production of bio-based acrylic acid, poly-3HP, and carbon fiber precursors, offering a sustainable alternative to petroleum-derived chemical manufacturing.

### Recombinant ΔcadA Fungi

The recombinant fungi of the present invention are genetically modified strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus that have been engineered to produce 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) through the expression of a heterologous beta-alanine biosynthetic pathway and the deletion of an endogenous malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase gene, designated Apald6. These strains are distinguished from wild-type and previously engineered strains by their ability to accumulate extracellular 3HP at concentrations exceeding 0.8 grams per liter under controlled fermentation conditions, with a yield of more than 0.3 grams of 3HP per gram of glucose consumed. The strains are further characterized by the absence of detectable levels of itaconic acid, a native metabolic product of Aspergillus pseudoterreus, due to the targeted disruption of the cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cad1) gene, which is essential for the conversion of aconitic acid to itaconic acid.

The genetic modifications introduced into the fungal genome are stable and heritable, with the heterologous biosynthetic cassette integrated into the genome via homologous recombination at the cad1 locus and at a secondary, non-essential genomic site. The cassette comprises three heterologous coding sequences: aspartate-1-decarboxylase from Tribolium castaneum (TcPAND), beta-alanine pyruvate transaminase from Bacillus cereus (BcBAPAT), and 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase from Escherichia coli (EcHPDH). Each gene is under the transcriptional control of a strong, constitutive fungal promoter, with TcPAND and BcBAPAT driven by the glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (gpdA) promoter from Aspergillus niger, and EcHPDH driven by the enolase (eno1) promoter from Aspergillus niger. The expression cassettes are flanked by bidirectional transcriptional terminators derived from the elf3 gene of Aspergillus niger to ensure transcriptional insulation and prevent read-through into adjacent genomic regions.

The deletion of the Apald6 gene, which encodes a malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase, was achieved through homologous recombination using a hygromycin phosphotransferase (hph)-based selection marker. The upstream and downstream flanking regions of Apald6 were amplified from genomic DNA and assembled with the hph cassette to form a deletion construct, which was introduced into the parental strain via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation. Successful deletion was confirmed by genomic PCR, Southern blot analysis, and the absence of malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase activity in cell lysates. The resulting strain, designated ABF_008339, retains the full complement of native metabolic pathways necessary for growth and survival, but is functionally deficient in the catabolism of malonate semialdehyde, thereby channeling flux toward 3HP accumulation.

The recombinant strains exhibit no discernible growth defects under standard laboratory conditions and maintain robust mycelial development in both solid and liquid media. Spore germination rates, hyphal elongation, and biomass yield are comparable to those of the wild-type parental strain, indicating that the introduced genetic modifications do not impose a significant fitness cost. The strains are capable of utilizing a wide range of carbon sources, including glucose, xylose, sucrose, and lactose, and can grow in the presence of nitrogen sources such as ammonium sulfate, urea, and amino acids. The strains are also tolerant of low pH conditions, maintaining viability and metabolic activity at pH values as low as 2.5, which is critical for the stability of 3HP in its protonated form.

The recombinant strains are further characterized by the absence of detectable extracellular beta-alanine, despite the substantial intracellular accumulation of this intermediate, indicating that the engineered pathway is not limited by transport or export mechanisms. The intracellular pools of beta-alanine and 3HP are maintained at relatively constant levels throughout the production phase, suggesting that the rate-limiting step in the pathway is not the biosynthesis of these intermediates but rather the conversion of malonate semialdehyde to 3HP. The deletion of Apald6 results in a significant increase in the intracellular concentration of malonate semialdehyde, confirming its role as a competitive sink and validating the hypothesis that its deletion enhances flux through the desired pathway.

The recombinant strains are amenable to large-scale fermentation in both shake-flask and bioreactor systems. When cultivated in minimal medium containing 100 grams per liter of glucose at pH 2.8 and 30°C, the strain ABF_008339 achieves a maximum extracellular 3HP titer of 0.88 grams per liter after seven days of cultivation, with a yield of 0.31 grams per gram of glucose consumed. This represents a 3.3-fold improvement over the parental strain expressing the same biosynthetic pathway but retaining the Apald6 gene. The strain also exhibits a 2.5-fold increase in volumetric productivity compared to the parental strain, with peak 3HP production occurring between 48 and 72 hours of cultivation.

The recombinant strains are further capable of producing aconitic acid as a primary metabolic product when the cad1 gene is left intact and the expression of the 3HP biosynthetic pathway is modulated to avoid competition for oxaloacetate. Under these conditions, the strain accumulates extracellular aconitic acid at concentrations exceeding 1.2 grams per liter, with minimal accumulation of itaconic acid or other TCA-cycle intermediates. This dual functionality enables the strain to be used for the production of either 3HP or aconitic acid, depending on the fermentation conditions and genetic background, thereby increasing the versatility and commercial value of the platform.

The recombinant strains are genetically stable over multiple subcultures, with no loss of 3HP production capability observed after ten consecutive transfers in non-selective medium. Spore stocks stored at –80°C in 15% glycerol retain full viability and productivity after one year of storage. The strains are also resistant to contamination by common bacterial and fungal contaminants under acidic fermentation conditions, owing to the low pH of the medium, which suppresses the growth of most non-filamentous microorganisms. These characteristics make the strains suitable for industrial-scale fermentation without the need for stringent sterilization protocols or the addition of antibiotics.

The recombinant strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus described herein represent a novel class of fungal biocatalysts that combine the metabolic versatility of filamentous fungi with the precision of synthetic biology. Their ability to produce 3-hydroxypropionic acid at high titers and yields under industrially relevant conditions establishes a new standard for bio-based chemical production and provides a foundation for the development of next-generation biomanufacturing platforms.

### Methods of Producing Aconitic Acid

The present invention provides novel methods for the industrial-scale production of aconitic acid using recombinant strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus that have been genetically modified to suppress the conversion of aconitic acid to itaconic acid while maintaining high flux through the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. These methods are distinct from conventional approaches to organic acid production in fungi, which typically rely on the overexpression of native biosynthetic enzymes or the deletion of competing pathways to enhance product accumulation. In contrast, the methods described herein leverage the inherent metabolic architecture of Aspergillus pseudoterreus, which naturally produces aconitic acid as an intermediate in the TCA cycle, and redirect metabolic flux toward its accumulation by modulating the expression of the heterologous 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) biosynthetic pathway without disrupting the native cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cad1) gene.

In one embodiment, the method comprises cultivating a recombinant strain of Aspergillus pseudoterreus that expresses the heterologous beta-alanine biosynthetic pathway—comprising aspartate-1-decarboxylase from Tribolium castaneum, beta-alanine pyruvate transaminase from Bacillus cereus, and 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase from Escherichia coli—under the control of strong constitutive promoters, while retaining the native cad1 gene intact. The strain is inoculated into a liquid minimal medium containing a fermentable carbohydrate source, such as glucose, at a concentration of 80 to 120 grams per liter, and the pH of the medium is maintained at a value between 3.0 and 4.0. The cultivation is carried out in a stirred-tank bioreactor at a temperature of 28 to 32°C, with agitation maintained at a rate sufficient to ensure adequate oxygen transfer, typically between 100 and 400 revolutions per minute, and with air sparging at a rate of 0.3 to 0.6 vessel volumes per minute.

Under these conditions, the expression of the heterologous 3HP biosynthetic pathway creates a metabolic demand for oxaloacetate and aspartate, which are precursors to beta-alanine synthesis. This demand diverts carbon flux away from the conversion of aconitic acid to itaconic acid, which is catalyzed by the cad1-encoded enzyme, and instead promotes the accumulation of aconitic acid as a metabolic endpoint. The intracellular concentration of oxaloacetate is maintained at elevated levels due to the increased consumption by the heterologous aspartate-1-decarboxylase, which competes with the native citrate synthase and aconitase enzymes for shared substrates. This competition, combined with the absence of a strong sink for aconitic acid, results in its extracellular accumulation at concentrations exceeding 1.2 grams per liter after seven days of cultivation.

The method further includes the step of monitoring extracellular metabolite concentrations using high-performance liquid chromatography or gas chromatography-mass spectrometry to ensure that the accumulation of aconitic acid is not accompanied by significant production of itaconic acid, citric acid, or other TCA-cycle intermediates. In the strains described herein, the production of itaconic acid is reduced by more than 95% compared to the wild-type strain, and the production of citric acid is maintained at levels below 0.1 grams per liter. This specificity is attributed to the precise modulation of metabolic flux through the TCA cycle, which is achieved without the need for direct genetic manipulation of the cad1 gene or other TCA-cycle enzymes.

The fermentation process is conducted in a two-phase mode, wherein the initial phase (0 to 48 hours) is optimized for rapid biomass accumulation under high aeration and neutral pH (pH 5.0 to 5.5), followed by a production phase (48 to 168 hours) wherein the pH is gradually lowered to 3.0 to 3.5 and aeration is increased to enhance oxygen transfer and product secretion. The transition between phases is triggered by a drop in dissolved oxygen tension or a rise in carbon dioxide evolution rate, which serves as an indicator of the shift from growth to production metabolism. This strategy ensures that the strain reaches a high biomass density before the metabolic redirection occurs, thereby maximizing the total yield of aconitic acid.

The extracellular aconitic acid is recovered from the fermentation broth by filtration to remove mycelial biomass, followed by concentration via vacuum evaporation and purification by crystallization or ion-exchange chromatography. The recovered aconitic acid is obtained in a form that is greater than 95% pure, with minimal contamination by other organic acids or sugars. The purified aconitic acid may be used directly as a food additive, chelating agent, or pharmaceutical intermediate, or it may be chemically decarboxylated to produce 3-hydroxypropionic acid for further conversion to acrylic acid or poly-3HP.

The method is scalable to industrial volumes, having been successfully demonstrated in 0.5-liter, 20-liter, and 30-liter bioreactor systems. The volumetric productivity of aconitic acid in the recombinant strains exceeds 0.007 grams per liter per hour, which is comparable to or exceeds the productivity of industrial citric acid production strains. The strains exhibit no loss of productivity over multiple fermentation cycles, and spore stocks remain viable and productive after long-term storage. The method further eliminates the need for costly downstream processing steps such as acidification, neutralization, or salt formation, as aconitic acid is secreted in its protonated form under the acidic conditions of the fermentation broth.

The invention further provides a method for producing aconitic acid in the absence of the heterologous 3HP biosynthetic pathway by simply cultivating the wild-type strain of Aspergillus pseudoterreus under conditions that suppress itaconic acid production. These conditions include the use of low-nitrogen media, elevated glucose concentrations, and controlled pH and aeration profiles that favor TCA-cycle activity over secondary metabolism. This alternative method provides a commercially viable route to aconitic acid production without the need for genetic engineering, thereby offering a lower-cost option for applications where the conversion of aconitic acid to 3HP is not required.

The methods described herein represent a novel and non-obvious approach to the production of aconitic acid, as they exploit the metabolic interplay between a heterologous biosynthetic pathway and native TCA-cycle enzymes to achieve product accumulation without direct manipulation of the target pathway. This strategy is broadly applicable to other organic acid-producing fungi and provides a template for the development of strains that produce other TCA-cycle intermediates, such as isocitrate or alpha-ketoglutarate, through similar metabolic redirection.

### Methods of Producing 3-HP

The present invention provides novel, industrially scalable methods for the production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) using recombinant strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus that have been genetically engineered to express a heterologous beta-alanine biosynthetic pathway and to lack the endogenous malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase gene Apald6. These methods are distinguished from prior art by their ability to produce 3HP at high titers and yields under acidic fermentation conditions, wherein the product remains predominantly in its protonated, free-acid form, thereby eliminating the need for costly downstream recovery processes such as neutralization, acidification, or salt extraction. The methods are optimized for use with renewable carbohydrate feedstocks, including glucose, xylose, and lignocellulosic hydrolysates, and are designed to minimize by-product formation, maximize volumetric productivity, and ensure genetic stability over multiple fermentation cycles.

In one embodiment, the method comprises inoculating a liquid fermentation medium with a spore suspension of the recombinant strain ABF_008339, which contains a single copy of the 3HP biosynthetic pathway integrated at the cad1 locus and a second copy integrated at a secondary genomic site, and which has been genetically modified to delete the Apald6 gene. The fermentation medium is a minimal medium containing 80 to 120 grams per liter of glucose as the primary carbon source, 2.0 to 3.0 grams per liter of ammonium sulfate as the nitrogen source, and essential trace elements including magnesium, calcium, potassium, zinc, manganese, iron, and copper, adjusted to an initial pH of 5.0 to 5.5. The medium is sterilized by autoclaving and cooled to 30°C prior to inoculation.

The inoculum is prepared by growing the recombinant strain on solid complete medium for five days to produce mature conidia, which are harvested by washing the agar surface with sterile 0.4% Tween 80 solution. The resulting spore suspension is diluted to a concentration of 2 × 10⁶ spores per milliliter and used to inoculate the fermentation medium at a volume of 1 to 5% (v/v). The fermentation is carried out in a stirred-tank bioreactor equipped with pH and dissolved oxygen control, with agitation maintained at 100 revolutions per minute during the initial growth phase. The pH of the medium is allowed to drop naturally as a result of organic acid production, and once it reaches 2.8, the agitation rate is increased to 400 revolutions per minute to enhance oxygen transfer, and the pH is maintained at 2.8 through the continuous addition of 5 M potassium hydroxide solution.

The fermentation is conducted at a temperature of 30°C, with air sparged at a rate of 0.4 vessel volumes per minute to maintain adequate oxygen levels. The duration of the fermentation is between 120 and 168 hours, during which time the extracellular concentration of 3HP increases steadily, reaching a maximum titer of 0.88 grams per liter by day seven. The production of 3HP is growth-associated, with the highest rates of accumulation occurring between 48 and 96 hours, coinciding with the exponential phase of biomass growth. The production rate declines after 96 hours as the culture enters a stationary phase and metabolic flux is redirected toward other secondary metabolites, although no significant degradation of 3HP is observed in the recombinant strain due to the deletion of Apald6.

The method further includes the step of monitoring extracellular metabolite concentrations using high-performance liquid chromatography or gas chromatography-mass spectrometry to ensure that the production of 3HP is not accompanied by significant accumulation of itaconic acid, citric acid, aconitic acid, or glycerol. In the recombinant strain ABF_008339, the concentration of itaconic acid is below the limit of detection, and the production of aconitic acid is reduced by more than 90% compared to the wild-type strain. The concentration of glycerol is maintained below 0.1 grams per liter, indicating that carbon flux is efficiently directed toward the 3HP pathway rather than toward alternative reduction pathways.

The method further comprises the step of harvesting the fermentation broth by filtration through a 0.2-micrometer membrane to remove mycelial biomass, followed by concentration of the filtrate by vacuum evaporation to reduce the volume by 50 to 80%. The concentrated broth is then subjected to purification by ion-exchange chromatography using a weakly basic anion-exchange resin, which selectively binds 3-hydroxypropionate under acidic conditions. The bound 3HP is eluted with a dilute acid solution, and the resulting solution is neutralized and concentrated to yield a purified 3HP product with a purity of greater than 95%. The purified 3HP may be crystallized as a solid or stored as a concentrated aqueous solution for downstream chemical conversion.

The method may be adapted for use with alternative carbon sources, including xylose, sucrose, lactose, or mixtures thereof, by adjusting the nutrient composition of the medium to ensure balanced nitrogen and carbon metabolism. The recombinant strain is capable of utilizing xylose as a sole carbon source, achieving a 3HP titer of 0.65 grams per liter after seven days of cultivation, demonstrating its suitability for the conversion of lignocellulosic hydrolysates. The method further permits the use of fed-batch or continuous fermentation modes, wherein additional carbohydrate is fed incrementally to maintain a low residual sugar concentration and prevent catabolite repression.

The method is scalable to industrial volumes, having been successfully demonstrated in 0.5-liter, 20-liter, and 30-liter bioreactor systems. The volumetric productivity of 3HP in the recombinant strain exceeds 0.005 grams per liter per hour, which is comparable to or exceeds the productivity of industrial citric acid production systems. The strain exhibits no loss of productivity over ten consecutive subcultures in non-selective medium, and spore stocks stored at –80°C retain full viability and productivity after one year of storage. The method further eliminates the need for antibiotics or antifungal agents, as the low pH of the fermentation broth (pH 2.8) effectively suppresses the growth of bacterial and fungal contaminants.

The method further provides a means of producing 3HP without the need for exogenous cofactor supplementation, as the heterologous 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase from Escherichia coli is NADPH-dependent, and the native fungal metabolism provides sufficient reducing power through the pentose phosphate pathway and malic enzyme activity. The intracellular concentration of NADPH is maintained at levels sufficient to support 3HP synthesis, as confirmed by targeted metabolomics and enzyme activity assays. The method further does not require the addition of inducers, inducible promoters, or complex media components, making it suitable for low-cost, large-scale manufacturing.

The method further includes the step of recovering and reusing the mycelial biomass as a source of protein or as a substrate for anaerobic digestion, thereby enhancing the overall sustainability and economic viability of the process. The spent broth may also be recycled as a nutrient base for subsequent fermentations, with appropriate supplementation of carbon and nitrogen sources, further reducing process costs.

The methods described herein represent a significant advancement over prior art in the field of microbial 3HP production, as they enable the production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid at high titers and yields under industrially relevant conditions, with minimal downstream processing requirements and without the need for genetic instability, costly media, or complex process controls. The combination of genetic engineering, systems-level metabolic understanding, and optimized fermentation strategy results in a process that is robust, scalable, and economically viable for the commercial production of bio-based 3HP.

### Example 1

The recombinant strain ABF_008339, engineered to produce 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) by expressing a heterologous beta-alanine biosynthetic pathway and deleting the malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase gene Apald6, was cultivated in 250-milliliter shake flasks containing 50 milliliters of minimal medium supplemented with 100 grams per liter of glucose as the carbon source. The medium was adjusted to an initial pH of 5.0 and sterilized by autoclaving at 121°C for 20 minutes. Spore inoculum was prepared by growing the strain on complete medium agar for five days, harvesting conidia with 0.4% Tween 80, and diluting the suspension to a concentration of 2 × 10⁶ spores per milliliter. Each flask was inoculated with 1 milliliter of the spore suspension, sealed with sterile cotton plugs, and incubated at 30°C in an orbital shaker at 200 revolutions per minute. The pH of the medium was allowed to decrease naturally over the course of the fermentation, and once it reached 2.8, the flasks were supplemented with 5 M potassium hydroxide solution as needed to maintain the pH at 2.8. Samples were collected at 24-hour intervals for the first 96 hours and then at 48-hour intervals thereafter until day 8.

Extracellular metabolites were quantified using high-performance liquid chromatography with a refractive index detector and an Aminex HPX-87H ion-exclusion column. The mobile phase consisted of 0.0045 M sulfuric acid at a flow rate of 0.55 milliliters per minute, with column temperature maintained at 65°C. The concentration of 3HP was determined by comparison to a calibration curve generated from pure 3HP standards. Intracellular metabolites were extracted using a chloroform-methanol-water phase separation method, derivatized by methoxyamination and trimethylsilylation, and analyzed by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry. Proteins were extracted from mycelial biomass, digested with trypsin, and analyzed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry using both global and targeted proteomic approaches.

The results demonstrated that the strain ABF_008339 produced 3HP at a maximum extracellular concentration of 0.82 ± 0.05 grams per liter after seven days of cultivation, with a yield of 0.30 ± 0.02 grams of 3HP per gram of glucose consumed. The production of itaconic acid was undetectable, and the concentration of aconitic acid was reduced by 92% compared to the wild-type strain. The intracellular concentration of malonate semialdehyde was elevated by 4.1-fold compared to the parental strain, confirming the metabolic block at the Apald6 locus. The expression levels of the heterologous genes TcPAND, BcBAPAT, and EcHPDH were confirmed to be stable throughout the fermentation, with no evidence of transcriptional silencing or protein degradation. The targeted proteomic analysis revealed no significant upregulation of enzymes involved in alternative 3HP degradation pathways, and no accumulation of 2,4-diaminobutanoate or other nitrogenous by-products was observed beyond day 4. The strain exhibited no growth defects, with a maximum dry cell weight of 8.2 grams per liter, comparable to the wild-type strain. These results confirm the stability, productivity, and metabolic fidelity of the recombinant strain under shake-flask conditions.

### Example 2

The recombinant strain ABF_008339 was cultivated in a 20-liter stirred-tank bioreactor to evaluate its performance under industrial-scale conditions. The bioreactor was filled with 15 liters of minimal medium containing 100 grams per liter of glucose, 2.36 grams per liter of ammonium sulfate, and trace elements, and was sterilized by autoclaving. The initial pH was adjusted to 5.0, and the temperature was maintained at 30°C. The inoculum consisted of 150 milliliters of a spore suspension at a concentration of 2 × 10⁶ spores per milliliter. Agitation was maintained at 100 revolutions per minute during the first 48 hours of cultivation, after which it was increased to 400 revolutions per minute upon detection of a pH drop to 2.8. The pH was controlled automatically through the continuous addition of 5 M potassium hydroxide solution, and air was sparged at a rate of 0.4 vessel volumes per minute. Off-gas analysis was performed using a mass spectrometer to monitor CO₂ evolution and O₂ consumption, and biomass was measured by dry weight after filtration and lyophilization.

The extracellular concentration of 3HP reached a maximum of 0.69 ± 0.08 grams per liter at 67 hours, after which it declined steadily to 0.17 ± 0.03 grams per liter by day 8. The production of aconitic acid began at 72 hours and increased to a concentration of 0.91 ± 0.12 grams per liter by day 7, coinciding with the decline in 3HP. The concentration of glycerol peaked at 0.35 ± 0.05 grams per liter at 96 hours, and the concentration of citric acid increased to 0.21 ± 0.04 grams per liter by day 7. The biomass reached a maximum of 11.5 grams per liter at 72 hours. The proteomic analysis of samples collected at 48, 72, and 96 hours revealed a significant upregulation of enzymes involved in the TCA cycle, including citrate synthase, aconitase, and isocitrate dehydrogenase, as well as enzymes associated with glycerol metabolism and amino acid catabolism. The targeted proteomic analysis confirmed the continued expression of the heterologous 3HP pathway enzymes, but also revealed the induction of a suite of aldehyde dehydrogenases and acyl-CoA transferases that were not detected in the shake-flask experiments. These findings suggest that the 20-liter bioreactor environment triggers a distinct metabolic response that activates competing pathways not observed at smaller scales, possibly due to differences in oxygen transfer, shear stress, or nutrient gradients. The results demonstrate that while the recombinant strain is capable of producing 3HP at industrially relevant scales, further optimization of bioreactor parameters is required to prevent the metabolic shift toward organic acid production and to sustain 3HP accumulation.

### Example 3

The recombinant strain ABF_008339 and its parental strain ABF_004528_6, which retains the Apald6 gene, were cultivated in 0.5-liter Sixfors bioreactors to evaluate the impact of improved aeration and pH control on 3HP production. The bioreactors were filled with 0.45 liters of minimal medium containing 100 grams per liter of glucose and were inoculated with 2 × 10⁶ spores per milliliter. Agitation was maintained at 200 revolutions per minute until the pH dropped to 2.8, after which it was increased to 600 revolutions per minute. The pH was maintained at 2.8 through the automated addition of 5 M potassium hydroxide, and air was sparged at a rate of 0.4 vessel volumes per minute. Cultivation was carried out for seven days, with samples collected at 24-hour intervals for extracellular metabolite analysis and biomass measurement.

The extracellular concentration of 3HP in the recombinant strain ABF_008339 reached a maximum of 0.88 ± 0.11 grams per liter by day 7, representing a 3.3-fold increase compared to the parental strain, which accumulated only 0.27 ± 0.10 grams per liter. The concentration of aconitic acid in the recombinant strain was below the limit of detection, whereas the parental strain produced 0.41 ± 0.07 grams per liter of aconitic acid. The concentration of glycerol in the recombinant strain was 0.11 ± 0.02 grams per liter, compared to 0.24 ± 0.05 grams per liter in the parental strain, indicating more efficient carbon flux toward 3HP. The dry cell weight of the recombinant strain was 9.8 ± 0.6 grams per liter, slightly higher than the parental strain at 9.1 ± 0.5 grams per liter, confirming that the deletion of Apald6 did not impair growth. The proteomic analysis of the recombinant strain revealed a 5.2-fold increase in the intracellular concentration of malonate semialdehyde and a 3.8-fold increase in the activity of the heterologous 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase, confirming that the deletion of Apald6 enhanced flux through the desired pathway. The targeted metabolomic analysis confirmed the absence of 2,4-diaminobutanoate accumulation beyond day 4, suggesting that the metabolic burden associated with nitrogen imbalance had been resolved. These results demonstrate that the combination of improved bioreactor conditions and the deletion of Apald6 synergistically enhances 3HP production, validating the engineered strain as a robust platform for industrial-scale manufacturing.

### Example 4

The recombinant strain ABF_008339 was cultivated in minimal medium containing 80 grams per liter of xylose as the sole carbon source to evaluate its ability to utilize lignocellulosic sugars for 3-hydroxypropionic acid production. The fermentation was carried out in 0.5-liter Sixfors bioreactors under the same conditions as described in Example 3, with pH maintained at 2.8 and agitation at 600 revolutions per minute. The culture was monitored for 120 hours, with samples collected at 24-hour intervals. Extracellular metabolites were quantified by HPLC, and intracellular metabolites were analyzed by GC-MS. The biomass was measured by dry weight, and the expression levels of the heterologous pathway enzymes were assessed by targeted proteomics.

The strain produced 3HP at a maximum concentration of 0.65 ± 0.08 grams per liter after 96 hours, with a yield of 0.27 ± 0.03 grams of 3HP per gram of xylose consumed. The production rate was slower than with glucose, with peak 3HP accumulation occurring at 96 hours rather than 72 hours, suggesting a delay in xylose uptake or catabolism. The concentration of glycerol was 0.14 ± 0.03 grams per liter, and the concentration of aconitic acid was below the limit of detection. The proteomic analysis confirmed that the expression levels of TcPAND, BcBAPAT, and EcHPDH were comparable to those observed in glucose-fed cultures, indicating that the heterologous pathway is not subject to carbon catabolite repression by xylose. The intracellular concentration of xylulose-5-phosphate and ribulose-5-phosphate was elevated, suggesting that the pentose phosphate pathway was active and contributing to NADPH generation. The concentration of ATP and NADPH was maintained at levels sufficient to support 3HP synthesis, with no indication of cofactor limitation. These results demonstrate that the recombinant strain is capable of efficiently converting xylose, a major component of lignocellulosic hydrolysates, into 3-hydroxypropionic acid, thereby expanding the range of feedstocks suitable for industrial bioconversion and enhancing the economic viability of the process.
