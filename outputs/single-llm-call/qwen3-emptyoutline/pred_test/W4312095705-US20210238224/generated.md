# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of targeted protein degradation therapeutics, specifically to a novel heterobifunctional small molecule compound designed to simultaneously induce the degradation of Smad3 and stabilize hypoxia-inducible factor 2-alpha (HIF-2α) through the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway. This compound represents a new class of proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs) that exploit the endogenous cellular machinery of ubiquitin ligases to achieve dual biological modulation—degrading a pathogenic signaling protein while concurrently preserving a protective transcription factor. The invention is particularly directed to the treatment of chronic kidney disease, including renal fibrosis, inflammation, impaired renal function, and renal anemia, by targeting the Smad3-mediated fibrotic pathway and enhancing the HIF-2α-mediated reparative and erythropoietic pathways. The compound is chemically engineered to bind with high specificity to Smad3 and the von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) E3 ubiquitin ligase, thereby facilitating the polyubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of Smad3 while preventing the degradation of HIF-2α, which is normally targeted by VHL under normoxic conditions. The invention further encompasses the pharmaceutical compositions, methods of synthesis, and therapeutic uses of this compound in human and veterinary medicine for the management of fibrotic, inflammatory, and anemic conditions associated with chronic kidney injury.

## BACKGROUND ART

The ubiquitin–proteasome system (UPS) is a highly conserved cellular mechanism responsible for the selective degradation of misfolded, damaged, or regulatory proteins. In recent years, this system has been harnessed for therapeutic purposes through the development of proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs), which are heterobifunctional molecules composed of three structural elements: a ligand that binds to a protein of interest (POI), a ligand that recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase, and a chemical linker connecting the two. By bringing the POI into proximity with the E3 ligase, PROTACs induce the transfer of ubiquitin moieties onto the target protein, marking it for destruction by the 26S proteasome. This mechanism enables the elimination of proteins previously considered “undruggable” by conventional inhibitors, as it does not require direct inhibition of enzymatic activity or binding at functional sites but rather exploits the cell’s own degradation machinery.

Smad3, a key intracellular mediator of transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) signaling, plays a central role in the pathogenesis of renal fibrosis, a common final pathway in chronic kidney disease (CKD). Upon activation by TGF-β, Smad3 translocates to the nucleus and drives the transcription of genes involved in extracellular matrix deposition, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and inflammatory cell recruitment. Genetic and pharmacological inhibition of Smad3 has been shown to attenuate fibrosis in multiple preclinical models, yet traditional small molecule inhibitors that block Smad3 phosphorylation or nuclear translocation require sustained high systemic concentrations to achieve efficacy, leading to off-target effects and limited therapeutic windows. Moreover, complete suppression of Smad3 may interfere with its physiological roles in immune regulation and tissue homeostasis, raising concerns regarding long-term safety.

Concurrently, hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs) have emerged as critical regulators of renal adaptation to injury. HIF-2α, in particular, exerts renoprotective effects by promoting erythropoietin (EPO) production, enhancing iron mobilization, stimulating angiogenesis, and suppressing fibrotic gene expression. Unlike HIF-1α, which is ubiquitously expressed and often associated with pro-fibrotic outcomes, HIF-2α is predominantly expressed in renal interstitial fibroblasts and endothelial cells and is tightly regulated by the von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex under normoxic conditions. Under normal oxygen tension, VHL recognizes hydroxylated HIF-2α and facilitates its polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. However, in pathological states such as CKD, where hypoxia is prevalent, HIF-2α stabilization has been shown to mitigate fibrosis, preserve peritubular capillaries, and correct anemia. Pharmacological stabilization of HIF-2α via inhibition of prolyl hydroxylase domain (PHD) enzymes has demonstrated clinical promise in treating renal anemia, but these agents globally stabilize all HIF isoforms, potentially activating HIF-1α-mediated pathways that may exacerbate fibrosis or promote tumorigenesis.

The concept of simultaneously degrading a pathogenic protein while stabilizing a protective one through a single molecular entity has not been previously realized in the context of CKD. While PROTACs have been developed to degrade a variety of disease-associated proteins—including androgen receptor, BRD4, and BCL-xL—their ability to simultaneously modulate the stability of endogenous E3 ligase substrates has remained largely unexplored. In particular, the exploitation of VHL as an E3 ligase recruiter in a PROTAC design presents a unique opportunity: by occupying VHL, the PROTAC may competitively inhibit the ubiquitination of HIF-2α, thereby stabilizing it without directly inhibiting its degradation machinery. This phenomenon, termed “substrate stabilization through E3 ligase sequestration,” has been observed in other contexts, such as with cereblon-based PROTACs stabilizing Ikaros and Aiolos, but has never been deliberately engineered into a therapeutic construct for renal fibrosis.

Prior art includes small molecule inhibitors of Smad3, such as SIS3, which block Smad3 phosphorylation, and VHL stabilizers such as roxadustat and vadadustat, which inhibit PHD enzymes to elevate HIF levels. However, these agents act independently and require co-administration, increasing the risk of drug–drug interactions, pharmacokinetic mismatch, and cumulative toxicity. Furthermore, PHD inhibitors lack specificity for HIF-2α over HIF-1α, potentially triggering undesirable downstream effects. No prior invention has described a single molecule capable of inducing Smad3 degradation while simultaneously stabilizing HIF-2α through VHL-mediated mechanisms. The present invention overcomes these limitations by designing a heterobifunctional PROTAC that physically links a Smad3-binding moiety to a VHL-recruiting ligand, thereby achieving coordinated degradation of a fibrotic driver and stabilization of a repair-promoting factor within the same molecular event.

The chemical optimization of PROTACs has historically focused on linker length, rigidity, and polarity to maximize ternary complex formation between the POI, PROTAC, and E3 ligase. Previous studies have demonstrated that even minor alterations in linker chemistry can dramatically affect degradation efficiency, cellular permeability, and in vivo pharmacokinetics. Yet, no prior work has addressed the dual functional consequence of E3 ligase engagement—namely, the unintended stabilization of its natural substrates—as a therapeutic advantage to be intentionally leveraged. The present invention not only exploits this phenomenon but systematically optimizes the PROTAC structure to maximize both Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization, while minimizing off-target effects and maximizing tissue specificity. The invention further demonstrates, for the first time, that such a dual-acting PROTAC can be administered in vivo with a favorable safety profile, achieving significant attenuation of renal fibrosis, improvement in renal function, and correction of renal anemia in multiple preclinical models of CKD.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel heterobifunctional small molecule compound capable of simultaneously inducing the degradation of Smad3 and stabilizing HIF-2α through the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway, thereby achieving a synergistic therapeutic effect in the treatment of chronic kidney disease. The compound comprises a first binding moiety that selectively interacts with Smad3, a second binding moiety that selectively recruits the von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) E3 ubiquitin ligase, and a chemical linker covalently connecting the two moieties, wherein the linker is selected from a group consisting of succinic acid derivatives, polyethylene glycol chains, alkyl spacers, or combinations thereof, and wherein the compound is configured such that its binding to Smad3 and VHL facilitates the formation of a ternary complex that results in the polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of Smad3 while simultaneously preventing the ubiquitination and degradation of HIF-2α. The compound is chemically characterized as a white lyophilized powder with a molecular weight of approximately 750.8 Daltons, and its structure has been confirmed by high-performance liquid chromatography and mass spectrometry.

The invention further encompasses the use of this compound for the treatment of renal fibrosis, chronic kidney disease, renal anemia, and associated complications including tubulointerstitial inflammation, peritubular capillary rarefaction, and impaired iron metabolism. The compound is administered in a therapeutically effective amount, either as a single agent or in combination with other renal protective agents, via routes including subcutaneous, intraperitoneal, intravenous, or oral administration, with subcutaneous administration being preferred for sustained plasma exposure and tissue penetration. The compound demonstrates efficacy at nanomolar concentrations in vitro and achieves plasma concentrations exceeding the effective threshold for at least 1.5 hours following subcutaneous dosing in murine models, with tissue distribution observed in the kidney, liver, and heart. The compound exhibits a wide therapeutic window, with no significant toxicity observed in mice following repeated administration at doses up to 300 mg/kg, and no alterations in hematological, biochemical, or histological parameters of major organs, except for a transient increase in nucleated cells in the spleen consistent with enhanced erythropoiesis.

The compound is synthesized by coupling a Smad3-binding small molecule with a VHL-binding ligand through a succinic acid-derived linker, with the Smad3-binding moiety being in the S-chiral configuration, which has been determined to confer superior degradation and stabilization activity compared to the R-chiral isomer. The compound does not alter the mRNA expression levels of Smad3 or HIF-2α, indicating that its effects are mediated exclusively at the post-translational level. The compound does not induce the stabilization of HIF-1α, owing to the presence of alternative E3 ligases such as RACK1, HAF, and CHIP in renal tubular cells that continue to mediate HIF-1α degradation even in the presence of the compound. The compound’s activity is dependent on the expression levels of both Smad3 and VHL, rendering it selectively active in diseased renal tissue where Smad3 is upregulated and VHL is abundantly expressed, while sparing healthy tissue with low Smad3 expression.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the compound in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent, formulated for parenteral, oral, or inhalation delivery. The compound may be formulated as a lyophilized powder for reconstitution, a solution for injection, or a solid dosage form for sustained release. The compound is stable under standard storage conditions and retains its biological activity for at least 24 months when stored at 2–8°C. The invention further includes methods of manufacturing the compound, including chemical synthesis routes, purification protocols, and analytical characterization methods to ensure batch consistency and purity.

The therapeutic effects of the compound are mediated through a dual mechanism: the degradation of Smad3 leads to the suppression of TGF-β-driven fibrotic gene expression, including collagen I and α-smooth muscle actin, resulting in reduced extracellular matrix accumulation and inhibition of epithelial–mesenchymal transition. Concurrently, the stabilization of HIF-2α leads to the upregulation of erythropoietin, increased expression of intestinal iron transporters including duodenal cytochrome b, divalent metal transporter 1, and ferroportin 1, decreased expression of hepcidin, and improved iron mobilization, collectively resulting in the correction of renal anemia. Additionally, HIF-2α stabilization enhances vascular endothelial growth factor production, leading to the restoration of peritubular capillary density and improved renal oxygenation. The compound’s ability to simultaneously target these two pathways results in a more comprehensive and durable therapeutic effect than either pathway modulation alone.

The invention further provides a method of treating chronic kidney disease in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of the compound, wherein the subject has been diagnosed with renal fibrosis, elevated serum creatinine, elevated blood urea nitrogen, reduced glomerular filtration rate, or anemia associated with chronic kidney disease. The method may further include monitoring serum levels of EPO, ferritin, hepcidin, cystatin C, or HIF-2α as biomarkers of therapeutic response. The compound may be administered intermittently, such as every 48 hours, to maintain therapeutic concentrations while minimizing potential off-target effects. The invention further provides a diagnostic kit for identifying subjects likely to respond to the compound, comprising reagents for detecting elevated Smad3 expression and/or VHL expression in renal tissue or circulating biomarkers indicative of active fibrosis and anemia.

## EMBODIMENTS

### Example 1

The compound of the present invention is a heterobifunctional proteolysis-targeting chimera composed of three essential structural components: a Smad3-binding ligand, a von Hippel–Lindau (VHL)-recruiting ligand, and a chemical linker connecting the two. The Smad3-binding ligand is derived from a previously identified small molecule compound that exhibits high-affinity binding to Smad3 without inhibiting its phosphorylation, thereby allowing the compound to bind Smad3 in its active, phosphorylated state and facilitate its recruitment to the ubiquitin machinery. This ligand is chemically modified to include a reactive functional group suitable for conjugation to a linker, and its stereochemistry has been optimized to the S-configuration, which confers significantly greater potency in inducing Smad3 degradation compared to the R-configuration. The VHL-recruiting ligand is a derivative of a known small molecule that binds with high specificity to the VHL protein within the E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, enabling the compound to hijack VHL’s ubiquitin-transfer activity. This ligand is structurally distinct from PHD inhibitors and does not interfere with the hydroxylation of HIF-2α; instead, it competes with HIF-2α for binding to VHL, thereby preventing HIF-2α ubiquitination while permitting the ubiquitination of Smad3.

The linker connecting the two ligands is a succinic acid derivative, specifically (CH₂COOH)₂, which provides optimal spatial orientation and conformational flexibility to enable simultaneous engagement of both Smad3 and VHL. This linker was selected after systematic evaluation of multiple alternatives, including polyethylene glycol (PEG) chains of varying lengths, alkyl spacers, and rigid aromatic linkers. Compounds incorporating PEG linkers demonstrated reduced degradation efficiency and slower cellular uptake, whereas shorter alkyl linkers failed to maintain the necessary distance for ternary complex formation. The succinic acid linker, by contrast, provided the ideal balance of length, polarity, and metabolic stability, resulting in maximal Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization at nanomolar concentrations. The compound was synthesized through a multi-step organic synthesis protocol involving amide coupling, ester hydrolysis, and purification by preparative high-performance liquid chromatography. The final product was confirmed to be a single enantiomer with a purity exceeding 98% by HPLC and a molecular weight of 750.8 Daltons as determined by high-resolution mass spectrometry.

In vitro studies using human renal tubular epithelial cells (HK2) and rat renal interstitial fibroblasts (NRK-49F) demonstrated that the compound induced dose-dependent degradation of Smad3 protein with an EC₅₀ of approximately 25 nM, while simultaneously increasing HIF-2α protein levels by up to two-fold without altering HIF-1α expression. The degradation of Smad3 was completely abrogated in the presence of the proteasome inhibitor MG132, confirming that the effect was mediated through the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway. Ubiquitination assays revealed a marked increase in polyubiquitin chains conjugated to Smad3 only in the presence of the compound and MG132, with no such bands observed in untreated controls or in samples treated with either ligand alone. Western blot analysis confirmed that the compound did not alter the total protein levels of VHL, indicating that its mechanism of action did not involve downregulation of the E3 ligase itself. Furthermore, the compound did not induce the degradation of other VHL substrates such as pVHL-associated proteins or non-HIF transcription factors, demonstrating its selectivity for HIF-2α among VHL targets.

The compound exhibited no cytotoxicity in HK2 or NRK-49F cells at concentrations up to 1 μM, as assessed by MTT and lactate dehydrogenase assays. Gene expression profiling via RNA sequencing revealed no significant changes in the transcriptome of treated cells, except for the downregulation of fibrotic genes such as COL1A1, ACTA2, and TGFB1, and the upregulation of HIF-2α target genes including EPO, SLC40A1 (ferroportin), and SLC11A2 (DMT1). These transcriptional changes were consistent with the protein-level effects and occurred without altering the mRNA abundance of Smad3 or HIF-2α, confirming that the compound’s mechanism was exclusively post-translational. The compound also demonstrated superior efficacy compared to individual administration of Smad3 inhibitors or HIF stabilizers, with combination therapy showing no additive benefit and, in some cases, increased off-target effects.

In vivo studies were conducted in two well-established murine models of chronic kidney disease: the unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) model, which recapitulates rapid interstitial fibrosis and inflammation, and the 5/6 nephrectomy (5/6Nx) model, which mimics progressive renal failure and anemia. In the UUO model, subcutaneous administration of the compound at 30 mg/kg twice daily for 14 days resulted in a 40% reduction in Smad3 protein levels in the obstructed kidney, a two-fold increase in HIF-2α expression, and a marked attenuation of collagen deposition as visualized by Masson’s trichrome staining. The number of F4/80-positive macrophages infiltrating the interstitium was reduced by more than 60%, and the density of CD34-positive peritubular capillaries was restored to near-normal levels. Serum vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) levels increased significantly, correlating with capillary regeneration. In contrast, vehicle-treated UUO mice exhibited severe fibrosis, capillary loss, and macrophage accumulation.

In the 5/6Nx model, the compound was administered subcutaneously at 24 mg/kg every 48 hours for five weeks. Treated mice showed a significant improvement in renal function, as evidenced by lower serum levels of cystatin C, creatinine, and blood urea nitrogen compared to vehicle-treated controls, with differences becoming statistically significant after the second week of treatment. Hemoglobin and red blood cell counts, which declined progressively in vehicle-treated 5/6Nx mice, were restored to near-normal levels by week five. Serum erythropoietin levels increased by more than three-fold in compound-treated mice, and serum hepcidin levels were significantly suppressed, accompanied by elevated serum iron and ferritin concentrations. The mRNA expression of intestinal iron transporters Dcytb, DMT1, and ferroportin was markedly upregulated in the duodenum, indicating enhanced iron absorption. Importantly, these effects were observed without any change in HIF-1α levels, and no significant alterations in liver or spleen iron stores were detected, suggesting that the compound’s action was specifically directed toward renal and intestinal HIF-2α pathways.

Pharmacokinetic analyses revealed that after a single subcutaneous dose of 5 mg/kg, the compound reached a maximum plasma concentration of 221 ng/mL within four hours and maintained concentrations above the in vitro effective threshold (2 ng/mL) for over 1.5 hours. The half-life of the compound was approximately 1.58 hours, and tissue distribution studies showed highest concentrations in the kidney, followed by the liver and heart, with minimal accumulation in the brain, indicating limited blood–brain barrier penetration. Following intraperitoneal administration, peak concentrations were higher but clearance was more rapid, with a half-life of less than 0.5 hours, making subcutaneous administration the preferred route for sustained therapeutic effect. No significant differences in pharmacokinetics were observed between male and female mice, and no accumulation was detected after repeated dosing.

Toxicity assessments were conducted in both subacute and chronic settings. Mice received daily subcutaneous injections of the compound at doses of 50, 150, and 300 mg/kg for seven days, followed by a seven-day recovery period. No behavioral abnormalities, weight loss, or mortality were observed. Hematological parameters, including white blood cell count, platelet count, and red blood cell indices, remained within normal ranges. Biochemical markers of liver and kidney function, including alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, creatinine, and blood urea nitrogen, were unchanged. Histopathological examination of the liver, kidney, spleen, heart, and lungs revealed no evidence of inflammation, necrosis, or fibrosis. Notably, a transient increase in nucleated cells in the spleen was observed in the high-dose group, consistent with extramedullary erythropoiesis driven by elevated EPO, but no splenomegaly or hematological abnormalities were detected. These findings indicate a wide therapeutic index, with the effective dose being at least 100-fold lower than the maximum tolerated dose.

The compound was stable under standard storage conditions for at least 24 months when stored as a lyophilized powder at 2–8°C. Reconstitution in sterile saline or 5% dextrose did not alter its chemical integrity or biological activity. The compound was compatible with standard pharmaceutical excipients, including mannitol, sucrose, and polysorbate 80, and could be formulated as a sterile injectable solution or as a lyophilized product for reconstitution immediately prior to administration. The compound demonstrated no significant binding to human serum albumin or plasma proteins, suggesting that its pharmacokinetics are not limited by protein binding. No immunogenicity was observed in repeated-dose studies, and no anti-drug antibodies were detected in serum samples from treated animals.

The compound’s mechanism of action is uniquely advantageous in that it does not require continuous inhibition of Smad3 activity or global stabilization of HIF pathways. Instead, it acts as a catalytic degrader, with each molecule capable of inducing the degradation of multiple Smad3 molecules over time, thereby requiring lower systemic exposure than traditional inhibitors. Simultaneously, by occupying VHL without degrading it, the compound creates a transient blockade of HIF-2α ubiquitination, allowing newly synthesized HIF-2α to accumulate and exert its protective effects. This dual action is not achievable by co-administration of separate agents, as the pharmacokinetic profiles, tissue distributions, and dosing regimens of individual Smad3 inhibitors and HIF stabilizers are inherently mismatched. The compound’s tissue specificity arises from the fact that its activity is contingent upon the co-expression of both Smad3 and VHL, which are elevated in diseased renal tissue but remain low in healthy parenchyma. This results in a self-limiting therapeutic effect that is amplified in injured kidneys and minimized in normal organs, reducing the risk of systemic toxicity.

The compound’s ability to correct renal anemia without the need for exogenous erythropoietin or iron supplementation represents a significant advance over current therapies. Unlike PHD inhibitors, which elevate HIF-1α and may promote tumor growth or vascular complications, the compound selectively stabilizes HIF-2α, avoiding the activation of pro-angiogenic and pro-inflammatory HIF-1α targets. The compound’s effect on iron metabolism is also distinct from iron chelators or intravenous iron formulations, as it enhances endogenous iron absorption and mobilization through physiological HIF-2α-mediated pathways, reducing the risk of iron overload and oxidative stress. The compound’s impact on peritubular capillary density further distinguishes it from existing antifibrotic agents, as it addresses the microvascular rarefaction that underlies progressive renal hypoxia and fibrosis.

In summary, the compound of this invention represents a first-in-class therapeutic agent that simultaneously degrades a pathogenic signaling protein and stabilizes a protective transcription factor through a single molecular entity. Its unique mechanism of action, favorable pharmacokinetic profile, wide therapeutic window, and dual efficacy in fibrosis and anemia make it a highly promising candidate for the treatment of chronic kidney disease. The compound’s structure, synthesis, and biological effects are entirely novel and are not rendered obvious by any prior art, as no previous invention has described a PROTAC designed to exploit the stabilization of an E3 ligase substrate as a therapeutic mechanism, nor has any prior compound been shown to achieve both Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization in vivo with such potency and selectivity. The invention provides a new paradigm in targeted protein degradation therapy, wherein the unintended consequence of E3 ligase sequestration is deliberately engineered into a therapeutic advantage.
