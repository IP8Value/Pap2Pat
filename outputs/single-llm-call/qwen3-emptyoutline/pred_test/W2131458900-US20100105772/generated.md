# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmology and inflammatory disease therapeutics, specifically to the use of specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators for the inhibition of pathological corneal angiogenesis. More particularly, the invention encompasses the therapeutic application of aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1) as potent, dual-function agents capable of simultaneously suppressing inflammation and blocking neovascularization in the cornea. The invention further pertains to the molecular mechanisms by which these lipid mediators modulate the expression of vascular endothelial growth factors and their receptors, as well as the infiltration and activation of innate immune cells, thereby achieving a unique and clinically significant suppression of hemangiogenesis without affecting lymphangiogenesis. The compositions and methods described herein are particularly useful in the treatment of corneal diseases characterized by excessive blood vessel growth, including but not limited to inflammatory corneal neovascularization induced by sutures, chemical injury, infection, or graft rejection, and represent a novel class of non-steroidal, non-anti-VEGF biologics that operate through endogenous resolution pathways rather than through direct receptor antagonism or broad immunosuppression.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Corneal neovascularization (CNV) is a sight-threatening condition that arises as a pathological response to chronic inflammation, trauma, infection, or hypoxia. It is characterized by the aberrant ingrowth of blood vessels from the limbal vascular arcade into the normally avascular corneal stroma, which compromises corneal transparency, promotes immune cell infiltration, increases the risk of graft rejection in transplant recipients, and ultimately leads to visual impairment or blindness. Current therapeutic strategies for CNV are limited and often inadequate. Corticosteroids, the most commonly used agents, suppress inflammation but carry significant side effects including elevated intraocular pressure, cataract formation, and delayed wound healing. Anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) therapies, while effective in some contexts such as age-related macular degeneration, have shown inconsistent results in corneal neovascularization due to the multifactorial nature of the disease and the involvement of multiple angiogenic pathways beyond VEGF-A. Moreover, these agents do not address the underlying inflammatory drivers of neovascularization and may even impair the natural resolution of inflammation, potentially leading to chronicity and recurrence.

In recent years, it has become increasingly evident that inflammation is not merely a precursor to angiogenesis but an active, dynamic process that must be actively resolved to restore tissue homeostasis. The discovery of specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs)—lipid-derived signaling molecules synthesized from essential polyunsaturated fatty acids—has revolutionized the understanding of inflammation as a self-limiting, actively terminated process rather than a passive decline. Among these, lipoxins and resolvins have emerged as key endogenous regulators that promote the clearance of neutrophils, enhance macrophage phagocytosis of apoptotic cells, and suppress the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. Notably, aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 (ATLa), a stable analog of endogenous lipoxin A4 generated through acetylation of cyclooxygenase-2 by aspirin, possesses enhanced metabolic stability and prolonged biological activity compared to its native counterpart. Similarly, resolvin D1 and resolvin E1 are biosynthesized from docosahexaenoic acid and eicosapentaenoic acid, respectively, and have been shown to exert potent anti-inflammatory actions in a variety of tissues.

Despite these advances, the role of these lipid mediators in the regulation of corneal angiogenesis has remained largely unexplored. Prior studies have demonstrated that lipoxins and resolvins can inhibit angiogenesis in retinal and tumor models, but their efficacy, specificity, and mechanistic basis in the cornea—particularly in relation to differential effects on hemangiogenesis versus lymphangiogenesis—have not been systematically investigated. Furthermore, while some SPMs have been shown to modulate VEGF expression indirectly through suppression of inflammatory stimuli, no prior work has demonstrated that a single agent can directly inhibit VEGF-A-driven angiogenesis independently of its anti-inflammatory effects. The identification of specific receptors for these mediators in corneal epithelial cells, stromal keratocytes, and infiltrating immune cells has further suggested the potential for targeted, cell-specific modulation of angiogenic pathways. However, the precise cellular and molecular mechanisms by which ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 exert their effects on corneal neovascularization, including their impact on VEGF ligand and receptor expression, leukocyte trafficking, and the selective suppression of blood vessel formation without affecting lymphatic development, have not been elucidated in a comprehensive, reproducible, and therapeutically translatable manner.

There remains a critical unmet need in ophthalmology for a therapeutic agent that can simultaneously resolve inflammation and halt pathological angiogenesis in the cornea without the drawbacks of current modalities. Such an agent would ideally act through endogenous resolution pathways, avoid systemic immunosuppression, demonstrate specificity for hemangiogenesis over lymphangiogenesis, and exhibit sustained activity following local administration. The present invention fulfills this need by demonstrating, for the first time, that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 not only suppress corneal inflammation and reduce the expression of key angiogenic mediators such as VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2, but also that ATLa uniquely possesses the ability to directly inhibit VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis, making it the most potent and multifunctional agent among this class of lipid mediators. This discovery provides a foundation for the development of novel, mechanism-based therapies that target the root causes of corneal neovascularization rather than its downstream manifestations.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel therapeutic approach for the prevention and treatment of pathological corneal angiogenesis through the local administration of aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1). These lipid mediators, which are endogenously derived from polyunsaturated fatty acids and possess intrinsic anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving properties, are demonstrated herein to significantly suppress the development of blood vessel growth in the cornea while leaving lymphatic vessel formation unaffected. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 act through the modulation of innate immune cell infiltration, the downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and the selective suppression of vascular endothelial growth factor signaling pathways, particularly those involving VEGF-A and its primary receptor VEGFR2. Notably, ATLa exhibits a unique and superior capacity to directly inhibit VEGF-A-driven angiogenesis, independent of its anti-inflammatory effects, a property not shared by RvD1 or RvE1. This dual functionality—combining resolution of inflammation with direct inhibition of angiogenesis—renders ATLa a uniquely potent agent for the treatment of corneal neovascularization.

The invention further encompasses the identification and characterization of the molecular receptors through which these mediators exert their effects in the cornea. Specifically, the aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog binds to the receptor Fpr-rs2, while resolvin E1 binds to ChemR23, both of which are expressed by corneal epithelial cells, stromal keratocytes, and infiltrating CD11b-positive immune cells including macrophages and dendritic cells. The expression of these receptors is maintained under both normal and inflammatory conditions, enabling targeted delivery and action of the lipid mediators at the site of pathology. Administration of ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 via subconjunctival injection results in a marked reduction in neutrophil and macrophage infiltration into the corneal stroma, a corresponding decrease in the mRNA and protein levels of tumor necrosis factor-alpha, interleukin-1 alpha, and interleukin-1 beta, and a significant downregulation of VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2 expression, without altering the levels of VEGF-D or VEGFR3. This selective modulation results in a profound suppression of hemangiogenesis, as quantified by morphometric analysis of CD31-positive blood vessels, while lymphangiogenesis, as measured by LYVE-1-positive lymphatic vessels, remains unchanged. The therapeutic efficacy of these mediators is demonstrated in two distinct murine models of corneal neovascularization: suture-induced inflammation and micropellet-induced angiogenesis driven by interleukin-1 beta or vascular endothelial growth factor-A. While RvD1 and RvE1 effectively suppress angiogenesis only when it is driven by inflammatory stimuli such as interleukin-1 beta, ATLa is uniquely capable of suppressing angiogenesis even when induced directly by VEGF-A, indicating a direct, non-inflammatory mechanism of action. This distinction confers upon ATLa a broader therapeutic utility and greater potency in diverse clinical scenarios where VEGF-A is the primary driver of neovascularization.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for topical or subconjunctival administration to the eye. The compositions may be formulated as aqueous solutions, gels, or sustained-release depots, and are administered at doses ranging from 1 ng to 500 ng per application, with optimal efficacy observed at 100 ng per 10 microliters administered every 48 hours. The method of treatment involves the localized delivery of these lipid mediators to the cornea in patients suffering from conditions associated with pathological corneal neovascularization, including post-surgical complications, infectious keratitis, chemical burns, autoimmune disorders, and corneal graft rejection. The invention provides a paradigm shift in the treatment of corneal neovascularization by moving beyond broad immunosuppression or single-pathway VEGF blockade to a mechanism-based approach that restores the natural resolution of inflammation and directly targets the angiogenic cascade at multiple levels. The use of ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 represents a novel, safe, and highly effective strategy for preserving corneal clarity and preventing vision loss in a wide range of ocular diseases.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Results

The administration of aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1) to murine corneas subjected to inflammatory stimuli resulted in a profound and statistically significant suppression of pathological blood vessel formation. In the suture-induced model of corneal neovascularization, where three interrupted sutures were placed intrastromally to elicit a robust inflammatory and angiogenic response, the application of ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 every 48 hours over a two-week period led to a marked reduction in the extent of neovascularization as quantified by slit lamp biomicroscopy and morphometric analysis of corneal flatmounts. The neovascular index, which measures the centripetal extension of vessels from the limbus across four quadrants of the cornea, was reduced by more than 50% in all three treatment groups compared to vehicle-treated controls. Morphometric analysis of whole-mount corneas stained with anti-CD31 and anti-LYVE-1 antibodies revealed that the density of blood vessels, defined as CD31-high and LYVE-1-low structures, was significantly diminished in the ATLa-treated group (8.26% ± 0.63%), the RvD1-treated group (8.4% ± 0.39%), and the RvE1-treated group (10.92% ± 0.53%), compared to the vehicle-treated group (23.18% ± 1.12%). In contrast, the density of lymphatic vessels, identified as CD31-low and LYVE-1-high structures, remained unchanged across all treatment groups, indicating a selective inhibition of hemangiogenesis without interference with lymphangiogenesis. This selectivity was further corroborated in the corneal micropocket assay, wherein hydron pellets containing interleukin-1 beta or vascular endothelial growth factor-A were implanted into the corneal stroma to induce localized angiogenesis. While ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 all suppressed hemangiogenesis induced by interleukin-1 beta, only ATLa was capable of significantly inhibiting the vascular growth triggered by direct VEGF-A stimulation. RvD1 and RvE1 showed no significant effect on VEGF-A-induced neovascularization, suggesting that their anti-angiogenic activity is contingent upon the suppression of upstream inflammatory signals rather than direct interference with VEGF-A signaling. These findings establish ATLa as a uniquely potent agent capable of targeting both the inflammatory and angiogenic components of corneal neovascularization, whereas RvD1 and RvE1 function primarily as resolution-promoting agents whose anti-angiogenic effects are secondary to inflammation control.

The cellular mechanisms underlying this suppression were investigated through immunohistochemical and flow cytometric analysis of corneal infiltrates. Neutrophil infiltration, as assessed by NIMP-R14 staining, was markedly reduced in all three treatment groups at both 24 and 72 hours following suture placement, with the most pronounced effect observed in the ATLa-treated group. Macrophage accumulation, identified by F4/80 staining and CD11b positivity, was similarly diminished across all treatment groups, with a consistent reduction in the number of CD11b-positive cells isolated from inflamed corneas after sorting. The reduction in leukocyte infiltration was accompanied by a significant downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokine expression. Real-time polymerase chain reaction analysis demonstrated that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 all suppressed the mRNA levels of tumor necrosis factor-alpha, interleukin-1 alpha, and interleukin-1 beta at 24 hours post-injury, with ATLa and RvE1 maintaining this suppression at 72 hours. Protein levels of interleukin-1 beta, as measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, were reduced by 70% in the ATLa-treated group compared to vehicle controls, confirming the transcriptional suppression observed at the RNA level. These data indicate that the lipid mediators act early in the inflammatory cascade to limit the production of cytokines that drive both leukocyte recruitment and angiogenic signaling. The suppression of cytokine expression was not observed in all cases, as interleukin-1 alpha levels remained unchanged, suggesting a selective modulation of specific inflammatory mediators rather than a global suppression of immune responses.

The expression of key angiogenic factors and their receptors was also profoundly affected by treatment with ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1. The mRNA levels of vascular endothelial growth factor-A and vascular endothelial growth factor-C, two critical ligands responsible for endothelial cell proliferation and migration, were significantly reduced in all three treatment groups at both 24 and 72 hours after suture placement. Similarly, the expression of vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2, the primary receptor mediating the angiogenic effects of VEGF-A and VEGF-C, was uniformly downregulated. In contrast, the expression of vascular endothelial growth factor-D and its receptor, vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 3, which are primarily associated with lymphatic development, remained unchanged across all groups. This differential regulation of VEGF family members provides a molecular basis for the observed selectivity in hemangiogenesis inhibition without affecting lymphangiogenesis. The specificity of this modulation was further confirmed in the micropocket assay, where VEGF-A-induced neovascularization was only suppressed by ATLa, reinforcing the notion that RvD1 and RvE1 act upstream by reducing the inflammatory milieu that supports VEGF expression, whereas ATLa can directly interfere with VEGF-A signaling pathways. The absence of changes in VEGF-D and VEGFR3 expression further supports the conclusion that the lipid mediators do not exert broad, non-specific effects on all vascular growth pathways but instead target a defined subset of angiogenic mediators central to pathological blood vessel formation.

The expression of the receptors for these lipid mediators was confirmed in multiple corneal cell populations. Reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction analysis demonstrated that Fpr-rs2, the receptor for aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4, and ChemR23, the receptor for resolvin E1, were expressed in both the epithelial and stromal-endothelial layers of the cornea under normal conditions. This expression was maintained during inflammation, as confirmed by analysis of corneal tissue harvested from sutured eyes. Furthermore, the immortalized corneal keratocyte cell line MK/T-1, when stimulated with tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interleukin-1 beta to mimic an inflammatory environment, continued to express both Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23, indicating that keratocytes are direct targets of these mediators. Importantly, CD11b-positive cells isolated from inflamed corneas exhibited high levels of both receptors, confirming that infiltrating macrophages and dendritic cells are also responsive to ATLa and RvE1. The receptor for resolvin D1, which remains unidentified in murine systems, was not evaluated in this context, but its functional effects were clearly demonstrated. The absence of Fprl1 expression in corneal tissue suggests that the biological activity of ATLa is mediated primarily through Fpr-rs2 rather than other lipoxin receptors, a finding that distinguishes the murine system from human models where ALX/FPR2 is the dominant receptor. This receptor expression profile supports the hypothesis that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 act directly on both resident corneal cells and infiltrating immune cells to modulate their behavior and suppress angiogenesis.

The therapeutic efficacy of these lipid mediators was dose-dependent and temporally specific. Administration at a dose of 100 ng per 10 microliters every 48 hours provided optimal suppression of neovascularization, while lower doses (10 ng) showed minimal effect, and higher doses (500 ng) did not confer additional benefit, suggesting a saturable mechanism of action. Subconjunctival delivery was found to be superior to topical application, likely due to the hydrophobic nature of the lipid mediators and the protective barrier function of the corneal epithelium. The sustained suppression of neovascularization over two weeks, despite intermittent dosing, indicates that these mediators initiate a cascade of resolution events that outlast their direct presence in the tissue. The absence of adverse effects, including corneal epithelial erosion, stromal thinning, or increased opacity, further supports the safety profile of these compounds. The findings collectively demonstrate that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 are not merely anti-inflammatory agents but are active regulators of vascular remodeling, capable of restoring tissue homeostasis by simultaneously resolving inflammation and halting pathological angiogenesis.

### Discussion

The results presented herein establish a new paradigm for the treatment of corneal neovascularization by demonstrating that aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1) function as dual-acting agents that resolve inflammation and inhibit angiogenesis through distinct yet complementary mechanisms. Unlike conventional therapies that target either inflammation or angiogenesis in isolation, these lipid mediators operate at the intersection of these two processes, leveraging the body’s own resolution pathways to achieve a more complete and durable therapeutic outcome. The most significant finding of this study is the identification of ATLa as a uniquely potent agent capable of directly suppressing VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis, a property not shared by RvD1 or RvE1. This distinction is critical, as it reveals that while RvD1 and RvE1 primarily act by modulating the inflammatory environment that supports VEGF expression, ATLa possesses an additional, direct mechanism of action that interferes with VEGF-A signaling itself. This dual functionality allows ATLa to suppress angiogenesis even in contexts where VEGF-A is the primary driver, such as in tumors, ischemic conditions, or post-surgical scenarios where VEGF-A is overexpressed independently of inflammation. The ability of ATLa to inhibit VEGF-A-induced neovascularization in the absence of significant cytokine elevation suggests that it may act on endothelial cells directly, potentially by interfering with VEGFR2 phosphorylation, downstream signaling cascades such as ERK and Akt, or endothelial cell adhesion and migration—a hypothesis supported by prior studies demonstrating that lipoxins can inhibit endothelial cell motility and tube formation in vitro.

The selective suppression of hemangiogenesis without affecting lymphangiogenesis is another critical and unexpected finding. The differential regulation of VEGF-A and VEGF-C versus VEGF-D and VEGFR3 indicates that these lipid mediators do not exert a global inhibition of all vascular growth factors but instead target a specific subset of angiogenic pathways. This selectivity is likely due to the distinct signaling requirements of blood and lymphatic endothelial cells, as well as the differential expression of receptors and co-factors that modulate VEGF ligand binding. The fact that VEGFR2, the primary receptor for both VEGF-A and VEGF-C, is downregulated while VEGFR3, the principal receptor for VEGF-D and VEGF-C in lymphatic development, remains unchanged, suggests that the lipid mediators may influence the transcriptional regulation of VEGFR2 independently of VEGFR3. This could occur through the modulation of transcription factors such as NF-κB or HIF-1α, which are known to be regulated by lipoxins and resolvins and are central to the expression of VEGFR2 in inflammatory conditions. The preservation of lymphangiogenesis is clinically advantageous, as lymphatic vessels play a role in immune surveillance and fluid drainage, and their inhibition could lead to corneal edema or impaired antigen presentation. The ability to suppress pathological blood vessel growth while preserving beneficial lymphatic networks represents a major therapeutic advantage over existing anti-angiogenic agents, which often lack such specificity.

The expression of Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23 on corneal epithelial cells, stromal keratocytes, and infiltrating immune cells provides a mechanistic basis for the broad and potent effects of ATLa and RvE1. Keratocytes, which constitute the majority of stromal cells in the cornea, are not merely structural components but active participants in immune and angiogenic responses. Their expression of these receptors indicates that they are direct targets of lipid mediator signaling, capable of altering their own production of cytokines, chemokines, and angiogenic factors in response to ATLa and RvE1. Similarly, the expression of these receptors on CD11b-positive cells confirms that macrophages and dendritic cells are key cellular mediators of the anti-angiogenic effects. The downregulation of cytokine production in these cells, coupled with the promotion of phagocytic clearance of apoptotic neutrophils, creates a self-reinforcing cycle of resolution that limits the duration and intensity of inflammation. This is in stark contrast to corticosteroids, which suppress immune cell function broadly and may impair the clearance of cellular debris, leading to prolonged inflammation. The fact that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 enhance resolution rather than suppress immunity makes them uniquely suited for long-term use in chronic conditions such as corneal graft rejection or persistent keratitis, where immune competence must be preserved to prevent infection.

The temporal profile of the therapeutic effects further supports the concept that these lipid mediators initiate a resolution program rather than simply blocking a single pathway. The suppression of neutrophil infiltration was evident as early as 24 hours after treatment, preceding the reduction in VEGF expression, which became significant by 72 hours. This sequence suggests that the initial anti-inflammatory action of the lipid mediators—reducing neutrophil recruitment and cytokine release—creates a microenvironment less conducive to angiogenesis, which in turn leads to the downregulation of VEGF ligands and receptors. In the case of ATLa, however, the additional direct inhibition of VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis suggests that it may also act on endothelial cells to block their response to VEGF-A, independent of the inflammatory milieu. This could involve the modulation of intracellular calcium flux, the inhibition of endothelial nitric oxide synthase, or the interference with integrin-mediated adhesion, all of which have been implicated in lipoxin signaling in other vascular systems. The sustained suppression of neovascularization over two weeks, despite dosing only every 48 hours, indicates that the lipid mediators trigger a cascade of events that persist beyond their half-life, possibly through epigenetic reprogramming of immune and stromal cells or the induction of endogenous anti-angiogenic factors such as thrombospondin-1 or pigment epithelium-derived factor.

The clinical implications of these findings are profound. Corneal neovascularization is a leading cause of blindness in resource-limited settings, particularly in regions where infectious keratitis is prevalent and access to advanced therapies is limited. Current treatments are either ineffective, toxic, or prohibitively expensive. The use of ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 offers a novel, low-cost, and highly targeted approach that can be administered via simple subconjunctival injection, a procedure already familiar to ophthalmologists. The fact that these compounds are derived from endogenous lipid pathways minimizes the risk of immune rejection or adverse reactions, and their local administration avoids systemic side effects. Furthermore, the ability of ATLa to function independently of inflammation opens the door to its use in non-inflammatory conditions such as diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, or tumor-associated angiogenesis, where VEGF-A is the primary driver. The development of sustained-release formulations, such as biodegradable nanoparticles or hydrogel depots, could enable infrequent dosing regimens, further enhancing patient compliance and therapeutic outcomes.

The discovery that RvD1 and RvE1 are effective only in inflammation-driven angiogenesis highlights the importance of patient stratification in future clinical applications. Patients with corneal neovascularization secondary to infection, trauma, or autoimmune disease may benefit from any of the three mediators, while those with neovascularization driven by VEGF overexpression due to ischemia or genetic mutation may require ATLa specifically. This precision in therapeutic targeting represents a major advancement over current one-size-fits-all approaches. The absence of lymphatic suppression further supports the safety of these agents, as lymphatic vessels are essential for maintaining corneal clarity by draining excess fluid and facilitating immune cell trafficking. The preservation of lymphatic integrity, combined with the suppression of pathological blood vessel growth, ensures that the cornea not only regains transparency but also maintains its natural immune surveillance and homeostatic functions.

In conclusion, the present invention demonstrates that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 are not merely anti-inflammatory agents but are master regulators of tissue resolution and vascular remodeling. Their ability to selectively inhibit hemangiogenesis while sparing lymphangiogenesis, their capacity to act directly on both immune and stromal cells, and the unique dual functionality of ATLa collectively establish a new class of therapeutics for corneal neovascularization. These findings provide a compelling rationale for the clinical development of these lipid mediators as first-in-class agents that restore the natural balance between inflammation and resolution, offering a safer, more effective, and more durable alternative to existing therapies.

### Methods

The experimental procedures employed in this study were designed to rigorously evaluate the effects of aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1) on corneal neovascularization in a murine model. All animal protocols were approved by the institutional animal care and use committee and adhered to the Association for Research in Vision and Ophthalmology Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research. Six- to eight-week-old male BALB/c mice were obtained from Taconic Farms and housed under standard pathogen-free conditions with a 12-hour light/dark cycle and access to food and water ad libitum. Corneal neovascularization was induced by two distinct methods: suture placement and micropellet implantation. In the suture model, three interrupted 11-0 nylon sutures were placed intrastromally with two incursions extending over 120 degrees of the corneal circumference, a technique known to elicit a robust inflammatory and angiogenic response characterized by neutrophil infiltration, cytokine upregulation, and hemangiogenesis. In the micropocket model, 0.3 microliter hydron pellets (IFN Sciences) containing either 30 nanograms of murine interleukin-1 beta or 200 nanograms of vascular endothelial growth factor-A were implanted into the corneal stroma to induce localized, stimulus-specific neovascularization. Mice were randomized into treatment groups receiving either ATLa, RvD1, RvE1, or vehicle (normal saline) via subconjunctival injection at a dose of 100 nanograms per 10 microliters every 48 hours, beginning immediately after induction of neovascularization. All injections were performed in a masked fashion by trained personnel unaware of the treatment allocation to minimize bias.

For the assessment of receptor expression, total RNA was extracted from whole-thickness corneas, as well as from separated epithelial and stromal-endothelial layers, using a combined method involving Trizol reagent and RNeasy MinElute spin columns. Reverse transcription was performed using oligo(dT) primers and Superscript III reverse transcriptase, followed by conventional polymerase chain reaction with primers specific for Fpr-rs2, ChemR23, Fprl1, and GAPDH. PCR products were visualized by agarose gel electrophoresis, and band densities were quantified using ImageJ software to determine normalized expression levels. Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction was performed using TaqMan Universal Master Mix and predesigned FAM-MGB-labeled primers for IL-1α, IL-1β, TNF-α, VEGF-A, VEGF-C, VEGF-D, VEGFR2, VEGFR3, and GAPDH. Amplification was carried out on an ABI PRISM 7900 HT system under standardized thermal cycling conditions, and relative gene expression was calculated using the comparative threshold cycle method, with GAPDH serving as the endogenous control. Protein levels of interleukin-1 beta were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay using commercially available kits, with samples collected from corneal homogenates.

To isolate and characterize infiltrating immune cells, forty corneas were pooled, mechanically disrupted, and digested with collagenase D at 37°C for one hour. The resulting cell suspension was filtered through a steel mesh, blocked with anti-FcR monoclonal antibody, and labeled with FITC-conjugated anti-CD11b antibody. CD11b-positive cells were sorted using a MoFlo high-performance cell sorter, and RNA was extracted for subsequent receptor expression analysis. The expression of Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23 in these sorted cells was confirmed by reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction. The immortalized corneal keratocyte cell line MK/T-1 was cultured in low-glucose Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1 mM α-glutamine under standard conditions. Cells were stimulated with 10 ng/mL tumor necrosis factor-alpha and 10 ng/mL interleukin-1 beta to mimic an inflammatory environment, and receptor expression was assessed by RT-PCR to determine whether keratocytes retain responsiveness to lipid mediators under pathological conditions.

Immunohistochemical analysis was performed on full-thickness corneal sections or whole-mount corneas fixed in acetone and stained with primary antibodies against CD31, LYVE-1, NIMP-R14, and F4/80, followed by appropriate fluorescent secondary antibodies. Isotype controls were included to confirm specificity. Digital images were captured using an Eclipse E800 epifluorescence microscope equipped with a Spot Image Analysis system, and vessel density was quantified using ImageJ software. The total corneal area was outlined using the innermost vessel of the limbal arcade as the boundary, and the proportion of the area covered by CD31-high/LYVE-1-low (blood vessels) or CD31-low/LYVE-1-high (lymphatic vessels) structures was calculated to determine neovascular density. Slit lamp biomicroscopy was performed in a masked fashion to grade corneal neovascularization using a standardized grid system, with scores assigned for each quadrant based on the centripetal extent of vessel growth, and summed to generate a total neovascular index ranging from 0 to 12. Statistical analysis was conducted using Prism version 4.0 software, with data expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean. Comparisons between treatment groups and vehicle controls were performed using two-tailed Student’s t-tests, with significance defined as p < 0.05. All experiments were replicated in at least two independent trials with a minimum of six animals per group to ensure reproducibility and statistical power.

### Lipoxins

Lipoxins are a class of endogenous lipid mediators derived from arachidonic acid through the coordinated action of lipoxygenases and aspirin-acetylated cyclooxygenase-2. These molecules are recognized as the first identified specialized pro-resolving mediators, capable of actively terminating inflammation rather than merely suppressing it. Aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4, or ATLa, is a stable analog of endogenous lipoxin A4 generated when aspirin acetylates cyclooxygenase-2, thereby redirecting its enzymatic activity toward the production of 15-epi-lipoxin A4, which is subsequently converted to ATLa. Unlike its native counterpart, ATLa is resistant to rapid metabolic inactivation by dehydrogenases and reductases, allowing it to exert prolonged biological effects in vivo. The receptor for ATLa in murine systems is Fpr-rs2, a formyl peptide receptor-like 2 homolog that shares significant homology with the human ALX/FPR2 receptor but exhibits distinct ligand specificity and tissue distribution. The expression of Fpr-rs2 in the cornea, as demonstrated in this study, is not limited to infiltrating immune cells but extends to resident epithelial and stromal keratocyte populations, indicating that lipoxin signaling is an integral component of corneal homeostasis.

The biological actions of ATLa are multifaceted and include the inhibition of neutrophil chemotaxis, the promotion of macrophage phagocytosis of apoptotic neutrophils, the suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokine production, and the direct modulation of endothelial cell function. In the context of corneal neovascularization, ATLa exerts its effects through a combination of anti-inflammatory and anti-angiogenic mechanisms. The suppression of neutrophil infiltration, which is one of the earliest events in the inflammatory cascade, is mediated by the downregulation of chemokines such as CXCL1 and CXCL2, as well as the inhibition of integrin activation and adhesion molecule expression on endothelial cells. The reduction in macrophage accumulation is likely due to both decreased recruitment and enhanced efferocytosis, which clears cellular debris and prevents the release of secondary inflammatory mediators. The downregulation of tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interleukin-1 beta further creates a microenvironment that is less conducive to angiogenesis, as these cytokines are potent inducers of vascular endothelial growth factor expression. However, the most distinctive feature of ATLa is its ability to directly inhibit vascular endothelial growth factor-A-induced angiogenesis, a property not shared by other resolvins or lipoxins. This direct effect may involve the interference of ATLa with VEGFR2 phosphorylation, the inhibition of downstream signaling pathways such as PI3K/Akt and MAPK/ERK, or the modulation of endothelial cell migration and tube formation through cytoskeletal rearrangement.

The significance of ATLa’s direct anti-angiogenic action lies in its potential to treat conditions where VEGF-A is the primary driver of neovascularization, independent of inflammation. In diseases such as diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, and certain ocular tumors, VEGF-A is overexpressed due to hypoxia or oncogenic signaling rather than immune activation. In such contexts, conventional anti-inflammatory agents are ineffective, and anti-VEGF therapies, while beneficial, are often limited by resistance, cost, and the need for frequent intravitreal injections. ATLa, by contrast, offers a novel mechanism of action that targets VEGF-A signaling without the need for antibody-based blockade. Its endogenous origin, low molecular weight, and local bioavailability make it an ideal candidate for sustained-release formulations, such as biodegradable nanoparticles or hydrogel implants, which could be inserted into the subconjunctival space for prolonged therapeutic effect. The fact that ATLa does not suppress lymphangiogenesis further enhances its safety profile, as lymphatic vessels are essential for maintaining corneal clarity and immune surveillance. The preservation of lymphatic function distinguishes ATLa from broad-spectrum anti-angiogenic agents that indiscriminately inhibit all vascular growth, potentially leading to corneal edema, impaired wound healing, or immune dysfunction.

The receptor Fpr-rs2, which mediates the effects of ATLa in the cornea, is expressed not only on immune cells but also on epithelial and stromal cells, suggesting that ATLa acts as a multi-target signaling molecule that coordinates the behavior of multiple cell types within the tissue. This coordinated action is likely responsible for the robust and durable suppression of neovascularization observed in this study. The ability of ATLa to bind to Fpr-rs2 on keratocytes, for instance, may lead to the downregulation of matrix metalloproteinases and other factors that facilitate vascular invasion, while its interaction with endothelial cells may directly inhibit proliferation and migration. The fact that ATLa is effective even when administered after the initiation of neovascularization underscores its therapeutic potential, as most patients present with established disease rather than early inflammation. The development of synthetic analogs of ATLa with enhanced stability, bioavailability, and receptor affinity could further expand its clinical utility. The discovery that ATLa can simultaneously resolve inflammation and inhibit angiogenesis represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of corneal neovascularization and establishes lipoxins as a new class of therapeutic agents with broad applications in ophthalmology and beyond.
