# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of neuropharmacology and the treatment of seizure disorders, particularly epilepsy. More specifically, the invention concerns a novel therapeutic approach involving the synergistic inhibition of AMPA-type ionotropic glutamate receptors through the combined administration of perampanel and decanoic acid. This combination provides a marked enhancement in the suppression of pathological neuronal hyperexcitability beyond what is achievable with either agent alone, thereby offering improved seizure control with the potential for reduced dosing and diminished adverse effects. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising perampanel and decanoic acid in defined ratios, methods of preparing such compositions, and their use in the prophylaxis and treatment of partial-onset seizures, primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and other hyperexcitability-related neurological conditions. The invention is particularly suited for patients with drug-resistant epilepsy who have demonstrated suboptimal response to monotherapy with AMPA receptor antagonists or who experience dose-limiting side effects from current antiepileptic regimens. The discovery of the synergistic interaction between these two structurally and mechanistically distinct compounds opens a new therapeutic paradigm in epilepsy management, one that leverages complementary binding sites on the AMPA receptor to achieve enhanced efficacy without proportional increases in systemic exposure or toxicity.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Epilepsy remains one of the most prevalent chronic neurological disorders, affecting approximately 50 million individuals worldwide, with nearly one-third of patients experiencing seizures that are refractory to existing pharmacological interventions. Despite advances in antiepileptic drug development, the majority of therapeutic strategies continue to target voltage-gated sodium channels, GABAergic enhancement, or calcium channel modulation, with limited success in achieving durable seizure freedom without significant adverse effects. Among the newer generation of antiepileptic agents, perampanel has emerged as a clinically significant noncompetitive antagonist of the AMPA receptor, a key mediator of fast excitatory synaptic transmission in the central nervous system. Approved for adjunctive treatment of partial-onset and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures, perampanel exerts its antiseizure effects by binding to a well-characterized site located at the interface of the S1-M1 and S2-M4 linkers of the AMPA receptor subunit, thereby stabilizing the receptor in a closed conformation and reducing the amplitude of glutamate-induced ionic currents. However, its clinical utility is constrained by dose-dependent neuropsychiatric side effects, including dizziness, somnolence, irritability, aggression, and in some cases, suicidal ideation, which frequently necessitate dose reduction or discontinuation, particularly in pediatric and elderly populations.

Concurrently, dietary interventions have gained increasing recognition as viable nonpharmacological approaches to seizure control, with the medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) ketogenic diet being one of the most extensively studied. Originally developed as a metabolic therapy to induce ketosis, the MCT diet has been shown to exert antiseizure effects even in the absence of sustained ketone body elevation, suggesting alternative mechanisms of action. Recent investigations have identified decanoic acid, a saturated medium-chain fatty acid derived from the hydrolysis of MCTs, as a direct modulator of neuronal excitability. Decanoic acid has been demonstrated to inhibit AMPA receptor-mediated currents in a noncompetitive manner, with binding localized to a distinct region of the receptor—specifically, the M3 transmembrane helix—thereby interfering with channel gating without competing with glutamate for its orthosteric binding site. This mechanistic divergence from traditional antiepileptic drugs, including perampanel, suggests the possibility of additive or synergistic interactions when the two agents are co-administered.

Prior to the present invention, no studies had systematically evaluated whether the simultaneous presence of perampanel and decanoic acid could produce a supra-additive effect on AMPA receptor inhibition. While both compounds were known individually to reduce AMPA receptor function, their potential for interaction had not been explored due to assumptions that their effects were merely additive, given their distinct binding loci. Furthermore, the pharmacological characterization of perampanel has been complicated by inconsistent solubility profiles, variable assay conditions, and discrepancies between in vitro and in vivo potency measurements, leading to uncertainty regarding its true therapeutic window. Similarly, the concentrations of decanoic acid achieved in human brain tissue under dietary MCT regimens have been poorly quantified in clinical contexts, with extrapolations from plasma levels and rodent pharmacokinetics introducing significant uncertainty. No prior work had established whether the concentrations of decanoic acid attainable through dietary intervention—typically in the range of 100 to 200 micromolar in human brain tissue—were sufficient to modulate the efficacy of perampanel in a clinically meaningful way. The absence of a mechanistic framework to explain how these two agents might interact at the receptor level, coupled with a lack of translational validation in human neural tissue, left a critical gap in the therapeutic landscape.

Moreover, the prevailing paradigm in epilepsy treatment has favored monotherapy or sequential polytherapy, with combinations typically selected based on pharmacokinetic compatibility rather than pharmacodynamic synergy. The notion that two compounds acting on the same receptor target through non-overlapping mechanisms could produce a cooperative, non-linear enhancement of inhibition had not been rigorously tested or clinically leveraged. The present invention fills this void by demonstrating, for the first time, that perampanel and decanoic acid, when administered together, produce a synergistic reduction in AMPA receptor function that is quantitatively greater than the sum of their individual effects. This synergy is not only reproducible across multiple expression systems and receptor subunit compositions—GluA1/2 and GluA2/3—but is also validated in physiologically relevant ex vivo models, including rat hippocampal slices and, critically, human neocortical tissue derived from patients with drug-resistant epilepsy. The discovery that the combination achieves near-complete suppression of epileptiform activity in human brain slices at concentrations where each agent alone has negligible effect represents a paradigm-shifting advance in the treatment of refractory epilepsy. This invention, therefore, transforms the understanding of AMPA receptor modulation from a linear, additive model to a cooperative, multi-site inhibition framework, enabling a new class of combination therapies that optimize efficacy while minimizing systemic burden.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious therapeutic strategy for the treatment of seizure disorders through the synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors by the combined administration of perampanel and decanoic acid. It has been discovered that these two compounds, despite acting on distinct binding sites on the AMPA receptor, produce a supra-additive reduction in receptor-mediated currents that cannot be predicted from their individual pharmacological profiles. This synergistic interaction results in a substantial leftward shift in the concentration-response curves of both agents, significantly lowering their respective half-maximal inhibitory concentrations and enabling potent suppression of pathological neuronal activity at doses that are subtherapeutic when administered alone. The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising perampanel and decanoic acid in specific molar ratios, wherein the combined effect exceeds the sum of their individual effects by a statistically significant margin, as demonstrated through rigorous isobolographic analysis and dose-response modeling. The invention further includes methods of treatment wherein a subject suffering from epilepsy or a related hyperexcitability disorder is administered a therapeutically effective amount of perampanel and decanoic acid simultaneously or in close temporal proximity, resulting in enhanced seizure control, reduced frequency and severity of seizures, and a decreased incidence of adverse effects attributable to high-dose monotherapy. The synergistic effect is observed across multiple AMPA receptor subunit combinations, including those most prevalent in the human hippocampus and neocortex, and is validated in both animal-derived and human-derived ex vivo tissue models, confirming its translational relevance. Importantly, the combination achieves near-complete abolition of epileptiform activity in human cortical tissue at concentrations of decanoic acid that are physiologically attainable through dietary intake of medium-chain triglycerides, thereby enabling a clinically feasible approach that integrates pharmacological and nutritional interventions. The invention further enables a reduction in the required dose of perampanel, potentially mitigating its dose-limiting neuropsychiatric side effects while maintaining or improving seizure control. This represents a significant advancement over existing therapies, which rely on escalating doses to achieve efficacy, often at the cost of tolerability. The invention also provides a mechanistic rationale for the clinical observation that some patients on MCT diets exhibit improved seizure control when treated with perampanel, offering a scientific foundation for personalized therapeutic regimens. By leveraging the complementary binding sites of perampanel and decanoic acid on the AMPA receptor, the invention establishes a new paradigm in antiepileptic drug development—one based on cooperative receptor modulation rather than additive pharmacology—thereby opening avenues for the design of next-generation combination therapies with superior efficacy and safety profiles.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Combination

The present invention is predicated on the unexpected and non-obvious discovery that perampanel and decanoic acid, when administered in combination, produce a synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptor-mediated currents that far exceeds the additive effects predicted by their individual pharmacological profiles. This synergy is not merely a result of pharmacokinetic interactions, such as altered absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion, but arises from a direct pharmacodynamic interaction at the molecular level of the AMPA receptor. Perampanel binds to a well-characterized site located at the interface of the S1-M1 and S2-M4 extracellular linkers of the AMPA receptor subunit, where it stabilizes the receptor in a closed, non-conducting state following glutamate binding. Decanoic acid, in contrast, binds to a distinct region within the transmembrane domain, specifically the M3 helix, where it interferes with the conformational transitions required for channel opening. The spatial separation of these two binding sites precludes direct steric competition, yet their simultaneous occupancy produces a cooperative effect that enhances the stability of the closed state beyond what either ligand can achieve independently. This phenomenon is not observed with other AMPA receptor antagonists or with structurally unrelated fatty acids, indicating that the synergy is highly specific to the unique molecular architecture of perampanel and decanoic acid. The synergistic interaction has been rigorously quantified using isobolographic analysis, a gold-standard method for distinguishing additive from synergistic drug interactions, and the results consistently demonstrate that the combination of perampanel and decanoic acid produces effect points that lie significantly below the line of additivity for both GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 receptor subtypes. This finding is particularly remarkable given that both compounds are noncompetitive antagonists, a class of agents typically assumed to act independently when binding to distinct sites. The discovery that their effects are not merely additive but cooperative suggests a novel mechanism of allosteric cooperativity between distant binding domains on the AMPA receptor, a concept previously unreported in the literature. The synergy is evident across a broad concentration range, with the most pronounced effects occurring at submicromolar concentrations of perampanel and low millimolar concentrations of decanoic acid—levels that are pharmacologically and physiologically attainable in clinical practice. This synergy translates directly into enhanced suppression of epileptiform activity in ex vivo models, including rat hippocampal slices and, critically, human neocortical tissue derived from patients with drug-resistant epilepsy. In these models, the combination achieves near-complete abolition of pathological network activity at concentrations where each agent alone has minimal effect, demonstrating a therapeutic potential that cannot be replicated by either compound in isolation. The invention therefore provides a fundamentally new approach to targeting AMPA receptor-mediated hyperexcitability, one that exploits the structural and functional complementarity of two distinct ligands to achieve a level of efficacy previously unattainable through monotherapy or conventional polytherapy.

### Decanoic Acid and Compositions Comprising the Same

Decanoic acid, also known as capric acid, is a saturated medium-chain fatty acid with the chemical formula C₁₀H₂₀O₂, and is naturally derived from the hydrolysis of medium-chain triglycerides found in coconut oil, palm kernel oil, and dairy fats. In the context of the present invention, decanoic acid serves as a critical pharmacologically active component that, when combined with perampanel, enables a synergistic suppression of AMPA receptor function. Unlike traditional antiepileptic drugs, decanoic acid does not require metabolic conversion to an active metabolite to exert its effect; it acts directly on the AMPA receptor as an intact molecule. Its mechanism of action involves binding to the M3 transmembrane helix of the AMPA receptor, thereby stabilizing a closed conformation and reducing the probability of channel opening following glutamate binding. This action is noncompetitive, voltage-independent, and reversible, making it suitable for chronic administration without the risk of cumulative toxicity or receptor desensitization. The invention encompasses decanoic acid in its free acid form, as well as in pharmaceutically acceptable salts, esters, or prodrug forms that enhance its bioavailability, stability, or tissue penetration. Preferred embodiments include decanoic acid esterified to glycerol to form tri-decanoate (triglyceride), or to mono- or di-glycerides, which are more readily absorbed through the intestinal mucosa and hydrolyzed in vivo to release free decanoic acid. The invention further includes formulations wherein decanoic acid is delivered as part of a medium-chain triglyceride mixture, such as those used in clinical ketogenic diets, wherein decanoic acid constitutes at least 10% by weight of the total fatty acid content. The concentration of decanoic acid in the final pharmaceutical composition is optimized to achieve a therapeutically effective concentration in the central nervous system, typically in the range of 50 to 500 micromolar, which corresponds to plasma concentrations of 100 to 700 micromolar under standard dietary or supplemental regimens. The invention further provides for the co-formulation of decanoic acid with other components of the MCT ketogenic diet, including caprylic acid (C8), lauric acid (C12), and other medium-chain fatty acids, provided that the synergistic effect with perampanel is preserved. In preferred embodiments, the composition is formulated as an oral liquid, soft gel capsule, or powdered supplement for ease of administration, particularly in pediatric and geriatric populations. The composition may be administered concurrently with perampanel or sequentially, with a time interval of no more than four hours to ensure overlapping pharmacodynamic activity. The invention further encompasses sustained-release formulations of decanoic acid to maintain steady-state concentrations in the brain over extended periods, thereby minimizing fluctuations in receptor inhibition and enhancing seizure control. The use of decanoic acid in combination with perampanel not only enhances efficacy but also reduces the systemic burden of perampanel, thereby mitigating its dose-dependent neuropsychiatric side effects. The invention thus represents a novel integration of nutritional biochemistry and molecular pharmacology, wherein a naturally occurring dietary component is leveraged as a precision therapeutic agent in the treatment of neurological disease.

### Perampanel

Perampanel, chemically designated as 2-tert-butyl-3-(2-oxo-2-phenylethyl)pyridine-4-carboxamide, is a noncompetitive antagonist of the AMPA receptor that has been clinically approved for the adjunctive treatment of partial-onset and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures. It is a small molecule with a molecular weight of approximately 378.5 g/mol and exhibits moderate lipophilicity, enabling efficient penetration of the blood-brain barrier. Perampanel binds to a well-defined site on the AMPA receptor located at the interface of the S1-M1 and S2-M4 extracellular linkers, where it stabilizes the receptor in a closed conformation following glutamate binding, thereby reducing the amplitude and duration of ionic currents. The invention demonstrates that perampanel, when administered alone, inhibits AMPA receptor currents with IC₅₀ values ranging from approximately 6 to 8 micromolar in recombinant GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 receptors expressed in Xenopus oocytes, consistent with prior reports in native tissue preparations. However, the invention reveals that these concentrations are significantly higher than those required for therapeutic efficacy when perampanel is co-administered with decanoic acid. In the presence of decanoic acid at concentrations of 50 to 100 micromolar, the IC₅₀ of perampanel is reduced by more than threefold, to levels as low as 1.6 to 2.2 micromolar, which fall within the lower end of the therapeutic plasma concentration range reported in clinical studies (1.06 to 3.26 micromolar). This finding is of profound clinical significance, as it suggests that the dose of perampanel required for effective seizure control may be substantially reduced when combined with decanoic acid, thereby minimizing the risk of dose-dependent adverse effects such as dizziness, somnolence, irritability, aggression, and suicidal ideation. The invention further provides for the use of perampanel in fixed-dose combinations with decanoic acid, wherein the ratio of perampanel to decanoic acid is optimized to achieve maximal synergy while maintaining a favorable safety profile. Preferred embodiments include formulations containing between 1 and 12 milligrams of perampanel per day in combination with sufficient decanoic acid to achieve a brain concentration of at least 100 micromolar. The invention also encompasses modified-release formulations of perampanel, such as extended-release tablets or capsules, to maintain stable plasma concentrations over 24 hours and to minimize peak-trough fluctuations that may contribute to neuropsychiatric side effects. The combination therapy of perampanel and decanoic acid is particularly advantageous in patient populations who have previously failed to tolerate standard doses of perampanel due to adverse effects, as it enables the use of lower perampanel doses while preserving or enhancing antiseizure efficacy. The invention further provides for the monitoring of plasma concentrations of perampanel and decanoic acid to guide individualized dosing, with therapeutic targets set at perampanel plasma levels of 0.5 to 3 micromolar and decanoic acid levels of 100 to 500 micromolar. This approach represents a paradigm shift in epilepsy management, moving from empirical dose escalation to mechanism-driven, synergistic combination therapy grounded in precise pharmacodynamic principles.

### Treatment

The invention provides a novel method of treating seizure disorders by exploiting the synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors through the combined administration of perampanel and decanoic acid. The method is applicable to a broad spectrum of seizure types, including partial-onset seizures with or without secondary generalization, primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and drug-resistant epilepsy that has failed to respond to at least two appropriately selected and tolerated antiepileptic drugs. The treatment paradigm is particularly suited for patients who have experienced dose-limiting neuropsychiatric side effects from perampanel monotherapy, as the synergistic effect allows for a substantial reduction in the required perampanel dose while maintaining or improving seizure control. The method involves the simultaneous or near-simultaneous administration of perampanel and decanoic acid, with the timing of administration optimized to ensure overlapping pharmacodynamic activity in the central nervous system. The combination may be administered orally, with perampanel delivered as a tablet or capsule and decanoic acid delivered as a dietary supplement in the form of medium-chain triglycerides, triglyceride esters, or free acid formulations. The treatment regimen is initiated at a low dose of perampanel, typically 2 to 4 milligrams per day, and titrated upward only if necessary, with decanoic acid administered at a fixed daily intake sufficient to maintain brain concentrations above 100 micromolar. The invention further encompasses the use of the combination in patients who are already on a medium-chain triglyceride ketogenic diet, wherein the addition of perampanel at a reduced dose produces a marked improvement in seizure control compared to diet alone. The method may also be applied to pediatric populations, including children with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome, or other developmental epileptic encephalopathies, where the burden of polypharmacy and adverse effects is particularly high. Clinical outcomes are measured by reduction in seizure frequency, duration, and intensity, as well as by improvements in quality of life, cognitive function, and behavioral parameters. The invention further provides for the use of the combination in patients with comorbid neuropsychiatric conditions, such as autism spectrum disorder or attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder, where the reduction in perampanel dose may mitigate behavioral side effects without compromising seizure control. The treatment is not limited to patients with epilepsy; it may also be extended to other hyperexcitability-related neurological conditions, including status epilepticus, acute brain injury, traumatic brain injury, and certain forms of neuropathic pain, where AMPA receptor overactivation contributes to pathological neuronal firing. The method is distinguished from conventional polytherapy by its reliance on a mechanistically defined synergy at the receptor level, rather than on empirical combinations based on pharmacokinetic compatibility or broad-spectrum activity. This precision approach enables a more predictable therapeutic response, reduces the risk of drug interactions, and minimizes the need for complex dose titration regimens.

### Administration

The administration of perampanel and decanoic acid in accordance with the present invention is designed to optimize the synergistic interaction at the level of the AMPA receptor while ensuring patient compliance, safety, and pharmacokinetic stability. Perampanel is preferably administered orally in the form of immediate-release or extended-release tablets, with a daily dose ranging from 1 to 12 milligrams, depending on the patient’s weight, age, concomitant medications, and prior exposure to antiepileptic drugs. The initial dose is typically set at 2 milligrams per day, with gradual titration upward in 2-milligram increments every week, based on clinical response and tolerability. Decanoic acid is administered in a form that ensures sustained delivery to the central nervous system, with preferred routes including oral ingestion of medium-chain triglyceride oils, encapsulated triglyceride esters, or powdered formulations mixed into food or beverages. The daily intake of decanoic acid is calibrated to achieve a steady-state concentration in the brain of at least 100 micromolar, which corresponds to a dietary intake of approximately 15 to 30 grams of medium-chain triglycerides per day, depending on the composition of the triglyceride mixture. In preferred embodiments, the medium-chain triglyceride formulation contains at least 20% decanoic acid by weight, with the remainder comprising caprylic acid, lauric acid, and other medium-chain fatty acids in proportions that maintain metabolic stability and palatability. The administration of decanoic acid is not restricted to a specific time of day and may be divided into two or three doses throughout the day to maintain consistent plasma and brain concentrations. The co-administration of perampanel and decanoic acid may occur simultaneously, with both agents taken at the same meal, or sequentially, with a time interval of no more than four hours between doses to ensure overlapping pharmacodynamic activity. The invention further encompasses the use of a single fixed-dose combination product containing both perampanel and decanoic acid in a predetermined ratio, formulated as a soft gel capsule, oral suspension, or chewable tablet, to simplify adherence and reduce the risk of noncompliance. The formulation may include excipients such as polysorbate 80, glycerol monostearate, or hydroxypropyl methylcellulose to enhance solubility, stability, and bioavailability. In patients with gastrointestinal disorders or impaired fat absorption, alternative delivery systems such as intranasal or transdermal formulations of decanoic acid may be employed, although these are less preferred due to challenges in achieving consistent brain concentrations. Monitoring of plasma concentrations of perampanel and decanoic acid is recommended during the titration phase to confirm that therapeutic targets are achieved, with perampanel levels maintained between 0.5 and 3 micromolar and decanoic acid levels between 100 and 500 micromolar. The invention further provides for the use of dietary counseling and nutritional support to assist patients in maintaining adequate decanoic acid intake, particularly in pediatric populations where adherence to a high-fat diet may be challenging. The administration protocol is designed to be compatible with existing clinical practices, allowing for seamless integration into current epilepsy management algorithms without requiring radical changes to patient care pathways.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Methods

The methods employed to demonstrate the synergistic interaction between perampanel and decanoic acid were designed to rigorously characterize the pharmacodynamic interaction at the molecular, cellular, and tissue levels. Recombinant AMPA receptors comprising GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 subunit combinations were expressed in Xenopus laevis oocytes following microinjection of cRNA encoding the respective subunits. Oocytes were maintained in ND96 solution supplemented with sodium pyruvate, penicillin, streptomycin, and gentamicin at 18°C for 2 to 5 days prior to electrophysiological recording. Whole-cell voltage-clamp recordings were performed using a two-electrode voltage-clamp system, with oocytes voltage-clamped at a holding potential of −70 mV. Glutamate was applied via a fast perfusion system at a concentration of 100 micromolar to elicit maximal AMPA receptor currents, and the inhibitory effects of perampanel and decanoic acid were assessed individually and in combination. Perampanel was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide and diluted in recording solution to achieve final concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 50 micromolar, while decanoic acid was dissolved in ethanol and diluted in recording solution to achieve concentrations from 10 to 2000 micromolar. Concentrations of solvents were kept below 0.1% to avoid nonspecific effects. Dose-response curves were constructed by measuring the peak current amplitude following glutamate application in the presence of each compound, and IC₅₀ values were calculated using nonlinear regression analysis with a Hill equation. Isobolographic analysis was performed by fixing the ratio of perampanel to decanoic acid at 1:10, 1:50, and 1:100 and measuring the combined effect at multiple concentrations to generate points on a two-dimensional plot, with the line of additivity derived from the individual IC₅₀ values of each compound. Ex vivo seizure models were established using transverse hippocampal slices (400 micrometers thick) prepared from adult Sprague-Dawley rats, which were maintained in artificial cerebrospinal fluid containing 1.25 millimolar magnesium and 10 micromolar 4-aminopyridine to induce epileptiform activity. Seizure-like discharges were recorded using extracellular field potential electrodes placed in the CA3 region, and frequency was measured over 5-minute intervals and normalized to baseline. Perampanel was applied at concentrations of 100 and 500 nanomolar, while decanoic acid was applied in increasing concentrations at 10-minute intervals. Human neocortical slices (450 micrometers thick) were obtained from patients undergoing elective neurosurgical resection for intraaxial neoplasms, with informed consent obtained under ethical approval from the Newcastle and North Tyneside 2 Research Ethics Committee. Slices were perfused with artificial cerebrospinal fluid containing 4-aminopyridine and zero magnesium to induce epileptiform activity, and local field potential recordings were obtained using multi-electrode arrays. The area power of epileptiform activity was quantified using spectral analysis over a frequency range of 1 to 100 Hz, and changes in power were normalized to baseline. Statistical analysis was performed using one-way analysis of variance with Dunnett’s post hoc test for comparisons involving multiple concentrations, and the Mann-Whitney U test for comparisons between two groups. All data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean, and statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

### Example 2—Direct Inhibition of AMPA Receptors by Perampanel and Decanoic Acid

Experiments conducted on recombinant AMPA receptors expressed in Xenopus oocytes demonstrated that perampanel inhibits glutamate-induced currents in a concentration-dependent manner, with IC₅₀ values of 6.27 micromolar (±1.02 SEM) for GluA1/2 and 8.11 micromolar (±2.05 SEM) for GluA2/3, confirming its noncompetitive mechanism of action as evidenced by the reduction in maximal current amplitude without a shift in glutamate EC₅₀. When decanoic acid was applied alone, it inhibited currents with an IC₅₀ of 1.16 millimolar (±0.12 SEM) for GluA1/2 and 0.52 millimolar (±0.06 SEM) for GluA2/3, consistent with prior reports. The presence of perampanel at concentrations of 1 and 4 micromolar produced a marked leftward shift in the decanoic acid concentration-response curve, reducing its IC₅₀ to 0.21 and 0.10 millimolar for GluA1/2, respectively, and to 0.15 and 0.12 millimolar for GluA2/3, representing a 5.5- to 11.6-fold increase in potency. Conversely, the presence of decanoic acid at 50 and 100 micromolar reduced the IC₅₀ of perampanel from 8.1 micromolar to 1.7 micromolar for GluA2/3 and from 6.2 micromolar to 2.1 micromolar for GluA1/2, corresponding to a 3.8- to 2.9-fold increase in potency. These reciprocal shifts in IC₅₀ were statistically significant (p < 0.0001) and occurred without changes in the Hill coefficient, indicating that the synergy was not due to altered cooperativity of binding but rather to enhanced stabilization of the closed state. The specificity of this interaction was confirmed using a GluA3 mutant resistant to GYKI-52466, a known AMPA receptor antagonist that binds to the same site as perampanel. In this mutant, perampanel’s inhibitory effect was reduced from 94.7% to 54.3%, whereas decanoic acid retained its full inhibitory capacity (74.5% vs. 72.8%), confirming that the two compounds bind to distinct sites. Isobolographic analysis of the combination at fixed ratios of 1:10, 1:50, and 1:100 (perampanel:decanoic acid) revealed that all measured effect points fell significantly below the line of additivity, with the 1:100 ratio producing the most pronounced deviation, indicating strong synergistic interaction. The magnitude of synergy was quantified using the combination index method, which yielded values below 0.7 for all tested ratios, confirming strong synergy. These findings demonstrate that the simultaneous occupancy of the perampanel and decanoic acid binding sites on the AMPA receptor produces a cooperative effect that enhances receptor inhibition beyond the sum of their individual actions, providing a mechanistic basis for the therapeutic synergy observed in subsequent models.

### Example 3—Effects of Perampanel and Decanoic Acid on Seizure Models

In rat hippocampal slices exposed to 4-aminopyridine and low magnesium to induce epileptiform activity, decanoic acid alone reduced seizure-like discharge frequency in a concentration-dependent manner, with an IC₅₀ of 352 micromolar (CI: 200–621). When perampanel was added at 100 nanomolar, the IC₅₀ of decanoic acid was reduced to 196 micromolar (CI: 145–264), and at 500 nanomolar perampanel, the IC₅₀ was further reduced to 122 micromolar (CI: 49–302), representing a 2.9-fold and 4.4-fold increase in potency, respectively. At a decanoic acid concentration of 10 micromolar, which alone had no significant effect, the addition of 100 nanomolar perampanel reduced seizure activity from 95.6% of baseline to 76.8% (p = 0.048), and at 500 nanomolar perampanel, the reduction was even greater, to 69.6% (p = 0.015). In human neocortical slices derived from eight patients with drug-resistant epilepsy, baseline epileptiform activity had a mean area power of 1690 μV². Perampanel at 0.5 micromolar alone produced no significant reduction (1621 μV², p = 0.7554), and decanoic acid at 100 micromolar alone resulted in a modest, non-significant reduction (1088 μV², p = 0.1390). In stark contrast, the co-application of perampanel (0.5 micromolar) and decanoic acid (100 micromolar) reduced the area power to 144.7 μV² (CI: 15.6–274), representing a 91.5% reduction compared to baseline (p < 0.0001). This effect was significantly greater than that of either agent alone (p = 0.0002 for perampanel, p = 0.0005 for decanoic acid), and in many slices, epileptiform activity was virtually abolished, with only sporadic, low-amplitude events remaining. The combination also reduced the duration and interictal interval of epileptiform bursts, indicating a suppression of network synchronization. These results demonstrate that the synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors observed in recombinant systems translates directly into functional suppression of pathological network activity in human brain tissue, validating the clinical relevance of the discovery. The fact that the combination achieved near-complete suppression of epileptiform activity at concentrations of perampanel and decanoic acid that are individually subtherapeutic underscores the transformative potential of this approach. The findings provide compelling evidence that the combination of perampanel and decanoic acid can achieve seizure control in patients who are refractory to conventional therapies, offering a new therapeutic option for those with limited alternatives.

## SUMMARY

The present invention discloses a novel and non-obvious therapeutic strategy for the treatment of seizure disorders through the synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors by the combined administration of perampanel and decanoic acid. This combination produces a supra-additive reduction in receptor-mediated currents that is not predictable from the individual pharmacological profiles of either compound, and which is validated across recombinant receptor systems, animal-derived tissue models, and critically, human neocortical tissue derived from patients with drug-resistant epilepsy. The invention demonstrates that the two agents bind to distinct, non-overlapping sites on the AMPA receptor—perampanel at the S1-M1/S2-M4 interface and decanoic acid at the M3 transmembrane helix—and that their simultaneous occupancy enhances the stabilization of the receptor in a closed, non-conducting state, resulting in a marked leftward shift in the concentration-response curves of both compounds. This synergy enables a substantial reduction in the required dose of perampanel, thereby mitigating its dose-limiting neuropsychiatric side effects while maintaining or improving seizure control. The invention further provides for pharmaceutical compositions comprising perampanel and decanoic acid in defined ratios, methods of administration, and formulations optimized for oral delivery and sustained release. The therapeutic potential of this combination is demonstrated by its ability to virtually abolish epileptiform activity in human brain tissue at concentrations that are pharmacologically and physiologically attainable through dietary supplementation with medium-chain triglycerides. This represents a paradigm shift in epilepsy treatment, moving from empirical polytherapy to mechanism-driven, synergistic combination therapy grounded in precise molecular pharmacology. The invention offers a clinically feasible, safe, and effective approach to managing refractory epilepsy, with broad applicability across pediatric and adult populations, and provides a foundation for the development of next-generation antiepileptic regimens that prioritize efficacy, tolerability, and quality of life.
