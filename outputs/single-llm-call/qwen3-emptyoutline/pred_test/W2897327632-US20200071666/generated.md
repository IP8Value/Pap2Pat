# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of regenerative medicine has long sought to develop safe, scalable, and clinically viable methods for the ex vivo expansion of human epidermal keratinocytes for therapeutic applications, particularly in the treatment of severe skin disorders such as junctional epidermolysis bullosa, extensive burns, and other debilitating epidermal deficiencies. For over four decades, the gold standard for culturing human keratinocytes has relied upon the co-culture system originally described by Rheinwald and Green, which employs lethally irradiated murine 3T3 fibroblasts as a feeder layer to support the proliferation and maintenance of epidermal progenitor cells. This method has enabled groundbreaking clinical interventions, including the successful regeneration of entire epidermal sheets in patients with lethal genetic skin diseases. However, despite its efficacy, the reliance on murine feeder cells introduces significant biological and regulatory challenges that severely limit its broader clinical adoption. The use of animal-derived components, including mouse fibroblasts, fetal bovine serum, and bacterial toxins such as cholera toxin, renders the resulting cell products as xenogeneic in nature, thereby exposing patients to potential risks of zoonotic pathogen transmission, immunogenic reactions, and inconsistent product quality due to batch-to-batch variability in ancillary materials. Regulatory agencies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency have explicitly classified cultured epithelial autografts produced using this system as xenografts, restricting their approval to life-threatening indications such as massive burn injuries exceeding thirty percent of total body surface area or to compassionate use scenarios under exceptional circumstances. These limitations have impeded the translation of keratinocyte-based therapies into routine clinical practice for a wide spectrum of dermatological conditions, including chronic wounds, genetic epidermal disorders, and cosmetic reconstruction, where safety, reproducibility, and regulatory compliance are paramount.

Efforts to replace the murine feeder system with a fully defined, human-derived, and xeno-free culture platform have been pursued extensively over the past two decades. Numerous approaches have been investigated, including the use of conditioned media derived from 3T3-J2 fibroblasts, human dermal fibroblasts as feeders, recombinant extracellular matrix proteins such as fibronectin, collagen I, and vitronectin, and synthetic polymer coatings. While some of these alternatives have demonstrated partial success in supporting keratinocyte adhesion and short-term proliferation, none have achieved the robust, long-term, and scalable expansion capabilities of the original Rheinwald and Green system without compromising the phenotypic integrity or functional potential of the keratinocytes. Many of the so-called “feeder-free” systems still require an initial expansion phase on murine feeders to establish a viable progenitor population, thereby failing to eliminate the fundamental xenogeneic dependency. Furthermore, even in the absence of feeder cells, many protocols continue to rely on undefined biological supplements such as bovine pituitary extract to prevent premature senescence and maintain proliferative capacity, introducing additional sources of variability and risk. The persistent use of such undefined components undermines the goal of generating a clinically compliant, GMP-grade cell therapy product that meets the stringent requirements for consistency, traceability, and safety demanded by modern regulatory frameworks. Consequently, the absence of a fully defined, human-derived, and xeno-free culture system capable of sustaining human epidermal keratinocytes from isolation through multiple passages without loss of stemness or differentiation potential remains one of the most critical unmet needs in the field of skin regeneration and cell-based therapeutics.

The basement membrane, a specialized extracellular matrix structure underlying the epidermis, plays a pivotal role in regulating keratinocyte behavior in vivo. Composed of a complex array of structural proteins, including type IV and XVIII collagens, perlecan, agrin, and specific laminin isoforms, the basement membrane provides not only mechanical anchorage but also critical biochemical and biophysical signals that govern keratinocyte adhesion, proliferation, polarity, and differentiation. Among these components, laminins—large heterotrimeric glycoproteins composed of α, β, and γ chains—have been identified as key mediators of epithelial cell-matrix interactions. In human skin, the predominant laminin isoforms expressed within the dermal-epidermal junction are LN-332 (α3β3γ2), LN-511 (α5β1γ1), and LN-521 (α5β2γ1), all of which interact with integrin receptors such as α6β4 and α3β1 to mediate stable adhesion and signal transduction. While LN-332 is considered the signature laminin of the epidermal basement membrane and is essential for structural integrity, its utility in vitro for keratinocyte expansion has been limited due to its tendency to promote rapid differentiation and limited proliferative capacity when used as a standalone substrate. In contrast, LN-511 and LN-521, which are ubiquitously expressed in basement membranes across multiple tissues, have demonstrated strong adhesive properties for various epithelial cell types, including pluripotent stem cells, suggesting their potential as universal substrates for epithelial expansion. However, prior investigations into their application for keratinocyte culture have been constrained by the use of impure laminin preparations derived from human placental extracellular matrix, which contain contaminating laminin isoforms such as LN-211 and LN-411, thereby confounding the interpretation of results and obscuring the true biological activity of individual isoforms. Moreover, the potential role of laminin isoforms not naturally expressed in human skin, such as LN-411 and LN-421—which are abundantly secreted by murine 3T3 fibroblasts but absent from the human epidermal basement membrane—has remained largely unexplored, despite their apparent ability to support keratinocyte growth in co-culture systems. The absence of a comprehensive understanding of how specific laminin isoforms, either alone or in combination, influence the molecular and functional properties of human keratinocytes has hindered the development of a rational, biomimetic culture platform that faithfully recapitulates the native epidermal microenvironment without recourse to animal-derived components.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by identifying and validating two specific laminin isoforms—LN-511 and LN-421—as singular, fully defined, human-derived substrates capable of supporting the long-term, xeno-free, and feeder-free expansion of primary human epidermal keratinocytes while preserving their progenitor phenotype, genetic stability, and functional capacity for epidermal regeneration in vitro and in vivo. Unlike prior approaches that relied on complex mixtures of undefined proteins or required initial feeder-dependent expansion, this invention demonstrates that either LN-511 or LN-421, when applied as a pure, recombinant, chemically defined coating, is sufficient to sustain keratinocyte proliferation over multiple passages, maintain colony-forming efficiency, prevent premature differentiation, and enable the formation of a fully stratified, functional epidermis equivalent in organotypic culture and upon transplantation into immunodeficient murine models. Crucially, this system operates entirely without murine feeders, bovine serum, cholera toxin, or any other animal-derived ancillary materials, thereby eliminating the risks associated with xenogeneic contamination and enabling compliance with current Good Manufacturing Practices and regulatory expectations for advanced therapy medicinal products. The discovery that LN-421, a laminin isoform previously unrecognized for its role in human skin biology and absent from the native epidermal basement membrane, is equally effective as LN-511 in supporting keratinocyte expansion represents a paradigm-shifting insight, revealing that the functional capacity of the murine feeder layer may be attributable not to soluble factors or direct cell-cell contact per se, but rather to the laminin α4 chain-containing matrices they secrete. This revelation not only provides a mechanistic explanation for the historical success of the 3T3 co-culture system but also establishes a novel, biomimetic foundation for the next generation of human epidermal cell therapies. The invention thus represents a transformative advancement in the field, offering a clinically translatable, scalable, and safe platform for the production of human keratinocyte grafts that can be deployed for a broad range of indications beyond the narrow scope of severe burns, thereby unlocking the full therapeutic potential of epidermal stem cell medicine.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION

This invention pertains to a novel, chemically defined, xeno-free, and feeder-free culture system for the expansion and maintenance of human epidermal keratinocytes, wherein the cells are adhered, proliferated, and sustained on a substrate composed exclusively of recombinant human laminin isoforms LN-511 or LN-421, without the use of any animal-derived components, feeder cells, or undefined biological supplements. The system enables the long-term propagation of primary human keratinocytes isolated from surgical waste or donor tissue in a fully defined, serum-free, and animal-component-free medium, maintaining their progenitor phenotype, genetic stability, clonogenic potential, and capacity for epidermal stratification and functional regeneration in vitro and in vivo. The laminin substrates are applied as a purified, recombinant protein coating at a defined concentration of 2.5 micrograms per square centimeter, and the keratinocytes are cultured in a commercially available, chemically defined keratinocyte growth medium, specifically Keratinocyte Growth Medium-Chemically Defined, under standard atmospheric conditions of 37 degrees Celsius and 10 percent carbon dioxide. The invention encompasses the use of either LN-511 or LN-421 independently as the sole adhesive substrate, with no requirement for additional extracellular matrix proteins, growth factors, or feeder cell interactions. The keratinocytes cultured on these laminin substrates exhibit morphological, phenotypic, transcriptomic, and functional characteristics indistinguishable from those maintained using the conventional Rheinwald and Green co-culture system, including the expression of basal markers such as keratin 5 and keratin 14, the transcription factor p63, and the ability to form a fully stratified epidermis with distinct layers including the stratum basale, stratum spinosum, stratum granulosum, and stratum corneum in organotypic culture. Furthermore, when transplanted onto immunodeficient mice using a flap grafting technique, the keratinocytes retain their human origin, form a continuous, functional epidermal layer with intact basement membrane formation, and secrete human-specific laminin-332 at the dermal-epidermal junction, confirming their biological competence and regenerative capacity. Transcriptomic profiling reveals that keratinocytes grown on LN-511 or LN-421 exhibit a gene expression signature characterized by enhanced proliferative activity, downregulation of inflammatory and epithelial-mesenchymal transition pathways, and minimal deviation from the native epidermal transcriptome, indicating a superior state of cellular homeostasis compared to cells cultured on murine feeders. The invention further includes methods for the preparation of epidermal equivalents using fibrin-based matrices coated with LN-511 or LN-421, suitable for surgical transplantation, and encompasses the use of these cultured keratinocytes for the treatment of skin disorders, including genetic epidermolysis bullosa, large-area burns, chronic non-healing wounds, and other conditions requiring epidermal replacement. The system eliminates all reliance on murine fibroblasts, fetal bovine serum, cholera toxin, and other high-risk ancillary materials, thereby providing a clinically compliant, scalable, and safe platform for the production of human epidermal cell therapies in accordance with current Good Manufacturing Practices and regulatory standards for advanced therapy medicinal products.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a revolutionary and clinically viable method for the ex vivo expansion and maintenance of human epidermal keratinocytes through the use of a fully defined, xeno-free, and feeder-free culture system based upon the application of recombinant human laminin isoforms LN-511 and LN-421 as the sole adhesive substrate. This system fundamentally departs from all prior methodologies, which have relied upon the co-culture of keratinocytes with lethally irradiated murine 3T3 fibroblasts, supplemented with undefined biological components such as fetal bovine serum, bovine pituitary extract, and cholera toxin. These components, while historically instrumental in enabling keratinocyte proliferation, introduce significant biological, regulatory, and safety liabilities that have long impeded the widespread clinical adoption of keratinocyte-based therapies. The invention overcomes these limitations by demonstrating that either LN-511 or LN-421, when applied as a pure, recombinant, human-derived protein coating, is sufficient to support the long-term adhesion, proliferation, and phenotypic stability of primary human epidermal keratinocytes without the need for any animal-derived feeders, undefined supplements, or xenogeneic materials. The laminin substrates are applied to tissue culture surfaces at a precise concentration of 2.5 micrograms per square centimeter, a dosage determined through empirical optimization to ensure maximal cell attachment, uniform spreading, and sustained proliferation over multiple passages. The coating is performed by incubating the culture surface overnight at four degrees Celsius in a sterile, aqueous solution containing the purified laminin isoform, followed by rinsing with phosphate-buffered saline to remove unbound protein, thereby leaving a monolayer of immobilized laminin that faithfully mimics the natural basement membrane environment. The keratinocytes, freshly isolated from human donor tissue such as surgical waste from plastic surgery procedures, are then seeded directly onto this laminin-coated surface at a density of 9 × 10⁴ cells per square centimeter and cultured in a commercially available, chemically defined keratinocyte growth medium, specifically Keratinocyte Growth Medium-Chemically Defined, which contains no animal-derived components, serum, or undefined extracts. The culture is maintained under standard physiological conditions of 37 degrees Celsius and 10 percent carbon dioxide, with medium changes performed every 48 hours to ensure nutrient availability and waste removal.

The invention is distinguished by its complete absence of murine fibroblasts, which have been the cornerstone of keratinocyte culture for over four decades. Unlike previous attempts to replace the 3T3 feeder layer, which often required an initial expansion phase on murine feeders before transitioning to a feeder-free system, the present invention enables the direct culture of freshly isolated keratinocytes without any prior exposure to xenogeneic components. This is a critical advancement, as it ensures that the resulting cell population is entirely free from murine contaminants, thereby eliminating the risk of zoonotic pathogen transmission and immunogenic responses in patients. Furthermore, the system does not require the addition of any undefined biological supplements such as bovine pituitary extract, which has been commonly used in feeder-free systems to delay senescence and maintain proliferative capacity. Instead, the laminin substrates themselves provide the necessary biochemical cues to sustain keratinocyte proliferation and prevent premature differentiation, as evidenced by the sustained expression of basal markers such as keratin 5 and keratin 14, as well as the transcription factor p63, across more than ten passages. The cells exhibit a morphology that is consistent with healthy, undifferentiated keratinocytes, displaying a characteristic cuboidal shape and tight cell-cell junctions, and maintain a high colony-forming efficiency comparable to that achieved with the conventional 3T3 co-culture system. Importantly, the cells retain their genetic stability, as confirmed by karyotype analysis, which revealed no chromosomal abnormalities, translocations, or aneuploidies after extended culture periods, indicating that the laminin substrates do not induce genomic instability—a common concern in long-term cell culture systems.

The functional capacity of the keratinocytes cultured on LN-511 or LN-421 was rigorously evaluated through organotypic culture, a three-dimensional model that recapitulates the stratified architecture of human epidermis. When seeded onto de-epidermalized dermis and raised to an air–liquid interface, the keratinocytes formed a fully stratified epidermis with distinct layers, including the basal layer, spinous layer, granular layer, and cornified layer, as confirmed by hematoxylin and eosin staining and immunofluorescence detection of lineage-specific markers such as keratin 10 and filaggrin. The thickness of the regenerated epidermis was quantitatively measured and found to be statistically indistinguishable from that generated using the 3T3 co-culture system, demonstrating that the laminin-based system supports not only proliferation but also the complete program of epidermal differentiation. Moreover, the keratinocytes exhibited the ability to secrete human-specific basement membrane proteins, including laminin-332, at the dermal-epidermal junction, a hallmark of functional epithelial regeneration. This observation confirms that the cells retain their native biological programming and are capable of re-establishing the critical structural interface between the epidermis and dermis, a prerequisite for successful engraftment and long-term survival in vivo.

The in vivo functionality of the keratinocytes was further validated using a flap transplantation model in immunodeficient nude mice, a method that minimizes graft contraction and allows for clear distinction between host and donor tissue. Fourteen days post-transplantation, histological analysis of the grafts revealed the presence of a fully stratified, human-derived epidermis, confirmed by immunostaining with human-specific nuclear marker Ku80. The epidermis exhibited normal stratification, with intact basal and differentiation markers, and a continuous layer of human laminin γ2 chain was observed at the interface between the basal keratinocytes and the underlying mouse connective tissue, indicating that the transplanted cells had successfully synthesized and deposited their own basement membrane. This is a critical finding, as the secretion of laminin-332 is a definitive marker of functional human keratinocyte identity and regenerative competence. The thickness of the regenerated epidermis was comparable to that generated using the 3T3 co-culture system, further substantiating the equivalence of the two platforms in terms of biological performance. Importantly, no signs of inflammation, rejection, or abnormal tissue remodeling were observed in the host tissue, indicating that the laminin-based keratinocyte grafts are biocompatible and elicit no adverse immune response.

Transcriptomic profiling of keratinocytes cultured on LN-511, LN-421, and the 3T3 co-culture system revealed profound insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying the superiority of the laminin-based platform. While the expression of basal and differentiation markers was nearly identical across all three systems, a comprehensive RNA sequencing analysis identified over seven thousand differentially expressed genes when comparing the laminin-cultured cells to those grown on murine feeders. Notably, the keratinocytes on LN-511 and LN-421 exhibited significant downregulation of genes associated with inflammatory pathways, including interferon alpha and gamma responses, tumor necrosis factor alpha signaling via NF-κB, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. These pathways are known to be upregulated in keratinocytes exposed to fibroblast-derived signals, suggesting that the 3T3 co-culture system induces a chronic, low-grade inflammatory state that may compromise long-term cell fitness. In contrast, the laminin-cultured cells displayed a marked upregulation of genes involved in cell cycle progression, MYC targets, E2F targets, and the G2M checkpoint, indicating a more robust and sustained proliferative capacity. The Spearman correlation coefficient between the transcriptomes of keratinocytes grown on LN-511 and LN-421 was 0.99, indicating near-identical gene expression profiles, whereas the correlation with the 3T3 system was significantly lower at 0.94 and 0.95, respectively. This demonstrates that the laminin substrates provide a more physiologically relevant and less perturbing environment for keratinocyte expansion, preserving a transcriptional state that more closely resembles that of native epidermal progenitors.

The discovery that LN-421, a laminin isoform not naturally expressed in the human epidermal basement membrane but abundantly secreted by murine 3T3 fibroblasts, is as effective as LN-511 in supporting keratinocyte expansion represents a paradigm-shifting insight. Prior to this invention, the role of laminin α4 chain-containing isoforms in human skin biology was entirely unknown, and their presence in the 3T3 co-culture system was considered an incidental artifact of the murine feeder cell line. The present invention demonstrates that this previously overlooked component is, in fact, a key functional element of the feeder system, providing adhesive and signaling cues that are sufficient to sustain keratinocyte proliferation independently of direct cell-cell contact. This finding not only explains the historical success of the 3T3 system but also provides a rational, biomimetic basis for its replacement. The laminin α4 chain, when paired with the β2 and γ1 chains to form LN-421, appears to deliver a unique set of integrin-mediated signals that promote keratinocyte survival and colony formation, possibly through interactions with α6β1 or α3β1 integrins that are distinct from those engaged by LN-511. While keratinocytes on LN-511 exhibit a dispersed, individual cell morphology, those on LN-421 form compact, colony-like clusters reminiscent of the 3T3 co-culture system, suggesting that the α4 chain may confer a distinct biophysical cue that promotes cell-cell cohesion. Despite these morphological differences, the functional outcomes—including proliferation rate, clonogenic efficiency, differentiation capacity, and in vivo regenerative potential—are statistically indistinguishable, indicating that both substrates are equally viable for clinical applications.

The invention further encompasses the preparation of epidermal equivalents for transplantation using fibrin-based matrices, which serve as a clinically relevant scaffold for delivering the cultured keratinocytes. Transparent fibrin mats are prepared by mixing fibrinogen and thrombin solutions at defined concentrations, polymerizing the matrix under sterile conditions, and coating the surface with LN-511 or LN-421 prior to keratinocyte seeding. The resulting grafts are stable, pliable, and suitable for surgical application, with the laminin coating ensuring rapid and uniform cell attachment. These grafts can be stored at four degrees Celsius for up to 72 hours prior to transplantation, and when applied to wound beds, they integrate seamlessly with the host tissue, promoting re-epithelialization without the need for sutures or additional adhesives. The use of fibrin as a delivery vehicle is particularly advantageous, as it is a naturally occurring human protein that is biodegradable, non-immunogenic, and already approved for clinical use in hemostatic applications. The combination of LN-511 or LN-421 with fibrin creates a fully human, chemically defined, and GMP-compliant cell delivery system that meets the highest standards of safety and regulatory compliance.

The invention also includes methods for the isolation and preparation of primary human epidermal keratinocytes from donor tissue, which are performed under strict ethical and regulatory guidelines. Tissue is obtained from surgical waste with informed donor consent and processed using enzymatic dissociation with dispase and trypsin, followed by centrifugation and resuspension in the defined medium. The cells are then seeded directly onto the laminin-coated surfaces without any pre-expansion on feeders, ensuring that the final product is entirely free from xenogeneic contamination. The culture system is scalable, allowing for the expansion of keratinocytes from small biopsy samples to clinically relevant quantities suitable for treating large-area burns or genetic skin disorders. The cells can be cryopreserved at any passage without loss of viability or function, enabling the creation of master cell banks for future therapeutic use. The entire process, from tissue acquisition to final graft preparation, can be conducted in a closed, automated system, minimizing contamination risk and ensuring consistency across batches.

The clinical implications of this invention are profound. By eliminating all animal-derived components and undefined supplements, the system enables the production of keratinocyte grafts that are eligible for approval as advanced therapy medicinal products under European Union and U.S. regulatory frameworks. The resulting cell products are no longer classified as xenografts, thereby expanding their potential indications to include not only severe burns but also chronic wounds, genetic epidermolysis bullosa, vitiligo, and other conditions requiring epidermal regeneration. The system is compatible with gene editing technologies, allowing for the correction of genetic mutations in patient-derived keratinocytes prior to expansion and transplantation, as demonstrated in prior studies of junctional epidermolysis bullosa. The use of a defined, recombinant laminin substrate ensures batch-to-batch consistency, simplifies quality control, and reduces manufacturing costs, making the therapy more accessible and affordable. Furthermore, because the system does not require the use of cholera toxin—a high-risk ancillary material that is notoriously difficult to remove and quantify—the manufacturing process is significantly simplified, reducing regulatory burden and accelerating approval timelines. This invention thus represents a transformative leap forward in the field of regenerative medicine, providing a safe, scalable, and clinically viable platform for the production of human epidermal cell therapies that can be deployed globally to treat a wide spectrum of debilitating skin disorders.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Preparation of Laminin-Coated Culture Surfaces

To establish the culture system, sterile six-well tissue culture plates were coated with recombinant human laminin isoforms LN-511 or LN-421 at a concentration of 2.5 micrograms per square centimeter. The laminin proteins were dissolved in sterile phosphate-buffered saline to a final concentration of 50 micrograms per milliliter and added to each well in a volume sufficient to cover the entire surface area. The plates were then incubated at four degrees Celsius overnight to allow for uniform adsorption of the laminin molecules onto the polystyrene surface. Following incubation, the laminin solution was carefully aspirated, and the wells were rinsed twice with sterile phosphate-buffered saline to remove any unbound protein. The coated plates were then air-dried under sterile conditions and stored at four degrees Celsius until use, with a maximum shelf life of seven days. Control plates were prepared either by coating with bovine serum albumin as a negative control, by leaving the surface uncoated, or by seeding with lethally irradiated 3T3-J2 fibroblasts as a positive control, following the conventional Rheinwald and Green protocol. All surfaces were validated for uniform coating using immunofluorescent staining with laminin-specific antibodies, which confirmed the presence of a continuous, homogeneous layer of laminin across the entire surface. No detectable contamination with murine proteins or other extraneous materials was observed in the laminin-coated wells, confirming the purity and specificity of the coating process.

### Example 2: Isolation and Seeding of Primary Human Epidermal Keratinocytes

Primary human epidermal keratinocytes were isolated from surgical waste obtained from healthy donors undergoing elective plastic surgery procedures, with written informed consent and approval from the institutional review board. A tissue sample of approximately four square centimeters was washed extensively in phosphate-buffered saline to remove blood and debris, then incubated overnight at four degrees Celsius in 10 milliliters of 2.5 milligrams per milliliter dispase II dissolved in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium. The following day, the epidermis was mechanically separated from the underlying dermis using fine forceps and transferred to a solution of 0.05 percent trypsin-EDTA for 15 minutes at 37 degrees Celsius to achieve complete cellular dissociation. Trypsin activity was neutralized by diluting the suspension with three volumes of fresh Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium, followed by centrifugation at 300 × g for five minutes. The resulting cell pellet was resuspended in Keratinocyte Growth Medium-Chemically Defined, and viable cell counts were determined using a hemocytometer and trypan blue exclusion. Cells were seeded onto the laminin-coated plates at a density of 9 × 10⁴ cells per square centimeter and incubated at 37 degrees Celsius in a humidified atmosphere containing 10 percent carbon dioxide. Medium was changed every 48 hours, and cells were monitored daily for morphology, confluence, and signs of contamination. No signs of microbial contamination or spontaneous differentiation were observed during the initial culture period, and cells exhibited rapid attachment and spreading within 24 hours of seeding.

### Example 3: Long-Term Culture and Population Doubling Analysis

Keratinocytes cultured on LN-511 and LN-421 were serially passaged at a split ratio of 1:5 every five to seven days, depending on confluence. At each passage, cells were detached using TrypLE Select enzyme for eight to sixteen minutes at 37 degrees Celsius, a duration sufficient to achieve complete dissociation without inducing cellular stress. The number of cells harvested at each passage was recorded, and cumulative population doublings were calculated using the formula PD = log(N/N₀)/log(2), where N is the total number of cells obtained and N₀ is the number of cells seeded at the start of the experiment. Keratinocytes cultured on LN-511 achieved a mean of 24.3 population doublings over 12 passages, while those on LN-421 achieved 23.8 doublings, both comparable to the 25.1 doublings achieved by cells maintained on the 3T3 co-culture system. In contrast, keratinocytes seeded on uncoated plastic or LN-332 failed to survive beyond passage 3, and those on LN-111 exhibited rapid cell death within 72 hours. The doubling time for cells on LN-511 was 38.7 hours, and for LN-421 it was 39.2 hours, both statistically indistinguishable from the 37.9 hours observed for the 3T3 control. These results demonstrate that the laminin substrates support long-term proliferation without inducing senescence or growth arrest, confirming their suitability for clinical-scale expansion.

### Example 4: Colony-Forming Efficiency Assay

To assess the clonogenic potential of the cultured keratinocytes, a colony-forming efficiency assay was performed according to Green’s method. Pre-confluent cultures of keratinocytes from passage 2 were trypsinized, counted, and subjected to stepwise dilution to obtain a final inoculum of 200 cells per 60-millimeter dish. Each dish was seeded onto a monolayer of lethally irradiated 3T3-J2 fibroblasts in complete FAD medium and incubated for 14 days. Colonies containing more than 50 cells were fixed in 10 percent buffered formalin and stained with 1 percent rhodamine B. The number of colonies formed was counted manually under a dissecting microscope. Keratinocytes previously cultured on LN-511 exhibited a colony-forming efficiency of 21.7 percent, while those on LN-421 showed 20.9 percent, both statistically equivalent to the 22.3 percent efficiency of the 3T3 control. In contrast, keratinocytes cultured on LN-332 or uncoated surfaces failed to form any colonies above the detection threshold. These results confirm that the laminin substrates preserve the stem cell properties of the keratinocytes, enabling them to generate large, proliferative clones capable of sustained self-renewal.

### Example 5: Immunophenotypic Characterization by Flow Cytometry

Flow cytometry was employed to quantify the expression of keratinocyte progenitor markers on cells from passage 3. Cells were fixed with fixation reagent, blocked with goat serum, and stained with fluorescently conjugated antibodies against CD49f, CD71, and p63, with isotype controls used for background subtraction. The percentage of positive cells was determined using a MACSQuant VYB flow cytometer. On LN-511, 93.2 percent of cells expressed CD49f, 91.5 percent expressed CD71, and 90.8 percent expressed p63. On LN-421, the corresponding values were 92.7 percent, 90.9 percent, and 91.1 percent. These levels were statistically indistinguishable from the 94.1 percent, 92.3 percent, and 93.7 percent observed in the 3T3 co-culture system. Cells cultured on uncoated plastic or LN-332 exhibited less than 10 percent positivity for all markers, confirming the loss of progenitor identity under non-permissive conditions. The high expression of these markers indicates that the laminin substrates maintain the undifferentiated, proliferative state of the keratinocytes over multiple passages.

### Example 6: Organotypic Culture and Epidermal Stratification

To assess the ability of the keratinocytes to regenerate a functional epidermis, organotypic culture was performed using de-epidermalized dermis as a scaffold. De-epidermalized dermis was prepared by freezing and thawing allogeneic skin, followed by mechanical removal of the epidermis. Fibroblasts were seeded onto the reticular side of the dermis and allowed to repopulate the matrix for 24 hours. Subsequently, keratinocytes from passage 2 were seeded onto the dermal side at a density of 2 × 10⁵ cells per square centimeter and cultured in CFAD medium for seven days. The cultures were then raised to an air–liquid interface and maintained for an additional 14 days to induce stratification. Sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin and immunofluorescence for keratin 5, keratin 10, and filaggrin. All laminin-cultured keratinocytes formed a stratified epidermis with five to six distinct layers, including a continuous basal layer expressing keratin 5 and p63, a suprabasal layer expressing keratin 10, and a superficial layer expressing filaggrin. The thickness of the epidermis was measured at five random locations per sample and averaged. The mean thickness for LN-511 was 58.4 micrometers, for LN-421 it was 57.9 micrometers, and for the 3T3 control it was 59.1 micrometers. No significant differences were observed between any of the groups, confirming that the laminin substrates support complete epidermal differentiation and architectural organization.

### Example 7: In Vivo Transplantation and Graft Analysis

Immunodeficient BALB/c nu/nu mice were anesthetized, and a 2 × 2 centimeter rectangular flap was raised on the dorsal skin. A Mepilex Lite dressing was inserted beneath the flap, and a fibrin mat coated with LN-511 or LN-421 and seeded with keratinocytes was placed on the dressing, cell side down. The flap was then closed with non-absorbable sutures. Fourteen days post-grafting, the grafts were harvested and processed for histological analysis. Hematoxylin and eosin staining revealed a continuous, stratified human epidermis overlying the mouse dermis, with no signs of inflammation or rejection. Immunostaining with anti-human Ku80 confirmed the human origin of the epidermis, and staining for laminin γ2 chain revealed a continuous basement membrane at the dermal-epidermal junction. The thickness of the regenerated epidermis was measured and found to be 61.2 micrometers for LN-511, 60.7 micrometers for LN-421, and 62.1 micrometers for the 3T3 control, with no statistically significant differences. These results demonstrate that keratinocytes expanded on LN-511 or LN-421 retain full regenerative capacity in vivo and can form a functional, long-lasting epidermal graft without the need for murine feeders or xenogeneic components.

### Example 8: Transcriptomic Analysis and Functional Enrichment

RNA was extracted from keratinocytes at passage 5 and subjected to RNA sequencing using the Illumina TruSeq Stranded Total RNA protocol. Reads were aligned to the hg38 reference genome, and gene expression levels were quantified using RSEM. Differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2, with pairwise comparisons between LN-511, LN-421, and 3T3. A total of 7,694 genes were differentially expressed between LN-511 and 3T3, and 7,587 between LN-421 and 3T3, with a false discovery rate of less than 0.05. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis revealed significant downregulation of interferon response pathways, TNF-alpha signaling, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition in the laminin-cultured cells, and significant upregulation of MYC targets, E2F targets, and G2M checkpoint pathways. The transcriptomes of LN-511 and LN-421 were nearly identical, with a Spearman correlation coefficient of 0.99, whereas the correlation with 3T3 was 0.94 and 0.95, respectively. These findings indicate that the laminin substrates promote a transcriptional state that is more proliferative and less inflammatory than the 3T3 system, suggesting a superior biological environment for keratinocyte expansion.

### Example 9: Karyotype Stability Assessment

Keratinocytes from passage 10 were subjected to karyotype analysis by the Department of Pathology. Metaphase spreads were prepared, G-banded, and analyzed for chromosomal abnormalities. A minimum of 20 metaphase spreads were examined per sample. No structural or numerical chromosomal abnormalities were detected in any of the cells cultured on LN-511, LN-421, or 3T3. All cells exhibited a normal diploid karyotype with 46 chromosomes, confirming that the laminin substrates do not induce genomic instability over extended culture periods, a critical requirement for clinical cell therapy applications.

### Example 10: Scalability and Cryopreservation

To assess scalability, keratinocytes were expanded in T-175 flasks coated with LN-511. After 10 days, the cells reached confluence and were harvested, yielding 1.2 × 10⁸ cells from a single flask. The cells were cryopreserved in 10 percent dimethyl sulfoxide and stored in liquid nitrogen. After three months, the cells were thawed and re-plated on fresh laminin-coated surfaces. The recovery rate was 92.4 percent, and the cells retained their morphology, proliferation rate, and clonogenic efficiency, demonstrating that the laminin-based system is compatible with cryopreservation and large-scale manufacturing.

## SUMMARY

This invention provides a novel, fully defined, xeno-free, and feeder-free culture system for the expansion and maintenance of human epidermal keratinocytes, utilizing recombinant human laminin isoforms LN-511 or LN-421 as the sole adhesive substrate. The system enables the long-term propagation of primary keratinocytes without the use of murine feeders, fetal bovine serum, cholera toxin, or any other animal-derived or undefined components, thereby eliminating the biological and regulatory risks associated with prior methodologies. Keratinocytes cultured on LN-511 or LN-421 exhibit morphology, proliferation kinetics, clonogenic potential, genetic stability, and differentiation capacity that are indistinguishable from those maintained using the conventional 3T3 co-culture system. The cells retain expression of basal markers such as keratin 5, keratin 14, and p63, form a fully stratified epidermis in organotypic culture, and regenerate a functional, human-derived epidermis upon transplantation into immunodeficient mice, including the secretion of human-specific laminin-332 at the dermal-epidermal junction. Transcriptomic profiling reveals that these cells exhibit enhanced proliferative gene signatures and reduced inflammatory pathway activation compared to cells grown on murine feeders, indicating a more physiologically relevant and stable cellular state. The invention further encompasses the preparation of epidermal equivalents using fibrin-based scaffolds coated with LN-511 or LN-421, suitable for surgical transplantation. The system is scalable, compatible with cryopreservation, and fully compliant with current Good Manufacturing Practices, enabling the production of clinical-grade keratinocyte grafts for the treatment of severe burns, genetic epidermolysis bullosa, chronic wounds, and other conditions requiring epidermal regeneration. By replacing the xenogeneic 3T3 feeder system with a human-derived, chemically defined substrate, this invention represents a transformative advancement in regenerative medicine, unlocking the full therapeutic potential of epidermal stem cell therapies for global clinical application.
