# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and molecular therapeutics, specifically to the use of Insulin-like 6 (Insl6) and its functional variants for the treatment of autoimmune and immune-mediated diseases, particularly those affecting skeletal muscle tissue. The invention encompasses novel therapeutic agents comprising Insl6 polypeptides, Insl6-Fc fusion proteins, nucleic acids encoding Insl6, and pharmaceutical compositions containing the same, for the modulation of T cell activation, suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokine production, and promotion of muscle tissue repair in the context of chronic inflammatory conditions. The invention further provides methods for the administration of such agents to patients suffering from idiopathic inflammatory myositis, including polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and related autoimmune myopathies, as well as other immune-mediated disorders characterized by aberrant T cell infiltration and persistent inflammation. The therapeutic utility of Insl6 is grounded in its previously unrecognized ability to directly inhibit T lymphocyte proliferation and interleukin-2 secretion, while simultaneously reducing the expression of key inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interferon-gamma in affected tissues. This dual mechanism of action—targeting both immune cell activation and tissue regeneration—distinguishes Insl6 from conventional immunosuppressive therapies and positions it as a uniquely suited agent for the treatment of chronic autoimmune muscle diseases with high unmet clinical need.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Idiopathic inflammatory myopathies represent a group of rare, debilitating autoimmune disorders that primarily target skeletal muscle, leading to progressive weakness, chronic inflammation, and significant impairment in mobility and quality of life. These conditions include polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and inclusion body myositis, each of which exhibits distinct histopathological features but shares a common underlying pathophysiology involving the dysregulated infiltration and activation of autoreactive T lymphocytes within muscle tissue. In polymyositis, cytotoxic CD8+ T cells are observed to surround and invade non-necrotic muscle fibers, whereas in dermatomyositis, the primary pathology centers on perimysial and perivascular inflammation driven by CD4+ helper T cells and complement-mediated vascular damage. Despite decades of clinical investigation, current therapeutic approaches remain largely inadequate, relying on non-specific immunosuppressants such as corticosteroids, methotrexate, and intravenous immunoglobulin, which often produce only partial responses and are associated with substantial systemic toxicity, including increased susceptibility to infection, metabolic disturbances, and long-term organ damage. There is a critical and persistent unmet need for targeted, disease-modifying therapies that can suppress pathogenic immune responses without compromising overall immune surveillance or impairing the intrinsic regenerative capacity of skeletal muscle.

The role of the immune system in muscle repair has emerged as a central theme in modern muscle biology. Following acute injury, immune cells—including neutrophils, macrophages, and T lymphocytes—are rapidly recruited to the site of damage, where they orchestrate the clearance of necrotic debris, secrete growth-promoting factors, and activate resident satellite cells to initiate regeneration. However, in chronic autoimmune conditions, this tightly regulated repair process becomes dysregulated, leading to persistent inflammation, fibrosis, and failure of muscle regeneration. The molecular signals that normally serve to dampen inflammation and promote tissue restoration in the aftermath of injury remain incompletely understood. Recent studies have identified members of the insulin-like/relaxin family of peptides as key regulators of tissue repair, with several family members—such as relaxin-2 and Insl3—demonstrating protective effects in cardiac and reproductive tissues. However, the function of Insulin-like 6 (Insl6), a lesser-characterized member of this family, had not been previously linked to immune modulation or muscle autoimmunity.

Prior work had established that Insl6 is expressed in skeletal muscle and is upregulated following acute chemical injury, such as that induced by cardiotoxin, where it promotes satellite cell activation and accelerates muscle regeneration. However, the broader implications of Insl6 expression in the context of chronic immune-mediated muscle disease had not been explored. Crucially, no prior studies had demonstrated that Insl6 directly influences T cell biology, nor had any therapeutic strategy been proposed that leverages Insl6 to suppress autoimmune inflammation in muscle. The discovery that Insl6 is downregulated in human biopsy samples from patients with polymyositis and dermatomyositis, and that its absence exacerbates disease severity in murine models of experimental autoimmune myositis, represents a fundamental shift in understanding the molecular underpinnings of these disorders. This observation suggests that Insl6 may function as an endogenous immunosuppressive and regenerative factor whose physiological expression is insufficiently induced or actively suppressed in chronic autoimmune disease, thereby permitting unchecked inflammation and progressive muscle degeneration. The identification of Insl6 as a novel endogenous regulator of T cell activation, capable of inhibiting proliferation and interleukin-2 secretion in human T lymphocytes, provides a compelling mechanistic basis for its therapeutic application. Furthermore, the demonstration that systemic delivery of Insl6, either via transgenic overexpression or hydrodynamic plasmid delivery, significantly ameliorates disease severity in preclinical models establishes a clear path toward clinical translation. The invention disclosed herein builds upon these findings to provide a comprehensive framework for the development of Insl6-based therapeutics, including recombinant proteins, fusion constructs, and gene therapy vectors, for the treatment of autoimmune myopathies and related immune-mediated conditions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel therapeutic approach for the treatment of autoimmune and immune-mediated diseases, particularly those affecting skeletal muscle, through the administration of Insulin-like 6 (Insl6) or functional variants thereof. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that Insl6 possesses potent immunomodulatory properties, specifically the ability to suppress the activation and proliferation of T lymphocytes, reduce the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interferon-gamma, and enhance skeletal muscle regeneration in the context of chronic inflammation. This dual functionality—targeting both the immune and muscular components of disease—distinguishes Insl6 from all previously known therapeutic agents used in the treatment of inflammatory myopathies. The invention encompasses a broad range of compositions and methods for delivering Insl6 to patients, including recombinant Insl6 polypeptides, Insl6-Fc fusion proteins, nucleic acid constructs encoding Insl6 for gene therapy, and pharmaceutical compositions formulated for systemic or localized administration. The invention further provides methods for treating patients diagnosed with autoimmune myositis, including polymyositis and dermatomyositis, by administering an effective amount of an Insl6-containing agent to reduce T cell infiltration, diminish inflammatory cytokine expression, improve muscle strength and motor function, and slow or halt disease progression. The therapeutic efficacy of Insl6 has been demonstrated in multiple preclinical models, including genetic knockout and transgenic mice, as well as through hydrodynamic plasmid delivery, all of which confirm that restoration of Insl6 expression leads to significant clinical improvement. Importantly, the invention also reveals that Insl6 expression is significantly reduced in human muscle biopsy samples from patients with polymyositis and dermatomyositis, correlating with disease severity, thereby validating its relevance to human pathology and supporting its development as a targeted biologic therapy. The invention further includes kits for the diagnosis and treatment of these conditions, comprising Insl6 agents alongside diagnostic reagents for assessing Insl6 expression levels in patient samples. The compositions and methods of this invention represent a paradigm shift in the treatment of autoimmune muscle diseases, offering a mechanism-based, tissue-specific, and physiologically coherent therapeutic strategy that addresses both the inflammatory and degenerative components of disease, without the broad immunosuppression associated with current standards of care.

### Insulin-Like 6 (Insl6) Agents

The present invention provides novel therapeutic agents based on the Insulin-like 6 (Insl6) polypeptide, which has been identified as a potent endogenous regulator of immune and muscle homeostasis. Insl6 is a secreted peptide belonging to the insulin-like/relaxin family, structurally characterized by a conserved A- and B-chain configuration linked by disulfide bonds, similar to insulin and relaxin. The human Insl6 polypeptide, as encoded by the INSL6 gene located on chromosome 19, consists of 112 amino acids and is expressed predominantly in skeletal muscle, testes, and placenta under physiological conditions. In the context of muscle injury or inflammation, Insl6 expression is dynamically upregulated, suggesting a role in tissue repair and immune modulation. The invention encompasses purified, recombinant forms of human and murine Insl6, produced using standard recombinant DNA techniques in bacterial, yeast, insect, or mammalian expression systems. In one embodiment, the Insl6 polypeptide is expressed in Escherichia coli as a fusion protein with a solubility-enhancing tag such as glutathione S-transferase or maltose-binding protein, followed by proteolytic cleavage and purification by affinity chromatography and reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography. In another embodiment, Insl6 is expressed in mammalian cell lines such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) or human embryonic kidney (HEK) 293 cells to ensure proper folding, disulfide bond formation, and post-translational modifications, including glycosylation, which may influence bioactivity and pharmacokinetics. The recombinant Insl6 polypeptide may be formulated in solution as a sterile, pyrogen-free, isotonic buffer suitable for parenteral administration. The biological activity of recombinant Insl6 is confirmed by its ability to inhibit Jurkat T cell proliferation in vitro, as measured by [3H]-thymidine incorporation, and to suppress interleukin-2 secretion in response to phorbol ester and phytohemagglutinin stimulation. In addition, Insl6 has been shown to reduce the expression of tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interferon-gamma in muscle tissue of mice with experimental autoimmune myositis, as determined by quantitative real-time PCR and immunohistochemistry. The invention further includes synthetic analogs of Insl6 designed to enhance stability, bioavailability, or receptor binding affinity. These analogs may include non-natural amino acid substitutions, cyclization of the peptide backbone, or conjugation with polyethylene glycol to extend serum half-life. The invention also encompasses Insl6 peptides derived from naturally occurring isoforms or splice variants, provided that they retain the core functional domains responsible for T cell suppression and muscle regeneration. In one embodiment, the Insl6 agent is a truncated form comprising amino acids 25–112 of the human sequence, which retains full biological activity in vitro and in vivo. In another embodiment, the Insl6 agent is a stabilized variant containing a cysteine-to-serine substitution at position 42 to prevent aberrant dimerization while preserving receptor interaction. The invention further includes Insl6 polypeptides modified to enhance tissue targeting, such as fusion with muscle-homing peptides or antibodies directed against muscle-specific antigens. The purity of the Insl6 agent is at least 95% as determined by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry, and the endotoxin content is less than 0.1 EU per microgram of protein. The Insl6 agent may be lyophilized for long-term storage and reconstituted immediately prior to administration. The therapeutic utility of Insl6 is further supported by the observation that endogenous Insl6 expression is significantly reduced in human muscle biopsy samples from patients with polymyositis and dermatomyositis, indicating that exogenous supplementation may restore a critical endogenous regulatory mechanism that is deficient in disease. The administration of recombinant Insl6 in preclinical models has been shown to reduce CD4+ and CD8+ T cell infiltration into muscle, decrease inflammatory cytokine mRNA levels, and improve motor performance on Rotarod and treadmill tests, demonstrating its potential as a disease-modifying therapy for autoimmune myopathies.

### Insl6-Fc Fusion Proteins

The invention further provides Insl6-Fc fusion proteins as a preferred class of therapeutic agents for the treatment of autoimmune and immune-mediated diseases. These fusion proteins are engineered chimeric molecules comprising the full-length or functional domain of the Insl6 polypeptide fused to the constant region (Fc) of an immunoglobulin, preferably the human IgG1 Fc domain, which confers several pharmacological advantages over the native Insl6 peptide. The Fc domain enhances serum half-life by engaging the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), which protects the fusion protein from lysosomal degradation and facilitates its recycling into the systemic circulation. In addition, the Fc domain promotes dimerization of the fusion protein, which may enhance receptor binding avidity and biological potency. The fusion construct is designed such that the Insl6 moiety is positioned at the N-terminus of the Fc domain, preceded by a flexible linker sequence such as (G4S)3 to ensure proper folding and accessibility of both domains. The fusion protein is expressed in mammalian cell systems, such as CHO or HEK293 cells, to ensure correct disulfide bond formation, glycosylation, and secretion. The expressed protein is purified from culture supernatants using protein A affinity chromatography, followed by ion-exchange and size-exclusion chromatography to achieve homogeneity. The resulting Insl6-Fc fusion protein migrates as a dimeric species under non-reducing conditions on SDS-PAGE, with an apparent molecular weight of approximately 75 kDa, consistent with the expected size of the fusion construct. The biological activity of the fusion protein is confirmed by its ability to inhibit Jurkat T cell proliferation and suppress interleukin-2 secretion in vitro, with an IC50 value of less than 10 nM. In vivo, the fusion protein demonstrates superior pharmacokinetics compared to native Insl6, with a serum half-life exceeding 48 hours in mice following intravenous administration, compared to less than 6 hours for the unfused peptide. Hydrodynamic delivery of a plasmid encoding the Insl6-Fc fusion protein results in sustained serum levels of the fusion protein for up to two weeks, leading to marked suppression of inflammatory markers in muscle tissue and significant improvement in motor function in murine models of experimental autoimmune myositis. The fusion protein is further shown to reduce the infiltration of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells into skeletal muscle, decrease mRNA expression of tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interferon-gamma, and improve muscle fiber cross-sectional area and regenerative capacity. The invention includes multiple variants of the Insl6-Fc fusion protein, including those incorporating mutations in the Fc domain to modulate effector functions. For example, the fusion protein may contain the L234A/L235A mutations to ablate Fcγ receptor binding and prevent antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, thereby minimizing potential inflammatory side effects. Alternatively, the fusion protein may contain the N297A mutation to eliminate Fc glycosylation and reduce immunogenicity. The invention also encompasses fusion proteins wherein the Fc domain is derived from other immunoglobulin isotypes, such as IgG2, IgG4, or IgA, to tailor pharmacokinetic or immunomodulatory properties. The Insl6-Fc fusion protein may be administered as a single-agent therapy or in combination with other immunomodulatory agents, such as low-dose corticosteroids or regulatory T cell enhancers. The fusion protein is formulated in sterile, isotonic, buffered solutions containing stabilizers such as human serum albumin, sucrose, or trehalose, and is suitable for intravenous, subcutaneous, or intramuscular administration. The invention further includes lyophilized formulations of the Insl6-Fc fusion protein for long-term storage and reconstitution prior to use. The therapeutic efficacy of the fusion protein has been demonstrated in multiple animal models, including Insl6-deficient mice and wild-type mice subjected to experimental autoimmune myositis, where administration of the fusion protein resulted in a dose-dependent reduction in disease severity, with maximal efficacy observed at doses of 1–5 mg/kg administered weekly. The fusion protein is further shown to be non-immunogenic in preclinical studies, with no detectable anti-drug antibody response following repeated dosing over a four-week period. The Insl6-Fc fusion protein represents a clinically viable, high-potency, long-acting therapeutic agent for the treatment of autoimmune myopathies and related immune-mediated conditions.

### Methods to Treat Autoimmune Diseases and Immune-Related Diseases and Disorders

The invention provides novel methods for the treatment of autoimmune diseases and immune-related disorders, particularly those characterized by chronic inflammation and muscle degeneration, through the administration of an effective amount of an Insl6-containing agent. The method comprises identifying a subject in need of treatment, such as a human patient diagnosed with polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion body myositis, or another immune-mediated myopathy, and administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an Insl6 polypeptide, an Insl6-Fc fusion protein, or a nucleic acid encoding Insl6. The method is based on the discovery that Insl6 suppresses T cell activation, reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, and promotes muscle regeneration, thereby addressing the core pathological features of these diseases. In one embodiment, the method involves the systemic administration of recombinant Insl6 or Insl6-Fc fusion protein via intravenous, subcutaneous, or intramuscular injection, with dosing regimens ranging from once daily to once weekly, depending on the pharmacokinetic profile of the agent and the severity of disease. The dosage is selected to achieve a serum concentration of Insl6 or the fusion protein in the range of 1–500 ng/mL, as determined by ELISA or mass spectrometry, which has been shown in preclinical models to correlate with maximal suppression of CD4+ and CD8+ T cell infiltration and reduction in tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interferon-gamma mRNA levels in muscle tissue. The method further includes the administration of the agent prior to or concomitant with the onset of clinical symptoms, such as muscle weakness or elevated creatine kinase levels, to prevent disease progression. In another embodiment, the method involves the use of gene therapy to deliver an Insl6-encoding nucleic acid construct to the subject, such as via hydrodynamic injection, adeno-associated viral vector, or lipid nanoparticle, resulting in sustained endogenous expression of Insl6 in hepatocytes or muscle tissue. The gene therapy approach is particularly suited for patients with chronic, refractory disease who require long-term suppression of inflammation without repeated injections. The method further includes monitoring the subject for clinical improvement, including assessment of muscle strength using the Rotarod test, treadmill endurance, manual muscle testing, or quantitative muscle imaging, as well as biochemical markers such as serum creatine kinase and inflammatory cytokines. The method further includes the concomitant use of physical therapy or rehabilitation protocols to enhance functional recovery. The invention further encompasses the use of Insl6 agents in combination with other therapeutic modalities, such as corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, or biologics targeting specific cytokines, to achieve synergistic effects while allowing for dose reduction of agents with greater toxicity. The method is applicable to both pediatric and adult patients, including those with newly diagnosed disease, those with relapsing or refractory disease, and those who are intolerant to conventional therapies. The invention further provides methods for stratifying patients based on baseline Insl6 expression levels in muscle biopsy samples, wherein subjects with low or absent Insl6 mRNA are identified as optimal candidates for Insl6 replacement therapy. The method further includes the periodic re-evaluation of Insl6 expression levels during treatment to assess therapeutic response and guide dosing adjustments. The therapeutic benefit of the method is demonstrated by a sustained reduction in muscle inflammation, as evidenced by histological analysis showing decreased T cell infiltration and reduced expression of pro-inflammatory markers, improved muscle fiber integrity, and enhanced motor function. The method further provides for the prevention of disease progression in pre-symptomatic individuals at high genetic or immunological risk, such as those with autoantibodies associated with myositis or a family history of autoimmune disease. The method is further applicable to other immune-mediated conditions beyond myositis, including but not limited to inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and multiple sclerosis, where T cell dysregulation and tissue inflammation are central to pathogenesis. The invention thus provides a broadly applicable therapeutic strategy for immune-mediated diseases by leveraging the endogenous regulatory function of Insl6 to restore immune-muscle homeostasis.

### Administration of Pharmaceutical Compositions

The invention provides detailed methods for the administration of pharmaceutical compositions comprising Insl6 or Insl6-Fc fusion proteins to subjects suffering from autoimmune or immune-related diseases. The compositions may be administered via a variety of routes, including intravenous, subcutaneous, intramuscular, intra-articular, or intrathecal injection, depending on the nature of the disease, the pharmacokinetic properties of the agent, and the desired site of action. Intravenous administration is preferred for systemic delivery, particularly when rapid distribution and high bioavailability are required, such as in acute exacerbations of myositis or during the initial phase of treatment. Subcutaneous administration is preferred for chronic, maintenance therapy due to its convenience, reduced risk of infection, and favorable pharmacokinetic profile. Intramuscular administration may be used to target local inflammation in specific muscle groups, particularly in cases of focal myositis or when systemic exposure is to be minimized. The pharmaceutical composition is formulated as a sterile, isotonic, pyrogen-free solution suitable for parenteral use, with a pH ranging from 5.5 to 7.5, and containing a buffer system such as phosphate-buffered saline, acetate buffer, or HEPES to maintain stability. The composition may further include stabilizing agents such as human serum albumin, sucrose, trehalose, or polysorbate 80 to prevent aggregation and adsorption to container surfaces. The concentration of the active agent in the formulation may range from 0.1 mg/mL to 100 mg/mL, depending on the intended dose and volume of administration. For intravenous administration, the composition is typically infused over a period of 15 to 60 minutes using a sterile infusion set, with the total volume not exceeding 250 mL per administration. For subcutaneous administration, the volume is limited to 1–2 mL per injection site, with multiple sites used if higher doses are required. The dosing regimen is determined based on the pharmacokinetic profile of the agent, the severity of disease, and the patient’s body weight, with a typical starting dose of 0.1 to 5 mg/kg administered weekly, which may be adjusted upward or downward based on clinical response and tolerability. In the case of gene therapy, the nucleic acid construct encoding Insl6 is administered via hydrodynamic injection, wherein a volume of saline equal to 8–10% of the subject’s body weight is rapidly injected into the tail vein over a period of 5 to 10 seconds, resulting in transient transfection of hepatocytes and sustained secretion of Insl6 into the circulation. Alternative gene delivery methods include adeno-associated viral vectors, such as AAV8 or AAV9, which exhibit tropism for skeletal muscle and liver, and are administered via intravenous or intramuscular injection at a viral titer of 1×10^11 to 1×10^13 genome copies per kilogram. Lipid nanoparticle formulations containing Insl6 mRNA are administered intravenously at a dose of 0.1 to 1.0 mg/kg, with repeat dosing every 2 to 4 weeks. The administration of the pharmaceutical composition is performed under sterile conditions by trained medical personnel, and the subject is monitored for acute adverse reactions, including fever, hypotension, or allergic response, for a period of at least 30 minutes following administration. The method further includes the use of pre-medication with antihistamines or corticosteroids in patients with a history of hypersensitivity reactions. The invention further provides for the use of implantable drug delivery devices, such as osmotic pumps or biodegradable polymer matrices, for continuous, controlled release of Insl6 over extended periods, ranging from weeks to months. The method further includes the co-administration of agents that enhance the stability or bioavailability of Insl6, such as protease inhibitors or albumin-binding domains. The invention further encompasses the use of inhalation or transdermal delivery systems for localized or systemic effects, although these routes are less preferred due to the size and stability constraints of the protein. The administration protocol is tailored to the individual patient based on disease subtype, age, comorbidities, and prior treatment history, with the goal of achieving sustained therapeutic levels of Insl6 in serum and muscle tissue to suppress inflammation and promote regeneration. The method further includes the collection of serial blood and muscle biopsy samples to monitor pharmacodynamic endpoints, including serum Insl6 concentration, muscle Insl6 mRNA levels, T cell infiltration, and cytokine expression, to guide personalized dosing and optimize therapeutic outcomes.

### Gene Therapy

The invention provides novel gene therapy approaches for the sustained delivery of Insulin-like 6 (Insl6) to subjects suffering from autoimmune and immune-related diseases, particularly those involving chronic muscle inflammation. Gene therapy offers a compelling alternative to repeated protein administration by enabling endogenous, long-term expression of Insl6 from transfected cells, thereby maintaining therapeutic concentrations without the need for frequent injections. The method involves the delivery of a nucleic acid construct encoding Insl6 into the cells of a subject, such that the encoded polypeptide is synthesized, processed, and secreted into the systemic circulation or local tissue microenvironment. The nucleic acid construct may be in the form of plasmid DNA, viral vectors, or messenger RNA, each of which offers distinct advantages in terms of transduction efficiency, duration of expression, and safety profile. In one embodiment, the gene therapy construct comprises a plasmid DNA vector encoding the full-length human or murine Insl6 cDNA under the control of a strong, constitutive promoter such as the cytomegalovirus immediate-early promoter or the elongation factor 1-alpha promoter, linked to a polyadenylation signal and a bacterial origin of replication. The plasmid is administered via hydrodynamic injection, wherein a large volume of saline solution (8–10% of body weight) containing 10–50 μg of plasmid DNA is rapidly injected into the tail vein of a mouse or the peripheral vein of a human subject over a period of 5–10 seconds. This method results in transient transfection of hepatocytes, which secrete Insl6 into the bloodstream, achieving serum concentrations of up to 1,800 ng/mL for a period of 7–14 days. In another embodiment, the gene therapy construct is packaged into a recombinant adeno-associated viral vector, preferably serotype AAV8 or AAV9, which exhibit high tropism for skeletal muscle and liver. The viral vector contains an Insl6 expression cassette under the control of a muscle-specific promoter such as the human skeletal muscle actin promoter or the creatine kinase M promoter, ensuring targeted expression in muscle tissue and minimizing off-target effects. The vector is administered intravenously at a dose of 1×10^11 to 1×10^13 vector genomes per kilogram, resulting in persistent transgene expression for months to years. In a further embodiment, the gene therapy construct comprises modified Insl6 mRNA encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles, which are administered intravenously at a dose of 0.1–1.0 mg/kg. The mRNA is engineered with 5’ and 3’ untranslated regions optimized for translation efficiency, a 5’ cap structure, and a poly(A) tail of 100–150 nucleotides to enhance stability and protein yield. The lipid nanoparticles are formulated to protect the mRNA from degradation and facilitate endosomal escape, resulting in efficient cytoplasmic delivery and sustained Insl6 expression for up to two weeks following a single dose. The gene therapy construct may further include regulatory elements such as microRNA target sequences to limit expression in non-target tissues or suicide genes to allow for termination of expression if adverse effects occur. The method further includes the use of inducible promoters, such as the tetracycline-responsive element, to enable temporal control of Insl6 expression in response to clinical need. The gene therapy approach is particularly advantageous for patients with refractory disease, those who develop neutralizing antibodies against recombinant protein, or those requiring lifelong therapy. Preclinical studies demonstrate that gene therapy with Insl6 leads to sustained suppression of CD4+ and CD8+ T cell infiltration, reduced expression of tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interferon-gamma in muscle tissue, and significant improvement in motor function on Rotarod and treadmill tests. The method further includes the monitoring of transgene expression using quantitative PCR or ELISA, and the assessment of potential immune responses to the vector or transgene product. The invention further provides for the use of CRISPR-based gene activation systems to upregulate endogenous INSL6 expression in patient-derived cells, thereby restoring physiological levels of the protein without introducing exogenous genetic material. The gene therapy compositions are manufactured under current good manufacturing practices and are formulated for sterile, single-dose administration. The method of gene therapy represents a transformative approach to the treatment of autoimmune myopathies, offering the potential for durable remission, reduced treatment burden, and improved quality of life for patients.

The invention provides novel kits for the diagnosis and treatment of autoimmune and immune-related diseases, particularly those involving skeletal muscle inflammation, such as polymyositis and dermatomyositis. Each kit comprises at least one therapeutic component and one diagnostic component, designed to enable personalized, biomarker-guided therapeutic intervention. The therapeutic component comprises a pharmaceutical composition containing an effective amount of an Insl6-containing agent, such as recombinant Insl6 polypeptide, Insl6-Fc fusion protein, plasmid DNA encoding Insl6, or lipid nanoparticle-encapsulated Insl6 mRNA, packaged in a sterile, single-use container suitable for parenteral administration. The diagnostic component comprises a set of reagents for measuring Insl6 expression levels in a biological sample obtained from the subject, such as a muscle biopsy, blood, or serum. The diagnostic reagents may include primers and probes for quantitative real-time PCR to detect INSL6 mRNA transcripts, antibodies for immunohistochemistry or Western blotting to detect Insl6 protein in tissue sections, or an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kit for quantifying Insl6 concentration in serum. The kit further includes instructions for use, detailing the procedures for sample collection, processing, and analysis, as well as guidelines for interpreting the results in the context of disease severity and therapeutic eligibility. In one embodiment, the kit includes a biopsy needle, sterile collection tubes, RNA stabilization buffer, and a pre-labeled slide for histological analysis, along with a pre-filled syringe containing the Insl6-Fc fusion protein and a detailed dosing algorithm based on the subject’s Insl6 expression level, body weight, and disease duration. In another embodiment, the kit includes a portable device for rapid point-of-care measurement of Insl6 mRNA in a fresh muscle biopsy sample using reverse transcription loop-mediated isothermal amplification, with results available within one hour. The kit further includes educational materials for clinicians, including flowcharts for patient selection, dosing recommendations, and monitoring protocols for therapeutic response. The kit may further include a control sample, such as a positive control for Insl6 detection or a negative control for background signal, to ensure assay accuracy. The kit is designed for use in clinical settings, research laboratories, or home-based care environments, and is labeled with a unique identifier for traceability and regulatory compliance. The invention further provides for the development of companion diagnostic tests that correlate Insl6 expression levels with clinical outcomes, enabling the identification of patients most likely to benefit from Insl6-based therapy. The kit further includes a patient diary or mobile application for tracking symptoms, medication adherence, and functional performance over time, with data upload capabilities for remote monitoring by healthcare providers. The kits of the invention facilitate a precision medicine approach to the treatment of autoimmune myopathies, ensuring that therapy is administered only to those patients whose disease is characterized by insufficient endogenous Insl6 expression, thereby maximizing therapeutic efficacy and minimizing unnecessary treatment. The kits are packaged in a temperature-controlled container and stored at 2–8°C, with the therapeutic component protected from light and the diagnostic components stored separately to maintain stability. The invention thus provides a comprehensive, integrated solution for the diagnosis, treatment, and monitoring of autoimmune muscle diseases, representing a significant advancement in the clinical management of these debilitating conditions.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

This example demonstrates the therapeutic efficacy of Insl6-Fc fusion protein in a murine model of experimental autoimmune myositis. Female C57BL/6 mice, eight to ten weeks of age, were immunized intradermally with 600 μg of recombinant human fast-type skeletal muscle myosin-binding protein C emulsified in complete Freund’s adjuvant containing 100 μg of heat-killed Mycobacterium butyricum, followed by intraperitoneal injection of 2 μg of pertussis toxin to enhance immune activation. Mice were divided into four treatment groups: (1) vehicle control (saline), (2) Insl6-Fc fusion protein at 1 mg/kg, (3) Insl6-Fc fusion protein at 5 mg/kg, and (4) a control Fc fusion protein lacking Insl6 (Fc-only) at 5 mg/kg. Treatment was initiated on the day of immunization and repeated weekly for a total of three doses. At 14 days post-immunization, mice were evaluated for motor function using the Rotarod and treadmill tests. On the Rotarod, mice were placed on a rotating cylinder accelerating from 4 to 40 revolutions per minute over five minutes, and latency to fall was recorded. Mice treated with the 5 mg/kg dose of Insl6-Fc exhibited a 65% improvement in latency to fall compared to vehicle controls (p < 0.001), while mice treated with the 1 mg/kg dose showed a 40% improvement (p < 0.01). Mice treated with the Fc-only control showed no significant improvement compared to vehicle. On the treadmill test, mice ran on a horizontal belt starting at 10 m/min and increasing by 5 m/min every five minutes until exhaustion. Mice receiving 5 mg/kg Insl6-Fc ran 2.5-fold farther than vehicle controls (p < 0.001), whereas mice receiving 1 mg/kg ran 1.8-fold farther (p < 0.01). Histological analysis of gastrocnemius and tibialis anterior muscles revealed a marked reduction in inflammatory cell infiltration in Insl6-Fc-treated mice. Hematoxylin and eosin staining showed a 70% reduction in inflammatory foci in the 5 mg/kg group compared to vehicle (p < 0.001). Immunofluorescence staining for CD4 and CD8 T cells demonstrated a 60–70% reduction in infiltrating cells in the perimysial and perivascular regions of Insl6-Fc-treated mice (p < 0.001). Quantitative real-time PCR of muscle tissue showed that transcript levels of CD4, CD8, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and interferon-gamma were reduced by 50–80% in the 5 mg/kg Insl6-Fc group compared to vehicle controls (all p < 0.01). Serum levels of the Insl6-Fc fusion protein were measured by human Fc ELISA and were found to be sustained at 800–1,200 ng/mL throughout the treatment period. No significant changes in body weight, liver enzymes, or hematologic parameters were observed in any group, indicating a favorable safety profile. In a separate cohort, mice were treated with a single intravenous dose of 5 mg/kg Insl6-Fc at day 7 post-immunization, and motor function and inflammation were assessed at day 14. These mice showed comparable improvements in Rotarod performance and T cell infiltration, demonstrating that therapeutic benefit can be achieved even after disease onset. No anti-drug antibodies against the Insl6-Fc fusion protein were detected in any treated mouse at the end of the study. These results establish that systemic administration of Insl6-Fc fusion protein significantly ameliorates disease severity in a murine model of autoimmune myositis by suppressing T cell infiltration and inflammatory cytokine expression, while improving muscle function, without inducing systemic toxicity or immunogenicity. The data support the clinical development of Insl6-Fc as a novel, targeted therapy for human polymyositis and dermatomyositis.
