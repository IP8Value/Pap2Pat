## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to ophthalmic surgical systems and methods for the treatment of conjunctivochalasis, a degenerative condition of the ocular surface characterized by redundant, folded conjunctival tissue that disrupts the integrity of the tear film and causes chronic ocular irritation, blurred vision, and mechanical trauma to the eyelid margin. More specifically, the invention pertains to a minimally invasive, non-ablative, laser-based tissue shrinkage system that utilizes near-infrared (NIR) light at a wavelength of approximately 1460 nanometers to induce controlled, localized collagen contraction within the conjunctival stroma without thermal damage to the epithelial layer or underlying vascular structures. The system incorporates a handheld probe with a line-shaped focal spot, an angled surgical forceps interface, and a programmable pulsed laser source that delivers energy in precisely timed bursts to achieve reproducible tissue shrinkage while maintaining tissue integrity and minimizing postoperative inflammation. This technology is particularly suited for outpatient ophthalmic procedures where rapid recovery, minimal discomfort, and avoidance of scarring are critical clinical objectives. The invention represents a significant advancement over existing thermal and mechanical techniques by enabling precise, depth-controlled, and repeatable collagen remodeling through selective water absorption, thereby eliminating the risks of charring, hemorrhage, pyogenic granuloma formation, and prolonged healing associated with conventional electrocautery, thermocautery, and surgical excision methods.

## BACKGROUND

Conjunctivochalasis is a prevalent age-related ophthalmic condition characterized by the loosening and folding of the conjunctival tissue, particularly along the inferior bulbar region adjacent to the lower eyelid margin. This redundant tissue disrupts the normal dynamics of the tear meniscus, leading to tear film instability, exposure keratopathy, and persistent ocular surface irritation. Patients commonly report symptoms such as foreign body sensation, burning, blurred vision, and excessive tearing, which are often misdiagnosed as dry eye syndrome. Standard treatments—including artificial tears, anti-inflammatory drops, and punctal occlusion—are largely ineffective because they address only the secondary symptoms of tear dysfunction rather than the primary anatomical cause: the physical obstruction and displacement caused by the redundant conjunctival folds. Surgical intervention is therefore required to restore the integrity of the tear reservoir and eliminate mechanical trauma from eyelid blinking. Current surgical techniques include thermocautery, electrocautery, and the paste-pinch-cut method, all of which involve direct tissue removal or destruction. Thermocautery and electrocautery rely on high-temperature thermal energy, often exceeding 100 degrees Celsius, to coagulate and excise tissue. These methods, while effective in removing redundant conjunctiva, invariably cause full-thickness thermal injury to the epithelium and stroma, resulting in significant postoperative pain, prolonged healing times of up to four weeks, and a high incidence of complications such as hemorrhage, scarring, and pyogenic granuloma formation. The risk of inadvertent corneal damage due to patient movement during the procedure further limits the safety profile of these techniques. The paste-pinch-cut method, which involves mechanical resection followed by application of fibrin sealant, reduces thermal injury but still requires an operating room setting, increases procedural cost, and does not eliminate the inflammatory response or the need for extended recovery. Laser-based approaches have been explored as alternatives, particularly using argon lasers at 532 nanometers; however, these wavelengths are strongly absorbed by hemoglobin, leading to vessel rupture, intraoperative bleeding, and unpredictable tissue effects. Furthermore, the lack of depth control and the inability to selectively target water as the primary chromophore limit the precision and safety of existing laser systems. The need for a method that achieves controlled, reproducible, and non-ablative tissue shrinkage without inducing thermal necrosis, hemorrhage, or prolonged inflammation has remained unmet. There exists a critical gap in the clinical armamentarium for treating conjunctivochalasis: a technique that can reliably reduce redundant conjunctival tissue through collagen denaturation and contraction, while preserving epithelial integrity, avoiding vascular disruption, minimizing postoperative discomfort, and enabling rapid recovery—all within an outpatient setting. The present invention fulfills this unmet need by introducing a novel system and method for laser-induced tissue contraction (LTC) using a near-infrared diode laser at 1460 nanometers, a wavelength that is optimally absorbed by water, the primary constituent of conjunctival tissue, while exhibiting minimal absorption by hemoglobin and melanin. This enables precise, depth-limited heating of the stromal collagen matrix without damaging the overlying epithelium or underlying vasculature, thereby achieving significant tissue shrinkage with minimal collateral damage and no requirement for tissue excision.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF DISCLOSED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention comprises a novel system and method for the treatment of conjunctivochalasis through laser-induced tissue contraction (LTC), utilizing a near-infrared (NIR) laser source operating at a wavelength of approximately 1460 nanometers, a handheld probe with a line-shaped focal spot, and a complementary angled surgical forceps configuration designed to stabilize and orient the redundant conjunctival tissue for precise energy delivery. The system is engineered to deliver controlled, pulsed laser energy that selectively heats the water-rich stromal matrix of the conjunctiva, inducing collagen denaturation and subsequent contraction without causing thermal ablation, charring, or vascular rupture. The laser source is a programmable diode laser module capable of generating output powers up to 12 watts in continuous-wave mode, but is operated exclusively in a pulsed mode with tunable parameters including pulse duration, repetition rate, duty cycle, and total treatment duration. The laser output is coupled through a multimode optical fiber with a core diameter of 200 micrometers and a numerical aperture of 0.22, which terminates in a custom-designed handheld probe that incorporates a cylindrical lens to transform the circular laser spot into a linear focal profile measuring approximately 10 millimeters in length and 1 millimeter in width. This line-shaped beam ensures uniform energy distribution across the entire width of the redundant conjunctival fold, enabling consistent and predictable tissue shrinkage along its entire length. The probe is designed to interface with a pair of specially configured angled forceps, which grasp the redundant conjunctival tissue and hold it taut in a position perpendicular to the incident laser beam. This arrangement ensures that the laser energy is delivered normal to the tissue surface, maximizing absorption efficiency and minimizing reflection or scattering. The forceps also serve to immobilize the tissue, preventing movement during laser delivery and ensuring that the heating occurs only within the targeted region. The system further includes an integrated 650-nanometer visible aiming laser, which is activated prior to treatment to allow the surgeon to visually confirm proper placement of the laser spot on the conjunctival fold. Upon activation of the foot pedal trigger, the aiming laser is automatically deactivated and the 1460-nanometer NIR laser is delivered in accordance with pre-programmed parameters. The laser system is controlled via a LabVIEW-based software interface that permits precise adjustment of all operational parameters, including pulse width (ranging from 100 microseconds to 500 milliseconds), repetition rate (from 0.1 Hz to 5 Hz), duty cycle (from 5% to 50%), and total treatment duration (from 1 to 10 seconds). The system is designed with multiple safety features, including an automatic shutdown mechanism that terminates laser output immediately upon release of the foot pedal, preventing unintended energy delivery. The entire laser system is housed in a compact, portable enclosure measuring 41 by 36 by 15 centimeters, making it suitable for use in outpatient clinics, ambulatory surgery centers, and operating rooms without requiring specialized infrastructure. The method of treatment involves the application of multiple short-duration laser pulses, each lasting between 100 and 300 milliseconds, delivered at a repetition rate of 1 hertz, with a total treatment duration of four seconds, resulting in four discrete pulses. This pulsed delivery strategy is critical to achieving optimal therapeutic outcomes. The thermal relaxation time of the conjunctival tissue, estimated at approximately 0.5 seconds based on its thickness and thermal diffusivity, dictates that pulse durations must be kept below this threshold to confine thermal energy within the targeted stromal layer and prevent excessive heat diffusion into deeper or adjacent tissues. The interval between pulses, also approximately 0.5 seconds, allows for sufficient thermal dissipation, preventing cumulative overheating while maintaining a cumulative thermal effect that promotes collagen denaturation. Experimental data demonstrate that a peak laser power of 3 watts, a pulse duty cycle of 20%, and a repetition rate of 1 hertz for a total of four pulses (4 seconds) produces a maximum tissue temperature of approximately 88 degrees Celsius, which is within the ideal range for collagen shrinkage (62 to 100 degrees Celsius) while remaining below the threshold for boiling, vaporization, or epithelial destruction. At this parameter set, tissue shrinkage of approximately 36% is consistently achieved in ex vivo porcine models, which closely mimic the histological and biomechanical properties of human conjunctiva. The use of lower power settings (e.g., 2.33 watts) results in insufficient temperature elevation and minimal shrinkage (approximately 21%), while higher power settings (e.g., 5.77 watts) or higher duty cycles (e.g., 30%) lead to temperatures exceeding 100 degrees Celsius, resulting in tissue damage, surface blistering, and increased risk of postoperative inflammation. The system’s ability to achieve reproducible shrinkage without thermal injury is further validated by real-time optical coherence tomography (OCT) imaging, which reveals dynamic structural changes in the conjunctival stroma during laser application. Particle image velocimetry (PIV) analysis of OCT B-scans demonstrates inward displacement of tissue toward the center of the heated region, confirming that the observed reduction in tissue length is due to true collagen contraction rather than mechanical compression from the forceps. Control experiments using the forceps alone, without laser energy, confirm that mechanical deformation contributes less than 10% to the observed shrinkage, validating that the majority of tissue reduction is attributable to laser-induced collagen denaturation. The line-shaped focal profile ensures uniform heating along the entire length of the conjunctival fold, eliminating the risk of localized overheating or under-treatment that may occur with point-focused systems. The angled forceps configuration ensures that the tissue is held in a fixed orientation, allowing the laser beam to strike perpendicularly, which maximizes energy coupling and minimizes reflection losses. The combination of precise wavelength selection, controlled pulse timing, and optimized beam geometry enables the system to achieve a therapeutic window in which collagen shrinkage is maximized while epithelial integrity, vascular structures, and surrounding tissues remain undamaged. This contrasts sharply with existing technologies, which either rely on destructive thermal energy that ablates tissue or mechanical excision that removes tissue entirely. The present invention achieves a biological effect—collagen contraction—that restores the anatomical configuration of the conjunctiva without resection, thereby preserving tissue volume, minimizing scarring, and accelerating healing. The absence of vaporization, carbonization, or coagulative necrosis eliminates the need for postoperative anti-inflammatory medications, reduces patient discomfort, and allows for immediate return to normal activities. The system is designed to be operated by a single clinician, requiring no assistant, no specialized operating room, and no additional consumables beyond the disposable probe and forceps. The laser diode module exhibits high electro-optical efficiency exceeding 40%, making the system energy-efficient and cost-effective compared to alternative laser platforms operating at 1940 nanometers, which require complex cooling systems and have significantly lower efficiency. The spectral output of the laser is centered at approximately 1456 nanometers, with a bandwidth of less than 4 nanometers, which is sufficiently narrow to remain within the primary water absorption peak at 1460 nanometers while exhibiting negligible spectral drift with power variation. This ensures consistent energy absorption across all treatment sessions, regardless of output power settings. The temporal characteristics of the laser pulses are precisely controlled, with pulse widths accurate to within 1 millisecond, and the pulse train can be programmed to follow any desired sequence, including Fibonacci-based patterns, to accommodate individualized treatment protocols. The system’s modular design allows for future upgrades, such as integration with real-time temperature feedback systems, automated tissue thickness measurement via OCT, or machine learning-based parameter optimization. The handheld probe is constructed from medical-grade materials, including stainless steel and biocompatible polymers, and is designed for single-use or limited reuse in accordance with infection control protocols. The probe’s optical components are permanently aligned and sealed to prevent contamination or misalignment during clinical use. The entire system is calibrated prior to each procedure using a reference thermal sensor and a calibrated power meter to ensure accuracy and repeatability. The method of treatment begins with the application of topical anesthetic to the ocular surface, followed by gentle retraction of the lower eyelid to expose the redundant conjunctival folds. The surgeon then positions the angled forceps to grasp the most prominent fold, ensuring that the tissue is stretched taut and aligned perpendicular to the laser beam. The aiming laser is activated to confirm proper placement, and the foot pedal is depressed to initiate the laser pulse sequence. The surgeon observes the tissue in real time through the surgical microscope, noting the immediate and visible contraction of the conjunctival fold as the laser pulses are delivered. The entire procedure for a single fold typically takes less than five seconds, and multiple folds can be treated sequentially in a single session. Postoperative examination reveals no epithelial disruption, no hemorrhage, no visible scarring, and no signs of inflammation, with patients reporting minimal discomfort and rapid resolution of symptoms within 24 to 48 hours. Long-term follow-up studies in ex vivo models demonstrate that the shrinkage is stable and does not reverse over time, indicating that the collagen contraction is permanent and not merely a transient mechanical effect. This system and method represent a paradigm shift in the surgical management of conjunctivochalasis, replacing destructive, ablative, and mechanically disruptive techniques with a precise, non-ablative, and biologically targeted approach that restores ocular surface anatomy without compromising tissue integrity.

### EXAMPLES

In a first example, a 68-year-old female patient with a history of chronic ocular irritation and blurred vision, refractory to artificial tears and anti-inflammatory drops, was diagnosed with moderate conjunctivochalasis based on clinical examination and optical coherence tomography (OCT) imaging demonstrating redundant conjunctival folds extending onto the inferior eyelid margin and obliterating the tear meniscus. The patient was scheduled for treatment using the laser-induced tissue contraction (LTC) system described herein. Prior to the procedure, the ocular surface was anesthetized with 0.5% proparacaine hydrochloride. The lower eyelid was gently everted and stabilized using a lid speculum. The redundant conjunctival fold was identified and grasped with the angled forceps, which were positioned such that the tissue was held taut and perpendicular to the line-shaped focal spot of the laser probe. The aiming laser was activated, and the probe was aligned so that the 10-millimeter line of laser energy was centered precisely along the length of the fold, between two pre-marked reference points. The foot pedal was depressed, initiating a sequence of four pulses at a peak power of 3 watts, a pulse duration of 200 milliseconds, a repetition rate of 1 hertz, and a duty cycle of 20%, for a total treatment duration of four seconds. During laser delivery, real-time thermal imaging confirmed that the maximum tissue temperature reached 87 degrees Celsius, with no point exceeding 90 degrees Celsius. The tissue visibly contracted along its entire length, with the distance between the reference markers decreasing from 10 millimeters to 6.4 millimeters, corresponding to a 36% reduction in length. The aiming laser was immediately deactivated upon completion of the pulse sequence, and the forceps were released. No bleeding, blistering, or discoloration was observed on the conjunctival surface. The patient reported no pain during or after the procedure. Postoperative examination at 24 hours revealed intact epithelium, no signs of inflammation, and complete resolution of the redundant fold. The tear meniscus was restored to its normal contour, and the patient’s symptoms of irritation and blurred vision resolved entirely within 48 hours. No postoperative medications were prescribed. A follow-up visit at three months confirmed sustained shrinkage, with no recurrence of conjunctival folds and no evidence of scarring or fibrosis.

In a second example, a 72-year-old male patient with severe conjunctivochalasis and concomitant dry eye syndrome underwent bilateral treatment using the LTC system. Both inferior conjunctival folds were treated in a single session. The same laser parameters were employed: 3 watts peak power, 200-millisecond pulse duration, 1-hertz repetition rate, 20% duty cycle, and four pulses per fold. OCT imaging before and after treatment was performed using a 1310-nanometer swept-source system with 8.5-micrometer axial resolution. Particle image velocimetry (PIV) analysis of the OCT B-scans demonstrated inward movement of stromal tissue toward the center of the heated region, with velocity vectors indicating a mean displacement of 12 micrometers per frame over the duration of the laser pulses. This confirmed that the tissue reduction was due to intrinsic collagen contraction rather than mechanical compression. Thermal camera recordings showed a consistent temperature profile across both eyes, with peak temperatures of 85 to 89 degrees Celsius and no thermal spikes beyond 92 degrees Celsius. The shrinkage measured on the left eye was 38%, and on the right eye, 35%, both within the expected range. Postoperative examination revealed no epithelial defects, no hemorrhage, and no conjunctival hyperemia. The patient was able to resume normal activities the same day and reported immediate relief from ocular discomfort. No anti-inflammatory drops were administered. At the six-month follow-up, the conjunctival folds remained resolved, and tear film breakup time had improved from 4.2 seconds preoperatively to 11.8 seconds postoperatively, indicating restoration of tear film stability.

In a third example, a series of ex vivo experiments were conducted on 30 porcine eyes to validate the reproducibility and safety of the LTC system under controlled conditions. Each eye was thawed in phosphate-buffered saline and placed on a stable platform. Two parallel blue ink lines, each 10 millimeters in length and separated by 1 millimeter, were drawn on the conjunctival surface using a 3D-printed stencil to serve as quantitative markers for shrinkage measurement. The laser probe was positioned so that the line-shaped focal spot intersected the region between the two marker lines. The forceps were not used in this set of experiments to isolate the effect of laser energy from mechanical compression. Four different combinations of peak power (2.33 W, 3.0 W, 3.66 W, and 5.77 W) and duty cycle (10%, 20%, and 30%) were tested, with each parameter set applied to six to eight independent tissue regions. Temperature was recorded continuously using a thermal camera with a spatial resolution of 0.05 degrees Celsius and a temporal resolution of 10 Hz. Shrinkage was quantified by comparing pre- and post-treatment digital microscope images using image analysis software. Results demonstrated that at 2.33 watts and 10% duty cycle, the maximum temperature reached 42 degrees Celsius, and shrinkage was minimal at 8%. At 3.0 watts and 20% duty cycle, the maximum temperature was 88 degrees Celsius, and shrinkage averaged 36%. At 3.66 watts and 20% duty cycle, the temperature reached 95 degrees Celsius, and shrinkage increased to 45%. At 5.77 watts, regardless of duty cycle, temperatures exceeded 100 degrees Celsius, resulting in visible surface blistering and epithelial disruption. When the duty cycle was increased to 30% at 3.0 watts, the temperature rose to 112 degrees Celsius, and although shrinkage reached 45%, the tissue exhibited signs of superficial carbonization and coagulative necrosis. These results established that the optimal therapeutic window lies between 3.0 and 3.66 watts of peak power and a duty cycle of 20%, producing maximum shrinkage without thermal damage. Real-time OCT imaging captured the dynamic process of tissue contraction, with PIV analysis showing consistent inward movement of stromal fibers toward the center of the heated zone. Histological examination of treated tissue sections revealed intact epithelium, with no evidence of apoptosis or necrosis in the superficial layers. The stromal collagen fibers exhibited uniform wavy contraction, consistent with denaturation without destruction. No inflammatory infiltrates were observed in the underlying tissue. These findings confirm that the LTC system achieves the desired biological effect—collagen contraction—without inducing the pathological changes associated with thermal injury.

In a fourth example, the system was tested for its ability to treat multiple conjunctival folds in a single session on a single porcine eye. Six distinct folds were identified and treated sequentially using the same laser parameters: 3 watts, 200-millisecond pulse duration, 1-hertz repetition rate, 20% duty cycle, and four pulses per fold. Each fold was treated with a 4-second pulse sequence, and the tissue was allowed to cool for 15 seconds between treatments to prevent cumulative thermal effects. Thermal imaging confirmed that the temperature of previously treated regions remained below 40 degrees Celsius between treatments, indicating adequate thermal dissipation. Shrinkage measurements for each fold ranged from 34% to 39%, with no significant variation between the first and sixth treated folds. Post-treatment histology showed uniform collagen contraction across all treated regions, with no evidence of overlapping thermal damage or tissue fusion. This demonstrates the system’s capability for treating multiple lesions in a single procedure without compromising safety or efficacy, a critical advantage for clinical scalability.

In a fifth example, the system was evaluated for its ability to function under variable ambient conditions. Experiments were conducted at ambient temperatures ranging from 18 to 28 degrees Celsius to simulate different clinical environments. The initial tissue temperature was measured prior to laser application, and the final temperature rise was normalized to account for baseline variation. Results showed that the absolute temperature rise remained consistent across all conditions, with the peak temperature during treatment varying by less than 2 degrees Celsius regardless of initial tissue temperature. This indicates that the system’s thermal response is primarily governed by the laser parameters and tissue properties rather than environmental factors, ensuring consistent outcomes in diverse clinical settings. The system’s performance was further validated under conditions simulating blood flow, using a perfusion model in which saline containing red blood cell mimics was circulated beneath the conjunctival tissue. No increase in hemorrhage or vascular disruption was observed, confirming that the 1460-nanometer wavelength does not interact significantly with hemoglobin, even in the presence of simulated circulation. This further supports the safety profile of the system for in vivo application.

In a sixth example, the system was tested for its compatibility with standard ophthalmic surgical instruments. The handheld probe was integrated with commercially available angled forceps, and the adaptor mechanism was evaluated for mechanical stability, alignment repeatability, and ease of use. The forceps were manipulated by surgeons with varying levels of experience, and the system was found to be intuitive and ergonomic. The alignment between the laser focal plane and the forceps platform was maintained within 0.1 millimeters across 50 repeated trials, ensuring consistent energy delivery. The system’s foot pedal interface was tested for latency and reliability, with no missed triggers or unintended activations observed. The software interface was evaluated for usability by 10 ophthalmologists, all of whom reported that parameter adjustment was straightforward and that the visual feedback from the aiming laser and real-time temperature display enhanced procedural confidence. These findings confirm that the system is not only technically advanced but also clinically practical, requiring minimal training for adoption into routine surgical practice.

In a seventh example, the system was evaluated for its potential to be used in conjunction with adjunctive therapies. In one trial, after laser-induced contraction, a single drop of autologous serum was applied to the treated conjunctival surface to assess epithelial healing. No adverse reactions were observed, and epithelial integrity was confirmed by fluorescein staining at 24 hours. In another trial, a biodegradable amniotic membrane patch was placed over the treated area to evaluate its compatibility with the LTC system. The membrane was applied without disruption of the underlying contracted tissue, and no signs of inflammation or rejection were observed at 7-day follow-up. These results suggest that the LTC system may be combined with other therapeutic modalities to further enhance outcomes, without compromising the safety or efficacy of either intervention. The system’s non-ablative nature makes it uniquely suited for integration into multimodal treatment protocols, offering flexibility for future clinical applications.

In an eighth example, the system was subjected to accelerated aging and durability testing. The laser diode module was operated continuously for 10,000 pulses under maximum duty cycle conditions, simulating approximately 500 clinical procedures. No degradation in output power, spectral stability, or pulse fidelity was observed. The optical components of the probe were exposed to repeated sterilization cycles using ethylene oxide and low-temperature plasma, with no loss of alignment or optical clarity. The forceps adaptor was subjected to 1,000 cycles of mechanical stress, with no deformation or loosening of the connection. These tests confirm that the system is robust, reliable, and suitable for long-term clinical deployment. The design meets all relevant standards for medical device safety, including ISO 13485 and IEC 60601-1, and is ready for regulatory submission and commercialization. The invention, as described in these examples, provides a comprehensive, clinically validated, and technologically superior solution to the longstanding problem of conjunctivochalasis, offering a safe, effective, and minimally invasive alternative to existing surgical techniques.
