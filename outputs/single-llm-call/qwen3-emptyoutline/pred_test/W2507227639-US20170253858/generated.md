# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and cardiac tissue engineering, specifically to methods and compositions for the maturation of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes using three-dimensional scaffolds composed of decellularized adult cardiac extracellular matrix. The invention provides a novel, physiologically relevant microenvironment that promotes the structural, functional, and electrophysiological maturation of human cardiomyocytes derived from induced pluripotent stem cells, thereby enabling their safe and effective use in therapeutic applications for myocardial repair, pharmacological screening, disease modeling, and cardiotoxicity assessment. The invention further encompasses the preparation, characterization, and application of hydrogels derived from decellularized adult human or non-human cardiac tissue, which exhibit mechanical and biochemical properties that closely mimic the native adult myocardial extracellular matrix, thereby inducing a maturation phenotype in cardiomyocytes that is significantly more advanced than that achieved through conventional two-dimensional culture systems or synthetic or single-protein matrices.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of mortality in developed nations, with myocardial infarction accounting for a substantial proportion of deaths due to the irreversible loss of functional cardiomyocytes. Unlike many other tissues, the adult human heart possesses an extremely limited capacity for endogenous regeneration, as cardiomyocytes exhibit minimal proliferative activity after birth. The death of millions to billions of these cells during ischemic injury leads to the formation of non-contractile fibrotic scar tissue, which compromises cardiac contractility, disrupts electrical conduction, and predisposes the organ to arrhythmias and progressive heart failure. Despite decades of research, no pharmacological agent has been approved that can restore lost myocardial tissue or stimulate meaningful regeneration of functional cardiomyocytes in vivo. Consequently, cell-based therapies have emerged as a promising strategy to replace damaged myocardium, with induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes offering a theoretically unlimited, patient-specific source of transplantable cells.

However, a major barrier to the clinical translation of such therapies lies in the immaturity of cardiomyocytes generated through current differentiation protocols. These cells, while expressing canonical cardiac markers such as troponin T and myosin light chain 2, retain a fetal-like phenotype characterized by disorganized sarcomeres, spontaneous automaticity, immature calcium handling, weak contractile force, aberrant electrophysiological properties, and altered responses to pharmacological agents. This immaturity renders them unsuitable for therapeutic transplantation, as their uncontrolled beating can induce lethal arrhythmias, and their poor integration with host tissue limits functional recovery. Furthermore, their use in drug screening and toxicity testing yields unreliable data, as their responses to beta-adrenergic agonists, calcium modulators, and other cardiac drugs diverge significantly from those of adult human cardiomyocytes, leading to false positives and false negatives in preclinical evaluations.

Numerous approaches have been explored to overcome this limitation, including prolonged culture durations, metabolic manipulation, hormonal stimulation with triiodothyronine, mechanical stretching, electrical pacing, co-culture with non-cardiomyocyte cell types, and genetic overexpression of ion channels or transcription factors. While some of these methods have demonstrated modest improvements in maturation markers, none have fully recapitulated the structural and functional complexity of adult cardiomyocytes. A critical shortcoming of most of these strategies is their reliance on two-dimensional culture systems, which fail to replicate the three-dimensional architecture, biomechanical cues, and biochemical composition of the native myocardial extracellular matrix. The extracellular matrix is not merely a passive scaffold but an active signaling platform that regulates cell adhesion, migration, polarization, differentiation, and functional maturation through integrin-mediated mechanotransduction, topographical guidance, and the presentation of bioactive ligands.

Recent studies have demonstrated that decellularized cardiac extracellular matrix—derived from the removal of cellular components from native heart tissue while preserving the structural proteins, glycosaminoglycans, and growth factors—can support cardiomyocyte attachment and survival. However, prior efforts have been limited by the use of fetal or non-human matrices, two-dimensional coatings, or synthetic matrices composed of single proteins such as collagen I or fibrin, none of which adequately mimic the mechanical stiffness, fiber organization, or molecular complexity of the adult human myocardium. Moreover, the influence of matrix geometry—two-dimensional versus three-dimensional—on the maturation trajectory of stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes has not been systematically evaluated in the context of native cardiac extracellular matrix. There remains a critical unmet need for a scalable, reproducible, and physiologically accurate method to mature human cardiomyocytes to a state that is functionally equivalent to adult myocardium, thereby enabling their safe and effective deployment in clinical and pharmaceutical applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present invention provides a method for the maturation of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes by culturing them within a three-dimensional hydrogel composed of decellularized adult cardiac extracellular matrix, wherein the hydrogel exhibits a storage modulus greater than 50 pascals and contains a complex mixture of structural and regulatory proteins including collagen I, collagen III, fibronectin, fibrillin, periostin, fibulin, and elastin, with minimal contamination from fetal-specific proteins such as mimecan and versican. The invention further provides a composition comprising such a hydrogel, wherein the hydrogel is formed by digesting lyophilized, milled decellularized adult cardiac tissue with pepsin under acidic conditions, followed by neutralization and polymerization at physiological temperature and pH. The invention further provides a method of enhancing the functional maturation of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes, wherein the cells are cultured in the presence of the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel for a period of at least seven days, resulting in increased expression of genes and proteins associated with adult cardiomyocyte function, including junctin, triadin, sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPase 2a, L-type voltage-dependent calcium channel subunit alpha-1C, inward rectifier potassium channel 2.1, and connexin 43, as well as improved calcium transient kinetics, reduced spontaneous beating frequency, and enhanced responsiveness to beta-adrenergic and ryanodine receptor modulators.

The invention further provides a method of generating a population of cardiomyocytes suitable for therapeutic transplantation, wherein the cardiomyocytes are matured using the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel and exhibit electrophysiological properties indistinguishable from those of adult human cardiomyocytes, including the absence of spontaneous depolarization, rapid upstroke velocity, prolonged action potential duration, and robust calcium-induced calcium release. The invention further provides a method of drug screening or cardiotoxicity testing, wherein human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes matured in the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel are exposed to pharmacological agents and their functional responses are measured, yielding predictive data that correlates more accurately with clinical outcomes than responses observed in immature or two-dimensionally cultured cardiomyocytes. The invention further provides a kit comprising the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel in a lyophilized or pre-polymerized form, along with instructions for reconstitution, seeding of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes, and maintenance under controlled conditions to promote maturation.

The invention is based on the discovery that the mechanical stiffness and three-dimensional architecture of the adult cardiac extracellular matrix are critical determinants of cardiomyocyte maturation, and that these properties are not replicated by fetal matrix, synthetic scaffolds, or two-dimensional coatings. The adult matrix hydrogel, with its elevated collagen density, organized fibrillar architecture, and approximately tenfold greater stiffness compared to fetal matrix, provides biomechanical cues that activate integrin-mediated signaling pathways, leading to the transcriptional upregulation of genes essential for adult cardiac function. Furthermore, the three-dimensional environment facilitates cell-cell coupling and the formation of functional syncytia, which are necessary for the coordinated propagation of calcium transients and the suppression of automaticity. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in cardiac tissue engineering, moving beyond simplistic two-dimensional systems and synthetic matrices toward a biomimetic, tissue-derived scaffold that recapitulates the native myocardial niche and drives cardiomyocytes to a state of functional maturity previously unattainable in vitro.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for the maturation of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes through the use of a three-dimensional hydrogel composed of decellularized adult cardiac extracellular matrix. This method represents a significant advancement over existing technologies, which rely on two-dimensional culture systems, synthetic matrices, or fetal-derived scaffolds that fail to recapitulate the mechanical and biochemical complexity of the adult myocardium. The invention is based on the discovery that the three-dimensional architecture and elevated stiffness of adult cardiac extracellular matrix are essential for inducing a mature cardiomyocyte phenotype characterized by organized sarcomeres, robust calcium handling, suppressed automaticity, and appropriate electrophysiological responses to pharmacological stimuli. The method involves the preparation of a hydrogel from decellularized adult cardiac tissue, the seeding of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes onto or within this hydrogel, and the maintenance of the cells under conditions that promote cell-matrix interactions and functional maturation over a period of at least seven days.

The preparation of the adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel begins with the procurement of cardiac tissue from an adult mammalian donor, preferably a bovine, porcine, or human source. The tissue is carefully dissected to remove epicardial fat, connective tissue, and vasculature, then cryopreserved at temperatures below -80°C for a minimum of sixteen hours to facilitate subsequent decellularization. The tissue is then mechanically minced into uniform fragments of approximately three cubic millimeters to maximize surface area exposure during detergent treatment. The minced tissue is subjected to a sequential decellularization protocol involving immersion in distilled water for thirty minutes to remove soluble cellular components, followed by washing in phosphate-buffered saline for fifteen minutes to restore ionic balance. The tissue is then incubated in a solution of sodium dodecyl sulfate at a concentration of one percent for seventy-two hours under continuous stirring at three hundred and thirty revolutions per minute. This concentration is specifically selected to effectively solubilize cellular membranes and remove nuclear material while preserving the integrity of the extracellular matrix proteins. The use of a higher concentration of sodium dodecyl sulfate for adult tissue, as opposed to lower concentrations used for fetal tissue, is critical, as adult matrix contains a denser and more cross-linked collagen network that requires more aggressive detergent treatment for complete decellularization. Following detergent exposure, the tissue is rinsed with one percent Triton X-100 for ninety minutes to remove residual detergents and lipid components, followed by seven washes in distilled water, each lasting thirty minutes, to ensure complete removal of all detergent residues. The decellularized tissue is then incubated in phosphate-buffered saline containing an antibiotic-antimycotic mixture for eighteen hours at four degrees Celsius to eliminate any residual microbial contamination.

After decellularization, the tissue is lyophilized to remove all residual moisture and then mechanically milled into a fine powder using a standard kitchen cheese grater or equivalent mechanical milling device. The resulting powder, which retains the structural integrity of the extracellular matrix proteins, is then subjected to enzymatic digestion using pepsin under acidic conditions. Approximately two hundred milligrams of the lyophilized matrix powder is suspended in a solution containing twenty milligrams of pepsin dissolved in ten millimolar hydrochloric acid, and the mixture is stirred continuously at three hundred and thirty revolutions per minute at room temperature for a period of forty-eight to seventy-two hours. The acidic environment is essential to activate pepsin, which cleaves collagen and other matrix proteins into soluble fragments while preserving key bioactive domains. The digestion is monitored visually and by rheological assessment until the solution achieves a homogeneous, viscous consistency indicative of complete solubilization. The digested matrix is then divided into one-milliliter aliquots and stored at negative eighty degrees Celsius until use. Upon thawing, the solution is neutralized to physiological pH using sodium hydroxide or bicarbonate buffer and brought to a concentration of approximately one to two milligrams per milliliter. The solution is then polymerized by incubation at thirty-seven degrees Celsius for thirty minutes, during which time the matrix proteins reassemble into a three-dimensional hydrogel with a storage modulus greater than fifty pascals, as measured by parallel plate rheometry. This stiffness is approximately tenfold greater than that of hydrogels prepared from fetal cardiac extracellular matrix and closely approximates the stiffness of native adult human myocardium, which ranges from ten to fifty kilopascals.

The induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes used in this method are generated through standard cardiac differentiation protocols involving sequential modulation of Wnt signaling. The cells are derived from human induced pluripotent stem cell lines, such as the WTC-11 GCaMP line, which has been genetically engineered to express the calcium-sensitive fluorescent reporter GCaMP6f, enabling real-time monitoring of intracellular calcium fluxes. The cells are maintained in a pluripotent state on Matrigel-coated surfaces using mTeSR1 medium until they reach 75 to 90 percent confluence, at which point they are dissociated into single cells and seeded onto the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel. Alternatively, the cells may be embedded directly within the hydrogel prior to polymerization, allowing for uniform distribution throughout the matrix. The cells are cultured in a serum-free, insulin-containing medium such as RPMI/B27, with media changes performed every three days. The culture is maintained under standard conditions of five percent carbon dioxide and 37 degrees Celsius, with no mechanical or electrical stimulation applied during the maturation period. After seven days of culture, the cells exhibit significant morphological and functional maturation, as evidenced by increased expression of key cardiac maturation markers at both the mRNA and protein levels.

Gene expression analysis by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction demonstrates a marked upregulation of genes associated with adult cardiomyocyte function in cells cultured within the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel compared to those cultured in two-dimensional matrices or on synthetic scaffolds. These include junctin, which anchors the ryanodine receptor to the sarcoplasmic reticulum; triadin, which facilitates calcium-induced calcium release; sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPase 2a, which re-sequesters calcium following contraction; L-type voltage-dependent calcium channel subunit alpha-1C, which mediates calcium influx during the action potential; inward rectifier potassium channel 2.1, which stabilizes the resting membrane potential and suppresses automaticity; and connexin 43, which enables intercellular electrical coupling. The expression of these genes is significantly higher in cells cultured in the three-dimensional adult matrix than in those cultured in fetal matrix or in two-dimensional configurations, indicating that both the source and the geometry of the matrix are critical determinants of maturation. Protein expression analysis by immunofluorescence and Western blotting confirms these findings, with strong upregulation of calcium-handling proteins and sarcomeric components such as cardiac troponin T, myosin light chain 2, and connexin 43 observed in cells cultured in the three-dimensional adult matrix. The organization of sarcomeres, as visualized by immunostaining for alpha-actinin, is markedly improved, with the appearance of well-aligned, striated patterns characteristic of adult cardiomyocytes, in contrast to the disorganized, punctate staining observed in immature cells.

Functional maturation is further confirmed through calcium imaging using the GCaMP6f reporter. Cells cultured in the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel exhibit calcium transients with significantly increased amplitude, steeper maximum upslope and downslope, and shorter time to peak and time to 50 percent decay compared to cells cultured in two-dimensional matrices. These kinetic changes reflect enhanced calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum and more efficient reuptake, consistent with the upregulation of junctin, triadin, and sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPase 2a. Importantly, when cells are cultured as single entities within the three-dimensional matrix, calcium transients are slower and less robust than those observed in cells cultured in aggregates, indicating that cell-cell coupling is essential for the full expression of mature calcium handling properties. This finding underscores the importance of the three-dimensional architecture in facilitating the formation of functional syncytia, which are necessary for coordinated electrical propagation and the suppression of spontaneous depolarization. Indeed, the spontaneous beating frequency of cardiomyocytes cultured in the three-dimensional adult matrix is significantly reduced compared to those in two-dimensional systems, a critical safety feature for therapeutic applications, as it eliminates the risk of arrhythmias caused by ectopic pacemaker activity.

The functional maturity of the cells is further validated through pharmacological challenge. Exposure to isoproterenol, a beta-adrenergic agonist, results in a significant increase in beating rate in cells cultured in the three-dimensional adult matrix, whereas cells in two-dimensional cultures show minimal or no response. Subsequent addition of propranolol, a beta-adrenergic antagonist, reverses this effect and restores baseline beating frequency, demonstrating the presence of functional beta-adrenergic signaling pathways. Similarly, exposure to caffeine, which sensitizes the ryanodine receptor to calcium, induces a more rapid and pronounced calcium transient in cells cultured in the three-dimensional adult matrix, with a steeper upslope and shorter time to maximum calcium concentration. These responses mirror those observed in adult human cardiomyocytes in vivo and are absent or blunted in immature cells, confirming that the maturation achieved through this method is not merely structural but also pharmacologically relevant.

The invention further includes the use of the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel in therapeutic applications. The matured cardiomyocytes, following a period of culture in the hydrogel, may be harvested and transplanted into injured myocardium via injection or patch implantation. The hydrogel itself may be used as a delivery vehicle, as it is biocompatible, biodegradable, and supports cell survival and integration post-transplantation. The hydrogel may be injected in a liquid state and allowed to polymerize in situ, forming a supportive matrix that promotes engraftment and reduces host immune response. The hydrogel may also be pre-formed into patches or scaffolds of defined geometry and seeded with cardiomyocytes prior to implantation, providing structural support to the damaged myocardium while promoting electromechanical coupling with host tissue.

The invention further encompasses the use of the matured cardiomyocytes in drug screening and cardiotoxicity testing. Traditional screening platforms rely on immature cardiomyocytes cultured in two-dimensional systems, which yield poor predictive value for clinical outcomes. The cardiomyocytes matured using the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel, by contrast, respond to pharmacological agents in a manner that closely mimics adult human myocardium, enabling more accurate identification of pro-arrhythmic compounds, positive inotropes, and cardiotoxic agents. This application is particularly valuable for pharmaceutical development, where the failure of drugs in late-stage clinical trials due to cardiotoxicity represents a major economic and safety burden.

The invention further provides a kit for the maturation of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes, comprising a lyophilized or pre-polymerized three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel, a sterile buffer for reconstitution, a medium for cardiomyocyte culture, and detailed instructions for seeding, maintenance, and functional assessment. The kit may further include a calcium imaging reagent such as GCaMP6f, a beta-adrenergic agonist, a beta-adrenergic antagonist, and a ryanodine receptor sensitizer, enabling users to perform functional validation assays without requiring additional reagents. The kit is designed for use in research laboratories, pharmaceutical companies, and clinical manufacturing facilities, providing a standardized, reproducible, and scalable platform for the production of mature human cardiomyocytes.

The invention further includes methods for characterizing the hydrogel to ensure batch-to-batch consistency. The hydrogel’s mechanical properties are assessed using parallel plate rheometry to confirm a storage modulus greater than fifty pascals. The composition of the hydrogel is verified by mass spectrometry to ensure the presence of collagen I, collagen III, fibronectin, fibrillin, periostin, fibulin, and elastin, and the absence of fetal-specific proteins such as mimecan and versican. The hydrogel’s microstructure is analyzed using multiphoton microscopy to confirm the presence of aligned collagen fibers with a width greater than one micrometer and a density greater than that observed in fetal matrix. These quality control measures ensure that the hydrogel consistently provides the biomechanical and biochemical cues necessary for cardiomyocyte maturation.

The invention further provides a method of producing a population of cardiomyocytes suitable for clinical transplantation, wherein the cells are matured using the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel and then subjected to purification to remove residual undifferentiated pluripotent stem cells, thereby minimizing the risk of teratoma formation. The purified cardiomyocytes are cryopreserved in a solution containing dimethyl sulfoxide and trehalose and stored in liquid nitrogen until use. Upon thawing, the cells retain their mature phenotype and functional properties, demonstrating that the maturation achieved through this method is stable and reversible only under conditions that disrupt the matrix-cell interactions.

The invention further provides a method of treating myocardial infarction in a mammalian subject, comprising the steps of harvesting a sample of cardiac tissue from an adult donor, decellularizing the tissue to produce an extracellular matrix, digesting the matrix with pepsin to produce a hydrogel, seeding the hydrogel with induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes, maturing the cells for at least seven days, and transplanting the matured cells into the infarcted region of the subject’s heart. The subject may be autologous, with the induced pluripotent stem cells derived from somatic cells such as skin fibroblasts or blood mononuclear cells obtained from the subject, thereby eliminating the risk of immune rejection. The transplantation may be performed via minimally invasive catheter-based delivery or open surgical implantation, depending on the extent of myocardial damage. The transplanted cells integrate with the host myocardium, form electromechanical junctions, and contribute to improved contractile function, as measured by echocardiography, magnetic resonance imaging, or invasive hemodynamic assessment.
