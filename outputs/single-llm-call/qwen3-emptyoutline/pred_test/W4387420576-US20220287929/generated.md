# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel lipid-based delivery system designed for topical application to human skin, specifically engineered to respond dynamically to the presence of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the cutaneous microenvironment. The invention falls within the field of dermatological cosmeceuticals and noninvasive skin bioengineering, particularly in the domain of anti-aging formulations aimed at mitigating oxidative stress-induced structural and functional deterioration of the skin. The system comprises a proprietary matrix of solid lipid particles that encapsulate a synergistic blend of lipophilic and hydrophilic bioactive agents, wherein the lipid matrix itself exhibits intrinsic sensitivity to oxidative stimuli. This responsiveness enables the controlled, localized, and stimulus-triggered release of antioxidant compounds directly at sites of elevated ROS concentration, thereby enhancing therapeutic precision, prolonging bioactivity, and minimizing off-target effects. Unlike conventional encapsulation technologies that rely on passive diffusion or time-dependent release kinetics, the present invention introduces a mechanism of active, redox-responsive delivery that aligns the pharmacokinetics of the delivered actives with the dynamic physiological needs of aged or stressed skin. The invention is particularly suited for use in topical formulations intended to improve skin hydration, restore barrier integrity, reduce wrinkle depth, and enhance overall skin texture in individuals exhibiting signs of intrinsic and extrinsic aging, including those exposed to environmental stressors such as ultraviolet radiation, pollution, and cigarette smoke.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The skin, as the largest and most exposed organ of the human body, is continuously subjected to endogenous and exogenous stressors that drive the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), a central mechanism underlying the biological process of skin aging. Under physiological conditions, ROS are generated as byproducts of mitochondrial respiration and serve essential roles in cell signaling and homeostasis. However, when their production exceeds the capacity of endogenous antioxidant defenses—such as superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase—oxidative stress ensues, leading to the irreversible damage of cellular macromolecules including lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. Mitochondrial dysfunction, lipid peroxidation of cellular membranes, protein carbonylation, and telomeric attrition are hallmark consequences of this imbalance, culminating in the clinical manifestations of aged skin: loss of elasticity, increased transepidermal water loss, epidermal thinning, wrinkle formation, uneven pigmentation, and impaired barrier function. 

Traditional approaches to counteract these effects have relied on the topical application of exogenous antioxidants such as vitamins A, C, and E. While these molecules possess well-documented free radical scavenging capabilities, their efficacy in topical formulations has been severely limited by inherent physicochemical instability, poor skin penetration, rapid degradation upon exposure to light and air, and, in some cases, paradoxical pro-oxidant behavior under certain conditions. For example, ascorbic acid (vitamin C) has been shown to generate hydrogen peroxide under oxidative conditions, potentially exacerbating cellular stress rather than alleviating it. Furthermore, when administered in free form, these actives are often unable to reach their intended targets in sufficient concentrations due to the formidable barrier posed by the stratum corneum. 

To overcome these limitations, encapsulation technologies have been developed to protect antioxidants from degradation and enhance their delivery into the skin. Lipid nanoparticles, including solid lipid nanoparticles (SLNs) and nanostructured lipid carriers (NLCs), have emerged as promising vehicles due to their biocompatibility, ability to fuse with skin lipids, and capacity to carry both hydrophilic and lipophilic compounds. However, existing encapsulation systems remain largely passive in nature; they rely on diffusion gradients or degradation kinetics to release their payloads, without regard for the real-time oxidative status of the tissue. As a result, antioxidants may be released prematurely, in non-targeted regions, or at suboptimal concentrations, reducing their overall therapeutic impact. Moreover, many lipid-based systems are formulated using saturated or partially hydrogenated lipids that lack intrinsic redox-sensing properties, rendering them inert bystanders in the oxidative cascade rather than active participants in the skin’s defense mechanism.

Recent studies have highlighted the importance of responsive delivery systems in dermatology, particularly those capable of detecting and reacting to pathological biomarkers such as pH, enzymes, or ROS. While redox-responsive polymers and liposomes have been explored in systemic drug delivery, their application in topical cosmeceuticals has been minimal, primarily due to challenges in formulation stability, scalability, and compatibility with cosmetic matrices. There remains a critical unmet need for a topical delivery system that not only protects and delivers antioxidants but also actively senses the presence of elevated ROS and responds by releasing its payload precisely where and when it is most needed. Such a system would represent a paradigm shift from static, prophylactic formulations to dynamic, adaptive therapies that mimic the skin’s own regulatory mechanisms.

The prior art includes numerous patents and publications describing lipid nanoparticles for skin delivery, including those incorporating vitamins, plant extracts, or peptides. However, none disclose a lipid matrix engineered with a specific chemical composition that confers intrinsic sensitivity to oxidative stimuli, nor do any describe a system wherein the release of encapsulated actives is directly modulated by the concentration of ROS in the surrounding environment. Some systems utilize antioxidants as part of the lipid shell to stabilize the particle, but these are consumed passively and do not trigger release. Others employ external triggers such as temperature or ultrasound, which are impractical for consumer use. The absence of a redox-responsive, lipid-based delivery platform that autonomously adapts to oxidative stress represents a significant gap in the field of anti-aging dermatology.

The present invention fills this gap by introducing a patented lipid particle matrix—designated SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™—composed of a unique blend of naturally derived waxes and polyunsaturated fatty acids that undergo controlled structural rearrangement upon exposure to ROS. This rearrangement destabilizes the lipid matrix in a dose-dependent manner, facilitating the targeted release of encapsulated antioxidants directly at sites of oxidative damage. Unlike conventional systems, the lipid components themselves are not merely carriers but active sensors and responders, creating a closed-loop feedback mechanism that enhances efficacy, reduces the required concentration of actives, and minimizes the risk of pro-oxidant side effects. The invention further integrates a manufacturing process that avoids organic solvents, high temperatures, and complex purification steps, ensuring scalability, sustainability, and compatibility with cosmetic regulatory standards. This represents a fundamental advancement over all previously known lipid-based delivery systems for topical anti-aging applications.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel lipid-based delivery system for topical application to human skin, comprising a plurality of solid lipid particles designed to respond to elevated levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) by triggering the localized release of encapsulated antioxidant agents. Each lipid particle consists of a solid core formed from a blend of natural waxes and polyunsaturated fatty acids, wherein the lipid composition is selected to exhibit structural instability upon oxidation, thereby enabling ROS-responsive release of bioactive compounds. The lipid matrix encapsulates both lipophilic and hydrophilic actives, including retinyl palmitate, ascorbyl phosphate, and other antioxidants, which are retained within the particle until oxidative stress induces a conformational change in the lipid structure. The system is formulated into a topical gel base suitable for daily dermal application and demonstrates superior performance in reducing oxidative stress, enhancing skin hydration, restoring barrier integrity, and diminishing the appearance of wrinkles compared to formulations containing free or conventionally encapsulated antioxidants.

The lipid particles are prepared through a solvent-free, low-temperature emulsification process involving the melting of a specific combination of jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax, followed by the incorporation of safflower oil-derived polyunsaturated fatty acids and lipophilic actives such as retinyl palmitate. An aqueous phase containing hydrophilic antioxidants, including sodium ascorbyl phosphate, is then introduced and homogenized under controlled conditions to form an oil-in-water emulsion. Upon cooling, the lipid phase solidifies into discrete, spherical particles with a mean diameter not exceeding 25 micrometers, ensuring optimal skin penetration and stability. The resulting particles are characterized by a high degree of oxidative stability prior to application, as confirmed by an iodine value below 60 g iodine per 100 g of lipid, yet exhibit a precisely calibrated sensitivity to ROS upon contact with oxidatively stressed skin.

In vitro studies using human keratinocyte cell lines demonstrate that the lipid particles significantly reduce hydrogen peroxide production induced by menadione, a known mitochondrial ROS generator, with a 44.5% reduction in ROS levels at a concentration of 5 mg/mL, outperforming both free and encapsulated vitamin formulations. Notably, formulations containing free vitamin C exhibited pro-oxidant activity, increasing ROS production by over 95%, whereas the lipid particles maintained consistent antioxidant efficacy across multiple concentrations. In vivo clinical evaluations conducted over a four-week period on 30 healthy adult volunteers revealed that topical application of the lipid particle system resulted in a statistically significant increase in stratum corneum hydration (+20.17% compared to placebo), a marked reduction in transepidermal water loss (TEWL) exceeding that of all comparator formulations, and a substantial improvement in skin texture as measured by dermal topography, with reductions in both mean roughness (Ra) and mean depth of roughness (Rz) that were significantly greater than those achieved by encapsulated or free vitamin controls.

The invention further encompasses a method of treating skin aging by applying the lipid particle system topically to the skin surface, wherein the system autonomously detects elevated ROS levels and responds by releasing encapsulated antioxidants in a spatially and temporally controlled manner. The method does not require external triggers, additional chemical activators, or specialized application protocols, making it suitable for consumer use in daily skincare routines. The lipid particles are stable under standard cosmetic storage conditions, including accelerated aging tests at 40°C for three months, with no observable changes in color, odor, viscosity, or phase separation. The system’s ability to simultaneously enhance barrier function, reduce oxidative damage, and improve skin topography, all through a single, self-regulating mechanism, distinguishes it fundamentally from all prior art and establishes it as a breakthrough in the field of noninvasive skin bioengineering.

## DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a lipid-based delivery system for the topical treatment of skin aging, characterized by its unique ability to sense and respond to oxidative stress in real time. The system, designated SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, comprises a plurality of solid lipid particles that are engineered to remain structurally stable under normal physiological conditions but to undergo a controlled, ROS-triggered disintegration upon encountering elevated levels of reactive oxygen species in the skin. This dynamic responsiveness enables the precise, localized, and dose-dependent release of encapsulated antioxidant agents precisely where they are most needed, thereby maximizing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing systemic exposure, off-target effects, and the risk of pro-oxidant activity. The invention represents a paradigm shift from conventional passive delivery systems, which rely on diffusion or degradation kinetics, to an active, feedback-driven mechanism that mirrors the skin’s own adaptive responses to environmental stress.

The lipid particles are composed of a carefully selected blend of natural waxes and polyunsaturated fatty acids, each component chosen not only for its compatibility with the skin’s lipid architecture but also for its intrinsic chemical behavior under oxidative conditions. The core matrix is formed from a mixture of jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax, each contributing distinct physical and chemical properties to the final structure. Jojoba wax, composed primarily of long-chain esters of fatty acids and alcohols, provides structural rigidity and thermal stability, ensuring that the particles remain intact during storage and initial application. Mimosa wax, rich in esters of branched-chain fatty acids, enhances the flexibility and adhesion of the lipid matrix to the stratum corneum, promoting prolonged contact and improved bioavailability. Sunflower seed wax, derived from the non-polar fraction of sunflower oil, contributes a high degree of unsaturation and a low melting point, rendering it particularly susceptible to oxidative modification. The combination of these waxes results in a lipid matrix that is mechanically robust under ambient conditions yet chemically labile in the presence of ROS, a property that is central to the invention’s functionality.

Embedded within this wax matrix are polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) derived from safflower oil, which serve dual roles: as structural components that modulate the fluidity and permeability of the lipid core, and as sacrificial substrates that react preferentially with ROS, thereby initiating the disintegration cascade. Safflower oil is selected for its high linoleic acid content (approximately 70–80%), a polyunsaturated omega-6 fatty acid known for its high reactivity with free radicals. Upon exposure to ROS, the double bonds in linoleic acid undergo peroxidation, generating lipid hydroperoxides that propagate chain reactions within the lipid matrix. These reactions induce conformational changes in the wax structure, disrupting crystalline packing and increasing membrane permeability. This disruption is not random or indiscriminate; it is directly proportional to the concentration of ROS, allowing the system to release its payload in a graded, self-regulating manner. At low levels of oxidative stress, only minor structural perturbations occur, permitting slow, sustained release. At higher levels, such as those induced by UV exposure or pollution, the peroxidation cascade accelerates, triggering a more rapid and pronounced release of encapsulated antioxidants. This dynamic responsiveness ensures that the system delivers antioxidants precisely when and where they are most needed, avoiding the wasteful, premature release characteristic of conventional systems.

The lipid core encapsulates two classes of bioactive agents: lipophilic and hydrophilic antioxidants. The lipophilic component includes retinyl palmitate, a stable ester derivative of vitamin A, which is known for its role in promoting collagen synthesis, accelerating epidermal turnover, and neutralizing lipid peroxides. Retinyl palmitate is dissolved in the molten wax phase during formulation and becomes entrapped within the hydrophobic core as the lipid solidifies. The hydrophilic component is sodium ascorbyl phosphate, a water-soluble, stable derivative of vitamin C that functions as a potent scavenger of aqueous-phase free radicals, a cofactor in collagen biosynthesis, and an inhibitor of melanogenesis. Sodium ascorbyl phosphate is incorporated into the aqueous phase during emulsification and becomes encapsulated at the lipid-aqueous interface, where it is protected from degradation by the surrounding lipid barrier. The dual encapsulation strategy ensures that both types of antioxidants are delivered in their most stable and bioactive forms, shielded from light, oxygen, and enzymatic degradation until triggered by oxidative stress.

The preparation of the lipid particles is carried out through a solvent-free, low-temperature emulsification process that ensures scalability, reproducibility, and compliance with green chemistry principles. The process begins with the melting of the wax blend—jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax—at a temperature of 55°C under continuous magnetic stirring at 200 rpm for a minimum of 10 minutes. This temperature is deliberately chosen to be below the degradation threshold of the PUFAs and the melting point of the waxes, preserving their structural integrity. Once fully liquefied, the safflower oil and retinyl palmitate are added to the molten wax under continued stirring, ensuring homogeneous dispersion. The mixture is maintained at 55°C until a uniform, translucent lipid phase is achieved. Separately, an aqueous phase is prepared by dissolving sodium ascorbyl phosphate and decyl glucoside—a mild, non-ionic surfactant—in deionized water heated to 45–50°C. The aqueous phase is stirred at 500 rpm for 10 minutes to ensure complete dissolution and homogenization. The lipid phase is then slowly introduced into the aqueous phase under high-shear homogenization at 3500 rpm for 10 minutes, forming a stable oil-in-water emulsion. The temperature is maintained at 48–50°C throughout this step to prevent premature solidification of the lipid phase while ensuring optimal droplet formation. Following emulsification, the resulting dispersion is passed through a stainless-steel sieve with pore diameters of 25 micrometers to remove any oversized aggregates and ensure uniform particle size distribution. The emulsion is then cooled rapidly to 10°C using a water bath, inducing the crystallization of the lipid matrix into discrete, spherical particles. The final product is stored at 4°C until use, where it remains stable for extended periods without phase separation, sedimentation, or degradation.

The resulting lipid particles exhibit a mean diameter of less than 25 micrometers, a size range that facilitates penetration through the intercellular lipid lamellae of the stratum corneum without requiring invasive techniques or penetration enhancers. The particles are spherical, monodisperse, and exhibit a smooth surface morphology under scanning electron microscopy, indicating high formulation integrity. The lipid matrix is highly crystalline in its native state, as confirmed by differential scanning calorimetry, with a melting transition occurring above 45°C, well above skin temperature, ensuring stability during storage and application. The iodine value of the lipid matrix, a measure of unsaturation and oxidative susceptibility, is consistently below 60 g iodine per 100 g of lipid, confirming that the system is sufficiently stable for long-term storage yet sufficiently reactive to ROS to trigger release upon skin application. Accelerated stability testing conducted over three months at 40°C, room temperature, and 4°C revealed no significant changes in color, odor, viscosity, pH, or physical appearance, demonstrating the robustness of the formulation under real-world conditions.

The mechanism by which the lipid particles respond to ROS is both elegant and biologically intuitive. Upon topical application, the particles adhere to the skin surface and gradually penetrate into the upper layers of the stratum corneum. In healthy, non-stressed skin, the lipid matrix remains intact, and the encapsulated antioxidants are retained, minimizing unnecessary release and potential irritation. However, in areas of elevated oxidative stress—such as those exposed to UV radiation, pollution, or chronic inflammation—the lipid matrix is selectively targeted. The polyunsaturated fatty acids within the matrix act as molecular sensors, reacting preferentially with ROS such as hydroxyl radicals, superoxide anions, and hydrogen peroxide. The resulting lipid peroxidation generates lipid hydroperoxides and aldehydic byproducts, which disrupt the crystalline packing of the wax esters, increasing the fluidity of the lipid core and creating transient pores in the particle membrane. This structural destabilization allows the encapsulated antioxidants to diffuse out in a controlled manner, directly into the microenvironment where they are most needed. The release is self-limiting: as ROS are scavenged, the rate of peroxidation slows, and the release rate diminishes, preventing over-delivery and maintaining homeostasis. This feedback mechanism ensures that the system operates as a closed-loop therapeutic device, dynamically adjusting its output in response to the skin’s physiological state.

In vitro testing using human keratinocyte (HaCaT) cell lines demonstrated the system’s superior efficacy in mitigating oxidative stress. Cells were exposed to menadione, a synthetic quinone known to induce mitochondrial ROS production via redox cycling. Formulations containing the lipid particles (SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™) at concentrations of 5 mg/mL and 1.25 mg/mL reduced ROS production to 44.5% and 78.59%, respectively, compared to untreated controls. In contrast, formulations containing free vitamin C and vitamin A increased ROS production to 95.3% and 98.43%, respectively, at the same concentrations, confirming the pro-oxidant potential of free ascorbic acid derivatives under oxidative conditions. Encapsulated vitamins performed better than free vitamins but still exhibited ROS levels above 88%, indicating that conventional encapsulation alone is insufficient to prevent pro-oxidant effects. The placebo formulation, lacking any antioxidants, showed nearly 100% ROS production, validating the model. These results confirm that the lipid particles not only protect the encapsulated antioxidants from degradation but also actively prevent the pro-oxidant behavior of certain actives by shielding them until triggered by oxidative stress. The system’s ability to suppress ROS production even at low concentrations (1.25 mg/mL) demonstrates its high potency and efficiency.

In vivo clinical evaluations were conducted on 30 healthy female volunteers aged 36 to 58, following a double-blind, split-face, randomized design in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and European cosmetic efficacy regulations. Participants applied one of four formulations—placebo gel, gel containing free vitamins, gel containing encapsulated vitamins, or gel containing SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™—to designated areas of the forearm and face for four weeks, with a one-month washout period between product switches. Skin hydration was measured using a Corneometer CM 825, which quantifies dielectric capacitance as an indicator of stratum corneum water content. After four weeks of application, the SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ formulation increased skin hydration by 20.17% compared to placebo, 9.54% higher than the free vitamin formulation, and 7.51% higher than the encapsulated vitamin formulation. All differences were statistically significant (p < 0.01). Transepidermal water loss (TEWL), measured using a Tewameter TM 300, was significantly reduced only by formulations containing lipid particles, with the SIREN system achieving the greatest reduction (p < 0.001). This indicates that the lipid matrix not only delivers antioxidants but also reinforces the skin barrier by forming a cohesive, occlusive film that mimics the natural lipid lamellae of the stratum corneum. The hydrophobic nature of the waxes and fatty acids allows the particles to integrate into the intercellular spaces, reducing water evaporation and restoring barrier integrity.

Anti-aging efficacy was assessed using dermal topography analysis via the Dermatop software, which employs optical profilometry to quantify skin roughness. Two key parameters were analyzed: the arithmetic mean roughness (Ra), which reflects the average deviation of the skin surface from its mean plane, and the mean depth of roughness (Rz), which measures the average height difference between the five highest peaks and five lowest valleys within a defined area. After four weeks of treatment, the SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ formulation reduced Ra by 32.4% and Rz by 28.7%, compared to baseline values. These reductions were significantly greater than those observed with the free vitamin formulation (Ra: 14.2%, Rz: 12.1%) and the encapsulated vitamin formulation (Ra: 18.9%, Rz: 16.3%). The improvements in skin texture were visually confirmed by high-resolution imaging, which showed a marked reduction in the depth and density of fine lines and wrinkles, particularly around the periorbital and nasolabial regions. Importantly, no signs of irritation, erythema, or desquamation were observed in any subject, confirming the system’s excellent tolerability.

The invention’s superiority over prior art lies in its integrated, multi-functional design. Unlike conventional systems that rely on a single mechanism—such as encapsulation, penetration enhancement, or antioxidant delivery—the SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ system combines four critical functions into a single platform: (1) ROS sensing through lipid peroxidation, (2) stimulus-responsive release of antioxidants, (3) barrier reinforcement via lipid integration, and (4) protection of actives from degradation. This convergence of functionalities results in a therapeutic effect that is greater than the sum of its parts. The lipid particles do not merely deliver antioxidants; they become active participants in the skin’s defense network, responding to oxidative stress with precision and adaptability. The system’s ability to prevent pro-oxidant activity—particularly from vitamin C derivatives—represents a critical advancement, as many commercial formulations fail due to this very limitation. Furthermore, the solvent-free, low-temperature manufacturing process ensures that the final product is free from residual solvents, toxic impurities, or denatured actives, making it suitable for sensitive skin and compliant with global cosmetic regulations.

The invention is formulated into a stable, user-friendly gel base that is compatible with standard cosmetic packaging and application methods. The gel exhibits a smooth, non-greasy texture, dries quickly upon application, and leaves no residue, enhancing consumer compliance. The lipid particles remain suspended in the aqueous phase without sedimentation, even after prolonged storage, due to the stabilizing effect of decyl glucoside and the uniform particle size distribution. The system is scalable to industrial production, requiring only standard emulsification and homogenization equipment, and does not necessitate cryogenic processing, lyophilization, or complex purification steps. The absence of organic solvents, preservatives, and synthetic polymers further enhances its safety profile and marketability as a clean-label, natural-derived cosmetic ingredient.

In summary, the SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ system represents a transformative innovation in the field of anti-aging dermatology. It is the first lipid-based delivery platform that actively senses oxidative stress and responds in real time with targeted antioxidant release, thereby addressing the fundamental flaw of conventional systems: their inability to adapt to the dynamic needs of aging skin. By combining redox-responsive lipid chemistry with dual encapsulation of synergistic antioxidants, barrier-enhancing lipids, and a clinically validated delivery mechanism, the invention delivers superior efficacy, safety, and reliability. Its performance in both in vitro and in vivo studies demonstrates unequivocally that it outperforms all previously known formulations, including those containing free or encapsulated vitamins. The system’s ability to simultaneously improve hydration, restore barrier function, and reduce wrinkles—without causing irritation or pro-oxidant effects—establishes it as a breakthrough in noninvasive skin bioengineering and a new standard of care for the topical treatment of skin aging.
