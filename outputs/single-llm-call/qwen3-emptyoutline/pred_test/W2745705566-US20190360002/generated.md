# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and genetic engineering, specifically to novel RNA-guided DNA endonucleases derived from thermophilic bacteria and their use in genome editing and transcriptional silencing across a broad temperature range. More particularly, the invention encompasses a thermostable Cas9 nuclease, herein designated ThermoCas9, isolated from the thermophilic bacterium Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, which exhibits robust enzymatic activity between 20°C and 70°C, thereby enabling precise DNA cleavage and gene regulation under conditions that render conventional mesophilic Cas9 proteins inactive. The invention further includes engineered variants of ThermoCas9, including catalytically inactive forms suitable for CRISPR interference (CRISPRi), as well as synthetic guide RNA constructs optimized for enhanced stability and target specificity at elevated temperatures. The utility of this invention spans industrial biotechnology, synthetic biology, metabolic engineering, and fundamental research involving thermophilic organisms, where high-temperature operations are essential to reduce contamination risks, enhance reaction kinetics, or enable the use of non-sterile bioreactor systems. This invention provides the first functional, RNA-guided DNA endonuclease system capable of efficient in vivo genome editing and transcriptional repression in obligate thermophiles at temperatures exceeding 50°C, thereby overcoming a longstanding technological barrier in the manipulation of thermophilic microorganisms.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

The advent of CRISPR-Cas systems as programmable tools for genome editing has revolutionized molecular biology, enabling precise, targeted modifications in a wide array of organisms ranging from bacteria to humans. Among the most widely adopted systems is the type-II CRISPR-Cas9 system, originally characterized in Streptococcus pyogenes, which utilizes a single endonuclease protein guided by a chimeric single-guide RNA (sgRNA) to introduce double-stranded breaks at specific genomic loci defined by complementary base pairing and the presence of a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM). Despite its transformative impact, the application of this system has been largely confined to mesophilic conditions, typically below 42°C, due to the thermal instability of the canonical SpCas9 protein. At elevated temperatures, SpCas9 undergoes irreversible denaturation, rendering it incapable of forming stable ribonucleoprotein complexes with sgRNA or recognizing target DNA sequences. This limitation has precluded the application of CRISPR-based technologies in obligate thermophiles, which thrive at temperatures between 50°C and 80°C and are of considerable interest for industrial bioprocessing due to their enhanced metabolic rates, reduced contamination susceptibility, and compatibility with high-temperature fermentation processes. 

Prior attempts to adapt CRISPR-Cas systems for thermophilic applications have relied on protein engineering strategies such as directed evolution or rational mutagenesis of mesophilic Cas9 orthologues. These approaches, while successful in modestly improving thermostability, have generally resulted in compromised activity, reduced specificity, or diminished sgRNA binding affinity. Moreover, the structural complexity of Cas9, comprising multiple lobes and dynamic conformational states, renders it highly sensitive to perturbations that may disrupt its functional architecture. Consequently, no prior system has demonstrated reliable in vivo genome editing in obligate thermophiles at temperatures above 50°C. The absence of a functional CRISPR-Cas9 tool for thermophiles has hindered genetic manipulation in industrially relevant strains such as Bacillus smithii, Geobacillus stearothermophilus, and Thermus thermophilus, limiting their potential as platforms for the production of biofuels, bioplastics, and specialty chemicals under high-temperature conditions. 

Furthermore, the development of CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) systems—wherein a catalytically inactive Cas9 variant binds to DNA without cleavage, thereby blocking transcription—has been equally constrained by thermal instability. While dCas9-based silencing has proven effective in mesophiles for gene knockdown and regulatory circuit design, no analogous system has been established for thermophiles, depriving researchers of a critical tool for studying gene function and metabolic flux under native growth conditions. In parallel, the identification of PAM sequences for novel Cas9 orthologues has been historically laborious, often requiring extensive in vitro screening with randomized target libraries and deep sequencing. Existing PAM prediction algorithms, largely trained on mesophilic Cas9 variants, exhibit poor accuracy when applied to thermophilic systems due to divergent sequence motifs and temperature-dependent binding preferences. The lack of a well-characterized, thermally stable Cas9 with a defined PAM requirement has therefore created a significant gap in the CRISPR toolbox for high-temperature applications.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by introducing a naturally evolved Cas9 nuclease from Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, a moderately thermophilic bacterium with an optimal growth temperature of 65°C. This protein, designated ThermoCas9, possesses intrinsic thermostability that enables it to retain full enzymatic activity across an unprecedented temperature range of 20°C to 70°C. Unlike engineered variants of mesophilic Cas9, ThermoCas9 has evolved under natural selective pressure to function optimally at elevated temperatures, resulting in a protein scaffold that is inherently resistant to thermal denaturation while maintaining high fidelity in target recognition. Its small size, compact structure, and unique sequence features distinguish it from all previously characterized Cas9 orthologues. The invention further provides a comprehensive characterization of its PAM requirements, which extend to an 8-nucleotide motif with defined positional preferences, and identifies critical structural elements within the sgRNA scaffold that confer enhanced stability at high temperatures. The invention also demonstrates, for the first time, the successful application of a Cas9-based system for both genome editing and transcriptional silencing in vivo in a thermophilic host at 55°C, as well as in a mesophilic host at 37°C, confirming its broad applicability across phylogenetic and thermal boundaries. This represents a paradigm shift in CRISPR technology, moving from the engineering of mesophilic proteins to the direct exploitation of naturally thermostable enzymes for high-temperature biotechnological applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel RNA-guided DNA endonuclease, herein designated ThermoCas9, derived from the thermophilic bacterium Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, which exhibits enzymatic activity across a temperature range extending from 20°C to 70°C, thereby enabling precise genome editing and transcriptional silencing under conditions that are incompatible with conventional Cas9 systems. ThermoCas9 is a type-IIC Cas9 protein comprising 1082 amino acids, characterized by a truncated REC lobe relative to other Cas9 orthologues, and containing conserved RuvC and HNH catalytic domains responsible for double-stranded DNA cleavage. The invention further encompasses catalytically inactive variants of ThermoCas9, generated by site-directed mutagenesis of key residues in the RuvC and HNH domains, which retain the ability to bind DNA in a sequence-specific manner without inducing cleavage, thereby enabling CRISPR interference applications. 

The invention includes a defined protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) requirement for ThermoCas9, which is a non-palindromic 8-nucleotide sequence located immediately 3′ of the target protospacer, with the consensus sequence 5′-NNNNCNRN-3′, wherein the fifth position is preferentially cytosine, the sixth position is variable but favors adenine or cytosine, the seventh position is variable but favors adenine, and the eighth position is strongly preferred as adenine. This PAM sequence is distinct from those recognized by other known Cas9 orthologues and is essential for target recognition and cleavage efficiency, particularly at temperatures below 30°C, where PAM specificity is stringent. At elevated temperatures between 37°C and 70°C, ThermoCas9 tolerates a broader range of nucleotides at the sixth and seventh positions, enabling greater targeting flexibility under operational conditions. 

The invention further provides synthetic guide RNA constructs optimized for use with ThermoCas9, wherein the sgRNA comprises a crRNA spacer sequence of 18 to 25 nucleotides, preferably 20 to 23 nucleotides, fused to a tracrRNA scaffold containing three stem-loop structures. The tracrRNA scaffold is essential for the structural integrity and thermostability of the ThermoCas9-sgRNA ribonucleoprotein complex, and truncation of the distal stem-loop results in significant loss of activity at temperatures above 60°C. The sgRNA may be expressed from a variety of promoters, including constitutive, inducible, or temperature-regulated promoters, depending on the host organism and application. 

The invention further encompasses expression vectors capable of delivering ThermoCas9 and its associated sgRNA into both thermophilic and mesophilic host cells, including plasmids containing the thermocas9 gene under the control of native or heterologous promoters, operably linked to one or more sgRNA expression cassettes. These vectors may further include homologous recombination templates for precise genome editing, antibiotic resistance markers for selection, and origins of replication compatible with Gram-positive and Gram-negative hosts. The invention further provides host cells, including thermophilic bacteria such as Bacillus smithii ET138, Geobacillus stearothermophilus, and Thermus thermophilus, as well as mesophilic bacteria such as Pseudomonas putida KT2440, Escherichia coli, and Bacillus subtilis, transformed with such vectors to enable in vivo genome editing or transcriptional silencing at temperatures ranging from 20°C to 70°C. 

The invention further provides methods for genome editing in thermophilic and mesophilic organisms, comprising the steps of introducing into a host cell an expression vector encoding ThermoCas9 and a sgRNA targeting a genomic locus of interest, optionally in combination with a homologous recombination template, and incubating the host cell at a temperature between 20°C and 70°C to enable cleavage of the target DNA and subsequent repair via homology-directed repair. The invention further provides methods for transcriptional silencing, comprising the introduction of an expression vector encoding a catalytically inactive ThermoCas9 variant and a sgRNA targeting the promoter or coding region of a gene of interest, followed by incubation at a temperature between 20°C and 70°C to achieve repression of gene expression without DNA cleavage. 

The invention further provides methods for determining the PAM sequence of ThermoCas9 in a given organism, comprising the steps of generating a library of randomized PAM sequences flanking a known protospacer, incubating the library with purified ThermoCas9 and a cognate sgRNA at a temperature between 20°C and 70°C, isolating cleaved DNA fragments, and sequencing the flanking regions to identify enriched PAM motifs. The invention further provides computational algorithms for predicting PAM sequences compatible with ThermoCas9 based on the identified consensus motif and temperature-dependent tolerance profiles. 

The invention further provides compositions comprising purified ThermoCas9 protein, either in its active or catalytically inactive form, complexed with a synthetic sgRNA, for use in in vitro DNA cleavage, genome editing in cell-free systems, or as a reagent for diagnostic or synthetic biology applications requiring high-temperature stability. The invention further provides kits comprising one or more of the following: expression vectors encoding ThermoCas9 or ThermodCas9, sgRNA expression cassettes, homologous recombination templates, selection markers, and instructions for use in genome editing or silencing in thermophilic or mesophilic hosts. 

The invention provides a transformative tool for genetic manipulation of thermophilic organisms, enabling precise, programmable genome editing and transcriptional control under conditions that were previously inaccessible to CRISPR-based technologies. It overcomes the thermal limitations of existing Cas9 systems, expands the range of organisms amenable to genetic engineering, and opens new avenues for industrial biotechnology, metabolic engineering, and fundamental research in thermophiles.

## RNA Guides and Target Sequences

The invention encompasses synthetic RNA guide molecules specifically designed to direct the ThermoCas9 endonuclease to target DNA sequences with high fidelity and efficiency across a broad temperature range. These RNA guides, herein referred to as single-guide RNAs (sgRNAs), are chimeric molecules comprising a crRNA-derived spacer sequence fused to a tracrRNA-derived scaffold, wherein the spacer is complementary to a target DNA sequence adjacent to a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) recognized by ThermoCas9. The spacer sequence is preferably between 18 and 25 nucleotides in length, with optimal cleavage efficiency observed when the spacer is 20, 21, 22, or 23 nucleotides long. Spacers shorter than 18 nucleotides result in a significant reduction in cleavage activity, while spacers longer than 25 nucleotides do not confer additional specificity or efficiency and may increase the risk of off-target binding. The spacer sequence must be selected such that its 3′ end is immediately adjacent to a PAM sequence of the form 5′-NNNNCNRN-3′, with the fifth nucleotide strongly preferring cytosine, the sixth and seventh nucleotides exhibiting flexibility but favoring adenine or cytosine, and the eighth nucleotide strongly preferring adenine. The position of the cleavage site is consistently located between the third and fourth nucleotides upstream of the PAM, resulting in a blunt-ended double-stranded break that facilitates subsequent repair by homology-directed recombination or non-homologous end joining, depending on the host organism’s native repair machinery.

The tracrRNA scaffold of the sgRNA is a critical determinant of ThermoCas9 stability and activity, particularly at elevated temperatures. The native tracrRNA sequence derived from Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12 contains three predicted stem-loop structures, the distal of which is essential for maintaining the structural integrity of the ribonucleoprotein complex at temperatures above 60°C. Truncation of the 3′ terminal stem-loop, even by a modest 41 nucleotides, results in a moderate reduction in cleavage efficiency at moderate temperatures but leads to a dramatic loss of activity at 65°C and above. Complete removal of the middle and distal stem-loops abolishes detectable cleavage activity even at 37°C, indicating that these structural elements are indispensable for the proper folding and thermal stabilization of the ThermoCas9-sgRNA complex. The sgRNA scaffold may be further optimized by preserving the conserved nucleotides within the hairpin loops and stem regions, which are predicted to mediate critical interactions with the REC and NUC lobes of ThermoCas9. The linker region connecting the crRNA and tracrRNA components, which in the native system is a short sequence of four nucleotides (5′-GAAA-3′), may be substituted with alternative linkers of similar length and flexibility without compromising function, provided that they do not introduce secondary structures that interfere with sgRNA folding or RNP assembly. 

The sgRNA may be expressed from a variety of promoters depending on the host organism and desired application. In thermophilic hosts such as Bacillus smithii, the native xylL promoter has been shown to provide robust expression at 55°C, while in mesophilic hosts such as Pseudomonas putida, the 3-methylbenzoate-inducible Pm promoter enables tight temporal control of ThermoCas9 expression. Constitutive promoters such as the pta promoter from Bacillus coagulans have also been successfully employed, particularly in applications requiring continuous silencing or editing. The sgRNA may be transcribed in vitro using T7 RNA polymerase or expressed in vivo from plasmid or chromosomal constructs, and may be chemically synthesized for use in cell-free systems. The sgRNA may be modified with 2′-O-methyl or phosphorothioate backbone modifications to enhance nuclease resistance in complex biological environments, although such modifications must be carefully evaluated to ensure they do not impair RNP formation or target recognition. 

The target sequences for ThermoCas9 must be selected with consideration of both PAM compatibility and spacer-protospacer complementarity. The invention demonstrates that ThermoCas9 exhibits a lower tolerance for mismatches between the spacer and target DNA than its mesophilic counterpart, SpCas9, particularly at temperatures below 30°C. Single mismatches at positions 4, 5, and 10 of the protospacer (counting from the PAM-distal end) severely impair cleavage activity, whereas mismatches at positions 1, 2, and 20 are more readily tolerated. This heightened specificity is particularly advantageous for applications requiring minimal off-target effects, such as therapeutic genome editing or metabolic pathway engineering in complex microbial communities. Notably, the presence of a supercoiled plasmid target significantly enhances cleavage efficiency in the presence of mismatched targets, suggesting that DNA topology plays a role in facilitating target accessibility. This phenomenon may be exploited in vivo by selecting target sites within highly transcribed or supercoiled genomic regions to improve editing efficiency. The invention further provides methods for designing sgRNAs with maximal on-target activity and minimal off-target potential by incorporating computational algorithms that account for the temperature-dependent mismatch tolerance profile of ThermoCas9, thereby enabling the rational design of highly specific guide RNAs for any given genomic locus.

## Binding, Cleavage, Marking and Modifying Temperatures

The ThermoCas9 endonuclease exhibits a uniquely broad temperature range of enzymatic activity, spanning from 20°C to 70°C, which far exceeds the operational limits of any previously characterized Cas9 protein. At temperatures below 20°C, the catalytic activity of ThermoCas9 is significantly reduced, likely due to decreased molecular motion and impaired conformational dynamics required for RNP assembly and DNA interrogation. Between 20°C and 30°C, ThermoCas9 retains detectable activity, but only when the PAM sequence conforms precisely to the optimal motif 5′-CVAA-3′ or 5′-CCCA-3′, indicating that at lower temperatures, the enzyme imposes stringent requirements on PAM identity to ensure target specificity and prevent spurious cleavage. This temperature-dependent PAM stringency provides a built-in mechanism for minimizing off-target effects during low-temperature applications, such as in cold-storage bioreactors or during the initial stages of transformation in mesophilic hosts. 

At temperatures between 30°C and 45°C, ThermoCas9 demonstrates robust cleavage activity across a broader spectrum of PAM sequences, including those with cytosine or adenine at the sixth and seventh positions, and the enzyme achieves maximal catalytic efficiency at approximately 55°C, coinciding with the optimal growth temperature of its native host, Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12. Under these conditions, the enzyme exhibits rapid RNP formation, high target binding affinity, and efficient double-stranded DNA cleavage, with complete digestion of target plasmids occurring within one hour under standard assay conditions. The enzyme remains active at temperatures up to 65°C, where it continues to cleave target DNA with high fidelity, although the rate of cleavage begins to plateau due to the increased kinetic energy of the system and potential partial destabilization of non-essential structural elements. 

Remarkably, ThermoCas9 retains full catalytic activity even after pre-incubation at 70°C for up to 10 minutes, provided that the enzyme is pre-associated with its cognate sgRNA. This observation underscores the critical role of sgRNA binding in stabilizing the tertiary structure of ThermoCas9 against thermal denaturation. In the absence of sgRNA, the apo-form of ThermoCas9 begins to denature irreversibly at 65°C, as evidenced by loss of solubility and aggregation upon SDS-PAGE analysis. However, when the sgRNA is bound prior to thermal exposure, the resulting ribonucleoprotein complex remains folded and functional, enabling cleavage of target DNA even after exposure to 70°C. This phenomenon is not observed with mesophilic Cas9 orthologues, which denature completely at temperatures above 45°C regardless of sgRNA presence. The thermal resilience of the ThermoCas9-sgRNA complex suggests that sgRNA binding induces a conformational shift from an open, flexible state to a compact, rigid state that protects critical structural domains from thermal unfolding. This property renders ThermoCas9 uniquely suited for applications involving high-temperature processing, such as in situ genome editing in thermophilic fermentations, where the culture temperature may fluctuate or be intentionally elevated to suppress contamination. 

The invention further provides methods for modulating the cleavage activity of ThermoCas9 through temperature control. By adjusting the incubation temperature during genome editing experiments, users can fine-tune the stringency of target recognition. For example, performing editing at 30°C ensures that only targets with the most optimal PAM sequences are cleaved, effectively filtering out potential off-target sites with suboptimal motifs. Conversely, performing editing at 55°C or 60°C allows for a broader range of PAM variants to be targeted, increasing the number of potential editing sites within a given genome. This temperature-dependent PAM flexibility provides an unprecedented level of control over editing specificity and efficiency, enabling context-specific applications that were previously unattainable with conventional CRISPR systems. Furthermore, the ability to perform editing at 70°C opens the possibility of using ThermoCas9 in extreme environments, such as industrial composting systems, geothermal bioreactors, or high-temperature waste treatment processes, where conventional enzymes rapidly lose function. The invention thus provides a novel paradigm for temperature-regulated genome engineering, wherein the same enzyme can be deployed across a wide range of thermal conditions, with its activity profile dynamically modulated by the ambient temperature of the system.

## Functional Moieties

The invention encompasses the use of functional moieties attached to the ThermoCas9 protein or its associated sgRNA to expand its utility beyond simple DNA cleavage and transcriptional repression. These moieties may be covalently or non-covalently linked to the protein or RNA components to confer new functionalities, including but not limited to fluorescent labeling, affinity purification, epigenetic modification, base editing, or targeted recruitment of chromatin-modifying enzymes. For example, ThermoCas9 may be fused at its N- or C-terminus to a fluorescent protein such as GFP, mCherry, or mNeonGreen, enabling real-time visualization of RNP localization in live cells under high-temperature conditions. Such fusion proteins retain full nuclease activity and thermostability, allowing for the tracking of genome editing events in thermophilic hosts during active growth. Alternatively, ThermoCas9 may be fused to epitope tags such as FLAG, HA, or hexahistidine for rapid purification from complex lysates, facilitating biochemical characterization and industrial-scale production. 

The invention further provides for the fusion of ThermoCas9 with catalytic domains derived from other enzymes to create novel genome editing tools. For instance, the catalytically inactive ThermodCas9 variant may be fused to a cytidine deaminase domain to enable targeted C-to-T base editing at temperatures up to 70°C, thereby expanding the scope of CRISPR base editing to thermophilic organisms. Similarly, fusion to an adenine deaminase enables A-to-G editing, while fusion to a reverse transcriptase domain enables prime editing in thermophilic hosts. These fusion proteins may be expressed from the same vector as the sgRNA, or from separate constructs, and are designed to maintain the structural integrity of both the ThermoCas9 scaffold and the appended functional domain. The invention further encompasses the use of chemical conjugation to attach small molecules, such as biotin, fluorophores, or crosslinking agents, to engineered cysteine residues introduced into ThermoCas9 via site-directed mutagenesis. These modifications enable the capture of ThermoCas9-DNA complexes for downstream analysis, such as ChIP-seq or crosslinking mass spectrometry, even under high-temperature conditions that would otherwise disrupt non-covalent interactions. 

The sgRNA itself may be modified with functional moieties to enhance its stability, delivery, or targeting capacity. For example, the 5′ or 3′ terminus of the sgRNA may be conjugated to cholesterol, lipid nanoparticles, or cell-penetrating peptides to facilitate delivery into eukaryotic cells or hard-to-transfect bacterial strains. The sgRNA may also be labeled with quantum dots, fluorescent dyes, or magnetic beads for detection, isolation, or spatial manipulation of target loci. In addition, the sgRNA may be engineered to include aptamer sequences that bind to small molecules or proteins, enabling conditional activation or repression of ThermoCas9 activity in response to environmental cues. For example, an sgRNA containing a theophylline-binding aptamer may be designed such that ThermoCas9 activity is only induced in the presence of theophylline, providing a chemical switch for precise temporal control of genome editing. The invention further encompasses the use of orthogonal sgRNA scaffolds that are not recognized by endogenous RNA-binding proteins in the host organism, thereby preventing degradation or sequestration and enhancing the longevity of the guide RNA in vivo. These modifications collectively expand the functional repertoire of ThermoCas9 beyond traditional genome editing, enabling its use in synthetic biology circuits, biosensors, epigenetic engineering, and high-throughput screening platforms operating under extreme thermal conditions.

## Nuclease Activity

The ThermoCas9 endonuclease functions as a double-stranded DNA-specific cleaving enzyme that introduces a blunt-ended break at a defined position relative to the protospacer adjacent motif (PAM). The enzyme requires the formation of a ribonucleoprotein complex with a cognate sgRNA to achieve target recognition and catalytic activation. In the absence of sgRNA, ThermoCas9 exists in an inactive, open conformation that is unable to bind DNA with high affinity. Upon binding of the sgRNA, a conformational rearrangement occurs that stabilizes the protein structure and positions the RuvC and HNH nuclease domains for coordinated cleavage of the complementary and non-complementary DNA strands, respectively. The cleavage event occurs between the third and fourth nucleotides upstream of the PAM, resulting in a double-stranded break with no overhangs, which is consistent with the cleavage pattern observed in other type-II Cas9 systems. 

The enzymatic activity of ThermoCas9 is dependent on the presence of divalent cations, with magnesium ions (Mg²⁺) and manganese ions (Mn²⁺) providing the highest levels of cleavage efficiency. Calcium ions (Ca²⁺) support only nicking of one DNA strand, consistent with the selective activation of one nuclease domain, while other divalent cations such as Fe²⁺, Co²⁺, Ni²⁺, and Zn²⁺ are capable of mediating partial cleavage, albeit at reduced efficiency. The enzyme is completely inactive in the presence of the chelating agent EDTA, confirming its dependence on metal ion cofactors for catalysis. The optimal concentration of Mg²⁺ for cleavage is approximately 25 mM, and the reaction proceeds efficiently across a broad pH range from 6.5 to 8.5, with maximal activity observed at pH 7.0 to 7.5. The enzyme is stable in the presence of glycerol, DTT, and NaCl, and its activity is not inhibited by common biological buffers such as HEPES, phosphate, or Tris, making it compatible with a wide range of in vitro and in vivo applications. 

The cleavage kinetics of ThermoCas9 are temperature-dependent, with reaction rates increasing with temperature up to 65°C, after which the rate of cleavage plateaus due to the saturation of enzyme-substrate interactions and the onset of non-productive thermal fluctuations. The enzyme exhibits a Michaelis-Menten kinetic profile, with a Km for target DNA in the low nanomolar range and a kcat that increases with temperature, consistent with its thermophilic origin. The enzyme is highly processive, capable of cleaving multiple target molecules in succession without dissociating from the sgRNA, and does not exhibit significant auto-cleavage or degradation under standard assay conditions. Importantly, ThermoCas9 shows no detectable activity on single-stranded DNA, RNA, or DNA-RNA hybrids, confirming its strict specificity for double-stranded DNA targets. The enzyme is also resistant to proteolytic degradation in crude lysates, retaining full activity even after prolonged incubation in bacterial cell extracts, which is a critical feature for in vivo applications where protease activity may be elevated. 

The invention further provides for the generation of catalytically inactive variants of ThermoCas9 through site-directed mutagenesis of key residues in the RuvC and HNH domains. Substitution of aspartic acid at position 8 with alanine (D8A) and histidine at position 582 with alanine (H582A) completely abolishes cleavage activity while preserving the ability of the protein to bind DNA in a sequence-specific manner. This catalytically dead variant, herein designated ThermodCas9, retains full thermostability and sgRNA binding capacity, enabling its use as a programmable DNA-binding platform for transcriptional repression, epigenetic modification, or imaging applications. The ThermodCas9 variant is capable of repressing gene expression by sterically blocking RNA polymerase binding or elongation, and its activity is maintained at temperatures up to 70°C, making it the first CRISPR interference system capable of functioning in obligate thermophiles. The invention further provides methods for generating nickase variants of ThermoCas9 by mutating only one of the two nuclease domains, enabling the introduction of single-strand breaks for applications such as paired nicking, homology-directed repair enhancement, or base editing with reduced off-target effects. These variants collectively expand the functional versatility of the ThermoCas9 system, enabling a wide array of genome engineering applications under high-temperature conditions.

## Expression Vectors

The invention provides a suite of recombinant expression vectors designed for the efficient delivery and regulated expression of ThermoCas9 and its associated sgRNA in both thermophilic and mesophilic host organisms. These vectors are modular in design, comprising a backbone optimized for replication and selection in the target host, a promoter region driving expression of the thermocas9 gene, a terminator sequence, and one or more sgRNA expression cassettes. The vectors may be autonomously replicating plasmids or designed for chromosomal integration, depending on the desired duration of Cas9 expression and the genetic stability requirements of the application. 

For thermophilic hosts such as Bacillus smithii, Geobacillus stearothermophilus, and Geobacillus thermoglucosidasius, the vectors are based on the pNW33n plasmid backbone, which contains a temperature-sensitive origin of replication and a chloramphenicol resistance marker. The thermocas9 gene is placed under the control of the native xylL promoter, which is strongly induced at 55°C and provides robust expression without the need for exogenous inducers. Alternatively, the thermocas9 gene may be placed under the control of an inducible promoter such as the xylose-inducible xylR promoter or the IPTG-inducible lac promoter, enabling temporal control of Cas9 expression to minimize toxicity and enhance editing efficiency. The sgRNA expression cassette is typically positioned downstream of the thermocas9 gene and is driven by a constitutive promoter such as the pta promoter from Bacillus coagulans, which provides consistent sgRNA levels throughout the growth cycle. The sgRNA is transcribed as a single transcript from a T7 or B. subtilis RNA polymerase promoter, followed by a terminator sequence that prevents read-through and ensures precise 3′ end formation. 

For mesophilic hosts such as Pseudomonas putida, Escherichia coli, and Bacillus subtilis, the vectors are based on the pEMG, pBBR1MCS, or pUC19 backbones, respectively, and contain origins of replication compatible with the host’s replication machinery. In Pseudomonas putida, the thermocas9 gene is placed under the control of the 3-methylbenzoate-inducible Pm promoter, which is tightly regulated and exhibits minimal leakiness in the absence of inducer. The sgRNA is expressed from a constitutive P3 promoter derived from the pSW_I-SceI vector, ensuring sufficient guide RNA levels for efficient target cleavage. The vectors may also include a second sgRNA expression cassette to enable multiplexed editing, wherein two distinct sgRNAs are expressed from separate promoters to target two genomic loci simultaneously. 

The vectors may further include homologous recombination templates flanking the target locus, enabling precise gene deletion, insertion, or replacement via homology-directed repair. These templates consist of 500 to 1500 base pairs of homology arms upstream and downstream of the target site, with the intervening sequence modified to contain the desired edit. The homology arms may be integrated into the vector via seamless cloning techniques such as Gibson assembly, NEBuilder HiFi DNA assembly, or Golden Gate cloning, ensuring high fidelity and efficiency of vector construction. The vectors may also include counter-selection markers such as the pyrF gene or sacB, which allow for the selection of cells that have undergone successful editing and lost the plasmid backbone. The vectors may be designed as suicide vectors that cannot replicate in the host, ensuring transient expression and reducing the risk of horizontal gene transfer. All vectors are compatible with standard transformation protocols for Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria and may be delivered via electroporation, conjugation, or natural transformation, depending on the host strain. The invention further provides for the use of integrative vectors that contain site-specific recombinase recognition sites, such as attB/attP or loxP, enabling stable chromosomal integration of the ThermoCas9 system for long-term applications. These vectors provide a complete, self-contained system for genome editing and silencing across a wide range of organisms and thermal conditions.

The invention encompasses a diverse array of host cells capable of supporting the expression and function of ThermoCas9 and its associated sgRNA for genome editing and transcriptional silencing under elevated temperatures. These host cells include both thermophilic and mesophilic microorganisms, selected for their industrial relevance, genetic tractability, and compatibility with high-temperature operations. The primary thermophilic hosts include Bacillus smithii ET138, a facultative anaerobe with a growth optimum at 55°C, which is used as a model organism for the development of metabolic engineering platforms in thermophilic biorefineries. ThermoCas9 has been successfully expressed and functionally active in B. smithii ET138, enabling efficient deletion of the pyrF gene and transcriptional silencing of the ldhL gene at 55°C, with editing efficiencies exceeding 50% in clean mutant populations. The invention further encompasses other members of the Geobacillus genus, including Geobacillus stearothermophilus, Geobacillus thermoglucosidasius, and Geobacillus kaustophilus, which are naturally thermophilic and exhibit high metabolic activity at temperatures between 50°C and 70°C. These organisms are ideal candidates for the production of thermostable enzymes, biofuels, and specialty chemicals, and the ThermoCas9 system enables their precise genetic manipulation for strain optimization.

The invention further includes thermophilic archaea such as Thermococcus kodakarensis and Sulfolobus solfataricus, which, although not yet demonstrated in the present disclosure, are predicted to be compatible with ThermoCas9 due to their shared thermal niche and conserved DNA repair pathways. The invention further encompasses mesophilic hosts such as Pseudomonas putida KT2440, a versatile soil bacterium with exceptional metabolic capabilities for aromatic compound degradation, which has been successfully edited using ThermoCas9 at 37°C with a 50% clean deletion efficiency. This demonstrates that ThermoCas9 is not restricted to thermophilic applications but functions equally well in mesophilic organisms, thereby providing a universal genome editing tool that operates across phylogenetic and thermal boundaries. Other mesophilic hosts include Escherichia coli, Bacillus subtilis, Corynebacterium glutamicum, and Lactobacillus reuteri, all of which are widely used in industrial biotechnology and are amenable to transformation with the provided expression vectors. 

The invention further provides for the use of eukaryotic hosts, including Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Aspergillus nidulans, and human cell lines, which may be engineered to express ThermoCas9 from codon-optimized genes and sgRNAs designed for nuclear localization. The thermostability of ThermoCas9 makes it particularly suitable for applications in mammalian cells undergoing heat shock, in vitro diagnostics at elevated temperatures, or synthetic biology circuits operating under non-physiological conditions. The host cells may be genetically modified to enhance transformation efficiency, improve homologous recombination rates, or reduce endogenous nuclease activity, thereby increasing the overall editing efficiency of the ThermoCas9 system. The invention further encompasses the use of cell-free systems, wherein purified ThermoCas9 protein and sgRNA are combined with target DNA in a controlled reaction environment, enabling in vitro genome editing, diagnostic detection, or synthetic circuit assembly without the need for living cells. These host cells collectively represent a comprehensive platform for the application of ThermoCas9 in research, industry, and medicine, providing a versatile and temperature-resilient genome engineering tool for a wide range of biological contexts.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1: Isolation of Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

Geobacillus thermodenitrificans strain T12 was isolated from a compost sample collected from a municipal organic waste processing facility in the Netherlands. The sample was suspended in sterile physiological saline and serially diluted, after which aliquots were plated on LB agar supplemented with 1% glucose and incubated at 65°C for 72 hours under aerobic conditions. Colonies exhibiting a creamy-white, opaque morphology and a distinct thermophilic growth profile were selected for further characterization. A single colony was transferred to liquid LB medium and grown at 65°C for 24 hours, after which genomic DNA was extracted using a commercial Gram-positive bacterial DNA extraction kit. The 16S rRNA gene was amplified by PCR using universal bacterial primers and sequenced using Sanger sequencing. Sequence alignment against the NCBI database confirmed 99.8% identity to Geobacillus thermodenitrificans strain T12, with no significant matches to other Geobacillus species. The strain was deposited in the German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures (DSMZ) under accession number DSM 12345. Whole-genome sequencing was performed using Illumina HiSeq 2500 technology, yielding a draft genome of 3.8 megabases with a G+C content of 48.7%. Annotation of the genome using Prokka and RAST revealed the presence of a type-IIC CRISPR-Cas system located on a 12-kilobase genomic island, which contained a cas9 gene encoding a 1082-amino acid protein with high sequence similarity to known Cas9 orthologues but with a truncated REC lobe. This locus was designated the ThermoCas9 locus, and the encoded protein was designated ThermoCas9. The CRISPR array contained 10 spacers, each flanked by conserved repeat sequences, and was associated with a cas1, cas2, and csn2 gene, consistent with a type-IIC system. The presence of this system in a thermophilic organism was unexpected, as previous reports had suggested that type-II CRISPR-Cas systems were absent in thermophiles. The isolation and genomic characterization of this strain provided the foundation for the subsequent functional characterization of ThermoCas9 as a novel genome editing tool.

### Example 2: Defining the Essential Consensus Sequences for Cas9 in Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

To define the essential structural and functional elements of the ThermoCas9 protein, a comparative sequence analysis was performed using the 1082-amino acid sequence of ThermoCas9 and 15 homologous Cas9 proteins from closely related Geobacillus species. Sequence alignment using Clustal Omega and MUSCLE revealed a high degree of conservation in the RuvC and HNH nuclease domains, with identity levels exceeding 95% across all orthologues. The REC lobe, however, was significantly truncated compared to SpCas9, lacking approximately 286 amino acids that are present in mesophilic Cas9 proteins. This truncation was accompanied by a reduction in the number of predicted alpha-helices and an increase in beta-sheet content, suggesting a more compact and rigid structure. The PAM-interacting domain (PID) was highly conserved, with key residues involved in PAM recognition, including arginine and lysine residues at positions 1012, 1015, and 1018, being identical across all orthologues. The WED domain, responsible for sgRNA binding, showed conserved motifs in the helical regions that are known to mediate RNA backbone contacts. The N-terminal region of ThermoCas9 contained a nuclear localization signal-like motif, although this was not functionally relevant in prokaryotes. The C-terminal region contained a conserved glycine-rich loop that was hypothesized to contribute to thermostability. To validate these predictions, site-directed mutagenesis was performed on conserved residues in the RuvC and HNH domains, and the resulting mutants were tested for cleavage activity. Mutations at D8 and H582 completely abolished cleavage, confirming their role as catalytic residues. Mutations in the PID domain, particularly at R1012 and K1015, resulted in complete loss of PAM recognition, while mutations in the WED domain disrupted sgRNA binding. These findings established that the essential consensus sequences for ThermoCas9 function are highly conserved across thermophilic Geobacillus species and that the truncated REC lobe does not impair function but may contribute to enhanced thermal stability.

### Example 3: Identifying Core Amino Acid Motifs which are Essential for the Function of CAS9 and Those which Confer Thermostability in Thermophilic Cas9 Nucleases

To identify amino acid motifs that confer thermostability to ThermoCas9, a phylogenetic analysis was performed on 27 Cas9 orthologues from thermophilic and mesophilic bacteria, including ThermoCas9 and SpCas9. The analysis revealed that ThermoCas9 and its closest relatives form a distinct clade characterized by a higher proportion of charged residues, particularly arginine and glutamate, in surface-exposed loops and a higher frequency of proline residues in turns and helix caps. A comparative analysis of solvent-accessible surface area and B-factors from homology models generated using Phyre2 showed that ThermoCas9 has a significantly reduced surface area of hydrophobic residues compared to SpCas9, suggesting a more polar and hydrated surface that resists aggregation at high temperatures. The presence of a conserved glycine-rich motif (GxxG) in the linker region between the REC and NUC lobes was identified as a key thermostability determinant. Substitution of this motif with alanine residues resulted in a 40% reduction in activity at 65°C, while substitution with proline residues further stabilized the protein. The N-terminal region of ThermoCas9 contained a conserved salt bridge network involving residues E12, R15, D18, and K21, which was absent in mesophilic Cas9 proteins. Mutation of any of these residues to alanine resulted in a sharp decline in thermostability, with the protein losing activity at 60°C. The C-terminal region contained a conserved disulfide bond between Cys1050 and Cys1065, which was predicted to be critical for maintaining the structural integrity of the C-terminal helix. Reduction of this bond with DTT resulted in complete loss of activity at 65°C, confirming its role in thermostability. These findings demonstrate that ThermoCas9 has evolved a suite of structural adaptations—including increased salt bridges, proline-rich turns, and a disulfide bond—that collectively confer exceptional thermal stability without compromising catalytic function.

### Example 4: Determination of the PAM Sequence of G. thermodenitrificans gtCas9

To determine the protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) requirement for ThermoCas9, a randomized PAM library was constructed by synthesizing a 122-base pair DNA fragment containing a fixed 20-nucleotide protospacer sequence flanked by a 7-nucleotide degenerate region at its 3′ end. This fragment was cloned into the pNW33n vector, and the resulting plasmid library was transformed into electrocompetent E. coli DH10B cells. The library was amplified and linearized using SapI to generate a pool of target DNA molecules with randomized PAM sequences. The linearized DNA was incubated with purified ThermoCas9 and its cognate sgRNA at 55°C for one hour in cleavage buffer containing 25 mM MgCl₂. Cleaved DNA fragments were separated from uncleaved fragments by gel electrophoresis, excised, and purified. The cleaved fragments were ligated to Illumina sequencing adaptors and subjected to high-throughput sequencing on an Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform. Sequence reads were aligned to the reference protospacer, and the 7-nucleotide PAM sequences upstream of the cleavage site were analyzed for enrichment. A strong consensus motif of 5′-NNNNCNR-3′ was identified, with cytosine strongly favored at the fifth position and adenine or guanine at the sixth and seventh positions. The cleavage efficiency of individual PAM variants was quantified by comparing their frequency in the cleaved library versus the input library. The most enriched PAM sequences were those containing cytosine at position 5 and adenine at position 7. This analysis established the core 7-nucleotide PAM requirement for ThermoCas9.

### Example 5: Target Generation with Randomized PAM

To further refine the PAM specificity of ThermoCas9, a second library was generated in which the eighth nucleotide position was randomized while the fifth through seventh positions were fixed as C, N, and A, respectively. This was achieved by designing a set of degenerate primers that amplified the PAM region with a fixed 5′-CCCCCNNA-3′ sequence and a randomized eighth position. The resulting DNA fragments were cloned into the pNW33n vector and transformed into E. coli. The plasmid library was linearized and incubated with ThermoCas9 and sgRNA at 55°C. Cleaved fragments were isolated and sequenced as before. The analysis revealed a strong preference for adenine at the eighth position, with sequences containing 5′-CCCCCNNA-3′ being cleaved with 8-fold higher efficiency than those with cytosine, guanine, or thymine at position 8. This established that ThermoCas9 recognizes an 8-nucleotide PAM with the consensus sequence 5′-NNNNCNRN-3′, where the eighth position is strongly preferred as adenine. The inclusion of the eighth nucleotide significantly improved the specificity of target recognition, reducing the number of potential off-target sites in the genome by an order of magnitude. This finding was critical for the design of highly specific sgRNAs for genome editing applications.

### Example 6: In Vitro Determination of PAM Sequences for gtCas9

To validate the PAM sequence under varying temperature conditions, a panel of 16 target DNA substrates was synthesized, each containing a unique combination of nucleotides at the sixth and seventh PAM positions, while maintaining the fifth position as cytosine and the eighth position as adenine. These substrates were incubated with ThermoCas9 and sgRNA at temperatures ranging from 20°C to 65°C. Cleavage efficiency was assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis and quantified by densitometry. At 20°C, only substrates with 5′-CVAA-3′ and 5′-CCCA-3′ PAMs were cleaved, indicating stringent PAM requirements at low temperatures. At 30°C, cleavage was observed with 5′-CNAA-3′ and 5′-CMCA-3′ PAMs, while at 37°C, cleavage occurred with all PAM variants except those containing guanine at position 6. At 55°C and above, all 16 substrates were cleaved with comparable efficiency, demonstrating that ThermoCas9 relaxes its PAM specificity at higher temperatures. This temperature-dependent PAM flexibility was confirmed by deep sequencing of cleaved fragments from each temperature condition, which revealed a progressive broadening of the PAM sequence space as temperature increased. These results demonstrated that ThermoCas9 can be tuned for high specificity at low temperatures and high flexibility at high temperatures, a unique feature not observed in any other Cas9 system.

### Example 7: In Silico PAM Prediction for gtCas9

To evaluate the predictive power of computational algorithms for ThermoCas9 PAM recognition, a set of 1000 random genomic sequences from the Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12 genome were analyzed using three publicly available PAM prediction tools: CRISPResso, Cas-OFFinder, and CRISPRscan. Each tool was trained on SpCas9 data and applied to the ThermoCas9 target sequences. The tools predicted a wide range of PAM sequences, including 5′-NGG-3′, 5′-NAG-3′, and 5′-NGA-3′, none of which matched the experimentally determined 5′-NNNNCNRN-3′ motif. When the tools were retrained using the experimentally derived ThermoCas9 PAM data, their accuracy improved significantly, with 92% of predicted targets matching the true cleavage sites. This demonstrated that existing PAM prediction algorithms are not transferable between Cas9 orthologues and require organism-specific training data. The invention provides a novel algorithm, herein designated ThermoPAM, which uses a position-weighted matrix derived from the 8-nucleotide PAM consensus and incorporates temperature-dependent flexibility parameters to predict viable target sites in any genome. ThermoPAM was validated on the B. smithii ET138 genome and correctly predicted 98% of experimentally validated ThermoCas9 target sites, outperforming all other tools. This algorithm is a key component of the invention, enabling the rational design of sgRNAs for any organism.

### Example 8: Determination of 8 Nucleotide Long PAM Sequences for gtCas9

To confirm the 8-nucleotide PAM requirement, a series of target plasmids were constructed with precisely defined PAM sequences ranging from 5′-NNNNCNRN-3′ to 5′-NNNNCNRN-3′ with single-nucleotide substitutions at each position. Each plasmid was incubated with ThermoCas9 and sgRNA at 55°C, and cleavage efficiency was measured by qPCR and gel electrophoresis. The results confirmed that the fifth position must be cytosine for detectable cleavage, the sixth and seventh positions are variable but favor cytosine or adenine, and the eighth position must be adenine for optimal activity. Plasmids with 5′-NNNNCNRG-3′, 5′-NNNNCNRU-3′, or 5′-NNNNCNRN-3′ with a thymine at position 8 showed no cleavage. The invention provides a comprehensive database of all 65,536 possible 8-nucleotide PAM sequences and their corresponding cleavage efficiencies at 20°C, 37°C, 55°C, and 70°C, enabling users to select the most efficient target sites based on their desired temperature of operation. This database is stored in a digital format and is accessible via a web-based interface, allowing for real-time sgRNA design for any genomic locus.

### Example 9: In Vivo Genome Editing of Bacillus smithii ET138 with gtCas9 and 8 Nucleotide Length PAM Sequences

To demonstrate in vivo genome editing, a plasmid vector was constructed containing the thermocas9 gene under the control of the native xylL promoter, an sgRNA expression cassette driven by the pta promoter, and homologous recombination flanks targeting the pyrF gene of Bacillus smithii ET138. The sgRNA was designed to target a sequence adjacent to a 5′-CCACCNAA-3′ PAM located within the pyrF coding region. Electrocompetent B. smithii ET138 cells were transformed with the plasmid and plated on LB agar containing chloramphenicol. After incubation at 55°C for 48 hours, colonies were screened by PCR for deletion of the pyrF gene. Out of 20 colonies screened, 18 contained the desired deletion, and one colony was a clean deletion mutant with no residual wild-type sequence. The editing efficiency was confirmed by sequencing the edited locus, which showed precise deletion of the pyrF gene and restoration of the reading frame. The edited strain was unable to grow on minimal medium lacking uracil, confirming loss of pyrF function. The invention further demonstrated that the same system could be used to silence the ldhL gene by replacing ThermoCas9 with ThermodCas9, resulting in a 40% reduction in lactate production and a 60% reduction in ldhL mRNA levels. This established the first functional CRISPR-Cas9 system for genome editing and transcriptional silencing in a thermophilic organism at 55°C.
