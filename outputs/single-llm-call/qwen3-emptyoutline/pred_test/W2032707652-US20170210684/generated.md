## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of molecular sieving and catalytic materials, particularly to the design, selection, and application of crystalline microporous zeolite structures for the separation of complex liquid mixtures and the catalytic transformation of long-chain hydrocarbons. More specifically, the invention encompasses novel zeolite frameworks identified through high-throughput computational screening that exhibit exceptional selectivity and adsorption capacity for ethanol over water in dilute aqueous solutions, as well as superior affinity for linear alkanes over their branched isomers under conditions relevant to industrial hydroisomerization and dewaxing processes. The invention further includes methods for identifying such zeolite structures through predictive modeling that account for non-ideal mixture behavior, conformational flexibility of large hydrocarbon molecules, and the precise geometric and electrostatic environment within nanoporous channels. These zeolites are suitable for use in adsorptive separation systems, membrane-based purification units, and bifunctional catalytic reactors employed in biofuel production, petroleum refining, and chemical manufacturing. The invention represents a paradigm shift from empirical trial-and-error approaches to materials discovery, replacing them with a systematic, physics-based computational framework capable of navigating vast chemical spaces to pinpoint structures with optimal performance characteristics for industrially relevant, multi-component systems involving hydrogen bonding and steric complexity.

## BACKGROUND

The petrochemical and biofuel industries rely heavily on the efficient separation and transformation of complex organic mixtures, many of which involve molecules with similar molecular weights, polarities, and shapes, making conventional separation techniques such as distillation economically and energetically prohibitive. In the context of biofuel production, the separation of ethanol from dilute aqueous fermentation broths—typically containing less than 15 weight percent ethanol—is a critical and costly step, primarily due to the formation of a low-boiling azeotrope at approximately 95.6 weight percent ethanol, which renders simple distillation insufficient for achieving fuel-grade purity. While membrane technologies and adsorbents have been explored as alternatives, the most widely studied material, silicalite-1 (MFI topology), suffers from reduced selectivity under realistic process conditions due to the co-adsorption of water molecules, which form hydrogen-bonded networks within the pore structure and compete with ethanol for adsorption sites. Similarly, in petroleum refining, the removal of long-chain linear alkanes (waxes) from lubricating oils and diesel fuels is essential to improve low-temperature flow properties. Hydroisomerization, a catalytic process that selectively converts these linear alkanes into slightly branched isomers without excessive cracking, is commonly performed using zeolites impregnated with noble metals such as platinum or palladium. The efficiency of this process depends critically on the ability of the zeolite framework to preferentially adsorb linear alkanes over their branched counterparts, thereby concentrating the desired reactants near catalytic sites while allowing the branched products to desorb rapidly and avoid further degradation. Existing zeolites such as FER, MTT, and ATO have demonstrated utility in these applications, but their performance is often suboptimal, and their structural features are not fully understood in terms of the precise molecular interactions that govern selectivity. Traditional approaches to identifying improved materials have relied on laborious synthesis and testing of known frameworks, or on heuristic rules based on simple geometric parameters such as pore diameter or surface area. These methods have proven inadequate for systems involving large, articulated molecules or polar, hydrogen-bonding species, where subtle variations in channel topology, atomic arrangement, and electrostatic potential can lead to dramatic differences in performance that are not predictable from first principles without detailed molecular simulations. Furthermore, the number of theoretically possible zeolite structures is vast, with over three hundred thousand thermodynamically accessible frameworks identified in computational databases, yet only a tiny fraction have been synthesized or tested experimentally. As a result, the discovery of next-generation materials for these applications has been hindered by the inability to efficiently screen candidate structures at the molecular level, particularly under industrially relevant conditions that involve multi-component mixtures, non-ideal thermodynamics, and complex conformational behavior. There exists, therefore, a critical need for a systematic, predictive methodology capable of identifying zeolite structures with unprecedented selectivity and capacity for challenging separations and transformations, without reliance on prior experimental knowledge or oversimplified assumptions.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel class of zeolite structures and a computational methodology for their identification, specifically designed for the selective adsorption of ethanol from aqueous solutions and the preferential adsorption of linear alkanes over branched isomers in hydrocarbon mixtures. The invention is based on the discovery that certain zeolite frameworks, previously unexplored or untested in these applications, exhibit extraordinary performance characteristics due to their unique combination of pore geometry, channel connectivity, and internal surface electrostatics. For ethanol/water separation, the invention identifies zeolites such as FER, ATN*, VFI*, and OWE* as superior candidates, with FER demonstrating exceptional selectivity even at extremely low ethanol concentrations (as low as 0.12 wt%), enabling retentate concentrations that exceed the ethanol/water azeotropic composition under optimized conditions. The high selectivity arises from a spatial arrangement of adsorption sites that permits ethanol molecules to bind favorably while preventing the formation of extended hydrogen-bonding networks with water, thereby minimizing co-adsorption. For hydroisomerization and dewaxing, the invention identifies novel zeolite structures from the Predicted Crystallography Open Database (PCOD), including PCOD-8113534, that exhibit selectivity for linear alkanes over branched isomers that exceeds known industrial materials by orders of magnitude. These structures feature one-dimensional 12-ring channels with elliptical cross-sections, narrow dimensions (semiminor axis ~0.47 nm), and minimal channel bumpiness, creating a steric environment that strongly favors the alignment of linear alkanes while sterically excluding branched isomers. The invention further encompasses a high-throughput computational screening workflow that integrates massively parallel grand canonical Monte Carlo simulations, accurate transferable force fields, and advanced conformational sampling algorithms to evaluate adsorption behavior across thousands of zeolite frameworks under realistic process conditions. This workflow accounts for the full complexity of multi-component mixtures, including explicit treatment of hydrogen bonding, molecular flexibility, and non-ideal solution behavior, and employs multiple performance metrics that are not tied to any specific separation or catalytic process, thereby identifying materials with broad applicability. The invention enables the rapid identification of optimal zeolite candidates for synthesis and implementation in industrial processes, significantly reducing the time, cost, and resource burden traditionally associated with materials discovery. The identified zeolites may be synthesized in their all-silica form for separation applications or doped with catalytic metals for hydroisomerization, and may be employed in fixed-bed adsorbers, membrane modules, or catalytic reactors to achieve unprecedented efficiency in biofuel purification and petroleum refining.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example

The invention is exemplified by two distinct but related applications: the purification of ethanol from dilute aqueous solutions and the selective conversion of long-chain linear alkanes into branched isomers for improved fuel and lubricant properties. In the first application, a zeolite material is employed in an adsorptive separation system to remove ethanol from a fermentation broth containing as little as 0.12 weight percent ethanol at a temperature of 323 Kelvin. The zeolite selected for this application possesses a framework topology designated as FER, characterized by a three-dimensional pore system composed of intersecting 10-membered ring channels with a unique arrangement of adsorption sites that spatially isolate ethanol molecules from water. Under these conditions, the zeolite achieves an ethanol selectivity exceeding 18,000 relative to water, meaning that the concentration of ethanol in the adsorbed phase is more than 18,000 times greater than its concentration in the bulk solution. This selectivity is sufficient to produce a retentate stream with an ethanol concentration exceeding 95.6 weight percent, surpassing the azeotropic composition and thereby enabling direct recovery of fuel-grade ethanol without the need for energy-intensive azeotropic distillation. The high performance of this material is not attributable to a single geometric parameter such as pore size or surface area, but rather to the precise spatial distribution of oxygen atoms and silicon tetrahedra within the pore walls, which create localized regions of favorable van der Waals interaction with ethanol while simultaneously disrupting the hydrogen-bonding network required for water to remain adsorbed. In contrast, the widely studied MFI topology, which has been the subject of decades of research for this application, exhibits significantly lower selectivity under the same conditions due to enlarged intersection regions that permit water molecules to form cooperative hydrogen-bonded clusters, thereby reducing the energetic advantage of ethanol adsorption. In a second application, a zeolite material is used as a catalyst support in a hydroisomerization reactor operating at a temperature of 573 Kelvin and a pressure of 3 megapascals, where a mixture of linear alkanes ranging from C18 to C30 is fed in the presence of a noble metal catalyst such as platinum. The zeolite selected for this application, designated PCOD-8113534, possesses a one-dimensional channel architecture with an elliptical cross-section, a semiminor axis of 0.47 nanometers, and a channel bumpiness value below 0.1 nanometers. This geometry allows linear alkanes to adopt extended zig-zag conformations aligned along the channel’s major axis, maximizing van der Waals interactions with the pore walls, while sterically excluding branched isomers such as 2-methylheptadecane and 2,2-dimethylhexadecane, which cannot adopt conformations compatible with the narrow, smooth channel. As a result, the linear alkanes are preferentially adsorbed and concentrated near catalytic sites, where they undergo isomerization to mono-branched derivatives, while the branched products, once formed, are rapidly desorbed due to their poor fit within the channel, preventing further cracking into low-value light gases. The selectivity of PCOD-8113534 for linear over branched alkanes is more than 16,000 times greater than that of conventional zeolites such as MTW, which possess larger, more open channels that accommodate branched species and thereby reduce catalytic efficiency. The invention further encompasses the use of these zeolites in multi-step separation processes, such as a two-stage adsorption system in which a first adsorbent, such as VFI*, is employed to concentrate ethanol from a 5 weight percent feed to a 43 weight percent retentate, which is then fed to a second adsorbent, such as ATN*, to achieve final purification to azeotropic purity. Similarly, in hydrocarbon processing, a combination of zeolites may be employed to selectively adsorb different fractions of a complex mixture, with one material targeting C18–C24 alkanes and another targeting C24–C30 alkanes, thereby enabling fine-tuned dewaxing across a broad molecular weight range. The invention thus provides not only specific materials but also a comprehensive framework for their deployment in industrial systems, enabling transformative improvements in energy efficiency, product yield, and process economics.

### Methods

The identification of the novel zeolite structures disclosed herein was achieved through a rigorous, multi-stage computational screening workflow that integrates advanced molecular simulation techniques, high-performance computing, and physics-based modeling of complex molecular systems. The process began with the compilation of a comprehensive database of zeolite frameworks, including all 402 experimentally observed and idealized structures from the Structure Commission of the International Zeolite Association (IZA-SC), as well as 331,172 thermodynamically stable, predicted structures from the Predicted Crystallography Open Database (PCOD). Each structure was represented as a rigid, all-silica framework with atomic coordinates refined to minimize strain and maximize symmetry, ensuring that the intrinsic adsorption properties of the pore architecture could be evaluated without interference from compositional variations such as aluminum substitution or cation occupancy. The adsorption behavior of each framework was then simulated using configurational-bias grand canonical Monte Carlo (CB-GCMC) methods, which enable the accurate calculation of adsorption isotherms, Henry’s constants, and selectivities under conditions that mimic real industrial processes. For ethanol/water separation, simulations were conducted at six distinct solution-phase ethanol concentrations ranging from 0.12 to 41 weight percent and at a temperature of 323 Kelvin, corresponding to the typical operating range of fermentation broths. The chemical potentials of ethanol and water in the bulk solution were derived from prior Gibbs ensemble simulations, ensuring that the adsorption conditions were thermodynamically consistent with experimental feed compositions. For hydrocarbon applications, simulations were performed at both the infinite-dilution limit and at a representative process pressure of 3 megapascals and a temperature of 573 Kelvin, using an equimolar mixture of n-octadecane, n-tetracosane, n-triacontane, 2-methylheptadecane, 4-methylheptadecane, and 2,2-dimethylhexadecane to represent the complexity of real feedstocks. All intermolecular interactions were modeled using the TraPPE force field for hydrocarbons and ethanol, the TIP4P force field for water, and the TraPPE-zeo force field for the zeolite framework, which together provide a balanced description of dispersion, electrostatic, and hydrogen-bonding interactions. The long-range electrostatic contributions were treated using Ewald summation, while van der Waals interactions were truncated at 1.4 nanometers with tail corrections applied to ensure energy conservation. To accurately sample the conformational space of long-chain alkanes, the coupled-decoupled configurational-bias Monte Carlo (CD-CBMC) algorithm was employed, which enables efficient insertion and deletion moves by biasing the sampling of internal dihedral angles and chain conformations. Identity-switch moves were also implemented to facilitate the exchange of different hydrocarbon molecules during simulations, thereby improving the convergence of selectivity calculations. The simulations were executed on Mira, a leadership-class supercomputer at Argonne National Laboratory, utilizing a two-level parallel execution hierarchy that simultaneously evaluated up to 16,384 zeolite structures, with each structure simulated across 256 to 1,024 compute cores to achieve sufficient statistical sampling. For ethanol/water systems, each concentration point required one million Monte Carlo steps, while for hydrocarbon systems, initial screening involved 90,000 steps per structure, followed by 1.6 million steps for the top candidates to ensure high-precision performance metrics. The performance of each zeolite was quantified using composite metrics that combined adsorption capacity, selectivity, and process relevance: for ethanol separation, the performance index was defined as the product of ethanol selectivity and ethanol loading, while for hydrocarbon processing, a multi-dimensional metric was constructed from the Henry’s constant for n-octadecane, the selectivity for linear over branched isomers, and the selectivity for longer over shorter linear alkanes. These metrics were designed to be process-agnostic, enabling the identification of materials suitable for a wide range of separation and catalytic technologies. The results of these simulations were analyzed to identify structural motifs associated with high performance, revealing that selectivity in ethanol/water systems is governed by the spatial separation of adsorption sites that prevent water network formation, while in hydrocarbon systems, selectivity is determined by the combination of narrow channel dimensions, elliptical cross-sections, and low channel bumpiness. The computational predictions were validated through experimental adsorption measurements on high-quality samples of MFI and FER zeolites, where the predicted ethanol loadings matched experimental data within 5 percent across the full concentration range, confirming the accuracy of the simulation protocol. This methodology represents a revolutionary departure from traditional materials discovery, replacing decades of empirical screening with a single, scalable computational platform capable of evaluating hundreds of thousands of candidate structures in a matter of weeks.

### Exemplary Embodiments

The invention encompasses a plurality of specific zeolite structures identified through the computational screening methodology described above, each of which is exemplified by its unique framework topology, pore geometry, and performance characteristics in the context of either ethanol/water separation or hydrocarbon isomerization. One exemplary embodiment is a zeolite material possessing the FER framework topology, characterized by a three-dimensional network of intersecting 10-membered ring channels with a mean pore diameter of approximately 0.55 nanometers and a distinct arrangement of adsorption sites that are spatially isolated by narrow windows and constricted linkages. In this embodiment, the zeolite is synthesized in its all-silica form to maximize hydrophobicity and minimize the presence of acidic sites that might promote water co-adsorption. When exposed to a dilute aqueous ethanol solution containing 0.43 weight percent ethanol at 323 Kelvin, this material achieves an ethanol selectivity of greater than 5,100 and an ethanol loading of 1.8 moles per kilogram, resulting in a retentate composition that exceeds the azeotropic point of 95.6 weight percent ethanol. The high selectivity arises from the fact that ethanol molecules preferentially occupy sites located within the 10-membered ring channels, where they form favorable van der Waals interactions with the silicate walls, while water molecules are sterically excluded from forming extended hydrogen-bonded networks due to the absence of adjacent, aligned oxygen atoms capable of sustaining cooperative interactions. This structural feature is absent in the MFI topology, where enlarged intersections permit water molecules to bridge multiple adsorption sites, thereby reducing selectivity. A second exemplary embodiment involves a zeolite with the ATN* framework topology, which exhibits exceptional selectivity at higher ethanol concentrations, such as 15 weight percent, where it achieves a selectivity exceeding 10,000 while maintaining a loading of 2.2 moles per kilogram. This material possesses a unique combination of narrow, one-dimensional channels and large, isolated cages that allow ethanol to adsorb densely while preventing water from entering the pore system. The ATN* structure is particularly suited for multi-stage separation processes, where it can be employed as a second-stage adsorbent to polish a pre-concentrated ethanol stream obtained from a first-stage material such as VFI*. A third exemplary embodiment is a zeolite possessing the VFI* framework, which exhibits a large pore diameter of approximately 1.2 nanometers and a high ethanol loading capacity of over 2.0 moles per kilogram even at the lowest ethanol concentration of 0.12 weight percent. Although its selectivity is modest at around 620, its high capacity makes it ideal for initial concentration steps, where it can extract a large fraction of ethanol from a fermentation broth, thereby reducing the load on downstream purification units. A fourth exemplary embodiment is a zeolite with the OWE* framework, which demonstrates high selectivity at intermediate ethanol concentrations and is characterized by a complex, interconnected pore system that provides multiple pathways for ethanol diffusion while maintaining steric exclusion of water. A fifth exemplary embodiment is a zeolite with the ESV* framework, which, although currently known only in aluminophosphate form, is predicted to exhibit superior performance in its all-silica variant and is therefore a prime target for synthetic efforts. In the context of hydrocarbon processing, a sixth exemplary embodiment is a zeolite possessing the PCOD-8113534 framework, which features a one-dimensional 12-membered ring channel with an elliptical cross-section, a semiminor axis of 0.47 nanometers, and a channel bumpiness value of less than 0.1 nanometers. This structure exhibits a selectivity for linear over branched alkanes exceeding 16,000 under hydroisomerization conditions, far surpassing the performance of conventional materials such as ATO, MRE, and MTW. The narrow, smooth channel forces linear alkanes to adopt fully extended zig-zag conformations aligned along the channel axis, maximizing contact with the pore walls and minimizing conformational entropy loss, while branched isomers are unable to enter the channel due to steric hindrance. A seventh exemplary embodiment is a zeolite with the ATO framework, which, while known in the prior art, is identified herein as a high-performing material when evaluated under the full complexity of a multi-component hydrocarbon mixture, exhibiting a high loading of n-octadecane and a selectivity for linear over branched isomers of approximately 21. An eighth exemplary embodiment is a zeolite with the MRE framework, which possesses a similar one-dimensional channel architecture but with slightly larger dimensions, resulting in a moderate selectivity of approximately 15 and a high loading capacity, making it suitable for applications where high throughput is prioritized over maximum selectivity. A ninth exemplary embodiment is a zeolite with the CAN framework, which, although not previously recognized for hydroisomerization, is identified herein as a high-performing material due to its favorable combination of moderate selectivity and high loading, particularly under conditions of elevated pressure. A tenth exemplary embodiment is a zeolite with the EUO framework, which exhibits a lower selectivity for linear over branched alkanes but compensates with a high selectivity for longer-chain alkanes, making it ideal for processing feeds rich in C24–C30 components. Each of these embodiments may be synthesized using conventional hydrothermal methods, with the all-silica variants produced using structure-directing agents such as tetrapropylammonium or pyridinium ions, and the catalytic variants prepared by ion exchange or impregnation with platinum, palladium, or other group VIII metals. The zeolites may be formed into pellets, extrudates, or membranes for use in fixed-bed adsorbers, pressure swing adsorption units, or membrane reactors, and may be integrated into existing refinery or biofuel production infrastructure with minimal modification. The invention further includes methods for synthesizing these materials in defect-free, high-purity form, as well as methods for regenerating and reusing them over multiple cycles without loss of performance, thereby ensuring long-term economic viability. The combination of these specific embodiments, each tailored to a distinct operational regime and feed composition, represents a comprehensive portfolio of materials capable of revolutionizing the efficiency and sustainability of biofuel and petroleum refining processes.
