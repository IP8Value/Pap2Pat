# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to programmatic advertising systems and, more specifically, to adaptive risk-aware bid optimization in real-time bidding environments. The invention provides a novel framework for dynamically determining optimal bid prices in online advertising auctions by integrating predictive uncertainty quantification with a context-sensitive representation of a demand-side platform’s risk tendency. This framework operates within the constraints of a limited budget and a finite number of remaining auction opportunities, leveraging reinforcement learning principles to maximize cumulative performance metrics such as total clicks or conversions while accounting for the inherent stochasticity of user response predictions and the evolving financial and temporal state of the advertiser’s campaign. The technical domain encompasses computational advertising, machine learning for sequential decision-making, financial risk modeling adapted to digital marketing, and model-based reinforcement learning applied to constrained optimization problems under partial observability. The invention is particularly suited for deployment in high-volume, low-latency advertising exchanges where bid decisions must be made in milliseconds, and where the accuracy of click-through rate or conversion rate predictions is subject to significant epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty due to sparse data, feature non-stationarity, and competitive market dynamics.

## BACKGROUND

In the modern digital advertising ecosystem, real-time bidding (RTB) has emerged as the dominant mechanism for the automated purchase of individual ad impressions across millions of websites and mobile applications. In this environment, demand-side platforms (DSPs) act on behalf of advertisers to compete in instantaneous auctions triggered by user interactions, with each auction determining whether an ad is displayed and at what cost. The success of a DSP hinges critically on its ability to optimize bid prices in a manner that maximizes the advertiser’s key performance indicator—such as total clicks, conversions, or return on ad spend—while adhering to a predetermined budget and a fixed number of available auction opportunities within a given campaign horizon. Traditional approaches to bid optimization have relied on two primary components: first, the estimation of an ad impression’s expected value, typically through machine learning models that predict click-through rates or conversion rates; and second, the application of a deterministic or heuristic pricing rule to convert this estimated value into a bid price. However, these methods operate under the fundamental assumption that the predicted value is a reliable and precise point estimate, effectively ignoring the inherent uncertainty in such predictions. In practice, this assumption is frequently violated due to incomplete historical data, feature sparsity, model overfitting, and non-stationary user behavior, all of which introduce significant variability into the estimated value of an impression. As a result, bid decisions based solely on point estimates often lead to suboptimal outcomes, including excessive spending on low-value impressions or missed opportunities on high-potential ones.

Further compounding this challenge is the dynamic and interdependent nature of the bidding process. Each bid decision affects not only the immediate outcome of the current auction but also the state of the campaign for all subsequent auctions, particularly through the depletion of budget and the reduction of remaining auction opportunities. A DSP that bids too aggressively early in a campaign may exhaust its budget before reaching high-value impressions later, while a DSP that bids too conservatively may fail to secure sufficient inventory to meet its performance targets. This sequential dependency renders independent bid decisions ineffective and necessitates a holistic, state-aware optimization strategy. While recent advances have introduced reinforcement learning techniques to model the bidding process as a Markov decision process, these methods continue to rely on point estimates of impression value and do not explicitly account for the varying risk preferences of the DSP across different stages of a campaign. For instance, a DSP with a large remaining budget and few remaining auctions may adopt a more aggressive stance, whereas one with limited budget and many remaining auctions may prioritize conservation. This risk tendency is not a static property but a dynamic function of the campaign’s resource state—namely, the remaining budget and the number of remaining auctions—and must be modeled adaptively to reflect the DSP’s strategic posture.

Moreover, existing approaches fail to capture the intrinsic relationship between prediction uncertainty and risk tendency. In financial risk management, the concept of Value at Risk (VaR) has long been used to quantify the potential loss under a given confidence level, effectively balancing expected return against volatility. Analogously, in the context of RTB, the uncertainty in a predicted click-through rate can be interpreted as a measure of the potential over- or under-estimation of an impression’s true value. When combined with a risk-sensitive weighting mechanism, this uncertainty can be leveraged to adjust the effective value of an impression in a way that aligns with the DSP’s current risk tolerance. However, no prior work has formally established this relationship or integrated it into a reinforcement learning framework that simultaneously optimizes for both the expected cumulative reward and the stability of outcomes under uncertainty. Consequently, current systems are unable to adaptively modulate their bidding behavior in response to both the reliability of their predictions and the urgency of their resource constraints. This gap results in suboptimal campaign performance, inefficient budget utilization, and an inability to respond to changing market conditions in a principled and scalable manner. The need therefore exists for a unified, theoretically grounded, and computationally efficient framework that explicitly incorporates prediction uncertainty and dynamically evolving risk tendency into the bid optimization process, enabling DSPs to make more informed, robust, and adaptive bidding decisions under real-world constraints.

## SUMMARY

The present invention discloses a novel adaptive risk-aware bidding algorithm that fundamentally advances the state of the art in real-time bidding systems by integrating the quantification of prediction uncertainty with a dynamically modeled risk tendency of a demand-side platform into a unified reinforcement learning framework. The invention operates by defining the effective value of an ad impression as a linear combination of its predicted mean value and a risk-adjusted term derived from the product of its prediction uncertainty and a context-dependent risk tendency factor. This formulation is grounded in a theoretical analysis that demonstrates the equivalence of this linear combination to the optimal Value at Risk (VaR) bid price under a second-price auction mechanism, thereby ensuring that the resulting bid strategy maximizes the probability of achieving a desired cumulative performance level while minimizing the risk of budget overruns or performance shortfalls. The risk tendency factor is a function of the DSP’s current resource state, specifically the number of remaining auctions and the remaining budget, and is independent of the specific features of the current bid request, reflecting the strategic posture of the DSP rather than the characteristics of the individual impression.

The invention provides two distinct instantiations for determining the risk tendency factor: a first instantiation based on expert knowledge that encodes three fundamental behavioral properties of rational bidders—namely, that risk tendency is non-negative when budget sufficiency is achieved, that it increases monotonically with remaining budget and decreases monotonically with remaining auction count, and that it is approximately proportional to the ratio of remaining budget to remaining auctions under conditions of abundant resources. This expert-driven formulation employs a hyperbolic tangent function scaled by a budget sufficiency threshold and a slope parameter to ensure bounded, interpretable, and behaviorally consistent risk tendencies. A second instantiation employs a self-supervised reinforcement learning mechanism that learns the risk tendency mapping directly from historical bidding experience using a multi-layer perceptron. This method maintains an experience buffer of high-performing episodes, samples batches of state-risk-reward tuples, and trains the neural network to approximate the risk tendency that historically led to superior cumulative rewards, thereby eliminating the need for manual hyperparameter tuning and enabling adaptation to new market conditions without domain-specific knowledge.

The bid price for each auction is determined through a model-based reinforcement learning procedure that iteratively computes the expected cumulative reward for each possible state, considering both the probability of winning the auction and the future state transitions under a given bid. The optimal bid price is then selected as the highest bid that does not cause the expected cumulative reward to decline, ensuring that each decision maximizes long-term performance under the constraints of the remaining budget and auction count. The invention is implemented as a software system capable of integration into existing DSP infrastructure, capable of processing bid requests in real time with minimal latency, and adaptable to different performance objectives, budget constraints, and market environments. Experimental evaluations on two large-scale public datasets demonstrate that the proposed framework consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, including linear bidding strategies and conventional model-based reinforcement learning methods, by achieving higher total click volumes and more efficient budget utilization. Ablation studies confirm that both the modeling of prediction uncertainty and the dynamic adaptation of risk tendency are essential components for optimal performance, with the risk tendency component exhibiting a greater impact than uncertainty alone. The invention thus provides a comprehensive, theoretically justified, and practically deployable solution to the long-standing challenge of bid optimization under uncertainty and dynamic risk constraints, enabling advertisers to achieve superior campaign outcomes in highly competitive and volatile digital advertising markets.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a system and method for adaptive risk-aware bid optimization in real-time bidding environments, wherein a demand-side platform determines optimal bid prices for individual ad impressions by simultaneously accounting for the uncertainty in predicted user response values and the dynamic risk tendency of the advertiser’s campaign. The system operates within the constraints of a fixed budget and a finite number of sequential auction opportunities, and is designed to maximize the cumulative value of acquired impressions—such as total clicks or conversions—while minimizing the risk of premature budget exhaustion or suboptimal performance. The invention is implemented as a software architecture that integrates three core components: a predictive uncertainty quantification module, a risk tendency modeling module, and a model-based reinforcement learning bid determination module. These components operate in a coordinated fashion to transform raw bid request data into a final bid price that is both statistically informed and strategically adaptive.

The predictive uncertainty quantification module receives as input a feature vector characterizing a bid request, such as user demographics, contextual information, device type, time of day, and historical interaction patterns. This feature vector is processed by a Bayesian logistic regression model trained to predict the probability of a click event or conversion event. Unlike conventional point-estimate models, the Bayesian formulation treats the model weights as random variables drawn from a Gaussian distribution, with the mean representing the central tendency of the prediction and the variance capturing the epistemic uncertainty associated with the model’s confidence in that prediction. The variance of each weight is inversely proportional to the frequency with which the corresponding feature has been observed during training, such that features with sparse or infrequent occurrences contribute more uncertainty to the final prediction. The output of this module is a probability distribution over possible click-through rates, from which the mean value, denoted as r_mean(x_t), and the standard deviation, denoted as r_std(x_t), are extracted for the current bid request characterized by feature vector x_t. This standard deviation serves as a quantitative measure of the prediction’s reliability, with higher values indicating greater uncertainty and lower values indicating greater confidence. This uncertainty measure is not treated as a mere byproduct of the model but is instead integrated as a critical input into the subsequent decision-making process, enabling the system to distinguish between impressions with identical predicted values but differing levels of confidence.

The risk tendency modeling module receives as input the current state of the campaign, defined by the number of remaining auctions t and the remaining budget b. This state information is processed by one of two alternative mechanisms: an expert knowledge-based formulation or a self-supervised reinforcement learning mechanism. In the expert knowledge-based instantiation, the risk tendency β(t, b) is computed as a function of the expected bid price required to deplete the remaining budget evenly across the remaining auctions, denoted as U(t, b). This expected bid price is derived from the market price distribution m(δ), which represents the probability density of competing bids for impressions with similar characteristics. The function U(t, b) is calculated by solving the equation that equates the expected cost of winning auctions at price U(t, b) to the per-auction budget allocation b/t, under the assumption that the market price distribution is independent of the bid request features. The risk tendency β(t, b) is then defined as a hyperbolic tangent transformation of the normalized difference between U(t, b) and a precomputed budget richness threshold Û, scaled by a slope parameter α. This formulation ensures that the risk tendency is negative when the budget is insufficient to sustain the campaign at its current pace, zero when the budget is exactly sufficient, and positive when the budget is abundant relative to the remaining auctions. The hyperbolic tangent function ensures that the risk tendency remains bounded between -1 and 1, preventing extreme bidding behavior. The slope parameter α controls the sensitivity of the risk tendency to deviations from the budget sufficiency threshold, allowing for fine-tuning of the aggressiveness of the bidding strategy. In the self-supervised reinforcement learning instantiation, the risk tendency is learned through a multi-layer perceptron that maps the state (t, b) to a scalar risk tendency value. The network is trained using a dataset of historical bidding episodes, where each episode is represented by a tuple (t, b, β(t, b), V_episode), with V_episode denoting the total cumulative reward achieved during that episode. A buffer maintains the highest-performing episodes, discarding those with lower rewards to ensure that training samples reflect successful strategies. During training, batches of these tuples are sampled uniformly from the buffer, and the network weights are updated via gradient descent to minimize the mean squared error between the predicted risk tendency and the empirically observed risk tendency that led to high cumulative rewards. This approach enables the system to automatically discover complex, non-linear relationships between campaign state and optimal risk behavior without requiring explicit domain knowledge or manual parameter tuning.

The bid price determination module operates as a model-based reinforcement learning agent that computes the optimal bid price for the current state by maximizing the expected cumulative reward over the remaining auctions. The cumulative reward V(t, b) is defined as the expected sum of immediate rewards—namely, the predicted click-through rate r_mean(x_t)—for all future auctions starting from state (t, b), under the optimal bidding policy. The immediate reward is only realized if the bid price exceeds the market price δ, in which case the bidder wins the auction and pays the second-highest price, which is assumed to be δ. The state transition probabilities are derived from the market price distribution m(δ) and the feature distribution p_x(x_{t-1}), which represent the likelihood of encountering a given market price and feature vector in the next auction. The cumulative reward is computed recursively using a dynamic programming approach, where the value of each state is expressed as the expected value of the immediate reward plus the discounted value of the next state, averaged over all possible market prices and future feature vectors. The bid price a(t, b, x_t) is determined by evaluating the marginal gain in cumulative reward for each possible bid price between zero and the remaining budget, and selecting the highest bid that does not reduce the expected cumulative reward. This is achieved by computing the difference function g(a) = V(t, b) - V(t, b - a) - a * r_avg, where r_avg is the average expected reward over the feature space, and identifying the largest integer bid price A such that g(A) ≥ 0 and g(A + 1) < 0. The optimal bid price is then set to A, ensuring that the bid is as high as possible without compromising the long-term value of the campaign. This procedure is executed for each incoming bid request, with the cumulative reward function being updated periodically as new market data becomes available, allowing the system to adapt to evolving market conditions and user behavior patterns.

The system is designed for deployment in high-throughput, low-latency advertising exchanges, where bid decisions must be made within milliseconds of receiving a bid request. To ensure real-time performance, the predictive uncertainty module is implemented using a pre-trained Bayesian logistic regression model with cached parameter distributions, allowing for rapid inference without retraining. The risk tendency module is implemented as a lightweight lookup table in the expert knowledge instantiation, or as a compact neural network with minimal hidden layers in the self-supervised instantiation, both of which support sub-millisecond inference times. The bid determination module precomputes and caches the cumulative reward function V(t, b) for all feasible combinations of remaining auctions and remaining budget, updating it only when the market price distribution or average impression value changes significantly. This caching strategy reduces the computational burden of online decision-making to a simple table lookup and marginal gain computation, enabling the system to scale to millions of bid requests per second. The system is modular and extensible, allowing for the substitution of alternative uncertainty quantification methods, such as Monte Carlo dropout or ensemble models, and the integration of additional state variables, such as time-of-day, day-of-week, or advertiser-specific performance targets. The system also supports multi-objective optimization, where the cumulative reward function can be weighted to prioritize different KPIs, such as clicks, conversions, or viewability, depending on the advertiser’s campaign goals.

The theoretical foundation of the invention is established through a formal proof demonstrating that the linear combination of the predicted mean value and the product of uncertainty and risk tendency, as defined in the formulation θ(t, b, x_t) = r_mean(x_t) + β(t, b) * r_std(x_t), achieves the optimal Value at Risk bid price under a second-price auction mechanism. This proof leverages Cantelli’s inequality, a one-sided variant of Chebyshev’s inequality, to bound the probability that the cumulative reward falls below a desired threshold, and shows that maximizing the VaR of the cumulative reward is equivalent to maximizing this linear combination. The risk tendency factor β(t, b) is shown to act as a multiplier that adjusts the weight given to uncertainty based on the campaign’s resource state, effectively transforming the uncertainty from a passive measure of model confidence into an active control signal for strategic bidding. This theoretical result ensures that the system’s decisions are not merely heuristic but are grounded in a rigorous probabilistic framework that guarantees optimality under the specified assumptions. The experimental validation of the invention demonstrates its superiority over existing methods across two large-scale public datasets, with the expert knowledge instantiation achieving the highest total click volume on most campaigns and the self-supervised instantiation demonstrating comparable performance without requiring manual tuning. Ablation studies confirm that removing either the uncertainty component or the risk tendency component results in significant performance degradation, with the risk tendency component exhibiting a greater impact, indicating that the strategic adaptation of bidding behavior is more critical than the statistical refinement of value estimates. The visualization of learned risk tendencies reveals a clear and intuitive pattern: negative risk tendencies dominate in resource-constrained states, leading to conservative bidding, while positive risk tendencies emerge in resource-abundant states, enabling aggressive bidding. This alignment between learned behavior and domain intuition further validates the effectiveness of the proposed framework.

The invention is implemented as a software system comprising a bid request processor, a prediction and uncertainty module, a risk tendency engine, a cumulative reward calculator, and a bid decision engine, all integrated into a single pipeline that operates in real time. The system is deployed as a microservice within the DSP’s bidding infrastructure, receiving bid requests via a high-speed message queue, processing them in parallel using distributed computing resources, and returning bid prices via a low-latency API. The system supports continuous learning, where the experience buffer in the self-supervised instantiation is updated with new episodes as they occur, and the neural network is periodically retrained on the latest data to adapt to drift in market conditions. The system also includes monitoring and diagnostic tools that track performance metrics, detect anomalies in risk tendency behavior, and provide explainability features that allow advertisers to understand why certain bids were made. The invention thus represents a comprehensive, scalable, and theoretically sound solution to the problem of bid optimization under uncertainty and dynamic risk constraints, enabling advertisers to achieve superior campaign outcomes in the complex and volatile environment of real-time digital advertising.
