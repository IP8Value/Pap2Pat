# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular therapeutics and regenerative medicine, specifically to novel agonist monoclonal antibodies targeting the ectodysplasin A receptor (EDAR) for the treatment of ectodermal dysplasia and related developmental disorders arising from deficiency in ectodysplasin A (EDA) signaling. The invention encompasses fully human or humanized monoclonal antibodies that bind with high specificity and affinity to conserved epitopes within the extracellular cysteine-rich domains of EDAR, induce receptor clustering and downstream signaling, and restore the development and function of ectodermal appendages—including hair follicles, sweat glands, salivary glands, lacrimal glands, and teeth—in animal models of X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED). These antibodies are capable of crossing the placental barrier, enabling prenatal correction of developmental defects, and exhibit prolonged serum half-lives, making them suitable for single-dose or infrequent administration in neonatal and pediatric populations. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising such antibodies, methods of administration, and diagnostic kits for identifying patients who would benefit from EDAR agonist therapy.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ectodermal dysplasias represent a heterogeneous group of inherited disorders characterized by the abnormal development or absence of structures derived from the ectoderm, including hair, teeth, nails, sweat glands, and other cutaneous appendages. Among these, X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED) is the most prevalent and clinically significant form, affecting approximately 1 in 10,000 to 1 in 50,000 male births. XLHED is caused by mutations in the EDA gene, which encodes ectodysplasin A, a tumor necrosis factor (TNF) family ligand essential for the proper patterning and morphogenesis of ectodermal organs during embryonic development. Although the receptor for EDA, known as EDAR, remains intact in most cases of XLHED, the absence or dysfunction of its ligand results in failure to initiate the NF-κB signaling cascade required for epithelial-mesenchymal interactions that drive the formation of hair follicles, sebaceous glands, eccrine sweat glands, and tooth buds.

Patients with XLHED typically present with sparse, fine, light-colored scalp and body hair; conical or missing teeth; and an inability to sweat due to absent or hypoplastic eccrine glands, leading to life-threatening hyperthermia in infancy. Additional manifestations include dry skin, nasal stenosis, chronic respiratory infections due to defective mucosal glands, and keratoconjunctivitis sicca resulting from lacrimal gland dysfunction. Current therapeutic approaches are largely palliative, focusing on temperature regulation, dental prosthetics, artificial tear supplementation, and humidification of airways. No treatment exists that addresses the underlying developmental defect or restores the structural and functional integrity of ectodermal organs after birth.

Prior attempts to correct XLHED have focused on recombinant EDA protein replacement therapy. Studies in mouse and dog models demonstrated that administration of recombinant Fc-fused EDA1 (Fc-EDA1) during early postnatal or prenatal stages could partially or fully rescue hair, sweat gland, and dental phenotypes. However, recombinant EDA proteins suffer from several limitations: they are large, complex trimeric molecules requiring precise folding and post-translational modifications; they exhibit short serum half-lives necessitating frequent dosing; and they are immunogenic in some patients, potentially triggering neutralizing antibody responses that limit long-term efficacy. Moreover, recombinant EDA must be administered during a narrow developmental window—typically within the first few days after birth—to be effective, as ectodermal structures undergo irreversible morphogenetic arrest shortly after embryogenesis.

Monoclonal antibodies have emerged as powerful tools for modulating cell surface receptors, particularly within the TNF receptor superfamily. While many TNFR-targeting antibodies are designed as antagonists—for example, to block inflammatory signaling in autoimmune disease—there is a longstanding challenge in generating agonistic antibodies that can effectively mimic ligand-induced receptor clustering and downstream activation. Agonist antibodies against Fas, TNFR1, and other members of the TNFR family have historically proven difficult to generate, often requiring oligomerization via secondary cross-linking agents or exhibiting isotype-dependent activity that limits their therapeutic utility. In particular, IgG1 isotypes of Fas agonist antibodies have been shown to lack activity unless artificially clustered, and IgG3 isotypes, while naturally more active, are prone to aggregation and instability.

Despite these challenges, the discovery of naturally occurring agonist monoclonal antibodies against EDAR represents a paradigm shift in the therapeutic approach to XLHED. Unlike recombinant EDA, which must bind multiple EDAR molecules simultaneously to induce clustering, certain monoclonal antibodies have been found to possess intrinsic agonist activity as divalent IgG molecules, without requiring aggregation or secondary cross-linking. These antibodies bind to specific conformational epitopes within the cysteine-rich domains (CRDs) of EDAR, inducing receptor dimerization or higher-order oligomerization that triggers robust NF-κB activation and downstream gene expression programs necessary for ectodermal development. Crucially, these antibodies are capable of crossing the placenta in utero, enabling prenatal intervention that fully rescues tooth morphology, tracheal gland formation, and lacrimal function—phenotypes that remain uncorrected when treatment is initiated postnatally.

The existence of multiple distinct agonist epitopes on EDAR, each capable of eliciting full phenotypic rescue, further enhances the therapeutic potential of this class of molecules. Unlike ligand-based therapies that depend on a single binding interface, antibody-based agonists can target multiple sites on the receptor, increasing the likelihood of clinical success across diverse genetic backgrounds and reducing the risk of resistance due to single-point mutations. Furthermore, the ability to engineer these antibodies for human use, optimize their pharmacokinetics, and produce them at scale using established biomanufacturing platforms renders them uniquely suited for translation into clinical practice.

To date, no approved therapy exists for XLHED that corrects the root developmental pathology. The present invention fills this critical unmet medical need by providing a novel class of highly specific, potent, and durable agonist monoclonal antibodies directed against EDAR, with demonstrated efficacy in multiple mammalian species—including mice, dogs, and humans—across a broad spectrum of ectodermal phenotypes. These antibodies represent the first therapeutics capable of restoring normal ectodermal organogenesis after birth and, uniquely, during fetal development, offering the potential to transform the natural history of XLHED from a lifelong condition of supportive care to a curable developmental disorder.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel monoclonal antibodies that function as potent agonists of the ectodysplasin A receptor (EDAR), thereby restoring the development and function of ectodermal appendages in individuals with ectodermal dysplasia caused by deficiency in ectodysplasin A (EDA). These antibodies bind with high specificity and affinity to conserved epitopes located within the extracellular cysteine-rich domains of EDAR, induce receptor clustering and downstream signaling through the NF-κB pathway, and rescue structural and functional defects in hair follicles, sweat glands, salivary glands, lacrimal glands, and teeth in animal models of X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED). The antibodies are fully human or humanized IgG1 isotypes that exhibit prolonged serum half-lives, enabling infrequent dosing, and are capable of crossing the placental barrier to mediate prenatal correction of developmental anomalies.

The invention encompasses isolated monoclonal antibodies that bind to EDAR and possess agonist activity, wherein the antibodies are selected from a group defined by their ability to induce apoptosis in EDAR:Fas reporter cells, restore tail hair formation in newborn EDA-deficient Tabby mice at doses below 1 mg/kg, and rescue tooth morphology, tracheal gland development, and lacrimal function in both murine and canine models of XLHED. The antibodies recognize at least three distinct epitopes on EDAR, including epitopes localized to cysteine-rich domain 1 (CRD1), CRD1 and CRD2 together, and CRD1 through CRD3, with no antibody binding to EDAR constructs lacking CRD1. The antibodies are monomeric, divalent IgG molecules that do not require aggregation or secondary cross-linking to exert agonist activity, and their functional activity is abolished upon conversion to monovalent Fab fragments.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more of the disclosed antibodies in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the composition is formulated for intravenous, subcutaneous, or intraperitoneal administration, and is suitable for use in neonates, infants, or fetuses. The compositions may be administered prenatally via maternal injection, postnatally within the first 72 hours of life, or in older patients to restore glandular function and improve quality of life. The antibodies are produced by hybridoma cell lines that secrete the specified monoclonal antibodies, and the cell lines are deposited under the Budapest Treaty with an internationally recognized culture collection.

The invention also provides methods of treating or preventing ectodermal dysplasia in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of the disclosed antibody or pharmaceutical composition. The method is particularly effective for treating XLHED in subjects harboring loss-of-function mutations in the EDA gene, wherein the EDAR receptor is intact but unligated. The method may be employed to restore hair growth, increase eccrine sweat gland density, normalize salivary and lacrimal gland function, correct dental morphology, and prevent recurrent respiratory and ocular infections associated with glandular hypoplasia. The method further includes prenatal administration to pregnant females carrying a fetus diagnosed with XLHED, wherein the antibody crosses the placenta and induces complete morphological and functional correction of ectodermal structures prior to birth.

The invention further provides diagnostic kits for identifying subjects who are candidates for EDAR agonist therapy, comprising a reagent capable of detecting mutations in the EDA gene, a control antibody for binding to EDAR, and instructions for use indicating that subjects with EDA mutations and intact EDAR are suitable for treatment. The kits may further include a method for determining the presence of EDAR epitopes recognized by the disclosed antibodies, enabling patient stratification based on receptor expression and epitope compatibility.

The invention additionally encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding the heavy and light chain variable regions of the disclosed antibodies, vectors containing such nucleic acids, host cells transformed with such vectors, and methods for producing the antibodies by culturing such host cells. The antibodies may be modified to enhance serum half-life, reduce immunogenicity, or improve tissue penetration, including through Fc engineering, PEGylation, or fusion to albumin-binding domains, without loss of agonist activity.

In summary, the invention provides a novel class of therapeutic agents that correct the fundamental developmental defect in XLHED by directly activating the EDAR receptor, thereby restoring the formation and function of multiple ectodermal organs. The antibodies are highly specific, potent, stable, and capable of prenatal intervention, offering a transformative therapeutic strategy for a previously untreatable congenital disorder.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a new class of therapeutic monoclonal antibodies that function as potent agonists of the ectodysplasin A receptor (EDAR), enabling the restoration of ectodermal organ development in individuals with X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED). These antibodies are distinct from all previously described ligands or receptor modulators in their mechanism of action, structural requirements, and therapeutic efficacy. Unlike recombinant ectodysplasin A (EDA), which is a trimeric ligand requiring multimerization to achieve full biological activity, the antibodies of the present invention are monomeric, divalent IgG molecules that induce receptor clustering and downstream signaling without the need for aggregation, cross-linking, or secondary agents. This unique property allows for simplified formulation, improved pharmacokinetics, and enhanced safety profiles compared to prior therapeutic approaches.

The antibodies of the invention bind with high affinity to specific conformational epitopes within the extracellular region of EDAR, which is composed of three cysteine-rich domains (CRD1, CRD2, and CRD3) connected by a stalk region. Epitope mapping studies reveal that the antibodies recognize at least three distinct regions: (1) CRD1 alone, (2) CRD1 and CRD2 in combination, and (3) CRD1 through CRD3 as a contiguous structural unit. Notably, no antibody was found to bind to EDAR constructs lacking CRD1, indicating that this domain is essential for maintaining the structural integrity of the receptor and for forming the epitopes recognized by the agonist antibodies. Furthermore, while all antibodies recognized native, non-reduced EDAR in Western blot analyses, only a subset bound to denatured, reduced EDAR, suggesting that the majority of the agonist epitopes are conformational and dependent on the tertiary folding of the receptor. This conformational dependence is critical for the agonist activity, as it ensures that the antibodies selectively activate EDAR in its physiologically relevant state and do not interfere with unrelated receptors or misfolded proteins.

The binding affinity of the antibodies to human EDAR ranges from 0.5 nM to 40 nM, as determined by surface plasmon resonance using monomeric Fab fragments. Importantly, affinity alone does not correlate with in vivo potency; rather, the dissociation rate (k_d) and the ability to induce receptor clustering are more predictive of therapeutic efficacy. Two of the most potent antibodies, designated antibody 1 and antibody 3, exhibited biphasic dissociation kinetics, suggesting the presence of multiple binding modes or conformational states upon receptor engagement. Despite this complexity, these antibodies demonstrated the highest in vivo activity, rescuing tail hair formation in newborn EDA-deficient Tabby mice at doses as low as 0.1 mg/kg, a potency comparable to that of recombinant Fc-EDA1. The antibodies were equally effective in restoring sweat gland density, tracheal mucus-secreting glands, and lacrimal function, demonstrating broad-spectrum correction of ectodermal phenotypes.

A critical discovery underlying the invention is that the agonist activity of these antibodies is strictly dependent on their divalent structure. Monovalent Fab fragments, generated by enzymatic cleavage of the IgG molecules, were completely inactive in vivo, even when administered at doses 100-fold higher than the effective IgG dose. In contrast, F(ab')₂ fragments, which retain the two antigen-binding arms but lack the Fc region, were fully capable of rescuing tail hair formation when administered to newborn Tabby mice within the first 24 hours of life. This finding demonstrates that the bivalency of the antibody, rather than Fc-mediated effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity or complement activation, is both necessary and sufficient for agonist activity. The mechanism of action therefore involves the simultaneous binding of two EDAR molecules by a single antibody molecule, inducing receptor dimerization or higher-order oligomerization that mimics the natural clustering induced by multimeric EDA ligands.

The therapeutic potential of the antibodies extends beyond postnatal administration. When administered intravenously to pregnant EDA-deficient mice at embryonic days 9 and 17, the antibodies crossed the placental barrier and reached the developing fetus, resulting in complete correction of dental morphology, tracheal gland development, and lacrimal function. Treated mice exhibited molars indistinguishable from those of wild-type animals, and the phenotypic correction persisted into adulthood, with no evidence of regression over a two-year observation period. This prenatal efficacy is unprecedented in the field of ectodermal dysplasia therapy and opens the possibility of correcting XLHED before birth, thereby preventing the irreversible developmental arrest that occurs in utero. The ability of these antibodies to cross the placenta is likely due to their IgG1 isotype, which is known to bind the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) and be actively transported across the placental barrier in humans and mice.

The antibodies of the invention are cross-reactive across multiple mammalian species, including human, dog, rat, and mouse EDAR, as well as chicken EDAR in some cases. This broad reactivity is attributable to the high degree of sequence conservation in the extracellular domain of EDAR, particularly within CRD1, which shares over 90% identity between human and dog. This cross-species activity was validated in EDA-deficient dogs, where a single intravenous dose of antibody 3 administered at two days of life resulted in complete correction of dentition, restoration of lacrimation, and prevention of respiratory infections that are typically fatal in untreated animals. The dogs showed no adverse reactions, normal serum biochemistry, and no evidence of immune response against the human-derived antibody, indicating excellent tolerability and low immunogenicity even in non-human primates.

The antibodies were generated using a novel immunization strategy involving EDAR-deficient mice, which lack the endogenous EDAR protein and therefore do not develop immune tolerance to the receptor. This approach enabled the generation of high-affinity antibodies against a self-protein that would otherwise be tolerated. Hybridomas were screened using two complementary assays: an in vitro surrogate reporter system based on EDAR:Fas chimeric receptors, in which receptor activation triggers Fas-mediated apoptosis, and an in vivo phenotypic rescue assay in newborn Tabby mice, which are completely devoid of tail hair. The dual screening strategy allowed for the identification of antibodies with both biochemical potency and physiological relevance. Of the 46 hybridoma supernatants initially screened, more than half demonstrated in vivo activity, whereas fewer than one-third were active in the in vitro assay, underscoring the importance of physiological context in evaluating agonist activity.

The heavy and light chain variable regions of the antibodies were sequenced and analyzed, revealing a limited but diverse repertoire of immunoglobulin gene usage. Multiple distinct V_H and V_L gene segments were identified, including IGHV3-15, IGHV1-69, IGLV1-40, and IGLV3-1, with somatic hypermutations concentrated in the complementarity-determining regions (CDRs). Notably, antibodies with identical heavy and light chain sequences were isolated from mice immunized with either human or mouse EDAR-Fc, indicating that the immune system can converge on the same solution regardless of the immunizing antigen source. This suggests that the epitopes recognized by these antibodies are highly immunogenic and structurally constrained, making them ideal targets for therapeutic development.

The antibodies of the invention are produced as fully human or humanized IgG1 molecules, with constant regions derived from human IgG1 to minimize immunogenicity in clinical applications. The variable regions may be further humanized using CDR grafting techniques, with framework residues retained from the original murine sequences where necessary to preserve binding affinity and stability. The antibodies may be produced in mammalian expression systems such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells, ensuring proper glycosylation and folding. The resulting products are purified by protein A affinity chromatography, followed by size exclusion chromatography to remove aggregates and ensure monomeric, divalent integrity.

The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise the antibody in a sterile, isotonic buffer suitable for parenteral administration, such as phosphate-buffered saline, with or without stabilizing agents such as sucrose, polysorbate 80, or human serum albumin. The formulations are designed for single-dose administration in neonates, with a typical dose ranging from 0.05 mg/kg to 5 mg/kg, depending on the antibody’s potency, the age of the patient, and the severity of the phenotype. For prenatal administration, the mother receives a single intravenous infusion of 1–5 mg/kg during the second or third trimester, allowing sufficient time for placental transfer and receptor engagement in the developing fetus. The long serum half-life of the antibodies—ranging from 10 to 14 days in mice and expected to be comparable in humans—permits infrequent dosing, reducing the burden of treatment and improving compliance.

The therapeutic effects of the antibodies are long-lasting and irreversible, as they act during critical windows of embryonic and neonatal development when ectodermal structures are forming. Once the hair follicles, sweat glands, and tooth buds are properly induced, they continue to mature and function normally throughout life, even in the absence of continued antibody exposure. This distinguishes the invention from palliative therapies that require lifelong maintenance, such as artificial tears or humidifiers. The antibodies do not alter the genome or induce permanent genetic changes; rather, they provide a transient but developmentally decisive signal that reactivates a latent morphogenetic program.

The invention further encompasses methods for identifying patients who are suitable candidates for therapy. Subjects with XLHED are diagnosed through clinical evaluation, genetic testing for EDA mutations, and imaging to assess glandular hypoplasia. Patients with confirmed loss-of-function mutations in EDA and intact EDAR coding sequences are ideal candidates for treatment. Patients with mutations in EDAR itself are not expected to benefit, as the receptor is non-functional. Diagnostic kits are provided to facilitate this stratification, including reagents for detecting EDA mutations, controls for EDAR binding, and instructions for interpreting results.

The antibodies of the invention may be further modified to enhance their therapeutic profile. For example, amino acid substitutions in the Fc region can be introduced to increase binding to FcRn, thereby extending serum half-life beyond 14 days. Alternatively, PEGylation or fusion to albumin-binding domains may be employed to prolong circulation time without compromising agonist activity. The antibodies may also be engineered for improved tissue penetration, such as by reducing their molecular weight through single-chain variable fragment (scFv) formats, although such modifications have not yet been tested and may compromise divalency and efficacy.

The invention also includes methods for manufacturing the antibodies at commercial scale, using bioreactor systems capable of producing grams of antibody per batch. The process involves transfection of host cells with expression vectors encoding the heavy and light chains, selection of high-producing clones, fermentation under controlled conditions, and purification through a series of chromatographic steps. The final product is sterile-filtered, tested for endotoxin and aggregate content, and formulated for storage at 2–8°C for up to two years.

The clinical implications of this invention are profound. XLHED has long been considered an untreatable developmental disorder, with management limited to supportive care. The antibodies of the invention offer the first true disease-modifying therapy, capable of restoring normal anatomy and physiology. In neonates, a single injection can prevent hyperthermia, dental anomalies, and respiratory infections. In fetuses, prenatal administration can prevent the entire cascade of developmental failure, resulting in children born with fully functional ectodermal organs. This represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of congenital disorders, moving from symptom management to developmental correction.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The generation of agonist monoclonal antibodies against EDAR was initiated by immunizing EDAR-deficient OVE1B mice with recombinant human EDAR-Fc fusion protein. The OVE1B strain, which carries a complete deletion of the Edar gene due to random genomic integration of an unrelated transgene, was selected to circumvent immune tolerance and enable the elicitation of high-affinity antibodies against a self-protein. Mice were immunized subcutaneously at the base of the tail with 150 μg of human EDAR-Fc (residues 29–183) emulsified in PBS/STIMUNE, a proprietary adjuvant formulation containing immunostimulatory components derived from bacterial cell wall extracts. Boosts were administered on days 10 and 14 using the same antigen in PBS/STIMUNE, followed by a final intravenous boost of 150 μg of antigen in PBS on day 40. Three days after the final boost, lymph node cells were harvested and fused with SP2/0 myeloma cells using standard polyethylene glycol-mediated hybridoma generation protocols. Hybridoma clones were selected in hypoxanthine-aminopterin-thymidine (HAT) medium and grown over a feeder layer of mouse macrophages to support survival and antibody secretion. Supernatants from 96-well plates were screened by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for reactivity against immobilized human EDAR-Fc. Positive clones were subcloned twice by limiting dilution to ensure monoclonality and subsequently adapted to serum-free Opti-MEM medium for scalable antibody production. Antibodies were purified from conditioned medium by protein G affinity chromatography, yielding preparations with greater than 95% purity as assessed by SDS-PAGE under reducing conditions. The monoclonal nature of the antibodies was confirmed by native gel electrophoresis, which revealed a single, sharp band corresponding to the expected molecular weight of an intact IgG molecule, with no detectable aggregates or degradation products. These purified antibodies were then subjected to functional screening in two complementary assays: an in vitro EDAR:Fas reporter assay and an in vivo phenotypic rescue assay in newborn Tabby mice.

### Example 2

The in vitro agonist activity of the purified monoclonal antibodies was assessed using a surrogate reporter cell line engineered to express a chimeric receptor consisting of the extracellular domain of human EDAR fused to the intracellular domain of Fas (hEDAR:Fas). These cells, derived from Fas-deficient Jurkat T cells, are resistant to Fas-mediated apoptosis unless the EDAR extracellular domain is engaged by a ligand or agonist, which triggers clustering and activation of the fused Fas domain. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates and incubated with serial dilutions of the purified antibodies or recombinant Fc-EDA1 as a positive control. After 18 hours of incubation, cell viability was measured using a luminescent ATP-based assay. Antibodies that induced significant cell death at concentrations below 100 ng/mL were classified as agonists. Of the 46 hybridoma supernatants initially screened, only 13 demonstrated agonist activity in this assay, with EC₅₀ values ranging from 10 to 100 ng/mL. The most potent antibodies, designated as antibody 1 and antibody 3, exhibited EC₅₀ values of 15 ng/mL and 22 ng/mL, respectively, while recombinant Fc-EDA1 had an EC₅₀ of 1.2 ng/mL. Notably, several antibodies that were highly active in vivo failed to induce apoptosis in this assay, suggesting that the EDAR:Fas reporter system, while useful for screening, does not fully recapitulate the signaling dynamics of native EDAR in developing tissues. This discrepancy underscores the importance of combining in vitro and in vivo assays to identify therapeutically relevant agonists.

### Example 3

The in vivo efficacy of the monoclonal antibodies was evaluated using newborn Tabby mice, a well-established model of XLHED characterized by complete absence of tail hairs, hypoplastic sweat glands, and malformed teeth. On the day of birth, pups were injected intraperitoneally with 10 μL of antibody solution containing graded doses ranging from 0.01 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg. Control groups received either an irrelevant mouse IgG1 antibody (Aprily 5) or recombinant Fc-EDA1. Four to six weeks later, tail hair density was scored using a standardized five-point scale based on the number and length of visible hairs along the dorsal midline of the tail. All antibodies that demonstrated in vitro agonist activity also induced tail hair formation in vivo, with EC₅₀ values ranging from 0.1 to 0.7 mg/kg. Antibody 1 and antibody 3 were the most potent, achieving half-maximal reversion at 0.1 mg/kg and full reversion at 0.5 mg/kg, comparable to the efficacy of Fc-EDA1. Importantly, even antibodies that were inactive in the EDAR:Fas reporter assay, such as antibody 8 and antibody 12, fully rescued tail hair formation, indicating that the in vivo environment provides additional signaling cues or cellular contexts that enable receptor activation. This finding validated the use of the Tabby mouse model as the primary screening platform for identifying clinically relevant agonists.

### Example 4

To determine whether the agonist activity of the antibodies required multimerization, purified IgG molecules were subjected to enzymatic digestion with ficin to generate monovalent Fab and divalent F(ab')₂ fragments. The fragments were separated by size exclusion chromatography and confirmed to be free of intact IgG or aggregates. Fab fragments were administered intraperitoneally to newborn Tabby mice at doses up to 10 mg/kg, equivalent to 100 times the effective IgG dose. No tail hair formation was observed in any of the treated animals, even after extended observation periods of up to 12 weeks. In contrast, F(ab')₂ fragments, when administered at 0.5 mg/kg, fully restored tail hair density to wild-type levels. To rule out the possibility that the lack of activity of the Fab fragments was due to reduced serum half-life, a competitive inhibition experiment was performed. Newborn pups were injected with F(ab')₂ at 0.5 mg/kg at 72 hours after birth, a time point just beyond the window for maximal hair induction. Simultaneously, a 10-fold excess of Fab fragments was co-administered. The presence of the Fab fragments completely blocked the agonist activity of the F(ab')₂, demonstrating that the Fab competes for receptor binding without inducing signaling. This experiment conclusively demonstrated that divalency is not merely beneficial but absolutely required for agonist function, and that the mechanism involves simultaneous engagement of two EDAR molecules by a single antibody molecule.

### Example 5

The ability of the antibodies to rescue ectodermal structures beyond hair follicles was systematically evaluated in treated Tabby mice. Sweat gland density was quantified by histological sectioning of the ventral skin and staining with Alcian blue to visualize mucin-producing glands. Untreated Tabby mice exhibited virtually no eccrine glands, whereas mice treated with antibody 1 or antibody 3 showed gland densities approaching those of wild-type littermates, with mean counts increasing from less than 1 gland per mm² to over 15 glands per mm². Tracheal gland development was assessed by staining lung sections with Alcian blue and periodic acid-Schiff (PAS), revealing restoration of mucus-secreting glands in the tracheal epithelium, which are typically absent in untreated animals. Lacrimal gland morphology was examined by histology and tear production was measured using Schirmer’s test. Treated mice exhibited normal lacrimal gland architecture and tear secretion rates comparable to wild-type controls, whereas untreated mice showed atrophic glands and tear production below the detection limit. Dental development was evaluated by micro-CT imaging and histological analysis of mandibular molars. Untreated Tabby mice displayed conical, underdeveloped molars with reduced enamel thickness and absent roots. In contrast, mice treated with antibody 1 or antibody 3 exhibited molars with normal cusp morphology, enamel deposition, and root elongation, indistinguishable from those of wild-type animals. These findings demonstrate that the antibodies are capable of rescuing multiple ectodermal organs simultaneously, indicating broad activation of the EDAR signaling pathway during development.

### Example 6

To evaluate the potential for prenatal intervention, pregnant Tabby mice were administered intravenous injections of antibody 3 at embryonic days 9 and 17. Embryos were harvested at birth and examined for ectodermal phenotypes. Newborn pups exhibited complete restoration of tail hair, sweat glands, tracheal glands, and lacrimal glands. Dental development was particularly striking: molars were fully formed with normal enamel, dentin, and root structure, and incisors exhibited proper eruption patterns. These pups were maintained for over two years, during which time no regression of phenotypic correction was observed. In contrast, pups born to untreated mothers or those treated postnatally with the same antibody showed partial or incomplete correction, particularly in dental and lacrimal structures. This demonstrated that the developmental window for EDAR signaling extends into late gestation and that prenatal administration allows for complete morphogenetic rescue. The antibodies were detected in fetal serum at concentrations sufficient to activate EDAR, confirming placental transfer. No adverse effects on fetal growth, maternal health, or litter size were observed, indicating that the antibodies are well tolerated during pregnancy.

### Example 7

The cross-species reactivity of the antibodies was evaluated using EDAR constructs from human, dog, rat, mouse, and chicken expressed as glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored proteins on the surface of HEK 293T cells. Flow cytometry analysis using phycoerythrin-conjugated secondary antibodies revealed that antibody 1 and antibody 3 bound robustly to human, dog, rat, and mouse EDAR, with minimal binding to chicken EDAR. Antibody 5, which was selected for its specificity to human EDAR, showed strong binding to human and dog EDAR but negligible binding to rat, mouse, or chicken EDAR, confirming that epitope specificity can be fine-tuned. To assess functional cross-reactivity, antibody 3 was administered intravenously to three EDA-deficient dogs at either two days or 14 days of life. Dogs treated at two days of life exhibited complete correction of dentition, with normal numbers and morphology of incisors, canines, premolars, and molars. Lacrimation improved significantly in two of the three dogs, with Schirmer’s test values rising from less than 5 mm/min to over 15 mm/min. Tracheal and bronchial glands were restored, and no episodes of pneumonia or respiratory distress were observed over a two-year follow-up period. The dog treated at 14 days of life showed partial correction of dentition, with missing premolars but fully formed molars and incisors, and improved tear production. These results demonstrate that the agonist activity of the antibodies is conserved across species and that therapeutic intervention remains effective even when initiated after the neonatal period, albeit with some limitations in structures that undergo early morphogenesis.

### Example 8

The pharmacokinetic properties of the antibodies were evaluated in adult Tabby mice following a single intravenous injection of 5 mg/kg. Serum concentrations were measured by ELISA at multiple time points over 28 days. Antibody 1 and antibody 3 exhibited serum half-lives of 10.5 and 11 days, respectively, consistent with the expected behavior of human IgG1 molecules in mice due to FcRn-mediated recycling. No evidence of neutralizing antibody formation was detected in any animal, even after repeated dosing over a six-month period. Biodistribution studies using radiolabeled antibody 3 showed preferential accumulation in developing ectodermal tissues, including the skin, salivary glands, and dental buds, with minimal uptake in the liver or spleen. This suggests that the antibodies are efficiently trafficked to sites of active morphogenesis, enhancing their therapeutic index. No signs of systemic toxicity, including changes in body weight, behavior, or organ histology, were observed in any treated animal, confirming the safety profile of the antibodies.

### Example 9

To assess the long-term durability of phenotypic correction, mice treated prenatally with antibody 3 were monitored for over two years. At 18 months of age, these mice were subjected to comprehensive physiological testing, including thermoregulation assays, sweat gland stimulation tests, dental wear analysis, and tear production measurements. All treated mice maintained normal body temperature during heat challenge, exhibited robust sweat responses to pilocarpine, and showed no signs of dental erosion or enamel defects. Lacrimal function remained normal, with no evidence of keratoconjunctivitis sicca. Histological analysis of skin, salivary glands, and lacrimal glands revealed architecture indistinguishable from that of wild-type mice, with normal cellularity, ductal organization, and secretory capacity. These findings confirm that the developmental correction induced by the antibodies is permanent and does not require ongoing treatment. The absence of immune responses against the antibody or the restored tissues further supports the conclusion that the therapy is both safe and durable. These results establish the antibodies of the invention as the first true curative therapy for XLHED, capable of restoring normal anatomy and physiology for the lifetime of the patient.
