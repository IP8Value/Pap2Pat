# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD AND INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel peptide-based inhibitors of Src family kinases that specifically target the functional signaling complex formed between the sodium/potassium-adenosine triphosphatase (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase) and Src kinase at the plasma membrane. More particularly, the invention encompasses synthetic peptides derived from the N-terminal domain of the α1 subunit of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, which are capable of selectively disrupting the interaction between Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and Src without broadly inhibiting other kinases or cellular signaling pathways. These peptides, when conjugated to cell-penetrating motifs, exhibit enhanced intracellular delivery and preferential localization at the plasma membrane, enabling them to function as highly specific antagonists of ouabain-induced signaling cascades. The invention finds substantial utility in the field of molecular therapeutics, particularly for the treatment of pathological conditions characterized by aberrant activation of Src-dependent signaling, including cardiac hypertrophy, hypertension, renal fibrosis, certain forms of cancer, and inflammatory disorders driven by Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase-mediated signal transduction. The peptides of the invention are not merely kinase inhibitors in the classical sense; rather, they represent a new class of molecular tools that interfere with protein-protein interactions within a defined receptor complex, thereby offering a level of specificity unattainable by conventional ATP-competitive inhibitors. This targeted approach minimizes off-target effects and preserves essential physiological functions of Src and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, making the invention uniquely suited for chronic therapeutic applications where long-term modulation of signaling pathways is required without systemic toxicity. Furthermore, the invention provides a platform for the development of diagnostic and research reagents capable of selectively probing the functional state of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling axis in live cells and tissues, thereby enabling both mechanistic studies and clinical monitoring of diseases involving this pathway.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The sodium/potassium-adenosine triphosphatase, commonly referred to as Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, has long been recognized as a critical membrane transporter responsible for maintaining electrochemical gradients across the plasma membrane of animal cells. Its canonical function involves the active transport of three sodium ions out of the cell and two potassium ions into the cell per molecule of ATP hydrolyzed, thereby sustaining membrane potential, osmotic balance, and secondary active transport processes. However, over the past two decades, compelling evidence has emerged demonstrating that Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase is not merely a passive ion pump but also functions as a multifunctional signaling scaffold capable of initiating intracellular signaling cascades in response to extracellular stimuli, particularly cardiotonic steroids such as ouabain. These steroids bind to the extracellular domain of the α-subunit of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and induce conformational changes that trigger the activation of intracellular kinases, most notably Src, leading to downstream phosphorylation of extracellular signal-regulated kinases 1 and 2 (ERK1/2), altered gene expression, and cellular remodeling. This paradigm shift in understanding Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase as a receptor-like signaling molecule has opened new avenues for pharmacological intervention in cardiovascular and renal diseases. Despite this progress, the molecular details of how Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase engages Src and how this interaction is regulated remain incompletely understood. Prior studies have identified regions within the cytoplasmic domain of the α1 subunit—specifically the CD3 region—as necessary for Src binding and inhibition. However, these regions are large, structurally complex, and not amenable to direct therapeutic application due to poor membrane permeability, rapid degradation, and lack of specificity. Conventional approaches to inhibit Src kinase activity have relied on small-molecule ATP-competitive inhibitors such as PP2 or dasatinib, which effectively block catalytic activity but lack selectivity, leading to widespread disruption of Src family kinases and other tyrosine kinases, resulting in significant cytotoxicity and unintended physiological consequences. These agents interfere with all Src-dependent processes, including those essential for normal cell adhesion, migration, and survival, thereby limiting their therapeutic utility. Moreover, such inhibitors do not distinguish between Src molecules that are constitutively active versus those that are dynamically regulated through interaction with Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need in the art for a therapeutic agent capable of selectively disrupting the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling complex without affecting other Src functions or unrelated kinase pathways. The identification of a discrete, functional motif within the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit that mediates high-affinity binding to Src, and the subsequent engineering of a cell-permeable peptide mimetic of this motif, represents a groundbreaking advance that addresses this long-standing challenge. The invention described herein fulfills this need by providing a novel class of compounds that exploit the natural regulatory mechanism of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex to achieve unprecedented specificity in modulating pathological signaling while preserving homeostatic functions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of synthetic peptides and their conjugates that specifically inhibit the interaction between the sodium/potassium-adenosine triphosphatase (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase) and Src kinase, thereby blocking the formation of a functional signaling complex critical for the transduction of ouabain-induced signals. The invention is based on the discovery that a specific 20-amino acid sequence within the N-terminal domain of the α1 subunit of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, designated herein as ND1, directly binds to the kinase domain of Src and inhibits its catalytic activity. This binding interaction is highly specific and does not occur with other Src family members to the same extent, nor does it affect serine/threonine kinases such as protein kinase C. The invention further provides a synthetic peptide, referred to as NaKtide, comprising the amino acid sequence corresponding to the ND1 domain, which retains the ability to bind and inhibit Src with an IC₅₀ in the low nanomolar range. To enable cellular uptake and plasma membrane targeting, the invention further provides conjugates of NaKtide with cell-penetrating peptides, most notably the HIV-Tat peptide sequence, resulting in a compound designated pNaKtide. The pNaKtide conjugate exhibits enhanced intracellular delivery, preferential localization at the plasma membrane, and the ability to effectively compete with endogenous Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase for Src binding, thereby preventing the ouabain-induced release and activation of Src. The invention demonstrates that pNaKtide selectively inhibits signaling pathways initiated by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex, including ERK1/2 activation and cellular hypertrophy, without affecting other Src-dependent processes such as insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1)-induced ERK activation or basal Src activity in cells lacking Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase expression. The invention further includes a control peptide, designated C1, which is derived from a non-interacting region of the α1 subunit and exhibits no inhibitory activity against Src, serving as a negative control for specificity. The peptides of the invention can be administered in vitro or in vivo to treat conditions characterized by pathological activation of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling axis, including cardiac hypertrophy, hypertension, renal fibrosis, and certain cancers. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the peptides of the invention in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, as well as methods of using these compositions for the prevention or treatment of disease. The invention also provides diagnostic and research tools for detecting and quantifying the functional state of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex in biological samples, thereby enabling personalized therapeutic strategies. The peptides of the invention represent a paradigm shift in kinase inhibition by targeting protein-protein interactions rather than catalytic sites, thereby achieving a level of specificity and safety unattainable by conventional small-molecule inhibitors.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Binding of Src to Na+/K+-ATPase Forms a Functional Signaling Complex

The sodium/potassium-adenosine triphosphatase, a transmembrane enzyme essential for maintaining cellular ion homeostasis, has been demonstrated to serve not only as an ion transporter but also as a critical signaling scaffold that regulates intracellular kinase activity through direct physical interaction with Src family kinases. This functional association occurs at the plasma membrane, where a subset of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase molecules exists in a conformation that permits binding to the Src kinase domain, resulting in the formation of a stable, high-affinity signaling complex. The interaction between these two proteins is not incidental but rather represents a tightly regulated molecular mechanism that controls the activation state of Src in response to extracellular stimuli, particularly cardiotonic steroids such as ouabain. In the absence of ligand binding, the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase acts as a suppressor of Src kinase activity, maintaining the enzyme in a low-activity state through direct contact with its regulatory domain. Upon binding of ouabain to the extracellular α-subunit, a conformational change is transmitted through the transmembrane helices to the cytoplasmic domains, resulting in the release of Src from its inhibitory constraint and its subsequent activation through autophosphorylation at tyrosine residue 418. This activation triggers a downstream cascade involving the phosphorylation and activation of extracellular signal-regulated kinases 1 and 2, ultimately leading to alterations in gene expression, protein synthesis, and cellular morphology. The discovery that this signaling complex is not merely a transient association but a structurally defined and functionally essential unit has profound implications for therapeutic intervention. Prior attempts to modulate this pathway have relied on broad-spectrum kinase inhibitors that indiscriminately target all Src family members and other tyrosine kinases, resulting in significant off-target effects and limited clinical utility. The present invention overcomes these limitations by identifying a discrete structural motif within the cytoplasmic domain of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit that is both necessary and sufficient for Src binding. This motif, located within the N-terminal region of the large cytoplasmic domain, has been designated ND1 and comprises a 20-amino acid segment that forms a unique structural interface with the kinase domain of Src. The binding of this region to Src is not mediated by electrostatic interactions alone but involves precise hydrophobic and van der Waals contacts that stabilize a conformational state incompatible with catalytic activity. Structural analysis reveals that the ND1 domain adopts a partially ordered conformation that complements a surface groove on the Src kinase domain, effectively blocking substrate access and preventing the transition to an active state. This interaction is highly selective, as other regions of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, including the adjacent P-domain and the C-terminal segment, do not exhibit comparable binding affinity for Src. Furthermore, the ND1 domain does not interact with other members of the Src family such as Lyn or Fyn to the same extent, indicating a unique molecular recognition event specific to Src. The functional significance of this interaction is underscored by the observation that disruption of the ND1-Src interface, either through genetic deletion, antibody blockade, or competitive peptide inhibition, results in the loss of ouabain-induced ERK1/2 activation and cellular hypertrophy, while leaving basal Src activity and other signaling pathways intact. This selectivity is further confirmed by experiments demonstrating that cells lacking Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase expression, such as SYF cells, show no response to the inhibitory peptides, whereas Src-rescued variants exhibit robust inhibition of ERK1/2 phosphorylation upon peptide treatment. The formation of this signaling complex is not restricted to epithelial cell lines but is conserved in primary cardiomyocytes, renal tubular cells, and vascular smooth muscle cells, indicating its physiological relevance across multiple organ systems. The existence of this complex provides a molecular explanation for the tissue-specific effects of ouabain and other cardiotonic steroids, which have long been observed but poorly understood. The invention leverages this discovery by engineering a synthetic peptide that mimics the ND1 domain with high fidelity, thereby acting as a molecular decoy that competes with endogenous Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase for Src binding. This approach is fundamentally distinct from traditional kinase inhibition because it does not interfere with the catalytic site of Src but instead prevents its recruitment into a signaling-competent complex. As a result, the peptides of the invention preserve the basal functions of Src in cell adhesion, cytoskeletal dynamics, and survival signaling, while selectively blocking pathological activation triggered by ouabain or other stimuli that engage the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase scaffold. The specificity of this mechanism is further validated by the observation that the inhibitory effect of the peptides is completely abolished in cells where the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase has been genetically knocked down, confirming that their action is entirely dependent on the presence of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex. This unique mode of action renders the peptides of the invention ideal candidates for long-term therapeutic use, where sustained inhibition of pathological signaling without disruption of homeostatic kinase functions is required.

### Functional Characterization of Src-Interacting Na+/K+-ATPase Using RNA Interference Assay

To rigorously establish the functional dependence of Src signaling on its interaction with the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit, a series of RNA interference experiments were conducted to selectively deplete endogenous Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase expression in cultured cells and assess the consequences on Src activation and downstream signaling pathways. These experiments were performed in parallel with the administration of the synthetic peptides of the invention to determine whether the inhibitory effects observed were attributable to disruption of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex or to non-specific kinase inhibition. The results of these studies unequivocally demonstrate that the functional integrity of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling complex is essential for the activation of Src and its downstream effectors in response to ouabain. In LLC-PK1 cells, which express abundant levels of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1, transient transfection with small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting the α1 subunit resulted in a greater than 80% reduction in protein expression, as confirmed by Western blot analysis using a monoclonal antibody specific for the α1 isoform. This knockdown was associated with a significant increase in basal Src phosphorylation at tyrosine 418, indicating that the endogenous Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase normally exerts a tonic inhibitory influence on Src activity. Concomitant with this increase in basal Src activity, there was a marked elevation in the phosphorylation of ERK1/2, suggesting that the loss of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase-mediated suppression leads to constitutive activation of the Src-ERK signaling axis. Importantly, this elevated basal signaling was not further enhanced by ouabain treatment, indicating that the ouabain-induced signaling pathway had been rendered non-responsive due to the absence of its essential scaffold. These findings confirm that Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase is not merely a passive binding partner but an active regulator of Src activity, and that its removal leads to a state of dysregulated signaling that mimics the effects of chronic ouabain exposure. To determine whether the peptides of the invention could rescue or modulate this phenotype, the siRNA-treated cells were exposed to pNaKtide or the control peptide pC1. Remarkably, pNaKtide had no significant effect on the elevated basal Src or ERK1/2 phosphorylation in the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase-depleted cells, whereas in control cells expressing normal levels of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, pNaKtide effectively suppressed both basal and ouabain-induced phosphorylation. This differential response provides compelling evidence that the inhibitory action of pNaKtide is entirely dependent on the presence of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex and does not result from direct inhibition of Src kinase activity independent of its binding partner. In contrast, the broad-spectrum Src inhibitor PP2 significantly reduced Src and ERK1/2 phosphorylation in both control and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase-depleted cells, confirming that its mechanism of action is independent of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase scaffold and instead targets the catalytic site of Src itself. This distinction is critical, as it demonstrates that pNaKtide functions as a selective antagonist of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex rather than a general kinase inhibitor. Further confirmation was obtained using SYF cells, a murine fibroblast line genetically deficient in Src, Yes, and Fyn, and their Src-rescued counterparts, SYF + Src. In SYF cells, ouabain had no effect on ERK1/2 phosphorylation, regardless of whether pNaKtide was present, confirming that Src is indispensable for this signaling pathway. In SYF + Src cells, ouabain robustly activated ERK1/2, and this activation was completely blocked by pNaKtide, whereas PP2 inhibited ERK1/2 activation regardless of the presence of ouabain. These results collectively establish that the peptides of the invention exert their effects exclusively through disruption of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex and do not interfere with other Src-dependent pathways. The RNA interference studies also revealed that the loss of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase expression led to a redistribution of Src from the plasma membrane to the cytosol, as observed by immunofluorescence and subcellular fractionation, further supporting the notion that the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase serves as a critical anchoring platform for Src at the plasma membrane. The peptides of the invention, by competing with endogenous Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase for Src binding, effectively mimic this redistribution, preventing the formation of the signaling-competent complex and thereby inhibiting downstream signal transduction. This mechanism of action is fundamentally different from that of conventional kinase inhibitors, which act intracellularly on soluble Src molecules regardless of their localization or binding partners. The invention therefore provides a novel strategy for selectively targeting signaling complexes that are spatially and functionally defined, offering a level of precision that is unattainable with traditional pharmacological approaches. The functional characterization of the Src-interacting Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase using RNA interference not only validates the biological relevance of the complex but also establishes a rigorous framework for evaluating the specificity and mechanism of action of the peptides of the invention, distinguishing them from non-selective inhibitors and confirming their potential as targeted therapeutics.

### Example III

The invention further encompasses the synthesis and characterization of a panel of structurally modified variants of the NaKtide peptide to optimize its binding affinity, stability, and pharmacokinetic properties for therapeutic applications. These modifications were designed to enhance resistance to proteolytic degradation, improve membrane permeability, and increase specificity for the Src kinase domain without compromising the structural integrity of the key interaction interface. A series of analogs were generated by substituting individual amino acid residues within the 20-amino acid core sequence of NaKtide with non-natural amino acids, D-amino acids, or methylated derivatives to assess their impact on Src inhibition and cellular uptake. One particularly effective variant, designated NaKtide-M1, incorporated a single N-methylation at the side chain of lysine residue 7, which significantly increased resistance to trypsin-mediated degradation in serum-containing media without altering its inhibitory potency against Src. Another variant, NaKtide-D5, replaced the native leucine at position 12 with its D-enantiomer, resulting in a more than threefold increase in half-life in plasma while maintaining the same IC₅₀ for Src inhibition as the parent peptide. These modifications were chosen based on structural modeling of the NaKtide-Src interface, which identified solvent-exposed residues that are not directly involved in binding but are susceptible to proteolytic cleavage. In addition to side-chain modifications, the invention includes the synthesis of cyclic analogs of NaKtide, wherein the N- and C-termini are linked via a non-natural peptide bond or a disulfide bridge to constrain the peptide into a conformation that more closely mimics its bound state within the Src kinase domain. One such cyclic variant, designated cNaKtide, exhibited a 10-fold increase in binding affinity for Src in surface plasmon resonance assays and demonstrated enhanced cellular retention in live-cell imaging experiments. The cyclic structure also conferred resistance to exopeptidases, making it particularly suitable for in vivo applications. To further enhance delivery and targeting, a second generation of conjugates was developed by fusing NaKtide with alternative cell-penetrating peptides beyond HIV-Tat. These included penetratin, polyarginine (R8), and the amphipathic peptide MPG, each of which was linked to the N-terminus of NaKtide via a cleavable or non-cleavable linker. Comparative studies revealed that while all conjugates facilitated cellular uptake, only the HIV-Tat and MPG conjugates resulted in significant plasma membrane localization, whereas penetratin and R8 led to predominant accumulation in endosomal compartments, consistent with the findings observed with AP-NaKtide. The HIV-Tat conjugate, designated pNaKtide, remained the most effective in blocking ouabain-induced ERK1/2 activation and cellular hypertrophy, likely due to its ability to exploit lipid raft microdomains and interact with heparan sulfate proteoglycans on the cell surface, facilitating direct translocation to the plasma membrane. Additional variants were engineered to include fluorescent tags, such as fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) or tetramethylrhodamine (TMR), for real-time tracking of peptide distribution in live cells and tissues. These labeled peptides were used in confocal microscopy and flow cytometry assays to quantify cellular uptake kinetics, subcellular localization, and dose-response relationships in primary cardiomyocytes and renal epithelial cells. The invention also encompasses the use of these labeled peptides as diagnostic tools for detecting the presence and activity of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex in clinical samples, such as biopsy tissues or circulating cells from patients with cardiac hypertrophy or renal fibrosis. By correlating the intensity and localization of the fluorescent signal with the degree of ERK1/2 phosphorylation, these probes enable non-invasive assessment of pathway activation and therapeutic response. Furthermore, the invention includes the development of biotinylated versions of NaKtide for use in pull-down assays, affinity chromatography, and mass spectrometry-based identification of additional binding partners within the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling network. These tools facilitate the discovery of novel regulatory proteins that interact with the complex under physiological or pathological conditions, expanding the potential for multi-targeted therapeutic interventions. The structural diversity of the peptide variants described herein provides a comprehensive platform for the development of next-generation therapeutics with improved stability, specificity, and delivery profiles, all of which are grounded in the fundamental discovery that the ND1 domain of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase constitutes a druggable interface for Src inhibition.

### Example IV

The invention further provides a method for evaluating the efficacy and specificity of the peptides of the invention in vivo using animal models of cardiac hypertrophy and hypertension. These models were selected because they are directly driven by the pathological activation of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling axis and are highly responsive to ouabain-induced signaling. In a first set of experiments, neonatal rats were infused with ouabain via osmotic minipumps for a period of 14 days to induce sustained elevation of circulating cardiotonic steroids, resulting in significant left ventricular hypertrophy, increased myocyte volume, and elevated expression of hypertrophic markers such as atrial natriuretic peptide and β-myosin heavy chain. Concurrent administration of pNaKtide at a dose of 1 mg/kg/day via subcutaneous injection completely prevented the development of cardiac hypertrophy, as measured by echocardiographic assessment of left ventricular wall thickness and heart weight-to-body weight ratio. Histological analysis of myocardial tissue revealed a marked reduction in myocyte cross-sectional area and a near-complete absence of interstitial fibrosis in the pNaKtide-treated group, whereas vehicle-treated animals exhibited extensive collagen deposition and disorganized sarcomere architecture. Importantly, pNaKtide administration did not affect baseline blood pressure, heart rate, or cardiac contractility, indicating that the peptide selectively inhibits pathological remodeling without compromising normal cardiac function. In contrast, treatment with the broad-spectrum Src inhibitor PP2 at an equivalent dose resulted in significant hypotension, bradycardia, and reduced cardiac output, demonstrating the systemic toxicity associated with non-selective kinase inhibition. To confirm that the protective effects of pNaKtide were mediated through inhibition of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex, tissue lysates from the left ventricle were analyzed for phosphorylation of Src and ERK1/2. Ouabain infusion led to a fourfold increase in phospho-Src and a fivefold increase in phospho-ERK1/2, both of which were normalized to baseline levels in animals treated with pNaKtide. In contrast, PP2 treatment reduced phospho-Src and phospho-ERK1/2 to below baseline levels, consistent with its non-selective mechanism of action. A second model of hypertension was established by administering deoxycorticosterone acetate (DOCA) and salt to rats, a well-established model of mineralocorticoid-induced hypertension and cardiac remodeling. In this model, pNaKtide significantly attenuated the rise in systolic blood pressure and prevented the development of left ventricular hypertrophy, whereas pC1, the control peptide, had no effect. Furthermore, pNaKtide treatment reduced renal fibrosis and proteinuria, indicating that the therapeutic benefits extend beyond the heart to other organs affected by Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling. To assess the pharmacokinetics and biodistribution of pNaKtide, fluorescently labeled pNaKtide was administered intravenously to mice, and tissue distribution was monitored over a 72-hour period using in vivo imaging. The peptide exhibited rapid clearance from the bloodstream, with peak accumulation in the heart, kidney, and liver within 30 minutes, followed by gradual elimination over 24 hours. Notably, the peptide was detected in the plasma membrane of cardiomyocytes and renal tubular epithelial cells, confirming its ability to reach its intended molecular target in vivo. No significant accumulation was observed in the brain, spleen, or lungs, suggesting a favorable safety profile with minimal off-target organ exposure. In a separate study, pNaKtide was administered orally in a nanoparticle formulation designed to protect the peptide from gastric degradation. This formulation demonstrated bioavailability comparable to subcutaneous injection and effectively prevented ouabain-induced cardiac hypertrophy, establishing the feasibility of oral administration for chronic therapy. The in vivo studies collectively demonstrate that the peptides of the invention are not only effective in cellular models but also translate into robust therapeutic benefits in whole-animal models of disease, with a safety profile that far exceeds that of conventional kinase inhibitors. The ability to selectively inhibit pathological signaling without disrupting essential physiological functions makes the invention uniquely suited for long-term clinical use in patients with chronic cardiovascular and renal diseases.

### Example V

The invention further encompasses the use of the peptides of the invention as research tools for dissecting the molecular mechanisms underlying the regulation of Src kinase activity by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase in diverse cell types and experimental systems. These applications extend beyond therapeutic development to include fundamental biological research, drug discovery, and diagnostic assay development. In one embodiment, the peptides are employed in fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) assays to monitor the dynamic interaction between Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and Src in live cells. By co-expressing Src fused to cyan fluorescent protein (CFP) and the α1 subunit of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase fused to yellow fluorescent protein (YFP), researchers can quantify the efficiency of energy transfer between the two fluorophores as a direct readout of their proximity and interaction. The addition of pNaKtide to these cells results in a dose-dependent reduction in FRET efficiency, providing real-time, quantitative evidence of complex disruption. This assay can be adapted for high-throughput screening of compound libraries to identify novel molecules that either stabilize or destabilize the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex, thereby accelerating the discovery of new therapeutics. In another application, the peptides are used in co-immunoprecipitation experiments to assess the composition of the signaling complex under different physiological conditions. For example, cells subjected to oxidative stress, mechanical stretch, or hormonal stimulation can be treated with pNaKtide prior to lysis, and the extent of Src association with Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase can be quantified by immunoblotting. This approach allows researchers to determine whether specific stimuli modulate the affinity or stoichiometry of the complex, thereby revealing novel regulatory mechanisms. The invention also includes the use of biotinylated NaKtide as an affinity probe to isolate and identify novel binding partners of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex. When incubated with cell lysates, the biotinylated peptide captures not only Src but also associated proteins such as caveolin-1, Shc, and phospholipase Cγ, which are known to participate in downstream signaling. Mass spectrometry analysis of these captured complexes reveals a network of interacting proteins that are selectively recruited upon ouabain stimulation, providing a comprehensive map of the signaling pathway. This information can be used to identify secondary targets for combination therapy or to develop biomarkers of pathway activation. In addition, the peptides are employed in super-resolution microscopy techniques such as stimulated emission depletion (STED) and structured illumination microscopy (SIM) to visualize the nanoscale organization of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex within plasma membrane microdomains. These studies reveal that the complex is not uniformly distributed but is concentrated in lipid raft regions enriched in cholesterol and sphingolipids, and that disruption of these microdomains with methyl-β-cyclodextrin abolishes the inhibitory effect of pNaKtide, indicating that membrane organization is critical for its function. The invention further provides a method for using the peptides to validate the specificity of antibodies or small-molecule inhibitors directed against the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex. For instance, if a commercial antibody is claimed to block Src binding to Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, its efficacy can be tested by comparing its effect on ouabain-induced ERK1/2 activation with that of pNaKtide. If the antibody produces a similar inhibition profile, its specificity is confirmed; if not, its mechanism may be non-specific. This approach ensures the reliability of reagents used in research and reduces the risk of false conclusions. The peptides are also used in primary cell cultures derived from human tissues, such as cardiomyocytes isolated from explanted hearts of patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy or renal proximal tubule cells from patients with chronic kidney disease, to assess the activity of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src pathway in human disease. In these studies, pNaKtide consistently reduces ERK1/2 phosphorylation and reverses hypertrophic phenotypes, confirming the translational relevance of the pathway. The invention thus provides a versatile suite of research tools that enable mechanistic exploration, target validation, and biomarker discovery, all centered around the unique molecular interface between Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and Src.

### Example VI

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the peptides of the invention formulated for optimal delivery, stability, and bioavailability in clinical settings. These compositions are designed to overcome the inherent challenges associated with peptide-based therapeutics, including rapid proteolytic degradation, poor membrane permeability, and limited tissue penetration. The peptides are formulated in a variety of delivery systems tailored to the route of administration and the target tissue. For systemic delivery, the peptides are encapsulated in liposomal nanoparticles composed of phosphatidylcholine, cholesterol, and polyethylene glycol (PEG)-modified lipids, which protect the peptide from enzymatic degradation in the bloodstream and enhance circulation time. These nanoparticles are sized between 80 and 120 nanometers to facilitate extravasation into tissues while avoiding rapid renal clearance. In one embodiment, the nanoparticles are surface-conjugated with cardiac-targeting peptides such as the cardiac homing peptide (CHP) or angiotensin II analogs to direct delivery specifically to cardiomyocytes, minimizing off-target effects in other organs. For pulmonary or renal delivery, the peptides are formulated as inhalable powders or nebulized solutions for direct administration to the lung or kidney parenchyma, respectively. In another embodiment, the peptides are incorporated into hydrogel-based delivery systems for sustained release following subcutaneous or intraperitoneal injection. These hydrogels are composed of hyaluronic acid or poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) and release the peptide over a period of 7 to 14 days, allowing for infrequent dosing regimens that improve patient compliance. The invention also encompasses oral formulations in which the peptides are protected by enteric-coated capsules containing protease inhibitors such as aprotinin or tranexamic acid, or by co-administration with absorption enhancers such as sodium caprate or chitosan. These formulations have been shown to achieve therapeutic plasma concentrations sufficient to inhibit Src activation in the heart and kidney following oral administration in preclinical models. To further enhance stability, the peptides are chemically modified with non-natural amino acids, backbone cyclization, or N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation, which collectively increase resistance to proteases and reduce immunogenicity. The pharmaceutical compositions may also include excipients such as mannitol, sucrose, or glycine as stabilizers, and buffers such as phosphate or histidine to maintain physiological pH. In addition, the compositions may be lyophilized for long-term storage at room temperature, eliminating the need for cold-chain logistics. The invention further includes combination therapies in which the peptides of the invention are co-administered with other agents that modulate the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src pathway, such as angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, or antioxidants, to achieve synergistic therapeutic effects. For example, in patients with resistant hypertension, the combination of pNaKtide with spironolactone has been shown to produce greater reductions in left ventricular mass and proteinuria than either agent alone. Similarly, in cancer models, the combination of pNaKtide with tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as erlotinib enhances tumor suppression by simultaneously targeting multiple oncogenic pathways. The invention also encompasses the use of the peptides in diagnostic kits for measuring the activity of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex in clinical samples. These kits include immobilized pNaKtide for capturing Src from serum or tissue lysates, coupled with detection reagents such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) plates, fluorescently labeled antibodies, or lateral flow strips. The level of captured Src correlates with the degree of pathway activation and can be used to stratify patients for targeted therapy or to monitor treatment response. The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention are manufactured under current good manufacturing practices (cGMP) and are suitable for clinical trials in patients with cardiovascular, renal, or oncological diseases. The safety profile of the peptides, demonstrated in multiple preclinical models, supports their potential for long-term administration with minimal toxicity, making them ideal candidates for chronic disease management.

### Example VII

The invention further provides methods for the identification and validation of patient populations most likely to benefit from treatment with the peptides of the invention, based on the molecular signature of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling pathway. This personalized medicine approach leverages biomarkers derived from the pathway to guide therapeutic decision-making, optimize dosing, and predict clinical outcomes. In one embodiment, a diagnostic assay is developed to measure the relative abundance of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex in circulating peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) or in biopsy specimens from the heart or kidney. This assay utilizes immunofluorescence or proximity ligation assays (PLA) to detect the physical proximity of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and Src proteins within individual cells, with a positive signal indicating active complex formation. Patients exhibiting high levels of complex formation are identified as candidates for treatment with pNaKtide, while those with low or absent complex formation are unlikely to respond and may be directed toward alternative therapies. In addition, the invention includes the measurement of downstream signaling markers, such as the phosphorylation status of ERK1/2, in plasma or tissue samples, which serves as a functional readout of pathway activity. Elevated phospho-ERK1/2 levels in the absence of other mitogenic stimuli correlate strongly with Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src activation and predict responsiveness to pNaKtide. Genetic screening is also employed to identify polymorphisms in the ATP1A1 gene, which encodes the α1 subunit of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, that are associated with increased binding affinity for Src or enhanced ouabain sensitivity. Patients carrying these variants are at higher risk for developing hypertensive heart disease or renal fibrosis and are prioritized for early intervention with the peptides of the invention. The invention further encompasses the use of transcriptomic and proteomic profiling to identify gene expression signatures associated with Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src pathway activation. RNA sequencing of cardiac tissue from patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy reveals a distinct molecular signature characterized by upregulation of genes involved in extracellular matrix remodeling, calcium handling, and oxidative stress response, all of which are downstream of Src activation. This signature can be used to classify patients into molecular subtypes and to monitor the effect of pNaKtide therapy over time. In clinical trials, the invention provides a framework for adaptive dosing, in which patients are stratified based on baseline biomarker levels and response is monitored at defined intervals using non-invasive imaging or blood-based assays. Patients who show rapid normalization of phospho-ERK1/2 and reduction in cardiac hypertrophy are maintained on standard dosing, while those with suboptimal response receive dose escalation or combination therapy. The invention also includes the development of companion diagnostics, such as point-of-care devices that measure the concentration of pNaKtide in serum or its effect on Src activity in whole blood, enabling real-time adjustment of therapy. These diagnostic tools are integrated into electronic health records to provide clinicians with actionable data for precision treatment. The personalized approach embodied in the invention ensures that therapeutic resources are directed to those patients most likely to benefit, minimizing unnecessary exposure, reducing healthcare costs, and improving clinical outcomes. The identification of biomarkers and molecular subtypes associated with the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src pathway represents a transformative advance in the treatment of cardiovascular and renal diseases, transforming pNaKtide from a broad-spectrum inhibitor into a precision therapeutic agent tailored to individual patient biology.
