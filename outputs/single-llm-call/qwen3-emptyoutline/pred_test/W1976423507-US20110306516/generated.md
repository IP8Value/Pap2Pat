# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods for the rapid, standardized, and highly efficient isolation of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from reprogramming cultures of somatic cells, particularly dermal fibroblasts, through the use of a defined combination of surface marker-based selection criteria. More specifically, the invention provides a novel fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) strategy that enables the purification of fully reprogrammed iPSCs by simultaneously identifying cells that are negative for the fibroblast-specific surface marker CD13 and positive for the pluripotency-associated markers SSEA4 and TRA-1-60. This method permits the isolation of clonal, high-fidelity iPSC populations as early as seven days post-reprogramming initiation, significantly reducing the time and labor traditionally required for manual colony picking, while simultaneously eliminating contaminating partially reprogrammed cells, non-transduced fibroblasts, and transformed cell populations that commonly compromise the purity and utility of conventionally derived iPSC lines. The invention is applicable across a broad range of reprogramming platforms, including both integrating viral vectors such as retroviruses and non-integrating vectors such as Sendai virus, and is compatible with fibroblasts derived from diverse human sources, including fresh biopsies, frozen tissue stocks, and established cell repositories from both healthy individuals and patients with genetic or acquired diseases. The method enables the generation of clinically relevant, genetically stable, and functionally validated iPSC lines with unprecedented consistency, scalability, and reproducibility, thereby addressing critical barriers to the translation of iPSC technology into regenerative medicine, disease modeling, and high-throughput drug screening applications.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of regenerative medicine has been profoundly transformed by the discovery that somatic cells can be reprogrammed into a pluripotent state through the ectopic expression of a defined set of transcription factors, originally identified as Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc. These reprogrammed cells, termed induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), exhibit morphological, molecular, and functional characteristics nearly indistinguishable from embryonic stem cells, including the capacity for indefinite self-renewal and the potential to differentiate into all three embryonic germ layers. This breakthrough has opened new avenues for patient-specific cell therapies, in vitro disease modeling, and personalized drug discovery, as iPSCs can be derived from easily accessible somatic tissues such as skin fibroblasts, thereby circumventing the ethical and immunological challenges associated with embryonic stem cells. However, despite the conceptual elegance of this reprogramming paradigm, the practical implementation of iPSC derivation has been severely hampered by persistent technical limitations that undermine the reliability, efficiency, and clinical applicability of the process.

The most widely used methods for generating iPSCs involve the delivery of reprogramming factors via viral vectors, particularly retroviruses or lentiviruses, which integrate into the host genome and drive sustained expression of the exogenous transcription factors. While effective, these integrating systems introduce a significant risk of insertional mutagenesis, potentially disrupting endogenous gene regulation or activating oncogenes, thereby rendering the resulting iPSC lines unsuitable for therapeutic use. To mitigate this risk, non-integrating approaches such as Sendai virus, episomal plasmids, mRNA transfection, and protein transduction have been developed. Although these methods eliminate the concern of genomic integration, they often result in lower reprogramming efficiencies and require extended culture periods to allow for the dilution or clearance of the reprogramming vectors, further complicating the isolation of pure iPSC colonies.

Regardless of the delivery system employed, the identification and isolation of successfully reprogrammed cells remain a major bottleneck in the workflow. Conventional protocols rely on manual selection of colonies based on morphological criteria—namely, compact, dome-shaped colonies with high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratios and well-defined borders—that resemble human embryonic stem cell colonies. This process is labor-intensive, subjective, and requires extensive training to distinguish true pluripotent colonies from partially reprogrammed or transformed cell aggregates that may exhibit similar morphology but lack full pluripotency. Moreover, the time required for colonies to become visually distinct typically spans four to six weeks, during which time heterogeneous populations of cells—including non-transduced fibroblasts, partially reprogrammed intermediates, and aberrantly proliferating cells—proliferate and overgrow the culture, necessitating repeated passaging and screening to isolate viable clones. Even after prolonged culture, many manually picked lines retain residual populations of somatic cells or partially reprogrammed intermediates, as evidenced by the continued expression of fibroblast markers such as CD13, which is a well-documented surface antigen expressed on mesenchymal cells including dermal fibroblasts but silenced upon successful reprogramming to pluripotency.

Previous efforts to improve the efficiency and objectivity of iPSC isolation have explored the use of fluorescent reporter systems, such as GFP under the control of pluripotency promoters like Oct4 or Nanog. While these systems offer real-time monitoring of reprogramming dynamics, they require genetic modification of the somatic cell source, which is incompatible with clinical applications and introduces additional regulatory hurdles. Alternative approaches have focused on the detection of cell surface markers associated with pluripotency, such as SSEA4, TRA-1-60, TRA-1-81, and SSEA3, which are upregulated during reprogramming and absent on somatic fibroblasts. Several studies have demonstrated that co-expression of SSEA4 and TRA-1-81 can enrich for pluripotent cells at later stages of reprogramming, but these methods still suffer from significant limitations. Notably, a substantial proportion of cells expressing these pluripotency markers continue to co-express CD13, indicating that they are not fully reprogrammed but rather represent transitional or aberrant intermediates that retain somatic identity. Consequently, selection based solely on positive markers leads to the isolation of heterogeneous populations that include non-functional or unstable iPSCs, which may exhibit epigenetic abnormalities, incomplete transgene silencing, or reduced differentiation potential.

Furthermore, the variability in reprogramming kinetics across different cell sources—such as fibroblasts derived from neonatal foreskin, adult skin biopsies, or frozen cell lines—compounds the difficulty of establishing standardized protocols. The timing of marker expression, the proportion of reprogrammed cells, and the persistence of contaminating populations differ significantly depending on the donor age, tissue origin, passage number, and reprogramming method employed. As a result, existing protocols lack the robustness and reproducibility necessary for large-scale applications, particularly in clinical settings where consistency and traceability are paramount. There is a critical and unmet need for a method that enables the early, objective, and highly specific isolation of fully reprogrammed iPSCs without reliance on morphological assessment, genetic modification, or prolonged culture periods. Such a method must be universally applicable across diverse reprogramming platforms and somatic cell sources, capable of eliminating contaminating cell types with high fidelity, and compatible with downstream applications requiring clonal purity and genomic stability. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing a novel, multi-parameter surface marker signature that combines negative selection against CD13 with positive selection for SSEA4 and TRA-1-60, enabling the precise purification of bona fide iPSCs as early as seven days post-reprogramming initiation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and highly effective method for the rapid and precise isolation of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from reprogramming cultures of somatic cells by employing a defined combination of surface marker expression profiles identified through fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS). The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that the co-expression of the pluripotency-associated surface markers SSEA4 and TRA-1-60, in the absence of the fibroblast-specific marker CD13, defines a population of cells that are not merely partially reprogrammed intermediates but are instead fully reprogrammed, clonogenic, and functionally pluripotent iPSCs. This tripartite signature—CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, TRA-1-60-positive—enables the selective enrichment of iPSCs as early as seven days following the initiation of reprogramming, thereby drastically reducing the time required to obtain viable, high-quality iPSC lines compared to conventional manual colony picking methods that require four to six weeks.

The method of the invention involves the reprogramming of somatic cells, preferably dermal fibroblasts, using either integrating viral vectors such as retroviruses or non-integrating vectors such as Sendai virus, followed by the enzymatic dissociation of the culture into a single-cell suspension at an early time point between seven and fourteen days post-infection. The suspended cells are then stained with a cocktail of fluorescently labeled antibodies specific for CD13, SSEA4, and TRA-1-60, and subjected to FACS under gentle sorting conditions that preserve cell viability. Cells exhibiting the CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, TRA-1-60-positive phenotype are sorted directly onto a feeder layer or feeder-free substrate, such as irradiated murine embryonic fibroblasts or Matrigel-coated plates, in the presence of a ROCK inhibitor to enhance survival. Following a brief recovery period, the sorted cells are cultured under standard pluripotent stem cell conditions without further manual intervention, and within seven to ten days, distinct, compact colonies emerge that are highly enriched for fully reprogrammed iPSCs, with minimal contamination from fibroblasts or partially reprogrammed cells.

The invention further encompasses the use of this sorting strategy to generate a large number of independent, clonally derived iPSC lines from diverse somatic cell sources, including fibroblasts obtained from healthy donors, patients with genetic disorders such as type I diabetes, Parkinson’s disease, Alzheimer’s disease, and cardiac laminopathies, and from established cell repositories. The resulting iPSC lines exhibit normal karyotypes, retain the genetic fingerprint of the parental fibroblast line, express endogenous pluripotency genes such as OCT4, SOX2, NANOG, and KLF4, silence exogenous reprogramming transgenes, and demonstrate robust differentiation potential into all three embryonic germ layers both in vitro, through embryoid body formation, and in vivo, through teratoma formation in immunocompromised mice. Importantly, the iPSC lines derived by this method exhibit significantly lower levels of residual CD13 expression and more complete transgene silencing compared to manually picked lines, indicating superior reprogramming fidelity and reduced heterogeneity.

The invention further provides that the CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, TRA-1-60-positive sorting strategy is effective across multiple reprogramming platforms, including retroviral and Sendai virus-mediated reprogramming, and is compatible with fibroblasts derived from fresh biopsies, frozen stocks, and long-term cell cultures. The method eliminates the need for morphological assessment, genetic modification, or extended culture periods, thereby enabling high-throughput, standardized, and scalable production of clinical-grade iPSCs. The invention further enables the purification of established iPSC lines by periodic sorting to remove differentiated or contaminating cells, ensuring long-term culture stability and homogeneity. This approach is particularly advantageous for applications requiring large numbers of genetically identical, functionally validated iPSCs, such as automated drug screening, disease modeling, and cell therapy development. The invention thus represents a transformative advancement in the field of cellular reprogramming, providing a robust, objective, and universally applicable method for the isolation of high-fidelity iPSCs that overcomes the longstanding limitations of manual selection and marker-based enrichment strategies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Rapid Production of iPS Cells and Colonies

The present invention enables the rapid and reliable generation of induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) colonies through a novel, marker-based selection strategy that significantly reduces the time required for reprogramming from the conventional four to six weeks to as little as seven to ten days following the initiation of reprogramming. This acceleration is achieved not by enhancing the intrinsic efficiency of the reprogramming process itself, but by enabling the precise and early isolation of cells that have successfully completed the reprogramming transition, thereby eliminating the need to wait for morphological changes to become visually apparent. In conventional protocols, reprogramming is monitored by the emergence of compact, dome-shaped colonies that resemble embryonic stem cells, a process that typically requires two to three weeks of culture under feeder-dependent conditions. During this extended period, the culture becomes increasingly heterogeneous, with non-transduced fibroblasts, partially reprogrammed intermediates, and aberrantly proliferating cells outgrowing the rare, fully reprogrammed clones. This heterogeneity necessitates labor-intensive manual picking, which is inherently subjective, prone to error, and incapable of distinguishing between true pluripotent colonies and morphologically similar aggregates of non-reprogrammed or transformed cells.

In contrast, the method of the invention identifies and isolates iPSCs at an early stage of reprogramming—specifically between seven and fourteen days post-infection—by exploiting a unique combination of surface marker expression that is both highly specific and functionally predictive of pluripotency. The key insight underlying this innovation is the recognition that while the pluripotency markers SSEA4 and TRA-1-60 are upregulated during reprogramming, their expression alone is insufficient to distinguish fully reprogrammed cells from partially reprogrammed intermediates that retain somatic identity. This is because a substantial proportion of SSEA4-positive and TRA-1-60-positive cells continue to express CD13, a cell surface glycoprotein that is a hallmark of mesenchymal fibroblasts and is normally silenced upon successful reprogramming to pluripotency. The presence of CD13 in these cells indicates incomplete reprogramming, and their persistence in culture leads to the contamination of iPSC lines with non-functional or unstable cell populations. By incorporating CD13 as a negative selection marker, the invention effectively excludes these partially reprogrammed intermediates, allowing for the isolation of only those cells that have fully silenced their somatic program and activated the pluripotency network.

The method begins with the reprogramming of somatic cells, preferably human dermal fibroblasts, using either integrating viral vectors such as retroviruses encoding the four Yamanaka factors (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc) or non-integrating vectors such as the CytoTune™ Sendai virus system. The fibroblasts are cultured under standard conditions until the onset of reprogramming, which is typically detectable by the appearance of SSEA4 and TRA-1-60 expression as early as seven days post-infection. At this time point, the culture is harvested by enzymatic dissociation using a combination of Dispase and Accutase to generate a single-cell suspension, which is then filtered through a 35 µm cell strainer to remove cellular debris and clumps. The cells are stained with a cocktail of fluorescently conjugated antibodies specific for CD13, SSEA4, and TRA-1-60, each labeled with distinct fluorophores to allow for multiplexed detection. The staining is performed in a sterile buffer containing bovine serum albumin, EDTA, and glucose to maintain cell viability and minimize non-specific binding. Following a fifteen-minute incubation at room temperature, the cells are washed and immediately subjected to fluorescence-activated cell sorting on a high-resolution flow cytometer equipped with a 100 µm ceramic nozzle and operated under gentle sorting conditions to preserve cell integrity.

The sorting strategy is designed to isolate only those cells that are negative for CD13 and positive for both SSEA4 and TRA-1-60. This population, designated CD13negSSEA4posTRA-1-60pos, is sorted directly onto a feeder layer of γ-irradiated murine embryonic fibroblasts or onto Matrigel-coated plates, at a density of between 2,000 and 50,000 cells per well, in the presence of a ROCK inhibitor such as Y-27632 to enhance survival following dissociation. Within two days of sorting, the ROCK inhibitor is removed and replaced with a small molecule cocktail containing an ALK5 inhibitor, a MEK inhibitor, and a ROCK inhibitor to promote the proliferation and stabilization of the reprogrammed cells. The culture is maintained under standard human embryonic stem cell medium conditions, with daily medium exchanges, and without any further manual intervention. Within seven to ten days, distinct, compact colonies emerge that are morphologically indistinguishable from those derived by conventional manual picking, but with a significantly higher degree of homogeneity and purity. These colonies are free of the fibroblast-like cells and transformed aggregates that commonly contaminate manually selected lines, and they exhibit rapid expansion rates and high cloning efficiency.

The ability to isolate iPSC colonies at such an early stage is a critical advantage of the invention. Whereas conventional methods require waiting for colonies to grow large enough to be visually identified, the method of the invention enables the selection of cells based on molecular markers that are functionally linked to the completion of reprogramming. This eliminates the need for extended culture periods, reduces the risk of spontaneous transformation or epigenetic drift, and allows for the simultaneous initiation of multiple reprogramming experiments in parallel. The result is a dramatic increase in throughput and reproducibility, enabling the generation of hundreds of iPSC lines from hundreds of fibroblast lines within a short timeframe. Furthermore, because the selection is based on objective, quantifiable criteria rather than subjective morphological assessment, the method is highly standardized and can be implemented in high-throughput screening environments, automated cell culture systems, and clinical manufacturing facilities. The invention thus transforms iPSC derivation from a slow, artisanal process into a rapid, scalable, and reliable technology suitable for widespread application in research and medicine.

### Immunofluorescence and Microscopy

The validation and characterization of the induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) lines generated by the method of the invention are supported by a comprehensive suite of immunofluorescence and microscopy techniques that confirm the expression of key pluripotency markers, the absence of somatic cell contaminants, and the structural integrity of the reprogrammed colonies. These techniques serve not only as essential quality control measures but also as powerful tools for demonstrating the fidelity and functionality of the iPSCs derived through the CD13negSSEA4posTRA-1-60pos sorting strategy. Immunofluorescence staining is performed on fixed colonies grown on feeder layers or Matrigel-coated substrates, using a panel of primary antibodies specific for core pluripotency transcription factors and surface antigens, followed by fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies and nuclear counterstaining with DAPI. The resulting images are acquired using high-resolution confocal or epifluorescence microscopes equipped with automated z-stack acquisition and image analysis software to quantify marker expression at the single-cell level.

Consistent with the expected phenotype of fully reprogrammed iPSCs, colonies derived by the method of the invention exhibit strong, uniform expression of the nuclear transcription factors OCT4, SOX2, and NANOG, as well as the cell surface markers SSEA4 and TRA-1-60. These markers are expressed in a pattern that is indistinguishable from that observed in human embryonic stem cell lines, with intense nuclear staining for OCT4 and SOX2 and robust membrane localization for SSEA4 and TRA-1-60. Importantly, the expression of these markers is homogeneous across the entire colony, with minimal heterogeneity or mosaic staining patterns that are commonly observed in manually picked lines. In contrast, manually derived colonies frequently display patchy or weak expression of NANOG and OCT4, indicative of incomplete reprogramming or the presence of residual fibroblasts that have not fully silenced their somatic program. The method of the invention eliminates this heterogeneity by selecting only those cells that have achieved complete activation of the endogenous pluripotency network and complete silencing of somatic markers.

A critical feature of the immunofluorescence analysis is the consistent absence of CD13 expression in the sorted iPSC colonies. While CD13 is readily detectable on the parental fibroblasts and on partially reprogrammed intermediates that persist in conventional cultures, it is entirely absent in the CD13negSSEA4posTRA-1-60pos-derived colonies, confirming that the sorting strategy effectively excludes cells that retain somatic identity. This absence is further corroborated by co-staining experiments that demonstrate the mutual exclusivity of CD13 and TRA-1-60 expression: cells expressing TRA-1-60 never co-express CD13, and vice versa. This clear dichotomy underscores the specificity of the sorting strategy and provides visual confirmation that the isolated colonies are composed of cells that have undergone a complete transition from a fibroblastic to a pluripotent state.

In addition to confirming the expression of pluripotency markers, immunofluorescence is used to assess the differentiation potential of the iPSC lines by examining their ability to form embryoid bodies (EBs) and to differentiate into cells of the three embryonic germ layers. EBs generated from sorted iPSCs are fixed, sectioned, and stained with antibodies specific for endodermal markers such as α1-fetoprotein (AFP), mesodermal markers such as smooth muscle actin (αSMA), and ectodermal markers such as βIII-tubulin (Tuj1). The resulting images reveal well-defined, spatially organized expression patterns of these lineage-specific markers, with AFP-positive epithelial structures, αSMA-positive smooth muscle bundles, and Tuj1-positive neuronal processes clearly identifiable within the same EB. This demonstrates that the iPSCs derived by the method of the invention retain the full developmental potential of pluripotent stem cells and are capable of generating a diverse array of differentiated cell types without bias or restriction.

Microscopy is also employed to monitor the morphology of the iPSC colonies over time, both during the early stages of colony formation and following extended passaging. Colonies derived by the sorting method exhibit a characteristic compact, three-dimensional architecture with sharp, well-defined borders and high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratios, consistent with the morphology of high-quality human embryonic stem cells. These colonies remain stable over multiple passages, even after adaptation to feeder-free conditions on Matrigel or in mTeSR1 medium, and do not exhibit signs of spontaneous differentiation, such as flattening, loss of colony cohesion, or the emergence of fibroblast-like outgrowths. In contrast, manually derived colonies frequently show signs of instability, including irregular borders, heterogeneous cell morphology, and the appearance of CD13-positive cells at later passages, indicating incomplete reprogramming or contamination. The method of the invention thus not only enables the isolation of high-quality iPSCs at an early stage but also ensures their long-term stability and homogeneity, making them suitable for downstream applications requiring consistent and reproducible cell behavior.

### Primers

The molecular characterization of the induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) lines generated by the method of the invention relies on a precisely defined set of primers used in quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) and other nucleic acid-based assays to assess the expression of endogenous pluripotency genes, the silencing of exogenous reprogramming transgenes, and the absence of somatic cell contamination. These primers are designed to be highly specific, with amplicons spanning exon-exon junctions to prevent amplification of genomic DNA, and are validated for efficiency, sensitivity, and reproducibility across multiple cell lines and experimental conditions. The primer sequences are selected to distinguish between endogenous and exogenous transcripts, enabling the accurate quantification of gene expression dynamics during reprogramming and the confirmation of complete transgene silencing, which is a critical indicator of full pluripotency.

For the detection of endogenous pluripotency genes, primers are designed to amplify the native human sequences of OCT4 (POU5F1), SOX2, NANOG, KLF4, and c-MYC, with amplicon lengths ranging from 80 to 150 base pairs to ensure optimal amplification efficiency. These primers are carefully validated to avoid cross-reactivity with pseudogenes or related family members, and their specificity is confirmed by sequencing of the PCR products and by melt curve analysis, which reveals a single, sharp peak corresponding to the expected melting temperature. The expression of these endogenous genes is consistently upregulated in iPSC lines derived by the method of the invention, reaching levels comparable to those observed in human embryonic stem cell controls. This upregulation is observed as early as passage five and remains stable through passage twenty, indicating that the reprogramming process has successfully reactivated the endogenous pluripotency network and that the resulting iPSCs are epigenetically stable.

In contrast, primers specific for the exogenous reprogramming transgenes are used to assess their silencing in the derived iPSC lines. For retroviral reprogramming, primers are designed to target the viral backbone sequences flanking the transgenes, such as the LTR regions of the retroviral vector, to distinguish between integrated viral transcripts and endogenous gene expression. For Sendai virus reprogramming, primers are designed to amplify the viral RNA genome, specifically targeting the nucleocapsid (N), polymerase (L), and fusion (F) genes, which are not present in the human genome. The qRT-PCR analysis reveals that iPSC lines derived by the method of the invention exhibit significantly lower levels of exogenous transgene expression compared to manually picked lines, with many sorted lines showing complete silencing by passage ten. This is in stark contrast to manually derived lines, which frequently retain detectable levels of retroviral transcripts even after prolonged culture, indicating incomplete reprogramming or the persistence of partially reprogrammed cells. The primers used in these assays are optimized for high sensitivity, allowing for the detection of as few as ten copies of viral RNA per reaction, thereby enabling the precise quantification of residual transgene expression and the identification of lines that are suitable for clinical applications.

In addition to pluripotency and transgene-specific primers, the invention employs primers to detect the presence of somatic cell contaminants, particularly those expressing CD13. Primers targeting the CD13 gene (ANPEP) are used to confirm the absence of fibroblast contamination in the sorted iPSC lines. These primers amplify a region of the CD13 transcript that is distinct from any potential pseudogenes and are validated to detect CD13 expression in parental fibroblasts but not in fully reprogrammed iPSCs. The consistent absence of CD13 amplification in sorted lines, even after multiple passages, confirms that the FACS sorting strategy effectively eliminates somatic cell contamination. Furthermore, primers for other fibroblast-specific markers, such as THY1 (CD90) and COL1A1, are used to provide additional confirmation of the purity of the iPSC lines. The use of these primers in conjunction with qRT-PCR and NanoString nCounter assays provides a multi-layered molecular validation of the iPSC lines, ensuring that they meet the stringent criteria required for research and clinical applications.

The primer sets employed in the invention are also used in Southern blot analysis to assess the integration status of retroviral transgenes. In this context, primers are used to generate DIG-labeled probes that hybridize to the integrated viral sequences, allowing for the visualization of distinct banding patterns that reflect the number and location of integration events. The presence of multiple, unique integration patterns in independently sorted iPSC lines confirms that each line is derived from a distinct reprogramming event, whereas identical banding patterns in manually picked lines suggest clonal expansion from a single reprogrammed cell. This molecular fingerprinting capability, enabled by the precise design of the primers, provides an additional layer of evidence for the clonal independence and genetic diversity of the iPSC lines generated by the method of the invention.

### Comparision of Retrovirus and Sendai Virus Vectors in Reprogramming

The method of the invention is uniquely versatile in its compatibility with both integrating and non-integrating reprogramming systems, demonstrating that the CD13negSSEA4posTRA-1-60pos sorting strategy is effective regardless of the delivery mechanism used to introduce the reprogramming factors. This versatility is a critical advantage, as it allows the method to be applied across a wide spectrum of research and clinical contexts, from basic science investigations requiring long-term genetic manipulation to therapeutic applications demanding genomic integrity. The comparison between retroviral and Sendai virus reprogramming reveals important differences in the kinetics and dynamics of reprogramming, yet the sorting strategy consistently yields high-quality, fully reprogrammed iPSC lines from both systems, underscoring its robustness and generalizability.

Retroviral reprogramming, which relies on the stable integration of the Yamanaka factors into the host genome, typically results in a slower and more heterogeneous reprogramming process. The integration of viral sequences leads to sustained expression of the exogenous transgenes, which can interfere with the natural epigenetic remodeling required for complete reprogramming. As a result, a significant proportion of cells expressing SSEA4 and TRA-1-60 remain positive for CD13, indicating incomplete silencing of the somatic program. Time-course analyses of retroviral reprogramming reveal that the CD13negSSEA4posTRA-1-60pos population emerges at low frequencies as early as seven days post-infection but increases gradually over the next two weeks, peaking at approximately 14 days. However, the proportion of CD13-positive cells within the SSEA4posTRA-1-60pos population remains elevated during this period, with up to 54% of pluripotency marker-positive cells still expressing CD13 at day 14. This persistent co-expression reflects the incomplete nature of reprogramming in the retroviral system, where the continued presence of exogenous transgenes delays the full silencing of somatic markers. Despite these challenges, the method of the invention successfully isolates a pure population of iPSCs by excluding these CD13-positive intermediates, resulting in lines that exhibit significantly lower levels of residual transgene expression and greater epigenetic stability compared to manually picked controls.

In contrast, Sendai virus reprogramming, which utilizes a non-integrating, RNA-based vector that replicates in the cytoplasm and is gradually diluted through cell division, results in a more rapid and synchronous reprogramming process. The absence of genomic integration allows for a cleaner transition from the somatic to the pluripotent state, with faster downregulation of fibroblast markers and more efficient silencing of exogenous reprogramming factors. Time-course analyses demonstrate that the CD13negSSEA4posTRA-1-60pos population emerges with similar kinetics to that observed in retroviral reprogramming, but the proportion of CD13-positive cells within this population is markedly lower, with less than 2% of SSEA4posTRA-1-60pos cells expressing CD13 by day 11. This accelerated silencing of CD13 suggests that Sendai virus reprogramming more closely mimics the natural epigenetic reprogramming events observed in embryonic development, resulting in a more homogeneous and functionally superior population of iPSCs. Nevertheless, the method of the invention remains equally effective in isolating these cells, as the CD13negSSEA4posTRA-1-60pos signature is equally predictive of pluripotency in both systems.

The comparison between the two systems reveals that while Sendai virus reprogramming produces iPSCs with faster kinetics and lower levels of somatic contamination, the sorting strategy is not dependent on the reprogramming method but rather on the biological state of the cell. This is evidenced by the fact that the same antibody cocktail and sorting parameters are used for both systems, and the resulting iPSC lines exhibit identical molecular and functional characteristics. Both retroviral- and Sendai virus-derived lines express endogenous pluripotency genes at levels comparable to human embryonic stem cells, silence exogenous transgenes completely by passage ten, and demonstrate robust differentiation potential into all three germ layers in vitro and in vivo. Furthermore, both types of lines remain stable over multiple passages, even after adaptation to feeder-free conditions, and retain the genetic fingerprint of the parental fibroblast line.

The ability of the method to function effectively across such disparate reprogramming platforms is a testament to its fundamental biological insight: that the CD13negSSEA4posTRA-1-60pos signature is a universal marker of complete reprogramming, irrespective of the mechanism by which reprogramming is induced. This universality is of immense practical value, as it allows researchers and clinicians to choose the reprogramming system best suited to their needs—whether it be retroviral vectors for high-efficiency research applications or Sendai virus for clinical-grade cell production—without compromising the quality or purity of the resulting iPSCs. The invention thus provides a standardized, platform-independent solution to the longstanding problem of iPSC heterogeneity and contamination, enabling the generation of high-fidelity, clinically relevant iPSC lines regardless of the reprogramming technology employed.
