# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of regenerative medicine has long sought to harness the body’s intrinsic capacity for tissue repair through the targeted delivery of autologous cells. While ex vivo expansion of cells—such as mesenchymal stromal cells, hematopoietic stem cells, or endothelial progenitor cells—has been explored extensively, the clinical translation of such approaches remains hindered by stringent regulatory requirements, prohibitive costs, and prolonged processing times. Regulatory agencies, including the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency, classify cell therapies that involve more than minimal manipulation as advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs), thereby subjecting them to complex manufacturing controls, extensive characterization, and lengthy approval pathways. These regulatory burdens are particularly acute when cell isolation procedures involve the use of magnetic beads, antibodies, or other exogenous ligands that remain bound to the cell surface post-isolation. Even trace contamination or incomplete removal of such agents can trigger reclassification of the product as genetically modified or chemically altered, thereby escalating the regulatory burden and impeding clinical adoption.

Intraoperative cell therapies represent a promising paradigm shift in this landscape. By harvesting, enriching, and re-administering autologous cells during a single surgical session, these approaches circumvent the need for ex vivo expansion, cryopreservation, and prolonged storage, thereby reducing both logistical complexity and the risk of contamination or phenotypic drift. Such procedures are especially compelling in orthopedic applications, where bone fractures occur at an estimated rate of 7.9 million annually in the United States alone, with 5 to 10 percent progressing to non-unions that require additional surgical intervention. The potential to intervene early in high-risk patients by delivering a concentrated, biologically active cell population directly to the fracture site could significantly reduce morbidity, hospitalization duration, and overall healthcare expenditures. However, the clinical feasibility of such strategies is contingent upon the development of isolation technologies that are not only efficient and reproducible but also compliant with the definition of minimal manipulation under regulatory frameworks.

Current cell separation techniques predominantly rely on immunomagnetic sorting systems, such as those employing antibody-conjugated magnetic beads. While these methods offer high specificity and robust enrichment, they inherently violate the principle of minimal manipulation because the beads, or the antibodies used to attach them, remain associated with the isolated cells. The persistence of these exogenous materials on the cell surface raises concerns regarding immunogenicity, altered cell signaling, and unintended modulation of cell behavior post-transplantation. Furthermore, the removal of these beads post-isolation is technically challenging and often incomplete, leading to variability in product quality and safety profiles. Alternative methods, such as fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), while highly precise, are incompatible with intraoperative settings due to their reliance on complex instrumentation, prolonged processing times, and the requirement for cryogenic storage of reagents. These limitations underscore the urgent need for a novel cell enrichment platform that achieves high-purity isolation without introducing persistent chemical or physical modifications to the target cells.

Aptamers—short, single-stranded oligonucleotides selected through systematic evolution of ligands by exponential enrichment (SELEX)—have emerged as compelling alternatives to antibodies in molecular recognition applications. Unlike antibodies, which are proteins derived from biological systems and subject to batch-to-batch variability, aptamers are chemically synthesized with precise sequence control, enabling consistent production at scale. They exhibit remarkable stability under ambient storage conditions, are resistant to denaturation, and can be readily modified with functional groups such as biotin, fluorophores, or linkers for immobilization. Importantly, aptamers bind their targets with high specificity, often comparable to that of antibodies, yet typically with lower binding affinity, a property that, when leveraged appropriately, enables reversible capture and release without the need for harsh elution buffers or enzymatic digestion. This characteristic renders them uniquely suited for applications requiring gentle, non-destructive cell isolation.

Despite these advantages, the clinical translation of aptamer-based cell enrichment has been limited by the absence of practical, scalable, and regulatory-compliant device architectures. Existing approaches have either relied on microfluidic systems that are incompatible with surgical environments, or have required the use of complementary oligonucleotide strands, dextran sulfate, or temperature shifts to disrupt aptamer-target interactions—modifications that introduce additional reagents, complicate procedural workflows, or risk cellular damage. Moreover, no prior system has successfully integrated aptamer-coated capture matrices with a physical filtration barrier that ensures complete retention of the capture beads while permitting the free passage of cells, thereby guaranteeing that the final cell product is entirely free of beads, ligands, or residual chemical agents. The development of such a system would not only represent a technical breakthrough but would also establish a new standard for minimally manipulative cell isolation, potentially enabling a broad range of intraoperative cell therapies to bypass ATMP classification and accelerate clinical implementation.

The present invention addresses these unmet needs by introducing a novel, integrated system for the reversible, biophysically mediated enrichment of CD31+ cells from whole blood or peripheral blood mononuclear cell suspensions. The system leverages a biotinylated aptamer specific for CD31, immobilized on neutravidin-coated agarose beads, within a disposable, tube-based filtration device. The design exploits the inherent mechanical sensitivity of aptamer-target binding to achieve cell release through controlled fluid shear during bead resuspension, without the addition of any chemical, enzymatic, or thermal stimuli. Crucially, the inclusion of a 20-micron polyethylene filter at the base of each enrichment column ensures that the capture beads—ranging in diameter from 45 to 165 microns—are entirely retained within the device, while the target cells, which measure between 6 and 10 microns, are efficiently recovered in the eluate. This physical separation guarantees that the final cell population is free of beads, aptamers, and any other exogenous materials, satisfying the regulatory criteria for minimal manipulation. The system is compatible with standard surgical workflows, requires no specialized equipment beyond a syringe pump and centrifuge, and can be assembled and deployed within minutes at the point of care. The resulting CD31+ enriched cell population retains high viability and demonstrates enhanced angiogenic and osteogenic potential, validating its therapeutic utility in regenerative applications. This invention thus provides a platform technology that is not only effective and scalable but also uniquely positioned to enable the next generation of clinically viable, regulatory-compliant, intraoperative cell therapies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel, minimally manipulative system and method for the enrichment of CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population, particularly from whole blood or peripheral blood mononuclear cells, using reversible, biophysically induced binding mediated by aptamers. The invention comprises a disposable, tube-based enrichment device that integrates a porous filtration barrier with a bed of neutravidin-coated agarose beads, each surface-functionalized with a biotinylated aptamer specific for the CD31 antigen. The device enables the selective capture of CD31+ cells from a flowing cell suspension, followed by the complete and chemical-free release of these cells through mechanical agitation alone, without the use of elution buffers, complementary oligonucleotides, enzymes, or temperature modulation. The filtration barrier, composed of a 20-micron polyethylene mesh, is positioned at the base of the device and is configured to retain the capture beads while permitting the passage of leukocytes and other target cells, thereby ensuring that the final enriched cell population is entirely free of beads and residual ligands. This design satisfies the regulatory definition of minimal manipulation, distinguishing the invention from conventional immunomagnetic separation techniques that leave exogenous materials bound to the isolated cells.

The aptamer employed in the invention is a commercially available, biotinylated oligonucleotide sequence that binds with high specificity and sensitivity to the CD31 antigen expressed on the surface of monocytes, naïve T cells, and other leukocyte subsets. The aptamer is conjugated to neutravidin-coated agarose beads through the high-affinity biotin-neutravidin interaction, forming a stable yet reversible capture matrix. The binding affinity of the aptamer for CD31 is deliberately lower than that of traditional antibody-antigen interactions, which facilitates the release of captured cells under physiologically compatible shear forces. During the enrichment process, a cell suspension is passed through the device at a controlled flow rate, allowing CD31+ cells to bind to the aptamer-coated beads while CD31- cells flow through and are discarded. Following capture, the device is disconnected from the flow system, and the bead-cell complex is gently resuspended by repeated aspiration and expulsion through a syringe and needle, generating sufficient fluid shear to disrupt the aptamer-CD31 bonds without inducing cellular damage. The released cells are then collected by centrifugation, yielding a pure, bead-free, ligand-free population of CD31+ cells with viability exceeding 95 percent.

The invention further encompasses an optimized configuration of the enrichment device, wherein a mixture of aptamer-coated and uncoated beads is employed to prevent clogging, enhance flow dynamics, and improve the efficiency of bead resuspension during cell release. The optimal ratio of coated to uncoated beads is approximately 50:50, and the total bead volume is maintained between 400 and 800 microliters to maximize cell yield while preserving cell viability and purity. The enrichment system achieves a significant increase in the proportion of CD31+ cells—from approximately 64 percent in the starting population to over 87 percent in the enriched fraction—while maintaining a recovery yield of approximately 25 percent. The enriched cell population is enriched in monocytes expressing CD31 and naïve T cells expressing CD31, both of which are known to contribute to angiogenic and osteogenic processes through the secretion of cytokines, growth factors, and extracellular matrix-modulating enzymes. Functional validation of the enriched population demonstrates a 1.9-fold increase in capillary-like tube formation by human umbilical vein endothelial cells in response to conditioned medium derived from the enriched cells, as well as a 1.8-fold enhancement in mineralization of mesenchymal stromal cells, confirming the retained biological activity of the isolated cells.

The invention further includes a kit for performing the enrichment method, comprising pre-assembled enrichment devices, binding buffer, wash buffer, and instructions for use, all packaged in a sterile, single-use format suitable for intraoperative deployment. The kit eliminates the need for manual bead coating or device assembly, ensuring consistency and reducing procedural variability. Additionally, the invention provides methods of treatment wherein the enriched CD31+ cell population is administered directly to a site of tissue injury, such as a fracture site, non-union, or ischemic tissue, to promote vascularization, bone regeneration, or immunomodulation. The method may be performed during the same surgical session in which the blood is drawn, thereby enabling a true point-of-care therapeutic approach. The invention further extends to the use of the same platform for the depletion of undesirable cell populations by coating the beads with aptamers or antibodies targeting other surface markers, such as CD8, thereby enabling both positive and negative selection strategies within a unified, modular system.

The invention represents a significant advancement over existing cell isolation technologies by combining the specificity of aptamer-based recognition with the simplicity, safety, and regulatory compliance of a physically constrained, biophysically driven release mechanism. It eliminates the need for exogenous reagents during cell release, avoids the introduction of foreign materials into the final cell product, and enables rapid, scalable, and cost-effective enrichment directly in the clinical setting. This innovation opens the door to a new class of minimally manipulative cell therapies that can be deployed without the burden of ATMP classification, accelerating the translation of regenerative medicine from research laboratories to the operating room.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. CD31+ Cells

CD31, also known as Platelet Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule 1 or PECAM-1, is a transmembrane glycoprotein belonging to the immunoglobulin superfamily that is expressed on the surface of a broad spectrum of leukocytes, including monocytes, endothelial cells, and subsets of T and B lymphocytes. Its expression is particularly prominent on circulating monocytes and naïve T cells, where it serves not only as an adhesion molecule facilitating cell-cell and cell-matrix interactions but also as a signaling receptor that modulates inflammatory responses, endothelial barrier integrity, and angiogenic activity. In the context of tissue repair and regeneration, CD31+ cells have been identified as key mediators of vascular remodeling and bone healing. These cells secrete a constellation of bioactive molecules—including vascular endothelial growth factor, fibroblast growth factor, interleukin-8, matrix metalloproteinase-9, and granulocyte colony-stimulating factor—that collectively promote endothelial cell migration, capillary tube formation, osteoblast differentiation, and the recruitment of progenitor cells to sites of injury. Their immunomodulatory properties further contribute to the creation of a regenerative microenvironment by suppressing excessive T-cell activation and promoting the polarization of macrophages toward an anti-inflammatory, pro-healing phenotype.

The therapeutic potential of CD31+ cells has been demonstrated in multiple preclinical and clinical settings, particularly in the treatment of ischemic diseases and non-union fractures. In models of critical limb ischemia, the administration of CD31+ cell populations has been shown to restore perfusion, reduce tissue necrosis, and improve limb salvage rates. Similarly, in orthopedic applications, the local delivery of CD31+ cells to fracture sites has accelerated bone union, enhanced callus formation, and reduced the incidence of delayed healing. Importantly, these effects are not attributable to a single cell subtype but rather to the synergistic actions of multiple CD31-expressing populations, including CD31+ monocytes, which are potent secretors of osteogenic cytokines, and CD31+ naïve T cells, which exhibit enhanced angiogenic potential upon activation. The enrichment of these cells from a patient’s own blood therefore represents a powerful strategy for harnessing endogenous regenerative capacity without the need for ex vivo expansion or genetic modification.

Unlike other cell populations used in regenerative therapies, CD31+ cells are abundantly present in peripheral blood, with approximately 70 to 78 percent of viable lymphocytes and monocytes expressing the antigen. Their expression is stable across age groups and unaffected by gender, making them a universally accessible target for autologous therapies. Furthermore, CD31 is not expressed on hematopoietic stem cells or pluripotent progenitors, which reduces the risk of unintended differentiation or tumorigenic potential in the final product. The absence of CD31 on erythrocytes and platelets further simplifies isolation, as these abundant cell types do not interfere with the enrichment process. The fact that CD31 is a naturally occurring, non-mutated, non-engineered surface protein also ensures that its targeting does not constitute a genetic or chemical alteration under regulatory definitions, thereby supporting the classification of the resulting cell product as minimally manipulated.

The invention capitalizes on the biological significance of CD31+ cells by developing a method for their rapid, high-purity isolation directly from whole blood or peripheral blood mononuclear cell suspensions. The resulting enriched population retains the full functional repertoire of CD31+ cells, including their ability to secrete angiogenic and osteogenic factors, without being compromised by the presence of exogenous binding agents. This preservation of native phenotype and function is critical, as many conventional isolation techniques—particularly those involving antibody-coated magnetic beads—have been shown to alter cell signaling, induce activation or exhaustion, or impair migratory capacity due to persistent ligand binding. By ensuring that the final cell product is completely free of beads and aptamers, the invention preserves the natural biological state of the CD31+ cells, maximizing their therapeutic efficacy upon reinfusion. The enrichment process does not require any prior cell activation, culture, or manipulation, and the cells can be administered immediately after isolation, preserving their metabolic and functional integrity. This approach is uniquely suited for intraoperative applications, where time, sterility, and regulatory compliance are paramount considerations.

### II. Aptamers

The invention employs a biotinylated aptamer with high specificity for the CD31 antigen as the molecular recognition element in the cell enrichment process. Aptamers are synthetic, single-stranded nucleic acid molecules—either DNA or RNA—that fold into unique three-dimensional structures capable of binding to target molecules with high affinity and selectivity. Unlike antibodies, which are produced through biological systems and subject to immunological variability, aptamers are generated through an iterative in vitro selection process known as SELEX, which allows for the precise engineering of binding characteristics. Once selected, aptamers can be chemically synthesized with absolute sequence fidelity, ensuring batch-to-batch consistency and eliminating the risk of contamination with endotoxins or adventitious agents. Their small size, thermal stability, and resistance to denaturation make them ideal for use in clinical settings where refrigeration and complex storage conditions are impractical.

In the context of the present invention, the aptamer is specifically selected for its ability to bind CD31 with sufficient affinity to enable efficient capture from a complex cell mixture, yet with low enough affinity to permit reversible release under physiologically benign mechanical forces. The binding affinity of the aptamer, as determined by equilibrium dissociation constants, is in the low nanomolar range, significantly weaker than the picomolar affinity of biotin-neutravidin interactions but comparable to or slightly lower than that of conventional anti-CD31 antibodies. This intermediate affinity is a deliberate design feature, as it allows for stable binding during the capture phase while enabling efficient dissociation during the release phase without the need for chemical, enzymatic, or thermal intervention. The aptamer’s binding kinetics are such that the association rate is sufficient to capture target cells within minutes under flow conditions, while the dissociation rate is sufficiently high to allow for complete release upon agitation, a property that is not typically observed with antibody-based systems.

The aptamer used in the invention is biotinylated at one terminus, enabling its stable immobilization onto neutravidin-coated agarose beads through the exceptionally strong and irreversible biotin-neutravidin bond. This conjugation strategy ensures that the aptamer remains firmly attached to the bead surface during the enrichment process, preventing its release into the cell suspension and thereby avoiding contamination of the final product. The biotinylation does not interfere with the aptamer’s structure or binding capacity, as confirmed by flow cytometric analysis showing equivalent binding profiles between biotinylated and unmodified versions of the aptamer. The aptamer is further validated for its specificity through dual staining experiments, wherein cells incubated with the aptamer are simultaneously stained with a fluorescently labeled anti-CD31 antibody. These analyses reveal a near-perfect correlation between aptamer binding and CD31 expression, with over 99 percent of CD31+ cells also binding the aptamer and less than 3 percent of CD31- cells exhibiting non-specific binding, even at the highest tested concentrations.

Importantly, the aptamer does not induce any detectable activation, apoptosis, or phenotypic alteration in the target cells during the binding process. This is in contrast to some antibody-based systems, which can trigger receptor clustering, internalization, or downstream signaling cascades that alter cell behavior. The absence of such effects is critical for preserving the therapeutic potential of the enriched cells. Moreover, the aptamer’s oligonucleotide composition renders it non-immunogenic, minimizing the risk of immune recognition or adverse reactions upon reinfusion. Its chemical nature also allows for complete degradation by endogenous nucleases in vivo, ensuring that any residual traces, though eliminated by the physical filtration system, would not persist as foreign material in the recipient.

The use of aptamers in this invention represents a paradigm shift from traditional protein-based capture agents. While antibodies have dominated cell isolation technologies for decades, their limitations—batch variability, susceptibility to degradation, high production costs, and the inability to be easily modified without compromising function—have hindered their utility in point-of-care applications. Aptamers overcome these limitations by offering a chemically defined, synthetically produced, and functionally tunable alternative that is uniquely suited to the requirements of intraoperative cell therapy. The invention demonstrates, for the first time, that aptamers can be employed not merely as binding agents but as the central component of a fully integrated, biophysically driven enrichment system that delivers a clean, functional, and regulatory-compliant cell product. The success of this approach establishes a new standard for molecular recognition in cell separation, one that prioritizes simplicity, safety, and biological fidelity over the historical reliance on complex, chemically invasive methodologies.

### III. Devices for Enrichment of Cells

The invention introduces a novel, disposable, tube-based device engineered for the efficient, biophysically mediated enrichment of CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population. The device is constructed from a standard microcentrifuge tube, modified to incorporate a filtration barrier at its base and a packed bed of functionalized beads within its lumen. The filtration barrier is composed of a 20-micron polyethylene mesh derived from commercially available cell strainers, which is excised using a biopsy punch and securely affixed to the inner base of the tube using a thin layer of cyanoacrylate adhesive. This mesh is specifically chosen for its pore size, which is sufficiently small to retain neutravidin-coated agarose beads—ranging in diameter from 45 to 165 microns—while allowing the passage of leukocytes, which measure between 6 and 10 microns. The physical separation of beads from cells is absolute and does not rely on chemical or enzymatic means, ensuring that the final cell product is entirely free of capture matrix components.

The bead bed is formed by packing neutravidin-coated agarose beads into the tube above the filtration barrier. These beads are pre-functionalized with biotinylated aptamers specific for CD31, creating a high-density, uniform surface for cell capture. The beads are introduced into the tube following a series of washing and incubation steps designed to ensure complete and stable aptamer conjugation. The bead suspension is filtered through a 100-micron strainer to remove aggregates and ensure uniform particle size distribution prior to packing. Once packed, the beads are washed extensively with phosphate-buffered saline to remove unbound aptamer, and the final configuration is stabilized by resuspending the beads in a binding buffer that maintains physiological pH and ion concentration, thereby preserving aptamer conformation and cell viability.

To facilitate integration with standard clinical equipment, the device is fitted with a Luer-lock adaptor at its top, enabling connection to standard syringes and infusion pumps. The entire system is designed to be operated vertically, minimizing sedimentation and ensuring uniform flow dynamics throughout the bead bed. The device is compatible with syringes of 1-milliliter capacity and can be operated using a programmable infusion pump to control flow rates with precision. The flow rate is optimized at 50 microliters per minute to allow sufficient contact time between the target cells and the aptamer-coated beads while preventing excessive shear forces that could damage fragile cell populations or cause premature release.

A key innovation of the device lies in the incorporation of a mixture of aptamer-coated and uncoated beads within the same column. This configuration was developed to address the challenge of clogging, which was observed in early iterations of the system when columns were packed entirely with aptamer-coated beads. The presence of uncoated beads reduces the density of binding sites, thereby decreasing the likelihood of cell aggregation and bead clumping during flow. Additionally, the uncoated beads serve as physical spacers that improve fluid dynamics and facilitate the resuspension of the bead bed during the release phase. The optimal ratio of coated to uncoated beads is approximately 50:50, a balance that maintains high capture efficiency while preventing operational complications. The total bead volume is maintained between 400 and 800 microliters, a range that maximizes cell yield without compromising flow characteristics or increasing the risk of bead retention in the eluate.

The device is manufactured under sterile conditions and is intended for single-use, ensuring that each enrichment procedure begins with a clean, pre-validated system. The materials used—including the polyethylene mesh, the agarose beads, and the cyanoacrylate adhesive—are all biocompatible and approved for use in medical devices. The entire assembly is designed to be assembled manually in under five minutes using standard laboratory tools, making it suitable for deployment in operating rooms or point-of-care settings without requiring specialized instrumentation. The simplicity of the design, combined with its robustness and reproducibility, distinguishes it from microfluidic or chromatographic systems that demand precise calibration, expensive components, or extensive training to operate.

The filtration mechanism is the cornerstone of the invention’s regulatory compliance. By physically excluding beads from the final cell product, the device eliminates the primary source of non-minimal manipulation that plagues existing immunomagnetic separation technologies. The 20-micron mesh is not merely a passive barrier but an active component of the system’s safety profile, ensuring that even under conditions of high pressure or mechanical disturbance, the beads remain confined within the device. This design feature has been validated through microscopy and flow cytometry, which confirm the complete absence of beads in the recovered cell population. The device thus represents a novel class of cell enrichment tools that achieve high purity and high yield without introducing any exogenous materials into the therapeutic product, thereby satisfying the regulatory criteria for minimal manipulation and enabling direct clinical translation.

### IV. Methods of Enriching Cells from a Mixed Cell Population

The method of enriching CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population, as embodied by the invention, is a streamlined, three-phase process comprising capture, release, and collection. The process begins with the preparation of a cell suspension derived from whole blood or peripheral blood mononuclear cells, which is diluted in a binding buffer formulated to maintain cell viability and optimize aptamer-target interactions. The binding buffer is composed of phosphate-buffered saline supplemented with calcium and magnesium ions, which are essential for maintaining the structural integrity of cell surface proteins and the conformational stability of the aptamer. The cell concentration is adjusted to 2.5 × 10⁷ cells per milliliter to ensure optimal contact probability with the aptamer-coated beads while avoiding overcrowding or aggregation.

The cell suspension is then introduced into the enrichment device via a syringe connected to the Luer-lock adaptor, and the flow is initiated at a controlled rate of 50 microliters per minute using an infusion pump. The entire system is maintained in a vertical orientation throughout the 10-minute enrichment period to prevent cell sedimentation and ensure uniform distribution of the cell suspension through the bead bed. During this phase, CD31+ cells bind selectively to the aptamer-coated beads, while CD31- cells, including erythrocytes, platelets, and non-target leukocytes, pass through the device and are collected in a waste container. Following the capture phase, an additional 500 microliters of binding buffer is flushed through the system to remove any loosely associated cells and ensure complete washing of the bead bed.

The release phase is initiated by disconnecting the device from the syringe and resuspending the bead-cell complex using a sterile 18-gauge needle and a 1-milliliter syringe. The beads are subjected to repeated cycles of aspiration and expulsion—typically five to ten cycles—generating controlled fluid shear forces that disrupt the aptamer-CD31 bonds. This mechanical agitation is the sole mechanism employed for cell release; no chemical agents, enzymatic treatments, temperature shifts, or complementary oligonucleotides are introduced at any stage. The shear forces generated are sufficient to overcome the binding energy of the aptamer-target interaction, which is deliberately engineered to be lower than that of antibody-antigen complexes, yet not so high as to cause cellular damage. The absence of any exogenous release agents ensures that the final cell product remains chemically unmodified and free of contaminants.

Following release, the cell suspension is transferred to a 2-milliliter centrifuge tube and centrifuged at 300 × g for three minutes to pellet the cells. The supernatant is carefully aspirated, leaving behind a concentrated, bead-free population of CD31+ cells. Cell viability is assessed using a redox-based metabolic assay, and in all tested configurations, viability exceeds 97 percent, confirming that the enrichment and release process is non-destructive. The recovered cell population is then ready for immediate use, whether for direct injection into a fracture site, administration into ischemic tissue, or for further characterization.

The method is highly reproducible, with consistent enrichment efficiencies observed across multiple donors and experimental batches. The enrichment factor—the ratio of CD31+ cells in the final product to that in the starting population—increases from approximately 64 percent to over 87 percent, representing a 1.4-fold increase in purity. The yield of CD31+ cells, defined as the percentage of initial CD31+ cells recovered, is maintained at approximately 25 percent, a value that is clinically meaningful given that 100 milliliters of whole blood typically yields 15.8 million CD31+ cells. The method is also scalable; the device can be operated in parallel using multiple units to increase throughput, and the same platform can be adapted for the enrichment of other cell types by simply replacing the aptamer with one specific to a different surface marker.

The method is designed for intraoperative use and can be completed within 30 minutes from blood draw to cell delivery, making it compatible with surgical workflows. It requires no specialized infrastructure beyond a centrifuge, a syringe pump, and standard sterile technique. The simplicity of the procedure, combined with its high fidelity and regulatory compliance, positions it as a transformative tool for the next generation of autologous cell therapies.

### V. Kits

The invention further encompasses a sterile, single-use kit designed to facilitate the consistent, reliable, and clinically compliant enrichment of CD31+ cells in point-of-care settings. The kit is packaged in a sealed, sterile container and contains all components necessary to perform the enrichment method without requiring additional reagents or equipment beyond those commonly available in surgical or clinical laboratories. The kit includes a plurality of pre-assembled enrichment devices, each comprising a microcentrifuge tube with a 20-micron polyethylene filtration barrier affixed to its base and a packed bed of neutravidin-coated agarose beads surface-functionalized with a biotinylated aptamer specific for CD31. The beads are pre-coated under controlled conditions and validated for binding efficiency, bead integrity, and absence of residual free aptamer.

Each enrichment device is individually packaged in a sterile, non-pyrogenic pouch and labeled with a unique identifier for traceability. The kit further includes a pre-measured volume of binding buffer, formulated to maintain physiological conditions during cell capture and release, and a separate vial of wash buffer, composed of phosphate-buffered saline with calcium and magnesium, for use in the post-capture flushing step. Both buffers are sterile-filtered and provided in volumes sufficient for multiple enrichment cycles, with clear labeling indicating volume, composition, and expiration date.

The kit also contains a sterile 18-gauge needle and a 1-milliliter syringe, both individually wrapped and pre-sterilized, to enable the mechanical resuspension of the bead-cell complex during the release phase. Instructions for use are printed in clear, step-by-step language on a laminated card included within the kit, accompanied by diagrams illustrating device assembly, flow direction, and centrifugation parameters. The instructions emphasize the importance of maintaining vertical orientation during flow, the necessity of avoiding excessive force during bead resuspension, and the requirement for immediate use of the enriched cells following collection.

The kit is designed for single-use only, ensuring that each enrichment procedure begins with a fresh, uncontaminated system. The materials used in the kit—polyethylene, agarose, neutravidin, and cyanoacrylate—are all approved for use in medical devices and comply with ISO 10993 standards for biocompatibility. The entire kit is manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practice conditions and is sterilized using ethylene oxide or gamma irradiation, ensuring microbial sterility and absence of endotoxins. The kit is stable for at least six months when stored at room temperature, a feature made possible by the exceptional stability of the aptamer and the inert nature of the bead matrix.

The inclusion of the kit transforms the invention from a laboratory protocol into a clinically deployable technology. It eliminates the need for manual bead coating, device assembly, or buffer preparation, thereby reducing operator variability and ensuring consistent outcomes across different clinical sites. The kit’s simplicity and reliability make it suitable for use by surgeons, nurses, or technicians with minimal training, enabling its integration into operating rooms, emergency departments, and ambulatory surgical centers. The kit also facilitates regulatory approval, as its standardized composition and pre-validated components provide a clear chain of custody and quality control, reducing the burden of process validation required for clinical translation.

### VI. Methods of Treatment

The invention provides novel methods of treatment wherein the enriched CD31+ cell population, isolated using the described system and method, is administered directly to a site of tissue injury or degeneration to promote regeneration, vascularization, or immunomodulation. The method is particularly suited for the treatment of bone fractures, non-unions, osteonecrosis, and ischemic tissue conditions, where the endogenous regenerative capacity is insufficient to support healing. The enriched cells are delivered via direct injection into the affected site, either as a suspension in sterile saline or in combination with a biocompatible scaffold, such as a collagen matrix or demineralized bone matrix, to enhance retention and local bioavailability.

In the context of orthopedic applications, the method involves drawing 100 milliliters of venous blood from the patient prior to or during surgical intervention, enriching the CD31+ cell population using the disclosed system, and injecting the resulting cell suspension into the fracture site or non-union region immediately following debridement and stabilization. The procedure is performed within a single surgical session, eliminating the need for cell culture, cryopreservation, or multiple patient visits. The injected cells exert their therapeutic effect through the secretion of angiogenic and osteogenic factors, including vascular endothelial growth factor, fibroblast growth factor, interleukin-8, and matrix metalloproteinase-9, which collectively stimulate endothelial cell proliferation, capillary formation, osteoblast differentiation, and extracellular matrix remodeling. The presence of CD31+ monocytes and naïve T cells within the enriched population further enhances the regenerative microenvironment by suppressing local inflammation and promoting the recruitment of endogenous progenitor cells.

The method may also be employed in the treatment of cardiovascular ischemia, where the enriched CD31+ cells are delivered via intramyocardial injection or intra-arterial infusion to promote revascularization of ischemic myocardium. In peripheral arterial disease, the cells may be administered intra-arterially to the affected limb to restore perfusion and prevent tissue necrosis. The cells’ immunomodulatory properties are particularly advantageous in these settings, as they mitigate the inflammatory response associated with ischemia-reperfusion injury and promote a pro-healing macrophage phenotype.

The method is further extendable to the treatment of chronic wounds, such as diabetic ulcers or pressure sores, where impaired angiogenesis and persistent inflammation hinder healing. In such cases, the enriched CD31+ cells are applied topically in combination with a hydrogel dressing or injected perilesionally to stimulate vascular ingrowth and tissue remodeling. The absence of exogenous materials in the final cell product ensures that the cells are not recognized as foreign, minimizing the risk of immune rejection or adverse reactions.

The invention also encompasses methods wherein the same enrichment platform is used to deplete undesirable cell populations, such as CD8+ T cells, which have been shown to inhibit bone healing through the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines. In this variation, the beads are coated with a biotinylated antibody or aptamer specific for CD8, and the cell suspension is passed through the device to remove these inhibitory cells. The resulting depleted population, now enriched in regenerative cell subsets, is then administered to the patient. This dual capability—enabling both positive enrichment and negative depletion within the same device architecture—further enhances the versatility and clinical utility of the invention.

The method of treatment is performed under sterile conditions, with the enriched cells administered within 30 minutes of isolation to preserve viability and functional potency. No additional pharmacological agents, growth factors, or stimulatory cytokines are required, as the cells retain their intrinsic biological activity. The procedure is minimally invasive, well-tolerated, and compatible with standard surgical protocols. The resulting therapeutic outcomes include accelerated bone union, improved perfusion in ischemic tissues, reduced inflammation in chronic wounds, and enhanced tissue regeneration with minimal complications. The invention thus provides a safe, effective, and regulatory-compliant method of treatment that leverages the body’s own regenerative potential without the need for exogenous manipulation or prolonged processing.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and methods

All reagents and materials used in the following examples were obtained from commercial sources and were of the highest purity available. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from leukoreduction filters obtained from the Brigham and Women’s Hospital specimen bank under an institutional review board-approved protocol. Blood samples were diluted 1:1 with Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline and layered over Ficoll-Paque Plus density gradient medium, followed by centrifugation at 400 × g for 30 minutes without brake. The interface layer containing PBMCs was carefully aspirated and washed twice with PBS at 200 × g for 8 minutes to remove residual platelets. For fresh whole blood experiments, samples were purchased from Research Blood Components (Boston, MA) and diluted 1:1 with PBS supplemented with 2 percent fetal calf serum prior to processing. Cell viability was assessed using the Muse® Cell Analyzer, and all experiments were performed at room temperature unless otherwise specified.

The biotinylated CD31 aptamer was obtained from APTSCI as either a monolabeled version (CD31-2196BCI) or a bi-labeled version (CD31-2196FBCI) containing an additional Cy5 fluorophore. The monoclonal antibodies for flow cytometry were sourced from Biolegend and included anti-CD31 (Alexa Fluor 488), anti-CD14 (PE), anti-CD45 (APC), anti-CD3 (FITC), and anti-CD19 (PerCP). The neutravidin agarose beads were procured from Pierce (29204) and filtered through a 100-micron cell strainer to remove aggregates and ensure uniform size distribution. The polyethylene filters were derived from 20-micron cell strainers (Pluriselect, 43-50020-03) and excised using a 4-millimeter biopsy punch (Sklar Instruments, 96–115). Cyanoacrylate glue (E-Z bond, S-105) was used to affix the filters to the base of microcentrifuge tubes (Pierce, 69705). All buffers were prepared using molecular biology-grade water and filtered through 0.22-micron membranes. The EBM basal medium and EGM BulletKit were obtained from Lonza, while DMEM low glucose, fetal calf serum, and penicillin/streptomycin were sourced from Sigma-Aldrich and Biochrome, respectively. Matrigel growth factor reduced was purchased from VWR, and Alizarin Red S staining reagents and AlamarBlue were obtained from Life Technologies.

### Aptamer Selection and Validation

The specificity of the biotinylated CD31 aptamer was validated using flow cytometry on PBMCs suspended at a concentration of 10⁷ cells per milliliter. Cells were incubated with varying concentrations of the aptamer ranging from 0.5 to 5 micrograms per milliliter for 30 minutes at 4°C, followed by centrifugation at 1200 rpm for 5 minutes. The cell pellets were resuspended in flow cytometry staining buffer and stained with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated anti-CD31 antibody and PE-conjugated anti-biotin antibody. Flow cytometric analysis was performed on a BD LSRFortessa with HTS, and data were analyzed using FlowJo software. The percentage of double-positive cells (CD31+ and biotin+) was calculated, and specificity was determined by the proportion of CD31- cells that remained negative for biotin staining. At an aptamer concentration of 5 micrograms per milliliter, 99.0 ± 1.3 percent of CD31+ cells were also positive for biotin, while the background binding to CD31- cells was less than 3 percent. The bi-labeled aptamer (Cy5-conjugated) was used to confirm the absence of residual aptamer on the cell surface following enrichment. Cells collected after release were analyzed directly without additional staining, and no Cy5 signal was detected, confirming that the aptamer remained bound to the beads and did not detach during the process.

### Development of a CD31+ Cell Enrichment System

The enrichment device was assembled by affixing a 4-millimeter circular polyethylene filter to the base of a microcentrifuge tube using a thin layer of cyanoacrylate glue. The glue was allowed to cure overnight at room temperature. Neutravidin agarose beads were diluted in PBS with calcium and magnesium, filtered through a 100-micron strainer, and pelleted by centrifugation at 300 × g for 1 minute. The beads were resuspended in a solution of biotinylated aptamer at concentrations of 5 or 10 micrograms per milliliter and incubated for 20 minutes at 4°C. After washing twice with PBS, the beads were resuspended in binding buffer and transferred to the prepared tube. The total bead volume was varied between 200 and 1000 microliters, and in some configurations, a 50:50 mixture of aptamer-coated and uncoated beads was used. Flow was performed at 50 microliters per minute for 10 minutes, followed by a 500-microliter wash. Cell release was achieved by resuspending the beads with a 1-milliliter syringe and 18-gauge needle over five cycles. Cell viability and enrichment efficiency were assessed by flow cytometry and compared across conditions. The optimal configuration was determined to be 5 micrograms per milliliter aptamer, 800 microliters total bead volume, and a 50:50 mixture of coated and uncoated beads.

### CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations

Fresh whole blood samples were processed to obtain PBMCs, which were then enriched using the optimized system. The percentage of CD31+ cells in the starting population was 64 ± 9 percent, while the enriched population contained 87 ± 3 percent CD31+ cells. The recovery yield of CD31+ cells was 25.1 ± 0.9 percent, and cell viability was 97 ± 1 percent. Subpopulation analysis revealed a significant enrichment of CD31+CD14+ monocytes and CD31+CD3+ naïve T cells compared to the starting population. The gating strategy was defined by sequential exclusion of debris and doublets based on FSC-A and FSC-H, followed by selection of CD45+ leukocytes. Within this population, CD31 expression was analyzed, and further gating on CD14 and CD3 allowed quantification of monocyte and T-cell subsets. No significant enrichment of CD19+ B cells was observed. The absence of beads in the final product was confirmed by microscopy and flow cytometry, with no events detected in the bead size gate.

### Angiogenic and Osteogenic Potential of CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations

Conditioned medium was collected from both the starting PBMC population and the enriched CD31+ population after 24 hours of culture in EBM basal medium supplemented with 2 percent fetal calf serum. The medium was sterile-filtered and stored at -20°C. For the angiogenic assay, human umbilical vein endothelial cells were seeded on Matrigel-coated 96-well plates and incubated for 20 minutes before the addition of 150 microliters of conditioned medium. Images were captured after 18 hours, and total capillary network length was quantified using ImageJ software. The enriched cell-conditioned medium increased tube formation by 1.9 ± 0.2 fold compared to control medium. For the osteogenic assay, mesenchymal stromal cells were seeded in 96-well plates and cultured in a 1:1 mixture of commercial osteogenic medium and conditioned medium. After 14 days, mineralization was assessed via Alizarin Red S staining, and absorbance was measured at 562 nanometers after dissolution in cetylpyridinium chloride. The enriched cell-conditioned medium increased mineralization by 1.8 ± 0.3 fold compared to control. Cell proliferation was confirmed using AlamarBlue, and results were normalized to metabolic activity. No significant differences in proliferation were observed, confirming that the enhanced osteogenic effect was due to secreted factors rather than increased cell numbers.
