# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccine development, specifically to recombinant proteins derived from the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia trachomatis and methods for their production, purification, and use as immunogenic agents. More particularly, the invention encompasses codon-harmonized nucleotide sequences encoding Chlamydia MOMP, recombinant expression systems for the surface localization of MOMP in the outer membrane of Escherichia coli, methods for the purification of structurally intact recombinant MOMP, and pharmaceutical compositions comprising such purified recombinant MOMP for the induction of protective immune responses against Chlamydia trachomatis infection. The invention further relates to methods of immunizing a subject to prevent or reduce the severity of chlamydial infections, including those leading to genital tract disease, ocular trachoma, lymphogranuloma venereum, neonatal conjunctivitis, and tubal infertility. The recombinant MOMP produced by the methods described herein retains structural features critical for immunogenicity and elicits antibody responses that recognize native Chlamydia elementary bodies, thereby providing a scalable, reproducible, and safe alternative to native antigen purification from infected eukaryotic cells.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Chlamydia trachomatis is a Gram-negative, obligate intracellular bacterial pathogen responsible for a wide spectrum of human diseases, including the most prevalent bacterial sexually transmitted infection worldwide. Different serovars of this organism are associated with distinct clinical manifestations: serovars A, B, Ba, and C cause trachoma, a leading infectious cause of blindness in developing regions; serovars D through K are primarily responsible for urogenital infections that can lead to pelvic inflammatory disease, ectopic pregnancy, and infertility; and serovars L1, L2, and L3 cause lymphogranuloma venereum, a systemic disease characterized by lymphadenopathy and granulomatous inflammation. Despite the high global burden of disease and the absence of a licensed vaccine, current treatment relies almost exclusively on antibiotic regimens, which do not prevent reinfection and contribute to the emergence of antimicrobial resistance. The development of an effective prophylactic vaccine remains a critical unmet medical need.

The major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia trachomatis has long been recognized as a prime candidate for subunit vaccine development due to its abundance on the surface of the infectious elementary body, its high immunogenicity, and its role in mediating host cell attachment and entry. MOMP constitutes approximately 60% of the total protein mass of the elementary body outer membrane and is highly conserved in its transmembrane β-barrel domain while exhibiting antigenic variability in its surface-exposed loops, which serve as targets for neutralizing antibodies and T-cell epitopes. Previous attempts to develop vaccines using formalin-inactivated whole elementary bodies yielded only transient protection and were associated with safety concerns. Subsequent efforts focused on purified native MOMP isolated from infected human epithelial cells demonstrated protective efficacy in murine challenge models, but the reliance on large-scale propagation of Chlamydia in cell culture presented insurmountable challenges for commercial manufacturing, including high costs, batch variability, contamination risks, and low yields.

Recombinant expression of MOMP in heterologous hosts such as Escherichia coli was pursued as a scalable alternative. However, early attempts to express full-length MOMP in the cytoplasm of E. coli resulted in the formation of insoluble inclusion bodies, from which the protein could be recovered only through denaturation and refolding procedures. These refolded proteins consistently failed to elicit protective immune responses equivalent to those induced by native MOMP, suggesting that the correct three-dimensional conformation of MOMP is essential for the presentation of key neutralizing epitopes. The β-barrel architecture of MOMP, which requires precise folding and insertion into a lipid bilayer, is particularly sensitive to misfolding in the reducing environment of the bacterial cytoplasm. Moreover, the presence of multiple disulfide bonds—characteristic of MOMP due to its high cysteine content—further complicates proper folding outside its native membrane context.

Alternative strategies to express MOMP in the outer membrane of E. coli were explored to preserve its native conformation. However, these efforts were hampered by severe host toxicity, poor expression yields, and inefficient translocation across the inner membrane. The overexpression of MOMP in the periplasm or outer membrane often led to cell lysis, reduced viability, and accumulation of misfolded or aggregated species. Previous studies reported partial success in surface expression using strong promoters and high-copy-number plasmids, but these conditions exacerbated cellular stress and failed to yield sufficient quantities of properly folded protein. Furthermore, the choice of signal peptide, induction parameters, and culture conditions were not systematically optimized, and no prior work had demonstrated that recombinant MOMP expressed on the E. coli outer membrane could elicit serum antibodies capable of recognizing native Chlamydia elementary bodies or confer protection in a murine genital challenge model.

The codon usage bias between Chlamydia trachomatis and E. coli presents another significant barrier to efficient recombinant expression. The native MOMP gene contains codons that are rare in E. coli, potentially leading to ribosomal stalling, translational errors, and premature termination. While standard codon optimization strategies replace rare codons with those most frequently used in E. coli, such approaches often disrupt the natural translational kinetics required for proper folding of complex membrane proteins. A more nuanced approach—codon harmonization—has been proposed to preserve the relative frequency of codon usage observed in the native host while adapting it to the tRNA pool of the expression host, thereby maintaining the timing and pauses in translation that facilitate co-translational folding. No prior application of codon harmonization to MOMP had been reported, nor had any study demonstrated its impact on outer membrane expression, structural integrity, or immunogenicity.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by providing a comprehensive and integrated solution for the recombinant production of immunologically active MOMP. It combines codon harmonization of the MOMP gene with the strategic selection of a low-copy-number expression vector, a moderate-strength promoter, a functionally optimized signal peptide, and precisely controlled cell culture conditions to achieve high-yield, non-toxic, surface-localized expression of MOMP in E. coli. The invention further provides a robust, scalable purification process that isolates structurally intact MOMP while minimizing contamination by host proteins and endotoxins. The resulting recombinant protein retains β-barrel secondary structure, elicits serum antibodies that bind native Chlamydia elementary bodies, and confers significant protection against genital challenge in a murine model, matching the efficacy of purified native MOMP. This invention thus represents a breakthrough in the development of a safe, manufacturable, and effective subunit vaccine against Chlamydia trachomatis.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for the recombinant expression, purification, and use of the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia trachomatis as a vaccine antigen. The invention is based on the surprising discovery that the structural and immunological integrity of MOMP can be preserved when expressed on the outer membrane of Escherichia coli through a combination of codon harmonization, controlled transcriptional regulation, optimized signal peptide selection, and precise modulation of cell culture parameters. This approach avoids the formation of inclusion bodies and eliminates the need for denaturation and refolding, thereby preserving the native β-barrel conformation critical for eliciting protective immune responses.

In one aspect, the invention provides a codon-harmonized nucleotide sequence encoding the major outer membrane protein of Chlamydia trachomatis, wherein the sequence is modified to approximate the codon usage profile of the native Chlamydia host while remaining compatible with translation in Escherichia coli. The codon harmonization is performed by identifying rare codons in the native Chlamydia MOMP gene and replacing them with synonymous codons that are similarly infrequent in E. coli, while for the remaining codons, those with the closest frequency match between the native and expression hosts are selected. The resulting nucleotide sequence avoids restriction sites such as NdeI and XhoI to facilitate cloning and is designed to encode the full-length MOMP protein, including its native secretion leader sequence or a functionally equivalent heterologous leader sequence.

In another aspect, the invention provides a recombinant expression system for the surface localization of MOMP in the outer membrane of Escherichia coli. The system comprises a low-copy-number expression vector, a promoter of moderate strength, and a codon-harmonized MOMP gene fused to a secretion leader sequence that facilitates translocation across the inner membrane and insertion into the outer membrane. The expression vector is selected from a group consisting of pAVE029 or other vectors derived from the pAT153 origin of replication, which maintains a low copy number per cell. The promoter is chosen to provide moderate transcriptional activity, such as the λPL promoter, to avoid overburdening the host’s protein folding and translocation machinery. The secretion leader sequence is selected from the group consisting of OmpA, OmpP, PelB, SopA, PgtE, Pla, or combinations thereof, each of which has been empirically demonstrated to direct efficient surface expression of MOMP in E. coli. The expression system is further optimized by cultivating the host strain in Cinnabar medium at a cell density of approximately 0.5 OD590 prior to induction with 0.4 mM IPTG, followed by a 4-hour induction period at 30°C, which maximizes surface expression while preserving cell viability.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method for the purification of recombinant MOMP from the outer membrane of Escherichia coli. The method involves harvesting induced bacterial cells, disrupting them by microfluidization, and isolating the membrane fraction by ultracentrifugation. The membrane fraction is washed sequentially with high-salt buffer to remove soluble contaminants, followed by treatment with Triton X-100 to remove the inner membrane, and then with β-octyl-glucoside to remove non-target outer membrane proteins. The recombinant MOMP is solubilized from the outer membrane using sarkosyl in the presence of dithiothreitol, extracted by ultracentrifugation, and subjected to size exclusion chromatography followed by ion exchange chromatography to yield a purified preparation. The final product is free of detectable endotoxin, contains minimal co-purified E. coli proteins, and exhibits a β-barrel secondary structure as confirmed by circular dichroism spectroscopy.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising the purified recombinant MOMP as an active ingredient, formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier and optionally combined with an immunostimulatory adjuvant. The adjuvant may include CpG oligonucleotides and Montanide ISA 720 VG, which together enhance the magnitude and quality of the humoral immune response. The composition is suitable for parenteral administration, including subcutaneous or intramuscular delivery, and is formulated to maintain the structural integrity of the MOMP protein during storage and transport.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method of immunizing a mammalian subject to prevent or reduce the severity of Chlamydia trachomatis infection. The method involves administering to the subject an effective amount of the pharmaceutical composition described above, wherein the administration is performed in a series of two or more doses separated by intervals of at least 14 days. The immunization induces a systemic immune response characterized by the production of serum antibodies that bind to native Chlamydia elementary bodies and confer significant protection against genital challenge, as evidenced by a substantial reduction in bacterial shedding following intravaginal inoculation with Chlamydia trachomatis serovar D. The immune response is predominantly IgG1 and IgG2c isotype-biased, indicating a balanced Th1/Th2 response. The method is applicable to humans and other mammals, including non-human primates, rabbits, and mice, and is particularly useful for preventing urogenital infections, trachoma, and neonatal complications associated with vertical transmission.

The invention further provides a method for evaluating the immunogenicity of recombinant MOMP by measuring serum antibody titers via enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay using native Chlamydia elementary bodies as the coating antigen, and for assessing protective efficacy by quantifying bacterial load in genital swabs using real-time quantitative PCR targeting the 16S ribosomal RNA gene. The invention demonstrates that the recombinant MOMP produced by the described methods elicits antibody titers and protective efficacy equivalent to those achieved with native MOMP purified from infected cells, thereby establishing its suitability as a vaccine candidate.

The present invention thus provides a complete, integrated platform for the production of a structurally authentic, immunogenic, and manufacturable subunit vaccine against Chlamydia trachomatis, overcoming the longstanding limitations of previous recombinant and native antigen approaches. The invention enables the scalable, cost-effective, and safe production of a vaccine antigen that can be deployed globally to reduce the burden of chlamydial disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The pharmaceutical compositions of the present invention are formulated to deliver the recombinant major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia trachomatis in a manner that preserves its structural integrity, enhances its immunogenicity, and ensures stability during storage and administration. The active ingredient in these compositions is the purified recombinant MOMP protein obtained through the codon-harmonized expression system and multi-step purification protocol described herein. The protein is present in a concentration ranging from 1 μg/mL to 100 μg/mL, with preferred concentrations between 5 μg/mL and 20 μg/mL, depending on the intended route of administration and the target population. The recombinant MOMP is dissolved in a buffered aqueous solution that maintains a pH between 6.8 and 7.8, with a preferred pH of 7.3, to prevent denaturation and aggregation. The buffer system typically comprises 10 mM Hepes, 150 mM sodium chloride, and 0.1% zwittergent 3–14, which mimics the final storage conditions used during purification and stabilizes the β-barrel conformation of the protein. Additional stabilizing agents such as sucrose, trehalose, or glycerol may be included at concentrations ranging from 5% to 15% (w/v) to protect the protein during lyophilization or long-term storage at 2°C to 8°C.

The pharmaceutical composition may optionally include an immunostimulatory adjuvant to enhance the magnitude, duration, and quality of the immune response elicited by the recombinant MOMP. The adjuvant is selected from a group comprising Toll-like receptor agonists, saponins, oil-in-water emulsions, and aluminum salts, with particular preference for combinations that promote both humoral and cellular immunity. In a preferred embodiment, the adjuvant consists of CpG oligodeoxynucleotides and Montanide ISA 720 VG, which together act synergistically to activate dendritic cells, promote Th1-type cytokine production, and enhance the generation of high-affinity IgG antibodies. The CpG oligonucleotides are formulated as a synthetic unmethylated CpG motif, typically containing a phosphorothioate backbone for increased nuclease resistance, and are present in the composition at a concentration of 10 μg to 100 μg per dose, with a preferred dose of 50 μg. Montanide ISA 720 VG is a water-in-oil emulsion composed of a proprietary mixture of mineral oil and surfactants, and is included at a concentration of 70% (w/v) in the final formulation. The adjuvant is mixed with the recombinant MOMP immediately prior to administration to ensure optimal dispersion and antigen presentation.

The composition may also contain minor excipients to improve physical stability, prevent microbial contamination, or facilitate administration. These may include preservatives such as benzyl alcohol or phenol at concentrations not exceeding 0.5% (w/v), surfactants such as polysorbate 80 at 0.01% to 0.1% (w/v) to minimize surface adsorption, and tonicity modifiers such as sodium chloride or dextrose to render the solution isotonic with human plasma. The final formulation is sterile-filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane and filled into single-dose vials or pre-filled syringes made of borosilicate glass or polymer-based materials, each containing a single dose of 10 μg of recombinant MOMP and 50 μg of CpG in 0.5 mL of adjuvant emulsion. The vials are sealed with rubber stoppers and aluminum crimps and stored under refrigerated conditions until use. For extended shelf life, the composition may be lyophilized into a stable powder form, with reconstitution performed immediately prior to injection using a sterile diluent provided separately. The lyophilized formulation retains full immunogenicity for at least 24 months when stored at 2°C to 8°C and exhibits no detectable loss of protein structure upon reconstitution, as confirmed by circular dichroism spectroscopy and SDS-PAGE analysis.

The composition is designed for parenteral administration, preferably by subcutaneous or intramuscular injection, and is suitable for use in both pediatric and adult populations. The dosage regimen comprises a primary series of two to three immunizations administered at intervals of 20 to 30 days, followed by a booster dose administered six to twelve months after the last primary dose. The volume of each injection is between 0.1 mL and 1.0 mL, depending on the age and weight of the recipient. The composition is compatible with standard immunization protocols and can be co-administered with other vaccines, provided that separate injection sites are used. The recombinant MOMP contained in the composition is free of viable Chlamydia organisms, endotoxin, and host cell DNA, as confirmed by microbial culture, Limulus amebocyte lysate assay, and quantitative PCR, respectively, ensuring a high safety profile. The absence of live pathogens eliminates the risk of reversion or dissemination, making this composition safer than whole-cell or attenuated vaccine candidates. The composition is particularly advantageous for use in resource-limited settings due to its thermal stability, ease of storage, and compatibility with existing cold chain infrastructure.

### Methods of Use

The methods of use described herein pertain to the administration of the pharmaceutical composition comprising the recombinant major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia trachomatis for the prevention or mitigation of chlamydial infections in mammals, including humans. The method involves the deliberate induction of a protective adaptive immune response through the delivery of a structurally authentic form of MOMP that retains the conformational epitopes necessary for neutralizing antibody binding and T-cell recognition. The recombinant MOMP, as produced by the codon-harmonized expression system and purified through the optimized extraction and chromatographic steps, is administered to a subject in need of protection against Chlamydia trachomatis infection. The subject may be identified as being at risk of exposure due to sexual activity, occupational exposure, or geographic prevalence of infection, or may be selected based on serological screening for prior exposure or susceptibility.

The method of immunization comprises the parenteral administration of the pharmaceutical composition in a series of two to four doses, with each dose separated by an interval of at least 14 days and preferably between 20 and 30 days. The first dose primes the immune system, initiating the activation of antigen-presenting cells and the clonal expansion of MOMP-specific B and T lymphocytes. Subsequent doses serve to boost the immune response, increasing the affinity of antibodies, expanding the memory B-cell pool, and promoting the development of long-lived plasma cells. The final dose is administered within a window of six to twelve months after the last primary dose to establish durable immunological memory. The total number of doses and the timing of administration may be adjusted based on the age of the recipient, the presence of pre-existing immunity, or the desired duration of protection. For neonates and infants, immunization may be initiated after the first six months of life, with subsequent doses administered according to a schedule that avoids interference from maternal antibodies. In adults, a two-dose regimen may be sufficient for short-term protection, while a three-dose regimen is preferred for long-term immunity.

The recombinant MOMP is administered in a volume of 0.1 to 1.0 mL, delivered subcutaneously into the deltoid region, the anterior thigh, or the subscapular area, or intramuscularly into the deltoid or gluteal muscle. The route of administration is selected based on the formulation, the age of the recipient, and the local immunization guidelines. The composition is not administered via oral, intranasal, or transdermal routes, as these have not been demonstrated to elicit sufficient systemic antibody titers against Chlamydia elementary bodies. The immunization protocol is designed to induce a systemic humoral response characterized by high-titer IgG antibodies that cross-react with native Chlamydia trachomatis elementary bodies, as confirmed by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. The antibody response includes both IgG1 and IgG2c subclasses, indicating a balanced T-helper 1 and T-helper 2 response, which is critical for both opsonization and intracellular clearance of the pathogen. The method further includes the monitoring of immune responses through serial blood sampling, with serum collected prior to the first immunization and at least two weeks after the final dose to assess the magnitude and persistence of the antibody titer.

The method of use is particularly effective in preventing genital tract infections caused by Chlamydia trachomatis serovars D through K, which are responsible for cervicitis, urethritis, endometritis, pelvic inflammatory disease, tubal infertility, and ectopic pregnancy. The immunization reduces the likelihood of ascending infection and subsequent scarring of the fallopian tubes, thereby preserving reproductive function. The method is also applicable to the prevention of ocular infections caused by serovars A, B, Ba, and C, particularly in endemic regions where trachoma is a leading cause of preventable blindness. In such settings, mass vaccination campaigns targeting children under the age of ten may be implemented to interrupt the cycle of transmission. The method further extends to the prevention of neonatal conjunctivitis and pneumonia, which occur following vertical transmission from an infected mother during childbirth. Immunization of adolescent girls and women of childbearing age prior to pregnancy significantly reduces the risk of neonatal infection, thereby protecting the newborn without the need for direct vaccination of the infant.

The method may be combined with other preventive strategies, including behavioral interventions, screening programs, and antibiotic treatment of infected partners, to achieve comprehensive control of chlamydial disease. The recombinant MOMP-based vaccine is not intended as a therapeutic agent for active infection, but rather as a prophylactic measure to prevent initial colonization and subsequent disease progression. The method is suitable for use in both immunocompetent and immunocompromised individuals, as the recombinant protein does not contain live organisms or replication-competent genetic material. The absence of endotoxin and host cell contaminants further enhances its safety profile, allowing for repeated administration without the risk of pyrogenic reactions. The method has been validated in preclinical models and is expected to translate effectively to human clinical trials, where it will be evaluated for safety, immunogenicity, and efficacy in reducing the incidence of chlamydial infection in high-risk populations.

### Codon-Harmonized MOMP Nucleotide Sequences

The codon-harmonized nucleotide sequences of the present invention represent a critical innovation in the recombinant expression of the Chlamydia trachomatis major outer membrane protein (MOMP). Unlike traditional codon optimization strategies that replace all rare codons with those most frequently used in the host organism, the codon harmonization approach employed herein preserves the relative frequency of codon usage observed in the native Chlamydia host while adapting the sequence to be efficiently translated in Escherichia coli. This method is based on the principle that the timing of translation elongation, influenced by the availability of cognate tRNAs, plays a pivotal role in the co-translational folding of complex membrane proteins. By maintaining pauses in translation that occur naturally in the native host, the harmonized sequence allows the nascent MOMP polypeptide to fold correctly as it emerges from the ribosome, facilitating the formation of disulfide bonds and the proper assembly of β-strands into the barrel structure essential for function.

The codon harmonization process begins with the retrieval of the amino acid sequence of MOMP from Chlamydia trachomatis serovars D, E, and muridarum strain MoPn, as well as from the closely related Chlamydia muridarum. The corresponding nucleotide sequences are obtained from public databases, and the codon usage frequencies for both the native Chlamydia host and the expression host, Escherichia coli, are compiled from the Codon Usage Database. For each amino acid residue in the MOMP sequence, the frequency of each synonymous codon in the native host is determined, and the corresponding codon in E. coli with the closest frequency is selected. For residues encoded by rare codons in Chlamydia, the rarest codon in E. coli is chosen to preserve the translational pause, thereby mimicking the native folding kinetics. This approach avoids the overuse of high-frequency codons that can lead to rapid, error-prone translation and misfolding. The resulting nucleotide sequence is designed to encode the full-length MOMP protein, including the N-terminal secretion leader sequence, and is synthesized de novo to ensure fidelity and to exclude restriction enzyme sites such as NdeI and XhoI, which could interfere with downstream cloning procedures.

The codon-harmonized sequences are specifically tailored for each serovar, including C. trachomatis serovar D (CT_681), serovar E (O175_03780), and C. muridarum (TC0052), to account for minor sequence variations that may influence immunogenicity. Each sequence is flanked by appropriate restriction sites, such as NdeI at the 5′ end and XhoI at the 3′ end, to facilitate directional cloning into the expression vector. The sequences are verified by sequencing and are functionally validated by their ability to support high-level surface expression of MOMP in E. coli when expressed under the optimized conditions described herein. The codon-harmonized sequence for serovar E, in particular, has been shown to produce the highest level of outer membrane expression, likely due to the optimal balance of codon usage and structural constraints in the transmembrane domains.

The codon-harmonized sequences differ significantly from both the native Chlamydia MOMP gene and from conventionally optimized sequences. While the native gene contains multiple codons that are extremely rare in E. coli, such as AGG for arginine and CUA for leucine, the harmonized versions replace these with codons that are similarly infrequent in E. coli, such as CGA for arginine and CUG for leucine. In contrast, conventional optimization would replace these with the most abundant codons in E. coli, such as CGT and CUC, respectively, which would eliminate the translational pauses critical for folding. The harmonized sequences also maintain the overall GC content of the native gene, avoiding the extreme AT-richness or GC-richness that can destabilize mRNA or interfere with ribosome binding. The resulting nucleotide sequences are stable, efficiently transcribed, and translated with high fidelity, yielding recombinant MOMP that is properly folded, surface-localized, and immunogenic.

The utility of these sequences extends beyond the expression of MOMP alone. The codon harmonization strategy described herein may be applied to other complex outer membrane proteins from Gram-negative pathogens, particularly those with multiple disulfide bonds, β-barrel structures, or high cysteine content, such as Neisseria gonorrhoeae PorB, Haemophilus influenzae P5, or Treponema pallidum Tp47. The sequences provided herein serve as a blueprint for the rational design of recombinant antigens that require precise folding kinetics for immunological efficacy. The codon-harmonized MOMP nucleotide sequences are therefore not merely a variation of existing sequences but represent a novel class of genetic constructs engineered to overcome the fundamental limitations of heterologous expression for structurally complex membrane proteins.

## EXAMPLE 1

### Expression of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP

The successful recombinant expression of Chlamydia trachomatis major outer membrane protein (MOMP) on the outer membrane of Escherichia coli was achieved through a systematic and integrated optimization of genetic, biochemical, and physiological parameters. The process began with the synthesis of codon-harmonized nucleotide sequences encoding the MOMP protein from Chlamydia trachomatis serovar E, muridarum strain MoPn, and serovar D, each designed to approximate the codon usage profile of the native Chlamydia host while remaining compatible with translation in E. coli. These sequences were cloned into the pAVE029 expression vector, which contains the λPL promoter and a low-copy-number origin of replication derived from pAT153. The vector was chosen after extensive screening of multiple expression systems, including high-copy-number plasmids such as pET derivatives and pUC-based vectors, which consistently resulted in low or undetectable surface expression of MOMP, likely due to excessive transcriptional burden and cellular toxicity. The pAVE029 vector, by contrast, supported moderate transcriptional activity that allowed sufficient time for the nascent MOMP polypeptide to be translocated across the inner membrane, folded in the periplasm, and inserted into the outer membrane without inducing lysis or growth arrest.

The codon-harmonized MOMP genes were fused to various secretion leader sequences, including the native Chlamydia leaders from serovars D and E, as well as heterologous leaders from E. coli (OmpA, OmpP, PelB), Shigella flexneri (SopA), Salmonella enterica (PgtE), Yersinia pestis (Pla), and Erwinia carotovora (PelB). Whole-cell flow cytometry using polyclonal mouse sera raised against native Chlamydia elementary bodies revealed that only the heterologous leaders—OmpA, OmpP, PelB, SopA, PgtE, and Pla—enabled detectable surface expression of MOMP. The native Chlamydia leaders, despite being evolutionarily adapted to their host, failed to direct efficient translocation in E. coli, suggesting that the recognition motifs for the Sec translocon or the β-barrel assembly machinery (BAM) are not fully conserved between Chlamydia and E. coli. Among the functional leaders, PelB conferred the highest level of surface expression, as measured by geometric mean fluorescence intensity, and was selected for all subsequent experiments.

The expression host strain E. coli K12 W25113 was transformed with the recombinant pAVE029 plasmid and grown in Cinnabar medium, a chemically defined medium that was empirically determined to support the highest level of MOMP surface expression compared to LB, 2YT, Azura, or auto-induction media. Induction was initiated when the culture reached an optical density at 590 nm of approximately 0.5, corresponding to mid-logarithmic phase. Induction was performed with 0.4 mM IPTG, a concentration that provided consistent and reproducible expression without triggering excessive stress responses. The temperature of induction was carefully controlled at 30°C, as lower temperatures (16°C, 25°C) resulted in markedly reduced expression, while higher temperatures (37°C) led to increased cell fragility and reduced viability, despite higher initial expression levels. A 4-hour induction period was found to be optimal; longer durations (6 or 16 hours) resulted in diminished surface expression, likely due to the degradation of misfolded protein or the activation of proteolytic systems. Under these conditions, the recombinant MOMP was localized predominantly to the outer membrane, as confirmed by whole-cell flow cytometry, which showed a clear shift in fluorescence intensity compared to uninduced controls or cells expressing MOMP with non-functional leaders.

The expression of MOMP was further validated by SDS-PAGE and Western blot analysis. Whole-cell lysates from induced cultures revealed a prominent band at approximately 37 kDa, corresponding to the predicted molecular weight of mature MOMP after cleavage of the PelB leader sequence. This band was absent in uninduced cultures and was recognized by polyclonal antisera raised against native Chlamydia elementary bodies, confirming the antigenic identity of the recombinant protein. The expression was consistent across multiple independent transformants and was reproducible over multiple passages, demonstrating the stability of the system. Importantly, no evidence of inclusion body formation was observed, and the protein remained soluble and accessible on the cell surface, as confirmed by protease accessibility assays and immunofluorescence microscopy. The combination of codon harmonization, moderate promoter strength, low-copy-number vector, functional leader sequence, and precisely controlled induction conditions collectively enabled the high-yield, non-toxic, and structurally faithful expression of MOMP on the outer membrane of E. coli, a result that had not been previously achieved with any other recombinant system.

## EXAMPLE 2

### Purification of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP

The purification of recombinant Chlamydia MOMP from the outer membrane of Escherichia coli involved a multi-step process designed to isolate the target protein with high purity while preserving its native β-barrel conformation and minimizing contamination by host components. The process commenced with the harvesting of induced E. coli cells expressing the codon-harmonized MOMP gene fused to the PelB leader sequence. Cells were collected by centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4°C, and the resulting pellets were weighed and resuspended in a buffer containing 50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0, supplemented with EDTA-free protease inhibitors to prevent degradation during subsequent steps. The cells were then disrupted by microfluidization using a high-pressure homogenizer, which efficiently lysed the cells while preserving the integrity of the outer membrane. The lysate was subjected to low-speed centrifugation at 9,700 × g for 15 minutes to remove intact cells and large debris, and the supernatant was further centrifuged at 23,800 × g for 90 minutes to pellet the membrane fraction, which contained the majority of the recombinant MOMP.

The membrane pellet was washed twice with a high-salt buffer containing 1 M NaCl and 0.05% Tween 20 to remove loosely associated periplasmic and cytoplasmic proteins. Following the wash, the membrane fraction was resuspended in buffer A (20 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0, 1 mM EDTA) containing 1% Triton X-100 and incubated at room temperature for 15 minutes to selectively solubilize the inner membrane while leaving the outer membrane intact. The mixture was then subjected to ultracentrifugation at 120,000 × g for 40 minutes, resulting in a new pellet enriched in outer membrane vesicles. This pellet was washed again and then resuspended in buffer A containing 3% β-octyl-glucoside, a mild non-ionic detergent known to solubilize many outer membrane proteins while preserving their tertiary structure. The suspension was incubated at room temperature for one hour to allow for the removal of contaminating outer membrane proteins, after which ultracentrifugation at 120,000 × g for 40 minutes yielded a pellet enriched in MOMP.

The next critical step involved the selective extraction of MOMP from the outer membrane. A panel of detergents, including sarkosyl, foscholine-14, and zwittergent 3–12, was evaluated for their ability to solubilize MOMP while minimizing co-extraction of host proteins. Sarkosyl, an anionic detergent, was found to be the most effective, yielding the highest recovery of MOMP with minimal contamination. The membrane pellet was therefore resuspended in buffer A containing 1% sarkosyl and 5 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) to reduce disulfide bonds and facilitate extraction. The mixture was incubated at room temperature for two hours with gentle agitation, followed by ultracentrifugation at 120,000 × g for 40 minutes to separate the solubilized MOMP from insoluble debris. The supernatant, now containing the solubilized recombinant MOMP, was collected and subjected to size exclusion chromatography using a Sephacryl S-300 column equilibrated with a buffer containing 10 mM Hepes, pH 7.3, 150 mM NaCl, and 0.1% zwittergent 3–14. This step separated MOMP from aggregates and smaller contaminants based on molecular size, with the monomeric and oligomeric forms of MOMP eluting in a single, symmetric peak corresponding to an apparent molecular weight of approximately 40 kDa.

The pooled fractions from size exclusion chromatography were then subjected to ion exchange chromatography using a Hitrap Q FF anion exchange column. The sample was loaded at pH 7.3, and proteins were eluted using a linear gradient of sodium chloride from 150 mM to 500 mM. MOMP eluted at approximately 300 mM NaCl, consistent with its isoelectric point and surface charge distribution. Fractions containing MOMP were identified by SDS-PAGE and Western blot, pooled, and concentrated using centrifugal filtration devices with a 10 kDa molecular weight cutoff. The final purified protein was analyzed for purity by mass spectrometry, which indicated that approximately 70% of the protein was MOMP, with the remaining 30% consisting of co-purified E. coli outer membrane proteins that were not fully removed during the detergent washes. Despite this, the preparation was deemed suitable for immunological studies, as the contaminating proteins were not immunogenic in the context of the adjuvant used and did not interfere with the antibody response to MOMP.

The endotoxin content of the final preparation was assessed by Western blot using a monoclonal antibody specific for E. coli lipooligosaccharide (LPS), and no signal was detected, confirming the absence of detectable endotoxin contamination. The protein concentration was determined by amino acid analysis following acid hydrolysis, yielding a final yield of approximately 6 mg of purified recombinant MOMP per liter of induced culture. Circular dichroism spectroscopy confirmed that the purified protein retained a β-barrel secondary structure, with a characteristic minimum at 215 nm, indistinguishable from that of native MOMP purified from Chlamydia elementary bodies. The purified recombinant MOMP was stored at 4°C in the presence of 0.1% zwittergent 3–14 and was stable for at least six months without significant loss of structure or immunogenicity. This purification protocol represents a scalable, reproducible, and cost-effective method for obtaining structurally authentic MOMP suitable for use as a vaccine antigen.

## EXAMPLE 3

### Mouse Immunogenicity and Challenge Study

To evaluate the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of the recombinant Chlamydia MOMP produced by the methods described herein, a series of immunization and challenge experiments were conducted in female C57BL/6 mice. The mice were divided into five groups: one group received purified native MOMP (nMOMP) isolated from Chlamydia trachomatis serovar D elementary bodies, another received the recombinant MOMP (rMOMP) purified from E. coli outer membrane, a third group received live Chlamydia elementary bodies (EBs), a fourth group received the adjuvant alone (CpG and Montanide ISA 720 VG), and a fifth group received no treatment. Each immunization consisted of 10 μg of MOMP (either native or recombinant) or 1 × 10⁶ live EBs, administered subcutaneously or intraperitoneally, respectively, in a volume of 0.2 mL, with adjuvant included in all antigen-containing groups. Immunizations were administered on days 0, 20, and 30, and serum samples were collected prior to the first immunization and two weeks after the final dose to assess the humoral immune response.

Serum antibody titers against Chlamydia trachomatis serovar D elementary bodies were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), using natively purified EBs as the coating antigen. The results demonstrated that mice immunized with recombinant MOMP elicited IgG antibody titers that were statistically indistinguishable from those induced by native MOMP, with geometric mean titers exceeding 1:10,000 in both groups. The antibody response was dominated by the IgG1 and IgG2c subclasses, with a ratio of IgG1 to IgG2c close to 1:1, indicating a balanced T-helper 1 and T-helper 2 immune response. In contrast, mice immunized with adjuvant alone exhibited no detectable antibody titers above background levels. The antibody response in the live EB group was comparable to that of the MOMP-immunized groups, confirming that both recombinant and native MOMP are capable of eliciting a robust humoral response equivalent to that induced by whole-pathogen immunization.

Following the final immunization, mice were treated with medroxyprogesterone acetate to synchronize the estrous cycle and enhance susceptibility to vaginal infection. Two weeks after the last immunization, mice were challenged intravaginally with 1 × 10⁵ Chlamydia trachomatis serovar D elementary bodies in a volume of 10 μL. Vaginal swabs were collected on days 7, 11, 14, 18, and 21 post-challenge, and the bacterial load was quantified using real-time quantitative PCR targeting the conserved 16S ribosomal RNA gene. The results revealed a dramatic reduction in bacterial shedding in both the native and recombinant MOMP-immunized groups compared to the adjuvant-only control. The area under the curve (AUC) for bacterial shedding over the entire time course was significantly lower in the rMOMP group than in the control group (p < 0.01), with no statistically significant difference between the rMOMP and nMOMP groups. The live EB group exhibited the lowest bacterial load, consistent with the induction of sterilizing immunity, but this group was not considered for further development due to safety concerns.

The reduction in bacterial shedding observed in the rMOMP-immunized mice correlated directly with the magnitude of the serum antibody response, suggesting that the antibodies induced by recombinant MOMP are capable of neutralizing Chlamydia elementary bodies in vivo. The protective efficacy of rMOMP was equivalent to that of native MOMP, demonstrating that the recombinant protein, despite being produced in a heterologous host and purified through a non-native extraction process, retains the structural and antigenic features necessary to elicit a protective immune response. The absence of significant differences between the rMOMP and nMOMP groups confirms that the codon-harmonized expression system and purification protocol preserve the immunologically relevant epitopes of MOMP. These findings establish recombinant MOMP as a viable and superior alternative to native antigen for vaccine development, offering the advantages of scalability, reproducibility, and safety without compromising immunogenicity or protective efficacy.

## EXAMPLE 4

The purification process for recombinant Chlamydia MOMP was further refined to assess the impact of detergent selection on protein yield, purity, and structural integrity. A comparative analysis was conducted using sarkosyl, foscholine-14, and zwittergent 3–12 as solubilizing agents for the outer membrane fraction following the removal of inner membrane components. Each detergent was tested under identical conditions, including buffer composition, incubation time, temperature, and ultracentrifugation parameters. The solubilized fractions were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and Western blot to determine the relative abundance of MOMP versus contaminating E. coli proteins. Sarkosyl consistently yielded the highest recovery of MOMP, with a clear single band at 37 kDa and minimal background staining, whereas foscholine-14 and zwittergent 3–12 resulted in lower recovery and increased contamination by host proteins, particularly lipoproteins and porins. The use of sarkosyl also facilitated more efficient separation during size exclusion chromatography, resulting in a sharper elution peak and higher purity of the final product.

To determine whether the presence of reducing agents was essential for extraction, parallel experiments were performed with and without dithiothreitol (DTT). In the absence of DTT, the yield of MOMP dropped by more than 60%, and the protein migrated as a higher molecular weight aggregate on SDS-PAGE under non-reducing conditions, suggesting that disulfide bonds were not properly reduced and that the protein remained trapped in the membrane. The addition of 5 mM DTT was therefore deemed critical for the efficient extraction of MOMP and was incorporated into all subsequent purification protocols. The inclusion of DTT did not affect the final structure of the protein, as confirmed by circular dichroism spectroscopy, which showed identical β-sheet signatures regardless of whether DTT was present during extraction or removed prior to analysis.

The impact of buffer composition during size exclusion chromatography was also evaluated. Buffers containing 0.1% zwittergent 3–14, 0.05% Triton X-100, or 1% octyl glucoside were tested for their ability to maintain MOMP solubility and prevent aggregation. Only the zwittergent 3–14-containing buffer allowed for stable elution without precipitation or peak broadening. The other detergents either caused protein aggregation or interfered with the column matrix, leading to poor resolution and low recovery. The final storage buffer was therefore standardized to contain 10 mM Hepes, pH 7.3, 150 mM NaCl, and 0.1% zwittergent 3–14, which maintained protein stability for at least six months at 4°C.

The endotoxin content of the purified protein was assessed using a Limulus amebocyte lysate assay, which confirmed that the final preparation contained less than 0.1 endotoxin units per microgram of protein, well below the threshold for safe parenteral administration. Mass spectrometry analysis of the purified protein revealed that the PelB leader sequence was completely cleaved, with the N-terminal methionine removed and the mature protein starting at the predicted residue. No post-translational modifications, such as glycosylation or acetylation, were detected, consistent with the absence of such modifications in native Chlamydia MOMP. The purity of the preparation, as determined by densitometry of Coomassie-stained gels, was approximately 70%, with the remaining 30% consisting of E. coli outer membrane proteins that co-purified due to their similar molecular weight and detergent solubility. These contaminants were deemed acceptable for immunological use, as they did not elicit a detectable immune response in the context of the adjuvant used and did not mask the immunodominant epitopes of MOMP.

## EXAMPLE 5

The immunogenicity of recombinant MOMP was further evaluated by analyzing the isotype profile of the induced antibody response in greater detail. Serum samples collected two weeks after the final immunization were subjected to ELISA using isotype-specific secondary antibodies to quantify IgG1, IgG2a, IgG2b, and IgG2c subclasses. The results demonstrated that the recombinant MOMP elicited a balanced Th1/Th2 response, with IgG1 and IgG2c being the dominant subclasses. The IgG1/IgG2c ratio was approximately 1.1, indicating a near-equivalent contribution from both pathways. This is in contrast to the response elicited by live Chlamydia infection, which tends to be skewed toward IgG2a and IgG2b, suggesting a more inflammatory Th1 bias. The recombinant MOMP formulation, by contrast, induced a more modulated response that may be advantageous for preventing immunopathology while still providing robust protection.

The specificity of the antibody response was confirmed by competitive ELISA, in which serum from immunized mice was pre-incubated with increasing concentrations of purified native MOMP or recombinant MOMP before being added to EB-coated plates. Both forms of MOMP effectively competed for antibody binding, with 50% inhibition achieved at similar concentrations, indicating that the epitopes recognized by the antibodies are identical or highly overlapping between the native and recombinant forms. In contrast, pre-incubation with unrelated bacterial proteins such as E. coli OmpA or Salmonella flagellin had no effect on antibody binding, confirming the specificity of the response.

To assess the functional capacity of the induced antibodies, an in vitro neutralization assay was performed using Chlamydia elementary bodies and HeLa cell monolayers. Serum from rMOMP-immunized mice was able to reduce the number of inclusions formed in a dose-dependent manner, with a 50% neutralization titer comparable to that of serum from nMOMP-immunized mice. The neutralizing activity correlated strongly with the total IgG titer, suggesting that the antibodies are capable of blocking the attachment or entry of elementary bodies into host cells. These findings confirm that the recombinant MOMP not only elicits antibodies that bind to the native antigen but also generates antibodies with functional neutralizing activity, a critical requirement for an effective vaccine.

## EXAMPLE 6

The stability of the purified recombinant MOMP under various storage conditions was evaluated to determine its suitability for long-term use in vaccine formulations. Aliquots of the purified protein were stored at 4°C, −20°C, −80°C, and at room temperature for periods ranging from one week to six months. At regular intervals, samples were analyzed by SDS-PAGE, circular dichroism spectroscopy, and ELISA for antigenicity. The protein remained stable at 4°C for at least six months, with no detectable aggregation, degradation, or loss of β-sheet structure. At −20°C and −80°C, the protein was also stable, but repeated freeze-thaw cycles led to a gradual increase in aggregation, as evidenced by the appearance of high molecular weight bands on SDS-PAGE and a reduction in the mean molar ellipticity at 215 nm. Storage at room temperature resulted in partial degradation within two weeks, with a 30% reduction in antigenicity as measured by ELISA.

Lyophilization was tested as a means of enhancing long-term stability. The purified MOMP was freeze-dried in the presence of 5% trehalose and stored at 4°C. After six months, the lyophilized protein was reconstituted and analyzed. The reconstituted protein exhibited identical migration on SDS-PAGE, identical circular dichroism spectra, and equivalent ELISA reactivity compared to the freshly purified protein. The addition of trehalose was found to be critical for preventing structural collapse during dehydration, as samples lyophilized without cryoprotectants showed significant aggregation and loss of immunogenicity. These results demonstrate that the recombinant MOMP can be formulated into a stable, lyophilized vaccine product that retains full immunogenicity for at least six months under refrigerated conditions, making it suitable for global distribution and deployment in resource-limited settings.

## EXAMPLE 7

The scalability of the recombinant MOMP production process was evaluated by conducting a pilot-scale fermentation in a 10-liter bioreactor under controlled conditions. The expression system, including the codon-harmonized MOMP gene in the pAVE029 vector and the E. coli K12 W25113 host strain, was transferred to the bioreactor and grown in Cinnabar medium with continuous pH control at 7.0 and dissolved oxygen maintained at 30%. Induction was initiated at an OD590 of 0.5 with 0.4 mM IPTG, and the temperature was maintained at 30°C for four hours. The culture was harvested, and the purification process was scaled proportionally. The yield of purified recombinant MOMP was 5.8 mg per liter of culture, consistent with the results obtained in shake flask cultures. The purity, as assessed by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry, remained at approximately 70%, and the endotoxin level was below the detection limit. The circular dichroism spectrum of the bioreactor-derived protein was identical to that of the shake flask-derived protein, confirming that the process is robust and scalable. These findings demonstrate that the method described herein is suitable for industrial-scale manufacturing and can be readily adapted to GMP-compliant production facilities.

## EXAMPLE 8

The immunogenicity of recombinant MOMP was tested in a second mouse strain, BALB/c, to assess whether the immune response was strain-dependent. Female BALB/c mice were immunized with rMOMP or nMOMP using the same protocol as in the C57BL/6 model. The antibody titers elicited in BALB/c mice were comparable to those in C57BL/6 mice, with no significant difference in IgG1 or IgG2c levels. The protective efficacy against vaginal challenge was also equivalent, with a significant reduction in bacterial shedding observed in both MOMP-immunized groups compared to controls. These results indicate that the recombinant MOMP is immunogenic across different genetic backgrounds and is not restricted to a single mouse strain, supporting its potential for broad application in human populations with diverse HLA haplotypes.

## EXAMPLE 9

To determine whether the recombinant MOMP could elicit a T-cell response, splenocytes were isolated from immunized mice and restimulated in vitro with recombinant MOMP or a pool of known MOMP-derived peptides. The production of interferon-gamma and interleukin-17 was measured by ELISPOT, and a significant increase in cytokine-secreting cells was observed in both rMOMP- and nMOMP-immunized mice compared to controls. The response was directed against multiple epitopes distributed across the variable and conserved domains of MOMP, indicating that the recombinant protein contains both B-cell and T-cell epitopes in their native conformation. The T-cell response was not observed in mice immunized with adjuvant alone, confirming that it was antigen-specific. These findings demonstrate that the recombinant MOMP is not only a potent B-cell antigen but also a source of T-cell epitopes, which may contribute to long-term immune memory and intracellular clearance of Chlamydia.

## EXAMPLE 10

The specificity of the recombinant MOMP was evaluated against other Chlamydia species, including Chlamydia pneumoniae and Chlamydia psittaci. Serum from mice immunized with C. trachomatis serovar E rMOMP was tested for cross-reactivity with elementary bodies from these species by ELISA and Western blot. No significant cross-reactivity was observed, indicating that the immune response is highly specific to C. trachomatis. This specificity is advantageous for diagnostic applications and for avoiding potential autoimmune cross-reactivity. The lack of cross-reactivity also suggests that the variable loops of MOMP, which are the primary targets of neutralizing antibodies, are sufficiently distinct between species to prevent cross-protective immunity, reinforcing the need for serovar-specific or multi-valent vaccine formulations.

## EXAMPLE 11

The effect of different adjuvant formulations on the immunogenicity of recombinant MOMP was evaluated. In addition to the CpG/Montanide combination, other adjuvants were tested, including alum, MPLA, and a saponin-based formulation (QS-21). The CpG/Montanide combination consistently induced the highest antibody titers and the most robust protective efficacy. Alum induced a strong IgG1 response but minimal IgG2c, resulting in reduced protection. MPLA induced a Th1-biased response but with lower overall titers. QS-21 induced a strong response but was associated with local inflammation and reduced animal welfare. The CpG/Montanide combination was therefore selected as the optimal adjuvant for further development due to its balanced immune profile, high efficacy, and favorable safety record.

## EXAMPLE 12

The recombinant MOMP was tested for its ability to induce mucosal immunity by measuring secretory IgA in vaginal washes following immunization. Although the immunization was administered subcutaneously, a significant increase in antigen-specific IgA was detected in vaginal secretions two weeks after the final dose. This suggests that the systemic immune response induced by the recombinant MOMP is capable of trafficking to mucosal surfaces, a critical feature for protection against genital Chlamydia infection. The presence of mucosal IgA was not observed in mice immunized with adjuvant alone, confirming its antigen-specific nature. This finding supports the use of subcutaneous immunization as a viable route for inducing both systemic and mucosal immunity, eliminating the need for invasive or complex delivery systems.

## EXAMPLE 13

The recombinant MOMP was tested for its ability to confer protection against heterologous serovars, including serovars F and G, which are closely related to serovar E. Mice immunized with rMOMP from serovar E were challenged intravaginally with serovars F and G. A modest but statistically significant reduction in bacterial shedding was observed, suggesting partial cross-protection. This cross-reactivity is likely due to the conservation of the β-barrel core domain of MOMP across serovars, which contains epitopes recognized by non-neutralizing but potentially opsonizing antibodies. These findings support the development of a multi-serovar vaccine formulation containing codon-harmonized MOMP from multiple serovars to broaden the spectrum of protection. The ability of a single serovar-derived antigen to provide partial cross-protection enhances the feasibility of a subunit vaccine strategy and reduces the complexity of manufacturing.
