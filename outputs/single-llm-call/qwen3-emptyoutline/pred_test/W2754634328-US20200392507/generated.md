# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic compositions and methods for the treatment of fibrotic disorders, particularly hypertrophic scarring and other pathological dermal fibroses, through the simultaneous silencing of two key profibrotic signaling molecules—transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2)—using small interfering RNA (siRNA) delivered via a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) nanoparticle system. More specifically, the invention encompasses a pharmaceutical formulation comprising HKP-packaged siRNA duplexes targeting TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA sequences, wherein the formulation is administered via intradermal or intrascar injection to achieve sustained, localized gene silencing, resulting in the reduction of fibroblast proliferation, inhibition of extracellular matrix deposition, and induction of fibroblast and myofibroblast apoptosis. The invention further encompasses methods of using such a formulation to treat human hypertrophic scars, keloids, and other fibrotic skin conditions characterized by excessive collagen accumulation and persistent myofibroblast activity. The therapeutic effect is mediated not merely through the suppression of individual profibrotic pathways, but through a synergistic disruption of a previously unrecognized regulatory axis between TGF-β1 and COX-2 signaling that governs fibroblast survival and ECM homeostasis in pathological scarring. This dual-targeting approach, combined with the enhanced delivery properties of HKP, enables potent anti-fibrotic activity at microgram-level siRNA doses, significantly surpassing the efficacy of single-target agents and overcoming the delivery barriers that have historically limited the clinical translation of RNA interference therapeutics in dermatological fibrosis.

## BACKGROUND

Fibrotic scarring represents a pervasive and clinically challenging consequence of dermal injury, affecting millions of individuals worldwide and resulting in significant functional impairment, cosmetic disfigurement, and psychological distress. Hypertrophic scars, in particular, arise from an aberrant wound healing response characterized by the persistent activation of fibroblasts and their differentiation into contractile myofibroblasts, which overproduce and fail to adequately degrade extracellular matrix components, primarily collagens I and III. Unlike normal scars, which undergo a controlled remodeling phase marked by fibroblast apoptosis and matrix reorganization, hypertrophic scars are distinguished by prolonged inflammation, excessive vascularization, hypercellularity, and resistance to programmed cell death. Despite decades of clinical investigation and the development of numerous therapeutic modalities—including corticosteroid injections, pressure therapy, silicone sheeting, cryotherapy, laser ablation, and surgical excision—no single intervention has consistently achieved durable resolution of established hypertrophic scars, and many of these approaches are associated with high recurrence rates, significant side effects, or limited efficacy in severe cases. The underlying pathophysiology of fibrosis remains incompletely understood, and current therapies largely address symptoms rather than the core cellular and molecular drivers of disease progression.

The transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) superfamily, particularly the TGF-β1 isoform, has long been recognized as the master regulator of fibrogenesis. TGF-β1 stimulates fibroblast proliferation, induces myofibroblast differentiation through upregulation of α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), and promotes the synthesis of collagen and other matrix proteins while simultaneously suppressing matrix metalloproteinases and enhancing the expression of tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs). Elevated levels of TGF-β1 have been consistently documented in human hypertrophic scar tissue compared to normal skin, and its persistent expression correlates directly with scar thickness, rigidity, and clinical persistence. Consequently, numerous therapeutic strategies have been developed to inhibit TGF-β1 signaling, including neutralizing antibodies, soluble receptor decoys, antisense oligonucleotides, and small molecule kinase inhibitors targeting the TGF-β receptor I (TBR1). While some of these agents have demonstrated promising results in preclinical models, their clinical translation has been hampered by systemic toxicity, poor bioavailability at the target site, and the inability to achieve sufficient intracellular concentrations within dense fibrotic tissue.

Cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), an inducible enzyme responsible for the biosynthesis of prostaglandins including prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), has traditionally been viewed as a mediator of inflammation and tissue repair. However, emerging evidence suggests a more complex and context-dependent role in fibrosis. In certain experimental systems, elevated COX-2 and PGE2 have been associated with anti-fibrotic effects, potentially through inhibition of TGF-β1-induced collagen synthesis. Yet, in human pathological scarring, COX-2 is markedly overexpressed alongside TGF-β1, and its inhibition has been linked to reduced fibrosis in fetal wound models and in vivo animal studies of dermal scarring. This paradox suggests that COX-2 may not simply act as a downstream effector of inflammation but may instead participate in a positive feedback loop that reinforces fibroblast survival and matrix deposition. Recent mechanistic studies have revealed that TGF-β1 can transcriptionally upregulate COX-2 expression via heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein A/B (HNRPAB), and conversely, COX-2 activity appears to enhance the phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of SMAD2/3, the canonical downstream effectors of TGF-β signaling. This reciprocal regulation implies that the two pathways are not independent but are functionally interdependent in the context of fibrotic disease, forming a self-sustaining signaling axis that promotes fibroblast persistence and resistance to apoptosis.

The therapeutic potential of RNA interference (RNAi) has long been recognized for its ability to achieve highly specific gene silencing with minimal off-target effects. Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) designed to target the mRNA of TGF-β1 or COX-2 have demonstrated potent inhibitory effects in vitro and in animal models. However, the clinical application of siRNA therapeutics for dermal fibrosis has been severely constrained by the formidable biological barriers to delivery. The stratum corneum, dense extracellular matrix, and limited vascularization of hypertrophic scar tissue prevent the passive diffusion of naked siRNA molecules into the dermal compartment where fibroblasts reside. Furthermore, siRNAs are rapidly degraded by nucleases in the extracellular environment and are inefficiently internalized by fibroblasts without a delivery vehicle. Conventional transfection reagents such as lipofectamine are cytotoxic and unsuitable for in vivo use, while viral vectors raise safety and immunogenicity concerns. Although lipid nanoparticles and polymeric carriers have been explored, many lack the necessary biocompatibility, stability, or tissue penetration capacity for effective intradermal delivery. Consequently, despite compelling preclinical data, no siRNA-based therapy has yet achieved regulatory approval for the treatment of hypertrophic scarring.

To address these challenges, the present invention introduces a novel biodegradable polypeptide-based nanoparticle system composed of histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP), which has been specifically engineered to encapsulate and protect siRNA duplexes targeting both TGF-β1 and COX-2. HKP forms stable, positively charged nanoparticles through electrostatic interactions with negatively charged siRNA, resulting in particles with a mean diameter of approximately 150 nanometers and a zeta potential of +40 mV, properties that facilitate cellular uptake and endosomal escape. Crucially, HKP nanoparticles are non-immunogenic, exhibit excellent stability upon lyophilization and reconstitution, and enable sustained release of siRNA within the scar tissue over a period of up to 48 hours—far exceeding the transient exposure achieved by naked siRNA. The invention further demonstrates that the simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 using this delivery platform not only reduces the expression of profibrotic markers such as α-SMA, collagen I, and collagen III but, more importantly, triggers a previously unreported apoptotic cascade in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts. This dual-gene silencing strategy disrupts the TGF-β1/COX-2 signaling axis at its core, leading to the dephosphorylation of SMAD2/3, downregulation of PAI-1, and activation of intrinsic apoptotic pathways involving caspase-3 and PARP cleavage. The resulting loss of fibroblast viability, coupled with reduced matrix synthesis, leads to a dramatic and sustained reduction in scar volume in both human tissue xenograft models and in vivo implant systems. This mechanism of action distinguishes the invention from all prior approaches, which typically aim to modulate fibroblast activity without inducing significant cell death. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in fibrosis therapy: rather than merely suppressing fibroblast function, it actively eliminates the pathological cell population responsible for scar persistence.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a pharmaceutical composition for the treatment of fibrotic skin disorders, comprising a biodegradable nanoparticle formed by the self-assembly of a histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) with at least two distinct small interfering RNA (siRNA) duplexes, wherein a first siRNA duplex is complementary to a sequence within the messenger RNA (mRNA) of transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) and a second siRNA duplex is complementary to a sequence within the mRNA of cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), and wherein the HKP and siRNA are present in a mass ratio of approximately 4:1. The composition is formulated as a sterile, aqueous suspension suitable for intradermal or intrascar administration, and exhibits a mean particle diameter of between 120 and 180 nanometers, a zeta potential of between +35 and +45 millivolts, and stability for at least twelve months when stored as a lyophilized powder at 4°C. The siRNA duplexes are each 21 to 25 nucleotides in length, with blunt-ended termini, and are chemically modified at the 2′-position of the ribose sugar to enhance nuclease resistance and reduce immunostimulatory potential. The first siRNA duplex targets a region of the human TGF-β1 mRNA spanning nucleotides 1024 to 1048 (SEQ ID NO: 1), and the second siRNA duplex targets a region of the human COX-2 mRNA spanning nucleotides 1456 to 1480 (SEQ ID NO: 2). The composition is free of viral vectors, lipid bilayers, and synthetic polymers other than HKP, and contains no adjuvants, surfactants, or preservatives beyond those necessary for formulation stability. The HKP polymer is a branched polypeptide comprising a lysine core with four arms, each arm containing alternating repeats of histidine and lysine residues, with a molecular weight of approximately 15,000 Daltons, and is synthesized via solid-phase peptide synthesis with purity exceeding 98% as determined by high-performance liquid chromatography. The siRNA duplexes are synthesized using phosphorothioate backbone modifications at the terminal three nucleotides of each strand to further enhance stability in biological fluids. The composition is administered via multiple intradermal injections into the dermal layer of the hypertrophic scar tissue, with each injection delivering between 1 and 50 micrograms of total siRNA per cubic centimeter of scar tissue, and the treatment regimen comprises three injections administered at five-day intervals, resulting in sustained silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA expression for up to seven days following each administration. The composition induces a statistically significant reduction in scar volume of at least 35% within 28 days of initiating treatment, as measured by planimetry or three-dimensional imaging, and concurrently reduces the expression of α-smooth muscle actin, collagen type I alpha 1 chain, and collagen type III alpha 1 chain at both the mRNA and protein levels. The composition further induces apoptosis in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts within the scar tissue, as evidenced by increased terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) positivity, caspase-3 activation, and DNA fragmentation, without inducing significant apoptosis in surrounding normal dermal cells or epidermal keratinocytes. The therapeutic effect is achieved without systemic exposure to siRNA, as confirmed by undetectable levels of siRNA in serum following local administration, and without eliciting an innate immune response, as demonstrated by the absence of elevated interferon-alpha, interleukin-6, or tumor necrosis factor-alpha in tissue homogenates. The invention also encompasses a method of preparing the composition, comprising the steps of dissolving HKP in sterile water to a concentration of 10 mg/mL, dissolving the TGF-β1 and COX-2 siRNA duplexes in sterile water to a concentration of 2.5 mg/mL each, mixing the two solutions at a 4:1 HKP-to-siRNA mass ratio, incubating the mixture at room temperature for 30 minutes to allow nanoparticle self-assembly, and filtering the resulting suspension through a 0.22-micron sterile filter to remove particulate matter and obtain a sterile, injectable formulation. The composition may be lyophilized by freezing at −80°C for 12 hours followed by lyophilization under vacuum for 48 hours, and the resulting powder may be reconstituted immediately prior to administration with sterile water or saline without loss of particle integrity or silencing activity. The invention further encompasses a kit comprising the lyophilized composition in a sealed vial, a sterile syringe, a 27-gauge needle, and instructions for reconstitution and intradermal injection into hypertrophic scar tissue. The invention further encompasses a method of treating a subject suffering from a fibrotic skin disorder, comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of the composition as described above, wherein the fibrotic skin disorder is selected from the group consisting of hypertrophic scar, keloid, scleroderma, dermal fibrosis secondary to burn injury, and post-surgical fibrosis. The method further comprises the step of evaluating the therapeutic response by measuring scar thickness, volume, pliability, or pigmentation at baseline and at intervals of 7, 14, 21, and 28 days following initiation of treatment, wherein a reduction of at least 30% in scar volume or thickness is indicative of a positive therapeutic response. The invention further encompasses a method of identifying subjects who are likely to respond to the composition, comprising the step of obtaining a biopsy sample of the fibrotic tissue and quantifying the mRNA expression levels of TGF-β1 and COX-2 by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), wherein subjects exhibiting expression levels of TGF-β1 and COX-2 that are at least twofold higher than those observed in normal skin tissue are selected for treatment. The invention further encompasses a method of preventing the formation of hypertrophic scar following dermal injury, comprising administering the composition to the wound site within 72 hours of injury, wherein the administration is performed via intradermal injection at the wound margins and at intervals of 48 hours for a total of three doses, thereby suppressing the early activation of fibroblasts and preventing the establishment of a persistent profibrotic microenvironment. The invention further encompasses a method of reducing scar contracture, comprising administering the composition to a fibrotic tissue exhibiting high α-SMA expression, wherein the reduction in contracture is measured by an increase in range of motion at a joint adjacent to the scar, and wherein the increase in range of motion is at least 20% following three treatment cycles. ## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions

Human fibroblasts were isolated from surgically excised hypertrophic scar tissue obtained from adult patients with informed consent, and cultured under standard conditions in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum, penicillin, and streptomycin. Cells between passages three and six were used for all experiments. Two distinct 21-nucleotide siRNA duplexes were designed to target human TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA sequences, with each duplex possessing blunt-ended termini and 2′-O-methyl modifications at the 3′ end of the sense strand to enhance stability and reduce immunogenicity. The TGF-β1 siRNA targeted nucleotides 1024–1048 of the human TGF-β1 transcript (SEQ ID NO: 1), and the COX-2 siRNA targeted nucleotides 1456–1480 of the human COX-2 transcript (SEQ ID NO: 2). These sequences were selected based on in silico prediction of target accessibility, minimal off-target potential, and high silencing efficiency in preliminary transfection assays using PC3 cells. Fibroblasts were transfected using Lipofectamine 2000 with either TGF-β1 siRNA alone, COX-2 siRNA alone, a combination of both siRNAs, or a non-targeting scrambled siRNA control. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by fluorescently labeled siRNA and flow cytometry, with greater than 90% cellular uptake observed in all groups. At 48 hours post-transfection, total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent, and quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed using SYBR Green chemistry and gene-specific primers. The expression levels of TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA were normalized to the housekeeping gene GAPDH. In cells treated with TGF-β1 siRNA alone, TGF-β1 mRNA expression was reduced by 78% compared to the scrambled control, while COX-2 mRNA expression was reduced by 42%. In cells treated with COX-2 siRNA alone, COX-2 mRNA expression was reduced by 81%, while TGF-β1 mRNA expression was reduced by 51%. In cells treated with the combination of TGF-β1 and COX-2 siRNAs, TGF-β1 mRNA expression was reduced by 92% and COX-2 mRNA expression was reduced by 94%, demonstrating a synergistic silencing effect that exceeded the sum of individual silencing efficiencies. No significant reduction in mRNA levels was observed in cells treated with scrambled siRNA or in untreated controls. The synergistic silencing was further confirmed by Western blot analysis, which demonstrated a corresponding reduction in TGF-β1 and COX-2 protein levels in the combination-treated group, with TGF-β1 protein reduced by 89% and COX-2 protein reduced by 87% compared to controls. The knockdown of TGF-β1 and COX-2 was accompanied by a marked reduction in downstream profibrotic markers, including α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), collagen type I alpha 1 chain (COL1A1), and collagen type III alpha 1 chain (COL3A1), as measured by qRT-PCR and immunofluorescence. α-SMA expression was reduced by 82% in the combination group compared to 45% and 53% in the single siRNA groups, respectively. COL1A1 and COL3A1 mRNA levels were reduced by 85% and 80% in the combination group, compared to 48% and 51% in the single siRNA groups. These results demonstrate that the simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 not only achieves more complete suppression of each target gene but also induces a broader suppression of the fibrotic phenotype, suggesting a functional interdependence between the two pathways. The reciprocal suppression observed—where silencing TGF-β1 reduced COX-2 expression and vice versa—indicates that these two molecules are not merely parallel effectors but are part of a tightly coupled regulatory network that maintains the fibrotic state. The data further suggest that targeting both nodes of this network simultaneously is necessary to achieve maximal disruption of the profibrotic signaling cascade, and that single-target approaches are inherently limited in their ability to fully reverse the fibrotic phenotype.

### Example 2: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions Resulted in Activation of Apoptosis of Fibroblasts/Myofibroblasts

To determine the cellular fate of fibroblasts following simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2, fibroblasts isolated from hypertrophic scar tissue were transfected with the same siRNA constructs as described in Example 1 and analyzed for apoptosis using multiple complementary methods. Four hours after transfection, cells were harvested and stained with Annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide (PI) for flow cytometric analysis. In the scrambled siRNA control group, 4.2% of cells were Annexin V-positive/PI-negative (early apoptotic), and 1.1% were Annexin V/PI-double positive (late apoptotic/necrotic). In the TGF-β1 siRNA-only group, early apoptosis increased to 12.7%, and late apoptosis to 3.8%. In the COX-2 siRNA-only group, early apoptosis increased to 14.1%, and late apoptosis to 4.5%. However, in the group treated with the combination of TGF-β1 and COX-2 siRNAs, early apoptosis increased to 48.3%, and late apoptosis to 32.6%, representing a nearly fivefold increase in total apoptotic cells compared to either single siRNA treatment. The difference between the combination group and the single siRNA groups was statistically significant (p < 0.001). Cell cycle analysis by PI staining revealed a marked accumulation of cells in the sub-G1 phase in the combination group (38.7%), compared to 8.9% in TGF-β1 siRNA alone and 9.2% in COX-2 siRNA alone, confirming the induction of DNA fragmentation characteristic of apoptosis. Morphological analysis by transmission electron microscopy revealed classic apoptotic features in the combination-treated cells, including chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, mitochondrial shrinkage, and membrane blebbing, whereas cells treated with single siRNAs retained normal nuclear architecture and organelle integrity. Immunofluorescence staining for cleaved caspase-3, a key executioner caspase in the apoptotic pathway, showed intense cytoplasmic staining in 72% of cells in the combination group, compared to 18% in TGF-β1 siRNA alone and 21% in COX-2 siRNA alone. Western blot analysis confirmed the presence of cleaved caspase-3 and cleaved PARP, two canonical markers of apoptosis, only in the combination-treated group, with no detectable cleavage in single siRNA or control groups. To determine whether the apoptotic effect was dependent on the TGF-β1/COX-2 signaling axis, fibroblasts were pre-treated with the TGF-β receptor I inhibitor Galunisertib (100 nM) or the COX-2 inhibitor Celecoxib (100 nM) for 24 hours prior to transfection with the combination siRNAs. Both inhibitors significantly attenuated the induction of apoptosis, reducing the percentage of Annexin V-positive cells from 48.3% to 21.5% and 23.1%, respectively, demonstrating that the apoptotic effect is mediated through the suppression of TGF-β1 and COX-2 signaling. Furthermore, overexpression of COX-2 using a pcDNA3.1-COX-2 plasmid vector reversed the apoptotic effect of the combination siRNAs, restoring cell viability and reducing caspase-3 cleavage to control levels. These findings indicate that the simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 triggers an intrinsic apoptotic pathway that is not activated when either target is silenced alone. The mechanism appears to involve the dephosphorylation of SMAD2/3, as Western blot analysis showed a 79% reduction in phospho-SMAD2/3 in the combination group compared to 35% and 41% in the single siRNA groups, and a corresponding reduction in the expression of PAI-1, a direct transcriptional target of SMAD signaling. The reduction in phospho-SMAD2/3 was accompanied by a decrease in Bcl-2 expression and an increase in Bax expression, shifting the mitochondrial apoptotic balance toward cell death. These results demonstrate that the dual silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 does not merely suppress fibroblast activity but actively eliminates the pathological cell population responsible for scar persistence, representing a fundamentally different therapeutic strategy from existing approaches that aim to modulate, but not eradicate, fibroblast function.

### Example 3: HKP Enhances siRNA Delivery Into Human Hypertrophic Scar

To overcome the barrier to intradermal siRNA delivery, a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) was employed as a nanoparticle carrier. HKP, a branched polypeptide with a lysine core and four arms containing alternating histidine and lysine repeats, was synthesized via solid-phase peptide synthesis and purified to greater than 98% homogeneity. The HKP was mixed with a 1:1 molar ratio of TGF-β1 and COX-2 siRNAs at a mass ratio of 4:1 (HKP:siRNA) in sterile water, resulting in spontaneous self-assembly into nanoparticles with a mean hydrodynamic diameter of 152 ± 8 nm as measured by dynamic light scattering, and a zeta potential of +41.3 ± 2.1 mV as measured by electrophoretic light scattering. The nanoparticles were stable for at least 12 months when lyophilized and stored at 4°C, and retained full silencing activity upon reconstitution. The encapsulation efficiency of siRNA was greater than 95%, as determined by anion-exchange chromatography and fluorescent dye exclusion assays. To evaluate the delivery efficiency of HKP-packaged siRNA in human hypertrophic scar tissue, a human tissue xenograft model was established by implanting 2 cm² pieces of freshly excised hypertrophic scar tissue subcutaneously into the backs of nude mice. Four weeks after implantation, when the grafts had fully vascularized and stabilized, Alexa Fluor 647-labeled siRNA was injected intradermally into the grafts either in naked form or packaged within HKP nanoparticles. Tissue samples were harvested at 4, 24, and 48 hours post-injection and analyzed by fluorescence microscopy. Naked siRNA was rapidly dispersed from the injection site and was undetectable by 24 hours, with no discernible signal within the dermal compartment. In contrast, HKP-packaged siRNA remained localized at the injection site for the entire 48-hour observation period, with strong fluorescence signal detected throughout the fibrotic tissue, including within fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, as confirmed by co-localization with α-SMA immunostaining. The HKP-siRNA nanoparticles were observed to be internalized by fibroblasts via endocytosis, as evidenced by punctate intracellular fluorescence, and were retained in endosomal compartments for up to 48 hours before gradual release into the cytoplasm. qRT-PCR analysis of TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA levels in the grafts at 24, 48, and 96 hours post-injection revealed a dose-dependent silencing effect. At 24 hours, TGF-β1 mRNA was reduced by 58% and COX-2 mRNA by 32%. At 48 hours, TGF-β1 mRNA was reduced by 79% and COX-2 mRNA by 61%. At 96 hours, TGF-β1 mRNA was reduced by 72% and COX-2 mRNA by 74%, demonstrating sustained silencing with a delayed but prolonged effect on COX-2, consistent with its slower turnover rate. The silencing effect was specific, as no reduction in mRNA levels was observed in control grafts injected with HKP alone or with scrambled siRNA. The HKP-siRNA nanoparticles were completely degraded by endogenous proteases and nucleases within seven days, with no residual polymer or siRNA detected in serum or distant organs. Histological analysis revealed no signs of inflammation, necrosis, or foreign body reaction at the injection site, confirming the biocompatibility of the HKP carrier. These results demonstrate that HKP enables efficient, sustained, and localized delivery of siRNA into the fibrotic dermal compartment, overcoming the limitations of naked siRNA and providing a clinically viable platform for intradermal RNA interference therapy.

### Example 4: HKP-Packaged TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNA Reduces Size of Human Hypertrophic Scar

To evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of HKP-packaged TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNA (designated STP705) in vivo, human hypertrophic scar tissue was implanted subcutaneously into nude mice as described in Example 3. Four weeks after implantation, when the grafts had fully integrated and exhibited stable fibrotic morphology, mice were randomly assigned to receive either STP705 (20 μg/cm³ total siRNA, delivered in 50 μL aliquots), HKP alone, scrambled siRNA in HKP, or saline. Each graft received five intradermal injections at equidistant points around the perimeter and center of the scar, with each injection delivering 10 μL. Injections were administered on days 0, 5, and 10, for a total of three doses. Scar size was measured weekly using digital planimetry and caliper-based measurements, and scar thickness was assessed by ultrasound. At day 28, the scar tissue was harvested for histological and molecular analysis. Untreated control scars exhibited a mean volume of 1.82 ± 0.14 cm³ at baseline and 1.79 ± 0.12 cm³ at day 28, indicating no spontaneous regression. Scars treated with HKP alone or scrambled siRNA in HKP showed no significant change in volume compared to controls. In contrast, scars treated with STP705 exhibited a mean volume reduction of 45.3% ± 3.1% compared to baseline, with a final volume of 0.99 ± 0.08 cm³ at day 28. The reduction was progressive, with 18.2% reduction at day 14 and 37.6% reduction at day 21, culminating in a near-complete flattening of the scar in many animals. Histological analysis using hematoxylin and eosin staining revealed a marked reduction in dermal thickness, fibroblast density, and collagen packing in the STP705-treated group compared to controls. Masson’s trichrome staining confirmed a significant reduction in blue-staining collagen fibers, with collagen volume fraction reduced from 68.7% ± 4.2% in controls to 31.4% ± 3.8% in the STP705 group. qRT-PCR analysis of the harvested tissue confirmed a 76% reduction in TGF-β1 mRNA and a 73% reduction in COX-2 mRNA in the STP705 group compared to controls. Protein expression of TGF-β1, COX-2, α-SMA, COL1A1, and COL3A1 was also significantly reduced, as confirmed by immunohistochemistry and Western blot. Apoptotic cells were detected using TUNEL staining, with a 4.8-fold increase in TUNEL-positive nuclei in the STP705 group compared to controls. No adverse events, including ulceration, infection, or systemic toxicity, were observed in any of the treated animals. These results demonstrate that STP705 induces a profound and durable reduction in hypertrophic scar volume through targeted gene silencing and fibroblast apoptosis, with no observable toxicity or off-target effects.

### Example 5: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Reduces Size of Human Skin Grafts

To determine whether the therapeutic effect of STP705 extends beyond pre-formed hypertrophic scars to the prevention and treatment of scar formation during active wound healing, a human skin graft model was employed. Skin excisions from three female donors (ages 23–36) undergoing breast reduction surgery were obtained under informed consent and cut into 2 cm × 1.6 cm pieces. These grafts were transplanted onto the dorsal surface of nude mice, replacing full-thickness excisions of mouse skin. The grafts were secured with sutures and covered with sterile cotton and compression dressing for two weeks. After suture removal, the grafts were allowed to heal for four weeks, during which time they developed a thickened, fibrotic appearance consistent with hypertrophic scarring. At day 28, mice were treated with STP705 (20 μg/cm³), HKP alone, scrambled siRNA in HKP, or saline, using the same injection protocol as in Example 4. Scar volume was measured at baseline and at days 14, 21, and 28 post-treatment. Control grafts exhibited a mean volume of 1.65 ± 0.11 cm³ at baseline and 1.62 ± 0.09 cm³ at day 28, indicating minimal spontaneous regression. Grafts treated with HKP alone or scrambled siRNA showed no significant change. In contrast, grafts treated with STP705 exhibited a mean volume reduction of 40.7% ± 2.8% compared to baseline, with a final volume of 0.98 ± 0.07 cm³ at day 28. The reduction was progressive and statistically significant at all time points. Histological analysis revealed a marked reduction in dermal thickness, fibroblast density, and collagen deposition in the STP705-treated grafts. Immunohistochemistry confirmed a 71% reduction in TGF-β1, 68% reduction in COX-2, and 74% reduction in α-SMA expression. TUNEL staining revealed a 4.5-fold increase in apoptotic fibroblasts in the STP705 group. qRT-PCR confirmed a 72% reduction in TGF-β1 mRNA and a 70% reduction in COX-2 mRNA. Hydroxyproline content, a quantitative measure of collagen, was reduced by 69% in the STP705 group compared to controls. No signs of necrosis, inflammation, or epidermal disruption were observed. These results demonstrate that STP705 is effective not only in reducing established hypertrophic scars but also in preventing the progression of fibrosis during active wound healing, suggesting a broad therapeutic application in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings.

### Example 6: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Demonstrates a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Mechanism of Action

To elucidate the mechanism underlying the anti-fibrotic effect of STP705, tissue samples from the human hypertrophic scar and skin graft models were subjected to comprehensive molecular and histological analysis. Hydroxyproline content, a direct biochemical marker of collagen deposition, was measured in tissue homogenates using a colorimetric assay. In untreated controls, hydroxyproline levels were 1.84 ± 0.12 μg/mg tissue in hypertrophic scar grafts and 1.79 ± 0.10 μg/mg tissue in skin grafts. In STP705-treated grafts, hydroxyproline levels were reduced to 0.57 ± 0.06 μg/mg and 0.59 ± 0.05 μg/mg, respectively, representing a 69–70% reduction. Masson’s trichrome staining confirmed a corresponding reduction in collagen fiber density and organization. Immunohistochemistry for α-SMA, VEGF, and CD31 revealed a marked reduction in myofibroblast density, vascular density, and endothelial cell proliferation in the STP705-treated group, indicating suppression of both fibrogenic and angiogenic components of fibrosis. TUNEL staining confirmed a 4.7-fold increase in apoptotic cells in the dermal compartment of STP705-treated grafts compared to controls, with co-localization of TUNEL signal and α-SMA expression confirming that the apoptotic cells were predominantly fibroblasts and myofibroblasts. Western blot analysis of tissue lysates demonstrated a 78% reduction in phospho-SMAD2/3, a 75% reduction in PAI-1, a 72% reduction in Bcl-2, and a 68% increase in Bax, confirming activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. Cleaved caspase-3 and cleaved PARP were detected only in the STP705-treated group. RNA sequencing of treated versus control tissue revealed downregulation of genes involved in ECM organization, collagen fibril assembly, fibroblast proliferation, and TGF-β signaling, and upregulation of genes involved in apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, and proteolysis. Pathway analysis confirmed enrichment of the TGF-β signaling pathway, the PI3K-AKT pathway, and the apoptosis pathway. No significant changes were observed in genes related to immune response, inflammation, or epidermal differentiation. These findings demonstrate that STP705 does not merely inhibit fibroblast activity but actively induces apoptosis in the pathological fibroblast population, leading to a reduction in ECM deposition and a restoration of tissue homeostasis. The mechanism is specific to the TGF-β1/COX-2 axis and does not involve non-specific immune activation or cytotoxicity.

### Example 7: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Induced Apoptotic Activities of the Fibroblast/Myofibroblasts In Vivo

To confirm that the observed reduction in scar volume was directly attributable to fibroblast/myofibroblast apoptosis in vivo, a series of co-localization and quantitative analyses were performed. Serial sections of treated and control scar tissue were stained with antibodies against α-SMA and cleaved caspase-3, and images were acquired using confocal microscopy. In control tissue, α-SMA-positive myofibroblasts were abundant and exhibited no caspase-3 staining, indicating a viable, contractile phenotype. In STP705-treated tissue, a subset of α-SMA-positive cells exhibited intense cytoplasmic caspase-3 staining, confirming that the apoptotic cells were indeed fibroblasts and myofibroblasts. The percentage of α-SMA-positive cells that were also caspase-3-positive increased from 1.2% in controls to 41.7% in the STP705 group. Flow cytometry of digested scar tissue confirmed a 5.1-fold increase in the population of α-SMA+/caspase-3+ cells in the STP705 group compared to controls. To determine whether apoptosis was selective for pathological fibroblasts, adjacent normal dermal tissue was analyzed. In the normal dermis, no increase in caspase-3 staining or decrease in fibroblast density was observed, demonstrating that the apoptotic effect was confined to the fibrotic lesion. To assess the temporal dynamics of apoptosis, tissue was harvested at 24, 48, and 72 hours post-injection. Apoptotic cells were first detected at 24 hours, peaked at 48 hours, and declined by 72 hours, consistent with the kinetics of siRNA-mediated gene silencing and caspase activation. The number of apoptotic cells at 48 hours correlated strongly with the degree of TGF-β1 and COX-2 silencing, with a Pearson correlation coefficient of r = 0.93 for TGF-β1 and r = 0.91 for COX-2. These results demonstrate that STP705 induces a rapid, specific, and transient wave of apoptosis in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, which is directly linked to the suppression of TGF-β1 and COX-2 expression, and that this apoptotic event is the primary driver of scar regression.

### Example 8: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Serves as a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Therapeutic Agent

The data presented in Examples 1 through 7 collectively demonstrate that HKP-packaged siRNA targeting TGF-β1 and COX-2 (STP705) represents a novel, potent, and clinically viable anti-fibrotic therapeutic agent. The composition achieves synergistic silencing of two key profibrotic mediators, induces apoptosis in pathological fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, reduces collagen deposition, and leads to a durable reduction in scar volume without toxicity, immunogenicity, or systemic effects. The mechanism of action is distinct from all prior therapies, which either modulate fibroblast function or suppress inflammation without eliminating the fibrotic cell population. STP705 uniquely eradicates the source of fibrosis, leading to a restoration of normal tissue architecture. The HKP carrier enables efficient, localized, and sustained delivery of siRNA to the dermal compartment, overcoming the major barrier to RNA interference therapy in fibrotic disease. The formulation is stable, scalable, and amenable to clinical manufacturing. The therapeutic effect is achieved with microgram-level doses, far below the milligram doses required by other siRNA-based agents. The treatment regimen is simple, requiring only three intradermal injections over fifteen days, with no need for daily applications, pressure garments, or lifestyle modifications. The safety profile is excellent, with no adverse events observed in preclinical models. The therapeutic effect is durable, with scar reduction maintained for at least six months in the absence of retreatment. The innovation is broadly applicable to all forms of pathological dermal fibrosis, including hypertrophic scars, keloids, and post-burn or post-surgical fibrosis. The invention represents a paradigm shift in fibrosis therapy, moving from suppression to eradication, and establishes a new standard of care for the treatment of fibrotic scarring.

## Discussion

The present invention discloses a novel therapeutic approach for the treatment of hypertrophic scarring and other fibrotic skin disorders through the simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 using a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP)-based nanoparticle delivery system. This approach represents a fundamental departure from existing therapies, which have historically focused on modulating fibroblast activity, suppressing inflammation, or mechanically reducing scar volume without addressing the underlying biological drivers of fibrosis. The central discovery of this invention is that TGF-β1 and COX-2 are not independent profibrotic mediators but are functionally interdependent, forming a self-reinforcing signaling axis that maintains fibroblast survival and extracellular matrix overproduction. Silencing either gene alone is insufficient to induce apoptosis or achieve durable scar regression, but simultaneous silencing of both genes triggers a powerful and specific apoptotic cascade in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, leading to the elimination of the pathological cell population responsible for scar persistence. This mechanism of action is unprecedented in the field of fibrosis therapeutics and distinguishes the invention from all prior approaches. The HKP nanoparticle system provides a critical enabling technology, allowing efficient, localized, and sustained delivery of siRNA to the dermal compartment, overcoming the biological barriers that have prevented the clinical translation of RNA interference for skin fibrosis. The formulation is stable, non-immunogenic, and biodegradable, with no evidence of systemic exposure or toxicity. The therapeutic effect is profound, with up to 45% reduction in scar volume achieved in human tissue xenograft models following a short, three-dose regimen. The reduction is durable, with no recurrence observed for at least six months, and is accompanied by restoration of normal dermal architecture, including reduced collagen density, decreased myofibroblast content, and normalization of vascular architecture. The invention demonstrates that fibrosis is not merely a disorder of excess matrix deposition but a disease of cellular persistence, and that the elimination of pathological fibroblasts is both necessary and sufficient to achieve true scar regression. This insight redefines the therapeutic goal in fibrosis: not to suppress, but to eradicate. The invention further demonstrates that the dual-targeting strategy is superior to single-target approaches, not merely in magnitude of effect but in quality of outcome, as it induces apoptosis rather than senescence or quiescence. The mechanism is specific, as normal fibroblasts and surrounding tissues are unaffected, and the effect is localized, with no systemic immunosuppression or off-target effects. The formulation is clinically practical, requiring no complex delivery devices, no daily patient compliance, and no adjunctive therapies. The innovation is scalable, manufacturable, and ready for clinical development. The invention establishes a new paradigm in fibrosis therapy—one that is mechanistic, curative, and transformative.

## Materials and Methods

### siRNA Sequence Design and Selection

Twenty-five nucleotide blunt-ended siRNA duplexes targeting homologous regions of human, mouse, and porcine TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA sequences were designed using bioinformatics tools (siRNA Wizard v3.1, InvivoGen). Eight candidate siRNAs were selected for each gene based on predicted target accessibility, thermodynamic stability, and minimal off-target potential. The siRNAs were synthesized by Qiagen (Valencia, CA) and Dharmacon (Boulder, CO) with 2′-O-methyl modifications at the 3′ end of the sense strand to enhance nuclease resistance and reduce immunogenicity. The most potent siRNAs were selected based on silencing efficiency in human PC3 cells. Briefly, 2.5 × 10⁵ PC3 cells were seeded in 6-well plates and transfected with 100 nM siRNA using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Cells were harvested at 48 hours post-transfection, and total RNA was extracted using the RNAqueous-4 PCR kit (Ambion). qRT-PCR was performed using SYBR Select Master Mix (Applied Biosystems) and gene-specific primers (Table 3). The siRNA with the highest knockdown efficiency (>80%) and lowest cytotoxicity was selected for further testing. The final TGF-β1 siRNA sequence (SEQ ID NO: 1) was: 5′-GGAUUCUGGAGAAGAAGAAGAAGAU-3′ (sense), 3′-CUUAAGAACCUCUUCUUCUUCUUA-5′ (antisense). The final COX-2 siRNA sequence (SEQ ID NO: 2) was: 5′-GGAUCCUCAUUGGAGAAGAAGAAGA-3′ (sense), 3′-CUUAAGGAUGUAACCUUCUUCUUCU-5′ (antisense). The scrambled control siRNA sequence was: 5′-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3′ (sense), 3′-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT-5′ (antisense).

### Growth Inhibition In Vitro

Human fibroblasts isolated from hypertrophic scar tissue were seeded at 1 × 10⁶ cells per well in 6-well plates and allowed to adhere for 6 hours. The media was replaced with fresh media containing HKP alone (10 μg/mL), HKP (TGF-β1 siRNA) (10 μg/mL HKP + 5 μg/mL siRNA), HKP (COX-2 siRNA) (10 μg/mL HKP + 5 μg/mL siRNA), or HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) (10 μg/mL HKP + 5 μg/mL each siRNA). After 48 hours, cells were harvested for apoptosis and cell cycle analysis. For Annexin V/PI staining, cells were washed with cold PBS, resuspended in binding buffer, and stained with Annexin V-FITC (4 μL, 20 μg/mL) and propidium iodide (4 μL, 50 μg/mL) (Dead Cell Apoptosis Kit, Invitrogen). Cells were incubated in the dark on ice for 15 minutes and analyzed by flow cytometry (EPICS Altra, Beckman Coulter). For cell cycle analysis, cells were fixed in 70% ethanol at 4°C overnight, washed with PBS, and stained with propidium iodide (50 μg/mL) for 24 hours at 4°C. Single-cell suspensions were analyzed by flow cytometry. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

### Nanoparticle Preparation

HKP (H3K4b, molecular weight ~15,000 Da) was dissolved in sterile water at 10 mg/mL. TGF-β1 and COX-2 siRNAs were dissolved in sterile water at 2.5 mg/mL each. The two solutions were mixed at a 4:1 mass ratio (HKP:siRNA) and incubated at room temperature for 30 minutes to allow nanoparticle self-assembly. The resulting suspension was filtered through a 0.22-μm sterile filter. Particle size and zeta potential were measured by dynamic light scattering and electrophoretic light scattering using a Zetasizer Nano ZS (Malvern Instruments). Encapsulation efficiency was determined by anion-exchange chromatography. Lyophilization was performed by freezing at −80°C for 12 hours followed by lyophilization for 48 hours under vacuum. Reconstitution was performed with sterile water, and particle integrity was confirmed by DLS.

### Mouse

Eight-week-old male nude mice (nu/nu Balb/c) were purchased from the Center for Experimental Animals in Shanghai, China. Animals were housed in specific pathogen-free conditions with controlled temperature, humidity, and light cycles. All procedures were approved by the Independent Ethics Committee of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. Mice were anesthetized with 10% chloral hydrate (0.3 mL/100 g body weight) prior to surgical procedures.

### Human Hypertrophic Scar and Skin Tissue Implant Models

Hypertrophic scar tissue was obtained from patients undergoing surgical excision with written informed consent. Subcutaneous fat was removed, and tissue was cut into 2 cm² pieces. Under anesthesia, the tissue was implanted subcutaneously on the dorsal surface of nude mice and sutured to the deep fascia. Skin grafts were obtained from breast reduction surgery patients with informed consent. Skin pieces (2 cm × 1.6 cm) were grafted onto full-thickness excisions on the dorsal surface of nude mice and sutured to the fascia and surrounding skin. Sterile cotton was placed on top and wrapped tightly. Sutures were removed after two weeks.

### Therapeutic Evaluation with the Human Tissue Implant Models

Four weeks after implantation, mice received intradermal injections of STP705 (20 μg/cm³ total siRNA), HKP alone, scrambled siRNA in HKP, or saline. Each scar received five injections (10 μL each) at equidistant points. Injections were repeated on days 5 and 10. Scar volume was measured weekly using digital planimetry and calipers. At day 28, tissue was harvested for qRT-PCR, Western blot, immunohistochemistry, TUNEL, and hydroxyproline assays.

### Statistical Analysis

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation for in vitro experiments and mean ± standard error for in vivo experiments. Statistical significance was determined using Student’s t-test for two-group comparisons and one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc test for multi-group comparisons. P-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. IBM SPSS Statistics version 20 was used for all analyses.
