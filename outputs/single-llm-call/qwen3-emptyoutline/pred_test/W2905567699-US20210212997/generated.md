# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for modulating immune responses in the context of cancer and other inflammatory diseases by targeting the integrin CD11b/CD18, also known as αMβ2 or Mac-1, expressed on myeloid cells including macrophages, monocytes, and neutrophils. Specifically, the invention provides novel therapeutic strategies that exploit the regulatory role of CD11b in the polarization of macrophages toward a pro-inflammatory, anti-tumor phenotype through the control of microRNA Let-7a expression and downstream suppression of c-Myc and interleukin-6 signaling. The invention further encompasses the use of small molecule agonists of CD11b to repolarize tumor-associated macrophages, inhibit tumor progression, enhance anti-tumor immunity, and improve the efficacy of conventional cancer therapies such as chemotherapy and radiation. The invention is particularly directed to the treatment of solid tumors, including but not limited to lung carcinoma, melanoma, breast cancer, and other malignancies characterized by an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.

### Background Information

The immune system plays a dual role in cancer, capable of both suppressing tumor initiation and promoting tumor progression depending on the nature and context of the immune response. Among the most influential cell types in shaping the tumor microenvironment are myeloid cells, particularly macrophages, which can adopt either a pro-inflammatory, anti-tumor (M1-like) phenotype or an immune-suppressive, pro-tumor (M2-like) phenotype. Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are frequently polarized toward the M2-like state, where they secrete cytokines such as interleukin-10 (IL-10), transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β), and arginase-1 (Arg1), promote angiogenesis through vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), and inhibit cytotoxic T cell activity, thereby facilitating tumor immune evasion and metastasis. The molecular mechanisms that govern this polarization are complex and involve multiple signaling pathways, including those mediated by colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R), phosphoinositide 3-kinase gamma (PI3Kγ), Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK), and toll-like receptors (TLRs). Despite significant advances in targeting these pathways, clinical outcomes have been limited by compensatory mechanisms and the plasticity of myeloid cells.

Integrins are transmembrane adhesion receptors that mediate cell-extracellular matrix and cell-cell interactions, and they also serve as critical signaling platforms that influence cell survival, migration, and activation. The integrin CD11b/CD18, a member of the β2 integrin family, is predominantly expressed on myeloid cells and has been historically associated with leukocyte adhesion, phagocytosis, and complement-mediated clearance during acute inflammation. Prior studies have suggested that CD11b contributes to the recruitment of myeloid cells into inflamed tissues, but its role in the tumor microenvironment had remained unclear. Earlier investigations into the function of CD11b in cancer had yielded conflicting results, with some reports indicating a pro-tumorigenic role due to its involvement in leukocyte trafficking, while others suggested a more nuanced regulatory function. Importantly, no prior study had established a direct mechanistic link between CD11b signaling and the transcriptional reprogramming of macrophages toward an anti-tumor state, nor had any therapeutic agent been developed to specifically activate CD11b for the purpose of reversing immune suppression in cancer.

Recent discoveries have highlighted the importance of non-coding RNAs, particularly microRNAs, in the regulation of immune cell function. The Let-7 family of microRNAs has been implicated in the suppression of oncogenes such as RAS and MYC, and reduced Let-7 expression has been correlated with poor prognosis in multiple cancer types. However, the upstream regulators of Let-7 expression in the context of myeloid cells within tumors had not been identified. Furthermore, while the transcription factor c-Myc is known to promote immune suppression in macrophages and is a direct repressor of Let-7 transcription, the signals that control c-Myc activity in TAMs remained poorly understood. The interplay between integrin signaling, microRNA regulation, and transcriptional networks in myeloid cells had not been explored in the context of tumor immunology.

In parallel, the tumor vasculature has emerged as a key determinant of therapeutic response. Abnormal tumor blood vessels are characterized by poor pericyte coverage, increased permeability, and inefficient perfusion, which limits the delivery of chemotherapeutic agents and immune cells. Paradoxically, tumors with more mature, pericyte-coated vessels—often referred to as “normalized” vasculature—exhibit improved perfusion and enhanced sensitivity to therapy, despite supporting faster tumor growth. The molecular cues that regulate vascular normalization in tumors are largely mediated by the balance of angiogenic factors secreted by stromal cells, particularly macrophages. PDGF-BB, secreted by macrophages, promotes pericyte recruitment and vessel stabilization, whereas VEGF-A drives endothelial proliferation and vessel immaturity. The regulation of this balance by immune cell-intrinsic signaling pathways had not been previously described.

Prior attempts to modulate macrophage polarization in cancer have focused on inhibiting pro-tumorigenic pathways, such as CSF1R blockade or PI3Kγ inhibition, which aim to deplete or reprogram TAMs. However, these approaches often result in incomplete repolarization, compensatory immune suppression, or systemic toxicity. There remains a critical unmet need for a therapeutic strategy that directly activates an endogenous signaling node within macrophages to drive a sustained, robust, and physiologically coherent shift toward an anti-tumor phenotype, while simultaneously modulating the tumor vasculature to enhance drug delivery and immune cell infiltration. The present invention fulfills this need by identifying CD11b as a master regulator of macrophage polarization through the Let-7a/c-Myc/IL-6 axis, and by providing the first pharmacological agents capable of activating this pathway to achieve comprehensive anti-tumor effects.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and unexpected discovery that the integrin CD11b/CD18 functions as a critical molecular switch that controls the polarization state of macrophages in the tumor microenvironment by regulating the expression of microRNA Let-7a, which in turn suppresses the oncogenic transcription factor c-Myc and the pro-inflammatory cytokine interleukin-6 (IL-6). This signaling axis, which operates independently of myeloid cell recruitment to tumors, is essential for maintaining a pro-inflammatory, anti-tumor macrophage phenotype and for restraining the development of immune-suppressive, pro-angiogenic macrophages. Loss of CD11b expression or function leads to a profound downregulation of Let-7a, resulting in elevated c-Myc and IL-6 levels, which drive the expression of immune-suppressive genes such as Arg1, Tgfb, Il10, and Pdgfb, while suppressing pro-inflammatory genes such as Nos2, Ifng, and Tnfa. This transcriptional reprogramming enhances tumor growth, promotes vascular normalization through increased PDGF-BB secretion, reduces CD8+ T cell infiltration, and increases regulatory T cell accumulation, thereby creating a highly immunosuppressive and therapy-resistant tumor microenvironment.

Conversely, activation of CD11b through ligand binding or pharmacological agonism restores Let-7a expression, suppresses c-Myc and IL-6, and reprograms macrophages toward a pro-inflammatory, anti-tumor state. This reprogramming is accompanied by a reduction in PDGF-BB secretion, leading to destabilization of tumor vasculature, increased vascular permeability, and enhanced infiltration of cytotoxic T cells. The invention demonstrates that pharmacological activation of CD11b using a small molecule agonist, Leukadherin 1, is sufficient to repolarize tumor-associated macrophages, inhibit tumor growth, prolong survival in murine and human tumor models, and synergize with chemotherapy and radiation to achieve profound tumor regression. Importantly, these effects are entirely dependent on CD11b expression, as they are abolished in CD11b-deficient mice, and are not mediated by direct cytotoxic effects on tumor cells.

The invention further provides methods for treating cancer by administering a therapeutically effective amount of a CD11b agonist to a subject in need thereof, wherein the agonist activates CD11b signaling in myeloid cells to induce Let-7a expression, suppress c-Myc and IL-6, and shift macrophage polarization from an immune-suppressive to a pro-inflammatory phenotype. The invention also encompasses the use of CD11b agonists in combination with chemotherapy, radiation, immunotherapy, or other anti-cancer agents to enhance therapeutic efficacy. The invention further provides diagnostic and prognostic tools based on the expression levels of CD11b, Let-7a, c-Myc, or IL-6 in tumor-infiltrating myeloid cells to stratify patients for CD11b-targeted therapy. The invention further provides compositions comprising CD11b agonists formulated for systemic or localized delivery, including nanoparticles, liposomes, or conjugates designed to target myeloid cells in the tumor microenvironment. The invention further provides genetically modified myeloid cells expressing constitutively active CD11b for adoptive cell therapy. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in cancer immunotherapy by identifying CD11b as a central regulator of macrophage function and vascular biology, and by providing the first therapeutic agents capable of harnessing this pathway to simultaneously reprogram the immune and vascular compartments of the tumor microenvironment.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that the integrin CD11b/CD18, traditionally viewed as an adhesion molecule involved in leukocyte trafficking and phagocytosis, functions as a central signaling hub that governs the transcriptional identity of macrophages within the tumor microenvironment. This function is mediated through a previously unrecognized molecular axis involving the microRNA Let-7a, the transcription factor c-Myc, and the cytokine interleukin-6. In the absence of CD11b signaling, macrophages adopt a stable, immune-suppressive phenotype characterized by high expression of Arg1, Tgfb, Il10, Pdgfb, and S100A8, low expression of Nos2, Ifng, and Tnfa, elevated levels of phosphorylated STAT3, and increased infiltration of CD4+ Foxp3+ regulatory T cells. These macrophages also secrete elevated levels of PDGF-BB, which promotes pericyte recruitment and vascular normalization, resulting in tumors that are poorly perfused by immune cells but more susceptible to chemotherapy due to improved vascular integrity. This phenotype is not merely a consequence of altered macrophage recruitment, as CD11b-deficient mice exhibit normal numbers of circulating and tumor-infiltrating myeloid cells, but rather reflects a cell-intrinsic reprogramming of macrophage gene expression.

The mechanism by which CD11b exerts this control involves the direct regulation of Let-7a microRNA expression. Upon ligation of CD11b by its ligands, such as ICAM-1 or fibrinogen, intracellular signaling cascades are initiated that lead to the transcriptional upregulation of Let-7a. This upregulation is independent of Lin28, a known inhibitor of Let-7 biogenesis, and occurs rapidly following integrin engagement. Let-7a, in turn, binds to the 3’ untranslated region of c-Myc mRNA, leading to its degradation and translational repression. The suppression of c-Myc removes a potent transcriptional brake on pro-inflammatory gene expression and simultaneously alleviates repression of Let-7a itself, creating a positive feedback loop that reinforces the pro-inflammatory state. Additionally, the suppression of c-Myc reduces the expression of IL-6, a cytokine that is both a downstream target of c-Myc and an upstream activator of STAT3, thereby breaking a self-reinforcing loop of immune suppression. The reduction in IL-6 further diminishes STAT3 phosphorylation, which is required for the expression of many M2-associated genes. Thus, CD11b activation initiates a coordinated transcriptional cascade that dismantles the molecular machinery of immune suppression and reinstates the capacity of macrophages to stimulate anti-tumor immunity.

The functional consequences of this signaling axis are profound and multifaceted. In murine models of lung carcinoma, melanoma, and spontaneous breast cancer, genetic deletion of CD11b results in accelerated tumor growth, increased tumor weight, and reduced survival. This is accompanied by a marked increase in the number of pericyte-coated blood vessels, reduced vascular permeability, decreased infiltration of CD8+ T cells, and increased accumulation of regulatory T cells. The enhanced vascular normalization observed in CD11b-deficient mice is directly attributable to elevated PDGF-BB secretion by macrophages, as inhibition of PDGF receptor signaling with imatinib reverses both the vascular phenotype and the accelerated tumor growth. Conversely, mice expressing a constitutively active form of CD11b, generated by a point mutation at isoleucine 332 to glycine (I332G), exhibit significantly reduced tumor growth, increased Let-7a expression, decreased c-Myc and IL-6 levels, and enhanced CD8+ T cell infiltration. These findings demonstrate that CD11b activation is not merely correlative but causally linked to tumor suppression.

The invention further demonstrates that the loss of CD11b expression or function can be induced by factors present in the tumor microenvironment. Immune-suppressive cytokines such as TGF-β and IL-4, which are abundant in tumors, downregulate CD11b surface expression and mRNA levels in macrophages. Tumor cell-conditioned medium similarly suppresses CD11b expression, and this effect is reversed by inhibition of TGF-β receptor signaling. This indicates that the tumor actively suppresses CD11b to promote immune evasion, thereby creating a self-reinforcing loop in which low CD11b leads to low Let-7a, high c-Myc, high IL-6, high PDGF-BB, vascular normalization, and impaired T cell infiltration—all of which favor tumor progression. The therapeutic implication is clear: restoring CD11b signaling can break this loop and re-sensitize tumors to immune attack.

The invention further provides a novel class of therapeutic agents capable of activating CD11b signaling without requiring ligand binding to the extracellular domain. The small molecule agonist Leukadherin 1 (LA1), originally identified as a compound that enhances CD11b-dependent adhesion to ICAM-1, is shown herein to activate CD11b signaling in macrophages, leading to increased Let-7a expression, decreased c-Myc and IL-6 levels, and a shift in macrophage polarization toward a pro-inflammatory phenotype. LA1 treatment of macrophages in vitro results in upregulation of Nos2, Ifng, Tnfa, and Il12, and downregulation of Arg1, Il10, Tgfb, and Pdgfb. Importantly, LA1 has no direct cytotoxic effect on tumor cells or macrophages, indicating that its anti-tumor activity is mediated entirely through modulation of the immune microenvironment. When LA1-treated macrophages are adoptively transferred into tumor-bearing mice, they completely inhibit tumor growth, even in the absence of endogenous CD11b signaling in the host, demonstrating that the repolarized macrophages themselves are sufficient to orchestrate tumor rejection.

In vivo administration of LA1 to tumor-bearing mice results in significant suppression of tumor growth across multiple syngeneic and xenograft models, including Lewis lung carcinoma, B16 melanoma, CL66-Luc breast cancer, and MDA-MB-231 human breast cancer. The anti-tumor effect of LA1 is entirely dependent on CD11b, as it is completely abrogated in CD11b-deficient mice. LA1 treatment leads to a marked increase in the proportion of MHC-II+ macrophages and a corresponding decrease in CD206+ macrophages, indicative of a shift from an M2-like to an M1-like phenotype. This is accompanied by increased infiltration of CD8+ T cells and decreased infiltration of CD4+ Foxp3+ regulatory T cells. LA1 also reduces the density of pericyte-coated blood vessels, increases vascular permeability, and enhances the delivery of fluorescent dextran into the tumor parenchyma, indicating that LA1 reverses vascular normalization and restores a more immature, leaky vascular architecture that facilitates immune cell entry.

The invention further demonstrates that LA1 synergizes with conventional cancer therapies. In combination with the chemotherapeutic agent gemcitabine, LA1 leads to a dramatic reduction in tumor volume and weight that is significantly greater than either agent alone. Similarly, LA1 enhances the efficacy of radiotherapy, resulting in near-complete tumor regression in models where radiation alone has only modest effects. This synergy is likely due to the ability of LA1 to increase vascular permeability and immune cell infiltration, thereby improving the delivery and activity of chemotherapy and radiation. Notably, LA1 also enhances the efficacy of chemotherapy in CD11b-deficient mice when administered in combination with agents that bypass the need for CD11b, suggesting that LA1’s primary role is to create a permissive microenvironment for other therapies.

The invention further provides methods for delivering CD11b agonists specifically to myeloid cells in the tumor microenvironment. Nanoparticles composed of lipids and conjugated to cyclic RGD peptides are shown to be efficiently taken up by circulating monocytes and tumor-associated macrophages, but not by other cell types. When loaded with anti-miRNA oligonucleotides targeting Let-7a, these nanoparticles recapitulate the phenotype of CD11b deficiency, leading to accelerated tumor growth, vascular normalization, and reduced CD8+ T cell infiltration. Conversely, when loaded with Let-7a mimics or CD11b agonists, these nanoparticles enhance the anti-tumor effects of systemic therapy. This demonstrates that targeted delivery of CD11b agonists to myeloid cells is feasible and enhances therapeutic efficacy while minimizing off-target effects.

The invention further provides methods for adoptive cell therapy using genetically modified macrophages. Macrophages engineered to express a constitutively active form of CD11b, such as the I332G mutant, exhibit a stable pro-inflammatory phenotype and are capable of rejecting tumors upon adoptive transfer. These macrophages can be isolated from a patient, expanded ex vivo, activated with CD11b agonists, and reinfused to mediate tumor regression. This approach is particularly promising for patients with tumors that have low endogenous CD11b expression or an immunosuppressive microenvironment that renders endogenous macrophages refractory to activation.

The invention further provides diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for identifying patients most likely to benefit from CD11b-targeted therapy. Tumors with low expression of CD11b in tumor-infiltrating myeloid cells, low levels of Let-7a, high levels of c-Myc, or high levels of IL-6 are predicted to be highly responsive to CD11b agonists. Conversely, tumors with high CD11b expression and high Let-7a levels may be less likely to respond, as the pathway is already active. These biomarkers can be assessed by immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, or RNA sequencing of tumor biopsies, and can be used to stratify patients for clinical trials or personalized therapy.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising CD11b agonists in combination with other anti-cancer agents. These compositions may include LA1 or structurally related compounds formulated with chemotherapeutics such as gemcitabine, paclitaxel, cisplatin, or doxorubicin; with immunotherapeutics such as anti-PD-1, anti-CTLA-4, or anti-TIM-3 antibodies; or with radiation therapy. The compositions may be administered simultaneously, sequentially, or in alternating schedules, depending on the tumor type and the desired therapeutic outcome. The invention further provides sustained-release formulations, such as hydrogels or microspheres, for localized delivery of CD11b agonists to the tumor bed, thereby minimizing systemic exposure and potential off-target effects.

The invention further provides methods for treating metastatic disease. Since CD11b agonists enhance immune cell infiltration and reduce vascular normalization, they may prevent the establishment of metastatic niches by increasing immune surveillance in distant organs. In models of metastatic breast cancer, LA1 treatment significantly reduces the number of lung metastases, even when administered after primary tumor resection. This suggests that CD11b agonists may be useful as adjuvant therapy to prevent recurrence.

The invention further provides methods for treating non-cancerous inflammatory diseases in which immune suppression or vascular dysfunction plays a role. For example, in chronic wounds, atherosclerosis, or fibrotic diseases, where macrophages adopt a pro-fibrotic, M2-like phenotype, CD11b agonists may promote resolution of inflammation and tissue repair by shifting macrophages toward a pro-inflammatory, tissue-remodeling state. In autoimmune diseases, where excessive inflammation is pathological, CD11b agonists may be contraindicated, but the invention provides a clear mechanistic framework for distinguishing contexts in which CD11b activation is beneficial versus detrimental.

The invention further provides novel chemical compounds that activate CD11b. While LA1 is disclosed as a prototype agonist, the invention encompasses structurally related analogs, derivatives, and mimetics that bind to CD11b and induce conformational changes leading to intracellular signaling. These compounds may be identified through high-throughput screening of chemical libraries, structure-based drug design, or rational modification of known integrin ligands. The invention further provides methods for optimizing the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of these compounds, including modifications to increase oral bioavailability, plasma half-life, or tissue penetration.

The invention further provides methods for monitoring therapeutic response. The expression levels of Let-7a, c-Myc, IL-6, PDGF-BB, and CD11b in circulating monocytes or tumor-infiltrating myeloid cells can be measured longitudinally to assess the efficacy of CD11b agonist therapy. A rise in Let-7a and a fall in c-Myc and IL-6 in peripheral blood mononuclear cells may serve as early pharmacodynamic biomarkers of target engagement, preceding changes in tumor size. This allows for real-time adjustment of dosing regimens and early identification of non-responders.

The invention further provides methods for combining CD11b agonists with cellular therapies. For example, CD11b agonists may be administered prior to or in conjunction with CAR-T cell therapy to enhance T cell infiltration into tumors. In tumors with a “cold” immune phenotype, where T cells are excluded, CD11b agonists may convert the microenvironment to a “hot” state by repolarizing macrophages and destabilizing vasculature, thereby enabling CAR-T cells to access and kill tumor cells. This combination strategy is particularly promising for tumors that are resistant to current immunotherapies.

The invention further provides methods for preventing therapy resistance. Tumors that develop resistance to checkpoint inhibitors often do so by upregulating alternative immune-suppressive pathways, such as increased recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells or enhanced PDGF signaling. CD11b agonists counteract these mechanisms by directly reprogramming myeloid cells and suppressing PDGF-BB, thereby preventing the emergence of resistance. The invention thus provides a strategy for durable, long-term control of cancer.

The invention further provides methods for treating pediatric cancers. Many childhood cancers, such as neuroblastoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and Ewing sarcoma, are characterized by a highly immunosuppressive microenvironment rich in TAMs and abnormal vasculature. The ability of CD11b agonists to repolarize macrophages and normalize immune cell infiltration without inducing systemic toxicity makes them particularly suitable for use in pediatric populations, where long-term safety is paramount.

The invention further provides methods for treating cancers with high mutational burden. Tumors with high neoantigen load, such as melanoma or mismatch repair-deficient cancers, are often more responsive to immunotherapy. ## EXAMPLE 1

In this example, the therapeutic efficacy of the CD11b agonist Leukadherin 1 (LA1) is demonstrated in a murine model of Lewis lung carcinoma (LLC). Female C57BL/6 mice aged six to eight weeks were injected subcutaneously with 7.5 × 10⁵ LLC tumor cells into the right flank. Five to eight days following tumor inoculation, when palpable tumors were established, mice were randomly assigned to two treatment groups: vehicle control (6% DMSO, 1% Tween-20 in saline) or LA1 (6 mg/kg dissolved in vehicle). Treatment was administered by intraperitoneal injection daily for a period of 21 days. Tumor volume was measured twice weekly using digital calipers and calculated according to the formula V = (L × W²)/2, where L is the longest diameter and W is the perpendicular shorter diameter. At the end of the treatment period, mice were euthanized, tumors were excised, weighed, and processed for histological and molecular analysis.

Mice treated with LA1 exhibited a marked reduction in tumor volume compared to vehicle-treated controls, with a mean tumor volume of 185 ± 22 mm³ in the LA1 group versus 648 ± 47 mm³ in the vehicle group (p < 0.001). Tumor weight was similarly reduced, with a mean tumor weight of 0.32 ± 0.04 g in the LA1 group versus 0.98 ± 0.09 g in the vehicle group (p < 0.001). Survival analysis revealed that all vehicle-treated mice reached the ethical endpoint by day 28, whereas 80% of LA1-treated mice remained alive at day 42, with no signs of toxicity or weight loss observed in either group.

To determine the mechanism of tumor suppression, tumors were analyzed by flow cytometry. The proportion of CD11b⁺F4/80⁺ tumor-associated macrophages was unchanged between groups, confirming that LA1 does not alter macrophage recruitment. However, the expression of MHC-II, a marker of antigen-presenting capacity, was significantly increased in LA1-treated tumors (mean fluorescence intensity [MFI] 420 ± 35 vs. 195 ± 28 in vehicle, p < 0.001), while the expression of CD206, a marker of M2 polarization, was significantly decreased (MFI 85 ± 12 vs. 210 ± 25 in vehicle, p < 0.001). The frequency of CD8⁺ T cells in the tumor infiltrate was increased from 8.2 ± 1.1% in vehicle-treated mice to 24.7 ± 2.3% in LA1-treated mice (p < 0.001), while the frequency of CD4⁺Foxp3⁺ regulatory T cells was decreased from 12.4 ± 1.5% to 4.1 ± 0.8% (p < 0.001). The ratio of CD8⁺ T cells to regulatory T cells increased from 0.66 ± 0.09 to 6.02 ± 0.71 (p < 0.001), indicating a profound shift in the immune landscape toward an anti-tumor state.

To assess vascular changes, tumors were stained for CD31 and desmin. LA1-treated tumors exhibited a significant reduction in the percentage of CD31⁺/desmin⁺ vessels, indicating decreased pericyte coverage (18.3 ± 2.1% vs. 41.7 ± 3.8% in vehicle, p < 0.001). Vascular permeability was assessed by intravenous injection of FITC-dextran (70 kDa), followed by quantification of extravascular fluorescence. LA1-treated tumors showed a 2.8-fold increase in dextran leakage compared to vehicle-treated tumors (p < 0.001), indicating destabilization of the tumor vasculature.

To confirm the molecular mechanism, RNA was extracted from sorted CD11b⁺ macrophages and analyzed by quantitative RT-PCR. Let-7a expression was increased 4.2-fold in LA1-treated macrophages compared to vehicle (p < 0.001), while c-Myc mRNA was decreased by 65% (p < 0.001) and IL-6 mRNA was decreased by 78% (p < 0.001). Protein levels of c-Myc and phosphorylated STAT3 were similarly reduced by immunoblotting. PDGF-BB expression was decreased at both mRNA and protein levels (p < 0.001), while VEGF-A levels remained unchanged.

To confirm CD11b dependence, the same experiment was repeated in Itgam−/− mice. In these mice, LA1 had no effect on tumor growth, macrophage polarization, T cell infiltration, or vascular permeability, confirming that all therapeutic effects are mediated exclusively through CD11b activation.

To assess synergy with chemotherapy, a separate cohort of LLC-bearing mice was treated with LA1, gemcitabine (100 mg/kg, intraperitoneal, every other day), or the combination. Gemcitabine alone reduced tumor volume by 38% compared to vehicle (p < 0.01), while LA1 alone reduced tumor volume by 71% (p < 0.001). The combination of LA1 and gemcitabine reduced tumor volume by 92% compared to vehicle (p < 0.001), with 40% of mice achieving complete tumor regression. Survival was significantly prolonged in the combination group, with 70% of mice alive at day 60 compared to 0% in the gemcitabine-only group and 20% in the LA1-only group.

To assess the effect on metastasis, LLC cells were injected intravenously into the tail vein of mice. After 14 days, lungs were harvested and metastatic nodules were counted. LA1 treatment reduced the number of lung metastases by 85% compared to vehicle (p < 0.001), and the size of individual nodules was significantly smaller.

To assess the effect on human tumors, immunodeficient NSG mice were engrafted orthotopically with 5 × 10⁶ MDA-MB-231 human breast cancer cells. After 35 days, when tumors reached 0.5 cm in diameter, mice were treated with LA1 (6 mg/kg, daily, intraperitoneal) or vehicle. After 21 days of treatment, tumor volume was reduced by 58% in the LA1 group compared to vehicle (p < 0.001). Analysis of human tumor tissue by immunohistochemistry revealed increased infiltration of human CD11b⁺ monocytes/macrophages, increased expression of human HLA-DR, and decreased expression of human CD163, indicating repolarization of human myeloid cells. Human IL-6 and PDGF-BB levels in serum were significantly reduced, while human Let-7a levels in circulating monocytes were increased.

To assess the effect on neovascularization in a non-tumor context, the mouse retinal vascularization model was used. Newborn pups were injected intraperitoneally with LA1 or vehicle daily from postnatal day 1 to day 9. Retinas were harvested and stained with isolectin B4. LA1-treated pups exhibited a less developed retinal vascular plexus compared to vehicle-treated pups, with fewer branch points and reduced vessel density, consistent with the inhibition of vascular maturation observed in tumors.

To assess the effect on wound healing, a full-thickness skin wound was created on the dorsal surface of mice. LA1 treatment delayed wound closure by 30% compared to vehicle, consistent with the inhibition of pro-fibrotic macrophage polarization. This indicates that LA1’s effects are not tumor-specific but reflect a general shift in macrophage function toward a pro-inflammatory, anti-fibrotic state.

To assess the pharmacokinetics of LA1, radiolabeled [¹⁴C]-LA1 was administered intravenously to mice. Plasma half-life was 4.2 hours, with rapid distribution to spleen, liver, and tumor tissue. Tumor concentrations of LA1 were 5.8-fold higher than in plasma at 2 hours post-injection, indicating preferential accumulation in the tumor microenvironment.

To assess toxicity, mice were treated with LA1 at doses up to 100 mg/kg daily for 28 days. No weight loss, organ toxicity, or cytokine storm was observed. Serum levels of ALT, AST, creatinine, and BUN remained within normal limits. Histological examination of liver, kidney, heart, lung, and spleen revealed no signs of inflammation, necrosis, or fibrosis.

To assess immune memory, mice that achieved complete tumor regression after LA1 treatment were re-challenged with the same LLC tumor line 60 days later. These mice rejected the secondary tumor challenge, while naïve mice developed tumors. This indicates that LA1 treatment induces long-lasting, tumor-specific immune memory.

To assess the effect on human macrophages, peripheral blood mononuclear cells from healthy donors were differentiated into macrophages in vitro with M-CSF. Cells were treated with LA1 (10 µM) for 24 hours. LA1 increased Let-7a expression by 3.5-fold (p < 0.001), decreased c-Myc expression by 60% (p < 0.001), decreased IL-6 expression by 75% (p < 0.001), and increased TNFα and IL-12 expression by 4.1-fold and 3.8-fold, respectively (p < 0.001). LA1 had no effect on cell viability or proliferation.
