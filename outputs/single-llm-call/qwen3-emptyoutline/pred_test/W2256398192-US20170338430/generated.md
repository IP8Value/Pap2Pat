# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of optoelectronic devices, specifically to perovskite-based solar cells with enhanced power conversion efficiency and long-term operational stability. More particularly, the invention concerns a novel hole transport layer architecture comprising a triple-layered structure of n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD, deposited via vacuum evaporation to achieve precise control over energy level alignment, interfacial charge extraction, and environmental barrier properties. The invention further encompasses methods for fabricating such a multilayered hole transport layer through controlled vapor pressure modulation during the deposition of n-type dopants, enabling the tuning of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level of spiro-OMeTAD to match the valence band maximum of the underlying perovskite absorber. This precise energy level engineering minimizes interfacial energy barriers, reduces charge recombination losses, and enhances built-in potential gradients across the hole transport stack, thereby significantly improving both the initial performance and long-term durability of perovskite solar cells under ambient and accelerated aging conditions. The invention is particularly applicable to thin-film photovoltaic systems where environmental stability, interfacial defect passivation, and carrier extraction efficiency are critical limiting factors, and it enables the fabrication of high-performance devices without reliance on solution-processing techniques that introduce pinholes, inhomogeneities, or chemical instability. The resulting device architecture is compatible with scalable vacuum deposition processes and is suitable for integration into flexible, tandem, or indoor photovoltaic applications requiring robustness against moisture, oxygen, and ion migration.

## BACKGROUND ART

Perovskite solar cells have emerged as a leading candidate for next-generation photovoltaic technology due to their exceptional light absorption coefficients, tunable bandgaps, low-temperature processability, and rapidly increasing power conversion efficiencies exceeding twenty percent in laboratory settings. Despite these advantages, commercial viability remains hindered by persistent challenges related to environmental instability, interfacial degradation, and inefficient charge extraction at the hole transport layer–perovskite interface. Conventional hole transport materials, such as spiro-OMeTAD, are typically deposited via spin-coating from organic solvents, which often results in films containing pinholes, morphological inhomogeneities, and poor interfacial contact with the perovskite layer. These structural defects permit the ingress of atmospheric moisture and oxygen, which accelerate the decomposition of the perovskite crystal lattice, particularly in the presence of methylammonium or formamidinium cations. Additionally, the mobile ionic species within the perovskite layer can migrate through these defects, leading to hysteresis in current-voltage characteristics, irreversible polarization effects, and long-term performance decay. 

To mitigate these issues, researchers have attempted to enhance the hole transport layer through chemical doping, primarily using lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) in solution-based formulations. However, these dopants are hygroscopic and volatile, promoting moisture absorption and phase segregation over time. Furthermore, the doping process in solution is inherently uncontrolled, leading to spatial inhomogeneities in dopant concentration and inconsistent energy level alignment across the film. Alternative approaches have explored the use of alternative hole transport materials such as PTAA, P3HT, or inorganic alternatives like CuI and NiOx, but these materials often suffer from low hole mobility, poor energy level matching, or additional processing complexity. 

Vacuum deposition techniques have been proposed as a means to achieve pinhole-free, uniform, and chemically stable hole transport layers. However, prior art has not successfully demonstrated the controlled, sequential deposition of n-type, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layers using vapor-phase doping with precise tuning of dopant partial pressure. While vacuum evaporation of undoped spiro-OMeTAD has been reported, the incorporation of n-type dopants such as 2,3-dichloro-5,6-dicyano-1,4-benzoquinone (DMC) into spiro-OMeTAD via vapor-phase deposition has not been previously achieved with the level of precision required for optimal energy level alignment. Moreover, the concept of a triple-layered hole transport architecture—comprising an n-type doped layer for direct energy level matching with the perovskite valence band, an intrinsic layer as a diffusion barrier, and a p-type doped layer for efficient hole collection at the metal contact—has not been disclosed or demonstrated in any prior publication or patent. 

Previous attempts to improve stability have focused on encapsulation, interface passivation, or compositional engineering of the perovskite itself, but these strategies do not address the fundamental issue of interfacial charge extraction inefficiency arising from mismatched energy levels. For instance, the HOMO level of undoped spiro-OMeTAD lies approximately 1.5 eV below the Fermi level, while the valence band maximum of typical methylammonium lead iodide perovskites resides at approximately 2.6 eV below the Fermi level, resulting in a significant energy offset of over one electronvolt. This large barrier impedes efficient hole extraction, increases interfacial recombination, and lowers the open-circuit voltage. Conversely, p-type doping with F4-TCNQ shifts the HOMO level to approximately 0.3 eV below the Fermi level, which creates an overly small offset with the perovskite valence band, reducing the built-in potential and diminishing the driving force for carrier separation. No prior art has recognized or exploited the opportunity to bridge this gap through a graded, multi-layered doping profile that sequentially aligns the energy levels from the perovskite interface to the metal contact. 

Furthermore, the role of the intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer as a diffusion barrier between n-type and p-type dopants has not been previously described or utilized in any perovskite solar cell architecture. In conventional devices, dopants are uniformly distributed throughout the hole transport layer, leading to interdiffusion, phase separation, and long-term degradation. The concept of isolating dopants in discrete layers to prevent cross-contamination while simultaneously enabling controlled, post-fabrication doping through ambient exposure has not been disclosed. Additionally, the observation that air exposure can enhance device performance through gradual dopant redistribution and Fermi level pinning—particularly in devices with an intrinsic spacer layer—is entirely novel and has not been predicted or demonstrated in any prior work. 

The present invention overcomes these limitations by introducing a vacuum-evaporated, triple-layered hole transport structure composed of n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD, where each layer is deposited under precisely controlled conditions to optimize energy level alignment, minimize interfacial defects, and enhance environmental stability. The invention further introduces a method for tuning the HOMO level of the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer by varying the vapor pressure of DMC during evaporation, enabling the HOMO level to be systematically adjusted from 4.6 eV to 1.5 eV below the Fermi level, with an optimal value of 2.6 eV below the Fermi level corresponding to the valence band maximum of the perovskite. This precise matching eliminates the energy barrier for hole extraction, maximizes the built-in potential, and significantly enhances the open-circuit voltage and fill factor. The intrinsic layer, deposited immediately following the n-type layer, acts as a physical and chemical barrier that prevents the interdiffusion of DMC and F4-TCNQ dopants while simultaneously allowing for controlled, slow diffusion of ambient species such as oxygen and water vapor over time. This controlled diffusion leads to a gradual, self-healing doping effect that enhances conductivity and reduces series resistance, resulting in a counterintuitive increase in power conversion efficiency during prolonged ambient storage. The p-type doped layer, deposited in a separate chamber to avoid contamination, ensures low-resistance contact with the gold electrode and maintains a stable Fermi level near the work function of the metal. Together, these features create a synergistic architecture that simultaneously improves initial performance, suppresses degradation pathways, and enables self-repair mechanisms under operational conditions. No prior art discloses, suggests, or enables such a structure or its associated fabrication method, and the resulting improvements in both efficiency and stability represent a substantial and unexpected advancement in the field of perovskite photovoltaics.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel hole transport layer architecture for perovskite solar cells, comprising a triple-layered stack of n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD, intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD, each layer being deposited via vacuum evaporation with precise control over dopant concentration and film morphology. The n-type doped layer is formed by co-evaporating spiro-OMeTAD and a n-type dopant, specifically 2,3-dichloro-5,6-dicyano-1,4-benzoquinone (DMC), under a controlled vapor pressure ranging from 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ Torr to 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr, wherein the vapor pressure is optimized at 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr to achieve a highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level positioned at 2.6 electronvolts below the Fermi level, thereby aligning precisely with the valence band maximum of the underlying perovskite absorber. This alignment eliminates the energy barrier for hole extraction, maximizes the built-in potential across the device, and significantly enhances the open-circuit voltage and fill factor. The intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer, deposited immediately following the n-type layer without interruption, serves as a pinhole-free, amorphous, and chemically inert barrier that prevents the interdiffusion of the n-type and p-type dopants while simultaneously acting as a gas diffusion barrier that protects the perovskite layer from environmental degradation. The p-type doped layer, deposited in a separate vacuum chamber using co-evaporation of spiro-OMeTAD and 2,3,5,6-tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8-tetracyanoquinodimethane (F4-TCNQ) at a weight ratio of 2%, is engineered to provide low-resistance ohmic contact with the metal electrode and to maintain a stable HOMO level at 0.3 electronvolts below the Fermi level, ensuring efficient hole collection. 

The invention further encompasses a method for fabricating the triple-layered hole transport structure, wherein the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited at a rate of 0.5 Å per second under a base vacuum pressure of 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ Torr, with the DMC dopant introduced via a heated glass ampule containing solid DMC powder, wherein the vapor pressure is regulated by a precision leak valve to achieve the desired doping concentration. The intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited immediately thereafter without breaking vacuum, at the same deposition rate, to a thickness of 20 nanometers, ensuring seamless interfacial contact and complete coverage of the n-type layer. The p-type doped layer is then deposited in a dedicated vacuum chamber to prevent cross-contamination, using a co-evaporation system with F4-TCNQ at a rate of 0.1 Å per second and spiro-OMeTAD at 0.5 Å per second, yielding a uniform, pinhole-free film of 30 nanometers thickness. The entire structure is completed by the deposition of a 60-nanometer-thick gold contact layer via thermal evaporation. 

The resulting device architecture exhibits a staircase energy level profile across the hole transport stack, with the HOMO levels of the n-type, intrinsic, and p-type layers positioned at 2.6 eV, 1.5 eV, and 0.3 eV below the Fermi level, respectively, creating a continuous and favorable energy gradient that facilitates unimpeded hole transport from the perovskite absorber to the metal electrode. This energy level engineering minimizes interfacial recombination losses, reduces series resistance, and enhances charge extraction efficiency. Crucially, the intrinsic layer enables a self-healing mechanism whereby ambient oxygen and moisture molecules slowly diffuse through the amorphous spiro-OMeTAD matrix over time, inducing a gradual and uniform doping effect that increases the conductivity of the intrinsic layer and reduces its resistivity. This phenomenon results in a counterintuitive increase in power conversion efficiency during prolonged storage under ambient conditions, with devices exhibiting improved open-circuit voltage and fill factor after 300 hours of exposure, ultimately stabilizing at performance levels exceeding those of freshly fabricated control devices. 

The triple-layered structure also provides superior environmental stability compared to conventional spin-coated hole transport layers, as the vacuum-evaporated films are completely pinhole-free, as confirmed by atomic force microscopy and scanning electron microscopy, and exhibit a high glass transition temperature of 121 °C, which ensures structural integrity under thermal stress. The absence of hygroscopic dopants in the intrinsic layer and the physical isolation of the n-type and p-type dopants prevent phase segregation, chemical degradation, and ion migration, thereby preserving the integrity of the perovskite layer. Devices fabricated using this architecture demonstrate a power conversion efficiency of greater than 18% under standard illumination conditions and retain over 95% of their initial efficiency after 840 hours of storage in ambient air, whereas conventional reference devices degrade to less than 60% of their initial efficiency under identical conditions. The invention thus provides a robust, scalable, and commercially viable solution to the dual challenges of efficiency and stability in perovskite solar cells, enabling the fabrication of high-performance devices that maintain their performance under real-world environmental conditions without the need for encapsulation or additional protective layers. 

The invention further enables the use of simplified fabrication protocols, as the vacuum deposition process eliminates the need for solvent-based processing, post-annealing steps, or chemical additives that introduce instability. The method is compatible with roll-to-roll manufacturing, large-area deposition, and flexible substrate integration, making it suitable for industrial-scale production. The precise control over dopant concentration via vapor pressure modulation represents a fundamental advancement in thin-film doping technology, allowing for the fine-tuning of electronic properties without altering the chemical composition of the host material. The synergistic combination of energy level alignment, interfacial passivation, and self-healing doping mechanisms renders the invention a transformative innovation in the field of photovoltaics, providing a pathway to commercially viable, long-lasting, and high-efficiency perovskite solar cells.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a perovskite solar cell comprising a layered architecture that includes a transparent conductive oxide substrate, a compact electron transport layer, a perovskite absorber layer, and a novel triple-layered hole transport layer composed of n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD, intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD, sequentially deposited via vacuum evaporation under precisely controlled conditions. The invention further encompasses the method of fabricating this architecture, the materials used therein, and the resulting improvements in device performance and stability. The transparent conductive oxide substrate is preferably composed of fluorine-doped tin oxide, deposited on a glass substrate with a sheet resistance of approximately 7 ohms per square, and is cleaned using a sequential treatment with hydrochloric acid and zinc powder to remove surface contaminants and ensure optimal adhesion of subsequent layers. Upon this substrate, a compact layer of titanium dioxide is deposited via spray pyrolysis using a precursor solution containing titanium isopropoxide, acetylacetone, and anhydrous ethanol in a molar ratio of 3:3:2, followed by thermal annealing at 480 degrees Celsius for a duration sufficient to achieve complete crystallization and dense morphology. The resulting TiO₂ layer, with a thickness of approximately 100 nanometers, serves as an electron transport and blocking layer, preventing hole injection into the substrate while facilitating efficient electron extraction from the perovskite absorber.

The perovskite absorber layer is formed by dissolving methylammonium iodide and lead chloride in N,N-dimethylformamide at molar concentrations of 2.2 M and 0.88 M, respectively, to yield a 2.5:1 stoichiometric ratio of MAI to PbCl₂. This solution is spin-coated onto the TiO₂ layer at a rotational speed of 2000 revolutions per minute for a duration of 45 seconds, followed by thermal annealing on a hotplate at a temperature sufficient to induce complete crystallization into the desired perovskite phase, typically for 45 minutes under an inert atmosphere with oxygen and water vapor concentrations below 0.1 parts per million. The resulting perovskite film exhibits a cubic crystal structure, as confirmed by X-ray diffraction analysis, with distinct (110) and (220) peaks observed at diffraction angles of 14.2 degrees and 28.5 degrees, respectively, indicating high crystallinity and phase purity. The valence band maximum of this perovskite layer is determined to be located at 2.6 electronvolts below the Fermi level, as measured by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy under in situ conditions, establishing the target energy level for optimal hole extraction.

The hole transport layer is then fabricated by sequentially depositing three distinct layers of spiro-OMeTAD using vacuum evaporation under ultra-high vacuum conditions. The base pressure of the deposition chamber is maintained at 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ Torr to ensure minimal contamination and high film purity. The first layer, comprising n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD, is formed by co-evaporating spiro-OMeTAD and the n-type dopant 2,3-dichloro-5,6-dicyano-1,4-benzoquinone (DMC). The DMC dopant is introduced into the vacuum chamber via a sealed glass ampule containing solid DMC powder, which is heated using a precision heating tape to a temperature of approximately 100 degrees Celsius. The vapor pressure of DMC is regulated by a calibrated all-metal leak valve, allowing for fine control over the partial pressure of DMC in the chamber. The vapor pressure is systematically varied between 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ Torr and 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr to determine the optimal doping concentration for energy level alignment. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy measurements conducted in situ reveal that the HOMO level of the resulting n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer shifts in a continuous and reproducible manner with increasing DMC vapor pressure, ranging from 4.6 electronvolts below the Fermi level at the lowest pressure to 1.5 electronvolts below the Fermi level at the highest pressure. At a DMC vapor pressure of 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr, the HOMO level is precisely aligned with the valence band maximum of the perovskite absorber at 2.6 electronvolts below the Fermi level, thereby eliminating the energy barrier for hole extraction and maximizing the built-in potential across the interface. This optimal condition is maintained throughout the deposition process, with the n-type doped layer grown at a constant rate of 0.5 Å per second, as monitored by a quartz crystal microbalance, to a final thickness of 30 nanometers. The resulting film is amorphous, homogeneous, and free of pinholes, as confirmed by atomic force microscopy and scanning electron microscopy, ensuring complete coverage of the underlying perovskite surface and minimizing interfacial recombination sites.

Immediately following the deposition of the n-type doped layer, without breaking vacuum or exposing the surface to ambient conditions, the intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited using the same evaporation source but without the introduction of any dopant. This layer is grown to a thickness of 20 nanometers at the same deposition rate of 0.5 Å per second, ensuring seamless interfacial contact and a continuous, defect-free transition between the doped and undoped regions. The intrinsic layer serves multiple critical functions: it acts as a physical barrier that prevents the interdiffusion of the n-type dopant DMC and the p-type dopant F4-TCNQ, which are introduced in separate chambers; it functions as a gas diffusion barrier that impedes the ingress of atmospheric moisture and oxygen into the perovskite layer; and it provides a chemically inert medium that stabilizes the energy levels of the underlying layers. The HOMO level of the intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer is measured to be 1.5 electronvolts below the Fermi level, consistent with previously reported values for undoped spiro-OMeTAD, and this value remains stable throughout the fabrication process. The amorphous nature of the spiro-OMeTAD matrix, combined with its high glass transition temperature of 121 degrees Celsius, ensures that the intrinsic layer remains structurally intact under thermal and mechanical stress, further enhancing the durability of the device.

Following the deposition of the intrinsic layer, the substrate is transferred to a second, dedicated vacuum chamber to prevent cross-contamination between dopants. In this chamber, the p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited via co-evaporation of spiro-OMeTAD and 2,3,5,6-tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8-tetracyanoquinodimethane (F4-TCNQ) at a weight ratio of 2%. The F4-TCNQ is introduced into the chamber using a separate effusion cell, and its deposition rate is precisely controlled at 0.1 Å per second, while the spiro-OMeTAD is evaporated at a rate of 0.5 Å per second, resulting in a uniform and stoichiometrically controlled film. The resulting p-type doped layer exhibits a HOMO level positioned at 0.3 electronvolts below the Fermi level, as confirmed by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy, which corresponds to the expected energy level shift induced by strong p-type doping. This layer is grown to a thickness of 30 nanometers, providing sufficient conductivity to form an ohmic contact with the subsequent metal electrode while maintaining optical transparency and minimizing parasitic absorption. The deposition is carried out under the same ultra-high vacuum conditions to preserve film purity, and the resulting film is again found to be pinhole-free and homogeneous, as verified by atomic force microscopy and scanning electron microscopy.

Upon completion of the triple-layered hole transport structure, a 60-nanometer-thick gold contact layer is deposited via thermal evaporation to serve as the top electrode. The entire device structure, comprising fluorine-doped tin oxide / compact titanium dioxide / perovskite absorber / n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD / intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD / p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD / gold, is then subjected to electrical characterization under standard illumination conditions of 100 milliwatts per square centimeter at AM1.5G spectrum. The resulting devices exhibit a power conversion efficiency exceeding 18%, with open-circuit voltages greater than 1.1 volts, short-circuit current densities above 22 milliamperes per square centimeter, and fill factors above 75%. These values represent a significant improvement over conventional reference devices fabricated using spin-coated spiro-OMeTAD doped with Li-TFSI and tBP, which typically achieve efficiencies below 15% under identical conditions. The enhancement in performance is attributed to the precise energy level alignment achieved by the n-type doped layer, the suppression of recombination losses due to the pinhole-free morphology of all three layers, and the efficient hole extraction facilitated by the continuous energy gradient across the triple-layered stack.

In addition to the enhanced initial performance, the devices fabricated according to this invention demonstrate unprecedented long-term stability under ambient conditions. When stored in air at room temperature and relative humidity of approximately 50%, the power conversion efficiency of the triple-layered devices increases over the first 300 hours of exposure, reaching values higher than those measured immediately after fabrication. This counterintuitive improvement is accompanied by increases in both open-circuit voltage and fill factor, while the short-circuit current density remains constant, indicating that the perovskite absorber remains intact and that the enhancement is primarily due to improved charge transport and reduced series resistance. This phenomenon is attributed to the slow diffusion of ambient oxygen and moisture molecules through the intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer, which induces a gradual and uniform doping effect within the intrinsic layer itself, effectively converting it into a lightly doped conductive medium. This self-doping process reduces the resistivity of the intrinsic layer over time, enhances the overall conductivity of the hole transport stack, and improves the contact between the p-type layer and the metal electrode. The absence of hygroscopic dopants in the intrinsic layer prevents the formation of corrosive byproducts, and the physical isolation of the n-type and p-type dopants prevents phase segregation and chemical degradation. In contrast, reference devices fabricated using spin-coated spiro-OMeTAD exhibit a rapid decline in performance, with power conversion efficiency dropping to less than 50% after 300 hours of ambient storage, primarily due to perovskite decomposition and increased series resistance caused by pinhole-induced ion migration and moisture penetration.

The stability of the devices is further confirmed under high-vacuum conditions, where the efficiency of the triple-layered devices remains stable over 840 hours of testing, with no measurable degradation. This demonstrates that the intrinsic layer not only protects against environmental degradation but also provides long-term structural and chemical stability even in the absence of ambient species. The amorphous nature of the spiro-OMeTAD films, combined with their high glass transition temperature, ensures that the layers do not crystallize, phase-separate, or delaminate under prolonged thermal stress. The use of vacuum evaporation eliminates the need for organic solvents, post-deposition annealing, or chemical additives that are known to contribute to instability, resulting in a simpler, more reproducible, and industrially scalable fabrication process. The entire device architecture is compatible with large-area deposition, roll-to-roll manufacturing, and flexible substrate integration, making it suitable for commercial applications in building-integrated photovoltaics, portable electronics, and indoor energy harvesting systems. The invention thus represents a fundamental advancement in perovskite solar cell technology, combining precise energy level engineering, defect-free film morphology, and self-healing doping mechanisms to achieve simultaneous improvements in efficiency, stability, and manufacturability that have not been previously possible. The method of fabricating the triple-layered hole transport structure through controlled vapor pressure modulation of n-type dopants constitutes a novel and non-obvious contribution to the field, enabling the fine-tuning of electronic properties without altering the chemical composition of the host material and providing a pathway to commercially viable, high-performance perovskite photovoltaics.
