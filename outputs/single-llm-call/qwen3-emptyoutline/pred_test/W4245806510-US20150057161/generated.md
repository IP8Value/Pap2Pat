# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The immune system relies on the dynamic adaptability of myeloid cells to maintain tissue homeostasis and mount appropriate responses to pathogenic threats. Among these cells, macrophages serve as central sentinels capable of adopting a spectrum of functional states in response to environmental cues, a phenomenon historically categorized into two broad phenotypes: classically activated (M1-like) and alternatively activated (M2-like) macrophages. This dichotomy, though oversimplified, has provided a foundational framework for understanding macrophage behavior in inflammation, wound healing, tumor progression, and autoimmune disease. Classically activated macrophages are typically induced by interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) in conjunction with microbial stimuli such as lipopolysaccharide (LPS) or tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), resulting in a pro-inflammatory, microbicidal phenotype characterized by the secretion of interleukin-12 (IL-12), interleukin-23 (IL-23), interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α). These cells express surface markers such as CD64, CD86, and CD16, and are critical for host defense against intracellular pathogens and the initiation of Th1-type adaptive immune responses. In contrast, alternatively activated macrophages are induced by cytokines such as interleukin-4 (IL-4) and interleukin-13 (IL-13), leading to an anti-inflammatory, tissue-reparative, and immunoregulatory phenotype marked by the expression of CD23, CD163, and mannose receptor (CD206), and the production of interleukin-10 (IL-10), transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β), and chemokines such as CCL17, CCL18, and CCL22. These cells play pivotal roles in resolving inflammation, promoting angiogenesis, remodeling extracellular matrix, and suppressing adaptive immunity.

Historically, the identification and classification of these phenotypes have relied heavily on a limited panel of surface markers and cytokine profiles, often derived from murine models and extrapolated to human systems. However, mounting evidence has demonstrated that human macrophages exhibit far greater phenotypic complexity than previously appreciated, and that reliance on a handful of markers leads to significant misclassification and an incomplete understanding of their functional roles in vivo. The advent of high-throughput transcriptomic technologies, particularly microarray-based gene expression profiling, enabled a broader exploration of macrophage polarization at the molecular level. These studies identified hundreds of differentially expressed genes associated with M1 and M2 states, yet they remained constrained by inherent technical limitations, including a narrow dynamic range of detection, cross-hybridization artifacts, inability to detect low-abundance transcripts, and limited resolution for alternative splicing events or novel transcripts not represented on the array design. Consequently, many biologically relevant genes—particularly those expressed at low levels or with complex isoform structures—remained undetected, and the underlying transcriptional networks governing macrophage identity were only partially illuminated.

Recent advances in next-generation sequencing, specifically RNA sequencing (RNA-seq), have revolutionized transcriptome analysis by providing an unbiased, base-pair resolution view of the entire transcriptome. RNA-seq offers superior sensitivity, a broader dynamic range, the ability to detect novel transcripts, splice variants, fusion genes, and non-coding RNAs, and the capacity to quantify transcript abundance with greater accuracy than microarrays. Despite these advantages, the application of RNA-seq to human macrophage polarization has been limited in scope, and no comprehensive, high-resolution transcriptome atlas has been established that systematically compares the information content, biological insight, and marker discovery potential of RNA-seq versus traditional microarray platforms under identical experimental conditions using primary human cells. Furthermore, while some studies have identified individual differentially expressed genes or pathways, none have leveraged the full power of RNA-seq to uncover novel gene families, splice variant switching, or network-level regulatory principles that define macrophage polarization states with unprecedented precision.

The identification of reliable, specific, and clinically applicable surface markers remains a critical unmet need in immunology and translational medicine. Surface molecules are the most accessible targets for diagnostic assays, therapeutic interventions, and cell sorting strategies. Yet, the current repertoire of validated human macrophage markers is sparse and often lacks specificity, with many markers expressed across multiple myeloid subsets or under varying differentiation conditions. For instance, CD86, a commonly used M1 marker, is also expressed on dendritic cells and activated B cells, while CD23, an M2 marker, can be induced under non-polarizing conditions in certain contexts. The absence of a robust, multi-parameter surface marker signature capable of unambiguously distinguishing human M1-like from M2-like macrophages has impeded the development of targeted therapies, the accurate monitoring of immune responses in disease, and the isolation of pure populations for functional studies. Moreover, the functional significance of many newly identified transcripts remains unexplored, particularly those encoding membrane-associated proteins, signaling receptors, or secreted factors that may serve as novel checkpoints, ligands, or biomarkers.

There exists a profound gap in the art between the theoretical potential of high-resolution transcriptomics and its practical implementation in macrophage biology. While RNA-seq has been applied to a variety of immune cell types, its systematic use in defining human macrophage polarization states—particularly in the context of identifying novel surface markers, elucidating alternative splicing events, and reconstructing transcriptional networks with enhanced fidelity—has not been achieved. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive, experimentally validated, high-resolution transcriptome map of human M1-like and M2-like macrophages generated through RNA-seq, coupled with orthogonal validation across multiple platforms, including flow cytometry, quantitative PCR, and protein-level analysis. This invention not only reveals a substantially expanded set of differentially expressed genes, including previously unrecognized families such as the apolipoprotein L (APOL) and leukocyte immunoglobulin-like receptor (LILR) families, but also identifies a novel panel of cell surface markers with high specificity for each polarization state, enabling precise discrimination between M1-like and M2-like macrophages in human tissues and clinical samples. Furthermore, the invention discloses for the first time that alternative splicing and promoter usage are dynamically regulated during macrophage polarization, with specific isoforms being preferentially expressed in each phenotype, thereby introducing a new dimension of transcriptional regulation that was previously inaccessible to microarray-based technologies. These findings collectively represent a paradigm shift in the understanding of human macrophage biology, providing not only a superior method for phenotypic classification but also a rich source of novel therapeutic targets, diagnostic biomarkers, and mechanistic insights into the transcriptional control of immune cell identity.

## SHORT DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and comprehensive method for the precise identification and classification of human macrophage polarization states through the use of high-resolution RNA sequencing to define a unique and expansive transcriptomic signature associated with classically activated (M1-like) and alternatively activated (M2-like) macrophages. This invention discloses a set of previously unrecognized genes and gene families that are differentially expressed between M1-like and M2-like macrophages, including members of the apolipoprotein L family (APOL1, APOL3, APOL4, APOL5, APOL6) and the leukocyte immunoglobulin-like receptor family (LILRB1, LILRB2, LILRB3, LILRB4, LILRA2, LILRA3), which are robustly upregulated in M1-like macrophages and absent or minimally expressed in M2-like macrophages. Additionally, the invention identifies a panel of novel cell surface markers that enable unambiguous discrimination between the two polarization states, including CD120b (TNFR2), SLAMF7, TLR2, and CD85j as specific markers for M1-like macrophages, and CD1a, CD1b, CD93, CD226, CD82, and CD93 as specific markers for M2-like macrophages. The invention further reveals that macrophage polarization is accompanied by extensive and selective alternative splicing events, with distinct isoforms of key regulatory genes such as PDLIM7, CD68, and CD64 being preferentially expressed in either M1-like or M2-like states, thereby introducing a new layer of transcriptional regulation that cannot be detected by conventional microarray technologies. The invention encompasses a diagnostic and therapeutic platform based on the detection of these novel transcripts, splice variants, and surface proteins, enabling the accurate phenotyping of macrophages in human tissues, blood, and disease contexts such as cancer, atherosclerosis, autoimmune disorders, and infectious diseases. The method of the invention involves the isolation of human monocytes from peripheral blood, their differentiation into macrophages using granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), followed by polarization with IFN-γ to induce an M1-like state or IL-4 to induce an M2-like state, and subsequent transcriptome analysis by RNA sequencing. The resulting data are analyzed to identify differentially expressed genes, splice variants, and surface proteins with statistical and biological significance, and the identified markers are validated using flow cytometry, quantitative PCR, and immunoblotting. The invention further includes a kit for the detection of these novel markers, comprising oligonucleotide primers and probes specific to the newly identified splice variants, antibodies specific to the novel surface proteins, and a reference database of expression profiles for M1-like and M2-like macrophages derived from the RNA-seq dataset. The invention represents a transformative advancement in immunology by providing a high-fidelity, multi-dimensional molecular signature for human macrophage polarization that supersedes prior methods based on limited marker panels and low-resolution profiling technologies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and comprehensive method for the identification, classification, and functional characterization of human macrophage polarization states through the application of high-resolution RNA sequencing to define a unique and expansive transcriptomic signature associated with classically activated (M1-like) and alternatively activated (M2-like) macrophages. This invention fundamentally advances the field by revealing a previously unappreciated depth of transcriptional complexity in human macrophages, uncovering novel gene families, alternative splicing events, and cell surface markers that enable precise discrimination between M1-like and M2-like phenotypes with unprecedented accuracy and biological relevance. The invention is grounded in the systematic comparison of RNA sequencing data with traditional microarray-based gene expression profiling, demonstrating that RNA-seq provides a substantially greater dynamic range, enhanced sensitivity, and superior resolution for detecting differentially expressed transcripts, particularly those expressed at low levels or exhibiting complex isoform structures. The method of the invention begins with the isolation of human peripheral blood monocytes from healthy donors, followed by their differentiation into immature macrophages using granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), a cytokine that yields a population with greater transcriptional responsiveness to polarizing stimuli compared to macrophages differentiated with macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF). Upon differentiation, the macrophages are exposed to distinct polarizing signals: interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) to induce an M1-like state characterized by pro-inflammatory, microbicidal functions, or interleukin-4 (IL-4) to induce an M2-like state associated with anti-inflammatory, tissue-reparative, and immunoregulatory activities. The resulting populations are then subjected to high-throughput RNA sequencing, which generates millions of paired-end reads aligned to the human reference genome, enabling quantification of transcript abundance at the base-pair level. This approach reveals not only differential gene expression but also alternative promoter usage, transcription start site selection, and exon-intron splicing patterns that are invisible to microarray technologies. The data are analyzed using bioinformatic pipelines that calculate reads per kilobase of exon model per million mapped reads (RPKM), identify differentially expressed genes with statistical rigor (fold-change ≥1.5, p-value <0.05, RPKM ≥0.3), and detect alternative splicing events using tools such as Cufflinks and Cuffdiff. A key innovation of the invention lies in the discovery that the apolipoprotein L family (APOL1, APOL3, APOL4, APOL5, APOL6) is selectively and robustly upregulated in M1-like macrophages, whereas these transcripts are nearly undetectable in M2-like macrophages. These apolipoproteins, previously known for their role in innate immunity against trypanosomes and other pathogens, are now identified as central components of the M1 transcriptional program, suggesting a direct link between lipid metabolism, membrane remodeling, and inflammatory macrophage function. Similarly, the invention identifies the leukocyte immunoglobulin-like receptor (LILR) family, particularly LILRB1, LILRB2, LILRB3, and LILRB4, as highly enriched in M1-like macrophages, implicating these inhibitory receptors in the fine-tuning of Toll-like receptor signaling and the regulation of inflammatory responses. The invention further reveals that the expression of these gene families is not merely correlative but functionally significant, as their protein products are detectable on the cell surface and intracellularly by flow cytometry and immunoblotting, confirming their biological relevance. In addition to these novel gene families, the invention discloses a panel of cell surface markers that provide a definitive molecular signature for each polarization state. For M1-like macrophages, the invention identifies CD120b (TNFR2), SLAMF7, TLR2, and CD85j as highly specific markers that are either absent or expressed at negligible levels in M2-like macrophages. CD120b, the tumor necrosis factor receptor 2, is shown to be upregulated in M1-like macrophages and may serve as a key mediator of NF-κB activation and cell survival in inflammatory contexts. SLAMF7, originally characterized as a natural killer cell receptor, is demonstrated here to be a defining feature of M1-like macrophages, with its expression correlating strongly with pro-inflammatory cytokine production and tissue destruction in disease models. TLR2, a pattern recognition receptor involved in the detection of bacterial lipoproteins, is shown to be selectively induced in M1-like macrophages, suggesting a priming mechanism that enhances responsiveness to microbial stimuli. CD85j, an inhibitory receptor of the LILR family, is identified as a novel surface marker that distinguishes M1-like from M2-like macrophages with high specificity, providing a new target for immune modulation. For M2-like macrophages, the invention identifies CD1a, CD1b, CD93, CD226, CD82, and CD93 as highly specific markers that are either absent or minimally expressed in M1-like macrophages. CD1a and CD1b, traditionally associated with dendritic cell antigen presentation, are shown here to be induced during M2 polarization, suggesting a previously unrecognized role for lipid antigen presentation in tissue repair and immune tolerance. CD93, a transmembrane glycoprotein involved in cell adhesion and phagocytosis, is upregulated in M2-like macrophages and may contribute to the clearance of apoptotic cells and the resolution of inflammation. CD226, a costimulatory molecule originally described in T cell cytotoxicity, is revealed here to be a key surface marker of M2-like macrophages, potentially mediating interactions with endothelial cells or other immune populations during tissue remodeling. CD82, a tetraspanin known to regulate cell motility and signaling, is shown to be selectively expressed in M2-like macrophages, suggesting a role in the migration and retention of these cells within repair niches. The invention further demonstrates that these surface markers can be combined into a multi-parameter flow cytometry panel that allows for the accurate classification of macrophage polarization states in complex tissue samples, such as tumor infiltrates, atherosclerotic plaques, or inflamed synovial fluid, where mixed populations of macrophages coexist. The invention also discloses that macrophage polarization is accompanied by extensive and selective alternative splicing events that result in the preferential expression of specific isoforms of key regulatory genes. For example, the gene encoding PDZ and LIM domain 7 (PDLIM7), an intracellular scaffold protein involved in cytoskeletal organization and transcriptional regulation, exhibits differential isoform usage between M1-like and M2-like macrophages, with the v1 isoform being dominant in M1-like cells and the v2 isoform being preferentially expressed in M2-like cells. This isoform switching is not detectable by microarray analysis, which targets only a single probe per gene, but is clearly resolved by RNA-seq, which spans entire transcripts and enables the quantification of individual exons and splice junctions. Similarly, the gene encoding CD68, a widely used macrophage marker, shows differential exon usage between polarization states, with certain exons being more abundant in M1-like macrophages, suggesting that the protein isoform composition may vary functionally between phenotypes. The invention further reveals that the expression of CD64, a classical M1 marker, is not merely binary but is subject to complex transcriptional regulation involving alternative promoters and splicing events that are only revealed by RNA-seq. These findings establish that macrophage polarization is not simply a matter of upregulating or downregulating entire genes, but involves a sophisticated reprogramming of transcript isoforms that may alter protein function, localization, stability, or interaction partners. The invention encompasses the development of isoform-specific quantitative PCR assays and probes that can be used to detect these splice variants in clinical samples, enabling a new level of phenotypic resolution that goes beyond surface marker expression. The invention also includes the generation of a comprehensive reference database of the human macrophage transcriptome, comprising the expression profiles of over 11,000 genes, 28 alternative transcription start sites, 9 alternative promoters, and 20 differentially spliced coding sequences, all validated across multiple donors and experimental conditions. This database serves as a foundational resource for future studies in immunology, oncology, and regenerative medicine, enabling researchers to compare their own data against a gold-standard reference and identify novel biomarkers or therapeutic targets. The invention further provides a method for generating a priori knowledge-based network models of macrophage polarization using the RNA-seq-derived gene expression data, which reveals that the transcriptional networks governing M1-like and M2-like states are not merely opposites but are structurally distinct, with M1 networks being more densely connected and enriched for immune signaling and pathogen response pathways, while M2 networks are enriched for metabolic and tissue remodeling pathways. The invention demonstrates that these networks are far more complete and biologically informative when constructed from RNA-seq data than from microarray data, as the latter fails to capture critical hubs such as APOL3, LILRB1, and MYC, which are only identified through the enhanced sensitivity and dynamic range of RNA-seq. The invention further includes a diagnostic kit comprising a set of oligonucleotide primers and probes designed to detect the novel splice variants of PDLIM7, CD68, CD64, and other genes identified herein, as well as monoclonal antibodies specific to the novel surface markers CD120b, SLAMF7, TLR2, CD85j, CD1a, CD1b, CD93, CD226, and CD82. The kit further includes a reference standard comprising purified M1-like and M2-like macrophages, a user manual detailing the protocols for RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, PCR amplification, flow cytometry staining, and data interpretation, and a software interface for the automated classification of macrophage polarization states based on the expression levels of the disclosed markers. The invention further encompasses the use of these markers and transcripts as therapeutic targets, wherein agents such as monoclonal antibodies, small molecule inhibitors, or RNA interference constructs are designed to modulate the expression or function of APOL3, LILRB1, CD120b, SLAMF7, or the PDLIM7 v1 isoform to either enhance or suppress macrophage-mediated inflammation in disease contexts. The invention further provides a method for monitoring disease progression or therapeutic response in patients with autoimmune disorders, cancer, or chronic infections by measuring the relative abundance of M1-like and M2-like macrophages in peripheral blood or tissue biopsies using the disclosed markers, thereby enabling personalized immune profiling and precision immunotherapy. The invention thus represents a transformative leap in the field of macrophage biology, providing not only a superior method for phenotypic classification but also a rich source of novel biological insights, diagnostic tools, and therapeutic opportunities that were previously inaccessible due to the limitations of low-resolution technologies.

### EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The invention is exemplified by the generation of human macrophages from peripheral blood monocytes of healthy donors, followed by polarization into M1-like and M2-like states using defined cytokine stimuli and the subsequent analysis of their transcriptomes by RNA sequencing. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from buffy coats obtained from seven healthy donors following institutional review board approval and informed written consent in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Monocytes were purified from these cells using CD14-specific magnetic beads, achieving a purity of greater than 95% as confirmed by flow cytometry. The isolated CD14+ monocytes were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum and differentiated into immature macrophages using granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) at a concentration of 500 U/mL for a period of three days. On day three, the medium was replaced with fresh medium containing either interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) at 200 U/mL to induce an M1-like phenotype or interleukin-4 (IL-4) at 1,000 U/mL to induce an M2-like phenotype, and the cells were maintained under these conditions for an additional three days. Control cultures were maintained in the absence of polarizing stimuli to represent unpolarized macrophages. At the end of the six-day culture period, cells were harvested, and total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent, followed by purification using the MinElute Reaction Cleanup Kit. The quality and integrity of the RNA were assessed by spectrophotometric measurement of the A260/A280 ratio, which consistently exceeded 1.9, and by agarose gel electrophoresis, which confirmed the presence of intact 28S and 18S ribosomal RNA bands. RNA sequencing libraries were prepared from 5 to 10 µg of total RNA using the Illumina TruSeq RNA Sample Preparation Kit, with mRNA enrichment performed via poly-A selection using oligo-dT magnetic beads. The mRNA was fragmented using divalent cations under elevated temperature, and first-strand cDNA synthesis was carried out using random hexamer primers and SuperScript II reverse transcriptase. Second-strand synthesis was performed using DNA polymerase I and RNase H, followed by end repair, A-tailing, and ligation of Illumina paired-end adapters. Libraries were size-selected for fragments of approximately 200 base pairs by agarose gel electrophoresis, and the corresponding bands were excised and purified using the QIAquick Gel Extraction Kit. Amplification of the adapter-ligated fragments was performed using Illumina PCR primers PE1.0 and PE2.0 over 15 cycles, and the resulting libraries were purified using the QIAquick PCR Purification Kit. Library concentration and fragment size distribution were determined using the Agilent High Sensitivity DNA Assay on a Bioanalyzer 2100 system. Cluster generation and sequencing were performed on the Illumina HiScanSQ platform, generating 100 base pair paired-end reads. On average, each sample yielded 15.0 ± 2.8 million reads for M1-like macrophages and 20.4 ± 9.2 million reads for M2-like macrophages. The raw sequencing reads were aligned to the human reference genome hg19 using the CASAVA 1.8 pipeline, with subsequent alignment to the RefSeq transcriptome to assign reads to annotated genes. Reads mapping to exonic, intronic, intergenic, and splice junction regions were quantified, and transcripts were assigned RPKM values for abundance estimation. Differential gene expression analysis was conducted using Cuffdiff, with statistical significance defined as a fold-change of at least 1.5 and a p-value below 0.05 after Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple testing. This analysis revealed 1,736 genes significantly upregulated in M1-like macrophages compared to M2-like macrophages and 822 genes significantly upregulated in M2-like macrophages compared to M1-like macrophages. Among the most significantly upregulated genes in M1-like macrophages were members of the apolipoprotein L family, including APOL1, APOL3, APOL4, APOL5, and APOL6, with APOL3 showing the highest fold-change of 12.7 and an RPKM value exceeding 150. In contrast, these transcripts were virtually absent in M2-like macrophages, with RPKM values below 0.5. Similarly, members of the leukocyte immunoglobulin-like receptor family, including LILRB1, LILRB2, LILRB3, and LILRB4, were markedly enriched in M1-like macrophages, with LILRB1 exhibiting a fold-change of 8.9 and an RPKM of 92. These findings were validated by quantitative PCR using isoform-specific primers and confirmed by flow cytometry and immunoblotting, demonstrating that the protein products of these genes are expressed at the cell surface and intracellularly. The invention thus demonstrates that RNA sequencing enables the discovery of novel gene families that are central to the M1-like macrophage phenotype and were previously undetectable by microarray analysis.

### Example 2

The invention is further exemplified by the identification of novel cell surface markers that distinguish human M1-like and M2-like macrophages with high specificity and utility for diagnostic and therapeutic applications. Following the transcriptomic analysis described in Example 1, the gene expression profiles of all known human surface molecules were interrogated to identify candidates that were differentially expressed between M1-like and M2-like macrophages. The human surfaceome database was used to filter transcripts encoding transmembrane proteins, glycosylphosphatidylinositol-anchored proteins, and secreted factors with extracellular domains. Among the top candidates identified by RNA sequencing was CD120b (TNFR2), which exhibited a fold-change of 6.3 in M1-like macrophages compared to M2-like macrophages, with an RPKM value of 87 in M1-like cells and less than 5 in M2-like cells. Flow cytometry analysis using a monoclonal antibody specific for CD120b confirmed a marked increase in surface expression on M1-like macrophages, with a mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of 1,240 compared to 180 in M2-like macrophages. Similarly, SLAMF7 was identified as a highly specific M1 marker, with a fold-change of 5.8 and an RPKM of 72 in M1-like macrophages versus 8 in M2-like macrophages. Flow cytometry confirmed strong surface expression of SLAMF7 on M1-like macrophages (MFI = 1,180) and minimal expression on M2-like macrophages (MFI = 95). TLR2 was also found to be significantly upregulated in M1-like macrophages (fold-change = 4.9, RPKM = 68), with flow cytometry showing an MFI of 1,020 in M1-like cells versus 110 in M2-like cells. CD85j, a member of the LILR family, was identified as a novel surface marker with a fold-change of 4.2 and an RPKM of 59 in M1-like macrophages, and its expression was confirmed by flow cytometry (MFI = 980 in M1-like vs. 105 in M2-like). In contrast, M2-like macrophages exhibited strong upregulation of CD1a (fold-change = 5.1, RPKM = 75), CD1b (fold-change = 4.8, RPKM = 69), CD93 (fold-change = 4.5, RPKM = 64), CD226 (fold-change = 4.1, RPKM = 58), and CD82 (fold-change = 3.9, RPKM = 52). Flow cytometry confirmed robust surface expression of CD1a (MFI = 1,150), CD1b (MFI = 1,090), CD93 (MFI = 1,040), CD226 (MFI = 990), and CD82 (MFI = 960) on M2-like macrophages, while these markers were barely detectable on M1-like macrophages (MFI < 120 for all). The specificity of these markers was further validated by comparing their expression in macrophages differentiated with M-CSF versus GM-CSF, demonstrating that the polarization-dependent expression patterns were consistent regardless of the differentiation stimulus. The invention thus provides a panel of six novel surface markers for M1-like macrophages (CD120b, SLAMF7, TLR2, CD85j, CD64, CD86) and six novel surface markers for M2-like macrophages (CD1a, CD1b, CD93, CD226, CD82, CD23), which can be combined into a multi-parameter flow cytometry panel capable of distinguishing M1-like from M2-like macrophages with over 95% accuracy in mixed populations. This panel represents a significant advance over existing methods, which rely on a limited number of poorly specific markers such as CD86 and CD23, and enables the precise phenotyping of macrophages in clinical samples from patients with cancer, autoimmune disease, or chronic infection.

### Example 3

The invention is exemplified by the discovery of alternative splicing events that are dynamically regulated during human macrophage polarization and that provide a new dimension of transcriptional regulation inaccessible to microarray technologies. RNA sequencing data were analyzed using Cufflinks and Cuffdiff to detect differential transcript usage, alternative promoter selection, and alternative splicing events between M1-like and M2-like macrophages. This analysis revealed 20 genes with differential coding sequence usage, 28 genes with alternative transcription start site selection, and 9 genes with alternative promoter usage. One of the most striking findings was the differential isoform expression of the gene encoding PDZ and LIM domain 7 (PDLIM7), a scaffold protein involved in cytoskeletal organization and transcriptional regulation. While the overall expression of PDLIM7 was only modestly increased in M1-like macrophages (fold-change = 1.8), RNA-seq analysis revealed that three distinct isoforms—PDLIM7 v1, v2, and v4—were differentially expressed. PDLIM7 v1, which includes exons 1–5 and a unique 3′ exon, was highly enriched in M1-like macrophages (fold-change = 4.3, RPKM = 42), whereas PDLIM7 v2, which skips exon 4 and includes an alternative 3′ exon, was preferentially expressed in M2-like macrophages (fold-change = 3.9, RPKM = 38). PDLIM7 v4, which contains an alternative first exon, showed no significant difference between the two phenotypes. Isoform-specific quantitative PCR assays were designed to amplify each variant separately, and the results confirmed the RNA-seq findings, with PDLIM7 v1 being 4.1-fold higher in M1-like macrophages and PDLIM7 v2 being 3.7-fold higher in M2-like macrophages. Microarray analysis, which targeted only a single probe located in exon 3, failed to detect any significant difference in PDLIM7 expression, demonstrating the critical limitation of microarray platforms in resolving isoform-specific regulation. The invention further identified differential splicing in CD68, a widely used macrophage marker. While overall CD68 expression was similar between M1-like and M2-like macrophages, RNA-seq revealed that exon 5 was significantly more abundant in M1-like macrophages (fold-change = 2.1), whereas exon 8 was more abundant in M2-like macrophages (fold-change = 1.9). This suggests that the protein isoforms expressed in each phenotype may differ in their intracellular localization or interaction partners. Similarly, the gene encoding CD64 (FCGR1A) exhibited differential exon usage, with exon 7 being preferentially included in M1-like macrophages, potentially altering the ligand-binding domain of the receptor. The invention further identified alternative splicing in the gene encoding CD23 (FCER2), where a novel isoform lacking exon 4 was detected exclusively in M2-like macrophages, potentially generating a soluble form of CD23 with altered signaling properties. These findings establish that macrophage polarization is not merely a matter of transcriptional upregulation or downregulation of entire genes but involves a sophisticated reprogramming of transcript isoforms that may confer distinct functional properties. The invention encompasses the development of isoform-specific quantitative PCR assays and RNA probes for the detection of these splice variants in clinical samples, enabling a new level of phenotypic resolution that goes beyond surface marker expression and provides insight into the functional state of macrophages at the protein isoform level.

### Example 4

The invention is exemplified by the construction of a priori knowledge-based transcriptional network model that reveals the enhanced biological insight provided by RNA sequencing compared to microarray analysis in the context of human macrophage polarization. Gene expression data derived from RNA sequencing and microarray platforms were analyzed using EGAN software to generate network models based on curated biological knowledge from pathway databases, protein-protein interaction networks, and functional annotation terms. For the M1-like macrophage network, genes with a fold-change greater than 4 in RNA-seq data were selected as seed nodes, resulting in a network comprising 127 genes with 214 interactions. This network was dominated by immune signaling pathways, including Toll-like receptor signaling, interferon response, antigen processing and presentation, and apoptosis, with central hubs including APOL3, LILRB1, STAT1, IRF1, and GBP1. When the same network topology was applied to microarray data, only 73% of the nodes were detected, and the central hubs APOL3 and LILRB1 were entirely absent due to their low expression levels and lack of probe coverage on the microarray platform. Similarly, for the M2-like macrophage network, genes with a fold-change greater than 2.5 in RNA-seq data were used to generate a network of 98 genes with 156 interactions, enriched for metabolic pathways, extracellular matrix remodeling, and cell adhesion, with central hubs including MYC, TP53, CCL18, and CD1a. When this network was visualized using microarray data, only 54% of the nodes were detected, and the key hubs MYC and TP53 were not identified due to insufficient sensitivity and dynamic range. The invention demonstrates that RNA sequencing not only identifies more differentially expressed genes but also reveals the true topology of transcriptional networks, exposing critical regulatory nodes that are invisible to microarray technologies. The invention further shows that the inclusion of low-abundance transcripts such as APOL3 and LILRB1, which are functionally significant in M1-like macrophages, fundamentally alters the biological interpretation of the network, revealing novel connections between lipid metabolism, innate immunity, and inflammatory regulation. The invention thus provides a method for constructing high-fidelity transcriptional networks that accurately reflect the biological complexity of macrophage polarization, enabling the identification of novel therapeutic targets, biomarkers, and regulatory mechanisms that were previously obscured by the limitations of low-resolution profiling technologies. The network models generated by this invention serve as a reference framework for future studies in immunology, systems biology, and precision medicine, providing a comprehensive map of the molecular interactions that define macrophage identity and function.

### Example 5

The invention is exemplified by the validation of the novel surface markers and splice variants identified through RNA sequencing using orthogonal analytical techniques, including flow cytometry, quantitative PCR, and immunoblotting. For each of the novel markers identified in Examples 1–4, protein expression was confirmed using flow cytometry with monoclonal antibodies specific for CD120b, SLAMF7, TLR2, CD85j, CD1a, CD1b, CD93, CD226, and CD82. Macrophages were stained with fluorescently labeled antibodies and analyzed on a BD LSR II flow cytometer, with data processed using FlowJo software. The results confirmed that CD120b, SLAMF7, TLR2, and CD85j were expressed at high levels on M1-like macrophages and were barely detectable on M2-like macrophages, with mean fluorescence intensities exceeding 900 in M1-like cells and less than 150 in M2-like cells. Conversely, CD1a, CD1b, CD93, CD226, and CD82 were highly expressed on M2-like macrophages (mean fluorescence intensities >950) and were nearly absent on M1-like macrophages (mean fluorescence intensities <120). The specificity of these markers was further validated by comparing their expression in macrophages differentiated with M-CSF versus GM-CSF, demonstrating that the polarization-dependent expression patterns were consistent across differentiation conditions. For the splice variants identified in Example 3, isoform-specific quantitative PCR assays were designed using primers spanning unique exon-exon junctions for PDLIM7 v1, v2, and v4, as well as for CD68 and CD64 isoforms. RNA was reverse transcribed, and qPCR was performed using SYBR Green chemistry on a LightCycler 480 II instrument, with GAPDH used as a reference gene. The 2−ΔΔCT method was used to calculate relative expression levels, and the results confirmed the RNA-seq findings, with PDLIM7 v1 being 4.1-fold higher in M1-like macrophages and PDLIM7 v2 being 3.7-fold higher in M2-like macrophages. Immunoblotting was performed to confirm the presence of the protein products of the novel markers. Cell lysates from M1-like and M2-like macrophages were prepared using RIPA buffer, separated by SDS-PAGE, and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes. Membranes were probed with polyclonal antibodies against APOL3 and LILRB1, and signal was detected using horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies and chemiluminescent substrate. Western blot analysis confirmed the presence of APOL3 and LILRB1 proteins in M1-like macrophages, with no detectable signal in M2-like macrophages. These orthogonal validations demonstrate that the novel markers and splice variants identified by RNA sequencing are not merely transcriptional artifacts but are expressed at the protein level and are therefore biologically relevant. The invention thus provides a robust and validated set of molecular tools for the precise identification and functional characterization of human macrophage polarization states.

### Example 6

The invention is exemplified by the development of a diagnostic kit for the detection and classification of human macrophage polarization states based on the novel markers and splice variants disclosed herein. The kit comprises a set of oligonucleotide primers and probes designed for the detection of the novel splice variants of PDLIM7, CD68, CD64, and other genes identified in Example 3. The primers are optimized for use in quantitative real-time PCR and are provided in lyophilized form in individual tubes, along with a master mix containing Taq polymerase, dNTPs, and a passive reference dye. The kit further includes a set of monoclonal antibodies specific for the novel surface markers CD120b, SLAMF7, TLR2, CD85j, CD1a, CD1b, CD93, CD226, and CD82, conjugated to fluorophores including FITC, PE, APC, and Pacific Blue, for use in flow cytometry. The kit also includes a reference standard comprising purified M1-like and M2-like macrophages generated according to the method described in Example 1, which serve as positive controls for marker expression. The kit further includes a user manual detailing step-by-step protocols for RNA extraction from tissue or blood samples, cDNA synthesis, quantitative PCR amplification, flow cytometry staining, and data analysis. The manual provides a decision tree for the classification of macrophage polarization states based on the expression levels of the disclosed markers, with a scoring algorithm that assigns a polarization score based on the combination of surface marker expression and splice variant ratios. The kit further includes a software interface that can be installed on a personal computer or tablet, which accepts raw flow cytometry or qPCR data and automatically classifies the sample as M1-like, M2-like, or mixed based on a pre-trained machine learning model trained on the RNA-seq dataset disclosed herein. The software generates a report that includes the expression levels of all markers, the calculated polarization score, and a recommendation for clinical interpretation based on disease context. The invention thus provides a complete, ready-to-use diagnostic platform that enables researchers and clinicians to accurately classify macrophage polarization states in clinical samples, facilitating personalized immune profiling, disease monitoring, and therapeutic decision-making in conditions such as cancer, atherosclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, and chronic infections.

### Example 7

The invention is exemplified by the use of the novel markers and transcripts disclosed herein as therapeutic targets for the modulation of macrophage-mediated inflammation in disease. Monoclonal antibodies directed against the novel M1-specific surface markers CD120b, SLAMF7, TLR2, and CD85j were generated and tested for their ability to inhibit macrophage activation in vitro. Human M1-like macrophages were treated with blocking antibodies against CD120b, and the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α was measured by ELISA. Treatment with anti-CD120b antibody resulted in a 65% reduction in TNF-α secretion and a 58% reduction in IL-6 secretion, demonstrating that CD120b signaling contributes to the maintenance of the M1-like phenotype. Similarly, treatment with anti-SLAMF7 antibody reduced IL-12 production by 72%, suggesting a role for SLAMF7 in Th1 polarization. In contrast, antibodies against the M2-specific markers CD1a, CD1b, and CD93 were tested for their ability to inhibit tissue repair functions. Human M2-like macrophages were treated with anti-CD93 antibody, and their ability to phagocytose apoptotic cells was assessed by flow cytometry. Anti-CD93 treatment reduced phagocytic capacity by 68%, indicating that CD93 is functionally required for efferocytosis. The invention further encompasses the use of RNA interference constructs targeting the M1-specific splice variant PDLIM7 v1, which was shown to be critical for cytoskeletal reorganization in M1-like macrophages. Lentiviral vectors expressing short hairpin RNAs against PDLIM7 v1 were transduced into human monocytes, which were then differentiated into M1-like macrophages. Knockdown of PDLIM7 v1 resulted in a 70% reduction in cell migration through transwell membranes and a 55% reduction in nitric oxide production, demonstrating that this isoform is functionally required for M1-like macrophage effector functions. The invention thus provides a novel class of therapeutic agents—monoclonal antibodies, small molecule inhibitors, and RNA interference constructs—that target the specific molecular signatures of M1-like and M2-like macrophages, enabling the selective suppression of pathological inflammation or the enhancement of tissue repair in a context-dependent manner. The invention further provides a method for screening compound libraries for modulators of the novel markers and splice variants, using high-throughput flow cytometry or qPCR-based assays to identify small molecules that shift macrophage polarization toward a desired phenotype.

### Example 8

The invention is exemplified by the application of the disclosed method and markers to the analysis of macrophage polarization in human disease tissues, demonstrating the clinical utility of the invention in real-world settings. Tissue biopsies were obtained from patients with rheumatoid arthritis (n=12), non-small cell lung cancer (n=15), and atherosclerotic plaques (n=10), and macrophages were isolated by enzymatic digestion and magnetic sorting based on CD14 expression. RNA was extracted from these cells and analyzed by quantitative PCR using the isoform-specific primers disclosed in Example 6. In rheumatoid arthritis synovial fluid, the PDLIM7 v1/v2 ratio was significantly elevated compared to healthy controls, indicating a predominance of M1-like macrophages. In lung cancer tumor-infiltrating macrophages, the expression of CD1a and CD1b was markedly increased, suggesting an M2-like polarization state associated with immune suppression. In atherosclerotic plaques, the expression of APOL3 and LILRB1 was elevated in the necrotic core region, correlating with areas of inflammation and macrophage apoptosis. Flow cytometry analysis of these tissue-derived macrophages using the multi-parameter panel disclosed in Example 2 confirmed that the majority of macrophages in rheumatoid arthritis synovium were M1-like (CD120b+ SLAMF7+), whereas the majority in lung cancer tumors were M2-like (CD1a+ CD93+). The invention thus demonstrates that the novel markers and transcripts disclosed herein can be successfully applied to clinical samples to provide a precise, molecularly defined assessment of macrophage polarization in human disease. This capability enables the stratification of patients based on their macrophage phenotype, the monitoring of therapeutic response to immunomodulatory agents, and the development of targeted therapies that selectively modulate macrophage function in disease contexts. The invention thus represents a transformative advance in translational immunology, bridging the gap between basic research and clinical application by providing a powerful, validated, and clinically actionable tool for the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of inflammatory and immune-mediated diseases.
