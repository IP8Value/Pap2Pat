# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNOLOGY FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and molecular therapeutics, specifically to the identification and therapeutic targeting of long non-coding RNAs that function as glioma-specific radiation sensitizers. The invention further encompasses methods for the diagnosis and treatment of malignant gliomas, including glioblastoma and diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma, through the selective inhibition of a novel long non-coding RNA designated lncGRS-1, which is encoded by the genomic locus CTC-338M12.4 on chromosome 5q35.3. The invention also includes the development and application of a novel three-dimensional human brain organoid model that recapitulates the cellular architecture and microenvironment of the mature human brain, enabling the evaluation of therapeutic efficacy and neurological toxicity in a physiologically relevant context. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising antisense oligonucleotides designed to target lncGRS-1, as well as systems for the delivery and administration of such compositions in clinical settings. This technology represents a paradigm shift in the discovery and validation of non-coding RNA targets for radiotherapy enhancement, particularly in cancers where traditional animal models are inadequate due to the absence of orthologous sequences in non-primate species.

## BACKGROUND

Malignant gliomas, including glioblastoma in adults and diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma in children, remain among the most lethal forms of cancer, with median survival times of approximately 14 months and 9 to 10 months, respectively, despite aggressive multimodal therapy. Standard-of-care treatment regimens typically involve maximal safe surgical resection followed by fractionated radiotherapy, often combined with temozolomide chemotherapy. However, the therapeutic window for radiotherapy is severely constrained by the radiosensitivity of surrounding normal brain tissue, particularly astrocytes and neurons, which limits the total cumulative radiation dose that can be safely administered. As a result, residual glioma cells frequently survive radiation exposure, leading to inevitable tumor recurrence and poor long-term outcomes. There is a critical and unmet clinical need for agents that selectively enhance the radiosensitivity of glioma cells without increasing toxicity to normal neural cells, thereby improving the therapeutic index of radiotherapy.

The human genome encodes thousands of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), transcripts exceeding 200 nucleotides in length that do not encode proteins but play diverse regulatory roles in gene expression, chromatin remodeling, and cellular signaling. Many lncRNAs exhibit highly cell type-specific expression patterns, making them attractive candidates for precision oncology. However, the functional annotation of lncRNAs remains incomplete, and their roles in modulating response to radiotherapy have not been systematically explored at scale. Conventional approaches to identifying radiosensitizing targets have relied on candidate gene studies focused on protein-coding genes involved in DNA repair, cell cycle control, and apoptosis pathways. These approaches have yielded limited clinical success, in part because they overlook the vast non-coding genome, which constitutes over 98% of the human genome and is increasingly recognized as a rich reservoir of disease-relevant regulatory elements.

CRISPR-based screening technologies have revolutionized functional genomics by enabling genome-wide interrogation of gene function in mammalian cells. CRISPR interference (CRISPRi), which employs a catalytically inactive Cas9 protein fused to the KRAB transcriptional repressor domain, allows for precise, reversible, and tunable suppression of gene transcription without inducing DNA damage. This technology has been successfully applied to identify essential coding and non-coding genes in cancer cell lines. However, prior efforts have not been directed toward identifying lncRNAs that specifically modulate the response to clinically relevant, fractionated radiotherapy regimens. Moreover, the majority of preclinical validation studies for novel cancer therapeutics rely on xenograft mouse models, which are inherently limited in their ability to recapitulate human-specific biology, particularly when the target of interest lacks a conserved ortholog in rodents. The absence of a lncGRS-1 ortholog in mice, for example, renders traditional in vivo models unsuitable for evaluating the neurological safety profile of lncGRS-1 inhibition, thereby creating a significant barrier to translational development.

Human brain organoids derived from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) have emerged as powerful in vitro models for studying neurodevelopment and neurological disease. However, embryonic brain organoids, which are commonly used to model glioma growth, are composed predominantly of immature neural progenitor cells and lack the mature glial and neuronal populations that constitute the adult human brain. Consequently, these models fail to accurately reflect the cellular context in which gliomas arise and respond to therapy in patients. Furthermore, tumor organoids derived directly from patient tissue, while useful for assessing drug efficacy, are composed exclusively of malignant cells and therefore cannot be used to evaluate potential toxicity to normal brain parenchyma. There is a substantial gap in the field for a three-dimensional human tissue model that integrates both mature, non-tumorigenic neural cells and invasive glioma cells, enabling simultaneous assessment of therapeutic efficacy and neurological safety in a physiologically relevant microenvironment.

The present invention addresses these critical limitations by identifying a novel lncRNA, lncGRS-1, as a glioma-specific radiation sensitizer through a high-throughput CRISPRi screen designed to detect modifiers of fractionated radiotherapy response. The invention further establishes that inhibition of lncGRS-1 using antisense oligonucleotides selectively impairs the proliferation and survival of malignant glioma cells while sparing normal human astrocytes and neurons. To overcome the limitations of rodent models, the invention introduces a novel class of mature human brain organoids assembled from iPSC-derived, postmitotic astrocytes and neurons, which support the invasive growth of human glioma cells and permit the longitudinal evaluation of therapeutic interventions in the context of a fully human neural tissue architecture. This integrated approach—combining systematic functional genomics, target validation in patient-derived cells, and a physiologically accurate 3D human tissue model—constitutes a transformative platform for the discovery and preclinical development of non-coding RNA-targeted therapies for malignant glioma.

## SUMMARY

The invention provides a novel long non-coding RNA, designated lncGRS-1, encoded by the genomic locus CTC-338M12.4 on chromosome 5q35.3, which functions as a glioma-specific radiation sensitizer. Knockdown of lncGRS-1, whether achieved through CRISPR interference or antisense oligonucleotide-mediated degradation, significantly enhances the sensitivity of malignant glioma cells—including both adult glioblastoma and pediatric diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma—to clinically relevant doses of fractionated ionizing radiation. In contrast, inhibition of lncGRS-1 in normal human astrocytes, neurons, and other non-glioma cell types does not impair cell viability, proliferation, or induce apoptosis, demonstrating a striking tumor-specific functional dependency. The lncGRS-1 transcript is nuclear-enriched, polyadenylated, and spliced, with multiple isoforms ranging from approximately 687 to 1013 nucleotides in length. The transcript is conserved across primates but absent in rodents, rendering conventional mouse models inadequate for preclinical safety evaluation.

The invention further provides a method for identifying radiation sensitizers in malignant glioma through a genome-scale CRISPRi screen that employs fractionated radiation regimens mimicking clinical protocols. This method involves the stable expression of dCas9-KRAB in glioma cell lines, infection with a pooled library of single guide RNAs targeting the transcriptional start sites of thousands of lncRNAs, exposure to fractionated radiation (e.g., 8 Gy delivered in four 2 Gy fractions every other day), and subsequent quantification of sgRNA abundance over time to identify lncRNAs whose knockdown synergizes with radiation to suppress cell proliferation. The screen identifies a subset of lncRNAs, termed lncRNA Glioma Radiation Sensitizers (lncGRS), whose radiation modifier scores exceed their growth modifier scores in the absence of radiation, indicating a true radiosensitizing phenotype rather than general growth suppression. lncGRS-1 is the top-ranked hit from this screen and is validated across multiple patient-derived glioma cultures.

The invention further provides a method of diagnosing and treating malignant glioma by detecting the expression level of lncGRS-1 in tumor tissue or circulating nucleic acids and administering a therapeutic agent that inhibits lncGRS-1 function in combination with radiotherapy. The diagnostic component includes the use of quantitative reverse transcription PCR, RNA in situ hybridization, or next-generation sequencing to determine lncGRS-1 expression levels relative to normal brain tissue or other glioma subtypes. The therapeutic component comprises the administration of antisense oligonucleotides designed to bind lncGRS-1 and induce its degradation via RNase H-mediated cleavage. These antisense oligonucleotides are chemically modified with locked nucleic acid residues to enhance binding affinity, nuclease resistance, and cellular uptake, and are formulated for intrathecal or intracerebroventricular delivery to achieve therapeutic concentrations in the central nervous system.

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising an antisense oligonucleotide targeting lncGRS-1, optionally formulated with a carrier suitable for central nervous system delivery, such as a lipid nanoparticle, polymeric nanoparticle, or synthetic cerebrospinal fluid-compatible solution. The composition may further include a radioprotective agent for normal brain tissue or a radiosensitizing agent targeting a complementary pathway, allowing for synergistic combination therapies. The invention also provides a system for the delivery of such compositions, including an implantable pump, a catheter system for intrathecal infusion, or a device for localized convection-enhanced delivery directly into the tumor resection cavity.

The invention further provides a three-dimensional human brain organoid model of malignant glioma, termed a mature brain organoid (MBO), assembled from induced pluripotent stem cell-derived mature astrocytes and neurons. The MBO model recapitulates the cellular composition, structural organization, and physiological properties of the postnatal human brain, including the presence of ramified astrocytes, synaptically connected neurons, and extracellular matrix components. Human glioma cells, when seeded onto the surface of MBOs, invade the organoid parenchyma in a manner that mirrors the infiltrative growth pattern observed in patients. The MBO model permits the longitudinal, non-invasive monitoring of tumor burden via fluorescent labeling and the simultaneous assessment of therapeutic efficacy and neurotoxicity during treatment with antisense oligonucleotides and/or radiation. This model enables the evaluation of therapeutic agents in a human-specific context, circumventing the limitations of xenograft models and providing a critical bridge between in vitro assays and clinical trials.

The invention further provides a method of validating therapeutic targets for malignant glioma by employing the mature brain organoid model to assess the impact of target inhibition on both tumor growth and host tissue integrity. This method involves the co-culture of patient-derived glioma cells with MBOs, treatment with candidate therapeutic agents, and quantitative measurement of tumor burden, organoid diameter, cell viability, and apoptosis markers over time. The absence of toxicity to the MBO host tissue, despite robust knockdown of the target, confirms a favorable therapeutic index. The invention thus establishes a comprehensive, integrated platform for the discovery, validation, and preclinical development of non-coding RNA-targeted radiosensitizers for malignant glioma, with lncGRS-1 as the first validated example.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the term “lncGRS-1” refers to the long non-coding RNA transcript encoded by the genomic locus CTC-338M12.4 on chromosome 5q35.3, including all naturally occurring splice variants, isoforms, and post-transcriptional modifications thereof. The transcript is approximately 687 to 1013 nucleotides in length, polyadenylated, and localized predominantly to the nucleus. The term “lncGRS-1” encompasses both the primary transcript and its mature, processed form as expressed in human glioma cells. The term “radiation sensitizer” refers to a molecular target whose inhibition enhances the cytotoxic effects of ionizing radiation on cancer cells without proportionally increasing toxicity to normal cells. The term “fractionated radiation” refers to the delivery of a total radiation dose in multiple smaller doses separated by intervals of time, typically daily or every other day, to allow for repair of normal tissue while maximizing cumulative damage to tumor cells. A clinically relevant fractionated radiation regimen for glioma is defined as a total dose of 54 to 60 Gy delivered in 1.8 to 2.0 Gy fractions over a period of 5 to 6 weeks. The term “antisense oligonucleotide” refers to a synthetic, single-stranded nucleic acid molecule, typically 15 to 30 nucleotides in length, designed to bind to a complementary sequence within a target RNA transcript via Watson-Crick base pairing, resulting in degradation of the RNA by RNase H or steric blockade of RNA function. The antisense oligonucleotides of the invention are chemically modified with locked nucleic acid (LNA) residues to enhance binding affinity, resistance to nucleases, and pharmacokinetic properties. The term “mature human brain organoid” or “MBO” refers to a three-dimensional, self-organizing tissue structure derived from induced pluripotent stem cells that contains a defined composition of mature, postmitotic human astrocytes and neurons, recapitulating the cellular architecture and microenvironment of the adult human brain. The term “mature astrocytes” refers to astrocytes that have undergone terminal differentiation, exhibit complex branched morphology, express canonical markers such as GFAP, S100B, ALDH1L1, and AQP4, and are functionally integrated into neural circuits. The term “mature neurons” refers to neurons that have exited the cell cycle, express synaptic markers such as MAP2, βIII-tubulin, and synaptophysin, and form functional connections. The term “tumor burden” refers to the quantitative measure of invasive glioma cell growth within a host tissue, determined by the area or volume of fluorescent signal derived from labeled tumor cells relative to the total organoid area. The term “therapeutic index” refers to the ratio of the dose of a therapeutic agent that produces a toxic effect in normal tissue to the dose that produces a therapeutic effect in tumor tissue. A high therapeutic index indicates that the agent selectively targets tumor cells while sparing normal cells. The term “CRISPR interference” or “CRISPRi” refers to a gene knockdown technique that utilizes a catalytically inactive Cas9 protein fused to a transcriptional repressor domain, such as KRAB, to bind to a target genomic locus and suppress transcription without inducing double-strand breaks. The term “screen score” refers to a composite metric derived from the average phenotype of the top three single guide RNAs targeting a given gene, multiplied by the negative logarithm of the Mann-Whitney U p-value, used to rank hits in a pooled CRISPRi screen. The term “sensitizer score” refers to the ratio of the screen score obtained in the presence of radiation to the screen score obtained in the absence of radiation, used to prioritize hits that specifically enhance radiosensitivity. The term “patient-derived glioma cells” refers to primary tumor cells isolated directly from the resected tissue of patients with glioblastoma or diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma and maintained in culture under conditions that preserve their molecular and phenotypic characteristics. The term “non-tumorigenic neural cells” refers to human astrocytes, neurons, or other glial cells derived from induced pluripotent stem cells that do not exhibit malignant transformation, tumorigenic potential, or uncontrolled proliferation. The term “convection-enhanced delivery” refers to a method of intracerebral drug administration that utilizes positive pressure to facilitate the distribution of therapeutic agents through the interstitial spaces of brain tissue, bypassing the blood-brain barrier.

### Method for Identification of a Radiotherapy Sensitizer

A method for identifying a radiotherapy sensitizer in malignant glioma comprises the steps of: (a) obtaining a population of glioma cells stably expressing a catalytically inactive Cas9 protein fused to a transcriptional repressor domain, wherein the repressor domain is selected from the group consisting of KRAB, SID, or Mxi1; (b) infecting the glioma cells with a pooled lentiviral library comprising a plurality of single guide RNAs, wherein each single guide RNA is designed to target the transcriptional start site of a long non-coding RNA expressed in human glioma, and wherein the library comprises at least 5,000 distinct single guide RNAs targeting at least 1,000 distinct long non-coding RNA loci; (c) selecting for infected cells using a selectable marker encoded by the lentiviral vector; (d) exposing the selected cells to a fractionated ionizing radiation regimen comprising at least two doses of ionizing radiation, each dose ranging from 1.5 to 2.5 Gy, delivered at intervals of 24 to 72 hours, for a total cumulative radiation dose of at least 6 Gy; (e) maintaining the irradiated cells in culture for a period of at least 10 days, during which time the cells undergo at least six population doublings; (f) isolating genomic DNA from the cell population at the beginning and end of the culture period; (g) amplifying and sequencing the integrated single guide RNA sequences from the genomic DNA using next-generation sequencing; (h) calculating the relative abundance of each single guide RNA at the end of the culture period relative to its abundance at the beginning, thereby generating a phenotype value for each targeted long non-coding RNA; (i) comparing the phenotype values of the irradiated cells to phenotype values obtained from an identical screen conducted in the absence of radiation; (j) identifying long non-coding RNAs whose phenotype value in the presence of radiation is significantly more negative than in the absence of radiation, thereby defining a radiation sensitizer; (k) validating the radiation sensitizer by performing individual knockdown of the identified long non-coding RNA using a distinct single guide RNA and confirming enhanced radiosensitivity in a growth inhibition assay; and (l) confirming that the radiation sensitizer is expressed in primary patient-derived glioma cells but not essential for the viability of normal human astrocytes or neurons. The fractionated radiation regimen may be delivered in four fractions of 2 Gy every other day, totaling 8 Gy. The phenotype value is calculated as the log2 fold change in the normalized read count of the single guide RNA between the end and beginning timepoints. The comparison step may further involve calculating a screen score for each long non-coding RNA as the product of the average phenotype of the top three single guide RNAs targeting the gene and the negative logarithm of the Mann-Whitney U p-value derived from the comparison of barcode abundance distributions. The validation step may further include measuring the expression level of the long non-coding RNA in patient-derived glioma cells using quantitative reverse transcription PCR and confirming that its knockdown does not reduce the viability of normal human astrocytes.
