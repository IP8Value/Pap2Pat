# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of infectious disease therapeutics has long been dominated by strategies that target pathogens directly, such as antibiotics, antivirals, and antitoxins designed to neutralize microbial virulence factors. While these approaches have yielded significant clinical successes, they are inherently limited in their scope and adaptability. Pathogens, particularly those that produce potent cytotoxic agents, evolve rapidly to evade targeted interventions, rendering many therapeutics obsolete shortly after deployment. Moreover, the increasing prevalence of multidrug-resistant strains and the emergence of novel biological threats—whether naturally occurring or engineered—have exposed critical vulnerabilities in the conventional paradigm of pathogen-centric drug development. The time-consuming nature of de novo drug discovery, which often spans over a decade and incurs billions of dollars in investment, renders it ill-suited for responding to acute public health emergencies, such as bioterrorism events or pandemic outbreaks. In response to these challenges, a paradigm shift has emerged toward host-directed therapies, which aim to modulate cellular processes exploited by pathogens rather than targeting the pathogens themselves. This strategy offers the potential for broad-spectrum efficacy against diverse agents that converge on shared host pathways, thereby circumventing pathogen-specific resistance mechanisms and enabling rapid repurposing of existing, well-characterized compounds.

Central to this emerging approach is the identification of key host proteins—termed “hub proteins”—that serve as critical convergence points for multiple pathogenic insults. These hub proteins are not merely passive participants in infection but are actively hijacked by evolutionarily distinct toxins and pathogens to execute their cytotoxic programs. For instance, bacterial exotoxins such as Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, anthrax lethal factor, diphtheria toxin, and botulinum neurotoxin A, despite differing in structure, receptor specificity, and intracellular trafficking routes, all ultimately trigger programmed cell death through the activation of host caspases. Similarly, plant-derived toxins like ricin and viral pathogens such as Zika virus exploit the same executioner caspases to induce apoptosis, thereby linking seemingly unrelated infectious agents through a common molecular mechanism. The discovery that these diverse pathogens rely on a limited set of host proteins for their pathogenic outcomes suggests that pharmacological inhibition of these hubs could yield a single therapeutic agent capable of mitigating the effects of multiple, unrelated biological threats. This concept, while theoretically compelling, has been historically underexplored due to the technical difficulty of identifying such hubs and the lack of high-throughput screening methodologies capable of simultaneously evaluating compound efficacy across multiple pathogenic contexts.

Prior efforts to identify host-directed therapeutics have largely focused on single-pathogen systems or have relied on genetic screens to pinpoint essential host factors. While informative, these approaches often fail to capture the polypharmacological nature of real-world infections, where a single agent may encounter multiple toxins or pathogens simultaneously. Furthermore, many host-directed candidates identified in such screens suffer from poor pharmacokinetic properties, high toxicity, or insufficient selectivity, rendering them unsuitable for clinical translation. There exists, therefore, a critical unmet need for a methodological framework that integrates multiplexed cellular and biochemical screening to simultaneously identify compounds with broad-spectrum anti-pathogenic activity and their corresponding molecular targets within the host. Such a framework must not only detect compounds that enhance cell survival in the presence of diverse toxins but must also establish a direct mechanistic link between compound action and inhibition of specific host enzymatic activities, thereby providing both efficacy data and target validation in a single, coherent workflow. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing a novel, systematic approach to drug discovery—termed Disparate Dual Multiplexing—that combines cellular viability assays across multiple toxin challenges with biochemical profiling of caspase inhibition, enabling the rapid identification of a single compound, Bithionol, as a potent, broad-spectrum inhibitor of host caspase-dependent cytotoxicity. This invention represents a foundational advance in the field of host-directed therapeutics, providing not only a new therapeutic agent but also a scalable, reproducible methodology for the discovery of future agents capable of neutralizing an expanding universe of biological threats through the modulation of conserved host pathways.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to the discovery and therapeutic application of Bithionol as a broad-spectrum host-directed inhibitor of cytotoxicity induced by diverse bacterial toxins, plant toxins, and viral pathogens. Specifically, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising Bithionol or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, derivative, or analog thereof, for use in the treatment or prevention of diseases or conditions caused by pathogenic agents that induce cell death through the activation of host caspases. The invention further encompasses methods of using Bithionol to inhibit the proteolytic activities of caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9 in mammalian cells, thereby conferring protection against cytotoxic insults from Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, anthrax lethal factor, diphtheria toxin, cholera toxin, ricin, botulinum neurotoxin A, and Zika virus. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that Bithionol, a previously approved antihelminthic agent with known safety profiles in humans, acts as a direct and potent inhibitor of multiple executioner and inflammatory caspases, thereby interrupting a conserved host cell death pathway exploited by phylogenetically unrelated pathogens. This dual mechanism of action—simultaneously protecting cells from toxin-mediated death while preserving essential host functions—distinguishes Bithionol from conventional pathogen-targeting agents and positions it as a uniquely versatile therapeutic candidate.

The invention demonstrates that Bithionol reduces cellular cytotoxicity induced by a wide array of toxins with an EC50 ranging from 6.3 to 10 micromolar in vitro, and that it confers complete protection against lethal doses of botulinum neurotoxin A in vivo when administered orally. Importantly, Bithionol does not inhibit the intrinsic proteolytic activity of the toxins themselves, as confirmed through biochemical assays with purified toxin enzymes, but instead acts exclusively on host caspases, thereby eliminating the risk of pathogen-specific resistance development. The compound exhibits no cytotoxicity in the absence of toxin challenge and maintains efficacy across multiple human and murine cell types, including macrophages, melanoma cells, lymphoblastoid cells, astrocytes, and Vero E6 cells. Furthermore, Bithionol’s ability to cross the blood-brain barrier and placental barrier, combined with its established oral bioavailability and serum concentrations in humans that exceed its effective in vitro concentrations by more than thirty-fold, supports its potential for systemic administration in acute clinical settings. The invention further includes a novel screening methodology, termed Disparate Dual Multiplexing, which integrates multiplexed cellular viability assays across multiple toxin challenges with parallel biochemical profiling of caspase-3, -6, -7, -1, and -9 inhibition to identify compounds that act as broad-spectrum host-directed therapeutics. This methodology enables the simultaneous discovery of both therapeutic candidates and their molecular targets, significantly accelerating the drug repurposing pipeline and reducing the time and cost associated with traditional drug development. The invention thus provides not only a new therapeutic agent for the treatment of toxin-mediated diseases and viral infections but also a transformative platform for the discovery of future host-directed therapeutics against emerging and re-emerging biological threats.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Combination Compositions and Treatments

The present invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising Bithionol as the sole active ingredient, as well as combination formulations in which Bithionol is co-administered with one or more additional therapeutic agents for the treatment of conditions characterized by caspase-dependent cytotoxicity. These combination therapies are designed to enhance efficacy, broaden the spectrum of protection, mitigate potential side effects, or overcome pharmacokinetic limitations associated with monotherapy. Bithionol may be formulated in combination with other caspase inhibitors, immunomodulators, antiviral agents, antibiotics, or supportive therapeutics such as fluid replacement agents, vasopressors, or anticonvulsants, depending on the clinical context of the infection or intoxication. For instance, in cases of anthrax exposure, Bithionol may be administered alongside antibiotics such as ciprofloxacin or doxycycline to simultaneously inhibit bacterial proliferation and neutralize the cytotoxic effects of lethal factor, thereby addressing both the infectious and toxic components of the disease. Similarly, in the context of botulinum intoxication, Bithionol may be combined with antitoxin antibodies to block extracellular toxin neutralization while concurrently inhibiting intracellular caspase activation, providing a dual-layered defense mechanism that targets both the pathogen’s secreted virulence factor and its downstream cellular consequences.

In addition to pharmacological combinations, Bithionol may be formulated with adjuvants or delivery enhancers to improve its bioavailability, tissue penetration, or stability. For example, Bithionol may be encapsulated in liposomes, polymeric nanoparticles, or cyclodextrin complexes to enhance its solubility in aqueous media and facilitate its transport across biological barriers such as the intestinal epithelium, the blood-brain barrier, or the placental membrane. Such formulations may be particularly beneficial in the treatment of neurotoxic conditions such as botulinum intoxication or Zika virus-associated encephalitis, where the therapeutic agent must reach neural tissues in sufficient concentrations to exert protective effects. The invention further contemplates the use of Bithionol in sustained-release formulations, including transdermal patches, implantable depots, or extended-release oral tablets, which maintain therapeutic serum concentrations over prolonged periods, thereby reducing the frequency of dosing and improving patient compliance in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings. The pharmaceutical compositions may be prepared in a variety of dosage forms suitable for oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, intranasal, or inhalation administration, depending on the urgency of the clinical scenario and the nature of the pathogenic exposure.

The invention further provides methods of treatment in which Bithionol is administered to a subject who has been exposed to, or is at risk of exposure to, a pathogenic agent that induces caspase-dependent cytotoxicity. The subject may be a human or non-human mammal, including but not limited to primates, rodents, canines, felines, equines, or livestock. The administration may occur prior to, during, or after exposure to the pathogen, and the timing of administration is determined by the clinical context and the kinetics of toxin internalization and caspase activation. In prophylactic applications, such as in military or laboratory personnel at risk of biological threat exposure, Bithionol may be administered orally in a single dose or as a multi-dose regimen prior to anticipated exposure. In therapeutic applications, such as in patients presenting with symptoms of botulinum intoxication, ricin poisoning, or Zika virus infection, Bithionol may be administered as soon as possible following diagnosis, with dosing adjusted based on the severity of symptoms, the route of exposure, and the patient’s body weight. The invention further encompasses the use of Bithionol in mass casualty scenarios, wherein it may be stockpiled as a medical countermeasure and distributed via oral tablets or liquid suspensions in emergency response settings, such as during bioterrorism events or outbreaks of toxin-mediated disease. The therapeutic efficacy of Bithionol is not dependent on the specific strain or serotype of the pathogen, as its mechanism of action targets a conserved host pathway rather than variable pathogen-specific determinants, rendering it effective against a broad range of clinically relevant isolates, including antibiotic-resistant strains and genetically engineered variants.

### Administration of the Bithionol Composition

The administration of Bithionol in accordance with the present invention is tailored to achieve and maintain serum concentrations sufficient to inhibit caspase activity in target tissues while minimizing potential adverse effects. Bithionol may be administered orally, intravenously, intramuscularly, subcutaneously, intranasally, or via inhalation, with oral administration being the preferred route due to its established safety profile, ease of deployment, and high bioavailability in humans. Upon oral ingestion, Bithionol is rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract, reaching peak serum concentrations within two to four hours, with a half-life of approximately six to eight hours in humans. Clinical data from prior use of Bithionol in the treatment of helminthic infections indicate that oral doses of 50 milligrams per kilogram of body weight result in serum concentrations ranging from 225 to 480 micromolar, which significantly exceed the in vitro EC50 values of 6.3 to 10 micromolar observed for protection against bacterial toxins, ricin, and Zika virus. This pharmacokinetic profile supports the feasibility of achieving therapeutic concentrations through standard oral dosing regimens, even in resource-limited settings where intravenous administration may be impractical.

For acute intoxication events, such as botulinum or ricin poisoning, a single oral dose of Bithionol ranging from 10 to 100 milligrams per kilogram of body weight may be administered immediately following exposure, with repeat dosing every six to twelve hours for up to 72 hours, depending on the persistence of toxin activity and the progression of clinical symptoms. In pediatric populations, dosing may be adjusted based on body surface area or weight, with a recommended range of 5 to 50 milligrams per kilogram, consistent with historical use in children for paragonimiasis. For prophylactic use in high-risk individuals, such as military personnel, first responders, or laboratory workers handling high-consequence pathogens, a lower maintenance dose of 5 to 20 milligrams per kilogram may be administered daily for up to seven days prior to anticipated exposure, with the option to extend the regimen if the threat persists. In cases of Zika virus infection, particularly in pregnant women or individuals with neurological complications, Bithionol may be administered at a dose of 10 to 30 milligrams per kilogram twice daily for a duration of five to ten days, with monitoring for potential hepatotoxicity or photosensitivity reactions.

Intravenous administration may be preferred in cases of severe intoxication where rapid systemic delivery is required, such as in patients with respiratory failure secondary to botulinum toxin-induced paralysis or in cases of septic shock triggered by overwhelming bacterial toxin exposure. Intravenous formulations of Bithionol may be prepared as sterile solutions in saline or dextrose, with concentrations ranging from 1 to 10 milligrams per milliliter, and administered as a slow infusion over 30 to 60 minutes to minimize the risk of hypotension or local irritation. Intramuscular or subcutaneous injections may be utilized in field settings where intravenous access is unavailable, with doses adjusted to account for slower absorption kinetics. Intranasal administration may be particularly advantageous for the prevention or early treatment of respiratory exposure to aerosolized toxins such as anthrax lethal factor or ricin, as it enables direct delivery to the upper respiratory tract and systemic absorption via the nasal mucosa. Inhalation formulations may be delivered via nebulizer or metered-dose inhaler, with particle sizes optimized for deep lung deposition. Regardless of the route of administration, the pharmaceutical composition may include excipients such as stabilizers, buffers, preservatives, and surfactants to ensure chemical stability, microbial sterility, and compatibility with the delivery system. The invention further includes methods for monitoring therapeutic efficacy through biomarkers of caspase activity, such as cleavage of caspase substrates in serum or tissue samples, or through clinical indicators of cell death, such as lactate dehydrogenase release or apoptotic markers in peripheral blood mononuclear cells.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Identification of Host Hub Proteins Exploited by Multiple Pathogenic Toxins

A systematic investigation was conducted to identify host proteins that serve as critical convergence points for the cytotoxic mechanisms of diverse bacterial and plant toxins. The rationale for this inquiry was grounded in the observation that, despite their structural and mechanistic diversity, multiple pathogenic agents—including Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, anthrax lethal factor, diphtheria toxin, cholera toxin, and ricin—all ultimately induce programmed cell death through the activation of intracellular proteolytic enzymes belonging to the caspase family. To determine whether these disparate toxins exploit a common set of host proteins, a comprehensive analysis was performed to assess whether genetic variations in known host factors associated with toxin sensitivity correlated with altered cellular responses across multiple intoxication models. Human B-lymphoblastoid cell lines derived from 234 individuals across four geographically and ethnically distinct populations—Yoruba in Ibadan, Nigeria (YRI), Utah residents with northern and western European ancestry (CEU), Japanese in Tokyo, Japan (JPT), and Han Chinese in Beijing, China (CHB)—were exposed to serial dilutions of Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, and the dose required to kill 20% of the cells (LD20) was quantified for each line. The resulting data revealed a striking 200-fold variation in toxin sensitivity across the cohort, with a unimodal distribution of log(1/LD20) values that suggested a polygenic basis for susceptibility. This variation was found to be heritable, as evidenced by parent-child trio analyses, with a statistical significance of P < 0.0001, indicating that genetic factors play a dominant role in determining cellular sensitivity to toxin-induced death.

To identify the genetic determinants underlying this variation, a targeted association analysis was performed across a panel of single nucleotide polymorphisms previously linked to dysregulation of apoptosis and inflammatory pathways. Among the most significant associations were variants in the CASP7 gene, including rs3814231 and rs2227309, which demonstrated a statistically significant correlation with increased sensitivity to exotoxin A in the CEU population (P = 0.01 and P = 0.02, respectively). Similarly, variants in the CASP3 gene—rs4647601, rs4647693, and rs1049216—showed a consistent association with altered toxin sensitivity in the combined East Asian populations (CHB and JPT), with each SNP yielding a P-value of 0.04. These findings provided compelling evidence that natural genetic variation in executioner caspases directly influences host cell vulnerability to bacterial toxins. Further investigation confirmed that these same caspases—caspase-3, -6, and -7—are activated by a broad spectrum of other toxins, including anthrax lethal factor, diphtheria toxin, cholera toxin, and ricin, suggesting that these enzymes function as central hubs in a conserved host cell death pathway exploited by evolutionarily distinct pathogens. The identification of caspase-3, -6, and -7 as critical convergence points for multiple toxin-mediated cytotoxicities established them as high-value targets for the discovery of broad-spectrum host-directed therapeutics. This discovery formed the foundation for subsequent screening efforts aimed at identifying compounds capable of inhibiting these caspases and thereby conferring protection against a wide array of toxin-induced pathologies.

### Example 2. The Effect of Caspase Mutations on the Sensitivity of Human B-Cells to P. aeruginosa Exotoxin A

To further elucidate the role of caspase polymorphisms in determining cellular sensitivity to Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, a detailed analysis was performed on a cohort of 234 human B-lymphoblastoid cell lines derived from individuals across four distinct populations: Yoruba in Ibadan, Nigeria (YRI), Utah residents with northern and western European ancestry (CEU), Japanese in Tokyo, Japan (JPT), and Han Chinese in Beijing, China (CHB). Each cell line was exposed to a range of exotoxin A concentrations for 48 hours, and cell viability was assessed using the Alamar Blue fluorescence assay, which measures metabolic activity as a proxy for live cell count. The dose required to kill 20% of the cells (LD20) was calculated for each line, and the logarithmic transformation of the reciprocal of LD20 (log(1/LD20)) was used as the primary metric of sensitivity. The resulting distribution of log(1/LD20) values exhibited a unimodal pattern with a range of approximately 200-fold differences in sensitivity, indicating that susceptibility to exotoxin A is not determined by a single gene but rather by the cumulative effect of multiple genetic variants. Notably, the magnitude of this variation was consistent across all four populations, suggesting that the underlying genetic architecture governing toxin sensitivity is evolutionarily conserved.

To determine whether specific genetic variants in caspase genes contribute to this variation, a targeted genotyping approach was employed to assess the frequency of single nucleotide polymorphisms previously associated with dysregulated apoptosis in human disease. The analysis revealed that two variants in the CASP7 gene—rs3814231 and rs2227309—were significantly associated with increased sensitivity to exotoxin A in the CEU population, with P-values of 0.01 and 0.02, respectively. In the combined East Asian populations (CHB and JPT), three variants in the CASP3 gene—rs4647601, rs4647693, and rs1049216—each demonstrated a statistically significant association with altered toxin sensitivity, with a P-value of 0.04 for each SNP. These findings were corroborated by functional validation in a subset of cell lines, wherein cells homozygous for the high-sensitivity alleles exhibited significantly higher levels of caspase-3 and -7 activation following toxin exposure compared to cells carrying the low-sensitivity alleles. The heritability of this trait was further confirmed through trio analysis of parent-child pairs, wherein the relative sensitivity to exotoxin A was strongly correlated between biological relatives, with a P-value of less than 0.0001. These results collectively demonstrate that natural genetic variation in executioner caspases directly modulates the cellular response to bacterial toxins, providing a mechanistic explanation for the observed heterogeneity in toxin susceptibility across human populations. Importantly, these findings establish caspase-3 and -7 as key determinants of host vulnerability and validate them as high-priority targets for pharmacological intervention.

### Example 3. A Multiplexed Cellular Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Cytotoxic Activities of Bacterial Toxins Exploiting Unique but Interconnected Host Pathways

To identify compounds capable of broadly inhibiting the cytotoxic effects of multiple bacterial toxins, a multiplexed cellular screening platform was developed to simultaneously evaluate the protective efficacy of a Clinical Compound Library (CCL) against three distinct toxins: Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, cholera toxin, and diphtheria toxin. The screening was conducted using two murine cell lines—RAW264.7 mouse macrophages and C32 human melanoma cells—chosen for their differential sensitivity to the toxins and their relevance to in vivo pathogenesis. RAW264.7 cells were selected for their high sensitivity to exotoxin A and cholera toxin, while C32 cells were utilized for their susceptibility to diphtheria toxin, as rodent cells are naturally resistant to this agent. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates and pre-treated with compounds from the CCL for one hour prior to exposure to the respective toxins at concentrations known to induce 80% cell death within 12 to 24 hours. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay, which measures mitochondrial reductase activity as an indicator of metabolic health. A compound was classified as a “hit” if it increased cell survival by at least 20 standard deviations above the mean survival of toxin-treated controls, corresponding to a hit rate of approximately 1%. Compounds that demonstrated protective effects against at least two of the three toxins were designated as “multiplex hits,” indicating broad-spectrum activity against toxins that exploit distinct entry and trafficking pathways.

From an initial screen of over 1,000 compounds, two multiplex hits were identified: Bithionol and Pyrogallol. Both compounds significantly enhanced cell survival in the presence of exotoxin A, cholera toxin, and diphtheria toxin, with Bithionol demonstrating the most consistent and potent effect across all conditions. Importantly, neither compound exhibited cytotoxicity in the absence of toxin exposure, confirming that their protective effects were not due to general cell toxicity or nonspecific inhibition of metabolic activity. The identification of Bithionol as a multiplex hit was particularly significant, as it suggested that a single compound could simultaneously interfere with the cytotoxic mechanisms of toxins that enter cells through fundamentally different routes—exotoxin A and diphtheria toxin via endocytosis and retrograde transport, and cholera toxin via cAMP-mediated signaling—yet all converge on the activation of executioner caspases. This finding validated the hypothesis that targeting a shared downstream host pathway, rather than individual toxin-specific mechanisms, could yield a broad-spectrum therapeutic agent. The multiplex screening approach thus provided not only a candidate compound but also a powerful validation of the underlying biological principle: that diverse toxins exploit interconnected host pathways, and that pharmacological inhibition of these hubs can confer protection against multiple threats simultaneously.

### Example 4. A Multiplex Protein Function-Based Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Proteolytic Activities of Host Caspase-3, -6, and -7

To complement the cellular screening efforts and establish a direct mechanistic link between compound efficacy and caspase inhibition, a parallel biochemical screen was conducted to identify compounds from the Clinical Compound Library (CCL) capable of inhibiting the proteolytic activities of caspase-3, caspase-6, and caspase-3/7. The rationale for this screen was based on the prior identification of caspase-3, -6, and -7 as key executioners of toxin-induced cell death and the hypothesis that compounds capable of inhibiting these enzymes would also protect cells from toxin-mediated cytotoxicity. To enable high-throughput screening, a fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) assay was developed using synthetic peptide substrates conjugated with a fluorogenic 7-amino-4-methylcoumarin (AMC) group at the N-terminus and an acetyl quenching group at the C-terminus. Upon cleavage by the target caspase, the AMC fluorophore is released, resulting in a measurable increase in fluorescence at 460 nm. Substrate sequences were selected based on known caspase cleavage motifs: DNLD for caspase-3, VEID for caspase-6, and DQTD for caspase-3/7, with the latter designed to be cleaved by both caspase-3 and caspase-7 due to their high sequence homology.

RAW264.7 cells were treated with Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A for four hours to induce endogenous caspase activation, after which cell lysates were harvested and used as the source of active caspases. Compounds from the CCL were tested at a final concentration of 33 micromolar, a concentration selected based on pilot experiments to ensure sufficient sensitivity for multiplex hit detection. Kinetic measurements of fluorescence were recorded every five minutes over a two-hour period using a fluorescent plate reader, and the rate of substrate cleavage was quantified using linear regression analysis. A compound was classified as a hit if it inhibited caspase activity by more than 80% compared to vehicle-treated controls, corresponding to a hit rate of approximately 1%. Compounds that inhibited the activity of at least two caspases were designated as multiplex hits. Among the top candidates identified were Bithionol, Pyrogallol, and one additional compound, with Bithionol demonstrating the most robust inhibition across all three substrates. The inhibition of caspase-3, -6, and -3/7 by Bithionol was dose-dependent and reproducible across multiple experimental replicates, confirming its direct interaction with the catalytic sites of these enzymes. The convergence of results from the cellular and biochemical screens—where Bithionol emerged as a multiplex hit in both contexts—provided compelling evidence that its protective effects against bacterial toxins are mediated through direct inhibition of executioner caspases. This dual validation established Bithionol as a lead candidate for further investigation and confirmed the utility of the Disparate Dual Multiplexing approach for simultaneously identifying therapeutic agents and their molecular targets.

### Example 5. Bithionol Reduces the Pathogenicity of a Range of Toxins by Inhibiting Host Caspases

To determine the potency and breadth of Bithionol’s protective effects, a series of dose-response experiments were conducted across multiple toxin models and cell types. RAW264.7 and C32 cells were pre-treated with increasing concentrations of Bithionol for one hour prior to exposure to Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, cholera toxin, or diphtheria toxin, and cell viability was assessed 12 to 24 hours later using the MTT assay. Bithionol consistently reduced toxin-induced cytotoxicity in a concentration-dependent manner, with an EC50 of approximately 10 micromolar across all three toxins. These results confirmed that Bithionol’s protective effects were not limited to a single toxin or cell type but extended to multiple agents that exploit distinct cellular entry and signaling pathways. To further elucidate the mechanism of action, the inhibitory potency of Bithionol against purified caspase enzymes was evaluated using FRET assays with recombinant human caspase-3, -6, and -3/7. Bithionol demonstrated a linear dose-dependent inhibition of caspase activity, with IC50 values of 21 micromolar for caspase-3, 13 micromolar for caspase-6, and 11 micromolar for caspase-3/7. The close correspondence between the cellular EC50 values and the biochemical IC50 values provided strong evidence that the protective effects of Bithionol in intact cells are mediated primarily through direct inhibition of caspase proteolytic activity.

To validate these findings in human cells, Bithionol was tested against exotoxin A in three randomly selected human B-lymphoblastoid cell lines exhibiting high sensitivity to the toxin. In each case, pretreatment with Bithionol at a concentration of 10 micromolar significantly increased cell survival following exposure to a lethal dose of exotoxin A sufficient to kill 80% of untreated cells. This result confirmed that Bithionol’s protective effects are not restricted to murine cell lines but extend to primary human cells derived from diverse genetic backgrounds. To assess the selectivity of Bithionol’s caspase inhibition, a comprehensive panel of ten purified recombinant human caspases—caspase-1 through caspase-10—was tested in FRET assays. Bithionol was found to significantly inhibit the activity of caspase-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9, with IC50 values ranging from 11 to 35 micromolar, while showing no appreciable inhibition of caspase-2, -4, -5, -8, or -10. This pattern of inhibition suggests that Bithionol selectively targets a subset of caspases involved in both inflammatory and apoptotic pathways, with particular potency against the executioner caspases-3 and -7 and the upstream initiator caspase-9. The inhibition of caspase-1, a key mediator of pyroptosis and inflammatory cytokine maturation, further expands the therapeutic potential of Bithionol to include conditions characterized by excessive inflammation, such as sepsis or cytokine storm syndromes. Collectively, these data demonstrate that Bithionol is a potent, direct, and selective inhibitor of a defined subset of host caspases, and that this inhibition is sufficient to confer broad protection against a diverse array of cytotoxic agents.

### Example 6. Bithionol Inhibits Cytotoxic Activity of Anthrax Toxins

To evaluate the efficacy of Bithionol against anthrax toxin, a model system was established using RAW264.7 macrophages exposed to a combination of protective antigen (PA) and lethal factor (LF), which together form the binary anthrax lethal toxin. Under standard conditions, exposure to this toxin complex results in 80% cell death within six hours, primarily through the cleavage of mitogen-activated protein kinase kinases (MAPKKs) by LF and the subsequent activation of caspase-1. Bithionol was administered to cells at concentrations ranging from 1 to 100 micromolar one hour prior to toxin exposure, and cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay. At a concentration of 33 micromolar, Bithionol conferred substantial protection, increasing cell survival from approximately 20% to over 80%, indicating a potent inhibitory effect on anthrax toxin-mediated cytotoxicity. To determine whether this protection was mediated through inhibition of caspase-1, a FRET-based assay was employed using a caspase-1-specific substrate. In toxin-treated cells without Bithionol, a robust increase in caspase-1 activity was observed, consistent with prior reports of anthrax toxin-induced pyroptosis. However, in cells pre-treated with Bithionol, caspase-1 activation was completely suppressed, demonstrating that the compound directly inhibits this key inflammatory caspase.

To rule out the possibility that Bithionol interferes with the upstream components of the anthrax toxin pathway, additional experiments were performed to assess its effects on furin-mediated PA cleavage, cathepsin-B-dependent endosomal escape, and LF-mediated MAPKK cleavage. Western blot analysis of MAPKK2 cleavage in toxin-treated cells revealed no difference in the extent of cleavage between Bithionol-treated and untreated cells, indicating that Bithionol does not inhibit the proteolytic activity of LF. Similarly, a FRET assay using a cathepsin-B-specific substrate showed no reduction in enzymatic activity in the presence of Bithionol, confirming that the compound does not interfere with endosomal acidification or lysosomal protease function. Furthermore, a hybrid toxin, FP59, which utilizes the PA receptor for cellular entry but kills cells through an LF-independent mechanism involving caspase-1 activation, was used to confirm that Bithionol’s protective effect is independent of LF’s enzymatic activity. In this model, Bithionol again conferred complete protection, reinforcing the conclusion that its mechanism of action is centered on caspase inhibition rather than toxin neutralization. These findings collectively demonstrate that Bithionol protects cells from anthrax toxin by specifically inhibiting caspase-1 activation, without affecting the toxin’s ability to bind, internalize, or cleave its primary substrates. This selective inhibition of downstream host effectors, rather than upstream pathogen components, underscores the therapeutic advantage of host-directed intervention and provides a mechanistic basis for the broad-spectrum efficacy of Bithionol.

### Example 7. Bithionol Inhibits Ricin and Botulinum Neurotoxin A-Induced Death In Vitro and In Vivo

To assess the efficacy of Bithionol against ricin, a potent plant-derived toxin that induces caspase-dependent apoptosis following retrograde transport to the endoplasmic reticulum, K562 human leukemia cells were pre-treated with increasing concentrations of Bithionol for two hours prior to exposure to 0.4 nanograms per milliliter of ricin. After 24 hours, cell viability was assessed by flow cytometry using forward and side scatter gating to distinguish live from dead cells. Bithionol significantly increased cell survival in a dose-dependent manner, with an EC50 of 10 micromolar, demonstrating its ability to protect cells from ricin-induced cytotoxicity. To determine whether this protection was mediated through inhibition of host caspases or direct interference with ricin’s ribosome-inactivating activity, a biochemical assay was performed using purified ricin enzyme and a synthetic rRNA substrate. No inhibition of ricin’s enzymatic activity was observed in the presence of Bithionol, confirming that the compound does not directly neutralize the toxin but instead acts on downstream host pathways.

To evaluate the in vivo efficacy of Bithionol against botulinum neurotoxin serotype A (BoNT/A), a lethal intoxication model was established in Swiss Webster mice. Animals were orally administered a lethal dose of BoNT/A complex (3 micrograms per mouse) in the presence or absence of Bithionol (6.0 milligrams per kilogram of body weight). All untreated mice succumbed to intoxication within 72 hours, exhibiting classic symptoms of botulism, including progressive paralysis, respiratory distress, and the characteristic “wasp waist” posture. In contrast, all mice treated with Bithionol survived the full seven-day observation period without displaying any clinical signs of intoxication. To confirm that Bithionol’s protective effect was not due to direct inhibition of BoNT/A’s proteolytic activity, a FRET assay was performed using a synthetic SNAP-25 substrate conjugated with a FITC fluorophore and a DABCYL quencher. Purified BoNT/A light chain was incubated with the substrate in the presence or absence of Bithionol, and fluorescence was measured over 50 minutes. No difference in the rate of substrate cleavage was observed between treated and untreated conditions, demonstrating that Bithionol does not inhibit the enzymatic activity of the toxin. These results confirm that Bithionol protects against botulinum intoxication by inhibiting host caspases activated downstream of SNAP-25 cleavage, thereby preventing the apoptotic cascade that leads to neuronal death and systemic paralysis. The complete survival of Bithionol-treated mice, combined with the absence of toxin inhibition in biochemical assays, establishes Bithionol as a highly effective host-directed therapeutic for botulinum intoxication and provides compelling evidence for its potential use in emergency medical countermeasures.

### Example 8. Bithionol Acts as a Zika Virus Inhibitor

To investigate whether Bithionol’s caspase-inhibitory activity extends to viral pathogens, the efficacy of the compound was evaluated against two genetically distinct strains of Zika virus (ZIKV): the African lineage strain DAK AR D 41525 and the Asian lineage strain PRVABC59. Vero E6 monkey kidney cells and primary human astrocytes were pre-treated with increasing concentrations of Bithionol for two hours prior to infection at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.5 and 15, respectively. After 48 hours, cells were fixed and stained with a monoclonal antibody against the flavivirus envelope protein, followed by a fluorescently labeled secondary antibody, and nuclei were counterstained with Hoechst. High-content imaging was performed using an Opera Phenix high-throughput microscope, and the percentage of infected cells was quantified using Harmony and Acapella PE software. Bithionol significantly reduced the abundance of infected cells in a dose-dependent manner, with EC50 values of 6.7 micromolar for PRVABC59 in Vero E6 cells, 5.5 micromolar for DAK AR D 41525 in Vero E6 cells, and 6.3 micromolar for DAK AR D 41525 in human astrocytes. These results demonstrate that Bithionol is effective against both major lineages of ZIKV and retains potency in human neural cell types, which are primary targets of viral replication and pathogenesis.

To determine whether the antiviral effect of Bithionol is mediated through caspase inhibition, the expression of cleaved caspase-3 was assessed in infected cells by immunoblotting. In untreated infected cells, a marked increase in caspase-3 cleavage was observed, consistent with prior reports of ZIKV-induced apoptosis. In cells treated with Bithionol, this cleavage was substantially reduced in a concentration-dependent manner, correlating with the decrease in viral protein expression. Furthermore, no direct inhibition of ZIKV protease activity was detected in biochemical assays using purified viral NS2B-NS3 protease and a fluorogenic substrate, confirming that Bithionol does not act as a direct antiviral agent but instead inhibits host pathways required for viral propagation. These findings suggest that ZIKV exploits caspase activation to facilitate viral replication, possibly through the cleavage of host proteins involved in innate immune signaling, autophagy, or viral assembly. The ability of Bithionol to inhibit this process without directly targeting the virus provides a compelling mechanism for its broad-spectrum antiviral activity and underscores its potential as a therapeutic agent for emerging flaviviral infections. The fact that Bithionol achieves effective concentrations in the brain and crosses the placental barrier further supports its potential use in treating neuroinvasive ZIKV infection and preventing congenital transmission, making it a uniquely promising candidate for clinical deployment in pregnant populations.

### Example 9. Material and Methods

All bacterial toxins, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, anthrax lethal factor, diphtheria toxin, and botulinum neurotoxin A, were obtained from List Biological Laboratories (Campbell, CA). Ricin was sourced from Vector Laboratories, and the hybrid toxin FP59 was generously provided by Dr. Stephen Leppla of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases. The Clinical Compound Library (CCL) was procured from the Johns Hopkins University Bloomberg School of Public Health, and Bithionol was repurchased from Sigma-Aldrich for validation studies. All compounds were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) at a concentration of 10 millimolar and stored at -20°C. Cell lines were maintained under standard conditions: RAW264.7 mouse macrophages and C32 human melanoma cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), penicillin, and streptomycin; human B-lymphoblastoid cells were grown in Iscove’s Modified Dulbecco’s Medium (IMDM) with 20% FBS; K562 cells were maintained in RPMI 1640 medium with 10% FBS; Vero E6 cells were cultured in Minimum Essential Medium (MEM) with 10% FBS; and primary human astrocytes were grown in Astrocyte Basal Medium supplemented with the AGM SingleQuot Kit (Lonza). All cell cultures were maintained at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere.

For cellular viability assays, cells were seeded at 10,000 cells per well in 96-well plates and allowed to adhere for 24 hours. Compounds were added at the indicated concentrations, followed by toxin exposure at concentrations previously determined to induce 80% cell death. Cell viability was assessed using either the MTT assay for macrophages and melanoma cells or the Alamar Blue assay for lymphoblastoid cells, as per manufacturer instructions. Fluorescence was measured using a SpectraMax 384 PLUS plate reader (Molecular Devices). For caspase FRET assays, cell lysates were prepared from RAW264.7 cells treated with exotoxin A for four hours, and reactions were performed in 96-well plates containing 16 micromolar substrate peptide, 6 micrograms per milliliter of lysate, and test compounds at 33 micromolar. Kinetic measurements were taken every five minutes for two hours at 37°C with excitation at 360 nm and emission at 460 nm. For recombinant caspase inhibition assays, one unit of purified human caspase-1 through -10 was incubated with substrate and Bithionol in 50 microliter reactions, and fluorescence was measured using a BioVision caspase assay kit. For MAPKK2 cleavage assays, RAW264.7 cells were treated with 0.5 micrograms per milliliter of PA and LF for three hours, lysed in RIPA buffer, and subjected to SDS-PAGE followed by immunoblotting with anti-MAPKK2 and anti-tubulin antibodies. Band intensities were quantified using the Odyssey infrared imaging system (LI-COR Biosciences). Cathepsin B and caspase-1 activities were measured using commercial kits from EMD Millipore and BioVision, respectively, following manufacturer protocols. For botulinum neurotoxin A FRET assays, 5 micromolar SNAPtide substrate was incubated with 5 nanomolar BoNT/A light chain in the presence of 33 micromolar Bithionol, and fluorescence was measured at 490 nm excitation and 523 nm emission over 50 minutes. For Zika virus infection assays, Vero E6 cells and human astrocytes were infected at MOI 0.5 and 15, respectively, fixed in formalin after 48 hours, stained with anti-flavivirus envelope antibody and Dylight488-conjugated secondary antibody, and imaged using the Opera Phenix system. All experiments were performed in triplicate, and data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance was determined using GraphPad Prism software with a P-value threshold of 0.05. Animal studies were conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Western Regional Research Center Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC), and euthanasia was performed in accordance with the American Veterinary Medical Association guidelines.
