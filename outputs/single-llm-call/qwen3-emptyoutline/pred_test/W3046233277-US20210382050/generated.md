# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Autoimmune diseases represent a complex and heterogeneous class of chronic conditions characterized by the loss of immune tolerance to self-antigens, resulting in persistent inflammation and progressive tissue damage across nearly every organ system. Collectively, these disorders affect an estimated 15 to 24 million individuals in the United States alone, with prevalence rates continuing to rise due to environmental, genetic, and immunological factors that remain incompletely understood. Despite decades of research and the development of numerous immunomodulatory therapies, the clinical management of autoimmune conditions remains largely reactive rather than predictive. Current diagnostic paradigms rely heavily on the appearance of overt clinical symptoms, structural damage detectable by imaging modalities such as magnetic resonance imaging, or nonspecific biomarkers that lack sensitivity to early immunological perturbations. By the time these indicators manifest, significant and often irreversible tissue injury has already occurred, limiting therapeutic efficacy and necessitating lifelong, broad-spectrum immunosuppression that carries substantial risks of infection, malignancy, and metabolic complications.

In the context of relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS), a prototypical organ-specific autoimmune disorder targeting the central nervous system, this diagnostic delay is particularly pronounced. Approximately 60% of patients experience progressive disability leading to loss of ambulatory independence within fifteen years of diagnosis, despite the availability of disease-modifying therapies that reduce relapse frequency and slow lesion accumulation on MRI. However, these therapies are not universally effective, exhibit marked interindividual variability in response, and must be administered continuously over extended periods, often resulting in cumulative toxicity. Furthermore, MRI, while valuable for visualizing demyelinated plaques and blood-brain barrier disruption, provides no insight into the underlying cellular and molecular dynamics driving disease activity. Histopathological analyses of postmortem CNS tissue have revealed heterogeneous patterns of immune cell infiltration, including distinct contributions from T-cell subsets, myeloid cells, and innate lymphoid populations, suggesting that different immunological endotypes may underlie clinical phenotypes and dictate differential responses to therapy. Yet, no clinically viable method exists to sample or monitor these dynamic, tissue-resident immune processes in living patients without invasive neurosurgical procedures.

Peripheral blood, the most accessible biological compartment for immune monitoring, has been extensively studied as a surrogate for CNS inflammation in MS and other autoimmune conditions. However, the majority of circulating leukocytes originate from systemic lymphoid organs and do not reflect the local immunological milieu within inflamed target tissues. Gene expression profiling of peripheral blood mononuclear cells in MS patients has yielded inconsistent results, with many differentially expressed genes showing weak or indirect associations with pathogenic mechanisms, and a substantial proportion of samples yielding inconclusive or non-reproducible data. This disconnect arises because blood serves as a conduit for immune cell trafficking rather than a site of active tissue-specific immune regulation. Consequently, attempts to derive predictive or diagnostic signatures from circulating biomarkers have largely failed to translate into clinically actionable tools. The inability to detect preclinical immune dysregulation—before symptom onset or radiological evidence of damage—represents a critical gap in autoimmune disease management, preventing the implementation of precision interventions that could halt disease progression at its earliest, most reversible stage.

The limitations of current monitoring strategies are further compounded by the absence of reliable biomarkers for treatment response. Clinicians often resort to trial-and-error approaches, switching between immunomodulatory agents based on clinical deterioration or radiographic progression, which may occur months after the underlying immunological failure has begun. This delay not only permits ongoing tissue injury but also increases the risk of developing treatment-resistant disease states. There is an urgent need for a minimally invasive, longitudinally accessible, and biologically relevant biosensor capable of detecting real-time changes in the local immune environment within affected tissues, providing early warning of disease reactivation, and enabling objective assessment of therapeutic efficacy. Such a tool would transform autoimmune disease from a condition managed reactively to one that is proactively controlled through timely, personalized interventions.

Recent advances in tissue engineering have demonstrated that biomaterial implants can serve as dynamic interfaces between the host immune system and the external environment, recruiting and retaining immune cells in a manner that mirrors endogenous inflammatory responses. Subcutaneous implants composed of biocompatible, porous scaffolds have been shown to undergo vascularization and cellular infiltration, forming ectopic tissue niches that reflect systemic physiological and pathological states, including those associated with metabolic disease, cancer, and aging. These engineered microenvironments, while not identical to native target organs, provide a stable, reproducible, and accessible platform for sampling immune cell phenotypes, cytokine profiles, and gene expression patterns that are otherwise confined to deep or surgically inaccessible tissues. The hypothesis that such engineered niches could serve as surrogate biosensors for immune activity in distant organs—particularly those protected by anatomical barriers such as the blood-brain barrier—has remained untested in the context of autoimmune neuroinflammation until now. The present invention addresses this unmet clinical need by introducing a novel, implantable immunological niche that captures and amplifies the molecular and cellular signatures of autoimmune activity within the central nervous system, enabling early detection of disease onset, prediction of relapse, and real-time monitoring of therapeutic response through a simple, repeatable subcutaneous biopsy procedure.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel and non-invasive method for monitoring immune dysregulation associated with autoimmune diseases through the use of an implantable immunological niche that dynamically reflects the pathogenic immune state of distant target tissues. The invention comprises a biocompatible, porous scaffold implanted subcutaneously in a subject, wherein the scaffold is configured to induce the formation of a vascularized, cell-infiltrated tissue niche that recapitulates key immunological features of autoimmune inflammation occurring in inaccessible organs, such as the central nervous system. This engineered niche serves as a biosensor capable of detecting molecular and cellular changes indicative of disease activity prior to the onset of clinical symptoms, during remission, and upon relapse, thereby enabling preemptive therapeutic intervention and real-time assessment of treatment efficacy.

The scaffold is fabricated from a biodegradable, porous polymer such as poly(ε-caprolactone), with pore architecture and surface properties optimized to promote sustained infiltration of innate and adaptive immune cells, including monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells, and T lymphocytes, while supporting vascular integration and extracellular matrix deposition. Upon implantation, the niche undergoes progressive cellular colonization and molecular reprogramming in response to systemic immune stimuli, mirroring the gene expression profiles and phenotypic shifts observed in inflamed target tissues. The invention further includes a defined molecular signature composed of a panel of genes whose expression levels within the niche are consistently and significantly altered during autoimmune activation, including but not limited to TREM1, CD163, IL1B, IL1F9, S100A9, and additional genes identified through high-throughput transcriptomic analysis and machine learning-based pattern recognition. This signature is quantifiable via standardized molecular assays such as quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction and is used to generate a composite disease activity score derived from unsupervised and supervised computational models, including singular value decomposition and bootstrap aggregated decision tree ensembles, which reliably distinguish between healthy, presymptomatic, symptomatic, remitting, and relapsing immune states.

The invention enables the detection of autoimmune disease activity before the emergence of clinical manifestations, as demonstrated by the identification of a robust gene expression signature in the immunological niche during the presymptomatic phase of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, a well-established model of multiple sclerosis. The signature remains stable across different disease induction modalities, including adoptive transfer of autoreactive T cells and active immunization with myelin peptides, and is distinct from gene expression patterns observed in peripheral blood, spleen, or lymph nodes, thereby confirming its tissue-specific origin and diagnostic specificity. The niche further demonstrates dynamic responsiveness to disease progression and resolution, with gene expression profiles reverting toward baseline during remission and reactivating in a manner consistent with disease relapse following secondary antigen exposure. This temporal fidelity allows for the prediction of impending relapse with high accuracy, enabling clinicians to initiate targeted, short-duration therapies—such as pulsed corticosteroids or antigen-specific tolerogenic nanoparticles—prior to irreversible tissue damage.

Moreover, the invention provides a means to evaluate the immunological efficacy of therapeutic interventions by detecting normalization of the molecular signature within the niche following treatment. In experimental models, administration of antigen-loaded tolerogenic nanoparticles resulted in the complete abrogation of disease symptoms and the concurrent restoration of the gene expression profile to that of healthy controls, whereas control nanoparticles lacking the relevant antigen failed to modulate the signature or prevent disease progression. This capability allows for the objective, molecular-level assessment of treatment response within days of intervention, far earlier than clinical or radiological endpoints can be observed, thereby facilitating personalized therapeutic optimization and reducing the need for prolonged, ineffective, or toxic regimens.

The implantable immunological niche is designed for repeated, minimally invasive biopsy under local anesthesia, permitting longitudinal monitoring without compromising the integrity of native tissues or requiring surgical intervention in deep organs. The scaffold is biocompatible, non-immunogenic, and engineered to remain stable and functional for extended periods, enabling chronic surveillance in both preclinical and clinical settings. The method of the invention is applicable to a broad spectrum of organ-specific autoimmune diseases, including but not limited to multiple sclerosis, type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and autoimmune encephalitis, as the underlying principle—capturing tissue-specific immune dynamics through an accessible, engineered microenvironment—is generalizable across inflammatory conditions. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in autoimmune disease management, transitioning from reactive, symptom-driven care to proactive, biomarker-guided precision medicine, with the potential to prevent disease progression, reduce treatment burden, and improve long-term outcomes for millions of patients worldwide.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to a method and system for monitoring immune dysregulation in autoimmune diseases through the use of an implantable immunological niche that functions as a dynamic biosensor for tissue-specific immune activity. The invention is based on the discovery that subcutaneously implanted porous scaffolds, upon vascularization and cellular infiltration, form a stable, reproducible, and biologically relevant microenvironment that faithfully recapitulates the molecular and cellular signatures of autoimmune inflammation occurring in distant, inaccessible target tissues such as the central nervous system. This engineered niche, which is distinct from naturally occurring tertiary lymphoid organs yet shares key functional characteristics, serves as a surrogate site for the detection of preclinical immune activation, prediction of disease relapse, and real-time assessment of therapeutic response. The scaffold is fabricated from a biodegradable polymer, such as poly(ε-caprolactone), with a controlled pore size, surface chemistry, and mechanical architecture designed to promote the recruitment, retention, and phenotypic modulation of innate and adaptive immune cells, including monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells, neutrophils, and T lymphocytes, in a manner that mirrors the immunopathological processes observed in diseased organs.

The method of the invention involves the subcutaneous implantation of the scaffold into a subject, followed by a period of tissue integration and vascularization, typically lasting between seven and fourteen days, during which the scaffold becomes infiltrated with host-derived immune cells and stromal components. Once the niche is established, a biopsy of the implant and its surrounding encapsulating tissue is performed at predetermined intervals or in response to clinical suspicion of disease activity. The biopsy sample is processed for molecular analysis, including RNA extraction and high-throughput gene expression profiling, using a predefined panel of genes whose expression levels are consistently and significantly altered during autoimmune activation. This gene panel, identified through comparative transcriptomic analysis of diseased and healthy subjects, includes a core set of molecular markers such as TREM1, CD163, IL1B, IL1F9, and S100A9, each of which is associated with specific immune cell phenotypes and inflammatory pathways relevant to autoimmune pathogenesis. The expression levels of these genes are quantified using standardized quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction techniques, and the resulting data are processed through a computational algorithm that integrates unsupervised and supervised machine learning models to generate a composite disease activity score.

The unsupervised component of the algorithm employs singular value decomposition to reduce the dimensionality of the gene expression matrix and extract latent patterns of variation that distinguish between healthy and diseased states, independent of prior labeling. The supervised component utilizes a bootstrap aggregated decision tree ensemble to classify samples as healthy, presymptomatic, symptomatic, remitting, or relapsing based on a training dataset derived from longitudinal biopsies of subjects with known disease status. The output of these models is combined into a single metric that provides a continuous, quantitative measure of immune dysregulation within the niche, with values that correlate strongly with clinical disease severity, radiological findings, and histopathological evidence of tissue inflammation. This score is calibrated to establish thresholds for normal, subclinical, and pathological immune states, enabling clinicians to determine the need for intervention before overt symptoms emerge.

The invention is particularly advantageous in the context of relapsing-remitting autoimmune conditions, where the timing of therapeutic intervention is critical. In experimental models of multiple sclerosis, the immunological niche demonstrated the ability to detect immune activation up to two days before the onset of clinical signs, with the disease activity score rising significantly in presymptomatic animals despite the absence of neurological deficits. During remission, the gene expression profile within the niche reverted toward baseline levels, indicating resolution of the inflammatory process, and upon relapse triggered by secondary antigen exposure, the signature reactivated with a pattern indistinguishable from the initial disease onset. This temporal fidelity allows for the prediction of impending relapse with high sensitivity and specificity, enabling clinicians to administer short-duration, high-efficacy therapies—such as pulsed corticosteroids or antigen-specific tolerogenic nanoparticles—precisely when needed, thereby minimizing systemic exposure and reducing the risk of long-term adverse effects. The ability to intervene preemptively, rather than reactively, fundamentally alters the therapeutic paradigm, shifting the goal from symptom suppression to disease prevention.

The invention further provides a means to evaluate the efficacy of therapeutic interventions at the molecular level. In experimental studies, administration of tolerogenic nanoparticles loaded with disease-relevant antigens resulted in the rapid normalization of the gene expression signature within the niche, concomitant with the complete absence of clinical disease. In contrast, control nanoparticles lacking the target antigen failed to modulate the signature, and disease progression proceeded unabated. This differential response demonstrates that the immunological niche does not merely reflect the presence of inflammation but is capable of detecting the functional immunological consequences of therapy, including the induction of immune tolerance, suppression of pathogenic cell phenotypes, or restoration of homeostatic balance. This capability allows for the objective, real-time assessment of treatment response, eliminating the need for prolonged observation periods and reducing the reliance on subjective clinical evaluations or delayed radiological endpoints. The system thus enables a precision medicine approach, in which therapy is tailored not only to the patient’s disease subtype but also to their individual immunological response, facilitating the selection of the most effective agent and the discontinuation of ineffective or toxic regimens at the earliest possible timepoint.

The scaffold itself is engineered to ensure long-term stability, biocompatibility, and ease of retrieval. It is composed of a biodegradable polymer such as poly(ε-caprolactone), which degrades slowly over months to years, maintaining structural integrity throughout the monitoring period while avoiding foreign body reactions. The scaffold is fabricated using a salt-leaching technique to generate a highly porous, interconnected architecture with pore diameters optimized for immune cell migration and vascular ingrowth, typically ranging between 50 and 200 micrometers. The surface is rendered hydrophilic through controlled chemical modification to enhance protein adsorption and cell adhesion, while minimizing fibrotic encapsulation. The scaffold may be implanted using a minimally invasive percutaneous procedure under local anesthesia, with the implant positioned in a subcutaneous location such as the flank or abdominal wall, where it is easily accessible for repeated biopsy. The biopsy procedure involves a small incision, extraction of the implant along with its surrounding tissue capsule, and closure of the wound with absorbable sutures, allowing for rapid recovery and repeat sampling at intervals as short as one week. The tissue sample is immediately processed for RNA extraction, with preservation in RNA-stabilizing solutions to maintain transcriptomic integrity, and analyzed using standardized, high-throughput molecular platforms such as TaqMan OpenArray or 384-well quantitative PCR systems.

The gene signature employed by the invention is derived from a rigorous, data-driven analysis of transcriptomic profiles obtained from multiple disease models and time points, ensuring robustness across genetic backgrounds, disease induction methods, and experimental conditions. The signature is not dependent on a single gene but rather on a combinatorial pattern of expression across a panel of 20 to 30 genes, each of which contributes to the overall predictive power of the model. The inclusion of both upregulated and downregulated genes enhances the stability and generalizability of the signature, reducing susceptibility to technical variability or biological noise. The signature has been validated across multiple independent cohorts and demonstrates high reproducibility, with inter-assay coefficients of variation below 10% and diagnostic accuracy exceeding 90% in distinguishing between healthy and diseased states. The signature is also distinct from peripheral blood gene expression profiles, which fail to capture the same patterns of dysregulation, confirming that the immunological niche provides unique, tissue-specific information not obtainable through conventional blood-based biomarkers.

The invention is not limited to multiple sclerosis or neuroinflammatory conditions. The underlying principle—that an engineered, subcutaneous tissue niche can serve as a dynamic biosensor for immune activity in distant organs—is broadly applicable to any autoimmune disease characterized by localized inflammation and immune cell infiltration. For example, in type 1 diabetes, the niche could reflect islet-specific T-cell activation and macrophage polarization, while in rheumatoid arthritis, it could mirror synovial inflammation and cytokine production. In inflammatory bowel disease, the niche may capture gut-homing lymphocyte phenotypes and epithelial barrier dysfunction markers. The scaffold may be further modified to incorporate specific chemokines, growth factors, or extracellular matrix components to bias immune cell recruitment toward particular subsets relevant to a given disease, thereby enhancing the sensitivity and specificity of the biosensor. The system may also be adapted for continuous monitoring through the integration of wireless, implantable sensors capable of detecting cytokine concentrations or pH changes within the niche in real time, although the current embodiment relies on periodic biopsy for molecular analysis.

The invention further encompasses a diagnostic kit comprising the implantable scaffold, a standardized protocol for implantation and biopsy, a defined panel of primers and probes for quantifying the signature genes, and software for computing the disease activity score from raw gene expression data. The kit is designed for use in both clinical and research settings, with the scaffold provided in sterile, pre-packaged form and the software accessible via secure cloud-based platforms or local installation. The method of the invention may be implemented in conjunction with existing clinical workflows, with biopsy results informing decisions regarding initiation, escalation, or cessation of therapy. The invention thus represents a transformative advance in autoimmune disease management, providing a reliable, reproducible, and minimally invasive tool for the early detection of immune dysregulation, the prediction of disease relapse, and the objective monitoring of therapeutic response, thereby enabling a new era of precision, proactive, and personalized medicine.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, the implantable immunological niche was evaluated for its ability to detect early immune dysregulation in a murine model of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis induced by adoptive transfer of autoreactive T cells. Microporous scaffolds composed of poly(ε-caprolactone) were fabricated using a salt-leaching technique to generate a porous structure with interconnected pores averaging 120 micrometers in diameter. Female SJL/J mice, aged six weeks, were anesthetized with isoflurane, and two scaffolds were implanted subcutaneously along the dorsal flank. After a 14-day period of tissue integration and vascularization, mice received an intraperitoneal injection of 30 million splenocytes and lymph node cells isolated from donor mice immunized with the myelin proteolipid protein peptide PLP139–151, while control mice received cells reactive to the non-self antigen ovalbumin (OVA323–339). Biopsies of the implanted scaffolds were performed on days 7 and 9 post-transfer, prior to and during the onset of clinical symptoms, respectively. RNA was extracted from the harvested tissue and analyzed using a high-throughput OpenArray platform containing 632 inflammation-related genes. Comparative transcriptomic analysis revealed 130 genes differentially expressed between diseased and control mice, with a subset of 21 genes selected based on fold change, expression stability, and elastic net regularization scores. A radar plot of the log2 fold change for these 21 genes demonstrated a consistent and distinct pattern in diseased mice compared to controls, regardless of whether the biopsy occurred at the presymptomatic (day 7) or symptomatic (day 9) stage. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of the gene expression data clearly segregated diseased and control mice into two distinct clusters, with no overlap between groups. The same pattern was not observed in peripheral blood samples, where clustering failed to distinguish between diseased and control animals. These findings demonstrate that the immunological niche captures a disease-specific molecular signature that is detectable before the emergence of clinical symptoms and is not reflected in circulating blood, establishing its utility as an early biosensor for autoimmune activation.

### Example 2

This example demonstrates the reproducibility and generalizability of the immunological niche signature across different disease induction modalities. In addition to the adoptive transfer model, an active immunization model of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis was employed, in which mice were immunized subcutaneously with an emulsion containing PLP139–151 peptide and complete Freund’s adjuvant. Biopsies of the implanted scaffolds were performed on days 7 and 13 post-immunization, corresponding to presymptomatic and symptomatic time points, respectively. Transcriptomic analysis of the niche tissue identified 222 differentially expressed genes between immunized and control mice. A refined signature of 25 genes was derived using the same computational criteria as in Example 1, and four of these genes—CD163, IL1B, IL1F9, and S100A9—overlapped with the signature identified in the adoptive transfer model. Despite differences in the immune activation kinetics and the inclusion of mycotoxin-induced systemic immune modulation in the active model, the computational scoring system based on the 25-gene signature successfully distinguished between healthy and diseased mice with high accuracy. The singular value decomposition and bootstrap aggregated decision tree ensemble scores for the active model showed clear separation between groups, mirroring the results from the adoptive transfer model. These findings confirm that the immunological niche responds to diverse immunological triggers with a consistent and identifiable molecular signature, validating its utility as a platform-independent biosensor for autoimmune activity.

### Example 3

This example illustrates the ability of the immunological niche to reflect disease dynamics during remission and relapse. Following induction of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis via adoptive transfer, mice were monitored until clinical symptoms resolved, at which point a biopsy was performed on day 21 to assess the niche during remission. A second adoptive transfer of T cells reactive to a different epitope, PLP178–191, was administered on day 23 to simulate epitope spreading and trigger relapse, with a second biopsy performed on day 28. Gene expression analysis revealed that the molecular signature of the niche during remission closely resembled that of healthy control mice, with all 21 signature genes returning to baseline expression levels. In contrast, upon relapse, the signature reactivated with a pattern nearly identical to that observed during the initial disease onset, including upregulation of TREM1, CD163, and IL1B. The composite disease activity score, derived from the combination of singular value decomposition and bootstrap aggregated decision tree ensemble metrics, was significantly reduced during remission and returned to levels comparable to acute disease during relapse. Clinical scores followed a parallel trajectory, with remission corresponding to low neurological deficits and relapse associated with a rapid return of severe symptoms. These results demonstrate that the immunological niche is not a static biomarker but a dynamic sensor capable of tracking the cyclical nature of autoimmune disease, providing a reliable indicator of disease state transitions that can inform the timing of therapeutic intervention.

### Example 4

This example evaluates the specificity of the immunological niche signature by comparing gene expression profiles in the niche with those in peripheral blood, spleen, and lymphoid tissues. In a cohort of mice undergoing adoptive transfer of autoreactive T cells, biopsies of the implanted scaffold, blood, spleen, and inguinal lymph nodes were collected on day 9 post-transfer. RNA was extracted and analyzed using the same 21-gene signature panel. The niche tissue exhibited a clear and consistent upregulation of the signature genes, while blood samples showed minimal and inconsistent changes, with approximately 18% of samples yielding undetectable levels of key transcripts such as EREG. Hierarchical clustering of the blood data failed to separate diseased from control mice, whereas clustering of niche data produced two distinct, non-overlapping clusters. Flow cytometric analysis of immune cell populations in each tissue revealed that while the absolute numbers of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells were highest in the spleen and lymph nodes, the proportions of pathogenic Th1 cells were significantly elevated only in the niche and spinal cord, with no corresponding increase in lymphoid organs. This indicates that the niche captures a tissue-specific immune phenotype that is not reflected in systemic lymphoid compartments, reinforcing its role as a localized biosensor rather than a general indicator of immune activation.

### Example 5

This example demonstrates the predictive power of the immunological niche for early therapeutic intervention. Mice were implanted with scaffolds and subjected to adoptive transfer of autoreactive T cells as described in Example 1. On day 7, prior to the onset of clinical symptoms, a biopsy was performed, and the disease activity score was calculated using the composite algorithm. Based on the elevated score, mice were randomized to receive either daily intraperitoneal injections of dexamethasone (5 mg/kg) from days 7 to 11 or a single intravenous injection of 2.5 mg of PLP139–151-loaded poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) nanoparticles. Control mice received no treatment. Clinical scores were monitored daily until day 18. Both treatment groups exhibited significantly reduced disease severity compared to untreated controls, with dexamethasone preventing symptom progression during the treatment window but showing a rebound in clinical scores after cessation, while nanoparticle-treated mice maintained near-normal clinical scores throughout the observation period. The gene expression signature in the niche of treated mice normalized rapidly following intervention, with scores returning to levels indistinguishable from healthy controls. These findings demonstrate that the immunological niche not only predicts disease onset but also enables preemptive treatment that can prevent clinical manifestations and alter the long-term disease trajectory, validating its utility as a guide for precision therapeutic decision-making.

### Example 6

This example evaluates the capacity of the immunological niche to monitor the efficacy of antigen-specific immunotherapy. Mice were implanted with scaffolds and adoptively transferred with autoreactive T cells reactive to PLP139–151. Two days post-transfer, mice received a single intravenous injection of either PLP139–151-loaded nanoparticles (effective treatment), OVA323–339-loaded nanoparticles (ineffective treatment), or no nanoparticles (control). Biopsies were performed on day 9, and gene expression was analyzed using the 21-gene signature. Mice receiving PLP-loaded nanoparticles exhibited a gene expression profile nearly identical to that of control mice injected with OVA-reactive T cells and OVA-loaded nanoparticles, indicating successful immune modulation. In contrast, mice receiving OVA-loaded nanoparticles displayed a signature indistinguishable from untreated diseased mice, with high expression of TREM1, IL1B, and S100A9. The composite disease activity score was significantly lower in the effective treatment group compared to the ineffective and control groups. Clinical scores mirrored these findings, with the effective treatment group showing minimal neurological deficits and the other groups exhibiting severe disease. Receiver operating characteristic analysis of the signature scores yielded an area under the curve of 0.97–1.00, demonstrating exceptional diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing responders from nonresponders. This example confirms that the immunological niche can serve as a real-time biomarker for therapeutic efficacy, enabling rapid identification of effective treatments and avoiding prolonged exposure to ineffective or toxic agents.

### Example 7

This example investigates the cellular composition of the immunological niche and its relationship to the observed gene expression signature. Biopsies of the implanted scaffolds were collected from mice at day 9 post-adoptive transfer and processed for flow cytometry to quantify immune cell populations. The niche was dominated by innate immune cells, with CD11b+ F4/80+ macrophages and CD11c+ dendritic cells comprising over 70% of the CD45+ population. T cells, including CD4+ and CD8+ subsets, were present but in lower proportions. No significant differences in the absolute numbers or proportions of these cell types were observed between diseased and control mice. However, intracellular cytokine staining revealed a marked increase in IFNγ-producing Th1 cells within the niche of diseased mice, consistent with the upregulation of Th1-associated genes such as TBX21 and IFNG in the transcriptomic data. Similarly, expression of TREM1 and CD163 correlated with the presence of a distinct subset of macrophages exhibiting an inflammatory, M1-like phenotype. These findings indicate that the gene signature reflects phenotypic shifts within resident immune cell populations rather than changes in cell trafficking or recruitment, underscoring the niche’s ability to capture functional immune states rather than mere cellularity.

### Example 8

This example assesses the stability and reproducibility of the immunological niche over time and across multiple biopsy events. A cohort of mice underwent implantation of the scaffold and induction of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis via adoptive transfer. Three sequential biopsies were performed on days 7, 14, and 21, with each biopsy harvested from a separate implant to avoid tissue disruption. Gene expression analysis of each biopsy revealed consistent patterns of signature gene expression across all time points, with no significant variation attributable to the biopsy procedure itself. The composite disease activity score remained stable within individual mice across repeated sampling, with inter-biopsy coefficients of variation below 8%. Histological examination of the scaffold tissue confirmed consistent vascularization, cellular infiltration, and absence of fibrotic encapsulation throughout the study period. These findings demonstrate that the immunological niche is a robust and reliable platform for longitudinal monitoring, capable of providing consistent, repeatable data over multiple sampling events without degradation in performance or structural integrity.

### Example 9

This example demonstrates the translational feasibility of the immunological niche for human application. A biocompatible, porous scaffold composed of poly(ε-caprolactone) was fabricated in clinical-grade materials and sterilized according to ISO 13485 standards. The scaffold was implanted subcutaneously in non-human primates (Macaca mulatta) under sterile conditions, and biopsies were performed at 14, 28, and 56 days post-implantation. Histological analysis confirmed robust vascularization, infiltration of mononuclear cells, and absence of foreign body reaction. RNA extracted from the tissue was analyzed using a human-specific version of the gene signature panel, which was developed by cross-referencing murine orthologs with human homologs and validating expression patterns in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells from patients with multiple sclerosis. The humanized signature demonstrated consistent expression patterns in response to systemic immune activation induced by lipopolysaccharide challenge, with a clear separation between baseline and activated states. These findings confirm that the immunological niche concept is translatable to human physiology, with the scaffold demonstrating biocompatibility, durability, and the capacity to capture immune-relevant molecular signals in a large mammalian model, paving the way for clinical trials in patients with autoimmune diseases.
