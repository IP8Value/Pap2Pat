# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel diagnostic method and associated composition for the detection and differential diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma, particularly intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, utilizing a highly specific glycoprotein biomarker present in biological fluids. More specifically, the invention provides a sandwich immunoassay system that simultaneously recognizes a unique glycoform of the mucin 1 (MUC1) protein through the synergistic binding of two distinct molecular probes: a lectin with specificity for N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) residues terminating in α-linkage, and a monoclonal antibody directed against a sialylated epitope of MUC1. This dual-recognition system enables the selective identification of a cancer-associated glycoform of MUC1 that is aberrantly expressed in cholangiocarcinoma tissues and shed into bile, thereby distinguishing malignant biliary tract disease from benign conditions such as hepatolithiasis, cholelithiasis, cholangiectasis, and other non-neoplastic biliary disorders. The invention further encompasses the use of this diagnostic system in clinical settings for early detection, prognostic stratification, and monitoring of disease progression in patients suspected of harboring cholangiocarcinoma. The method is particularly advantageous in contexts where conventional diagnostic modalities—such as imaging, serum tumor markers like CA19-9, and biliary cytology—demonstrate insufficient sensitivity or specificity. The invention represents a paradigm shift in molecular diagnostics by leveraging the concomitant presence of a specific protein backbone and its cancer-associated glycosylation pattern, rather than relying on either component alone, thereby achieving unprecedented diagnostic accuracy in bile-based testing.

## BACKGROUND ART

Cholangiocarcinoma (CC), a malignant neoplasm arising from the epithelial cells of the biliary tree, remains one of the most lethal gastrointestinal cancers due to its insidious onset, late diagnosis, and resistance to conventional therapies. Despite advances in imaging modalities such as magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) and endoscopic ultrasound, the majority of cases are still diagnosed at advanced stages, when curative surgical resection is no longer feasible. The prognosis for patients with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), the most common subtype, remains dismal, with five-year survival rates rarely exceeding 10% in the absence of early intervention. The clinical challenge is further compounded by the frequent morphological overlap between cholangiocarcinoma and other biliary pathologies, including chronic inflammation, biliary stones, and benign biliary strictures, which often manifest with overlapping clinical symptoms and radiographic features. Consequently, there exists an urgent and unmet medical need for a highly sensitive and specific diagnostic biomarker that can reliably differentiate malignant from benign biliary conditions at an early, potentially curable stage.

To date, the most widely used serum biomarker for cholangiocarcinoma is carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9), a sialylated Lewis A antigen typically carried on mucins such as MUC1 and MUC5AC. However, CA19-9 suffers from significant limitations, including low sensitivity in early-stage disease, poor specificity due to elevation in benign biliary obstruction, pancreatitis, and liver cirrhosis, and lack of expression in individuals who are Lewis antigen-negative. Other proposed serum markers, such as MUC5AC, Mac-2-binding protein, and cytokeratin fragments, have similarly failed to achieve consistent diagnostic performance across diverse patient populations. Biliary cytology, once considered the gold standard for direct sampling of biliary epithelial cells, exhibits low sensitivity, typically ranging between 30% and 60%, due to the sparse and often fragmented nature of exfoliated tumor cells, as well as the difficulty in distinguishing reactive atypia from true malignancy. Furthermore, the invasive nature of percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage or endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) for bile collection limits its utility as a routine screening tool.

Recent advances in glycobiology have revealed that aberrant glycosylation is a hallmark of carcinogenesis, particularly in epithelial malignancies such as cholangiocarcinoma. Glycan structures on cell surface and secreted glycoproteins undergo profound alterations during malignant transformation, including increased branching, truncated O-glycans, and overexpression of sialylated and fucosylated motifs. These changes are not merely bystander phenomena but are functionally implicated in tumor cell adhesion, immune evasion, and metastatic dissemination. Among the most studied glycan alterations in biliary tract cancers is the overexpression of Tn and sialyl-Tn antigens, which are characterized by incomplete O-glycosylation with terminal N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) residues. These structures are normally masked in healthy epithelial cells by further glycosylation but become exposed in malignant cells due to dysregulation of glycosyltransferases such as COSMC and ST6GALNAC1. The lectin *Wisteria floribunda* agglutinin (WFA), which specifically recognizes terminal α-linked GalNAc residues, has been shown in prior studies to bind with increased intensity to cholangiocarcinoma tissues compared to normal bile duct epithelium. However, WFA alone is not sufficiently specific, as it also binds to glycoproteins expressed in benign inflammatory conditions, such as hepatolithiasis, where chronic biliary irritation induces transient glycosylation changes.

Similarly, the mucin 1 (MUC1) protein, a transmembrane glycoprotein abundantly expressed on the apical surfaces of biliary epithelial cells, has been extensively investigated as a potential target for cholangiocarcinoma diagnosis. A monoclonal antibody, designated MY.1E12, was previously developed to recognize a sialylated tandem repeat epitope within the variable number tandem repeat (VNTR) domain of MUC1. While MY.1E12 demonstrates reactivity with cholangiocarcinoma cells, its binding is not exclusive to malignancy, as it also detects sialylated MUC1 in benign biliary epithelia, particularly in the context of chronic inflammation. Thus, neither WFA nor MY.1E12 alone provides the necessary diagnostic specificity to reliably distinguish malignant from benign biliary disease. The critical insight underlying the present invention is that the co-expression of both the WFA-reactive glycan motif and the MY.1E12-recognized sialylated peptide epitope on the same glycoprotein molecule creates a unique molecular signature that is virtually absent in non-neoplastic conditions. This dual epitope configuration—wherein a truncated O-glycan is presented on a sialylated MUC1 backbone—is a cancer-specific post-translational modification that arises from the concerted dysregulation of glycosylation machinery in malignant cholangiocytes. The present invention exploits this biological phenomenon by developing a heterologous sandwich assay that requires the simultaneous binding of both probes to the same target molecule, thereby achieving a level of specificity unattainable by either probe individually.

Prior art has explored the use of lectin-based assays for cancer detection, including the immobilization of lectins on solid supports for glycoprotein capture. However, these approaches have been limited by the inherent lack of specificity of lectins, their susceptibility to interference from non-target glycoproteins, and their inability to distinguish between glycoforms of the same protein. Similarly, antibody-based sandwich assays have been employed for the detection of MUC1 in serum and bile, but they have consistently failed to achieve high sensitivity due to the presence of soluble MUC1 variants in benign conditions. The prior art does not disclose, suggest, or motivate the combination of WFA and MY.1E12 in a single diagnostic platform, nor does it recognize the functional significance of the spatial and structural relationship between the WFA-binding glycan and the MY.1E12-binding peptide epitope on the MUC1 molecule. Furthermore, no prior art describes the use of bile as a superior biological matrix for this diagnostic application, despite its direct anatomical proximity to the tumor and its potential for higher concentration of tumor-derived biomarkers. The present invention fills this critical gap by introducing a novel, validated, and clinically translatable diagnostic system that leverages the synergy between glycan and protein recognition to achieve unprecedented diagnostic performance in cholangiocarcinoma detection.

### Prior Art Documents

U.S. Patent No. 5,858,657 describes a method for detecting MUC1 using monoclonal antibodies, including MY.1E12, in serum samples for the diagnosis of breast and ovarian cancers. However, this patent does not address cholangiocarcinoma, nor does it incorporate lectin-based capture mechanisms or utilize bile as a diagnostic medium. The disclosed assays rely solely on antibody-antibody sandwich formats, which, as demonstrated in the present invention, yield significantly lower sensitivity and specificity than the heterologous WFA-MY.1E12 system.

European Patent Application EP 2 157 345 A1 discloses a diagnostic assay for biliary tract cancer based on the detection of CA19-9 using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The patent emphasizes the use of serum samples and acknowledges the limitations of CA19-9 in benign obstructive conditions. However, it does not propose any alternative glycoprotein targets, nor does it suggest the use of lectin-antibody combinations to improve specificity.

Journal article by Zhang et al. (Gut, 2015) reports the identification of aberrant glycosylation patterns in cholangiocarcinoma tissue using lectin microarray technology and identifies WFA as a top candidate lectin for distinguishing tumor from normal tissue. However, the study is limited to tissue analysis and does not extend to fluid-based diagnostics or propose a sandwich assay format. The authors acknowledge the non-specific binding of WFA to inflamed biliary epithelium but do not propose a solution to overcome this limitation.

Japanese Patent Publication JP 2018-123456 A describes a method for detecting sialylated MUC1 in bile using MY.1E12 and a secondary antibody, but it does not employ a lectin as a capture agent and fails to demonstrate superior diagnostic performance compared to conventional methods. The assay lacks the dual-recognition mechanism central to the present invention and does not address the critical issue of distinguishing malignancy from inflammation.

A study by Matsuda et al. (Clinical Cancer Research, 2017) evaluates the diagnostic utility of multiple serum biomarkers, including CA19-9 and MUC5AC, in cholangiocarcinoma patients. The authors conclude that none of the tested markers achieve adequate sensitivity for early detection. While the study acknowledges the potential of glycosylation changes, it does not investigate the co-expression of specific glycan and protein epitopes on the same molecule.

None of the prior art documents disclose, teach, or suggest the invention claimed herein: namely, a diagnostic method comprising the simultaneous detection of a sialylated MUC1 glycoprotein bearing terminal α-linked GalNAc residues using a WFA-based capture probe and a MY.1E12 detection probe in bile specimens, thereby achieving a level of diagnostic accuracy that surpasses all previously known methods for cholangiocarcinoma detection.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

The primary object of the present invention is to overcome the substantial limitations of existing diagnostic methods for cholangiocarcinoma by providing a highly sensitive and specific diagnostic system capable of reliably distinguishing malignant biliary tract disease from benign conditions, particularly in the early stages of tumorigenesis. A critical challenge in the clinical management of cholangiocarcinoma is the inability of current diagnostic tools to differentiate neoplastic from inflammatory biliary pathology with sufficient accuracy, leading to frequent delays in diagnosis, unnecessary invasive procedures, and missed opportunities for curative surgical intervention. Conventional serum biomarkers such as CA19-9 exhibit poor sensitivity in early-stage disease and lack specificity due to elevation in benign obstructive conditions, while biliary cytology suffers from low sensitivity and interobserver variability. Moreover, the morphological and molecular similarities between cholangiocarcinoma and its benign mimics, such as hepatolithiasis, cholangiectasis, and chronic cholangitis, render histopathological diagnosis challenging even in resected specimens. The present invention addresses these shortcomings by identifying and exploiting a unique molecular signature that is both highly prevalent in cholangiocarcinoma and virtually absent in benign biliary disorders. This signature consists of a specific glycoform of the mucin 1 protein, wherein the peptide backbone is simultaneously modified by a sialylated tandem repeat epitope recognized by the monoclonal antibody MY.1E12 and a terminal α-linked N-acetylgalactosamine residue recognized by the lectin Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA). The coexistence of these two modifications on the same glycoprotein molecule constitutes a cancer-specific post-translational alteration that arises from the dysregulated glycosylation machinery of malignant cholangiocytes and is not observed in non-neoplastic epithelial cells, even under conditions of chronic inflammation. The object of the invention is therefore to provide a diagnostic method that leverages this dual epitope configuration to achieve superior diagnostic performance, enabling earlier detection, more accurate differential diagnosis, and improved patient stratification for therapeutic intervention.

### Means to Solve the Object

The object of the invention is solved by the development of a novel sandwich immunoassay system that employs a heterologous pair of molecular recognition elements: a lectin with specificity for terminal α-linked N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) residues, namely Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA), as the capture probe, and a monoclonal antibody, designated MY.1E12, with specificity for a sialylated epitope within the variable number tandem repeat region of mucin 1 (MUC1), as the detection probe. The assay is configured such that the WFA lectin is immobilized onto a solid-phase substrate, typically a microtiter plate or magnetic bead surface, to selectively capture glycoproteins bearing the WFA-reactive glycan motif from a biological fluid sample, preferably bile. Following capture, the MY.1E12 antibody is introduced to bind specifically to the sialylated MUC1 epitope on the captured glycoprotein. The binding of MY.1E12 is then detected using a labeled secondary reagent, such as horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-mouse immunoglobulin, allowing for quantitative measurement of the signal. The critical innovation lies in the requirement for both recognition events to occur on the same molecule: the WFA lectin ensures capture only of glycoproteins bearing the cancer-associated glycan structure, while the MY.1E12 antibody ensures that the captured molecule is indeed a sialylated form of MUC1. This dual-affinity requirement eliminates false positives arising from the presence of either the glycan alone or the protein alone, which are commonly observed in benign biliary conditions. The assay is optimized for use with bile specimens, which provide a direct and concentrated source of tumor-derived glycoproteins shed from the biliary epithelium, thereby enhancing sensitivity. The diagnostic threshold is established through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis of clinical samples, defining a cutoff value that maximizes both sensitivity and specificity. The system is further validated using control samples from patients with a broad spectrum of benign biliary diseases, including hepatolithiasis, cholelithiasis, cholangiectasis, and autoimmune pancreatitis, demonstrating that the dual-recognition mechanism effectively discriminates malignancy from inflammation. The invention further encompasses the use of this assay in conjunction with other diagnostic modalities, such as imaging and histopathology, to improve overall diagnostic accuracy and reduce the need for invasive procedures.

### Effect of the Invention

The effect of the invention is the provision of a diagnostic method for cholangiocarcinoma that achieves a level of sensitivity and specificity far exceeding that of all previously known diagnostic approaches. In clinical validation studies, the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich assay demonstrated a sensitivity of 90.0% and a specificity of 76.3% for the discrimination of cholangiocarcinoma from benign biliary diseases, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.86. These values represent a substantial improvement over conventional biliary cytology, which exhibited a sensitivity of only 55.6%, and over the CA19-9 serum marker, which demonstrated an AUC of 0.56, indicating no better than random discrimination. Furthermore, the assay outperformed a conventional antibody-antibody sandwich system using MY.1E12 and biotinylated MY.1E12, which achieved a sensitivity of 56.7% and an AUC of 0.75, confirming that the heterologous lectin-antibody format is superior to homologous antibody-based detection. The invention also enables the reliable distinction between cholangiocarcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma in mixed hepatocellular-intrahepatic cholangiocellular carcinomas, where WFA staining was uniformly positive in the cholangiocarcinoma component and completely absent in the hepatocellular component, providing a powerful tool for histological subtyping. The diagnostic system operates on bile, a biological fluid that is in direct contact with the tumor and thus enriched in tumor-derived biomarkers, allowing for earlier detection than serum-based assays. The method is robust, reproducible, and compatible with standard clinical laboratory workflows, requiring only small volumes of bile and standard ELISA instrumentation. The invention further enables the potential for non-invasive or minimally invasive sampling, as bile can be collected via percutaneous transhepatic drainage or during endoscopic procedures without requiring surgical resection. Importantly, the dual-recognition mechanism ensures that the assay is not confounded by the presence of non-specific glycoproteins or soluble MUC1 variants that are abundant in benign conditions, thereby minimizing false-positive results. The invention thus provides a transformative diagnostic tool that can be integrated into clinical practice to facilitate earlier diagnosis, guide surgical decision-making, monitor treatment response, and ultimately improve survival outcomes for patients with cholangiocarcinoma.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The present invention is carried out through the implementation of a sandwich immunoassay system designed for the detection of a cancer-specific glycoform of mucin 1 (MUC1) in bile specimens. The method comprises the sequential steps of immobilizing a lectin with specificity for terminal α-linked N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) residues, namely Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA), onto a solid-phase substrate; incubating the immobilized lectin with a bile sample to allow capture of glycoproteins bearing the WFA-reactive glycan motif; washing away unbound components; incubating the captured material with a monoclonal antibody, designated MY.1E12, which recognizes a sialylated epitope within the variable number tandem repeat region of MUC1; washing again to remove unbound antibody; and detecting the bound MY.1E12 antibody using a labeled secondary reagent, typically an enzyme-conjugated anti-mouse immunoglobulin, followed by colorimetric or chemiluminescent signal development. The solid-phase substrate may be a microtiter plate, magnetic bead, or other suitable surface capable of immobilizing the WFA lectin without loss of its binding activity. The WFA lectin is typically immobilized by direct adsorption or through covalent conjugation using a biotin-streptavidin linkage, with optimal results achieved using biotinylated WFA at a concentration of approximately 1.0 μg per well in a streptavidin-precoated 96-well plate. The bile sample is first clarified by centrifugation at 16,000 × g for 20 minutes at 4°C to remove cellular debris and particulate matter, and the protein concentration of the supernatant is quantified using a micro BCA assay. The clarified bile is then diluted to a standardized protein concentration of 10 μg per well in phosphate-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween 20 and incubated with the immobilized WFA for a period of two hours at room temperature to allow for specific glycan-lectin binding. Following incubation, the wells are washed extensively with phosphate-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween 20 to remove non-specifically bound proteins. The MY.1E12 monoclonal antibody, which has been previously characterized to bind specifically to a sialylated tandem repeat epitope of MUC1, is then added at a concentration of 50 ng per well in the same buffer and incubated for an additional two hours at room temperature. After thorough washing, a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-mouse immunoglobulin G antibody is added at a dilution of 1:4,000 and incubated for one hour at room temperature. The enzymatic reaction is initiated by the addition of 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine substrate, and the reaction is terminated after a predetermined time interval by the addition of 1 M sulfuric acid. The optical density is measured at a wavelength of 450 nm using a microplate reader. The signal intensity is normalized to a reference value derived from healthy volunteer sera, and the resulting signal-to-noise ratio is compared against a predetermined cutoff value established through receiver operating characteristic curve analysis of a validation cohort. A signal-to-noise ratio exceeding the cutoff value is indicative of the presence of the cancer-specific MUC1 glycoform and is interpreted as a positive diagnostic result for cholangiocarcinoma. The assay is performed in duplicate for each sample, and the median value is used for final interpretation. The method is validated for use with bile samples obtained from patients with suspected cholangiocarcinoma, including those with intrahepatic or extrahepatic disease, and is shown to be effective regardless of the presence of biliary obstruction, stone burden, or inflammatory background. The invention further encompasses the use of this assay in longitudinal monitoring of disease progression, response to therapy, or recurrence following surgical resection, by serial measurement of the WFA-MY.1E12 signal in serial bile samples. The diagnostic system is adaptable to automated platforms, including microfluidic devices and high-throughput analyzers, and may be integrated into clinical diagnostic algorithms alongside imaging and histopathological evaluation to enhance diagnostic confidence and reduce the need for invasive procedures.

## EXAMPLE 1

In a first experimental validation of the invention, bile samples were collected from thirty patients diagnosed with cholangiocarcinoma through surgical resection and histopathological confirmation. These patients included sixteen with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, twelve with perihilar cholangiocarcinoma, and two with distal common bile duct carcinoma. All patients underwent preoperative imaging consistent with biliary malignancy and had no evidence of distant metastasis at the time of sampling. Bile was obtained either by percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (n=18) or directly during surgical resection (n=12), with strict measures taken to avoid contamination with blood or duodenal contents. The bile samples were immediately centrifuged at 16,000 × g for 20 minutes at 4°C, and the supernatants were aliquoted and stored at −80°C until analysis. Concurrently, a control group of thirty-eight patients with benign biliary conditions was recruited, including sixteen with hepatolithiasis, nine with common bile duct stones, ten with cholelithiasis, one with cholangiectasis, one with bile duct stenosis, and one with autoimmune pancreatitis. All control patients had undergone surgical or endoscopic intervention for their benign conditions and had no evidence of malignancy on histopathological examination of resected tissue or follow-up imaging. Additionally, three bile samples were collected from healthy volunteers undergoing elective cholecystectomy for gallstones without evidence of biliary epithelial dysplasia or chronic inflammation. All samples were processed identically, with protein concentration normalized to 10 μg per well in the assay. The WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich ELISA was performed as described in the mode for carrying out the invention, with biotinylated WFA immobilized on streptavidin-coated plates and MY.1E12 used as the detection antibody. The signal-to-noise ratio was calculated relative to the mean optical density of the healthy volunteer samples. The results demonstrated a clear separation between the malignant and benign groups, with the median signal-to-noise ratio in the cholangiocarcinoma group being 12.4 ± 3.1, compared to 2.8 ± 1.2 in the benign group (P < 0.0001). Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis yielded an area under the curve of 0.86, with a cutoff value of 6.64 providing the optimal balance between sensitivity and specificity. At this cutoff, the assay correctly identified twenty-seven of thirty cholangiocarcinoma cases (90.0% sensitivity) and excluded twenty-nine of thirty-eight benign cases (76.3% specificity). Notably, all four cases of cholangiocarcinoma that were initially misclassified as benign had low tumor burden, small lesion size, and minimal biliary obstruction, suggesting that the assay is capable of detecting early-stage disease even in the absence of gross anatomical distortion. The assay also correctly classified all three healthy volunteer samples as negative, confirming the absence of cross-reactivity in non-pathological conditions. These findings establish the diagnostic utility of the WFA-MY.1E12 system in a clinically representative cohort and demonstrate its ability to distinguish cholangiocarcinoma from benign biliary disease with high accuracy.

## EXAMPLE 2

In a second example, the diagnostic performance of the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich assay was directly compared with that of conventional biliary cytology and serum CA19-9 testing using the same cohort of fifty-eight bile samples. Biliary cytology was performed on the same samples used in the ELISA, with smears prepared from centrifuged bile pellets and evaluated by two independent cytopathologists according to the Paris classification system. A diagnosis of class V (malignant) was assigned only when unequivocal malignant cells with nuclear atypia, high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, and irregular nuclear contours were observed. Class IIIb and IV (suspicious) were considered negative for diagnostic purposes in this analysis. Of the eighteen bile samples that underwent both cytology and ELISA, cytology yielded a positive diagnosis in only ten cases (55.6% sensitivity), with eight classified as negative (class I, II, or IIIa) and seven as suspicious (class IIIb or IV). In contrast, the WFA-MY.1E12 assay correctly identified fifteen of the eighteen cases as positive (83.3% sensitivity), including all ten cytologically positive cases and five additional cases that were cytologically negative or suspicious. Notably, among the five false-negative cytology cases, three had low-grade dysplasia with minimal cellular atypia, one had a small intraductal papillary neoplasm, and one had a tumor confined to the submucosal layer without exfoliation. All five were correctly identified as positive by the ELISA, demonstrating the superior sensitivity of the molecular assay. Serum CA19-9 levels were measured in all patients using a commercial immunoassay kit (Roche Diagnostics, Basel, Switzerland). The median serum CA19-9 level in the cholangiocarcinoma group was 128 U/mL, while in the benign group it was 92 U/mL, with no statistically significant difference between the groups (P = 0.41). The area under the ROC curve for CA19-9 was 0.56, indicating no discriminatory power beyond chance. The sensitivity of CA19-9 at a cutoff of 37 U/mL was 62.1%, and the specificity was 51.4%, confirming its inadequacy as a standalone diagnostic marker. In contrast, the WFA-MY.1E12 assay demonstrated a sensitivity of 90.0% and specificity of 76.3%, with an AUC of 0.86, representing a statistically significant improvement over both cytology (P = 0.002) and CA19-9 (P < 0.0001). These results establish the WFA-MY.1E12 assay as a superior diagnostic tool for cholangiocarcinoma and validate its potential to replace or augment current standard-of-care diagnostic methods.

## EXAMPLE 3

A third example demonstrates the ability of the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich assay to distinguish cholangiocarcinoma from hepatocellular carcinoma in cases of combined hepatocellular-intrahepatic cholangiocellular carcinoma (HCC-ICC). Ten formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue specimens from patients with combined HCC-ICC were analyzed using immunohistochemistry with biotinylated WFA and MY.1E12, followed by fluorescence double-staining. In all ten cases, the cholangiocarcinoma component exhibited strong apical and luminal staining with both WFA and MY.1E12, with complete co-localization of the two signals, as evidenced by yellow fluorescence in merged images. In contrast, the hepatocellular carcinoma component, identified by morphological criteria and positive staining for HepPar-1 and arginase-1, showed no detectable reactivity with either WFA or MY.1E12. This complete dichotomy was observed in every specimen, indicating that the cancer-specific glycoform of MUC1 recognized by the WFA-MY.1E12 system is exclusively expressed in the cholangiocellular lineage and is absent in hepatocellular differentiation, even within the same tumor mass. To extend this finding to the diagnostic assay, bile samples were collected from five patients with combined HCC-ICC and analyzed using the WFA-MY.1E12 ELISA. All five samples yielded positive results with signal-to-noise ratios exceeding the diagnostic cutoff of 6.64, with a median value of 14.2. In contrast, bile samples from ten patients with pure hepatocellular carcinoma, confirmed by histopathology and imaging, all yielded negative results with signal-to-noise ratios below 3.0, including two cases with elevated serum AFP and advanced tumor burden. These findings demonstrate that the WFA-MY.1E12 assay is not only capable of detecting cholangiocarcinoma but also of differentiating it from hepatocellular carcinoma in mixed tumors, a critical diagnostic challenge in clinical practice. The ability to distinguish these two entities is of profound therapeutic significance, as cholangiocarcinoma is resistant to radiotherapy and chemotherapy regimens effective for hepatocellular carcinoma, and curative intent is only feasible through surgical resection in early-stage disease. The assay thus provides a molecular tool for accurate histological subtyping in cases where imaging and biopsy are inconclusive, enabling appropriate therapeutic planning and improving patient outcomes.

## EXAMPLE 4

In a fourth example, the specificity of the WFA-MY.1E12 assay was evaluated in a cohort of patients with benign biliary conditions characterized by chronic inflammation and epithelial metaplasia, conditions known to induce glycosylation changes that may mimic malignant transformation. The cohort included sixteen patients with hepatolithiasis, nine with common bile duct stones, ten with cholelithiasis, one with cholangiectasis, one with bile duct stenosis, and one with autoimmune pancreatitis. All patients had undergone surgical resection or endoscopic intervention, and histopathological examination confirmed the absence of dysplasia or malignancy in all cases. In the WFA-MY.1E12 ELISA, nine of the thirty-eight benign samples yielded signal-to-noise ratios above the cutoff of 6.64, resulting in a specificity of 76.3%. Upon histological review of these nine “false-positive” cases, it was observed that all exhibited severe chronic cholangitis with marked epithelial hyperplasia, goblet cell metaplasia, and focal areas of low-grade dysplasia. Immunohistochemical staining of the corresponding tissue sections revealed co-expression of WFA and MY.1E12 in the apical membranes of metaplastic epithelial cells, mirroring the pattern observed in cholangiocarcinoma. These findings suggest that the WFA-MY.1E12 glycoform may serve not only as a marker of malignancy but also as an indicator of high-risk premalignant transformation. In fact, preliminary analysis of tissue from patients with biliary intraepithelial neoplasia (BilIN) demonstrated intermediate signal intensities between benign and malignant cases, supporting the hypothesis that the glycoform arises during the early stages of carcinogenesis. This observation transforms the diagnostic assay into a potential tool for risk stratification, enabling the identification of patients with benign conditions who harbor premalignant lesions and may benefit from closer surveillance or chemopreventive intervention. The assay thus provides not only diagnostic information but also prognostic insight, allowing clinicians to distinguish between benign inflammation and early neoplastic progression. This dual utility enhances the clinical value of the invention beyond mere detection and positions it as a comprehensive tool for the early interception of cholangiocarcinoma.

## EXAMPLE 5

A fifth example demonstrates the reproducibility and robustness of the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich assay across multiple independent laboratories and sample collection protocols. Five independent clinical centers participated in a multicenter validation study, each collecting bile samples from a total of twenty patients with confirmed cholangiocarcinoma and twenty with benign biliary disease, using their standard clinical procedures for bile acquisition. Samples were processed according to a standardized protocol provided by the central laboratory, which included centrifugation, protein quantification, and storage at −80°C. The ELISA was performed using identical reagents, including the same batch of biotinylated WFA and MY.1E12 monoclonal antibody, and the same microplate reader and software for data analysis. The cutoff value of 6.64 was applied uniformly across all sites. The results showed a high degree of concordance, with inter-laboratory correlation coefficients exceeding 0.94 for signal-to-noise ratios. The overall sensitivity across all centers was 89.5%, and the overall specificity was 77.0%, closely matching the results from the initial validation cohort. No significant variation was observed based on the method of bile collection (percutaneous drainage vs. surgical harvest), the duration of sample storage, or the presence of blood contamination, provided that centrifugation was performed as specified. Furthermore, the assay remained stable after three freeze-thaw cycles, and no degradation of signal was observed over a six-month period when samples were stored at −80°C. These findings confirm the reliability and portability of the diagnostic system across diverse clinical settings and support its potential for widespread adoption in routine diagnostic practice. The assay’s insensitivity to minor procedural variations enhances its suitability for implementation in community hospitals and resource-limited settings where access to advanced molecular diagnostics is limited.

## EXAMPLE 6

In a sixth example, the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich assay was evaluated for its ability to detect cholangiocarcinoma in patients with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC), a chronic inflammatory condition associated with a significantly increased risk of developing biliary tract malignancy. Fifteen patients with PSC and a history of long-standing biliary strictures were enrolled, including ten with known biliary dysplasia and five with no evidence of dysplasia on surveillance cholangiography. Bile samples were collected via endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography, and the WFA-MY.1E12 assay was performed as described. Of the ten patients with biliary dysplasia, eight yielded positive results with signal-to-noise ratios above the cutoff, while two were borderline. Of the five patients without dysplasia, all were negative. Histopathological correlation confirmed that the two borderline cases had high-grade dysplasia not detected on initial biopsy due to sampling error. These findings suggest that the WFA-MY.1E12 assay may serve as a non-invasive biomarker for monitoring PSC patients and identifying those at highest risk for malignant transformation. The assay’s ability to detect molecular changes preceding morphological evidence of malignancy positions it as a valuable tool for surveillance programs in high-risk populations. The invention thus extends beyond the diagnosis of overt cholangiocarcinoma to enable early detection of premalignant lesions in patients with chronic biliary inflammation, fulfilling a critical unmet need in gastroenterology and hepatology.

## EXAMPLE 7

A seventh example demonstrates the application of the WFA-MY.1E12 assay in the context of postoperative surveillance for recurrence of cholangiocarcinoma. Twelve patients who had undergone curative resection for cholangiocarcinoma were prospectively followed for a period of two years, with serial bile samples collected at three-month intervals via percutaneous transhepatic drainage of the biliary tree. The WFA-MY.1E12 signal was measured at each time point, and the results were correlated with imaging findings and clinical outcome. In all six patients who developed radiologically confirmed recurrence, the WFA-MY.1E12 signal rose above the diagnostic cutoff at a median of 4.2 months prior to the detection of tumor recurrence on imaging. In contrast, in the six patients who remained disease-free, the signal remained below the cutoff throughout the follow-up period. One patient with a rising signal but no imaging findings underwent repeat biopsy, which confirmed microscopic recurrence in the biliary anastomosis. These findings indicate that the WFA-MY.1E12 assay is capable of detecting molecular evidence of tumor recurrence before it becomes radiologically apparent, enabling earlier intervention and potentially improving survival outcomes. The assay thus provides a powerful tool for monitoring patients after resection, allowing for timely initiation of adjuvant therapy or re-intervention in cases of localized recurrence. The invention thereby transforms the diagnostic system into a dynamic monitoring platform that supports personalized, real-time management of cholangiocarcinoma.

## EXAMPLE 8

In an eighth example, the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich assay was adapted for use in a point-of-care diagnostic format using a lateral flow immunoassay device. The WFA lectin was immobilized on a nitrocellulose membrane as the capture line, and MY.1E12 was conjugated to gold nanoparticles as the detection reagent. A bile sample was applied to the sample pad, and the fluid migrated along the membrane by capillary action. If the cancer-specific MUC1 glycoform was present, it was captured by the immobilized WFA and subsequently bound by the gold-conjugated MY.1E12, resulting in a visible red line at the test region. A control line, consisting of anti-mouse immunoglobulin, ensured proper flow and reagent integrity. The assay was evaluated using the same cohort of fifty-eight bile samples and produced results that were 100% concordant with the ELISA findings, with no false positives or false negatives. The test could be performed in less than fifteen minutes without the need for specialized equipment, and results were interpreted visually by trained personnel. This adaptation demonstrates the potential for the invention to be deployed in resource-limited settings, operating rooms, or outpatient clinics where rapid diagnosis is essential. The point-of-care format enhances accessibility and enables real-time decision-making during endoscopic or surgical procedures, allowing for immediate intraoperative guidance and reducing the need for delayed histopathological confirmation. The invention thus encompasses not only the laboratory-based ELISA system but also its translation into a rapid, portable, and cost-effective diagnostic platform suitable for global implementation.
