# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL DOMAIN

The present invention resides in the technical domain of industrial biotechnology, specifically within the field of microbial enzyme production for the efficient and cost-effective conversion of lignocellulosic biomass into fermentable sugars. The invention pertains to a genetically engineered strain of the filamentous fungus Trichoderma reesei, optimized for the high-yield secretion of a comprehensive cocktail of cellulolytic and hemicellulolytic enzymes under industrially viable fermentation conditions. This strain is designed to operate without reliance on expensive inducing sugars such as lactose or cellulose derivatives, and instead utilizes low-cost, readily available agroindustrial by-products—particularly sugarcane molasses—as its primary carbon source. The invention further encompasses a simplified, scalable bioprocess that integrates this engineered strain with a nutrient formulation comprising molasses-derived sugars and whole-cell yeast biomass as an organic nitrogen source, thereby eliminating the need for purified, commercially sourced supplements such as yeast extract or peptone. The resulting system enables the production of enzyme cocktails with superior specific activities, particularly in β-glucosidase and xylanase, which are critical for the efficient saccharification of pretreated lignocellulosic feedstocks. The technological advancement lies in the synergistic combination of multiple precise genetic modifications—targeting transcriptional regulation, proteolytic degradation, carbon utilization, and enzymatic complementation—within a single, publicly accessible strain background, coupled with a process design that minimizes raw material complexity and maximizes operational robustness. This innovation represents a paradigm shift in industrial enzyme manufacturing by bridging the gap between academic strain engineering and commercial-scale feasibility, offering a reproducible, economically sustainable platform for the production of bio-based catalysts essential to the emerging bioeconomy.

## PRIOR ART

Prior to the present invention, the industrial production of cellulolytic enzymes was dominated by proprietary strains of Trichoderma reesei, primarily derived from the QM6a isolate and further refined through decades of random mutagenesis and selection. Among these, the RUT-C30 strain emerged as the most widely studied and publicly available reference strain, characterized by an 85-kilobase chromosomal deletion and a truncated cre1 gene, which partially alleviates carbon catabolite repression and permits extracellular protein titers of up to 20 grams per liter under optimized laboratory conditions. Despite its widespread use in research, RUT-C30 remains significantly inferior in productivity to industrial strains whose genetic details and production protocols are closely guarded trade secrets, with reported titers exceeding 100 grams per liter. The scientific literature has documented numerous genetic modifications intended to enhance enzyme production in T. reesei, including the constitutive overexpression of the transcription factor XYR1, the deletion of repressors such as ACE1, the removal of extracellular proteases like SLP1 and PEP1, and the introduction of heterologous enzymes to compensate for deficiencies in the native secretome, particularly β-glucosidase activity. However, these modifications were typically implemented in isolation or in small combinations, due to the technical challenges associated with genetic manipulation of T. reesei, including low transformation efficiency, limited selectable markers, and the absence of robust systems for iterative genome editing. While the use of CRISPR/Cas9 technology has recently enabled more precise and efficient genome editing in this organism, its application has largely been confined to proof-of-concept studies involving single or dual modifications, rarely extending to the systematic, multi-gene engineering required to achieve industrially relevant performance. Furthermore, the majority of published studies have relied on synthetic, expensive media formulations containing purified sugars such as lactose, cellobiose, or Avicel as inducers, rendering the resulting processes economically unviable for large-scale deployment. Attempts to utilize low-cost carbon sources such as molasses have been hindered by the inherent inability of T. reesei to metabolize sucrose, the dominant sugar in molasses, due to the absence of invertase activity. Although the heterologous expression of the Aspergillus niger suc1 gene has been demonstrated to confer sucrose utilization, such efforts have not been integrated with other key enhancements in a single strain, nor have they been combined with a process that replaces purified organic nitrogen sources with whole-cell biomass derived from the same agroindustrial stream. Consequently, prior art fails to present a coherent, reproducible, and economically scalable solution that simultaneously addresses the four critical barriers to industrial enzyme production: low secretion titers, dependence on expensive inducers, inadequate enzymatic complementation, and high medium costs. No prior system has achieved a protein titer exceeding 80 grams per liter using a medium composed entirely of non-purified, waste-derived components, nor has any publicly accessible strain been engineered to produce an enzyme cocktail with saccharification efficiency comparable to commercial products such as CTec2 under such conditions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a genetically engineered strain of Trichoderma reesei, designated herein as Br_TrR03, which exhibits unprecedented levels of extracellular protein secretion and enzymatic activity under industrially relevant fermentation conditions using a simplified, low-cost medium based on sugarcane molasses and molasses-grown yeast biomass. This strain is the product of a precisely orchestrated series of six genetic modifications introduced into the RUT-C30 background through iterative CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing, each targeting a distinct bottleneck in the enzyme production pathway. The first modification involves the replacement of the subtilisin protease-encoding gene slp1 with a cassette expressing the β-glucosidase gene cel3a from Talaromyces emersonii under the control of the xylanase 1 promoter, thereby enhancing the β-glucosidase content of the secreted enzyme cocktail while simultaneously eliminating a major proteolytic component of the secretome. The second modification entails the substitution of the transcriptional repressor ace1 with a constitutively expressed allele of xyr1 carrying the V821F mutation, driven by the pyruvate decarboxylase 1 promoter, which confers glucose-blind hyperproduction of cellulase and hemicellulase genes and abolishes the requirement for inducing sugars. The third modification introduces the invertase-encoding gene suc1 from Aspergillus niger, flanked by its native regulatory sequences, into the locus of the aspartic protease gene pep1, enabling the efficient hydrolysis of sucrose into glucose and fructose and thereby permitting the direct utilization of sugarcane molasses as the sole carbon source. These three modifications, implemented in sequential rounds of engineering, yield a strain capable of producing over 34 grams per liter of extracellular protein in bioreactors using a medium containing lactose and Avicel, representing a near threefold increase over the parental RUT-C30 strain. When cultivated in a bioreactor using a medium composed of sugarcane molasses as the carbon source and molasses-grown yeast cells as the organic nitrogen source, the engineered strain achieves a final extracellular protein concentration of 80.6 grams per liter with an average production rate of 0.24 grams per liter per hour, the highest experimentally verified titer reported for any publicly accessible T. reesei strain. The enzyme cocktail produced by this strain exhibits a 72-fold increase in specific β-glucosidase activity and a 42-fold increase in specific xylanase activity compared to the parental strain, resulting in a saccharification efficiency on pretreated sugarcane straw that matches or exceeds that of the commercial enzyme preparation CTec2. Critically, the addition of the whole fermentation broth to the saccharification reaction does not impair enzyme performance, enabling the potential for on-site manufacturing without downstream purification. The invention further encompasses a simplified, scalable bioprocess that eliminates the need for purified yeast extract, peptone, or other expensive supplements by utilizing whole-cell yeast biomass grown on molasses as the organic nitrogen source, thereby reducing the number of outsourced components and increasing process resilience against supply chain fluctuations. The genetic modifications are stable, marker-free, and homokaryotic, and the entire strain construction process is fully reproducible using standard molecular biology techniques and publicly available strain collections. This invention represents a transformative advance in industrial enzyme production by integrating high-yield secretion, broad substrate utilization, enhanced enzymatic functionality, and process simplification into a single, commercially viable system that can be readily adopted by biofuel and biorefinery operators seeking to reduce costs and increase sustainability.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS

The invention is embodied in a genetically modified strain of Trichoderma reesei, designated Br_TrR03, which has been systematically engineered to overcome the principal limitations of prior strains in industrial enzyme production. This strain is derived from the publicly available RUT-C30 isolate and has undergone three sequential rounds of CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing, each round introducing two precisely targeted modifications that collectively enhance enzyme secretion, broaden substrate utilization, and reduce proteolytic degradation. The first round of engineering involved the deletion of the slp1 gene, which encodes a subtilisin-type extracellular protease responsible for a significant proportion of total proteolytic activity in the secretome, and its replacement with a markerless donor cassette containing the β-glucosidase-encoding gene cel3a from Talaromyces emersonii, under the transcriptional control of the xyn1 promoter derived from the native xylanase 1 gene of T. reesei. This modification was designed to simultaneously eliminate a major protease that degrades secreted enzymes and to augment the β-glucosidase activity of the enzyme cocktail, which is known to be deficient in wild-type and even hyperproducing strains of T. reesei. The insertion of the cel3a gene was confirmed by PCR amplification across the integration junctions and by Sanger sequencing of the modified locus, which demonstrated the precise replacement of the slp1 coding region with the heterologous expression cassette without any unintended rearrangements. The resulting strain, designated BR_TrR01, exhibited a fourfold increase in specific β-glucosidase activity compared to RUT-C30 when cultivated in shake flasks with lactose as the carbon source, while extracellular protein titers remained unchanged, indicating that the primary effect of this modification was enzymatic enhancement rather than increased secretion. The second round of engineering targeted the transcriptional regulation of cellulase gene expression. The ace1 gene, which encodes a transcriptional repressor that suppresses cellulase gene expression under repressive carbon conditions, was deleted and replaced with a cassette containing the mutated xyr1-V821F allele under the control of the constitutive pdc1 promoter. The xyr1 gene encodes the master transcriptional activator of cellulase and hemicellulase genes in T. reesei, and the V821F mutation within its regulatory domain has been previously shown to confer a glucose-blind phenotype, permitting constitutive expression of cellulolytic genes even in the presence of high glucose concentrations. The pdc1 promoter, derived from the pyruvate decarboxylase gene, ensures continuous, high-level expression of the modified xyr1 allele independent of carbon source or growth phase. The successful integration of this cassette was verified by PCR and sequencing, confirming the replacement of the ace1 locus with the expression cassette and the absence of residual plasmid backbone sequences. The resulting strain, BR_TrR02, displayed a threefold increase in extracellular protein titer compared to BR_TrR01 under both inducing (lactose) and repressing (glucose) conditions, demonstrating that the constitutive expression of the mutated xyr1 allele effectively overcomes carbon catabolite repression and dramatically enhances overall enzyme secretion. The third and final round of engineering addressed the inability of T. reesei to utilize sucrose, the predominant sugar in sugarcane molasses. The pep1 gene, encoding the most abundant aspartic protease in the T. reesei secretome, was deleted and replaced with a cassette containing the invertase-encoding gene suc1 from Aspergillus niger, flanked by its native upstream and downstream regulatory sequences, including the promoter, introns, and terminator. This design ensures that the heterologous invertase is expressed at physiological levels under native regulatory control, avoiding potential toxicity or metabolic imbalance that could arise from overexpression. The suc1 cassette was assembled using homologous recombination in Saccharomyces cerevisiae and delivered as a linear DNA fragment for transformation. Integration at the pep1 locus was confirmed by PCR using primers flanking the integration site and by sequencing the modified region, which verified the precise replacement of the pep1 coding sequence with the suc1 expression cassette. The resulting strain, Br_TrR03, was capable of growing and producing extracellular proteins at levels comparable to those achieved in glucose or lactose media when cultivated in media containing sucrose as the sole carbon source, a capability absent in all previous strains derived from RUT-C30. The strain exhibited no detectable loss of viability or growth rate in the presence of high sucrose concentrations, and the invertase activity was confirmed by enzymatic assays showing complete hydrolysis of sucrose to glucose and fructose within 24 hours of inoculation. The cumulative effect of these six genetic modifications—deletion of slp1 and pep1, insertion of cel3a and suc1, and replacement of ace1 with xyr1-V821F—resulted in a strain that simultaneously exhibits enhanced enzyme secretion, improved enzymatic composition, and expanded carbon substrate utilization. The strain is homokaryotic, stable over multiple generations, and free of selectable markers, as confirmed by repeated streaking on selective media and the absence of hygromycin resistance in subsequent generations. The strain does not require the presence of inducing sugars such as lactose or Avicel for high-level enzyme production, and its secretome is enriched in β-glucosidase and xylanase, two activities critical for efficient lignocellulosic saccharification. The genetic modifications are stably maintained in the absence of selective pressure, and the strain can be cryopreserved and reactivated without loss of productivity. The strain is deposited in a public strain collection and is available to any researcher or industrial partner under standard material transfer agreements, ensuring reproducibility and accessibility.

### Examples

The invention is further exemplified by a scalable, industrially compatible bioprocess for the production of cellulolytic and hemicellulolytic enzymes using the Br_TrR03 strain and a simplified medium composed of sugarcane molasses and molasses-grown yeast cells. In a representative embodiment, the fermentation process is conducted in a 3.0-liter bioreactor equipped with temperature control, pH regulation, aeration, and automated feeding systems. The batch medium is prepared by dissolving 30 grams per liter of ammonium sulfate as the inorganic nitrogen source and 40 grams per liter of total sugars derived from sugarcane molasses, which contains approximately 65% sucrose, 20% glucose, and 15% fructose, along with trace minerals and vitamins. To this base medium, 20 grams per liter of whole-cell yeast biomass is added, where the yeast cells have been previously cultivated in a separate fermentation using the same sugarcane molasses as the carbon source and a minimal inorganic salt medium containing 1.0 gram per liter of dipotassium hydrogen phosphate, 2.2 grams per liter of magnesium sulfate, and 2.0 grams per liter of zinc sulfate. The yeast culture is grown for 48 hours at 30°C with agitation, harvested by centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 10 minutes, and added directly to the fungal fermentation medium without washing, dilution, or purification. The pH of the medium is adjusted to 5.0 using phosphoric acid, and 1.0 milliliter per liter of a commercial antifoam agent is added to prevent excessive foaming during aeration. The bioreactor is inoculated with 100 milliliters of a preculture of Br_TrR03 grown for 72 hours in shake flasks containing the same medium composition as the batch phase. The fermentation is maintained at 28°C, with dissolved oxygen maintained at 30% saturation through automated control of agitation speed and air flow rate, and pH is regulated at 5.0 by the automatic addition of 10% aqueous ammonia solution. Feeding of sugarcane molasses is initiated after 44 hours, when the initial sugar concentration has been substantially consumed, as indicated by a decline in the volume of ammonia required to maintain pH stability. The feed solution consists of undiluted sugarcane molasses, delivered at a constant rate of 1.0 gram of total sugars per liter per hour for the first 124 hours. The fermentation is then extended to 336 hours, at which point the extracellular protein concentration reaches 80.6 grams per liter, with an average production rate of 0.24 grams per liter per hour. The enzyme cocktail produced under these conditions exhibits a specific β-glucosidase activity of 13.2 international units per milligram of protein, a specific xylanase activity of 28.7 international units per milligram of protein, and a filter paper activity of 1.8 international units per milligram of protein, all of which exceed the corresponding values obtained from the parental RUT-C30 strain cultivated in a complex medium containing yeast extract and lactose. The saccharification efficiency of this enzyme cocktail on industrially pretreated sugarcane straw at 20% solids loading is equivalent to that of the commercial enzyme preparation CTec2, releasing 92% of the theoretical glucose yield and 89% of the theoretical xylose yield after 72 hours of hydrolysis, with no detectable accumulation of cellobiose, a hallmark of insufficient β-glucosidase activity. In a comparative embodiment, the same fermentation process is conducted using Br_TrR02, the strain lacking the suc1 gene, with acid-inverted molasses as the carbon source. Under these conditions, Br_TrR02 achieves a maximum protein titer of 59.2 grams per liter over 310 hours, with a production rate of 0.19 grams per liter per hour, demonstrating that the suc1-mediated sucrose utilization is essential for achieving the full productivity potential of the strain under non-inverted molasses conditions. When the Br_TrR03 strain is cultivated in a medium containing acid-inverted molasses and molasses-grown yeast, it achieves a protein titer of 79.7 grams per liter, identical to that obtained with non-inverted molasses, confirming that the invertase activity is fully functional and that acid hydrolysis does not impair the nutritional quality of the molasses. The strain is also cultivated in a medium containing yeast extract instead of molasses-grown yeast, achieving a protein titer of 46.8 grams per liter, demonstrating that the yeast biomass serves as a superior and more cost-effective organic nitrogen source. The use of YEPD-grown yeast, washed and added to the medium, yields a titer of 41.7 grams per liter, indicating that washing reduces the nutritional value of the yeast, likely due to the loss of intracellular metabolites and cofactors. The entire process requires only three raw materials: sugarcane molasses, inorganic salts, and yeast, all of which are either waste streams or low-cost commodities. The process generates no toxic by-products, requires no sterile filtration or chromatographic purification, and operates under conditions compatible with existing industrial fermentation infrastructure. The enzyme cocktail produced is stable for months at 4°C and retains full activity after lyophilization, making it suitable for storage and transport. The strain and process are fully scalable from laboratory to industrial scale, with no observed loss of productivity in pilot-scale fermentations exceeding 100 liters. The economic analysis of the process indicates that the cost of enzyme production is reduced by more than 65% compared to conventional systems using purified sugars and yeast extract, primarily due to the elimination of three high-cost components: lactose, Avicel, and yeast extract. The invention thus provides a complete, integrated solution for industrial enzyme production that is not only technologically superior but also economically transformative.
