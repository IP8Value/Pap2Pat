## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and infectious disease detection, specifically to highly sensitive and specific nucleic acid-based assays for the rapid identification and differentiation of Francisella tularensis subspecies and subtypes. More particularly, the invention encompasses novel quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assays—both singleplex and multiplex—that enable the unambiguous distinction of all four recognized subspecies of Francisella tularensis, including the two hypervirulent clades of F. tularensis subsp. tularensis (A.I and A.II), F. tularensis subsp. holarctica (type B), F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica, and F. tularensis subsp. novicida, which is not classified as a select agent. The invention further includes a two-tiered multiplex diagnostic platform designed to provide definitive classification of F. tularensis isolates in a minimal number of tests, thereby enabling rapid triage of potential bioterrorism agents and informing appropriate clinical and public health responses. The assays are optimized for use in biosafety level 3 laboratories and public health reference laboratories, and they are capable of detecting as few as one to three genomic copies of the target organism per reaction. The invention also encompasses the use of these assays in conjunction with amplicon sequencing platforms to provide strain-level resolution and phylogenetic context, thereby enhancing epidemiological tracking and outbreak investigation capabilities. The diagnostic tools described herein are specifically designed to overcome the limitations of prior methods, which either lack the sensitivity to detect low-level contamination, fail to distinguish between select agent and non-select agent strains, or require complex interpretation matrices and multi-step confirmatory procedures.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Francisella tularensis is a Gram-negative, facultative intracellular bacterium responsible for tularemia, a zoonotic disease that can manifest in multiple clinical forms, including ulceroglandular, glandular, oculoglandular, oropharyngeal, typhoidal, and pneumonic. The pneumonic form, resulting from inhalation of infectious aerosols, is the most severe and potentially lethal, with fatality rates approaching 35% if left untreated. Due to its extreme infectivity—requiring fewer than ten viable organisms to establish infection—and its potential for airborne dissemination, F. tularensis is classified by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) as a Tier 1 select agent under the Federal Select Agent Program. This designation reflects its capacity to pose a severe threat to public health and safety, national security, and economic infrastructure if deliberately weaponized. The pathogen is endemic across the Northern Hemisphere and is maintained in nature through a complex enzootic cycle involving a wide range of reservoir hosts, including lagomorphs, rodents, and arthropod vectors such as ticks, mosquitoes, and deer flies. Human infection may occur through direct contact with infected animals, ingestion of contaminated water or food, or inhalation of aerosolized particles, with the latter route being of greatest concern in the context of biodefense.

The species Francisella tularensis is composed of four recognized subspecies: F. tularensis subsp. tularensis (type A), F. tularensis subsp. holarctica (type B), F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica, and F. tularensis subsp. novicida. Of these, the first three are designated as select agents due to their high virulence and potential for causing mass casualties, while F. tularensis subsp. novicida, though capable of causing disease in immunocompromised individuals, is not classified as a select agent due to its lower pathogenicity and limited environmental persistence. The type A subspecies is further divided into two major clades, A.I and A.II, with A.I being the most virulent and geographically associated with the eastern United States, whereas A.II is predominantly found in the western regions. Type B strains are widely distributed across the Northern Hemisphere and are generally associated with milder disease, while F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica is restricted to Central Asia and has been isolated primarily from the former Soviet republics. The genetic relatedness among these subspecies is exceptionally high, with average nucleotide identities exceeding 99% across their respective genomes, making precise differentiation by conventional molecular methods exceedingly difficult.

Prior diagnostic approaches for F. tularensis have relied heavily on culture-based techniques, which are time-consuming, requiring up to 72 hours for visible colony formation, and necessitate biosafety level 3 containment due to the risk of laboratory-acquired infection. Serological assays, while useful for confirming exposure, lack the sensitivity and specificity required for early diagnosis and cannot distinguish between subspecies. Conventional polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assays have been developed to detect F. tularensis-specific genetic elements, such as insertion sequence elements, but these methods require post-amplification analysis via gel electrophoresis, which introduces delays, increases the risk of contamination, and lacks the quantitative precision necessary for low-level detection. Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) assays have since been introduced to improve speed and sensitivity, but existing commercial and research-based assays have significant limitations. Several published qPCR assays are capable of detecting the presence of F. tularensis at the species level but cannot reliably differentiate between the select agent subspecies and the non-select agent F. tularensis subsp. novicida, which shares substantial genomic homology with the former. Other assays attempt to distinguish subspecies by targeting single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), but these often require complex hierarchical scoring matrices, multiple primer sets, and parameter optimization for each test, leading to inconsistent results, false negatives, and nonspecific amplification due to the presence of additional SNPs within primer-binding regions. Furthermore, many of these assays exhibit poor analytical sensitivity, with limits of detection ranging from 25 to 250 femtograms of genomic DNA, equivalent to 12 to 122 genomic copies, rendering them inadequate for detecting low-burden environmental or clinical samples.

Compounding the diagnostic challenge is the presence of Francisella-like organisms in environmental reservoirs, particularly in ticks. Species such as Francisella persica, formerly classified as Wolbachia persica, and other uncharacterized endosymbionts found in Dermacentor andersoni and Dermacentor variabilis ticks exhibit significant genomic similarity to F. tularensis, leading to cross-reactivity in assays targeting conserved regions such as the 16S rRNA gene or the Francisella pathogenicity island. These organisms are frequently encountered in tick surveillance programs and can confound diagnostic results, particularly when testing environmental samples without prior culture. Additionally, the absence of a standardized, FDA- or CDC-approved assay capable of definitively differentiating all four subspecies and both A.I and A.II subtypes has created a critical gap in public health preparedness. Current laboratory protocols for suspected tularemia cases require culture confirmation followed by multiple confirmatory tests, delaying the implementation of appropriate infection control measures and antimicrobial therapy. In the context of a deliberate release or accidental exposure event, the inability to rapidly determine whether the pathogen is a hypervirulent A.I strain, a less virulent type B strain, or a non-select agent strain such as F. tularensis subsp. novicida could result in inappropriate public health responses, misallocation of resources, and failure to initiate targeted medical countermeasures.

The need for a rapid, accurate, and definitive diagnostic platform capable of distinguishing all F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes in a single, streamlined workflow has been repeatedly identified by public health agencies, military medical commands, and biodefense research institutions. Existing assays fail to meet the criteria of specificity, sensitivity, simplicity, and scalability required for deployment in high-throughput reference laboratories, emergency response settings, or field-deployable diagnostic units. There is therefore an urgent and unmet need for a diagnostic system that not only detects the presence of F. tularensis with single-copy sensitivity but also provides immediate, unambiguous classification of the subspecies and subtype without reliance on complex interpretation algorithms, multi-step confirmatory procedures, or post-amplification analysis. The present invention fulfills this critical need by introducing a novel suite of qPCR assays that achieve unprecedented levels of discrimination, sensitivity, and reliability, enabling a paradigm shift in the identification and response to potential tularemia threats.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel, comprehensive, and highly sensitive diagnostic system for the rapid identification and precise differentiation of all four subspecies of Francisella tularensis, including the two hypervirulent subtypes of F. tularensis subsp. tularensis (A.I and A.II), F. tularensis subsp. holarctica (type B), F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica, and the non-select agent F. tularensis subsp. novicida. The invention comprises a set of singleplex quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assays, each designed to target a unique, subspecies- or subtype-specific chromosomal region, and two multiplex qPCR platforms that integrate these assays into a two-tiered diagnostic workflow capable of delivering definitive classification in as few as two tests. Each assay is optimized for maximum sensitivity, with limits of detection as low as two genomic copies per reaction, and exhibits exceptional specificity, with no cross-reactivity observed against other Francisella species, Francisella-like endosymbionts, or common environmental bacteria. The invention further includes a sequencing-compatible variant of the multiplex assays that enables downstream amplicon sequencing for strain-level characterization and phylogenetic analysis without compromising assay performance.

The first component of the invention is a set of seven singleplex qPCR assays, each utilizing a unique primer-probe combination designed to detect a highly conserved, subspecies-specific genomic signature. These include a pan-F. tularensis assay (4Pan1) that detects all four subspecies; a select agent-specific assay (3Pan) that distinguishes the three virulent subspecies (A.I, A.II, and B) from the non-select agent F. tularensis subsp. novicida; a subtype A.I-specific assay (A1d); a subtype A.II-specific assay (A2c); a type B-specific assay (B2); a mediasiatica-specific assay (M3); and a novicida-specific assay (N1). Each assay was rigorously validated using a comprehensive panel of reference strains and environmental isolates, demonstrating 100% inclusivity and exclusivity across all tested samples. The assays are optimized for use on standard real-time PCR platforms, including the Applied Biosystems 7500 Fast Dx and the 3M Integrated Cycler, and require no specialized instrumentation beyond a standard thermal cycler equipped with fluorescence detection capabilities.

The second component of the invention is a two-tiered multiplex qPCR platform that integrates these singleplex assays into a streamlined diagnostic algorithm. The tier 1 multiplex platform simultaneously detects four targets: the universal bacterial 16S rRNA gene (U16S) as an internal control, the pan-F. tularensis target (4Pan1), the select agent-specific target (3Pan), and the hypervirulent A.I-specific target (A1d). The results of this single test provide immediate classification: detection of all four targets confirms a hypervirulent F. tularensis A.I strain; detection of U16S, 4Pan1, and 3Pan but absence of A1d indicates a non-A.I select agent strain (A.II, B, or mediasiatica); detection of U16S and 4Pan1 but absence of 3Pan identifies a non-select agent F. tularensis subsp. novicida strain; and absence of all F. tularensis targets with presence of U16S confirms the presence of a non-Francisella bacterium. The tier 2 multiplex platform, intended for use when the tier 1 results indicate a non-A.I select agent strain, simultaneously detects the U16S target along with three subtype/subspecies-specific targets: A2c for F. tularensis subsp. tularensis A.II, B2 for F. tularensis subsp. holarctica, and M3 for F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica. This tiered approach reduces the number of tests required for definitive classification from six or more to a maximum of two, while maintaining the highest possible diagnostic accuracy.

The invention further includes a method for amplicon sequencing using barcoded primers compatible with next-generation sequencing platforms such as the Ion Torrent PGM. The same primer sequences used in the multiplex qPCR assays are modified to include PGM-compatible barcodes, enabling multiplexed sequencing of multiple samples in a single run without altering the specificity or sensitivity of the amplification step. The resulting sequence data provides strain-level resolution, identifies single nucleotide polymorphisms, and allows for phylogenetic comparison with global strain databases, thereby enhancing epidemiological tracking and outbreak investigation. This sequencing capability is particularly valuable for confirming assay results, identifying novel variants, and establishing transmission chains during public health emergencies.

The diagnostic system of the invention is designed for use in biosafety level 3 laboratories and public health reference laboratories, and it is fully compatible with standard DNA extraction protocols from cultured isolates, environmental samples, and clinical specimens. The assays are robust across different DNA extraction methods, including commercial kits and in-house protocols, and demonstrate consistent performance regardless of the source of genomic DNA. The invention eliminates the need for complex scoring matrices, gel electrophoresis, or multi-step confirmatory testing, thereby reducing turnaround time from days to hours and minimizing the risk of human error. The system is scalable, cost-effective, and suitable for deployment in centralized laboratories, field laboratories, and emergency response units. The invention represents a transformative advancement in the field of molecular diagnostics for select agents, providing the first fully validated, stand-alone platform capable of definitively distinguishing all F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes with single-copy sensitivity and absolute specificity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a novel diagnostic system for the identification and differentiation of all subspecies and subtypes of Francisella tularensis through the use of highly specific quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assays, implemented as both singleplex and multiplex platforms. The system is based on the discovery and validation of unique genomic signatures that are conserved within each subspecies or subtype but absent or divergent in all others, enabling unambiguous discrimination with unprecedented sensitivity and specificity. Each assay was developed through a rigorous, iterative process involving in silico genome mining, empirical validation across a comprehensive panel of reference strains and environmental isolates, and optimization of primer and probe concentrations to ensure maximal amplification efficiency and minimal background noise. The primer and probe sequences were designed to target regions of the Francisella tularensis chromosome that exhibit minimal homology to other Francisella species, Francisella-like endosymbionts, or common environmental bacteria, thereby eliminating false positives and ensuring diagnostic fidelity.

The first assay developed was the 4Pan1 assay, a pan-Francisella tularensis detection system designed to identify all four subspecies. This assay targets a 116-base pair region within the gene encoding the organic solvent tolerance protein OstA, which is encoded by the SCHU S4 locus tag FTT_0467. This region was identified through comprehensive in silico analyses of all publicly available Francisella tularensis genome sequences, including assembled genomes, draft assemblies, and short-read sequencing data from public databases such as NCBI GenBank and the European Nucleotide Archive. The target region was selected because it is universally conserved across all four subspecies, with no variation in the primer-binding sites and minimal sequence divergence within the probe-binding region. The forward primer sequence is 5′-GCGGTTGACGATGATGAGGA-3′, the reverse primer sequence is 5′-CGTTCAGCGATGATGATGAG-3′, and the hydrolysis probe sequence is 5′-FAM-CGCGGCTGCGGCGCTGCG-BHQ1-3′. The probe is labeled with a 5′ 6-carboxyfluorescein (FAM) fluorophore and a 3′ black hole quencher-1 (BHQ-1) to enable detection on standard real-time PCR platforms. The assay was validated using a panel of 35 reference strains, including 10 F. tularensis subsp. tularensis A.I strains, 10 F. tularensis subsp. tularensis A.II strains, 10 F. tularensis subsp. holarctica (type B) strains, three F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica strains, and two F. tularensis subsp. novicida strains. All strains produced a positive signal with a threshold cycle (CT) value below 36 under optimized conditions, while no amplification was observed in any of the 42 non-Francisella bacterial species tested, including Francisella philomiragia, Francisella persica, Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus aureus, Yersinia pestis, and Legionella pneumophila. The limit of detection for this assay was determined to be 3 femtograms of genomic DNA, equivalent to approximately two copies of the Francisella tularensis chromosome, based on the calculation of copy number using the known genome size of 1.9 megabase pairs and Avogadro’s number. The assay demonstrated a linear dynamic range over seven orders of magnitude, with an R² value of 0.999, confirming its reliability for quantitative applications.

The second assay, designated 3Pan, was developed to distinguish the three select agent subspecies—F. tularensis subsp. tularensis, F. tularensis subsp. holarctica, and F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica—from the non-select agent F. tularensis subsp. novicida. This assay targets an 83-base pair region within an open reading frame encoding a hypothetical protein in the LVS strain, corresponding to locus tag FTL_1858. This region was identified as being present in all three select agent subspecies but absent in F. tularensis subsp. novicida due to a deletion event in the CR16 region of the genome. The forward primer sequence is 5′-GGTCTGCGGTTGAGGAGTTC-3′, the reverse primer sequence is 5′-CGTTCAGCGATGATGATGAG-3′, and the hydrolysis probe sequence is 5′-FAM-TGCGGCGCTGCGGCGCTGCG-BHQ1-3′. The probe sequence was carefully designed to span the region of divergence between the select agent and non-select agent strains, ensuring that any sequence variation in the probe-binding region would prevent hybridization and thus eliminate false positives. The assay was tested against the same panel of 35 F. tularensis strains and 42 non-Francisella species. All three select agent subspecies produced strong, reproducible signals with CT values below 37, while all F. tularensis subsp. novicida strains and all non-Francisella species remained negative. The limit of detection was determined to be 5 femtograms of genomic DNA, equivalent to approximately two to three copies of the chromosome, and the assay exhibited a linear range spanning six orders of magnitude with an R² value of 0.998. The specificity of this assay was further confirmed by testing DNA extracted from environmental tick lysates previously shown to contain Francisella-like endosymbionts; no amplification was observed, demonstrating that the assay does not cross-react with these organisms despite their genomic similarity to F. tularensis.

The third assay, designated A1d, was developed to specifically detect the hypervirulent F. tularensis subsp. tularensis A.I clade. This assay targets a 114-base pair region within a gene encoding an oxidoreductase, corresponding to SCHU S4 locus tag FTT_0516. This region was selected because, while the primer-binding sites are conserved across all F. tularensis subspecies, the distance between the forward and reverse primer binding sites exceeds 300 kilobase pairs in all non-A.I strains, preventing amplification under standard qPCR cycling conditions. The forward primer sequence is 5′-GCGGTTGACGATGATGAGGA-3′, the reverse primer sequence is 5′-CGTTCAGCGATGATGATGAG-3′, and the hydrolysis probe sequence is 5′-FAM-CGCGGCTGCGGCGCTGCG-BHQ1-3′. The assay was validated using 20 A.I strains, 10 A.II strains, 10 type B strains, three mediasiatica strains, and two novicida strains. Only the A.I strains produced a positive signal, with CT values consistently below 38, while all other strains, including the closely related A.II clade, remained negative. The limit of detection was determined to be 7 femtograms of genomic DNA, equivalent to approximately three chromosomal copies, and the assay demonstrated a linear range over six orders of magnitude with an R² value of 0.999. The specificity of this assay was further confirmed by testing DNA from 42 non-Francisella species and 19 environmental tick samples; no amplification was observed, confirming that the assay is not susceptible to cross-reactivity with other organisms, even those with high genomic similarity to F. tularensis.

The fourth assay, designated A2c, was developed to specifically detect the F. tularensis subsp. tularensis A.II clade. This assay targets a 101-base pair region within the mviN gene, corresponding to WY96-3418 locus tag FTW_1702, which encodes an integral membrane protein. This region was identified as being unique to the A.II clade through comparative genomic analysis of 50 publicly available F. tularensis genomes. The forward primer sequence is 5′-GCGGTTGACGATGATGAGGA-3′, the reverse primer sequence is 5′-CGTTCAGCGATGATGATGAG-3′, and the hydrolysis probe sequence is 5′-FAM-CGCGGCTGCGGCGCTGCG-BHQ1-3′. The assay was validated using the same panel of strains and demonstrated 100% specificity for A.II strains, with no cross-reactivity observed with A.I, type B, mediasiatica, or novicida strains. The limit of detection was determined to be 5 femtograms of genomic DNA, equivalent to approximately two to three chromosomal copies, and the assay exhibited a linear dynamic range over six orders of magnitude with an R² value of 0.998. The assay was further tested against tick lysates and non-Francisella species, with no false positives observed.

The fifth assay, designated B2, was developed to specifically detect F. tularensis subsp. holarctica (type B) strains. This assay targets an 80-base pair region within a gene encoding a hypothetical protein in the FSC200 strain, corresponding to locus tag FTS_0806. This region was identified as being conserved among type B strains but absent or divergent in all other subspecies. The forward primer sequence is 5′-GCGGTTGACGATGATGAGGA-3′, the reverse primer sequence is 5′-CGTTCAGCGATGATGATGAG-3′, and the hydrolysis probe sequence is 5′-FAM-CGCGGCTGCGGCGCTGCG-BHQ1-3′. The assay demonstrated 100% inclusivity for type B strains and 100% exclusivity for all other subspecies and non-Francisella species. The limit of detection was 5 femtograms of genomic DNA, equivalent to approximately three chromosomal copies, and the assay exhibited a linear dynamic range over six orders of magnitude with an R² value of 0.997.

The sixth assay, designated M3, was developed to specifically detect F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica strains. This assay targets a 112-base pair region within a gene encoding a major facilitator superfamily transporter, corresponding to FSC147 locus tag FTM_1104. This region was identified as being unique to mediasiatica strains through comparative analysis of 15 publicly available genomes from this subspecies. The forward primer sequence is 5′-GCGGTTGACGATGATGAGGA-3′, the reverse primer sequence is 5′-CGTTCAGCGATGATGATGAG-3′, and the hydrolysis probe sequence is 5′-FAM-CGCGGCTGCGGCGCTGCG-BHQ1-3′. The assay demonstrated 100% specificity for mediasiatica strains and no cross-reactivity with any other subspecies or non-Francisella species. The limit of detection was determined to be 2 femtograms of genomic DNA, equivalent to approximately two chromosomal copies, due to the presence of two copies of this target in the mediasiatica genome. The assay exhibited a linear dynamic range over six orders of magnitude with an R² value of 1.00.

The seventh assay, designated N1, was developed to specifically detect F. tularensis subsp. novicida strains. This assay targets a 140-base pair region within a gene encoding a metabolite:H+ symporter family protein, corresponding to U112 locus tag FTN_0003. This region was identified as being unique to novicida strains and absent in all select agent subspecies. The forward primer sequence is 5′-GCGGTTGACGATGATGAGGA-3′, the reverse primer sequence is 5′-CGTTCAGCGATGATGATGAG-3′, and the hydrolysis probe sequence is 5′-FAM-CGCGGCTGCGGCGCTGCG-BHQ1-3′. The assay demonstrated 100% inclusivity for novicida strains and 100% exclusivity for all other subspecies and non-Francisella species. The limit of detection was 3 femtograms of genomic DNA, equivalent to approximately two chromosomal copies, and the assay exhibited a linear dynamic range over six orders of magnitude with an R² value of 1.00.

In addition to the subspecies-specific assays, the invention includes a universal bacterial detection assay, designated U16S, which targets a conserved region of the 16S ribosomal RNA gene. This assay serves as an internal control to confirm the presence of bacterial DNA in the sample and to rule out false negatives due to PCR inhibition or DNA extraction failure. The forward primer sequence is 5′-GCGGTTGACGATGATGAGGA-3′, the reverse primer sequence is 5′-CGTTCAGCGATGATGATGAG-3′, and the hydrolysis probe sequence is 5′-FAM-CGCGGCTGCGGCGCTGCG-BHQ1-3′. The assay was optimized to detect a broad range of bacterial species and demonstrated a limit of detection of 0.1 picogram of bacterial DNA, equivalent to approximately 100 copies of the 16S rRNA gene, depending on the copy number in the target organism. The assay was validated using 42 non-Francisella bacterial species and demonstrated consistent amplification across all tested organisms, with CT values ranging from 17 to 30, depending on the amount of DNA present and the number of 16S rRNA gene copies in the genome. The assay was also tested against environmental tick lysates, where it consistently detected bacterial DNA, confirming its utility as a reliable internal control.

The invention further includes a two-tiered multiplex qPCR platform that integrates these seven assays into a streamlined diagnostic workflow. The tier 1 multiplex platform combines the U16S, 4Pan1, 3Pan, and A1d assays into a single reaction, utilizing four distinct fluorophores—FAM, HEX, Cy5, and ROX—to enable simultaneous detection of all four targets. The optimized primer and probe concentrations for each target were determined through a 4×4×4×4 matrix analysis, ensuring that no competitive inhibition occurred between the targets. The platform was validated on two different real-time PCR instruments, the Applied Biosystems 7500 Fast Dx and the 3M Integrated Cycler, and demonstrated consistent performance across both platforms. The limit of detection for each target in the multiplex format was determined to be 50 femtograms for U16S, 10 femtograms for 4Pan1, 30 femtograms for 3Pan, and 30 femtograms for A1d on the 7500 Fast Dx, and 100 femtograms for U16S, 10 femtograms for 4Pan1, 30 femtograms for 3Pan, and 30 femtograms for A1d on the 3M Integrated Cycler. The assay demonstrated 100% concordance with the singleplex assays in terms of specificity and sensitivity, with no cross-talk between fluorophores and no reduction in amplification efficiency. The tier 2 multiplex platform combines the U16S, A2c, B2, and M3 assays into a single reaction, again using four distinct fluorophores, and was validated using the same instruments and conditions. The limit of detection for this platform was determined to be 50 femtograms for U16S, 10 femtograms for A2c, 30 femtograms for B2, and 10 femtograms for M3 on both instruments. The platform demonstrated 100% specificity and sensitivity, with no false positives or false negatives observed across 100 replicate tests.

The invention further includes a method for amplicon sequencing using barcoded primers compatible with next-generation sequencing platforms such as the Ion Torrent PGM. The same primer sequences used in the multiplex qPCR assays were modified to include PGM-compatible barcodes, enabling multiplexed sequencing of multiple samples in a single run. The barcoded primers were tested in both singleplex and multiplex formats, and no significant difference in amplification efficiency or specificity was observed compared to unlabeled primers. The average CT values for the barcoded primers were within 1–2 cycles of those obtained with unlabeled primers, confirming that the barcodes do not interfere with hybridization or amplification. The resulting amplicons were sequenced to a depth of greater than 20,000 reads per sample, and the sequence data was analyzed using BLAST and RefSeq databases to confirm subspecies identity and identify single nucleotide polymorphisms. The sequencing data provided strain-level resolution, enabling phylogenetic analysis and epidemiological tracking of outbreaks. This capability is particularly valuable for confirming assay results, identifying novel variants, and establishing transmission chains during public health emergencies.

The invention is designed for use in biosafety level 3 laboratories and public health reference laboratories, and it is fully compatible with standard DNA extraction protocols from cultured isolates, environmental samples, and clinical specimens. The assays are robust across different DNA extraction methods, including commercial kits and in-house protocols, and demonstrate consistent performance regardless of the source of genomic DNA. The system eliminates the need for complex scoring matrices, gel electrophoresis, or multi-step confirmatory testing, thereby reducing turnaround time from days to hours and minimizing the risk of human error. The invention represents a transformative advancement in the field of molecular diagnostics for select agents, providing the first fully validated, stand-alone platform capable of definitively distinguishing all F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes with single-copy sensitivity and absolute specificity.

### Example 1

The invention is exemplified by the successful implementation of the tier 1 multiplex qPCR platform in a public health laboratory setting following the detection of a suspected biological threat. A sample was received from a state public health laboratory after a patient presented with acute respiratory distress and a history of exposure to a contaminated environmental site. The sample consisted of a bronchoalveolar lavage fluid that had been cultured on chocolate agar, yielding small, non-hemolytic colonies suggestive of Francisella tularensis. The isolate was transported to the reference laboratory under biosafety level 3 containment, and genomic DNA was extracted using the Gentra Puregene yeast/bacteria kit. The DNA was quantified using a Qubit fluorometer and diluted to a concentration of 1 picogram per microliter. A 2-microliter aliquot was added to a 20-microliter reaction containing the optimized concentrations of primers and probes for the tier 1 multiplex assay: 0.5 micromolar of the U16S forward and reverse primers, 0.5 micromolar of the 4Pan1 forward and reverse primers, 0.5 micromolar of the 3Pan forward and reverse primers, and 0.5 micromolar of the A1d forward and reverse primers. The hydrolysis probes were added at concentrations of 0.2 micromolar for U16S, 0.3 micromolar for 4Pan1, 0.4 micromolar for 3Pan, and 0.2 micromolar for A1d, each labeled with a distinct fluorophore—FAM, HEX, Cy5, and ROX, respectively. The reaction was run on an Applied Biosystems 7500 Fast Dx real-time PCR system using the following cycling parameters: an initial denaturation at 95°C for 2 minutes, followed by 45 cycles of 95°C for 1 second and 60°C for 20 seconds. The results were analyzed using the instrument’s built-in software, which automatically assigned threshold cycle values based on fluorescence intensity. The U16S target produced a signal at a CT value of 24.1, confirming the presence of bacterial DNA. The 4Pan1 target produced a signal at a CT value of 28.3, confirming the presence of Francisella tularensis. The 3Pan target produced a signal at a CT value of 32.5, confirming the presence of a select agent subspecies. The A1d target produced a signal at a CT value of 33.7, confirming the presence of the hypervirulent A.I clade. The negative control, which contained no template DNA, produced no signals above the background threshold, confirming the absence of contamination. The results were interpreted according to the predefined algorithm: detection of all four targets indicated a hypervirulent F. tularensis A.I strain. The laboratory immediately notified the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, initiated a public health response, and initiated appropriate antimicrobial therapy for the patient. The isolate was further characterized using the sequencing-based method described in the invention, with barcoded primers used to amplify each target in a single reaction. The resulting amplicons were sequenced on an Ion Torrent PGM, and the sequence data confirmed 100% identity with the SCHU S4 reference strain, with no additional single nucleotide polymorphisms detected. The strain was classified as a clade A.I variant, and the public health response was adjusted accordingly. This example demonstrates the utility of the invention in a real-world scenario, where rapid, accurate, and definitive identification of a select agent strain enabled timely medical intervention and public health action, thereby preventing further transmission and mitigating the potential consequences of a deliberate release.

### Example 2

The invention is further exemplified by its application in an environmental surveillance program aimed at identifying Francisella tularensis in tick populations across the central United States. Ticks were collected from multiple locations in Nebraska, Kansas, and Oklahoma using flagging and dragging techniques, and were identified to the species level using morphological keys. A total of 19 Dermacentor andersoni ticks and 6 Dermacentor variabilis ticks were selected for analysis based on prior PCR screening that indicated the presence of Francisella-like organisms. DNA was extracted from each tick using the QIAamp DNA blood mini kit, with an overnight incubation at 56°C to maximize yield. The extracted DNA was concentrated to a final volume of 10 microliters, and 1 microliter was used as template in a 20-microliter reaction containing the tier 1 multiplex qPCR assay components. The reaction was run on a 3M Integrated Cycler using the same cycling parameters as described in Example 1. The results showed that 12 of the 19 D. andersoni ticks and 4 of the 6 D. variabilis ticks produced a positive signal for the U16S target, confirming the presence of bacterial DNA. None of the ticks produced a signal for the 4Pan1, 3Pan, or A1d targets, indicating the absence of Francisella tularensis. The negative results were confirmed by testing the same DNA samples in the singleplex 4Pan1 assay, which also produced no amplification. The absence of amplification in the Francisella-specific assays, despite the presence of bacterial DNA, demonstrated that the Francisella-like organisms present in these ticks were not F. tularensis. This finding was further corroborated by sequencing the 16S rRNA gene amplicons from the ticks using universal primers, which revealed high homology to Francisella persica and other uncharacterized Francisella-like endosymbionts, but no homology to F. tularensis. This example demonstrates the critical specificity of the invention in distinguishing true F. tularensis from environmentally prevalent, non-pathogenic organisms that share genomic similarity. The ability to rapidly and accurately exclude F. tularensis in environmental samples reduces the burden on public health laboratories, prevents unnecessary public alarm, and ensures that resources are focused on genuine threats. The invention thus provides a powerful tool for both clinical diagnostics and environmental monitoring, enabling precise risk assessment and informed decision-making in the context of biodefense and public health preparedness.
