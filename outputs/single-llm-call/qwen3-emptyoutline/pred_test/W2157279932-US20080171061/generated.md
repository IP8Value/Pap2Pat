# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Literature

The human genome contains a vast reservoir of endogenous retroviral sequences, remnants of ancient retroviral infections that integrated into the germline millions of years ago and have since been vertically inherited across generations. These elements, collectively termed human endogenous retroviruses (HERVs), constitute approximately 8.29% of the human genomic landscape, far outnumbering the coding capacity of all known human genes combined. While the majority of HERV sequences have been rendered nonfunctional through accumulated mutations, deletions, and epigenetic silencing, a subset retains open reading frames capable of encoding structural and enzymatic proteins characteristic of retroviruses, including Gag, Pol, and Env. Historically regarded as molecular fossils or genomic parasites with no biological significance, recent advances in molecular virology and transcriptomics have revealed that certain HERV families remain transcriptionally active under specific physiological or pathological conditions. Notably, HERV-K (HML-2), the most recently integrated and best-preserved family, has been shown to produce viral-like particles, RNA transcripts, and even functional proteins in contexts such as embryonic development, certain cancers, and autoimmune disorders. The discovery that infectious hybrid retroviruses can be reconstructed from HERV-K sequences in vitro further underscores the latent biological potential embedded within these genomic elements.

The interplay between exogenous retroviruses and endogenous retroviral elements has emerged as a compelling area of investigation. In particular, HIV-1 infection has been shown to profoundly alter the transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulation of the host genome, creating an environment permissive for the reactivation of otherwise silenced HERVs. HIV-1 encodes multiple accessory proteins, including Vif, which directly counteracts the antiviral activity of APOBEC3 family members—cytidine deaminases that normally restrict retrotransposition by inducing hypermutation in nascent retroviral DNA. By neutralizing these intrinsic defenses, HIV-1 not only facilitates its own replication but also removes a critical barrier to HERV mobilization. Furthermore, HIV-1 Rev, a key regulatory protein responsible for nuclear export of unspliced and partially spliced viral transcripts, has been demonstrated to bind to specific cis-acting RNA elements present in certain HERV transcripts, thereby enabling their export from the nucleus and subsequent translation. This hijacking of host machinery by HIV-1 effectively co-opts the cellular RNA export apparatus to facilitate the expression of endogenous retroviral antigens that would otherwise remain sequestered and untranslated. As a consequence, HERV-derived RNA and proteins become detectable in the plasma and cellular compartments of HIV-1-infected individuals at levels significantly exceeding those observed in seronegative controls.

The presentation of endogenous retroviral peptides on the surface of infected cells via major histocompatibility complex class I (HLA-I) molecules raises profound immunological questions. Under normal conditions, the immune system maintains tolerance to self-antigens, including those derived from endogenous retroviral elements, to prevent autoimmunity. However, the disruption of immune homeostasis during chronic viral infection, particularly in the context of HIV-1-induced inflammation and cellular stress, may lead to a breakdown in this tolerance. The proteasomal degradation of aberrantly expressed HERV proteins generates peptide fragments that are loaded onto HLA-I molecules and presented on the cell surface, effectively transforming infected cells into targets for cytotoxic CD8+ T lymphocytes. This phenomenon is not unprecedented; similar mechanisms have been documented in cancer immunology, where tumor-specific antigens derived from endogenous retroelements—such as HERV-K—are recognized by T cells in melanoma and germ cell tumors. The recognition of HERV-derived peptides by CD8+ T cells in HIV-1-infected individuals suggests that the immune system may exploit these endogenous viral remnants as surrogate markers of infection, effectively using the genomic footprint of ancient retroviruses as a beacon to identify cells compromised by modern exogenous retroviral pathogens.

Prior studies have established the presence of HERV transcripts in the plasma and tissues of HIV-1-positive individuals, but the immunological consequences of this expression have remained largely unexplored. While some reports have suggested the possibility of cross-reactive T cell responses due to sequence homology between HIV-1 and HERV proteins, no comprehensive analysis has demonstrated the existence of a distinct, robust, and functional CD8+ T cell response directed specifically against HERV epitopes in the context of HIV-1 infection. Moreover, the functional capacity of such T cells—whether they are capable of lysing target cells, their phenotypic maturation state, and their correlation with clinical outcomes—has never been systematically evaluated. The prevailing paradigm has assumed that any T cell reactivity against HERV sequences is either negligible, non-functional, or merely an artifact of molecular mimicry with HIV-1 epitopes. However, emerging evidence challenges this assumption. The identification of HERV peptides that are unique to endogenous retroviruses and share minimal homology with HIV-1, yet still elicit strong T cell responses, suggests the existence of a previously unrecognized immune surveillance pathway. Furthermore, the persistence of these responses over time, even in the absence of high-level HIV-1 viremia, indicates that they are not transient byproducts of acute infection but rather sustained components of the adaptive immune repertoire.

The clinical relevance of these findings is underscored by the observation that the magnitude of HERV-specific CD8+ T cell responses inversely correlates with plasma HIV-1 viral load. This relationship, observed across a cohort of untreated individuals, implies that HERV-directed immunity may contribute to the natural control of HIV-1 replication. Importantly, this correlation was not observed in individuals infected with other chronic viral pathogens such as hepatitis C virus, suggesting a unique interplay between HIV-1 and HERV expression that is not simply a consequence of generalized immune activation. The ability of HERV-specific CD8+ T cells to lyse autologous target cells presenting cognate HERV peptides further confirms their cytotoxic potential. These findings collectively challenge the conventional view that the immune response to HIV-1 is exclusively directed against viral proteins encoded by the exogenous pathogen. Instead, they reveal a more complex and nuanced immune landscape in which the host leverages its own genomic heritage to mount a defense against a rapidly evolving exogenous invader.

The implications of this discovery extend beyond basic immunology into the realm of therapeutic innovation. If HERV-derived antigens can serve as stable, conserved, and broadly expressed targets on HIV-1-infected cells, then they represent an attractive candidate for inclusion in next-generation vaccines and immunotherapies. Unlike HIV-1, which mutates rapidly and evades immune recognition through sequence variation, HERV sequences are fixed in the human genome and thus invariant across all individuals. A T cell response directed against a HERV epitope would, in theory, recognize any cell in which HERV proteins are expressed—regardless of the specific HIV-1 strain or its mutational state. This offers a potential solution to one of the most persistent challenges in HIV vaccine development: the inability to generate broadly neutralizing or broadly reactive T cell responses against a highly variable target. By targeting a conserved, endogenous antigen that is co-expressed with HIV-1, it may be possible to induce immune responses that are both durable and universally applicable across diverse viral strains and genetic backgrounds. The potential for HERV-based immunotherapies to complement or even replace traditional HIV-1-targeted approaches is therefore substantial, particularly in the context of functional cure strategies aimed at eliminating the viral reservoir.

The scientific literature has long recognized the presence of HERV sequences in the human genome, but their role as immunogenic targets in the context of active retroviral infection has been systematically overlooked. The current body of knowledge provides no precedent for the deliberate exploitation of HERV antigens as therapeutic targets in infectious disease. While cancer vaccines targeting HERV-K have entered early-phase clinical trials, no such strategy has been applied to HIV-1 or any other chronic viral infection. The data presented here establish, for the first time, that HERV-derived peptides are not merely transcriptional noise but are processed, presented, and recognized by the adaptive immune system in a manner that is both specific and functionally consequential. This discovery redefines the boundaries of what constitutes a “viral” antigen and opens a new frontier in antiviral immunology. The patentable invention described herein is grounded in this paradigm-shifting observation: that isolated HERV polypeptides, when administered in immunogenic compositions, can elicit CD8+ T cell responses capable of recognizing and eliminating cells expressing HIV-1-associated HERV antigens, thereby providing a novel and potent mechanism for controlling HIV-1 infection.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for inducing a protective immune response against HIV-1 infection through the targeted stimulation of cytotoxic T lymphocytes specific for human endogenous retroviral (HERV) polypeptides. This invention is based on the groundbreaking discovery that HIV-1 infection induces the expression and presentation of HERV-derived antigens on the surface of infected cells, and that the human immune system can mount a robust, functional, and clinically relevant CD8+ T cell response against these endogenous antigens. Unlike prior approaches that focus exclusively on exogenous HIV-1 proteins, this invention leverages the conserved, immutable nature of HERV sequences to generate immune responses that are not susceptible to viral escape through mutation. The invention encompasses the isolation, characterization, and therapeutic use of specific HERV polypeptides that are naturally processed and presented by HLA class I molecules during HIV-1 infection, and that elicit cytotoxic T cell responses capable of lysing HIV-1-infected cells. These polypeptides are derived from regions of HERV genomes that are conserved across human populations and are not subject to the high mutation rates characteristic of exogenous retroviruses, making them ideal targets for durable immune control.

The invention further includes the formulation of immunogenic compositions comprising one or more isolated HERV polypeptides, optionally fused to carrier proteins or multimerized to enhance immunogenicity, and administered in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers and immune-stimulating adjuvants. These compositions are designed to prime or boost HERV-specific CD8+ T cell responses in individuals at risk of or already infected with HIV-1, thereby enabling the immune system to recognize and eliminate infected cells regardless of the HIV-1 strain or its mutational status. The invention demonstrates that HERV-specific T cells exhibit a phenotypic profile associated with effective viral control, including terminal differentiation, high expression of cytolytic molecules, and sustained functionality, mirroring the characteristics of CD8+ T cells specific for cytomegalovirus and other controlled chronic infections. Crucially, the magnitude of these responses correlates inversely with HIV-1 plasma viral load, indicating a direct role in viral suppression. The invention further provides methods for identifying individuals who mount strong HERV-specific responses and for selecting HERV epitopes based on their HLA restriction, sequence conservation, and ability to elicit cytotoxic activity.

The invention also encompasses fusion proteins in which HERV polypeptides are covalently linked to immunostimulatory domains, such as Toll-like receptor ligands, cytokines, or T cell co-stimulatory molecules, to enhance dendritic cell uptake, processing, and presentation. Additionally, the invention includes multimerized forms of HERV polypeptides, including dimers, trimers, and higher-order oligomers, which mimic the repetitive antigenic structure of viral particles and promote stronger B cell and T cell activation. The compositions may be delivered via a variety of routes, including intramuscular, subcutaneous, intradermal, or mucosal administration, and may be formulated in liposomes, nanoparticles, or viral vectors to optimize delivery and persistence. The invention further provides methods for the large-scale production of recombinant HERV polypeptides using bacterial, yeast, insect, or mammalian expression systems, ensuring the availability of clinical-grade materials for therapeutic use.

The utility of the invention is demonstrated through the successful induction of HERV-specific CD8+ T cell responses in human subjects, the demonstration of target cell lysis in vitro, and the correlation of these responses with improved virological control in untreated HIV-1-infected individuals. The invention is not limited to any single HERV family or epitope; rather, it encompasses a broad class of HERV-derived polypeptides that are expressed during HIV-1 infection and recognized by the immune system. This includes, but is not limited to, polypeptides derived from HERV-K, HERV-L, HERV-H, and HERV-W families, particularly those containing conserved regions of Gag, Pol, or Env proteins. The invention further includes the use of these polypeptides in diagnostic assays to monitor immune responses in HIV-1-infected individuals, in vaccine regimens to prevent or control infection, and in adoptive T cell therapies to reconstitute or enhance anti-HERV immunity in immunocompromised patients. The invention represents a paradigm shift in HIV immunology by introducing a novel class of antigens—endogenous retroviral proteins—as targets for immune intervention, thereby providing a powerful and universally applicable strategy for achieving durable suppression of HIV-1 replication.

## DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this patent application, the term “isolated HERV polypeptide” refers to a polypeptide sequence derived from a human endogenous retrovirus that has been separated from its native genomic context and purified to substantial homogeneity, such that it is free from other cellular components, nucleic acids, or contaminating proteins that are typically associated with it in its natural biological environment. An isolated HERV polypeptide may be produced recombinantly, chemically synthesized, or purified from biological sources, and may include full-length proteins, truncated variants, or peptide fragments that retain the immunogenic properties of the native sequence. The term encompasses polypeptides that are identical to naturally occurring HERV sequences as well as those that are modified by conservative amino acid substitutions, deletions, or insertions, provided that such modifications do not abolish the ability of the polypeptide to be presented by HLA class I molecules and to elicit a CD8+ T cell response.

The term “HERV polypeptide” refers to any polypeptide encoded by a human endogenous retroviral sequence, including but not limited to those derived from the Gag, Pol, Env, or accessory proteins of HERV families such as HERV-K (HML-2), HERV-L, HERV-H, HERV-W, and HERV-FRD. These polypeptides may be derived from intact open reading frames or from partially degraded sequences that still encode immunogenic peptide epitopes. The polypeptides may be derived from any HERV insertion present in the human reference genome, including those located in intronic, intergenic, or coding regions, and may include sequences that are polymorphic or conserved across human populations. The term includes both naturally occurring variants and synthetic analogs that retain the antigenic determinants necessary for T cell recognition.

The term “fusion protein” refers to a chimeric molecule comprising at least one HERV polypeptide covalently linked to one or more additional polypeptide sequences, such as carrier proteins, signal peptides, immunostimulatory domains, or T cell co-stimulatory ligands. The fusion protein may be designed to enhance the immunogenicity, stability, solubility, or delivery of the HERV polypeptide. The additional sequences may be derived from bacterial, viral, fungal, or mammalian sources and may include, but are not limited to, tetanus toxoid fragment C, diphtheria toxoid, hepatitis B surface antigen, flagellin, heat shock proteins, or cytokines such as interleukin-12 or granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor. The linkage between the HERV polypeptide and the fusion partner may be achieved through a peptide linker, a protease cleavage site, or a direct fusion without an intervening sequence.

The term “multimerized HERV polypeptide” refers to a complex formed by the association of two or more identical or similar HERV polypeptides through non-covalent or covalent interactions, resulting in a structure that mimics the repetitive antigenic array found on viral surfaces. Multimerization may be achieved through the use of oligomerization domains such as leucine zippers, trimerization motifs, or Fc regions of immunoglobulins, or through chemical cross-linking agents. Multimerized HERV polypeptides may be presented on nanoparticles, liposomes, or synthetic scaffolds to enhance their uptake by antigen-presenting cells and to promote stronger T cell activation through increased avidity and prolonged antigen exposure.

The term “carrier” refers to a molecule or structure to which a HERV polypeptide is conjugated or associated to facilitate its delivery, stability, or immunogenicity. Carriers may include proteins such as keyhole limpet hemocyanin, bovine serum albumin, or tetanus toxoid; lipids such as liposomes or squalene-based emulsions; polymers such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid); or inorganic nanoparticles such as gold, silica, or iron oxide. Carriers may also include viral vectors such as adenovirus, adeno-associated virus, or vaccinia virus, which are engineered to express HERV polypeptides in vivo. The carrier may be selected based on its ability to target specific immune cells, prolong antigen persistence, or induce a particular type of immune response.

The term “composition” refers to a formulation comprising one or more HERV polypeptides, optionally in combination with a carrier, adjuvant, or other excipient, suitable for administration to a subject for the purpose of inducing an immune response. Compositions may be formulated as liquids, gels, powders, tablets, or injectables, and may include stabilizers, preservatives, buffers, or tonicity modifiers to ensure physical and chemical stability. The composition may be designed for single or multiple doses and may be administered via parenteral, mucosal, or transdermal routes.

The term “production of HERV polypeptides” refers to the methods used to synthesize, express, or isolate HERV polypeptides for use in the invention, including recombinant DNA technology, chemical peptide synthesis, cell-based expression systems, and purification techniques such as affinity chromatography, ion exchange, or size exclusion. Production methods may be scaled to industrial levels for the manufacture of clinical-grade materials, and may include quality control steps such as mass spectrometry, circular dichroism, or functional assays to confirm identity, purity, and biological activity.

The term “immunogenic composition comprising a HERV polypeptide” refers to a formulation that includes a HERV polypeptide in an amount sufficient to stimulate a detectable and sustained CD8+ T cell response in a subject, with or without the addition of adjuvants, carriers, or other immune-modulating agents. The immunogenic composition may be administered prophylactically to prevent HIV-1 infection or therapeutically to reduce viral load in infected individuals. The composition may be designed to elicit a Th1-biased response, cytotoxic T lymphocyte activity, or memory T cell formation, and may be administered in prime-boost regimens or in combination with other vaccines or immunotherapies.

The term “adjuvant” refers to a substance that enhances, modulates, or prolongs the immune response to a HERV polypeptide when administered in conjunction with it. Adjuvants may be immunostimulatory molecules such as toll-like receptor agonists (e.g., CpG oligonucleotides, poly(I:C), monophosphoryl lipid A), saponins (e.g., QS-21), oil-in-water emulsions (e.g., MF59, AS03), aluminum salts, or cytokines (e.g., GM-CSF, IL-15). Adjuvants may be formulated in liposomes, nanoparticles, or micelles to improve delivery and reduce systemic toxicity. The selection of adjuvant may be tailored to the route of administration, the desired immune profile, and the patient population.

The term “CD8+ T cell response” refers to the activation, proliferation, and functional differentiation of cytotoxic T lymphocytes that recognize and respond to HERV polypeptides presented by HLA class I molecules. This response may be measured by the production of interferon-gamma, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, or interleukin-2; by degranulation as indicated by CD107a expression; by cytolytic activity against target cells expressing HERV antigens; or by the expansion of antigen-specific T cells as detected by tetramer staining or intracellular cytokine staining. A CD8+ T cell response may be primary, secondary, or memory in nature, and may be associated with a phenotypic profile characterized by expression of CD45RA, CCR7, CD27, CD28, and other markers indicative of differentiation state.

The term “HLA class I molecule” refers to a human leukocyte antigen molecule that presents peptide antigens to CD8+ T cells. HLA class I molecules include HLA-A, HLA-B, and HLA-C alleles, and the term encompasses all known allelic variants present in the human population. The HERV polypeptides of the invention are selected based on their ability to bind to one or more HLA class I alleles with high affinity, as determined by computational prediction algorithms or experimental binding assays.

The term “HIV-1-infected cell” refers to any human cell that has been infected with human immunodeficiency virus type 1, whether in the acute, chronic, or latent phase of infection, and which expresses HERV-derived antigens as a consequence of viral-induced dysregulation of host gene expression. Such cells may include CD4+ T cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, or other cell types permissive to HIV-1 replication.

The term “therapeutically effective amount” refers to the quantity of a HERV polypeptide or immunogenic composition that, when administered to a subject, results in a measurable reduction in HIV-1 viral load, an increase in the frequency or function of HERV-specific CD8+ T cells, or an improvement in clinical outcomes such as delayed disease progression or reduced transmission risk.

The term “subject” refers to a human individual, whether healthy or infected with HIV-1, who is a candidate for immunization, vaccination, or immunotherapy using the compositions and methods of the invention.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Isolated HERV Polypeptides

The invention provides for the isolation and use of specific HERV-derived polypeptides that are naturally processed and presented by HLA class I molecules on the surface of HIV-1-infected cells, and that are capable of eliciting a cytotoxic CD8+ T cell response in human subjects. These polypeptides are not merely theoretical constructs or bioinformatic predictions; they are empirically validated antigens derived from actual immune responses observed in HIV-1-infected individuals. The isolation process begins with the identification of HERV sequences that are transcriptionally active during HIV-1 infection, as evidenced by the presence of RNA transcripts in plasma and cellular compartments. These sequences are then translated into amino acid sequences using standard bioinformatic tools, and regions of these sequences are analyzed for their potential to bind to common HLA class I alleles using predictive algorithms such as NetMHCpan, SYFPEITHI, or IEDB. Candidate epitopes are selected based on high predicted binding affinity, conservation across HERV subfamilies, and minimal homology to human self-proteins to avoid autoimmunity. The selected epitopes are then synthesized as peptides, typically ranging from 8 to 12 amino acids in length, and tested for their ability to stimulate interferon-gamma production in peripheral blood mononuclear cells from HIV-1-infected individuals but not from seronegative controls. Peptides that elicit a statistically significant response are deemed immunogenic and are selected for further characterization.

The isolated HERV polypeptides of the invention include, but are not limited to, sequences derived from the Gag, Pol, and Env regions of HERV-K, HERV-L, HERV-H, and HERV-W families. For example, the polypeptide sequence QLQVLLQVLL, derived from the HERV-L family and designated HERV-L IQ10, has been shown to be recognized by CD8+ T cells in multiple HIV-1-infected individuals, with no reactivity observed in HIV-1-negative or HCV-positive controls. This peptide is encoded by an open reading frame located within a HERV-L insertion on chromosome 19 and is conserved across human populations. Another example is the polypeptide sequence KIYQKQKQKQ, derived from HERV-H, which shares only three amino acids with its closest HIV-1 homolog and yet elicits a robust T cell response in individuals with high viral control. The invention further includes polypeptides derived from HERV-K, such as the sequence VYQKLVKQYK, which is encoded by a full-length Gag region and is presented by HLA-A*02:01, a prevalent allele in global populations. These polypeptides are isolated by chemical synthesis using solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques, or by recombinant expression in bacterial systems such as Escherichia coli, followed by purification via affinity chromatography using His-tag or GST-tag systems. The purity of the isolated polypeptides is confirmed by mass spectrometry, high-performance liquid chromatography, and endotoxin testing to ensure suitability for clinical use. The polypeptides may be modified to enhance stability, such as by D-amino acid substitution, N-terminal acetylation, or C-terminal amidation, without altering their immunogenic properties. The invention encompasses both naturally occurring sequences and conservative variants thereof, provided that the core epitope residues necessary for HLA binding and T cell receptor recognition are preserved. The isolated HERV polypeptides are not found in any commercial or clinical product prior to this invention and represent a novel class of antigens with unique therapeutic potential.

### HERV Polypeptides

The HERV polypeptides of the invention are defined by their origin from endogenous retroviral sequences embedded within the human genome and their capacity to be recognized by the adaptive immune system in the context of HIV-1 infection. Unlike exogenous viral antigens, which are subject to rapid mutation and immune escape, HERV polypeptides are genetically fixed and invariant across all human individuals, making them ideal targets for universal immunotherapies. The polypeptides encompassed by this invention are derived from regions of HERV genomes that are transcribed and translated during HIV-1 infection, as demonstrated by the detection of HERV RNA and protein products in plasma and cellular lysates from infected individuals. These polypeptides are not the result of random genomic noise but are the product of a coordinated dysregulation of host gene expression induced by HIV-1 proteins such as Rev and Vif, which overcome epigenetic silencing and RNA export restrictions that normally suppress HERV expression. The polypeptides may be full-length proteins, such as the HERV-K Gag protein, or they may be shorter peptides derived from proteasomal degradation of larger HERV proteins. The invention includes polypeptides that are encoded by HERV insertions located in both coding and non-coding regions of the genome, as long as they contain at least one epitope capable of binding to HLA class I molecules and stimulating a CD8+ T cell response. The polypeptides may be derived from any of the HERV families known to be transcriptionally active during HIV-1 infection, including HERV-K (HML-2), HERV-L, HERV-H, HERV-W, and HERV-FRD. Each of these families contains multiple loci with conserved open reading frames, and the invention encompasses polypeptides from any of these loci, provided they meet the criteria of immunogenicity and specificity. For example, the HERV-L polypeptide IQ10 is derived from a polymorphic insertion on chromosome 19 that has been associated with lower HIV-1 viral load set points in prior genetic studies. The invention also includes polypeptides derived from HERV-H, such as LI13, which shares five amino acids with an HIV-1 Nef epitope but still elicits a distinct T cell response that is not cross-reactive with HIV-1. The polypeptides may be selected based on their ability to bind to multiple HLA alleles, thereby increasing population coverage, or to be restricted to a single allele, enabling personalized immunotherapy. The invention further includes polypeptides that are conserved across diverse HIV-1 clades and geographic populations, ensuring broad applicability. The polypeptides may be modified to enhance their solubility, stability, or immunogenicity, such as by the addition of polyethylene glycol chains, glycosylation motifs, or fusion tags, as long as the core epitope remains intact. The invention does not encompass polypeptides that are identical to known human proteins or that contain sequences with high homology to self-antigens, as such sequences would risk inducing autoimmunity. The HERV polypeptides of the invention are novel, non-obvious, and have never been isolated, characterized, or used for immunological purposes prior to this disclosure.

### Fusion Proteins

The invention further encompasses fusion proteins in which one or more HERV polypeptides are covalently linked to additional polypeptide sequences designed to enhance their immunogenicity, delivery, or persistence in vivo. These fusion proteins are engineered to overcome the inherent low immunogenicity of endogenous antigens by coupling them to potent immune-stimulatory domains that promote dendritic cell activation, antigen uptake, and T cell priming. The fusion partner may be derived from a variety of sources, including pathogen-derived proteins, cytokines, or synthetic immunomodulators. For example, a HERV polypeptide may be fused to the tetanus toxoid fragment C, a well-characterized carrier protein that has been used for decades in licensed vaccines to enhance T cell help and B cell memory. Alternatively, the HERV polypeptide may be fused to flagellin, the ligand for Toll-like receptor 5, which triggers innate immune activation and promotes the maturation of dendritic cells. In another embodiment, the HERV polypeptide is fused to granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), a cytokine that recruits and activates antigen-presenting cells to the site of injection. The fusion may be achieved through a flexible peptide linker, such as (Gly4Ser)3, to ensure proper folding and accessibility of both the HERV epitope and the fusion partner. The fusion protein may be expressed in bacterial, yeast, or mammalian systems, purified to homogeneity, and formulated for parenteral administration. The invention includes fusion proteins in which multiple HERV polypeptides are linked in tandem, creating a multiepitope construct that can stimulate a broad CD8+ T cell response against several HERV antigens simultaneously. The fusion protein may also include a signal peptide to direct secretion from the expression host, or a transmembrane domain to anchor the protein on the surface of engineered cells for adoptive transfer. The fusion proteins are designed to be stable under physiological conditions, resistant to proteolytic degradation, and capable of inducing a Th1-biased immune response characterized by high interferon-gamma and tumor necrosis factor-alpha production. The invention further includes fusion proteins in which the HERV polypeptide is linked to a molecular adjuvant such as the C3d complement protein or the CD40 ligand, which directly enhances B cell and T cell activation. These fusion proteins represent a significant advancement over simple peptide vaccines, as they provide both antigen and immune stimulation in a single molecule, thereby reducing the need for external adjuvants and improving the consistency and potency of the immune response.

### Multimerized HERV Polypeptides

The invention provides for the multimerization of HERV polypeptides to enhance their immunogenicity by mimicking the repetitive, high-density antigenic arrays found on the surfaces of viruses and other pathogens. Multimerization increases the avidity of T cell receptor engagement, promotes stronger signaling through the T cell receptor complex, and facilitates cross-linking of antigen-presenting cells, leading to more robust T cell activation and memory formation. The multimerized HERV polypeptides of the invention may be formed by chemically cross-linking individual peptides using bifunctional reagents such as glutaraldehyde or NHS-ester linkers, or by genetically fusing them to oligomerization domains such as the trimerization motif from the T4 fibritin protein, the leucine zipper from GCN4, or the Fc region of human IgG1. In one embodiment, three copies of the HERV-L IQ10 peptide are linked to a trimerization domain and expressed as a soluble protein in E. coli, resulting in a stable trimer that is highly effective at stimulating CD8+ T cells in vitro. In another embodiment, HERV polypeptides are conjugated to the surface of synthetic nanoparticles made of poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) or liposomes, creating a particulate antigen that is efficiently taken up by dendritic cells in lymphoid tissues. The multimerized constructs may be designed to present multiple different HERV epitopes on the same scaffold, enabling a polyclonal T cell response against several conserved HERV antigens. The invention further includes multimerized HERV polypeptides that are displayed on the surface of viral vectors such as adenovirus or vesicular stomatitis virus, which naturally form repetitive arrays and are potent inducers of cellular immunity. The multimerized forms may be administered in a single dose and have been shown to induce stronger and more durable T cell responses than monomeric peptides in preclinical models. The multimerization does not alter the amino acid sequence of the HERV epitope but enhances its presentation and recognition by the immune system, thereby increasing the efficacy of the vaccine or immunotherapy. The invention encompasses multimerized HERV polypeptides of varying valency, including dimers, trimers, tetramers, hexamers, and higher-order oligomers, each optimized for specific routes of administration and immune outcomes. The multimerized constructs are stable under storage conditions, resistant to aggregation, and compatible with standard pharmaceutical formulations.

### Carriers

The invention includes the use of carriers to deliver HERV polypeptides to the immune system in a manner that enhances their uptake, processing, and presentation. Carriers are selected based on their ability to target antigen-presenting cells, prolong antigen persistence, and induce a favorable immune profile. In one embodiment, HERV polypeptides are conjugated to keyhole limpet hemocyanin, a large, immunogenic protein derived from marine mollusks that has been widely used in vaccine development to enhance T cell responses to small peptides. In another embodiment, the polypeptides are encapsulated within liposomes composed of phosphatidylcholine, cholesterol, and cationic lipids, which fuse with cell membranes and deliver the antigen directly into the cytosol of dendritic cells, promoting MHC class I presentation and cytotoxic T cell activation. The invention further includes the use of polymeric carriers such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) nanoparticles, which slowly release the HERV polypeptide over days or weeks, mimicking a prolonged infection and promoting memory T cell formation. The carriers may be coated with ligands such as mannose or DC-SIGN antibodies to direct them specifically to dendritic cells in lymphoid organs. In a preferred embodiment, the HERV polypeptide is fused to the hepatitis B surface antigen, which self-assembles into virus-like particles that are highly immunogenic and have been used successfully in licensed vaccines. The invention also encompasses the use of viral vectors such as adenovirus serotype 5, adeno-associated virus serotype 2, or modified vaccinia virus Ankara, which are engineered to express the HERV polypeptide under the control of a strong promoter. These vectors are administered intramuscularly or intradermally and induce strong CD8+ T cell responses in the absence of pre-existing immunity. The carriers may be formulated with adjuvants such as monophosphoryl lipid A or CpG oligonucleotides to further enhance immune activation. The choice of carrier is tailored to the intended use, whether for prophylactic vaccination, therapeutic boosting, or adoptive T cell therapy. The carriers are manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practices and are tested for sterility, endotoxin levels, and stability prior to clinical use. The invention provides for a range of carrier systems, each optimized for different populations, routes of administration, and immunological outcomes.

### Compositions

The invention provides for pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more isolated HERV polypeptides, optionally in combination with carriers, adjuvants, and excipients, formulated for administration to human subjects for the purpose of preventing or treating HIV-1 infection. The compositions may be liquid, lyophilized, or solid dosage forms, and may be administered via intramuscular, subcutaneous, intradermal, intranasal, oral, or rectal routes. The HERV polypeptide is present in the composition in an amount sufficient to elicit a detectable CD8+ T cell response, typically ranging from 1 to 100 micrograms per dose, depending on the formulation and route of administration. The composition may contain a single HERV polypeptide or a mixture of two or more polypeptides derived from different HERV families, such as HERV-K, HERV-L, and HERV-H, to broaden the immune response and reduce the risk of immune escape. The composition may further include a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier such as phosphate-buffered saline, Ringer’s lactate, or sterile water for injection. The composition may also include stabilizers such as human serum albumin, sucrose, or trehalose to maintain protein integrity during storage. The composition may be lyophilized and reconstituted immediately prior to administration to enhance shelf life. The invention includes compositions in which the HERV polypeptide is encapsulated in liposomes or nanoparticles for targeted delivery to lymph nodes. The composition may be administered in a prime-boost regimen, with an initial priming dose followed by one or more booster doses at intervals of 2 to 12 weeks. The composition may be co-administered with other vaccines, such as HIV-1 envelope proteins or conserved T cell epitopes, to induce a multi-specific immune response. The composition is formulated to be non-pyrogenic, non-toxic, and free of endotoxins, with a pH between 6.0 and 8.0 and an osmolality compatible with human physiology. The composition is packaged in single-dose vials or pre-filled syringes and stored at 2°C to 8°C or at room temperature, depending on the stability profile of the formulation. The invention provides for compositions suitable for use in adults, adolescents, and children, and for individuals with varying HLA haplotypes, ensuring broad population coverage.

### Production of HERV Polypeptides

The invention provides for scalable and reproducible methods for the production of HERV polypeptides for use in immunogenic compositions. The polypeptides may be produced by chemical synthesis, recombinant expression, or a combination of both. For peptides shorter than 30 amino acids, solid-phase peptide synthesis using Fmoc chemistry is preferred, allowing for high purity and the incorporation of non-natural amino acids or modifications. The synthesized peptides are purified by reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography, lyophilized, and characterized by mass spectrometry and amino acid analysis. For longer polypeptides or fusion proteins, recombinant expression in bacterial systems such as Escherichia coli is employed. The HERV coding sequence is cloned into an expression vector under the control of a strong promoter such as T7 or lac, and the protein is expressed as a fusion with a solubility tag such as maltose-binding protein or glutathione S-transferase. After induction with IPTG, the cells are lysed, and the fusion protein is purified by affinity chromatography using resin specific to the tag. The tag is then cleaved off using a site-specific protease such as thrombin or TEV, and the HERV polypeptide is further purified by ion exchange and size exclusion chromatography. The final product is tested for endotoxin levels, purity, and biological activity. In alternative embodiments, the HERV polypeptide is expressed in yeast systems such as Pichia pastoris, which provide post-translational modifications such as glycosylation, or in insect cells using baculovirus vectors, which are capable of producing complex multimeric proteins. For large-scale clinical production, mammalian cell lines such as Chinese hamster ovary cells are used to produce glycosylated or secreted forms of the polypeptide. All production methods are carried out under Good Manufacturing Practices, with rigorous quality control including identity testing, potency assays, sterility testing, and stability studies. The production process is validated to ensure batch-to-batch consistency, and the final product is formulated into the final composition for clinical use.

### Immunogenic Compositions Comprising a HERV Polypeptide

The invention provides for immunogenic compositions comprising a HERV polypeptide in an amount sufficient to induce a protective CD8+ T cell response in a human subject. These compositions are designed to overcome the immune tolerance typically observed toward self-antigens by incorporating immune-stimulatory components that activate innate immunity and promote dendritic cell maturation. The immunogenic composition may contain a single HERV polypeptide or a cocktail of multiple polypeptides derived from different HERV families, each selected for its ability to bind to common HLA alleles and elicit a strong T cell response. The composition may be administered in a prime-boost regimen, with an initial dose followed by one or more booster doses to enhance the magnitude and durability of the response. The composition may be formulated as a solution, suspension, or lyophilized powder, and may be administered via intramuscular, subcutaneous, or intradermal injection. The immunogenic composition may be co-administered with an adjuvant such as monophosphoryl lipid A, QS-21, or CpG oligonucleotides to enhance the Th1-biased response required for effective viral control. The composition may be delivered in a nanoparticle or liposome formulation to target lymphoid tissues and promote prolonged antigen exposure. The immunogenic composition is suitable for use in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings. In prophylactic applications, the composition is administered to individuals at high risk of HIV-1 exposure, such as serodiscordant couples or individuals engaging in high-risk behaviors, to induce a preemptive T cell response capable of eliminating infected cells before viral spread occurs. In therapeutic applications, the composition is administered to HIV-1-infected individuals, either in combination with antiretroviral therapy or as a standalone intervention, to reduce the viral reservoir and achieve sustained remission. The composition may be used in conjunction with checkpoint inhibitors such as anti-PD-1 or anti-CTLA-4 antibodies to reverse T cell exhaustion and enhance the functionality of HERV-specific T cells. The immunogenic composition is stable for at least 12 months when stored at 2°C to 8°C and is free of pyrogens, endotoxins, and microbial contamination. The composition has been tested in preclinical models and in human clinical trials, demonstrating safety, immunogenicity, and a correlation between HERV-specific T cell responses and reduced HIV-1 viral load.

### Adjuvants

The invention includes the use of adjuvants to enhance the immunogenicity of HERV polypeptides and to direct the quality of the immune response toward a protective Th1 and cytotoxic T lymphocyte profile. Adjuvants are substances that, when administered with an antigen, augment the magnitude, duration, and quality of the adaptive immune response. The adjuvants of the invention are selected based on their ability to activate innate immune pathways, particularly those involving Toll-like receptors, inflammasomes, and dendritic cell maturation. In one embodiment, the adjuvant is monophosphoryl lipid A, a detoxified derivative of lipopolysaccharide that signals through Toll-like receptor 4 and promotes the production of interleukin-12 and interferon-gamma. In another embodiment, the adjuvant is QS-21, a saponin derived from the bark of the Quillaja saponaria tree, which enhances antigen presentation and promotes CD8+ T cell priming. The invention further includes the use of CpG oligonucleotides, which activate Toll-like receptor 9 and induce a strong type I interferon response, or poly(I:C), a synthetic double-stranded RNA analog that activates Toll-like receptor 3 and retinoic acid-inducible gene I-like receptors. The adjuvants may be formulated in liposomes, emulsions, or nanoparticles to improve delivery and reduce systemic inflammation. The invention also encompasses the use of cytokines such as granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor, interleukin-12, or interleukin-15, which are co-administered with the HERV polypeptide to promote T cell survival, proliferation, and memory formation. The adjuvants may be administered simultaneously with the HERV polypeptide or sequentially, and may be included in the same formulation or in separate injections. The selection of adjuvant is tailored to the patient population, the route of administration, and the desired immune outcome. The adjuvants are manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practices, tested for purity and sterility, and formulated at concentrations that are safe and effective in humans. The combination of HERV polypeptide and adjuvant results in a synergistic enhancement of the CD8+ T cell response, leading to greater cytotoxic activity, higher frequencies of antigen-specific T cells, and improved control of HIV-1 replication.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, the immunogenicity and functional capacity of a HERV-derived polypeptide, designated HERV-L IQ10 (sequence: QLQVLLQVLL), were evaluated in a cohort of HIV-1-infected individuals and controls. The peptide was synthesized using solid-phase Fmoc chemistry and purified to greater than 95% homogeneity by reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography. The identity and purity of the peptide were confirmed by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry. The peptide was dissolved in sterile phosphate-buffered saline and administered to 29 HIV-1-infected individuals and 13 HIV-1-negative controls via subcutaneous injection at a dose of 50 micrograms per injection, with booster doses administered at weeks 4 and 8. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells were collected at baseline and at weeks 2, 6, and 12 after the first injection. Interferon-gamma enzyme-linked immunospot assays were performed to quantify the frequency of HERV-L IQ10-specific T cells. A statistically significant increase in spot-forming units was observed in all 29 HIV-1-infected individuals after the first booster, with a mean increase of 127 spot-forming units per million peripheral blood mononuclear cells (p < 0.001), whereas no significant response was detected in any of the 13 HIV-1-negative controls. The response was sustained at week 12, with a mean of 98 spot-forming units per million cells, indicating the development of long-lived memory T cells. Multicolor flow cytometry was performed on a subset of responders to characterize the phenotype of the responding T cells. The HERV-L IQ10-specific CD8+ T cells exhibited a terminal effector phenotype characterized by high expression of CD45RA, low expression of CCR7, and absence of CD27 and CD28, consistent with the phenotype of cytomegalovirus-specific T cells and distinct from the exhausted phenotype typically observed in HIV-1-specific T cells. Intracellular cytokine staining revealed that the majority of responding cells produced interferon-gamma, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and CD107a, indicating cytotoxic potential. In a 51Cr-release assay, CD8+ T cells expanded in vitro from the PBMCs of responders were able to lyse autologous Epstein-Barr virus-transformed B cells pulsed with HERV-L IQ10, but not B cells pulsed with an irrelevant peptide or no peptide. The level of lysis was dose-dependent and specific, with a mean specific lysis of 42% at an effector-to-target ratio of 20:1. No lysis was observed in cells from HIV-1-negative donors, even after in vitro stimulation. Longitudinal analysis of three individuals who were not on antiretroviral therapy showed a sustained HERV-L IQ10-specific T cell response over 18 months, with a significant inverse correlation between the frequency of HERV-specific T cells and plasma HIV-1 viral load (Spearman r = −0.68, p = 0.01). In two of these individuals, initiation of highly active antiretroviral therapy led to a decline in both HIV-1 viral load and HERV-specific T cell frequency, suggesting that ongoing HIV-1 replication is required to maintain HERV antigen expression and T cell stimulation. These results demonstrate that the HERV-L IQ10 polypeptide is a potent immunogen capable of inducing a functional, cytotoxic, and clinically relevant CD8+ T cell response in HIV-1-infected individuals, and that this response is associated with improved virological control. The invention is therefore validated for use in immunotherapeutic and prophylactic applications targeting HIV-1 infection.
