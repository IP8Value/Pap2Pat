# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and cellular reprogramming, specifically to a novel method for the direct conversion of somatic cells into induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) capable of self-renewal and terminal differentiation into functional, mature dopaminergic neurons. The invention further encompasses the use of these induced dopaminergic progenitors for the treatment of neurodegenerative disorders, particularly Parkinson’s disease, through transplantation into affected neural circuits. The method described herein bypasses the need for pluripotent intermediates and eliminates reliance on exogenous morphogens such as sonic hedgehog (SHH) or fibroblast growth factor 8 (FGF8) to specify midbrain dopaminergic identity, thereby providing a more efficient, reproducible, and clinically translatable approach for generating patient-specific dopaminergic cell sources. The invention also includes engineered variants of the induced dopaminergic progenitors that exhibit enhanced proliferative capacity under controlled conditions, enabling scalable production for therapeutic applications without compromising lineage fidelity or safety.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Neurodegenerative diseases characterized by the progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta, most notably Parkinson’s disease, remain among the most challenging conditions to treat with conventional pharmacological or surgical interventions. While dopamine replacement therapies such as levodopa provide symptomatic relief in early stages, they fail to halt disease progression and are often accompanied by debilitating motor complications over time. Cell replacement strategies aimed at restoring lost dopaminergic innervation in the striatum have emerged as a promising therapeutic avenue, with efforts focused on deriving dopaminergic neurons from pluripotent stem cells, including embryonic stem cells and induced pluripotent stem cells. However, these approaches are burdened by prolonged differentiation protocols, low efficiency of subtype specification, heterogeneity of resulting cell populations, and the risk of tumorigenesis due to residual undifferentiated cells.

Alternative strategies involving direct reprogramming of somatic cells into neural lineages have gained traction as a means to circumvent the pluripotent state and reduce the time and complexity associated with conventional differentiation. Previous studies have demonstrated the feasibility of converting fibroblasts into induced neural progenitor cells or even directly into neurons using defined transcription factors. However, these methods have largely failed to generate regionally specified dopaminergic progenitors with the fidelity required for functional integration into the midbrain circuitry. In particular, the generation of midbrain dopaminergic progenitors—those capable of giving rise to neurons expressing tyrosine hydroxylase, aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, the dopamine transporter, and the vesicular monoamine transporter 2—has remained elusive without the addition of exogenous patterning signals such as SHH and FGF8, which are difficult to control precisely in vivo and introduce variability into the differentiation outcome.

Moreover, existing protocols for generating neural progenitors from somatic cells typically yield populations with broad neuroectodermal potential, including the capacity to differentiate into astrocytes and oligodendrocytes, which compromises the purity and therapeutic specificity of the resulting grafts. The presence of non-dopaminergic cell types in transplanted populations not only reduces efficacy but may also contribute to aberrant circuit integration or inflammatory responses. Furthermore, the limited self-renewal capacity of directly reprogrammed neural progenitors has posed a significant barrier to their clinical translation, as sufficient cell numbers for transplantation cannot be reliably expanded without inducing genetic instability or loss of identity.

Recent advances in transcription factor-mediated reprogramming have identified key regulators of midbrain dopaminergic development, including Foxa2, Lmx1a, and Otx2, which are essential for the specification of floor plate-derived dopaminergic progenitors during embryogenesis. However, prior attempts to exploit these factors in reprogramming contexts have either required their co-expression with multiple additional factors, failed to produce expandable progenitor populations, or resulted in cells that still depended on morphogen stimulation for dopaminergic commitment. There remains a critical unmet need for a method that enables the direct, efficient, and robust conversion of readily accessible somatic cells—such as dermal fibroblasts—into a homogeneous, lineage-restricted, and expandable population of dopaminergic progenitors that intrinsically possess midbrain identity and the capacity to mature into fully functional dopaminergic neurons without external patterning cues. Such a method would represent a transformative advance in the field of cell therapy for Parkinson’s disease and related disorders, offering a safer, faster, and more scalable alternative to existing approaches.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for the direct reprogramming of somatic cells into induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) through the ectopic expression of three transcription factors: Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. The invention demonstrates that the combined expression of these three factors is sufficient to convert fibroblasts into a stable, clonogenic, and expandable progenitor population that exhibits molecular and functional characteristics of midbrain dopaminergic progenitors without the need for exogenous morphogens such as SHH or FGF8. The resulting iDPs express a comprehensive panel of markers associated with dopaminergic progenitor identity, including Aldh1A1, Corin (Lrp4), Lmx1a, Otx2, Ngn2, Mash1, Pitx3, and Nkx6.1, while simultaneously suppressing genes associated with glial lineage commitment, such as Glast, Olig1/2, NG2, GFAP, and S100β. The iDPs demonstrate a strict neuronal lineage restriction, differentiating exclusively into tyrosine hydroxylase-positive dopaminergic neurons upon maturation, with no detectable generation of astrocytes or oligodendrocytes under standard differentiation conditions.

Importantly, the iDPs generated by this method are capable of maturing into fully functional dopaminergic neurons that exhibit electrophysiological properties consistent with mature midbrain dopaminergic neurons, including the ability to generate action potentials in response to depolarizing current injections and to express voltage-gated sodium and potassium currents with kinetics typical of mature neurons. These neurons also express the full complement of dopaminergic machinery, including aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, the dopamine transporter, and the vesicular monoamine transporter 2, and form synaptic puncta positive for synaptophysin, indicating the capacity for neurotransmitter release and circuit integration. The iDPs retain their dopaminergic identity and differentiation potential even in the absence of SHH and FGF8, a feature that distinguishes them from all previously described neural progenitor populations derived from direct reprogramming and underscores the intrinsic specification conferred by the Brn2-Sox2-Foxa2 triad.

The invention further encompasses an engineered variant of the iDPs in which the expression of the proto-oncogene L-Myc is conditionally activated via a tetracycline-inducible system, enabling the controlled expansion of the progenitor population without altering their dopaminergic identity or compromising their safety profile. Upon withdrawal of the inducer, the cells resume their differentiation program and mature into functional dopaminergic neurons. This feature allows for scalable production of clinically relevant cell numbers while maintaining lineage fidelity and eliminating the risk of tumorigenesis associated with constitutive oncogene expression.

The invention further provides for the transplantation of these iDPs into animal models of Parkinson’s disease, where they survive long-term, integrate into host neural circuitry, differentiate predominantly into dopaminergic neurons in vivo, and partially restore motor function. Transplanted iDPs show no evidence of tumor formation, ectopic tissue growth, or aberrant differentiation into non-neuronal lineages, demonstrating their safety and therapeutic potential. The method described herein represents a paradigm shift in cell replacement therapy for Parkinson’s disease, offering a direct, efficient, and clinically viable route to generating patient-specific dopaminergic progenitors for autologous transplantation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the term “somatic cell” refers to any differentiated cell of the body that is not a germ cell or a pluripotent stem cell, including but not limited to dermal fibroblasts, keratinocytes, adipocytes, and peripheral blood mononuclear cells. The term “direct reprogramming” refers to the process of converting one differentiated somatic cell type directly into another differentiated or progenitor cell type without passing through a pluripotent intermediate state. The term “induced dopaminergic progenitor” or “iDP” refers to a cell that has been directly reprogrammed from a somatic cell through the ectopic expression of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2, and which exhibits the molecular, phenotypic, and functional characteristics of a midbrain dopaminergic progenitor, including the capacity for self-renewal under controlled conditions and the exclusive potential to differentiate into dopaminergic neurons. The term “midbrain identity” refers to the molecular signature characteristic of dopaminergic progenitors arising from the ventral midbrain floor plate, including the expression of Otx2, Lmx1a, Aldh1A1, Corin, Nkx6.1, and Pitx3, and the absence of forebrain-specific markers such as FoxG1, GSX2, and Nkx2.1. The term “lineage-restricted” refers to the capacity of a progenitor cell to differentiate exclusively into one lineage, in this case the dopaminergic neuronal lineage, without giving rise to astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, or other non-dopaminergic neuronal subtypes. The term “functional dopaminergic neuron” refers to a neuron that expresses the key molecular markers of dopaminergic identity—including tyrosine hydroxylase, aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, the dopamine transporter, and the vesicular monoamine transporter 2—and exhibits electrophysiological properties consistent with mature midbrain dopaminergic neurons, such as the ability to generate action potentials and voltage-gated sodium and potassium currents. The term “conditional expression” refers to the controlled activation or suppression of a gene product through an inducible system, such as a tetracycline-responsive promoter, wherein the expression of the gene is dependent on the presence or absence of a small molecule inducer, such as doxycycline. The term “self-renewal” refers to the capacity of a progenitor cell to undergo mitotic division while maintaining its undifferentiated state and lineage potential. The term “transplantation” refers to the surgical introduction of cells into a living organism for the purpose of replacing lost or damaged tissue, in this case the striatal dopaminergic innervation in a model of Parkinson’s disease. The term “therapeutically effective amount” refers to the quantity of iDPs sufficient to produce a measurable improvement in motor function or restoration of dopaminergic innervation in a subject suffering from Parkinson’s disease or a related neurodegenerative disorder, without inducing adverse effects such as tumor formation or aberrant neural connectivity.

### Somatic Cells

The method of the present invention is initiated with the isolation and culture of somatic cells, preferably dermal fibroblasts, obtained from a mammalian subject. The fibroblasts may be derived from a variety of sources, including skin biopsies, foreskin, or other connective tissues, and may be obtained from human or non-human mammals, including but not limited to mice, rats, rabbits, non-human primates, or humans. The fibroblasts may be obtained from individuals of any age, including neonatal, juvenile, adult, or elderly subjects, and may be derived from both healthy individuals and those suffering from neurodegenerative disorders such as Parkinson’s disease. The cells are cultured in standard fibroblast growth medium, such as Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, non-essential amino acids, and antibiotics, under standard atmospheric conditions of 37°C and 5% CO₂. The fibroblasts are maintained in culture for no more than five passages to avoid replicative senescence and to preserve their reprogramming competence. The cells are harvested at a density of approximately 2 × 10⁵ per 35-mm dish and are prepared for viral transduction by ensuring a confluence of approximately 50–70% at the time of infection. The use of fibroblasts as the starting cell type is preferred due to their accessibility, ease of culture, and high reprogramming efficiency, but other somatic cell types, including keratinocytes, adipose-derived stromal cells, or peripheral blood mononuclear cells, may be substituted with comparable success following optimization of culture and transduction conditions. The somatic cells used in this method are not genetically modified prior to reprogramming, and no selection or enrichment steps are performed prior to viral transduction, ensuring that the reprogramming process is initiated from a heterogeneous, unselected population of cells representative of the original tissue source.

### Reprogramming (Transdifferentiation)

The reprogramming of somatic cells into induced dopaminergic progenitors is achieved through the stable ectopic expression of three transcription factors: Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. These factors are delivered to the somatic cells via retroviral vectors, although alternative delivery systems such as lentiviral vectors, episomal plasmids, Sendai virus, or mRNA transfection may be employed with appropriate modifications to the protocol. The retroviral vectors encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 are constructed using a pMXs-based backbone, with the open reading frames of each gene cloned into separate vectors or co-expressed in a single vector using internal ribosome entry sites or 2A peptide linkers. The Brn2 gene is derived from mouse or human genomic sequence and encodes a POU-domain transcription factor essential for neural progenitor specification. The Sox2 gene encodes an HMG-box transcription factor critical for maintaining neural progenitor identity and pluripotency. The Foxa2 gene encodes a forkhead box transcription factor that functions as a master regulator of midbrain floor plate development and dopaminergic progenitor specification. The expression of these three factors is sufficient to initiate a cascade of endogenous gene activation, leading to the silencing of fibroblast-specific programs and the activation of a neural progenitor transcriptional network with exclusive dopaminergic potential. The reprogramming process is initiated by infecting the somatic cells with a mixture of retroviral particles encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2, using a polybrene-enhanced protocol to maximize transduction efficiency. The infected cells are maintained in fibroblast medium for 48 hours following infection, after which the medium is replaced with neural progenitor medium, comprising NeuroCult® NSC Basal Medium supplemented with NeuroCult® NSC Proliferation Supplements, 20 ng/mL basic fibroblast growth factor, and 20 ng/mL epidermal growth factor. Under these conditions, the cells undergo morphological changes within seven days, transitioning from a spindle-shaped fibroblastic morphology to a compact, rounded, colony-forming neural progenitor phenotype. By day 14, discrete colonies emerge that are morphologically distinct from surrounding fibroblasts and express the neural progenitor marker Nestin. These colonies are manually picked, dissociated, and replated for expansion. The resulting cell population, termed induced dopaminergic progenitors, retains the expression of the exogenous transcription factors Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2, while simultaneously upregulating endogenous expression of these same genes, indicating the establishment of a self-sustaining transcriptional network. The reprogramming efficiency, as measured by the number of Nestin-positive colonies formed per 10⁵ input fibroblasts, exceeds 1%, a significant improvement over prior methods that yield efficiencies below 0.1%. The reprogramming process is completed within 14 to 21 days, and the resulting iDPs are ready for expansion, characterization, or transplantation without requiring additional manipulation or selection.

### iDP Production

The production of induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) is a multi-step process that begins with the isolation of somatic fibroblasts and culminates in the generation of a homogeneous, expandable, and lineage-restricted progenitor population. The process is initiated by transducing the fibroblasts with a combination of retroviral vectors encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2, as described above. Following transduction, the cells are maintained in fibroblast medium for 48 hours to allow for viral integration and initial gene expression. The medium is then replaced with neural progenitor medium, which supports the survival and proliferation of neural progenitors while suppressing fibroblast growth. Within seven days, the first morphological changes become apparent, with cells adopting a rounded, refractile appearance and forming small clusters. By day 14, these clusters develop into discrete, well-defined colonies that are visually distinct from the surrounding fibroblasts. These colonies are manually isolated using a sterile pipette tip under a stereomicroscope and transferred to fresh neural progenitor medium in low-adhesion plates to promote clonal expansion. Each colony is expanded independently to ensure clonal purity and to prevent contamination from non-reprogrammed cells. The expanded colonies are then characterized by immunocytochemistry and quantitative real-time PCR to confirm the expression of neural progenitor markers such as Nestin, Sox1, PAX6, and CD133, as well as dopaminergic progenitor markers including Aldh1A1, Corin, Lmx1a, Otx2, Ngn2, Mash1, Pitx3, and Nkx6.1. The iDPs are confirmed to be negative for glial lineage markers such as GFAP, Olig2, and S100β, and for pluripotency markers such as Oct4, Nanog, and Sox2 in the absence of exogenous expression. The iDPs are maintained in neural progenitor medium supplemented with growth factors and can be passaged every 5 to 7 days using enzymatic dissociation with Accutase or TrypLE. The cells retain their progenitor phenotype for at least 15 passages without loss of dopaminergic identity or acquisition of senescence markers. The iDPs are cryopreserved in standard freezing medium containing 10% dimethyl sulfoxide and stored in liquid nitrogen for long-term storage. Upon thawing, the cells retain their ability to proliferate and differentiate into dopaminergic neurons with the same efficiency as freshly isolated cells. The entire production process, from fibroblast isolation to the generation of a stable, expandable iDP line, can be completed within 21 to 30 days, making it significantly faster than pluripotent stem cell-based approaches that require several months for differentiation.

### Human iDP Upon Ectopic Expression of Brn2, Sox2 and Foxa2

The method of the present invention is applicable to human somatic cells, including dermal fibroblasts isolated from human skin biopsies. Human fibroblasts are cultured under identical conditions as murine fibroblasts and are transduced with retroviral vectors encoding human orthologs of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. The human Brn2 gene is derived from the POU4F2 locus, the human Sox2 gene is derived from chromosome 3q26.33, and the human Foxa2 gene is derived from chromosome 20q13.12. The transduction protocol is optimized for human cells by adjusting the viral titer, polybrene concentration, and infection duration to account for differences in cell membrane composition and transduction efficiency. Following transduction, the cells are maintained in human neural progenitor medium, which is formulated to support the growth of human neural precursors and includes NeuroCult® NSC Basal Medium supplemented with human recombinant bFGF, EGF, and additional supplements such as N2 and B27. Within 14 to 21 days, human iDP colonies emerge that are morphologically indistinguishable from their murine counterparts, exhibiting compact, spherical morphology and high expression of Nestin and Sox2. These human iDPs express the full complement of midbrain dopaminergic progenitor markers, including Aldh1A1, Corin, Lmx1a, Otx2, Ngn2, Mash1, Pitx3, and Nkx6.1, and are negative for forebrain markers such as FoxG1 and GSX2. The human iDPs demonstrate lineage restriction by differentiating exclusively into tyrosine hydroxylase-positive neurons upon maturation, with no detectable generation of astrocytes or oligodendrocytes under standard differentiation conditions. The human iDP-derived neurons express the full complement of dopaminergic machinery, including aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, the dopamine transporter, and the vesicular monoamine transporter 2, and exhibit electrophysiological properties consistent with mature human midbrain dopaminergic neurons, including the ability to generate repetitive action potentials and voltage-gated sodium and potassium currents. The human iDPs can be expanded under conditional L-Myc expression, as described below, to generate clinically relevant quantities of cells for transplantation. The ability to generate human iDPs from patient-derived fibroblasts enables the creation of personalized cell therapies for Parkinson’s disease, allowing for autologous transplantation without the need for immunosuppression. The human iDPs retain their dopaminergic identity and functional properties following cryopreservation and thawing, and can be stored for extended periods without loss of viability or differentiation potential. The method described herein represents the first successful generation of human induced dopaminergic progenitors through direct reprogramming without the use of pluripotent intermediates or exogenous morphogens.

## DISCUSSION

The invention described herein represents a fundamental advancement in the field of cellular reprogramming and regenerative medicine for Parkinson’s disease. Previous attempts to generate dopaminergic progenitors from somatic cells have relied on either pluripotent intermediates or the use of multiple transcription factors in combination with exogenous morphogens to achieve regional specification. The present invention demonstrates that the triad of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 is sufficient to convert fibroblasts directly into a lineage-restricted dopaminergic progenitor population with intrinsic midbrain identity. This finding is unexpected, as prior studies have shown that Foxa2 alone is insufficient to drive reprogramming and that the combination of Brn2 and Sox2 typically yields neural progenitors with broad regional potential, not restricted to the dopaminergic lineage. The discovery that Foxa2 acts as a decisive factor in conferring midbrain identity and dopaminergic restriction within this context reveals a previously unrecognized functional synergy among these three transcription factors. Foxa2 does not merely enhance the efficiency of reprogramming; it fundamentally redirects the transcriptional trajectory of the reprogrammed cells toward a dopaminergic progenitor fate, suppressing alternative neural and glial fates while activating a suite of genes essential for midbrain development. The fact that the resulting iDPs do not require SHH or FGF8 for dopaminergic specification is particularly significant, as these morphogens are difficult to control in vivo, introduce batch-to-batch variability, and are associated with off-target effects on other neural lineages. The iDPs generated by this method are therefore more reliable, reproducible, and suitable for clinical translation.

The lineage restriction of the iDPs is another critical feature that distinguishes this invention from all prior approaches. Unlike neural progenitors generated by other direct reprogramming methods, which retain the capacity to differentiate into astrocytes and oligodendrocytes, the iDPs generated by Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 exhibit an absolute commitment to the dopaminergic neuronal lineage. This is demonstrated both in vitro and in vivo, where transplantation of iDPs into the striatum of Parkinson’s disease models results in the exclusive generation of tyrosine hydroxylase-positive neurons, with no detectable glial contamination. This purity is essential for therapeutic efficacy, as the presence of non-dopaminergic cells in grafts can lead to aberrant circuit integration, inflammation, or even tumor formation. The suppression of glial lineage genes such as GFAP, Olig2, and S100β indicates that the iDPs have undergone a complete epigenetic reprogramming that extinguishes alternative lineage potentials, a phenomenon rarely observed in direct reprogramming systems.

The conditional expression of L-Myc to enable controlled expansion of the iDPs represents a practical solution to a major translational bottleneck. Prior reprogrammed progenitor populations have been limited in their scalability due to their inability to proliferate beyond a few passages without losing identity or acquiring genomic instability. The use of a tetracycline-inducible system to transiently activate L-Myc allows for the generation of large numbers of iDPs without permanent genetic modification, and the withdrawal of doxycycline restores the cells’ differentiation potential. This system is safe, reversible, and compatible with clinical manufacturing standards. The absence of tumor formation in transplanted animals, even after six weeks of observation, confirms that the conditional expression of L-Myc does not lead to oncogenic transformation, a concern that has plagued many stem cell therapies.

The functional maturation of iDP-derived neurons is another hallmark of this invention. The neurons not only express the correct molecular markers but also exhibit the electrophysiological properties of mature midbrain dopaminergic neurons, including the ability to fire action potentials in response to physiological current injections and to generate voltage-gated sodium and potassium currents with kinetics indistinguishable from native neurons. The presence of synaptophysin-positive puncta further indicates that these neurons are capable of forming synaptic connections, a prerequisite for functional integration into host neural circuits. The partial but significant recovery of motor function in the MPTP mouse model following transplantation provides compelling evidence that these cells are not merely phenotypically dopaminergic but are functionally integrated and capable of restoring lost neural activity.

The applicability of this method to human cells opens the door to personalized medicine for Parkinson’s disease. Patient-derived fibroblasts can be reprogrammed into iDPs, expanded under controlled conditions, and transplanted back into the same patient, eliminating the need for immunosuppression and reducing the risk of immune rejection. The ability to generate iDPs from patients with genetic forms of Parkinson’s disease also provides a powerful platform for disease modeling, drug screening, and the study of disease mechanisms in a human-relevant context.

In summary, the invention described herein provides a complete, end-to-end solution for the generation of safe, functional, and scalable dopaminergic progenitors for the treatment of Parkinson’s disease. It eliminates the need for pluripotent intermediates, bypasses the requirement for exogenous morphogens, ensures lineage fidelity, enables scalable production, and demonstrates therapeutic efficacy in a preclinical model. This represents a transformative advance over all existing approaches and establishes a new standard for cell replacement therapy in neurodegenerative disease.

## Methods

### Cell Preparation, Retroviral Packaging, Infection and Direct Reprogramming

Somatic cells, specifically dermal fibroblasts, are isolated from adult mice or humans using standard enzymatic digestion protocols. In the case of murine fibroblasts, skin biopsies are obtained from Nestin-EGFP transgenic mice to enable visual tracking of reprogrammed cells. The tissue is minced and digested with collagenase type I and dispase for one hour at 37°C, followed by filtration through a 70-micron cell strainer. Cells are plated in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% non-essential amino acids, 100 units per milliliter penicillin, and 100 micrograms per milliliter streptomycin. Cultures are maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% carbon dioxide. Fibroblasts are used between passages two and five to ensure optimal reprogramming efficiency and to avoid senescence-associated changes. For human fibroblasts, skin punch biopsies are obtained under informed consent, processed similarly, and cultured under identical conditions. Retroviral vectors encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 are constructed using the pMXs retroviral backbone. The open reading frames of each gene are amplified by PCR using gene-specific primers and cloned into the vector using restriction enzyme digestion and ligation. The Brn2 ORF is cloned between EcoRI and NotI sites, the Sox2 ORF is obtained from a commercially available plasmid (Addgene #13367), and the Foxa2 ORF is cloned between BamHI and XhoI sites. The vectors are verified by DNA sequencing. Retroviral particles are produced by transfecting Plat-E packaging cells with the respective plasmids using Lipofectamine LTX transfection reagent. Transfection is performed in 100-mm dishes with 15 micrograms of total DNA per dish. Forty-eight hours post-transfection, viral supernatants are collected, filtered through a 0.45-micron cellulose acetate filter, and concentrated by ultracentrifugation if necessary. Fibroblasts are seeded at a density of 2 × 10⁵ per 35-mm dish and infected with a 1:1:1 mixture of the three retroviral supernatants in the presence of 10 micrograms per milliliter polybrene. Infection is performed overnight, after which the medium is replaced with fresh fibroblast medium. Forty-eight hours post-infection, the medium is switched to neural progenitor medium consisting of NeuroCult® NSC Basal Medium supplemented with NeuroCult® NSC Proliferation Supplements, 20 nanograms per milliliter basic fibroblast growth factor, and 20 nanograms per milliliter epidermal growth factor. The cells are monitored daily for morphological changes, and colonies are manually picked between days 14 and 21 using a sterile pipette tip under a stereomicroscope. Picked colonies are dissociated with Accutase and replated in neural progenitor medium for expansion.

### Quantitative Real-Time RT-PCR

Total RNA is extracted from cultured cells using TRIzol Reagent followed by purification with the RNeasy Mini Kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration and purity are assessed by spectrophotometry using a NanoDrop instrument. One microgram of total RNA is reverse transcribed into cDNA using the Transcriptor First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit. Quantitative real-time PCR is performed using SYBR Green Master Mix on a real-time PCR system. Each reaction contains 0.5 microliters of cDNA, 7.5 microliters of SYBR Green Master Mix, 5.5 microliters of primer mix (10 micromolar each forward and reverse primer), and 1.5 microliters of nuclease-free water, for a final volume of 15 microliters. The thermal cycling protocol consists of an initial denaturation at 95°C for two minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds, a gene-specific annealing temperature for 15 seconds, and 72°C for one minute. All reactions are performed in triplicate. Gene expression levels are normalized to the housekeeping gene Gapdh, and relative quantification is calculated using the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method. Primer sequences for all target genes are listed in Table S1 and are designed to span intron-exon boundaries to prevent amplification of genomic DNA.

### Immunocytochemistry

Cells are fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde in phosphate-buffered saline for 20 minutes at room temperature, washed three times with PBS, and permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 in PBS for 10 minutes. Cells are blocked with 2% bovine serum albumin in PBS for one hour at room temperature. Primary antibodies are diluted in blocking solution and applied overnight at 4°C. Antibodies used include anti-Nestin (1:500), anti-Sox2 (1:200), anti-Foxa2 (1:200), anti-Brn2 (1:200), anti-Tyrosine Hydroxylase (1:500), anti-βIII-tubulin (1:1000), anti-GFAP (1:500), anti-Olig2 (1:200), anti-Aldh1A1 (1:200), anti-Corin (1:200), anti-Lmx1a (1:200), anti-Otx2 (1:200), anti-Pitx3 (1:200), anti-Nkx6.1 (1:200), anti-synaptophysin (1:200), anti-dopamine transporter (1:200), anti-aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (1:200), and anti-vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (1:200). After washing three times with PBS, cells are incubated with fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies (e.g., Alexa Fluor 488, 594, or 647) at a dilution of 1:1000 for two hours at room temperature in the dark. Nuclei are counterstained with Hoechst 33342. Images are acquired using a Zeiss 710 Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope with a 40× or 63× oil immersion objective. Z-stack images are captured at 0.5-micron intervals and projected using maximum intensity projection software. Image analysis is performed using ImageJ or Zen software to quantify the percentage of positive cells.

### Differentiation

For neuronal differentiation, iDPs are plated on poly-L-ornithine/laminin-coated coverslips in 24-well plates at a density of 5 × 10⁴ cells per well. The medium is replaced with neuronal maturation medium consisting of DMEM/F12 supplemented with 1× N2, 1× B27, 1.0 mM GlutaMAX, 0.11 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 1.0 mM dibutyryl cyclic AMP, 0.2 mM ascorbic acid, 10 ng/mL brain-derived neurotrophic factor, and 10 ng/mL glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor. The medium is changed every three to four days for a period of four weeks. For astrocyte differentiation, iDPs are plated on uncoated coverslips and cultured in DMEM/F12 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum for seven days, with medium changes every other day. For oligodendrocyte differentiation, iDPs are cultured in DMEM/F12 supplemented with 1× N2, 10 ng/mL platelet-derived growth factor, 10 ng/mL fibroblast growth factor 2, and 10 μM forskolin for four days, followed by replacement of PDGF and forskolin with 30 ng/mL triiodothyronine and 200 μM ascorbic acid for an additional seven days. All differentiation conditions are performed in triplicate, and cells are fixed and immunostained for lineage-specific markers as described above.

### Recording of Action Potentials and Total Currents

Whole-cell patch-clamp recordings are performed using an Axonpatch 200B amplifier and pClamp 10.2 software. Patch pipettes are pulled from borosilicate glass capillaries and have a resistance of 3–5 MΩ when filled with intracellular solution containing 105 mM potassium aspartate, 20 mM KCl, 1 mM CaCl₂, 5 mM MgATP, 10 mM HEPES, 10 mM EGTA, and 25 mM glucose, adjusted to pH 7.2 and osmolarity of 320 mOsm/L. The extracellular bath solution contains 140 mM NaCl, 5.4 mM KCl, 0.5 mM MgCl₂, 2.5 mM CaCl₂, 5.5 mM HEPES, 11 mM glucose, and 10 mM sucrose, adjusted to pH 7.4 and osmolarity of 330 mOsm/L. Cells are held at −80 mV, and voltage-gated currents are evoked by 500-millisecond voltage steps from −70 mV to +70 mV in 10-mV increments. Action potentials are elicited by injecting depolarizing current pulses of 20 to 100 pA in 20-pA increments for one second. Series resistance is compensated by 80–90%, and only cells with series resistance below 13 MΩ and stable access for more than 10 minutes are included in the analysis. Data are sampled at 10 kHz and filtered at 5 kHz. All experiments are performed at room temperature.

### Motor Function and Behavior Tests

Motor function is assessed using the accelerating rotarod test. Mice are trained for three consecutive days prior to MPTP intoxication, with two trials per day at an accelerating speed from 2 to 12 revolutions per minute over five minutes. Baseline performance is recorded as the average latency to fall from the rod over two trials. After MPTP intoxication and cell transplantation, mice are tested twice weekly for three weeks. Each trial lasts a maximum of 90 seconds, and the latency to fall is recorded automatically. Performance is expressed as a percentage of baseline performance for each animal, and group means are compared using statistical analysis. All testing is performed in a blinded manner, with experimenters unaware of the treatment group assignment.

### Statistical Analysis

All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance is determined using one-way or two-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. A p-value of less than 0.05 is considered statistically significant. All experiments are performed in triplicate, and at least three independent biological replicates are used for each condition. Graphical representations and statistical analyses are performed using GraphPad Prism software.

### Cell Labeling, Transplantation and Histology

For transplantation studies, iDPs are labeled with a lentiviral vector expressing green fluorescent protein under the control of a constitutive promoter and selected for 72 hours in puromycin-containing medium to enrich for transduced cells. Cells are resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline at a concentration of 0.25 million cells per microliter. Adult male C57BL/6J mice are anesthetized with ketamine and xylazine and placed in a stereotaxic frame. Two burr holes are drilled at coordinates 0.22 mm posterior and 3.25 mm lateral to bregma, and 2.9 mm ventral from the dura mater. A total of 2 microliters of cell suspension is injected into each hemisphere using a Hamilton syringe at a rate of 0.2 microliters per minute. The needle is left in place for five minutes post-injection to prevent backflow. Control animals receive an equal volume of phosphate-buffered saline. Mice are sacrificed at 21 days post-transplantation and transcardially perfused with 4% paraformaldehyde. Brains are post-fixed overnight, cryoprotected in 30% sucrose, sectioned coronally at 30 micrometers thickness, and stored in cryoprotectant. Sections are immunostained for tyrosine hydroxylase, GFP, and glial fibrillary acidic protein. Images are acquired using a confocal microscope, and the density of tyrosine hydroxylase-positive terminals in the striatum is quantified using ImageJ software. Tumor formation is assessed by histological examination of the injection site for abnormal cell proliferation, mitotic figures, or ectopic tissue structures. All analyses are performed in a blinded manner.
