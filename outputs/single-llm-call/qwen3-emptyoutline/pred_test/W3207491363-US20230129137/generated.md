# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The mammalian heart possesses an extremely limited capacity for endogenous regeneration following injury, particularly after myocardial infarction, which results in the irreversible loss of cardiomyocytes and their replacement with non-contractile fibrotic scar tissue. This inability to regenerate functional myocardium is a primary reason for the progression of heart failure and the high morbidity and mortality associated with ischemic heart disease in adult humans. While neonatal mammals exhibit a transient but robust capacity for cardiac regeneration through the proliferation of existing cardiomyocytes, this regenerative potential is rapidly lost within the first week of life, coinciding with the terminal differentiation and cell cycle arrest of cardiomyocytes. The molecular mechanisms underlying this developmental switch have been extensively studied, with the Hippo signaling pathway emerging as a central regulator of cardiac growth and regeneration. The Hippo pathway suppresses cardiomyocyte proliferation by phosphorylating and sequestering the transcriptional coactivators YAP and TAZ in the cytoplasm, thereby preventing their nuclear translocation and subsequent activation of pro-proliferative and anti-apoptotic gene programs. In contrast, inhibition of Hippo signaling or direct activation of YAP and TAZ has been shown to promote cardiomyocyte re-entry into the cell cycle, enhance survival, and improve functional recovery in experimental models of myocardial infarction. 

Several small-molecule compounds have been identified that modulate the Hippo pathway and enhance cardiac regeneration, including TT-10, a fluorinated derivative of TAZ-12, which has been demonstrated to reduce infarct size and improve short-term cardiac function when administered systemically in murine models of myocardial infarction. However, the therapeutic utility of TT-10 is severely constrained by its pharmacokinetic profile: rapid systemic clearance, poor tissue retention, and suboptimal bioavailability at the site of injury necessitate high dosing regimens that increase the risk of off-target effects and limit clinical translatability. Conventional delivery methods such as intraperitoneal or intravenous injection fail to sustain therapeutic concentrations of TT-10 within the myocardium over the critical window following infarction, during which cardiomyocyte proliferation and survival signals are most effective. Furthermore, the transient nature of TT-10 exposure may lead to incomplete or unstable regenerative responses, as sustained activation of YAP signaling appears necessary to drive meaningful cardiomyocyte renewal and tissue remodeling. 

To address these limitations, advanced drug delivery systems have been explored in cardiovascular regenerative medicine, particularly biodegradable polymeric nanoparticles that enable controlled, localized, and prolonged release of therapeutic agents. Poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticles have emerged as a clinically viable platform due to their biocompatibility, tunable degradation kinetics, and ability to encapsulate both hydrophobic and hydrophilic molecules. When administered directly into the myocardium, PLGA nanoparticles can remain localized at the site of injury for extended periods, releasing their cargo in a sustained manner over days to weeks, thereby mimicking the natural temporal dynamics of endogenous regenerative signaling. Previous studies have demonstrated that PLGA nanoparticles loaded with growth factors, microRNAs, or small-molecule inhibitors can significantly enhance cardiac repair by prolonging the exposure of cardiomyocytes to regenerative stimuli, reducing fibrosis, and improving vascularization. However, no prior work has successfully combined TT-10 with PLGA nanoparticle delivery to exploit its mechanistic potential while overcoming its pharmacokinetic shortcomings. 

The present invention addresses these critical gaps by introducing a novel therapeutic formulation comprising TT-10 encapsulated within PLGA nanoparticles, designed for direct intramyocardial administration following myocardial infarction. This formulation achieves a sustained release profile that maintains therapeutic concentrations of TT-10 within the peri-infarct region for up to nine days, thereby enabling prolonged activation of YAP signaling, enhanced cardiomyocyte proliferation, reduced apoptosis, and improved angiogenesis. Unlike prior approaches that rely on bolus administration of free TT-10, which results in transient and insufficient target engagement, the nanoparticle-based delivery system described herein ensures continuous exposure of cardiomyocytes to TT-10 during the critical early phase of post-infarction remodeling, when endogenous repair mechanisms are most responsive. The resulting therapeutic effect is not only more potent but also more durable, as evidenced by sustained improvements in cardiac function, reduced scar formation, and enhanced tissue perfusion that persist for at least four weeks post-treatment. Importantly, this approach achieves these outcomes with a dose of TT-10 that is more than three orders of magnitude lower than previously reported effective doses, thereby minimizing the potential for systemic toxicity and reducing the manufacturing burden associated with large-scale drug production. The invention further demonstrates that the therapeutic benefits are not merely additive but synergistic, arising from the precise spatiotemporal coordination of drug release with the biological windows of cardiomyocyte plasticity and tissue repair. This formulation represents a paradigm shift in the delivery of regenerative therapeutics for heart disease, transforming a promising but pharmacokinetically flawed molecule into a clinically viable, high-efficiency therapeutic agent with the potential to fundamentally alter the trajectory of post-infarction recovery.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising TT-10 encapsulated within poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) nanoparticles, wherein the nanoparticles are configured to release TT-10 in a sustained manner over a period of at least seven days following intramyocardial administration, thereby promoting cardiomyocyte proliferation, reducing cardiomyocyte apoptosis, enhancing vascularization of the peri-infarct region, and improving cardiac function in a subject suffering from myocardial infarction. The composition is characterized by a particle size ranging from approximately 120 to 180 nanometers, a TT-10 loading efficiency of at least 2.0 micrograms per milligram of PLGA, and a release profile wherein at least 45% of the encapsulated TT-10 is released within the first 24 hours and at least 85% is released within nine days under physiological conditions. The invention further provides a method of treating myocardial infarction in a mammalian subject by administering a therapeutically effective amount of the pharmaceutical composition directly into the myocardium surrounding the infarct zone, wherein the administration results in a significant reduction in infarct size, an increase in left ventricular ejection fraction, a decrease in left ventricular end-systolic and end-diastolic diameters, and a marked increase in the number of proliferating cardiomyocytes expressing Ki67, phospho-histone H3, or nuclear YAP within the border zone of the infarct, as compared to untreated controls or subjects treated with free TT-10 or empty nanoparticles. 

The invention further encompasses the use of the pharmaceutical composition for the manufacture of a medicament for the treatment of myocardial infarction, wherein the medicament is administered via intramyocardial injection, catheter-based delivery, or echo-guided transthoracic injection, and wherein the therapeutic effect is mediated through the sustained activation of the Hippo/YAP signaling pathway, leading to the downregulation of phosphorylated YAP, the nuclear translocation of unphosphorylated YAP, and the subsequent transcriptional upregulation of genes involved in cell cycle progression, survival, and angiogenesis. The invention further provides a kit comprising the pharmaceutical composition in a sterile, lyophilized form, a diluent for reconstitution, and a syringe or catheter system configured for direct myocardial delivery, wherein the kit is labeled with instructions for administration within 72 hours following the onset of myocardial infarction. The invention further provides a method of enhancing cardiac regeneration in a subject with myocardial infarction, wherein the method comprises administering the pharmaceutical composition to the subject, and wherein the administration results in a measurable increase in the density of capillaries and arterioles in the peri-infarct region, as evidenced by immunohistochemical staining for endothelial markers such as isolectin B4 or α-smooth muscle actin, without inducing arrhythmias, cardiomegaly, or systemic toxicity. The invention further provides a method of reducing fibrosis in the myocardium following infarction, wherein the reduction in fibrotic tissue is quantified as a decrease in collagen deposition as measured by picrosirius red staining, and wherein the reduction correlates with the sustained presence of TT-10 in the myocardial tissue for a period exceeding five days following administration. The invention further provides a method of improving survival and functional recovery in a subject with myocardial infarction, wherein the improvement is demonstrated by a sustained elevation in left ventricular fractional shortening and ejection fraction over a period of at least four weeks following treatment, and wherein the subject does not require additional pharmacological intervention to maintain cardiac function beyond the initial administration of the pharmaceutical composition. The invention further provides a method of minimizing the required dose of TT-10 for therapeutic efficacy, wherein the dose is less than 100 nanograms per kilogram of body weight, and wherein the therapeutic effect is achieved without systemic exposure exceeding a plasma concentration of 5 nanomolar at any time point following administration. The invention further provides a method of promoting the maturation of newly regenerated cardiomyocytes, wherein the proliferative response is transient, peaking within one week of administration and declining thereafter, such that the regenerated cardiomyocytes exit the cell cycle and acquire mature contractile properties without persistent mitotic activity that could predispose to arrhythmogenesis. The invention further provides a method of enhancing the bioavailability of TT-10 in the myocardium, wherein the local concentration of TT-10 in the peri-infarct region is maintained above 100 nanomolar for a period of at least five days, thereby enabling continuous modulation of YAP phosphorylation status and sustained transcriptional activation of regenerative gene networks. The invention further provides a method of reducing oxidative stress and DNA damage in cardiomyocytes following myocardial infarction, wherein the reduction is mediated by the sustained inhibition of reactive oxygen species accumulation and the downregulation of markers of DNA damage such as γH2AX, thereby enhancing cardiomyocyte survival and reducing the extent of secondary injury. The invention further provides a method of promoting the integration of regenerated cardiomyocytes into the native myocardial syncytium, wherein the regenerated cells exhibit coordinated calcium transients, connexin 43 expression at intercalated discs, and alignment with adjacent cardiomyocytes, thereby restoring synchronous contractile function without the formation of ectopic pacemaker activity or conduction abnormalities. The invention further provides a method of achieving these therapeutic outcomes without the need for genetic manipulation, cell transplantation, or exogenous stem cell delivery, thereby circumventing the immunogenic, tumorigenic, and logistical challenges associated with alternative regenerative strategies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “TT-10” refers to the chemical compound 2-(4-fluorophenyl)-N-(2-(methylsulfonyl)ethyl)-1H-benzo[d]imidazole-5-sulfonamide, with the molecular formula C11H10FN3OS2, which is a fluorinated derivative of TAZ-12 and functions as a modulator of the Hippo signaling pathway by promoting the nuclear translocation of YAP and TAZ through inhibition of their phosphorylation. The term “poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid)” or “PLGA” refers to a biodegradable copolymer composed of lactic acid and glycolic acid monomers, wherein the ratio of lactic acid to glycolic acid ranges from 50:50 to 85:15 by weight, and wherein the molecular weight of the polymer ranges from 10,000 to 30,000 Daltons, such that the resulting nanoparticles degrade in vivo over a period of seven to fourteen days, releasing encapsulated cargo in a controlled and sustained manner. The term “nanoparticle” refers to a spherical or near-spherical particulate structure with a mean diameter ranging from 100 to 200 nanometers, as determined by nanoparticle tracking analysis or dynamic light scattering, and wherein the particle size distribution is narrow, with a polydispersity index less than 0.2, such that the particles exhibit uniform pharmacokinetic behavior and consistent tissue penetration. The term “encapsulated” refers to the physical entrapment of TT-10 within the polymeric matrix of the PLGA nanoparticle, such that the compound is not surface-bound or adsorbed, but rather distributed homogeneously throughout the polymer core, thereby protecting it from premature degradation and enabling controlled release kinetics. The term “sustained release” refers to the gradual and prolonged release of TT-10 from the nanoparticles over a period of at least seven days, wherein at least 45% of the total encapsulated TT-10 is released within the first 24 hours and at least 85% is released by day nine under physiological conditions of 37°C and pH 7.4, as measured in phosphate-buffered saline containing 0.1% bovine serum albumin. The term “intramyocardial administration” refers to the direct delivery of the pharmaceutical composition into the myocardial tissue surrounding the area of infarction, via injection using a fine-gauge needle, catheter, or ultrasound-guided device, such that the composition remains localized within the peri-infarct border zone and does not significantly disseminate into the systemic circulation. The term “therapeutically effective amount” refers to the quantity of TT-10 encapsulated in nanoparticles that, when administered to a subject suffering from myocardial infarction, produces a statistically significant improvement in cardiac function, reduction in infarct size, increase in cardiomyocyte proliferation, or decrease in cardiomyocyte apoptosis, as compared to a control group receiving vehicle or free TT-10, without inducing significant systemic toxicity or adverse effects. The term “cardiomyocyte proliferation” refers to the re-entry of terminally differentiated cardiomyocytes into the cell cycle, as evidenced by the expression of proliferation markers such as Ki67, bromodeoxyuridine incorporation, phosphorylation of histone H3 at serine 10, or Aurora B kinase activity, and wherein the proliferating cells retain their cardiomyocyte identity as confirmed by co-expression of cardiac troponin T or Nkx2.5. The term “nuclear YAP” refers to the unphosphorylated form of Yes-associated protein that has translocated from the cytoplasm to the nucleus, where it binds to TEAD transcription factors and activates the expression of genes involved in cell proliferation, survival, and tissue regeneration, as detected by immunofluorescence or Western blot analysis using antibodies specific for total YAP and phospho-YAP. The term “peri-infarct region” refers to the myocardial tissue immediately adjacent to the necrotic core of the infarct, extending approximately 1 to 2 millimeters into viable myocardium, wherein the tissue is characterized by hypoxia, inflammation, and the presence of reparative cell populations including fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and surviving cardiomyocytes capable of regeneration. The term “fibrosis” refers to the excessive deposition of extracellular matrix components, primarily collagen types I and III, within the myocardium following injury, as quantified by picrosirius red or Masson’s trichrome staining, and wherein the reduction of fibrosis is indicative of improved tissue remodeling and decreased stiffness. The term “angiogenesis” refers to the formation of new microvessels and arterioles from pre-existing vasculature, as evidenced by increased density of endothelial structures positive for isolectin B4 or α-smooth muscle actin, and wherein the enhancement of vascularization supports oxygen and nutrient delivery to regenerating tissue. The term “left ventricular ejection fraction” refers to the percentage of blood ejected from the left ventricle during each cardiac cycle, as measured by echocardiography, and wherein an increase in ejection fraction indicates improved systolic function. The term “left ventricular fractional shortening” refers to the percentage change in left ventricular diameter between diastole and systole, as measured by M-mode echocardiography, and wherein an increase in fractional shortening reflects enhanced contractility. The term “left ventricular end-systolic diameter” refers to the minimum diameter of the left ventricle at the end of systole, and wherein a reduction in this parameter indicates improved ventricular remodeling and reduced dilation. The term “left ventricular end-diastolic diameter” refers to the maximum diameter of the left ventricle at the end of diastole, and wherein a reduction in this parameter indicates decreased ventricular remodeling and improved compliance. The term “myocardial infarction” refers to the ischemic necrosis of myocardial tissue resulting from the occlusion of a coronary artery, leading to cardiomyocyte death, inflammation, and subsequent fibrotic scarring, and wherein the infarction is induced experimentally or occurs spontaneously in a mammalian subject. The term “subject” refers to a mammal, including but not limited to a human, mouse, rat, rabbit, pig, or non-human primate, suffering from or at risk of myocardial infarction. The term “biocompatible” refers to the property of the PLGA nanoparticles and their degradation products to elicit no significant immune response, cytotoxicity, or inflammatory reaction when introduced into the myocardial tissue. The term “lyophilized” refers to the process of freeze-drying the pharmaceutical composition to remove water, thereby enhancing stability and shelf life, and wherein the lyophilized form is reconstituted immediately prior to administration with a sterile aqueous solution. The term “controlled release” refers to the predictable and time-dependent release of TT-10 from the nanoparticles, wherein the release rate is governed by the degradation kinetics of the PLGA polymer and the diffusion of TT-10 through the polymer matrix, rather than by passive surface erosion or burst release. The term “regenerative therapy” refers to any therapeutic intervention that promotes the restoration of lost or damaged myocardial tissue through the activation of endogenous repair mechanisms, including cardiomyocyte proliferation, vascularization, and reduction of fibrosis, without the need for exogenous cell transplantation or gene therapy. The term “YAP signaling pathway” refers to the intracellular signaling cascade initiated by the nuclear translocation of YAP, leading to the transcriptional activation of downstream target genes such as CTGF, CYR61, BIRC5, AXL, and EGFR, which collectively promote cell proliferation, survival, and tissue regeneration. The term “transient proliferative response” refers to a temporary increase in cardiomyocyte cell cycle activity that peaks within one week of administration and declines thereafter, such that the regenerated cardiomyocytes exit the cell cycle and mature into functional, contractile cells without persistent mitotic activity that could lead to genomic instability or arrhythmias. The term “minimal systemic exposure” refers to the absence of detectable levels of TT-10 in the plasma or non-cardiac tissues at concentrations exceeding 5 nanomolar at any time point following administration, thereby minimizing the risk of off-target effects in other organs. The term “local concentration” refers to the concentration of TT-10 within the myocardial tissue surrounding the injection site, as opposed to the concentration in the systemic circulation, and wherein the local concentration is maintained above 100 nanomolar for at least five days following administration. The term “infarct size” refers to the proportion of the left ventricular wall that is replaced by fibrotic scar tissue following myocardial infarction, as quantified by planimetry of picrosirius red-stained histological sections, and wherein a reduction in infarct size indicates successful myocardial repair. The term “heart weight-to-body weight ratio” refers to the ratio of the mass of the heart to the mass of the entire body, and wherein a reduction in this ratio indicates attenuation of pathological hypertrophy and improved cardiac remodeling. The term “apoptosis” refers to programmed cell death of cardiomyocytes, as detected by terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) staining, and wherein a reduction in apoptotic cardiomyocytes indicates enhanced cell survival. The term “immunofluorescence” refers to the technique of visualizing specific proteins or antigens within tissue sections or cultured cells using fluorescently labeled antibodies, wherein the intensity and distribution of fluorescence are quantified to determine the abundance and localization of the target protein. The term “Western blot” refers to the technique of separating proteins by electrophoresis, transferring them to a membrane, and detecting specific proteins using antibody-based methods, wherein the relative abundance of phosphorylated and total YAP is used to assess Hippo pathway activity. The term “echocardiography” refers to the non-invasive imaging modality that uses ultrasound to assess cardiac structure and function, wherein parameters such as ejection fraction, fractional shortening, and ventricular dimensions are measured to evaluate therapeutic efficacy. The term “PLGA nanoparticle” refers to a particulate formulation composed of poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) encapsulating TT-10, wherein the particle is spherical, monodisperse, and stable in aqueous suspension, and wherein the encapsulation efficiency is at least 90% as determined by high-performance liquid chromatography. The term “border zone” refers to the transitional region between the infarcted myocardium and the healthy myocardium, wherein cardiomyocytes are viable but stressed, and wherein the regenerative response is most active following therapeutic intervention. The term “myocardial regeneration” refers to the restoration of contractile myocardial tissue following injury through the proliferation and maturation of endogenous cardiomyocytes, as opposed to fibrotic replacement, and wherein the regenerated tissue exhibits structural and functional integration with the native myocardium. The term “pharmaceutical composition” refers to the formulation comprising TT-10 encapsulated in PLGA nanoparticles, optionally with additional excipients such as stabilizers, cryoprotectants, or surfactants, and wherein the composition is sterile, pyrogen-free, and suitable for direct injection into the myocardium. The term “controlled degradation” refers to the predictable and time-dependent breakdown of the PLGA polymer into lactic acid and glycolic acid, which are metabolized via the citric acid cycle and excreted as carbon dioxide and water, thereby ensuring complete biocompatibility and absence of residual polymer accumulation. The term “regenerative window” refers to the period of time following myocardial infarction, typically within the first 72 hours, during which the myocardial tissue is most responsive to regenerative stimuli, and wherein administration of the pharmaceutical composition during this window yields the greatest therapeutic benefit. The term “clinical translation” refers to the adaptation of the pharmaceutical composition for use in human patients, including formulation for intramyocardial delivery via catheter-based or transthoracic techniques, and wherein the composition is manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practices and meets regulatory standards for safety, purity, and potency.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The pharmaceutical composition of the present invention comprises TT-10 encapsulated within poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) nanoparticles, wherein the nanoparticles are formulated to achieve a sustained release profile that maintains therapeutic concentrations of TT-10 within the myocardial tissue for a duration sufficient to induce cardiomyocyte proliferation, reduce apoptosis, and enhance vascularization following myocardial infarction. The composition is prepared using a single-emulsion solvent evaporation technique, wherein TT-10 is dissolved in a solution of PLGA in dichloromethane, and the organic phase is emulsified into an aqueous phase containing polyvinyl alcohol as a stabilizer. The resulting emulsion is subjected to ultrasonication to ensure homogeneity, followed by solvent evaporation under controlled conditions to allow for the solidification of the nanoparticles. The nanoparticles are then collected by centrifugation, washed to remove unencapsulated drug and residual surfactant, and lyophilized for long-term storage. The resulting lyophilized powder is reconstituted immediately prior to administration with a sterile, isotonic, and pyrogen-free buffer such as Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline, optionally containing 0.1% bovine serum albumin to prevent aggregation. The final formulation is sterile-filtered through a 0.22-micron membrane and stored at 4°C until use. The nanoparticles exhibit a mean diameter of approximately 155 nanometers, with a polydispersity index of less than 0.15, as determined by nanoparticle tracking analysis, and a zeta potential of approximately -25 millivolts, indicating sufficient surface charge to prevent particle aggregation in physiological fluids. The encapsulation efficiency of TT-10 is consistently greater than 90%, with a loading capacity of 2.5 micrograms of TT-10 per milligram of PLGA, as determined by high-performance liquid chromatography following complete dissolution of the nanoparticles in dimethyl sulfoxide. The release profile of TT-10 from the nanoparticles is characterized by an initial burst release of approximately 47% within the first 24 hours, followed by a sustained release phase wherein an additional 43% is released over the next seven days, reaching a cumulative release of 90% by day nine. This release kinetics is governed by the degradation of the PLGA polymer, which undergoes hydrolytic cleavage of ester bonds in a time-dependent manner, allowing for the gradual diffusion of TT-10 from the polymer matrix. The degradation rate of the PLGA is modulated by the lactic acid to glycolic acid ratio, which in this formulation is maintained at 75:25 to balance degradation speed with drug release duration. The nanoparticles are stable in aqueous suspension for at least 48 hours at 37°C, with no significant change in size, morphology, or drug content, as confirmed by scanning electron microscopy and dynamic light scattering. The composition is free of endotoxins, as determined by the Limulus amebocyte lysate assay, and exhibits no cytotoxicity toward cardiomyocytes or endothelial cells in vitro at concentrations up to 100 micrograms per milliliter. The composition is suitable for direct intramyocardial injection, as demonstrated by its ability to remain localized at the injection site for at least 72 hours without significant dispersion into the systemic circulation, as confirmed by fluorescence imaging of coumarin-6-labeled nanoparticles. The lyophilized form of the composition is stable for at least six months when stored at -80°C, with no loss of encapsulation efficiency or particle integrity upon reconstitution. The final formulation contains no preservatives, adjuvants, or immunostimulatory agents, ensuring compatibility with the immunologically sensitive environment of the post-infarct myocardium. The composition may be administered in volumes ranging from 5 to 50 microliters per injection site, with a total dose of TT-10 ranging from 10 to 200 nanograms per kilogram of body weight, depending on the size of the subject and the extent of myocardial injury. The composition may be administered in multiple injections, typically three, spaced evenly around the perimeter of the infarct border zone, to ensure uniform distribution of the therapeutic agent. The composition may be delivered using a modified Hamilton syringe with a 33-gauge needle, or via catheter-based systems such as the NOGA system, or under echocardiographic guidance using a 14-MHz transducer, thereby enabling minimally invasive delivery in both preclinical and clinical settings. The composition may be co-formulated with additional therapeutic agents, such as anti-inflammatory compounds, microRNAs, or growth factors, to further enhance regenerative outcomes, provided that such agents are compatible with the PLGA matrix and do not interfere with the sustained release kinetics of TT-10. The composition may be packaged in single-use, sterile vials with a rubber stopper and aluminum crimp seal, and may be accompanied by a reconstitution diluent and a delivery device, forming a complete kit for clinical use. The composition is manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practices, with rigorous quality control measures including particle size analysis, drug content determination, sterility testing, and endotoxin assays, ensuring batch-to-batch consistency and regulatory compliance. The composition is designed for single-dose administration, with the sustained release profile eliminating the need for repeated injections, thereby improving patient compliance and reducing procedural risks. The composition is stable under normal ambient conditions for at least 24 hours following reconstitution, allowing for practical use in clinical environments without the need for continuous refrigeration. The composition does not induce significant immune cell infiltration, cytokine release, or complement activation when administered to the myocardium, as confirmed by histological analysis and cytokine profiling, thereby ensuring a favorable safety profile. The composition is capable of penetrating the interstitial space of the myocardium, as demonstrated by confocal imaging of fluorescently labeled nanoparticles, and is internalized by cardiomyocytes, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells within the peri-infarct region, as confirmed by co-localization with cell-specific markers. The composition does not alter the mechanical properties of the myocardial tissue, as confirmed by elastography and pressure-volume loop analysis, and does not induce arrhythmias, as confirmed by continuous electrocardiographic monitoring. The composition is scalable for industrial production, with batch sizes ranging from 10 milligrams to 10 grams of PLGA, and the manufacturing process is amenable to automation and quality-by-design principles. The composition is suitable for use in both acute and subacute myocardial infarction, with optimal efficacy demonstrated when administered within 72 hours of the ischemic event. The composition is compatible with standard clinical imaging modalities, including echocardiography, magnetic resonance imaging, and positron emission tomography, allowing for non-invasive monitoring of therapeutic response. The composition may be used in conjunction with standard-of-care therapies such as reperfusion, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, or statins, without evidence of pharmacological interaction or reduced efficacy. The composition is not intended for systemic administration, as intravenous or intraperitoneal delivery results in rapid clearance and negligible myocardial uptake, thereby underscoring the necessity of direct intramyocardial delivery for therapeutic efficacy. The composition is specifically designed to exploit the temporal and spatial dynamics of myocardial regeneration, providing a sustained, localized, and physiologically appropriate stimulus for endogenous repair that cannot be achieved with conventional drug delivery systems.
