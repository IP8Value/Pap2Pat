# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The development of an effective vaccine against HIV-1 infection remains one of the most formidable challenges in modern medicine. Despite decades of intensive research and substantial global investment, no licensed vaccine has yet been developed to prevent HIV-1 transmission or to control established infection. The primary obstacle lies in the extraordinary genetic diversity of the virus, its rapid mutation rate, and its ability to evade the host immune system through structural masking, glycan shielding, and conformational dynamics of its envelope glycoprotein complex. The envelope (Env) spike, composed of a trimeric assembly of gp120 and gp41 subunits, is the sole target for neutralizing antibodies capable of blocking viral entry into host cells. Consequently, the induction of broadly neutralizing antibodies (bNAbs) that recognize conserved, functionally critical regions of the Env trimer has become the central objective of contemporary HIV-1 vaccine design. However, the native Env trimer is inherently unstable when isolated from the viral membrane, tending to dissociate into non-native monomers or adopt aberrant conformations that expose immunodominant but non-neutralizing epitopes. These non-neutralizing epitopes elicit antibody responses that are ineffective at blocking viral entry and may even interfere with the development of protective immunity by diverting the immune response away from conserved, vulnerable sites. Thus, the generation of immunogens that faithfully mimic the structure and antigenic profile of the native, pre-fusion Env trimer has been a critical bottleneck in vaccine development.

Previous efforts to stabilize the Env trimer have relied on transient transfection of mammalian cells with plasmids encoding modified gp140 constructs. These constructs typically incorporate two key stabilizing modifications: a disulfide bond (SOS) engineered between gp120 and gp41 ectodomain to covalently link the subunits, and a proline substitution at position 559 (I559P) in gp41 to enhance trimer stability and prevent premature conformational changes. Additionally, proteolytic cleavage between gp120 and gp41 is essential for achieving a native-like conformation, and this has been facilitated by optimizing the cleavage site and co-expressing the cellular protease Furin. The most advanced of these constructs, known as BG505 SOSIP.664, has demonstrated remarkable success in recapitulating the structural and antigenic features of the native viral spike. When purified and analyzed, these trimers exhibit high homogeneity, display key bNAb epitopes while concealing non-neutralizing epitopes, and adopt a morphology indistinguishable from authentic virion-associated spikes under electron microscopy. Furthermore, high-resolution structural studies using cryo-electron microscopy and X-ray crystallography have confirmed their structural fidelity, making them invaluable tools for structural biology and immunogen design. Despite these advances, the reliance on transient transfection for production has imposed severe limitations. Transient transfection is labor-intensive, costly, and yields highly variable quantities of protein from batch to batch. The process requires repeated transfections, extensive purification efforts, and the use of large volumes of expensive reagents, rendering it unsuitable for large-scale manufacturing or clinical-grade production. Moreover, the instability of transiently transfected cell populations over time introduces additional variability, and the inability to maintain consistent expression levels over multiple passages precludes the establishment of a reliable, scalable production platform.

The need for a stable, reproducible, and scalable source of native-like Env trimers has therefore become urgent. While stable cell lines have long been the gold standard for the production of recombinant therapeutic proteins—offering consistent yields, reduced costs, and compatibility with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards—no such system had been successfully established for the production of cleaved, native-like HIV-1 Env trimers. The principal challenges to developing such a system include the cytotoxicity associated with high-level expression of Env and Furin, the complexity of co-expressing two large, membrane-associated proteins within a single cell line, the requirement for precise post-translational modifications—including glycosylation and disulfide bond formation—that are sensitive to cellular context, and the risk of genetic instability leading to loss of expression over time. Previous attempts to generate stable cell lines expressing Env proteins had largely failed due to the accumulation of non-native, uncleaved, or degraded forms of the protein, or due to the rapid silencing of transgene expression. Furthermore, the integration of transgenes into random genomic loci often results in position effects that lead to unpredictable and unstable expression levels. To overcome these barriers, a system was required that could ensure targeted, single-copy integration of both the Env and Furin transgenes into a transcriptionally permissive genomic site, while simultaneously maintaining the viability and productivity of the host cells over extended periods of culture.

The Flp-In™ system, which enables site-specific recombination between a Flp Recombination Target (FRT) site integrated into the host genome and a matching FRT site flanking the transgene cassette on the plasmid vector, presented a compelling solution. This system allows for the generation of isogenic cell lines with a single, defined integration event, thereby eliminating the variability associated with random integration and ensuring consistent transgene expression across clonal populations. The system has been successfully employed in the production of other viral glycoproteins, including influenza hemagglutinin, but its application to the complex, multi-component, and highly unstable HIV-1 Env trimer had not been demonstrated. The successful implementation of this system would not only provide a robust platform for the production of BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers but would also serve as a generalizable blueprint for the development of stable cell lines expressing other SOSIP-stabilized Env constructs derived from diverse HIV-1 strains. The ability to produce these trimers in a scalable, reproducible manner under controlled conditions would open new avenues for structural studies, immunological characterization, and ultimately, the clinical development of HIV-1 vaccine candidates. The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel, engineered cell line system capable of the continuous, high-yield production of homogeneous, fully cleaved, native-like HIV-1 Env trimers without degradation, aggregation, or loss of antigenic integrity over extended culture periods.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention disclosed herein is a genetically engineered mammalian cell line capable of the continuous, high-yield production of soluble, cleaved, native-like HIV-1 envelope glycoprotein trimers, specifically the BG505 SOSIP.664 variant, without degradation, aggregation, or loss of structural integrity over multiple passages. The cell line is derived from either human embryonic kidney 293T cells or Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells and contains a single, site-specific integration of a recombinant expression cassette encoding both the BG505 SOSIP.664 gp140 Env protein and the Furin protease under the control of distinct, optimized promoters. The Env transgene is driven by the strong, constitutive cytomegalovirus (CMV) immediate-early promoter to ensure high-level expression, while the Furin transgene is under the control of the weaker, yet stable, elongation factor 1 alpha (EF1α) promoter to mitigate cytotoxicity and maintain cellular viability. The expression cassette is flanked by Flp Recombination Target (FRT) sites and integrates specifically into a pre-defined FRT site within the host genome via Flp recombinase-mediated recombination, ensuring clonal homogeneity and transcriptional consistency. The resulting cell lines stably express the Env protein intracellularly, with efficient trafficking through the secretory pathway, and secrete fully cleaved trimers into the culture supernatant at yields of up to 15 milligrams per 1 × 10⁹ cells after purification. The secreted trimers are homogeneous, exhibit the native trimeric conformation as confirmed by blue native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and size exclusion chromatography, and are fully cleaved at the gp120-gp41 junction, as demonstrated by reducing SDS-PAGE analysis. The trimers display the expected antigenic profile, binding strongly to broadly neutralizing antibodies targeting quaternary epitopes such as PGT145 while remaining non-reactive to non-neutralizing antibodies such as F105 that recognize epitopes occluded on the native trimer. The trimers are resistant to proteolytic degradation, showing no evidence of cleavage at the V3 loop or other vulnerable regions, in contrast to monomeric gp120 or uncleaved gp140 controls, which exhibit extensive fragmentation. Negative-stain electron microscopy confirms that the trimers adopt a morphology identical to those produced by transient transfection, with over 80% of particles exhibiting a native-like structure. Differential scanning calorimetry reveals a single, sharp thermal transition at approximately 67°C, indicating high structural homogeneity and stability. The cell lines remain viable and productive over at least ten passages, with no decline in Env expression, trimer yield, or cell viability, demonstrating long-term genetic and phenotypic stability. The invention further encompasses the purified trimers produced by these cell lines, which are suitable for use in structural biology, immunological assays, and as vaccine immunogens in preclinical and clinical studies. The method of generating these cell lines is rapid, reproducible, and adaptable to GMP conditions, providing a scalable and cost-effective platform for the manufacturing of HIV-1 vaccine candidates.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and highly advantageous system for the production of native-like HIV-1 envelope glycoprotein trimers, specifically the BG505 SOSIP.664 variant, through the use of genetically stable, clonally derived mammalian cell lines. This system overcomes longstanding limitations in the field of HIV-1 vaccine development by enabling the continuous, high-yield, and reproducible secretion of structurally authentic Env trimers that retain their native conformation, antigenic profile, and resistance to degradation over extended periods of culture. The invention is based on the discovery that the co-expression of the BG505 SOSIP.664 gp140 Env protein and the Furin protease within a single, genetically defined mammalian cell line, using a precisely engineered expression vector and the Flp-In™ site-specific integration system, results in the sustained production of homogeneous, fully cleaved, and non-degraded trimers that are indistinguishable from those produced by transient transfection methods, but at significantly higher yields and with unparalleled consistency. The cell lines of the invention are derived from either human embryonic kidney 293T cells or Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells, each of which has been modified to contain a single, targeted integration of a recombinant DNA construct encoding both the Env protein and the Furin protease. This targeted integration is achieved through the use of the Flp-In™ system, which ensures that the transgene cassette is inserted into a single, well-characterized genomic locus known to support high-level, stable transgene expression. By eliminating the variability inherent in random integration events, this system guarantees that all cells within a clonal population express the transgenes at consistent levels, thereby enabling the generation of large, homogeneous batches of purified trimers with minimal batch-to-batch variation. The expression cassette is contained within a modified pcDNA5/FRT vector, which has been strategically redesigned to accommodate both transgenes while preserving the functionality of the Flp-In™ system. The BG505 SOSIP.664 gp140 gene, which includes the SOS disulfide bond and the I559P substitution for trimer stabilization, is placed downstream of the human cytomegalovirus (CMV) immediate-early promoter, a strong, constitutive promoter known to drive high levels of protein expression in mammalian cells. This promoter choice was selected to maximize the production of the Env protein, which is the primary product of interest. The Furin protease gene, which is essential for the proteolytic cleavage of gp140 into its gp120 and gp41ECTO subunits, is placed downstream of the elongation factor 1 alpha (EF1α) promoter, a weaker but highly stable promoter that minimizes the cytotoxic effects associated with high-level Furin expression. The use of a weaker promoter for Furin was a critical innovation, as uncontrolled overexpression of Furin had previously been observed to induce endoplasmic reticulum stress and reduce cell viability, thereby compromising the long-term stability of any potential cell line. By balancing the expression levels of the two transgenes, the invention ensures that Furin is present in sufficient quantity to achieve complete and consistent cleavage of the Env protein, while avoiding the toxic effects that would otherwise lead to cell death or transgene silencing. The expression cassette also includes a synthetic polyadenylation signal derived from the herpes simplex virus thymidine kinase gene, which enhances mRNA stability and translation efficiency. The entire cassette is flanked by Flp Recombination Target (FRT) sites, allowing for the precise, site-specific integration of the construct into the FRT site present in the Flp-In™ host cells. The host cells themselves are genetically engineered to contain a single integrated FRT site linked to a hygromycin resistance gene, enabling selection of cells that have undergone successful recombination. The integration process is mediated by co-transfection with a plasmid encoding the Flp recombinase enzyme, which catalyzes the recombination event between the FRT sites on the vector and the genomic locus, resulting in the stable insertion of the expression cassette. Following transfection, cells are selected using hygromycin B, which eliminates non-integrated cells and allows for the outgrowth of clonal populations. To ensure that the resulting cell lines are genetically homogeneous and not mixed populations of cells with varying expression levels, limiting dilution cloning is performed on the initial hygromycin-resistant pools. This process isolates individual cells, each of which gives rise to a clonal population derived from a single progenitor, thereby ensuring that every cell in the final line carries the same integrated transgene cassette at the same genomic location. The resulting cell lines, designated 293T clone 13 #3-5 and CHO clone B-D7, are maintained in standard culture conditions and are capable of continuous growth and Env production over multiple passages. Intracellular expression of the Env protein is confirmed by flow cytometry and fluorescent microscopy using a fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated monoclonal antibody specific for the Env protein, which reveals robust intracellular accumulation of Env in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus, consistent with proper trafficking through the secretory pathway. The secretion of fully cleaved trimers into the culture supernatant is quantified using a capture ELISA system in which the Env protein is captured by the broadly neutralizing antibody 2G12 and detected using a biotinylated version of the quaternary epitope-specific antibody PGT145. This assay confirms that the secreted protein is not only present in high concentrations but is also correctly folded and assembled into trimers, as PGT145 binds exclusively to the native trimeric conformation. The absence of binding by the non-neutralizing antibody F105, which recognizes the CD4-binding site only when it is exposed on monomeric or uncleaved forms of Env, further confirms the structural authenticity of the secreted trimers. The purified trimers are subjected to a series of biochemical and biophysical analyses to confirm their structural integrity. Blue native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis demonstrates that the majority of the secreted Env protein exists as a trimer, with only minor amounts of dimers or monomers detectable. Size exclusion chromatography further confirms the homogeneity of the trimer population, yielding a single, symmetric peak corresponding to the expected molecular weight of the trimeric complex. Reducing SDS-PAGE analysis reveals the complete cleavage of the gp140 precursor into its constituent gp120 and gp41ECTO subunits, with no detectable uncleaved full-length gp140. The gp41ECTO subunit, which is less efficiently stained due to its lower molecular weight and hydrophobic nature, is nonetheless confirmed to be present through Western blotting with specific antibodies. Critically, when the trimers are analyzed under reducing conditions using Western blotting with the monoclonal antibody ARP 3119, which recognizes a conserved linear epitope in the N-terminal region of gp120, no degradation products are observed. This is in stark contrast to monomeric gp120 or uncleaved gp140 controls, which exhibit multiple degradation fragments, including the characteristic 70 kDa and 50 kDa bands resulting from proteolytic cleavage at the V3 loop. The absence of such degradation in the trimers produced by the cell lines of the invention indicates that the native-like trimeric structure effectively shields vulnerable protease-sensitive sites, preventing enzymatic cleavage during production and purification. This resistance to degradation is further corroborated by Western blotting with a polyclonal HIV-Ig pool derived from individuals infected with diverse HIV-1 subtypes, which again reveals a single, clean band corresponding to the gp120 subunit, with no evidence of fragmentation or aggregation. The purified trimers are also analyzed by negative-stain electron microscopy, which reveals a uniform population of particles with the characteristic native-like morphology of the HIV-1 Env trimer. The trimers appear as three-lobed structures with a central stalk, consistent with previously published structures of BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers generated by transient transfection. Quantitative analysis of the electron micrographs shows that 80 to 100% of the particles exhibit this native-like conformation, with no evidence of aberrant shapes, aggregates, or misfolded structures. The homogeneity of the trimers is further confirmed by differential scanning calorimetry, which detects a single, sharp thermal transition at approximately 67°C, indicating that the entire population of trimers unfolds cooperatively at the same temperature, a hallmark of structural homogeneity. In contrast, heterogeneous or aggregated protein preparations typically exhibit broad, multi-peaked thermal transitions. The cell lines of the invention are capable of sustained production over extended culture periods, with Env expression and trimer yield remaining constant over at least ten passages, each separated by four days, and with cell viability consistently maintained above 90% for the 293T line and above 85% for the CHO line. This long-term stability is a critical advantage over transient transfection systems, which require repeated transfections and yield declining protein levels over time. The ability to maintain a single batch of cells for weeks, continuously harvesting supernatant and re-feeding the culture, enables the production of 20 to 40 milligrams of purified trimers from a single 10-hyperflask culture over a two- to three-week period, a yield that is ten to thirty-two times greater than that obtained from transient transfection using the same number of cells. This dramatic increase in yield, combined with the consistency and scalability of the system, renders the cell lines of the invention uniquely suited for the production of clinical-grade vaccine immunogens. The invention further encompasses the purified trimers themselves, which are isolated from the culture supernatant by a two-step purification process involving affinity chromatography on a 2G12-immobilized resin followed by size exclusion chromatography. The resulting trimers are free of contaminants, including non-trimeric forms, aggregates, and cellular debris, and are suitable for use in structural studies, immunological assays, and as immunogens in animal models. The trimers produced by the 293T and CHO cell lines are functionally equivalent in their antigenic properties, despite differences in glycosylation patterns that may arise from the species-specific glycosylation machinery of the host cells. No significant differences in binding to a panel of broadly neutralizing antibodies targeting glycan-dependent epitopes have been observed, suggesting that the trimers retain their immunogenic potential regardless of the host cell origin. The invention thus provides not only a novel cell line system but also a complete, end-to-end platform for the scalable, reproducible, and clinically relevant production of HIV-1 Env trimers, representing a transformative advancement in the field of HIV-1 vaccine development.

### EXAMPLE 1

The invention is exemplified by the generation of a stable, clonally derived human embryonic kidney 293T cell line designated 293T clone 13 #3-5, which is capable of the continuous, high-yield secretion of native-like BG505 SOSIP.664 gp140 trimers. This cell line was established through a precisely controlled sequence of genetic engineering steps, beginning with the design and construction of a recombinant expression vector designated pAM/C BG505. This vector was derived from the pcDNA5/FRT plasmid, which contains a single Flp Recombination Target (FRT) site and a hygromycin resistance gene, both of which are necessary for the Flp-In™ system. The BG505 SOSIP.664 gp140 gene, which encodes the envelope glycoprotein with the SOS disulfide bond and the I559P substitution for trimer stabilization, was cloned into the vector between the HindIII and NotI restriction sites, placing it under the transcriptional control of the human cytomegalovirus (CMV) immediate-early promoter. This promoter was selected for its ability to drive high-level, constitutive expression of recombinant proteins in mammalian cells. To ensure that the Env protein is properly cleaved into its gp120 and gp41ECTO subunits—a critical requirement for achieving a native-like conformation—the furin protease gene was inserted into the same vector, positioned between the HindIII and NotI sites but under the control of the elongation factor 1 alpha (EF1α) promoter. The EF1α promoter was chosen because it provides sufficient expression of Furin to enable complete cleavage of the Env protein while avoiding the cytotoxicity associated with overexpression of this protease. The vector also incorporates a synthetic polyadenylation signal derived from the herpes simplex virus thymidine kinase gene, which enhances mRNA stability and translational efficiency. The entire expression cassette, including the CMV-driven Env gene and the EF1α-driven Furin gene, is flanked by FRT sites, allowing for site-specific integration into the host genome. The pAM/C BG505 vector was co-transfected into Flp-In™ 293T cells along with a plasmid encoding the Flp recombinase enzyme, which catalyzes the recombination event between the FRT sites on the vector and the genomic FRT site present in the host cells. This results in the precise, single-copy integration of the entire expression cassette into a transcriptionally active locus, ensuring consistent expression across all cells in the population. Following transfection, cells were selected using hygromycin B at a concentration of 150 μg/mL, which eliminated all non-integrated cells and allowed for the outgrowth of hygromycin-resistant colonies. After three weeks of selection, discrete colonies were observed, and these were subjected to limiting dilution cloning to isolate individual clonal populations. This process involved serial dilution of cells into 96-well plates, such that statistically, each well received either zero or one cell. After approximately two and a half weeks of culture, wells containing single-cell-derived colonies were identified based on their growth rate and Env expression levels. The expression of Env in these clones was assessed using a dot blot assay with the monoclonal antibody 2G12, which recognizes a conserved glycan-dependent epitope on the Env trimer. The clone exhibiting the highest level of Env expression, designated 293T clone 13 #3-5, was selected for further characterization. Flow cytometry using a fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated version of 2G12 confirmed that this clone expressed high levels of intracellular Env, and that this expression was retained even in the presence of Brefeldin A, which blocks protein trafficking from the endoplasmic reticulum, indicating that the Env protein is efficiently synthesized and retained within the secretory pathway. The secretion of trimers into the culture supernatant was quantified using a capture ELISA system in which 2G12 was used to immobilize Env proteins on a microplate, followed by detection with biotinylated PGT145, a broadly neutralizing antibody that binds exclusively to the native trimeric conformation. This assay confirmed that the supernatant from 293T clone 13 #3-5 contained approximately 2.1 μg/mL of fully assembled, native-like trimers. The cell line was then passaged ten times over a period of five weeks, with Env expression and trimer yield measured at each passage. No decline in intracellular Env levels, trimer concentration in the supernatant, or cell viability was observed, demonstrating the long-term genetic and phenotypic stability of the line. The purified trimers, obtained by 2G12-affinity chromatography followed by size exclusion chromatography, were analyzed by blue native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, which revealed a predominant trimeric band with minimal amounts of dimers or monomers. Reducing SDS-PAGE confirmed complete cleavage of the gp140 precursor into gp120 and gp41ECTO subunits, with no detectable uncleaved protein. Western blotting with the monoclonal antibody ARP 3119, which targets a conserved linear epitope in gp120, showed a single band at approximately 120 kDa, with no evidence of proteolytic degradation products. In contrast, control samples of monomeric gp120 or uncleaved gp140 exhibited multiple degradation fragments, confirming the resistance of the trimers produced by this cell line to proteolytic cleavage. Surface plasmon resonance analysis demonstrated that the trimers bound strongly to the broadly neutralizing antibody PGT145 but showed negligible binding to the non-neutralizing antibody F105, confirming their native-like antigenic profile. Negative-stain electron microscopy revealed that over 90% of the particles exhibited the characteristic native-like trimeric morphology, and differential scanning calorimetry showed a single, sharp thermal transition at 66.9°C, indicative of high structural homogeneity. The yield of purified trimers from this cell line was determined to be 12 milligrams per 1 × 10⁹ cells, a tenfold increase over the yield obtained from transient transfection of the same number of 293T cells. These results demonstrate that the 293T clone 13 #3-5 cell line is a stable, high-yielding, and structurally faithful source of BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers, suitable for large-scale production and clinical development.

### EXAMPLE 3

The invention is further exemplified by the biochemical characterization of the BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers produced by the 293T clone 13 #3-5 and CHO clone B-D7 cell lines, demonstrating their structural fidelity, homogeneity, and resistance to degradation. The trimers were purified from culture supernatants using a two-step process involving 2G12-affinity chromatography followed by size exclusion chromatography. The purified fractions were analyzed by blue native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, which separates protein complexes based on their native molecular weight and conformation. The results showed that the vast majority of the Env protein migrated as a single, discrete band corresponding to the trimeric form, with only minor amounts of dimeric or monomeric species detectable. This indicates that the trimers are assembled correctly and remain stable during purification. The migration pattern of the trimers from the CHO cell line was slightly faster than that of the 293T-derived trimers, a difference attributed to subtle variations in glycosylation patterns between human and hamster cells. This difference in electrophoretic mobility does not reflect any structural defect but rather a natural consequence of species-specific glycosylation machinery. The trimers were then analyzed under reducing conditions using SDS-PAGE, which denatures proteins and separates them based on molecular weight. Upon reduction, the gp140 precursor dissociated into its constituent gp120 and gp41ECTO subunits, confirming that the trimers were fully cleaved at the gp120-gp41 junction. The gp41ECTO subunit, which is less efficiently stained due to its smaller size and hydrophobic nature, was confirmed to be present through Western blotting with specific antibodies. Critically, when the trimers were analyzed by Western blotting using the monoclonal antibody ARP 3119, which recognizes a conserved linear epitope in the N-terminal region of gp120, only a single band at approximately 120 kDa was detected, with no evidence of smaller degradation fragments. This is in stark contrast to control samples of monomeric gp120 or uncleaved gp140, which exhibited multiple degradation products, including the characteristic 70 kDa and 50 kDa fragments resulting from cleavage at the V3 loop. The absence of such degradation in the trimers produced by the cell lines of the invention indicates that the native-like trimeric structure effectively shields vulnerable protease-sensitive sites, preventing enzymatic cleavage during production and purification. This resistance to degradation was further confirmed using a polyclonal HIV-Ig pool derived from individuals infected with diverse HIV-1 subtypes, which again revealed a single, clean band corresponding to the gp120 subunit, with no evidence of fragmentation or aggregation. The trimers were also analyzed for their antigenic profile using surface plasmon resonance and capture ELISA assays. In surface plasmon resonance, the trimers were flowed over CM5 chips coated with anti-Env monoclonal antibodies. The trimers bound strongly to the broadly neutralizing antibody PGT145, which recognizes a quaternary epitope dependent on the native trimeric conformation, but showed negligible binding to the non-neutralizing antibody F105, which recognizes the CD4-binding site only when it is exposed on monomeric or uncleaved forms of Env. This antigenic profile is identical to that of trimers produced by transient transfection and confirms that the trimers produced by the cell lines of the invention are structurally authentic. In the capture ELISA, the trimers were captured on a microplate using 2G12 and detected with biotinylated PGT145 or F105. Again, strong binding to PGT145 and no binding to F105 were observed, regardless of whether the trimers were derived from the 293T or CHO cell line. These results demonstrate that the trimers produced by the invention are not only structurally intact but also antigenically authentic, displaying the correct balance of exposed neutralizing epitopes and occluded non-neutralizing epitopes. The homogeneity of the trimers was further confirmed by differential scanning calorimetry, which measures the thermal stability of proteins. The trimers from both cell lines exhibited a single, sharp thermal transition at approximately 67°C, indicating that the entire population of trimers unfolds cooperatively at the same temperature, a hallmark of structural homogeneity. In contrast, heterogeneous or aggregated protein preparations typically exhibit broad, multi-peaked thermal transitions. The yield of purified trimers from the 293T cell line was 12 milligrams per 1 × 10⁹ cells, and from the CHO cell line was also 12 milligrams per 1 × 10⁹ cells, representing a tenfold and thirty-twofold increase, respectively, over the yields obtained from transient transfection. These results collectively demonstrate that the trimers produced by the cell lines of the invention are not only produced in high yield but are also structurally, biochemically, and antigenically identical to those produced by transient transfection, while offering the critical advantages of scalability, consistency, and long-term stability.

### EXAMPLE 4

The invention is further exemplified by the structural analysis of the BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers produced by the 293T clone 13 #3-5 and CHO clone B-D7 cell lines using negative-stain electron microscopy and differential scanning calorimetry, which provide direct visual and thermodynamic confirmation of their native-like conformation and homogeneity. Negative-stain electron microscopy was performed on purified trimers from both cell lines, as well as on trimers produced by transient transfection for comparison. The trimers were applied to carbon-coated grids, negatively stained with uranyl formate, and imaged using a transmission electron microscope operating at 120 keV. The resulting images were subjected to reference-free 2D class averaging, a computational method that aligns and averages thousands of individual particle images to generate high-resolution structural models. The analysis revealed that the trimers from both the 293T and CHO cell lines exhibited a consistent, native-like morphology characterized by a three-lobed structure with a central stalk, identical to the morphology of trimers produced by transient transfection. The percentage of particles exhibiting this native-like conformation ranged from 81% to 100% across all samples, with the 293T-derived trimers showing a slightly higher proportion (90–100%) compared to the CHO-derived trimers (80–90%). However, this difference was within the expected range of experimental variability for this method and did not reflect any fundamental structural defect. The trimers showed no evidence of aggregation, misfolding, or the presence of non-native conformations such as splayed-out monomers or uncleaved gp140 proteins, which are known to adopt distinct, aberrant shapes. The high proportion of native-like trimers confirms that the expression and secretion system of the invention is capable of producing a highly homogeneous population of Env trimers that accurately mimic the structure of the native viral spike. This structural fidelity is critical for the induction of broadly neutralizing antibodies, as only trimers with the correct conformation present the epitopes necessary for eliciting protective immune responses. The homogeneity of the trimers was further confirmed by differential scanning calorimetry, which measures the thermal stability of proteins by detecting the heat absorbed as they unfold. The trimers from both the 293T and CHO cell lines exhibited a single, sharp thermal transition at 66.9°C and 66.3°C, respectively. These values are nearly identical to the thermal transition temperature of 68.1°C observed for trimers produced by transient transfection of 293T cells and 67.9°C for those produced by transient transfection of 293S cells. The presence of a single, sharp transition indicates that the entire population of trimers unfolds cooperatively at the same temperature, a hallmark of structural homogeneity and the absence of heterogeneous or aggregated species. In contrast, heterogeneous protein preparations typically exhibit broad, multi-peaked transitions, reflecting the presence of multiple conformational states with different stabilities. The close agreement between the thermal profiles of the trimers produced by the cell lines and those produced by transient transfection confirms that the trimers generated by the invention are not only structurally similar but also thermodynamically equivalent to the gold-standard material used in high-resolution structural studies. The fact that the trimers from the CHO cell line, which is of hamster origin, exhibits a thermal transition temperature nearly identical to that of the 293T cell line, which is of human origin, further supports the conclusion that the differences in glycosylation between the two cell types do not significantly impact the overall stability or conformation of the trimers. This is a critical finding, as it suggests that the trimers produced by either cell line are equally suitable for use in structural studies and as immunogens in preclinical and clinical trials. The combination of negative-stain electron microscopy and differential scanning calorimetry provides complementary evidence that the trimers produced by the invention are structurally authentic, homogeneous, and stable, making them ideal candidates for further development as HIV-1 vaccine immunogens.

### EXAMPLE 6

The invention is further exemplified by the antigenic characterization of the BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers produced by the 293T clone 13 #3-5 and CHO clone B-D7 cell lines using a novel, non-tagged capture ELISA system and surface plasmon resonance analysis, which confirm their structural authenticity and suitability as immunogens. Traditional methods for assessing the antigenic properties of Env trimers often rely on the incorporation of epitope tags, such as His-tags or D7324 epitopes, into the Env protein to facilitate detection and quantification. However, the inclusion of such tags can alter the structure or immunogenicity of the trimer, potentially introducing artifacts that compromise their use as vaccine candidates. To overcome this limitation, the invention employs a tag-free, highly sensitive ELISA system in which the trimers are captured on a solid phase using the broadly neutralizing antibody 2G12, which binds to a conserved glycan-dependent epitope on the native trimer, and detected using biotinylated versions of the quaternary epitope-specific antibody PGT145 or the non-neutralizing antibody F105. This system allows for the direct assessment of trimer integrity without the need for engineered tags. In the capture ELISA, microplates were coated with 2G12, and culture supernatants from the 293T and CHO cell lines were added. After washing, biotinylated PGT145 or F105 was added, and binding was detected using streptavidin-conjugated horseradish peroxidase. The results showed that PGT145 bound efficiently to trimers from both cell lines, while F105 showed no detectable binding. This antigenic profile—strong binding to PGT145 and no binding to F105—is the hallmark of a native-like Env trimer, as PGT145 recognizes a conformational epitope that is only present on the properly assembled trimer, whereas F105 binds to the CD4-binding site, which is occluded on the native trimer but exposed on monomeric or uncleaved forms of Env. The lack of F105 binding confirms the absence of contaminating monomers or uncleaved gp140 proteins, which are common impurities in transient transfection systems. The same antigenic profile was observed for trimers produced by transient transfection, confirming that the trimers from the cell lines of the invention are structurally identical to the gold-standard material. Surface plasmon resonance analysis was performed using a Biacore 3000 instrument, in which anti-Env monoclonal antibodies were immobilized on CM5 chips via an anti-Fc antibody, and purified trimers were flowed over the surface. The binding response was measured in real time, and the results showed that the trimers from both the 293T and CHO cell lines bound strongly to PGT145, with response units comparable to those of transiently transfected trimers, while showing minimal binding to F105. The binding kinetics were consistent across all samples, with no significant differences in association or dissociation rates, further confirming the structural equivalence of the trimers. The ability to perform these assays without the use of epitope tags is a critical advantage, as it ensures that the antigenic profile measured reflects the true, unmodified structure of the trimer. This is particularly important for vaccine development, where the immunogenicity of the trimer must be representative of the native viral spike. The results demonstrate that the trimers produced by the invention are not only structurally and biochemically authentic but also antigenically identical to those produced by transient transfection, making them ideal candidates for use in immunological studies and as vaccine immunogens in preclinical and clinical trials.
