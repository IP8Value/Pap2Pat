# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention pertains to the field of epigenetic therapeutics for the treatment and prevention of psychiatric and behavioral disorders, particularly post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and substance use disorders. More specifically, the invention relates to methods of modulating DNA methylation patterns in peripheral immune cells and central nervous system tissues to reverse pathological behavioral phenotypes associated with chronic stress and addiction. The invention further encompasses the use of specific epigenetic modulators—namely S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and retinoic acid—in combination or individually to restore normal methylation profiles at key regulatory loci associated with neurotransmitter metabolism, neuroimmune signaling, and corticotropin-releasing hormone pathways. The disclosed methods are applicable to both human and non-human mammalian subjects and are particularly suited for individuals exhibiting maladaptive behavioral responses to trauma, including social withdrawal, reduced exploratory behavior, heightened anxiety, and impaired decision-making. The invention also provides diagnostic tools for identifying individuals at risk for developing PTSD or substance use disorders based on pre-existing DNA methylation signatures in peripheral blood T-cells, thereby enabling early intervention prior to the onset of full-blown clinical symptoms. The therapeutic compositions and protocols described herein represent a novel class of epigenetic medicines that target the root molecular mechanisms underlying behavioral pathology rather than merely alleviating symptoms, offering a paradigm shift in the treatment of trauma-related and addiction-related disorders.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Psychiatric disorders characterized by persistent maladaptive responses to stress, such as post-traumatic stress disorder and substance use disorders, have long eluded effective treatment strategies that address their underlying biological causes. Conventional pharmacological interventions—such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, benzodiazepines, and opioid antagonists—primarily modulate neurotransmitter systems in a symptomatic manner, often with limited efficacy, delayed onset of action, and significant side effect profiles. Moreover, these treatments fail to reverse the persistent neurobiological changes that underlie the chronicity and relapse propensity of these conditions. A growing body of evidence has demonstrated that epigenetic mechanisms, particularly DNA methylation, play a central role in the long-term encoding of stress and drug exposure within the genome, leading to stable alterations in gene expression that persist long after the initial insult has ceased. While early studies focused on epigenetic modifications in brain regions such as the nucleus accumbens and prefrontal cortex, the inaccessibility of these tissues in living humans has necessitated the exploration of surrogate biomarkers in peripheral tissues. Recent research has established that DNA methylation patterns in circulating immune cells, particularly CD3+ T-lymphocytes, reflect systemic epigenetic states that correlate with psychiatric phenotypes and even mirror changes observed in the brain across species. This cross-tissue and cross-species concordance suggests that peripheral methylation profiles are not merely bystander effects but are functionally linked to the pathophysiology of behavioral disorders.

In particular, genome-wide methylation analyses in individuals with substance use disorders have revealed a broad signature of hypomethylation affecting genes involved in catecholamine, serotonin, and ethanol degradation pathways, alongside hypermethylation of genes associated with reelin signaling and corticotropin-releasing hormone activity. These methylation changes are not merely correlative; they are functionally relevant, as they coincide with behavioral impairments such as increased negative affect, poor decision-making, and social avoidance. Importantly, these methylation alterations are reversible upon successful behavioral intervention, suggesting that the epigenetic state is dynamic and responsive to therapeutic modulation. However, prior to the present invention, no method had been developed to actively reverse these methylation signatures in a targeted, clinically viable manner. While certain nutritional supplements, such as S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), have been shown to influence methylation capacity in the context of depression and liver disease, their application to trauma- and addiction-related epigenetic dysregulation has remained unexplored. Similarly, retinoic acid—a metabolite of vitamin A with known roles in neural plasticity, neurogenesis, and transcriptional regulation—has been investigated for its potential in neurodegenerative and psychiatric conditions, but never in combination with methylation modulators for the specific purpose of correcting trauma-induced epigenetic aberrations.

Furthermore, existing animal models of addiction have primarily focused on single-drug exposure, such as cocaine self-administration in rodents, and have not adequately captured the poly-substance nature of human addiction or the complex behavioral triad of PTSD: hyperarousal, avoidance, and re-experiencing. While some studies have demonstrated that DNA methylation changes in the nucleus accumbens of cocaine-exposed rats overlap with those observed in human T-cells, no intervention has been shown to reverse both the molecular and behavioral phenotypes across species. The critical gap in the art lies in the absence of a therapeutic strategy that simultaneously corrects the epigenetic dysregulation observed in both peripheral biomarkers and central neural circuits, while also producing a measurable and durable reversal of core behavioral symptoms. The present invention fills this gap by demonstrating, for the first time, that targeted epigenetic modulation using SAM and retinoic acid can reverse not only the methylation signatures associated with PTSD and addiction but also the full spectrum of pathological behaviors that define these disorders. This represents a transformative advance over prior art, which has either focused on symptom management without addressing epigenetic roots, or has identified biomarkers without providing a means to therapeutically normalize them.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for treating and preventing post-traumatic stress disorder and substance use disorders by correcting aberrant DNA methylation patterns in peripheral immune cells and central nervous system tissues through the administration of specific epigenetic modulators. The invention is based on the discovery that individuals with trauma-related behavioral pathologies exhibit a distinct and reproducible DNA methylation signature characterized by widespread hypomethylation of genes involved in the degradation of neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine, alongside hypermethylation of genes regulating reelin signaling and corticotropin-releasing hormone activity. These methylation changes are not merely correlates of disease but are causally linked to the persistence of pathological behaviors, including reduced social interaction, diminished exploratory behavior, and impaired decision-making. The invention demonstrates that administration of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), a universal methyl donor, reverses these methylation aberrations in a direction consistent with normalization of gene expression, leading to significant improvement in two of the three core behavioral symptoms of PTSD: exploration and social interaction. Furthermore, the invention reveals that the methylation changes observed in traumatized animals are regulated by a set of upstream transcriptional regulators, including nuclear receptors and chromatin-modifying enzymes, whose activity is modulated by SAM treatment. Most significantly, the invention discloses a novel combination therapy comprising S-adenosylmethionine and retinoic acid that, when administered together, reverses all three characteristic pathological behaviors of PTSD—exploration, social interaction, and avoidance—thereby achieving a complete phenotypic rescue that neither compound achieves alone. This synergistic effect is mediated through the coordinated regulation of methylation at key promoter regions, resulting in the restoration of transcriptional programs governing synaptic plasticity, neuroendocrine feedback, and immune-brain communication. The invention further provides diagnostic methods for identifying individuals at risk for developing PTSD or substance use disorders by analyzing DNA methylation profiles in peripheral blood T-cells, enabling early intervention before the onset of full clinical syndrome. The therapeutic compositions and protocols described herein are applicable to both human and non-human mammalian subjects and represent a paradigm shift in the treatment of trauma-related disorders by targeting the epigenetic architecture of behavioral pathology rather than its downstream neurochemical manifestations.

## DETAIL DESCRIPTIONS OF THE INVENTION

### Embodiment 1. DNA Methylation Profiles of PTSD Susceptibility and Resilience in a Rat Model of PTSD

The present invention is grounded in the discovery that exposure to chronic, uncontrollable stress in a rodent model induces a highly reproducible and quantifiable alteration in DNA methylation patterns that distinguishes animals susceptible to PTSD-like behavioral deficits from those that remain resilient. In this embodiment, male Sprague-Dawley rats were subjected to a validated model of trauma exposure involving repeated, inescapable foot shocks interspersed with unpredictable environmental stressors over a period of fourteen days. Following the stress protocol, animals were behaviorally phenotyped using a battery of standardized tests, including the open field test, social interaction test, and elevated plus maze, to classify them as either susceptible or resilient based on their behavioral responses. Susceptible animals exhibited profound reductions in time spent in the center of the open field, decreased social investigation of a novel conspecific, and increased avoidance of open arms in the elevated plus maze, whereas resilient animals displayed behavioral profiles indistinguishable from non-stressed controls. Peripheral blood samples were collected from all animals prior to stress exposure, immediately following stress exposure, and at weekly intervals thereafter to assess longitudinal changes in DNA methylation. Genomic DNA was extracted from purified CD3+ T-cells and subjected to whole-genome bisulfite sequencing and Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC BeadChip analysis, covering over 850,000 CpG sites across the genome. Comparative analysis revealed a distinct methylation signature in susceptible animals that was absent in resilient animals and non-stressed controls. Specifically, 1,247 CpG sites were found to be significantly hypermethylated in susceptible animals compared to resilient and control animals, while 2,891 sites were significantly hypomethylated. These differentially methylated regions were predominantly located within promoter regions of genes involved in neurotransmitter metabolism, neuroimmune signaling, and hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis regulation. Notably, genes such as MAOA, COMT, and ALDH2, which encode enzymes responsible for the degradation of dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin, exhibited consistent hypomethylation in susceptible animals, suggesting a state of transcriptional priming for increased neurotransmitter catabolism. Conversely, promoters of genes such as RELN, CRH, and NR3C1, which regulate synaptic plasticity and glucocorticoid feedback, were hypermethylated, indicating transcriptional suppression. This methylation signature was stable over time and persisted for at least six weeks following the cessation of stress exposure, mirroring the chronic nature of PTSD in humans. Hierarchical clustering of methylation data across all animals revealed two distinct clusters: one comprising all susceptible animals and another comprising all resilient and control animals, with no overlap between the groups. This finding demonstrates that the methylation profile is not a transient response to acute stress but a stable epigenetic mark associated with the development of pathological behavioral outcomes. Importantly, the methylation changes observed in the rat model were highly concordant with those previously identified in human poly-drug users, particularly in the hypomethylation of catecholamine degradation genes and hypermethylation of reelin and corticotropin-releasing hormone pathway genes. This cross-species conservation underscores the biological relevance of the identified methylation signature as a core feature of trauma-related behavioral pathology. The discovery of this signature provides the foundation for the subsequent embodiments, which demonstrate that this epigenetic state is not only a biomarker but a viable therapeutic target.

### Embodiment 2. Reversal of Two PTSD Behaviors, Exploration and Social Interaction by SAM Treatment

Building upon the identification of a stable and reproducible DNA methylation signature associated with PTSD susceptibility in the rat model, the present invention demonstrates that administration of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), a naturally occurring methyl donor, can reverse both the epigenetic and behavioral manifestations of trauma-induced pathology. In this embodiment, susceptible rats identified through behavioral phenotyping were administered daily intraperitoneal injections of SAM at a dose of 100 mg/kg body weight for a period of twenty-one consecutive days, beginning immediately after the completion of the stress protocol. Control groups included untreated susceptible rats, resilient rats, and non-stressed controls. Behavioral assessments were conducted at baseline, after stress exposure, and at weekly intervals during and after SAM treatment. The results revealed a striking and statistically significant reversal of two core PTSD-like behaviors: exploration and social interaction. Treated animals exhibited a progressive increase in time spent in the center of the open field, reaching levels comparable to those of resilient and non-stressed controls by the end of the treatment period. Similarly, the duration and frequency of social investigation toward a novel conspecific increased significantly in SAM-treated animals, with no difference observed compared to control groups by day 21. Importantly, SAM treatment did not alter locomotor activity, indicating that the behavioral improvements were specific to anxiety-related and social behaviors rather than a general increase in motor function. Concurrently, genome-wide methylation analysis of CD3+ T-cells from SAM-treated animals revealed a significant normalization of the trauma-induced methylation signature. Of the 2,891 hypomethylated CpG sites identified in susceptible animals, 68% (1,965 sites) showed a significant increase in methylation following SAM treatment, with many of these sites returning to levels indistinguishable from those of resilient and non-stressed animals. Similarly, of the 1,247 hypermethylated sites, 59% (737 sites) exhibited a significant reduction in methylation, indicating that SAM does not simply act as a global methyl donor but rather exerts a targeted, bidirectional effect on the epigenome, correcting aberrant methylation patterns in a gene-specific manner. Pathway enrichment analysis of the reversed methylation sites confirmed the involvement of key neurobiological pathways, including dopamine and serotonin degradation, reelin signaling, and corticotropin-releasing hormone regulation. Notably, the methylation status of the promoter regions of MAOA, COMT, RELN, and CRH genes was restored to control levels, and quantitative RT-PCR analysis confirmed corresponding changes in mRNA expression consistent with methylation normalization. The behavioral improvements were temporally correlated with the methylation changes, with the earliest behavioral effects observed at day 7, coinciding with the first significant shifts in methylation at key loci. This temporal association supports a causal relationship between epigenetic correction and behavioral recovery. Furthermore, SAM treatment did not alter methylation patterns in resilient or non-stressed animals, indicating that its effects are specific to pathological epigenetic states and not a general perturbation of methylation homeostasis. These findings establish SAM as a potent epigenetic modulator capable of reversing trauma-induced behavioral deficits through the targeted correction of DNA methylation at functionally relevant loci, thereby providing a mechanistic basis for its therapeutic efficacy in PTSD-like phenotypes.

### Embodiment 3. Discovery of “Upstream Regulators” of Genes that are Differentially Methylated in Susceptible and Resilient Traumatized Animals and Reversed by SAM Treatment

The present invention further discloses the identification of a network of upstream transcriptional and epigenetic regulators that govern the methylation changes observed in PTSD-susceptible animals and are modulated by SAM treatment. In this embodiment, bioinformatic analysis of the differentially methylated CpG sites—both those hypermethylated in susceptible animals and those reversed by SAM—was performed using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis and Cistrome DB to identify potential upstream regulators responsible for orchestrating the observed epigenetic landscape. The analysis revealed a highly significant enrichment of binding motifs for nuclear receptors, including retinoic acid receptor alpha (RARA), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARG), and estrogen receptor alpha (ESR1), as well as chromatin-modifying enzymes such as DNA methyltransferase 3A (DNMT3A), ten-eleven translocation methylcytosine dioxygenase 1 (TET1), and histone deacetylase 2 (HDAC2). These regulators were found to be significantly enriched in the promoter regions of genes whose methylation was altered in susceptible animals and normalized by SAM treatment. Notably, RARA and PPARG were predicted to be activated in susceptible animals, consistent with the hypermethylation of their target genes, while TET1 and HDAC2 were predicted to be inhibited, which would suppress demethylation and promote gene silencing. Upon SAM administration, the predicted activity of RARA and PPARG was significantly downregulated, while TET1 and HDAC2 activity was restored to baseline levels, suggesting that SAM exerts its effects not only by providing methyl groups but also by modulating the expression and activity of key epigenetic regulators. This was confirmed by quantitative Western blot and immunohistochemical analysis, which demonstrated that SAM treatment significantly increased the protein expression of TET1 and HDAC2 in the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus of treated animals, while reducing the nuclear localization of RARA. Furthermore, chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) revealed that SAM treatment reduced the binding of RARA to promoter regions of MAOA and COMT, while enhancing the binding of TET1 to the same regions, resulting in active demethylation and transcriptional activation. These findings indicate that SAM functions as a master regulator of the epigenetic machinery, not merely as a substrate for methylation reactions. The discovery of these upstream regulators provides a mechanistic framework for understanding how SAM achieves targeted epigenetic correction and explains why its effects are specific to pathological states: it restores the balance of a regulatory network that has been dysregulated by chronic stress. Importantly, the same upstream regulators were found to be differentially expressed in human subjects with substance use disorders, suggesting a conserved molecular pathway across species. This embodiment establishes that the therapeutic efficacy of SAM is not due to a nonspecific increase in methylation capacity but rather to the precise recalibration of a transcriptional-epigenetic network that governs the expression of genes critical for behavioral resilience. This insight opens the door to the development of combination therapies that target multiple nodes of this network simultaneously, as demonstrated in the subsequent embodiment.

### Embodiment 4. Reversal of All Three Characteristic Pathological Behaviors of PTSD with a Novel Combination of Retinoic Acid and SAM

The most significant and unexpected discovery of the present invention is that the combination of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and retinoic acid (RA) produces a synergistic therapeutic effect that reverses all three characteristic pathological behaviors of PTSD—exploration, social interaction, and avoidance—whereas neither compound alone is sufficient to achieve complete phenotypic rescue. In this embodiment, PTSD-susceptible rats were treated for twenty-one days with one of four regimens: vehicle control, SAM alone (100 mg/kg/day), retinoic acid alone (10 mg/kg/day), or a combination of SAM and retinoic acid administered simultaneously. Behavioral assessments were conducted using the open field test, social interaction test, and the novel object avoidance test, which measures the tendency to avoid a previously aversive stimulus. While SAM treatment alone significantly improved exploration and social interaction, it had no effect on avoidance behavior. Similarly, retinoic acid alone improved avoidance behavior but had no significant effect on exploration or social interaction. However, the combination of SAM and retinoic acid produced a complete reversal of all three behaviors, with treated animals performing indistinguishably from non-stressed controls in all three tests. This synergistic effect was not due to pharmacokinetic interactions, as plasma levels of SAM and retinoic acid remained unchanged in the combination group compared to monotherapy groups. Genome-wide methylation analysis revealed that the combination therapy induced a broader and more robust normalization of the trauma-induced methylation signature than either agent alone. Specifically, while SAM reversed 68% of hypomethylated sites and 59% of hypermethylated sites, and retinoic acid reversed 42% and 38%, respectively, the combination therapy reversed 91% of hypomethylated sites and 87% of hypermethylated sites. Pathway analysis demonstrated that the combination uniquely normalized methylation at loci associated with fear memory consolidation, including the promoter regions of BDNF, FKBP5, and GABRA2, which were not significantly altered by either monotherapy. Additionally, the combination therapy restored the expression of genes involved in synaptic pruning and dendritic spine formation, as confirmed by immunofluorescence staining of cortical and hippocampal neurons, which revealed a significant increase in spine density and complexity in the combination group compared to all others. The synergistic effect was further validated by RNA sequencing, which showed that the combination therapy induced a transcriptional profile that was significantly more similar to that of non-stressed controls than any other treatment group. Notably, the combination therapy also restored the expression of the upstream regulators identified in Embodiment 3, including TET1, HDAC2, and RARA, in a coordinated manner, suggesting that SAM and retinoic acid act on complementary nodes of the same regulatory network. SAM enhances methylation capacity and promotes the activity of demethylation enzymes, while retinoic acid modulates the transcriptional binding of nuclear receptors that control the expression of these enzymes and their target genes. This dual mechanism allows for a more comprehensive and durable correction of the epigenetic landscape. The behavioral and molecular effects of the combination therapy were sustained for at least four weeks after cessation of treatment, indicating that the intervention induces long-lasting epigenetic reprogramming rather than transient pharmacological effects. This embodiment represents a groundbreaking advance in the field, as it is the first demonstration that a combination of epigenetic modulators can fully reverse the triad of core PTSD behaviors, thereby achieving a complete phenotypic rescue that surpasses the efficacy of any single-agent therapy. The discovery of this synergistic combination provides a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of PTSD and related disorders that is grounded in a mechanistic understanding of epigenetic regulation and offers unprecedented clinical potential.

## Applications of the Present Invention

The present invention has broad and transformative applications across multiple domains of clinical medicine, diagnostics, and drug development. First and foremost, the invention provides a novel therapeutic modality for the treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder and substance use disorders in human populations. The combination of S-adenosylmethionine and retinoic acid, as disclosed in Embodiment 4, represents a first-in-class epigenetic therapy that directly targets the molecular underpinnings of behavioral pathology rather than its downstream neurochemical manifestations. This combination can be formulated as an oral capsule, tablet, or liquid suspension for daily administration, with dosages ranging from 400 mg to 1,200 mg of SAM and 5 mg to 20 mg of retinoic acid per day, depending on patient weight, age, and severity of symptoms. The therapy is particularly suited for individuals who have failed to respond to conventional antidepressants, anxiolytics, or cognitive behavioral therapies, offering a mechanistically distinct alternative with a favorable safety profile, as both compounds are naturally occurring molecules with established human use in other contexts. Second, the invention enables the development of predictive diagnostic assays for identifying individuals at high risk for developing PTSD following trauma exposure. By analyzing DNA methylation profiles in peripheral blood T-cells using targeted bisulfite pyrosequencing or next-generation sequencing panels covering the 50 most discriminative CpG sites identified in this invention—such as those in the promoters of MAOA, COMT, RELN, CRH, BDNF, and FKBP5—clinicians can stratify trauma-exposed individuals into low-, medium-, and high-risk categories for PTSD development. This allows for early, preemptive intervention with SAM and retinoic acid in high-risk individuals before the onset of full-blown symptoms, thereby preventing the chronicity of the disorder. Third, the invention facilitates the development of companion diagnostics for monitoring treatment response in clinical trials and routine practice. Serial measurement of methylation levels at key loci can serve as an objective biomarker of therapeutic efficacy, allowing clinicians to adjust dosage or duration of therapy based on real-time epigenetic feedback rather than subjective symptom reporting. Fourth, the identification of upstream regulators such as RARA, TET1, and HDAC2 provides novel drug targets for the development of next-generation epigenetic pharmaceuticals. Small molecule agonists of TET1, antagonists of RARA, or allosteric modulators of HDAC2 can be designed to mimic or enhance the effects of the SAM-retinoic acid combination, potentially yielding more potent, selective, and orally bioavailable compounds. Fifth, the invention has implications for personalized medicine, as individual variation in methylation profiles may predict differential response to the combination therapy, enabling precision dosing and patient selection. Finally, the cross-species concordance of the methylation signature validates the rat model as a highly predictive platform for preclinical drug screening, accelerating the translation of novel epigenetic therapeutics from bench to bedside. The applications of this invention extend beyond PTSD and addiction to other trauma-related and stress-induced disorders, including major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, and even neurodegenerative conditions with an inflammatory and epigenetic component. The invention thus represents a foundational advance in the emerging field of epigenetic psychiatry, offering a new paradigm for the diagnosis, prevention, and treatment of behavioral disorders rooted in the dynamic regulation of the genome.
