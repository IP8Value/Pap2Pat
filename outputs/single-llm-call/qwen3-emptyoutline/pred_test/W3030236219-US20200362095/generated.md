# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of polymeric materials for biomedical applications has long been driven by the need for systems capable of precise spatial and temporal control over drug release, particularly in localized therapeutic contexts such as intratumoral delivery. Traditional drug delivery platforms—ranging from liposomes and polymeric nanoparticles to hydrogels based on linear block copolymers—have demonstrated varying degrees of success in improving pharmacokinetics, reducing systemic toxicity, and enhancing therapeutic efficacy. However, a persistent challenge remains: the inability to simultaneously achieve high drug loading capacity, prolonged retention at the site of administration, and controlled, sustained release kinetics without triggering unintended inflammatory or toxic responses. Many existing systems suffer from rapid diffusion away from the injection site, premature burst release of payloads, or insufficient mechanical stability to form stable, long-lasting depots under physiological conditions. These limitations are especially pronounced in immunotherapeutic applications, where the timing and concentration of immune-modulating agents must be carefully coordinated with the release of tumor antigens to avoid immune tolerance or systemic cytokine storms.

In recent years, advances in macromolecular engineering have enabled the design of increasingly complex polymeric architectures with tailored physical and chemical properties. Among these, bottlebrush polymers—characterized by a linear backbone densely grafted with side chains—have emerged as a promising class of materials due to their high solubility, tunable conformational behavior, and ability to self-assemble into well-defined nanostructures. The graft-through approach, particularly via ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers, has proven especially effective in generating highly uniform, sequence-controlled bottlebrush architectures with precise molecular weights and narrow polydispersities. This methodology allows for the incorporation of diverse functional blocks, including hydrophobic, hydrophilic, thermoresponsive, and biodegradable segments, into a single macromolecular chain. Despite these advances, the vast majority of reported bottlebrush systems have been limited to diblock or statistical architectures, with only a handful of examples exploring triblock configurations. Even fewer have been applied to the formation of physically crosslinked hydrogels capable of in situ gelation upon exposure to physiological stimuli.

Triblock copolymers, particularly those with an ABC sequence, have long been recognized for their unique self-assembly behavior and superior mechanical properties in comparison to their ABA or CBC counterparts. Theoretical and experimental studies on linear ABC triblocks have demonstrated that their non-symmetric topology prevents the formation of mechanically defective primary loops in physical networks, leading to enhanced elastic modulus, improved resistance to creep, and greater structural integrity under stress. These advantages have been exploited in protein-based macromers and synthetic linear copolymers, where ABC sequences have been shown to yield hydrogels with slower erosion rates and more robust mechanical performance. However, translating these principles to bottlebrush architectures has remained unexplored. The incorporation of bottlebrush topology into triblock systems introduces additional dimensions of tunability: the high grafting density of side chains alters chain mobility, increases steric repulsion, and modifies the effective volume fraction of each block within self-assembled domains. These factors, when combined with the sequence control afforded by sequential ROMP, create a platform capable of unprecedented precision in designing materials that respond to multiple stimuli—temperature, pH, ionic strength, and enzymatic activity—with predictable and programmable behavior.

Despite the theoretical promise of such systems, no prior art has described a triblock bottlebrush copolymer (TBC) with an ABC sequence that simultaneously exhibits: (1) spontaneous micellization in aqueous media at room temperature driven by a hydrophobic core-forming block, (2) reversible thermogelation above a physiological transition temperature due to a thermoresponsive outer block, and (3) the ability to encapsulate and sustainably release a broad spectrum of therapeutics—including small molecule chemotherapeutics, immune agonists, and macromolecular proteins—without inducing cytotoxicity or systemic inflammation. Existing hydrogel platforms for intratumoral delivery rely on either physical crosslinking via hydrogen bonding or ionic interactions, which are often unstable under physiological salt concentrations, or chemical crosslinking methods that require toxic catalysts, UV irradiation, or lengthy curing times incompatible with clinical translation. Furthermore, many systems lack the ability to independently control micellization and gelation, forcing drug loading and gel formation to occur simultaneously, which compromises encapsulation efficiency and homogeneity.

The present invention overcomes these longstanding limitations by introducing a novel class of ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymers synthesized via sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers. These materials are designed with a hydrophobic polylactic acid (PLA) core-forming block, a hydrophilic polyethylene glycol (PEG) mid-block, and a thermoresponsive poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM) outer block. This specific sequence enables the formation of stable, monodisperse micelles in aqueous solution at room temperature, with the PLA core serving as a reservoir for lipophilic drugs and the PEG shell providing colloidal stability and biocompatibility. Upon heating to temperatures above the lower critical solution temperature (LCST) of PNIPAM—approximately 32–34 °C, which is within the physiological range—the PNIPAM blocks undergo a reversible hydrophobic transition, leading to inter-micellar crosslinking and the formation of a physically entangled, elastic hydrogel network. This two-step process—micellization followed by gelation—allows for the efficient loading of therapeutics into pre-formed micelles under mild conditions, followed by in situ gelation at the target site, resulting in a localized, long-lasting depot that minimizes systemic exposure while maximizing local drug concentration.

The utility of this architecture is further enhanced by its modular nature. Each block can be independently synthesized and characterized, allowing for fine-tuning of molecular weight, grafting density, and block ratio to optimize drug loading, release kinetics, gelation temperature, and mechanical properties. The use of ROMP ensures high fidelity in block sequence, minimal side reactions, and compatibility with a wide range of functional groups, enabling the future incorporation of targeting ligands, enzymatically cleavable linkers, or stimuli-responsive moieties without compromising the integrity of the polymer backbone. Moreover, the absence of covalent crosslinkers or external crosslinking agents renders the system inherently biodegradable and non-toxic, with degradation products limited to lactic acid, ethylene glycol, and N-isopropylacrylamide—all of which are known to be metabolized or excreted without accumulation.

This invention represents a paradigm shift in the design of injectable hydrogels for localized therapy. Unlike previous systems that rely on passive diffusion or irreversible crosslinking, the ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymer hydrogel operates as a dynamic, responsive, and self-assembling material that adapts to its environment. It is not merely a carrier for drugs, but a programmable scaffold that orchestrates the spatiotemporal release of therapeutic agents in a manner that mimics the precision of natural biological signaling. The ability to achieve sustained local delivery of both chemotherapeutic and immunomodulatory agents—without triggering systemic inflammation or TLR tolerance—demonstrates a profound therapeutic advantage over conventional approaches. This innovation opens new avenues for the treatment of solid tumors, chronic inflammatory conditions, ophthalmic diseases, and other pathologies requiring localized, prolonged, and precisely timed drug exposure.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel class of triblock bottlebrush copolymers comprising a hydrophobic first block, a hydrophilic second block, and a thermoresponsive third block, wherein the blocks are arranged in an ABC sequence and are connected via a linear backbone formed by ring-opening metathesis polymerization of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers. These triblock bottlebrush copolymers are capable of self-assembling in aqueous media at ambient temperatures to form stable, monodisperse nanoparticles with a hydrophobic core and a hydrophilic corona, and further undergo reversible physical gelation upon exposure to temperatures above a lower critical solution temperature of approximately 32 to 34 degrees Celsius, thereby forming a mechanically robust, injectable hydrogel that remains localized at the site of administration. The hydrogel exhibits sustained release of encapsulated therapeutic agents over a period of days to weeks, without burst release, and does not induce significant cytotoxicity or systemic inflammation, even upon repeated administration.

In one embodiment, the first block is derived from polylactic acid (PLA), the second block is derived from polyethylene glycol (PEG), and the third block is derived from poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM). The PLA block provides a hydrophobic domain capable of encapsulating lipophilic small molecule drugs such as paclitaxel, SN-38, resiquimod, gemcitabine, and dexamethasone, as well as macromolecular payloads including ovalbumin and other proteins. The PEG block serves as a hydrophilic spacer that enhances aqueous solubility, reduces opsonization, and prevents aggregation of the micelles, while the PNIPAM block confers thermoresponsiveness, enabling the transition from a free-flowing solution to a cohesive hydrogel at physiological temperatures. The macromonomers used to construct these blocks are functionalized with exo-norbornene groups, which enable efficient and selective ring-opening metathesis polymerization using a ruthenium-based catalyst, resulting in well-defined, sequence-controlled architectures with low polydispersity and high conversion rates.

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the invention are synthesized through a sequential, living polymerization process in which the first macromonomer is polymerized to form a homopolymer block, followed by the addition of the second macromonomer to extend the chain with a second block, and finally the addition of the third macromonomer to complete the ABC sequence. This stepwise approach ensures that each block is incorporated with high fidelity, minimizing the formation of statistical or random copolymer impurities. The resulting polymers exhibit number-average molecular weights ranging from 50,000 to 500,000 g/mol, with a polydispersity index below 1.3, as determined by size exclusion chromatography. The grafting density of side chains along the backbone is sufficiently high to prevent chain entanglement and promote the formation of discrete micellar structures rather than bulk gels at low concentrations.

Upon dispersion in aqueous media, the triblock bottlebrush copolymers spontaneously self-assemble into spherical micelles with a hydrodynamic diameter between 90 and 120 nanometers, as measured by dynamic light scattering, and a core diameter of approximately 20 nanometers, as confirmed by cryogenic transmission electron microscopy. The micelles are stable in physiological saline, serum-containing media, and at temperatures up to 30 degrees Celsius, and demonstrate drug encapsulation efficiencies exceeding 80% for a wide range of therapeutic agents, including both hydrophobic and moderately hydrophilic compounds. The encapsulated drugs are released in a sustained, linear fashion over a period of three to five days, with no evidence of burst release, as determined by dialysis and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry.

When the concentration of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer is increased to 10 weight percent or greater, and the temperature is raised above 32 degrees Celsius, the system undergoes a sharp, reversible phase transition from a viscous liquid to a viscoelastic hydrogel, as evidenced by a crossover point between storage modulus and loss modulus in rheological measurements. The resulting hydrogel exhibits a storage modulus between 100 and 1,000 Pascals at 37 degrees Celsius, sufficient to maintain structural integrity under physiological shear forces, and demonstrates complete reversibility upon cooling, with no hysteresis or permanent deformation. This thermogelation behavior is not observed in diblock analogues lacking the PNIPAM block, nor in statistical copolymers with the same overall composition, confirming that the ABC sequence and bottlebrush topology are essential for the formation of a functional hydrogel network.

The hydrogels formed by these triblock bottlebrush copolymers are suitable for intratumoral, subcutaneous, intra-articular, or intradermal administration. When injected into a tumor-bearing mouse model, the hydrogel remains localized at the injection site for at least 24 hours, whereas control diblock copolymers diffuse rapidly throughout the surrounding tissue. In vivo studies demonstrate that hydrogels loaded with paclitaxel and resiquimod significantly improve survival rates in immunocompetent mice bearing CT26 colon carcinoma tumors, with a majority of treated animals achieving complete tumor regression and long-term immunity upon re-challenge. This therapeutic benefit is accompanied by a marked reduction in systemic cytokine levels—including MCP-1, IL-6, IL-12, and TNF—compared to administration of the same drugs in free form or encapsulated in non-gelling diblock copolymers. Histological analysis reveals no evidence of chronic inflammation, fibrosis, or macrophage infiltration at the injection site, in stark contrast to the severe tissue damage and scar formation observed with free drug administration.

The invention further encompasses formulations comprising the triblock bottlebrush copolymer in lyophilized form, reconstitutable with sterile aqueous solutions, and kits containing the polymer, a lyophilization agent, a drug, and instructions for reconstitution and administration. The polymers are stable for extended periods under dry conditions and retain their self-assembly and thermogelation properties upon rehydration. The invention also includes methods of treatment comprising the administration of the hydrogel to a subject in need thereof, particularly for the treatment of cancer, chronic inflammatory diseases, or localized infections, wherein the hydrogel provides sustained release of one or more therapeutic agents directly at the site of pathology.

The invention further provides a method of preparing the triblock bottlebrush copolymer comprising: (a) synthesizing a first macromonomer comprising a norbornene group and a first polymer block; (b) initiating ring-opening metathesis polymerization of the first macromonomer with a ruthenium-based catalyst to form a first polymer block; (c) adding a second macromonomer comprising a norbornene group and a second polymer block to the reaction mixture to extend the polymer chain with a second block; (d) adding a third macromonomer comprising a norbornene group and a third polymer block to the reaction mixture to complete the triblock architecture; and (e) terminating the polymerization and isolating the triblock bottlebrush copolymer. The method further includes purification by dialysis, ultrafiltration, or precipitation, and optional lyophilization for storage.

The invention further provides a method of forming a hydrogel comprising: (a) dissolving the triblock bottlebrush copolymer in an organic solvent; (b) adding a therapeutic agent to the solution; (c) diluting the solution with an aqueous medium to induce micelle formation; (d) concentrating the micellar solution to a concentration of 5 to 20 weight percent; and (e) heating the solution to a temperature above 32 degrees Celsius to induce gelation. The method further includes the step of administering the resulting hydrogel to a subject via injection, implantation, or topical application.

The invention further provides a method of treating a disease comprising: (a) preparing a hydrogel as described above; (b) injecting the hydrogel into a tumor, an inflamed joint, a wound, or a mucosal surface; and (c) allowing the hydrogel to remain at the site of administration for a period of at least 24 hours, during which time the therapeutic agent is released in a sustained manner. The disease is selected from the group consisting of cancer, rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, psoriasis, inflammatory bowel disease, and localized bacterial or fungal infections.

The invention further provides a method of generating an adaptive immune response comprising: (a) administering the hydrogel to a subject, wherein the hydrogel contains a chemotherapeutic agent and a toll-like receptor agonist; (b) allowing the chemotherapeutic agent to induce immunogenic cell death and release of tumor-associated antigens; (c) allowing the toll-like receptor agonist to activate dendritic cells and other antigen-presenting cells in the local microenvironment; and (d) observing the subsequent expansion of tumor-specific CD8+ T cells in the systemic circulation and the rejection of re-challenged tumor cells, indicating the establishment of immunological memory.

The invention further provides a method of reducing systemic toxicity associated with the administration of a potent immunomodulatory agent comprising: (a) encapsulating the agent within the hydrophobic core of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer micelle; (b) forming a hydrogel at the site of administration; and (c) releasing the agent in a controlled manner over a period of days, thereby maintaining local concentrations above the therapeutic threshold while preventing systemic exposure above a toxic threshold.

The invention further provides a method of improving the therapeutic index of a combination therapy comprising a chemotherapeutic agent and an immunomodulator, wherein the improvement is quantified by an increase in the number of subjects achieving complete tumor regression, a reduction in systemic cytokine levels, a decrease in tissue fibrosis at the injection site, and an increase in the frequency of tumor-specific memory T cells, compared to administration of the same agents in free form or in non-gelling formulations.

The invention further provides a method of formulating a stable, lyophilized powder comprising the triblock bottlebrush copolymer and a cryoprotectant, wherein the powder is reconstituted with sterile water or saline immediately prior to injection, and wherein the reconstituted solution retains its ability to form micelles and subsequently gel upon heating to physiological temperature.

The invention further provides a method of manufacturing the triblock bottlebrush copolymer at scale, comprising continuous flow polymerization of the macromonomers using a ruthenium catalyst, followed by inline purification via membrane filtration and lyophilization, resulting in a product with consistent molecular weight, block composition, and thermogelation properties suitable for clinical use.

## Definitions

### Chemical Definitions

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “macromonomer” refers to a monomeric unit comprising a polymer chain terminally functionalized with a reactive group capable of participating in a polymerization reaction, wherein the polymer chain itself is not a monomer in the traditional sense but serves as a side chain in the resulting polymer architecture. In the context of this invention, macromonomers are norbornene-functionalized polymers, wherein the norbornene group is covalently attached to one terminus of a polymer chain, enabling ring-opening metathesis polymerization to proceed from that site, thereby incorporating the entire polymer chain as a side chain on a linear backbone.

The term “ring-opening metathesis polymerization” or “ROMP” refers to a catalytic chain-growth polymerization reaction in which a cyclic olefin, such as norbornene, undergoes cleavage of its double bond in the presence of a transition metal catalyst, typically a ruthenium-based carbene complex, to form a linear polymer with a backbone containing carbon-carbon double bonds. ROMP is distinguished by its tolerance to a wide range of functional groups, its living or quasi-living character under controlled conditions, and its ability to polymerize macromonomers without side reactions, making it ideal for the synthesis of sequence-controlled bottlebrush polymers.

The term “triblock bottlebrush copolymer” or “TBC” refers to a macromolecule comprising a linear backbone with three distinct blocks of side chains, each block derived from a different macromonomer, arranged in a sequential A-B-C order. Each block consists of multiple side chains grafted at high density along the backbone, with the side chains of the first block being hydrophobic, the side chains of the second block being hydrophilic, and the side chains of the third block being thermoresponsive. The backbone is formed by the polymerization of norbornene units, and the side chains are derived from the corresponding macromonomers used in the polymerization process.

The term “hydrophobic block” refers to a segment of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer composed of side chains that are insoluble or poorly soluble in aqueous media at ambient temperature and which drive the self-assembly of the polymer into micellar structures. In preferred embodiments, the hydrophobic block is derived from polylactic acid, poly(caprolactone), poly(butylene adipate), or poly(styrene), with polylactic acid being the most preferred due to its biodegradability, biocompatibility, and established use in medical devices.

The term “hydrophilic block” refers to a segment of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer composed of side chains that are highly soluble in aqueous media and serve to stabilize the micellar structure by forming a hydrated corona that prevents aggregation and reduces opsonization. In preferred embodiments, the hydrophilic block is derived from polyethylene glycol, poly(ethylene oxide), poly(vinyl alcohol), or poly(2-oxazoline), with polyethylene glycol being the most preferred due to its proven safety profile, low immunogenicity, and ability to extend circulation time in vivo.

The term “thermoresponsive block” refers to a segment of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer composed of side chains that undergo a reversible phase transition from hydrophilic to hydrophobic upon heating above a specific temperature, known as the lower critical solution temperature (LCST). This transition enables the formation of inter-micellar crosslinks and the subsequent gelation of concentrated solutions. In preferred embodiments, the thermoresponsive block is derived from poly(N-isopropylacrylamide), poly(N,N-diethylacrylamide), or poly(oligo(ethylene glycol) methyl ether methacrylate), with poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) being the most preferred due to its LCST near physiological temperature and its well-characterized behavior in biological environments.

The term “grafting density” refers to the number of side chains per unit length of the polymer backbone, typically expressed as the number of side chains per nanometer or per repeating unit of the backbone. In the context of this invention, grafting density is sufficiently high to prevent backbone entanglement and to promote the formation of discrete micelles rather than bulk gels at low concentrations, with preferred grafting densities ranging from 0.5 to 2.0 side chains per backbone carbon atom.

The term “lower critical solution temperature” or “LCST” refers to the temperature above which a polymer or polymer segment becomes insoluble in a given solvent, typically water, due to the disruption of hydrogen bonding and increased hydrophobic interactions. For poly(N-isopropylacrylamide), the LCST is approximately 32 to 34 degrees Celsius in aqueous media, which is critical for the thermogelation behavior of the invention.

The term “micelle” refers to a supramolecular assembly formed in aqueous solution by amphiphilic molecules, wherein hydrophobic segments aggregate to form a core and hydrophilic segments extend outward to form a stabilizing corona. In this invention, the micelles are formed by the self-assembly of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer, with the hydrophobic block forming the core, the hydrophilic block forming the corona, and the thermoresponsive block contributing to inter-micellar interactions upon heating.

The term “hydrogel” refers to a three-dimensional, crosslinked polymeric network capable of absorbing and retaining a large volume of water while maintaining structural integrity. In this invention, the hydrogel is formed by physical crosslinking of micelles via hydrophobic interactions between thermoresponsive blocks above the LCST, and does not involve covalent crosslinking agents, chemical initiators, or external crosslinkers.

The term “sustained release” refers to the controlled, prolonged release of a therapeutic agent from a delivery system over a period of days to weeks, as opposed to a rapid burst release occurring within minutes or hours. In this invention, sustained release is achieved through the slow diffusion of the drug through the hydrophobic core and the physical barrier formed by the gel network, with release kinetics characterized by linear or near-linear profiles over time.

The term “biodegradable” refers to the ability of a material to be broken down into non-toxic, metabolizable, or excretable components by enzymatic, hydrolytic, or physiological processes. In this invention, the polylactic acid block degrades via hydrolysis of ester linkages into lactic acid, which is metabolized through the Krebs cycle, and the polyethylene glycol and poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) blocks are cleared renally or via hepatic metabolism without accumulation.

The term “non-toxic” refers to the absence of measurable cytotoxicity, inflammatory response, or organ toxicity in vitro or in vivo at concentrations used for therapeutic delivery. In this invention, the triblock bottlebrush copolymers do not induce cell membrane rupture, as evidenced by negligible lactate dehydrogenase release from human endothelial cells, and do not cause systemic cytokine elevation beyond baseline levels when administered locally.

The term “therapeutic index” refers to the ratio between the dose of a therapeutic agent that produces a desired effect and the dose that produces toxicity. In this invention, the therapeutic index is improved by increasing the local concentration of the therapeutic agent at the site of action while minimizing systemic exposure, thereby enhancing efficacy and reducing adverse effects.

The term “immunogenic cell death” refers to a form of programmed cell death that results in the release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) such as calreticulin, ATP, and high-mobility group box 1 (HMGB1), which activate dendritic cells and initiate an adaptive immune response. In this invention, the chemotherapeutic agent encapsulated within the hydrogel induces immunogenic cell death in tumor cells, thereby promoting antigen presentation and T-cell priming.

The term “toll-like receptor agonist” refers to a molecule that binds to and activates a toll-like receptor, a class of pattern recognition receptors expressed on immune cells that initiate innate immune responses. In this invention, the toll-like receptor agonist is resiquimod, which activates TLR7 and TLR8, leading to the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and the maturation of dendritic cells.

The term “lyophilized” refers to a state in which a substance has been freeze-dried to remove water under vacuum, resulting in a stable, solid powder that can be reconstituted with an aqueous solvent without loss of function. In this invention, the triblock bottlebrush copolymer may be lyophilized with a cryoprotectant such as sucrose or mannitol to preserve its structure and self-assembly properties during storage.

The term “cryoprotectant” refers to a substance added to a solution prior to freezing to prevent damage to the molecular structure during lyophilization. In this invention, cryoprotectants such as sucrose, trehalose, or mannitol are used to maintain the conformational integrity of the polymer and prevent aggregation upon rehydration.

The term “polydispersity index” refers to the ratio of the weight-average molecular weight to the number-average molecular weight of a polymer sample, and is a measure of the breadth of the molecular weight distribution. In this invention, a polydispersity index below 1.3 is required to ensure uniformity in micelle size, drug loading, and gelation behavior.

The term “ruthenium-based catalyst” refers to a transition metal complex containing ruthenium that is capable of initiating and propagating ring-opening metathesis polymerization. In this invention, the catalyst is selected from the group consisting of Grubbs first-generation, second-generation, and third-generation catalysts, with the third-generation bispyridyl complex being the most preferred due to its high activity, stability, and tolerance to functional groups.

The term “norbornene-functionalized macromonomer” refers to a polymer chain terminated with a norbornene group, wherein the norbornene moiety is attached via a stable linkage such as an ester, amide, or imide bond. In this invention, the norbornene group is attached to the polymer chain through an imide linkage derived from N-hydroxysuccinimide ester chemistry, enabling high-yield coupling without side reactions.

The term “polyethylene glycol” or “PEG” refers to a polyether compound composed of repeating ethylene oxide units, with a molecular weight ranging from 1,000 to 20,000 daltons in the context of this invention. PEG is used as the hydrophilic block and is typically amine-terminated, with the amine group coupled to a norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimide ester to form the macromonomer.

The term “polylactic acid” or “PLA” refers to a biodegradable polyester derived from lactic acid, with molecular weights ranging from 2,000 to 10,000 daltons in the context of this invention. PLA is used as the hydrophobic block and is end-functionalized with a norbornene group via carboxylic acid activation and coupling to a norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimide ester.

The term “poly(N-isopropylacrylamide)” or “PNIPAM” refers to a thermoresponsive polymer composed of N-isopropylacrylamide monomers, with molecular weights ranging from 4,000 to 8,000 daltons in the context of this invention. PNIPAM is used as the thermoresponsive block and is typically amine-terminated, with the amine group coupled to a norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimide ester to form the macromonomer.

### Other Definitions

The term “subject” refers to a mammal, including but not limited to a human, mouse, rat, rabbit, dog, cat, or non-human primate, in need of therapeutic intervention. In preferred embodiments, the subject is a human patient suffering from a solid tumor, chronic inflammatory disease, or localized infection.

The term “therapeutic agent” refers to any molecule, compound, or biological entity that exerts a pharmacological, immunological, or biochemical effect in a subject, including but not limited to chemotherapeutics, immunomodulators, vaccines, cytokines, growth factors, antibiotics, antifungals, and nucleic acids. The term “intratumoral administration” refers to the direct injection of a formulation into a tumor mass, as opposed to systemic administration via intravenous, intramuscular, or oral routes. In this invention, intratumoral administration is the preferred route for delivering the hydrogel to treat solid tumors.

The term “subcutaneous administration” refers to the injection of a formulation into the layer of tissue located between the skin and the muscle, typically used for depot formation. In this invention, subcutaneous administration is used for localized delivery of vaccines or anti-inflammatory agents.

The term “intra-articular administration” refers to the injection of a formulation directly into a joint space, typically used for the treatment of arthritis. In this invention, the hydrogel may be administered intra-articularly to provide sustained release of anti-inflammatory agents.

The term “local drug delivery” refers to the targeted delivery of a therapeutic agent to a specific anatomical site, with the goal of maximizing local concentration while minimizing systemic exposure. In this invention, local drug delivery is achieved through the formation of a hydrogel that remains at the site of injection for extended periods.

The term “immune memory” refers to the ability of the immune system to recognize and rapidly respond to a previously encountered antigen, resulting in long-term protection against recurrence. The term “viscoelastic” refers to a material that exhibits both viscous and elastic characteristics when undergoing deformation. In this invention, the hydrogel is viscoelastic, with a storage modulus greater than the loss modulus above the gelation temperature, indicating solid-like behavior.

The term “reversible gelation” refers to the ability of a hydrogel to transition between a sol and a gel state in response to temperature changes, with no permanent alteration in structure or function upon cycling. The term “biocompatible” refers to the ability of a material to perform with an appropriate host response in a specific application, without eliciting a significant immune, inflammatory, or toxic reaction. ## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS

### Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers

The present invention provides a class of triblock bottlebrush copolymers characterized by a linear backbone with three distinct, sequence-controlled blocks of side chains arranged in an A-B-C configuration, wherein the A block is hydrophobic, the B block is hydrophilic, and the C block is thermoresponsive. These copolymers are synthesized via sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers, ensuring precise control over molecular weight, block length, and architecture. The resulting polymers exhibit a high degree of structural uniformity, with number-average molecular weights ranging from 50,000 to 500,000 g/mol and polydispersity indices below 1.3, as determined by size exclusion chromatography and nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy. The high grafting density of side chains along the backbone—typically between 0.7 and 1.8 side chains per backbone carbon atom—prevents chain entanglement and promotes the formation of discrete, spherical micelles in aqueous solution rather than bulk aggregates or phase-separated domains.

The hydrophobic A block is derived from polylactic acid, a biodegradable polyester formed by the ring-opening polymerization of lactide. In preferred embodiments, the PLA block has a molecular weight between 2,000 and 10,000 g/mol, with a lactide-to-glycolide ratio of 100:0 to 80:20, and is end-functionalized with an exo-norbornene group via activation of the terminal carboxylic acid with N-hydroxysuccinimide and subsequent coupling to a norbornene amine. The PLA block serves as the core-forming segment of the micelle, providing a hydrophobic environment capable of encapsulating lipophilic drugs such as paclitaxel, SN-38, resiquimod, gemcitabine, and dexamethasone. The hydrophobicity of the PLA block is sufficient to drive spontaneous micellization in aqueous media at concentrations as low as 0.1 mg/mL, with critical micelle concentrations below 1 µg/mL, as determined by pyrene fluorescence assays. The core diameter of the micelles, as measured by cryogenic transmission electron microscopy, ranges from 15 to 25 nm, with a packing density consistent with tightly packed PLA chains, as estimated using the known density of PLA (1.24 g/cm³).

The hydrophilic B block is derived from polyethylene glycol, a water-soluble polymer composed of repeating ethylene oxide units. In preferred embodiments, the PEG block has a molecular weight between 3,000 and 15,000 g/mol and is end-functionalized with an exo-norbornene group via reaction of an amine-terminated PEG with a norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimide ester. The PEG block serves as a stabilizing corona around the micellar core, preventing aggregation through steric repulsion and hydration. The length of the PEG block is optimized to balance colloidal stability with micelle packing efficiency; shorter PEG chains result in micelle fusion and increased polydispersity, while longer chains reduce drug loading capacity due to increased hydrophilic volume fraction. The PEG block also contributes to the biocompatibility of the system, as it is known to reduce opsonization, prolong circulation time, and minimize immune recognition. In vivo studies confirm that the PEG-containing micelles exhibit minimal uptake by the reticuloendothelial system, with no significant accumulation in the liver or spleen beyond background levels.

The thermoresponsive C block is derived from poly(N-isopropylacrylamide), a polymer that undergoes a reversible phase transition from hydrophilic to hydrophobic upon heating above its lower critical solution temperature, which is approximately 32 to 34 degrees Celsius in aqueous media. In preferred embodiments, the PNIPAM block has a molecular weight between 4,000 and 8,000 g/mol and is end-functionalized with an exo-norbornene group via coupling of an amine-terminated PNIPAM to a norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimide ester. Below the LCST, the PNIPAM side chains are hydrated and extended, contributing to the overall hydrodynamic diameter of the micelle without inducing inter-micellar interactions. Above the LCST, the PNIPAM blocks dehydrate and collapse, exposing hydrophobic isopropyl groups that interact with those of neighboring micelles, leading to physical crosslinking and the formation of a continuous, elastic hydrogel network. This transition is reversible, with no hysteresis observed over multiple heating and cooling cycles, and the gelation temperature is tunable by adjusting the molecular weight of the PNIPAM block or by incorporating co-monomers such as N,N-dimethylacrylamide or poly(ethylene glycol) methyl ether acrylate.

The ABC sequence is critical to the function of the invention. In contrast to ABA or CBC architectures, the ABC sequence prevents the formation of mechanically defective primary loops in the hydrogel network, as the hydrophobic A and C blocks are separated by the hydrophilic B block, thereby forcing crosslinks to occur between the outer thermoresponsive domains rather than within the same micelle. This topology results in a more uniform, interconnected network with higher elastic modulus and slower erosion kinetics. Triblock bottlebrush copolymers with an ABA sequence, in which the thermoresponsive block is flanked by hydrophobic blocks, exhibit premature gelation at lower temperatures and reduced drug loading due to the hydrophobic blocks occupying both core and shell regions. Similarly, CBC architectures, in which the hydrophobic block is located at the terminus, fail to form stable micelles due to insufficient hydrophobic driving force. Only the ABC sequence enables the formation of stable micelles at room temperature followed by thermally triggered gelation at physiological temperature.

The backbone of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer is formed by the polymerization of norbornene units via ring-opening metathesis polymerization using a ruthenium-based catalyst, preferably the third-generation Grubbs catalyst bearing a pyridyl ligand. This catalyst system enables living polymerization under mild conditions, with high conversion rates (>98%) and minimal side reactions, even in the presence of polar functional groups such as esters, amides, and ethers. The polymerization proceeds via a chain-growth mechanism, with each macromonomer added sequentially to ensure block fidelity. After each polymerization step, the reaction is monitored by size exclusion chromatography to confirm complete consumption of the preceding macromonomer before the next is introduced. The polymerization is terminated by addition of ethyl vinyl ether, which quenches the active ruthenium carbene species, and the resulting polymer is purified by dialysis against water using membranes with a 50 kDa molecular weight cutoff, followed by concentration via ultrafiltration and lyophilization.

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the invention are characterized by a combination of structural and functional properties that distinguish them from all previously reported polymeric systems. They are the first known bottlebrush polymers to exhibit both spontaneous micellization and thermogelation in a single architecture. They are the first to form hydrogels via physical crosslinking of micelles without the use of covalent crosslinkers, chemical initiators, or external stimuli such as pH or ionic strength. They are the first to achieve sustained release of both small molecule drugs and macromolecular proteins from a single platform, with encapsulation efficiencies exceeding 80% for a wide range of payloads. They are the first to demonstrate the ability to induce long-term immune memory following intratumoral administration of a chemotherapeutic and immunomodulator combination. And they are the first to do so without inducing systemic inflammation, fibrosis, or TLR tolerance.

The physical properties of the triblock bottlebrush copolymers are highly tunable. By varying the molecular weight of each block, the molar ratio of macromonomers, or the grafting density of side chains, the micelle size, gelation temperature, mechanical strength, and drug release kinetics can be precisely controlled. For example, increasing the length of the PLA block enhances drug loading capacity but increases micelle core size and may reduce colloidal stability. Increasing the length of the PEG block improves stability but reduces the volume fraction available for drug encapsulation. Increasing the length of the PNIPAM block lowers the gelation temperature and increases the modulus of the resulting hydrogel, but may also increase the risk of premature aggregation if the LCST is too close to room temperature. Optimal compositions have been identified for specific applications: for intratumoral delivery of paclitaxel and resiquimod, a PLA:PEG:PNIPAM molar ratio of 1:1:1 with molecular weights of 5,000:8,000:6,000 g/mol yields micelles with a hydrodynamic diameter of 110 nm, a gelation temperature of 36 °C, a storage modulus of 500 Pa at 37 °C, and a drug release half-life of 4.2 days.

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers are stable in aqueous solution for at least 72 hours at 4 °C and for at least 24 hours at 37 °C, with no evidence of degradation, aggregation, or drug leakage. They are also stable in serum-containing media, with no significant protein adsorption or opsonization observed after 24 hours of incubation in 50% fetal bovine serum. The polymers are non-toxic to human endothelial cells, as demonstrated by negligible lactate dehydrogenase release in cell culture assays, and do not induce complement activation or hemolysis in red blood cell assays. They are also non-immunogenic, with no detectable anti-polymer antibodies produced in mice following repeated administration.

The unique combination of properties possessed by these triblock bottlebrush copolymers renders them uniquely suited for applications in localized drug delivery, particularly in oncology, immunology, and regenerative medicine. Their ability to form micelles and gels in response to physiological cues, their capacity for high drug loading and sustained release, their biocompatibility, and their capacity to induce adaptive immunity represent a significant advancement over existing technologies. These materials are not merely carriers for drugs, but intelligent, programmable scaffolds that respond to their environment and orchestrate therapeutic outcomes in a manner analogous to biological systems.

### Methods for Preparing Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers

The preparation of the triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the invention involves a sequential, living ring-opening metathesis polymerization process that ensures precise control over block sequence, molecular weight, and architecture. The synthesis begins with the preparation of three distinct norbornene-functionalized macromonomers: a polylactic acid-derived macromonomer (Nb-PLA), a polyethylene glycol-derived macromonomer (Nb-PEG), and a poly(N-isopropylacrylamide)-derived macromonomer (Nb-PNIPAM). Each macromonomer is synthesized independently using established coupling chemistry, ensuring high purity and quantitative yield. The Nb-PLA macromonomer is prepared by activating the terminal carboxylic acid of a pre-synthesized PLA oligomer with N-hydroxysuccinimide in the presence of a carbodiimide coupling agent such as 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide, followed by reaction with a norbornene amine to form an amide linkage. The Nb-PEG macromonomer is prepared by reacting an amine-terminated PEG with a norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimide ester in dimethylformamide at room temperature for 24 hours, with excess reagent removed by dialysis. The Nb-PNIPAM macromonomer is prepared similarly, using an amine-terminated PNIPAM polymer with a molecular weight of approximately 5.5 kDa, coupled to the same norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimide ester under mild conditions to preserve the thermoresponsive properties of the PNIPAM block.

Once the macromonomers are purified and characterized by nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy and size exclusion chromatography, the sequential polymerization is initiated. The polymerization is carried out in an inert atmosphere under anhydrous conditions using a ruthenium-based catalyst, preferably the third-generation Grubbs catalyst bearing a pyridyl ligand, which is known for its high activity, functional group tolerance, and ability to sustain living polymerization. The polymerization is conducted in anhydrous dichloromethane or toluene at a concentration of 0.1 to 0.5 M, with the catalyst added in a molar ratio of 1:100 relative to the first macromonomer. The first macromonomer, Nb-PLA, is introduced into the reaction vessel, and polymerization is allowed to proceed for 2 to 4 hours at room temperature, until complete consumption of the macromonomer is confirmed by size exclusion chromatography. The reaction is then cooled to 0 °C, and the second macromonomer, Nb-PEG, is added in a stoichiometric excess to ensure complete chain extension. Polymerization continues for an additional 2 to 4 hours, during which time the molecular weight increases in a linear fashion, as confirmed by SEC. The third macromonomer, Nb-PNIPAM, is then added, and polymerization proceeds for a final 2 to 4 hours, resulting in the formation of the complete ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymer.

The polymerization is terminated by the addition of ethyl vinyl ether, which reacts with the active ruthenium carbene species to form a stable, inactive end group. The reaction mixture is then concentrated under reduced pressure and transferred to a dialysis bag with a molecular weight cutoff of 50 kDa. Dialysis is performed against distilled water for 48 hours, with three changes of water to remove unreacted macromonomers, catalyst residues, and low molecular weight impurities. The dialyzed solution is then concentrated using a 100 kDa molecular weight cutoff centrifugal filter, and the polymer is lyophilized to obtain a white, amorphous powder. The yield of the final product is typically greater than 85%, with no detectable residual macromonomer or homopolymer impurities by SEC or NMR.

The molecular weight and block composition of the resulting triblock bottlebrush copolymer are confirmed by a combination of analytical techniques. Size exclusion chromatography, calibrated with polystyrene standards, reveals a unimodal peak with a polydispersity index below 1.3, indicating a highly uniform product. Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, performed in deuterated chloroform, allows for the quantification of the relative integration of protons from each block, enabling precise determination of the molar ratio of PLA:PEG:PNIPAM. The number-average molecular weight is calculated by assuming complete conversion of the first macromonomer and comparing the NMR signal intensities of the PEG and PNIPAM segments to those of the PLA segment. Differential scanning calorimetry confirms the presence of a single, sharp glass transition temperature for the PLA block and a single, reversible phase transition for the PNIPAM block, with no evidence of phase separation or block mixing. Thermogravimetric analysis demonstrates that the polymer is stable up to 250 °C, with decomposition occurring in distinct stages corresponding to the degradation of each block.

The synthesis method is scalable and amenable to continuous flow processing. In a continuous flow reactor, the three macromonomers are introduced sequentially into a heated tubular reactor containing a fixed-bed catalyst, with residence times controlled to ensure complete polymerization of each block. The reaction stream is then passed through a quenching unit containing ethyl vinyl ether, followed by inline membrane filtration to remove catalyst residues, and finally collected in a lyophilization chamber. This continuous process enables the production of kilograms of polymer per day with consistent molecular weight, block composition, and purity, suitable for clinical manufacturing under current good manufacturing practices.

The method further includes optional modifications to tailor the properties of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer. For example, the PLA block may be synthesized from a mixture of D- and L-lactide to alter crystallinity and degradation rate. The PEG block may be replaced with poly(ethylene oxide) or poly(2-oxazoline) to modify hydrophilicity and biocompatibility. The PNIPAM block may be copolymerized with small amounts of N,N-dimethylacrylamide or poly(ethylene glycol) methyl ether acrylate to fine-tune the LCST. The backbone may be functionalized with trace amounts of a reactive group, such as a maleimide or azide, to allow for post-polymerization conjugation of targeting ligands, fluorescent dyes, or cleavable linkers. The polymer may also be blended with other biodegradable polymers, such as poly(caprolactone) or poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid), to modulate degradation kinetics or mechanical properties.

The triblock bottlebrush copolymer may also be formulated into a lyophilized powder for long-term storage. Lyophilization is performed in the presence of a cryoprotectant such as sucrose, trehalose, or mannitol, at a weight ratio of 1:1 to 1:5 (polymer:cryoprotectant), to prevent aggregation and preserve the polymer’s self-assembly properties upon rehydration. The lyophilized powder is stable for at least six months at 4 °C and retains its ability to form micelles and gels upon reconstitution with sterile water or saline. The reconstituted solution exhibits identical micelle size, gelation temperature, and drug release kinetics as freshly synthesized polymer, confirming the robustness of the formulation.

The method of preparation described herein is superior to all previously reported methods for synthesizing triblock bottlebrush copolymers. Unlike methods based on atom transfer radical polymerization or reversible addition-fragmentation chain transfer polymerization, which suffer from low functional group tolerance and poor block fidelity, the ROMP-based approach enables the incorporation of sensitive functional groups such as esters, amides, and tertiary amines without side reactions. Unlike methods that rely on post-polymerization conjugation or click chemistry, which introduce heterogeneity and require multiple purification steps, the sequential ROMP approach ensures homogeneity and single-step synthesis. And unlike methods that produce statistical copolymers or random architectures, the living nature of the ROMP process ensures precise control over block sequence and molecular weight, enabling the creation of materials with predictable, reproducible, and clinically relevant properties.

### Particles and Hydrogels

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the invention spontaneously self-assemble in aqueous media to form spherical nanoparticles with a well-defined core-shell morphology, which further transition into physically crosslinked hydrogels upon exposure to physiological temperatures. The formation of these particles and hydrogels is not merely a consequence of amphiphilicity, but a direct result of the precise ABC block sequence and bottlebrush architecture, which together dictate the thermodynamic and kinetic pathways of self-assembly. At concentrations below 1 mg/mL, the copolymers form discrete micelles with a hydrodynamic diameter ranging from 90 to 120 nanometers, as measured by dynamic light scattering, and a core diameter of approximately 20 nanometers, as confirmed by cryogenic transmission electron microscopy. The micelles are stable in a variety of biological media, including phosphate-buffered saline, cell culture medium containing serum, and whole blood, with no evidence of aggregation, fusion, or drug leakage over a period of 72 hours.

The micelle formation is driven by the hydrophobic PLA block, which collapses into a dense core, while the hydrophilic PEG block extends outward to form a hydrated corona that prevents inter-particle interactions. The thermoresponsive PNIPAM block, which remains hydrated and extended below its lower critical solution temperature, contributes to the overall size of the micelle but does not participate in core formation. The high grafting density of side chains along the backbone ensures that each micelle is composed of a discrete number of polymer chains—approximately 100 molecules per micelle—resulting in exceptional uniformity in size and composition. This level of monodispersity is critical for predictable drug loading, biodistribution, and clearance, and is not achievable with conventional linear block copolymers or statistical copolymers, which exhibit broad size distributions and heterogeneous drug partitioning.

Upon increasing the concentration of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer to 5 weight percent or greater, and raising the temperature above 32 degrees Celsius, the system undergoes a reversible phase transition from a free-flowing solution to a viscoelastic hydrogel. This transition is not due to the formation of covalent crosslinks, nor is it induced by changes in pH, ionic strength, or enzymatic activity. Instead, it is driven by the dehydration and collapse of the PNIPAM blocks, which exposes hydrophobic isopropyl groups that interact with those of neighboring micelles, creating physical crosslinks between micellar units. These crosslinks are transient and reversible, allowing the hydrogel to flow under shear stress and recover its structure upon removal of the stress, a hallmark of physical networks. Rheological measurements confirm that the storage modulus (G′) exceeds the loss modulus (G′′) above 36 degrees Celsius, indicating the formation of a solid-like network, while below this temperature, the material behaves as a viscous liquid.

The hydrogel exhibits mechanical properties suitable for injection and retention at the site of administration. The storage modulus ranges from 100 to 1,000 Pascals at 37 degrees Celsius, depending on polymer concentration and block lengths, and is sufficient to resist displacement by interstitial fluid flow or tissue movement. The hydrogel is also elastic, with a recovery rate of greater than 90% after 10 cycles of compression and relaxation, and shows no evidence of creep or permanent deformation over 24 hours. The gelation process is rapid, occurring within seconds to minutes upon reaching the transition temperature, and is fully reversible upon cooling, with no hysteresis observed over multiple thermal cycles. This reversibility is critical for applications requiring removal or degradation of the hydrogel, as it allows for controlled dissolution without the need for enzymatic or chemical triggers.

The hydrogel is not merely a passive scaffold, but an active delivery system that modulates the release of encapsulated therapeutics. The drug is loaded into the hydrophobic PLA core during micelle formation, prior to gelation, ensuring high encapsulation efficiency and homogeneous distribution. Once the hydrogel is formed, the drug is released through a combination of diffusion through the hydrophobic core and erosion of the PLA block, resulting in a linear, sustained release profile with no burst release. The release rate is tunable by altering the molecular weight of the PLA block, the drug’s lipophilicity, or the crosslink density of the hydrogel. For example, increasing the PLA block length from 5,000 to 10,000 g/mol increases the drug loading capacity from 15% to 25% by weight, while simultaneously extending the release half-life from 3 to 6 days. Similarly, increasing the PNIPAM block length from 4,000 to 8,000 g/mol lowers the gelation temperature from 36 to 34 degrees Celsius and increases the storage modulus from 300 to 800 Pascals, slowing drug diffusion and prolonging release.

The hydrogel is also capable of encapsulating and releasing macromolecular payloads, including proteins such as ovalbumin, with encapsulation efficiencies exceeding 70%. This is a significant advancement over existing hydrogel systems, which typically fail to retain large molecules due to their porous structure or rapid degradation. The dense, non-porous nature of the PLA core, combined with the physical barrier formed by the PNIPAM crosslinks, prevents the escape of proteins while allowing slow diffusion of water and small molecules. The release of ovalbumin follows a near-linear profile over five days, with no evidence of denaturation or aggregation, as confirmed by SDS-PAGE and fluorescence spectroscopy.

The hydrogel is stable in vivo for at least 24 hours at the site of injection, as demonstrated by fluorescence imaging of a cyanine 7.5-labeled triblock bottlebrush copolymer injected subcutaneously into mice. In contrast, a control diblock copolymer lacking the PNIPAM block diffused rapidly from the injection site within hours, indicating that the thermogelation property is essential for local retention. The hydrogel remains intact at the injection site even under conditions of high interstitial pressure, such as those found in solid tumors, and does not migrate to lymph nodes or distant organs. This localized retention enables sustained exposure of the therapeutic agent to the target tissue while minimizing systemic exposure.

The hydrogel is also non-adherent to surrounding tissues, allowing for easy removal if necessary, and does not induce fibrosis, granuloma formation, or foreign body response. Histological analysis of tissue surrounding the injection site reveals no signs of chronic inflammation, macrophage infiltration, or collagen deposition, in stark contrast to the severe scarring and immune cell accumulation observed with free drug administration. The hydrogel is completely biodegradable, with the PLA block hydrolyzing into lactic acid over a period of weeks, and the PEG and PNIPAM blocks being cleared renally or metabolized by the liver. No polymer residues are detected in tissues or organs after 30 days, confirming complete clearance.

The particles and hydrogels formed by the triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the invention represent a novel class of materials that combine the advantages of micellar drug delivery with the mechanical stability and localized retention of physical hydrogels. They are the first system to achieve this combination without the use of covalent crosslinkers, chemical initiators, or external triggers. They are the first to do so with a single, sequence-controlled polymer architecture. And they are the first to demonstrate that such a system can be used to deliver a combination of chemotherapeutic and immunomodulatory agents in a manner that enhances efficacy, reduces toxicity, and induces long-term immunity. The particles and hydrogels are not merely delivery vehicles, but intelligent, responsive, and programmable platforms that adapt to their environment and orchestrate therapeutic outcomes in a manner never before achieved.

### Formulations, Kits, and Administration

The invention encompasses a variety of formulations, kits, and administration methods designed to facilitate the clinical translation and practical use of the triblock bottlebrush copolymer hydrogels. The formulations are designed to ensure stability, sterility, reproducibility, and ease of use in both preclinical and clinical settings. The primary formulation is a lyophilized powder comprising the triblock bottlebrush copolymer and a cryoprotectant, such as sucrose, trehalose, or mannitol, at a weight ratio of 1:1 to 1:5. The lyophilized powder is stable for at least six months when stored at 4 °C or below, and retains its ability to self-assemble into micelles and form hydrogels upon reconstitution with sterile water, saline, or buffered solutions. The lyophilization process is optimized to prevent aggregation, preserve molecular integrity, and ensure complete rehydration within seconds of addition of the aqueous medium. The reconstituted solution is clear, colorless, and free of particulates, with micelle size, drug loading, and gelation temperature identical to those of freshly synthesized polymer.

The formulations may be prepared with or without therapeutic agents. In one embodiment, the hydrogel is formulated as a blank system, containing no drug, for use as a control or as a scaffold for subsequent drug loading. In another embodiment, the hydrogel is pre-loaded with one or more therapeutic agents, including chemotherapeutics such as paclitaxel, SN-38, gemcitabine, or dexamethasone; immunomodulators such as resiquimod, imiquimod, or CpG oligonucleotides; proteins such as ovalbumin, interferon-alpha, or interleukin-2; or nucleic acids such as siRNA or mRNA. The drug is incorporated into the formulation during the micelle formation step, prior to lyophilization, by dissolving the drug and the polymer together in an organic solvent such as dimethyl sulfoxide, followed by slow dilution with an aqueous medium to induce micellization. The solution is then concentrated to a polymer concentration of 5 to 20 weight percent and lyophilized. The resulting lyophilized powder contains the drug uniformly distributed within the micellar core, with encapsulation efficiencies exceeding 80% for small molecules and 70% for proteins.

The formulations may also be prepared as ready-to-use solutions for immediate administration. In this embodiment, the triblock bottlebrush copolymer and drug are dissolved in a sterile, isotonic aqueous solution at a concentration of 10 weight percent, and stored at 4 °C until use. The solution remains in a liquid state at 4 °C and undergoes gelation upon warming to 37 °C, either in vivo following injection or in vitro prior to implantation. The solution is free of preservatives, stabilizers, or surfactants, and is suitable for parenteral administration.

## EXAMPLES

### Thermoresponsive ABC Bottlebrush Copolymer Amphiphiles

The synthesis and characterization of the thermoresponsive ABC bottlebrush copolymer amphiphiles were performed to establish the structural and functional properties of the invention. The triblock copolymer was prepared by sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization of norbornene-functionalized polylactic acid (Nb-PLA), polyethylene glycol (Nb-PEG), and poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (Nb-PNIPAM) macromonomers, using the third-generation Grubbs catalyst. The polymerization was carried out in anhydrous dichloromethane under an inert atmosphere, with each macromonomer added sequentially to ensure block fidelity. The reaction was monitored by size exclusion chromatography after each step, confirming complete consumption of the preceding macromonomer before the next was introduced. Upon completion, the polymerization was quenched with ethyl vinyl ether, and the resulting polymer was purified by dialysis against water using a 50 kDa molecular weight cutoff membrane, followed by concentration via ultrafiltration and lyophilization.

The molecular weight of the final ABC triblock copolymer was determined to be 210,000 g/mol by size exclusion chromatography, with a polydispersity index of 1.22, indicating a highly uniform product. Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy confirmed the molar ratio of PLA:PEG:PNIPAM to be 1.0:1.1:0.9, consistent with the feed ratio of macromonomers. Differential scanning calorimetry revealed a glass transition temperature of 58 °C for the PLA block and a reversible phase transition at 33.5 °C for the PNIPAM block, confirming the presence of both hydrophobic and thermoresponsive domains. Thermogravimetric analysis demonstrated that the polymer remained stable up to 250 °C, with distinct decomposition steps corresponding to each block.

The self-assembly behavior of the ABC copolymer was evaluated in aqueous solution at a concentration of 1 mg/mL. Dynamic light scattering revealed a hydrodynamic diameter of 113 nm with a polydispersity index of 0.05, indicating a highly monodisperse population of micelles. Cryogenic transmission electron microscopy confirmed the spherical morphology of the micelles, with a core diameter of 20 ± 3 nm and a well-defined corona. The micelles remained stable in phosphate-buffered saline, cell culture medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum, and whole mouse serum for at least 72 hours, with no evidence of aggregation or fusion.

The thermoresponsive behavior of the micelles was assessed by variable-temperature dynamic light scattering. Below 32 °C, the micelle size remained constant, but upon heating above 32 °C, the hydrodynamic diameter increased gradually, reaching a maximum of 145 nm at 38 °C, consistent with the collapse of the PNIPAM blocks and the formation of inter-micellar crosslinks. Upon cooling back to 25 °C, the micelle size returned to its original value, demonstrating complete reversibility. Small angle X-ray scattering confirmed these observations, showing a shift in the structure factor peaks to higher q values upon heating, indicative of decreased inter-micelle spacing due to PNIPAM dehydration and crosslinking.

To evaluate the ability of the micelles to form a hydrogel, a 10 wt% aqueous solution of the ABC copolymer was prepared and subjected to temperature sweep rheology. At 25 °C, the solution exhibited a loss modulus greater than the storage modulus, characteristic of a viscous liquid. As the temperature was increased, both moduli increased, but the storage modulus rose more rapidly, crossing the loss modulus at 36 °C, indicative of gelation. Above 37 °C, the storage modulus plateaued at approximately 500 Pa, while the loss modulus remained below 200 Pa, confirming the formation of a stable, elastic hydrogel. The hydrogel remained intact for over 24 hours at 37 °C and could be reversibly liquefied by cooling to 4 °C and re-gelled by reheating, with no loss of mechanical properties over five cycles.

The hydrogel was further tested for its ability to retain a fluorescent label in vivo. A cyanine 7.5-labeled version of the ABC copolymer was injected subcutaneously into the hind flank of healthy balb/c mice. Fluorescence imaging at 0 and 24 hours revealed that the labeled hydrogel remained localized at the injection site, with no detectable diffusion into surrounding tissue. In contrast, a control diblock copolymer lacking the PNIPAM block diffused rapidly from the injection site within 6 hours, confirming that the thermogelation property is essential for local retention.

These results demonstrate that the ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymer amphiphile is capable of forming stable, monodisperse micelles in aqueous media, undergoing reversible thermogelation at physiological temperature, and remaining localized at the site of injection. The combination of these properties renders the material uniquely suited for sustained, localized drug delivery.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-N-Hydroxysuccinimidyl (NHS)-Ester

The norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl ester was synthesized by reacting norbornene-2-carboxylic acid with N-hydroxysuccinimide in the presence of 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide hydrochloride in anhydrous dichloromethane. The reaction was stirred at room temperature for 24 hours under argon, after which the solvent was removed under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by flash chromatography on silica gel using a gradient of ethyl acetate in hexane (0 to 30%). The product was obtained as a white solid in 89% yield. The structure was confirmed by ¹H NMR and mass spectrometry. The norbornene-NHS ester was stored at −20 °C under desiccating conditions and used within one week of synthesis.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PEG Macromonomer (Nor-PEG)

A 5.5 kDa amine-terminated polyethylene glycol was dissolved in anhydrous dimethylformamide at a concentration of 10 mg/mL. The norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl ester was added in a 1.2-fold molar excess relative to the amine groups, and the reaction was stirred at room temperature for 24 hours under argon. The reaction mixture was then dialyzed against distilled water using a 10 kDa molecular weight cutoff membrane for 48 hours, with three changes of water. The resulting solution was concentrated by ultrafiltration and lyophilized to yield a white, amorphous powder. The product was characterized by ¹H NMR, which confirmed the disappearance of the amine proton signal and the appearance of the norbornene vinyl proton signals. The molecular weight was confirmed by size exclusion chromatography, and the yield was 92%.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PNIPAM Macromonomer (Nor-PNIPAM)

A 5.5 kDa amine-terminated poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) was dissolved in anhydrous dimethylformamide at a concentration of 10 mg/mL. The norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl ester was added in a 1.2-fold molar excess relative to the amine groups, and the reaction was stirred at room temperature for 24 hours under argon. The reaction mixture was then dialyzed against distilled water using a 10 kDa molecular weight cutoff membrane for 48 hours, with three changes of water. The resulting solution was concentrated by ultrafiltration and lyophilized to yield a white, amorphous powder. The product was characterized by ¹H NMR, which confirmed the disappearance of the amine proton signal and the appearance of the norbornene vinyl proton signals. The molecular weight was confirmed by size exclusion chromatography, and the yield was 88%. The lower critical solution temperature of the PNIPAM block was measured by dynamic light scattering and found to be 33.2 °C.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-Propanol

Norbornene-propanol was synthesized as a model compound for end-group analysis. Norbornene-2-carboxylic acid was activated with N-hydroxysuccinimide and 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide hydrochloride in dichloromethane, and the resulting NHS ester was reacted with 1-propanol in the presence of triethylamine. The reaction was stirred for 12 hours, after which the solvent was removed under reduced pressure. The product was purified by flash chromatography and characterized by ¹H NMR and mass spectrometry. The yield was 85%.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PLA Macromonomer (Nor-PLA)

Polylactic acid with a molecular weight of 5 kDa was synthesized by ring-opening polymerization of L-lactide using stannous octoate as a catalyst. The resulting PLA was end-functionalized with a carboxylic acid group by terminating the polymerization with water. The carboxylic acid was then activated with N-hydroxysuccinimide and 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide hydrochloride in dichloromethane, and the resulting NHS ester was reacted with norbornene-2-amine. The reaction was stirred for 24 hours, after which the solvent was removed under reduced pressure. The product was purified by precipitation into cold diethyl ether and dried under vacuum. The structure was confirmed by ¹H NMR and size exclusion chromatography, and the yield was 82%.

### Ring-Opening Metathesis (ROMP) Polymerization

The triblock bottlebrush copolymer was synthesized by sequential ROMP of the three macromonomers. In a Schlenk flask under argon, the third-generation Grubbs catalyst (1.5 mol% relative to the first macromonomer) was added to anhydrous dichloromethane. The norbornene-PLA macromonomer was added, and the reaction was stirred at room temperature for 3 hours. Size exclusion chromatography confirmed complete consumption of the macromonomer. The norbornene-PEG macromonomer was then added, and the reaction was stirred for an additional 3 hours. Size exclusion chromatography again confirmed complete consumption. The norbornene-PNIPAM macromonomer was then added, and the reaction was stirred for 3 hours. The polymerization was quenched by the addition of ethyl vinyl ether (5 equivalents), and the solvent was removed under reduced pressure. The crude polymer was purified by dialysis against distilled water using a 50 kDa molecular weight cutoff membrane for 48 hours, with three changes of water. The solution was concentrated by ultrafiltration and lyophilized to yield a white, amorphous powder. The molecular weight was determined by SEC and NMR, and the block composition was confirmed by ¹H NMR integration. The yield was 87%.
