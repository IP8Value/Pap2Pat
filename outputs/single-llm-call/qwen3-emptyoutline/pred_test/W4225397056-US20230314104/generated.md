## BACKGROUND

The field of missile guidance has long been dominated by proportional navigation (PN) and its variants, which rely on the principle that the rate of change of the line-of-sight (LOS) vector between a missile and a target must be counteracted by a proportional lateral acceleration command to achieve interception. This approach, while mathematically elegant and computationally efficient, assumes that the target maintains a constant velocity or exhibits only predictable motion patterns. In real-world scenarios, however, maneuvering targets—particularly those equipped with high-acceleration capabilities such as supersonic cruise missiles or agile fighter-launched interceptors—can execute abrupt, non-linear, and time-varying accelerations that significantly degrade the performance of conventional PN systems. The miss distance under such conditions can increase substantially, often exceeding acceptable thresholds for mission success, particularly when the target’s acceleration capability approaches or even matches that of the missile. To mitigate this limitation, researchers have proposed augmented proportional navigation (APN) laws, which incorporate an estimate of the target’s acceleration into the guidance command. These methods, while theoretically superior for step-acceleration maneuvers, require accurate and continuous estimation of the target’s motion dynamics, a task complicated by sensor noise, time delays, and unmodeled disturbances such as atmospheric turbulence and seeker refraction effects. Moreover, estimation-based approaches are inherently vulnerable to divergence when confronted with complex, high-frequency, or unpredictable target maneuvers, leading to unstable guidance responses and, in some cases, catastrophic increases in miss distance.

Alternative approaches have been explored to circumvent the need for explicit target acceleration estimation. One such method involves the use of sliding mode control to introduce a bias term based solely on the sign of the LOS rotation rate, resulting in a switched bias proportional navigation (SBPN) law that approximates the acceleration profile of APN without requiring an internal state estimator. While SBPN demonstrates improved robustness compared to standard PN, its performance remains constrained by the binary nature of the switching logic, which cannot adapt to the magnitude or frequency of target maneuvers. Other geometric guidance techniques have been proposed, parameterizing the missile trajectory within the Frenet-Serret reference frame; however, these methods typically require prior knowledge of the target’s acceleration vector or are limited to low-speed scenarios, rendering them inapplicable to high-performance air-to-air engagements. Furthermore, many of these approaches are sensitive to sensor imperfections, particularly those arising from radome refraction, which introduces a parasitic attitude loop by distorting the apparent direction of the target relative to the missile’s velocity vector. This distortion, often modeled as a look-angle-dependent angular offset, can be misinterpreted by the guidance system as genuine target motion, causing erroneous acceleration commands and degraded interception performance. Existing guidance laws have largely failed to account for this coupling between sensor physics and control logic, instead treating sensor noise as an external disturbance to be filtered rather than an intrinsic component of the decision-making process.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for designing adaptive guidance systems that learn direct mappings from sensor observations to actuation commands without relying on explicit physical models or state estimators. Recent applications of RL in aerospace domains—including planetary landing, exoatmospheric interception, and hypersonic vehicle control—have demonstrated the ability of deep neural networks to synthesize complex, non-linear control policies that outperform traditional model-based approaches in dynamic, uncertain environments. However, prior RL-based guidance systems have generally focused on optimizing the entire trajectory from launch to intercept, often at the expense of interpretability, computational efficiency, and compatibility with existing guidance architectures. Furthermore, most RL implementations have been trained on fixed environmental conditions, limiting their ability to generalize across variations in target dynamics, aerodynamic characteristics, sensor noise profiles, or engagement geometries. This lack of adaptability is particularly problematic in operational contexts where the missile must engage a diverse population of threats with unknown or rapidly evolving maneuvering behaviors. Meta-reinforcement learning (meta-RL), which trains an agent to rapidly adapt to new environments using only a few interactions, offers a promising pathway to overcome these limitations by embedding within the policy a latent representation of environmental variability. Yet, no prior work has applied meta-RL to the specific problem of dynamically shaping the line-of-sight vector input to a classical guidance law such as proportional navigation, nor has any method been developed that simultaneously leverages recurrent neural network architectures to encode temporal dependencies in sensor history while maintaining compatibility with existing hardware and software systems.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by introducing a novel guidance architecture that decouples the problem of target maneuver response from explicit state estimation by instead learning a policy that directly modulates the orientation of the line-of-sight vector as perceived by the guidance system. Rather than attempting to estimate the target’s acceleration or compensate for sensor distortions through post-processing filters, the invention employs a deep recurrent neural network optimized via meta-reinforcement learning to generate a time-varying Euler 321 attitude parameterization that rotates the measured LOS vector in real time. This rotated LOS vector, referred to as the shaped line-of-sight, is then fed directly into a standard proportional navigation law, effectively transforming the guidance system into a closed-loop adaptive controller that implicitly learns to counteract target maneuvers and sensor artifacts through the curvature of the LOS trajectory. This approach fundamentally redefines the role of the guidance law: instead of reacting to estimated target dynamics, the system manipulates the very perception of those dynamics to achieve superior interception performance. The invention further ensures compatibility with existing missile systems by preserving the structure of the underlying PN algorithm, requiring only a modification to the input signal rather than a complete redesign of the guidance architecture. Crucially, the method does not require any prior knowledge of target behavior, does not rely on acceleration estimation, and is robust to a wide range of environmental uncertainties—including radome refraction, aerodynamic drag variations, sensor noise, and dynamic pressure effects—while simultaneously reducing the total control effort required to achieve interception. This combination of performance, adaptability, and architectural simplicity represents a significant departure from conventional guidance paradigms and provides a scalable foundation for next-generation autonomous intercept systems.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a guidance system for intercepting maneuvering targets that dynamically shapes the line-of-sight vector input to a proportional navigation law using a deep recurrent neural network optimized via meta-reinforcement learning. The system comprises a line-of-sight curvature policy that maps a sequence of sensor observations—including relative position, velocity, and angular rate measurements—to a time-varying Euler 321 attitude parameterization, which is then used to rotate the measured line-of-sight vector prior to its application in a proportional navigation guidance law. The curvature policy is implemented as a deep neural network containing recurrent layers that encode temporal dependencies in the observation history, enabling the policy to infer the nature of the target’s maneuvering behavior without requiring explicit estimation of target acceleration. The policy is optimized using a meta-reinforcement learning framework that trains the network across an ensemble of simulated engagement scenarios, each varying in target acceleration profile, aerodynamic drag characteristics, radome refraction parameters, initial engagement geometry, and missile-to-target acceleration capability ratios. The optimization objective minimizes terminal miss distance while simultaneously penalizing excessive control effort, thereby inducing a policy that achieves high interception accuracy with minimal lateral acceleration commands. The shaped line-of-sight vector, generated by applying the computed attitude rotation to the raw line-of-sight vector, is provided as input to a standard proportional navigation law, which computes the required lateral acceleration command perpendicular to the missile’s velocity vector. The resulting guidance law, referred to as PN-LOSC, retains the computational simplicity and hardware compatibility of conventional proportional navigation while achieving superior performance against highly maneuvering targets whose acceleration capability approaches that of the missile. The invention does not require any estimation of target acceleration, nor does it rely on external models of target dynamics, making it inherently robust to model mismatch, sensor noise, and unmodeled disturbances such as radome refraction. Experimental results demonstrate that the PN-LOSC guidance law achieves significantly lower miss distances than both conventional proportional navigation and augmented proportional navigation across a wide range of target maneuvers—including bang-bang, weave, and jinking motions—while requiring up to 40% less control effort. The performance advantage is particularly pronounced under conditions of high sensor distortion and variable aerodynamic loading, where the policy learns to attenuate spurious LOS perturbations and anticipate target maneuvers through the curvature of the shaped line-of-sight trajectory. The invention is applicable to any guidance law that operates on the line-of-sight vector, and may be extended to incorporate additional constraints such as impact angle, look angle limits, thermal loading, or structural load limits by modifying the reward function during optimization. The system is fully compatible with existing strap-down seeker architectures and flight control systems, requiring only a software update to the guidance processor to implement the curvature policy, without modification to hardware, sensor interfaces, or communication protocols.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention disclosed herein is a guidance system for intercepting highly maneuvering targets that employs a deep recurrent neural network to dynamically curve the line-of-sight vector input to a proportional navigation guidance law, thereby achieving superior interception performance without requiring explicit estimation of target acceleration. The system operates in a three-degree-of-freedom engagement environment wherein a missile, equipped with a strap-down seeker and aerodynamic control surfaces, pursues a target that may execute complex, time-varying accelerations orthogonal to its velocity vector. The missile’s guidance system receives a stream of sensor measurements at a fixed update rate of 20 milliseconds, including the relative position vector between the missile and the target, the relative velocity vector, the line-of-sight unit vector pointing from the missile to the target, and the angular rate of rotation of the line-of-sight vector. These measurements are subject to noise and distortion, including Gaussian white noise with a standard deviation of one milliradian and a first-order low-pass filter with a time constant of 20 milliseconds to simulate seeker lag. Additionally, the line-of-sight vector is distorted by a radome refraction model that induces an angular offset dependent on the look angle between the missile’s velocity vector and the true line-of-sight vector. This refraction model is parameterized by azimuthal and elevation refraction coefficients sampled uniformly at the start of each engagement, resulting in a non-linear, non-stationary distortion that varies across the engagement envelope. The missile’s maximum lateral acceleration is constrained by dynamic pressure, which decreases with altitude, and is further limited by a hard ceiling of 40 gravitational accelerations to prevent structural failure. The target’s maximum acceleration capability is similarly constrained by aerodynamic drag, with its maximum speed capped at 600 meters per second and its drag coefficient and mass ratio randomized across engagements to simulate unpowered ballistic trajectories or powered cruise missile behavior. The engagement scenarios are initialized with a randomly generated heading error between the missile’s initial velocity vector and the collision heading required to intercept the target, with the error magnitude sampled uniformly between 0.5 and 15 degrees to ensure that the missile does not begin on a collision course. The target’s maneuvering behavior is selected at the start of each episode with equal probability from a set of three distinct profiles: a bang-bang maneuver, a sinusoidal weave, and a jinking maneuver, each characterized by random start time, duration, switching time, and acceleration magnitude, with the acceleration level either set to the target’s maximum capability or sampled uniformly between zero and maximum with equal probability. The engagement terminates when the closing velocity between the missile and the target becomes negative, indicating that the missile has passed the target without interception, or when the relative distance between the two falls below a predefined threshold, at which point the miss distance is recorded as the Euclidean distance between the missile and target positions at the final time step.

The core innovation of the invention lies in the line-of-sight curvature policy, a deep recurrent neural network that transforms the raw sensor observations into a time-varying Euler 321 attitude parameterization, which is then applied to rotate the measured line-of-sight vector before it is passed to the proportional navigation guidance law. The policy is implemented as a four-layer neural network with hyperbolic tangent activation functions on all hidden layers, where the second layer is a gated recurrent unit (GRU) with 128 units, enabling the network to maintain an internal state that evolves over time in response to the sequence of observations. The input to the policy consists of a 12-dimensional observation vector comprising the relative position vector (3 components), the relative velocity vector (3 components), the line-of-sight unit vector (3 components), the line-of-sight rotation rate (3 components), and a normalized time-to-go estimate derived from the inverse of the closing velocity. The output of the policy is a three-dimensional action vector, which is scaled by a factor of two and interpreted as a set of Euler 321 roll, pitch, and yaw angles that define a rotation matrix. This rotation matrix, computed using standard direction cosine matrix formulation from the Euler angles, is then applied to the measured line-of-sight unit vector to produce a shaped line-of-sight vector that differs from the raw measurement by a time-varying angular offset. The shaped line-of-sight vector is then used to compute the line-of-sight rotation rate, which is fed into the proportional navigation guidance law as if it were the true rotation rate of the target. The proportional navigation law itself is implemented as a true proportional navigation (TPN) law with a navigation constant of three, which computes a lateral acceleration command perpendicular to the missile’s velocity vector proportional to the line-of-sight rotation rate. The commanded acceleration is then passed through a flight control system model with a first-order lag of 80 milliseconds and an actuator model with a first-order lag of 20 milliseconds, both of which are implemented as low-pass filters to simulate the dynamics of the missile’s control surfaces and hydraulic actuators. The commanded acceleration is further clipped based on dynamic pressure, ensuring that the missile’s maximum acceleration capability decreases with altitude, and is bounded by the structural limit of 40 gravitational accelerations. The missile’s position and velocity are updated using a fourth-order Runge-Kutta integration scheme with a variable time step: 20 milliseconds for the majority of the engagement and 0.2 milliseconds during the final 80 meters of flight to ensure accurate measurement of miss distance. The target’s motion is modeled using a similar integration scheme, with its acceleration commanded according to the selected maneuver profile and its speed adjusted for aerodynamic drag using a drag coefficient and mass ratio that are randomized across engagements to simulate both powered and unpowered targets.

The line-of-sight curvature policy is optimized using a meta-reinforcement learning framework that trains the neural network across an ensemble of 90,000 simulated engagement episodes, each with unique combinations of target maneuver type, acceleration magnitude, aerodynamic drag parameters, radome refraction coefficients, initial heading error, and missile-to-target acceleration capability ratios. The optimization algorithm employed is proximal policy optimization (PPO), which updates the policy using a clipped surrogate objective function that constrains the magnitude of policy updates to ensure stable convergence. The policy and value function networks are updated jointly after every 60 episodes, with each update performed using gradient ascent on the policy parameters and gradient descent on the value function parameters. The value function is trained to predict the expected cumulative reward from a given observation sequence, using a mean squared error loss function between the predicted value and the empirical return computed from the trajectory. The reward function is designed to encourage high interception accuracy while minimizing control effort, and is defined as a combination of four components: a terminal reward that is inversely proportional to the miss distance at the end of the engagement, a control effort penalty that scales quadratically with the magnitude of the commanded lateral acceleration, a line-of-sight curvature penalty that penalizes excessive rotation of the shaped line-of-sight vector, and a small constant reward for each time step during which the missile remains within a predefined engagement envelope. The terminal reward is discounted with a factor of 0.995, while all other rewards are discounted with a factor of 0.95 to prioritize early interception and penalize prolonged engagements. The line-of-sight curvature penalty is scaled by a factor of one and is computed as the squared magnitude of the difference between consecutive Euler angle commands, thereby discouraging rapid, high-frequency rotations of the shaped line-of-sight vector that could induce instability or excessive control effort. The control effort penalty is scaled by a factor of ten and is computed as the squared magnitude of the commanded lateral acceleration, normalized by the maximum possible acceleration at sea level. The policy is trained using a learning rate of 0.0003 for the policy network and 0.001 for the value function network, with the clipping parameter for the PPO objective set to 0.2 to ensure that policy updates remain within a trust region that prevents catastrophic divergence. The observations are normalized to have zero mean and unit variance across the training set, and the learning rates and clipping parameters are adaptively adjusted during training to maintain a Kullback-Leibler divergence between successive policy distributions of approximately 0.001, which has been shown empirically to promote stable and efficient learning.

The resulting line-of-sight curvature policy, once optimized, is a deterministic mapping from observation sequences to Euler angle commands that implicitly learns to counteract target maneuvers and sensor distortions by shaping the line-of-sight vector in a manner that causes the proportional navigation law to generate optimal acceleration commands without requiring any explicit knowledge of the target’s motion. During testing, the policy is evaluated across 5,000 independent engagement scenarios, each drawn from the same distribution of parameters used during training, with the exception that the target’s acceleration capability is varied between 10, 20, and 30 gravitational accelerations, and the target’s aerodynamic drag is either modeled with randomized coefficients or set to zero to simulate a powered cruise missile. The performance of the PN-LOSC guidance law is compared against two benchmarks: conventional proportional navigation with a navigation constant of three and augmented proportional navigation with an acceleration estimate derived from a first-order low-pass filter of the line-of-sight rotation rate. The results demonstrate that PN-LOSC achieves a mean miss distance of 0.87 meters under conditions of 30g target acceleration and randomized drag, compared to 2.14 meters for APN and 4.89 meters for PN. Under conditions of no target drag and 30g target acceleration, PN-LOSC achieves a mean miss distance of 1.02 meters, compared to 1.98 meters for APN and 5.12 meters for PN. The control effort, measured as the integral of the squared lateral acceleration over the engagement duration, is reduced by an average of 38% compared to APN and by 56% compared to PN. The performance advantage of PN-LOSC is most pronounced in scenarios involving high-frequency weave maneuvers, where the policy learns to attenuate the response to target oscillations at longer ranges and amplify it only as the missile closes in, thereby reducing the total energy expenditure and preserving missile speed and acceleration capability for the final interception phase. In contrast, APN, which relies on an estimate of target acceleration, frequently overreacts to sensor noise and radome refraction, generating excessive acceleration commands that increase drag and reduce missile performance. The shaped line-of-sight trajectories generated by the policy exhibit a clear temporal structure: during the early phase of engagement, the curvature is minimal, allowing the missile to maintain a relatively straight trajectory; as the range decreases, the curvature increases in a manner that correlates with the expected frequency and amplitude of the target’s maneuver, effectively pre-compensating for the target’s motion before it is fully manifest in the sensor measurements. This behavior is not explicitly programmed but emerges as a consequence of the meta-reinforcement learning process, which rewards policies that achieve interception with minimal control effort and maximum robustness to environmental variability.

The invention’s ability to achieve superior performance without requiring target acceleration estimation is a direct consequence of the recurrent architecture embedded within the policy network. The gated recurrent unit in the second layer of the network maintains a hidden state that evolves over time in response to the sequence of observations, allowing the policy to infer the type, timing, and magnitude of the target’s maneuver based on the history of line-of-sight deviations. This enables the policy to distinguish between true target motion and sensor artifacts such as radome refraction, which, while correlated with the look angle, do not exhibit the same temporal structure as genuine target accelerations. In simulations where the radome refraction parameters are set to zero, the performance of PN-LOSC remains superior to that of APN, indicating that the advantage is not solely attributable to robustness to sensor distortion but also to an intrinsic ability to anticipate and counteract target maneuvers through the curvature of the shaped line-of-sight trajectory. The policy learns to exploit the coupling between the missile’s dynamics and the guidance law by generating LOS rotations that induce a desired acceleration profile in the proportional navigation law, effectively turning the guidance system into a closed-loop adaptive controller that learns to manipulate its own perception of the target’s motion. This approach eliminates the need for separate estimation filters, reduces computational load, and avoids the risk of estimator divergence that plagues APN and other estimation-based methods. Furthermore, because the policy is trained across a wide range of environmental conditions, it generalizes effectively to novel scenarios not encountered during training, including targets with acceleration profiles outside the training distribution, radome parameters not included in the training set, and missile dynamics that differ from those used during optimization. This adaptability is a key advantage of the meta-reinforcement learning framework, which ensures that the policy does not overfit to a single set of conditions but instead learns a general strategy for shaping the line-of-sight vector to achieve interception under uncertainty.

The invention is fully compatible with existing missile guidance architectures and requires no modification to the seeker hardware, flight control system, or communication interfaces. The only change required is the replacement of the raw line-of-sight vector input to the proportional navigation law with the shaped line-of-sight vector generated by the curvature policy. The policy can be implemented as a software module running on a standard onboard guidance processor, with the neural network weights stored in non-volatile memory and the forward pass executed at the 20-millisecond update rate using optimized linear algebra libraries. The computational complexity of the policy is low, requiring fewer than 10,000 floating-point operations per update, making it feasible for deployment on embedded systems with limited processing power. The policy is deterministic and does not require online learning or adaptation during operation, ensuring stability and predictability in operational environments. The invention may be extended to incorporate additional constraints by modifying the reward function during training. For example, a penalty term may be added to discourage the shaped line-of-sight vector from deviating beyond a specified look angle limit, thereby ensuring that the seeker remains within its field of view. Similarly, a constraint on the rate of change of the shaped line-of-sight vector may be introduced to limit the angular acceleration of the missile’s line-of-sight axis, thereby reducing structural loads and thermal heating. The policy may also be trained to satisfy impact angle constraints by incorporating a terminal reward that depends on the angle between the missile’s velocity vector and the target’s velocity vector at interception. These extensions do not alter the fundamental architecture of the invention but rather modify the optimization objective to prioritize additional mission objectives, demonstrating the flexibility and scalability of the approach.

The invention represents a paradigm shift in missile guidance by decoupling the problem of target maneuver response from explicit state estimation and instead treating the line-of-sight vector as a control variable that can be shaped to achieve desired guidance outcomes. This approach fundamentally redefines the role of the guidance law, transforming it from a passive observer of target motion into an active participant in the perception-action loop. By leveraging the representational power of deep recurrent neural networks and the adaptability of meta-reinforcement learning, the invention achieves unprecedented levels of accuracy and efficiency against highly maneuvering targets, without the computational burden, instability risks, or model dependencies of traditional estimation-based methods. The system is robust to sensor noise, aerodynamic uncertainty, and environmental variability, and its performance is demonstrably superior to both conventional proportional navigation and augmented proportional navigation across a wide range of operational conditions. The invention is applicable to any guidance law that operates on the line-of-sight vector, including geometric guidance, optimal control laws, and model predictive control frameworks, and may be integrated into future autonomous intercept systems for air-to-air, surface-to-air, and anti-ballistic missile applications. The method does not require prior knowledge of target behavior, does not rely on external models, and does not introduce new hardware components, making it a practical, scalable, and deployable solution for next-generation missile guidance systems.
