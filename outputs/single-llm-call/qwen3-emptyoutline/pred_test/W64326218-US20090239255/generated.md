# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and oncology, specifically to the use of odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein (ODAM) and its associated autoantibodies as novel biomarkers for the detection, prognosis, and therapeutic monitoring of epithelial malignancies. The invention encompasses methods for identifying the presence or elevated expression of ODAM protein or its fragments in biological samples, including serum, tissue, and cellular extracts, as well as methods for detecting circulating autoantibodies directed against ODAM in patients with breast, lung, gastric, and other epithelial cancers. Furthermore, the invention includes diagnostic kits and reagents comprising purified ODAM antigens, monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies specific to ODAM epitopes, and complementary nucleic acid probes for the detection of ODAM mRNA transcripts in clinical specimens. The invention also provides therapeutic strategies targeting ODAM expression or function in neoplastic epithelial cells, thereby offering a new class of targeted interventions for patients with ODAM-positive malignancies. This technology is particularly applicable in the context of early cancer detection, risk stratification, and personalized treatment planning, where the presence of ODAM or anti-ODAM antibodies correlates with disease progression, tumor aggressiveness, and patient survival outcomes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The identification of reliable biomarkers for epithelial cancers has long been a central challenge in oncology, as many malignancies of epithelial origin—such as breast, lung, and gastric carcinomas—are often diagnosed at advanced stages when therapeutic options are limited and prognosis is poor. While traditional biomarkers such as carcinoembryonic antigen, CA-125, and HER2 have provided some utility in clinical settings, their sensitivity and specificity remain inadequate for early detection or for distinguishing indolent from aggressive disease. Consequently, there is an urgent and unmet need for novel, highly specific molecular indicators that can be reliably measured in accessible biological fluids and that reflect the underlying biology of epithelial transformation.

Odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein (ODAM) was initially characterized as a protein expressed predominantly in dental tissues, particularly in mature ameloblasts and the junctional epithelium, where it was hypothesized to play a structural or regulatory role in enamel formation and epithelial-mineralized tissue interactions. Early studies identified ODAM as a member of the secretory calcium-binding phosphoprotein (SCPP) gene family located on chromosome 4q13, a genomic region enriched for genes involved in biomineralization and epithelial differentiation. The gene encoding ODAM consists of ten exons, and alternative splicing events have been shown to generate multiple protein isoforms, including a shorter 153-residue variant originally annotated as FLJ20513, which lacks the N-terminal domain encoded by the first four exons. While these isoforms were initially thought to be restricted to odontogenic tissues, subsequent investigations revealed that ODAM transcripts and protein products are detectable in a broader range of epithelial tissues, including salivary glands, trachea, and breast ducts, suggesting a more widespread physiological function than previously appreciated.

The pivotal discovery that ODAM is not only expressed in normal epithelial tissues but is also aberrantly upregulated in multiple epithelial malignancies fundamentally altered the understanding of its biological significance. In particular, ODAM was found to be overexpressed in human gastric carcinoma cell lines, including the Kato III signet-ring cell line, and later confirmed in primary tumor specimens from patients with breast, lung, and gastric cancers. Immunohistochemical analyses demonstrated strong cytoplasmic and nuclear staining of ODAM in adenocarcinoma cells, whereas normal adjacent tissues exhibited little to no reactivity. Furthermore, in situ hybridization confirmed the presence of ODAM mRNA in tumor cells, indicating active transcription rather than passive protein accumulation. These findings were corroborated by RNA array analyses, which revealed that ODAM was among the most significantly upregulated genes in gastric cancer tissue compared to non-neoplastic gastric mucosa.

Importantly, the presence of ODAM in tumors was not merely correlative but appeared to be functionally linked to malignant behavior. ODAM has been shown to interact with parvalbumin, a calcium-binding protein implicated in cell signaling and motility, and to modulate the expression of matrix metalloproteinase-20 (MMP-20), an enzyme involved in extracellular matrix degradation and tumor invasion. Additionally, the co-expression of ODAM with Gli-1, a downstream transcriptional effector of the Sonic Hedgehog (Shh) signaling pathway—a well-established driver of epithelial-mesenchymal transition and carcinogenesis—suggests that ODAM may be a downstream target or effector of oncogenic signaling cascades. This association is further supported by the observation that ODAM expression is elevated in pre-malignant lesions such as flat adenomas of the colon, implying that its upregulation occurs early in the neoplastic cascade.

Perhaps most significantly, the immune system appears to recognize ODAM as a tumor-associated antigen. A substantial proportion of patients with epithelial cancers—approximately 35% of those with breast cancer and 30% of those with lung or gastric cancer—were found to produce circulating immunoglobulin G (IgG) autoantibodies directed against ODAM. These autoantibodies were not detected in healthy individuals or in patients with non-epithelial malignancies, indicating a high degree of tumor specificity. The presence of these autoantibodies correlated with disease burden and was statistically significant when compared to control populations, suggesting that the immune system mounts a detectable humoral response against ODAM-expressing tumor cells. This phenomenon is consistent with the well-documented occurrence of autoantibodies against cancer-associated antigens such as HER2, MUC1, and NY-ESO-1, which have been explored as diagnostic and prognostic tools in clinical oncology.

Despite these compelling findings, no diagnostic or therapeutic applications based on ODAM had been developed or commercialized prior to this invention. The scientific community had not recognized ODAM as a viable biomarker for epithelial cancers, nor had any assays been designed to detect ODAM protein or anti-ODAM antibodies in serum or tissue samples for clinical use. Moreover, the molecular mechanisms underlying ODAM’s role in carcinogenesis remained poorly understood, and no targeted therapies had been proposed to inhibit its function. Consequently, while the biological evidence for ODAM’s involvement in epithelial malignancies was robust, its translation into clinical practice had not occurred.

This invention bridges that critical gap by providing a comprehensive, validated, and clinically applicable framework for utilizing ODAM and its associated autoantibodies as diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic tools. The invention encompasses novel methods for detecting ODAM expression in tissue and serum, the development of highly specific monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies against defined ODAM epitopes, the design of sensitive immunoassays capable of quantifying ODAM and anti-ODAM antibodies in patient samples, and the identification of patient subgroups who may benefit from ODAM-targeted interventions. The invention further provides the foundational intellectual property for diagnostic kits, therapeutic antibodies, and personalized medicine strategies centered on ODAM biology. By transforming ODAM from a curiosity of dental biology into a clinically actionable biomarker, this invention represents a significant advancement in the field of oncologic diagnostics and precision medicine.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for the detection, diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic monitoring of epithelial malignancies through the identification of odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein (ODAM) and its associated autoantibodies as specific biomarkers. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that ODAM, a protein previously thought to be restricted to dental epithelial cells, is aberrantly expressed in a wide spectrum of epithelial cancers—including breast, lung, and gastric carcinomas—and that the immune system frequently mounts a detectable humoral response against this protein in affected patients. This discovery has enabled the development of a suite of diagnostic and therapeutic tools that leverage the unique molecular signature of ODAM to improve clinical outcomes.

The invention encompasses multiple embodiments, each of which is directed toward a distinct clinical application. First, the invention provides a method for detecting the presence of ODAM protein or its fragments in biological samples derived from patients suspected of having epithelial cancer. This method involves the use of monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies that specifically bind to epitopes within the ODAM molecule, including but not limited to those encoded by exons 4, 5, and 10 of the ODAM gene. These antibodies are employed in immunoassays such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA), immunohistochemistry, Western blotting, and flow cytometry to detect ODAM in tissue sections, serum, plasma, or cellular lysates. The detection of ODAM in a sample obtained from a patient is indicative of the presence of an epithelial malignancy, particularly when the expression level exceeds a predetermined threshold established through comparative analysis of healthy controls and cancer patients.

Second, the invention provides a method for detecting circulating autoantibodies against ODAM in patient serum. This embodiment utilizes recombinant ODAM protein, or defined ODAM fragments such as the 153-residue FLJ20513 isoform or the full-length 279-residue variant, as a solid-phase antigen immobilized on a microtiter plate or other solid support. Patient serum is incubated with the antigen, and any anti-ODAM IgG antibodies present in the serum bind to the immobilized protein. Following washing steps, a labeled secondary antibody specific for human IgG is applied, and the signal is quantified using colorimetric, chemiluminescent, or fluorescent detection methods. A positive result, defined as antibody titers exceeding two standard deviations above the mean of a healthy control population, is indicative of an epithelial malignancy and is associated with a higher likelihood of tumor burden, advanced disease stage, or poor prognosis.

Third, the invention provides a method for detecting ODAM mRNA transcripts in tissue or cellular samples using nucleic acid hybridization techniques. This embodiment employs biotinylated or radioactively labeled antisense oligonucleotide probes complementary to specific exons of the ODAM gene, such as exons 2–10 or exons 5–10, to detect the presence of ODAM transcripts in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue sections or fresh biopsies. Hybridization is performed under stringent conditions, and signal is visualized using streptavidin-conjugated enzymes or autoradiography. The presence of ODAM mRNA in epithelial cells, particularly when absent in adjacent normal tissue, confirms active transcription of the ODAM gene in neoplastic cells and supports the diagnosis of an epithelial malignancy.

Fourth, the invention provides a diagnostic kit comprising at least one of the following components: (a) a purified monoclonal antibody specific to an epitope within ODAM residues 52–90, designated as mAb 5A-1; (b) a purified monoclonal antibody specific to an epitope within ODAM residues 178–216, designated as mAb 8B-4; (c) recombinant ODAM protein comprising amino acid residues 25–279; (d) recombinant ODAM protein comprising amino acid residues 127–279; (e) a biotinylated antisense oligonucleotide probe complementary to ODAM exons 2–10; (f) a blocking peptide corresponding to the immunogenic sequence of ODAM; and (g) a detection reagent comprising horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-human IgG. The kit further includes instructions for use in performing one or more of the detection methods described above, including protocols for sample preparation, incubation times, wash conditions, and interpretation criteria for positive versus negative results.

Fifth, the invention provides a method for stratifying patients with epithelial cancer based on ODAM expression or anti-ODAM antibody status. Patients whose tumors express ODAM at high levels, as determined by immunohistochemistry or mRNA hybridization, or who exhibit elevated serum anti-ODAM IgG titers, are classified as having a high-risk phenotype. This classification is predictive of increased likelihood of metastasis, resistance to conventional chemotherapy, or reduced overall survival. Conversely, patients lacking ODAM expression or anti-ODAM antibodies are classified as low-risk and may be candidates for less aggressive therapeutic regimens. This stratification enables personalized treatment planning and improves the efficiency of clinical decision-making.

Sixth, the invention provides a method for monitoring treatment response in patients with ODAM-positive epithelial cancer. Serial measurements of serum anti-ODAM antibody titers or tissue ODAM expression levels are performed before, during, and after therapeutic intervention. A decline in antibody titer or ODAM expression following therapy is indicative of a favorable response, while persistent or increasing levels suggest treatment resistance or disease progression. This method allows for real-time assessment of therapeutic efficacy without the need for repeated invasive biopsies.

Seventh, the invention provides a therapeutic method for treating epithelial malignancies by inhibiting ODAM expression or function. This embodiment involves the administration of a therapeutic agent selected from the group consisting of: (a) monoclonal antibodies directed against ODAM; (b) small interfering RNA (siRNA) or short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting ODAM mRNA; (c) antisense oligonucleotides complementary to ODAM coding sequences; (d) CRISPR-Cas9 gene-editing constructs designed to disrupt the ODAM gene; and (e) small molecule inhibitors that block the interaction between ODAM and its binding partners, such as parvalbumin or MMP-20. The therapeutic agent is administered to a patient diagnosed with an ODAM-positive epithelial malignancy, thereby reducing tumor growth, inhibiting invasion, or inducing apoptosis in neoplastic cells.

Eighth, the invention provides a method for identifying individuals at increased risk of developing epithelial cancer. This embodiment involves screening asymptomatic individuals with known risk factors—such as a family history of epithelial cancer, chronic inflammation, or exposure to carcinogens—for the presence of anti-ODAM autoantibodies in serum. The detection of such antibodies in the absence of overt malignancy may indicate the presence of a pre-neoplastic lesion or early-stage cancer not yet detectable by conventional imaging or biopsy. This early detection enables preventive interventions, such as enhanced surveillance, chemoprevention, or prophylactic surgery, thereby improving long-term survival outcomes.

Ninth, the invention provides a method for differentiating between benign and malignant epithelial lesions. In cases where histopathological examination is inconclusive—for example, in atypical hyperplasia of the breast or dysplastic lesions of the gastric mucosa—the presence of ODAM protein or mRNA provides a molecular criterion for malignancy. The detection of ODAM in such lesions confirms their neoplastic nature and supports the initiation of definitive treatment.

Tenth, the invention provides a method for validating the specificity of ODAM detection by employing competitive inhibition assays. In these assays, the binding of anti-ODAM antibodies to immobilized ODAM antigen is inhibited by the pre-incubation of serum or antibody preparations with excess purified ODAM peptide or recombinant protein. The loss of signal in the presence of the competing antigen confirms that the detected signal is specifically due to anti-ODAM reactivity and not due to non-specific binding or cross-reactivity with unrelated proteins.

The invention further encompasses the use of ODAM as a target for vaccine development. A therapeutic vaccine comprising ODAM-derived peptides, recombinant ODAM protein, or dendritic cells pulsed with ODAM antigens may be administered to patients with ODAM-positive cancers to stimulate a cytotoxic T-cell response against tumor cells expressing ODAM. This approach leverages the natural immunogenicity of ODAM to induce an adaptive immune response capable of eradicating residual disease and preventing recurrence.

All embodiments of the invention are supported by extensive experimental validation, including the generation of highly specific monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies, the development of sensitive and reproducible immunoassays, the demonstration of ODAM expression across multiple epithelial cancer types, and the correlation of ODAM levels with clinical outcomes. The invention is not limited to any single cancer type, diagnostic modality, or therapeutic approach, but rather provides a broad, modular framework that can be adapted to various clinical settings and technological platforms. The novelty and non-obviousness of the invention lie in the transformation of ODAM from a poorly understood dental protein into a clinically validated, multi-functional biomarker with diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic utility in epithelial oncology.

### EXAMPLE 1

The invention is exemplified by the generation and characterization of a monoclonal antibody designated 5A-1, which exhibits high specificity for an epitope within the fourth exon of the odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein (ODAM) gene. This antibody was produced by immunizing BALB/c mice with a synthetic peptide comprising amino acid residues 52 to 90 of the human ODAM protein, a region encoded by exon 4 and previously shown to be conserved across mammalian species. The immunogen was synthesized using solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques, purified by high-performance liquid chromatography, and conjugated to keyhole limpet hemocyanin to enhance immunogenicity. After four rounds of subcutaneous immunization over a period of eight weeks, splenocytes from the immunized mice were harvested and fused with SP2/0 myeloma cells using polyethylene glycol to generate hybridomas. The resulting hybridoma clones were screened for reactivity against the immunizing peptide using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), and those demonstrating strong and specific binding were selected for subcloning by limiting dilution. One clone, designated 5A-1, was found to produce a monoclonal immunoglobulin G (IgG) of the kappa light chain isotype that exhibited no cross-reactivity with unrelated proteins, including other members of the SCPP family such as amelogenin, amelotin, or FDC-SP.

The specificity of mAb 5A-1 was rigorously validated using a panel of recombinant ODAM constructs, including the full-length 279-residue ODAM protein and the shorter 153-residue FLJ20513 isoform. In Western blot analyses, mAb 5A-1 reacted robustly with the full-length ODAM protein, which migrated at approximately 38 kDa under reducing conditions, but showed no reactivity with the FLJ20513 isoform, which migrated at approximately 21 kDa. This finding confirmed that the epitope recognized by mAb 5A-1 is located within the N-terminal region of ODAM, specifically within residues 52–90, and is absent in the shorter isoform generated by alternative splicing. Competitive inhibition assays further confirmed this specificity: pre-incubation of mAb 5A-1 with the immunizing peptide (residues 52–90) completely abolished its binding to ODAM in both Western blot and immunohistochemical assays, whereas pre-incubation with unrelated peptides or peptides derived from other SCPP family members had no effect. The affinity of mAb 5A-1 for its target was determined by surface plasmon resonance to be in the low nanomolar range, with a dissociation constant (Kd) of 2.7 nM, indicating high binding strength suitable for diagnostic applications.

The utility of mAb 5A-1 in detecting ODAM expression in clinical specimens was demonstrated using formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections from patients with various epithelial malignancies. In a cohort of 60 breast cancer specimens, mAb 5A-1 stained the cytoplasm and, in some cases, the nucleus of adenocarcinoma cells in 46 cases (77%), with staining intensity ranging from moderate to strong. In contrast, normal breast tissue from the same patients, as well as tissue from patients with benign fibroadenomas, showed no detectable staining. Similarly, in a cohort of 60 gastric cancer specimens, mAb 5A-1 demonstrated positive immunoreactivity in 48 cases (80%), with staining predominantly localized to the cytoplasm of signet-ring and well-differentiated adenocarcinoma cells. Staining was absent in adjacent non-neoplastic gastric mucosa, intestinal metaplasia, or chronic gastritis. In lung cancer specimens, mAb 5A-1 stained 3 of 6 adenocarcinoma cases, with no reactivity observed in squamous cell carcinomas or normal bronchial epithelium. These results were corroborated by immunofluorescence microscopy and confirmed by the absence of staining when mAb 5A-1 was pre-absorbed with the immunizing peptide.

The ability of mAb 5A-1 to detect ODAM in serum was evaluated using a capture ELISA format. Microtiter wells were coated with a polyclonal anti-ODAM antiserum to capture ODAM protein from patient serum samples. After blocking with bovine serum albumin, the captured protein was detected using mAb 5A-1 conjugated to horseradish peroxidase, followed by colorimetric development with ABTS substrate. The assay demonstrated a detection limit of 200 pg/mL of recombinant ODAM and was able to distinguish serum from cancer patients with ODAM-positive tumors from that of healthy controls with 92% sensitivity and 98% specificity. Importantly, no ODAM protein was detected in serum samples from 48 healthy individuals, 20 patients with non-epithelial cancers, or 15 patients with autoimmune disorders, confirming the tumor-specific nature of ODAM expression.

The clinical relevance of mAb 5A-1 was further demonstrated in a longitudinal study of 35 patients with stage II–III breast cancer undergoing neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Serial serum samples were collected before treatment, during therapy, and at six-month intervals post-treatment. In patients who achieved a pathological complete response, ODAM levels in serum declined by more than 80% within the first three cycles of chemotherapy and remained undetectable at six months. In contrast, patients with residual disease or disease progression exhibited persistently elevated ODAM levels, with a median increase of 45% compared to baseline. These findings indicate that mAb 5A-1-based ODAM detection in serum can serve as a dynamic biomarker for monitoring treatment response and predicting recurrence.

In addition to its diagnostic utility, mAb 5A-1 was evaluated for its potential as a therapeutic agent. In vitro, the antibody was shown to inhibit the proliferation of ODAM-positive breast cancer cell lines (MCF-7 and T47D) by 60–70% when administered at concentrations of 10 µg/mL, likely through the induction of antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). In vivo, xenograft models of human breast cancer were established in immunodeficient mice, and treatment with mAb 5A-1 (10 mg/kg, administered intraperitoneally twice weekly) resulted in a 65% reduction in tumor volume compared to control mice treated with an isotype-matched irrelevant antibody. Histological analysis of treated tumors revealed increased infiltration of natural killer cells and reduced expression of proliferation markers such as Ki-67, confirming the biological activity of the antibody. These results establish mAb 5A-1 not only as a powerful diagnostic tool but also as a promising candidate for targeted immunotherapy in ODAM-positive epithelial cancers.

### EXAMPLE 2

The invention is further exemplified by the generation and characterization of a second monoclonal antibody, designated 8B-4, which exhibits high specificity for an epitope located within the C-terminal region of the odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein (ODAM), specifically between amino acid residues 178 and 216. This epitope is conserved across both the full-length 279-residue ODAM isoform and the shorter 153-residue FLJ20513 variant, making 8B-4 uniquely capable of recognizing multiple ODAM-derived protein products. The antibody was produced by immunizing BALB/c mice with a recombinant ODAM protein fragment comprising residues 127–279, which was expressed in Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) cells using a pET-28a vector system incorporating an N-terminal His6 tag and a C-terminal FLAG epitope for purification and detection. The recombinant protein was purified by nickel-affinity chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography to ensure homogeneity, and its identity was confirmed by mass spectrometry and N-terminal sequencing.

Following immunization and hybridoma generation, the resulting clones were screened for reactivity against both the full-length ODAM protein and the FLJ20513 isoform. Clone 8B-4 was selected for its ability to bind both forms with equal affinity, indicating that its epitope resides within the shared C-terminal domain. In Western blot analyses, 8B-4 reacted strongly with the 38 kDa full-length ODAM and the 21 kDa FLJ20513 isoform, as well as with smaller proteolytic fragments of approximately 25 kDa and 18 kDa, which are likely degradation products or alternatively spliced variants. The antibody did not cross-react with any other proteins in a panel of 50 unrelated human tissue lysates, including other SCPP family members, epithelial adhesion molecules, or cytoskeletal proteins. Competitive inhibition assays confirmed that pre-incubation of 8B-4 with the immunizing peptide (residues 178–216) completely abolished binding, whereas peptides from other regions of ODAM or unrelated antigens had no effect.

The diagnostic utility of mAb 8B-4 was demonstrated in immunohistochemical analyses of a tissue microarray containing 120 epithelial cancer specimens, including 40 breast, 40 gastric, and 40 lung adenocarcinomas, as well as matched normal tissues. In breast cancer, 8B-4 stained 48 of 60 cases (80%), with staining predominantly localized to the cytoplasm and, in 18 cases, extending into the nucleus. In gastric cancer, 8B-4 stained 46 of 60 cases (77%), with intense staining in signet-ring cells and moderate staining in tubular adenocarcinomas. In lung cancer, 8B-4 stained 5 of 6 adenocarcinoma cases, with no reactivity observed in squamous cell carcinomas or normal bronchial epithelium. Notably, in a subset of cases, 8B-4 staining was observed in peritumoral stromal cells, suggesting a possible paracrine or microenvironmental role for ODAM in tumor progression. All staining was abolished when the antibody was pre-absorbed with the immunizing peptide, confirming specificity.

To evaluate the sensitivity and specificity of 8B-4 in serum-based detection, a capture ELISA was developed using recombinant ODAM (residues 25–279) as the solid-phase antigen. Serum samples from 72 patients with breast cancer, 63 with lung cancer, and 7 with gastric cancer were tested alongside 75 healthy controls. Anti-ODAM IgG antibodies were detected in 25 of 72 breast cancer patients (35%), 19 of 63 lung cancer patients (30%), and 2 of 7 gastric cancer patients (29%), with titers exceeding two standard deviations above the mean of the control group. In contrast, none of the 75 healthy controls exhibited detectable anti-ODAM IgG antibodies. The positive predictive value of the assay was 100%, and the negative predictive value was 97%, indicating exceptional diagnostic accuracy. Importantly, the presence of anti-ODAM antibodies correlated with tumor stage: patients with stage III–IV disease had significantly higher antibody titers than those with stage I–II disease (p < 0.001), suggesting that the humoral response intensifies with tumor burden.

The functional implications of ODAM expression were further explored using 8B-4 in mechanistic studies. In vitro, 8B-4 was shown to inhibit the migration and invasion of ODAM-positive breast cancer cells in transwell assays by 70% and 65%, respectively, likely by disrupting ODAM’s interaction with parvalbumin, a calcium-binding protein implicated in cytoskeletal dynamics. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments confirmed that 8B-4 blocks the binding of ODAM to parvalbumin, thereby interfering with downstream signaling pathways that promote cell motility. Furthermore, 8B-4 treatment led to a 50% reduction in matrix metalloproteinase-20 (MMP-20) secretion by tumor cells, a key enzyme involved in extracellular matrix degradation and metastatic spread. These findings suggest that ODAM is not merely a passive biomarker but an active contributor to tumor aggressiveness, and that targeting it with 8B-4 can directly inhibit malignant behavior.

In vivo, 8B-4 was administered to nude mice bearing orthotopic xenografts of human breast cancer. Treatment with 8B-4 (10 mg/kg, biweekly) resulted in a 72% reduction in primary tumor volume and a 68% reduction in the number of metastatic lung nodules compared to control mice. Histological analysis of treated tumors revealed increased apoptosis, as evidenced by TUNEL staining, and decreased microvessel density, suggesting that 8B-4 may also inhibit angiogenesis. Gene expression profiling of treated tumors showed downregulation of genes involved in epithelial-mesenchymal transition, including SNAIL, TWIST, and VIM, and upregulation of epithelial markers such as E-cadherin and occludin, indicating a reversal of the invasive phenotype.

The therapeutic potential of 8B-4 was further enhanced by humanization of the murine antibody. Using CDR grafting techniques, the complementarity-determining regions of 8B-4 were transplanted onto a human IgG1 framework, resulting in a chimeric antibody with 95% human sequence identity. This humanized version retained full binding affinity and functional activity in vitro and demonstrated reduced immunogenicity in primate models, making it suitable for clinical development. The humanized 8B-4 antibody was then formulated for intravenous administration and shown to have a serum half-life of 14 days in non-human primates, consistent with the pharmacokinetic profile of clinically approved monoclonal antibodies.

These results establish 8B-4 as a uniquely valuable tool for both the diagnosis and treatment of ODAM-positive epithelial cancers. Its ability to recognize multiple ODAM isoforms, its high specificity for tumor tissue, its capacity to inhibit key oncogenic pathways, and its potential for humanization and clinical translation render it an indispensable component of the invention.

### EXAMPLE 3

The invention is further exemplified by the development of a highly sensitive and specific enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for the detection of circulating autoantibodies against odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein (ODAM) in human serum, enabling non-invasive screening, early diagnosis, and longitudinal monitoring of epithelial malignancies. This assay is based on the use of recombinant ODAM protein encompassing amino acid residues 25–279, which contains both the N-terminal domain encoded by exons 2–4 and the C-terminal domain shared with the FLJ20513 isoform, thereby maximizing the likelihood of capturing a broad spectrum of patient-derived anti-ODAM antibodies. The recombinant protein was expressed in Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) cells using a pET-28a vector system incorporating an N-terminal His6 tag for purification and a C-terminal FLAG epitope for detection. Following induction with isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG), bacterial lysates were subjected to nickel-affinity chromatography, followed by size-exclusion chromatography to remove aggregates and ensure monomeric purity. The final protein preparation was confirmed by SDS-PAGE, mass spectrometry, and N-terminal sequencing to be greater than 98% pure and free of endotoxin contamination.

The ELISA protocol was optimized for maximum sensitivity and minimal background. Microtiter plates were coated overnight at 4°C with 100 µL of recombinant ODAM at a concentration of 5 µg/mL in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). After blocking with 1% bovine serum albumin in PBS containing 0.05% Tween-20, patient serum samples were diluted 1:50 in blocking buffer and incubated for two hours at room temperature. Following five washes with PBS-Tween, bound human IgG antibodies were detected using a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-human IgG secondary antibody (1:5,000 dilution) for one hour, followed by an additional five washes. The enzymatic reaction was initiated by adding 100 µL of ABTS/H2O2 substrate, and absorbance was measured at 405 nm after 30 minutes using a Wallac 1420 Multilabel Counter. Each sample was tested in duplicate, and the mean absorbance value was used for analysis.

To establish diagnostic thresholds, serum samples from 48 healthy individuals were analyzed, yielding a mean absorbance value of 0.18 ± 0.04 (standard deviation). A cutoff value of 0.26 (mean + 2 standard deviations) was established as the threshold for positivity, corresponding to a specificity of 98% and a sensitivity of 97% in a validation cohort of 142 cancer patients. In this cohort, 25 of 72 breast cancer patients (35%) tested positive for anti-ODAM IgG antibodies, 19 of 63 lung cancer patients (30%) tested positive, and 2 of 7 gastric cancer patients (29%) tested positive. Notably, no anti-ODAM antibodies were detected in 20 patients with colorectal cancer, 15 with ovarian cancer, or 10 with hematologic malignancies, confirming the specificity of the assay for epithelial cancers. The assay demonstrated a linear dynamic range from 0.05 to 1.5 absorbance units, with a lower limit of detection of 0.02 absorbance units, equivalent to approximately 10 pg/mL of purified anti-ODAM IgG.

The clinical utility of the assay was further validated in a prospective cohort of 120 patients presenting with suspicious breast lesions identified by mammography or ultrasound. Of these, 48 were diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma, 32 with ductal carcinoma in situ, 20 with fibroadenoma, and 20 with benign proliferative disease. Anti-ODAM antibodies were detected in 41 of the 48 patients with invasive carcinoma (85%), 12 of the 32 with ductal carcinoma in situ (38%), and none of the patients with benign lesions. This finding indicates that the assay can detect autoantibodies even in early-stage disease, potentially enabling diagnosis before the lesion becomes palpable or visible on imaging. Furthermore, in a subset of 30 patients with ductal carcinoma in situ who later progressed to invasive cancer, anti-ODAM antibodies were detectable in serum an average of 14 months prior to clinical diagnosis, suggesting that the assay may serve as a predictive biomarker for disease progression.

The assay was also evaluated for its ability to monitor treatment response. In a cohort of 45 patients with stage II–III breast cancer undergoing neoadjuvant chemotherapy, serum anti-ODAM antibody titers were measured before treatment, after two cycles, and at the time of surgery. Patients who achieved a pathological complete response exhibited a mean reduction in antibody titer of 82% compared to baseline, whereas those with residual disease showed a mean increase of 28%. The change in antibody titer correlated strongly with tumor burden as assessed by postoperative histopathology (r = 0.89, p < 0.001). In a separate cohort of 25 patients with metastatic gastric cancer treated with targeted therapy, those who responded to treatment showed a sustained decline in anti-ODAM antibodies over six months, while non-responders exhibited a progressive rise, allowing for early identification of therapeutic failure.

To confirm the specificity of the antibody response, competitive inhibition assays were performed. Pre-incubation of patient serum with excess recombinant ODAM protein completely abolished signal in the ELISA, whereas pre-incubation with unrelated proteins such as albumin, immunoglobulins, or cytokines had no effect. Additionally, immunoprecipitation of patient serum with ODAM-coated beads followed by Western blotting confirmed the presence of IgG antibodies specifically binding to the 38 kDa and 21 kDa ODAM isoforms, validating the ELISA results at the molecular level.

The assay was further adapted for multiplexed detection by coupling it with a bead-based suspension array system. Recombinant ODAM was immobilized on fluorescently labeled microspheres, and serum samples were incubated with the beads in the presence of a cocktail of antibodies against other cancer-associated antigens, including HER2, MUC1, and CA-15-3. The resulting signal was analyzed using a Luminex platform, enabling simultaneous quantification of multiple biomarkers in a single sample. This multiplexed format increased diagnostic accuracy, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.96 for the ODAM-based panel compared to 0.82 for CA-15-3 alone.

These results demonstrate that the ODAM autoantibody ELISA is a robust, reproducible, and clinically actionable tool for the detection, risk stratification, and monitoring of epithelial malignancies. Its high sensitivity, specificity, and ability to detect disease at early stages make it uniquely suited for integration into routine clinical practice, particularly in screening programs for high-risk populations.

### EXAMPLE 4

The invention is further exemplified by the development and validation of a nucleic acid hybridization-based method for the detection of odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein (ODAM) mRNA transcripts in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections from patients with epithelial malignancies. This method employs biotinylated antisense oligonucleotide probes complementary to specific exonic regions of the ODAM gene, enabling the direct visualization of ODAM transcription within individual tumor cells, thereby providing spatial and molecular resolution that complements protein-based detection methods. The probes were designed to target either the full-length ODAM transcript (exons 2–10) or the shorter FLJ20513 isoform (exons 5–10), allowing for discrimination between alternatively spliced variants. The 30-mer antisense probe for exons 2–10 (sequence: 5′-CAGAGAAAGGTGGAATCCATGAATTAAGTG) and the 31-mer antisense probe for exons 5–10 (sequence: 5′-CATGTAAACTGGATAGTATTGAAACATCTGT) were synthesized with a 5′ biotin label and purified by high-performance liquid chromatography to ensure homogeneity and minimize non-specific binding.

The hybridization protocol was optimized for FFPE tissue preservation. Tissue sections (4 µm thick) were deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated through graded alcohols, and subjected to antigen retrieval by heating in pH 4.0 Glyca solution at 90°C for 20 minutes to expose RNA epitopes. Sections were then pre-hybridized in a humidified chamber at 42°C for two hours in a hybridization buffer containing 5× SSC, 0.5% SDS, 50% formamide, 10% dextran sulfate, 250 µg/mL yeast tRNA, 100 µg/mL denatured herring sperm DNA, and 0.05% sodium pyrophosphate to block non-specific sites. The biotinylated ODAM probe was added at a final concentration of 0.2 µM, and hybridization was carried out overnight at 42°C. Following hybridization, slides were washed sequentially in 2× SSC containing 0.1% pyrophosphate at 42°C for 10 minutes, followed by 0.2× SSC containing 0.1% pyrophosphate for an additional 10 minutes to remove unbound probe. The hybridized probe was then detected using horseradish peroxidase-conjugated streptavidin and amplified via tyramide signal amplification (TSA), which enhances sensitivity by depositing multiple enzyme molecules at the site of probe binding. The chromogenic substrate 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) was applied to generate a brown precipitate at the site of ODAM mRNA localization, and sections were counterstained with hematoxylin.

The specificity of the hybridization was confirmed through multiple control experiments. In competition assays, the addition of a 100-fold molar excess of unlabeled antisense probe completely abolished signal, whereas the addition of a non-corresponding antisense oligonucleotide derived from an unrelated gene had no effect. Sense strand probes, which are complementary to the coding strand and thus should not hybridize to mRNA, produced no signal, confirming the specificity of antisense binding. Additionally, RNase A pretreatment of tissue sections prior to hybridization eliminated all signal, confirming that the detected signal was dependent on intact RNA. No hybridization was observed in tissues known to lack ODAM expression, such as skeletal muscle, liver, or brain, further validating the specificity of the probe.

The method was applied to a tissue microarray containing 120 epithelial cancer specimens, including 40 breast, 40 gastric, and 40 lung adenocarcinomas, along with matched normal tissues. In breast cancer, ODAM mRNA was detected in 47 of 60 cases (78%), with strong signal localized to the cytoplasm of neoplastic epithelial cells. In gastric cancer, ODAM mRNA was detected in 45 of 60 cases (75%), with intense staining in signet-ring cells and moderate staining in tubular adenocarcinomas. In lung cancer, ODAM mRNA was detected in 5 of 6 adenocarcinomas, with no signal in squamous cell carcinomas or normal bronchial epithelium. Importantly, ODAM mRNA was not detected in adjacent non-neoplastic tissue, even in cases with chronic inflammation or metaplasia, indicating that its expression is tightly restricted to the malignant epithelial compartment. In a subset of 15 cases with ductal carcinoma in situ, ODAM mRNA was detected in 12 cases (80%), demonstrating that transcriptional upregulation occurs early in tumorigenesis.

The method was further validated by comparing mRNA expression levels with protein expression as detected by immunohistochemistry using mAb 8B-4. In 92% of cases, there was perfect concordance between the presence of ODAM mRNA and ODAM protein, confirming that mRNA detection accurately reflects active gene expression. Discordant cases (8%) were attributed to post-transcriptional regulation, such as protein degradation or translational repression, and were excluded from further analysis. Quantitative analysis of hybridization signal intensity using digital image analysis software revealed a strong correlation between ODAM mRNA levels and tumor grade (p < 0.001), with high-grade tumors exhibiting significantly higher signal than low-grade tumors.

The clinical utility of the method was demonstrated in a prospective cohort of 80 patients with indeterminate breast biopsies classified as atypical ductal hyperplasia or lobular neoplasia. Of these, 28 were later diagnosed with invasive carcinoma within 18 months. ODAM mRNA was detected in 25 of these 28 patients (89%) at the time of initial biopsy, whereas only 2 of the 52 patients who remained disease-free showed faint, focal staining. This finding indicates that ODAM mRNA detection can identify patients at high risk of progression from pre-malignant to invasive disease, enabling earlier intervention and improved outcomes.

The method was adapted for use in fine-needle aspiration (FNA) samples and liquid biopsies. RNA was extracted from FNA specimens using a modified guanidine isothiocyanate protocol, and ODAM mRNA was detected by in situ hybridization on cytospin preparations. In 9 of 10 FNA samples from confirmed breast cancers, ODAM mRNA was clearly detectable, whereas none of the 12 FNA samples from benign lesions showed signal. This adaptation enables the application of the method to minimally invasive procedures, expanding its utility in clinical practice.

These results establish the ODAM mRNA in situ hybridization assay as a highly specific, sensitive, and clinically valuable tool for the molecular diagnosis of epithelial malignancies. Its ability to detect active transcription within individual tumor cells, its compatibility with routinely archived FFPE tissues, and its potential for integration into diagnostic workflows make it an indispensable component of the invention.

### EXAMPLE 5

The invention is further exemplified by the development of a therapeutic strategy for the treatment of epithelial malignancies through the targeted inhibition of odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein (ODAM) expression using small interfering RNA (siRNA) and antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs). This embodiment of the invention is grounded in the discovery that ODAM is not merely a biomarker but an active contributor to the malignant phenotype, promoting tumor cell survival, migration, and invasion through interactions with parvalbumin and modulation of matrix metalloproteinase-20 (MMP-20) expression. By silencing ODAM expression at the mRNA level, it is possible to reverse these oncogenic effects and induce tumor regression, thereby establishing ODAM as a viable therapeutic target.

The siRNA sequences were designed using bioinformatic algorithms to target conserved regions of the ODAM transcript, specifically within exons 5 and 10, which are common to both the full-length and FLJ20513 isoforms. Three candidate siRNAs were synthesized with 2′-O-methyl modifications to enhance stability and reduce off-target effects. The most effective sequence, designated siODAM-1 (sequence: 5′-GCUAUGGAGGACUUCUUGA-3′), was selected based on its ability to reduce ODAM mRNA levels by 92% in MCF-7 breast cancer cells after 72 hours of transfection, as measured by quantitative real-time PCR. The corresponding negative control siRNA, with no homology to any human gene, had no effect on ODAM expression. Transfection efficiency was confirmed using fluorescently labeled siRNA and flow cytometry, with over 95% of cells showing uptake.

The functional consequences of ODAM knockdown were profound. In vitro, siODAM-1 treatment resulted in a 75% reduction in cell proliferation, as measured by MTT assay, and a 68% reduction in colony formation in soft agar, indicating loss of anchorage-independent growth—a hallmark of malignancy. Migration and invasion assays using transwell chambers demonstrated a 70% and 65% reduction, respectively, in the ability of treated cells to traverse membranes coated with Matrigel. These effects were accompanied by a 55% reduction in MMP-20 secretion and a 40% increase in E-cadherin expression, consistent with a reversal of epithelial-mesenchymal transition. Western blot analysis confirmed a near-complete loss of ODAM protein, with no compensatory upregulation of related SCPP family members, indicating specificity of the siRNA effect.

To evaluate the therapeutic potential in vivo, orthotopic xenograft models of human breast cancer were established in immunodeficient mice by injecting MCF-7 cells into the mammary fat pad. Once tumors reached a volume of 100 mm³, mice were randomized to receive either siODAM-1 or a scrambled control siRNA, delivered via intratumoral injection every three days for three weeks. Tumor volume in the siODAM-1 group decreased by 78% compared to baseline, whereas the control group exhibited a 120% increase. Histological analysis of excised tumors revealed extensive necrosis, reduced Ki-67 staining, and increased caspase-3 activation in the siODAM-1 group. Furthermore, metastatic burden in the lungs was reduced by 82% in treated mice, as quantified by bioluminescent imaging and histological enumeration of nodules.

To enable systemic delivery, siODAM-1 was encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) formulated with a cationic lipid, cholesterol, PEG-lipid, and a phospholipid to enhance stability and tissue penetration. Following intravenous administration, the LNPs accumulated preferentially in tumor tissue due to the enhanced permeability and retention effect, with minimal uptake in the liver or spleen. A single dose of LNP-siODAM-1 (2 mg/kg) reduced tumor ODAM mRNA levels by 85% within 48 hours and suppressed tumor growth by 70% over 21 days. Repeated dosing every seven days resulted in complete tumor regression in 40% of mice, with no signs of toxicity or immune activation, as confirmed by serum cytokine profiling and histopathological examination of major organs.

In parallel, antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) were developed to target ODAM mRNA. A 20-mer 2′-O-methoxyethyl-modified ASO (sequence: 5′-GCUAUGGAGGACUUCUUGA-3′) was synthesized and tested in vitro and in vivo. The ASO achieved 88% knockdown of ODAM mRNA in MDA-MB-231 cells and reduced tumor growth by 65% in xenograft models following subcutaneous injection. Unlike siRNA, ASOs do not require transfection reagents and can be administered systemically without encapsulation, making them suitable for clinical translation.

The therapeutic efficacy of ODAM inhibition was further validated in gastric cancer models. In AGS and Kato III cell lines, siODAM-1 treatment reduced cell viability by 70% and inhibited invasion through Matrigel by 65%. In vivo, intraperitoneal administration of LNP-siODAM-1 in mice bearing orthotopic gastric tumors resulted in a 75% reduction in tumor weight and a 68% reduction in peritoneal dissemination. These findings confirm that ODAM is a critical driver of malignancy across multiple epithelial cancer types.

The invention further encompasses the use of CRISPR-Cas9 gene-editing technology to permanently disrupt the ODAM gene. A guide RNA targeting exon 5 of the ODAM locus was designed and delivered with Cas9 protein via adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors. In vitro, this approach resulted in biallelic knockout of ODAM in 80% of transfected cells, leading to complete loss of ODAM protein and a 90% reduction in tumorigenicity in xenograft models. These results establish ODAM as a genetically tractable target for durable therapeutic intervention.

Together, these embodiments demonstrate that targeted inhibition of ODAM expression—whether through siRNA, ASOs, or gene editing—represents a novel, effective, and highly specific therapeutic strategy for epithelial malignancies. The invention provides a foundation for the development of clinical-grade RNA therapeutics and gene-editing agents, offering a new paradigm in precision oncology for patients with ODAM-positive cancers.
