# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel class of aryl ether compounds designed for use as high-performance heat transfer fluids in industrial thermal energy systems, particularly those operating at elevated temperatures ranging from ambient conditions up to 300 °C. More specifically, the invention encompasses a family of structurally extended benzyl ether derivatives, synthesized via a one-pot sequential etherification and Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling methodology, which exhibit exceptional thermal stability, high specific heat capacity, low viscosity at operational temperatures, and favorable boiling and melting point profiles that render them uniquely suited for applications in concentrated solar power systems, industrial heat exchangers, molten salt替代系统, and other high-temperature thermal storage and transport processes. The compounds of this invention are distinguished by their molecular architecture, which combines aromatic rings, alkyl spacers, and ether linkages in a manner that minimizes intermolecular packing forces while maximizing molecular rigidity and electron delocalization, thereby enhancing both thermal resilience and energy storage density. Unlike conventional heat transfer fluids derived from petroleum-based hydrocarbons or silicone oils, which degrade above 250 °C or require high pumping power due to elevated viscosity, the compounds of this invention maintain structural integrity under prolonged thermal stress, demonstrate superior volumetric heat capacity, and operate effectively without the need for pressurized containment or inert atmospheres during circulation. The invention further includes methods for the efficient, scalable, and environmentally optimized synthesis of these compounds, incorporating solvent-minimized reaction protocols, phase-transfer catalysis, and catalyst recycling strategies that reduce solvent consumption and waste generation compared to traditional synthetic routes. This invention fills a critical gap in the field of thermal energy systems by providing a new generation of organic heat transfer fluids that combine the thermal performance advantages of synthetic fluids with the operational simplicity and sustainability of non-halogenated, non-silicone, non-toxic chemical architectures.

## BACKGROUND ART

Historically, heat transfer fluids employed in high-temperature industrial applications have been dominated by mineral oils, synthetic hydrocarbons, silicone-based fluids, and molten salts such as sodium nitrate-potassium nitrate eutectics. These fluids, while widely adopted, suffer from significant limitations that impede their efficiency, safety, and environmental compatibility. Mineral oils, for instance, exhibit poor thermal stability above 250 °C, leading to oxidative decomposition, coke formation, and reduced heat transfer efficiency over time. Silicone oils, although thermally robust up to approximately 300 °C, possess inherently low thermal conductivity and high viscosity at low temperatures, necessitating substantial pumping energy to achieve adequate flow rates. Molten nitrate salts, while offering high volumetric heat capacity and stability at temperatures exceeding 300 °C, require complex containment systems due to their corrosive nature, high melting points near 220 °C, and risk of crystallization during system shutdowns, which can lead to catastrophic blockages. Furthermore, the handling and disposal of these salts pose significant environmental hazards due to their alkaline reactivity and potential for soil and groundwater contamination. Organic heat transfer fluids based on aromatic hydrocarbons, such as biphenyl-diphenyl oxide mixtures, have been used extensively in the past, but their thermal degradation products are often toxic, flammable, and difficult to remediate. Additionally, many of these fluids have low specific heat capacities, requiring higher mass flow rates to achieve equivalent energy transport, which in turn increases the cost of pumping, piping, and system maintenance. Recent efforts to improve upon these systems have focused on the development of novel synthetic fluids, including esters, polyglycols, and fluorinated compounds, but these alternatives often come with drawbacks such as high cost, limited availability, or incompatibility with common system materials. Moreover, the synthesis of these advanced fluids typically involves multi-step processes that rely heavily on volatile organic solvents, energy-intensive purification techniques, and inert atmosphere conditions, resulting in high environmental burdens and economic inefficiencies. In particular, the use of solvents such as tetrahydrofuran, dioxane, and dichloromethane in cross-coupling reactions—commonly employed to construct complex organic architectures—generates large volumes of hazardous waste, and the requirement for anhydrous and oxygen-free environments increases operational complexity and capital expenditure. The prevailing paradigm in heat transfer fluid design has prioritized thermal stability and boiling point elevation at the expense of other critical parameters such as volumetric heat capacity, viscosity-temperature behavior, and synthetic sustainability. As a result, there remains a substantial unmet need for heat transfer fluids that simultaneously offer high thermal stability, high specific heat capacity, low viscosity across a broad temperature range, low toxicity, and environmentally benign synthesis pathways. Furthermore, no prior art discloses a class of aryl ether compounds constructed through a one-pot sequential etherification and Suzuki-Miyaura coupling process under solvent-minimized or solvent-free conditions, nor has any prior art demonstrated that such compounds, when structurally extended with alkyl spacers and aromatic substituents, exhibit the unique combination of thermal properties described herein. While some literature has described the use of phase-transfer catalysis for ether synthesis and the application of palladium catalysts in Suzuki couplings, none have integrated these methodologies into a single, continuous, one-pot protocol capable of producing complex, thermally stable ethers with properties optimized for heat transfer applications. Additionally, no prior art has employed group contribution methods to predict and validate the specific heat capacity of such extended aryl ethers, nor has any prior art demonstrated that these compounds remain stable in air at temperatures up to 300 °C without degradation, as confirmed by thermal gravimetric analysis. The absence of such a fluid in the prior art represents a significant technological gap that this invention directly addresses by introducing a new class of chemically engineered, structurally optimized, and synthetically sustainable heat transfer fluids with demonstrably superior performance characteristics.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of aryl ether compounds characterized by a central ether linkage connecting two distinct aromatic moieties, at least one of which is substituted with a branched or linear alkyl chain of three or more carbon atoms, and wherein the aromatic rings are further functionalized with electron-donating or electron-withdrawing groups that enhance thermal stability and molecular rigidity. These compounds are synthesized via a one-pot, sequential reaction sequence comprising an initial phase-transfer-catalyzed etherification of an aryl halide with an aliphatic alcohol under solvent-minimized or solvent-free conditions, followed by an in situ Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling reaction between the resulting ether intermediate and an arylboronic acid, wherein the coupling is facilitated by a palladium catalyst and optionally supplemented with a minor amount of co-solvent to enhance reactivity without compromising the efficiency of the one-pot process. The resulting compounds exhibit a unique molecular architecture that balances steric bulk, electronic delocalization, and conformational flexibility, resulting in exceptionally high specific heat capacity, low viscosity at elevated temperatures, and thermal decomposition onset temperatures exceeding 300 °C under ambient atmospheric conditions. The invention further encompasses the use of these compounds as heat transfer fluids in systems operating between −10 °C and 300 °C, wherein the fluids demonstrate linear density-temperature relationships, negligible vapor pressure at operational temperatures, and resistance to oxidative degradation in the presence of air. The compounds of the invention are distinguished from prior art heat transfer fluids by their molecular composition, which avoids the use of silicon, halogens, or highly polar functional groups that contribute to corrosion or environmental persistence. Instead, the compounds are composed entirely of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and optionally nitrogen, rendering them non-toxic, biodegradable under appropriate conditions, and compatible with standard metallic and polymeric system components. The invention also includes a method for the preparation of these compounds that minimizes solvent usage by conducting the initial etherification step under solvent-free conditions using ground potassium hydroxide as a base and a tetralkylammonium halide as a phase-transfer catalyst, followed by the addition of a minimal volume of dioxane or tetrahydrofuran and a palladium catalyst such as Pd(PPh₃)₂Cl₂ in the presence of triphenylphosphine to effect the Suzuki coupling, thereby avoiding the need for inert gas purging, high-pressure reactors, or extensive purification steps. The resulting products are isolated by simple extraction and filtration, with many compounds requiring no chromatographic purification due to their high crystallinity or phase separation properties. The invention further encompasses derivatives of these core structures, including those wherein the alkyl chain is terminated in a hydroxymethyl group, allowing for subsequent etherification to form bis-ether structures with enhanced thermal stability and molecular symmetry. Thermal gravimetric analysis of the extended derivatives, such as those bearing two aromatic rings and two alkyl spacers, confirms stability up to 300 °C in air, with weight loss below 5% over a 24-hour period at this temperature. The specific heat capacity of these compounds, estimated using a three-level group contribution method and validated experimentally, ranges from 1.30 to 1.75 J/g·K at 300 K, significantly exceeding the values of conventional heat transfer fluids such as thermal oils (typically 1.2–1.4 J/g·K) and silicone fluids (typically 1.0–1.2 J/g·K). The density of the compounds decreases linearly with increasing temperature from 20 °C to 90 °C, with coefficients of thermal expansion consistent with those of water-based systems, enabling predictable thermal expansion behavior in closed-loop systems. The melting points of the compounds are below 25 °C for the majority of derivatives, ensuring fluidity at ambient conditions, while the boiling points exceed 400 °C, eliminating concerns regarding vaporization under normal operating conditions. The viscosity of the compounds at 100 °C ranges from 5 to 15 cP, comparable to or lower than that of mineral oils, and significantly lower than that of molten salts at equivalent temperatures, thereby reducing pumping energy requirements. The invention further includes the use of these compounds in concentrated solar power plants, industrial heat exchangers, thermal energy storage units, and high-temperature chemical reactors, where they serve as both heat transport media and energy storage media, replacing conventional fluids that require pressurization, inert atmospheres, or frequent replacement due to degradation. The compounds of the invention are particularly advantageous in systems where safety, environmental compliance, and operational simplicity are paramount, as they do not emit toxic fumes upon thermal stress, do not corrode standard stainless steel or copper components, and can be recycled through simple distillation or membrane separation techniques. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in the design of heat transfer fluids by integrating molecular engineering, synthetic efficiency, and thermal performance into a single, cohesive technological solution that is both scientifically novel and industrially practical.

## MODE(S) FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The invention may be carried out through the synthesis and utilization of a series of aryl ether compounds as detailed herein, with specific embodiments encompassing the preparation of benzyl ether derivatives through a one-pot, sequential etherification-Suzuki coupling protocol under solvent-minimized conditions, followed by their application as high-performance heat transfer fluids in thermal energy systems. A representative embodiment of the synthesis process begins with the selection of an aryl halide, preferably 4-bromobenzyl bromide or 3-bromobenzyl bromide, as the electrophilic substrate, and an aliphatic alcohol, such as 2-phenylethanol or n-pentanol, as the nucleophilic partner. These reagents are combined in a reaction vessel equipped with mechanical stirring and temperature control, and ground potassium hydroxide is added in stoichiometric excess relative to the aryl halide to ensure complete deprotonation of the alcohol. A phase-transfer catalyst, such as benzyltributylammonium bromide, is introduced in catalytic amounts, typically between 0.5 and 5 mol%, to facilitate the transfer of the alkoxide ion into the organic phase. The reaction mixture is then heated to a temperature between 65 °C and 75 °C and maintained for a period of 18 to 30 hours under ambient atmospheric conditions, without the use of nitrogen or argon purging. During this time, the etherification proceeds to completion, yielding the corresponding benzyl ether intermediate with yields exceeding 80% in most cases. Upon completion of the etherification, the reaction mixture is cooled to room temperature, and a minimal volume of dioxane or tetrahydrofuran, typically between 1 and 5 mL per gram of substrate, is added to solubilize any residual solids and facilitate the subsequent coupling step. A palladium catalyst, such as Pd(PPh₃)₂Cl₂, is then introduced along with triphenylphosphine in a molar ratio of 1:2 to 1:4 relative to the palladium, and an arylboronic acid, such as 4-methoxyphenylboronic acid or 3-formylphenylboronic acid, is added in a slight excess to ensure complete consumption of the aryl halide functionality. The reaction mixture is then reheated to 75 °C and stirred for an additional 12 to 24 hours, during which time the Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling proceeds efficiently, yielding the biphenylated ether product in yields ranging from 65% to 85%. In cases where the arylboronic acid contains electron-withdrawing substituents, such as aldehydes or nitriles, the yield may be lower, but the reaction remains viable, and the product may be purified by simple extraction with chloroform or ethyl acetate following aqueous workup. The crude product is then washed with water to remove inorganic salts, dried over anhydrous sodium sulfate, and concentrated under reduced pressure. In many instances, the resulting product crystallizes directly upon cooling, allowing for isolation by filtration without the need for chromatography. For compounds that remain as oils, column chromatography on silica gel may be employed, but the solvent volume required is significantly less than in conventional synthetic protocols due to the high purity of the reaction mixture. In a further embodiment, the hydroxymethyl-functionalized derivatives are prepared by reduction of the aldehyde-containing coupling products using sodium borohydride in a methanol-dioxane mixture at room temperature for 2 to 4 hours. The resulting alcohols are extracted, dried, and subjected to a second etherification reaction with another aryl halide, such as 4-bromobenzyl bromide, under the same phase-transfer catalytic conditions used in the first step, yielding bis-ether structures with enhanced molecular symmetry and thermal stability. These bis-ethers, such as those bearing two 4-bromobenzyl moieties and two phenylpropyl chains, exhibit melting points below 20 °C and thermal decomposition onset temperatures exceeding 300 °C, as confirmed by thermogravimetric analysis conducted under air at a heating rate of 10 °C/min. The physical properties of the synthesized compounds are determined using standard analytical techniques, including differential scanning calorimetry for melting and boiling points, gas chromatography-mass spectrometry for purity assessment, and a calibrated differential scanning calorimeter for specific heat capacity measurements at 300 K, with values ranging from 1.35 J/g·K to 1.72 J/g·K. Density is measured using a digital pycnometer over the temperature range of 20 °C to 90 °C, revealing a linear decrease in density with increasing temperature, with coefficients of thermal expansion between 0.0007 and 0.0009 per degree Celsius, comparable to water-based systems. Viscosity is measured using a rotational viscometer at 100 °C, with values consistently falling within the range of 5 to 15 cP, indicating low resistance to flow and minimal pumping energy requirements. In operational applications, the compounds are introduced into closed-loop thermal systems such as solar thermal power plants, where they circulate through evacuated tube collectors, heat exchangers, and molten salt storage tanks, transferring thermal energy from the solar field to a power generation unit. The fluids are circulated at velocities between 0.5 and 2.0 m/s, with system pressures maintained below 1.5 bar to prevent vaporization, and temperatures cycled between 40 °C and 280 °C over daily operational cycles. No signs of degradation, viscosity increase, or deposit formation are observed after 1000 hours of continuous operation, even in the presence of trace oxygen. The fluids are recovered at the end of their service life by distillation under vacuum, with recovery yields exceeding 95%, and the recovered material may be reused without significant loss of thermal performance. Alternative embodiments include the use of these compounds in industrial heat exchangers for chemical processing, where they replace steam or thermal oils in processes requiring precise temperature control between 150 °C and 250 °C, and in thermal energy storage units for renewable energy systems, where their high volumetric heat capacity allows for compact storage designs. The compounds may also be blended with minor amounts of stabilizing additives, such as phenolic antioxidants or metal deactivators, to further enhance long-term performance, though such additives are not required for basic operation. The invention thus encompasses not only the novel chemical compounds themselves but also the methods for their synthesis, purification, and application in thermal systems, providing a comprehensive technological solution that is both scientifically innovative and industrially scalable. The compounds of the invention represent a new class of heat transfer fluids that combine the thermal stability of synthetic aromatics, the low viscosity of aliphatic ethers, and the synthetic efficiency of one-pot methodologies, thereby addressing the longstanding challenges of energy efficiency, environmental impact, and operational reliability in high-temperature thermal systems.
