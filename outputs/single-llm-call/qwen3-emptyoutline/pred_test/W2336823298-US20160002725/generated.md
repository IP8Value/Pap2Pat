# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of neurodegenerative disease therapeutics, specifically to novel methods and compositions for the selective modulation of amyloid-beta peptide production in the context of Alzheimer’s disease. More particularly, the invention encompasses compositions and methods for inhibiting the pathological cleavage of the amyloid precursor protein (APP) at the γ-secretase site without interfering with the cleavage of other essential γ-secretase substrates, such as Notch, thereby avoiding the severe side effects associated with broad-spectrum γ-secretase inhibition. The invention further relates to the identification of a specific protein domain within the transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily C member 6 (TRPC6), namely the second transmembrane domain (TM2), as a critical mediator of this selective modulation. The invention provides for the use of peptides, polypeptides, or small molecules that mimic or enhance the interaction of the TRPC6 TM2 domain with the C-terminal fragment of APP (C99), thereby preventing its association with the γ-secretase complex and reducing the generation of neurotoxic amyloid-beta peptides. This approach represents a paradigm shift in Alzheimer’s disease therapeutics by targeting substrate-specific regulation of γ-secretase rather than its catalytic activity, offering a novel and highly selective strategy for reducing amyloid-beta accumulation while preserving physiological functions of γ-secretase in cell signaling and development.

## BACKGROUND

Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the accumulation of extracellular amyloid-beta plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein. The amyloid cascade hypothesis posits that the overproduction and/or impaired clearance of amyloid-beta peptides, particularly the 42-amino acid isoform (Aβ42), initiates a cascade of neuroinflammatory, synaptic, and neuronal degenerative events that ultimately lead to cognitive decline and dementia. The generation of amyloid-beta peptides occurs through the sequential proteolytic cleavage of the amyloid precursor protein (APP) by β-secretase (BACE1) and γ-secretase. While β-secretase cleavage is rate-limiting and highly regulated, γ-secretase cleavage is the final and determining step in the production of Aβ40 and Aβ42, and is carried out by a multi-subunit complex comprising presenilin (PS1 or PS2), nicastrin, anterior pharynx defective 1 (Aph1), and presenilin enhancer 2 (PEN2). 

Despite decades of research and numerous clinical trials, therapeutic strategies aimed at reducing amyloid-beta levels have largely failed. Early efforts focused on inhibiting γ-secretase activity directly using small molecule inhibitors or modulators. However, these compounds often caused severe adverse effects, including gastrointestinal toxicity, skin cancer, and impaired immune function, due to the inhibition of γ-secretase-mediated cleavage of essential substrates such as Notch, which plays a critical role in cell fate determination, hematopoiesis, and neuronal development. The failure of these agents in clinical trials underscored the urgent need for a therapeutic strategy that selectively reduces amyloid-beta production without disrupting the physiological functions of γ-secretase.

Alternative approaches have sought to modulate γ-secretase activity indirectly by targeting regulatory proteins or altering the subcellular localization of APP or the γ-secretase complex. Some proteins, such as GSAP, TMP21, GPR3, and Aph1B/C, have been identified as modulators that influence γ-secretase substrate preference, but their therapeutic utility remains limited. For instance, TMP21 has been shown to affect Aβ production, yet its overexpression leads to severe developmental abnormalities and premature death in animal models. Similarly, GSAP enhances Aβ generation but does not reduce it, and its mechanism of action involves binding to presenilin, potentially still interfering with other γ-secretase functions. Thus, there remains a critical unmet need for a therapeutic agent that can specifically interfere with the interaction between APP (C99) and the γ-secretase complex without affecting the cleavage of other substrates or altering the catalytic core of the enzyme.

The transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily C member 6 (TRPC6) is a non-selective cation channel known to mediate calcium influx in response to various stimuli, including diacylglycerol and receptor tyrosine kinase activation. TRPC6 has been implicated in diverse physiological processes such as neuronal development, cardiac hypertrophy, vascular tone regulation, and podocyte function in the kidney. Its activity has traditionally been attributed to its ion channel function, with all known cellular roles dependent on calcium entry. However, recent findings have begun to suggest that certain ion channels may exert functions independent of their ion-conducting properties, such as through scaffolding, signaling complex formation, or direct protein-protein interactions. Despite these emerging concepts, no prior art has described TRPC6 as a modulator of amyloid-beta production, nor has any study suggested that TRPC6 interacts directly with APP or influences γ-secretase cleavage specificity.

Furthermore, no prior art discloses the identification of a discrete domain within TRPC6—specifically the second transmembrane region (TM2)—as the critical mediator of its interaction with APP (C99) and its ability to inhibit γ-secretase cleavage of APP without affecting Notch or other substrates. The concept that a transmembrane domain of a cation channel, rather than its cytoplasmic regions or channel activity, is responsible for a selective protein-protein interaction with APP to modulate amyloid-beta production was entirely unexpected and unanticipated. Moreover, the development of a cell-permeable peptide derived from this domain—TAT-TM2—that recapitulates the inhibitory effect of full-length TRPC6 on Aβ production in vitro and in vivo, without altering γ-secretase activity toward Notch, represents a groundbreaking advance in the field. The prior art contains no teaching, suggestion, or motivation to synthesize or utilize such a peptide for the treatment of Alzheimer’s disease, nor does it disclose the structural determinants necessary for this specific interaction. Therefore, the invention described herein fills a substantial gap in the therapeutic landscape of Alzheimer’s disease by providing a novel, highly selective, and mechanistically distinct approach to reducing amyloid-beta accumulation through targeted disruption of the APP–γ-secretase interface.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of Alzheimer’s disease and other amyloid-beta-associated neurodegenerative disorders by selectively inhibiting the cleavage of amyloid precursor protein (APP) by γ-secretase without impairing the cleavage of other physiologically essential substrates such as Notch. This is achieved through the discovery that the transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily C member 6 (TRPC6) interacts directly with the C-terminal fragment of APP (C99) and prevents its association with the γ-secretase complex, thereby reducing the production of amyloid-beta peptides. Crucially, this inhibitory effect is mediated by a specific transmembrane domain of TRPC6, known as the second transmembrane domain (TM2), and does not require the ion channel activity of TRPC6. The invention further provides for the use of peptides, polypeptides, or small molecules that mimic the structure and function of the TRPC6 TM2 domain to achieve the same selective inhibition of Aβ production. These agents bind to C99 and sterically hinder its interaction with the γ-secretase complex, thereby reducing Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels in neuronal and non-neuronal cells, in animal models of Alzheimer’s disease, and in vitro cleavage assays using purified components. The invention demonstrates that such inhibition does not affect the cleavage of Notch, E-cadherin, N-cadherin, or other γ-secretase substrates, thereby avoiding the severe toxicities associated with global γ-secretase inhibitors. The invention also provides compositions comprising the TAT-TM2 peptide or its functional analogs, methods of administering such compositions to patients, and diagnostic tools for identifying individuals who would benefit from such therapy based on TRPC6 expression levels or APP genotype. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in Alzheimer’s disease therapeutics by targeting substrate-specific regulation of γ-secretase rather than its catalytic activity, offering a safe, effective, and mechanistically novel approach to reducing amyloid-beta accumulation and preserving synaptic and cognitive function.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is based on the unprecedented discovery that the transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily C member 6 (TRPC6) functions as a selective endogenous modulator of γ-secretase cleavage of the amyloid precursor protein (APP), specifically inhibiting the production of amyloid-beta peptides without affecting the cleavage of other essential γ-secretase substrates such as Notch. This function is mediated not by the ion channel activity of TRPC6, which has been the sole focus of prior investigations into its biological roles, but by a direct, high-affinity protein-protein interaction between a specific transmembrane domain of TRPC6 and the C-terminal fragment of APP (C99). The interaction occurs in intracellular membrane compartments, likely within endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, or endosomal membranes, where γ-secretase activity is known to occur, and prevents the recruitment of C99 into the γ-secretase complex, thereby reducing the efficiency of its cleavage. Importantly, the presence of TRPC6 does not alter the expression levels of γ-secretase components, including presenilin-1, presenilin-2, nicastrin, or PEN2, nor does it affect the intrinsic enzymatic activity of γ-secretase as measured by fluorogenic substrate assays. Furthermore, TRPC6 does not influence the cleavage of APP by α- or β-secretase, nor does it alter the expression or activity of known Aβ-degrading enzymes such as neprilysin, insulin-degrading enzyme, or clusterin. The inhibitory effect of TRPC6 on Aβ production is conserved across multiple cell types, including primary cortical neurons, HEK293 cells stably expressing APP, and COS7 cells expressing C99, and is recapitulated in vivo in transgenic mouse models of Alzheimer’s disease. The discovery that TRPC6’s function in this context is independent of its channel activity was confirmed through multiple lines of evidence: pharmacological activation or inhibition of TRPC6 channels using OAG or SKF96365 had no effect on Aβ levels; expression of a dominant-negative TRPC6 mutant, which abolishes channel function, still reduced Aβ production; and mutations that disrupt TRPC6-mediated calcium influx did not impair its ability to suppress Aβ generation. This indicates that TRPC6 acts as a scaffold or competitive inhibitor in this context, rather than as a signaling molecule dependent on ion flux. The critical domain responsible for this interaction was identified as the second transmembrane helix (TM2) of TRPC6, located between amino acid residues 487 and 508. Mutations within this region, including point mutations, amino acid substitutions, or helix reversals, completely abolished the ability of TRPC6 to reduce Aβ levels, as confirmed by co-immunoprecipitation assays showing loss of interaction with C99. Conversely, synthetic peptides derived from this domain, fused to the cell-penetrating TAT sequence, were able to enter cells, bind to C99, and inhibit Aβ production in a dose-dependent manner both in vitro and in vivo. These peptides did not affect the cleavage of Notch or other γ-secretase substrates, demonstrating exquisite substrate specificity. The invention further demonstrates that the TAT-TM2 peptide reduces amyloid plaque burden in the brains of APP/PS1 transgenic mice, improves synaptic density as measured by Golgi staining, and rescues spatial learning deficits in the Morris water maze, without inducing any observable toxicity or behavioral abnormalities. The mechanism of action is proposed to involve direct binding of the TM2 domain to the transmembrane or juxtamembrane region of C99, thereby sterically occluding its access to the active site of γ-secretase or altering its conformation to render it a poor substrate. This mechanism is distinct from all previously described γ-secretase modulators, which either bind to presenilin, alter complex assembly, or change the lipid microenvironment. The invention provides for the use of the TM2 domain or its functional mimetics as therapeutic agents for Alzheimer’s disease, either as synthetic peptides, peptidomimetics, or small molecules designed to replicate its structure and binding interface. The invention further encompasses methods of identifying patients who would benefit from such therapy, including those with low endogenous TRPC6 expression, specific APP mutations near the γ-secretase cleavage site, or elevated Aβ42/Aβ40 ratios. The invention also provides diagnostic kits for detecting TRPC6 levels in cerebrospinal fluid, blood-derived exosomes, or brain tissue biopsies, as well as methods for monitoring therapeutic response through longitudinal measurement of Aβ levels in biofluids. The invention thus represents a fundamentally new approach to Alzheimer’s disease therapeutics, one that leverages an endogenous regulatory mechanism to achieve selective inhibition of pathogenic amyloid-beta production while preserving the vital physiological functions of γ-secretase.

### EXAMPLE 1

In this example, the effect of TRPC6 expression on amyloid-beta production was systematically evaluated in primary cultured rat cortical neurons and in HEK293 cells stably expressing the full-length human amyloid precursor protein (HEK293APP). Primary cortical neurons were isolated from embryonic day 17 rat brains and maintained in Neurobasal A medium supplemented with B27 and Glutamax. At 3 days in vitro, neurons were transfected with small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting TRPC6, TRPC5, Cav1.2, Cav3.1, or Cav3.3, or with a non-targeting control siRNA. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by quantitative PCR and immunoblotting, and knockdown of target proteins was consistently greater than 70% compared to control. Culture medium was collected after 48 hours and analyzed for Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) with commercially available kits. The results demonstrated a significant and selective increase in both Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels in neurons transfected with TRPC6-targeting siRNAs, whereas knockdown of TRPC5, Cav1.2, Cav3.1, or Cav3.3 had no significant effect on Aβ levels. This finding indicated that TRPC6, and not other calcium-permeable channels, plays a unique and specific role in suppressing Aβ production in neurons. To confirm this observation in a heterologous system, HEK293APP cells were transfected with plasmids encoding either full-length human TRPC6 or TRPC5. After 48 hours, culture medium was collected and analyzed for Aβ40, Aβ42, Aβ38, and total Aβ1-x peptides. Expression of TRPC6 resulted in a marked and statistically significant reduction in all measured Aβ species, with reductions ranging from 50% to 70% depending on the isoform, whereas expression of TRPC5 had no effect on Aβ levels. These results were consistent across multiple independent transfections and were confirmed using two different ELISA kits from distinct manufacturers. To determine whether this effect was dependent on TRPC6 channel activity, cells were treated with SKF96365, a well-characterized inhibitor of TRPC channels, or OAG, a diacylglycerol analog known to activate TRPC6. Neither compound altered Aβ levels in TRPC6-expressing cells, indicating that modulation of ion flux through TRPC6 was not required for its inhibitory effect. Furthermore, expression of a dominant-negative TRPC6 mutant (DN-TRPC6), which lacks functional channel activity due to a point mutation in the pore region, still significantly reduced Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels to the same extent as wild-type TRPC6. In fact, DN-TRPC6 expression alone, even in the absence of exogenous TRPC6, was sufficient to reduce Aβ levels, suggesting that the mutant may act as a competitive inhibitor by forming non-functional heteromers with endogenous TRPC6 or by binding to C99 without permitting channel assembly. These findings collectively demonstrate that TRPC6 suppresses Aβ production through a mechanism entirely independent of its canonical ion channel function, and that this function is specific to TRPC6 and not shared by other members of the TRPC family or voltage-gated calcium channels.

### EXAMPLE 2

This example investigates the mechanism by which TRPC6 reduces amyloid-beta production, focusing on the processing of amyloid precursor protein (APP) and the activity of secretases involved in its cleavage. First, the levels of full-length APP and its cleavage products were analyzed in HEK293APP cells expressing TRPC6 versus control cells. Immunoblotting revealed no significant difference in the abundance of full-length APP, sAPPα (the product of α-secretase cleavage), or the C-terminal fragments C83 and C99 (products of α- and β-secretase cleavage, respectively). This indicated that TRPC6 does not influence the initial cleavage events mediated by α- or β-secretase. To further confirm this, in vitro enzymatic assays were performed using fluorogenic substrates specific for α-secretase (Abz-APP-KLVFFAEDV-EDDnp) and β-secretase (Abz-SEVNLDAEFRHDSGYEVK-EDDnp). The fluorescence signal generated by the cleavage of these substrates by lysates from TRPC6-expressing cells was indistinguishable from that of control lysates, confirming that TRPC6 does not alter the catalytic activity of either secretase. Next, the expression levels of key enzymes involved in Aβ degradation were examined. Immunoblots of cell lysates showed no change in the abundance of insulin-degrading enzyme, neprilysin, or clusterin in TRPC6-expressing cells compared to controls. To assess whether TRPC6 enhances Aβ clearance, an Aβ clearance assay was performed in which HEK293 cells were incubated with exogenous synthetic Aβ42 for 12 hours. The amount of Aβ remaining in the culture medium and the amount internalized into the cells were quantified by ELISA. No difference was observed between TRPC6-expressing and control cells, indicating that TRPC6 does not enhance the degradation or efflux of Aβ. These findings collectively ruled out the possibility that TRPC6 reduces Aβ levels by altering APP processing upstream of γ-secretase or by enhancing Aβ clearance. The focus therefore shifted to γ-secretase, the enzyme complex responsible for the final cleavage of C99 to generate Aβ peptides and the APP intracellular domain (AICD). In HEK293C99 cells stably expressing the C99 fragment, expression of TRPC6 significantly reduced both Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels in the culture medium and decreased the generation of AICD, as detected by immunoblotting with an antibody specific for the C-terminal end of AICD. Importantly, the levels of C99 itself were unchanged, indicating that TRPC6 does not promote its degradation but rather inhibits its cleavage. To determine whether TRPC6 affects the expression of γ-secretase components, immunoblots were performed for presenilin-1, presenilin-2, nicastrin, and PEN2. No alterations in protein levels were detected in TRPC6-expressing cells. Furthermore, in vitro γ-secretase activity assays using a fluorogenic substrate (Abz-VTSEVNLDAEFRHDSGYEVK-EDDnp) revealed no difference in enzymatic activity between lysates from TRPC6-expressing and control cells. These results demonstrated that TRPC6 does not inhibit the catalytic function of γ-secretase as a whole. To test whether TRPC6 selectively affects APP cleavage while sparing other γ-secretase substrates, the cleavage of Notch and cadherins was examined. In HEK293 cells expressing NotchΔE, a constitutively active form of Notch that is directly cleaved by γ-secretase, the levels of the Notch intracellular domain (NICD) were unchanged in the presence of TRPC6. Similarly, the cleavage of E-cadherin and N-cadherin, assessed by the ratio of CTF2 (the γ-secretase-derived fragment) to CTF1 (the α-secretase-derived fragment), was unaffected by TRPC6 expression. These findings established that TRPC6 specifically inhibits γ-secretase cleavage of C99 without affecting the cleavage of other substrates, a property that distinguishes it from all previously described γ-secretase inhibitors and positions it as a uniquely selective modulator of amyloid-beta production.

### EXAMPLE 3

This example provides direct evidence that TRPC6 physically interacts with the C-terminal fragment of APP (C99) and that this interaction is essential for its inhibitory effect on γ-secretase cleavage. To investigate subcellular co-localization, HEK293 cells were co-transfected with TRPC6 tagged with a hemagglutinin (HA) epitope and C99 tagged with a Myc epitope. After 48 hours, cells were fixed, permeabilized, and stained with fluorescently labeled antibodies against HA and Myc. High-resolution confocal microscopy revealed a significant degree of co-localization between TRPC6 and C99 in punctate structures within the cytoplasm, particularly in regions adjacent to the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus, as confirmed by co-staining with markers such as Calnexin and GM130. This co-localization was not observed when TRPC5 was co-expressed with C99, indicating specificity. To determine whether this co-localization reflects a direct physical interaction, co-immunoprecipitation experiments were performed using lysates from HEK293 cells overexpressing TRPC6 and APP. Immunoprecipitation with an antibody against APP successfully pulled down TRPC6, as detected by immunoblotting with an anti-TRPC6 antibody. Conversely, immunoprecipitation with an anti-TRPC6 antibody pulled down APP, confirming a reciprocal interaction. This interaction was specific, as TRPC6 was not co-immunoprecipitated with presenilin-1, nicastrin, Notch, or APLP2 under identical conditions. The interaction was also observed in lysates from mouse brain tissue, where endogenous TRPC6 and APP fragments (CTF) were found in the same fractions following sucrose gradient fractionation, further supporting physiological relevance. To determine whether the interaction occurs with the C99 fragment specifically, reciprocal immunoprecipitations were performed in HEK293C99 cells expressing TRPC6. TRPC6 was robustly co-immunoprecipitated with C99, while TRPC5 was not. Moreover, when TRPC6 was expressed in HEK293 cells carrying familial Alzheimer’s disease-linked mutations in APP (I716F or V717F), which lie adjacent to the γ-secretase cleavage site, the interaction with TRPC6 was preserved and even enhanced, suggesting that the binding interface involves the transmembrane or juxtamembrane region of C99. To further confirm that TRPC6 interferes with the interaction between C99 and the γ-secretase complex, co-immunoprecipitation experiments were performed to assess the association between C99 and presenilin-1. In control cells, C99 and PS1 were readily co-immunoprecipitated. However, when TRPC6 was co-expressed, the amount of PS1 pulled down with C99 was dramatically reduced, while the amount of TRPC6 pulled down with C99 increased. This suggests that TRPC6 competes with PS1 for binding to C99, thereby preventing the formation of the productive γ-secretase–C99 complex. Importantly, the interaction between TRPC6 and C99 was not affected by the presence of APP, as shown by calcium imaging experiments in which hyperforin-induced calcium influx through TRPC6 was unchanged in the presence or absence of APP, indicating that APP does not modulate TRPC6 channel activity. These findings collectively establish that TRPC6 binds directly and selectively to C99, and that this binding displaces or prevents the association of C99 with the γ-secretase complex, thereby inhibiting Aβ generation without altering the catalytic machinery of γ-secretase itself.

### EXAMPLE 4

This example identifies the specific domain within TRPC6 that is responsible for its interaction with C99 and its ability to inhibit amyloid-beta production. TRPC6 is a six-transmembrane domain protein with cytoplasmic N- and C-terminal regions. To determine which region mediates the interaction with C99, a series of truncation mutants of TRPC6 were generated and expressed in HEK293APP cells. Constructs encompassing the N-terminal region (amino acids 1–405), the C-terminal region (725–930), and various internal fragments were fused to a C-terminal HA tag for detection. Aβ levels in the culture medium were measured by ELISA after 48 hours. None of the constructs containing only the N- or C-terminal domains were able to reduce Aβ levels, indicating that these regions are not sufficient for the inhibitory function. However, constructs containing amino acids 437–508 consistently reduced Aβ production by approximately 60%, while constructs lacking this region, even if they contained other transmembrane domains, had no effect. This identified a minimal functional domain between residues 437 and 508 as critical for TRPC6’s ability to suppress Aβ generation. Further analysis revealed that this region contains the first and second transmembrane helices (TM1 and TM2) and the intervening extracellular loop. To determine which of these subdomains was essential, site-directed mutagenesis was performed on the TM2 region (residues 487–508). Four distinct mutations were introduced: C6-mut1 (C496A and C497A), C6-mut2 (I498A and L499A), C6-mut3 (replacement of TM2 with a helix from TRPC5), and C6-mut4 (reversal of the amino acid sequence of TM2). All four mutants were expressed at levels comparable to wild-type TRPC6 and retained normal subcellular localization, as confirmed by immunofluorescence and co-localization with APP. However, none of the mutants were able to reduce Aβ levels, and immunoprecipitation experiments showed that they failed to bind to APP or C99. This demonstrated that the precise amino acid sequence and structural conformation of the TM2 domain are essential for the interaction with C99. To confirm that the loss of function was not due to misfolding or aggregation, the mutants were tested for their ability to mediate calcium influx in response to hyperforin. All mutants retained normal channel activity, confirming that the mutations specifically disrupted the protein-protein interaction function without affecting the overall folding or ion channel function of TRPC6. These results establish that the TM2 domain of TRPC6 is both necessary and sufficient for its interaction with C99 and its inhibition of γ-secretase cleavage, and that this function is entirely separable from its ion channel activity. The identification of this domain provides a precise molecular target for therapeutic intervention, enabling the design of peptides, peptidomimetics, or small molecules that mimic or stabilize this interaction.

### EXAMPLE 5

This example demonstrates that a synthetic cell-permeable peptide derived from the TM2 domain of TRPC6, fused to the HIV TAT protein transduction domain, effectively inhibits amyloid-beta production in vitro and recapitulates the biological effects of full-length TRPC6. A 22-amino acid peptide encompassing residues 487–508 of human TRPC6 (sequence: KFVLLFVLLVLLVLLVLLVLLV) was synthesized and fused at its N-terminus to the TAT peptide (YGRKKRRQRRR), resulting in the construct TAT-TM2. As a control, a mutated version of this peptide (TAT-TM2-mut1) was generated by substituting five key hydrophobic residues (L494, L496, L498, L500, L502) with alanine, disrupting the helical hydrophobic face critical for interaction. A second control peptide, TAT-TM1, containing the first transmembrane domain of TRPC6 (residues 437–458), was also synthesized. All peptides were purified by HPLC and confirmed for purity and sequence by mass spectrometry. HEK293APP cells and primary rat cortical neurons were treated with increasing concentrations of TAT-TM2, TAT-TM2-mut1, or TAT-TM1 for 24 to 48 hours. Culture medium was collected and Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels were quantified by ELISA. TAT-TM2 caused a dose-dependent reduction in Aβ levels, with maximal inhibition of 65–70% at 10 μM, while TAT-TM2-mut1 and TAT-TM1 had no effect at any concentration tested. To determine which portion of the TM2 domain was critical, two shorter peptides were synthesized: TAT-TM2-N (residues 487–496) and TAT-TM2-C (residues 497–508). Only TAT-TM2-C, the C-terminal half of the domain, significantly reduced Aβ levels, indicating that the C-terminal 11 amino acids are sufficient for biological activity. Mutations within this 11-amino acid segment (e.g., L498A, L500A, L502A) abolished the inhibitory effect, confirming the importance of these residues. To confirm that TAT-TM2 binds directly to APP, a biotinylated version of TAT-TM2 was synthesized and incubated with HEK293APP cell lysates. Streptavidin pull-down assays revealed that TAT-TM2-biotin specifically bound to APP, while TAT-biotin alone did not. Binding was competed by excess unlabeled TAT-TM2 or full-length TRPC6, but not by TAT-TM2-mut1. Furthermore, TAT-TM2 did not bind to TRPC6 itself, confirming that it acts as a decoy for APP, not as a TRPC6 mimetic. These results demonstrate that TAT-TM2 functions as a molecular mimic of the TRPC6 TM2 domain, binding directly to C99 and preventing its interaction with γ-secretase. The peptide’s ability to cross cell membranes without the need for transfection or viral delivery makes it a highly viable therapeutic candidate, and its specificity for APP over other γ-secretase substrates ensures a favorable safety profile.

### EXAMPLE 6

This example evaluates the ability of the TAT-TM2 peptide to inhibit γ-secretase cleavage of C99 in a cell-free, purified system, thereby confirming that its mechanism of action is direct and does not require cellular signaling or trafficking machinery. The C99 fragment of APP was expressed in Escherichia coli as a His-tagged fusion protein and purified to homogeneity using nickel-affinity chromatography. The purified C99 was reconstituted in a lipid environment mimicking endosomal membranes. γ-Secretase activity was isolated from HEK293 cell membranes by solubilization with CHAPSO detergent and purification by ultracentrifugation. The in vitro cleavage assay was performed by incubating purified C99 with γ-secretase in a buffered reaction mixture containing 50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 6.8, 2 mM EDTA, and 0.25% CHAPSO at 37°C for four hours. Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels in the supernatant were quantified by ELISA. Addition of TAT-TM2 peptide at concentrations of 1, 5, and 10 μM resulted in a dose-dependent reduction in Aβ production, with maximal inhibition of 68% at 10 μM. In contrast, TAT-TM2-mut1, TAT-TM1, and TAT alone had no effect on Aβ levels at any concentration tested. The cleavage of C99 to generate AICD was also assessed by immunoblotting, and a corresponding reduction in AICD levels was observed in the presence of TAT-TM2. To confirm substrate specificity, the same assay was performed using purified NotchΔE as the substrate. TAT-TM2 had no effect on the production of the Notch intracellular domain (NICD), whereas a known γ-secretase inhibitor, L685,458, completely blocked NICD generation. These results provide unambiguous evidence that TAT-TM2 directly interferes with the cleavage of C99 by γ-secretase in the absence of any cellular components, confirming that its mechanism is based on steric hindrance or conformational disruption of the C99–γ-secretase interface. The peptide does not inhibit the catalytic site of γ-secretase, nor does it alter the stability of C99 or the activity of the enzyme complex. This cell-free system eliminates any confounding variables related to cellular uptake, trafficking, or signaling, and establishes TAT-TM2 as a direct, selective, and potent inhibitor of C99 cleavage.

### EXAMPLE 7

This example demonstrates that TAT-TM2 reduces amyloid-beta pathology and improves cognitive function in a transgenic mouse model of Alzheimer’s disease. Male and female APP/PS1 transgenic mice, which overexpress mutant human APP and presenilin-1 and develop robust amyloid plaque deposition by six months of age, were used. Mice were injected intraperitoneally with either TAT-TM2 (2.5 mM, 200 μL), TAT-TM2-mut1 (2.5 mM, 200 μL), or saline vehicle three times per week for four weeks. At the end of the treatment period, mice were sacrificed and brains were collected for histological and biochemical analysis. Immunohistochemistry using the 6E10 antibody revealed a marked reduction in amyloid plaque burden in the cortex and hippocampus of TAT-TM2-treated mice compared to vehicle- or TAT-TM2-mut1-treated controls. Plaque load, quantified as the percentage of area covered by Aβ-positive staining, was reduced by approximately 55% in TAT-TM2-treated mice. Similar results were obtained using the MOAB-2 antibody, which recognizes a conformational epitope on Aβ, confirming the specificity of the reduction. Biochemical analysis of brain homogenates showed a significant decrease in both soluble and insoluble Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels in TAT-TM2-treated mice, as measured by ELISA after extraction with TBST and guanidine hydrochloride. Total Aβ levels were reduced by 50–60%, while Aβ42/Aβ40 ratios were unchanged, indicating that the peptide reduces overall Aβ production without altering cleavage site preference. To assess synaptic integrity, Golgi staining was performed on brain sections from treated mice. Dendritic spine density in the CA1 region of the hippocampus was significantly increased in TAT-TM2-treated APP/PS1 mice compared to vehicle-treated controls, reaching levels comparable to those in non-transgenic wild-type mice. Behavioral testing using the Morris water maze was conducted at 11 months of age. During the training phase, TAT-TM2-treated mice showed significantly shorter escape latencies than vehicle-treated APP/PS1 mice, indicating improved spatial learning. In the probe trial, where the platform was removed, TAT-TM2-treated mice crossed the former platform location significantly more times, demonstrating enhanced spatial memory retention. No differences in swim speed or thigmotaxis were observed, ruling out motor or anxiety-related confounds. Importantly, TAT-TM2 treatment did not induce any signs of toxicity, weight loss, or behavioral abnormalities in wild-type mice, and did not affect the cleavage of Notch in brain tissue, as confirmed by immunoblotting for NICD. These results demonstrate that TAT-TM2 not only reduces amyloid pathology but also rescues synaptic and cognitive deficits in a relevant animal model of Alzheimer’s disease, validating its therapeutic potential.

### EXAMPLE 8

This example explores the therapeutic potential of TAT-TM2 in diverse genetic and pathological contexts relevant to Alzheimer’s disease. To determine whether TAT-TM2 is effective in the presence of familial Alzheimer’s disease mutations, HEK293APP cells expressing either wild-type APP, APP-I716F, or APP-V717F were treated with TAT-TM2. All three cell lines showed a comparable reduction in Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels upon peptide treatment, indicating that the presence of these mutations does not impair the ability of TAT-TM2 to bind C99 or inhibit γ-secretase cleavage. This is consistent with the finding that TRPC6 interacts with both wild-type and mutant APP with similar affinity. To assess whether TAT-TM2 can be effective in aged or advanced pathology, APP/PS1 mice were treated with TAT-TM2 starting at 10 months of age, when amyloid plaques are already abundant and cognitive deficits are pronounced. Even under these conditions, TAT-TM2 treatment for four weeks significantly reduced plaque burden and improved performance in the Morris water maze, suggesting that the peptide can exert therapeutic effects even after pathology is established. To evaluate the effect of TRPC6 expression levels on TAT-TM2 efficacy, TRPC6 knockout mice were crossed with APP/PS1 mice to generate APP/PS1/TRPC6−/− animals. These mice exhibited higher Aβ levels and more severe cognitive deficits than APP/PS1 mice with intact TRPC6. Treatment of these mice with TAT-TM2 restored Aβ levels and cognitive performance to those seen in APP/PS1 mice with normal TRPC6 expression, demonstrating that TAT-TM2 can compensate for endogenous TRPC6 deficiency. To determine whether TAT-TM2 affects other neurodegenerative pathways, its effect on tau phosphorylation was assessed. No change in phospho-tau levels at Thr181, Ser202/Thr205, or Ser396/404 was observed in TAT-TM2-treated mice, indicating that the peptide does not influence tau pathology directly. To assess potential off-target effects, a proteomic screen was performed on brain lysates from TAT-TM2-treated versus control mice using mass spectrometry. No significant changes in the abundance of proteins involved in synaptic function, inflammation, apoptosis, or γ-secretase complex assembly were detected, further supporting the specificity of the peptide’s action. These findings collectively demonstrate that TAT-TM2 is effective across multiple genetic backgrounds, at different stages of disease progression, and in the absence of endogenous TRPC6, making it a broadly applicable therapeutic strategy for Alzheimer’s disease.

### EXAMPLE 9

This example describes the development of pharmaceutical compositions and formulations for the delivery of TAT-TM2 or its functional analogs for the treatment of Alzheimer’s disease and related disorders. The TAT-TM2 peptide, or its stabilized variants, may be formulated for systemic, intranasal, or intracerebroventricular administration. For systemic delivery, the peptide may be conjugated to polyethylene glycol (PEG) to enhance plasma half-life and reduce renal clearance, or encapsulated in liposomes or nanoparticles designed to cross the blood-brain barrier via receptor-mediated transcytosis. For intranasal delivery, TAT-TM2 may be formulated as a nasal spray or gel in a buffered saline solution containing penetration enhancers such as chitosan or zonula occludens toxin, facilitating direct transport from the nasal epithelium to the olfactory bulb and cerebrospinal fluid. For sustained release, TAT-TM2 may be incorporated into biodegradable polymers such as PLGA (poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid)) microspheres, which release the peptide over periods of weeks to months following subcutaneous or intramuscular injection. To improve stability against proteolytic degradation, the peptide may be modified with D-amino acids, N-methylation, or cyclization while retaining its binding affinity for C99. Alternatively, small molecule mimetics of the TM2 domain may be developed using structure-based drug design, guided by the crystal structure of the TRPC6 TM2–C99 interface, if obtainable, or by computational docking and virtual screening of compound libraries. These small molecules may be optimized for oral bioavailability, blood-brain barrier penetration, and metabolic stability. The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits for identifying patients most likely to benefit from TAT-TM2 therapy, including assays for measuring TRPC6 mRNA or protein levels in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, cerebrospinal fluid, or exosomes derived from brain tissue. Patients with low TRPC6 expression, elevated Aβ42/Aβ40 ratios, or specific APP mutations near the γ-secretase cleavage site may be prioritized for treatment. The invention also includes methods for monitoring therapeutic response, such as serial measurement of Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels in cerebrospinal fluid or plasma using ultrasensitive immunoassays, or imaging of amyloid burden using PET tracers such as florbetapir or flutemetamol. The invention further provides for combination therapies, in which TAT-TM2 is administered alongside anti-tau antibodies, neuroinflammatory modulators, or synaptic enhancers to achieve synergistic effects. The safety profile of TAT-TM2, as demonstrated in multiple animal models, supports its potential for clinical translation, and its mechanism of action—targeting substrate-specific regulation of γ-secretase—offers a fundamentally new approach to Alzheimer’s disease therapeutics that avoids the toxicities associated with global γ-secretase inhibition. The invention thus provides a comprehensive platform for the development of a novel class of disease-modifying therapies for Alzheimer’s disease and related amyloidopathies.
