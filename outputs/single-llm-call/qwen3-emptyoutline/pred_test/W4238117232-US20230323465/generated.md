# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics, specifically to methods and compositions for the preoperative assessment of thyroid nodules using quantitative nucleic acid profiling of specific transcript isoforms. More particularly, the invention provides a novel biomarker panel comprising five distinct splice variants—CDH3 isoform, FNDC4 isoform, HMGA2 isoform, KLK7 isoform, and PLAG1 isoform—each selected for its differential expression in malignant versus benign thyroid neoplasms, including the recently reclassified noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP). The invention further encompasses a reference load-control marker, TPO1, a specific isoform of thyroid peroxidase that is stably expressed in thyrocyte-derived tissues but absent in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, thereby enabling accurate normalization of target gene expression levels in fine needle aspiration (FNA) samples contaminated with non-thyroid cellular components. The disclosed methods enable the reliable classification of thyroid nodules into malignant (papillary thyroid carcinoma and follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma) and benign (adenomatoid nodules, follicular adenomas, Hürthle cell adenomas, and NIFTP) categories using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) performed on RNA extracted from minimally invasive FNA samples. The invention further includes kits for the detection of these biomarkers and algorithms for calculating diagnostic probabilities based on composite expression scores, thereby facilitating clinical decision-making regarding surgical intervention, observation, or other therapeutic strategies. The invention is particularly suited for use in clinical laboratories performing molecular diagnostics on cytologically indeterminate thyroid FNA samples, where traditional histopathological evaluation is insufficient to distinguish between indolent and aggressive lesions.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Thyroid cancer has emerged as one of the most rapidly increasing malignancies in the United States and globally, with incidence rates nearly quadrupling over the past fifteen years, primarily driven by the rising detection of papillary thyroid carcinoma. The majority of thyroid cancers are discovered incidentally through the evaluation of palpable or imaging-detected nodules, which are present in over half of the adult population. Fine needle aspiration (FNA) cytology remains the standard initial diagnostic modality for evaluating these nodules due to its minimally invasive nature, cost-effectiveness, and high negative predictive value for benign disease. However, a substantial proportion of FNA samples—approximately 20 to 30 percent—are classified as indeterminate under the Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology, encompassing categories III (atypia of undetermined significance/follicular lesion of undetermined significance), IV (follicular neoplasm/suspicious for a follicular neoplasm), and V (suspicious for malignancy). These indeterminate results create significant clinical uncertainty, often leading to diagnostic thyroid lobectomy for definitive histopathological diagnosis, despite the fact that the majority of such nodules ultimately prove to be benign.

The introduction of the noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP) as a distinct diagnostic entity has further complicated the diagnostic landscape. Previously classified as a variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma, NIFTP was reclassified in 2016 as a non-malignant neoplasm due to its extremely low potential for metastasis or recurrence, with a reported recurrence rate of less than one percent. This reclassification has profound implications for patient management, as it suggests that many patients previously subjected to total thyroidectomy and radioactive iodine ablation for what was considered malignant disease may now be candidates for less aggressive interventions, such as lobectomy alone or even active surveillance. Unfortunately, NIFTP cannot be reliably distinguished from invasive follicular variant papillary thyroid carcinoma (FVPTC) by cytological examination alone, as both share identical nuclear features and follicular architecture. Histopathological confirmation of capsular and vascular invasion remains the only definitive method to differentiate NIFTP from FVPTC, necessitating surgical resection for diagnosis—a paradigm that undermines the goal of avoiding overtreatment.

In response to these challenges, molecular diagnostic assays have been developed to supplement cytological evaluation. Commercially available platforms such as ThyroSeq v3, Afirma Gene Expression Classifier, and ThygenX/ThyraMIR have been widely adopted in clinical practice. These tests employ next-generation sequencing to detect somatic mutations, gene fusions, and gene expression signatures associated with thyroid malignancy. However, their diagnostic accuracy has been substantially compromised by the reclassification of NIFTP. Because these assays were developed and validated prior to the formal recognition of NIFTP as a non-malignant entity, they were trained on datasets in which NIFTP was categorized as malignant. Consequently, these tests continue to classify NIFTP as “suspicious” or “positive” for malignancy, leading to a marked reduction in their positive predictive value (PPV). For instance, studies have shown that the PPV of ThyroSeq v2 decreased from approximately 43% to 14%, and the PPV of the Afirma classifier dropped from 30% to 25% when NIFTP cases were reclassified as benign. This decline reflects a fundamental limitation of mutation-based and expression-based classifiers that lack the specificity to distinguish between molecularly similar but clinically distinct entities.

Moreover, existing molecular tests are subject to additional limitations. Mutation-based assays are inherently constrained by the low prevalence of driver mutations in benign nodules, such as RAS mutations, which occur in up to 40% of follicular adenomas and NIFTPs, thereby generating a high rate of false positives. Gene expression classifiers, while more sensitive, often lack the specificity to discriminate between histologically distinct subtypes of follicular-patterned lesions. Furthermore, all current molecular platforms are vulnerable to sample quality issues inherent to FNA specimens. FNA samples are typically composed of a heterogeneous mixture of thyroid follicular cells, stromal elements, inflammatory cells, and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), with the proportion of thyrocytes varying widely between samples. Standard housekeeping genes such as glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) or beta-actin, commonly used for normalization in quantitative PCR, reflect total RNA content rather than thyrocyte-specific content. As a result, samples with low thyrocyte abundance may yield falsely low expression values for target genes, leading to false-negative interpretations, or conversely, high expression of non-thyroid transcripts may lead to false-positive signals. The absence of a reliable, thyrocyte-specific internal control has thus remained a persistent technical obstacle in the development of robust molecular diagnostic assays for thyroid FNA.

Previous research by the inventors identified a panel of differentially expressed transcripts in frozen thyroid tumor tissues using genome-wide microarray analysis. Subsequent validation studies narrowed this panel to 14 candidate genes, of which three were initially proposed as potential biomarkers. However, these early studies did not account for the full spectrum of splice variants, nor did they incorporate a thyrocyte-specific load-control marker. The present invention overcomes these limitations by systematically evaluating a broader set of transcript isoforms derived from 12 candidate genes, identifying five that exhibit robust and reproducible differential expression between malignant and benign thyroid neoplasms—including NIFTP—and by discovering a novel isoform of thyroid peroxidase (TPO1) that serves as a highly specific and stable reference for thyrocyte content. This combination of biomarkers and normalization strategy enables the first molecular diagnostic test capable of accurately distinguishing NIFTP from invasive FVPTC and PTC in preoperative FNA samples, thereby addressing the most pressing clinical need in thyroid nodule management today.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for the preoperative molecular classification of thyroid nodules using a panel of five specific transcript isoforms—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—each selected for its ability to distinguish malignant thyroid carcinomas (papillary thyroid carcinoma and follicular variant papillary thyroid carcinoma) from benign thyroid neoplasms (adenomatoid nodules, follicular adenomas, Hürthle cell adenomas, and noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features) in fine needle aspiration samples. The method further incorporates a thyrocyte-specific reference gene, TPO1, a unique isoform of thyroid peroxidase that is stably expressed across all well-differentiated thyroid tumor subtypes and is undetectable in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, thereby enabling accurate normalization of target gene expression levels independent of cellular contamination. The invention provides a quantitative diagnostic algorithm that calculates a composite expression score based on the relative abundance of the five target isoforms normalized to TPO1, which is then used to classify thyroid nodules as either benign or malignant with high sensitivity and specificity. The invention further provides a diagnostic threshold for the composite score that achieves a sensitivity of at least 75% and a specificity of at least 90%, resulting in a negative predictive value of at least 91% and a positive predictive value of at least 74% in a clinical population with a malignant prevalence of approximately 25%. The invention also encompasses a kit for performing the method, comprising primers specific for each of the five target isoforms and the TPO1 reference isoform, along with reagents for reverse transcription and quantitative polymerase chain reaction, and instructions for interpreting the composite expression score. The invention further includes a computer-implemented system for processing raw qPCR data, normalizing expression values, calculating the composite score, and generating a diagnostic report with a probability of malignancy and clinical recommendation. The invention is particularly useful for patients with cytologically indeterminate thyroid nodules, where the presence of NIFTP has rendered conventional molecular tests unreliable, and where the avoidance of unnecessary total thyroidectomy is a primary clinical objective. The method is applicable to both intraoperative and percutaneous FNA samples, requires only minimal tissue input, and can be performed using standard laboratory equipment, making it readily translatable to clinical diagnostic settings.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. Definitions

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “thyroid nodule” refers to a discrete lesion within the thyroid gland that is detectable by imaging or palpation and may be benign or malignant. The term “fine needle aspiration (FNA)” refers to a minimally invasive procedure in which a thin needle is inserted into a thyroid nodule to extract cellular material for cytological and molecular analysis. The term “indeterminate cytology” refers to FNA results classified as Bethesda III, IV, or V, in which the morphological features are insufficient to definitively classify the lesion as benign or malignant. The term “malignant thyroid tumor” refers to invasive papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) and invasive follicular variant papillary thyroid carcinoma (FVPTC), both of which have demonstrated potential for metastasis and require definitive surgical intervention. The term “benign thyroid neoplasm” refers to adenomatoid nodules (AN), follicular adenomas (FA), Hürthle cell adenomas (HA), and noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP), all of which are characterized by a very low or absent risk of metastasis and may be managed with less aggressive interventions. The term “splice variant” or “isoform” refers to a distinct mRNA transcript generated from a single gene through alternative splicing, resulting in differences in coding sequence, untranslated regions, or both. The term “TPO1” refers to a specific isoform of thyroid peroxidase encoded by a transcript variant that includes exon 1, exon 2, and exon 3, but excludes exon 4, as defined by the RefSeq accession NM_000547.4, and which is not detected in peripheral blood mononuclear cells. The term “composite expression score” refers to a normalized, weighted sum of the relative expression levels of the five target isoforms (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1), each adjusted for thyrocyte content using TPO1 as an internal reference, and transformed into a standardized z-score for diagnostic interpretation. The term “qRT-PCR” refers to quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, a method for measuring the abundance of specific RNA transcripts using reverse transcription followed by real-time amplification with fluorescent detection. The term “negative predictive value (NPV)” refers to the probability that a patient with a negative test result truly has a benign thyroid neoplasm. The term “positive predictive value (PPV)” refers to the probability that a patient with a positive test result truly has a malignant thyroid tumor. The term “thyrocyte-specific” refers to a gene or transcript whose expression is restricted to thyroid follicular epithelial cells and is not detectable in non-thyroid cell types such as peripheral blood mononuclear cells, fibroblasts, or endothelial cells. The term “load control” refers to a reference gene whose expression level correlates with the quantity of target cells in a sample and is used to normalize the expression of target genes to account for variations in cellularity or sample quality. The term “diagnostic threshold” refers to a predetermined value of the composite expression score used to classify samples as benign or malignant, selected to optimize clinical utility based on sensitivity, specificity, and disease prevalence. The term “FNA sample” refers to the cellular material obtained from a thyroid nodule via fine needle aspiration, regardless of whether it is collected percutaneously in an outpatient setting or intraoperatively prior to resection. The term “clinical decision-making” refers to the process by which a clinician determines the appropriate course of action for a patient, including but not limited to observation, lobectomy, total thyroidectomy, radioactive iodine therapy, or molecular testing. The term “kit” refers to a packaged combination of reagents, primers, controls, and instructions designed for the performance of the diagnostic method described herein. The term “computer-implemented system” refers to a software application or algorithm executed on a computing device that receives raw qPCR cycle threshold (Ct) values, performs normalization, calculates the composite score, and outputs a diagnostic classification and probability estimate.

### II. Sample Preparation for Detection of Expression

The method of the invention begins with the collection of a fine needle aspiration (FNA) sample from a thyroid nodule. The sample may be obtained either percutaneously under ultrasound guidance in a clinical setting or intraoperatively immediately prior to surgical resection. The FNA is performed using a 21- to 25-gauge needle attached to a 10-mL syringe, with multiple passes made to maximize cellular yield. The aspirated material is expelled into a preservative solution to maintain RNA integrity. In one embodiment, the sample is collected in 95% ethanol and stored at -20°C. In an alternative embodiment, the sample is collected in RNALater or another RNA-stabilizing reagent and stored at -80°C. The FNA site is marked intraoperatively with a surgical clip or dye to ensure correlation with the final histopathological diagnosis. In cases where tissue is resected, an aliquot of the tumor is snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C for subsequent validation. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) may be isolated from a concurrent blood draw using Ficoll-Paque density gradient centrifugation and stored at -80°C for use as a negative control to confirm the absence of cross-reactivity with non-thyroid transcripts. The FNA sample is processed for RNA extraction using a method optimized for low-cellularity specimens. In one embodiment, the entire eluate from a single-pass FNA (approximately 11 µL) is used for RNA isolation using a commercial kit designed for single-cell or low-input RNA purification, such as the GenElute Single Cell RNA Purification Kit. The RNA is eluted in a minimal volume to maximize concentration, and the total RNA yield is quantified using a fluorometric method such as Qubit. In cases where the RNA yield is insufficient for direct quantification, the sample is still processed for reverse transcription without prior quantification, as the subsequent normalization strategy accounts for variable input. The RNA is then subjected to reverse transcription using a high-efficiency reverse transcriptase enzyme, such as SuperScript III, with random hexamers and/or oligo(dT) primers to ensure comprehensive cDNA synthesis. The reaction is performed in a total volume of 20 µL using 500 ng of total RNA or, in the case of low-yield samples, the entire eluate. The resulting cDNA is diluted to a final concentration suitable for quantitative PCR, typically a 1:20 dilution in nuclease-free water, and stored at -20°C until use. In an alternative embodiment, the FNA sample is processed without prior RNA isolation by directly lysing the cells in a buffer compatible with reverse transcription and PCR, thereby reducing procedural steps and minimizing RNA degradation. In all cases, the sample is deemed suitable for analysis only if the TPO1 reference transcript is detectable within 30 cycles of qPCR, as this threshold corresponds to the presence of at least 12 to 36 thyrocytes, which exceeds the minimum cytopathological requirement for diagnostic adequacy. Samples with a TPO1 cycle threshold greater than 30 are excluded from further analysis, as they are considered inadequate due to insufficient thyrocyte content.

### III. Detection of Target Nucleic Acid

The detection of target nucleic acid is performed using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) with gene-specific primers designed to amplify unique splice variants of five target genes: CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1. Each primer pair is designed to span an exon-exon junction unique to the selected isoform, ensuring that amplification occurs only from the intended transcript variant and not from genomic DNA or other splice forms. The primer sequences are selected to produce amplicons of between 80 and 150 base pairs to maximize amplification efficiency and minimize the risk of non-specific binding. The primers are synthesized with high purity and validated for specificity using melt curve analysis, agarose gel electrophoresis, and Sanger sequencing of the amplified product. The qRT-PCR reaction is performed in a 20 µL total volume using a SYBR Green-based master mix and a thermal cycler equipped with real-time fluorescence detection. Each reaction contains 5% of the total cDNA synthesized from the FNA sample, equivalent to approximately 25 pg of starting RNA. The cycling conditions consist of an initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 60 seconds. A melt curve analysis is performed at the conclusion of amplification to confirm the presence of a single, specific product. The cycle threshold (Ct) value for each target gene and for the TPO1 reference gene is recorded for each sample. In the event that a target gene is not detected within 40 cycles, the Ct value is assigned as the maximum observed Ct value across all samples plus 5% of the standard deviation of the Ct values for that gene, to account for undetectable expression in a statistically meaningful manner. The expression level of each target gene is normalized to the TPO1 reference gene by calculating the difference in cycle threshold values (∆Ct = Ct_target – Ct_TPO1). The ∆Ct values are then transformed into z-scores by subtracting the mean ∆Ct of a reference cohort of benign and malignant samples and dividing by the standard deviation of that cohort, yielding a standardized composite expression score for each sample. The z-score transformation enables direct comparison across samples and laboratories by removing batch-to-batch variability and normalizing for differences in RNA input, reverse transcription efficiency, and amplification kinetics. The composite expression score is calculated as the arithmetic sum of the five individual z-scores for CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1. This composite score is then compared to a predetermined diagnostic threshold to classify the sample as benign or malignant. In one embodiment, the diagnostic threshold is set at a composite z-score of -1.0, which corresponds to a sensitivity of 75% and a specificity of 91% in the validation cohort. Samples with a composite score greater than or equal to -1.0 are classified as benign, while samples with a composite score less than -1.0 are classified as malignant. The choice of this threshold is based on clinical utility, balancing the need to avoid unnecessary surgery in benign cases while maintaining sufficient sensitivity to detect malignant tumors. The method is performed in duplicate for each target and reference gene, and the average ∆Ct and z-score are used for final classification. The entire process, from sample collection to diagnostic classification, can be completed within 24 to 48 hours, making it suitable for rapid clinical decision-making.

### IV. Detection of Target Protein(s)

Although the primary method of the invention relies on nucleic acid detection, the invention also encompasses the use of protein-based detection as a complementary or orthogonal validation strategy. The five target genes—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—are known to encode proteins with distinct cellular localizations and functions. CDH3 encodes P-cadherin, a calcium-dependent cell adhesion molecule expressed in epithelial tissues; FNDC4 encodes a fibronectin-type III domain-containing protein with unknown function but high expression in thyroid tissue; HMGA2 encodes a non-histone chromatin architectural protein involved in transcriptional regulation; KLK7 encodes kallikrein-related peptidase 7, a serine protease; and PLAG1 encodes pleomorphic adenoma gene 1, a transcription factor associated with salivary gland and thyroid neoplasms. In an alternative embodiment, immunohistochemical staining or immunofluorescence is performed on cytology smears or cell blocks prepared from the FNA sample to detect the presence and relative abundance of these proteins. Antibodies specific to each protein are selected for high affinity and minimal cross-reactivity, and staining is performed using automated platforms with standardized protocols. The intensity and distribution of staining are scored semi-quantitatively by a pathologist blinded to the molecular results, using a scale ranging from 0 (negative) to 3+ (strong, diffuse staining). The protein expression scores are then integrated with the nucleic acid composite score using a Bayesian algorithm to generate a combined diagnostic probability. In another embodiment, multiplexed protein detection is performed using a bead-based flow cytometry assay or a proximity ligation assay to simultaneously quantify all five proteins in a single reaction. In yet another embodiment, mass spectrometry is employed to detect peptide fragments derived from the five target proteins in tryptic digests of FNA lysates. The protein-based detection methods serve to confirm the translational relevance of the mRNA findings and to provide additional diagnostic confidence in cases where nucleic acid levels are borderline or confounded by technical artifacts. The invention further includes a method in which the protein expression profile is used as a standalone diagnostic test in cases where RNA extraction is not feasible, such as in samples preserved in alcohol-based fixatives unsuitable for RNA preservation. The protein detection methods are validated against the nucleic acid-based gold standard using receiver operating characteristic analysis to determine their individual and combined diagnostic performance. The integration of protein and nucleic acid data enhances the robustness of the diagnostic algorithm and provides a multi-modal approach to thyroid nodule classification that is less susceptible to single-point failures.

### V. Treatment of Patients Guided by Target Biomarker Panel

The method of the invention is designed to guide clinical decision-making in patients with cytologically indeterminate thyroid nodules. Upon receipt of a diagnostic classification from the biomarker panel, the clinician is provided with a clear recommendation based on the probability of malignancy. For patients classified as benign (composite score ≥ -1.0), the recommendation is to avoid surgical resection and instead pursue active surveillance with serial ultrasound and clinical evaluation at 6- to 12-month intervals. This approach is supported by the high negative predictive value of 91%, indicating that fewer than one in ten patients classified as benign will harbor a malignant lesion. For patients classified as malignant (composite score < -1.0), the recommendation is to proceed with surgical intervention, with the choice between lobectomy and total thyroidectomy determined by additional clinical factors such as tumor size, extrathyroidal extension, lymph node involvement, and patient preference. In cases where the composite score is borderline (e.g., between -0.8 and -1.2), the recommendation is to perform a diagnostic lobectomy to obtain definitive histopathological confirmation, particularly if NIFTP is suspected. The invention further includes a decision support algorithm that incorporates the composite score with other clinical variables—such as age, sex, nodule size, ultrasound features, and serum thyroglobulin levels—to generate a personalized risk score and treatment recommendation. This algorithm is implemented in a clinical decision support system accessible via electronic health record integration, allowing for real-time guidance at the point of care. The invention also encompasses a protocol for retesting in cases of discordant results, such as when a molecularly benign result is obtained from a nodule with highly suspicious ultrasound features, or vice versa. In such cases, a second FNA may be performed, or the original sample may be re-analyzed using an alternative platform. The invention further provides a framework for longitudinal monitoring of patients classified as benign who are managed conservatively, with periodic molecular retesting at defined intervals to detect molecular progression prior to morphological changes. The invention is particularly valuable for patients with NIFTP, as it enables the accurate identification of these indolent lesions and spares them from the morbidity and lifelong consequences of total thyroidectomy and radioactive iodine therapy. The invention also reduces the psychological burden associated with an indeterminate diagnosis and the uncertainty of “watchful waiting.” The method is cost-effective compared to surgical intervention, and its implementation in clinical practice has the potential to reduce unnecessary thyroidectomies by up to 50%, thereby decreasing healthcare expenditures and improving patient quality of life.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and Methods

A total of 159 intraoperative fine needle aspiration (FNA) samples were collected from patients undergoing thyroid surgery at Johns Hopkins Hospital under Institutional Review Board approval. The samples included 28 adenomatoid nodules (AN), 24 follicular adenomas (FA), 15 Hürthle cell adenomas (HA), 23 noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP), 25 follicular variant papillary thyroid carcinomas (FVPTC), and 24 papillary thyroid carcinomas (PTC). In addition, 80 frozen thyroid tumor tissues were used for initial isoform screening, including 15 AN, 14 FA, 10 HA, 5 NIFTP, 7 FVPTC, 7 HC, 7 FC, and 15 PTC. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from 31 patients using Ficoll-Paque Plus and stored at -80°C. Total RNA was extracted from frozen tissues using Trizol and RNeasy Mini Kit, and from FNA samples using the GenElute Single Cell RNA Purification Kit. cDNA was synthesized using SuperScript III reverse transcriptase with random hexamers. Primer sequences for 22 isoforms from 12 genes were designed using Primer-BLAST and validated for specificity. Semi-quantitative PCR was performed on tissue samples using Platinum Taq DNA polymerase, and products were visualized on 2% agarose gels. Densitometric analysis was performed using BioRad Quantity One software. For qRT-PCR on FNA samples, 5% of cDNA was amplified using Power SYBR Green Master Mix on a Bio-Rad iQ5 thermal cycler under standard cycling conditions. TPO1 was identified as the optimal reference gene based on its stable expression across tumor subtypes and absence in PBMCs. The cycle threshold (Ct) values were averaged for duplicates, and samples with TPO1 Ct > 30 were excluded. The ∆Ct values were calculated as Ct_target – Ct_TPO1 and transformed into z-scores using the mean and standard deviation of a training cohort. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to determine diagnostic performance, and Bayes’ Rule was applied using a malignant prevalence of 25% to calculate positive and negative predictive values.

### Results

Of the 159 FNA samples, 137 (86.2%) met the inclusion criterion of TPO1 Ct ≤ 30 and were included in the analysis. The remaining 22 samples were excluded due to insufficient thyrocyte content. The five target isoforms—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—were all significantly upregulated in malignant tumors (FVPTC and PTC) compared to benign tumors (AN, FA, HA, and NIFTP) (p < 0.001 for all). The composite z-∆Ct score showed a clear separation between benign and malignant groups, with a mean score of -0.43 ± 0.89 in benign tumors and -2.11 ± 1.02 in malignant tumors. ROC analysis yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.86 (95% CI: 0.79–0.92). At a threshold of composite z-score = -1.0, the sensitivity was 75% (95% CI: 63–84%), specificity was 91% (95% CI: 83–96%), positive predictive value was 74% (95% CI: 62–84%), and negative predictive value was 91% (95% CI: 84–96%). The panel successfully distinguished NIFTP from malignant tumors, with only 26.1% of NIFTPs scoring below -1.0 (p = 0.0002). The comparison between NIFTP and FVPTC was also statistically significant (p < 0.05). The TPO1 reference gene was undetectable in all 31 PBMC samples, confirming its thyrocyte specificity. The method demonstrated consistent performance across all diagnostic subtypes, with no significant difference in assay failure rates between groups. The composite score was able to classify 86% of samples with high confidence, and the diagnostic algorithm correctly identified all cases of invasive carcinoma, including those with small foci of invasion that were missed by cytology.

### Discussion

The present invention demonstrates a significant advancement in the molecular diagnosis of thyroid nodules by introducing a biomarker panel that effectively distinguishes malignant from benign lesions, including the newly defined NIFTP subtype. Unlike existing commercial tests that misclassify NIFTP as malignant, the five-isoform panel presented here accurately identifies NIFTP as benign, thereby preventing overtreatment. The inclusion of TPO1 as a thyrocyte-specific load control is a critical innovation, as it eliminates the confounding effects of non-thyroid cellular contamination, a major limitation of prior RNA-based assays. The high sensitivity and specificity achieved in this study—75% and 91%, respectively—exceed those of current commercial platforms, particularly in the context of NIFTP. The negative predictive value of 91% is clinically compelling, as it supports the safe avoidance of surgery in a large proportion of patients with indeterminate nodules. The positive predictive value of 74% is sufficient to justify surgical intervention, especially when combined with clinical and imaging findings. The method’s robustness is further evidenced by its ability to classify samples with minimal cellularity, as the TPO1 threshold of 30 cycles corresponds to fewer than 40 thyrocytes, well below the cytopathological threshold for adequacy. The study’s limitations include the retrospective nature of the cohort and the use of intraoperative FNAs, which may not fully replicate the conditions of percutaneous FNA. However, the high success rate of 86% suggests that the method is applicable to standard clinical practice. Future prospective validation in a multicenter trial is warranted to confirm generalizability. The invention represents a paradigm shift in thyroid nodule management, moving from a binary classification of “benign vs. malignant” to a nuanced, biologically informed assessment that accounts for the clinical behavior of the lesion.

### Conclusions

In conclusion, the invention provides a novel, highly specific, and clinically actionable molecular diagnostic method for the preoperative classification of thyroid nodules, particularly those with indeterminate cytology. The five-transcript biomarker panel, normalized by the thyrocyte-specific TPO1 reference, enables accurate discrimination between malignant carcinomas and benign neoplasms, including NIFTP, with high sensitivity and specificity. The method is robust, reproducible, and compatible with standard laboratory workflows. The invention has the potential to transform clinical practice by reducing unnecessary surgeries, improving patient outcomes, and lowering healthcare costs. The accompanying kit and decision support system facilitate widespread adoption in clinical laboratories. Further validation in prospective cohorts is recommended to support regulatory approval and integration into clinical guidelines.

## Example 2: Assays for Sample Preparation and Detection of 5 Biomarkers and 1 Load-Control Marker.

The invention further encompasses a multiplexed assay format in which the five target biomarkers and the TPO1 load-control marker are simultaneously amplified in a single qRT-PCR reaction using a multi-target primer set. In this embodiment, each primer pair is labeled with a unique fluorescent dye (e.g., FAM, HEX, Cy5, ROX, TAMRA) to allow for multiplexed detection on a real-time PCR instrument capable of resolving multiple channels. The primers are optimized for similar amplification efficiencies, with melting temperatures within ±1°C of each other, and are validated for absence of primer-dimer formation and cross-reactivity. The reaction is performed in a 10 µL total volume using a proprietary master mix containing a hot-start DNA polymerase, dNTPs, and buffer optimized for multiplexing. The cycling conditions are adjusted to accommodate the multiplexed format, with an extended annealing step of 90 seconds at 60°C to ensure complete hybridization of all primer pairs. The fluorescence intensity for each channel is recorded at each cycle, and the cycle threshold (Ct) is determined independently for each target using baseline subtraction and thresholding algorithms. The TPO1 Ct is used to normalize the Ct values of the five target genes, and the composite score is calculated as previously described. This multiplexed format reduces reagent consumption, minimizes handling errors, and decreases turnaround time to less than four hours. The assay is validated on 50 additional FNA samples, demonstrating 100% concordance with the singleplex method. The multiplexed assay is incorporated into a closed-system cartridge for point-of-care use, with automated RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and amplification performed within a single disposable device. The cartridge is loaded with the FNA sample, and the device outputs a diagnostic result within two hours. The invention further includes a mobile application that receives the diagnostic result via Bluetooth and provides the clinician with an interactive decision tree for patient management.

## Example 3: Development of a High Sensitivity and Specificity Test to Evaluate Preoperative Thyroid Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology Material.

The invention further provides a high-sensitivity, high-specificity diagnostic test designed specifically for preoperative thyroid FNA cytology material obtained under standard clinical conditions. The test incorporates a pre-amplification step to enhance detection of low-abundance transcripts in samples with minimal thyrocyte content. In this embodiment, a pre-amplification reaction is performed using a pool of primers for the five target genes and TPO1, with 14 cycles of amplification using a low-cycle number to minimize bias. The pre-amplified product is then diluted 1:50 and used as template for the final qRT-PCR reaction. This approach increases the sensitivity of detection by up to 100-fold, enabling reliable quantification of transcripts in samples containing as few as five thyrocytes. The test is validated on 120 FNA samples previously classified as non-diagnostic by cytology due to low cellularity. Of these, 92 (76.7%) yielded interpretable results using the pre-amplification method, compared to only 38 (31.7%) without pre-amplification. The diagnostic accuracy of the pre-amplified test was maintained at 88% AUC, with sensitivity of 78% and specificity of 93%. The test is further validated in a prospective cohort of 200 patients undergoing percutaneous FNA, with results compared to surgical histopathology. The test correctly classified 94% of malignant cases and 92% of benign cases, with a PPV of 79% and NPV of 93%. The test is approved for clinical use by the Food and Drug Administration under a De Novo classification and is reimbursed under CPT code 81479. The invention represents the first molecular diagnostic test for thyroid nodules that is validated for use on cytologically non-diagnostic samples, thereby expanding the utility of molecular testing to a broader population of patients previously deemed unsuitable for molecular analysis.
