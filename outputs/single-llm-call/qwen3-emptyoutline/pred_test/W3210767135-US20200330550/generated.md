# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology, pharmacology, and metabolic physiology, specifically to the use of teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP) for enhancing energy metabolism and contractile performance in skeletal muscle tissue. More particularly, the invention encompasses novel therapeutic methods and compositions involving TCAP-1, or biologically active fragments thereof, for increasing glucose uptake, mitochondrial activation, ATP production, and NADH turnover in skeletal muscle cells. The invention further pertains to the modulation of intracellular calcium flux through interaction with latrophilin receptors, particularly LPHN-1 and LPHN-3, thereby improving muscle endurance, reducing fatigue, and promoting a shift toward oxidative fiber phenotypes. This technology finds application in the treatment of metabolic disorders characterized by impaired skeletal muscle function, including type II diabetes, sarcopenia, frailty, mitochondrial myopathies, and other conditions associated with reduced muscular energy availability and diminished physical performance.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Skeletal muscle is one of the most metabolically active tissues in the human body, responsible for approximately 40% of total glucose utilization under basal conditions and up to 80% of insulin-stimulated glucose uptake. The ability of skeletal muscle to efficiently generate and sustain energy through aerobic metabolism is essential for voluntary movement, posture maintenance, thermoregulation, and overall metabolic homeostasis. Dysfunction in skeletal muscle energy metabolism is a hallmark of numerous pathological states, including insulin resistance, type II diabetes, aging-related muscle atrophy, and inherited mitochondrial diseases. Despite extensive research into insulin-dependent glucose transport mechanisms mediated by GLUT4 translocation, a significant proportion of patients exhibit diminished responsiveness to insulin, necessitating the discovery of alternative pathways capable of enhancing muscle glucose uptake and mitochondrial function independently of the insulin signaling cascade.

Historically, the regulation of skeletal muscle metabolism has been primarily attributed to hormonal signals such as insulin, glucagon, epinephrine, and adipokines, as well as mechanical stimuli and exercise-induced signaling through AMPK and PGC-1α pathways. While these mechanisms are well-characterized, they are often insufficient to restore normal metabolic function in disease states where insulin receptor sensitivity is compromised or where mitochondrial biogenesis is impaired. Furthermore, pharmacological interventions targeting these pathways frequently suffer from systemic side effects, limited tissue specificity, or incomplete efficacy in improving muscle contractile performance.

Recent advances in evolutionary biology and neuroendocrinology have revealed the existence of ancient signaling systems that predate the divergence of vertebrates and are conserved across diverse metazoan lineages. Among these systems, the teneurin family of transmembrane proteins and their cognate receptors, the latrophilins (LPHNs), have emerged as critical mediators of cell adhesion, synaptic organization, and metabolic regulation in the central nervous system. Teneurins are type II transmembrane proteins characterized by a large extracellular domain containing a conserved β-barrel structure and a C-terminal peptide sequence known as the teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP). TCAP is generated either through proteolytic cleavage of the full-length teneurin protein or through independent transcription and translation, and it possesses structural homology to the secretin family of G protein-coupled receptor ligands. Prior studies have demonstrated that TCAP-1, when administered exogenously, enhances neuronal glucose uptake, stimulates mitochondrial respiration, and increases ATP turnover via calcium-dependent mechanisms involving phospholipase C, inositol trisphosphate, and intracellular calcium mobilization.

Despite these findings in neural tissue, no prior investigation had established the presence or functional relevance of the teneurin-TCAP-LPHN signaling axis in skeletal muscle. The expression of teneurins and latrophilins in muscle tissue remained undocumented, and the potential for TCAP to influence muscle metabolism, contractile kinetics, or fiber-type composition had never been explored. Moreover, the molecular mechanisms underlying glucose transport and mitochondrial activation in skeletal muscle were presumed to be exclusively governed by canonical insulin-dependent and exercise-induced pathways. The absence of any known endogenous peptide capable of directly modulating skeletal muscle energetics through a non-insulin mechanism represented a significant gap in therapeutic development.

The present invention fills this critical void by demonstrating, for the first time, that TCAP-1 is not only expressed in skeletal muscle but also functions as a potent endogenous regulator of muscle metabolism and contractile efficiency. Through comprehensive in vivo and in vitro analyses, the invention establishes that TCAP-1 interacts with latrophilin-1 and latrophilin-3 receptors on the sarcolemma of skeletal muscle cells, triggering a cascade of intracellular events that culminate in increased glucose importation, enhanced mitochondrial calcium uptake, elevated succinate dehydrogenase activity, augmented ATP and NADH production, and improved resistance to fatigue. These effects occur independently of insulin signaling and are accompanied by a phenotypic shift toward oxidative, fatigue-resistant muscle fibers, as evidenced by increased expression of myosin heavy chain isoform I and elevated mitochondrial density. The discovery of this novel signaling pathway provides a foundation for the development of targeted therapeutics that can restore metabolic function in skeletal muscle without relying on insulin sensitization, thereby offering a transformative approach to the treatment of metabolic and neuromuscular disorders.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for enhancing energy metabolism and contractile performance in skeletal muscle by administering a therapeutically effective amount of teneurin C-terminal associated peptide-1 (TCAP-1) or a biologically active variant thereof. The invention is based on the groundbreaking discovery that TCAP-1 interacts with latrophilin-1 and latrophilin-3 receptors expressed on the surface of skeletal muscle cells, initiating a signaling cascade that increases intracellular calcium concentrations, stimulates mitochondrial respiration, enhances glucose uptake via GLUT4 translocation, and elevates ATP and NADH levels. This mechanism operates independently of insulin and does not require activation of the insulin receptor or downstream components of the PI3K-AKT pathway. The invention further demonstrates that TCAP-1 administration results in a phenotypic transformation of skeletal muscle fibers toward a more oxidative, fatigue-resistant profile, characterized by increased expression of myosin heavy chain I and elevated mitochondrial enzyme activity, without altering overall muscle mass.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for increasing glucose uptake in skeletal muscle cells by contacting the cells with TCAP-1, wherein the increase in glucose uptake is mediated through the activation of latrophilin-1 and latrophilin-3 receptors, leading to phospholipase C activation, inositol trisphosphate production, and subsequent release of calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. The elevated cytosolic calcium is subsequently taken up by mitochondria, resulting in the activation of key enzymes in the tricarboxylic acid cycle, including succinate dehydrogenase, and an increase in mitochondrial membrane potential, which together drive enhanced ATP synthesis. The invention further encompasses the use of TCAP-1 to increase the expression and membrane translocation of the glucose transporter GLUT4 in skeletal muscle cells, thereby facilitating greater glucose importation and utilization for energy production.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a method for improving skeletal muscle endurance and reducing fatigue during prolonged contractile activity by administering TCAP-1 to a subject in need thereof. The method involves the administration of TCAP-1 in a manner that results in sustained elevation of intracellular calcium levels in muscle fibers, which enhances sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium reuptake efficiency via SERCA pumps, thereby preventing the accumulation of cytosolic calcium that leads to contractile dysfunction during fatigue. The invention further demonstrates that TCAP-1 treatment improves the rate of muscle relaxation and maintains maximal twitch force during repetitive stimulation, indicating enhanced excitation-contraction coupling and improved metabolic support for calcium handling.

In a further embodiment, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising TCAP-1 or a biologically active fragment thereof, formulated for systemic or localized delivery to skeletal muscle tissue. The compositions may be administered via subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intravenous routes and may include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, stabilizers, and delivery enhancers designed to prolong the half-life of the peptide and facilitate its penetration into muscle tissue. The invention further encompasses the use of TCAP-1 in combination with other metabolic modulators, exercise regimens, or nutritional interventions to synergistically enhance muscle function and metabolic health.

The invention also provides a method for diagnosing or monitoring metabolic dysfunction in skeletal muscle by detecting the expression levels of teneurin-3, latrophilin-1, or latrophilin-3 in muscle biopsy samples, wherein reduced expression of these components correlates with impaired glucose metabolism and decreased oxidative capacity. The invention further includes diagnostic kits for the detection of these biomarkers in clinical settings.

Importantly, the invention demonstrates that the beneficial effects of TCAP-1 on skeletal muscle are not mediated through changes in muscle mass, body weight, or systemic hormone levels such as insulin or glucagon, but rather through direct, receptor-mediated actions on muscle cells. This distinguishes the invention from prior therapies that rely on systemic metabolic modulation and positions TCAP-1 as a first-in-class muscle-specific metabolic enhancer with minimal off-target effects. The invention further provides evidence that TCAP-1 does not induce hypoglycemia or alter blood glucose levels in the absence of metabolic challenge, indicating a safety profile suitable for chronic administration.

### Applications: The Use of TCAP to Increase Energy Available to Skeletal Muscle Cells

The present invention demonstrates that TCAP-1, when administered to skeletal muscle tissue, acts as a potent endogenous metabolic enhancer that directly increases the availability of energy substrates and mitochondrial capacity within muscle cells. Unlike insulin, which requires receptor binding and downstream kinase activation to stimulate GLUT4 translocation, TCAP-1 operates through a distinct and previously unrecognized signaling pathway initiated by its interaction with latrophilin-1 and latrophilin-3 receptors on the sarcolemma. This interaction triggers the activation of phospholipase C, which catalyzes the hydrolysis of phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate into inositol trisphosphate and diacylglycerol. The resulting increase in intracellular inositol trisphosphate leads to the opening of calcium channels on the sarcoplasmic reticulum, causing a rapid and transient elevation in cytosolic calcium concentration. This calcium signal is not merely a secondary messenger for contraction but serves as a critical regulator of mitochondrial metabolism.

The elevated cytosolic calcium is actively transported into the mitochondrial matrix via the mitochondrial calcium uniporter, a process that stimulates key enzymes in the tricarboxylic acid cycle, including pyruvate dehydrogenase, isocitrate dehydrogenase, and succinate dehydrogenase. The activation of these enzymes enhances the flux of carbon substrates through the TCA cycle, increasing the production of NADH and FADH2, which in turn drive the electron transport chain to generate a stronger proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This results in increased ATP synthesis by ATP synthase and a measurable rise in cellular ATP concentration. The invention further demonstrates that TCAP-1 treatment leads to a sustained elevation in NADH levels, indicating enhanced mitochondrial redox activity and a shift toward aerobic metabolism. This effect is not observed in cells lacking functional latrophilin receptors, confirming the specificity of the pathway.

In parallel with mitochondrial activation, TCAP-1 induces the translocation of GLUT4 glucose transporters from intracellular vesicles to the plasma membrane, thereby facilitating increased glucose importation into the muscle cell. This translocation occurs independently of insulin signaling and is dependent on the calcium flux initiated by TCAP-1, as demonstrated by the complete inhibition of GLUT4 movement in the presence of the IP3 receptor antagonist 2-APB. The increased glucose uptake provides additional substrate for glycolysis and the TCA cycle, creating a positive feedback loop that amplifies energy production. The invention further shows that this glucose uptake is not merely transient but is sufficient to sustain elevated ATP levels for prolonged periods, as evidenced by the maintenance of ATP and NADH concentrations beyond 60 minutes post-treatment.

The metabolic effects of TCAP-1 are accompanied by a phenotypic transformation of skeletal muscle fibers. In vivo administration of TCAP-1 results in a significant increase in the proportion of type I oxidative muscle fibers, as determined by immunohistochemical staining for myosin heavy chain I and confirmed by quantitative PCR analysis of MHC isoform mRNA expression. This shift is associated with increased mitochondrial density, as evidenced by elevated NADH staining intensity and increased succinate dehydrogenase protein levels. Importantly, this transformation occurs without an increase in muscle mass, indicating that TCAP-1 enhances the functional quality of existing muscle tissue rather than promoting hypertrophy. The invention further demonstrates that TCAP-1 treatment improves muscle endurance during repetitive stimulation, as shown by the maintenance of twitch force and relaxation kinetics during a fatigue protocol, whereas vehicle-treated animals exhibit rapid decline in contractile performance. These findings indicate that TCAP-1 not only increases energy availability but also optimizes the efficiency of energy utilization during sustained muscle activity.

The therapeutic implications of this mechanism are profound. In conditions such as type II diabetes, where insulin resistance impairs glucose uptake in skeletal muscle, TCAP-1 offers a novel alternative pathway to restore metabolic function. In aging populations, where mitochondrial dysfunction and loss of oxidative fibers contribute to sarcopenia and frailty, TCAP-1 may reverse metabolic decline and improve physical performance. In patients with inherited mitochondrial disorders, where electron transport chain efficiency is compromised, TCAP-1 may enhance residual mitochondrial capacity by boosting substrate flux and calcium signaling. The invention further suggests that TCAP-1 may be beneficial in athletic performance, rehabilitation, and recovery from muscle injury, where rapid restoration of energy reserves and resistance to fatigue are critical. The fact that TCAP-1 does not alter systemic insulin or glucagon levels, nor induce hypoglycemia, further supports its safety and specificity as a muscle-targeted metabolic agent.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Modes of Administration

The present invention encompasses a variety of pharmaceutical compositions designed for the effective delivery of TCAP-1 or its biologically active fragments to skeletal muscle tissue. These compositions are formulated to maximize peptide stability, bioavailability, tissue penetration, and duration of action while minimizing degradation by proteolytic enzymes and renal clearance. The core active ingredient in all compositions is TCAP-1, which may be administered in its native, synthetic, or modified form, including N-terminal pyroglutamylation and C-terminal amidation to enhance resistance to aminopeptidases and carboxypeptidases. Alternative formulations may include D-amino acid substitutions, cyclization via disulfide bridges, or conjugation with polyethylene glycol (PEG) to prolong plasma half-life and reduce immunogenicity.

In one embodiment, the pharmaceutical composition comprises TCAP-1 dissolved in a sterile, isotonic aqueous solution, such as 0.9% sodium chloride or phosphate-buffered saline, with optional inclusion of stabilizing agents such as human serum albumin, glycine, or mannitol to prevent aggregation and adsorption to container surfaces. The composition may further include preservatives such as benzyl alcohol or parabens for multi-dose vials, or may be formulated as a single-use, preservative-free solution for subcutaneous or intramuscular injection. The concentration of TCAP-1 in such formulations may range from 0.1 nM to 100 nM, depending on the intended route of administration and the clinical indication. For systemic delivery, a typical therapeutically effective dose may be between 1 nM and 50 nM per kilogram of body weight, administered once daily or once weekly, depending on the pharmacokinetic profile and desired duration of effect.

In another embodiment, the composition is formulated as a sustained-release depot system, such as a microsphere or nanoparticle matrix composed of biodegradable polymers such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA), polycaprolactone, or chitosan. These systems allow for the controlled release of TCAP-1 over periods ranging from several days to several weeks, reducing the frequency of administration and improving patient compliance. The release kinetics can be tuned by varying the polymer composition, molecular weight, and particle size, enabling tailored delivery profiles for acute versus chronic conditions. For example, in the treatment of sarcopenia or chronic metabolic syndrome, a monthly subcutaneous injection of a PLGA-encapsulated TCAP-1 formulation may maintain therapeutic concentrations in muscle tissue for up to four weeks.

In yet another embodiment, the composition is formulated as a transdermal patch or iontophoretic delivery system, designed to facilitate the penetration of TCAP-1 across the skin and into underlying skeletal muscle. This approach may be particularly advantageous for localized treatment of limb muscles in patients with peripheral neuropathy or post-surgical muscle weakness. The patch may incorporate penetration enhancers such as fatty acids, terpenes, or surfactants to improve peptide permeability, and may be combined with low-voltage electrical current to drive charged TCAP-1 molecules across the stratum corneum.

For intramuscular delivery, TCAP-1 may be formulated as a viscous gel or hydrogel composed of hyaluronic acid, alginate, or collagen, which promotes localized retention at the injection site and gradual diffusion into muscle fibers. This approach is particularly suitable for targeted treatment of specific muscle groups, such as the quadriceps or deltoid, in patients with focal muscle dysfunction. The composition may also be co-administered with agents that enhance vascular permeability, such as hyaluronidase, to facilitate broader distribution within the muscle tissue.

In addition to parenteral routes, the invention encompasses oral formulations designed for systemic absorption, although the peptide’s susceptibility to gastrointestinal degradation necessitates advanced delivery technologies. Such formulations may include enteric-coated capsules containing TCAP-1 encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles or protected by protease inhibitors such as aprotinin or tranexamic acid. Alternatively, TCAP-1 may be conjugated to a cell-penetrating peptide or fused to a carrier protein such as albumin to enhance intestinal uptake and resistance to enzymatic cleavage.

The invention further includes combination therapies in which TCAP-1 is administered concurrently with other agents that enhance muscle metabolism or function. For example, TCAP-1 may be co-administered with exercise regimens, creatine monohydrate, beta-hydroxy-beta-methylbutyrate (HMB), or resveratrol to synergistically enhance mitochondrial biogenesis and muscle strength. In patients with type II diabetes, TCAP-1 may be combined with metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, or GLP-1 receptor agonists to provide complementary mechanisms of glucose control, with TCAP-1 acting directly on muscle and the other agents targeting hepatic or renal glucose handling. The invention further encompasses the use of TCAP-1 in conjunction with gene therapy vectors designed to upregulate endogenous expression of LPHN-1 or LPHN-3 in muscle tissue, thereby amplifying the responsiveness to exogenous TCAP-1 administration.

All pharmaceutical compositions are manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) and are tested for sterility, endotoxin content, peptide purity, and biological activity prior to administration. The compositions are stable for at least 12 months when stored at 2–8°C and for up to 30 days at room temperature, depending on formulation. The invention further includes kits comprising a vial of lyophilized TCAP-1, a sterile diluent, a syringe, and instructions for administration, designed for use in clinical or home settings.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Peptide Synthesis

The synthetic peptide TCAP-1 was produced using solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) on a Rink amide resin using Fmoc chemistry. The sequence of TCAP-1 was designed to correspond to the C-terminal region of the rat teneurin-3 gene, with an N-terminal pyroglutamic acid residue and a C-terminal amide group to mimic the naturally processed form. The peptide chain was assembled stepwise using HBTU/DIPEA as coupling reagents and piperidine for Fmoc deprotection. After completion of synthesis, the peptide was cleaved from the resin using a mixture of trifluoroacetic acid, water, and triisopropylsilane (95:2.5:2.5) for 3 hours at room temperature. The crude peptide was precipitated in cold diethyl ether, centrifuged, and washed three times to remove residual solvents. The pellet was dissolved in acetic acid and purified by preparative reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) using a C18 column and a gradient of water/acetonitrile containing 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid. Fractions containing the target peptide were collected, lyophilized, and analyzed by mass spectrometry to confirm molecular weight and purity. The final product exhibited a molecular mass of 7,892 Da, consistent with the theoretical mass of the pyroglutamylated, amidated TCAP-1 peptide. Purity was confirmed to be greater than 95% by analytical HPLC and amino acid analysis. A scrambled control peptide (sc-TCAP-1) was synthesized using the same methodology, with the amino acid sequence randomized except for the N-terminal pyroglutamate and C-terminal amide, ensuring identical physicochemical properties without biological activity. All peptides were stored at -80°C in lyophilized form until use and were reconstituted in sterile saline immediately prior to administration.

### Example 2—In-Vivo Experiments

Adult male Sprague-Dawley rats weighing approximately 250 grams were acclimated to a 12-hour light/dark cycle for one week prior to experimentation. Animals were divided into two groups: one received subcutaneous injections of TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) in 0.9% saline, while the control group received an equal volume of saline alone. Injections were administered daily for five consecutive days into the intrascapular region. Three days after the final injection, animals were anesthetized with isoflurane and subjected to electrical muscle stimulation of the tibialis anterior muscle to assess contractile function. A needle electrode was inserted beneath the skin adjacent to the muscle, and a lever connected to a servomotor was attached to the foot to measure force output. A baseline single twitch and tetanic contraction were recorded, followed by a 6-minute fatigue protocol consisting of repeated 300-millisecond stimuli at 200 Hz and 8 volts. Force parameters, including peak twitch force, maximal rate of force development (dx/dt), and half-relaxation time (1/2RT), were recorded at 0, 1, and 5 minutes post-fatigue. Animals were immediately euthanized, and tibialis anterior muscles were excised, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen-cooled isopentane, and stored at -80°C for subsequent molecular and histological analyses. A separate cohort of animals underwent long-term treatment, receiving weekly subcutaneous injections of TCAP-1 (25 nmoles/kg) for three months. Two weeks after the final injection, contractile function was assessed using the same protocol. All procedures were conducted in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines and approved by the University of Toronto Animal Care Committee.

### Example 3—Effect of TCAP on Blood Glucose Levels

To assess the systemic metabolic effects of TCAP-1, adult male Wistar rats were injected subcutaneously with either TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) or saline vehicle. Blood samples were collected from the retro-orbital sinus at 0, 30, 60, and 120 minutes post-injection. Plasma glucose concentrations were measured using a glucometer calibrated for rodent blood. Insulin and glucagon levels were quantified using commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits. The results demonstrated that TCAP-1 administration did not significantly alter plasma glucose levels compared to vehicle-treated controls at any time point. Similarly, serum insulin and glucagon concentrations remained unchanged throughout the observation period, indicating that TCAP-1 does not exert systemic endocrine effects on pancreatic hormone secretion. These findings confirm that the metabolic benefits of TCAP-1 are mediated locally within skeletal muscle and do not involve alterations in circulating glucose-regulating hormones.

### Example 4—Glucose Insulin and Glucagon Changes in Blood

In a follow-up experiment, a cohort of rats was subjected to an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) following a 12-hour fast. Animals were administered either TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) or saline 30 minutes prior to oral glucose gavage (2 g/kg). Blood samples were collected at 0, 15, 30, 60, and 120 minutes after glucose administration. Plasma glucose, insulin, and glucagon levels were measured using standardized assays. The results showed that TCAP-1-treated animals exhibited a significantly faster clearance of glucose from the bloodstream compared to controls, despite no difference in insulin secretion. This enhanced glucose disposal occurred without an increase in insulin levels, confirming that TCAP-1 potentiates glucose uptake independently of insulin. Glucagon levels remained stable in both groups, indicating no compensatory counter-regulatory response. These findings further support the conclusion that TCAP-1 enhances skeletal muscle glucose utilization through a non-insulin-dependent mechanism.

### Example 5—Liver and Muscle Glycogen Content in Extracted Liver of TCAP-1 Treated Wistar Rats

To determine whether TCAP-1 influences glycogen storage in peripheral tissues, Wistar rats were treated with TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) or saline for five consecutive days. At the end of the treatment period, animals were euthanized, and liver and tibialis anterior muscle tissues were rapidly excised, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80°C. Glycogen content was quantified using a standard enzymatic assay. Tissue samples were homogenized in perchloric acid, neutralized with potassium hydroxide, and incubated with amyloglucosidase to hydrolyze glycogen to glucose. Glucose concentration was measured spectrophotometrically using a glucose oxidase-peroxidase reaction. The results showed no significant difference in liver glycogen content between TCAP-1-treated and vehicle-treated animals. However, muscle glycogen content was significantly elevated in the TCAP-1 group, indicating that the peptide promotes glycogen synthesis specifically in skeletal muscle. This finding is consistent with the observed increase in glucose uptake and suggests that TCAP-1 enhances the capacity of muscle to store glucose in the form of glycogen for future energy demands.

### Example 6—Haematology Assays

To evaluate potential systemic toxicity or hematological side effects of chronic TCAP-1 administration, blood samples were collected from rats treated with TCAP-1 (25 nmoles/kg weekly for 3 months) and from vehicle-treated controls. Complete blood counts were performed using an automated hematology analyzer to assess red blood cell count, white blood cell count, hemoglobin concentration, hematocrit, mean corpuscular volume, platelet count, and differential leukocyte counts. No significant differences were observed between the two groups in any hematological parameter. Additionally, serum markers of liver function (ALT, AST, bilirubin) and kidney function (creatinine, blood urea nitrogen) were measured and found to be within normal physiological ranges in both groups. These results indicate that long-term administration of TCAP-1 does not induce hematological, hepatic, or renal toxicity, supporting its safety profile for chronic therapeutic use.

### Example 7—Localization of TCAP in Muscle Tissue

To determine the cellular distribution of TCAP-1 in skeletal muscle, immunohistochemical staining was performed on cryosections of tibialis anterior muscle from rats treated with TCAP-1 or saline. Tissues were fixed in ice-cold methanol, blocked with 10% normal goat serum, and incubated overnight at 4°C with a polyclonal antibody specific for TCAP-1. Fluorescent secondary antibodies were applied, and sections were imaged using confocal microscopy. TCAP-1 immunoreactivity was observed as discrete punctate staining localized to the sarcolemma of muscle fibers, with minimal intracellular signal. The staining pattern was absent in vehicle-treated animals and was not detected in tissues incubated with pre-immune serum. Co-staining with β-dystroglycan, a marker of the sarcolemmal membrane, revealed co-localization of TCAP-1 with dystroglycan complexes, suggesting that TCAP-1 is positioned at sites of membrane adhesion and signaling. These findings confirm that TCAP-1 is expressed and localized at the muscle cell surface, consistent with its role as a ligand for transmembrane latrophilin receptors.

### Example 8—Localization of TCAP-1 Relative to β-Dystroglycan in Skeletal Cells

To further characterize the spatial relationship between TCAP-1 and the dystrophin-glycoprotein complex, dual immunofluorescence staining was performed on muscle cryosections using antibodies against TCAP-1 and β-dystroglycan. Confocal microscopy revealed a high degree of co-localization between TCAP-1 and β-dystroglycan along the sarcolemma, with overlapping fluorescent signals at discrete nodes rather than uniform distribution. Quantitative analysis using ImageJ software showed a Pearson’s correlation coefficient of 0.82 between TCAP-1 and β-dystroglycan fluorescence intensities, indicating strong spatial association. This co-localization was consistent across all fiber types and was not observed in tissues treated with scrambled TCAP-1. The proximity of TCAP-1 to β-dystroglycan suggests that TCAP-1 may function within a specialized signaling microdomain at the muscle membrane, potentially interacting with latrophilin receptors that are physically associated with the dystroglycan complex. This arrangement may facilitate rapid and localized signaling in response to mechanical or metabolic stimuli.

### Example 9—Localization of TCAP-1 Relative to Rapsyn in Skeletal Cells

To investigate whether TCAP-1 localizes to neuromuscular junctions, muscle sections were co-stained with antibodies against TCAP-1 and rapsyn, a postsynaptic scaffolding protein essential for clustering acetylcholine receptors at the motor endplate. Confocal imaging revealed that TCAP-1 immunoreactivity was distributed along the entire sarcolemma, including regions distant from the endplate, whereas rapsyn staining was confined to discrete, punctate structures corresponding to neuromuscular junctions. There was no significant co-localization between TCAP-1 and rapsyn, indicating that TCAP-1 is not restricted to the neuromuscular junction but is broadly expressed across the muscle fiber membrane. This distribution supports a role for TCAP-1 in regulating metabolic and contractile functions throughout the muscle fiber, rather than solely modulating synaptic transmission. The absence of rapsyn co-localization further distinguishes TCAP-1 from neurotransmitter-related signaling systems and reinforces its function as a metabolic regulator independent of neural input.
