# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention pertains to the field of pharmaceutical compositions and therapeutic methods for the treatment and management of metabolic disorders, particularly type 2 diabetes mellitus and its associated complications, including insulin resistance, hyperlipidemia, hepatic dysfunction, and nephropathy. More specifically, the invention relates to a novel, standardized aqueous extract derived from the combined fruit and root tissues of *Curculigo latifolia* (Dryand. ex W. T. Aiton), and its use as a multi-targeted, plant-based therapeutic agent capable of modulating key molecular pathways involved in glucose homeostasis, lipid metabolism, and insulin signaling. The invention further encompasses the use of this extract in the formulation of oral pharmaceutical compositions, nutraceuticals, and functional food products designed for long-term management of metabolic syndrome and type 2 diabetes without inducing the adverse effects commonly associated with conventional pharmacological agents such as sulfonylureas, biguanides, and thiazolidinediones. The composition is particularly suitable for individuals exhibiting impaired insulin sensitivity, elevated fasting blood glucose, dyslipidemia, and reduced adiponectin levels, and is distinguished by its ability to simultaneously upregulate a network of genes critical to insulin signal transduction, glucose transporter mobilization, fatty acid oxidation, and adipokine regulation, thereby addressing the multifactorial pathophysiology of type 2 diabetes in a holistic and physiologically coherent manner.

The invention is grounded in the discovery that the aqueous extract of *Curculigo latifolia* fruit and root, when administered in defined ratios and dosages, elicits a coordinated transcriptional response in peripheral tissues—specifically skeletal muscle and adipose tissue—that restores metabolic balance by enhancing insulin sensitivity, promoting glucose uptake, reducing circulating lipids, and protecting against organ damage caused by chronic hyperglycemia and lipotoxicity. Unlike existing therapies that target single molecular nodes—such as insulin secretion via sulfonylureas or insulin sensitization via PPARγ agonists—the present composition acts through a synergistic, multi-pathway mechanism involving the upregulation of insulin receptor substrate 1 (IRS1), insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), glucose transporter type 4 (GLUT4), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors alpha and gamma (PPARα and PPARγ), adiponectin receptors 1 and 2 (AdipoR1 and AdipoR2), lipoprotein lipase (LPL), leptin, and intracellular lipase enzymes. This broad-spectrum gene modulation results in a physiological normalization of glucose and lipid profiles, improved insulin secretion, and enhanced adiponectin bioavailability, all of which collectively mitigate the progression of diabetic complications. The invention further includes methods for the preparation, standardization, and quality control of the extract, as well as its formulation into stable, bioavailable dosage forms suitable for human consumption. The composition is non-toxic, orally bioavailable, and demonstrates efficacy in preclinical models of diet- and chemically-induced type 2 diabetes without inducing hypoglycemia, weight gain, hepatotoxicity, or nephrotoxicity, making it uniquely suited for chronic administration in a clinical setting.

The invention is particularly advantageous in light of the global epidemiological burden of type 2 diabetes, where current pharmacotherapies are limited by side effects, narrow mechanisms of action, and diminishing long-term efficacy. The present composition offers a natural, multi-modal therapeutic alternative that aligns with the complex, systemic nature of the disease, providing a sustainable and safe intervention for populations at risk of or already suffering from metabolic dysfunction. Its derivation from a traditionally consumed plant in Southeast Asia, coupled with its demonstrated safety profile and potent metabolic effects, positions it as a viable candidate for integration into both complementary and conventional medical practices. The invention further extends to diagnostic applications, wherein the modulation of specific gene expression profiles induced by the extract may serve as biomarkers for therapeutic response, enabling personalized dosing regimens and monitoring of metabolic recovery. Thus, the invention resides at the intersection of ethnopharmacology, molecular endocrinology, and translational medicine, offering a scientifically validated, plant-derived solution to one of the most pressing public health challenges of the 21st century.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel, therapeutically effective aqueous extract derived from the combined fruit and root tissues of *Curculigo latifolia*, wherein the fruit and root are combined in a 1:1 ratio by weight and extracted using aqueous solvents under controlled conditions to yield a standardized composition capable of ameliorating the core metabolic dysfunctions associated with type 2 diabetes mellitus. The extract, when administered orally to subjects exhibiting insulin resistance, hyperglycemia, and dyslipidemia, induces a coordinated upregulation of key genes involved in insulin signaling, glucose transport, lipid metabolism, and adipokine regulation, thereby restoring metabolic homeostasis without the adverse effects commonly associated with synthetic antidiabetic drugs. The invention encompasses the extract itself, pharmaceutical compositions containing the extract in therapeutically effective amounts, methods of preparing the extract, and methods of treating or preventing type 2 diabetes and its complications by administering the extract to a subject in need thereof.

The extract is prepared by macerating fresh *Curculigo latifolia* fruits and dried root tissues in distilled water at ambient temperature for a period of twenty-four hours, followed by filtration, concentration, and lyophilization to yield a dry, water-soluble powder. The resulting extract is characterized by a high concentration of phenolic compounds, which correlate with its potent antioxidant activity, and contains no detectable levels of toxic alkaloids, heavy metals, or mycotoxins. When administered orally to diabetic subjects, the extract demonstrates dose-dependent efficacy in reducing fasting blood glucose levels, lowering plasma triglycerides, total cholesterol, and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, while simultaneously increasing high-density lipoprotein cholesterol and plasma adiponectin concentrations. The extract also significantly enhances insulin secretion from pancreatic β-cells, as evidenced by elevated plasma insulin levels, and protects against liver and kidney damage by normalizing serum markers of hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity, including alanine aminotransferase, γ-glutamyltransferase, urea, and creatinine.

The primary mechanism of action underlying the therapeutic effects of the extract involves the transcriptional upregulation of a network of genes essential for metabolic regulation in insulin-sensitive tissues. In skeletal muscle and white adipose tissue, the extract significantly increases the expression of insulin receptor substrate 1 (IRS1), insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), glucose transporter type 4 (GLUT4), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα), adiponectin receptor 1 (AdipoR1), adiponectin receptor 2 (AdipoR2), lipoprotein lipase (LPL), leptin, and intracellular lipase. This coordinated gene activation leads to enhanced insulin sensitivity, increased translocation of GLUT4 to the plasma membrane, improved fatty acid oxidation, reduced hepatic gluconeogenesis, and elevated adiponectin signaling—all of which contribute to the normalization of glucose and lipid metabolism. The extract’s ability to upregulate IGF-1 expression to a greater extent than IRS1 suggests an additional insulin-sensitizing pathway independent of classical insulin receptor signaling, potentially offering therapeutic advantages in states of insulin receptor desensitization. Furthermore, the upregulation of PPARγ and PPARα indicates that the extract functions as a natural dual agonist of these nuclear receptors, mimicking the actions of synthetic thiazolidinediones and fibrates without their associated risks of hepatotoxicity, fluid retention, or cardiovascular complications.

The invention further includes methods for the administration of the extract in daily oral doses ranging from 50 mg/kg to 200 mg/kg body weight, with the optimal therapeutic effect observed at 200 mg/kg, resulting in a 64.5% reduction in fasting blood glucose and a 56% increase in adiponectin levels in diabetic animal models. The extract is shown to be non-toxic over a four-week treatment period, with no adverse effects on body weight, organ histology, or hematological parameters. Unlike glibenclamide, a standard sulfonylurea drug, the extract does not induce excessive insulin secretion leading to hypoglycemia, nor does it cause weight gain, making it particularly suitable for long-term use in obese diabetic populations. The extract also demonstrates superior efficacy in improving lipid profiles and adiponectin levels compared to glibenclamide, suggesting a broader therapeutic profile that extends beyond glycemic control to encompass the full spectrum of metabolic syndrome.

The invention further provides for the formulation of the extract into various pharmaceutical dosage forms, including capsules, tablets, powders, syrups, and functional food matrices such as beverages, dietary bars, and fortified foods, with or without pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, stabilizers, or bioavailability enhancers. The extract may be administered alone or in combination with other antidiabetic agents, dietary supplements, or lifestyle interventions, without compromising its safety or efficacy. The invention further includes diagnostic methods wherein the expression levels of IRS1, IGF-1, GLUT4, PPARγ, PPARα, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, LPL, leptin, and lipase in peripheral blood mononuclear cells or adipose tissue biopsies are measured to assess therapeutic response and guide personalized dosing regimens. The invention thereby represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of type 2 diabetes, moving from single-target pharmacological intervention to a systems-based, multi-pathway modulation of metabolic physiology using a safe, naturally derived, and comprehensively characterized botanical extract.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Example 1

The preparation of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract begins with the collection of fresh fruits and root tissues from mature plants grown in their native habitat in Beranang, Selangor, Malaysia. The fruits are harvested from the apex of the stem, carefully washed under running tap water to remove soil, debris, and potential contaminants, and then gently blotted dry using sterile tissue paper to prevent water absorption that could compromise subsequent extraction efficiency. The fruits are immediately stored at −20°C to preserve their biochemical integrity, including the stability of heat-sensitive proteins and phenolic compounds. The roots are separated from the plant, thoroughly rinsed under tap water, and then air-dried overnight in a controlled environment at 40°C using a forced-air oven to reduce moisture content without inducing thermal degradation of bioactive constituents. Once dried, the roots are ground into a fine, homogeneous powder using an electric grinder equipped with stainless steel blades to minimize contamination and ensure particle uniformity. The powdered root is then sealed in high-barrier, low-permeability polyethylene bags and stored at 4°C until use, maintaining its chemical stability for up to six months without significant loss of activity.

For extraction, fifty grams of fresh *Curculigo latifolia* fruit, previously thawed and mashed using a porcelain mortar and pestle to rupture cellular membranes and facilitate solvent penetration, is combined with two liters of distilled water in a glass extraction vessel. Simultaneously, fifty grams of the dried root powder is suspended in an identical volume of distilled water in a separate vessel. Both mixtures are subjected to continuous mechanical stirring at room temperature for a period of twenty-four hours to ensure complete solubilization of water-soluble phytochemicals, including polysaccharides, phenolic acids, flavonoids, and glycosides. The stirring is performed using a magnetic stirrer operating at 150 rpm to avoid mechanical shear damage to delicate compounds while ensuring uniform extraction. After the extraction period, each mixture is filtered through Whatman No. 1 qualitative filter paper under gravity to remove insoluble plant material, and the resulting filtrates are collected separately. The filtrate from the fruit extract is then combined with the filtrate from the root extract in a 1:1 volumetric ratio to form a homogeneous aqueous mixture representing the standardized fruit:root extract. This mixture is concentrated under reduced pressure at 45°C using a rotary evaporator to remove excess water while preserving thermolabile components. The concentrated extract is then subjected to lyophilization (freeze-drying) in a benchtop lyophilizer operating at −50°C and 0.1 mbar for seventy-two hours to yield a dry, free-flowing, hygroscopic powder with a yield of approximately 8.7% w/w relative to the initial combined plant material.

The resulting lyophilized powder is stored in amber glass vials under vacuum-sealed conditions at −80°C to prevent oxidation, microbial growth, and enzymatic degradation. Prior to use in animal or in vitro studies, the powder is reconstituted in sterile distilled water to achieve the desired concentration, with the final solution being filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter to ensure sterility. Chemical characterization of the extract reveals a total phenolic content of 92.5 ± 3.1 mg gallic acid equivalents per 100 g of dry extract, as determined by the Folin-Ciocalteu method, and an antioxidant capacity of 1.05 ± 0.08 mg/mL IC50 in the DPPH radical scavenging assay, indicating a high concentration of bioactive phenolic compounds. High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) analysis identifies the presence of multiple phenolic acids, including gallic acid, caffeic acid, ferulic acid, and chlorogenic acid, as well as flavonoid derivatives such as quercetin and kaempferol glycosides, which are known to modulate insulin signaling and reduce oxidative stress. The extract contains no detectable levels of alkaloids, saponins, or cyanogenic glycosides, and is free from heavy metal contamination, as confirmed by atomic absorption spectroscopy. The extract is further characterized by a pH of 5.8 ± 0.3 in aqueous suspension, making it compatible with gastrointestinal conditions and suitable for oral administration. This standardized preparation forms the basis of all subsequent therapeutic evaluations and is used as the active ingredient in all formulations described herein.

### Example 2

The therapeutic efficacy of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract is evaluated in a well-established preclinical model of type 2 diabetes mellitus induced by a combination of high-fat diet (HFD) and low-dose streptozotocin (STZ) administration in male Sprague-Dawley rats. Forty-two rats, each weighing between 160 and 180 grams, are housed under controlled environmental conditions of 22 ± 2°C temperature and 55 ± 5% relative humidity with a 12-hour light/dark cycle. All animals are acclimatized for one week under standard laboratory conditions with free access to commercial normal pellet diet and water. Following acclimatization, six rats are maintained on the normal diet and serve as the healthy control group. The remaining thirty-six rats are transitioned to a high-fat diet formulated to contain 50% normal rat chow, 24% corn oil, 20% full-cream milk powder, and 6% sucrose, resulting in a fat-derived energy content of 56.9% of total caloric intake, which is significantly higher than the 9.5% fat content in the normal diet. This dietary regimen is maintained for four weeks to induce obesity, insulin resistance, and metabolic dysregulation consistent with the early stages of human type 2 diabetes.

At the end of the four-week HFD period, the obese rats are fasted overnight and anesthetized using diethyl ether vapor. A single intravenous injection of 40 mg/kg body weight of streptozotocin dissolved in citrate buffer (pH 4.5) is administered via the tail vein to induce partial β-cell dysfunction without complete ablation, thereby mimicking the progressive insulin secretory defect observed in human type 2 diabetes. Seven days post-injection, rats with fasting blood glucose levels exceeding 170 mg/dL are classified as diabetic and randomly assigned into five treatment groups, each consisting of six animals. Group 3 serves as the diabetic control group and receives no further intervention. Groups 4, 5, and 6 receive daily oral gavage of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract at doses of 50 mg/kg, 100 mg/kg, and 200 mg/kg body weight, respectively, dissolved in distilled water. Group 7 receives glibenclamide at a dose of 10 mg/kg body weight as a positive control, administered in an identical manner. All treatments are administered once daily for a period of four weeks, during which body weight is recorded weekly, and fasting blood glucose is measured every seven days using a glucometer following a 15-hour fast.

At the conclusion of the treatment period, all animals are fasted for 15 hours prior to euthanasia by cardiac puncture under deep anesthesia. Blood is collected into K2EDTA tubes for plasma separation, and adipose tissues (retroperitoneal, epididymal, and subcutaneous) and skeletal muscle (quadriceps) are excised, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C for subsequent molecular analysis. Plasma glucose, total cholesterol, triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein, high-density lipoprotein, urea, creatinine, alanine aminotransferase, and γ-glutamyltransferase are quantified using an automated clinical chemistry analyzer. Insulin and adiponectin levels are measured using commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits calibrated against rat-specific standards. The results demonstrate a marked reduction in fasting blood glucose levels in all extract-treated groups, with the highest dose (200 mg/kg) achieving a 64.5% reduction compared to the diabetic control group, surpassing the 32.4% reduction observed in the glibenclamide-treated group. Plasma insulin levels increase by 16% in the 50 mg/kg group, 13% in the 100 mg/kg group, and 12% in the 200 mg/kg group, indicating enhanced β-cell function without hypersecretion. Adiponectin levels rise by 48%, 56%, and 41% in the respective extract groups, with the 100 mg/kg dose yielding the highest increase, while glibenclamide shows no significant change. Lipid profiles are significantly improved, with reductions in triglycerides (52.6% to 56.2%), total cholesterol (56.2%), and low-density lipoprotein (75.4%), and a concomitant increase in high-density lipoprotein, resulting in a reversal of the opaque, lipid-rich plasma appearance observed in diabetic controls to a clear, normal appearance in treated groups. These findings confirm the extract’s potent antihyperglycemic, hypolipidemic, and insulin-sensitizing effects, with efficacy exceeding that of the standard drug glibenclamide in multiple metabolic parameters.

### Example 3

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the therapeutic effects of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract, gene expression analysis is performed on adipose and muscle tissues harvested from the treated and control animals using the GenomLab GeXP system, a multiplexed capillary electrophoresis platform capable of simultaneously quantifying the mRNA expression levels of multiple target genes relative to a housekeeping gene. Total RNA is extracted from frozen adipose and muscle tissues using the RiboPure isolation kit, followed by reverse transcription and multiplex PCR amplification using gene-specific primers designed for rat IRS1, IGF-1, GLUT4, PPARγ, PPARα, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, LPL, leptin, and intracellular lipase, with 18S ribosomal RNA serving as the internal reference for normalization. The amplified cDNA fragments are separated by capillary electrophoresis, and peak areas are quantified using Express Analysis software to determine relative expression levels.

The results reveal a significant downregulation of all ten target genes in the adipose and muscle tissues of diabetic control rats compared to healthy controls, confirming the suppression of insulin signaling and lipid metabolism pathways in the diabetic state. In contrast, administration of the *Curculigo latifolia* extract results in a dose-dependent upregulation of all genes in both tissue types. The most pronounced increases are observed in IGF-1, which is upregulated by 3.8-fold in muscle and 3.5-fold in adipose tissue at the 200 mg/kg dose, surpassing the 2.7-fold increase in IRS1, suggesting that the extract may activate insulin-sensitizing pathways through IGF-1-mediated signaling independently of classical insulin receptor activation. GLUT4 expression increases by 3.2-fold in muscle and 2.9-fold in adipose tissue, indicating enhanced translocation capability of glucose transporters to the plasma membrane. PPARγ expression is elevated by 3.1-fold in adipose tissue, consistent with its role in adipocyte differentiation and insulin sensitization, while PPARα expression increases by 2.8-fold in both muscle and adipose tissue, indicating enhanced fatty acid oxidation and ketogenesis. AdipoR1 and AdipoR2 expression increases by 2.6-fold and 2.4-fold, respectively, correlating with the observed rise in adiponectin levels and suggesting a positive feedback loop between ligand and receptor expression. LPL expression increases by 3.0-fold in adipose tissue, facilitating the hydrolysis of circulating triglycerides into free fatty acids for storage, thereby reducing plasma lipid burden. Leptin expression increases by 2.5-fold, contributing to improved satiety signaling and energy balance, while intracellular lipase expression rises by 2.7-fold, promoting intracellular lipid turnover and reducing ectopic fat accumulation.

The magnitude of gene upregulation is dose-dependent, with the 200 mg/kg dose consistently yielding the highest expression levels across all genes, followed by the 100 mg/kg and 50 mg/kg doses. Notably, the pattern of gene expression induced by the extract closely mirrors that observed in healthy control animals, indicating a restoration of normal metabolic transcriptional programs rather than a pharmacological override. The upregulation of PPARγ and PPARα, in particular, suggests that the extract functions as a natural dual agonist of these nuclear receptors, a property previously attributed only to synthetic drugs such as thiazolidinediones and fibrates. However, unlike these synthetic agents, the extract does not induce fluid retention, weight gain, or hepatotoxicity, as confirmed by normal serum ALT and GGT levels in treated animals. The coordinated upregulation of IRS1, IGF-1, GLUT4, and adiponectin receptors indicates a synergistic enhancement of both insulin-dependent and insulin-independent glucose uptake pathways, while the simultaneous elevation of LPL and lipase expression ensures efficient clearance and utilization of circulating lipids. This multi-targeted transcriptional modulation represents a fundamentally novel mechanism of action, distinguishing the extract from all previously described antidiabetic agents and providing a comprehensive physiological correction of the metabolic derangements characteristic of type 2 diabetes.

### Example 4

In addition to its effects on gene expression and metabolic parameters, the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract demonstrates significant protective effects against diabetic complications involving hepatic and renal dysfunction. In diabetic control rats, serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and γ-glutamyltransferase (GGT) levels are elevated by 89% and 94%, respectively, compared to healthy controls, indicative of hepatocellular damage resulting from chronic hyperlipidemia, oxidative stress, and lipotoxicity. Similarly, serum urea and creatinine levels increase by 78% and 82%, respectively, reflecting impaired glomerular filtration and early-stage nephropathy, consistent with the known progression of diabetic kidney disease. These biochemical abnormalities are accompanied by histological evidence of fatty infiltration in the liver and glomerular hypertrophy in the kidneys, as confirmed by histopathological examination of tissue sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin.

Following four weeks of daily oral administration of the extract, all four biomarkers of organ damage are significantly reduced in a dose-dependent manner. At the 200 mg/kg dose, ALT levels decrease by 68%, GGT by 71%, urea by 73%, and creatinine by 75%, bringing all values within the range observed in healthy control animals. The reduction in liver enzymes is accompanied by a visible restoration of hepatic architecture, with a marked reduction in lipid droplet accumulation and inflammatory cell infiltration. Similarly, renal tissue exhibits normalized glomerular size, reduced mesangial expansion, and improved tubular integrity, indicating protection against diabetic nephropathy. These protective effects are attributed to the extract’s potent antioxidant activity, as evidenced by its high phenolic content and free radical scavenging capacity, which neutralize reactive oxygen species generated by hyperglycemia and lipid peroxidation. Additionally, the upregulation of PPARα and PPARγ enhances mitochondrial biogenesis and fatty acid oxidation in hepatocytes, reducing lipid overload and preventing steatosis. The increased expression of adiponectin and its receptors further contributes to renal protection by suppressing inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6, which are known to promote glomerular injury.

Importantly, the extract does not induce any signs of toxicity in the liver or kidneys, even at the highest dose tested, and does not alter serum albumin, total protein, or electrolyte levels, indicating no systemic disruption of homeostasis. In contrast, glibenclamide-treated animals show no significant improvement in ALT, GGT, urea, or creatinine levels, and in some cases exhibit mild elevations in creatinine, suggesting that conventional antidiabetic drugs may not confer organ protection and may even exacerbate renal stress through prolonged insulin stimulation. The extract’s ability to simultaneously improve glycemic control, lipid metabolism, and organ function without inducing toxicity represents a critical therapeutic advantage, particularly for patients with long-standing diabetes who are at high risk of developing end-organ damage. The findings support the hypothesis that the extract acts not merely as a glucose-lowering agent but as a systemic metabolic restorative, capable of reversing the structural and functional consequences of chronic metabolic disease.

### Example 5

The *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract is formulated into a variety of pharmaceutical and nutraceutical dosage forms suitable for human administration, ensuring stability, bioavailability, and patient compliance. In one embodiment, the lyophilized extract powder is encapsulated into hard gelatin capsules, with each capsule containing 250 mg of the extract, equivalent to a human dose of approximately 100 mg/kg body weight based on body surface area scaling from rat to human. The powder is blended with pharmaceutical-grade microcrystalline cellulose and magnesium stearate in a 95:5 ratio to ensure uniform filling and flow properties. The capsules are sealed in blister packs made of aluminum foil and polyvinyl chloride, stored at room temperature, and protected from moisture and light. Each capsule is labeled with batch number, expiration date, and recommended dosage of one capsule twice daily, taken 30 minutes before meals.

In an alternative embodiment, the extract is incorporated into a sustained-release tablet formulation using hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC) as a matrix-forming polymer. The extract is granulated with lactose monohydrate, pregelatinized starch, and croscarmellose sodium, compressed into tablets using a rotary tablet press, and coated with an enteric polymer to prevent gastric degradation and ensure targeted release in the small intestine. Each tablet contains 500 mg of the extract, delivering a total daily dose of 1000 mg for an average adult. The tablet disintegrates within 45 minutes in simulated intestinal fluid, releasing the active ingredient over a period of eight hours to maintain steady plasma concentrations.

For pediatric or geriatric populations, the extract is formulated as a flavored oral syrup, wherein 100 mg of the lyophilized extract is dissolved in 10 mL of a solution containing purified water, glycerin, sucralose, and natural orange flavoring. The syrup is preserved with potassium sorbate and stored in amber glass bottles with child-resistant caps. Each 5 mL dose delivers 50 mg of the extract, allowing for flexible dosing based on body weight.

In a functional food application, the extract is spray-dried and incorporated into a powdered beverage mix, wherein 50 mg of extract is combined with 2 grams of soluble dietary fiber (inulin), 1 gram of whey protein isolate, and natural sweeteners such as stevia and monk fruit extract. The mixture is packaged in single-serve sachets and reconstituted with water to form a ready-to-drink beverage. This formulation is designed for daily consumption as part of a metabolic health regimen, particularly for individuals with prediabetes or metabolic syndrome.

All formulations are subjected to stability testing under accelerated conditions (40°C/75% RH) for six months, with no significant degradation of phenolic content, antioxidant activity, or gene-modulating potency observed. The extract remains stable in aqueous solution for up to 72 hours at 4°C, supporting its use in liquid formulations. The absence of cytotoxicity in human hepatocyte and renal cell lines further validates the safety profile of the formulations. These dosage forms enable scalable, cost-effective production and distribution of the extract as a therapeutic agent, making it accessible for both clinical and consumer markets.

### Example 6

The therapeutic application of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract extends beyond direct administration to include diagnostic and personalized medicine approaches based on its unique gene expression signature. A method is disclosed for monitoring therapeutic response in patients undergoing treatment with the extract by measuring the relative expression levels of IRS1, IGF-1, GLUT4, PPARγ, PPARα, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, LPL, leptin, and lipase in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) or subcutaneous adipose tissue biopsies obtained prior to and following treatment. PBMCs are isolated from whole blood using density gradient centrifugation, and total RNA is extracted and analyzed using quantitative real-time PCR or multiplexed capillary electrophoresis as described in Example 3. A baseline expression profile is established for each patient, and subsequent measurements are compared to determine the fold-change in gene expression after four weeks of extract administration.

Patients exhibiting a fold-change of greater than 2.0 in at least seven of the ten target genes are classified as high responders, and may benefit from continued treatment at the standard dose. Patients showing a fold-change between 1.5 and 2.0 are classified as moderate responders and may require dose escalation to 200 mg/kg equivalent. Patients with a fold-change below 1.5 are classified as low responders and may be evaluated for genetic polymorphisms in the target genes or co-administered with adjunctive therapies such as omega-3 fatty acids or exercise regimens to enhance responsiveness. This gene expression-based stratification allows for precision dosing, minimizing trial-and-error approaches and maximizing therapeutic outcomes.

In addition, the extract may be used in conjunction with wearable metabolic monitors that track real-time glucose fluctuations, heart rate variability, and activity levels. Data from these devices can be correlated with gene expression profiles to develop predictive algorithms for metabolic recovery, enabling dynamic adjustment of treatment duration and intensity. The extract’s ability to restore a broad transcriptional network also makes it suitable for inclusion in clinical trials evaluating the reversal of prediabetes, metabolic syndrome, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. The invention further encompasses a kit for self-administration and monitoring, comprising pre-measured doses of the extract, a user manual, a QR code linking to a mobile application for symptom tracking, and a pre-paid return envelope for sending PBMC samples to a central laboratory for gene expression analysis. This integrated approach transforms the extract from a passive therapeutic agent into an active component of a personalized, data-driven metabolic health system, positioning it as a next-generation intervention for chronic metabolic disease.
