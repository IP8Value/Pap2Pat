# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to a method for predicting the risk of disease recurrence in patients diagnosed with hormone receptor-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2−) luminal breast cancer, utilizing the combined assessment of two specific molecular biomarkers: the expression level of pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and the level of O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) modification. This method provides a clinically actionable, reproducible, and cost-effective diagnostic tool that enhances the precision of prognostic stratification beyond conventional clinicopathological parameters such as tumor size, nodal status, and histological grade. The invention is particularly directed toward the identification of patients at high risk of late recurrence following endocrine therapy, thereby enabling personalized treatment decisions that optimize therapeutic benefit while minimizing unnecessary systemic interventions. The biomarkers are detectable through standard immunohistochemical techniques performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections, making the method readily integrable into existing pathology workflows without requiring specialized instrumentation or complex molecular assays. The invention further encompasses the development of a composite prognostic model that integrates these metabolic biomarkers with the T stage of tumor invasion, yielding a statistically superior predictive performance compared to existing clinical scoring systems or single biomarker approaches. This innovation addresses a critical unmet need in oncology: the inability of current prognostic tools to reliably distinguish between HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients who will remain disease-free for extended periods and those who are at substantial risk of recurrence despite favorable clinical features, particularly in the context of luminal B subtype disease characterized by elevated proliferation and metabolic reprogramming.

### 2. The Prior Art

Prior art in the field of breast cancer prognosis has long relied on clinicopathological parameters including tumor size, lymph node involvement, histological grade, and hormone receptor status to guide therapeutic decisions. The Nottingham Prognostic Index, one of the earliest multivariable models, combines these factors into a single score to estimate survival probability, but its predictive accuracy diminishes over time, particularly beyond the first five years after diagnosis. Subsequent developments introduced clinical scoring systems such as the Clinical Treatment Score post-5 years (CTS5), which incorporates tumor size, grade, patient age, and nodal count to estimate the risk of distant recurrence after five years of endocrine therapy. While CTS5 has demonstrated utility in postmenopausal populations, its prognostic power is significantly attenuated in younger women and in cohorts with higher rates of premenopausal disease, such as those in East Asia. Furthermore, CTS5 does not account for underlying tumor biology beyond proliferative and anatomical features, leaving a substantial proportion of patients misclassified in terms of recurrence risk.

In parallel, molecular diagnostic platforms have emerged as powerful tools for risk stratification. Gene expression assays such as Oncotype DX, MammaPrint, EndoPredict, Prosigna, and Breast Cancer Index analyze panels of genes associated with proliferation, hormone signaling, and invasion to generate recurrence scores. These assays have been validated in large prospective trials and are widely adopted in clinical practice, particularly for determining the necessity of adjuvant chemotherapy in ER-positive disease. However, these tests are limited by their reliance on RNA extraction from fresh or specially preserved tissue, high cost, prolonged turnaround time, and limited accessibility in resource-constrained healthcare settings. Moreover, while these assays provide valuable information regarding tumor biology, they do not directly interrogate the metabolic state of the tumor, which has been increasingly recognized as a fundamental driver of cancer progression, therapy resistance, and recurrence.

Metabolic reprogramming, particularly the Warburg effect—characterized by increased glucose uptake and aerobic glycolysis despite the presence of oxygen—has been identified as a hallmark of many cancers, including breast cancer. Pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), a splice variant of the pyruvate kinase enzyme, plays a central role in this metabolic shift. Unlike its constitutively active tetrameric form, PKM2 exists predominantly in a less active dimeric state in cancer cells, allowing the accumulation of glycolytic intermediates that are diverted into biosynthetic pathways supporting rapid proliferation. Beyond its enzymatic function, PKM2 translocates to the nucleus where it acts as a protein kinase and transcriptional coactivator, promoting the expression of genes involved in cell cycle progression, angiogenesis, and metastasis. Elevated PKM2 expression has been associated with poor prognosis in multiple cancer types, including breast cancer, and has been linked to endocrine resistance in estrogen receptor-positive disease. However, prior art has not established PKM2 as a standalone prognostic biomarker in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer with sufficient clinical validation to support its routine use in decision-making.

Simultaneously, the post-translational modification of proteins by O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) has gained attention as a nutrient-sensitive regulatory mechanism that integrates glucose, amino acid, fatty acid, and nucleotide metabolism into cellular signaling networks. O-GlcNAcylation, mediated by the enzyme O-GlcNAc transferase (OGT) and reversed by O-GlcNAcase (OGA), modulates the activity, stability, localization, and interactions of numerous oncogenic transcription factors, kinases, and epigenetic regulators. In breast cancer, hyper-O-GlcNAcylation has been shown to promote tumor growth, invasion, and resistance to endocrine therapies such as tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors. Notably, PKM2 itself is a direct target of O-GlcNAcylation at specific residues, including threonine 405 and serine 406, which enhances its nuclear translocation, stabilizes its dimeric form, and amplifies its non-metabolic functions. The interplay between O-GlcNAcylation and PKM2 has been demonstrated in preclinical models to drive aggressive tumor phenotypes, yet no prior art has proposed or validated a clinical method that exploits the co-expression of these two interconnected molecules as a synergistic prognostic indicator in human breast cancer cohorts.

Existing literature has examined PKM2 and O-GlcNAc independently in breast cancer, with some studies reporting associations between elevated levels of either molecule and adverse outcomes. However, these studies have been limited by small sample sizes, lack of standardized quantification methods, inconsistent cutoff values, absence of multivariate validation, and failure to compare performance against established clinical tools. Moreover, no prior art has demonstrated that the combination of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression provides statistically significant incremental prognostic value beyond conventional parameters such as T stage or CTS5, nor has any prior art established a validated threshold for clinical application using immunohistochemistry on routine tissue specimens. The use of the Q score—a product of staining intensity and percentage of positive cells—as a quantitative metric for both biomarkers in a single cohort has not been previously described in the context of prognostic modeling for HR+/HER2− disease. Furthermore, while ROC curve analyses have been employed to evaluate diagnostic accuracy of individual biomarkers, no prior study has systematically compared the area under the curve for multiple multivariate models incorporating PKM2 and O-GlcNAc alongside clinical variables to identify the optimal combination for predicting long-term disease-free survival.

The prior art also fails to recognize the biological plausibility underlying the synergy between PKM2 and O-GlcNAc. The hexosamine biosynthetic pathway, which generates the substrate UDP-GlcNAc for O-GlcNAcylation, is fueled by glucose, glutamine, acetyl-CoA, and UTP—all of which are metabolites whose flux is directly influenced by PKM2 activity. Thus, PKM2 not only serves as a downstream effector of metabolic reprogramming but also as a regulator of the very pathway that modifies it. This reciprocal relationship creates a feed-forward loop that amplifies oncogenic signaling and confers a selective advantage to tumor cells under nutrient stress. The prior art has not leveraged this mechanistic interdependence to develop a clinically applicable biomarker panel. Consequently, there remains a significant gap in the ability to identify HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer patients who, despite favorable clinical features, harbor metabolically aggressive tumors with high recurrence potential. The present invention fills this gap by introducing a novel, biologically grounded, and clinically feasible method for risk stratification that integrates metabolic biomarkers with anatomical staging to achieve superior prognostic discrimination.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for predicting the risk of disease recurrence in patients diagnosed with hormone receptor-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2−) luminal breast cancer by assessing the expression levels of pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) in tumor tissue specimens obtained from the primary tumor. The method comprises the steps of obtaining a formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue section from the patient’s breast tumor, performing immunohistochemical staining to detect the presence and abundance of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc, quantifying the expression of each biomarker using a standardized scoring system based on staining intensity and the percentage of immunopositive tumor cells, and combining the resulting scores to determine a composite metabolic risk profile. The invention further comprises the use of this composite profile in conjunction with the T stage of tumor invasion to generate a prognostic model that significantly outperforms existing clinical scoring systems in predicting disease-free survival over a ten-year period following diagnosis and initial treatment.

The invention is based on the discovery that high expression of PKM2 and high levels of O-GlcNAc modification, when assessed together, are strongly and independently associated with a significantly increased risk of recurrence in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer, even after adjustment for conventional clinicopathological variables. This association is not merely additive but synergistic, such that patients exhibiting high expression of both markers simultaneously demonstrate a markedly worse prognosis than those with high expression of only one or neither marker. The invention further demonstrates that the combination of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression provides prognostic information that is distinct from and complementary to that provided by the T stage, a key anatomical measure of tumor invasion depth. When integrated into a multivariate model, the triple combination of PKM2, O-GlcNAc, and T stage yields the highest area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) for predicting 10-year disease-free survival, surpassing the performance of models incorporating other clinical parameters such as tumor size or the CTS5 score.

The method of the invention is designed for routine clinical implementation using standard pathology infrastructure. The immunohistochemical staining protocols employed are compatible with widely available antibodies, automated staining platforms, and existing histopathology workflows. The scoring system, based on the Q score—a product of staining intensity (graded from 0 to 3) and the percentage of positively stained tumor cells (0 to 100%)—is objective, reproducible, and requires no specialized equipment beyond a light microscope and standardized image analysis software. The invention defines a clinically actionable threshold for high expression as a Q score greater than the mean value observed in the validation cohort, thereby eliminating the need for arbitrary cutoffs and ensuring generalizability across diverse populations. The invention further provides a statistical framework for interpreting the combined biomarker results, enabling clinicians to stratify patients into low-risk and high-risk categories for recurrence, with high-risk patients being candidates for intensified adjuvant therapy, extended endocrine treatment, or enrollment in clinical trials targeting metabolic vulnerabilities.

The invention also encompasses a diagnostic kit comprising reagents for immunohistochemical detection of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc, including validated primary antibodies, detection systems, antigen retrieval buffers, and a standardized scoring guide. The kit further includes a decision support algorithm that integrates the Q scores for PKM2 and O-GlcNAc with the T stage to calculate a composite prognostic index, which can be implemented as a digital tool or printed reference chart for use in pathology reports. The invention further provides a computer-readable medium storing instructions that, when executed by a processor, perform the steps of receiving input data comprising the Q scores for PKM2 and O-GlcNAc and the T stage, applying a predefined logistic regression model trained on a validation cohort, and outputting a predicted probability of 10-year disease-free survival along with a risk category classification. The invention further includes a method of selecting a therapeutic regimen for a patient diagnosed with HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer based on the output of the prognostic model, wherein patients classified as high-risk receive more aggressive adjuvant therapy, including extended endocrine therapy, addition of CDK4/6 inhibitors, or consideration of chemotherapy, whereas low-risk patients are managed with standard endocrine therapy alone.

The invention represents a paradigm shift in breast cancer prognostication by shifting the focus from purely genomic or anatomical features to the metabolic phenotype of the tumor. Unlike gene expression assays that require fresh tissue and complex bioinformatics, the method of the invention utilizes routinely processed tissue and provides results within the standard turnaround time of pathological diagnosis. The invention further overcomes the limitations of single biomarker approaches by exploiting the biological interdependence between PKM2 and O-GlcNAc, resulting in a synergistic signal that reflects the underlying metabolic dysregulation driving recurrence. The invention is particularly valuable for identifying patients with luminal B subtype disease who may appear clinically low-risk based on tumor size or nodal status but harbor metabolically aggressive tumors prone to late recurrence. By enabling more precise risk stratification, the invention facilitates personalized treatment decisions that improve outcomes while reducing overtreatment and associated toxicity, thereby transforming the standard of care for HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Definition

For the purposes of this patent application, the term “hormone receptor-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2−) luminal breast cancer” refers to a molecular subtype of breast cancer characterized by the expression of estrogen receptor (ER) and/or progesterone receptor (PR) in the absence of amplification or overexpression of the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), as determined by immunohistochemistry and fluorescence in situ hybridization according to established clinical guidelines. This subtype encompasses both luminal A and luminal B classifications, with luminal B typically defined by higher proliferative activity, as indicated by elevated Ki-67 levels or higher histological grade, and is associated with a greater risk of late recurrence despite endocrine therapy. The term “pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2)” refers to the M2 splice variant of the pyruvate kinase enzyme, which is expressed predominantly in embryonic tissues and transformed cells, and functions both as a glycolytic enzyme and as a nuclear transcriptional coactivator that promotes tumor progression. The term “O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc)” refers to a dynamic, reversible post-translational modification of serine and threonine residues on nuclear and cytoplasmic proteins, mediated by the enzyme O-GlcNAc transferase and reversed by O-GlcNAcase, which serves as a sensor of cellular nutrient status and regulates key signaling pathways involved in cancer cell survival, proliferation, and therapy resistance. The term “formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue section” refers to a histological specimen prepared by fixation of tumor tissue in 10% neutral buffered formalin followed by embedding in paraffin wax, a standard procedure in clinical pathology that preserves tissue architecture and antigenicity for immunohistochemical analysis. The term “immunohistochemical staining” refers to a laboratory technique that uses specific antibodies to detect the presence and localization of target antigens within tissue sections, allowing for visual assessment of protein expression patterns under a light microscope. The term “Q score” refers to a quantitative metric derived by multiplying the staining intensity score (ranging from 0 to 3, where 0 = no staining, 1 = weak, 2 = moderate, and 3 = strong) by the percentage of immunopositive tumor cells (ranging from 0 to 100), resulting in a composite score ranging from 0 to 300. The term “T stage” refers to the anatomical classification of the primary tumor according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system, specifically the extent of local invasion, with categories ranging from T1 (tumor ≤2 cm) to T4 (tumor invading the chest wall or skin). The term “disease-free survival” refers to the length of time after primary treatment during which a patient survives without any signs or symptoms of cancer recurrence. The term “composite prognostic model” refers to a statistical algorithm that integrates multiple variables—specifically, the Q scores for PKM2 and O-GlcNAc and the T stage—to predict the probability of disease recurrence over a defined time period, in this case, ten years. The term “clinically actionable” refers to a diagnostic result that can be used to guide therapeutic decisions in a manner that is reproducible, reliable, and implementable within standard clinical practice without requiring specialized infrastructure or prolonged turnaround time. The term “high expression” refers to a Q score for PKM2 or O-GlcNAc that exceeds the mean value observed in a validation cohort of patients with HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer, as determined by statistical analysis of survival outcomes. The term “low expression” refers to a Q score for PKM2 or O-GlcNAc that is less than or equal to the mean value in the validation cohort. The term “synergistic effect” refers to the phenomenon in which the combined predictive power of two biomarkers is greater than the sum of their individual predictive powers, indicating biological interdependence and enhanced biological relevance. The term “area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC)” refers to a statistical measure of diagnostic accuracy ranging from 0.5 (no discrimination) to 1.0 (perfect discrimination), used to evaluate the performance of a binary classifier system in distinguishing between patients with and without an event, such as disease recurrence.

### High Expression of Pyruvate Kinase Isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) is Associated With Poor Clinical Outcome in Luminal Breast Cancer

The present invention is grounded in the discovery that elevated expression of pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) is a robust and independent predictor of poor clinical outcome in patients diagnosed with luminal breast cancer, particularly in the HR+/HER2− subtype. This finding was established through comprehensive analysis of both large-scale genomic datasets and a well-characterized retrospective clinical cohort. Analysis of the METABRIC dataset, which comprises molecular and clinical data from over two thousand breast cancer patients, revealed that individuals in the top quartile of PKM2 mRNA expression exhibited significantly reduced overall survival and relapse-free survival compared to those in the bottom quartile. The difference in survival outcomes was statistically significant, with a p-value of 0.005 for overall survival and 0.045 for relapse-free survival, demonstrating that high PKM2 expression is not merely correlated with aggressive disease but is a strong prognostic indicator in luminal tumors. This association was not observed in basal-like or HER2-enriched subtypes, suggesting a subtype-specific role for PKM2 in luminal breast cancer biology. In the clinical cohort of 162 HR+/HER2− patients followed for a median of over ten years, immunohistochemical analysis confirmed that high PKM2 protein expression, as quantified by the Q score, was significantly associated with disease recurrence. Patients with high PKM2 expression had a hazard ratio of 1.87 for disease-free survival in univariate analysis, with a p-value of 0.048, indicating that elevated PKM2 levels confer a nearly two-fold increased risk of recurrence. Importantly, this association persisted even after adjustment for tumor size, nodal status, and histological grade, suggesting that PKM2 expression provides prognostic information distinct from conventional clinicopathological parameters. The biological rationale for this observation lies in the dual role of PKM2 as both a metabolic enzyme and a nuclear signaling molecule. In cancer cells, PKM2 exists predominantly in a less active dimeric form, which allows glycolytic intermediates to accumulate and be diverted into biosynthetic pathways that support rapid proliferation. Additionally, PKM2 translocates to the nucleus, where it acts as a protein kinase that phosphorylates histone H3 and transcription factors such as STAT3 and β-catenin, thereby promoting the expression of genes involved in cell cycle progression, angiogenesis, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. This non-metabolic function of PKM2 is particularly relevant in luminal breast cancer, where estrogen signaling and metabolic reprogramming converge to drive tumor persistence and late recurrence. The finding that PKM2 expression is associated with recurrence in patients treated with endocrine therapy further supports its role in mediating therapy resistance, as nuclear PKM2 has been shown to enhance the transcriptional activity of estrogen receptor coactivators and suppress apoptotic pathways. The clinical relevance of this finding is underscored by the fact that PKM2 expression was not correlated with traditional markers of proliferation such as Ki-67, indicating that it captures a distinct biological process. This independence from established markers enhances its utility as a novel prognostic tool, particularly for identifying patients who may benefit from intensified therapy despite having seemingly favorable clinical features. The invention therefore establishes PKM2 as a critical biomarker in luminal breast cancer, whose expression level can be reliably assessed using standard immunohistochemical techniques and used to stratify patients into distinct risk categories for recurrence.

### High Expression of O-Linked β-N-Acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) and PKM2 Can Predict Poor Prognosis of HR+/HER2- Breast Cancer

The invention further demonstrates that the co-expression of O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) and pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) provides a synergistic and highly significant prognostic signal in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer, surpassing the predictive power of either biomarker alone. O-GlcNAc, a nutrient-sensitive post-translational modification, has been implicated in cancer progression through its regulation of key signaling pathways, transcription factors, and metabolic enzymes. In the context of breast cancer, elevated O-GlcNAc levels have been associated with enhanced cell survival, increased resistance to endocrine therapy, and promotion of metastatic potential. The present invention reveals that high O-GlcNAc expression, as measured by immunohistochemical Q score, is significantly associated with disease recurrence in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer, with a p-value of 0.038 for disease-free survival. This finding is biologically compelling because O-GlcNAc directly modifies PKM2 at specific residues, including threonine 405 and serine 406, which stabilizes the dimeric form of the enzyme, enhances its nuclear translocation, and amplifies its non-metabolic functions. This post-translational modification creates a feed-forward loop in which increased nutrient flux through the hexosamine biosynthetic pathway leads to higher O-GlcNAcylation of PKM2, which in turn promotes metabolic reprogramming and oncogenic signaling that drives tumor recurrence. The invention demonstrates that patients with high expression of both O-GlcNAc and PKM2 exhibit a dramatically worse prognosis than those with high expression of only one or neither marker. The combined double-marker status of O-GlcNAcHigh/PKM2High was associated with a p-value of 0.004 for disease-free survival, indicating a statistically robust and clinically meaningful separation between risk groups. This synergistic effect was not observed in any other combination of biomarkers, underscoring the unique biological interdependence between these two molecules. The Kaplan–Meier survival curves for the double-positive group showed a steep decline in disease-free survival over time, with a pronounced separation from the double-negative group as early as five years post-diagnosis, suggesting that this metabolic signature identifies tumors with an intrinsic propensity for late recurrence. Importantly, neither O-GlcNAc nor PKM2 expression showed strong correlations with conventional clinicopathological parameters such as tumor size, nodal status, or histological grade, indicating that their prognostic value is independent of anatomical or morphological features. This independence is critical for clinical utility, as it allows the biomarkers to refine risk stratification in patients who would otherwise be classified as low-risk based on standard criteria. The invention further confirms that the Q score, derived from the product of staining intensity and percentage of positive cells, provides a reliable and reproducible quantification method that is suitable for routine clinical use. The consistency of results across multiple pathologists, who were blinded to clinical outcomes, further validates the objectivity of the scoring system. The biological plausibility of this finding is reinforced by the fact that O-GlcNAcylation of PKM2 has been shown in preclinical models to enhance tumor growth and confer resistance to tamoxifen, directly linking the molecular mechanism to clinical outcomes. The invention thus establishes that the co-expression of O-GlcNAc and PKM2 is not merely a coincidental association but a functionally integrated metabolic signature that reflects the underlying biology of aggressive luminal breast cancer. This signature can be reliably detected in routine pathology specimens and used to identify patients who are at high risk of recurrence despite favorable clinical features, thereby enabling more precise and personalized therapeutic interventions.

### Logistic Regression and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve Analysis

The invention further advances the field by employing rigorous statistical modeling to determine the optimal combination of biomarkers and clinical variables for predicting disease-free survival in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer. Logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the contribution of PKM2, O-GlcNAc, and various clinicopathological parameters—including invasive tumor size, T stage, and the Clinical Treatment Score post-5 years (CTS5)—to the likelihood of recurrence over a ten-year period. The results demonstrated that each of these variables, when analyzed individually, contributed significantly to the prediction model, but the most robust performance was achieved when PKM2 and O-GlcNAc were combined with T stage. Multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that O-GlcNAc expression was a consistently significant predictor of disease-free survival across all models, while PKM2 showed a trend toward significance, particularly when analyzed in conjunction with O-GlcNAc. Notably, CTS5, a widely used clinical scoring system, did not achieve statistical significance in the multivariate model, suggesting that its prognostic power may be limited in the context of metabolic biomarkers. The true innovation of the invention lies in the comparative analysis of multiple multivariate models using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, which provides a quantitative measure of diagnostic accuracy through the calculation of the area under the curve (AUC). The AUC for the model incorporating O-GlcNAc, PKM2, and T stage was 0.722, which was significantly higher than the AUC for models incorporating O-GlcNAc and PKM2 with invasive tumor size (0.699) or with CTS5 (0.712). This difference, although seemingly modest, is clinically substantial, as an increase in AUC of 0.03 or greater is considered to represent a meaningful improvement in predictive performance. The single-marker models for PKM2 or O-GlcNAc alone yielded AUC values below 0.65, and the combination of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc without T stage achieved an AUC of 0.685, further underscoring the importance of integrating metabolic biomarkers with anatomical staging. The ROC curve analysis also revealed that the O-GlcNAc-PKM2-T stage model provided the highest sensitivity and specificity across a range of probability thresholds, indicating that it is the most reliable classifier for distinguishing between patients who will and will not experience recurrence. The superiority of this model is not merely statistical but biological, as T stage reflects the extent of local tumor invasion, which is a direct consequence of the metabolic reprogramming driven by PKM2 and O-GlcNAc. The invention therefore establishes that the integration of metabolic biomarkers with anatomical staging provides a more comprehensive assessment of tumor aggressiveness than either component alone. The model was further validated through bootstrapping and cross-validation techniques, confirming its robustness and generalizability. The invention thus provides a validated, statistically rigorous, and clinically interpretable prognostic tool that can be implemented in routine pathology practice to guide therapeutic decisions. The method is not dependent on complex genomic assays or expensive sequencing technologies but instead leverages widely available immunohistochemical techniques and a simple, standardized scoring system, making it accessible across diverse healthcare settings. The invention further enables the development of decision support tools, such as digital algorithms or printed risk charts, that translate the Q scores for PKM2 and O-GlcNAc and the T stage into a predicted probability of recurrence, thereby facilitating clinical adoption and improving patient outcomes through personalized risk stratification.
