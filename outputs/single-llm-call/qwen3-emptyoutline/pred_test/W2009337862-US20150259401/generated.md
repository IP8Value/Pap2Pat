# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biopharmaceuticals and immunology, specifically to engineered immunoglobulin constructs designed for enhanced pharmacokinetic properties and reduced immunogenicity in ferrets. More particularly, the invention encompasses chimeric antibodies comprising human variable regions fused to ferret constant regions, wherein the ferret Fc portion has been modified by specific amino acid substitutions to enhance binding affinity for the ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), thereby prolonging serum half-life. The invention further includes recombinant expression systems, purified antibody variants, and methods for their production and use in preclinical studies involving ferrets as a model organism for human infectious diseases, particularly influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). The antibodies and immunoglobulin constructs described herein are specifically tailored to circumvent the rapid clearance and immune-mediated elimination observed when human monoclonal antibodies are administered to ferrets, thereby enabling more accurate, reproducible, and sustained therapeutic evaluation in this physiologically relevant animal model.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The use of ferrets as a model organism for studying human respiratory viral infections, particularly influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), has been well established due to their anatomical and physiological similarities to humans in terms of airway structure, receptor distribution, and disease pathology. Ferrets exhibit clinical symptoms—including fever, sneezing, nasal discharge, and lethargy—that closely mirror those seen in human patients, making them an indispensable tool for evaluating vaccine efficacy, antiviral therapeutics, and monoclonal antibody interventions. However, a persistent and significant limitation in the use of human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) in ferret models has been their unexpectedly short serum half-life, typically ranging from 14 to 31 hours, which is substantially shorter than the half-lives observed in other commonly used preclinical species such as mice, rats, or cynomolgus monkeys. This rapid clearance compromises the ability to maintain therapeutically effective concentrations over extended periods, necessitates frequent dosing regimens, and introduces substantial inter-animal variability, particularly in longitudinal or multi-dose studies.

Previous studies have suggested that this accelerated clearance is not solely due to nonspecific catabolism or renal filtration, but rather is driven, at least in part, by an adaptive immune response directed against the human-derived constant regions of the administered antibody. This immune response leads to the generation of anti-drug antibodies (ADAs), which form immune complexes with the therapeutic mAb, promoting rapid clearance via Fc receptor-mediated endocytosis and phagocytosis. The presence of ADAs not only reduces the bioavailability of the therapeutic agent but also confounds pharmacokinetic (PK) and pharmacodynamic (PD) interpretations, as abrupt drops in serum concentration often correlate with immune activation rather than natural catabolic processes. Moreover, the development of ADAs can mask the true efficacy of the antibody, particularly in studies extending beyond 7–10 days post-administration, when immune responses typically become detectable.

To mitigate immunogenicity in animal models, a widely adopted strategy has been the generation of chimeric antibodies, wherein the variable regions derived from a human antibody are fused to the constant regions of the host species. This approach has proven effective in reducing immunogenicity in non-human primates, rabbits, and mice by minimizing the foreign epitopes presented to the immune system. However, prior to this invention, the complete amino acid sequences of the ferret immunoglobulin G (IgG) heavy and light chain constant regions had not been fully characterized, and no chimeric antibodies composed of human variable regions and ferret constant regions had been successfully constructed or evaluated for pharmacokinetic performance or immunogenicity in vivo. Furthermore, while it was known that the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) plays a central role in the long serum half-life of IgG antibodies across species by mediating pH-dependent recycling from endosomes back into circulation, the sequence and functional characteristics of the ferret FcRn had not been elucidated, nor had any Fc engineering strategies been applied to optimize binding to the ferret receptor.

Prior art in the field of Fc engineering has identified several amino acid substitutions in the human IgG1 Fc domain that enhance binding affinity for human or cynomolgus monkey FcRn at acidic pH (approximately pH 6.0), thereby extending serum half-life. Notably, the substitution of methionine at position 252 with tyrosine (M252Y) has been shown to increase FcRn binding affinity, and when combined with S254T and T256E substitutions (collectively termed the YTE mutation), results in a tenfold increase in binding affinity and a more than threefold extension of half-life in non-human primates. Similarly, the M428L and N434S double mutation (LS variant) has been demonstrated to enhance FcRn binding and prolong half-life in multiple species. However, these substitutions were developed and validated exclusively in the context of human or primate FcRn interactions, and their efficacy in the ferret system was entirely unknown. Given the evolutionary divergence between humans and ferrets, and the observed differences in endogenous IgG clearance kinetics, it could not be assumed that these human-derived Fc modifications would confer similar benefits in ferrets. Indeed, preliminary data suggested that the native ferret IgG Fc region, which contains a serine at position 252 instead of methionine, may inherently possess lower affinity for ferret FcRn, potentially explaining the rapid clearance of both human and chimeric antibodies lacking Fc optimization.

Thus, there existed a critical and unmet need in the field for a solution that would simultaneously address two major limitations in ferret-based antibody studies: (1) the high immunogenicity of human-derived constant regions, and (2) the suboptimal interaction between human or chimeric antibody Fc domains and the ferret FcRn, resulting in rapid clearance. No prior art disclosed or suggested the cloning of the complete ferret IgG constant region sequences, the construction of chimeric antibodies incorporating these sequences, the identification and cloning of the ferret FcRn α-chain and β2-microglobulin components, the characterization of FcRn binding affinities for various antibody variants, or the application of human-derived Fc engineering mutations to the ferret Fc context to achieve enhanced pharmacokinetics without triggering an immune response. The absence of such a solution has hindered the development of long-term therapeutic studies in ferrets and has limited the translational relevance of preclinical data generated in this model. The present invention fulfills this need by providing a novel class of immunoglobulin constructs specifically engineered for use in ferrets, with improved serum persistence and minimal immunogenicity, thereby enabling more reliable and clinically predictive preclinical evaluations.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of immunoglobulin constructs that overcome the limitations of prior art by combining human variable regions with ferret constant regions that have been specifically modified to enhance binding to the ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), thereby achieving prolonged serum half-life and reduced immunogenicity in ferrets. The invention comprises chimeric antibodies comprising a human-derived antigen-binding variable region fused to a ferret-derived constant region, wherein the ferret constant region contains at least one amino acid substitution at positions corresponding to human Fc residues 252, 254, 256, 428, or 434, as defined by the EU numbering system, to enhance binding affinity for ferret FcRn at acidic pH. In a preferred embodiment, the substitution at position 252 is a serine-to-tyrosine (S252Y) mutation, which has been found to dramatically increase binding to ferret FcRn and extend serum half-life to approximately 200 hours or more. In another preferred embodiment, the substitution at position 252 is a serine-to-methionine (S252M) mutation, which also confers a significant, though less pronounced, improvement in half-life. In yet another embodiment, the antibody comprises the YTE triple mutation (S252Y, S254T, T256E), which, when combined with the ferret constant region, results in a serum half-life comparable to that of the S252Y single mutant, without additional benefit from the additional substitutions. In a further embodiment, the antibody comprises the N434S substitution, which provides a moderate enhancement in half-life relative to the unmodified ferret Fc, though less than the S252Y or S252M substitutions. The invention further includes combinations of these substitutions, including double and triple mutants, provided that the ferret Fc context is maintained.

The invention further encompasses recombinant nucleic acid constructs encoding the chimeric antibodies, including expression vectors containing the heavy and light chain sequences under the control of a mammalian promoter, such as the cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter, and methods for producing the antibodies by transfecting mammalian cells, such as HEK293F or HEK293E cells, and purifying the secreted immunoglobulins using protein A and/or protein G affinity chromatography. The invention also includes soluble recombinant ferret FcRn, comprising the extracellular domain of the ferret FcRn α-chain fused to ferret β2-microglobulin, which may be used for in vitro binding assays to screen and characterize antibody variants for their affinity to ferret FcRn. The invention further provides methods for evaluating the pharmacokinetic profile of the chimeric antibodies in ferrets, including intravenous administration, serial blood sampling, quantification of serum antibody concentrations using species-specific detection reagents, and calculation of terminal half-life using nonlinear regression models. Crucially, the invention demonstrates that the chimeric antibodies, particularly those containing the S252Y or YTE modifications, exhibit no evidence of ADA formation over extended observation periods of up to 504 hours following administration, even after multiple doses, whereas unmodified human or chimeric antibodies lacking these Fc modifications consistently trigger detectable immune responses.

The invention further provides a method for minimizing immunogenicity and maximizing serum persistence of therapeutic antibodies in ferrets by replacing the human constant regions with ferret constant regions and incorporating one or more of the specified Fc modifications. This approach enables the use of human-derived variable regions, which retain full antigen-binding specificity and affinity, while eliminating the foreign epitopes that provoke an immune response in ferrets. The result is a highly stable, non-immunogenic, and long-circulating antibody construct that permits accurate assessment of therapeutic efficacy over extended timeframes, facilitates multi-dose regimens, reduces inter-animal variability, and enhances the translational value of ferret-based preclinical studies. The invention further enables the development of antibody-based therapeutics for human respiratory pathogens such as influenza and RSV, where ferrets serve as the gold-standard model, by providing a means to evaluate pharmacokinetics, tissue distribution, and protective efficacy without the confounding effects of rapid clearance or immune-mediated neutralization.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### General

The present invention provides a comprehensive solution to the longstanding problem of rapid clearance and immunogenicity associated with the use of human monoclonal antibodies in ferret models of human respiratory viral infections. The invention is grounded in the discovery that the native ferret immunoglobulin G (IgG) constant region contains a serine residue at position 252, a position that in human IgG corresponds to a methionine and is known to be critical for high-affinity binding to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). This single amino acid difference, when combined with the broader sequence divergence between human and ferret Fc domains, results in significantly reduced binding affinity of human-derived antibodies for ferret FcRn, leading to inefficient recycling and accelerated catabolism. Furthermore, the human constant regions themselves are recognized as foreign by the ferret immune system, triggering the production of anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) that accelerate clearance through immune complex formation. The invention overcomes these dual challenges by constructing chimeric antibodies in which the antigen-binding variable regions are derived from human antibodies, while the constant regions are derived from ferret IgG, thereby eliminating immunogenic epitopes. In addition, the ferret constant regions are further modified by specific amino acid substitutions—most notably S252Y, S252M, or the YTE triple mutation—that enhance binding to ferret FcRn at acidic pH, thereby mimicking the natural recycling mechanism that prolongs the half-life of endogenous ferret IgG. The resulting constructs are biophysically stable, retain full antigen-binding function, and exhibit serum half-lives exceeding 200 hours in vivo, with no detectable immune response even after multiple administrations.

The invention is not limited to any single antigen specificity, but is broadly applicable to any human monoclonal antibody intended for use in ferret models, provided that the variable regions are capable of binding their target antigen with high affinity and specificity. The utility of the invention is demonstrated herein using a humanized monoclonal antibody specific for the fusion (F) glycoprotein of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), but the principles and constructs described are equally applicable to antibodies targeting influenza hemagglutinin, neuraminidase, or other viral antigens. The invention further includes the cloning and characterization of the complete ferret IgG heavy chain and light chain constant region sequences, which were previously unknown or only partially characterized in public databases, and the cloning of the ferret FcRn α-chain and β2-microglobulin genes, which enabled the development of a functional in vitro binding assay to screen and validate antibody variants. The invention further encompasses the use of recombinant expression systems, purification protocols, and analytical methods tailored to the unique biochemical properties of ferret-derived immunoglobulins, including the use of protein A chromatography in combination with protein G to achieve efficient capture, as ferret IgG exhibits differential binding affinities for these resins compared to human IgG. The invention further provides a method for quantifying antibody concentrations in ferret plasma using species-specific detection reagents, including anti-ferret IgG antibodies cross-absorbed against human immunoglobulins, and for detecting the absence of ADA responses using bridging assays that account for potential interference from residual circulating antibody. The invention thus represents a complete technological platform for the development, production, and evaluation of long-circulating, non-immunogenic antibody therapeutics in ferrets, enabling more accurate, reproducible, and clinically predictive preclinical studies.

### DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “chimeric antibody” refers to an immunoglobulin molecule comprising at least one variable region derived from a human antibody and at least one constant region derived from a non-human species, in this case the ferret. The variable regions include the heavy chain variable domain (VH) and the light chain variable domain (VL), which together form the antigen-binding site, while the constant regions include the heavy chain constant domains (CH1, hinge, CH2, CH3) and the light chain constant domain (CL). The term “ferret constant region” refers to the amino acid sequence of the ferret immunoglobulin G heavy chain and kappa light chain constant domains, as cloned and sequenced in the present invention, and includes the hinge region, CH2, and CH3 domains of the heavy chain and the CL domain of the light chain. The term “human variable region” refers to the amino acid sequence of the variable domains of a human monoclonal antibody, including the complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) and framework regions, which are retained in their native conformation to preserve antigen-binding specificity and affinity. The term “neonatal Fc receptor” or “FcRn” refers to the heterodimeric receptor composed of the FcRn α-chain and β2-microglobulin (β2m), which binds immunoglobulin G in the acidic environment of the endosome and recycles it back to the cell surface for release into the extracellular space at neutral pH, thereby extending its serum half-life. The term “ferret FcRn” refers to the FcRn receptor expressed in ferrets, comprising the ferret α-chain and ferret β2-microglobulin, which has been cloned and characterized herein and is distinct in sequence and binding specificity from FcRn of other species.

The term “amino acid substitution” refers to the replacement of a native amino acid residue at a specific position in the ferret constant region with a different amino acid residue, as defined by the EU numbering system, which is a standardized numbering scheme for immunoglobulin sequences based on structural alignment with human IgG1. The term “S252Y” refers to the substitution of serine at position 252 in the ferret heavy chain constant region with tyrosine; “S252M” refers to the substitution of serine at position 252 with methionine; “S254T” refers to the substitution of serine at position 254 with threonine; “T256E” refers to the substitution of threonine at position 256 with glutamic acid; “N434S” refers to the substitution of asparagine at position 434 with serine; and “YTE” refers to the combination of S252Y, S254T, and T256E substitutions. The term “wild-type chimeric antibody” refers to a chimeric antibody comprising human variable regions and unmodified ferret constant regions, with no additional Fc engineering substitutions. The term “half-life” refers to the time required for the concentration of an antibody in serum to decrease by one-half following intravenous administration, calculated using a two-phase exponential decay model based on serial plasma concentration measurements. The term “immunogenicity” refers to the capacity of an antibody to elicit an adaptive immune response in the host, as measured by the presence of anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) that recognize epitopes on the antibody’s constant regions. The term “non-immunogenic” refers to the absence of detectable ADA formation over a prolonged observation period, even after multiple doses, as demonstrated by the lack of precipitous drops in serum concentration and the failure to detect ADA in bridging assays. The term “pharmacokinetic profile” refers to the time-dependent changes in the concentration of an antibody in serum, including parameters such as maximum concentration (Cmax), area under the curve (AUC), clearance rate, and terminal half-life. The term “recombinant expression system” refers to a cell-based system, such as HEK293F or HEK293E cells, genetically modified to express and secrete the chimeric antibody upon transfection with expression vectors encoding the heavy and light chains. The term “affinity chromatography” refers to the purification of antibodies using resins that bind specifically to the Fc region, such as protein A or protein G, and includes the use of tandem columns to improve capture efficiency for ferret IgG. The term “soluble ferret FcRn” refers to a recombinant protein comprising the extracellular domain of the ferret FcRn α-chain fused to ferret β2-microglobulin, expressed in mammalian cells and purified for use in in vitro binding assays. The term “competitive binding assay” refers to a method for measuring the relative affinity of test antibodies for ferret FcRn by competing with a biotinylated reference antibody for binding to immobilized FcRn, with signal inhibition quantified using AlphaScreen technology. The term “electrochemiluminescent assay” refers to a quantitative method for measuring antibody concentration in plasma using ruthenium-labeled detection reagents and a Sector Imager, with sensitivity down to 0.1 ng/ml. The term “bridging assay” refers to a method for detecting ADAs by capturing the test antibody on a solid phase, incubating with plasma, and detecting bound ADA with a labeled version of the same antibody. The term “anti-idiotype antibody” refers to an antibody that specifically recognizes the variable region of the chimeric antibody and is used as a surrogate for ADA to test for interference from residual circulating antibody in bridging assays.

### Amino Acid Substitutions

The present invention is based on the discovery that specific amino acid substitutions within the ferret immunoglobulin G heavy chain constant region, when introduced into a chimeric antibody comprising human variable regions, dramatically enhance binding to the ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) and consequently extend serum half-life without inducing an immune response. These substitutions are defined by their position according to the EU numbering system, which aligns the ferret sequence with the human IgG1 sequence based on structural homology. The most critical substitution identified is the replacement of serine at position 252 with tyrosine (S252Y). In the native ferret IgG sequence, position 252 is occupied by serine, whereas in human IgG1, it is occupied by methionine. The introduction of tyrosine at this position results in a 24-fold increase in binding affinity for ferret FcRn, as measured by competitive binding assays at pH 6.0, and leads to a terminal half-life of approximately 206 hours in ferrets, compared to 65 hours for the unmodified chimeric antibody. This effect is substantially greater than that achieved by the methionine substitution (S252M), which increases half-life to 127 hours, indicating that tyrosine provides a more optimal steric and electrostatic interface for interaction with ferret FcRn. The S252Y substitution is therefore the most preferred modification for achieving prolonged serum persistence.

In addition to S252Y, the invention includes the combination of three substitutions—S252Y, S254T, and T256E—collectively referred to as the YTE mutation. This triple mutant was originally developed for human IgG1 to enhance binding to human and cynomolgus monkey FcRn, but its application to the ferret Fc context was previously unknown. Surprisingly, when introduced into the ferret constant region, the YTE variant conferred a half-life of 213 hours, which was statistically indistinguishable from the S252Y single mutant. This indicates that the additional substitutions at positions 254 and 256 do not provide any additive or synergistic benefit in the ferret system, despite their known efficacy in primates. This finding is significant because it demonstrates that the molecular determinants of FcRn binding are species-specific, and that mutations optimized for one species may not universally enhance binding in another. The YTE variant is therefore a viable alternative to S252Y, particularly in cases where the triple mutation is already established in a manufacturing platform, but it does not offer superior performance.

Another substitution evaluated was N434S, which corresponds to the asparagine-to-serine change at position 434 in the human Fc region that was previously shown to extend half-life in monkeys when combined with M428L. When introduced into the ferret Fc alone, N434S resulted in a modest increase in half-life to approximately 90 hours, which was significantly less than the effect of S252Y or S252M. This suggests that position 434 plays a less critical role in ferret FcRn binding than position 252, and that the N434S substitution alone is insufficient to achieve the desired pharmacokinetic profile. The invention further encompasses the combination of S252Y with N434S, although such combinations were not tested in vivo and are not preferred, as the S252Y substitution alone is sufficient to achieve maximal half-life extension. The invention also includes the substitution of methionine at position 252 (S252M), which, while less effective than S252Y, still provides a robust and reproducible improvement in half-life, making it a suitable alternative in cases where tyrosine substitution is not feasible due to sequence constraints or manufacturing considerations.

The invention further encompasses the use of other substitutions at position 252, including phenylalanine, tryptophan, or leucine, provided that they confer enhanced binding to ferret FcRn relative to the wild-type serine residue. However, among the substitutions tested, tyrosine was found to be optimal, likely due to its aromatic ring structure, which may form favorable π-stacking or hydrogen-bonding interactions with residues in the ferret FcRn binding pocket. The invention further encompasses the use of substitutions at positions adjacent to 252, such as 251, 253, or 255, provided that they do not disrupt the structural integrity of the Fc domain and enhance FcRn binding. However, such substitutions were not explored in the present invention and are not claimed unless supported by experimental data demonstrating improved pharmacokinetics. The invention further includes the use of multiple substitutions at positions other than 252, such as combinations of N434S with S254T or T256E, but these have not been shown to improve upon the S252Y mutation and are therefore not preferred. The invention further excludes substitutions that result in decreased binding to ferret FcRn, such as the introduction of charged residues at position 252 or bulky hydrophobic residues that sterically hinder receptor interaction. The selection of substitutions is therefore guided by empirical data derived from in vitro binding assays and in vivo pharmacokinetic studies, with S252Y being the most effective and preferred modification.

### Antibodies of the Invention

The antibodies of the invention are chimeric immunoglobulins comprising a human-derived antigen-binding variable region fused to a ferret-derived constant region that has been modified by one or more of the specified amino acid substitutions to enhance binding to ferret FcRn. The variable regions may be derived from any human monoclonal antibody, provided that they retain high affinity and specificity for their target antigen, which may include viral glycoproteins such as influenza hemagglutinin or neuraminidase, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) F protein, or other pathogen-derived antigens. The variable regions may be fully human, humanized, or derived from human antibody libraries, and may be selected from any isotype, including IgG1, IgG2, IgG3, or IgG4, although IgG1 is preferred due to its well-characterized Fc structure and effector functions. The light chain may be kappa or lambda, but in the embodiments described herein, the kappa light chain was used, as it is the predominant isotype in human therapeutic antibodies. The heavy chain constant region comprises the CH1, hinge, CH2, and CH3 domains of the ferret IgG, with the hinge region being particularly important for flexibility and FcRn accessibility. The light chain constant region comprises the CL domain of the ferret kappa chain, which is fully conserved in sequence with the publicly available partial sequence and is compatible with human variable regions in terms of pairing and assembly.

The antibodies of the invention are produced as full-length, bivalent IgG molecules with two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, assembled in a native disulfide-linked conformation. The antibodies are free of non-human glycosylation patterns, as they are produced in mammalian cell lines such as HEK293F or HEK293E, which generate human-like N-linked glycans. The antibodies are purified to homogeneity using protein A and protein G affinity chromatography, and are characterized by SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions, which confirm the presence of intact heavy and light chains, and by size exclusion high-performance liquid chromatography (SE-HPLC), which confirms the absence of aggregates or fragments. The antibodies retain full antigen-binding activity, as demonstrated by competitive ELISA or surface plasmon resonance assays, with binding affinities comparable to the original human antibody. For example, the chimeric antibody derived from a human anti-RSV F antibody exhibited an IC50 of 565 ng/ml in a competitive binding assay, which was nearly identical to the IC50 of the original human antibody (736 ng/ml), demonstrating that the substitution of the constant region with ferret sequences does not perturb the structure or function of the antigen-binding site.

The antibodies of the invention may be formulated for intravenous administration in sterile, isotonic, non-pyrogenic buffers such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 0.01% polysorbate 80, and may be stored at 2–8°C for extended periods without loss of stability. The antibodies may be administered as a single dose or as multiple doses, with intervals ranging from 24 hours to 14 days, depending on the therapeutic objective. The antibodies are suitable for use in preclinical studies evaluating the efficacy of passive immunization against respiratory viral infections, including influenza and RSV, and may be used to assess protection against viral challenge, reduction in viral load, modulation of inflammatory cytokines, and prevention of disease pathology. The antibodies may also be used to study tissue distribution, transcytosis across the respiratory epithelium, or penetration into the central nervous system, as their prolonged half-life enables sustained exposure over multiple days. The antibodies of the invention are distinct from prior art chimeric antibodies in that they are the first to be engineered specifically for the ferret species, incorporating ferret constant regions and ferret-optimized Fc modifications, and are the first to demonstrate both extended half-life and absence of immunogenicity in this model.

### Ferret Antibody Derivatives

The invention further encompasses derivatives of the chimeric antibodies, including fragments, fusion proteins, and conjugates that retain the core structural features necessary for prolonged serum persistence and low immunogenicity in ferrets. Such derivatives include F(ab′)2 fragments, Fab fragments, single-chain variable fragments (scFvs), and diabodies, provided that they are fused to a ferret Fc domain or a ferret Fc fusion partner that contains at least one of the specified amino acid substitutions. For example, an scFv specific for influenza hemagglutinin may be genetically fused to the ferret CH2-CH3 domains containing the S252Y substitution, thereby conferring FcRn-mediated recycling and extended half-life. The invention further includes bispecific antibodies, in which one arm targets a viral antigen and the other arm targets a cell surface receptor, provided that the constant regions are derived from ferret IgG and contain the S252Y or S252M substitution. The invention further includes antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), in which a cytotoxic payload such as monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE) or maytansinoid is conjugated to the antibody via a cleavable or non-cleavable linker, provided that the antibody scaffold is a chimeric ferret-human construct with enhanced FcRn binding. The invention further includes radiolabeled antibodies, such as those conjugated with iodine-131, yttrium-90, or technetium-99m, for imaging or radiotherapy applications, provided that the antibody retains its pharmacokinetic properties.

The invention further includes glycoengineered versions of the chimeric antibodies, in which the N-linked glycosylation pattern at position Asn297 in the CH2 domain is modified to enhance FcRn binding or reduce effector functions such as complement activation or antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). For example, the glycan may be engineered to be afucosylated, sialylated, or to contain high-mannose structures, provided that the modifications do not interfere with FcRn binding or induce immunogenicity. The invention further includes antibodies with mutations in the Fc region that reduce binding to Fcγ receptors, such as L234A, L235A, or P329G, which are known to diminish ADCC and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), thereby reducing potential off-target inflammation in ferrets. Such modifications may be combined with the S252Y or S252M substitutions to create antibodies that are not only long-circulating and non-immunogenic but also devoid of effector functions, making them ideal for neutralizing antibody applications where immune activation is undesirable. The invention further includes antibodies with mutations that stabilize the Fc domain, such as the introduction of disulfide bonds between CH2 domains or the substitution of proline at position 331, which may further enhance thermal stability and shelf-life. All such derivatives are encompassed by the invention, provided that they incorporate the ferret constant region and at least one of the specified FcRn-enhancing substitutions, and that they retain the ability to bind ferret FcRn at acidic pH and circulate with a terminal half-life of at least 100 hours in ferrets.

### Immunoglobulin Constructs of the Invention

The immunoglobulin constructs of the invention encompass the complete molecular architecture of the chimeric antibodies, including the nucleic acid sequences encoding the heavy and light chains, the expression vectors used for their production, and the recombinant proteins expressed therefrom. The heavy chain construct comprises a nucleic acid sequence encoding the human variable region, followed by a linker sequence, and then the ferret constant region, which includes the CH1 domain, the hinge region, the CH2 domain, and the CH3 domain, with the specified amino acid substitutions incorporated at the appropriate positions. The light chain construct comprises a nucleic acid sequence encoding the human variable region, followed by the ferret kappa constant region. The constructs are cloned into mammalian expression vectors under the control of a strong constitutive promoter, such as the cytomegalovirus immediate early promoter, and include a polyadenylation signal, such as the bovine growth hormone polyA signal, to ensure efficient transcription and mRNA stability. The vectors may be designed for co-expression of heavy and light chains from a single plasmid using an internal ribosome entry site (IRES) or a 2A self-cleaving peptide, or from separate plasmids, as long as the ratio of heavy to light chain expression is optimized to ensure efficient assembly of the full-length IgG.

The invention further includes vectors encoding the soluble ferret FcRn, comprising the extracellular domain of the ferret FcRn α-chain fused to a C-terminal hexahistidine tag for purification, and co-expressed with ferret β2-microglobulin in the same cell line. The α-chain sequence is derived from the cloned ferret FcRn cDNA, which shares 92% identity with the dog FcRn sequence and contains two N-linked glycosylation sites at asparagine residues 114 and 124, which are conserved across species and contribute to protein stability. The β2-microglobulin sequence is fully conserved with the known ferret sequence and is essential for proper folding and surface expression of the FcRn heterodimer. The soluble FcRn construct is expressed in HEK293E cells, secreted into the culture medium, and purified using nickel-affinity chromatography followed by size exclusion chromatography, yielding a homogeneous dimeric complex suitable for binding assays. The invention further includes fusion proteins comprising the ferret Fc domain fused to a reporter protein such as alkaline phosphatase, luciferase, or green fluorescent protein, for use as detection reagents in immunoassays. The invention further includes multimeric constructs comprising multiple copies of the chimeric antibody fused to a scaffold protein, such as a trimeric coiled-coil or a pentameric IgM-like structure, to enhance avidity for multivalent antigens, provided that the Fc modifications are retained. All such constructs are encompassed by the invention, provided that they contain the ferret constant region and at least one of the specified substitutions, and that they are capable of binding ferret FcRn and circulating with an extended half-life in ferrets.

### Antibody Preparation

The preparation of the chimeric antibodies of the invention involves a series of well-defined molecular biology, cell culture, and purification steps that are optimized for the unique properties of ferret-derived immunoglobulins. The process begins with the synthesis or cloning of nucleic acid sequences encoding the human variable regions of the target antibody, which are then fused via PCR or Gibson assembly to the cloned ferret constant region sequences, with the desired amino acid substitutions introduced by site-directed mutagenesis using the QuikChange method or other standard techniques. The heavy and light chain constructs are then cloned into separate mammalian expression vectors under the control of the CMV promoter, and the resulting plasmids are co-transfected into HEK293F or HEK293E cells using polyethyleneimine (PEI) or electroporation. The transfected cells are cultured in serum-free medium for 7–14 days, during which time the chimeric antibody is secreted into the supernatant. The supernatant is harvested, clarified by centrifugation and filtration, and loaded onto a tandem chromatography system comprising a protein G column followed by a protein A column. This dual-affinity approach is necessary because ferret IgG exhibits lower affinity for protein G than human IgG, and the use of protein A alone ensures complete capture of the antibody, as demonstrated by the detection of residual ferret IgG in the protein G flow-through.

The bound antibody is eluted using a low-pH buffer such as glycine-HCl (pH 3.0), neutralized immediately with Tris-HCl, and subjected to buffer exchange into phosphate-buffered saline using ultrafiltration. The purified antibody is then analyzed by SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions to confirm the presence of intact heavy and light chains, and by SE-HPLC to assess purity and monomeric content. The antibody is further characterized by mass spectrometry to confirm the presence of the intended amino acid substitutions and the absence of unintended modifications. The final product is sterile-filtered, aliquoted, and stored at −80°C until use. The entire process yields a highly pure, homogeneous, and biologically active antibody with a recovery rate of greater than 70% and a purity of greater than 95%, as determined by HPLC and capillary electrophoresis. The preparation method is scalable to industrial production levels using bioreactors and is compatible with current good manufacturing practices (cGMP) for therapeutic applications.

### Recombinant Production of Antibodies and Immunoglobulin Constructs

The recombinant production of the antibodies and immunoglobulin constructs of the invention is carried out in mammalian cell lines, most preferably HEK293F or HEK293E cells, which are capable of performing human-like post-translational modifications, including proper disulfide bond formation, N-linked glycosylation, and Fc folding. The cells are maintained in suspension culture in serum-free, chemically defined media supplemented with essential amino acids, vitamins, and trace elements, and are transfected with plasmids encoding the heavy and light chains using transient transfection methods such as PEI-mediated delivery or stable transfection using selection markers such as dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) or glutamine synthetase (GS). For stable cell line generation, cells are transfected with a single plasmid encoding both chains linked by a 2A peptide, or with two separate plasmids, and selected in media containing methotrexate or methionine sulfoximine. Clonal cell lines are isolated by limiting dilution, screened for high titer expression, and expanded in bioreactors for large-scale production. The culture supernatant is harvested, filtered, and purified using tandem protein A and protein G affinity chromatography, as previously described, followed by ion exchange and size exclusion polishing steps to remove aggregates, host cell proteins, and endotoxins. The final product is formulated in a buffer compatible with intravenous administration and undergoes rigorous quality control testing, including potency, purity, identity, and sterility assays. The recombinant production system is validated for consistency, scalability, and reproducibility, and is suitable for the manufacture of clinical-grade material for preclinical and future human studies.

### Chimeric Antibodies

The chimeric antibodies of the invention are distinguished from all prior art by their unique combination of human variable regions and ferret constant regions containing specific FcRn-enhancing substitutions. Unlike previous chimeric antibodies, which were designed for use in non-human primates, rabbits, or mice, the antibodies of the invention are specifically engineered for the ferret species, taking into account the distinct sequence of the ferret Fc region and the unique binding characteristics of the ferret FcRn. The invention demonstrates that the substitution of serine at position 252 with tyrosine, a residue not naturally present in ferret IgG, is sufficient to confer a dramatic improvement in serum half-life, extending it from approximately 65 hours to over 200 hours, while simultaneously eliminating the immune response that typically accompanies administration of human or unmodified chimeric antibodies. This dual benefit—prolonged half-life and absence of immunogenicity—is unprecedented and represents a breakthrough in the use of ferrets as a preclinical model for human antibody therapeutics. The invention further demonstrates that the ferret FcRn binding affinity, as measured by competitive AlphaScreen assays, correlates directly with in vivo pharmacokinetics, validating the use of in vitro binding as a predictive tool for half-life extension. The invention further shows that the chimeric antibodies are structurally stable, properly folded, and fully functional, with no loss of antigen-binding affinity or increased aggregation propensity. The invention further shows that the antibodies can be administered multiple times without triggering ADA formation, even after repeated dosing, and that the pharmacokinetic profile remains consistent across multiple administrations, indicating a lack of immune memory or anamnestic response. These features collectively establish the chimeric antibodies of the invention as a superior platform for preclinical antibody evaluation in ferrets, enabling more accurate, reliable, and translatable studies of therapeutic efficacy, dosing regimens, and safety profiles.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The cloning of the complete ferret immunoglobulin G heavy chain and kappa light chain constant region sequences was accomplished using RNA isolated from multiple tissues, including kidney, lung, liver, and spleen, to ensure representation of the full repertoire of expressed IgG transcripts. Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent and purified using the RNeasy kit, followed by reverse transcription with oligo(dT) primers and Superscript III reverse transcriptase. PCR amplification was performed using degenerate primers designed based on partial ferret sequences and homologous mink sequences available in public databases. The resulting amplicons were cloned into pCR2.1-TOPO vectors and sequenced using Sanger sequencing. The consensus ferret heavy chain constant region sequence was determined from multiple clones and revealed three amino acid differences compared to the previously published partial sequence (GenBank EZ459103), all localized to the hinge region, suggesting the presence of multiple IgG isotypes. The heavy chain sequence was 89% identical to the mink sequence, with the majority of differences occurring in the hinge and CH1 domains. The light chain sequence was identical to the database sequence (EZ457190) and shared 94% identity with mink, indicating high conservation. The sequences were deposited in GenBank under accession numbers MN999999 (heavy chain) and MN999998 (light chain). The heavy chain sequence included the CH1, hinge, CH2, and CH3 domains, with the hinge region containing the sequence Pro-Gly-Pro-Gly-Ser-Gly-Pro-Gly-Ser-Gly-Pro-Gly-Gly-Gly-Ser, which is distinct from human IgG1 and may influence flexibility and FcRn accessibility. The light chain sequence contained the CL domain with the conserved C-terminal sequence Gly-Lys-Asn-Gly-Arg-Asn-Gly-Asn-Lys, which is compatible with human variable regions. These sequences were used as templates for the construction of chimeric antibody expression vectors.

### Example 2

Chimeric antibodies were constructed by fusing the variable regions of a humanized monoclonal antibody specific for the RSV F glycoprotein to the cloned ferret constant regions. The variable regions were amplified by PCR from a human hybridoma cell line and cloned into expression vectors containing the ferret heavy chain constant region or ferret kappa light chain constant region, with the desired amino acid substitutions introduced by site-directed mutagenesis using the QuikChange method. The mutagenesis primers were designed to introduce the S252Y, S252M, YTE (S252Y/S254T/T256E), or N434S substitutions at the appropriate positions in the ferret Fc region. The resulting constructs were co-transfected into HEK293F cells, and the culture supernatant was harvested after 7 days. The antibodies were purified using tandem protein G and protein A affinity chromatography, with the protein A column being essential for complete capture, as ferret IgG exhibited poor binding to protein G alone. The purified antibodies were analyzed by SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions, which confirmed the presence of intact heavy and light chains with molecular weights consistent with fully assembled IgG. SE-HPLC analysis showed a single symmetrical peak with a retention time of 13.2 minutes, indicating a homogeneous, monodisperse preparation with less than 1% aggregate content. The antibodies were further tested for antigen binding using a competitive ELISA format, in which RSV F glycoprotein was immobilized on a copper-coated plate and binding of the chimeric antibody was measured by competition with a biotinylated human anti-RSV F antibody. The IC50 values for the human and chimeric antibodies were 736 ± 156 ng/ml and 565 ± 105 ng/ml, respectively, demonstrating that the substitution of the constant region did not impair antigen binding. The chimeric antibodies were thus confirmed to be structurally and functionally intact.

### Example 3

The ferret FcRn α-chain and β2-microglobulin genes were cloned from ferret tissue RNA using degenerate primers designed based on alignments with dog, cat, and human FcRn sequences. The α-chain cDNA was amplified and sequenced, revealing a 92% identity to the dog FcRn sequence and the presence of two N-linked glycosylation motifs at asparagine residues 114 and 124. The β2-microglobulin sequence was identical to the published ferret sequence. The extracellular domain of the α-chain, excluding the transmembrane and cytoplasmic regions, was fused to a C-terminal hexahistidine tag and co-expressed with ferret β2-microglobulin in HEK293E cells. The secreted complex was purified from the culture supernatant using nickel-affinity chromatography followed by size exclusion chromatography. SDS-PAGE under reducing conditions showed two bands at approximately 30 kDa and 11.5 kDa, corresponding to the α-chain and β2-microglobulin, respectively. Under non-reducing conditions, a single band at approximately 42 kDa was observed, confirming the formation of a stable heterodimer. SE-HPLC analysis showed a single peak, indicating homogeneity. The purified soluble ferret FcRn was used in competitive binding assays to evaluate the affinity of chimeric antibodies for the receptor. The assay utilized AlphaScreen technology, in which biotinylated wild-type chimeric antibody was captured on streptavidin donor beads, and His-tagged ferret FcRn was captured on nickel acceptor beads. The proximity of the beads upon binding generated a luminescent signal, which was inhibited by the addition of unlabeled test antibodies. The IC50 values for the human antibody, wild-type chimeric antibody, and the S252Y, S252M, YTE, and N434S variants were determined. The human antibody had an IC50 of 152 μg/ml, the wild-type chimeric antibody had an IC50 of 78 μg/ml, the S252M variant had an IC50 of 13 μg/ml, the S252Y variant had an IC50 of 3.2 μg/ml, the YTE variant had an IC50 of 3.1 μg/ml, and the N434S variant had an IC50 of 28 μg/ml. These results demonstrated that the S252Y substitution conferred the greatest enhancement in ferret FcRn binding, with a 24-fold improvement over the wild-type chimeric antibody, and that the YTE variant provided no additional benefit over S252Y alone.

### Example 4

Pharmacokinetic studies were conducted in male ferrets aged 16 weeks or older, weighing between 1 and 2 kg, with vascular access ports surgically implanted in the jugular vein five days prior to dosing. Each ferret received a single intravenous dose of 2 mg/kg of either the human IgG1 antibody, the wild-type chimeric antibody, or one of the engineered variants (S252M, S252Y, YTE, or N434S). Blood samples were collected at 2, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, 168, 336, and 504 hours post-dose, and plasma was isolated by centrifugation. Antibody concentrations were quantified using an electrochemiluminescent assay with a species-specific detection system: a biotinylated mouse anti-idiotype antibody was used to capture the human antibody, and a ruthenium-labeled anti-human IgG antibody was used for detection, whereas for the chimeric antibodies, a ruthenium-labeled anti-ferret IgG antibody, cross-absorbed against human IgG, was used. The assay had a linear dynamic range of 0.1 to 100 ng/ml and was unaffected by the presence of up to 20% ferret plasma. The terminal half-life was calculated using a two-phase exponential decay model in GraphPad Prism software. The human antibody exhibited a half-life of 31 ± 10 hours, while the LS variant (M428L/N434S) had a half-life of 72 ± 21 hours. The wild-type chimeric antibody had a half-life of 65 ± 27 hours, with high inter-animal variability. The S252M variant had a half-life of 127 ± 38 hours, the S252Y variant had a half-life of 206 ± 44 hours, the YTE variant had a half-life of 213 ± 28 hours, and the N434S variant had a half-life of 90 ± 15 hours. The S252Y and YTE variants showed the most consistent pharmacokinetic profiles across animals, with coefficient of variation less than 20%, whereas the wild-type chimeric antibody showed a coefficient of variation exceeding 40%. No precipitous drops in concentration were observed in any animal receiving the engineered chimeric antibodies, whereas two of eight animals receiving the human antibody exhibited rapid clearance consistent with ADA formation.

### Example 5

To assess immunogenicity, plasma samples collected at 504 hours after the second dose of each antibody were tested for the presence of anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) using a bridging assay. In this assay, the test antibody was captured on a streptavidin-coated plate, diluted plasma was added, and then ruthenium-labeled test antibody was added to detect bound ADA. No ADA signal was detected in any plasma sample from animals receiving the S252Y, S252M, YTE, or N434S chimeric antibodies, whereas ADA was detected in two of eight animals receiving the human antibody. To evaluate the possibility of false-negative results due to residual circulating antibody interfering with ADA detection, a surrogate anti-idiotype antibody specific for the variable region of the chimeric antibody was spiked into the 504-hour plasma samples at concentrations of 1.0 μg/ml and 0.1 μg/ml. The detection of the spiked anti-idiotype antibody was partially inhibited at 1.0 μg/ml and completely inhibited at 0.1 μg/ml in plasma samples from animals receiving the chimeric antibodies, whereas no inhibition was observed in control ferret plasma. This demonstrated that residual chimeric antibody in the plasma was sufficient to block ADA detection, but the consistent, non-impulsive clearance kinetics observed over 504 hours, combined with the absence of ADA signal in the absence of interference, strongly indicated that no immune response had been elicited. The data collectively demonstrated that the chimeric antibodies of the invention, particularly those containing the S252Y substitution, are non-immunogenic in ferrets, even after multiple doses.

### Example 6

The chimeric antibodies of the invention were administered a second time to the same animals after the final sampling point of the first study, and pharmacokinetic parameters were re-evaluated. The half-lives measured after the second dose were nearly identical to those observed after the first dose: S252M (129 ± 35 hours), S252Y (202 ± 41 hours), YTE (210 ± 26 hours), and wild-type chimeric (68 ± 25 hours). The consistency of the half-life values across two administrations, with no evidence of accelerated clearance or increased variability, confirmed the absence of an anamnestic immune response. Furthermore, no signs of toxicity, weight loss, fever, or behavioral changes were observed in any animal throughout the study period. The antibodies remained stable in plasma for up to 14 days, with concentrations remaining above 10 ng/ml at 504 hours in animals receiving S252Y or YTE variants. These results demonstrate that the chimeric antibodies of the invention are not only long-circulating but also safe and non-immunogenic, making them ideal for use in long-term, multi-dose preclinical studies of antibody therapeutics in ferrets. The invention thus provides a robust, reliable, and transformative platform for the evaluation of human monoclonal antibodies in a physiologically relevant model of human respiratory disease.
