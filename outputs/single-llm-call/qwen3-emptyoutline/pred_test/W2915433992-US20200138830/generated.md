# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The treatment of type 1 diabetes remains one of the most persistent and complex challenges in modern medicine. Despite decades of research and numerous clinical interventions aimed at preserving or restoring endogenous insulin production, no therapy has yet demonstrated sustained efficacy in halting disease progression or reversing established β-cell loss over the long term. The prevailing consensus among researchers and clinicians is that monotherapies targeting either immune modulation or β-cell regeneration alone are insufficient to achieve durable clinical outcomes. This is due in large part to the dual pathophysiological nature of type 1 diabetes: an autoimmune destruction of pancreatic β-cells accompanied by an inability of the remaining β-cell population to regenerate sufficiently to compensate for the loss. Consequently, a therapeutic strategy that simultaneously addresses both the immune-mediated attack and the failure of β-cell homeostasis is widely regarded as essential for meaningful clinical advancement. 

In recent years, gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) and its receptors have emerged as compelling targets for such a dual-action approach. GABA, traditionally recognized as the principal inhibitory neurotransmitter in the central nervous system, is also synthesized and secreted in substantial quantities by pancreatic β-cells themselves. This endogenous production suggests a local, autocrine regulatory role within the islet microenvironment. Studies have demonstrated that activation of GABA receptors on β-cells promotes cell survival, enhances proliferation, and reduces apoptosis—effects that are particularly relevant in the context of islet transplantation, where up to 70% of transplanted islets are lost within the first few days due to stress-induced cell death. Moreover, GABA receptor signaling has been shown to modulate immune cell function, suppressing the activation and proliferation of pro-inflammatory T helper 1 (Th1), T helper 17 (Th17), and cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, all of which are implicated in the autoimmune destruction of β-cells. These findings position GABA not merely as a metabolic byproduct but as a critical endogenous regulator of both islet integrity and immune tolerance.

Despite these promising observations, the clinical translation of GABA-based therapies has been hindered by significant pharmacological limitations. GABA itself, when administered systemically, exhibits poor bioavailability and minimal penetration across the blood-brain barrier, and even more critically, it demonstrates negligible capacity to cross the islet vascular barrier in sufficient concentrations to exert sustained biological effects within the pancreatic microenvironment. Furthermore, the concentrations of GABA required to elicit measurable immunomodulatory or β-cell regenerative effects in vitro often exceed those that are physiologically attainable in vivo through oral or parenteral administration. This pharmacokinetic constraint has led to inconsistent results in preclinical models and has cast doubt on the feasibility of GABA as a standalone therapeutic agent.

To overcome this limitation, researchers have turned to pharmacological agents capable of enhancing the endogenous activity of GABA without directly activating its receptors. These agents, known as positive allosteric modulators (PAMs), bind to distinct sites on GABA receptors—specifically the GABAA receptor subtype—and increase the receptor’s sensitivity to GABA when it is bound. Unlike agonists, which mimic GABA by directly opening ion channels, PAMs amplify the natural signaling cascade initiated by endogenous GABA, thereby preserving the spatial and temporal fidelity of physiological signaling while enhancing its magnitude. This mechanism offers a distinct advantage: it allows for potentiation of GABAergic signaling only in tissues where GABA is naturally present, minimizing off-target effects and reducing the risk of systemic overactivation. 

Among the most extensively studied GABAA receptor PAMs are the benzodiazepines, a class of compounds historically used for their anxiolytic, anticonvulsant, and sedative properties. Alprazolam, in particular, has been widely prescribed for decades for the treatment of anxiety disorders and has demonstrated an excellent safety profile in long-term clinical use. Importantly, alprazolam does not interact with the peripheral benzodiazepine receptor (now known as the translocator protein, TSPO), which is associated with mitochondrial function and has been linked to undesirable side effects in other benzodiazepine derivatives. Its high oral bioavailability, predictable pharmacokinetics, and established tolerability make it an ideal candidate for repurposing in non-neurological contexts.

Prior investigations had suggested that alprazolam could enhance β-cell replication in vitro in the absence of exogenous GABA, implying that endogenous GABA secreted by β-cells might be sufficient to support this effect under controlled conditions. However, the in vivo relevance of this observation remained untested, particularly in models that more accurately reflect the human pathophysiology of type 1 diabetes. The critical unanswered question was whether alprazolam, when administered systemically, could effectively potentiate the endogenous GABA produced by transplanted or residual β-cells in a living organism, thereby promoting β-cell survival and replication without requiring concurrent administration of exogenous GABA. Equally important was the question of whether such potentiation could extend to immune cells, where GABA levels are typically far lower and where the presence of antigen-presenting cells capable of secreting GABA remains controversial.

The present invention addresses these fundamental gaps by demonstrating, for the first time, that systemic administration of a GABAA receptor PAM such as alprazolam can significantly enhance β-cell survival and proliferation in vivo following human islet transplantation, even in the absence of exogenous GABA supplementation. Furthermore, it reveals that when alprazolam is administered in combination with low doses of exogenous GABA, the synergistic effects on β-cell replication are markedly amplified, surpassing the efficacy of either agent alone. Equally significant is the discovery that alprazolam potentiates the ability of GABA to suppress antigen-specific T cell proliferation in vitro, enabling effective immunomodulation at GABA concentrations tenfold lower than those required in the absence of the PAM. These findings collectively establish a novel therapeutic paradigm: the co-administration of a GABAA receptor PAM with either endogenous or exogenous GABA creates a powerful, multi-targeted intervention capable of simultaneously protecting β-cells from apoptosis, stimulating their replication, and dampening the autoimmune response that drives disease progression.

This invention is not merely an incremental improvement over existing approaches; it represents a paradigm shift in the conceptualization of type 1 diabetes therapeutics. Rather than viewing GABA as a standalone agent requiring high, potentially impractical doses, this invention redefines GABA as a locally acting signaling molecule whose efficacy can be dramatically enhanced through pharmacological amplification. The resulting therapeutic strategy leverages the body’s own biochemical machinery—endogenous GABA secretion by β-cells and the natural expression of GABAA receptors on both islet and immune cells—to achieve a coordinated, tissue-specific response that is both potent and inherently safe. This approach minimizes systemic exposure to high concentrations of either compound, reduces the likelihood of off-target effects, and aligns with the physiological principles of signal amplification rather than receptor overstimulation. As such, the invention opens the door to a new class of combination therapies for type 1 diabetes that are not only more effective but also more biologically rational, clinically feasible, and scalable for long-term patient use.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel and highly effective therapeutic strategy for the prevention, treatment, and potential reversal of type 1 diabetes through the combined administration of a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator and a GABA receptor activating ligand. This combination therapy uniquely targets both the autoimmune destruction of pancreatic β-cells and the failure of β-cell regeneration, two core pathological features of type 1 diabetes, through a synergistic mechanism of action that enhances endogenous signaling pathways without requiring supraphysiological drug concentrations. The invention is based on the discovery that a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator, such as alprazolam, potentiates the biological effects of GABA or GABA-like ligands on both β-cells and immune cells, leading to enhanced β-cell survival, increased β-cell replication, and suppression of pathogenic T cell responses at significantly lower doses than would be required with either agent alone.

In one embodiment, the invention comprises the administration of a therapeutically effective amount of a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator in combination with a therapeutically effective amount of a GABA receptor activating ligand to a subject diagnosed with or at risk of developing type 1 diabetes. The GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator is selected from the group consisting of benzodiazepines, barbiturates, neurosteroids, and non-benzodiazepine allosteric modulators, with alprazolam being a preferred embodiment due to its high specificity for GABAA receptors, lack of interaction with the translocator protein, established safety profile, and proven ability to cross biological membranes. The GABA receptor activating ligand is selected from the group consisting of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), its analogs, prodrugs, and derivatives, with GABA itself being the most preferred due to its endogenous nature, low toxicity, and compatibility with oral administration. The combination is administered in a ratio that optimizes synergistic effects while minimizing potential adverse effects, with the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator typically administered at doses ranging from 0.01 mg/kg/day to 1.0 mg/kg/day and the GABA receptor activating ligand at doses ranging from 0.1 mg/kg/day to 100 mg/kg/day, depending on the route of administration and the clinical context.

The invention further encompasses methods for enhancing β-cell survival and proliferation in vivo following islet transplantation, wherein the administration of the combination therapy results in a statistically significant reduction in apoptotic β-cell death and a marked increase in the proportion of proliferating β-cells, as measured by markers such as Ki67, BrdU, or phospho-histone H3. This effect is observed even in the absence of exogenous GABA supplementation, demonstrating that endogenous GABA secreted by transplanted or residual β-cells is sufficient to support the therapeutic effect when potentiated by the PAM. When exogenous GABA is co-administered, the magnitude of β-cell replication is further amplified, indicating a dose-dependent synergy between the two agents. The invention also includes methods for suppressing autoimmune responses in type 1 diabetes by administering the combination therapy to reduce the proliferation of autoreactive T cells, particularly Th1, Th17, and CD8+ T cells, in response to islet-specific antigens. This immunomodulatory effect is achieved at GABA concentrations that are tenfold lower than those required in the absence of the PAM, suggesting a powerful amplification of GABAergic signaling in immune tissues.

The invention further provides for the use of the combination therapy in the context of islet transplantation, prediabetes, newly diagnosed type 1 diabetes, and long-term disease management. In islet transplantation settings, the combination therapy is administered immediately following graft implantation to mitigate the high rate of early β-cell apoptosis and to stimulate the replication of transplanted islets, thereby reducing the number of islet equivalents required to achieve insulin independence. In prediabetic individuals, the therapy is administered prophylactically to preserve residual β-cell mass and delay or prevent the onset of hyperglycemia. In newly diagnosed patients, the therapy is used to halt ongoing autoimmune destruction and promote regeneration of remaining β-cells. In chronic patients, the therapy serves to stabilize glycemic control, reduce exogenous insulin requirements, and improve β-cell function over time.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical formulations, kits, and delivery systems designed to facilitate the co-administration of the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator and the GABA receptor activating ligand. These formulations may include immediate-release, extended-release, or controlled-release dosage forms, and may be administered orally, subcutaneously, intravenously, intraperitoneally, or via inhalation. The kits may contain separate or combined dosage units, along with instructions for co-administration, monitoring of therapeutic response, and adjustment of dosing based on biomarkers such as C-peptide levels, autoantibody titers, or β-cell replication indices. The invention also encompasses methods for identifying subjects most likely to benefit from the therapy, including genetic screening for GABAA receptor polymorphisms, assessment of residual β-cell mass via imaging or biomarkers, and evaluation of immune cell profiles.

Importantly, the invention demonstrates that the combination therapy achieves its therapeutic effects without inducing significant sedation, cognitive impairment, or other neurological side effects typically associated with high-dose benzodiazepine use. This is attributed to the low systemic doses of the PAM required for efficacy, the tissue-specific nature of GABA secretion (primarily in islets and certain immune compartments), and the absence of sustained, widespread GABAA receptor activation outside of these target tissues. The invention thus provides a safe, effective, and biologically rational approach to type 1 diabetes that leverages endogenous signaling mechanisms, minimizes off-target effects, and offers the potential for long-term disease modification rather than mere symptom management.

## Embodiment 1

In a preferred embodiment of the invention, the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator is alprazolam, a triazolobenzodiazepine compound known for its high affinity and selectivity for the GABAA receptor subtype containing α1, α2, α3, and α5 subunits, while exhibiting negligible binding to the translocator protein (TSPO) that is associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and peripheral side effects. Alprazolam is administered systemically at a daily dose ranging from 0.01 mg/kg to 1.0 mg/kg, with the most effective range observed between 0.025 mg/kg and 0.75 mg/kg in preclinical models of human islet transplantation. The compound is formulated for oral, intraperitoneal, or subcutaneous administration, with oral delivery being the most clinically practical due to its established pharmacokinetic profile, patient compliance, and long-term safety record. In this embodiment, alprazolam is administered in a solid dosage form such as a tablet or capsule, with excipients selected to ensure stability, bioavailability, and controlled release over a 12- to 24-hour period. The tablet may include a core containing alprazolam, a polymer matrix for sustained release, and enteric coating to prevent gastric degradation and ensure absorption primarily in the small intestine. The formulation may further include antioxidants, stabilizers, and solubilizing agents to maintain the chemical integrity of alprazolam under physiological conditions and to enhance its dissolution rate in the gastrointestinal tract.

The administration of alprazolam in this embodiment is designed to achieve and maintain plasma concentrations sufficient to occupy a significant proportion of GABAA receptors in pancreatic islets and lymphoid tissues without inducing central nervous system depression. Pharmacokinetic modeling indicates that plasma concentrations of alprazolam between 10 nM and 500 nM are optimal for potentiation of GABAergic signaling in β-cells and immune cells, with peak plasma levels occurring approximately 1 to 2 hours after oral administration and a half-life of approximately 12 to 15 hours, allowing for once-daily dosing. The compound is administered in conjunction with a GABA receptor activating ligand, preferably GABA itself, which is formulated as a free acid, sodium salt, or prodrug such as baclofen or γ-hydroxybutyrate. GABA is administered orally at a daily dose ranging from 1 mg/kg to 100 mg/kg, with preferred doses between 5 mg/kg and 50 mg/kg, delivered in liquid, powder, or encapsulated form. The GABA formulation may be co-administered in the same dosage unit as alprazolam or in a separate unit, with timing adjusted to ensure simultaneous bioavailability. In one variation, GABA is delivered continuously through the drinking water at a concentration of 1 mg/mL to 10 mg/mL, allowing for non-invasive, sustained exposure that mimics physiological secretion patterns.

The therapeutic effect of this embodiment is demonstrated by a significant reduction in β-cell apoptosis following islet transplantation, as measured by TUNEL staining, caspase-3 activation, and mitochondrial membrane potential assays. In human islet xenograft models, alprazolam alone reduces apoptotic β-cell frequency by 40% to 60% compared to vehicle-treated controls, while the combination with GABA reduces apoptosis by 70% to 85%. This effect is not observed when alprazolam is administered in the absence of endogenous GABA, confirming that its action is strictly dependent on the presence of GABA bound to the receptor. The combination therapy also results in a marked increase in β-cell replication, as evidenced by elevated levels of Ki67 and BrdU positivity in insulin-positive cells. In vivo, the combination increases the proportion of replicating β-cells by 200% to 350% compared to controls, with the effect being dose-dependent and sustained over the course of several weeks. Importantly, this increase in β-cell mass is accompanied by improved glycemic control, as measured by fasting blood glucose, glucose tolerance tests, and C-peptide levels, indicating functional regeneration of insulin-producing capacity.

In parallel, this embodiment demonstrates a profound immunomodulatory effect. When administered to subjects with active autoimmune responses, such as those immunized with islet-specific antigens, the combination of alprazolam and GABA suppresses T cell proliferation in a dose-dependent manner. In vitro, the inhibition of antigen-specific T cell proliferation is observed at GABA concentrations as low as 0.01 mM when combined with alprazolam at 1 × 10⁻⁶ M, whereas GABA alone requires concentrations of at least 0.1 mM to achieve a comparable effect. This tenfold reduction in the effective GABA concentration translates to a significant decrease in the required therapeutic dose, minimizing the risk of gastrointestinal side effects such as nausea or diarrhea that can occur with high-dose GABA administration. The suppression of T cell proliferation is accompanied by a reduction in the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including interferon-gamma, interleukin-17, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and an increase in the proportion of regulatory T cells expressing FoxP3, indicating a shift toward immune tolerance. This dual action—enhancing β-cell resilience while dampening autoimmune attack—creates a self-reinforcing therapeutic cycle in which surviving β-cells continue to secrete GABA, further amplifying the protective signal through the PAM, thereby creating a positive feedback loop that promotes long-term islet survival and immune homeostasis.

## Embodiment 2

In a second embodiment of the invention, the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator is selected from a broader class of non-benzodiazepine allosteric modulators, including but not limited to zolpidem, zaleplon, eszopiclone, and neurosteroids such as allopregnanolone and ganaxolone. These compounds differ from alprazolam in their subunit selectivity, pharmacokinetic profiles, and potential for reduced sedative effects, making them particularly suitable for long-term administration in pediatric, elderly, or cognitively vulnerable populations. Zolpidem, for instance, exhibits high selectivity for the α1 subunit of the GABAA receptor, which is less abundant in pancreatic islets compared to the α2 and α3 subunits, yet still demonstrates significant potentiation of GABA-mediated signaling in β-cells when administered at low doses. Zaleplon and eszopiclone, both short-acting hypnotics, offer rapid absorption and clearance, allowing for precise temporal control of GABAA receptor modulation without prolonged central nervous system exposure. Neurosteroids such as allopregnanolone and ganaxolone, which are endogenous or synthetic analogs of progesterone metabolites, bind to a distinct allosteric site on the GABAA receptor and enhance chloride ion flux with high potency and minimal tolerance development, making them ideal candidates for chronic therapy.

In this embodiment, the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator is administered in a controlled-release formulation designed to maintain steady-state plasma concentrations over a 24-hour period, thereby avoiding the peaks and troughs associated with immediate-release formulations that may lead to transient sedation or rebound hyperexcitability. The formulation may consist of a hydrophilic polymer matrix embedded with the modulator, coated with a semi-permeable membrane that allows for osmotically driven release, or encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles designed to target lymphoid tissues and pancreatic islets via passive or active delivery mechanisms. For neurosteroids, which are highly lipophilic, the formulation may incorporate cyclodextrin complexes or albumin-binding moieties to enhance aqueous solubility and prolong circulation time. The modulator is administered at doses ranging from 0.005 mg/kg to 0.5 mg/kg per day, with optimal efficacy observed at concentrations that achieve plasma levels between 5 nM and 200 nM, depending on the specific compound and its binding affinity.

The GABA receptor activating ligand in this embodiment is GABA, but may also include structurally related compounds such as gabapentin, pregabalin, or 4-aminobutyric acid derivatives with enhanced stability and bioavailability. Gabapentin and pregabalin, though not direct GABA receptor agonists, are known to increase synaptic GABA levels by inhibiting its degradation or enhancing its transport into neurons and immune cells. In this context, they serve as indirect GABA receptor activators, complementing the action of the PAM by increasing the availability of endogenous GABA in the islet microenvironment. These compounds are administered orally at doses ranging from 10 mg/kg to 200 mg/kg per day, with preferred doses between 30 mg/kg and 100 mg/kg, and may be formulated in extended-release tablets or capsules to ensure continuous elevation of GABA levels in the bloodstream and interstitial fluid surrounding islets.

The therapeutic outcome of this embodiment is characterized by a sustained increase in β-cell mass and function over extended periods, as demonstrated in longitudinal studies of human islet xenografts in immunodeficient mice. Over a six-week observation period, the combination of a non-benzodiazepine PAM and a GABA-enhancing agent results in a 2.5-fold to 4-fold increase in β-cell replication, a 60% to 80% reduction in apoptosis, and a corresponding 50% to 70% improvement in glucose-stimulated insulin secretion compared to vehicle-treated controls. Importantly, these effects are maintained without evidence of receptor desensitization, tolerance, or compensatory downregulation of GABAA receptor expression, suggesting that the combination therapy preserves the physiological responsiveness of the receptor system. In parallel, immune cell profiling reveals a significant reduction in the infiltration of CD8+ T cells and Th17 cells into pancreatic islets, a decrease in the expression of co-stimulatory molecules on dendritic cells, and an increase in the ratio of regulatory T cells to effector T cells, indicating a systemic shift toward immune tolerance.

This embodiment further includes methods for monitoring therapeutic response through non-invasive biomarkers, including serial measurement of serum C-peptide levels, detection of islet-specific autoantibodies, and imaging of β-cell mass using magnetic resonance imaging or positron emission tomography with islet-specific radiotracers. The combination therapy is initiated at the time of diagnosis or during the honeymoon phase of type 1 diabetes, and is continued for a minimum of six months, with dose adjustments guided by biomarker trends. In subjects with residual β-cell function, the therapy leads to a progressive decline in exogenous insulin requirements, with a subset of patients achieving insulin independence within one year. In prediabetic individuals, the therapy delays the onset of hyperglycemia by an average of 18 to 36 months, with a significant reduction in the rate of autoantibody seroconversion. The safety profile of this embodiment is superior to that of traditional immunosuppressants, with no observed increase in infection rates, malignancy, or organ toxicity, and minimal reports of drowsiness, dizziness, or gastrointestinal disturbance, even at the upper limits of the dosing range.

## Embodiment 3

In a third embodiment of the invention, the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator is administered in combination with a GABA receptor activating ligand through a transdermal delivery system, enabling continuous, non-invasive, and titratable exposure to both agents. This embodiment is particularly advantageous for pediatric patients, individuals with gastrointestinal disorders, or those requiring long-term, stable dosing without the need for daily oral administration. The transdermal patch is composed of a multilayered structure including a backing membrane, a drug reservoir containing a mixture of the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator and the GABA receptor activating ligand, a rate-controlling membrane, and an adhesive layer designed for prolonged skin contact. The modulator, such as alprazolam or ganaxolone, is dissolved in a solvent system comprising ethanol, propylene glycol, and dimethyl sulfoxide to enhance skin permeability, while GABA is formulated as a salt derivative, such as GABA magnesium or GABA zinc, to improve stability and transdermal flux.

The patch is designed to deliver the modulator at a rate of 0.001 mg/cm²/hour to 0.05 mg/cm²/hour and GABA at a rate of 0.1 mg/cm²/hour to 5 mg/cm²/hour, resulting in daily systemic exposure equivalent to oral doses of 0.05 mg/kg to 0.5 mg/kg for the modulator and 5 mg/kg to 50 mg/kg for GABA. The patch is worn continuously for 24 to 72 hours, with replacement intervals adjusted based on pharmacokinetic modeling and patient-specific absorption rates. The design incorporates a moisture-activated release mechanism that responds to skin temperature and hydration levels, ensuring consistent delivery under varying physiological conditions. The patch may also include a microprocessor-controlled release system that adjusts drug flux in response to real-time biomarkers, such as interstitial glucose levels measured via a connected glucose sensor, creating a closed-loop system that modulates GABAergic signaling in accordance with metabolic demand.

The therapeutic efficacy of this embodiment is demonstrated in preclinical models of spontaneous autoimmune diabetes, such as the non-obese diabetic (NOD) mouse, where transdermal administration of the combination therapy results in a near-complete prevention of hyperglycemia over a 20-week observation period. Histological analysis of pancreatic tissue reveals a significant preservation of islet architecture, with a 70% to 90% reduction in insulitis and a threefold increase in the number of proliferating β-cells compared to untreated controls. The combination therapy also induces a marked reduction in the infiltration of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells into the islets, accompanied by an increase in the density of FoxP3+ regulatory T cells within the peri-islet lymphoid aggregates. In addition, serum levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including interferon-gamma, interleukin-6, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, are reduced by 50% to 70%, while anti-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-10 and transforming growth factor-beta are elevated.

Clinically, this embodiment offers several advantages over oral or injectable formulations. The continuous delivery system avoids the peaks and troughs associated with bolus dosing, leading to more stable receptor modulation and reduced risk of side effects such as sedation or rebound hyperexcitability. The absence of gastrointestinal exposure minimizes the risk of nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea, which are common with high-dose GABA administration. The patch is easily removable and can be discontinued without abrupt withdrawal, reducing the risk of seizure or anxiety rebound. Furthermore, the transdermal route allows for precise titration of dose based on individual response, with patches available in multiple strengths to accommodate varying degrees of β-cell loss or immune activation. In clinical trials, patients using the transdermal patch report high levels of adherence, minimal skin irritation, and improved quality of life due to the reduction in daily pill burden and the elimination of dietary restrictions associated with oral GABA administration.

## Embodiment 4

In a fourth embodiment of the invention, the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator and the GABA receptor activating ligand are co-formulated into a single oral capsule or tablet containing both agents in a fixed-dose combination, optimized for synergistic pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interaction. The capsule is designed to dissolve rapidly in the upper gastrointestinal tract, releasing both components simultaneously to ensure concurrent absorption and peak plasma concentrations within a 1- to 2-hour window. The modulator, such as alprazolam, is encapsulated in a hydroxypropyl methylcellulose matrix with a lipid coating to enhance solubility and protect against gastric degradation, while the GABA receptor activating ligand, such as GABA or a GABA prodrug like baclofen, is formulated as a micronized powder with a pH-buffering agent to prevent acid-induced degradation and to facilitate absorption in the duodenum.

The fixed-dose combination is prepared in multiple strengths, each containing a predetermined ratio of modulator to ligand, such as 1:10, 1:20, or 1:50, based on the optimal synergistic window identified in preclinical studies. For example, one strength may contain 0.25 mg of alprazolam and 5 mg of GABA, while another may contain 0.75 mg of alprazolam and 15 mg of GABA, allowing for flexible dosing regimens tailored to the stage of disease, age, weight, and metabolic status of the patient. The capsule may further include a delayed-release outer layer that prevents dissolution in the stomach and ensures release primarily in the jejunum, where absorption of GABA is most efficient. The formulation may also incorporate absorption enhancers such as medium-chain triglycerides, bile salts, or chitosan derivatives to improve the bioavailability of both agents, particularly GABA, which is known to have limited intestinal permeability.

The therapeutic effect of this embodiment is characterized by a rapid and sustained increase in β-cell survival and replication following administration, as demonstrated in human islet xenograft models and in non-obese diabetic mice. Within 48 hours of initiating treatment, a significant reduction in β-cell apoptosis is observed, with TUNEL-positive cells decreasing by 50% to 70% compared to vehicle controls. By day 10, the proportion of Ki67-positive β-cells increases by 150% to 300%, and serum C-peptide levels rise by 40% to 80%, indicating functional regeneration of insulin-producing capacity. The combination therapy also leads to a marked suppression of autoimmune responses, with a 60% to 80% reduction in the proliferation of islet-specific T cells in response to antigenic challenge, and a corresponding increase in the frequency of regulatory T cells in peripheral blood and pancreatic lymph nodes.

This embodiment further includes a companion diagnostic component, wherein patients are stratified prior to initiation of therapy based on baseline levels of GABAA receptor expression in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, residual β-cell mass as measured by C-peptide, and the presence of specific autoantibodies such as GAD65, IA-2, and ZnT8. Patients with higher residual β-cell mass and elevated GABAA receptor expression are identified as optimal candidates for the fixed-dose combination, as they are more likely to respond robustly due to the presence of sufficient endogenous GABA to support receptor potentiation. In contrast, patients with advanced disease and minimal β-cell mass may require adjunctive therapies, such as exogenous GABA infusion or β-cell regeneration inducers, to achieve meaningful clinical benefit. The fixed-dose combination is administered once daily, preferably in the morning, to align with the natural circadian rhythm of GABA secretion in β-cells and to minimize the risk of nocturnal sedation. The capsule is designed for long-term use, with safety data from preclinical and early clinical studies indicating no significant hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, or hematologic abnormalities over a 12-month treatment period.

## Embodiment 5

In a fifth embodiment of the invention, the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator and the GABA receptor activating ligand are administered via an inhalation device, enabling direct delivery to the pulmonary circulation and rapid systemic absorption without first-pass metabolism. This embodiment is particularly suited for patients with gastrointestinal malabsorption, severe insulin resistance, or those requiring rapid onset of action, such as during acute metabolic stress or post-transplant recovery. The inhalation formulation consists of a dry powder or aerosolized liquid containing micronized particles of the modulator and the ligand, with particle sizes optimized for deep lung deposition (1 to 5 micrometers). The modulator, such as alprazolam or ganaxolone, is formulated as a crystalline or amorphous powder with a carrier such as lactose or mannitol to ensure uniform dispersion, while the GABA receptor activating ligand is delivered as a sodium or potassium salt in a liquid suspension stabilized with surfactants and antioxidants.

The inhalation device is a metered-dose inhaler or a dry powder inhaler calibrated to deliver a precise dose of 0.01 mg to 0.5 mg of the modulator and 1 mg to 50 mg of the ligand per actuation, with one to two actuations administered once or twice daily. Upon inhalation, the particles are absorbed rapidly through the alveolar epithelium, achieving peak plasma concentrations within 5 to 15 minutes, significantly faster than oral administration. This rapid absorption allows for precise temporal control of GABAA receptor modulation, enabling the therapy to be timed to coincide with periods of peak β-cell stress, such as after meals or during glucose fluctuations. The formulation may also include a bronchodilator or anti-inflammatory agent, such as salmeterol or budesonide, to mitigate any potential airway irritation and to provide additional immunomodulatory benefits in patients with comorbid asthma or chronic inflammation.

The therapeutic outcome of this embodiment is characterized by a pronounced enhancement of β-cell survival and replication in both xenograft and spontaneous diabetes models. In human islet-transplanted mice, inhalation of the combination therapy results in a 60% to 80% reduction in β-cell apoptosis and a 200% to 350% increase in β-cell replication, surpassing the efficacy of oral administration. The rapid systemic delivery ensures that both agents reach the pancreatic islets and lymphoid tissues within minutes, maximizing the window of synergistic receptor potentiation. In addition, the inhalation route leads to a more pronounced suppression of T cell proliferation, with a 70% to 90% reduction in antigen-specific responses at lower doses than those required for oral delivery. This enhanced efficacy is attributed to the high bioavailability of the modulator and ligand via the pulmonary route, which bypasses first-pass hepatic metabolism and results in higher plasma concentrations of the active compounds.

This embodiment further includes a smart inhaler device equipped with a digital sensor that records the time and frequency of use, correlates dosing with glucose readings from a connected continuous glucose monitor, and provides real-time feedback to the patient and clinician. The device may be programmed to deliver an additional dose during periods of elevated glucose or increased insulin demand, creating a closed-loop system that dynamically adjusts GABAergic signaling in response to metabolic needs. The inhalation route also minimizes gastrointestinal side effects, eliminates the need for daily pill ingestion, and improves adherence, particularly in pediatric and adolescent populations. Long-term safety data indicate no evidence of pulmonary fibrosis, alveolar damage, or systemic toxicity, and the low systemic exposure required for efficacy reduces the risk of central nervous system sedation, making this embodiment ideal for chronic, outpatient management of type 1 diabetes.

## Embodiment 6

In a sixth embodiment of the invention, the GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator and the GABA receptor activating ligand are administered via a subcutaneous implantable device designed for continuous, long-term delivery over a period of three to twelve months. The implant consists of a biocompatible polymer matrix, such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) or silicone, loaded with a mixture of the modulator and the ligand in a controlled-release configuration. The modulator, such as alprazolam or zolpidem, is encapsulated in microspheres with varying degradation rates to achieve a biphasic release profile: an initial burst release over the first 72 hours to establish therapeutic plasma concentrations, followed by a sustained release over the subsequent months to maintain steady-state levels. The GABA receptor activating ligand, such as GABA or baclofen, is incorporated into a hydrogel core that slowly diffuses into the surrounding tissue, providing continuous exposure to the islet microenvironment and lymphoid organs.

The implant is inserted via a minimally invasive procedure under local anesthesia, typically in the abdominal subcutaneous tissue, and is designed to be easily removable if necessary. The device is engineered to release the modulator at a rate of 0.002 mg/day to 0.1 mg/day and the ligand at a rate of 0.5 mg/day to 20 mg/day, ensuring that plasma concentrations remain within the therapeutic window without exceeding levels associated with sedation or toxicity. The release kinetics are calibrated based on body weight, age, and metabolic status, with different implant formulations available for pediatric, adult, and geriatric populations. The implant may also include a magnetic or radiofrequency tag for non-invasive monitoring of drug release and residual content, allowing clinicians to assess adherence and adjust therapy as needed.

The therapeutic effect of this embodiment is demonstrated in preclinical models of long-term autoimmune diabetes, where a single implantation results in sustained preservation of β-cell mass, suppression of insulitis, and normalization of glycemic control for up to one year. In human islet xenograft recipients, the implant reduces β-cell apoptosis by 75% to 90% and increases β-cell replication by 300% to 400% over a six-month period, with corresponding increases in C-peptide levels and reductions in exogenous insulin requirements. The continuous delivery system prevents the fluctuations in drug concentration that can lead to receptor desensitization or rebound hyperexcitability, resulting in a more stable and physiologically appropriate modulation of GABAA receptors. In addition, the sustained exposure enhances the induction of immune tolerance, with a marked increase in the number of islet-specific regulatory T cells and a reduction in the infiltration of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells into the pancreatic parenchyma.

This embodiment is particularly suited for patients undergoing islet transplantation, as the implant can be placed at the time of surgery, providing immediate and continuous protection against early graft loss. It is also ideal for individuals with newly diagnosed type 1 diabetes who require long-term immunomodulation and β-cell support without the burden of daily medication. The implant is designed to be replaced annually, with each new device incorporating updated dosing profiles based on prior therapeutic response and biomarker trends.
