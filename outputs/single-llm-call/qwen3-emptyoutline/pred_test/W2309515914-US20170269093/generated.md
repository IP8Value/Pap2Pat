## BACKGROUND

The metastatic spread of breast cancer remains one of the most formidable challenges in oncology, accounting for the overwhelming majority of cancer-related deaths among women globally. While primary tumors are often treatable through surgical resection, radiation, and targeted therapies, the ability of malignant cells to disseminate from the primary site, invade surrounding tissues, intravasate into the vasculature, survive in circulation, extravasate into distant organs, and establish secondary colonies is a complex, multi-step process that defies current therapeutic interventions. The molecular mechanisms governing this transition from localized growth to systemic dissemination have been the focus of intense investigation for decades, yet critical gaps persist in our understanding of how cancer cells dynamically coordinate cytoskeletal remodeling to navigate the dense, three-dimensional extracellular matrix of the tumor stroma.

Cell migration in three-dimensional environments, such as those encountered during tumor invasion, is fundamentally distinct from migration on two-dimensional substrates. In vivo, cancer cells do not glide across flat surfaces but must deform their shape, generate protrusive forces, degrade extracellular barriers, and respond to chemotactic gradients—all simultaneously and with high spatial precision. These behaviors are orchestrated by the actin cytoskeleton, which serves as the primary mechanical engine of cellular motility. Actin polymerization drives membrane protrusion through two major, interdependent pathways: the Arp2/3-mediated nucleation of branched actin networks and the Ena/VASP-mediated elongation of unbranched filaments. The former generates the dense, dendritic meshwork that forms the structural core of lamellipodia, while the latter promotes the extension and stabilization of individual filaments at the leading edge, enabling persistent directional movement.

Regulatory proteins that integrate these two pathways have emerged as critical nodes in the control of invasive migration. Among these, Lamellipodin (Lpd) has been identified as a multifunctional scaffold protein that physically links key actin regulators to the plasma membrane. Prior studies have established that Lpd localizes to the leading edge of migrating cells and binds directly to both the Scar/WAVE complex, which activates the Arp2/3 complex, and to Ena/VASP family proteins, which promote filament elongation. These interactions suggest that Lpd may serve as a central coordinator of actin dynamics during protrusion. However, the precise functional requirements for these interactions in the context of cancer progression, particularly in three-dimensional invasion and metastasis, remained unresolved. Moreover, while Lpd was known to be phosphorylated by the Abl kinase family upon growth factor stimulation—a modification that enhances its binding to Ena/VASP proteins—the broader regulatory network governing Lpd’s interactions, especially in the context of oncogenic signaling, had not been systematically explored.

The Src family of non-receptor tyrosine kinases, which are frequently hyperactivated in breast cancer and associated with aggressive phenotypes, had been implicated in promoting cell motility and invasion, but their direct substrates and downstream effectors in the context of actin remodeling were incompletely defined. While Abl kinases had been shown to regulate Lpd-Ena/VASP binding, it was unknown whether Src kinases also modulated Lpd function, and if so, whether they acted redundantly, synergistically, or antagonistically with Abl. Furthermore, the possibility that Lpd might differentially regulate its binding partners in response to distinct kinase inputs—thereby enabling context-specific control of actin architecture—had not been investigated.

Clinically, the relevance of Lpd expression in human breast cancer had been partially explored through mRNA expression databases, which suggested a correlation between elevated Lpd transcript levels and poor prognosis. However, these analyses lacked validation at the protein level in large, well-annotated patient cohorts, and no mechanistic link had been established between Lpd expression, tumor cell behavior in vivo, and patient survival outcomes. The absence of comprehensive immunohistochemical data correlating Lpd protein localization (cytoplasmic versus membrane) with clinical endpoints left open the critical question of whether Lpd’s function in metastasis was simply a consequence of its overexpression or whether its spatial regulation and post-translational modification were essential determinants of its pro-invasive activity.

The invention disclosed herein addresses these critical gaps by revealing that Lpd is not merely a passive scaffold but an actively regulated molecular switch whose phosphorylation by both Src and Abl kinases governs its ability to coordinate actin nucleation and elongation during invasive migration. This invention demonstrates that Lpd’s interaction with the Scar/WAVE complex is uniquely dependent on Src-mediated tyrosine phosphorylation, while its interaction with Ena/VASP proteins is regulated by Abl phosphorylation, and that both pathways are required for efficient 3D invasion and metastasis. It further establishes that moderate, but not excessive, cytoplasmic Lpd expression correlates with increased risk of cancer-associated mortality, whereas high plasma membrane localization of Lpd is a robust predictor of reduced disease-free and metastasis-free survival. Critically, this invention provides the first evidence that Lpd functions as a central node in a pro-metastatic signaling axis composed of Src and Abl kinases, Lpd, Ena/VASP proteins, and the Scar/WAVE complex, and that disruption of any component of this axis impairs tumor cell invasion and metastatic dissemination in vivo. These findings fundamentally redefine the understanding of how cancer cells translate extracellular chemotactic cues into coordinated cytoskeletal responses and identify Lpd and its regulatory phosphorylation sites as novel therapeutic targets for preventing metastatic progression in breast cancer.

## SUMMARY

The invention relates to a novel biological pathway and molecular mechanism by which Lamellipodin (Lpd) functions as a critical regulator of breast cancer metastasis through the coordinated, kinase-dependent regulation of actin cytoskeletal dynamics. Specifically, the invention provides a comprehensive understanding of how Lpd integrates signals from the Src and Abl tyrosine kinases to differentially modulate its interactions with the Scar/WAVE complex and Ena/VASP proteins, thereby enabling the precise spatiotemporal control of actin nucleation and filament elongation necessary for invasive migration and metastatic dissemination. The invention demonstrates that Lpd is phosphorylated at specific tyrosine residues by Src kinases, and that this phosphorylation is essential for the stable association of Lpd with the Scar/WAVE complex, which in turn activates the Arp2/3 complex to drive branched actin network formation. Concurrently, Lpd is phosphorylated by Abl kinases at overlapping and distinct tyrosine residues, which enhances its binding to Ena/VASP proteins to promote unbranched filament elongation. The invention further establishes that both of these phosphorylation events are required for efficient 3D chemotactic invasion toward growth factors such as epidermal growth factor (EGF), and that loss of either Src- or Abl-mediated Lpd phosphorylation abolishes the ability of breast cancer cells to invade through extracellular matrix and metastasize in vivo.

The invention provides a method for identifying patients at high risk of metastatic progression based on the expression level and subcellular localization of Lpd protein in primary breast tumor tissue. Specifically, the invention reveals that moderate cytoplasmic expression of Lpd (as measured by histoscore 2) is significantly associated with increased risk of cancer-associated mortality, whereas high plasma membrane localization of Lpd (histoscore 3) is strongly predictive of reduced disease-free and metastasis-free survival. This differential correlation between subcellular localization and clinical outcome provides a novel diagnostic biomarker for stratifying breast cancer patients according to their metastatic potential.

The invention further provides a method for inhibiting breast cancer metastasis by targeting the Lpd-mediated signaling axis. This includes the use of small molecule inhibitors of Src and Abl kinases, such as Dasatinib and STI571, to disrupt Lpd phosphorylation and thereby impair its ability to recruit both Scar/WAVE and Ena/VASP complexes. The invention also encompasses the use of genetically engineered Lpd mutants that lack phosphorylation sites for Src and/or Abl kinases, which act as dominant-negative inhibitors of endogenous Lpd function. These mutants, when expressed in metastatic breast cancer cells, abolish their ability to form invasive protrusions, degrade extracellular matrix via invadopodia, intravasate into blood vessels, and establish metastatic colonies in distant organs.

The invention further provides a method for diagnosing and monitoring metastatic breast cancer by detecting the phosphorylation status of Lpd in tumor tissue or circulating tumor cells. Specifically, the invention discloses antibodies and assays capable of detecting phospho-tyrosine residues on Lpd at positions 366, 426, 456, 466, 481, 510, 513, and 1226, which are critical for its interaction with Scar/WAVE and Ena/VASP. The presence of phosphorylated Lpd in tumor biopsies or liquid biopsies serves as a functional biomarker of active metastatic signaling and predicts resistance to conventional therapies.

The invention further provides a method for therapeutic intervention by targeting the Lpd-Scar/WAVE or Lpd-Ena/VASP protein-protein interactions directly, using peptides, monoclonal antibodies, or small molecules designed to disrupt these interfaces. The invention demonstrates that disruption of either interaction alone is insufficient to block metastasis, but simultaneous inhibition of both interactions completely abrogates invasive migration and metastatic dissemination in vivo, establishing the Lpd signaling axis as a viable dual-target therapeutic strategy.

The invention further provides a method for screening compounds for anti-metastatic activity by measuring their ability to inhibit Lpd phosphorylation, disrupt Lpd-Scar/WAVE or Lpd-Ena/VASP binding, reduce actin barbed-end formation, impair lamellipodial persistence, or suppress invadopodium-mediated matrix degradation in breast cancer cells cultured in three-dimensional matrices. The invention further provides a method for evaluating the efficacy of anti-metastatic therapies in preclinical models by quantifying the number of motile tumor cells, the frequency of protrusion formation, the directionality of migration, and the extent of lung metastasis in orthotopic mouse models following treatment with inhibitors of Src, Abl, or Lpd function.

The invention further provides a method for gene therapy or RNA interference to suppress Lpd expression in breast cancer cells, thereby reducing their invasive and metastatic potential. The invention demonstrates that stable knockdown of Lpd in highly metastatic breast cancer cell lines significantly reduces tumor invasion into surrounding stroma, decreases intravasation into blood vessels, and markedly suppresses the formation of lung metastases in immunodeficient mice, without affecting primary tumor growth, thereby uncoupling tumor proliferation from metastatic dissemination.

The invention further provides a method for predicting patient response to Src or Abl kinase inhibitors by assessing the expression level and phosphorylation status of Lpd in tumor tissue. Patients with high Lpd expression and phosphorylation are predicted to respond favorably to such inhibitors, while those with low or absent Lpd expression are predicted to be resistant, enabling precision medicine approaches to metastatic breast cancer treatment.

The invention thus provides a comprehensive, mechanistically grounded framework for understanding, diagnosing, and therapeutically targeting the Lpd-mediated signaling axis in metastatic breast cancer, offering novel biomarkers, therapeutic targets, and diagnostic tools that are not disclosed in the prior art.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention provides a novel and non-obvious understanding of the molecular mechanisms by which Lamellipodin (Lpd) functions as a central regulator of breast cancer metastasis through the integration of tyrosine kinase signaling with actin cytoskeletal dynamics. Prior to this invention, Lpd was recognized as a scaffold protein that binds both the Scar/WAVE complex and Ena/VASP proteins, suggesting a potential role in coordinating actin nucleation and elongation. However, the functional significance of these interactions in the context of cancer progression, particularly in three-dimensional invasion and metastasis, remained speculative. Furthermore, while Abl kinase-mediated phosphorylation of Lpd had been shown to enhance its binding to Ena/VASP proteins, the contribution of other oncogenic kinases, particularly Src, to Lpd regulation was entirely unknown. The invention discloses for the first time that Src kinases directly phosphorylate Lpd at specific tyrosine residues, and that this phosphorylation is essential for the stable recruitment of the Scar/WAVE complex to the leading edge of migrating cancer cells. Crucially, the invention demonstrates that this Src-dependent interaction is distinct from the Abl-dependent enhancement of Lpd-Ena/VASP binding, and that both pathways are required for efficient invasive migration and metastatic dissemination.

The invention further establishes that Lpd does not merely act as a passive linker between actin regulators but functions as a phosphorylation-dependent molecular switch whose activity is precisely tuned by the combinatorial input of Src and Abl kinases. This discovery fundamentally alters the understanding of how cancer cells translate extracellular chemotactic signals into cytoskeletal responses. In non-metastatic cells, Lpd may be present at low levels and unphosphorylated, resulting in minimal actin remodeling and limited motility. In contrast, in metastatic breast cancer cells, elevated Lpd expression, coupled with hyperactivation of Src and Abl kinases downstream of growth factor receptors such as EGFR, results in robust tyrosine phosphorylation of Lpd at multiple sites. This phosphorylation enhances Lpd’s affinity for both the Scar/WAVE complex and Ena/VASP proteins, enabling the simultaneous activation of branched actin nucleation and filament elongation. The resulting synergistic effect generates persistent, directional lamellipodia capable of navigating dense extracellular matrices, promoting tumor cell invasion into surrounding stroma, and facilitating intravasation into blood vessels.

The invention further provides the first evidence that the subcellular localization of Lpd is a critical determinant of its pro-metastatic function. While cytoplasmic accumulation of Lpd at moderate levels correlates with increased cancer-associated mortality, high plasma membrane localization is a strong predictor of reduced disease-free and metastasis-free survival. This indicates that the spatial targeting of Lpd to the leading edge of migrating cells—not merely its total abundance—is essential for its role in metastasis. The invention further demonstrates that Lpd is recruited to the plasma membrane within seconds of EGF stimulation, and that this recruitment is dependent on its phosphorylation state. Mutants of Lpd lacking Src- or Abl-phosphorylation sites are unable to localize efficiently to the cell periphery, even when overexpressed, and fail to rescue the invasive defects of Lpd-depleted cells. This establishes that phosphorylation is not only required for binding to downstream effectors but also for the proper subcellular targeting of Lpd itself.

The invention further reveals that Lpd plays a dual role in both lamellipodial protrusion and invadopodium formation. In addition to promoting membrane extension through actin polymerization, Lpd localizes to invadopodia—specialized actin-rich structures that secrete matrix metalloproteinases to degrade the basement membrane and interstitial stroma. The invention shows that Lpd co-localizes with the invadopodial marker cortactin and that its depletion reduces the number of both precursor and mature invadopodia. Furthermore, Lpd depletion impairs the ability of cancer cells to degrade gelatin matrices, indicating that Lpd is required not only for protrusion but also for the execution of matrix degradation. This dual function positions Lpd as a master regulator of the invasive phenotype, coordinating both the mechanical force generation and enzymatic breakdown necessary for tissue penetration.

The invention further provides a method for identifying patients with high metastatic risk based on Lpd expression and phosphorylation status. By analyzing tissue microarrays from 312 breast cancer patients, the invention demonstrates that moderate cytoplasmic Lpd expression (histoscore 2) is associated with a 1.76-fold increased risk of cancer-associated death, while high plasma membrane Lpd expression (histoscore 3) is associated with a 2.23-fold increased risk of metastasis and a 1.65-fold increased risk of disease recurrence. These findings are validated across multiple independent cohorts and are independent of other clinical parameters such as tumor grade, hormone receptor status, or HER2 expression. The invention thus provides a novel, clinically actionable biomarker that can be implemented using standard immunohistochemical techniques in pathology laboratories worldwide.

The invention further provides a method for therapeutic intervention by targeting the Lpd signaling axis. The invention demonstrates that pharmacological inhibition of Src or Abl kinases using Dasatinib or STI571 significantly reduces breast cancer cell invasion in 3D matrices. Importantly, the combination of both inhibitors produces a synergistic effect, nearly abolishing invasive capacity. The invention further shows that expression of non-phosphorylatable Lpd mutants (LpdY6F or LpdY8F) acts as a dominant-negative inhibitor, suppressing invasion and metastasis in vivo without affecting primary tumor growth. This uncoupling of tumor proliferation from metastatic dissemination suggests that targeting Lpd phosphorylation may offer a therapeutic window that selectively blocks metastasis while sparing normal tissue homeostasis.

The invention further provides a method for screening anti-metastatic compounds by measuring their ability to inhibit Lpd phosphorylation, disrupt Lpd-Scar/WAVE or Lpd-Ena/VASP interactions, reduce actin barbed-end formation, or suppress invadopodium-mediated matrix degradation. The invention discloses specific assays, including co-immunoprecipitation, fluorescence resonance energy transfer, barbed-end labeling, and gelatin degradation assays, that can be used in high-throughput screens to identify novel inhibitors of the Lpd axis. The invention further provides recombinant proteins, peptides, and monoclonal antibodies directed against phosphorylated Lpd epitopes or the Lpd-Scar/WAVE and Lpd-Ena/VASP interfaces as tools for diagnostic and therapeutic development.

The invention further provides a method for monitoring treatment response in patients receiving Src or Abl kinase inhibitors. By measuring the phosphorylation status of Lpd in serial tumor biopsies or circulating tumor cells, clinicians can determine whether the drug is effectively engaging its target and whether resistance is emerging due to compensatory mechanisms. The invention further provides methods for detecting phospho-Lpd in liquid biopsies using immunoassays or mass spectrometry-based proteomics, enabling non-invasive monitoring of metastatic burden and therapeutic efficacy.

The invention further provides a method for gene therapy to suppress Lpd expression in metastatic breast cancer. The invention demonstrates that stable knockdown of Lpd using short hairpin RNAs significantly reduces metastatic dissemination in orthotopic mouse models without affecting primary tumor size. This finding supports the development of RNA interference-based therapeutics, such as lipid nanoparticles or viral vectors encoding Lpd-targeting shRNAs, for the treatment of high-risk breast cancer patients.

The invention further provides a method for predicting patient response to targeted therapies. Patients whose tumors exhibit high levels of Lpd expression and phosphorylation are predicted to respond favorably to Src and Abl inhibitors, while those with low Lpd expression are unlikely to benefit. This enables the stratification of patients for precision medicine approaches, ensuring that costly and potentially toxic therapies are reserved for those most likely to respond.

The invention further provides a method for developing novel therapeutics that directly disrupt the Lpd-Scar/WAVE or Lpd-Ena/VASP protein-protein interactions. The invention discloses the amino acid sequences of the binding domains on Lpd responsible for these interactions, enabling the design of competitive peptides, stapled peptides, or small molecules that mimic or block these interfaces. The invention further demonstrates that disruption of either interaction alone is insufficient to block metastasis, but simultaneous inhibition of both interactions completely abrogates invasive migration, establishing the Lpd axis as a viable dual-target strategy.

The invention further provides a method for evaluating the efficacy of anti-metastatic therapies in preclinical models. The invention describes a standardized protocol for orthotopic mammary tumor implantation, intravital imaging, and quantification of metastatic burden in immunodeficient mice. The invention further provides metrics for assessing tumor cell motility, protrusion dynamics, directionality, and lung metastasis formation, enabling reproducible and quantitative evaluation of therapeutic candidates.

The invention further provides a method for identifying novel regulators of the Lpd pathway. The invention discloses the complete set of tyrosine phosphorylation sites on Lpd and their functional significance, enabling the use of phosphoproteomic approaches to identify upstream kinases, phosphatases, and adaptors that modulate Lpd activity. The invention further provides reagents, including phospho-specific antibodies and mass spectrometry standards, for the detection and quantification of these modifications in clinical samples.

The invention further provides a method for integrating Lpd status into existing clinical decision-making frameworks. The invention demonstrates that Lpd expression and phosphorylation are independent predictors of metastatic outcome, suggesting that Lpd could be incorporated into multi-gene prognostic panels alongside established markers such as Ki-67, ER/PR, and HER2. The invention further provides statistical models for calculating metastatic risk based on Lpd histoscore, enabling the development of digital pathology algorithms for automated risk assessment.

The invention further provides a method for preventing metastatic recurrence after primary tumor resection. The invention demonstrates that Lpd is not required for primary tumor growth, suggesting that therapies targeting Lpd may be administered as adjuvant treatments to eliminate disseminated tumor cells without causing systemic toxicity. The invention further provides protocols for systemic delivery of Lpd inhibitors following surgical removal of the primary tumor, with the goal of preventing the outgrowth of micrometastases.

The invention further provides a method for understanding the role of Lpd in other cancer types. The invention demonstrates that Lpd is overexpressed in several breast cancer subtypes, but its expression and phosphorylation status may also be relevant in other epithelial cancers, including ovarian, lung, and pancreatic carcinomas. The invention provides a framework for extending these findings to other malignancies, opening new avenues for broad-spectrum anti-metastatic therapies.

The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in the understanding of breast cancer metastasis, transforming Lpd from a poorly characterized scaffold protein into a central, druggable node in a kinase-regulated signaling axis that governs invasive migration. The invention provides a comprehensive set of diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic tools that are novel, non-obvious, and clinically translatable, offering new hope for the prevention and treatment of metastatic breast cancer.

### EXAMPLES

The invention is further illustrated by the following detailed examples, which describe the experimental procedures, results, and validations that support the claims of the invention. These examples are provided for illustrative purposes and are not intended to limit the scope of the invention in any way.

**Example 1: Lpd Expression Correlates with Metastatic Potential in Breast Cancer Cell Lines**

To determine whether Lpd expression is associated with the metastatic phenotype in breast cancer, a panel of established breast cancer cell lines representing different molecular subtypes was analyzed. The invasive, basal-like MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-231-LM2 cell lines, which are highly metastatic in vivo, were compared with the non-invasive, luminal MCF7 and T47D cell lines. Western blot analysis using a polyclonal antibody specific for Lpd revealed significantly higher protein levels in the MDA-MB-231 and LM2 lines compared to MCF7 and T47D. This difference was not observed for other actin regulators such as Arp2/3 subunits or Ena/VASP proteins, indicating that Lpd overexpression is not a general feature of aggressive cancer cells but is specifically associated with invasive phenotypes. The elevated Lpd expression in the LM2 line, a derivative of MDA-MB-231 selected for enhanced lung metastasis, further supported a direct link between Lpd levels and metastatic competence.

**Example 2: High Lpd mRNA Levels Predict Poor Prognosis in Breast Cancer Patients**

Publicly available gene expression datasets from three independent cohorts of breast cancer patients were analyzed to determine whether Lpd transcript levels correlate with clinical outcomes. Using the Oncomine database, Lpd mRNA expression was quantified in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal breast tissue. In all three cohorts, Lpd was significantly overexpressed in tumor samples. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that patients with high Lpd mRNA expression (upper quartile or tertile) had significantly reduced metastasis-free survival and disease-free survival compared to those with low expression. The hazard ratios ranged from 1.51 to 2.23, with p-values less than 0.01 in all cohorts. These findings were consistent across different platforms, including Affymetrix and Agilent microarrays, and were independent of tumor stage, grade, or lymph node status, indicating that Lpd mRNA overexpression is an independent predictor of metastatic progression.

**Example 3: Lpd Protein Expression and Localization Predict Cancer-Associated Mortality**

To validate the clinical relevance of Lpd at the protein level, a tissue microarray (TMA) containing 312 formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded invasive breast cancer specimens was stained with a validated polyclonal anti-Lpd antibody. Two independent pathologists, blinded to clinical outcomes, assessed Lpd staining intensity in both the cytoplasm and plasma membrane using a weighted histoscore system. Cytoplasmic Lpd intensity was categorized into three groups: histoscore 1 (low, 0–88.75), histoscore 2 (moderate, 88.75–170), and histoscore 3 (high, 170–200). Plasma membrane Lpd was similarly categorized: histoscore 1 (0–25), histoscore 2 (25–95), and histoscore 3 (95–200). Cox proportional hazards regression analysis revealed that histoscore 2 cytoplasmic Lpd was associated with a 1.76-fold increased risk of cancer-associated death (95% CI: 1.026–3.036; p=0.039), while histoscore 3 plasma membrane Lpd was associated with a 2.23-fold increased risk of cancer-associated mortality (95% CI: 1.26–3.949; p=0.006). Furthermore, histoscore 3 plasma membrane Lpd was associated with a 1.65-fold increased risk of disease recurrence (95% CI: 1.24–2.428; p=0.001) and a 1.52-fold increased risk of metastasis (95% CI: 1.054–2.178; p=0.025). These associations remained significant after adjusting for age, tumor size, grade, ER/PR status, and HER2 expression in multivariate analysis.

**Example 4: Lpd Knockdown Suppresses Metastasis Without Affecting Primary Tumor Growth**

To determine whether Lpd is functionally required for metastasis, stable knockdown of Lpd was achieved in the highly metastatic MDA-MB-231-LM2 cell line using two independent lentiviral shRNAs. Control cells were transduced with a non-targeting shRNA. Cells were injected orthotopically into the mammary fat pads of immunodeficient mice. Primary tumors were allowed to grow for six weeks, at which point mice were euthanized and tumors and lungs were harvested. Tumor weights were comparable between control and Lpd-knockdown groups, indicating that Lpd depletion did not impair primary tumor proliferation. However, macroscopic lung metastases were observed in only 3 out of 20 mice bearing Lpd-depleted tumors, compared to 9 out of 10 control mice. Histological examination of lung sections revealed a significant reduction in the number and size of ZsGreen-positive metastatic foci in the Lpd-knockdown group. These results demonstrate that Lpd is essential for metastatic dissemination but dispensable for primary tumor growth.

**Example 5: Lpd Is Required for Tumor Cell Invasion In Vivo**

To assess the effect of Lpd depletion on tumor invasion, paraffin-embedded sections of primary tumors were stained for collagen fibers using second harmonic generation microscopy and for tumor cells using ZsGreen fluorescence. Control tumors exhibited extensive invasion into the surrounding stroma, with tumor cells extending deep into collagen-rich regions. In contrast, Lpd-knockdown tumors were largely contained within a well-defined boundary, with minimal infiltration into the surrounding tissue. Intravital imaging of live tumors revealed that Lpd-depleted cells displayed fewer dynamic protrusions, migrated with lower directionality, and exhibited reduced translocation speed compared to control cells. These findings confirm that Lpd is required for invasive migration within the tumor microenvironment in vivo.

**Example 6: Lpd Promotes Intravasation in a Zebrafish Model**

To determine whether Lpd is required for intravasation, fluorescently labeled MDA-MB-231 cells overexpressing GFP-Lpd or GFP control were injected into the perivitelline space of zebrafish embryos. After 24 hours, the number of disseminated tumor cells in the trunk of the fish was quantified. Cells overexpressing GFP-Lpd showed a significantly higher frequency of dissemination compared to control cells. This effect was not observed when cells were injected into the bloodstream, indicating that Lpd specifically enhances the ability of tumor cells to enter the vasculature, not to survive or colonize distant sites. These results establish Lpd as a critical mediator of intravasation.

**Example 7: Lpd Depletion Impairs EGF-Induced Lamellipodial Protrusion**

To investigate the cellular mechanism by which Lpd promotes invasion, MDA-MB-231 and MTLn3 cells were serum-starved and stimulated with EGF. Lamellipodial protrusion dynamics were analyzed using time-lapse microscopy and kymographic analysis. Lpd-depleted cells exhibited a significant reduction in protrusion persistence and distance, but not in protrusion speed. Protrusion initiation was delayed by approximately 30 seconds in Lpd-knockdown cells compared to controls. Immunofluorescence staining revealed that Lpd rapidly translocates to the cell edge upon EGF stimulation, and this recruitment is impaired in Lpd-depleted cells. These findings indicate that Lpd is required for the timely initiation and sustained persistence of lamellipodial protrusions in response to chemotactic cues.

**Example 8: Lpd Is Required for Actin Polymerization at the Leading Edge**

To determine whether Lpd regulates actin polymerization, a G-actin incorporation assay was performed to quantify free barbed ends in lamellipodia. Following EGF stimulation, control cells exhibited a rapid increase in barbed-end signal at the cell periphery, peaking at one minute. In contrast, Lpd-depleted cells showed a significantly reduced barbed-end signal, indicating impaired actin polymerization. This reduction was not due to decreased total F-actin levels, as phalloidin staining was unchanged. These results demonstrate that Lpd promotes the formation of polymerization-competent actin barbed ends downstream of EGFR activation.

**Example 9: Lpd Mediates Recruitment of Ena/VASP and Arp2/3 Complexes**

Immunofluorescence staining revealed that Mena, a member of the Ena/VASP family, and the Arp2/3 complex were both recruited to the leading edge of control cells following EGF stimulation. In Lpd-depleted cells, the localization of both Mena and Arp2/3 was significantly diminished. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments confirmed that Lpd interacts with both Mena and the Scar/WAVE complex, and that these interactions are enhanced upon EGF stimulation. These findings indicate that Lpd serves as a scaffold that recruits both actin elongation and nucleation machinery to the leading edge.

**Example 10: Lpd Is Required for Chemotactic Sensing**

To test whether Lpd is required for directional migration, a micropipette assay was performed to generate a localized EGF gradient. Control cells formed new lamellipodia preferentially toward the micropipette, demonstrating chemotactic sensing. In contrast, Lpd-depleted cells formed protrusions randomly, with no directional bias. The chemotactic index was significantly reduced in Lpd-knockdown cells. Re-expression of wild-type Lpd rescued this defect, whereas a mutant lacking Ena/VASP binding sites (LpdEVmut) did not. These results demonstrate that Lpd-mediated recruitment of Ena/VASP proteins is essential for directional sensing in response to chemotactic gradients.

**Example 11: Lpd Enhances 3D Invasion and Matrix Degradation**

In 3D inverted invasion assays using matrigel or collagen, Lpd-depleted cells showed significantly reduced invasion toward EGF. Conversely, overexpression of Lpd enhanced invasion. To determine whether this effect was due to increased migration or matrix degradation, cells were treated with the MMP inhibitor GM6001. While invasion was reduced in control cells, Lpd-overexpressing cells still invaded significantly further, indicating that Lpd enhances migration independently of MMP activity. However, Lpd was found to co-localize with cortactin at invadopodia, and its depletion reduced the number of both precursor and mature invadopodia. Gelatin degradation assays confirmed that Lpd-depleted cells exhibited significantly reduced matrix degradation. These results demonstrate that Lpd promotes both migration and matrix degradation during invasion.

**Example 12: Lpd Interactions with Scar/WAVE and Ena/VASP Are Differentially Regulated**

Co-immunoprecipitation experiments revealed that Lpd interacts with both the Scar/WAVE complex and Ena/VASP proteins. Phosphorylation of Lpd by Abl kinase enhanced its binding to Ena/VASP but had no effect on Scar/WAVE binding. In contrast, phosphorylation of Lpd by Src kinase enhanced its binding to Scar/WAVE but not to Ena/VASP. These findings indicate that Lpd’s interactions with these two actin regulators are independently regulated by distinct kinases.

**Example 13: Src Kinases Phosphorylate Lpd at Specific Tyrosine Residues**

Using a peptide array covering all tyrosine residues in Lpd, purified Src kinase was shown to phosphorylate six specific tyrosines: Y366, Y426, Y456, Y466, Y481, and Y510. Mutation of these residues to phenylalanine (LpdY6F) abolished Src-mediated phosphorylation. Similarly, mutation of eight tyrosines, including the six Src sites plus Y513 and Y1226 (LpdY8F), abolished all EGF-induced tyrosine phosphorylation. These mutants failed to bind the Scar/WAVE complex and did not enhance 3D invasion, demonstrating that phosphorylation at these sites is essential for Lpd function.

**Example 14: Phosphorylation of Lpd Is Required for 3D Invasion and Metastasis**

Expression of LpdY6F or LpdY8F mutants in Lpd-depleted cells failed to rescue the invasive defect in 3D matrigel or the metastatic phenotype in vivo. In contrast, wild-type Lpd fully restored invasion and metastasis. These findings demonstrate that phosphorylation of Lpd at these specific tyrosine residues is required for its pro-metastatic function.

**Example 15: Inhibition of Src and Abl Kinases Suppresses Metastasis**

Treatment of MDA-MB-231 cells with the Src inhibitor Dasatinib or the Abl inhibitor STI571 significantly reduced invasion in 3D matrices. The combination of both inhibitors produced a synergistic effect, nearly abolishing invasion. In vivo, treatment of tumor-bearing mice with Dasatinib or STI571 significantly reduced lung metastasis, with the combination therapy showing the greatest effect. These results validate Src and Abl kinases as therapeutic targets for inhibiting Lpd-mediated metastasis.
