# DESCRIPTION

The invention relates to a novel class of bidirectional neural interfaces based on three-dimensionally nanostructured boron-doped diamond (3D-nanostructured BDD) microelectrode arrays, engineered to simultaneously achieve low electrical impedance, high charge injection capacity, minimal intrinsic noise, and long-term biocompatibility for high-fidelity neural recording and stimulation. These microelectrodes are fabricated through a multi-step microfabrication process that integrates vertically aligned carbon nanotubes (VACNTs) as a conductive interlayer template, encapsulated between two thin layers of boron-doped diamond, thereby creating a porous, high-surface-area electrode architecture that overcomes the fundamental limitations of conventional planar boron-doped diamond electrodes. The resulting electrode structure exhibits a dramatic reduction in impedance—approximately forty-fold lower than conventional BDD electrodes at 1 kHz—while maintaining the electrochemical stability, wide potential window, and chemical inertness inherent to diamond. This unique combination enables the reliable detection of low-amplitude neural signals, including action potentials as small as 10 to 20 millivolts, which are typically undetectable with conventional BDD electrodes due to their high intrinsic noise and impedance. Furthermore, the 3D-nanostructured BDD electrodes demonstrate a charge storage capacity exceeding ten millicoulombs per square centimeter under physiological conditions, surpassing that of titanium nitride and approaching that of iridium oxide, while preserving a safe electrochemical potential window extending from −1.5 volts to +1.1 volts, thereby minimizing the risk of deleterious electrochemical reactions such as water electrolysis or chloride oxidation during neural stimulation. The mechanical robustness of the electrode structure is enhanced by the encapsulation of the VACNTs within the diamond matrix, rendering the electrodes resistant to degradation under repeated physical cleaning, prolonged immersion in physiological fluids, and extended operational use. The biocompatibility of the 3D-nanostructured BDD surface has been validated through long-term cultures of embryonic hippocampal and spinal cord neurons, which exhibit normal attachment, survival, and neurite extension comparable to that observed on conventional BDD and standard tissue culture substrates, without the induction of glial scarring or inflammatory responses. The integration of this material into microelectrode arrays with precisely defined geometries—such as 20-micrometer-diameter electrodes arranged in 4×15 or 1×60 configurations—enables spatially resolved, multiplexed recording and stimulation across neural tissue, making it particularly suitable for applications in brain-machine interfaces, retinal and spinal cord prostheses, and neuroscientific investigations requiring high spatiotemporal resolution. The fabrication process is compatible with standard semiconductor microfabrication techniques, including photolithography, reactive ion etching, chemical vapor deposition, and lift-off metallization, allowing for scalable, reproducible production of arrays with consistent electrical performance across hundreds of electrodes. The absence of conductive polymers, fragile coatings, or unstable metallic layers ensures long-term stability in vivo, while the diamond-based composition provides resistance to corrosion, biofouling, and mechanical wear, thereby addressing the primary failure modes of existing neural interfaces. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in neural electrode design by merging the biostability of diamond with the electrochemical advantages of nanostructured carbon architectures, creating a durable, high-performance platform capable of bridging the gap between the demands of chronic neural interfacing and the limitations of current materials.

The microelectrode array of the invention comprises a substrate of fused silica or another electrically insulating material, upon which an array of discrete microelectrodes is formed, each microelectrode having a diameter of approximately 20 micrometers and a height extending vertically from the substrate surface due to the three-dimensional architecture of the electrode structure. Each microelectrode consists of a first layer of boron-doped diamond deposited directly onto the substrate, followed by a vertically aligned carbon nanotube interlayer grown from catalytic nickel nanoparticles patterned atop the first diamond layer, and capped by a second layer of boron-doped diamond that fully encapsulates the nanotubes, forming a continuous, mechanically integrated composite. The boron doping concentration in both diamond layers is maintained at approximately 2×10²¹ atoms per cubic centimeter, ensuring optimal electrical conductivity while preserving the biocompatible and electrochemically inert properties of the diamond lattice. The vertically aligned carbon nanotubes, with lengths of approximately three micrometers, are uniformly distributed across the electrode surface and serve as a highly conductive scaffold that bridges the resistive diamond layers, effectively reducing the effective series resistance of the electrode and increasing the electrochemically active surface area by a factor of approximately forty compared to a planar BDD electrode of identical geometric footprint. The surface of the final electrode structure is oxygen-terminated through a controlled ozone treatment, rendering it hydrophilic and promoting favorable interactions with biological tissues and aqueous electrolytes. The microelectrodes are electrically connected to external circuitry via thin-film metal traces composed of chromium and gold, deposited using lift-off photolithography, with each trace terminating in a metal ring that overlaps the edge of the diamond electrode by at least five micrometers to ensure low-resistance, mechanically robust electrical contact. The entire array is passivated with a layer of SU-8 photoresist, approximately two micrometers thick, which insulates all metal traces and non-electrode regions of the substrate, with only the top surface of each microelectrode exposed through a precisely defined aperture of 20-micrometer diameter to ensure that electrical stimulation and recording occur exclusively at the intended interface. The fabrication process is designed to eliminate electrical crosstalk between adjacent electrodes by utilizing a fused silica substrate, which is inherently non-conductive and prevents capacitive or resistive coupling between neighboring microelectrodes, thereby enabling independent, simultaneous recording and stimulation across the entire array without signal interference. The resulting microelectrode array exhibits a median impedance of approximately 50 kilo-ohms at 1 kilohertz, a value comparable to state-of-the-art neural electrode materials such as titanium nitride and poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene), while maintaining a significantly wider electrochemical potential window and superior mechanical durability. The intrinsic noise level of the 3D-nanostructured BDD electrodes, measured in artificial cerebrospinal fluid under standard recording conditions, was found to be approximately 3.1 microvolts root mean square, which is substantially lower than that of conventional BDD electrodes (10.7 microvolts) and only marginally higher than that of black platinum electrodes (2.0 microvolts), yet without the mechanical fragility associated with black platinum. This low noise level permits the reliable detection of small-amplitude neural spikes that are typically buried in the noise floor of conventional BDD electrodes, thereby expanding the dynamic range of neural signal acquisition. The electrodes demonstrate exceptional mechanical robustness, as evidenced by the absence of measurable degradation in noise level or impedance following ten cycles of aggressive cleaning using standard household detergent and soft-bristled brushing, a test that would severely damage or destroy conventional polymer-coated or metal-based electrodes. The encapsulation of the carbon nanotubes within the diamond matrix prevents their release into the surrounding biological environment, mitigating potential concerns regarding nanomaterial toxicity and ensuring long-term biocompatibility. The surface topography of the 3D-nanostructured BDD electrode, characterized by a dense forest of nanoscale diamond-coated carbon nanotubes protruding from the surface, provides a biomimetic architecture that supports the adhesion and neurite outgrowth of neurons without promoting glial proliferation, as demonstrated in long-term cultures of embryonic hippocampal and spinal cord neurons over fourteen days in vitro. The absence of chemical surface modifications or extracellular matrix coatings further confirms that the topographical features of the 3D-nanostructured BDD itself are sufficient to support neuronal viability and network formation, indicating that the material is inherently conducive to neural integration. The electrochemical behavior of the electrodes, as characterized by cyclic voltammetry in phosphate-buffered saline and artificial cerebrospinal fluid, reveals a capacitive charge storage mechanism dominating within the −1.5 V to +1.1 V potential window, with no evidence of irreversible faradaic reactions such as water splitting or chloride oxidation within this range, thereby ensuring safe and reversible charge injection during neural stimulation. A small peak observed at approximately +1.3 V in chloride-containing solutions is attributed to the oxidation of chloride ions and does not compromise the safety of the electrode within the physiologically relevant stimulation range. The charge storage capacity of the 3D-nanostructured BDD electrode, calculated from the integrated capacitive current, exceeds 10 millicoulombs per square centimeter under physiological conditions, a value sufficient to deliver therapeutic levels of stimulation to neural tissue without exceeding safe charge density thresholds. The wide potential window of over three volts, preserved throughout the fabrication process, allows for the use of high-amplitude stimulation pulses without triggering parasitic electrochemical reactions, a critical advantage for microelectrodes with small geometric dimensions where charge density is inherently elevated. The ability of the 3D-nanostructured BDD electrodes to record and stimulate neural activity has been validated in both acute ex vivo preparations of embryonic mouse hindbrain-spinal cord tissue and in long-term cultures of dissociated hippocampal neurons. In the acute preparation, the electrodes successfully detected propagating waves of local field potentials and high-frequency bursts of action potentials with amplitudes as low as 10 to 20 microvolts, which were not reliably detectable using conventional BDD electrodes due to their elevated noise levels. Electrical stimulation delivered via a single 3D-nanostructured BDD electrode triggered reproducible, synchronized bursts of multiunit activity across multiple adjacent electrodes, demonstrating bidirectional functionality and spatial precision. In hippocampal cultures, the electrodes recorded spontaneous bursting activity characteristic of developing neuronal networks, and stimulation pulses reliably elicited coordinated responses across the cultured network, confirming the electrode’s capacity to both sense and modulate neural activity with high fidelity. The durability of the electrodes was further demonstrated by their repeated use over multiple recording sessions without degradation in signal quality, impedance, or noise characteristics, indicating that the electrode structure remains stable under prolonged exposure to physiological conditions and mechanical stress. The fabrication process is scalable and compatible with standard microfabrication facilities, enabling the production of arrays with hundreds of electrodes on a single substrate, and the use of fused silica as the substrate material ensures compatibility with optical imaging techniques, allowing for simultaneous electrophysiological recording and fluorescence microscopy of neural activity. The invention thus provides a comprehensive solution to the longstanding challenges of neural interfacing by integrating high performance, long-term stability, and biocompatibility into a single, manufacturable electrode architecture that is uniquely suited for both acute experimental applications and chronic clinical implants. The 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrode array represents a transformative advancement in neural interface technology, offering a platform that is not merely an incremental improvement over existing materials but a fundamentally new class of electrode capable of meeting the stringent requirements of next-generation neuroprosthetics and brain-machine interfaces.

The invention further encompasses the method of fabricating the 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrode array, which begins with the deposition of detonation diamond nanoparticles onto a fused silica substrate using spin coating, followed by the growth of a first layer of boron-doped diamond via microwave plasma chemical vapor deposition under controlled conditions of temperature, pressure, and gas composition to achieve a thickness of approximately 500 nanometers and a boron doping concentration of 2×10²¹ atoms per cubic centimeter. The surface of this first diamond layer is then patterned using photolithography to define the locations of future microelectrodes, with an aluminum mask deposited via electron beam evaporation to protect the desired electrode regions. Nickel is selectively deposited onto the exposed diamond surface at these locations using electron beam evaporation to form a thin layer of approximately eight nanometers in thickness, which is subsequently annealed at 700 degrees Celsius for three minutes to induce the formation of discrete nickel nanoparticles that serve as catalysts for the growth of vertically aligned carbon nanotubes. The substrate is then transferred to a plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition reactor, where carbon nanotubes are grown for a duration of twenty minutes using a carbon-containing gas mixture, resulting in the formation of vertically oriented nanotubes with lengths of approximately three micrometers. Following growth, the substrate is immersed in hot aqua regia to remove any residual nickel catalyst that may remain at the tips of the nanotubes, ensuring that no metallic contaminants are present at the electrode-tissue interface. The entire substrate is then coated with a dense layer of nanodiamond particles using an electrostatic grafting technique to provide nucleation sites for the subsequent diamond growth step. A second layer of boron-doped diamond, approximately fifty nanometers in thickness, is then deposited using microwave plasma chemical vapor deposition under soft growth conditions to ensure conformal coverage of the nanotube scaffold without collapsing the structure, thereby encapsulating the nanotubes within the diamond matrix and forming a continuous, mechanically integrated 3D electrode architecture. The aluminum mask is then chemically removed, and the substrate is subjected to reactive ion etching under an oxygen plasma to remove any excess diamond material that has grown outside the defined electrode regions, leaving behind only the patterned 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrodes. Metal contacts are then formed by depositing a bilayer of chromium and gold using lift-off photolithography, with the metal traces extending beyond the electrode edges by at least five micrometers to ensure a low-resistance, mechanically stable connection. A final passivation layer of SU-8 photoresist, approximately two micrometers thick, is deposited and patterned to expose only the top surface of each microelectrode, defining the active recording and stimulation area. The entire array is then subjected to an ozone treatment for two hours to terminate the diamond surface with oxygen-containing functional groups, enhancing its hydrophilicity and promoting favorable interactions with biological fluids. This fabrication sequence is entirely compatible with standard semiconductor manufacturing processes and can be performed on wafer-scale substrates, enabling the simultaneous production of hundreds of identical microelectrode arrays with high yield and reproducibility. The use of fused silica as the substrate material ensures electrical isolation between electrodes, prevents signal crosstalk, and allows for optical transparency, facilitating multimodal experiments combining electrophysiology with fluorescence imaging. The precise control over the thickness of each diamond layer, the density and length of the carbon nanotubes, and the uniformity of the boron doping concentration ensures that the electrical and mechanical properties of the electrodes are consistent across the entire array, making the invention suitable for high-density, multiplexed neural recording and stimulation applications. The method further includes a post-fabrication cleaning and conditioning protocol that involves immersion in ethanol, drying under sterile conditions, and sterilization via heat treatment, ensuring that the electrodes are ready for immediate use in biological environments without requiring additional surface modifications. The resulting microelectrode array is not merely a composite of disparate materials but a monolithic, integrally formed structure in which the diamond and carbon nanotube components are chemically bonded and mechanically interlocked, eliminating the risk of delamination, peeling, or detachment during use. This structural integrity is a critical advantage over polymer-coated or nanoparticle-laden electrodes, which are prone to degradation under mechanical stress or prolonged immersion. The fabrication method thus provides a robust, scalable, and reproducible pathway to producing neural interfaces with performance characteristics that exceed those of any previously reported material system, while maintaining the biocompatibility and longevity required for chronic implantation.

The invention further includes the use of the 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrode array for neural recording and stimulation in both acute and chronic experimental settings, including but not limited to the recording of spontaneous and evoked neural activity from embryonic spinal cord and hippocampal preparations, the stimulation of neural circuits to elicit controlled responses, and the long-term monitoring of neuronal network dynamics in vitro. The electrodes are particularly suited for applications requiring the detection of low-amplitude signals, such as those generated by immature neurons, small-diameter axons, or densely packed neural populations, where conventional electrodes fail due to high noise or impedance. The low intrinsic noise level of the 3D-nanostructured BDD electrodes, measured at approximately 3.1 microvolts root mean square, enables the reliable detection of action potentials with amplitudes as small as 10 to 20 microvolts, which are typically indistinguishable from background noise in conventional BDD or even platinum-based systems. This capability is critical for studying early-stage neural development, where neuronal signals are inherently weak due to high membrane resistance and immature ion channel expression. The electrodes have been successfully employed to record propagating waves of local field potentials and high-frequency bursts of multiunit activity from embryonic mouse hindbrain-spinal cord preparations, demonstrating their ability to capture spatially organized neural dynamics with high fidelity. The spatial resolution of the array, enabled by the 20-micrometer electrode diameter and 150-micrometer inter-electrode spacing, allows for the precise localization of signal sources and the mapping of neural propagation patterns across the tissue. Electrical stimulation delivered through individual electrodes has been shown to trigger reproducible, synchronized bursts of activity across multiple adjacent recording sites, confirming the bidirectional nature of the interface and its capacity to modulate neural circuitry with high precision. The stimulation parameters, including pulse duration, amplitude, and polarity, can be tailored to the specific neural target, with biphasic cathodic-first pulses of 1 millisecond duration and 10 microamperes amplitude proving effective in eliciting robust responses without inducing tissue damage or electrode degradation. The stability of the stimulation response over repeated sessions, with no measurable change in threshold or efficacy, confirms the long-term reliability of the electrode interface. The electrodes are also suitable for long-term recordings in dissociated neuronal cultures, where they have been used to monitor spontaneous bursting activity over fourteen days in vitro, capturing the maturation of neuronal networks and the emergence of synchronized firing patterns. The absence of glial overgrowth or inflammatory response on the electrode surface, as confirmed by immunocytochemical staining for neuronal and glial markers, demonstrates the biocompatibility of the 3D-nanostructured BDD material and its suitability for chronic implantation. The electrodes exhibit no signs of corrosion, delamination, or surface fouling even after prolonged exposure to physiological fluids, and their performance remains unchanged after multiple cleaning cycles, indicating that they are not susceptible to biofouling or mechanical degradation. The invention thus enables a new paradigm in neural interfacing, where the same electrode can be used for both high-sensitivity recording and safe, high-capacity stimulation over extended periods, eliminating the need for separate recording and stimulation electrodes and simplifying the design of neural prosthetic devices. The ability to record and stimulate with the same microelectrode also facilitates closed-loop feedback systems, in which neural activity is continuously monitored and stimulation is dynamically adjusted in real time based on the recorded signal, a critical requirement for adaptive neuroprosthetics. The invention further enables the simultaneous acquisition of electrophysiological data and optical imaging, as the fused silica substrate is transparent to visible and near-infrared light, allowing for the use of fluorescence microscopy to correlate neural activity with cellular morphology, calcium dynamics, or gene expression. This multimodal capability is not feasible with opaque substrates such as silicon or metal, making the invention uniquely suited for advanced neuroscientific investigations. The electrodes are also compatible with standard electrophysiological amplifiers and data acquisition systems, requiring no specialized instrumentation, and can be seamlessly integrated into existing experimental workflows. The invention thus provides a complete, end-to-end solution for neural interfacing, combining a novel material architecture, a scalable fabrication method, and validated performance in biological systems to create a platform that is ready for translation into clinical and research applications.
