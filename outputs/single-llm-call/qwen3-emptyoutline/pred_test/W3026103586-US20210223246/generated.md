# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel, real-time, minimally invasive diagnostic system and method for the intraoperative detection of metastatic cancer cells in lymph nodes, particularly sentinel and auxiliary lymph nodes in patients with breast carcinoma and other epithelial malignancies. More specifically, the invention encompasses a handheld, disposable, pH-sensing device integrated into a standard medical syringe needle that rapidly assesses the acidity of lymphatic fluid extracted directly from lymph nodes during surgical procedures. The device leverages the well-documented metabolic shift in metastatic cancer cells—from glycolysis to fatty acid oxidation—as a biochemical biomarker, wherein the oxidation of fatty acids generates protons that acidify the local lymphatic microenvironment. This acidification, measurable as a pH drop below 6.0, is reliably detected by a chemically embedded litmus indicator within the lumen of a gauge 18 hypodermic needle, enabling a visual, instantaneous, and operator-independent determination of metastatic involvement. The invention eliminates the need for time-consuming tissue processing, frozen section analysis, or complex instrumentation, and provides a diagnostic accuracy superior to conventional intraoperative pathology methods. The system is designed for seamless integration into standard surgical workflows, requiring no preoperative labeling, no radioactive agents, no imaging modalities, and no specialized training beyond basic surgical technique. It is particularly suited for use in breast cancer surgery, where the accurate identification of sentinel lymph node status directly dictates the extent of lymphadenectomy and subsequent oncologic management, thereby reducing both overtreatment and undertreatment. The invention further extends to potential applications in other cancers where lymphatic metastasis is a known prognostic factor, including melanoma, head and neck carcinomas, and gastrointestinal malignancies, provided that the same metabolic signature of fatty acid oxidation-induced acidosis is present in the metastatic lymph node microenvironment.

## BACKGROUND

The clinical management of solid tumors, particularly breast carcinoma, has long been guided by the status of regional lymph nodes, which serve as the primary conduit for cancer cell dissemination from the primary tumor site. The sentinel lymph node, defined as the first lymph node or group of nodes to receive lymphatic drainage from the primary tumor, is universally recognized as the most critical indicator of disease progression. When cancer cells metastasize to the sentinel lymph node, they often precede systemic spread, making its evaluation indispensable for staging, prognosis, and therapeutic decision-making. Current standard-of-care protocols mandate the surgical removal of the sentinel lymph node followed by intraoperative pathological assessment via frozen section analysis. This procedure, while widely adopted, is fraught with significant limitations. Frozen section pathology is highly dependent on the experience and judgment of the pathologist, is subject to sampling error due to time constraints, and frequently involves the examination of only a portion of the lymph node—typically the most visually abnormal regions based on color, texture, or firmness. As a consequence, false-negative results occur in up to 17% of cases, leading to missed metastases and the subsequent need for secondary surgeries, increased patient morbidity, and suboptimal oncologic outcomes. False-positive results, though less common, can lead to unnecessary axillary lymph node dissection, which carries well-documented risks including lymphedema, chronic pain, sensory disturbances, and impaired mobility. The consequences of these diagnostic inaccuracies are not merely clinical—they are profound in terms of patient quality of life, healthcare costs, and long-term survival.

Alternative methods have been explored to overcome the shortcomings of frozen section analysis. Radiolabeling techniques employing technetium-99m-labeled colloids or blue dyes have been used to identify sentinel lymph nodes preoperatively, but these methods do not provide information regarding the cellular composition of the node. Fluorescent probes targeting tumor-specific antigens such as epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) or human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) have been investigated in preclinical models, but they require complex synthesis, regulatory approval, and prolonged imaging times, rendering them impractical for real-time intraoperative use. Microwave-based detection systems, which exploit dielectric property differences between normal and malignant tissues, have demonstrated feasibility in laboratory settings but require bulky equipment, extended scanning durations of 15 to 30 minutes, and are susceptible to interference from surrounding tissues and fluid dynamics. Similarly, tissue impedance imaging techniques, which measure electrical resistance across tissue interfaces, have been proposed as non-invasive alternatives; however, these methods require direct contact with the tissue, prolonged acquisition times of 5 to 10 minutes per node, and are highly sensitive to moisture, temperature, and electrode placement, resulting in inconsistent reproducibility in the dynamic surgical environment. Furthermore, all of these approaches rely on indirect physical or molecular signatures that may vary between patients, tumor subtypes, or even within different regions of the same lymph node, limiting their reliability as universal diagnostic tools.

A critical gap in the field remains the absence of a rapid, simple, cost-effective, and biologically grounded method capable of providing real-time, high-accuracy detection of metastatic cancer cells within lymph nodes during surgery. No existing technique directly interrogates the metabolic state of the cancer cells themselves, instead relying on morphological, electromagnetic, or immunological proxies that are inherently indirect and subject to confounding variables. The metabolic reprogramming of cancer cells upon lymphatic metastasis—a phenomenon first demonstrated in murine models and later corroborated in human tissue—offers a compelling and previously unexploited opportunity. Recent studies have established that metastatic cancer cells within lymph nodes undergo a fundamental shift in energy metabolism, transitioning from the Warburg effect (aerobic glycolysis) to fatty acid oxidation (FAO) as their primary source of ATP. This metabolic adaptation is driven by the activation of specific transcriptional regulators such as Yes-Associated Protein (YAP), which upregulates enzymes involved in β-oxidation of long-chain fatty acids. The biochemical consequences of this shift are profound: the complete oxidation of fatty acids generates acetyl-CoA, which enters the tricarboxylic acid cycle, leading to the production of carbon dioxide as a metabolic byproduct. In the confined, fluid-rich environment of the lymph node, this carbon dioxide dissolves in the lymphatic fluid and undergoes rapid hydration to form carbonic acid, which dissociates into bicarbonate and hydrogen ions. The resulting accumulation of hydrogen ions lowers the local pH, creating a distinct and measurable acidic microenvironment within metastatic lymph nodes. This acidification is not observed in healthy lymph nodes, which maintain a near-neutral to slightly alkaline pH due to the absence of such intense metabolic activity. Despite this compelling biological insight, no prior invention has translated this metabolic signature into a practical, point-of-care diagnostic tool suitable for the operating room. The present invention bridges this critical translational gap by integrating a simple, robust, and chemically sensitive pH indicator—commercially available litmus paper—directly into the lumen of a standard surgical needle, enabling the direct, real-time measurement of lymphatic fluid pH immediately after needle insertion into the lymph node. This innovation transforms a fundamental biochemical discovery into a clinically actionable diagnostic modality that is immediately deployable, scalable, and cost-effective.

## SUMMARY

The present invention is directed to a novel, minimally invasive, real-time diagnostic device and method for detecting metastatic cancer cells in lymph nodes during surgical procedures, particularly in the context of breast cancer staging. The invention comprises a disposable, single-use diagnostic instrument comprising a standard gauge 18 hypodermic needle with an internal lumen into which a thin strip of pH-sensitive litmus paper is permanently embedded and sealed under sterile conditions. The litmus paper is chemically calibrated to exhibit a distinct and irreversible color change when exposed to a pH below 6.0, corresponding to the acidic microenvironment generated by fatty acid oxidation in metastatic cancer cells. The device is pre-filled with a sterile, non-reactive, isotonic phosphate-buffered saline solution (100 µL) to facilitate the gentle aspiration and mixing of lymphatic fluid upon insertion into the lymph node. During surgical intervention, the needle is inserted into the lymph node, the plunger is withdrawn slightly to allow lymphatic fluid to enter the needle lumen and come into direct contact with the embedded litmus paper, and the resulting color change is visually assessed within 15 to 30 seconds. A color shift from blue or green (indicative of neutral or alkaline pH) to red or orange (indicative of acidic pH below 6.0) is interpreted as a positive indication of metastatic involvement. The device is designed for single use, is compatible with standard surgical sterilization protocols, requires no external power source, requires no specialized training for interpretation, and generates no hazardous waste. The method of use involves no preoperative preparation, no contrast agents, no imaging systems, and no post-procedure processing. The invention achieves a diagnostic sensitivity of at least 90% and a specificity of at least 92% in clinical validation studies, significantly outperforming conventional frozen section pathology, which typically demonstrates sensitivities and specificities in the range of 80% to 85%. The invention enables surgeons to make immediate, evidence-based decisions regarding the extent of lymph node dissection, thereby preventing the unnecessary removal of healthy lymph nodes and reducing the incidence of postoperative complications such as lymphedema, chronic pain, and sensory deficits. The device is scalable for use in multiple lymph nodes during a single procedure, and multiple needles may be employed concurrently to increase diagnostic confidence. The invention further includes a calibration protocol based on histopathological correlation, wherein the pH threshold of 6.0 is established through retrospective analysis of paired lymph node samples subjected to both litmus-based pH measurement and gold-standard hematoxylin and eosin staining. The invention is applicable not only to sentinel lymph nodes but also to auxiliary lymph nodes, and may be extended to other cancer types where lymphatic metastasis is associated with fatty acid oxidation-driven acidosis. The invention represents a paradigm shift in intraoperative diagnostics by replacing complex, time-intensive, and error-prone morphological assessments with a direct, biologically grounded, and metabolically specific chemical detection system that is simple, reliable, and immediately implementable in any surgical setting.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is a diagnostic system and method for the real-time, intraoperative detection of metastatic cancer cells in lymph nodes, based on the measurement of lymphatic fluid pH as a direct biomarker of cancer cell metabolic activity. The invention is embodied in a novel device, referred to herein as the Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD), which integrates a chemically sensitive pH indicator into the lumen of a standard medical syringe needle to enable immediate, visual, and operator-independent assessment of lymph node status. The device operates on the principle that metastatic cancer cells within lymph nodes undergo a metabolic reprogramming characterized by a shift from glycolysis to fatty acid oxidation, a process that generates carbon dioxide as a byproduct. This carbon dioxide dissolves in the lymphatic fluid and reacts with water to form carbonic acid, which dissociates into hydrogen ions and bicarbonate, thereby lowering the local pH. In healthy lymph nodes, which rely on normal immune cell metabolism and lack the intense oxidative activity of metastatic tumor cells, the lymphatic fluid remains near-neutral to slightly alkaline, with a pH ranging from 7.0 to 10.0. In contrast, lymph nodes containing metastatic cancer cells exhibit a consistent and significant acidification, with pH values consistently falling below 6.0. This pH differential is not a secondary or indirect phenomenon but a direct consequence of the biochemical activity of the cancer cells themselves, making it a highly specific and reliable biomarker for metastatic involvement.

The MMLD device is constructed using a commercially available, sterile, single-use gauge 18 hypodermic needle with an inner diameter of 838 micrometers, selected for its optimal balance between fluid flow dynamics and mechanical rigidity during insertion into lymph nodes. The needle is prefilled with 100 microliters of sterile, pyrogen-free, isotonic phosphate-buffered saline solution (PBS), which serves a dual purpose: first, to flush the needle lumen and ensure patency prior to insertion; and second, to act as a carrier medium that facilitates the gentle mixing and extraction of lymphatic fluid upon aspiration. The PBS is introduced into the needle using aseptic techniques under a laminar flow hood and is sealed within the lumen using a sterile, non-reactive, medical-grade plunger. The key innovation of the invention lies in the integration of a thin, rectangular strip of commercially available ColorpHast® pH Test Strips (Merck, Germany), a litmus-based indicator paper calibrated to change color at specific pH thresholds. The litmus paper strip, measuring approximately 5 millimeters in length and 1 millimeter in width, is cut from the commercial strip and inserted longitudinally into the interior lumen of the needle, positioned such that it spans the entire length of the distal 10 millimeters of the needle shaft. The paper is secured in place using a biocompatible, non-toxic, and non-reactive adhesive that is resistant to moisture, mechanical shear, and sterilization processes. The adhesive is applied in a manner that ensures the litmus paper remains fully exposed to the fluid within the lumen while preventing detachment or migration during use. The entire assembly—needle, PBS, and embedded litmus paper—is then subjected to a validated plasma sterilization protocol in accordance with ISO/NP 22441, ensuring the device is sterile, endotoxin-free, and safe for direct intraoperative use. Each device is individually packaged in a sterile, moisture-resistant, peel-open pouch and labeled with a unique batch number and expiration date. The device is designed for single-use only and is discarded immediately after use in accordance with biohazard protocols.

The method of operation is straightforward and designed for seamless integration into standard surgical workflows. Prior to lymph node dissection, the surgeon selects the lymph node to be evaluated, typically the sentinel lymph node identified by preoperative lymphoscintigraphy or blue dye mapping. The MMLD device is removed from its sterile packaging and held in the dominant hand, with the needle oriented perpendicular to the anticipated insertion path. The surgeon gently inserts the needle into the lymph node, ensuring that the distal tip penetrates the parenchyma without excessive force. Once the needle is fully inserted, the plunger is withdrawn slightly—by approximately 1 to 2 millimeters—to create a negative pressure gradient that allows lymphatic fluid to be drawn into the needle lumen through capillary action and passive diffusion. The fluid comes into direct contact with the embedded litmus paper, which responds to the hydrogen ion concentration within the lymphatic fluid. Within 10 to 30 seconds, the litmus paper exhibits a visible color change. If the lymph node is healthy, the pH of the fluid remains above 7.0, and the litmus paper retains its original blue or green hue, indicating a neutral or alkaline environment. If the lymph node contains metastatic cancer cells, the pH drops below 6.0 due to the accumulation of hydrogen ions from fatty acid oxidation, and the litmus paper turns red or orange, signaling a positive result. This color change is permanent, irreversible, and discernible to the naked eye under standard operating room lighting conditions, requiring no magnification, no electronic reader, and no interpretation algorithm. The surgeon may repeat the procedure with additional MMLD devices on the same lymph node or on adjacent lymph nodes to increase diagnostic confidence. In cases where multiple devices are used, if any single device indicates an acidic pH (below 6.0), the lymph node is classified as metastatic. This conservative approach ensures that no metastatic node is missed, even in cases of focal or heterogeneous involvement. The entire process—from needle insertion to visual interpretation—takes less than 30 seconds, enabling real-time decision-making without interrupting the surgical timeline. Upon completion of the assessment, the needle is withdrawn, the litmus paper is inspected, and the result is documented. If the result is positive, the surgeon proceeds with a full axillary lymph node dissection. If the result is negative, the lymph node may be preserved, thereby avoiding the morbidity associated with unnecessary lymphadenectomy.

The calibration of the pH threshold at 6.0 is a critical component of the invention and is established through retrospective correlation with the gold-standard histopathological diagnosis. In clinical validation studies involving 65 lymph nodes from 25 patients with breast carcinoma, each lymph node was subjected to both MMLD testing and standard hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining following formalin fixation and paraffin embedding. The H&E staining procedure involved deparaffinization of tissue sections using xylene, rehydration through a graded alcohol series, staining with hematoxylin for 3 to 5 minutes to visualize nuclei, differentiation in 1% acid alcohol to remove excess stain, bluing in an alkaline solution to restore the blue nuclear color, counterstaining with eosin for 10 minutes to highlight cytoplasmic structures, dehydration through ascending alcohol concentrations, and clearing in xylene prior to mounting. Pathologists blinded to the MMLD results independently reviewed all slides and classified each lymph node as either negative for metastasis (no tumor cells identified) or positive for metastasis (focal or diffuse presence of malignant epithelial cells). The pH values obtained from the MMLD devices were then correlated with these histopathological diagnoses. Statistical analysis revealed that all lymph nodes classified as metastatic by H&E staining exhibited a pH value below 6.0, while all lymph nodes classified as negative for metastasis exhibited a pH value of 7.0 or higher. One false-positive result occurred in a lymph node with extensive reactive lymphoid hyperplasia and macrophage infiltration, which transiently lowered the pH due to inflammatory metabolic activity; however, subsequent permanent section analysis confirmed the absence of epithelial malignancy. One false-negative result occurred in a lymph node with minimal metastatic burden (a single isolated tumor cell), which was not detected by H&E staining due to sampling error but was correctly identified by MMLD. These results demonstrate that the pH threshold of 6.0 is both sensitive and specific for the detection of metastatic cancer cells, and that the MMLD device outperforms conventional frozen section pathology, which in the same cohort demonstrated a sensitivity of 83% and a specificity of 83%. The MMLD device achieved a sensitivity of 92% and a specificity of 90%, with a negative predictive value of 95% and a positive predictive value of 89%. These results confirm that the device reliably detects metastatic involvement even in cases where morphological features are subtle or sparse, and that it is not subject to the sampling errors inherent in frozen section analysis, which typically examines only a fraction of the lymph node tissue.

The biological basis for the pH shift is rooted in the metabolic transformation of cancer cells upon lymphatic dissemination. In primary tumors, cancer cells predominantly rely on aerobic glycolysis—the Warburg effect—to generate ATP, even in the presence of oxygen. However, upon entering the lymphatic system and colonizing lymph nodes, these cells undergo a profound metabolic reprogramming mediated by transcriptional regulators such as Yes-Associated Protein (YAP), which activates a suite of genes encoding enzymes involved in fatty acid transport, activation, and β-oxidation. Fatty acid oxidation occurs in the mitochondria, where long-chain fatty acyl-CoA molecules are sequentially cleaved into two-carbon units of acetyl-CoA. Each cycle of β-oxidation produces one molecule of acetyl-CoA, one molecule of NADH, and one molecule of FADH2. The acetyl-CoA enters the tricarboxylic acid cycle, where it is oxidized to carbon dioxide and water, generating additional NADH and FADH2. These reduced coenzymes then donate electrons to the mitochondrial electron transport chain, ultimately reducing molecular oxygen to water and driving ATP synthesis. The carbon dioxide produced in these reactions diffuses into the surrounding lymphatic fluid, where it undergoes the reversible hydration reaction: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. The equilibrium of this reaction is strongly influenced by the buffering capacity of the fluid, which in lymph nodes is relatively low compared to blood or interstitial fluid. As a result, the continuous production of carbon dioxide by metabolically active cancer cells leads to a progressive accumulation of hydrogen ions, lowering the pH below 6.0. This acidification is not observed in healthy lymph nodes, where immune cells such as lymphocytes and macrophages rely primarily on oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis at low rates, producing minimal carbon dioxide. The pH threshold of 6.0 is therefore not arbitrary but is a direct biochemical consequence of the metabolic phenotype of metastatic cancer cells. The invention exploits this phenomenon by converting a complex metabolic process into a simple, visual, and immediate diagnostic signal.

The advantages of the MMLD device over existing technologies are numerous and profound. First, it is the only intraoperative diagnostic tool that directly measures the metabolic activity of cancer cells rather than relying on indirect proxies such as morphology, electrical impedance, or fluorescent labeling. Second, it requires no preoperative preparation, no injection of contrast agents, no radiation, no imaging equipment, and no specialized training. Third, it provides results in less than 30 seconds, compared to the 20 to 45 minutes required for frozen section analysis. Fourth, it is cost-effective, with each device costing less than $5 to manufacture, compared to the hundreds of dollars associated with radiolabeling, fluorescent probes, or impedance imaging systems. Fifth, it is highly reproducible, with inter-operator variability approaching zero due to the objective nature of the color change. Sixth, it reduces surgical morbidity by enabling the preservation of healthy lymph nodes, thereby minimizing the risk of lymphedema, chronic pain, and sensory disturbances. Seventh, it reduces healthcare costs by eliminating the need for secondary surgeries due to false-negative frozen sections. Eighth, it is scalable and can be used simultaneously on multiple lymph nodes during a single procedure. Ninth, it is compatible with existing surgical workflows and does not require modification of operating room protocols. Tenth, it is environmentally sustainable, as it generates no radioactive waste, no chemical solvents, and no electronic components requiring disposal.

The device may be further enhanced through minor modifications to improve its utility. For example, the litmus paper may be coated with a thin, hydrophilic membrane to enhance fluid absorption and reduce the time required for color development. The needle may be modified to include a transparent, non-reactive sleeve around the distal tip to allow for better visualization of the color change without removing the plunger. The device may be manufactured in multiple sizes to accommodate different lymph node anatomies, such as smaller gauges for cervical or inguinal nodes. The litmus paper may be replaced with alternative pH indicators such as bromocresol green or methyl red, which exhibit similar sensitivity in the pH 5.5 to 7.0 range but with different color transitions, allowing for customization based on surgeon preference or lighting conditions. The device may also be integrated into an automated reader system for digital color analysis, although such a system is not required for the core functionality of the invention. The device may be stored at room temperature and has a shelf life of at least 12 months under dry, sterile conditions. The device may be manufactured in bulk under Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and distributed globally through standard medical supply channels.

In clinical practice, the MMLD device is expected to transform the standard of care for breast cancer surgery. Surgeons will no longer be forced to choose between the risk of undertreating a patient by missing metastatic disease and the risk of overtreating a patient by removing healthy lymph nodes. Instead, they will have access to a rapid, accurate, and biologically grounded diagnostic tool that provides definitive information within seconds. The device will be particularly valuable in resource-limited settings where access to pathology services is limited, and in high-volume surgical centers where efficiency and throughput are paramount. It will also be invaluable in training environments, where novice surgeons can rely on objective, real-time feedback rather than subjective interpretation. The invention may also be adapted for use in other cancers where lymphatic metastasis is a key prognostic factor, including melanoma, where sentinel lymph node biopsy is standard, and head and neck cancers, where cervical lymph node involvement dictates the extent of neck dissection. Preliminary studies in murine models of melanoma and colorectal cancer have demonstrated similar pH acidification in metastatic lymph nodes, suggesting broad applicability. The invention represents a paradigm shift in intraoperative diagnostics, moving from morphology-based interpretation to metabolism-based detection, and from delayed, error-prone analysis to immediate, objective decision-making. It is a simple solution to a complex clinical problem, grounded in rigorous biochemistry, validated by clinical evidence, and designed for real-world implementation.

The invention further encompasses a method of manufacturing the MMLD device, which includes the following steps: (a) procuring sterile, single-use gauge 18 hypodermic needles from a certified medical supplier; (b) obtaining commercially available ColorpHast® pH Test Strips from Merck, Germany, and cutting them into strips of specified dimensions; (c) loading each needle with 100 microliters of sterile, isotonic phosphate-buffered saline using aseptic technique; (d) inserting the litmus paper strip into the needle lumen using a micro-manipulator to ensure precise positioning; (e) securing the litmus paper with a biocompatible, non-reactive adhesive that does not interfere with pH sensitivity; (f) sealing the needle with a sterile, non-porous plunger; (g) placing each assembled device into a sterile, moisture-resistant pouch; (h) subjecting the pouches to plasma sterilization in accordance with ISO/NP 22441; (i) labeling each device with a unique batch number, expiration date, and lot identifier; and (j) storing the devices at ambient temperature until use. The manufacturing process is fully automated in a Class 10,000 cleanroom environment, and each batch undergoes quality control testing for sterility, pH calibration accuracy, and mechanical integrity. The device is not intended for reuse, and any attempt to re-sterilize or re-use the device is explicitly prohibited, as it may compromise the integrity of the litmus paper and lead to false results.

The invention further includes a method of validating the diagnostic accuracy of the MMLD device, which involves the following steps: (a) enrolling patients undergoing surgical resection of breast cancer with indication for sentinel lymph node biopsy; (b) obtaining informed consent in accordance with institutional review board guidelines; (c) performing lymph node dissection according to standard surgical protocols; (d) immediately after removal, inserting the MMLD device into each lymph node and recording the color change; (e) submitting each lymph node for routine histopathological evaluation using hematoxylin and eosin staining; (f) correlating the MMLD result with the gold-standard histopathological diagnosis; (g) calculating sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, and overall accuracy; and (h) establishing a definitive pH threshold for metastatic involvement based on statistical analysis. The threshold of pH 6.0 has been validated across multiple patient cohorts and is not subject to variation based on tumor subtype, patient age, or hormonal status, indicating that the metabolic signature of metastatic cancer cells is conserved across diverse clinical populations.

The invention is not limited to breast cancer and may be applied to any malignancy in which lymphatic metastasis is associated with fatty acid oxidation and subsequent lymphatic acidification. This includes, but is not limited to, melanoma, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, esophageal adenocarcinoma, gastric cancer, and cervical cancer. The invention may also be adapted for use in minimally invasive procedures, such as endoscopic ultrasound-guided fine needle aspiration, where the MMLD device could be inserted through the aspiration needle to provide real-time feedback during sampling. The invention may also be combined with other diagnostic modalities, such as intraoperative ultrasound or fluorescence imaging, to provide multimodal confirmation of lymph node status, although such combination is not required for the invention to function.

By leveraging a fundamental biochemical property of metastatic cancer cells—their ability to acidify their microenvironment through fatty acid oxidation—the invention provides a simple, accurate, rapid, and cost-effective solution to a longstanding clinical challenge. It eliminates the need for complex, time-consuming, and error-prone pathological assessments, reduces patient morbidity, improves surgical outcomes, and lowers healthcare costs. The invention is ready for immediate clinical implementation and represents a significant step forward in the personalized, metabolically informed management of cancer.
