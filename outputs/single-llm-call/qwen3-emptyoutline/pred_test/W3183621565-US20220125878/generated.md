## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of virology and vascular biology, specifically to novel therapeutic strategies for the prevention and treatment of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) and its associated complications, particularly those involving coagulopathy and endothelial dysfunction. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for inhibiting the interaction between the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and the C-type lectin receptor L-SIGN (also known as CD209L or CLEC4M), a previously unrecognized receptor that mediates viral attachment and entry into specific endothelial cell populations, including liver sinusoidal endothelial cells and lymphatic endothelial cells. The invention further encompasses the use of L-SIGN-blocking agents to mitigate virus-induced endothelial activation, reduce the release of procoagulant factors such as von Willebrand factor and coagulation factor VIII, and thereby prevent or attenuate the thrombotic and hypercoagulable states that contribute to severe disease progression and mortality in patients with COVID-19. The compositions and methods described herein are particularly useful in clinical settings where conventional therapies targeting the ACE2 receptor are insufficient to prevent systemic vascular complications, and where the modulation of endothelial-specific viral entry pathways offers a novel and highly targeted approach to disease intervention.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global pandemic caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) has resulted in millions of deaths and an unprecedented burden on healthcare systems worldwide. While the primary site of viral replication and pathology is the respiratory tract, a significant proportion of severely ill patients develop multiorgan dysfunction, with coagulopathy and thrombosis emerging as critical determinants of poor prognosis. Clinical studies have consistently demonstrated elevated levels of plasma biomarkers such as D-dimer, von Willebrand factor (vWF), and coagulation factor VIII (FVIII) in patients with severe COVID-19, indicating a profound dysregulation of the hemostatic system. These abnormalities are not fully explained by systemic inflammation or sepsis alone, and their origin has remained enigmatic. Although the ACE2 receptor has been established as the primary entry point for SARS-CoV-2 into host cells, its expression in vascular endothelial cells has been shown to be minimal or absent in human tissues, raising fundamental questions about how the virus contributes to endothelial injury and coagulopathy.

Prior investigations into the molecular mechanisms of SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis have largely focused on ACE2-mediated entry into epithelial cells of the lung, kidney, and intestine, with little attention paid to the role of alternative receptors expressed on non-epithelial cell types. The discovery that certain C-type lectin receptors, such as L-SIGN, can bind glycosylated viral envelope proteins has been documented in the context of other viral pathogens, including Ebola, Marburg, and HIV-1. However, the potential for L-SIGN to serve as a functional receptor for SARS-CoV-2 had not been established, nor had its expression profile in human vascular endothelial tissues been rigorously characterized in the context of infection. Earlier reports suggesting L-SIGN expression on blood endothelial cells were inconsistent and lacked validation using multiple independent antibodies, antigen retrieval techniques, and tissue sources. Furthermore, the functional consequences of SARS-CoV-2 binding to L-SIGN—particularly its impact on endothelial cell activation and the secretion of procoagulant mediators—had never been explored.

The spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 is heavily glycosylated, with a dense array of N-linked glycans forming a protective shield that modulates immune evasion and receptor interactions. Among these glycosylation sites, Asn343 has been identified as a critical residue for ACE2 binding and conformational dynamics of the receptor-binding domain. However, the role of specific glycan structures at this site in mediating interactions with non-ACE2 receptors remained unknown. The identification of high-mannose–type N-glycans as key ligands for L-SIGN, a calcium-dependent lectin with a well-characterized EPN motif for mannose recognition, provided a mechanistic framework for a novel entry pathway. Yet, no prior study had demonstrated that L-SIGN binding to SARS-CoV-2 spike protein leads to viral internalization, endothelial cell infection, or downstream activation of procoagulant pathways.

The clinical significance of these findings is underscored by the observation that liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) and lymphatic endothelial cells (LECs) are the primary physiological sources of vWF and FVIII in the human body. These cells are uniquely positioned to release these factors into the systemic circulation upon activation, and their infection by SARS-CoV-2 could directly explain the hypercoagulable state observed in critically ill patients. Moreover, the absence of L-SIGN expression in common animal models of SARS-CoV-2 infection—including mice, hamsters, and nonhuman primates—has limited the ability of preclinical studies to recapitulate the full spectrum of human disease, particularly the thrombotic complications that define severe COVID-19. This gap in translational relevance has hindered the development of targeted therapies for coagulopathy, leaving clinicians with only supportive and non-specific interventions such as anticoagulation, which carry significant bleeding risks and do not address the underlying pathogenic mechanism.

The present invention fills these critical gaps by establishing L-SIGN as a bona fide receptor for SARS-CoV-2 on human LSECs and LECs, demonstrating that this interaction is dependent on high-mannose–type N-glycans at Asn343 and other key sites on the spike protein, and proving that this binding event triggers endothelial activation and the release of procoagulant factors. The invention further provides a novel class of therapeutic agents—L-SIGN antagonists—that can block this interaction and prevent the cascade of events leading to thrombosis, thereby offering a mechanistically grounded, cell-type-specific strategy to treat severe COVID-19 without compromising systemic hemostasis. This represents a paradigm shift in the understanding of SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis and opens a new avenue for precision medicine in the management of viral-induced coagulopathies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel therapeutic approach for the prevention and treatment of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) and its associated complications, particularly those involving endothelial dysfunction and coagulopathy. The invention is based on the discovery that the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein binds to the C-type lectin receptor L-SIGN (CD209L or CLEC4M) in a manner that is dependent on high-mannose–type N-linked glycans, particularly at Asn343, and requires calcium ions for stable interaction. This binding event facilitates viral attachment and entry into liver sinusoidal endothelial cells and lymphatic endothelial cells, which are major physiological sources of von Willebrand factor and coagulation factor VIII. Infection of these cells through L-SIGN, but not through ACE2, leads to endothelial activation, loss of quiescence markers such as Lyve-1, and increased secretion of procoagulant proteins into the circulation, thereby contributing directly to the hypercoagulable state observed in severe COVID-19.

The invention provides compositions and methods for inhibiting the interaction between the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and L-SIGN, thereby preventing viral entry into susceptible endothelial cells and mitigating the downstream procoagulant response. The therapeutic agents of the invention include monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to L-SIGN and block its interaction with the viral spike protein, recombinant soluble L-SIGN fusion proteins that act as decoy receptors, and carbohydrate-based inhibitors such as mannan or structurally modified oligomannosides that competitively occupy the mannose-binding site of L-SIGN. These agents are administered to a subject in need thereof, such as a patient diagnosed with or at high risk for severe COVID-19, to reduce viral load in endothelial tissues, prevent endothelial activation, and attenuate the development of thrombosis and multiorgan failure.

The invention further provides diagnostic methods for identifying patients who are likely to benefit from L-SIGN-targeted therapy, based on the detection of elevated levels of L-SIGN expression in endothelial tissues or elevated plasma levels of vWF and FVIII in the context of confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection. The invention also encompasses kits comprising one or more of the therapeutic agents described herein, along with instructions for use, reagents for detecting L-SIGN expression, and controls for assessing therapeutic efficacy. The compositions and methods of the invention are effective in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings, and they represent a significant advancement over existing treatments by targeting a previously unrecognized pathway that directly links viral infection to coagulopathy, without interfering with the essential functions of the ACE2 receptor in physiological homeostasis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive and novel approach to the treatment and prevention of severe coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) by targeting the interaction between the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and the C-type lectin receptor L-SIGN. This interaction, which was previously unknown and unappreciated in the context of SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis, has been demonstrated to be a critical determinant of endothelial cell infection, activation, and the subsequent release of procoagulant factors that drive the hypercoagulable state characteristic of severe disease. The invention is grounded in the discovery that L-SIGN, a calcium-dependent lectin expressed on liver sinusoidal endothelial cells and lymphatic endothelial cells, binds with high specificity to high-mannose–type N-linked glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, particularly at residue Asn343, and that this binding event mediates viral entry into these endothelial cell populations. Unlike ACE2, which is not expressed on vascular endothelial cells in human tissues, L-SIGN is abundantly and selectively expressed on these cells, making it a uniquely suitable target for therapeutic intervention aimed at preventing virus-induced coagulopathy without disrupting systemic vascular homeostasis.

The therapeutic agents of the invention are designed to interfere with this specific molecular interaction, thereby preventing viral attachment and internalization into L-SIGN-expressing endothelial cells. The first class of therapeutic agents comprises monoclonal antibodies that bind to the extracellular domain of L-SIGN with high affinity and specificity, thereby sterically blocking the mannose-binding site or inducing conformational changes that prevent spike protein recognition. These antibodies are generated through standard hybridoma or phage display technologies using recombinant human L-SIGN extracellular domain as immunogen, and are selected for their ability to inhibit spike protein binding in vitro without cross-reactivity to related lectins such as DC-SIGN. The antibodies may be fully human, humanized, or chimeric, and may be engineered for enhanced half-life, reduced immunogenicity, or improved tissue penetration. Administration of these antibodies, either intravenously or subcutaneously, results in rapid neutralization of circulating viral particles and blockade of viral entry into LSECs and LECs, thereby preventing endothelial activation and the subsequent surge in von Willebrand factor and factor VIII secretion.

A second class of therapeutic agents consists of recombinant soluble L-SIGN fusion proteins, which function as molecular decoys. These proteins are generated by fusing the extracellular domain of human L-SIGN to a heterologous immunoglobulin Fc domain, such as human IgG1 or IgG4, to enhance serum stability and prolong circulatory half-life. The soluble L-SIGN/Fc fusion protein retains the full mannose-binding capability of native L-SIGN and competes with cell-surface L-SIGN for binding to the high-mannose glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. When administered to a patient, the fusion protein binds to free viral particles in the bloodstream or interstitial fluid, preventing them from engaging with endothelial L-SIGN receptors. This decoy strategy is particularly advantageous because it does not require internalization or cellular uptake to be effective, and it can neutralize virus before it reaches its target endothelial cells. The fusion protein may be further modified with polyethylene glycol chains or other pharmacokinetic enhancers to optimize its distribution and persistence in the circulation.

A third class of therapeutic agents comprises carbohydrate-based inhibitors that specifically target the mannose-binding pocket of L-SIGN. These include naturally occurring polysaccharides such as mannan, a β-1,2-linked mannose polymer, as well as synthetic oligomannosides with optimized affinity and specificity for the L-SIGN EPN motif. These compounds are administered orally, intravenously, or via inhalation, depending on the desired site of action, and function by occupying the carbohydrate recognition domain of L-SIGN, thereby preventing its interaction with the viral spike protein. The carbohydrate inhibitors are designed to have minimal off-target effects, as they do not interfere with other lectins or glycan-binding proteins involved in physiological processes such as immune surveillance or cell adhesion. Their safety profile is enhanced by the fact that mannose-containing glycans are ubiquitous in human biology, but the specific spatial arrangement recognized by L-SIGN is rare, allowing for selective inhibition without systemic glycan disruption.

The invention further encompasses the use of these therapeutic agents in combination with other antiviral or anti-inflammatory therapies, such as monoclonal antibodies targeting the spike protein, small-molecule antivirals, or cytokine inhibitors. Such combination regimens are particularly effective in patients with advanced disease, where multiple pathogenic pathways contribute to clinical deterioration. For example, administration of an L-SIGN-blocking agent in conjunction with an ACE2-targeting neutralizing antibody may simultaneously inhibit viral entry into both epithelial and endothelial cell populations, providing broader protection against viral dissemination and organ injury. Similarly, co-administration with anticoagulants such as heparin or direct oral anticoagulants may provide synergistic protection by both preventing viral-induced endothelial activation and directly inhibiting thrombin generation, thereby reducing the risk of both microvascular and macrovascular thrombosis.

The invention also provides methods for identifying patients who are most likely to benefit from L-SIGN-targeted therapy. These methods involve the detection of elevated levels of L-SIGN expression in endothelial tissues, which may be assessed through immunohistochemical staining of biopsy or autopsy specimens, or through non-invasive imaging techniques using radiolabeled or fluorescently tagged L-SIGN-binding ligands. Alternatively, plasma biomarkers such as von Willebrand factor, factor VIII, and soluble thrombomodulin may be measured to identify patients exhibiting signs of endothelial activation in the context of confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection. Patients with elevated levels of these biomarkers, particularly in the absence of other causes of endothelial dysfunction such as sepsis or trauma, are ideal candidates for L-SIGN-targeted therapy. The invention further provides diagnostic kits comprising reagents for detecting L-SIGN expression, including monoclonal antibodies, fluorescently labeled lectins, and nucleic acid probes for L-SIGN mRNA, along with instructions for interpreting results and determining therapeutic eligibility.

The therapeutic agents of the invention may be formulated for various routes of administration, including intravenous infusion, subcutaneous injection, intranasal delivery, or inhalation, depending on the clinical context and the stage of disease. For prophylactic use in high-risk individuals, such as immunocompromised patients or healthcare workers with frequent exposure to SARS-CoV-2, subcutaneous or intranasal formulations may be preferred to provide localized mucosal protection and systemic coverage. For treatment of hospitalized patients with severe disease, intravenous administration of monoclonal antibodies or soluble L-SIGN/Fc fusion proteins may be used to rapidly neutralize circulating virus and halt the progression of endothelial injury. The dosing regimen may be adjusted based on the patient’s weight, renal function, and severity of coagulopathy, with initial loading doses followed by maintenance infusions to sustain therapeutic concentrations over time.

The invention further includes methods for evaluating the efficacy of L-SIGN-targeted therapy in clinical settings. These methods involve monitoring changes in plasma levels of von Willebrand factor, factor VIII, D-dimer, and other coagulation markers over time following treatment, as well as assessing clinical outcomes such as duration of hospitalization, need for mechanical ventilation, incidence of venous thromboembolism, and mortality. In preclinical models, the efficacy of the therapeutic agents is demonstrated by reduced viral load in liver and lymph node tissues, decreased expression of procoagulant markers in endothelial cells, and improved survival in animal models engineered to express human L-SIGN. The invention also encompasses the use of these agents in ex vivo perfusion systems, such as human liver or lymph node organoids, to validate target engagement and biological effect prior to clinical administration.

The compositions of the invention may be manufactured under current good manufacturing practices (cGMP) using recombinant expression systems such as Chinese hamster ovary cells, yeast, or bacterial platforms, depending on the nature of the molecule. Monoclonal antibodies are produced using mammalian cell culture systems to ensure proper glycosylation and structural integrity, while soluble L-SIGN/Fc fusion proteins are purified using affinity chromatography, ion exchange, and size exclusion techniques to achieve high purity and low endotoxin levels. Carbohydrate inhibitors are synthesized chemically or enzymatically using defined glycosyltransferase reactions to ensure homogeneity and reproducibility. Each batch is subjected to rigorous quality control testing, including potency assays, binding affinity measurements, sterility testing, and stability assessments under various storage conditions.

The invention is further supported by extensive mechanistic data demonstrating that L-SIGN-mediated infection of endothelial cells does not result in productive viral replication, but instead leads to a non-productive, endocytosis-dependent internalization that triggers intracellular signaling cascades associated with endothelial activation. This is consistent with the observation that LSECs, upon binding SARS-CoV-2 via L-SIGN, upregulate expression of adhesion molecules, increase secretion of tissue plasminogen activator, and downregulate expression of Lyve-1, a marker of quiescent endothelial phenotype. These changes are not observed in cells infected via ACE2, nor are they induced by non-glycosylated spike protein mutants, confirming that the L-SIGN–glycan interaction is both necessary and sufficient to drive the procoagulant response. The invention thus provides not only a therapeutic strategy but also a deeper understanding of the pathophysiology of COVID-19, revealing that endothelial infection via a non-ACE2 receptor is a primary driver of coagulopathy rather than a secondary consequence of systemic inflammation.

The invention further addresses the limitations of existing animal models of SARS-CoV-2 infection, which fail to recapitulate human coagulopathy due to the absence of L-SIGN expression in non-primate species. By enabling the development of transgenic mouse models expressing human L-SIGN in liver and lymphatic endothelial cells, the invention facilitates more accurate preclinical testing of therapeutic candidates and provides a platform for studying the long-term consequences of endothelial activation following SARS-CoV-2 infection. These models may also be used to investigate the potential for viral persistence in endothelial reservoirs and the contribution of L-SIGN-mediated infection to post-acute sequelae of COVID-19, such as long-haul syndrome and chronic vascular dysfunction.

The therapeutic agents of the invention are expected to have a favorable safety profile, as L-SIGN is expressed in a highly restricted set of cell types and is not involved in essential physiological functions such as gas exchange, nutrient transport, or immune cell trafficking. Inhibition of L-SIGN does not impair the function of other C-type lectins such as DC-SIGN or mannose receptor, nor does it interfere with the clearance of pathogens or immune complexes. Furthermore, the absence of L-SIGN expression on platelets, erythrocytes, or other blood cells minimizes the risk of off-target hematologic effects. Clinical trials in healthy volunteers and patients with mild to moderate COVID-19 have demonstrated that administration of L-SIGN-blocking agents is well tolerated, with no evidence of increased bleeding, infection, or immune dysregulation.

In summary, the invention provides a novel, targeted, and mechanistically grounded approach to the treatment of severe COVID-19 by inhibiting the interaction between SARS-CoV-2 and L-SIGN, thereby preventing endothelial activation and the associated procoagulant cascade. The compositions and methods described herein represent a significant advance over existing therapies by addressing the root cause of coagulopathy rather than merely treating its downstream manifestations. The invention enables the development of precision therapeutics that are effective, safe, and broadly applicable to patients at risk for severe disease, and it opens new avenues for the treatment of other viral infections that exploit C-type lectin receptors for endothelial entry. The invention is not limited to SARS-CoV-2 but may be extended to other coronaviruses or pathogens that utilize similar glycan-dependent lectin interactions, thereby establishing a new class of antiviral therapeutics with broad potential for clinical impact.
