# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic agents for the treatment of cancer, particularly breast cancer, comprising human monoclonal antibodies directed against the prolactin receptor (PRLR) and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) formed by linking such antibodies to cytotoxic payloads. More specifically, the invention encompasses fully human anti-PRLR antibodies with high binding affinity and rapid internalization properties, as well as conjugates of these antibodies with microtubule-disrupting agents such as maytansinoids and auristatins, which are capable of selectively delivering potent cytotoxic drugs to tumor cells expressing low to moderate levels of PRLR. The invention further includes methods of using these antibodies and conjugates for the treatment of malignancies characterized by aberrant expression of PRLR, including but not limited to estrogen receptor-positive, progesterone receptor-negative, HER2-negative breast cancers, as well as triple-negative breast cancers and other solid tumors where PRLR is overexpressed relative to normal tissues. The antibodies and conjugates described herein are distinguished by their ability to mediate potent tumor cell killing even in the context of limited target antigen density, a feature that expands the therapeutic utility of ADC technology beyond targets traditionally considered suitable due to high surface expression.

## BACKGROUND

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of mortality worldwide, and breast cancer continues to represent a substantial clinical burden despite advances in early detection and systemic therapies. The classification of breast cancer into molecular subtypes based on the expression of hormone receptors and HER2 has guided the development of targeted therapies, including endocrine agents such as tamoxifen and fulvestrant for estrogen receptor-positive disease, and monoclonal antibodies such as trastuzumab and pertuzumab for HER2-overexpressing tumors. However, a significant proportion of breast cancer patients—approximately 15% to 20%—present with tumors that lack expression of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2, a phenotype classified as triple-negative breast cancer. These tumors are associated with aggressive clinical behavior, limited treatment options, and poor long-term survival outcomes. Even among patients with hormone receptor-positive or HER2-positive disease, resistance to existing therapies frequently develops, leading to disease progression and the need for novel therapeutic strategies.

The prolactin receptor (PRLR), a single-pass transmembrane receptor belonging to the type 1 cytokine receptor superfamily, has emerged as a potential target for cancer therapy. PRLR is activated by prolactin, a polypeptide hormone secreted primarily by the anterior pituitary gland, and signals through the JAK2/STAT5, MAPK, and PI3K pathways to regulate diverse physiological processes including lactation, mammary gland development, and cell proliferation. While PRLR expression in normal adult tissues is generally restricted to the mammary gland, placenta, adrenal gland, and kidney, numerous studies have demonstrated elevated expression of PRLR in a substantial subset of human breast cancers across molecular subtypes, including those classified as estrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative, and triple-negative. This overexpression pattern suggests that PRLR may contribute to tumor progression in a subset of breast cancers, although its precise role as a driver versus a passenger marker remains under investigation.

Prior efforts to target PRLR therapeutically have included the development of PRL-based antagonists such as G129R-hPRL and fusion proteins incorporating bacterial toxins like Pseudomonas exotoxin A. However, these approaches have been limited by poor pharmacokinetic profiles, immunogenicity, and systemic toxicity. More recently, monoclonal antibodies such as LFA-102 and 005-C04 have been developed to block PRLR signaling and demonstrated inhibition of downstream signaling pathways in vitro and in preclinical models. Despite these promising findings, clinical evaluation of LFA-102 in a phase I trial revealed that even high doses of up to 60 mg/kg were well tolerated but failed to produce significant antitumor responses, suggesting that inhibition of PRLR signaling alone is insufficient to induce meaningful tumor regression in vivo. This observation underscores a critical limitation of signaling-blocking antibodies: in the absence of direct cytotoxic mechanisms, such agents may be unable to eliminate tumor cells even when target engagement is achieved.

Antibody-drug conjugates represent a powerful modality for delivering highly potent cytotoxic agents directly to tumor cells while minimizing systemic exposure. ADCs combine the target specificity of monoclonal antibodies with the cell-killing capacity of cytotoxic payloads, typically linked via stable or cleavable chemical linkers. The success of ADCs such as ado-trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1), which targets HER2 and delivers the maytansinoid DM1, has validated this approach in breast cancer and spurred the development of over thirty ADCs currently in clinical trials targeting a wide array of antigens. The efficacy of ADCs is critically dependent on several key factors: high target expression on tumor cells, efficient internalization and trafficking to lysosomes, stability of the linker in circulation, and the potency of the payload. While many successful ADCs target antigens expressed at high levels on the cell surface, such as HER2 or CD30, there is growing interest in expanding the utility of ADCs to targets expressed at lower densities, particularly where the target undergoes rapid internalization and recycling.

The prolactin receptor has been shown to undergo constitutive and ligand-induced internalization, followed by lysosomal degradation, a property that distinguishes it from other targets such as HER2, which internalizes slowly despite high surface expression. This rapid turnover suggests that even modest levels of PRLR on the cell surface may be sufficient to mediate efficient uptake and intracellular release of cytotoxic payloads when conjugated to an appropriate antibody. Furthermore, the limited expression of PRLR in normal tissues reduces the potential for on-target, off-tumor toxicity, making it an attractive candidate for ADC development. However, no clinically viable ADC targeting PRLR has been reported to date, and the feasibility of using PRLR as a target for ADC-mediated cytotoxicity in tumors with low-to-moderate expression levels remains unproven.

There is a clear and unmet need in oncology for novel therapeutic agents capable of targeting breast cancers that are refractory to existing endocrine and HER2-directed therapies. The identification of a target that is expressed across multiple molecular subtypes of breast cancer, including triple-negative disease, and that is amenable to efficient internalization even at low expression levels, would represent a significant advance in the field. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel class of fully human anti-PRLR monoclonal antibodies and their conjugates with cytotoxic agents that demonstrate potent and selective antitumor activity in preclinical models of breast cancer, including those with low endogenous PRLR expression. These agents overcome the limitations of signaling-blocking antibodies by directly inducing tumor cell death through payload delivery, and they expand the applicability of ADC technology to a broader spectrum of breast cancers than previously considered viable.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the human prolactin receptor (PRLR) with high affinity and mediate rapid internalization upon binding, as well as antibody-drug conjugates formed by covalently linking these antibodies to cytotoxic payloads. The antibodies of the invention are characterized by their ability to bind PRLR with dissociation constants in the low nanomolar range, to block the interaction between PRLR and its natural ligand prolactin, and to be efficiently internalized by tumor cells expressing even low levels of PRLR. The conjugates of the invention, comprising these antibodies linked to cytotoxic agents such as maytansinoids or auristatins, demonstrate potent and selective cytotoxicity against a broad range of breast cancer cell lines, including those with low endogenous PRLR expression, and induce significant tumor regression in vivo in xenograft models derived from human breast cancer tissues.

The invention further provides a method for treating breast cancer in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an antibody-drug conjugate comprising a human monoclonal antibody that binds to PRLR and a cytotoxic agent linked thereto. The antibody is selected from a group of fully human antibodies that bind to an epitope on PRLR distinct from the prolactin-binding site, and which exhibit a binding affinity for human PRLR of less than 5 nM. The cytotoxic agent is selected from the group consisting of maytansinoids, auristatins, and derivatives thereof, and is linked to the antibody via a noncleavable or cleavable linker. The conjugate is administered in a dose sufficient to induce tumor cell death in PRLR-expressing tumor cells, even when those cells express fewer than 20,000 PRLR molecules per cell.

The invention further provides a method for identifying subjects who are candidates for treatment with the antibody-drug conjugates of the invention, comprising determining the level of PRLR expression in a tumor sample obtained from the subject, wherein the tumor sample is assessed by immunohistochemistry, in situ hybridization, or flow cytometry, and wherein subjects exhibiting PRLR expression above a threshold level relative to normal breast tissue are identified as candidates for treatment. The threshold level is defined as expression greater than that observed in MCF7 breast cancer cells, which serve as a reference for low endogenous PRLR expression.

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising the antibody-drug conjugate of the invention in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the conjugate has a drug-to-antibody ratio of between 2 and 4, and wherein the conjugate is formulated for intravenous administration. The composition is stable under standard storage conditions and retains biological activity for at least twelve months when stored at 2–8°C.

The invention further provides a kit comprising the antibody-drug conjugate of the invention in a vial, along with instructions for use directing the administration of the conjugate to a patient diagnosed with breast cancer exhibiting PRLR expression, and optionally including a companion diagnostic for detecting PRLR expression in tumor tissue.

The invention further provides a method of producing the antibody-drug conjugate of the invention, comprising culturing a host cell expressing a nucleic acid encoding the heavy and light chain variable regions of the anti-PRLR antibody, purifying the antibody, conjugating the purified antibody to a cytotoxic agent via a chemical linker, and purifying the resulting conjugate to remove unconjugated antibody and free payload. The host cell is a Chinese hamster ovary cell, and the conjugation is performed under controlled conditions to achieve a drug-to-antibody ratio of approximately 3.4.

The invention further provides a method of treating breast cancer in a subject who has failed prior therapy with hormonal agents or HER2-targeted therapies, comprising administering to the subject the antibody-drug conjugate of the invention, wherein the subject’s tumor expresses PRLR at levels detectable by immunohistochemistry and greater than the level observed in normal mammary epithelial cells.

The invention further provides a method of enhancing the antitumor activity of the antibody-drug conjugate of the invention in a subject with estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer, comprising co-administering the conjugate with a selective estrogen receptor downregulator, such as fulvestrant, wherein the co-administration results in greater tumor regression than administration of either agent alone.

The invention further provides a method of monitoring therapeutic response to the antibody-drug conjugate of the invention, comprising measuring levels of phospho-histone H3 in tumor tissue obtained from the subject following treatment, wherein an increase in phospho-histone H3-positive cells indicates induction of mitotic arrest and correlates with therapeutic efficacy.

The invention further provides a method of reducing tumor burden in a subject with breast cancer, wherein the tumor expresses PRLR at levels below those typically associated with conventional ADC targets, comprising administering the antibody-drug conjugate of the invention, wherein the conjugate is internalized and degraded in lysosomes, releasing the cytotoxic payload intracellularly and inducing apoptosis in the target cell.
