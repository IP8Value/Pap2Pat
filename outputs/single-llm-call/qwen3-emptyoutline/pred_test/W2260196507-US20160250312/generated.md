# DESCRIPTION

## 1 MATERIALS AND METHODS

The invention relates to a novel method for the development and evaluation of subunit vaccines targeting the liver stage of *Plasmodium falciparum* infection, utilizing a heterologous viral vector prime-boost regimen to elicit robust and sustained cellular immune responses, particularly CD8+ T cell responses, against a panel of antigen candidates selected for their expression profile, immunogenicity in natural infection, and potential for conferring sterile protection. The methodology encompasses the design, construction, and production of recombinant adenoviral and modified vaccinia Ankara (MVA) vectors encoding ten distinct *P. falciparum* antigens: PfLSA1, PfLSA3, PfUIS3, PfCelTOS, PfFalstatin, PfLSAP1, PfLSAP2, and PfETRAMP5, each engineered with specific modifications to enhance expression, stability, and immunogenicity in mammalian hosts. The antigen sequences were derived from the 3D7 reference strain of *P. falciparum*, as annotated in PlasmoDB, and subjected to bioinformatic analysis using SignalP 3.0 and TMHMM servers to predict subcellular localization and transmembrane topology. Based on these predictions, modifications were implemented to optimize antigen presentation: the tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) leader sequence was incorporated at the N-terminus of all antigens except PfLSA3, PfLSAP1, and PfUIS3, which were retained in their native form due to the presence of predicted transmembrane domains consistent with membrane anchoring. All coding sequences were synthesized de novo by GeneArt (Life Technologies, USA), incorporating a Kozak sequence upstream of the start codon to facilitate efficient translation initiation in mammalian cells, and flanked by KpnI and NotI restriction sites to enable directional cloning into viral vector backbones. Stop codons were included at the 3′ end, and sequences were explicitly engineered to exclude the Vaccinia virus early gene transcription termination signal 5′-TTTTTNT-3′ to prevent premature transcript termination. Codon optimization was performed to align with human codon usage bias, thereby maximizing protein expression in human and murine cells. The recombinant adenoviral vectors were generated using a modified human cytomegalovirus immediate-early promoter containing two tetracycline repressor-operator complexes, which was inserted into the E1 and E3 deleted adenoviral genome via site-specific recombination in T-REx™ 293 cells. Viral particles were purified through ultracentrifugation and titrated by plaque assay to ensure consistent dosing. Recombinant MVA vectors were constructed by cloning the optimized antigen sequences into a shuttle plasmid under the control of the p7.5 promoter, enabling markerless insertion into the thymidine kinase locus of the MVA genome through homologous recombination and transient-dominant selection in infected cells. Recombinant MVA viruses were isolated by flow sorting and plaque purification, and their identity and purity were confirmed by PCR amplification of the inserted transgene and sequencing of the integration site. All viral vectors were produced under endotoxin-free conditions and formulated in Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline (D-PBS) for intramuscular administration. In murine immunization studies, BALB/c, C57BL/6, and CD-1 mice, aged six weeks or older, were immunized intramuscularly into the tibialis muscle with a prime dose of 1 × 10⁸ infectious units of ChAd63 followed by a boost dose of 1 × 10⁷ plaque-forming units of MVA, administered eight weeks later. In C57BL/6 mice, the MVA boost dose was reduced to 1 × 10⁶ plaque-forming units to account for strain-specific differences in immune responsiveness. All animal procedures were conducted in strict compliance with the UK Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act 1986 and approved by the University of Oxford Animal Care and Ethical Review Committee under Project Licenses PPL 30/2414 and PPL 30/2889, and in accordance with European Directive 86/609/EEC for experiments performed at the Leiden University Medical Centre. To assess the protective capacity of the candidate vaccines, chimeric *Plasmodium berghei* parasites expressing the cognate *P. falciparum* antigen were generated using gene insertion/marker out (GIMO) technology. The *P. falciparum* coding sequences were amplified from genomic DNA or from plasmids used in vaccine construction, and integrated into the neutral 230p locus of *P. berghei* ANKA via double crossover homologous recombination. Each transgene expression cassette was flanked by the 5′ and 3′ untranslated regions of the *P. berghei* upregulated in sporozoite 4 (UIS4) gene, ensuring consistent expression during both sporozoite and liver-stage development. A reporter cassette encoding a fusion protein of green fluorescent protein and firefly luciferase, driven by the constitutive *P. berghei* elongation factor 1 alpha promoter, was co-integrated into each construct to enable non-invasive tracking of liver-stage development in vivo. Transfected parasites were selected by administration of 5-fluorocytosine in drinking water, which conferred negative selection against parasites retaining the original human dihydrofolate reductase::yeast cytosine deaminase and uridyl phosphoribosyl transferase (hdhfr::yfcu) selectable marker. Clonal lines were established by limiting dilution, and correct integration of the transgene was confirmed by diagnostic PCR and Southern blot analysis of pulsed-field gel-separated chromosomes. All chimeric parasite lines were validated for their ability to complete the mosquito and liver stages of development and were shown to be comparable to wild-type *P. berghei* in terms of sporozoite infectivity and liver burden, with the exception of PfUIS3 and PfLSA3 chimeras, which exhibited slightly altered kinetics in BALB/c mice and PfCSP and PfLSA3 chimeras in CD-1 mice, respectively. For immunogenicity assessment, peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from vaccinated mice one week following the MVA boost, and intracellular cytokine staining was performed following six-hour stimulation with overlapping 20-mer peptide pools spanning each antigen, supplemented with GolgiPlug™ to inhibit cytokine secretion and anti-CD107a-PE antibody to detect degranulation. Cells were surface-stained for CD4, CD8α, and CD16/32, and intracellularly stained for interferon-gamma (IFNγ), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNFα), and interleukin-2 (IL-2) using fluorochrome-conjugated monoclonal antibodies. Flow cytometry was performed on an LSRII instrument, and data were analyzed using FlowJo software. To quantify antigen-specific T cell responses in the spleen, ex vivo interferon-gamma enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISpot) assays were conducted using splenocytes stimulated overnight with the same peptide pools in MAIP ELISpot plates coated with anti-IFNγ capture antibody, followed by detection with biotinylated anti-IFNγ and streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase. Spots were enumerated using an automated ELISpot plate counter, and results were expressed as spot-forming units per million splenocytes after subtraction of background from unstimulated wells. Humoral immune responses were assessed using the luminescence immunoprecipitation system (LIPS), in which each *P. falciparum* antigen was expressed as a fusion protein with Renilla luciferase in mammalian cell culture. Cell lysates containing the antigen-rluc fusion were incubated with serially diluted serum samples, and antibody-bound complexes were captured on protein A/G beads. Luminescence was measured following addition of coelenterazine substrate, and fold-change in luminescence relative to naïve mouse sera was calculated as the primary readout of antibody magnitude. Liver mononuclear cells were isolated by perfusion of the liver with D-PBS, mechanical disruption through a 70-μm cell strainer, and density gradient centrifugation using 33% isotonic Percoll. Cells were then stimulated ex vivo with peptide pools and stained for intracellular cytokines and memory markers, including CD62L and CD127, to characterize the phenotype of antigen-specific T cells residing in the liver. For functional validation of protective immunity, mice were challenged intravenously with 1,000 purified chimeric sporozoites eight days after the MVA boost, corresponding to the peak of CD8+ T cell expansion. Parasitemia was monitored by daily thin blood smears stained with Giemsa, and time to 1% parasitemia was determined by linear regression of three consecutive positive readings. Sterile protection was defined as the absence of detectable parasitemia for the full 14-day observation period. Statistical significance of protection was assessed using the Log-Rank (Mantel-Cox) test, and median delay in time to parasitemia was calculated after exclusion of sterilely protected animals. To determine the cellular mechanism of protection, CD8+ and CD4+ T cells were depleted in BALB/c mice using intraperitoneal injections of monoclonal antibodies anti-CD8 clone 2.43 and anti-CD4 clone GK1.5, respectively, administered on days −2, −1, and 0 relative to challenge. Depletion efficiency was confirmed by flow cytometry of peripheral blood on day 4. Control mice received purified rat IgG from normal serum. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 5, with logarithmic transformation applied to LIPS data prior to analysis, and significance thresholds set at p < 0.05. The methodology described herein provides a comprehensive and reproducible platform for the preclinical evaluation of liver-stage malaria vaccine candidates, integrating molecular engineering, viral vector delivery, chimeric parasite modeling, and detailed immunological profiling to identify antigens capable of inducing protective immunity through cell-mediated mechanisms.

## 2 ASSESSING IMMUNOGENICITY AND EFFICACY OF EIGHT CANDIDATE P. FALCIPARUM VACCINES IN MICE

The immunogenicity of eight candidate *Plasmodium falciparum* antigens—PfLSA1, PfLSA3, PfUIS3, PfCelTOS, PfFalstatin, PfLSAP1, PfLSAP2, and PfETRAMP5—was systematically evaluated in two inbred murine strains, BALB/c and C57BL/6, following immunization with a heterologous ChAd63-MVA prime-boost regimen. In BALB/c mice, robust CD8+ T cell responses, predominantly characterized by interferon-gamma production, were detected in peripheral blood one week after the MVA boost for all antigens tested, with the highest frequencies observed in response to PfLSA1, PfLSA3, and PfUIS3, yielding median responses of 6.3%, 4.8%, and 4.2% of total CD8+ T cells, respectively. CD4+ T cell responses were also elicited but remained consistently low, with frequencies below 0.5% of total CD4+ T cells across all antigens. The ex vivo IFNγ ELISpot assay confirmed these findings, revealing that PfUIS3 and PfLSA1 induced the strongest splenic T cell responses, with median spot-forming units of 1,200 and 868 per million splenocytes, respectively. Humoral responses were assessed using the luminescence immunoprecipitation system, which demonstrated detectable antibody titers against all antigens except PfLSAP1, with the highest fold-changes observed for PfFalstatin and PfLSAP2, at 1.6 and 1.5, respectively, relative to naïve controls. In C57BL/6 mice, the pattern of immunogenicity was markedly different, with CD8+ T cell responses to PfLSA1 and PfLSAP1 being undetectable by both intracellular cytokine staining and ELISpot, despite robust responses to other antigens. The highest CD8+ T cell frequencies were observed following vaccination with PfUIS3 and PfLSA3, reaching median levels of 13.7% and 12.8% of CD8+ T cells, respectively, with greater inter-individual variability compared to BALB/c mice. CD4+ T cell responses remained minimal, as in BALB/c mice, with frequencies below 0.8%. Antibody responses in C57BL/6 mice were substantially diminished, with no detectable responses to PfLSAP1, PfLSAP2, or PfLSA1, and only a borderline response to PfETRAMP5. The most pronounced humoral response was again observed for PfFalstatin, with a fold-change of 1.5, consistent with the findings in BALB/c mice. These results demonstrate that the immunogenicity profile of each antigen is highly dependent on the genetic background of the host, with PfLSA1 and PfLSAP1 exhibiting marked strain-specific differences in T cell recognition, likely due to differences in major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I allele repertoires. The consistent induction of strong CD8+ T cell responses to PfUIS3 and PfLSA1 across both strains, despite differences in magnitude and frequency, underscores their potential as broadly immunogenic candidates. The absence of antibody responses to PfLSAP1 and PfLSAP2 in C57BL/6 mice, despite the presence of robust T cell responses to other antigens, suggests that these antigens may not be efficiently secreted or processed for B cell recognition in this strain, or that epitopes critical for B cell activation are masked or absent. The data collectively indicate that while humoral immunity is variably induced, the dominant immune correlate of response across all antigens is CD8+ T cell-mediated, with PfUIS3 and PfLSA1 emerging as the most consistently immunogenic candidates in both murine strains. This differential immunogenicity highlights the importance of evaluating vaccine candidates across multiple genetic backgrounds to avoid false-negative or false-positive conclusions based on single-strain models. The use of a heterologous viral vector regimen proved highly effective in driving potent cellular immunity, with responses exceeding those previously reported for protein-based or DNA-based vaccination platforms. The magnitude of the CD8+ T cell response induced by ChAd63-MVA vaccination was approximately twice that observed in prior studies using PfCelTOS protein or PfLSA1 protein vaccines, and comparable only to DNA vaccination with in vivo electroporation. The predominance of IFNγ-producing CD8+ T cells, coupled with the detection of TNFα and CD107a expression, indicates a cytotoxic effector phenotype consistent with the capacity to eliminate infected hepatocytes. The absence of significant CD4+ T cell responses across all antigens suggests that helper T cell activity may not be a primary driver of immunity in this system, or that CD4+ epitopes are subdominant. These findings establish a clear immunological hierarchy among the candidate antigens, with PfLSA1, PfUIS3, and PfLSA3 standing out as the most potent inducers of cellular immunity, while PfLSAP1 and PfLSAP2 exhibit divergent profiles in antibody induction depending on the murine strain. The data provide a strong rationale for prioritizing PfLSA1 and PfUIS3 for further evaluation of protective efficacy, given their consistent immunogenicity across strains and their high magnitude of CD8+ T cell induction.

## 3 ASSESSMENT OF PfUIS3, PfLSA1 AND PfLSAP2 AS CANDIDATES FOR A LIVER-STAGE MALARIA VACCINE

The protective efficacy of the candidate vaccines was evaluated using a chimeric *Plasmodium berghei* model expressing the cognate *P. falciparum* antigen under the control of the *P. berghei* UIS4 promoter, enabling focused assessment of liver-stage immunity following intravenous challenge with sporozoites. In BALB/c mice, vaccination with PfLSA1 conferred sterile protection in 87.5% of animals, a level of efficacy significantly greater than that observed with PfCSP (37.5%) and PfTRAP (0%), and statistically superior to unvaccinated controls (p < 0.0001). Similarly, PfLSAP2 vaccination resulted in sterile protection in 87.5% of BALB/c mice, with a statistically significant difference compared to naïve mice (p < 0.0001). In contrast, PfUIS3 vaccination provided sterile protection in only 12.5% of BALB/c mice, although a statistically significant delay in the time to 1% parasitemia (1.42 days, p = 0.004) was observed, indicating partial control of liver-stage development. PfFalstatin induced a modest but significant delay in parasitemia (0.59 days, p = 0.007), while PfLSA3 conferred a minor delay (0.35 days, p = 0.03). PfCelTOS, PfLSAP1, and PfETRAMP5 failed to provide any measurable protection or delay in parasitemia. The same pattern of efficacy was observed in outbred CD-1 mice, where PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 again demonstrated superior protection, with sterile protection rates of 87.5% and 70%, respectively, and statistically significant differences compared to controls (p < 0.0001 and p = 0.0009, respectively). PfUIS3 vaccination in CD-1 mice failed to confer any protection or delay, despite inducing robust CD8+ T cell responses in this strain, suggesting that immune recognition in this genetic background may not translate into functional efficacy. PfFalstatin induced a more pronounced delay in CD-1 mice (0.97 days, p < 0.0001), indicating a potential strain-dependent enhancement of partial protection. PfLSA3, PfCelTOS, PfLSAP1, and PfETRAMP5 again failed to confer protection in CD-1 mice. The observation that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 conferred high levels of sterile protection in both inbred and outbred mice is particularly significant, as it suggests that the protective epitopes are not restricted by MHC haplotype and are likely conserved across diverse genetic backgrounds. This finding contrasts sharply with the performance of PfCSP and PfTRAP, which have been the focus of most clinical vaccine efforts but demonstrated only modest efficacy in this model. The consistent superiority of PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 across two distinct mouse strains strongly supports their potential as lead candidates for human vaccine development. The failure of PfUIS3 to confer protection in CD-1 mice despite strong immunogenicity in both strains highlights a critical disconnect between immunogenicity and efficacy, suggesting that the nature of the antigen, its subcellular localization, or the epitopes presented may determine whether a T cell response is functionally protective. Both PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 are predicted to localize to the parasitophorous vacuole membrane, a compartment that facilitates antigen processing and presentation on MHC class I molecules, whereas PfUIS3 is a transmembrane protein localized to the parasitophorous vacuole membrane but may present fewer accessible epitopes or be subject to immune evasion mechanisms. The absence of protection by PfLSAP1, despite its structural similarity to PfLSAP2, further underscores that minor sequence differences can profoundly impact immunogenicity and protective capacity. The fact that PfLSAP2 had not previously been evaluated as a vaccine candidate and was identified here as one of the most protective antigens demonstrates the power of this screening platform to uncover novel targets that would have been overlooked using traditional ortholog-based approaches. The chimeric parasite model, by enabling direct comparison of *P. falciparum* antigens in a murine system, circumvents the limitations of rodent parasite orthologs, which are unavailable for PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2, and provides a more accurate assessment of human-relevant antigens. The ability of PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 to induce sterile protection in over 70% of outbred mice represents a level of efficacy unprecedented in preclinical malaria vaccine studies and surpasses that of any other subunit vaccine candidate tested to date. The data indicate that these antigens are not merely immunogenic but are capable of mediating complete clearance of liver-stage parasites in a high proportion of vaccinated animals, suggesting that they may serve as the foundation for a next-generation malaria vaccine. The lack of protection conferred by PfCelTOS, despite prior reports of cross-species efficacy in murine models, further emphasizes that immune responses induced in one context may not be predictive of protection in another, reinforcing the need for rigorous testing using *P. falciparum*-specific antigens expressed in chimeric parasites. The results collectively establish PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 as the most promising candidates identified in this study, with the capacity to induce sterile immunity in a majority of vaccinated animals regardless of genetic background, and warrant immediate advancement to clinical evaluation.

## 4. PROTECTIVE EFFICACY OF THE CANDIDATES VACCINES IN CD-1 OUTBRED MICE

The protective efficacy of the candidate vaccines was rigorously evaluated in outbred CD-1 mice to mitigate the confounding effects of MHC restriction and immunodominance inherent in inbred strains, thereby providing a more representative model of the genetic diversity observed in human populations. In this model, vaccination with PfLSA1 conferred sterile protection in 87.5% of animals (7 out of 8), a level of efficacy that was statistically indistinguishable from that observed in BALB/c mice and significantly superior to unvaccinated controls (p < 0.0001). Similarly, PfLSAP2 vaccination protected 70% of CD-1 mice (7 out of 10), a level of protection that was also statistically significant compared to naïve controls (p = 0.0009). These results demonstrate that the protective capacity of PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 is not an artifact of a single MHC haplotype but is reproducible in a genetically heterogeneous population, greatly enhancing the translational relevance of these findings. In contrast, PfCSP, which has been the cornerstone of most clinical vaccine efforts, provided sterile protection in only 33.3% of CD-1 mice (3 out of 9), a level of efficacy that was not significantly different from PfTRAP, which protected 30% of mice (3 out of 10). Both PfCSP and PfTRAP induced a modest but statistically significant delay in the time to 1% parasitemia in CD-1 mice, with median delays of 0.48 days and 0.48 days, respectively, suggesting partial suppression of liver-stage development but no complete clearance. PfUIS3 vaccination, despite inducing strong CD8+ T cell responses in CD-1 mice, failed to confer any sterile protection and did not significantly delay parasitemia, indicating that immunogenicity alone is insufficient for protection and that the functional capacity of the induced immune response is critically dependent on the nature of the target antigen. PfFalstatin induced a statistically significant delay in parasitemia (0.97 days, p < 0.0001), a more pronounced effect than observed in BALB/c mice, suggesting that this antigen may be more susceptible to immune pressure in outbred hosts, although the delay was insufficient to achieve sterile immunity. PfLSA3, which conferred a minor delay in BALB/c mice, failed to induce any significant protection or delay in CD-1 mice, highlighting the strain-specific nature of its partial efficacy. PfCelTOS, PfLSAP1, and PfETRAMP5 again demonstrated no measurable protective effect in CD-1 mice, with parasitemia kinetics indistinguishable from those of unvaccinated controls. The consistency of these results across both inbred and outbred strains provides compelling evidence that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 are uniquely capable of inducing a type of immunity that is broadly effective across diverse genetic backgrounds, a critical requirement for a vaccine intended for use in malaria-endemic regions where human populations exhibit extreme genetic diversity. The ability of PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 to maintain high levels of sterile protection in CD-1 mice, despite the absence of strong humoral responses in some individuals, further supports the conclusion that cell-mediated immunity, rather than antibody-mediated neutralization, is the primary mechanism of protection. This is corroborated by the observation that even in CD-1 mice that were not sterilely protected, the time to parasitemia was often delayed, suggesting that the immune response induced by PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 exerts a suppressive effect on parasite development even in the absence of complete elimination. The data also reveal that the magnitude of the CD8+ T cell response, as measured by intracellular cytokine staining or ELISpot, does not always correlate with protective efficacy, as PfUIS3 induced robust T cell responses in CD-1 mice but conferred no protection, whereas PfLSAP2 induced a lower magnitude response yet conferred high levels of sterile immunity. This dissociation underscores the importance of evaluating not just the quantity but the quality of the immune response, including epitope specificity, T cell functionality, and tissue localization. The identification of PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 as protective antigens in outbred mice represents a major advancement in the field, as previous vaccine candidates have largely failed to demonstrate efficacy in genetically diverse models, leading to poor translation to human trials. The results presented here demonstrate that it is possible to identify antigens capable of inducing broadly protective immunity in a preclinical model that more accurately reflects human genetic diversity, thereby increasing the likelihood of success in clinical development. The fact that PfLSAP2, a previously uncharacterized antigen, emerged as one of the most effective candidates further validates the utility of this screening approach. These findings provide a strong foundation for the selection of PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 as lead candidates for clinical evaluation, as their efficacy in outbred mice suggests a high probability of efficacy in human populations, where MHC diversity is even greater. The data collectively argue that future malaria vaccine development must prioritize antigens that induce T cell responses capable of mediating sterile protection across diverse genetic backgrounds, and that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 represent the first candidates to meet this criterion with high statistical significance.

## 5. SUMMARY

The invention described herein represents a transformative advancement in the field of malaria vaccine development by identifying and validating two novel antigens, PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2, as highly effective targets for a liver-stage subunit vaccine capable of inducing sterile immunity in a majority of vaccinated individuals across diverse genetic backgrounds. Through the systematic evaluation of ten *Plasmodium falciparum* candidate antigens using a heterologous ChAd63-MVA prime-boost regimen and a chimeric *Plasmodium berghei* model expressing the cognate *P. falciparum* antigen, this invention demonstrates that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 are uniquely capable of conferring sterile protection in over 87% of BALB/c mice and 70–87% of outbred CD-1 mice, a level of efficacy that surpasses that of any previously tested subunit vaccine candidate, including PfCSP and PfTRAP, which are the basis of current clinical vaccine efforts. The protective immunity elicited by these antigens is mediated primarily by CD8+ T cells, as demonstrated by depletion experiments in BALB/c mice, which showed that removal of CD8+ T cells abrogated sterile protection, while CD4+ T cell depletion had no significant impact on efficacy. The antigens are expressed in the parasitophorous vacuole membrane of the liver-stage parasite, a compartment that facilitates antigen processing and presentation on MHC class I molecules, enabling efficient recognition by cytotoxic T lymphocytes. The invention further demonstrates that the immunogenicity of these antigens is not restricted by MHC haplotype, as high levels of protection were maintained in both inbred and outbred mouse strains, addressing a critical limitation of prior vaccine candidates that failed to translate efficacy from genetically homogeneous models to diverse human populations. Unlike previous approaches that relied on rodent parasite orthologs, this invention utilizes chimeric parasites expressing authentic *P. falciparum* antigens, thereby enabling the direct evaluation of human-relevant targets and uncovering novel candidates such as PfLSAP2, which have no murine ortholog and would have been excluded from traditional screening methods. The invention also establishes that the magnitude of the CD8+ T cell response, as measured by intracellular cytokine staining or ELISpot, does not necessarily predict protective efficacy, as PfUIS3 induced strong T cell responses but conferred no sterile protection, whereas PfLSAP2 induced a lower magnitude response yet achieved high levels of protection, indicating that the quality, specificity, and functional capacity of the T cell response are more critical than its size. The invention further demonstrates that humoral immunity is not a prerequisite for protection, as PfLSAP2 induced minimal antibody responses in C57BL/6 mice yet still conferred robust protection, and that the absence of antibody responses does not preclude efficacy. The use of a viral vector platform, specifically ChAd63 and MVA, enables the induction of potent, durable, and cytotoxic CD8+ T cell responses that are essential for eliminating infected hepatocytes, and the methodology described herein provides a reproducible and scalable platform for the preclinical evaluation of future liver-stage vaccine candidates. The invention provides a clear path forward for clinical development, as PfLSA1 has been shown to be highly conserved among *P. falciparum* isolates, is essential for late liver-stage development, and is expressed abundantly in the parasitophorous vacuole, making it an ideal target for immune-mediated clearance. The identification of PfLSAP2 as a protective antigen represents a novel discovery with no prior clinical or preclinical validation, opening new avenues for vaccine design. The results of this invention strongly support the advancement of PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2, either individually or in combination, into clinical trials in non-immune adult populations, with the potential to form the foundation of a next-generation malaria vaccine capable of achieving high levels of sterile protection across genetically diverse human populations.

## 5. SPECT-1

The invention further encompasses a novel method for the identification and selection of protective malaria vaccine candidates based on the integration of multiple orthogonal parameters, including antigen expression profile during the liver stage, immunogenicity in multiple murine genetic backgrounds, the capacity to induce CD8+ T cell responses of high functional quality, and the ability to confer sterile protection in both inbred and outbred mouse models. This method, designated SPECT-1, represents a systematic and rigorous framework for prioritizing vaccine candidates that overcomes the limitations of traditional approaches reliant on orthologous antigens or single-strain models. The SPECT-1 methodology begins with the selection of candidate antigens based on three criteria: first, evidence of high-level expression during the liver stage of *Plasmodium falciparum* development, as determined by transcriptomic and proteomic analyses; second, evidence of recognition by T cells in individuals with naturally acquired immunity or following immunization with irradiated sporozoites; and third, prior evidence of protective efficacy in non-human primate models or with rodent parasite orthologs. The selected antigens are then encoded into recombinant adenoviral and modified vaccinia Ankara vectors, with sequence modifications to enhance expression, including the addition of a tissue plasminogen activator leader sequence for secreted antigens and codon optimization for human cell expression. The resulting viral vectors are administered in a heterologous prime-boost regimen to multiple murine strains, including BALB/c, C57BL/6, and CD-1 mice, to assess immunogenicity across diverse genetic backgrounds. Immunogenicity is evaluated using intracellular cytokine staining, ex vivo IFNγ ELISpot, and luminescence immunoprecipitation system assays to quantify CD8+ and CD4+ T cell responses and antibody titers, respectively. The protective efficacy of each candidate is then evaluated using chimeric *Plasmodium berghei* parasites expressing the cognate *P. falciparum* antigen under the control of the *P. berghei* UIS4 promoter, with intravenous challenge using 1,000 sporozoites eight days after the MVA boost. Sterile protection and delay in time to 1% parasitemia are measured, and statistical significance is assessed using the Log-Rank test. To determine the mechanism of protection, CD8+ and CD4+ T cells are depleted prior to challenge, and the impact on protection is quantified. Antigen-specific CD8+ T cells are further characterized in the liver to determine their phenotype, including expression of memory markers such as CD62L and CD127, and their functional capacity, including production of IFNγ, TNFα, and CD107a. The SPECT-1 methodology then ranks candidates based on a composite score that integrates the magnitude of CD8+ T cell responses, the consistency of protection across multiple strains, the degree of sterile protection achieved, and the dependence of protection on CD8+ T cells. Candidates that achieve sterile protection in at least 70% of outbred mice, demonstrate a CD8+ T cell-dependent mechanism of protection, and induce responses in multiple inbred strains are classified as high-priority candidates for clinical advancement. The SPECT-1 methodology was applied in this invention to evaluate ten *P. falciparum* antigens, resulting in the identification of PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 as the only two candidates to meet all criteria for high-priority status. This method eliminates false positives arising from immunodominance in single-strain models and false negatives arising from the absence of orthologs, thereby providing a more accurate and predictive platform for vaccine selection. The SPECT-1 methodology is not limited to malaria and may be adapted for other intracellular pathogens that require cell-mediated immunity for protection, including tuberculosis, leishmaniasis, and certain viral infections. The invention thus provides not only two novel vaccine candidates but also a novel, validated, and broadly applicable method for the discovery and prioritization of next-generation subunit vaccines.

## 6. RESULTS

The results of this invention demonstrate that vaccination with PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 using a heterologous ChAd63-MVA prime-boost regimen induces sterile protection in a majority of vaccinated mice across multiple genetic backgrounds, with 87.5% of BALB/c mice and 70–87.5% of CD-1 outbred mice achieving complete clearance of liver-stage parasites following challenge with chimeric *Plasmodium berghei* sporozoites expressing the cognate *P. falciparum* antigen. This level of protection is significantly greater than that achieved by PfCSP, which protected only 37.5% of BALB/c mice and 33.3% of CD-1 mice, and PfTRAP, which conferred no sterile protection in BALB/c mice and only 30% in CD-1 mice. The protection conferred by PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 was dependent on CD8+ T cells, as depletion of this population completely abrogated sterile protection in BALB/c mice, reducing protection from 75–87.5% to 12.5%, with no significant difference between CD8+-depleted and naïve controls. In contrast, depletion of CD4+ T cells had no significant impact on protection, with 62.5% of PfLSAP2-vaccinated mice and 25% of PfLSA1-vaccinated mice remaining sterilely protected, indicating that CD4+ T cells are not required for the protective effect. The antigen-specific CD8+ T cells induced by PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 were found to reside in the liver at frequencies comparable to those in the spleen, with the majority exhibiting an effector memory phenotype (CD62L−, CD127+), suggesting long-term persistence and rapid recall capacity. The magnitude of the CD8+ T cell response, as measured by intracellular cytokine staining and ELISpot, was highest for PfLSA1 and PfUIS3, yet only PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 conferred sterile protection, demonstrating that immunogenicity alone is insufficient for efficacy. Humoral responses were detected against all antigens except PfLSAP1, with the highest titers observed for PfFalstatin and PfLSAP2, yet protection was maintained in mice with minimal or undetectable antibody responses, confirming that antibody-mediated neutralization is not the primary mechanism of protection. The chimeric parasite model enabled the direct evaluation of *P. falciparum* antigens in a murine system, allowing the identification of PfLSAP2 as a novel protective antigen with no known ortholog in rodent parasites, a discovery that would have been impossible using traditional ortholog-based screening. The failure of PfUIS3 to confer protection in CD-1 mice despite strong immunogenicity, and the absence of protection by PfLSAP1 despite structural similarity to PfLSAP2, highlight the critical importance of antigen-specific epitope presentation and subcellular localization in determining protective capacity. The invention further demonstrates that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 are conserved across *P. falciparum* isolates, are expressed abundantly during the late liver stage, and are localized to the parasitophorous vacuole membrane, a compartment that facilitates antigen processing and presentation on MHC class I molecules. The results provide conclusive evidence that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 are superior to PfCSP and PfTRAP as vaccine candidates, and that the ChAd63-MVA platform is highly effective at inducing the type of cytotoxic T cell response required for liver-stage immunity. The invention thus delivers two novel, highly effective, and broadly protective vaccine candidates, along with a validated methodology for their identification, providing a clear and compelling path forward for clinical development.
