# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel bivalent oral vaccine composition for the prevention of enteric fever caused by *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A. Specifically, the invention encompasses a vaccine formulation comprising outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) derived from both *S.* Typhi and *S.* Paratyphi A, wherein the OMVs are naturally secreted during bacterial growth in exponential phase and retain their native structural integrity, including associated lipopolysaccharides, outer membrane proteins, inner membrane components, periplasmic proteins, and other pathogen-associated molecular patterns. The invention further relates to methods of inducing a broad-spectrum, long-lasting protective immune response in mammals, particularly humans, through oral administration of the bivalent OMV formulation. The vaccine elicits a coordinated humoral and cell-mediated immune response that targets both systemic dissemination and intestinal invasion by typhoidal salmonellae, thereby providing cross-protection against both serovars without the need for exogenous adjuvants. The invention is particularly suited for use in endemic regions where enteric fever remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality, especially in children and young adults, and where current vaccines fail to provide adequate protection against paratyphoid fever due to the absence of the Vi polysaccharide antigen in *S.* Paratyphi A. The vaccine composition is stable, scalable, and amenable to production under controlled, GMP-compliant conditions, making it suitable for global deployment in both resource-limited and high-income settings.

## BACKGROUND AND PRIOR ART OF THE INVENTION

Enteric fever, a systemic infection caused primarily by *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A, continues to pose a substantial global health burden, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where sanitation infrastructure is inadequate and access to clean water remains limited. Each year, an estimated 21.7 million cases of typhoid fever occur worldwide, resulting in approximately 200,000 deaths, while paratyphoid fever, historically considered less prevalent, is now emerging as a significant and growing threat, particularly in South Asia, where incidence rates have risen sharply over the past two decades. The clinical manifestations of both infections are indistinguishable, involving prolonged fever, gastrointestinal distress, hepatosplenomegaly, and, in severe cases, intestinal perforation and septicemia. The increasing prevalence of multidrug-resistant strains has further complicated treatment regimens, rendering many first-line antibiotics ineffective and heightening the urgency for effective preventive strategies.

To date, three vaccines have received global licensure for the prevention of typhoid fever: the live attenuated oral vaccine Ty21a, the purified Vi polysaccharide vaccine administered parenterally, and a newer Vi-tetanus toxoid conjugate vaccine. While Ty21a offers partial cross-protection against *S.* Paratyphi A due to shared genetic and antigenic features, its efficacy is inconsistent and wanes over time, requiring multiple doses and strict cold-chain maintenance. The Vi polysaccharide vaccine, though highly effective against *S.* Typhi, provides no meaningful protection against *S.* Paratyphi A because the latter lacks the Vi capsular polysaccharide entirely. The Vi-conjugate vaccine, while superior in inducing T-cell-dependent immune memory and longer duration of protection, remains ineffective against paratyphoid fever for the same structural reason. Consequently, there is currently no licensed vaccine capable of conferring simultaneous, robust, and durable protection against both major causative agents of enteric fever.

Alternative approaches to vaccine development have focused on subunit antigens, including outer membrane proteins (OMPs), flagellin, and other virulence factors. However, these strategies have been hampered by the immunological redundancy and antigenic variability among typhoidal strains, as well as the inability of individual antigens to induce a comprehensive immune response that targets both mucosal entry and systemic dissemination. Moreover, most subunit vaccines require the addition of potent adjuvants to achieve sufficient immunogenicity, which introduces regulatory, safety, and cost-related challenges, particularly in pediatric populations and resource-constrained environments.

Outer membrane vesicles (OMVs), naturally released by Gram-negative bacteria during growth, have emerged as promising next-generation vaccine platforms due to their intrinsic adjuvant properties and structural fidelity to the parent organism. OMVs contain a complex mixture of outer membrane proteins, lipopolysaccharides, phospholipids, periplasmic proteins, and nucleic acids, all of which serve as pathogen-associated molecular patterns capable of activating innate immune receptors such as Toll-like receptors 2, 4, and 9. The only licensed OMV-based vaccine to date is MeNZB, a meningococcal serogroup B vaccine that demonstrated over 80% efficacy in a large-scale outbreak setting in New Zealand and Norway. This success validated the principle that native OMVs can elicit protective immunity without exogenous adjuvants, primarily through the induction of opsonizing antibodies and T-cell-mediated responses.

Despite these advances, no OMV-based vaccine has been developed or tested for typhoidal salmonellae. Previous attempts to use whole-cell killed or lysed *Salmonella* preparations have yielded inconsistent results, often due to the loss of critical surface antigens during inactivation, the absence of native membrane architecture, and the failure to replicate the immunomodulatory properties conferred by the vesicular structure. Furthermore, prior studies have focused exclusively on *S.* Typhi, neglecting the rising threat posed by *S.* Paratyphi A. Attempts to combine multiple antigens into multivalent formulations have been limited by poor antigen stability, immunological interference, and the lack of a delivery system capable of simultaneously stimulating mucosal and systemic immunity.

The present invention overcomes these longstanding limitations by exploiting the natural biology of *S.* Typhi and *S.* Paratyphi A to produce a bivalent OMV vaccine that preserves the full complement of immunogenic components in their native conformation. Unlike previous approaches, the invention does not rely on chemical or thermal inactivation, recombinant expression, or conjugation chemistry. Instead, it leverages the inherent capacity of these pathogens to secrete OMVs during exponential growth, resulting in vesicles that contain not only surface-exposed antigens but also the critical lipid A and core oligosaccharide structures of lipopolysaccharide, which function as built-in adjuvants. The formulation is administered orally, enabling direct stimulation of gut-associated lymphoid tissue, which is the primary site of pathogen entry and initial immune encounter. This approach uniquely bridges mucosal and systemic immunity, providing a comprehensive defense that prevents both intestinal colonization and systemic spread.

The novelty of this invention lies not merely in the selection of OMVs as a vaccine platform, but in the precise combination of two distinct serovars’ OMVs in a 1:1 ratio, the method of their isolation and purification, and the demonstration that this bivalent formulation induces a synergistic immune response that is qualitatively and quantitatively superior to either monovalent component or traditional whole-cell vaccines. The invention further establishes, for the first time, that oral delivery of these OMVs elicits a Th1/Th17-polarized T-cell response, enhances antigen-specific IgG and secretory IgA production, promotes bacterial agglutination, inhibits motility and mucin penetration, and facilitates opsonophagocytosis—all critical mechanisms required for complete protection against enteric fever. No prior art discloses or suggests the use of bivalent OMVs from *S.* Typhi and *S.* Paratyphi A as a single, orally administered vaccine capable of conferring broad-spectrum, durable, and adjuvant-free protection against both pathogens simultaneously.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

The primary objective of the present invention is to provide a novel, safe, and highly effective bivalent oral vaccine for the prevention of enteric fever caused by *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A. This vaccine is designed to overcome the limitations of existing vaccines by inducing a comprehensive and long-lasting immune response that targets both systemic and mucosal phases of infection without the need for exogenous adjuvants. A key objective is to achieve cross-protection against both serovars through a single formulation, thereby addressing the growing epidemiological threat posed by paratyphoid fever, which remains unaddressed by all currently licensed vaccines.

Another critical objective is to develop a vaccine that stimulates both humoral and cell-mediated arms of the adaptive immune system in a coordinated and synergistic manner. This includes the induction of high-titer, antigen-specific serum immunoglobulins—particularly IgG subclasses IgG1, IgG2a, and IgG3—as well as secretory IgA in the intestinal lumen, which serves as the first line of defense at the site of pathogen entry. Additionally, the vaccine is intended to activate T-helper 1 and T-helper 17 cell responses, as evidenced by the upregulation of key cytokines such as interferon-gamma, interleukin-17, and interleukin-6, which are essential for macrophage activation, neutrophil recruitment, and maintenance of the intestinal epithelial barrier.

A further objective is to ensure that the vaccine induces immune memory capable of sustaining protective antibody titers and T-cell populations for at least 90 days following a three-dose oral regimen, thereby reducing the need for frequent boosters and enhancing compliance in endemic populations. The invention also aims to demonstrate that the protective efficacy of the vaccine is not dependent on a single antigenic component but is instead the result of a polyclonal response targeting multiple conserved outer membrane and periplasmic proteins, lipopolysaccharide structures, and Vi polysaccharide when present.

Another objective is to establish that the vaccine’s mechanism of protection involves multiple functional immune effector mechanisms, including bacterial agglutination, inhibition of motility, blockade of mucin penetration, and enhancement of opsonophagocytosis by macrophages. These mechanisms collectively prevent the initial colonization of the intestinal epithelium and limit systemic dissemination following breach of the mucosal barrier.

The invention further seeks to provide a scalable, cost-effective manufacturing process that relies on natural bacterial secretion of OMVs during standard culture conditions, avoiding complex purification steps, chemical conjugation, or genetic modification. The resulting vaccine is stable under refrigerated conditions, does not require lyophilization, and can be formulated in an aqueous buffer compatible with oral delivery systems such as capsules or liquid suspensions.

An additional objective is to validate the vaccine’s safety profile in preclinical models, demonstrating the absence of systemic inflammation, endotoxic shock, or other adverse effects associated with high-dose lipopolysaccharide exposure, by virtue of the natural deacylation and structural modification of LPS within OMVs. The invention also aims to show that the immune response elicited is not only protective but also broadly cross-reactive, capable of recognizing heterologous strains of both serovars, thereby enhancing its utility in diverse geographic settings where antigenic variation may occur.

Finally, the invention seeks to establish a new paradigm in typhoid vaccine development by proving that a naturally derived, non-recombinant, non-conjugated, orally administered OMV formulation can outperform existing licensed vaccines in terms of breadth of protection, durability of response, and mechanistic comprehensiveness. This approach represents a paradigm shift from antigen-specific targeting to pathogen-structure-based immunity, leveraging the evolutionary sophistication of the bacterium’s own secreted vesicles to train the host immune system for optimal defense.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Bacterial Strains and Culture Conditions

The bivalent outer membrane vesicle vaccine of the present invention is produced using two distinct clinical isolates of *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A, each selected for their virulence, genetic stability, and representative antigenic profile. The *S.* Typhi strain designated C-6953 and the *S.* Paratyphi A strain designated C-6915 are maintained in a glycerol stock at −80°C in brain heart infusion broth to preserve viability and phenotypic consistency. Prior to OMV production, each strain is revived by subculturing in tryptic soy broth under aerobic, shaking conditions at 37°C until the culture reaches mid-exponential phase, as determined by optical density measurements at 600 nm. This growth phase is critical, as it corresponds to the period of maximal OMV secretion, during which the bacterial outer membrane undergoes dynamic remodeling and vesiculation occurs at a significantly elevated rate compared to stationary phase. The culture conditions are maintained strictly to avoid stress-induced artifacts, including the use of sterile, endotoxin-free media, controlled agitation at 100 rpm to ensure uniform oxygenation, and avoidance of antibiotic selection pressure that could alter membrane composition. The cultures are monitored for purity by Gram staining and confirmed for serovar identity through PCR amplification of genus-specific and serovar-specific genes, including *prtA*, *fliC-j* for *S.* Typhi, and *fliC-a* for *S.* Paratyphi A, ensuring that the starting material is genetically authentic and free from contamination. The use of clinical isolates, rather than laboratory-adapted strains, ensures that the OMVs produced reflect the true antigenic repertoire encountered during natural human infection, thereby maximizing the vaccine’s relevance and protective potential.

### Preparation of OMVs

Outer membrane vesicles are isolated from the culture supernatants of exponentially growing *S.* Typhi C-6953 and *S.* Paratyphi A C-6915 using a standardized, scalable protocol that preserves the structural and immunological integrity of the vesicles. Following growth to mid-exponential phase, the bacterial cultures are centrifuged at 8,000 × g for 40 minutes at 4°C to remove intact cells and large debris. The resulting supernatant is then passed through a 0.22 μm pore-size membrane filter to eliminate any remaining bacterial cells or large aggregates, ensuring that only vesicles and soluble components proceed to the next stage. The filtered supernatant is subjected to ultracentrifugation at 140,000 × g for four hours at 4°C using a Sorvall T-865 rotor, which pellets the OMVs while leaving soluble proteins and nucleic acids in the supernatant. The resulting OMV pellet is gently resuspended in sterile phosphate-buffered saline (pH 7.4) and subjected to a second ultracentrifugation step to remove residual contaminants. The final OMV suspension is quantified for protein content using the modified Lowry assay, with each batch consistently yielding between 400 and 440 μg of protein per milliliter. The lipopolysaccharide content, specifically the O-antigen component, is determined using the phenol-sulfuric acid method described by Dubois et al., with each batch containing approximately 250 μg of O-antigen per milliliter. The OMVs are then aliquoted, sterile-filtered, and stored at 4°C until use. The absence of endotoxin contamination from non-vesicular sources is confirmed by Limulus amebocyte lysate assay, and the vesicles are verified for structural integrity by transmission electron microscopy. This method ensures that the OMVs retain their native lipid bilayer, associated proteins, and lipopolysaccharide structures, which are essential for their immunogenicity and adjuvant activity. The 1:1 mixture of *S.* Typhi and *S.* Paratyphi A OMVs is prepared immediately prior to administration to ensure homogeneity and optimal antigenic presentation.

### Negative Staining of OMVs and OMV-Secreting Bacteria

To confirm the structural characteristics of the isolated vesicles and their mode of release from the bacterial surface, negative staining followed by transmission electron microscopy is performed. A 5 μl aliquot of the OMV suspension is applied to a carbon-coated copper grid and allowed to adsorb for one minute. The grid is then washed with two drops of Tris-HCl buffer to remove unbound material and subsequently stained with a 2% aqueous solution of uranyl acetate for 30 seconds. Excess stain is blotted away, and the grid is air-dried before imaging under a Tecnai 12 BioTwin transmission electron microscope operating at 80 kV. The resulting images reveal spherical, bilayered vesicles with diameters ranging from 130 to 300 nanometers, consistent with the size profile of naturally secreted OMVs. The vesicles exhibit a dense, electron-opaque outer membrane and a lighter, less dense lumen, indicating the presence of periplasmic contents. In parallel, log-phase bacterial cells are similarly prepared and imaged, revealing the presence of vesicles budding directly from the outer membrane surface, often in clusters, confirming that the isolated vesicles are not artifacts of purification but are genuinely secreted during active bacterial growth. The size distribution of *S.* Typhi OMVs is consistently larger than that of *S.* Paratyphi A OMVs, reflecting differences in membrane composition and vesiculation dynamics between the two serovars. These observations validate the authenticity of the OMV preparation and confirm that the vaccine contains the full spectrum of outer membrane-derived structures that are naturally presented to the immune system during infection.

### Animals

The immunogenicity and protective efficacy of the bivalent OMV vaccine are evaluated in female BALB/c mice, a well-established model for studying typhoid fever and mucosal immunity. Seven-week-old mice are obtained from a certified animal resource facility and housed under specific pathogen-free conditions at a constant temperature of 25°C and relative humidity of 75%. Mice are maintained on autoclaved chow and sterile water ad libitum, and all experimental procedures are conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals, Ministry of Environment and Forests, Government of India. The experimental protocol has been reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Ethical Committee under project approval number PRO/117/June, 2015–June, 2018. Prior to each immunization, mice are fasted for three to four hours to enhance the uptake of the oral vaccine by reducing gastric motility and increasing the residence time of the formulation in the small intestine. This fasting protocol is maintained consistently across all immunization and challenge time points to ensure reproducibility. Mice are divided into experimental groups of ten animals each, with appropriate controls including non-immunized mice, mice immunized with monovalent OMVs, and mice receiving phosphate-buffered saline as a vehicle control. All handling, immunization, and sampling procedures are performed under aseptic conditions using sterile equipment to prevent confounding infections. The use of female mice is preferred due to their greater consistency in immune response compared to males, and all animals are monitored daily for signs of distress, weight loss, or morbidity throughout the study period.

### Collection of Serum and Stool

To assess the systemic and mucosal immune responses elicited by the bivalent OMV vaccine, serum and fecal samples are collected at multiple time points following immunization. Blood is drawn from the lateral tail vein on days 0, 14, 21, 28, 35, 78, and 90 after the first immunization, using BD Microtainer tubes. The collected blood is allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 minutes before centrifugation at 1,000 × g for 10 minutes at 4°C to separate serum, which is then aliquoted and stored at −20°C until analysis. Stool samples are collected aseptically from individual mice at the same time points, immediately placed in sterile tubes containing protease inhibitor (MP Biomedicals, USA), and homogenized in phosphate-buffered saline at a ratio of 100 mg of stool per milliliter of buffer. The homogenates are centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 minutes to remove particulate matter, and the supernatants are collected, aliquoted, and stored at −20°C. These samples are used for the quantification of antigen-specific immunoglobulins, including IgA and secretory IgA, which are critical indicators of mucosal immunity. The use of serial sampling allows for the longitudinal tracking of antibody kinetics, revealing that systemic IgG titers peak between days 28 and 35, while mucosal IgA levels remain elevated through day 90, indicating the induction of durable immune memory. The collection protocol ensures that samples are processed uniformly and that degradation of immunoglobulins is minimized through the use of protease inhibitors and rapid freezing.

### SDS-PAGE and Immunoblot

The protein composition of the OMVs, as well as their immunogenic components, is analyzed by sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and subsequent immunoblotting. OMVs are treated with proteinase K and sodium dodecyl sulfate at 60°C for two hours to isolate OMV-associated lipopolysaccharide, while whole OMV preparations are boiled in SDS-PAGE loading buffer to denature proteins. A total of 80 μg of each sample is loaded onto a 12% polyacrylamide gel and electrophoresed at 100 volts using an ATTO Corporation AE-6530 apparatus. Gels are stained with Coomassie Brilliant Blue or silver stain to visualize protein profiles, revealing distinct banding patterns between *S.* Typhi and *S.* Paratyphi A OMVs, with protein bands ranging from 20 to 90 kDa. Immunoblot analysis is performed by transferring the separated proteins onto nitrocellulose membranes using a semi-dry blotting apparatus. The membranes are blocked with bovine serum albumin and incubated overnight with serum from mice immunized with the bivalent OMV formulation. Bound antibodies are detected using horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG and visualized using chemiluminescent substrates. The immunoblots reveal multiple immunodominant protein bands in both OMV preparations, with strong reactivity observed in the 20–40 kDa and 40–62 kDa ranges, indicating the presence of conserved outer membrane and periplasmic antigens. Similar reactivity is observed when blots are probed with convalescent sera from mice previously infected with wild-type *S.* Typhi or *S.* Paratyphi A, confirming that the OMVs contain antigens recognized during natural infection. These data demonstrate that the OMVs are not merely structural artifacts but contain a rich repertoire of immunogenic proteins that are relevant to protective immunity.

### Dot Blot Assay

To evaluate the immunogenicity of lipopolysaccharide components within the OMVs, a dot blot assay is performed using purified LPS from both *S.* Typhi and *S.* Paratyphi A. Serial dilutions of LPS are spotted directly onto nitrocellulose membranes and allowed to dry. The membranes are then blocked and incubated with serum from bivalent OMV-immunized mice, followed by horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody. Chemiluminescent detection reveals strong reactivity even at low LPS concentrations, indicating that the immune response generated by the OMV vaccine includes antibodies capable of recognizing both native and OMV-associated forms of lipopolysaccharide. Importantly, this reactivity is observed despite structural differences between the LPS in OMVs and that extracted from whole bacterial cells, suggesting that the immune system recognizes conserved epitopes within the core oligosaccharide and lipid A regions. The dot blot assay further confirms that the OMVs are not merely carriers of protein antigens but are also potent sources of immunogenic carbohydrate structures, which are critical for the induction of protective immunity against typhoidal salmonellae.

### ELISA

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays are employed to quantify the magnitude and isotype profile of the antibody response induced by the bivalent OMV vaccine. Microtiter plates are coated with 5 μg per well of purified *S.* Typhi or *S.* Paratyphi A OMVs and incubated overnight at 4°C. After blocking with bovine serum albumin, serial dilutions of serum and stool supernatants are added and incubated for two hours. Bound immunoglobulins are detected using horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG, IgG1, IgG2a, IgG3, IgM, and IgA, followed by colorimetric development with o-phenylenediamine dihydrochloride and termination with sulfuric acid. Absorbance is measured at 492 nm. The results demonstrate a robust, dose-dependent increase in all immunoglobulin isotypes following immunization, with peak titers observed between days 28 and 35. Notably, the IgG3 response is significantly higher than IgG1, indicating a Th1-biased immune polarization. Secretory IgA levels in stool supernatants mirror the serum IgG kinetics, confirming the induction of mucosal immunity. Furthermore, ELISA against Vi polysaccharide demonstrates the presence of anti-Vi antibodies in immunized mice, despite the absence of Vi in *S.* Paratyphi A, suggesting cross-reactivity or shared epitopes between the two serovars. The assay is repeated three times per sample, with intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation below 10%, ensuring high reproducibility and reliability.

### Ex Vivo Studies on Isolated Dendritic Cells

To investigate the cellular immune mechanisms underlying the vaccine’s efficacy, bone marrow-derived dendritic cells are generated from naïve BALB/c mice and matured in the presence of granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor. These dendritic cells are then stimulated with 10 μg/ml of the bivalent OMV formulation for 24 hours, after which supernatants are collected and analyzed for cytokine production using commercial ELISA kits. The results show significant upregulation of interleukin-6, interleukin-12p70, and interleukin-23, indicating that the OMVs are capable of activating dendritic cells and promoting a Th17-polarizing environment. In parallel, splenic CD4+ T cells from immunized mice are co-cultured with these dendritic cells at a 4:1 ratio in the presence of OMVs, resulting in the production of interferon-gamma, interleukin-17, and interleukin-6, confirming the induction of functional T-cell responses. These ex vivo findings demonstrate that the bivalent OMV vaccine not only induces antibody production but also activates the antigen-presenting machinery of the immune system, leading to the priming of effector T cells that are critical for long-term protection against intracellular pathogens such as *Salmonella*. The data collectively establish that the vaccine engages both innate and adaptive immunity in a synergistic and physiologically relevant manner.

## The Non-Limiting Advantages are Given Below:

The bivalent outer membrane vesicle vaccine of the present invention offers a multitude of non-limiting advantages that distinguish it from all previously known typhoid vaccine candidates. First and foremost, it provides simultaneous, broad-spectrum protection against both *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A, a feat unattainable by any currently licensed vaccine due to the absence of the Vi polysaccharide in *S.* Paratyphi A. This dual coverage eliminates the need for separate vaccination regimens and ensures that public health interventions can be streamlined in regions where both pathogens circulate. Second, the vaccine is entirely self-adjuvanting, as the outer membrane vesicles inherently contain lipopolysaccharide and other pathogen-associated molecular patterns that activate innate immune receptors without the addition of exogenous adjuvants, thereby reducing the risk of reactogenicity and simplifying regulatory approval pathways. Third, the vaccine is administered orally, enabling direct stimulation of gut-associated lymphoid tissue, which is the primary site of pathogen entry, thereby inducing both mucosal and systemic immunity in a single dose regimen. This route of administration enhances compliance, eliminates the need for needles, and is particularly advantageous in pediatric and resource-limited settings. Fourth, the vaccine elicits a balanced Th1/Th17 immune response, characterized by elevated levels of interferon-gamma and interleukin-17, which are essential for macrophage activation, neutrophil recruitment, and maintenance of the intestinal barrier—mechanisms critical for preventing both systemic dissemination and intestinal colonization. Fifth, the vaccine induces high-titer, long-lasting antibody responses, including IgG subclasses IgG1, IgG2a, and IgG3, as well as secretory IgA, which persist for at least 90 days after the final immunization, suggesting durable immune memory. Sixth, the vaccine’s protective efficacy is multifactorial, involving not only opsonization and complement-mediated killing but also the inhibition of bacterial motility and mucin penetration, which directly block the initial stages of intestinal invasion. Seventh, the production process is simple, scalable, and cost-effective, relying on natural bacterial secretion during standard culture conditions without the need for genetic modification, chemical conjugation, or complex purification, making it suitable for large-scale manufacturing in low-income countries. Eighth, the vaccine is stable under refrigerated conditions and does not require lyophilization, reducing cold-chain burdens and enhancing accessibility. Ninth, the use of clinical isolates ensures that the antigenic profile of the vaccine reflects the true diversity of circulating strains, enhancing its real-world relevance and cross-reactivity. Tenth, the vaccine’s safety profile is exceptional, as the naturally deacylated lipopolysaccharide within the vesicles exhibits reduced endotoxicity compared to free LPS, minimizing the risk of inflammatory adverse events. Collectively, these advantages position the bivalent OMV vaccine as a transformative solution to the global challenge of enteric fever, offering a single-dose, broadly protective, orally administered, and adjuvant-free vaccine that addresses the critical gaps left by existing technologies.
