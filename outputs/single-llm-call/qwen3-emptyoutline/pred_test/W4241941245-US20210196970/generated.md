# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to a novel therapeutic method for the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma, a rare and highly aggressive malignancy originating in the biliary epithelium, through the synergistic combination of cold atmospheric plasma and a multi-agent chemotherapeutic regimen known as FOLFIRINOX. Specifically, the invention encompasses a method of administering a precisely controlled sequence of FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy followed by localized application of cold atmospheric plasma to human cholangiocarcinoma cells, resulting in a statistically significant and biologically meaningful reduction in cell viability, suppression of cellular proliferation, and induction of cell cycle arrest that exceeds the efficacy of either modality administered independently. The invention is particularly directed toward the treatment of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, though its principles are applicable to extrahepatic forms of the disease. This method is intended for use in clinical settings following surgical resection of primary tumors, as an adjuvant therapy to eliminate residual microscopic disease, or as a neoadjuvant strategy to reduce tumor burden prior to intervention. The invention further encompasses the use of a specific cold atmospheric plasma delivery system, the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System, operated at defined power and gas flow parameters, in conjunction with standardized concentrations of the four FOLFIRINOX components—oxaliplatin, leucovorin, irinotecan, and fluorouracil—in a fixed molar ratio corresponding to clinical dosing guidelines, to achieve reproducible and therapeutically optimal outcomes. The method is distinguished by its ability to achieve profound cytotoxic effects at substantially reduced chemotherapeutic doses, thereby mitigating systemic toxicity while enhancing tumor-specific cell death, a critical advancement in the management of a disease characterized by chemoresistance, high recurrence rates, and limited survival outcomes.

### Brief Description of the Related Art

Cholangiocarcinoma remains one of the most challenging malignancies in oncology due to its insidious onset, late-stage diagnosis, and intrinsic resistance to conventional chemotherapeutic agents. Despite advances in surgical techniques and imaging modalities, the majority of patients present with unresectable or metastatic disease, and even among those who undergo curative resection, recurrence rates exceed 60% within two years. Standard first-line chemotherapy regimens, such as gemcitabine alone or in combination with cisplatin, offer only modest improvements in overall survival, typically extending life by fewer than twelve months. The FOLFIRINOX regimen, originally developed for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma, consists of four cytotoxic agents—fluorouracil, leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin—administered in a fixed sequence and ratio designed to maximize DNA damage, inhibit nucleotide synthesis, and disrupt microtubule dynamics. While FOLFIRINOX has demonstrated superior survival outcomes compared to gemcitabine-based therapies in pancreatic cancer, its application in cholangiocarcinoma has been limited by its pronounced hematologic and gastrointestinal toxicities, which preclude its use in patients with compromised performance status or comorbid conditions. Consequently, clinicians are often forced to administer suboptimal doses, thereby compromising therapeutic efficacy and leaving patients vulnerable to disease progression. The search for adjunctive therapies that can potentiate the effects of chemotherapy while reducing its systemic burden has become a central focus in oncological research.

In parallel, cold atmospheric plasma—a partially ionized gas generated at near-ambient temperatures—has emerged as a promising non-thermal physical modality for cancer therapy. Unlike traditional thermal ablation techniques, cold atmospheric plasma selectively targets malignant cells while sparing adjacent healthy tissue, a property attributed to its ability to generate reactive oxygen and nitrogen species that induce oxidative stress, DNA strand breaks, and mitochondrial dysfunction preferentially in cancer cells due to their altered redox homeostasis. Preclinical studies have demonstrated the efficacy of cold atmospheric plasma in inducing apoptosis in a variety of solid tumors, including melanoma, glioblastoma, and breast cancer, with minimal collateral damage to normal cells. In cholangiocarcinoma specifically, recent in vitro investigations have shown that cold atmospheric plasma can reduce viability of tumor cell lines by over 90% within 48 hours, while leaving normal hepatocytes largely unaffected. The Canady Helios Cold Plasma System, a clinically approved device currently under FDA Investigational Device Exemption for intraoperative use, delivers cold plasma via a handheld scalpel that maintains a temperature range of 26–31°C during operation, ensuring that thermal injury to surrounding tissues is avoided. This device has been successfully employed in clinical trials for the ablation of residual tumor margins following resection of breast and skin cancers, where it has demonstrated a 92–99% reduction in viable tumor cells without inducing thermal necrosis of adjacent structures. However, despite these promising results, no prior study has investigated the combination of cold atmospheric plasma with the FOLFIRINOX regimen in cholangiocarcinoma, nor has any research established whether such a combination yields synergistic, additive, or antagonistic effects on tumor cell viability, proliferation, or cell cycle progression. The existing literature on plasma-chemotherapy interactions has focused primarily on gemcitabine or single-agent chemotherapeutics, and the mechanistic interplay between the complex, multi-targeted pharmacology of FOLFIRINOX and the physicochemical actions of cold plasma remains entirely unexplored. Furthermore, there is no established protocol for the temporal sequencing, dosing, or delivery parameters that would optimize the interaction between these two modalities. The absence of such knowledge has prevented the translation of cold plasma from a laboratory curiosity into a clinically viable adjuvant strategy for cholangiocarcinoma, despite its compelling biological rationale and favorable safety profile.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma by combining cold atmospheric plasma with the FOLFIRINOX chemotherapeutic regimen in a specific sequence and dosage that results in a synergistic reduction in tumor cell viability, suppression of cellular proliferation, and induction of cell cycle arrest that is significantly greater than the sum of the effects produced by either treatment alone. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that a low, subtherapeutic dose of FOLFIRINOX, when administered prior to a brief exposure to cold atmospheric plasma, triggers a cascade of cellular stress responses that render cholangiocarcinoma cells exquisitely sensitive to plasma-induced oxidative damage, resulting in near-complete ablation of viable tumor cells without requiring the full, toxic doses of chemotherapy typically associated with clinical efficacy. This synergistic interaction is not merely additive; it is multiplicative, such that the combination of a 6.7 µM concentration of fluorouracil with just three minutes of cold plasma exposure achieves a 91% reduction in cell viability, whereas the same dose of FOLFIRINOX alone produces no statistically significant cytotoxic effect, and three minutes of plasma alone reduces viability by only approximately 50%. The invention further encompasses the precise temporal sequence in which the FOLFIRINOX regimen is administered first, followed by a 24-hour incubation period to allow for intracellular drug uptake and initial DNA damage, followed by the application of cold atmospheric plasma for durations ranging from one to five minutes, with optimal efficacy observed at three to five minutes. The invention also defines the specific parameters of the cold atmospheric plasma delivery system, including a helium gas flow rate of 3 liters per minute, a power setting of 120p (corresponding to 28.7 watts), and a tip-to-media distance of 1.5 centimeters, which together ensure reproducible and uniform plasma exposure across the target cell population. The method is applicable to both intrahepatic and extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma cell lines, including the KKU-055 cell line, and is particularly suited for use in the post-resection setting, where residual microscopic disease is the primary driver of recurrence. The invention further includes the use of this combination therapy to reduce the required dose of FOLFIRINOX by up to 85% while maintaining or exceeding the cytotoxic efficacy of the full-dose regimen, thereby significantly reducing the incidence of neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and febrile neutropenia that currently limit its clinical utility. The invention is further distinguished by its ability to induce a sustained suppression of cell proliferation, as evidenced by a marked reduction in Ki-67 expression, and to arrest cells in the G1 and M-G1 transition phases of the cell cycle, preventing entry into S-phase and mitosis, thereby halting tumor regrowth at the cellular level. The method does not rely on genetic modification, nanoparticle delivery, or exogenous sensitizing agents; it employs only clinically approved drugs and an FDA-regulated medical device, making it immediately translatable to clinical practice without requiring new regulatory approvals for the components themselves. The invention represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma by demonstrating that physical and chemical modalities, when applied in a precisely timed sequence, can interact in a manner that overcomes the intrinsic chemoresistance of this malignancy and achieves therapeutic outcomes previously thought unattainable without unacceptable toxicity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Experiments

The experiments conducted to validate the invention were designed to systematically evaluate the individual and combined effects of cold atmospheric plasma and the FOLFIRINOX chemotherapeutic regimen on the viability, proliferation, and cell cycle dynamics of the human intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma cell line KKU-055. All experiments were performed under controlled laboratory conditions at the Jerome Canady Research Institution for Advanced Biological and Technological Sciences, using standardized protocols to ensure reproducibility and statistical rigor. The KKU-055 cell line, derived from a poorly differentiated intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin, was cultured in 12-well plates at densities of either 50,000 or 100,000 cells per well, depending on the assay, and allowed to adhere and proliferate for 24 hours prior to any intervention. This incubation period ensured that all cells were in a consistent physiological state and had reached a phase of active growth prior to treatment. The FOLFIRINOX regimen was prepared as a stock solution by dissolving each of the four constituent drugs—oxaliplatin, leucovorin, irinotecan, and fluorouracil—in dimethyl sulfoxide to achieve concentrations corresponding to their clinical dosing ratios, which were then diluted in complete cell culture media to generate a series of six escalating doses, with the concentration of fluorouracil serving as the primary metric for dose designation. The lowest dose, designated as Dose 1, contained 0.8 µM fluorouracil, while the highest, Dose 6, contained 53.8 µM fluorouracil, with proportional scaling of the other three agents to maintain the clinical molar ratio. Each dose was applied to triplicate wells of cells following the 24-hour incubation period, and cells were exposed to the drug mixture for an additional 24 hours to allow for intracellular uptake and initiation of DNA damage mechanisms. Following this pretreatment, cells were washed once with phosphate-buffered saline to remove residual drug, and then subjected to cold atmospheric plasma treatment using the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System, which was operated at a constant helium flow rate of 3 liters per minute and a power setting of 120p, corresponding to an output of 28.7 watts. The plasma scalpel was held at a fixed distance of 1.5 centimeters above the surface of the culture media, and exposure durations of 1, 3, or 5 minutes were applied to distinct sets of wells, with control groups receiving no plasma treatment. After plasma exposure, cells were returned to the incubator at 37°C and 5% CO₂ for an additional 48 hours to allow for the manifestation of cytotoxic effects. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay, in which viable cells reduce tetrazolium salts to formazan crystals, the absorbance of which is quantified spectrophotometrically at 570 nm. Each viability experiment was repeated a minimum of three times, with at least two technical replicates per experimental condition, and all data were normalized to untreated control wells to account for baseline variability. To evaluate the impact of treatment on cellular proliferation, a subset of cells was seeded onto fibronectin- and collagen II-coated platinum-lined cover slips to facilitate high-resolution imaging, and after treatment, cells were fixed with ice-cold methanol and stained with an Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated antibody against Ki-67, a nuclear protein expressed during all active phases of the cell cycle except G₀. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and images were captured using a Zeiss Confocal 510 LSM microscope. The mean fluorescence intensity of Ki-67 staining was quantified across five representative fields per condition, and cells were classified as Ki-67 positive or negative based on a threshold established relative to the isotype control. To assess cell cycle progression, a stable KKU-055 cell line was generated by transfection with a lentiviral construct encoding a dual-fluorescent reporter system that labels cells in G₁ phase with red fluorescence and cells in S, G₂, or M phases with green fluorescence, while unlabeled cells represent cells in transition or undergoing apoptosis. These cells were seeded in 12-well plates and monitored continuously for up to 72 hours using the IncuCyte Live-Cell Analysis System, which captured phase-contrast and fluorescence images at one-hour intervals. The system automatically quantified cell confluence, total cell count, and the proportion of cells in each cell cycle phase based on fluorescence intensity and morphological characteristics. All statistical analyses were performed using Microsoft Excel 2019 and GraphPad Prism, with Student’s unpaired t-tests used for pairwise comparisons and two-way analysis of variance followed by Bonferroni-corrected post hoc tests to evaluate interactions between plasma and drug treatments. The experimental design was structured to test three primary hypotheses: first, that FOLFIRINOX alone would demonstrate a dose-dependent reduction in cell viability; second, that cold atmospheric plasma alone would reduce viability in a duration-dependent manner; and third, that the combination of subtherapeutic FOLFIRINOX and plasma would produce a synergistic effect that exceeded the additive predictions of each modality alone. All experiments were conducted under blinded conditions, with the experimenter unaware of the treatment group assignments during image acquisition and data analysis to minimize bias. The entire experimental workflow, from cell seeding to final data collection, was completed within a single continuous protocol to ensure temporal consistency and eliminate variability introduced by repeated passaging or media changes.

### Results

The results of the experiments unequivocally demonstrated that the combination of cold atmospheric plasma and FOLFIRINOX produces a synergistic reduction in cholangiocarcinoma cell viability that is not achievable by either modality alone. When FOLFIRINOX was administered as a single agent, a clear dose-response relationship was observed, with cell viability decreasing progressively as the concentration of fluorouracil increased. At the lowest dose of 0.8 µM fluorouracil, cell viability remained at 103% ± 3% relative to untreated controls, indicating no significant cytotoxic effect. At 3.4 µM fluorouracil, viability dropped to 94% ± 3%, still not statistically significant. However, at 6.7 µM fluorouracil, viability decreased to 76% ± 4%, and at 13.5 µM, it fell to 57% ± 5%. The highest dose of 53.8 µM fluorouracil reduced viability to 19% ± 1.9%, a statistically profound reduction (p < 0.005). In contrast, cold atmospheric plasma alone, applied for durations of 1 to 7 minutes, also reduced viability in a time-dependent manner, with 1 minute of exposure reducing viability to 78% ± 5%, 3 minutes to 51% ± 4%, 5 minutes to 28% ± 3%, and 7 minutes to 3% ± 1%, all of which were statistically significant compared to untreated controls (p < 0.005). When the two modalities were combined, however, the results revealed a striking departure from additivity. At the subtherapeutic FOLFIRINOX dose of 6.7 µM fluorouracil, which alone reduced viability by only 24%, the addition of 1 minute of plasma reduced viability to 48% ± 3%, a statistically significant improvement over plasma alone (p < 1 × 10⁻⁵). With 3 minutes of plasma, viability dropped to 15% ± 2%, and with 5 minutes of plasma, it fell to 1% ± 0.5%, representing a 99% reduction in viable cells. This effect was not observed at any other combination of drug and plasma dose; only at this specific low drug dose did the plasma exert a synergistic effect. At higher FOLFIRINOX doses, such as 13.5 µM or above, the drug alone was so cytotoxic that the addition of plasma provided no further significant benefit, suggesting that the synergistic interaction is confined to a narrow therapeutic window where the drug primes the cells for plasma-induced death without fully eliminating them. Two-way ANOVA confirmed that the interaction between FOLFIRINOX dose and plasma duration was statistically significant (p < 0.001), rejecting the null hypothesis that the effects of the two treatments are independent. Post hoc testing with Bonferroni correction identified the combination of 6.7 µM fluorouracil and 5 minutes of plasma as the most effective regimen, with a p-value of less than 1 × 10⁻⁶ compared to all other groups. The Ki-67 staining results further corroborated these findings. In untreated controls, nearly all cells exhibited strong nuclear Ki-67 staining, indicating active proliferation. After 6.7 µM FOLFIRINOX alone, Ki-67 expression was reduced by only 18%, whereas after 3 minutes of plasma alone, it was reduced by 42%. In the combination group, however, Ki-67 expression was reduced by 83%, with a corresponding decrease in total cell count by 75% at the 6-hour timepoint, indicating not only a reduction in proliferation but also an increase in cell death. Confocal images revealed that Ki-67-positive nuclei were sparse and fragmented in the combination group, with many cells exhibiting pyknotic nuclei and cytoplasmic blebbing, consistent with apoptotic morphology. The IncuCyte cell cycle analysis provided mechanistic insight into the temporal dynamics of the response. In untreated and FOLFIRINOX-only treated cells, the proportion of cells in S/G2/M phase increased steadily over 48 hours, indicating active cell cycling and proliferation. In contrast, cells treated with the combination of 6.7 µM FOLFIRINOX and 3 minutes of plasma showed an immediate and sustained decline in the number of cells entering S-phase, with a sharp increase in the proportion of cells arrested in the M-G1 transition phase, a phase associated with mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis. By 24 hours, the majority of cells in the combination group had either detached from the substrate or exhibited no fluorescence, indicating cell death. At 5 minutes of plasma exposure, cell death occurred so rapidly that the majority of cells were non-viable within 6 hours, precluding further cell cycle analysis beyond this point. The phase-contrast images from the IncuCyte system showed clear morphological differences: control wells were confluent and densely packed with fluorescent cells, while combination-treated wells were sparse, with large clusters of cellular debris and minimal fluorescence. These results collectively demonstrate that the combination therapy does not merely enhance cytotoxicity—it fundamentally alters the biological response of the cancer cells, forcing them into an irreversible state of cell cycle arrest and programmed death that neither agent can achieve independently. The data also reveal that the synergy is not a function of increased drug uptake or prolonged plasma exposure, but rather a specific biochemical interaction triggered by the sequence of drug-induced DNA damage followed by plasma-induced oxidative stress, which together overwhelm the cell’s repair mechanisms.

### Discussion

The findings presented in this invention represent a transformative advance in the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma, a disease that has long resisted efforts to improve survival through conventional chemotherapy. The central insight of this invention—that a subtherapeutic dose of FOLFIRINOX can be rendered profoundly cytotoxic through the addition of cold atmospheric plasma—is both unexpected and mechanistically profound. Prior to this work, the prevailing assumption in oncology was that chemotherapeutic agents and physical modalities such as plasma acted through independent pathways, and that their combination would yield at best an additive effect. The results presented here demonstrate conclusively that this assumption is incorrect. The synergy observed is not merely quantitative but qualitative: the combination does not simply kill more cells—it kills them in a different, more efficient, and more irreversible manner. The fact that the synergy is most pronounced at the lowest dose of FOLFIRINOX suggests that the drug is not acting as a direct cytotoxic agent in this context, but rather as a molecular primer that sensitizes the cancer cells to plasma-induced oxidative stress. This is supported by the observation that higher doses of FOLFIRINOX, which cause direct and overwhelming DNA damage, eliminate the need for plasma, as the cells are already beyond the point of recovery. The low-dose regimen, by contrast, appears to induce a sublethal level of DNA damage that activates stress-response pathways, such as the p53 and ATM/ATR kinases, which in turn increase the expression of pro-apoptotic proteins and reduce the levels of antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase and catalase. This creates a cellular environment in which the reactive oxygen and nitrogen species generated by cold plasma—hydroxyl radicals, nitric oxide, and peroxynitrite—are no longer neutralized, leading to catastrophic lipid peroxidation, mitochondrial membrane depolarization, and caspase activation. The timing of the plasma application—24 hours after drug exposure—is critical, as it allows sufficient time for the drug to be internalized and for the cellular stress response to be fully engaged, but not so long that the cells initiate repair mechanisms or enter a quiescent state. The specificity of the effect to cholangiocarcinoma cells, and the lack of toxicity to normal hepatocytes in prior studies, further supports the hypothesis that the synergy exploits a fundamental vulnerability of cancer cells: their altered redox balance. Normal cells maintain robust antioxidant defenses and efficient DNA repair machinery, allowing them to withstand the transient oxidative insult of plasma. Cancer cells, however, operate under chronic oxidative stress and have compromised repair pathways, making them uniquely susceptible to a second hit. This invention leverages that vulnerability in a way that no prior therapy has attempted. The clinical implications are profound. The standard FOLFIRINOX regimen, while effective in pancreatic cancer, is often abandoned in cholangiocarcinoma due to its toxicity profile. This invention demonstrates that a dose reduction of up to 85%—from 53.8 µM fluorouracil down to 6.7 µM—can achieve the same or greater cytotoxic effect when combined with plasma, thereby making the regimen tolerable for patients who are currently excluded from treatment due to poor performance status, advanced age, or comorbid conditions. Furthermore, the ability to apply plasma immediately after surgical resection, as is already being done in clinical trials for other cancers, provides a seamless integration into existing oncological workflows. Instead of relying on systemic chemotherapy to eliminate microscopic residual disease—a strategy that is often ineffective due to poor drug penetration and tumor heterogeneity—this invention enables the direct, localized, and immediate eradication of residual cells at the resection margin, where they are most vulnerable and most likely to recur. The reduction in Ki-67 expression and the arrest of cells in the M-G1 transition phase further suggest that this combination not only kills existing tumor cells but also prevents the proliferation of any surviving clones, thereby reducing the likelihood of recurrence. The fact that the effect is so rapid—complete cell death within 24 hours in the most effective regimen—also suggests that this therapy could be administered in a single session, eliminating the need for prolonged hospitalization or repeated infusions. The limitations of the study are acknowledged: the experiments were conducted in vitro, and the tumor microenvironment in vivo includes immune cells, extracellular matrix, hypoxic regions, and vascular barriers that may modulate the response. However, the consistency of the results across multiple assays—viability, proliferation, cell cycle, and morphology—strengthens the validity of the findings. The use of a clinically approved device and FDA-approved drugs means that translation to human trials requires only protocol development and regulatory submission, not new compound development. The invention therefore represents not merely a scientific discovery, but a practical, scalable, and immediately implementable solution to one of the most intractable problems in oncology.

### Conclusion

In conclusion, the present invention provides a revolutionary method for the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma through the synergistic combination of cold atmospheric plasma and a subtherapeutic dose of the FOLFIRINOX chemotherapeutic regimen. The invention demonstrates, for the first time, that a precise sequence of drug administration followed by plasma exposure can induce a multiplicative reduction in tumor cell viability that far exceeds the effects of either modality alone. The optimal regimen, comprising 6.7 µM fluorouracil (and corresponding molar doses of leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin) administered for 24 hours prior to 3 to 5 minutes of cold atmospheric plasma delivered at 28.7 watts with a helium flow rate of 3 liters per minute, results in a 91–99% reduction in viable cholangiocarcinoma cells, suppresses cellular proliferation as measured by Ki-67 downregulation, and induces irreversible cell cycle arrest in the M-G1 transition phase. This combination therapy achieves therapeutic efficacy at a fraction of the chemotherapeutic dose required for monotherapy, thereby mitigating the severe hematologic and gastrointestinal toxicities that have historically limited the clinical utility of FOLFIRINOX. The method is applicable to both intrahepatic and extrahepatic forms of cholangiocarcinoma and is particularly suited for adjuvant use following surgical resection, where the localized application of plasma can target residual microscopic disease with precision and minimal impact on surrounding healthy tissue. The invention is grounded in rigorous experimental validation, employing multiple complementary assays to confirm the biological significance of the observed effects, and is based on the use of clinically approved drugs and a device already under FDA Investigational Device Exemption for other oncological applications. The findings presented herein establish a new paradigm in cancer therapy: that physical and chemical modalities, when applied in a temporally optimized sequence, can interact in a synergistic manner to overcome the intrinsic resistance mechanisms of aggressive malignancies. The invention opens the door to a new class of combination therapies that do not rely on novel drug discovery but instead on the intelligent reconfiguration of existing tools to achieve unprecedented therapeutic outcomes. Future clinical trials are warranted to validate these in vitro findings in vivo, but the mechanistic plausibility, translational feasibility, and profound clinical potential of this invention render it a landmark advancement in the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma and a model for the development of similar strategies in other chemoresistant cancers.
