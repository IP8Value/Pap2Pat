# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of virology and gene therapy, specifically to novel recombinant adeno-associated viral vectors derived from an ancient, endogenized marsupial adeno-associated virus sequence. The invention encompasses the reconstruction, characterization, and utilization of a phylogenetically distinct and evolutionarily conserved capsid protein derived from a mid-Cenozoic era endogenous viral element, designated mAAV-EVE1, which has been preserved in the germline of macropodoid marsupials for over twenty-seven million years. The invention further includes the production of recombinant viral vectors incorporating this reconstructed capsid, methods for their manufacture, and their use in the delivery of therapeutic nucleic acids to mammalian cells, particularly human cells, with enhanced tropism, reduced immunogenicity, and improved transduction efficiency compared to existing adeno-associated viral serotypes. The vectors of the present invention are particularly suited for applications in gene therapy targeting the central nervous system, skeletal muscle, liver, and retinal tissues, and represent a new class of gene delivery vehicles derived not from extant viral isolates but from ancestral viral sequences inferred from genomic fossils.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) are small, non-enveloped, single-stranded DNA viruses belonging to the genus Dependoparvovirus within the family Parvoviridae. Naturally occurring AAVs are non-pathogenic in humans and require a helper virus, such as adenovirus or herpesvirus, for productive replication. Their ability to infect both dividing and non-dividing cells, long-term persistence of transgene expression, low immunogenicity, and lack of integration into host chromosomes under standard conditions have rendered them the most widely used viral vectors in clinical gene therapy trials. To date, over two hundred clinical trials have employed AAV vectors, with several products receiving regulatory approval for the treatment of inherited retinal diseases, spinal muscular atrophy, and hemophilia B.

Despite their success, current AAV-based gene therapies are limited by several persistent challenges. First, pre-existing humoral immunity against naturally circulating AAV serotypes in the human population—particularly against serotypes such as AAV2, AAV5, and AAV8—can neutralize vector administration and prevent transduction. Second, the tropism of existing AAV capsids is often suboptimal for specific target tissues, necessitating high vector doses that increase the risk of hepatotoxicity and immune activation. Third, cross-reactivity among AAV serotypes limits the possibility of re-administration following initial treatment. Fourth, the capsid proteins of naturally occurring AAVs exhibit limited structural diversity, constrained by evolutionary pressures to maintain interactions with conserved cellular receptors and intracellular trafficking pathways. Consequently, the pool of naturally occurring AAV variants available for vector engineering remains narrow, and attempts to generate novel capsids through directed evolution or rational design have yielded only incremental improvements.

The discovery of endogenous viral elements (EVEs)—fossilized remnants of ancient viral infections integrated into the genomes of their hosts—has opened a new frontier in virology and vector development. Unlike exogenous viruses, EVEs represent evolutionary snapshots of viral sequences that have been preserved over millions of years, free from the selective pressures of active replication and immune evasion in contemporary hosts. These sequences may encode capsid proteins with structural features that have been lost in modern viral lineages, offering access to a broader and more ancient diversity of viral architectures. While EVEs have been identified in numerous vertebrate genomes, including those of primates, rodents, and birds, none have been systematically reconstructed and utilized to generate functional viral vectors for gene delivery.

The present invention is based on the identification and characterization of a lineage-specific endogenous parvoviral element, designated mAAV-EVE1, within the genome of macropodoid marsupials. This element, which originated in the common ancestor of kangaroos, wallabies, and their relatives approximately forty-five million years ago, encodes a complete set of viral genes including rep and cap, albeit with numerous inactivating mutations accumulated over evolutionary time. Through phylogenetic analysis and ancestral sequence reconstruction, the full-length ancestral capsid protein of mAAV-EVE1 was inferred, revealing a structure that is both highly conserved in its core fold and uniquely divergent in its surface-exposed variable regions compared to all known extant AAV serotypes. The reconstructed capsid protein exhibits a novel surface topology, including an elongated variable region VII that extends into the depression surrounding the icosahedral five-fold axis, a feature absent in all previously characterized AAVs. This structural novelty suggests a distinct receptor recognition profile and intracellular trafficking pathway, properties that are highly desirable for the next generation of gene therapy vectors.

Prior efforts to exploit EVEs for vector development have been limited to computational analyses or the identification of fragmented sequences without functional validation. No prior art describes the reconstruction of a full-length, functional viral capsid from an ancient EVE, nor the production of recombinant AAV particles incorporating such a capsid for use in mammalian gene delivery. Furthermore, no prior art teaches the use of an ancestral viral sequence derived from a non-primate, non-eutherian mammal as the basis for a human gene therapy vector. The present invention overcomes these limitations by providing a method for the de novo reconstruction of a functional viral capsid from a paleoviral genome, its expression in a recombinant system, and its assembly into infectious viral particles capable of transducing human cells with high efficiency and low immunogenicity. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in vector design, moving from the exploitation of extant viral diversity to the mining of deep evolutionary history for novel biological functions.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present invention provides a novel class of recombinant adeno-associated viral vectors comprising a capsid protein derived from an ancestral marsupial adeno-associated virus, designated mAAV-EVE1, reconstructed from endogenous viral elements preserved in the genomes of macropodoid marsupials. The capsid protein of the present invention is encoded by a nucleic acid sequence that is substantially identical to the inferred ancestral sequence of mAAV-EVE1, as determined by maximum likelihood phylogenetic analysis of sixteen orthologous loci across macropodoid species. The reconstructed capsid protein exhibits a conserved β-barrel core structure typical of parvoviral capsids but contains unique surface variable regions, most notably an extended variable region VII that alters the topology of the icosahedral three-fold and five-fold axes, conferring novel tropism and immune evasion properties not found in any known AAV serotype.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a recombinant AAV vector comprising a nucleic acid sequence encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid protein, operably linked to a promoter capable of driving expression in a host cell, wherein the capsid protein is assembled into a viral particle with a diameter of approximately 22–26 nanometers and an icosahedral symmetry of T=1. The vector further comprises a transgene expression cassette flanked by adeno-associated virus inverted terminal repeats, wherein the transgene is selected from the group consisting of therapeutic nucleic acids, reporter genes, regulatory RNAs, and CRISPR-Cas components. The vector is produced in a host cell system that supports AAV replication and packaging, and the resulting particles are purified to a high degree of homogeneity, free from replication-competent adenovirus or helper virus contamination.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a method for producing the recombinant AAV vector, comprising the steps of: (a) introducing into a host cell a first nucleic acid construct encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid protein, a second nucleic acid construct encoding the AAV rep gene, and a third nucleic acid construct comprising a transgene expression cassette flanked by ITRs; (b) culturing the host cell under conditions permitting expression of the rep and cap proteins and packaging of the transgene cassette; and (c) harvesting and purifying the recombinant AAV particles from the cell lysate or culture supernatant. The host cell may be a mammalian cell line, such as HEK293 or HeLa, or an insect cell line, such as Sf9, and may be co-transfected with a helper virus or transfected with helper plasmids encoding adenoviral genes E1A, E1B, E2A, E4, and VA RNA.

In a further embodiment, the invention provides a composition comprising the recombinant AAV vector of the invention in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the vector is formulated for systemic, intrathecal, intravitreal, or intramuscular administration. The composition may be lyophilized or stored in aqueous solution at a concentration of at least 1×10¹² vector genomes per milliliter, and may include stabilizing agents such as sucrose, polysorbate 80, or human serum albumin. The composition is intended for use in the treatment of genetic disorders, neurodegenerative diseases, metabolic disorders, or ocular diseases, and is characterized by a reduced neutralizing antibody titer in human sera compared to AAV2, AAV8, or AAV9, and by enhanced transduction of neurons, hepatocytes, or retinal pigment epithelial cells in vivo.

In yet another embodiment, the invention provides a method for treating a disease in a mammalian subject, comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of the recombinant AAV vector of the invention. The disease may be selected from the group consisting of spinal muscular atrophy, Leber congenital amaurosis, Duchenne muscular dystrophy, hemophilia A or B, Parkinson’s disease, Huntington’s disease, Tay-Sachs disease, and lysosomal storage disorders. The vector is administered in a dose ranging from 1×10¹⁰ to 1×10¹⁴ vector genomes per kilogram of body weight, depending on the target tissue and route of administration.

The invention further provides a diagnostic kit comprising the recombinant AAV vector of the invention, a control vector, and instructions for use in determining the presence or absence of neutralizing antibodies against the mAAV-EVE1 capsid in a biological sample obtained from a subject. The kit may be used to screen patients prior to gene therapy administration to identify individuals who are likely to respond to treatment with the mAAV-EVE1 vector, thereby enabling personalized therapeutic strategies.

The mAAV-EVE1 capsid protein of the invention is distinguished from all known AAV capsids by its evolutionary origin, its unique surface topology, and its functional properties. Unlike capsids derived from modern AAV isolates, the mAAV-EVE1 capsid has not been subjected to recent selective pressures from human immune systems, resulting in a surface that is antigenically novel and less likely to be recognized by pre-existing neutralizing antibodies. The capsid retains the conserved structural motifs required for receptor binding, endocytosis, endosomal escape, nuclear import, and capsid uncoating, while exhibiting altered interactions with known AAV receptors such as heparan sulfate proteoglycan, galactose, and sialic acid. The vector demonstrates superior transduction efficiency in human primary cells, including neurons, cardiomyocytes, and hepatocytes, in comparison to AAV9 and AAVrh.10, and exhibits minimal off-target transduction in non-human primates. Furthermore, the mAAV-EVE1 vector shows reduced activation of innate immune sensors such as TLR9 and cGAS-STING pathways, resulting in lower levels of pro-inflammatory cytokine induction.

The invention thus provides a fundamentally new class of AAV vectors derived not from contemporary viral isolates but from a paleoviral sequence reconstructed from genomic fossils, offering unprecedented advantages in tropism, immune evasion, and transduction efficiency for next-generation gene therapy applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Vectors

The recombinant adeno-associated viral vectors of the present invention are non-replicating, non-pathogenic gene delivery vehicles that consist of a protein capsid enclosing a single-stranded DNA genome containing a transgene expression cassette flanked by inverted terminal repeats derived from adeno-associated virus. The capsid of the present invention is composed of three viral proteins, VP1, VP2, and VP3, which are encoded by a single open reading frame that is translated from a single mRNA transcript through alternative splicing and non-canonical translational initiation. The VP1 protein contains an N-terminal extension that includes a phospholipase A2 domain essential for endosomal escape, while VP2 and VP3 are truncated isoforms that share the C-terminal region corresponding to the VP3 structural core. The mAAV-EVE1 capsid protein of the present invention is encoded by a nucleic acid sequence that is substantially identical to the inferred ancestral sequence of the marsupial endogenous viral element mAAV-EVE1, as determined by maximum likelihood phylogenetic analysis of sixteen orthologous loci from macropodoid marsupials. The sequence has been optimized for codon usage in human cells without altering the amino acid sequence, and is operably linked to a promoter capable of driving high-level expression in mammalian cells, such as the cytomegalovirus immediate early promoter, the elongation factor 1-alpha promoter, or the chicken β-actin promoter.

The recombinant vector genome is flanked by adeno-associated virus inverted terminal repeats, which are approximately 145 base pairs in length and contain cis-acting elements necessary for viral DNA replication, packaging, and integration. The ITRs may be derived from AAV2, AAV5, or any other serotype, and may be modified to enhance packaging efficiency or reduce recombination. The transgene expression cassette may contain a tissue-specific promoter, such as the synapsin promoter for neuronal expression, the albumin promoter for hepatocyte-specific expression, or the rhodopsin promoter for retinal expression, followed by a polyadenylation signal, such as the bovine growth hormone polyA or the SV40 polyA signal. The transgene may encode a therapeutic protein, a short hairpin RNA, a microRNA, a CRISPR-Cas9 nuclease, a base editor, or a prime editor, and may be designed to correct a genetic mutation, silence a pathogenic gene, or express a missing protein. The vector genome may be packaged as a self-complementary DNA molecule to enable rapid transgene expression without requiring second-strand synthesis, or as a single-stranded molecule to accommodate larger transgenes up to 4.7 kilobases in length.

The recombinant vector particles are purified by a combination of affinity chromatography, ion exchange chromatography, and density gradient ultracentrifugation to achieve a purity of at least 95% and a vector genome-to-particle ratio of at least 1:10. The purified vector is formulated in a buffer containing a physiological salt concentration, a stabilizing sugar such as sucrose or trehalose, a surfactant such as polysorbate 80, and optionally a human serum albumin to prevent adsorption to container surfaces. The final formulation is sterile-filtered, tested for endotoxin levels, and stored at −80°C or lyophilized for long-term stability. The vector is free from replication-competent adenovirus, helper virus, and host cell DNA, as determined by PCR, qPCR, and next-generation sequencing methods. The vector is suitable for administration to humans via intravenous, intrathecal, intramuscular, intraocular, or intracerebral routes, depending on the target tissue.

### Recombinant AAV

The recombinant AAV particles of the present invention are produced by co-transfection of a host cell with three plasmids: a rep-cap plasmid encoding the mAAV-EVE1 rep and cap genes, a helper plasmid encoding adenoviral genes E1A, E1B, E2A, E4, and VA RNA, and a transfer plasmid containing the transgene expression cassette flanked by ITRs. The rep gene encodes four non-structural proteins, Rep78, Rep68, Rep52, and Rep40, which are essential for viral DNA replication, packaging, and site-specific integration. The cap gene encodes the VP1, VP2, and VP3 structural proteins, which assemble into the viral capsid. The mAAV-EVE1 rep gene has been reconstructed to contain a functional start codon at the appropriate position to permit translation of Rep78 and Rep68, and the cap gene has been reconstructed to contain a functional start codon for VP1 and a non-canonical CTG codon for VP2, consistent with the translational initiation mechanisms observed in extant AAVs. The nucleic acid sequences encoding the rep and cap proteins have been optimized for expression in human cells by replacing rare codons with synonymous codons that are frequently used in highly expressed human genes, while preserving the amino acid sequence of the original ancestral protein.

The host cell used for production may be a human embryonic kidney cell line such as HEK293 or HEK293T, which express the adenoviral E1 gene and thus support replication of the helper plasmid. Alternatively, the host cell may be an insect cell line such as Sf9 or High Five, which are used in baculovirus expression systems. In the baculovirus system, the rep and cap genes are cloned into a baculovirus transfer vector under the control of a strong viral promoter such as the polyhedrin or p10 promoter, and the transfer plasmid is co-infected with a baculovirus expressing the adenoviral helper genes. The recombinant AAV particles are harvested from the cell lysate or culture supernatant 48 to 72 hours post-transfection or infection, and are purified by iodixanol gradient centrifugation followed by anion exchange chromatography. The purified vector is concentrated by ultrafiltration and buffer-exchanged into a formulation buffer suitable for clinical use.

The yield of recombinant AAV particles produced using the mAAV-EVE1 capsid is comparable to or exceeds that of AAV2, AAV5, and AAV9 vectors, with titers ranging from 1×10¹² to 5×10¹³ vector genomes per milliliter. The particles are stable under standard storage conditions and retain full transduction activity after multiple freeze-thaw cycles. The vector genome is packaged with high fidelity, and the ratio of full to empty capsids is greater than 70%, as determined by transmission electron microscopy and capsid ELISA. The recombinant AAV particles of the present invention are infectious and capable of transducing a wide range of mammalian cell types, including primary neurons, hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes, and retinal pigment epithelial cells, with efficiency superior to that of AAV9 and AAVrh.10 in comparative studies.

### Host Cells

The host cells used for the production of the recombinant AAV vectors of the present invention are selected based on their ability to support the replication of the AAV genome, the expression of the rep and cap proteins, and the assembly of infectious viral particles. Preferred host cells include human embryonic kidney cell lines such as HEK293, HEK293T, and HEK293FT, which are readily transfectable and express the adenoviral E1 gene, thereby obviating the need for a separate helper plasmid encoding E1. These cells are cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium supplemented with fetal bovine serum, L-glutamine, and antibiotics, and are maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% carbon dioxide. Prior to transfection, cells are seeded at a density of 1.5×10⁶ to 2.5×10⁶ cells per 10 cm dish and allowed to reach 70–90% confluence. Transfection is performed using a cationic lipid-based reagent such as polyethylenimine or Lipofectamine 3000, or by calcium phosphate precipitation, using a 1:1:1 molar ratio of rep-cap plasmid, helper plasmid, and transfer plasmid.

Alternative host cells include HeLa cells, Chinese hamster ovary cells, and human hepatoma cells such as HepG2, which may be used for specific applications requiring tissue-specific expression or for large-scale production. Insect cells such as Sf9 and High Five are also suitable for production using the baculovirus expression system. In this system, the rep and cap genes are cloned into a baculovirus transfer vector under the control of the polyhedrin or p10 promoter, and the transfer plasmid is co-transfected into Sf9 cells with a baculovirus expressing the adenoviral helper genes. The recombinant baculovirus is amplified through multiple rounds of infection, and the final virus stock is used to infect a large-scale culture of Sf9 cells. The recombinant AAV particles are harvested 72 hours post-infection and purified as described above.

The host cells may be genetically modified to enhance vector production or to reduce contamination. For example, cells may be engineered to stably express the adenoviral helper genes, eliminating the need for co-transfection of a helper plasmid. Cells may also be engineered to express a suppressor of RNA interference to enhance transgene expression, or to delete genes involved in innate immune sensing, such as TLR9 or cGAS, to reduce inflammatory responses during vector production. The cells may be grown in suspension culture in bioreactors to enable scalable, GMP-compliant manufacturing of the recombinant AAV vectors. The resulting cell culture supernatant and lysate are processed using automated purification systems to yield a clinical-grade product suitable for human administration.

### Compositions and Methods

The compositions of the present invention comprise the recombinant AAV vector of the invention in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the carrier is selected from the group consisting of saline, phosphate-buffered saline, Ringer’s lactate, dextrose solution, and isotonic buffer containing 5% sucrose, 0.01% polysorbate 80, and 0.1 mg/mL human serum albumin. The vector is formulated at a concentration of at least 1×10¹² vector genomes per milliliter, and may be lyophilized for long-term storage. The composition may be packaged in single-use vials made of borosilicate glass or polypropylene, with rubber stoppers and aluminum seals, and labeled with instructions for reconstitution, storage, and administration. The composition is sterile, pyrogen-free, and tested for endotoxin levels, microbial contamination, and residual host cell DNA and proteins.

The methods of the present invention include the use of the recombinant AAV vector for the treatment of genetic and acquired diseases in mammals, particularly in humans. The vector is administered to a subject in need thereof in an effective amount sufficient to achieve therapeutic transgene expression in the target tissue. The effective amount is determined based on the route of administration, the target tissue, the transgene, and the disease state, and may range from 1×10¹⁰ to 1×10¹⁴ vector genomes per kilogram of body weight. For systemic administration, the vector is delivered intravenously at a dose of 1×10¹² to 5×10¹³ vg/kg. For intrathecal administration, the vector is delivered into the cerebrospinal fluid at a dose of 1×10¹¹ to 5×10¹² vg per injection. For intravitreal administration, the vector is delivered into the vitreous humor at a dose of 1×10¹⁰ to 5×10¹¹ vg per eye. For intramuscular administration, the vector is delivered into skeletal muscle at a dose of 1×10¹¹ to 5×10¹² vg per site. The vector may be administered as a single dose or as multiple doses, depending on the disease and the persistence of transgene expression.

The methods further include the use of the recombinant AAV vector for gene editing applications, wherein the transgene encodes a CRISPR-Cas9 nuclease, a base editor, or a prime editor, and is delivered to correct a point mutation, insert a missing exon, or disrupt a pathogenic allele. The vector may be used in ex vivo applications, wherein cells are harvested from the subject, transduced in culture, and reinfused, or in in vivo applications, wherein the vector is directly administered to the patient. The vector may be used in combination with other therapies, such as small molecule drugs, antibodies, or cell-based therapies, to enhance therapeutic efficacy.

The methods also include the use of the recombinant AAV vector as a diagnostic tool to detect neutralizing antibodies against the mAAV-EVE1 capsid in human serum. A microplate-based assay is performed by coating wells with purified mAAV-EVE1 capsid particles, incubating with serial dilutions of patient serum, and detecting bound antibodies using an enzyme-conjugated anti-human IgG secondary antibody. The titer of neutralizing antibodies is determined by a transduction inhibition assay, wherein the ability of serum to block transduction of HEK293 cells by mAAV-EVE1 vector is measured. Subjects with low or undetectable neutralizing antibody titers are identified as suitable candidates for gene therapy with the mAAV-EVE1 vector, while subjects with high titers are excluded or pre-treated with plasmapheresis or immunosuppressive regimens.

### Capsid Diversification

The capsid protein of the present invention is distinguished from all previously known AAV capsids by its unique evolutionary origin, its conserved structural core, and its divergent surface topology. The ancestral mAAV-EVE1 capsid sequence was reconstructed using maximum likelihood phylogenetic analysis of sixteen orthologous loci from macropodoid marsupials, and the resulting sequence was manually edited to correct frameshifts, nonsense codons, and indels based on homology with extant AAV proteins. The reconstructed capsid protein contains a conserved β-barrel core composed of eight antiparallel β-strands arranged in a jelly-roll fold, which is characteristic of all parvoviral capsids. However, the surface-exposed variable regions, particularly VR-VII, exhibit a dramatic elongation compared to all known AAV serotypes, extending approximately 15 angstroms beyond the corresponding region in AAV2 and AAV8. This elongation alters the geometry of the icosahedral three-fold and five-fold axes, creating a unique surface landscape that is not recognized by antibodies raised against any known AAV serotype.

The variable regions of the mAAV-EVE1 capsid were mapped using structural alignment with AAV2, AAV4, and AAV8, and were found to correspond to known receptor-binding domains. However, the amino acid residues within these regions are largely non-conserved, with substitutions at positions known to mediate binding to heparan sulfate, galactose, and sialic acid. The mAAV-EVE1 capsid does not bind to heparan sulfate proteoglycan, the primary receptor for AAV2, and exhibits minimal binding to galactose and sialic acid, the receptors for AAV9 and AAVrh.10, respectively. Instead, the capsid appears to utilize a novel, as-yet-unidentified receptor that is highly conserved across mammalian species, including humans. This novel receptor interaction enables the vector to transduce a broad range of tissues, including the central nervous system, liver, and skeletal muscle, with high efficiency and low off-target transduction.

The mAAV-EVE1 capsid also exhibits reduced immunogenicity compared to other AAV serotypes. In vitro assays using human peripheral blood mononuclear cells show that the mAAV-EVE1 vector induces significantly lower levels of interferon-α, interleukin-6, and tumor necrosis factor-α compared to AAV2, AAV8, and AAV9. In vivo studies in non-human primates demonstrate that administration of the mAAV-EVE1 vector results in minimal activation of innate immune sensors such as TLR9 and cGAS-STING, and does not elicit a robust cytotoxic T-cell response against transduced cells. The capsid is also resistant to neutralization by pooled human immunoglobulin, with a 50% inhibitory concentration greater than 1:1000, compared to 1:10 for AAV2 and 1:50 for AAV9. These properties make the mAAV-EVE1 vector particularly suitable for re-administration in clinical settings, where pre-existing immunity to conventional AAV serotypes is a major barrier to effective therapy.

The capsid may be further modified by directed evolution or rational design to enhance specific properties. For example, point mutations may be introduced into the variable regions to alter tropism, improve endosomal escape, or reduce aggregation. The capsid may be fused to peptide ligands, antibody fragments, or lectins to target specific cell types. The capsid may be chemically modified with polyethylene glycol to extend circulation time or reduce immune recognition. All such modifications are encompassed within the scope of the present invention, provided that the core structure and ancestral sequence identity are preserved.

### Other Uses

Beyond gene therapy, the recombinant AAV vectors of the present invention have a wide range of additional applications in research, diagnostics, and biotechnology. The vectors may be used as tools for gene delivery in animal models of human disease, enabling the creation of transgenic mice, rats, pigs, and non-human primates with tissue-specific expression of human genes or disease-causing mutations. The vectors may be used for lineage tracing in developmental biology, wherein a fluorescent reporter gene is delivered to a progenitor cell population and its descendants are tracked over time. The vectors may be used for circuit mapping in neuroscience, wherein transsynaptic tracing is achieved by delivering a transgene encoding a fluorescent protein or a synaptic marker to specific neuronal populations. The vectors may be used for high-throughput screening of gene function, wherein libraries of sgRNAs or cDNAs are packaged into mAAV-EVE1 vectors and delivered to pooled cell populations for functional genomics studies.

The vectors may also be used in synthetic biology applications, wherein they serve as delivery vehicles for engineered genetic circuits, biosensors, or logic gates. The vectors may be used for the delivery of mRNA or CRISPR guide RNAs in transient expression systems, circumventing the need for genomic integration. The vectors may be used for the delivery of epigenetic modifiers to reprogram cell fate, such as the conversion of fibroblasts into neurons or hepatocytes. The vectors may be used in agriculture for the delivery of genes to livestock or model organisms to enhance disease resistance, improve productivity, or study gene function.

The mAAV-EVE1 capsid may also be used as a scaffold for nanoparticle engineering, wherein the capsid is chemically or genetically modified to encapsulate small molecules, proteins, or nucleic acids for targeted delivery. The capsid may be conjugated with fluorescent dyes, magnetic nanoparticles, or radiolabels for imaging applications in vivo. The capsid may be used as a template for the design of synthetic viral-like particles that mimic its structure but lack viral genetic material, serving as safe and stable platforms for vaccine delivery. The capsid may be used in nanotechnology to assemble ordered arrays on surfaces or in solution, leveraging its icosahedral symmetry and stability.

The invention thus encompasses all uses of the recombinant AAV vector comprising the mAAV-EVE1 capsid, whether for therapeutic, diagnostic, research, or industrial purposes, provided that the capsid is derived from the reconstructed ancestral sequence of the marsupial endogenous viral element mAAV-EVE1.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Identification and Characterization of mAAV-EVE1 Genome and Proteins

Genomic DNA was extracted from liver tissue samples of Macropus giganteus, Macropus rufus, Macropus eugenii, and other macropodoid marsupials using a standard phenol-chloroform extraction protocol. PCR amplification was performed using primers designed to target conserved regions of the AAV rep and cap genes, yielding a product of approximately 1.2 kilobases. Sequencing of the amplicon revealed a high degree of sequence similarity to known AAV genomes, with the highest identity to primate AAV serotypes AAVhu.S17 and AAV13. To determine the full-length sequence of the endogenous viral element, linker-mediated genome walking was performed using the GenomeWalker Universal Kit. Restriction enzyme digestion of genomic DNA with DraI, NruI, ScaI, and StuI generated blunt-ended fragments, which were ligated to adapter sequences and subjected to nested PCR using primers specific to the initial amplicon and the adapter. Multiple PCR products were cloned into pCR4-TOPO vectors and sequenced. The assembled sequence revealed a full-length endogenous AAV element of approximately 4.4 kilobases, designated mAAV-EVE1, which contained intact open reading frames for rep and cap, but lacked inverted terminal repeats and contained multiple stop codons and frameshift mutations.

To reconstruct the ancestral sequence of mAAV-EVE1, genomic DNA from sixteen macropodoid species was amplified using degenerate primers targeting the upstream and downstream flanking regions of the locus. The amplicons were cloned and sequenced, and the sequences were aligned using MUSCLE. A maximum likelihood phylogenetic tree was constructed using MEGA6.0, and the most probable ancestral sequence at the node corresponding to the common ancestor of all macropodoids was inferred using the T92+G model of nucleotide substitution. The inferred sequence contained 21 nonsense codons and 12 frameshift mutations relative to a functional AAV genome. Manual editing was performed to restore the reading frame and eliminate premature stop codons, based on homology with extant AAV proteins and conservation of critical residues in the Rep nuclease and helicase domains. The final reconstructed sequence encoded a Rep protein of 581 amino acids and a Cap protein of 704 amino acids, with a predicted molecular weight of 67.6 kDa and 82.2 kDa, respectively. The Rep protein contained conserved RCR motifs II and III, and Walker A and B motifs, but exhibited a substitution of the catalytic lysine in the Walker A site to asparagine. The Cap protein contained a phospholipase A2 domain with a glutamine substitution at the catalytic histidine residue. The VP3 core structure was modeled using SWISS-MODEL with AAV8 as a template, revealing a conserved β-barrel fold with a uniquely elongated VR-VII loop. The reconstructed sequence was synthesized de novo and cloned into a mammalian expression vector under the control of the CMV promoter.

### Example 2. Modification of mAAV-EVE Capsid

The reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 cap gene was cloned into a plasmid vector alongside the rep gene from AAV2, and the resulting construct was transfected into HEK293 cells along with a transfer plasmid containing a GFP expression cassette flanked by AAV2 ITRs. Western blot analysis of cell lysates confirmed expression of VP1, VP2, and VP3 proteins of the expected molecular weights. Immunofluorescence staining using a polyclonal antibody raised against the reconstructed VP3 protein revealed cytoplasmic accumulation of capsid proteins and formation of nuclear inclusions, consistent with capsid assembly. To enhance expression, the cap gene was codon-optimized for human cells by replacing rare codons with synonymous codons that are frequently used in highly expressed human genes. The optimized sequence was synthesized and cloned into the expression vector. The resulting protein was expressed at a level 3.5-fold higher than the native sequence. To test for functionality, the optimized cap gene was co-transfected with rep and ITR-flanked transgene plasmids, and recombinant AAV particles were harvested and purified. Transmission electron microscopy revealed the presence of icosahedral particles of approximately 22–26 nm in diameter, with a morphology indistinguishable from wild-type AAV2. Capsid ELISA confirmed the presence of VP3 protein in the purified preparation, and qPCR confirmed the presence of vector genomes. The particles were infectious, as demonstrated by transduction of HEK293 cells with a GFP reporter, yielding a transduction efficiency of 68% at a multiplicity of infection of 1000 vg/cell.

### Example 3. Production of rAAV

Recombinant AAV vectors were produced using the triple-plasmid transfection method in HEK293 cells. The rep-cap plasmid contained the codon-optimized mAAV-EVE1 rep and cap genes under the control of the CMV promoter. The helper plasmid contained the adenoviral genes E1A, E1B, E2A, E4, and VA RNA under the control of their native promoters. The transfer plasmid contained a human codon-optimized factor IX transgene flanked by AAV2 ITRs and a woodchuck hepatitis virus post-transcriptional regulatory element. Cells were transfected using polyethylenimine at a 1:1:1 molar ratio of plasmids. Forty-eight hours post-transfection, cells were harvested and lysed by freeze-thaw cycles. The lysate was treated with Benzonase to degrade free nucleic acids and clarified by centrifugation. The vector was purified by iodixanol gradient ultracentrifugation, followed by anion exchange chromatography on a HiTrap Q column. The purified vector was concentrated by ultrafiltration and buffer-exchanged into a formulation buffer containing 10 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0, 150 mM NaCl, 5% sucrose, and 0.01% polysorbate 80. The final vector titer was 3.2×10¹³ vector genomes per milliliter, as determined by qPCR. The ratio of full to empty capsids was 78%, as determined by capsid ELISA and transmission electron microscopy. The vector was free from replication-competent adenovirus, as confirmed by PCR for the adenoviral E4 gene, and from host cell DNA, as confirmed by qPCR for human β-actin. The vector was stable for at least six months at −80°C.

### Example 4. Assessment of rAAV

The recombinant AAV vector containing the mAAV-EVE1 capsid was assessed for transduction efficiency, tropism, and immunogenicity in vitro and in vivo. In vitro, the vector was tested on a panel of human cell lines, including HEK293, HepG2, Huh7, SH-SY5Y, and primary human neurons derived from induced pluripotent stem cells. At a multiplicity of infection of 1000 vg/cell, the mAAV-EVE1 vector transduced HEK293 cells with 72% efficiency, HepG2 cells with 65% efficiency, and primary neurons with 58% efficiency, compared to 45%, 52%, and 38% for AAV9, respectively. The vector showed minimal transduction of HeLa and MCF7 cells, indicating a distinct tropism profile. Neutralization assays using pooled human immunoglobulin showed that the mAAV-EVE1 vector was resistant to neutralization, with a 50% inhibitory concentration greater than 1:1000, compared to 1:10 for AAV2 and 1:50 for AAV9. In vivo, the vector was administered intravenously to C57BL/6 mice at a dose of 2×10¹¹ vg/mouse. At day 14, transgene expression was detected in liver, heart, skeletal muscle, and brain, with the highest levels in liver and heart. No significant elevation in serum transaminases or pro-inflammatory cytokines was observed. In non-human primates, the vector was administered intravenously at a dose of 5×10¹² vg/kg. Transgene expression was detected in liver and skeletal muscle at day 28, with no evidence of hepatotoxicity or immune cell infiltration. Pre-existing antibodies against AAV2, AAV8, and AAV9 did not cross-neutralize the mAAV-EVE1 vector. The vector was re-administered at day 60, and transgene expression was restored to baseline levels, demonstrating the ability to overcome pre-existing immunity. These results confirm that the mAAV-EVE1 vector is a potent, safe, and immunologically evasive gene delivery vehicle with broad therapeutic potential.
