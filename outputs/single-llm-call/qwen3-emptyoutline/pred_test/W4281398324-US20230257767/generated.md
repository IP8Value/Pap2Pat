# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biopharmaceutical manufacturing, specifically to the production of monoclonal antibodies in mammalian cell expression systems. More particularly, the invention concerns a novel promoter sequence derived from the Chinese hamster heat shock protein 5 gene (Hspa5), which enables sustained and enhanced transcriptional activity of recombinant antibody genes throughout the entire duration of fed-batch culture, including the late-phase growth period where conventional promoters typically exhibit significant decline in activity. The invention further encompasses recombinant expression vectors comprising this promoter, stably transfected host cell lines expressing monoclonal antibodies under its control, and methods for high-yield, consistent production of therapeutic antibodies using such systems. The technical domain encompasses molecular biology, genetic engineering, cell culture technology, and industrial bioprocessing, with particular relevance to the manufacturing of immunoglobulin G (IgG) subclasses for clinical and commercial applications in oncology, autoimmune diseases, infectious diseases, and other therapeutic areas requiring high-purity, high-titer antibody products.

## BACKGROUND ART

The biopharmaceutical industry has witnessed exponential growth in the development and commercialization of monoclonal antibodies over the past three decades, with over one hundred such products approved globally for the treatment of a wide spectrum of diseases. Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells have emerged as the dominant host system for the industrial-scale production of these biologics due to their capacity for complex post-translational modifications, robust growth under serum-free suspension conditions, and compatibility with established good manufacturing practices. Despite extensive optimization efforts across multiple domains—including vector design, gene copy number amplification, promoter selection, culture medium formulation, feeding strategies, and process control parameters—the consistent and efficient production of high yields of monoclonal antibodies remains a persistent challenge, particularly during the late stages of fed-batch culture.

Conventional expression systems for monoclonal antibody production have largely relied on viral promoters such as those derived from human cytomegalovirus (hCMV), simian virus 40 early region (SV40E), and Rous sarcoma virus (RSV), as well as heterogeneous mammalian promoters including phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK), ubiquitin C (UBC), and elongation factor-1 alpha (EF1α). Among these, the human EF1α promoter has been widely adopted due to its relatively stable activity across various culture conditions and its ability to support moderate to high levels of transgene expression. However, a recurring limitation observed across nearly all such systems is the marked decline in transgene transcription and subsequent protein productivity during the late phase of fed-batch culture, typically after day 7–10. This phenomenon, often referred to as transcriptional silencing or promoter shutdown, results in suboptimal final product titers, increased batch-to-batch variability, and reduced process efficiency, thereby limiting the economic viability and scalability of antibody manufacturing.

The underlying causes of this decline are multifactorial and include epigenetic silencing of transgene loci, metabolic stress, accumulation of toxic metabolites, depletion of critical nutrients, and activation of cellular stress responses that divert transcriptional and translational resources away from recombinant protein synthesis. While numerous strategies have been explored to mitigate this issue—such as the inclusion of insulator elements, chromatin-opening sequences, and enhancer elements—the majority of these approaches have yielded only marginal improvements or have introduced unintended consequences, such as reduced cell viability, altered glycosylation profiles, or increased heterogeneity in product quality.

Recent advances in transcriptomic profiling have enabled the identification of endogenous genes whose expression remains stable or even increases during extended culture periods, suggesting that native cellular promoters may offer superior performance compared to heterologous or synthetic constructs. Among such genes, those involved in the unfolded protein response (UPR) and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress management have garnered particular interest, given the intrinsic link between antibody production and ER burden. The chaperone protein Hspa5, also known as BiP or GRP78, plays a central role in ER homeostasis by facilitating protein folding, preventing aggregation, and regulating the UPR signaling pathways through interactions with PERK, IRE1, and ATF6. While previous studies have demonstrated that overexpression of Hspa5 protein can enhance antibody productivity and reduce aggregation, no prior art has disclosed the use of the Hspa5 gene’s own promoter to drive recombinant antibody expression in a manner that leverages the natural feedback mechanism between antibody production and ER stress.

Moreover, although the Hspa5 promoter has been investigated in the context of selecting high-producing clones or as a reporter for UPR activation, there is no existing disclosure or teaching in the prior art that identifies the Hspa5 promoter as a standalone, industrially applicable tool for achieving sustained, high-level antibody production throughout the entire fed-batch cycle. The promoter’s unique ability to remain active or become increasingly active during the late culture phase—precisely when conventional promoters fail—has not been recognized, exploited, or optimized for industrial bioprocessing. Furthermore, the minimal functional region of the Hspa5 promoter required for optimal performance, its cross-species functionality, and its compatibility with diverse IgG subclasses have not been systematically evaluated or disclosed.

Thus, there remains a critical unmet need in the field for a promoter system that not only initiates high-level transgene expression but also maintains or enhances that expression during the late-phase culture period, thereby maximizing volumetric productivity without compromising cell viability, product quality, or process robustness. The present invention fulfills this need by providing a novel, endogenous, and highly optimized promoter sequence derived from the Chinese hamster Hspa5 gene, which enables unprecedented levels of monoclonal antibody production in CHO cell systems through a mechanism intrinsically linked to the cellular response to the very burden of antibody synthesis.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the persistent and widespread limitation in the industrial production of monoclonal antibodies wherein recombinant gene expression driven by conventional promoters exhibits a substantial and often irreversible decline during the late phase of fed-batch culture, resulting in suboptimal final product titers, increased manufacturing costs, and reduced process consistency. This decline is not attributable to cell death or nutrient depletion alone, but rather stems from transcriptional silencing mechanisms that disproportionately affect heterologous and even some endogenous promoters, leading to a decoupling between cell growth and product formation. As a consequence, manufacturers are forced to extend culture durations, increase inoculum densities, or employ costly feed strategies to compensate for diminished productivity, all of which contribute to process inefficiency and economic burden. There is a clear and urgent need for a promoter system that remains transcriptionally active during the late culture phase, aligns with the physiological state of the cell under high protein-folding demand, and does not compromise cell viability or product quality. The absence of such a system in the prior art represents a significant barrier to achieving consistent, high-yield, and cost-effective manufacturing of therapeutic antibodies at commercial scale.

### Solution to Problem

The solution to this technical problem is provided by the identification, isolation, and utilization of a novel promoter sequence derived from the upstream regulatory region of the Chinese hamster heat shock protein 5 gene (Hspa5), which exhibits sustained and enhanced transcriptional activity throughout the entire fed-batch culture period, including the late phase where conventional promoters fail. This promoter, designated herein as Hspa5p, is characterized by a minimal functional region of approximately 600 base pairs located upstream of the transcription start site, which contains conserved cis-regulatory elements including a TATA-box, a CpG island, a CCAAT-box, a GC-box, cAMP-responsive elements (CREB), and endoplasmic reticulum stress-responsive elements (ERSE). The Hspa5p promoter is operably linked to a recombinant antibody gene in an expression vector, enabling the continuous and high-level transcription of heavy and light chain transcripts even as the culture enters its stationary and late-phase growth stages. Unlike conventional promoters, Hspa5p is not subject to transcriptional silencing; instead, its activity is positively modulated by the cellular stress induced by the overexpression of recombinant antibodies, creating a self-amplifying feedback loop wherein antibody production enhances its own transcriptional drive. This mechanism ensures that the highest levels of transcription coincide with the period of maximal cell density and metabolic capacity, thereby maximizing volumetric yield without requiring external induction or process modifications. The promoter is functionally conserved across species, as evidenced by comparable performance of human, mouse, and rat Hspa5 promoter sequences, but the Chinese hamster-derived variant is preferred for use in CHO cell systems due to its optimal compatibility and absence of xenogeneic regulatory interference. The invention further encompasses the use of this promoter to drive the expression of multiple IgG subclasses—including IgG1, IgG2, and IgG4Pro—without differential effects on productivity, demonstrating its broad applicability across diverse therapeutic antibody formats.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The present invention delivers a multitude of advantageous effects that collectively transform the landscape of monoclonal antibody manufacturing. First and foremost, the use of the Hspa5p promoter results in a marked and statistically significant increase in specific production rate (SPR) and final antibody titer during the late phase of fed-batch culture, with improvements of up to 40% compared to systems employing the human EF1α promoter, the current industry standard. This enhancement is not accompanied by a reduction in cell growth, viability, or metabolic efficiency, nor does it lead to increased aggregation or altered glycosylation profiles, thereby preserving product quality and regulatory compliance. The promoter’s activity is intrinsically linked to the endogenous unfolded protein response, meaning that its induction is naturally synchronized with the cellular capacity to fold and process the recombinant protein, thereby preventing the accumulation of misfolded species and reducing the burden on the secretory pathway. This self-regulating mechanism enhances process robustness and reduces batch-to-batch variability, a critical factor for regulatory approval and commercial manufacturing. Furthermore, the minimal 600-base-pair length of the functional promoter region enables efficient vector construction, reduces the risk of recombination or instability during plasmid amplification, and allows for the inclusion of additional regulatory elements without exceeding practical vector size constraints. The promoter’s cross-species functionality provides flexibility in vector design and facilitates preclinical validation using non-CHO cell models. Importantly, unlike previous approaches that rely on external inducers such as tunicamycin or thapsigargin to enhance ER stress and thereby increase chaperone expression, the Hspa5p promoter achieves superior productivity without the need for chemical additives, which would otherwise introduce regulatory, safety, and purification complications. The invention thus enables a more streamlined, cost-effective, and scalable manufacturing process that is fully compatible with existing CHO-based platforms, requiring no fundamental changes to cell line development, media formulation, or downstream processing protocols. The ability to consistently achieve high titers in the late culture phase also reduces the need for extended culture durations or larger bioreactor volumes, thereby lowering capital and operational expenditures. Collectively, these advantages translate into a substantial improvement in process economics, product yield, and manufacturing reliability, positioning the Hspa5p promoter as a transformative tool for the next generation of biopharmaceutical production systems.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the construction, validation, and application of the Hspa5 promoter in the context of monoclonal antibody production in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells. These examples are provided to demonstrate the operability, reproducibility, and industrial relevance of the invention and are not intended to limit its scope in any manner.

In the first example, a comprehensive transcriptome analysis was performed on two distinct clones of IgG1-expressing CHO-K1 cells cultured in two different serum-free media—custom medium G13 and CD DA1—over a 14-day fed-batch period. Cells were harvested at five time points (days 4, 7, 9, 11, and 14), and total RNA was extracted and subjected to high-throughput sequencing using the Illumina HiSeq platform. The resulting reads were mapped to the Chinese hamster genome, and gene expression levels were normalized using reads per kilobase per million mapped reads (RPKM). Among the top 20 most highly expressed genes on day 4, several housekeeping genes such as gapdh, actβ, and ef1α were identified, as expected. However, the gene encoding heat shock protein 5 (hspa5) exhibited a distinctive pattern: while its expression level on day 4 was not among the highest, it demonstrated a progressive and significant increase in transcriptional abundance throughout the culture period, reaching peak levels on day 14. This behavior contrasted sharply with all other highly expressed genes, which showed a consistent decline in expression after day 7. The unique temporal profile of hspa5 suggested that its promoter might possess regulatory features that respond to prolonged cellular stress, making it a compelling candidate for driving sustained transgene expression.

To evaluate the promoter activity of hspa5 and other top candidate genes, a luciferase reporter assay was conducted. The 3-kilobase upstream regions of each of the top 20 genes were cloned into the pGL4.10 vector upstream of the firefly luciferase gene (luc2), with the human EF1α promoter serving as a positive control. Transient transfection of CHO-K1 cells with these constructs revealed that both the hspa5 and ef1α promoters conferred the highest levels of luciferase activity, with hspa5p showing comparable activity to ef1αp during the early phase (24–48 hours post-transfection). However, when the same constructs were used to generate stably transfected pools, a dramatic divergence in performance was observed. While luciferase expression under the ef1α promoter declined by more than 60% between days 7 and 14, luciferase activity under the hspa5 promoter remained stable and even increased by approximately 30% over the same period. This confirmed that the hspa5 promoter retained its transcriptional competence under prolonged culture conditions, whereas the ef1α promoter succumbed to silencing mechanisms.

To determine whether this enhanced activity translated to actual antibody production, stable CHO-K1 cell pools were generated expressing a full-length IgG1 antibody, with both heavy and light chains under the control of either the hspa5p or ef1αp promoter. The cultures were maintained under identical fed-batch conditions in 125 mL Erlenmeyer flasks using custom medium G13 and feed medium F13. Cell growth profiles, viability, glucose consumption, lactate production, and ammonium accumulation were monitored and found to be statistically indistinguishable between the two systems, indicating that the promoter did not impose an undue metabolic burden. However, quantitative PCR analysis of IgG heavy chain mRNA revealed a 2.3-fold higher transcript level in the hspa5p system on day 14 compared to the ef1αp system. This was corroborated by specific production rate (SPR) measurements, which showed a 35% increase in antibody synthesis per viable cell at day 14. Most significantly, the final IgG titer in the hspa5p system reached 4.8 g/L, compared to 3.4 g/L in the ef1αp control—a 41% improvement that was statistically significant (p < 0.01). These results demonstrated that the hspa5 promoter not only maintained transcriptional activity but actively enhanced antibody production during the late culture phase, directly translating into superior volumetric yield.

In the second example, the minimal functional region of the hspa5 promoter was identified through a series of truncation experiments. Six different promoter fragments—ranging from 3.0 kb to 0.6 kb upstream of the transcription start site—were cloned into the IgG1 expression vector and tested in stable pools. The 3.0 kb fragment conferred high initial activity but did not outperform the 1.0 kb or 0.6 kb fragments in the late phase. Surprisingly, the 0.6 kb fragment consistently yielded the highest SPR and final titer, with no significant difference in performance compared to the 1.0 kb construct. Further truncation to 0.4 kb or less resulted in a sharp decline in activity, indicating that the core functional elements reside within the 0.6 kb region. In silico analysis of this region revealed the presence of a TATA-box at position −28, a CpG island spanning −150 to −350, a CCAAT-box at −85, a GC-box at −120, a CREB-binding site at −210, and two ERSE motifs at −180 and −240. These elements are known to be involved in basal transcription, stress response, and regulation by transcription factors such as ATF4, ATF6, and XBP1. Deletion of any one of these elements individually resulted in a significant reduction in late-phase activity, confirming their collective necessity for the promoter’s unique behavior. The 0.6 kb fragment was therefore selected as the minimal functional promoter for all subsequent experiments.

In the third example, the cross-species functionality of the hspa5 promoter was evaluated. The upstream regions of the hspa5 gene from human, mouse, and rat were cloned into the IgG1 expression vector and tested in parallel with the Chinese hamster-derived promoter. All four constructs produced comparable levels of IgG1 titer and SPR throughout the culture period, with no statistically significant differences observed between day 7 and day 14. This indicated that the critical cis-regulatory elements responsible for sustained activity are evolutionarily conserved across mammalian species. However, the Chinese hamster promoter was retained as the preferred embodiment due to its origin in the host cell line, minimizing the risk of immune recognition or regulatory incompatibility in a manufacturing context. Additionally, the Chinese hamster sequence contains species-specific nucleotide variations that may optimize transcription factor binding affinity in CHO cells, further enhancing its suitability for industrial use.

In the fourth example, the versatility of the Hspa5p promoter was tested across three clinically relevant IgG subclasses: IgG1, IgG2, and IgG4Pro. Each subclass was expressed in CHO-K1 cells under the control of either Hspa5p or the ef1αp promoter. All three subclasses exhibited significantly higher SPR and final titer under Hspa5p control, with improvements ranging from 32% to 45% depending on the subclass. Importantly, the relative enhancement was consistent across all subclasses, despite their differing disulfide bond patterns, which are known to influence folding kinetics and ER stress induction. Quantitative PCR confirmed that the transcriptional advantage conferred by Hspa5p was not due to differential stability of the mRNA transcripts, as the half-lives of the heavy chain mRNAs were identical between the two systems. Furthermore, endogenous hspa5 gene expression remained unchanged across all clones, indicating that the promoter’s activity was driven solely by the recombinant antibody burden and not by global cellular stress. This demonstrated that Hspa5p functions as a universal, sequence-independent enhancer of antibody production, irrespective of the structural characteristics of the target protein.

In the fifth example, the mechanistic link between Hspa5p activity and the unfolded protein response was investigated. Five clonal cell lines expressing IgG1 under Hspa5p control were selected based on their wide range of productivities, from low to high. Transcript levels of key UPR-related genes—including PERK, IRE1, ATF6, XBP1, GRP94, and ERdj3—were measured on days 7 and 10. Strong positive correlations were observed between IgG-specific production rate and the transcriptional levels of PERK (R² = 0.87), IRE1 (R² = 0.82), and ATF6 (R² = 0.79). Similarly, the levels of ER chaperones GRP94 and ERdj3 correlated strongly with the accumulation of high-molecular-weight aggregates (R² = 0.85 and 0.81, respectively). The splicing efficiency of XBP1 mRNA, a direct indicator of IRE1 pathway activation, also showed a significant correlation with both productivity and aggregate formation. These findings support a model in which the overexpression of recombinant antibody induces ER stress, which in turn activates the UPR pathways, leading to the upregulation of Hspa5p through transcription factors such as ATF4 and XBP1. This creates a self-reinforcing loop wherein antibody production stimulates its own transcriptional machinery, resulting in a dynamic equilibrium that maximizes yield without triggering apoptosis. In contrast, the expression of the endogenous hspa5 gene showed only a weak correlation with productivity (R² = 0.31), suggesting that the recombinant promoter is subject to distinct regulatory mechanisms, possibly due to its integration site, copy number, or chromatin context.

In the sixth example, the effect of exogenous ER stress inducers on Hspa5p-driven antibody production was assessed. CHO-K1 cells expressing IgG1 under Hspa5p control were treated with known inducers of ER stress—including dithiothreitol (DTT), tunicamycin, and thapsigargin—on day 7 of culture. While all inducers significantly increased the transcription of the endogenous hspa5 gene, none enhanced the transcription of the recombinant IgG gene under Hspa5p control. In fact, at higher concentrations, these agents led to a reduction in cell viability and a decrease in final titer. This demonstrated that the Hspa5p promoter is not simply responsive to general ER stress but is specifically tuned to respond to the physiological burden of antibody synthesis. Artificially inducing stress without enhancing the protein-folding machinery does not improve productivity and may be detrimental. This further supports the conclusion that the Hspa5p promoter operates through a finely balanced, endogenous feedback mechanism that is optimally activated by the natural conditions of high-level antibody production, rendering it superior to any externally induced system.

In the seventh example, the stability and scalability of the Hspa5p system were validated in a 15 mL small-scale bioreactor under controlled conditions of pH 7.0 ± 0.05, 50% dissolved oxygen, and 850 rpm agitation. The same IgG1-producing clone expressing Hspa5p was compared to the ef1αp control under these more industrially relevant conditions. The results mirrored those observed in shake flasks: Hspa5p maintained a 38% higher SPR and achieved a final titer of 5.1 g/L compared to 3.7 g/L for ef1αp. Product quality analysis by size-exclusion chromatography revealed no increase in high-molecular-weight aggregates or fragmentation products, and glycosylation profiling by LC-MS showed identical N-glycan profiles, including galactosylation, sialylation, and fucosylation levels. This confirmed that the Hspa5p promoter does not compromise product quality, even under tightly controlled, high-density culture conditions.

In the eighth example, monoclonal cell lines derived from the Hspa5p-IgG1 pool were screened for productivity and stability over multiple passages. Ten high-producing clones were isolated and cultured for 60 days in serial fed-batch cycles. All clones maintained stable IgG1 titers above 4.5 g/L, with no evidence of transcriptional silencing or phenotypic drift. In contrast, the best ef1αp clone showed a 22% decline in titer after 40 days. This demonstrated that the Hspa5p promoter confers long-term genetic stability, a critical requirement for commercial cell line development and regulatory approval.

In the ninth example, the Hspa5p promoter was incorporated into a dual-expression vector system containing both heavy and light chain cassettes under separate but identical promoter control. This configuration allowed for independent optimization of chain stoichiometry and eliminated the risk of chain imbalance. The resulting system produced IgG1 at a titer of 5.4 g/L, representing a 59% improvement over the ef1αp control. This further illustrates the flexibility and scalability of the invention for use in complex expression architectures.

In the tenth example, the Hspa5p promoter was tested in a different CHO cell line, CHO-S, and in a suspension-adapted CHO-DG44 line. In both cases, the promoter conferred a 30–40% increase in final titer compared to ef1αp, demonstrating its broad applicability across CHO substrains. This confirms that the invention is not limited to a single cell line or genetic background but represents a generalizable solution for antibody production in mammalian systems.

These examples collectively demonstrate that the Hspa5p promoter is a novel, highly effective, and industrially viable tool for enhancing monoclonal antibody production. Its unique ability to remain active during the late culture phase, its compatibility with diverse IgG subclasses, its stability over extended culture periods, and its lack of negative impact on product quality or cell viability make it superior to all previously known promoters. The invention thus provides a transformative solution to the longstanding problem of transcriptional decline in biopharmaceutical manufacturing, enabling higher yields, lower costs, and greater process reliability.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention possesses substantial and immediate industrial applicability across the entire spectrum of monoclonal antibody manufacturing, from early-stage process development to commercial-scale biopharmaceutical production. The Hspa5 promoter, as disclosed herein, is directly transferable to existing CHO-based cell line development platforms, requiring no modification to standard transfection protocols, selection markers, or culture media. Its minimal 600-base-pair size facilitates seamless integration into plasmid vectors, enabling efficient cloning, high transformation efficiency, and reduced risk of genetic instability during large-scale plasmid amplification. The promoter’s compatibility with both transient and stable expression systems allows for its use in rapid screening of lead candidates during upstream development, as well as in the generation of high-producing, clonally derived cell lines for commercial manufacturing. The consistent enhancement of final antibody titers by 30–40% translates directly into increased manufacturing capacity, reduced bioreactor footprint, lower media and feed consumption, and decreased downstream purification costs—all of which contribute to a significant reduction in the cost of goods sold (COGS) for therapeutic antibodies.

The invention is particularly valuable for the production of high-dose biologics, such as those used in oncology and autoimmune disease treatments, where titers exceeding 4 g/L are often required to meet global demand. By enabling higher yields without extending culture duration, the Hspa5p promoter reduces the risk of product degradation, microbial contamination, and process drift, thereby improving batch consistency and regulatory compliance. The promoter’s intrinsic link to the unfolded protein response ensures that antibody production is naturally synchronized with the cell’s protein-folding capacity, minimizing the formation of aggregates and misfolded species that complicate purification and compromise safety. This feature is especially critical for the production of IgG4 variants, which are prone to structural instability and are increasingly used in next-generation therapeutics.

Furthermore, the cross-species functionality of the Hspa5 promoter allows for its use in preclinical models and non-CHO host systems, facilitating accelerated development timelines and reducing the need for redundant vector construction. The promoter’s stability over multiple passages and its resistance to silencing under prolonged culture conditions make it ideal for the generation of master cell banks and working cell banks, ensuring long-term production consistency across manufacturing campaigns. Regulatory agencies, including the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency, increasingly emphasize process robustness and product quality consistency, and the Hspa5p system directly addresses these requirements by providing a genetically stable, self-regulating, and high-yielding expression platform.

The invention is also compatible with current industry trends toward continuous manufacturing, perfusion culture, and single-use bioreactors, as the promoter’s sustained activity eliminates the need for frequent cell line re-engineering or process re-optimization. It can be readily combined with other productivity-enhancing technologies, such as CRISPR-mediated gene editing, epigenetic modifiers, or metabolic engineering, without interference, thereby serving as a foundational component of next-generation cell line platforms. The absence of chemical inducers or xenogeneic elements further enhances its regulatory acceptability, as no additional safety or toxicology assessments are required beyond those already performed for the antibody product itself.

In summary, the Hspa5 promoter represents a breakthrough in biopharmaceutical manufacturing technology, offering a simple, scalable, and economically transformative solution to one of the most persistent challenges in the field. Its industrial applicability is not hypothetical but demonstrable, reproducible, and immediately implementable in any commercial antibody production facility, making it a cornerstone innovation for the future of biologics manufacturing.
