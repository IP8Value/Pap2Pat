## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and targeted protein quantification, specifically to the selective isolation and precise measurement of activated transcription factors within complex biological matrices. More particularly, the invention encompasses a novel class of single-stranded DNA aptamers that bind with high specificity and affinity to the activated form of the nuclear factor kappa B subunit RelA (p65), excluding its inactive complex with the inhibitory protein IκBα. The invention further encompasses methods for utilizing these aptamers as affinity capture agents in tandem with stable isotope dilution selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry to quantify the absolute abundance of activated RelA in subcellular compartments of mammalian cells, including but not limited to human epithelial, immune, and inflammatory cell types. The invention also provides for the use of these aptamers in the context of chromatin immunoprecipitation, confocal imaging, and functional inhibition of NF-κB-dependent gene expression, enabling both diagnostic and therapeutic applications in diseases characterized by aberrant NF-κB signaling, such as chronic inflammation, autoimmune disorders, and certain cancers. The disclosed compositions and methods represent a significant advancement over existing antibody-based and DNA-binding site competition approaches by enabling the selective detection of a functionally active protein conformation rather than total protein levels, thereby providing a direct readout of biological activity rather than mere abundance.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathway is a central regulator of innate and adaptive immune responses, inflammation, cell survival, and proliferation. Among its key components, the RelA subunit, also known as p65, serves as the primary transcriptional activator responsible for inducing the expression of hundreds of genes involved in cytokine production, anti-apoptotic signaling, and acute-phase responses. In resting cells, RelA is sequestered in the cytoplasm through its tight association with the inhibitor protein IκBα, which masks its nuclear localization signal and prevents its translocation into the nucleus. Upon stimulation by pro-inflammatory signals such as tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFα), interleukin-1, or pathogen-associated molecular patterns, the IκB kinase complex is activated, leading to the phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and subsequent proteasomal degradation of IκBα. This liberation of RelA permits its rapid nuclear translocation, where it binds to specific κB DNA sequences in the promoters of target genes, recruits co-activators such as p300 and CBP, and initiates transcription. The dynamic nature of this pathway—characterized by rapid nuclear entry, transient DNA binding, feedback inhibition via IκBα resynthesis, and nuclear export—has made precise quantification of the activated, DNA-binding competent form of RelA exceptionally challenging using conventional biochemical methods.

Traditional approaches to studying RelA activation have relied heavily on immunoblotting of subcellular fractions, electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSAs), and chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) using antibodies against RelA or its phosphorylated forms. While these techniques have provided valuable insights, they suffer from significant limitations. Antibodies often recognize epitopes that are accessible regardless of the functional state of the protein, meaning they cannot distinguish between IκBα-bound, inactive RelA and free, activated RelA. EMSAs, though capable of detecting DNA-binding activity, require nuclear extracts, are low-throughput, and are susceptible to non-specific protein-DNA interactions that obscure true biological signals. ChIP assays, while powerful for mapping genomic binding sites, are labor-intensive, require large numbers of cells, and provide only relative enrichment values that are dependent on antibody efficiency and chromatin cross-linking artifacts. Furthermore, none of these methods permit absolute quantification of the number of activated RelA molecules per cell, a critical parameter for understanding the stoichiometry of signaling events and for developing predictive models of pathway dynamics.

Recent advances in mass spectrometry have enabled the absolute quantification of proteins using stable isotope-labeled internal standards and selected reaction monitoring (SRM). However, the application of SRM to low-abundance transcription factors like RelA in complex cellular lysates remains problematic due to overwhelming background noise from highly abundant proteins, poor ionization efficiency of nuclear proteins, and the presence of post-translationally modified isoforms that complicate peptide assignment. Attempts to enrich RelA prior to mass spectrometric analysis have typically employed affinity tags, such as FLAG or HA epitopes, which require genetic overexpression and are not applicable to endogenous protein analysis in primary tissues or clinical samples. Moreover, even when overexpression is employed, these tags do not distinguish between the activated and inactive states of the protein, rendering them incapable of reporting on functional activity.

In parallel, nucleic acid aptamers—short, single-stranded oligonucleotides selected through systematic evolution of ligands by exponential enrichment (SELEX)—have emerged as promising alternatives to antibodies due to their high specificity, chemical stability, and ease of modification. However, despite their success in targeting enzymes, cytokines, and cell surface receptors, there has been no prior report of a DNA aptamer capable of selectively recognizing the activated form of a transcription factor in a manner that discriminates against its inhibitory complex. In particular, no aptamer has been described that binds RelA only when it is dissociated from IκBα, thereby serving as a molecular sensor of pathway activation. The absence of such a tool has hindered the development of robust, quantitative assays for activated RelA in clinical specimens, where the distinction between total and activated protein levels is essential for predicting disease progression, response to therapy, and patient stratification.

Thus, there remains a critical unmet need in the field for a reagent that can selectively isolate, detect, and quantify the activated, IκBα-free form of RelA in complex biological samples with high sensitivity, specificity, and quantitative accuracy. Such a reagent would enable the direct measurement of pathway activity rather than mere protein abundance, facilitate the discovery of novel regulators of NF-κB activation, and provide a platform for diagnostic and therapeutic interventions in diseases driven by dysregulated NF-κB signaling. The present invention fulfills this need by providing a novel DNA aptamer and a suite of methods based on its unique binding properties.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel single-stranded DNA aptamer, designated herein as P028F4, which binds with high affinity and specificity to the activated form of the nuclear factor kappa B subunit RelA, while exhibiting no detectable binding to RelA when it is complexed with its inhibitory protein IκBα. The aptamer comprises a 30-nucleotide variable sequence flanked by conserved primer binding regions, wherein the variable region contains a core motif that is essential for recognition of the activated conformation of RelA. The aptamer is capable of competing with natural double-stranded κB DNA binding sites for interaction with RelA, thereby functionally inhibiting its ability to bind DNA and activate transcription. The aptamer exhibits a dissociation constant (K_D) of approximately 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ M when binding to recombinant RelA, as determined by surface plasmon resonance, and demonstrates exceptional specificity in complex cellular environments, as evidenced by its ability to selectively enrich activated RelA from whole-cell lysates without co-isolating IκBα or other non-target proteins.

The invention further provides a method for quantifying the absolute abundance of activated RelA in a biological sample by combining the aptamer with stable isotope dilution selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry. This method involves the incubation of a cell lysate with a biotinylated form of the aptamer, capture of the aptamer-RelA complex on streptavidin-coated magnetic beads, on-bead tryptic digestion of the bound proteins, spiking of the digest with a known quantity of stable isotope-labeled signature peptides derived from RelA, and quantitative analysis by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry in selected reaction monitoring mode. The method enables the precise determination of the number of activated RelA molecules per cell, with a lower limit of quantification of 200 attomoles per sample and a dynamic range spanning over three orders of magnitude. The method is applicable to both cytoplasmic and nuclear fractions, and has been validated in human epithelial, monocytic, and lymphoid cell lines, as well as in primary human tissue samples.

The invention further provides a method for isolating and characterizing protein complexes associated with activated RelA, wherein the aptamer is used as an affinity reagent to enrich not only free RelA but also its transiently associated co-factors, such as p300 and phosphorylated kinases, while excluding inhibitory proteins such as IκBα. This tandem affinity purification strategy allows for the identification of novel interaction partners of activated RelA and provides a platform for mapping the composition of the transcriptional activation complex in real time.

The invention further provides a method for inhibiting NF-κB-dependent gene expression in living cells by introducing the aptamer into the cytoplasm or nucleus, where it competes with endogenous κB DNA elements for binding to activated RelA, thereby preventing its transcriptional activity. The aptamer, when delivered via transfection or other nucleic acid delivery methods, reduces the expression of downstream target genes such as Groβ and TNFAIP3/A20 without affecting housekeeping genes, demonstrating its utility as a functional antagonist of the NF-κB pathway.

The invention further provides a method for visualizing the subcellular localization of activated RelA in fixed cells using fluorescently labeled versions of the aptamer, wherein the aptamer colocalizes with nuclear RelA only after cellular stimulation, providing a direct, label-free readout of pathway activation without the need for antibodies or genetic tagging.

The invention further provides a kit comprising the aptamer, biotinylated aptamer, streptavidin magnetic beads, tryptic digestion buffer, stable isotope-labeled RelA signature peptides, and instructions for performing the quantitative assay. The kit is configured for use in research, diagnostic, and pharmaceutical development settings to measure the activated fraction of RelA in clinical samples such as peripheral blood mononuclear cells, synovial fluid, tumor biopsies, and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid.

The invention represents a paradigm shift in the study of transcription factor activation by enabling the direct, quantitative, and functional assessment of a key signaling molecule in its biologically active state, thereby providing unprecedented insight into the dynamics of intracellular signaling networks.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is directed to a novel single-stranded DNA aptamer, designated P028F4, which binds with high specificity and affinity to the activated form of the nuclear factor kappa B subunit RelA, while exhibiting no detectable binding to RelA when it is complexed with its inhibitory protein IκBα. The aptamer is selected from a randomized DNA library through iterative rounds of binding, partitioning, and amplification, with stringent counter-selection against IκBα-bound RelA and non-specific nucleic acid-binding proteins. The resulting aptamer consists of a 30-nucleotide variable region flanked by 25-nucleotide conserved primer binding sequences, wherein the variable region contains a core sequence motif that is indispensable for recognition of the activated conformation of RelA. The sequence of the variable region, as determined by sequencing of individual clones and confirmed by synthetic oligonucleotide synthesis, is GGGAC, which corresponds to half of the canonical NF-κB binding site GGGACTTTCC. However, unlike double-stranded DNA, the aptamer exists as a single strand and does not form a stable duplex, indicating that its binding is not based on Watson-Crick complementarity but rather on a three-dimensional structural interaction with a specific surface on the RelA protein. This surface is only exposed when RelA is dissociated from IκBα, explaining the aptamer’s exquisite selectivity for the activated state. The aptamer binds to recombinant RelA with a dissociation constant of approximately 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ M, as measured by surface plasmon resonance, and exhibits a slow off-rate (k_d = 0.0018 s⁻¹), making it particularly suitable for affinity capture applications. The aptamer does not bind to other members of the NF-κB family, including p50, c-Rel, RelB, or p52, nor does it bind to unrelated transcription factors such as AP-1, STAT3, or NFAT, demonstrating its high degree of specificity. When tested against a panel of 50 non-target DNA sequences, including random sequences, scrambled variants, and known binding sites for other transcription factors, the aptamer showed no significant binding, confirming that its interaction is highly selective for the activated form of RelA.

The invention further encompasses a method for the quantitative measurement of activated RelA in biological samples, wherein the aptamer is employed as an affinity capture agent in conjunction with stable isotope dilution selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry. The method begins with the preparation of a cell lysate, which may be derived from any mammalian cell type, including but not limited to human epithelial cells, immune cells, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, or primary tissue samples. The lysate may be fractionated into cytoplasmic and nuclear compartments to enable compartment-specific quantification. The lysate is then incubated with a biotinylated form of the aptamer at a concentration of 20 nanomolar for a period of two hours at four degrees Celsius, under conditions that preserve protein-protein interactions and prevent non-specific binding. During this incubation, the aptamer selectively binds to activated, IκBα-free RelA, forming a stable complex. Following incubation, a slurry of streptavidin-coated magnetic beads, pre-blocked with acetylated casein to minimize non-specific protein adsorption, is added to the mixture. The beads are then incubated for an additional twenty minutes at four degrees Celsius to allow the biotinylated aptamer-RelA complexes to bind to the streptavidin surface. The beads are subsequently washed six times with a buffer containing 150 millimolar sodium chloride, 20 millimolar HEPES at pH 7.4, and 0.01% Triton X-100 to remove unbound proteins, non-specifically bound nucleic acids, and residual cellular debris. After washing, the beads are resuspended in a solution of 50 millimolar ammonium hydrogen carbonate at pH 7.8, and trypsin is added at a concentration of 0.1 micrograms per microliter. The sample is then incubated overnight at thirty-seven degrees Celsius with gentle shaking to allow for on-bead proteolytic digestion of the captured proteins. The resulting tryptic peptides are eluted from the beads by three washes with 50% acetonitrile in water, and the supernatants are pooled and dried under vacuum. The dried peptide digest is then reconstituted in a solution of 5% formic acid and 0.01% trifluoroacetic acid, and a known quantity of stable isotope-labeled internal standard peptides corresponding to signature sequences of RelA is added. These internal standards, which contain [¹³C₆¹⁵N₄] arginine at a 98% isotopic enrichment, are synthesized using solid-phase peptide chemistry and purified to greater than 98% homogeneity by high-performance liquid chromatography. The signature peptides selected for quantification include TPPYADPSLQAPVR, VFRTPPYADPSLQAPVRVSM, and other tryptic fragments that are unique to RelA, contain no missed cleavages, and exhibit high ionization efficiency in mass spectrometry. The final sample is then analyzed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry using a triple quadrupole instrument operating in selected reaction monitoring mode. The chromatographic separation is performed using a reverse-phase nano-HPLC column with a 20-minute linear gradient from 2% to 40% acetonitrile in 0.1% formic acid, followed by a brief wash at 90% acetonitrile. The mass spectrometer is operated in high-resolution SRM mode, with Q1 and Q3 resolution set to 0.2 and 0.7 Da full width at half maximum, respectively. For each signature peptide, four to five precursor-to-product ion transitions are monitored, and the peak areas for both the natural and isotope-labeled peptides are integrated. The ratio of the natural peptide signal to the internal standard signal is calculated, and the absolute quantity of activated RelA is determined by interpolation against a calibration curve generated by spiking known amounts of unlabeled RelA peptide into a blank matrix and measuring the resulting ratios. The method has been validated to exhibit a linear dynamic range spanning from 200 attomoles to 20 femtomoles of RelA peptide loaded onto the column, with a coefficient of determination greater than 0.997, and a coefficient of variation less than 10% across replicate analyses. The lower limit of quantification is defined as 200 attomoles, corresponding to approximately 50,000 molecules of activated RelA per cell, assuming a typical cell volume of 4 picoliters and a total protein content of 100 micrograms per million cells. The method has been successfully applied to quantify activated RelA in both cytoplasmic and nuclear fractions of human A549 lung epithelial cells, demonstrating that in unstimulated cells, approximately 5% of total cytoplasmic RelA is in the activated state, while after TNFα stimulation, the amount of activated RelA in the cytoplasm increases sixfold and becomes equivalent to the total amount of RelA in the nucleus, indicating that nearly all nuclear RelA is in the activated form. Importantly, no IκBα peptides were detected in any of the aptamer-enriched samples, confirming the specificity of the capture and the absence of contaminating inhibitory complexes.

The invention further provides a method for the isolation and characterization of protein complexes associated with activated RelA, wherein the aptamer is used as a first affinity reagent in a tandem purification strategy. In this method, cells are first transfected with a FLAG-tagged version of RelA, and whole-cell lysates are prepared. The lysate is then incubated with anti-FLAG affinity resin to capture FLAG-RelA and its associated proteins. After washing, the bound material is eluted with excess 3× FLAG peptide. The eluate is then subjected to a second round of affinity purification using the biotinylated P028F4 aptamer and streptavidin magnetic beads. This two-step procedure results in the enrichment of only those RelA molecules that are both FLAG-tagged and in the activated, IκBα-free state. Western blot analysis of the final eluate reveals the presence of phospho-Ser536 RelA, a marker of kinase-mediated activation, as well as the histone acetyltransferase p300, which is recruited to RelA during transcriptional activation. In contrast, IκBα is completely absent from the final eluate, confirming that the aptamer excludes inhibitory complexes. Mass spectrometric analysis of the tandem purified material identifies additional proteins that co-purify with activated RelA, including kinases, ubiquitin ligases, and chromatin remodelers, thereby providing a comprehensive profile of the activated RelA interactome. This method enables the discovery of novel regulators of NF-κB signaling and provides a platform for functional validation of candidate interaction partners.

The invention further provides a method for the functional inhibition of NF-κB-dependent gene expression in living cells, wherein the aptamer is introduced into the cytoplasm or nucleus of mammalian cells via transfection, electroporation, or lipid-mediated delivery. Cells are transfected with synthetic, chemically modified versions of the aptamer at a concentration of four micromolar, and then stimulated with TNFα. Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction analysis of downstream target genes reveals a significant reduction in the induction of Groβ and TNFAIP3/A20 transcripts, with induction levels reduced from fivefold to less than twofold in the case of Groβ and from sevenfold to less than twofold in the case of TNFAIP3/A20. In contrast, transfection with a control aptamer lacking the critical GGGAC motif has no significant effect on gene expression. Importantly, the expression of housekeeping genes such as GAPDH and β-actin remains unchanged, indicating that the aptamer does not exert non-specific effects on global transcription. The mechanism of inhibition is believed to involve direct competition between the aptamer and endogenous κB DNA binding sites for occupancy by activated RelA, thereby preventing the recruitment of transcriptional machinery to target promoters. This functional inhibition is corroborated by chromatin immunoprecipitation assays, which show a significant reduction in the binding of RelA to the promoters of Groβ and TNFAIP3/A20 in aptamer-treated cells following TNFα stimulation. The aptamer thus functions as a potent and specific transcriptional antagonist of NF-κB, offering a novel therapeutic strategy for diseases driven by chronic NF-κB activation.

The invention further provides a method for the visualization of activated RelA in fixed cells using fluorescently labeled versions of the aptamer. In this method, cells expressing a fluorescently tagged version of RelA, such as EGFP-RelA, are stimulated with TNFα and then fixed with paraformaldehyde. Nuclei are isolated and mounted on slides, and then incubated overnight at four degrees Celsius with a TEX 615-conjugated version of the aptamer at a concentration of five nanomolar. After washing, the nuclei are counterstained with DRAQ5, a DNA-binding dye, and imaged using confocal microscopy. In unstimulated cells, no fluorescent signal is detected from the aptamer, even though EGFP-RelA is present in the cytoplasm. In TNFα-stimulated cells, however, a strong co-localization of the red TEX 615 signal from the aptamer and the green EGFP signal from RelA is observed in the nucleus, producing a distinct yellow signal in merged images. This co-localization is not observed with a control aptamer lacking the GGGAC motif, confirming that the signal is specific to activated RelA. The method provides a direct, antibody-free means of visualizing the spatial and temporal dynamics of RelA activation in individual cells, enabling high-resolution imaging of pathway activation in response to stimuli, drug treatments, or genetic perturbations.

The invention further provides a kit for the quantitative measurement of activated RelA in biological samples. The kit comprises a lyophilized form of the biotinylated P028F4 aptamer, streptavidin-coated magnetic beads pre-blocked with acetylated casein, a buffer formulation optimized for aptamer binding and washing, a set of stable isotope-labeled signature peptides corresponding to RelA, a tryptic digestion buffer, and a detailed protocol for performing the quantitative assay. The kit further includes a calibration curve standard containing known concentrations of unlabeled RelA peptide spiked into a blank matrix, as well as a negative control aptamer lacking the GGGAC motif. The kit is configured for use in research laboratories, diagnostic centers, and pharmaceutical development facilities, and is suitable for processing clinical samples such as peripheral blood mononuclear cells, synovial fluid, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid, and formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue sections. The kit enables the rapid, reproducible, and quantitative assessment of NF-κB pathway activation in patient samples, facilitating personalized medicine approaches in inflammatory and autoimmune diseases.

The invention further provides for the use of the aptamer in the context of high-throughput screening for modulators of RelA activation. Libraries of small molecules, peptides, or nucleic acids can be screened for their ability to disrupt the aptamer-RelA interaction, thereby identifying compounds that either stabilize the inactive state of RelA or promote its dissociation from IκBα. Screening is performed by immobilizing the biotinylated aptamer on streptavidin-coated plates, adding recombinant RelA, and measuring binding in the presence or absence of test compounds using a fluorescence polarization or luminescence-based readout. Compounds that inhibit binding are identified as potential inhibitors of RelA activation, while compounds that enhance binding may represent activators of the pathway.
