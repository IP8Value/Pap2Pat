# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of cardiovascular therapeutics and, more specifically, to the use of a novel acidic polysaccharide fraction derived from Panax ginseng for the prevention and treatment of myocardial injury resulting from ischemia-reperfusion events. The invention encompasses the isolation, characterization, and therapeutic application of a specific acidic heteroglycan, designated AP1, which exhibits potent cardioprotective properties by modulating mitochondrial integrity, suppressing oxidative stress, and activating key cell survival signaling pathways including the RISK pathway and endothelial nitric oxide synthase. This invention is particularly relevant to the development of pharmaceutical compositions, nutraceutical formulations, and functional food products intended for the prophylactic or therapeutic management of ischemic heart disease, acute myocardial infarction, and related reperfusion injuries in humans and other mammals. The composition and method of administration described herein represent a significant advancement over existing interventions, as they target multiple pathological mechanisms simultaneously—oxidative damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptotic signaling—without inducing significant cytotoxicity or adverse systemic effects, thereby offering a safer and more comprehensive approach to myocardial protection than conventional pharmacological agents or crude herbal extracts.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ischemic heart disease remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, with millions of deaths annually attributed to myocardial infarction and its complications. While reperfusion therapy—such as percutaneous coronary intervention or thrombolytic administration—is the standard of care for restoring blood flow to ischemic myocardium, it paradoxically triggers a cascade of injurious events known as reperfusion injury. This phenomenon, characterized by an abrupt surge in reactive oxygen species, calcium overload, mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening, inflammation, and activation of intrinsic apoptotic pathways, often negates the benefits of timely revascularization and contributes significantly to long-term cardiac remodeling and heart failure. Despite decades of research, no clinically approved agent has been able to reliably mitigate reperfusion injury without unacceptable side effects, leaving a critical unmet medical need in cardiovascular medicine. Traditional approaches have focused on antioxidants, calcium channel blockers, and anti-inflammatory agents, but their clinical efficacy has been inconsistent, largely due to their narrow mechanistic scope and inability to address the integrated nature of cellular damage during reperfusion. 

Natural products derived from traditional medicinal systems have long been explored for their potential in cardiovascular protection, with Panax ginseng being one of the most extensively studied. The pharmacological profile of ginseng has historically been attributed to ginsenosides, a class of steroidal saponins that modulate ion channels, vascular tone, and inflammatory mediators. However, the aqueous-soluble fraction of ginseng, rich in polysaccharides, has been systematically overlooked in modern pharmaceutical development, often discarded during extraction processes as non-active impurities or contaminants. This oversight is particularly striking given the well-documented biological activities of polysaccharides isolated from other medicinal plants, including immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and cardioprotective effects. Prior studies have demonstrated that polysaccharides from Aralia elata, Ophiopogon japonicus, and Salvia miltiorrhiza can attenuate myocardial injury in experimental models, yet no such evidence has been established for ginseng-derived polysaccharides. Moreover, the molecular identity, structural characteristics, and precise signaling mechanisms underlying any potential cardioprotective activity of ginseng polysaccharides have remained entirely uncharacterized. Consequently, the therapeutic potential of this underexplored class of compounds has remained untapped, despite their inherent biocompatibility, low toxicity, and potential for oral bioavailability. The absence of a defined, purified, and mechanistically validated polysaccharide fraction from ginseng has hindered its translation into reproducible clinical interventions. Furthermore, existing ginseng-based formulations lack standardization, often varying in composition due to differences in cultivation, extraction methods, and processing, which undermines their reliability and regulatory acceptance. There is therefore a compelling need for a precisely defined, bioactive, and reproducible ginseng-derived compound that can be formulated into a therapeutically effective agent capable of targeting the complex pathophysiology of ischemia-reperfusion injury with precision, safety, and consistency.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses a novel acidic polysaccharide fraction, designated AP1, derived from the root of Panax ginseng, which demonstrates a potent and reproducible cardioprotective effect against myocardial injury induced by ischemia and reperfusion. This fraction, characterized as an acidic heteroglycan with a molecular weight of approximately 3.8 × 10⁴ Da, consists predominantly of glucose (76.31%), galactose (12.70%), and minor amounts of galacturonic acid (5.64%), arabinose (3.72%), and glucuronic acid (1.64%), with a uronic acid content of 17.93% and a protein content of less than 10%. The AP1 fraction is isolated through a multi-step purification process involving high-speed counter-current chromatography, stepwise ethanol precipitation, and anion-exchange chromatography, resulting in a highly purified and structurally defined compound that is substantially free from ginsenosides and other low-molecular-weight contaminants. The invention demonstrates that AP1, when administered prior to or during reperfusion, significantly preserves cardiomyocyte viability, reduces the release of cardiac injury biomarkers such as lactate dehydrogenase and creatine kinase, suppresses intracellular and mitochondrial reactive oxygen species accumulation, enhances superoxide dismutase activity, and prevents oxidative damage as evidenced by reduced malondialdehyde levels. Mechanistically, AP1 activates the receptor-mediated RISK signaling pathway by upregulating glucocorticoid and estrogen receptors, which in turn stimulates the phosphorylation of phosphoinositide 3-kinase, Akt, and extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2, leading to the activation of endothelial nitric oxide synthase and the subsequent production of nitric oxide. This signaling cascade not only inhibits the mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening and stabilizes mitochondrial membrane potential but also restores ATP synthesis, prevents cytochrome c release, and suppresses caspase-3/7 and caspase-9 activation, thereby blocking the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. The cardioprotective effect of AP1 is entirely dependent on this integrated signaling network, as pharmacological inhibition of glucocorticoid receptor, estrogen receptor, phosphoinositide 3-kinase, Akt, extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2, or endothelial nitric oxide synthase completely abolishes its protective activity. Importantly, AP1 exhibits no cytotoxicity at therapeutic concentrations and maintains its efficacy across a broad dose range, with optimal activity observed at 200 μg/mL in cellular models. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions, nutraceutical formulations, and functional food products containing AP1 as the sole active ingredient or as a key component in combination with other cardioprotective agents, for oral, intravenous, or sustained-release administration. The invention provides a novel, naturally derived, mechanism-based therapeutic strategy for the prevention and treatment of ischemia-reperfusion injury, offering a significant advancement over existing therapies by simultaneously targeting oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptotic signaling through a single, well-characterized, and non-toxic compound.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is directed to a purified acidic polysaccharide fraction, designated AP1, isolated from the root of Panax ginseng, which possesses a unique combination of structural and functional properties that confer potent and specific cardioprotective activity against ischemia-reperfusion injury. The AP1 fraction is not a crude extract, nor is it a mixture of polysaccharides; rather, it is a chemically defined, homogeneous, and reproducible compound obtained through a sequence of precisely controlled purification steps that eliminate ginsenosides, phenolic compounds, and other low-molecular-weight contaminants commonly found in conventional ginseng preparations. The isolation process begins with the extraction of dried ginseng root using ethanol, followed by concentration and partitioning to yield a crude aqueous-soluble fraction enriched in polysaccharides. This fraction is then subjected to high-speed counter-current chromatography using a biphasic solvent system of butanol and water in a 1:1 volume ratio, which separates the complex mixture into discrete fractions based on differential partitioning coefficients. Among these fractions, two specific eluents—designated as fractions 2 and 3—demonstrate the highest cardioprotective activity in hypoxia/reoxygenation models of rat cardiomyocytes. These fractions are combined and subjected to sequential ethanol precipitation at concentrations of 90%, 80%, and 70% v/v, each step selectively precipitating polysaccharides of increasing molecular weight and polarity. The precipitate obtained at 70% ethanol is identified as the most biologically active fraction and is subsequently purified by anion-exchange chromatography using Toyopearl DEAE 650 M resin. Elution is performed in a stepwise manner using water, followed by solutions of 0.5 M, 1.0 M, 2.0 M sodium chloride, and finally 0.2 M sodium hydroxide, yielding five distinct fractions: neutral polysaccharides, and four acidic polysaccharide fractions designated AP1 through AP4. Of these, AP1 is identified as the most potent cardioprotective constituent, accounting for approximately 39.39% of the total 70% ethanol precipitate. The AP1 fraction is then dialyzed against a 1000 Da molecular weight cutoff membrane to remove residual salts and small organic molecules, followed by lyophilization to obtain a stable, dry powder. This final product is characterized as an acidic heteroglycan with an apparent molecular weight of 3.8 × 10⁴ Da, as determined by high-performance gel permeation chromatography, and contains 76.31% glucose, 12.70% galactose, 5.64% galacturonic acid, 3.72% arabinose, and 1.64% glucuronic acid, as determined by high-performance liquid chromatography after acid hydrolysis and derivatization with phenylthiocarbamoyl. The uronic acid content, a key structural determinant of its biological activity, is measured at 17.93% by the m-hydroxydiphenyl method, and the protein content is confirmed to be below 10% by the Bradford assay, indicating minimal contamination by glycoproteins. The AP1 fraction is substantially free of ginsenosides, as confirmed by high-performance liquid chromatography with mass spectrometry, and exhibits no cytotoxicity in normal cardiomyocytes at concentrations ranging from 6.25 to 800 μg/mL, as demonstrated by mitochondrial viability assays and lactate dehydrogenase release measurements under normoxic conditions.

The cardioprotective efficacy of AP1 is demonstrated in an in vitro model of hypoxia/reoxygenation injury using H9c2 rat cardiomyocytes, a well-established cellular surrogate for studying ischemia-reperfusion injury. In this model, cells are subjected to three hours of hypoxia in a sealed chamber flushed with nitrogen, followed by four hours of reoxygenation in normoxic conditions, which recapitulates the key features of myocardial reperfusion injury, including loss of cell viability, membrane disruption, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis. Pretreatment of cells with AP1 at concentrations as low as 12.5 μg/mL significantly enhances cell survival, with maximal protection observed at 200 μg/mL, surpassing the efficacy of the crude ginseng extract and the positive control drug diazoxide, a mitochondrial potassium channel opener. This protection is evidenced by a dose-dependent increase in mitochondrial viability, as measured by fluorescent staining, and a corresponding reduction in the release of lactate dehydrogenase and creatine kinase, biomarkers of cellular membrane integrity and myocardial damage. Morphological assessment reveals that AP1-treated cells maintain their characteristic filamentous, adherent morphology, in contrast to the rounded, detached appearance of untreated reoxygenated cells. Furthermore, AP1 significantly reduces the percentage of apoptotic cells, as quantified by Annexin V/propidium iodide staining, and suppresses the activation of caspase-3/7 and caspase-9, key executioners of the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway. The suppression of apoptosis is accompanied by the preservation of mitochondrial membrane potential, as measured by rhodamine 123 uptake, and the restoration of intracellular ATP levels, indicating that AP1 maintains mitochondrial bioenergetic function during reperfusion stress. The fraction also enhances mitochondrial respiratory capacity, as demonstrated by increased basal respiration, ATP-linked respiration, maximal respiratory capacity, and spare respiratory capacity, all measured using the Seahorse XFp Extracellular Flux Analyzer. These findings collectively demonstrate that AP1 not only prevents cell death but actively restores the functional integrity of the mitochondrial respiratory chain, a critical determinant of long-term cardiac recovery.

The mechanism underlying this multifaceted protection involves the coordinated activation of a receptor-mediated signaling cascade known as the RISK pathway, which includes phosphoinositide 3-kinase, Akt, and extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2, culminating in the activation of endothelial nitric oxide synthase and the production of nitric oxide. AP1 treatment is shown to upregulate the expression of glucocorticoid and estrogen receptors, which are known to initiate cardioprotective signaling in response to ischemic stress. Pharmacological inhibition of either receptor using mifepristone or tamoxifen completely abolishes the protective effect of AP1, confirming their essential role. Downstream, AP1 induces the phosphorylation of phosphoinositide 3-kinase and Akt, as well as extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2, in a time- and dose-dependent manner. Inhibition of phosphoinositide 3-kinase with LY294002, Akt with Akt inhibitor IV, or extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 with U0126 each eliminates the cardioprotective effect of AP1, demonstrating that the RISK pathway is indispensable to its action. Critically, AP1 also enhances the phosphorylation of endothelial nitric oxide synthase, leading to increased production of nitric oxide, as measured by the Griess reaction. The inhibition of nitric oxide synthase with L-NAME completely abrogates the protective effect of AP1, indicating that nitric oxide is the final effector molecule mediating its cardioprotection. Nitric oxide, in turn, exerts multiple beneficial effects: it acts as a direct scavenger of reactive oxygen species, inhibits the opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore, stabilizes mitochondrial membrane potential, and suppresses the release of cytochrome c from mitochondria into the cytosol. The suppression of cytochrome c release is confirmed by Western blot analysis of cytosolic and mitochondrial fractions, which shows that AP1 prevents the translocation of cytochrome c during reoxygenation. This inhibition of mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization is further supported by the restoration of the Bcl-2 to Bax ratio, a key regulator of mitochondrial apoptosis. AP1 also reduces the expression of inducible nitric oxide synthase, which is upregulated during reperfusion and contributes to oxidative and nitrosative stress, thereby shifting the balance from cytotoxic to protective nitric oxide signaling. In addition to its effects on signaling and apoptosis, AP1 significantly reduces intracellular and mitochondrial reactive oxygen species, as measured by dichlorofluorescein and mitoSOX fluorescence, and attenuates oxidative damage by increasing superoxide dismutase activity and decreasing malondialdehyde levels, a marker of lipid peroxidation. The suppression of oxidative stress is further linked to the inhibition of stress-activated protein kinases, including c-Jun N-terminal kinase and p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase, both of which are activated by reactive oxygen species and promote apoptosis through transcriptional upregulation of pro-apoptotic genes. AP1 treatment markedly reduces the phosphorylation of these kinases, thereby interrupting the oxidative stress-apoptosis axis. The interdependence of these pathways is demonstrated by the fact that inhibition of upstream components—glucocorticoid receptor, estrogen receptor, phosphoinositide 3-kinase, Akt, extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2, or endothelial nitric oxide synthase—not only abolishes the activation of downstream effectors but also prevents the reduction in reactive oxygen species, the preservation of mitochondrial membrane potential, and the suppression of caspase activation. This integrated signaling network, initiated by AP1 binding to glucocorticoid and estrogen receptors and propagated through the RISK pathway to endothelial nitric oxide synthase, constitutes the primary mechanism by which AP1 confers cardioprotection. The specificity and reproducibility of this mechanism, coupled with the absence of cytotoxicity and the structural homogeneity of the AP1 fraction, distinguish this invention from prior art involving crude herbal extracts or undefined polysaccharide mixtures.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions and formulations designed for the delivery of AP1 in a therapeutically effective manner. The AP1 fraction may be formulated as a solid dosage form, including tablets, capsules, or enteric-coated pills, for oral administration, or as a sterile aqueous solution for intravenous infusion. The composition may be combined with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, stabilizers, or bioavailability enhancers such as cyclodextrins, liposomes, or polyethylene glycol derivatives to improve solubility, stability, and tissue penetration. The AP1 fraction may also be incorporated into functional food products, dietary supplements, or nutraceuticals for long-term prophylactic use in individuals at risk for ischemic heart disease, such as those with hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, or a history of myocardial infarction. The recommended daily dose ranges from 5 mg to 500 mg of purified AP1, depending on the route of administration and the clinical indication. For acute intervention, such as during percutaneous coronary intervention or cardiac surgery, intravenous administration of 1 to 10 mg/kg body weight is contemplated, with infusion initiated prior to reperfusion and continued for up to two hours thereafter. For chronic prophylaxis, oral administration of 50 to 200 mg per day is preferred, with sustained-release formulations designed to maintain therapeutic plasma concentrations over extended periods. The AP1 fraction may also be co-administered with other cardioprotective agents, including beta-blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, statins, or aspirin, without evidence of pharmacological interaction or increased adverse effects, thereby enabling its integration into existing clinical regimens. The invention further includes methods of manufacturing the AP1 fraction under Good Manufacturing Practice conditions, including standardized extraction, purification, lyophilization, and quality control protocols based on high-performance liquid chromatography, nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and mass spectrometry to ensure batch-to-batch consistency and structural integrity. The AP1 fraction is stable under ambient storage conditions for at least two years when protected from moisture and light, making it suitable for commercial development and long-term distribution.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, the isolation and characterization of the acidic polysaccharide fraction AP1 from Panax ginseng root is described in detail, including the specific parameters of each purification step and the analytical methods used to confirm its structural identity and purity. The dried root of Panax ginseng, sourced from Jinlin Province, China, and certified to meet the Hong Kong Standard of Chinese Materia Medica and the Chinese Pharmacopeia, was ground into a fine powder and extracted with 95% ethanol at a ratio of 1:10 (w/v) for 72 hours at room temperature with continuous agitation. The ethanolic extract was filtered through Whatman No. 1 paper, concentrated under reduced pressure at 40°C using a rotary evaporator, and then suspended in distilled water to a final concentration of 100 mg/mL. The aqueous suspension was centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 30 minutes at 4°C to remove insoluble debris, and the supernatant was collected and subjected to ethanol precipitation at 80% v/v final concentration to remove low-molecular-weight contaminants. The precipitate was collected by centrifugation, washed with 80% ethanol, and lyophilized to yield the crude polysaccharide-enriched fraction. This fraction was then dissolved in distilled water and loaded onto a TBE-1000A high-speed counter-current chromatography system equipped with a 1000 mL three-phase coil and a Waters HPLC system with a 2489 UV detector set at 210 nm. The biphasic solvent system consisted of n-butanol, water, and acetic acid in a volume ratio of 1:1:0.05, which had been equilibrated at room temperature for 24 hours and degassed by ultrasonication for 30 minutes. The upper phase was used as the stationary phase, and the lower phase was used as the mobile phase. The column was first filled with the upper phase at a flow rate of 8 mL/min, and the sample (4.12 g) was injected via a 5 mL loop. The mobile phase was then introduced at a flow rate of 8 mL/min, and the elution was performed for 100 minutes, during which fractions were collected every five minutes. The elution profile was monitored by UV absorbance, and fractions with similar chromatographic profiles were pooled into nine composite fractions based on thin-layer chromatography analysis using silica gel plates developed with a solvent system of chloroform:methanol:water (13:7:2) and visualized under UV light at 254 nm. The cardioprotective activity of each fraction was assessed in H9c2 cardiomyocytes subjected to hypoxia/reoxygenation, as described in the detailed description. Fractions 2 and 3, which exhibited the highest viability-enhancing activity, were combined in a 1:1 ratio and subjected to stepwise ethanol precipitation. The combined fraction (0.74 g) was dissolved in distilled water and sequentially precipitated by the addition of ethanol to final concentrations of 90%, 80%, and 70% v/v, with each step followed by centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 20 minutes at 4°C. The precipitates were collected, washed with 70% ethanol, and lyophilized. The precipitate obtained at 70% ethanol showed the highest activity and was selected for further purification. This precipitate was dissolved in distilled water and loaded onto a Toyopearl DEAE 650 M anion-exchange column (2.6 cm × 30 cm) previously equilibrated with distilled water. The column was washed with 10 column volumes of water to remove neutral polysaccharides, followed by stepwise elution with 0.5 M, 1.0 M, 2.0 M, and 0.2 M NaOH, each at a flow rate of 1 mL/min and a volume of 100 mL. Five fractions were collected: neutral polysaccharides, acidic polysaccharide 1 (AP1), acidic polysaccharide 2 (AP2), acidic polysaccharide 3 (AP3), and acidic polysaccharide 4 (AP4). Each fraction was dialyzed against distilled water using a 1000 Da molecular weight cutoff membrane for 24 hours at 4°C, with three changes of water, and then lyophilized. The yield of AP1 was 39.39% of the 70% ethanol precipitate. The AP1 fraction was analyzed for molecular weight using high-performance gel permeation chromatography with a TSKgel G2000SWxl column and a mobile phase of 0.1 M sodium nitrate at a flow rate of 0.8 mL/min, calibrated with dextran standards of known molecular weights ranging from 1,000 to 2,000,000 Da. The apparent molecular weight was determined to be 3.8 × 10⁴ Da. Monosaccharide composition was analyzed by acid hydrolysis with 2 M trifluoroacetic acid at 120°C for 4 hours, followed by derivatization with 1-phenyl-3-methyl-5-pyrazolone and separation by high-performance liquid chromatography using a C18 column with a gradient of acetonitrile and water. The relative percentages of glucose, galactose, galacturonic acid, arabinose, and glucuronic acid were quantified by comparison with authentic standards. The uronic acid content was determined using the m-hydroxydiphenyl method, with galacturonic acid as the reference standard, and the protein content was measured using the Bradford method with bovine serum albumin as the standard. The AP1 fraction was confirmed to contain less than 10% protein and to be free of ginsenosides, as verified by high-performance liquid chromatography with diode array detection and mass spectrometry using a C18 column and a mobile phase of water and acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. The final AP1 fraction was stored at -20°C in sealed vials under nitrogen atmosphere and was found to be stable for at least two years under these conditions.

### Example 2

In this example, the cardioprotective efficacy and mechanistic basis of the AP1 fraction are demonstrated in an in vitro model of hypoxia/reoxygenation injury using H9c2 rat cardiomyocytes. H9c2 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO₂ and 95% air. Cells between passages three and four were used for all experiments. For hypoxia/reoxygenation injury, cells were seeded at a density of 8 × 10³ cells per well in 96-well plates or 2 × 10⁴ cells per well in six-well plates and allowed to adhere overnight. The medium was then replaced with Krebs-Ringer bicarbonate buffer pre-equilibrated with nitrogen at 4°C, and the cells were transferred to a hypoxic chamber flushed with 95% nitrogen and 5% carbon dioxide for three hours at 37°C. Following hypoxia, the buffer was replaced with fresh Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium, and the cells were returned to the normoxic incubator for four hours to complete the reoxygenation phase. For treatment groups, AP1 was added to the culture medium at concentrations of 6.25, 12.5, 25, 50, 100, 200, 400, and 800 μg/mL one hour prior to hypoxia and remained present throughout the entire hypoxia/reoxygenation period. A positive control group received diazoxide at 100 μM, and a negative control group received vehicle (distilled water). Cell viability was assessed using a mitochondrial viability stain assay, in which 100 μL of 2× mitochondrial viability stain was added to each well and incubated for four hours at 37°C in the dark, followed by measurement of fluorescence intensity at excitation 550 nm and emission 590 nm using a Tecan Infinite M200 microplate reader. Cell viability was expressed as a percentage relative to the normoxic control group. Lactate dehydrogenase and creatine kinase activities in the culture medium were measured using commercial assay kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions, with absorbance read at 450 nm and 660 nm, respectively. Apoptosis was quantified using an Annexin V-FITC/propidium iodide apoptosis detection kit, with cells analyzed by flow cytometry using a BD FACSAria III instrument, and the percentage of early and late apoptotic cells was determined using FlowJo software. Intracellular and mitochondrial reactive oxygen species were measured using dichlorofluorescein diacetate and mitoSOX Red, respectively, with fluorescence intensity measured by flow cytometry after 30 minutes of probe incubation. Malondialdehyde levels and superoxide dismutase activity in cell lysates were determined using commercial assay kits from Beyotime Institute of Biotechnology, with absorbance measured at 532 nm and 450 nm, respectively. Mitochondrial membrane potential was assessed using rhodamine 123, with fluorescence intensity measured by both flow cytometry and DeltaVision Elite Cell Imaging System. ATP levels were measured using an ATP luminescence assay kit, with luminescence quantified using the Tecan Infinite M200 reader. Oxygen consumption rate was measured using a Seahorse XFp Extracellular Flux Analyzer, with cells seeded at 5,000 cells per well and subjected to sequential injections of oligomycin, FCCP, and antimycin A/rotenone to assess basal respiration, ATP-linked respiration, maximal respiration, and spare respiratory capacity. Western blot analysis was performed to assess the phosphorylation status of glucocorticoid receptor, estrogen receptor, phosphoinositide 3-kinase, Akt, extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2, endothelial nitric oxide synthase, inducible nitric oxide synthase, cytochrome c, Bcl-2, Bax, JNK, and p38. Protein lysates were prepared using RIPA buffer, separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, transferred to nitrocellulose membranes, blocked with 5% non-fat milk, incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C, and then incubated with IRDye-conjugated secondary antibodies. Bands were visualized using an Odyssey CLx Imaging System and quantified using Odyssey v3.0 software. Nitric oxide production was measured using the Griess reaction, with culture supernatants mixed with NADPH and Griess reagent, and absorbance read at 540 nm. To determine the dependence of AP1’s effect on specific signaling components, cells were pretreated for one hour with pharmacological inhibitors: 100 μM mifepristone (glucocorticoid receptor inhibitor), 100 μM tamoxifen (estrogen receptor inhibitor), 40 μM LY294002 (phosphoinositide 3-kinase inhibitor), 10 μM Akt inhibitor IV, 40 μM U0126 (extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 inhibitor), 10 μM L-NAME (nitric oxide synthase inhibitor), or vehicle control, followed by AP1 treatment and hypoxia/reoxygenation. All experiments were performed in triplicate and repeated at least three times independently. Statistical analysis was performed using one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post hoc test, with significance defined as p < 0.05. The results demonstrated that AP1 at 200 μg/mL significantly increased cell viability by 68% compared to the hypoxia/reoxygenation control group (p < 0.001), reduced lactate dehydrogenase release by 62% (p < 0.001), decreased creatine kinase release by 59% (p < 0.001), reduced apoptosis by 55% (p < 0.001), suppressed intracellular reactive oxygen species by 58% (p < 0.001), reduced mitochondrial reactive oxygen species by 61% (p < 0.001), increased superoxide dismutase activity by 72% (p < 0.001), decreased malondialdehyde levels by 65% (p < 0.001), preserved mitochondrial membrane potential by 57% (p < 0.001), increased ATP levels by 63% (p < 0.001), enhanced basal respiration by 52% (p < 0.01), increased maximal respiration by 54% (p < 0.01), and increased spare respiratory capacity by 59% (p < 0.01). AP1 also increased the phosphorylation of glucocorticoid receptor, estrogen receptor, phosphoinositide 3-kinase, Akt, extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2, and endothelial nitric oxide synthase, while decreasing the phosphorylation of JNK and p38, reducing cytochrome c release, increasing the Bcl-2/Bax ratio, and increasing nitric oxide production by 75% (p < 0.001). All protective effects of AP1 were completely abolished by pretreatment with any of the inhibitors, confirming the dependence of AP1’s action on the glucocorticoid receptor/estrogen receptor/RISK pathway/nitric oxide axis. These data establish AP1 as a potent, specific, and mechanistically defined cardioprotective agent with significant therapeutic potential.
