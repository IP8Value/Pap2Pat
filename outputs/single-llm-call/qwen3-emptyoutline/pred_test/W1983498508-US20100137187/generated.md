## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The therapeutic potential of oligonucleotide-based agents for the treatment of cancer has been widely recognized, particularly in the context of targeting oncogenic transcription factors whose aberrant activity drives malignant proliferation, survival, and metastasis. Among these, the signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) protein has emerged as a compelling target due to its persistent activation in a broad spectrum of solid and hematological malignancies, including prostate and pancreatic cancers. STAT3 regulates the expression of genes involved in cell cycle progression, inhibition of apoptosis, angiogenesis, and immune evasion, making its suppression a rational strategy for cancer therapy. However, the clinical translation of oligonucleotide therapeutics has been severely hampered by the inability of these molecules to efficiently penetrate cell membranes and reach their intracellular targets, particularly within the nucleus where transcription factor activity is concentrated. Traditional antisense oligonucleotides and small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) suffer from poor cellular uptake, rapid degradation by nucleases, and inefficient nuclear delivery, which collectively limit their bioavailability and functional potency. While peptide nucleic acids (PNAs) offer superior binding affinity, nuclease resistance, and stability compared to conventional oligonucleotides, their neutral backbone and lack of innate cell-penetrating properties render them incapable of crossing biological membranes without exogenous assistance. Consequently, the development of a delivery system capable of facilitating the intracellular and intranuclear transport of PNAs remains an unmet need in oncology therapeutics.

Cell-penetrating peptides (CPPs) have been extensively investigated as carriers for the delivery of macromolecular therapeutics, including oligonucleotides, proteins, and nanoparticles. Among the most widely studied CPPs are those derived from viral proteins, such as the HIV-1 transactivator of transcription (TAT) peptide, or from endogenous proteins, such as the antennapedia homeodomain. These peptides typically rely on cationic residues to interact with negatively charged membrane components, promoting internalization through energy-dependent endocytosis or, in some cases, direct translocation. However, despite their demonstrated ability to facilitate cytoplasmic delivery, many CPPs exhibit poor nuclear localization, a critical limitation when targeting transcription factors like STAT3, which exert their biological effects primarily within the nucleus. Furthermore, the efficiency of CPP-mediated delivery is often compromised under physiologically relevant conditions, such as in the presence of serum proteins, which can sequester the peptide-carrier complex, reduce bioavailability, and promote nonspecific binding. In addition, many CPPs require high concentrations—often in the micromolar range—to achieve meaningful intracellular uptake, rendering them economically and clinically impractical for systemic administration. The development of CPPs that combine high uptake efficiency, low effective concentration, serum stability, and precise nuclear targeting remains a significant challenge in the field.

Previous efforts to enhance nuclear delivery of oligonucleotides have focused on the incorporation of nuclear localization signals (NLSs), short amino acid motifs derived from transcription factors that are recognized by the nuclear import machinery, including importin α and β. NLS sequences such as those from the SV40 large T antigen (PKKKRKV) or the Oct6 transcription factor (KRKRKPPRKKRK) have been conjugated to oligonucleotides in an attempt to exploit the endogenous nuclear transport pathway. However, these strategies have yielded inconsistent results. In many cases, NLS-containing conjugates accumulate in endosomal compartments, where they are sequestered and degraded, rather than being efficiently released into the cytosol and translocated to the nucleus. Moreover, the presence of an NLS alone does not guarantee enhanced cellular uptake; in fact, some NLS peptides exhibit minimal internalization in the absence of additional membrane-interacting motifs. This dichotomy—where nuclear targeting is possible but cellular entry is inefficient, or vice versa—has created a fundamental barrier to the development of effective transcription factor inhibitors. The need for a novel class of CPPs that simultaneously overcome both barriers—enhancing cellular internalization and promoting nuclear translocation—has become increasingly urgent.

In the context of prostate and pancreatic cancers, additional challenges arise from the heterogeneity of target cell populations, the dense stromal microenvironment, and the presence of efflux pumps and extracellular barriers that impede drug penetration. Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-targeted delivery systems have been explored for prostate cancer, leveraging the enzyme’s ability to cleave C-terminal glutamate residues. However, PSMA is not universally expressed across all prostate cancer subtypes, and its absence in cell lines such as DU-145 renders such approaches ineffective for a substantial subset of patients. Similarly, while TAT-based delivery systems have shown promise in viral and hematological contexts, their performance in solid tumors has been inconsistent, with uptake rates often below 50% and minimal nuclear accumulation. The failure of these established strategies to achieve robust, reproducible, and therapeutically relevant delivery underscores the necessity for a fundamentally new approach to CPP design—one that integrates structural elements known to enhance membrane interaction with nuclear targeting motifs in a synergistic manner.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by introducing a novel, rationally designed cell-penetrating peptide that combines a glutamate-rich motif with the nuclear localization signal of the Oct6 transcription factor. This fusion peptide, designated Glu-Oct6, exhibits unprecedented efficiency in cellular uptake and nuclear localization at nanomolar concentrations, even in the presence of serum, and enables the effective delivery of therapeutic PNAs into prostate and pancreatic cancer cells. The glutamate-rich component, EEEAA, is not merely a spacer but an active participant in the internalization process, conferring sequence-specific binding to cell surface receptors that are overexpressed in malignant cells. Unlike previous attempts to use glutamate-containing peptides for targeting, which relied on C-terminal presentation and enzymatic cleavage by PSMA, the N-terminal placement of the glutamate motif in Glu-Oct6 allows for receptor-mediated endocytosis independent of PSMA expression, thereby broadening its applicability to PSMA-negative tumors. The Oct6 NLS, meanwhile, ensures that once internalized, the peptide and its cargo are efficiently directed to the nucleus via the classical importin pathway. Crucially, the combination of these two elements results in a synergistic effect: the glutamate-rich motif enhances the rate and extent of cellular entry, while the NLS ensures that a significant proportion of the internalized material reaches its intended site of action. This dual functionality is not observed when either component is used in isolation, nor is it replicated by substitution of the glutamate residues with structurally similar amino acids such as phenylalanine, tyrosine, or asparagine, which completely abrogate uptake. The invention further demonstrates that the resulting peptide-PNA conjugates induce potent biological effects, including apoptosis in cancer cell lines, when targeted to oncogenic transcripts such as STAT3, thereby validating the therapeutic utility of the delivery platform. This invention represents a transformative advance in the field of oligonucleotide delivery, providing a highly efficient, sequence-specific, and broadly applicable system for the nuclear targeting of therapeutic agents in solid tumors.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of cell-penetrating peptides and their conjugates for the targeted delivery of therapeutic nucleic acid analogs into cancer cells, particularly prostate and pancreatic carcinoma cells, with exceptional efficiency and specificity. The invention comprises a synthetic peptide comprising an N-terminal glutamate-rich sequence followed by a nuclear localization signal derived from the Oct6 transcription factor, wherein the glutamate-rich sequence consists of three or more consecutive glutamate residues optionally flanked by alanine or other non-charged amino acids, and the nuclear localization signal comprises the amino acid sequence KRKRKPPRKKRK. The preferred embodiment of the peptide, designated Glu-Oct6, has the sequence EEEAA-KRKRKPPRKKRK, wherein the glutamate-rich segment enhances cellular uptake and the NLS segment directs nuclear translocation, resulting in a synergistic enhancement of intracellular and intranuclear delivery that is not achievable with either component alone. The peptide may be covalently linked at its N-terminus, C-terminus, or internally to a therapeutic payload, including but not limited to peptide nucleic acids (PNAs), locked nucleic acids (LNAs), antisense oligonucleotides, small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), microRNA mimics or inhibitors, or other nucleic acid analogs that require intracellular delivery for biological activity. In a particularly preferred embodiment, the peptide is conjugated to a PNA sequence complementary to the STAT3 mRNA transcript at position 13410a, resulting in a conjugate that effectively inhibits STAT3 expression and induces apoptosis in prostate and pancreatic cancer cell lines at concentrations as low as 600 nM. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the peptide or peptide-nucleic acid conjugate in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, for administration via intravenous, intratumoral, or other parenteral routes. The invention also provides methods for treating cancer in a subject, comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of the peptide or conjugate, wherein the cancer is selected from the group consisting of prostate cancer, pancreatic cancer, and other malignancies characterized by aberrant STAT3 signaling. The invention further includes diagnostic methods for detecting the presence of cancer cells expressing glutamate receptor-like proteins, wherein the peptide is labeled with a detectable moiety and used to identify tumor tissue based on its specific binding and internalization properties. The peptide is characterized by its ability to achieve cellular uptake at concentrations below 100 nM, nuclear localization in greater than 60% of treated cells, and resistance to inhibition by serum proteins, and it is not recognized by endogenous proteases or nucleases, thereby conferring exceptional stability in biological fluids. The invention further demonstrates that substitution of the glutamate residues with phenylalanine, tyrosine, or asparagine abolishes cellular uptake, confirming that the sequence specificity of the glutamate-rich motif is essential for function, and that the uptake mechanism is not mediated by nonspecific charge interactions or membrane disruption. Furthermore, the internalization of the peptide is shown to be temperature-dependent and inhibited by sodium azide, indicating an energy-dependent, receptor-mediated endocytic process distinct from passive diffusion or membrane translocation. The invention further provides a method of screening for compounds that modulate the uptake of the peptide by competitive inhibition assays, wherein unlabeled variants of the peptide are used to assess binding specificity. The invention further provides a kit comprising the peptide or conjugate, a buffer for reconstitution, and instructions for use in the treatment or diagnosis of cancer. The invention represents a significant advancement over prior art by providing a delivery system that overcomes the dual barriers of cellular entry and nuclear targeting simultaneously, with high specificity, low toxicity, and therapeutic efficacy in clinically relevant cancer models.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel class of synthetic peptides and their conjugates for the targeted delivery of therapeutic nucleic acid analogs into cancer cells, with particular utility in the treatment of prostate and pancreatic carcinomas. The invention is based on the discovery that the combination of a glutamate-rich amino acid sequence with a nuclear localization signal derived from the Oct6 transcription factor results in a synergistic enhancement of cellular uptake and nuclear translocation that is not observed when either component is used independently. This discovery has enabled the development of a delivery system that achieves efficient intracellular delivery of peptide nucleic acids (PNAs) at nanomolar concentrations, with minimal cytotoxicity and high specificity for malignant cells. The peptide of the invention, referred to herein as Glu-Oct6, comprises the sequence EEEAA-KRKRKPPRKKRK, wherein the N-terminal segment EEEAA serves as a cell-binding motif that facilitates receptor-mediated internalization, and the C-terminal segment KRKRKPPRKKRK functions as a nuclear localization signal that directs the peptide and its cargo to the nucleus via the importin α/β pathway. The alanine residues (AA) between the glutamate-rich segment and the NLS serve as a flexible spacer that prevents steric interference between the two functional domains, thereby optimizing the conformational accessibility of each motif for its respective biological interaction. The peptide may be synthesized using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques employing Fmoc chemistry, purified by high-performance liquid chromatography, and characterized by mass spectrometry and amino acid sequencing to ensure structural fidelity. The peptide may be conjugated to a therapeutic nucleic acid analog, such as a PNA, via a covalent linkage at either the N-terminus or C-terminus, with the preferred orientation being N-terminal coupling to preserve the integrity of the cell-penetrating and nuclear targeting motifs. The conjugate may be further modified with a fluorescent label, such as carboxyfluorescein or carboxydansyl, for tracking purposes, or with a stabilizing moiety, such as polyethylene glycol, to enhance pharmacokinetic properties. The peptide and its conjugates are stable under physiological conditions, resistant to degradation by serum proteases and nucleases, and exhibit no significant cytotoxicity at concentrations up to 1 μM, as demonstrated by viability assays using annexin V/propidium iodide staining and fluorescein diacetate uptake. The invention further encompasses the use of the peptide to deliver other therapeutic agents, including but not limited to siRNAs, LNAs, antisense oligonucleotides, ribozymes, aptamers, or small molecule inhibitors, provided that the agent is capable of being covalently linked to the peptide without compromising its biological activity. The invention further provides methods for the preparation of pharmaceutical compositions comprising the peptide or conjugate in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as saline, phosphate-buffered saline, dextrose solution, or liposomal formulations, for administration by intravenous, intratumoral, intraperitoneal, or subcutaneous routes. The invention further provides methods for treating cancer in a subject, comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of the peptide or conjugate, wherein the cancer is selected from the group consisting of prostate cancer, pancreatic cancer, breast cancer, ovarian cancer, glioblastoma, and other malignancies characterized by constitutive activation of STAT3 signaling. The invention further provides diagnostic methods for detecting cancer cells expressing glutamate receptor-like proteins, wherein the peptide is labeled with a detectable moiety and administered to a subject, followed by imaging of tissue samples to identify areas of specific peptide binding and internalization. The invention further provides kits comprising the peptide or conjugate, a buffer for reconstitution, instructions for use, and optionally, control peptides for comparative analysis. The invention further encompasses variants of the peptide in which the glutamate-rich segment comprises four or five consecutive glutamate residues, optionally flanked by one or two alanine residues, and wherein the nuclear localization signal comprises a sequence homologous to the Oct6 NLS, with up to two conservative amino acid substitutions, provided that the resulting peptide retains the ability to mediate nuclear translocation and cellular uptake. The invention further provides methods for screening for compounds that inhibit or enhance the uptake of the peptide, wherein the peptide is labeled with a fluorescent dye and incubated with cells in the presence of test compounds, followed by quantification of intracellular fluorescence by flow cytometry or microscopy. The invention further provides methods for determining the specificity of the peptide’s interaction with cell surface receptors, wherein unlabeled variants of the peptide bearing substitutions at the glutamate residues are used as competitive inhibitors, and wherein the absence of competitive inhibition confirms that the uptake mechanism is sequence-specific and not mediated by charge-based interactions. The invention further demonstrates that the peptide is internalized via an energy-dependent, endocytic pathway, as evidenced by temperature sensitivity and inhibition by sodium azide, and that the internalized peptide accumulates in the nucleus, as confirmed by imaging flow cytometry and live-cell microscopy using nuclear stains such as DRAQ5. The invention further provides evidence that the peptide does not induce apoptosis or cytotoxicity in the absence of a therapeutic payload, indicating that its function is purely as a delivery vehicle and not as a bioactive agent per se.
