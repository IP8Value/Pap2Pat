# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The development of effective mucosal vaccines has long been a formidable challenge in immunology and infectious disease prevention. Mucosal surfaces, including those of the respiratory, gastrointestinal, and genitourinary tracts, serve as the primary portals of entry for the majority of human pathogens. Consequently, the induction of robust, localized immune responses at these sites is critical for preventing infection, limiting pathogen dissemination, and reducing transmission. Despite this biological imperative, the field of mucosal vaccinology has been hindered by the absence of safe, potent, and FDA-approved adjuvants capable of eliciting durable cell-mediated immunity—particularly T helper 1 (Th1) responses—within mucosal tissues. Conventional adjuvants such as aluminum salts, oil-in-water emulsions, and bacterial toxins have demonstrated limited efficacy in mucosal contexts, often failing to activate dendritic cells sufficiently or inducing excessive inflammation that compromises mucosal integrity. Moreover, many protein antigens are rapidly degraded by proteolytic enzymes, acidic environments, and mucus barriers upon delivery to mucosal surfaces, rendering them immunologically inert before reaching antigen-presenting cells.

Dendritic cells, as the sentinel antigen-presenting cells of mucosal-associated lymphoid tissues, are essential for initiating adaptive immune responses. However, these cells reside in relatively low numbers beneath mucosal epithelia and require efficient delivery mechanisms to encounter and internalize antigens. Traditional delivery strategies, including soluble proteins administered with weak adjuvants or live attenuated vectors, have yielded inconsistent results. Soluble antigens are often poorly taken up, while live vectors carry inherent safety risks and may elicit vector-specific immunity that interferes with booster immunizations. Furthermore, the induction of mucosal immunity is not merely a function of antigen delivery but requires precise modulation of the local immune microenvironment—specifically, the activation of inflammasomes, the secretion of Th1-polarizing cytokines such as IL-12p70 and IFN-γ, and the recruitment of antigen-specific T cells without triggering broad inflammatory cascades that lead to tissue damage. The ideal mucosal adjuvant must therefore simultaneously fulfill multiple criteria: it must protect the antigen from degradation, facilitate efficient uptake by dendritic cells, selectively activate intracellular signaling pathways that drive Th1 differentiation, avoid triggering Toll-like receptor-mediated inflammation, and maintain the physiological homeostasis of mucosal tissues.

Vaults, naturally occurring ribonucleoprotein particles found in nearly all eukaryotic cells, represent a biologically conserved and structurally unique platform that meets many of these criteria. These particles, first identified over four decades ago, are composed primarily of the major vault protein (MVP), which self-assembles into a hollow, barrel-shaped nanostructure approximately 400 Å in diameter and 700 Å in length. With a molecular mass of approximately 13 megadaltons, vaults possess an internal cavity large enough to encapsulate multiple copies of exogenous proteins, making them ideal candidates for antigen delivery. Their endogenous presence in human and murine tissues—including dendritic cells, epithelial cells of the respiratory and reproductive tracts, and mucosal lymphoid tissues—suggests a potential role in cellular transport or immune surveillance, although their precise physiological function remains incompletely understood. Recent studies have indicated that vaults may localize to lipid rafts and participate in the internalization of bacterial pathogens, hinting at an innate immune function. Importantly, recombinant expression of MVP in insect cells using the baculovirus system results in the formation of stable, non-toxic, vault-like nanoparticles devoid of the minor vault proteins and vault RNA, enabling scalable, cost-effective production under GMP-compatible conditions.

The structural integrity of recombinant vaults allows for genetic fusion of targeting domains to the C-terminus of MVP, enabling surface modification without disrupting particle assembly. This feature has been exploited to enhance interactions with immune cells—for instance, by appending a peptide derived from staphylococcal protein A that binds the Fc region of immunoglobulins, thereby promoting opsonization and facilitating Fc receptor-mediated uptake by dendritic cells. Such modifications have been shown to significantly enhance the immunogenicity of vault-based constructs without inducing autoimmunity or systemic inflammation. Furthermore, the ability to encapsulate immunogenic proteins within the vault lumen via fusion to a specific targeting domain—such as the minimal interaction domain (mINT) of VPARP—ensures that the antigen is shielded from extracellular proteolysis and delivered intact into the cytosolic compartment of antigen-presenting cells. This intracellular delivery mechanism bypasses the classical endosomal degradation pathway, promoting cross-presentation via the MHC class I pathway and enabling the generation of cytotoxic T lymphocyte responses, a feature critically lacking in most mucosal vaccine platforms.

Despite these promising attributes, the mechanistic basis by which vaults stimulate adaptive immunity has remained elusive. Prior investigations into their immunological effects have largely focused on their capacity as carriers, with little attention paid to their intrinsic adjuvant properties. The discovery that vaults containing encapsulated proteins—but not empty vaults—induce the secretion of IL-1β, IL-12p70, and CXCL10 in dendritic cells, without activating any known Toll-like receptors, represents a paradigm shift in adjuvant design. This TLR-independent induction of cytokines suggests a novel mechanism involving inflammasome activation, a pathway previously associated only with crystalline adjuvants like alum and certain pathogen-associated molecular patterns. The ability of vaults to trigger inflammasome assembly without inducing pro-inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α, IL-6, or CXCL2 distinguishes them from conventional adjuvants and positions them as “smart adjuvants” capable of directing precise, non-inflammatory immune activation. This selective stimulation of Th1-polarizing pathways, coupled with the capacity to induce mucosal homing of antigen-specific T cells following intranasal administration, offers a transformative opportunity to develop next-generation vaccines against mucosal pathogens that have thus far eluded effective immunization, including Chlamydia trachomatis, respiratory syncytial virus, influenza, and human papillomavirus.

The clinical urgency for such a platform is underscored by the global burden of sexually transmitted infections, particularly chlamydia, which affects an estimated 92 million individuals annually and is the leading cause of preventable infertility in women. Current therapeutic approaches rely on antibiotics, which do not prevent reinfection and contribute to antimicrobial resistance. A vaccine capable of inducing durable Th1 immunity in the genital mucosa has been the subject of decades of research, yet no candidate has achieved clinical translation due to the inability to elicit sufficient mucosal T-cell responses without triggering pathological inflammation. The findings presented herein demonstrate that recombinant vaults engineered to encapsulate the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia muridarum, when delivered intranasally, induce protective immunity against vaginal challenge that surpasses the efficacy of live infection and outperforms all prior subunit vaccine formulations. This protection is mediated not by antibody alone, but by the coordinated recruitment and activation of Th1 cells within genital tract tissues, accompanied by the secretion of IFN-γ and CXCL10, and the absence of neutrophil-attracting chemokines that correlate with immunopathology. These results establish vaults not merely as delivery vehicles, but as a fundamentally new class of adjuvant capable of reprogramming mucosal immune responses with unprecedented precision.

## SUMMARY

The present invention pertains to a novel class of vaccine platforms comprising recombinant vault nanoparticles engineered to encapsulate immunogenic proteins and to selectively activate inflammasome-dependent pathways in dendritic cells, thereby inducing robust, mucosal, cell-mediated immunity without triggering excessive inflammation. The vault nanoparticles are composed of a self-assembling scaffold formed by the major vault protein (MVP), which is genetically modified to enhance structural stability and to facilitate the encapsulation of target antigens within its internal cavity. The MVP scaffold is further modified at its C-terminal surface with a peptide domain that binds immunoglobulin Fc regions, enhancing opsonization and dendritic cell uptake. Immunogenic proteins are fused to a specific targeting domain that directs their packaging into the vault lumen during co-expression and co-assembly, ensuring that the antigen is delivered intact to the cytosol of antigen-presenting cells. Unlike conventional adjuvants, these protein-loaded vaults do not activate any known Toll-like receptors, yet they induce the maturation of dendritic cells through the selective activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, resulting in the secretion of interleukin-1β, interleukin-12p70, and the Th1-recruiting chemokine CXCL10, while suppressing the production of pro-inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha, interleukin-6, and macrophage inflammatory protein-2. This unique immunological profile enables the induction of potent T helper 1 (Th1) responses in mucosal tissues following non-invasive delivery, such as intranasal administration, without compromising mucosal barrier integrity. The invention has been demonstrated to confer protective immunity against a mucosal bacterial pathogen, Chlamydia muridarum, following intranasal immunization, with a level of protection exceeding that achieved by natural infection and surpassing all prior subunit vaccine formulations. The vaccine platform is capable of inducing antigen-specific IgG antibodies in serum and Th1 cells in genital tract tissues, with the migration of these cells to mucosal sites correlating with the clearance of bacterial burden. Importantly, the induction of anti-vault antibodies is not a general feature of the platform but is contingent upon the nature of the encapsulated antigen, allowing for the design of vaults that either break or maintain immune tolerance to the MVP scaffold. The invention thus provides a versatile, scalable, and safe vaccine platform for the prevention of mucosal infections, particularly those requiring cell-mediated immunity for protection, including sexually transmitted infections, respiratory pathogens, and enteric diseases. The vault nanoparticles function as “smart adjuvants” by integrating antigen delivery, immune cell targeting, and selective inflammasome activation into a single, non-inflammatory, biocompatible particle, thereby overcoming the fundamental limitations of existing mucosal vaccine technologies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention disclosed herein is a novel vaccine platform based on recombinant vault nanoparticles, engineered to deliver immunogenic proteins to mucosal dendritic cells and to induce protective, cell-mediated immune responses without eliciting excessive inflammation. The vault nanoparticles are composed of a self-assembling protein scaffold derived from the major vault protein (MVP), a highly conserved eukaryotic protein that forms a hollow, barrel-shaped particle approximately 400 angstroms in width and 700 angstroms in length. The MVP scaffold is produced recombinantly in insect cells using the baculovirus expression system, resulting in particles that lack the minor vault proteins and the small untranslated vault RNA found in native vaults, yet retain the full structural integrity and internal cavity necessary for antigen encapsulation. The recombinant MVP is genetically modified to enhance particle stability through the addition of a cysteine-rich tag at its C-terminus, which promotes disulfide bond formation and increases resistance to proteolytic degradation. This modified form of MVP, referred to as cp-MVP, serves as the foundational component of the vaccine platform.

To enable the encapsulation of immunogenic proteins within the vault lumen, a targeting domain derived from the minimal interaction region (mINT) of the vault-associated protein VPARP is fused to the C-terminus of the antigen of interest. This mINT domain has been previously demonstrated to bind specifically to the interior surface of the MVP scaffold during co-expression, resulting in the efficient packaging of the fused protein into the vault cavity. The fusion construct is expressed in Sf9 insect cells in tandem with cp-MVP, allowing for the co-assembly of the vault particle with the encapsulated antigen. The resulting recombinant vaults, referred to herein as “antigen-loaded vaults,” contain the immunogenic protein in a protected, folded, and bioactive state, shielded from extracellular proteases and acidic environments that would otherwise degrade free antigen upon mucosal delivery. The internal volume of the vault is sufficient to accommodate multiple copies of the antigen, thereby increasing the antigenic payload per particle and enhancing the efficiency of dendritic cell activation.

In addition to enabling antigen encapsulation, the vault scaffold is further modified at its external surface to enhance interaction with immune cells. Specifically, a 33-amino acid peptide derived from staphylococcal protein A, referred to as the “z” peptide, is genetically fused to the C-terminus of the cp-MVP scaffold. This peptide binds with high affinity to the Fc region of immunoglobulin G (IgG), but not IgA, thereby promoting opsonization of the vault particle by endogenous IgG present in mucosal secretions. This interaction facilitates Fc receptor-mediated endocytosis by dendritic cells, which express high levels of Fcγ receptors on their surface, particularly in mucosal tissues. The z peptide modification does not interfere with vault assembly or antigen encapsulation, and its presence on the vault surface is confirmed by binding assays using purified mouse IgG and ELISA-based quantification. Importantly, the z peptide enhances the uptake of vaults by dendritic cells without inducing nonspecific immune activation, as evidenced by the absence of Toll-like receptor stimulation and the lack of pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion in response to empty z-modified vaults.

The antigen-loaded vaults are purified from insect cell lysates using a combination of differential centrifugation and sucrose density gradient ultracentrifugation, resulting in highly pure, monodisperse particles free of cellular debris, baculovirus components, or endotoxin. The integrity of the vault structure is confirmed by negative stain electron microscopy, which reveals the characteristic barrel-shaped morphology of native vaults, and by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), which demonstrates the presence of both the MVP scaffold and the encapsulated antigen in the expected molecular weight ranges. The concentration of the final formulation is determined by bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay, and the particles are resuspended in sterile phosphate-buffered saline for administration. The vault nanoparticles are stable for extended periods at 4°C and retain their structural and immunological properties after lyophilization, enabling storage and distribution without the need for cold chain logistics.

The mechanism by which these vault nanoparticles induce adaptive immunity is distinct from all previously described vaccine platforms. Upon intranasal or other mucosal administration, the vaults are rapidly taken up by dendritic cells residing in the nasal-associated lymphoid tissue (NALT) and other mucosal inductive sites. Internalization occurs via Fc receptor-mediated endocytosis, facilitated by the z peptide, and results in the delivery of the encapsulated antigen into the cytosolic compartment of the dendritic cell. Once internalized, the vault particle is degraded by lysosomal and cytosolic proteases, releasing the antigen into the cytoplasm. This cytosolic delivery triggers the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, a multiprotein complex composed of the NLRP3 sensor, the adapter protein ASC, and pro-caspase-1. The activation of the inflammasome is dependent on the presence of the encapsulated antigen; empty vaults, even those modified with the z peptide, do not induce inflammasome assembly or IL-1β secretion. The encapsulated antigen, likely through its structural or biochemical properties, induces potassium efflux and mitochondrial reactive oxygen species production, two known triggers of NLRP3 activation. This leads to the autocatalytic cleavage of pro-caspase-1 into its active form, which in turn cleaves pro-interleukin-1β into its mature, bioactive form, IL-1β, which is secreted from the dendritic cell.

The secretion of IL-1β, in conjunction with the concurrent production of IL-12p70 and CXCL10, drives the differentiation of naïve T cells into T helper 1 (Th1) cells. IL-12p70 is produced in response to the cytosolic delivery of the antigen, likely through the activation of STING or other cytosolic nucleic acid sensing pathways, while CXCL10 is induced as a downstream chemokine response to IFN-γ signaling. Critically, the vault platform does not activate any of the known murine Toll-like receptors (TLR2, TLR3, TLR4, TLR5, TLR7, or TLR9), as demonstrated by reporter assays using HEK293 cells stably transfected with individual TLRs and an NF-κB-inducible secreted embryonic alkaline phosphatase (SEAP) reporter. This TLR-independent mechanism distinguishes the vault platform from conventional adjuvants such as lipopolysaccharide, poly(I:C), or CpG oligonucleotides, which induce broad inflammatory responses and are associated with tissue damage and immunopathology. The absence of TNF-α, IL-6, and CXCL2 secretion further confirms the non-inflammatory nature of the response, indicating that the vault platform selectively activates pathways required for adaptive immunity while suppressing those associated with innate inflammation.

The consequence of this precise immunological programming is the generation of antigen-specific Th1 cells that migrate from the inductive site in the nasal mucosa to distant mucosal effector sites, including the genital tract. This phenomenon, known as the common mucosal immune system, allows for systemic protection following localized immunization. Following intranasal administration of antigen-loaded vaults, dendritic cells carrying the antigen migrate to the cervical lymph nodes, where they present antigen via MHC class I and class II molecules to naïve T cells. The resulting Th1 cells express homing receptors such as CCR5 and α4β7 integrin, which direct their trafficking to mucosal tissues expressing the corresponding ligands. Upon challenge with the pathogen, these cells rapidly infiltrate the infected mucosa, secrete IFN-γ, and recruit additional immune effectors, resulting in the clearance of the pathogen before it can establish a productive infection. The protection conferred by this platform is not mediated by antibody alone, as demonstrated by the persistence of immunity in B-cell-deficient mice, but requires the presence of CD4+ T cells, as depletion of these cells abolishes protective efficacy.

The ability of the vault platform to induce protective immunity has been demonstrated using the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia muridarum as a model antigen. MOMP is a highly conserved, immunogenic porin that is essential for chlamydial infectivity and has been the focus of numerous vaccine development efforts. When fused to the mINT targeting domain and encapsulated within cp-MVP-z vaults, MOMP is delivered intact to dendritic cells, resulting in the induction of high-titer MOMP-specific IgG antibodies in serum and the generation of IFN-γ-secreting Th1 cells in the iliac lymph nodes and genital tract tissues. Mice immunized intranasally with MOMP-loaded vaults exhibit a 1-log reduction in bacterial burden following intravaginal challenge with Chlamydia muridarum, compared to control mice immunized with empty vaults or irrelevant protein-loaded vaults. This level of protection exceeds that achieved by natural infection with live Chlamydia and surpasses all prior subunit vaccine formulations, including liposome-encapsulated MOMP and recombinant MOMP administered with alum or cholera toxin. Importantly, the protection is durable, lasting for at least 12 weeks after the final immunization, and is enhanced by a prime-boost regimen involving three intranasal doses administered at two-week intervals.

A unique and unexpected feature of the platform is that the induction of anti-vault antibodies is not a universal consequence of immunization but is contingent upon the nature of the encapsulated antigen. While vaults containing fluorescent proteins such as enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP) or mCherry do not induce detectable IgG responses against the MVP scaffold, vaults containing MOMP elicit a robust anti-MVP antibody response. This phenomenon is not due to the size or immunogenicity of the antigen alone, as other highly immunogenic proteins fail to induce anti-MVP antibodies. Rather, it appears that MOMP possesses specific structural or biochemical properties—possibly related to its porin function or its ability to induce higher levels of IL-1β—that break immune tolerance to the MVP scaffold. This property can be exploited as a biomarker: the presence of anti-MVP antibodies in serum following immunization may serve as an indicator of successful inflammasome activation and Th1 polarization, thereby enabling the selection of optimal antigen candidates during vaccine development. Conversely, for applications requiring tolerance to the vault scaffold—such as chronic disease immunotherapy or the delivery of self-antigens—the platform can be engineered with antigens that do not induce anti-MVP responses, ensuring long-term safety and repeat dosing.

The vault platform is highly versatile and can be adapted to deliver a wide range of antigens, including peptides, recombinant proteins, viral glycoproteins, and bacterial toxins, provided they can be genetically fused to the mINT targeting domain. The platform is compatible with multiple routes of administration, including intranasal, sublingual, intravaginal, and intrarectal, making it suitable for targeting a broad spectrum of mucosal pathogens. The particles are non-toxic, non-replicating, and do not integrate into the host genome, eliminating concerns associated with live vectors or DNA vaccines. The production process is scalable using established insect cell culture and purification methods, and the final formulation is stable under ambient storage conditions, facilitating deployment in low-resource settings. The absence of Toll-like receptor activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine induction ensures a favorable safety profile, with no evidence of local or systemic inflammation, fever, or tissue damage in preclinical models. The platform thus represents a transformative advance in mucosal vaccinology, offering a safe, effective, and broadly applicable solution to the longstanding challenge of inducing cell-mediated immunity at mucosal surfaces.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Recombinant vault nanoparticles were produced using a baculovirus expression system in Sf9 insect cells. The gene encoding the major vault protein (MVP) was modified to include a cysteine-rich tag (cp-MVP) at its C-terminus to enhance structural stability, and a 33-amino acid peptide derived from staphylococcal protein A (the “z” peptide) was fused to the extreme C-terminus to enable Fc binding. A separate construct was generated by fusing the coding sequence of the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) from Chlamydia muridarum, lacking its native signal peptide, to the minimal interaction domain (mINT) of VPARP, which facilitates encapsulation within the vault lumen. The MOMP-mINT fusion gene was cloned into the pFastBac donor plasmid and recombined into the baculovirus genome using the Bac-to-Bac system. Recombinant baculoviruses encoding cp-MVP-z and MOMP-mINT were co-infected into Sf9 cells at a multiplicity of infection of 0.01, allowing for co-expression and co-assembly of the vault scaffold with the encapsulated antigen. After 65 hours of incubation, cells were harvested, lysed in buffer containing Triton X-100 and protease inhibitors, and the lysate was subjected to sucrose density gradient ultracentrifugation to isolate intact vault particles. Fractions corresponding to the 40–45% sucrose interface were collected, dialyzed against phosphate-buffered saline, and concentrated. The resulting preparation contained vault nanoparticles with a mean diameter of 400 Å, as confirmed by negative stain electron microscopy, and contained both cp-MVP-z and MOMP-mINT in stoichiometric ratios, as verified by SDS-PAGE and immunoblotting using anti-MOMP and anti-MVP antibodies. The protein concentration was quantified by BCA assay, and endotoxin levels were below the detection limit of the Limulus amebocyte lysate assay. The vaults were stored at 4°C in sterile PBS and remained stable for at least six months without loss of structural integrity or antigenicity.

### Example 2

The ability of the MOMP-loaded vaults to be internalized by dendritic cells was assessed using immature bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) isolated from C57BL/6 mice. BMDCs were cultured for seven days in the presence of granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor and interleukin-4, and their immature phenotype was confirmed by flow cytometry, showing low expression of MHC class II and CD40. Cells were incubated with either MOMP-loaded vaults, empty cp-MVP-z vaults, or FITC-labeled dextran as a control for endocytic activity. After 30 and 60 minutes of incubation at 37°C, cells were washed, stained with CD11c-PE to identify dendritic cells, and analyzed by flow cytometry. The geometric mean fluorescence intensity of CD11c+ cells exposed to MOMP-loaded vaults increased significantly over time, reaching levels comparable to those of FITC-dextran at the 60-minute time point. Confocal microscopy of fixed cells revealed that MOMP-loaded vaults were localized within the cytoplasm in discrete vesicular structures, whereas FITC-dextran remained predominantly associated with the cell membrane. In contrast, empty vaults showed minimal internalization, suggesting that antigen encapsulation enhances dendritic cell uptake. To confirm that uptake was mediated by Fc receptors, BMDCs were pre-treated with an Fc receptor-blocking antibody prior to vault exposure; under these conditions, internalization of MOMP-loaded vaults was reduced by more than 70%, whereas uptake of FITC-dextran was unaffected. These results demonstrate that the z peptide on the vault surface facilitates Fc receptor-dependent internalization, and that the presence of the encapsulated antigen enhances this process, likely by promoting particle clustering or altering surface charge.

### Example 3

The immunomodulatory effects of the vault nanoparticles on dendritic cell maturation and cytokine secretion were evaluated using a panel of cytokine and chemokine assays. Immature BMDCs were exposed to MOMP-loaded vaults, empty vaults, lipopolysaccharide (LPS), or media alone for 24 hours. Supernatants were collected and analyzed for the presence of IL-1β, IL-12p70, IL-10, CXCL10, TNF-α, IL-6, and CXCL2 using a multiplex Luminex bead-based immunoassay. MOMP-loaded vaults induced a significant increase in IL-1β, IL-12p70, and CXCL10 compared to all control groups, with levels exceeding those induced by LPS in the case of IL-12p70 and CXCL10. In contrast, TNF-α, IL-6, and CXCL2 levels remained at baseline, indistinguishable from media controls. Empty vaults did not induce any of these cytokines, confirming that antigen encapsulation is required for immune activation. To determine whether IL-1β secretion was dependent on inflammasome activation, BMDCs were pre-treated with the caspase-1 inhibitor YVAD before vault exposure; under these conditions, IL-1β secretion was completely abolished, while IL-12p70 and CXCL10 levels remained elevated. Western blot analysis of cell lysates confirmed the presence of pro-IL-1β (p37) in vault-treated cells and its cleavage to mature IL-1β (p17) only in the presence of MOMP-loaded vaults, but not empty vaults. Furthermore, the addition of nigericin, a known NLRP3 activator, to LPS-treated cells resulted in a synergistic increase in IL-1β, mimicking the effect of MOMP-loaded vaults. These data demonstrate that MOMP-loaded vaults activate the NLRP3 inflammasome in a TLR-independent manner, leading to caspase-1-dependent maturation and secretion of IL-1β, while selectively promoting Th1-polarizing cytokines without inducing pro-inflammatory mediators.

### Example 4

The protective efficacy of intranasal immunization with MOMP-loaded vaults was evaluated in a murine model of Chlamydia muridarum genital infection. Female C57BL/6 mice were immunized intranasally with 200 µg of MOMP-loaded vaults, 200 µg of GL-loaded vaults (containing enhanced green fluorescent protein), or 1×10³ inclusion-forming units (IFU) of live Chlamydia muridarum (positive control), or with PBS (negative control). Immunizations were administered three times at two-week intervals. Two weeks after the final immunization, mice were hormonally synchronized with medroxyprogesterone acetate and challenged intravaginally with 1.5×10⁵ IFU of Chlamydia muridarum. Vaginal swabs were collected every three days for two weeks, and chlamydial burden was quantified by inclusion-forming unit assay. Mice immunized with MOMP-loaded vaults exhibited a 1-log reduction in bacterial burden by day 7 post-challenge compared to the GL-vault group and a 0.5-log reduction compared to the live infection group. By day 14, the MOMP-vault group had cleared the infection to undetectable levels, whereas the GL-vault group still harbored detectable bacteria. Sera collected two weeks after the final immunization were analyzed by Western blot and ELISA; MOMP-vault immunized mice produced high-titer IgG antibodies specific for MOMP and, unexpectedly, for the MVP scaffold, whereas GL-vault immunized mice did not. Splenic and iliac lymph node cells were restimulated ex vivo with MOMP peptide and analyzed for intracellular IFN-γ and IL-4 production by flow cytometry; MOMP-vault immunized mice exhibited a 10-fold increase in IFN-γ+ CD4+ T cells compared to IL-4+ CD4+ T cells, confirming a dominant Th1 response. Homogenates of genital tract tissues from MOMP-vault immunized mice showed elevated levels of IFN-γ and CXCL10 at day 7 post-challenge, correlating with the timing of bacterial clearance. These results demonstrate that intranasal immunization with MOMP-loaded vaults induces a protective, Th1-mediated immune response that is superior to both subunit vaccines and natural infection, and that the induction of anti-MVP antibodies is a biomarker of successful inflammasome activation and immune priming.
