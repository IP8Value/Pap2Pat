# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to novel cyclic peptidomimetic compounds that specifically inhibit protein-protein interactions within the human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER) family, particularly those involving HER2 and its heterodimerization partners EGFR and HER3. More specifically, the invention encompasses a class of symmetric, conformationally constrained peptidomimetics designed to bind with high affinity and selectivity to domain IV of the HER2 extracellular region, thereby preventing the formation of oncogenic heterodimers that drive aberrant signaling in breast, lung, and ovarian cancers. These compounds are engineered to mimic key structural and functional motifs of natural protein-protein interaction interfaces, incorporating chiral β-amino acid residues and a Pro-Pro turn motif that collectively confer enhanced metabolic stability, target specificity, and biological potency. The invention further includes methods of using these compounds for the treatment of cancers characterized by overexpression or mutation of HER2, including but not limited to HER2-positive breast cancer, non-small cell lung cancer harboring EGFR mutations, and other malignancies dependent on HER2-mediated signaling pathways. The compounds disclosed herein represent a significant advancement over existing therapeutic modalities, including monoclonal antibodies and small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors, by directly targeting the extracellular dimerization interface rather than the intracellular kinase domain, thereby circumventing common mechanisms of acquired resistance and offering a novel mechanism of action with improved pharmacokinetic properties.

## BACKGROUND ART

The human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER) family comprises four structurally related receptor tyrosine kinases—HER1 (EGFR), HER2, HER3, and HER4—that play essential roles in regulating cell proliferation, survival, differentiation, and migration under physiological conditions. Upon ligand binding to their extracellular domains, these receptors undergo conformational changes that promote homo- or heterodimerization, leading to trans-autophosphorylation of intracellular tyrosine residues and activation of downstream signaling cascades such as the RAS-MAPK and PI3K-AKT pathways. Dysregulation of this signaling network, whether through gene amplification, overexpression, or activating mutations, is a well-established driver of tumorigenesis in multiple epithelial cancers. Among the HER family members, HER2 stands out as a critical oncogenic node due to its lack of a known ligand, its propensity to form heterodimers with other HER receptors, and its frequent amplification in aggressive cancers. In particular, the HER2-HER3 heterodimer is recognized as the most potent signaling unit in the family, exhibiting strong kinase activity and robust activation of the PI3K pathway, while the EGFR-HER2 heterodimer contributes significantly to resistance mechanisms against EGFR-targeted therapies in non-small cell lung cancer.

Therapeutic strategies aimed at inhibiting HER2 signaling have evolved over the past two decades, beginning with the development of monoclonal antibodies such as trastuzumab and pertuzumab. Trastuzumab binds to domain IV of HER2 and exerts its effects through multiple mechanisms, including inhibition of receptor shedding, induction of antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, and interference with downstream signaling. Pertuzumab, in contrast, binds to domain II of HER2 and is specifically designed to block dimerization by sterically hindering the interface required for heterodimer formation with HER3 and EGFR. While both agents have demonstrated clinical efficacy in HER2-positive breast cancer, their use is limited by several inherent drawbacks. Monoclonal antibodies are large biologics with poor tissue penetration, high production costs, susceptibility to immunogenicity, and limited stability under non-ideal storage conditions. Furthermore, resistance to these agents frequently develops through compensatory signaling via alternative HER family members, upregulation of bypass pathways, or acquisition of mutations that alter the extracellular binding epitope.

Small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) such as lapatinib, neratinib, and erlotinib offer an alternative approach by targeting the intracellular kinase domain of HER2 or EGFR. However, these compounds are often hampered by poor selectivity, off-target effects, and the rapid emergence of resistance mutations, most notably the T790M gatekeeper mutation in EGFR, which reduces drug binding affinity by steric hindrance and increased ATP affinity. Moreover, TKIs do not prevent the initial dimerization event that triggers receptor activation, leaving the upstream signaling cascade intact and allowing for continued survival signaling through alternative dimer partners. Peptide-based inhibitors have been explored as intermediates between antibodies and small molecules, offering greater specificity than TKIs and improved tissue penetration compared to antibodies. However, linear peptides are inherently unstable in biological fluids due to rapid proteolytic degradation by serum and cellular proteases, rendering them unsuitable for systemic administration without extensive chemical modification.

Previous attempts to develop dimerization inhibitors have focused on linear peptidomimetics derived from the interface regions of HER2 or its binding partners. These compounds, while demonstrating promising in vitro activity, suffered from poor pharmacokinetic profiles, low oral bioavailability, and insufficient metabolic stability. Cyclization of peptides has emerged as a powerful strategy to enhance conformational rigidity, improve binding affinity through pre-organization of pharmacophoric elements, and confer resistance to enzymatic degradation. However, prior art has not disclosed a cyclic peptidomimetic that simultaneously achieves high affinity for HER2 domain IV, specificity over other HER family members, inhibition of both EGFR-HER2 and HER2-HER3 heterodimerization, and metabolic stability sufficient for in vivo efficacy. Furthermore, no prior compound has demonstrated the critical dependence of biological activity on the precise stereochemical configuration of both the β-amino acid side chain and the Pro-Pro dipeptide turn, a feature that enables fine-tuning of molecular orientation and interaction with the dimerization interface. The present invention addresses these longstanding deficiencies by introducing a novel class of symmetric, cyclic peptidomimetics whose design is informed by detailed structural and biophysical analysis of the HER2 extracellular domain and its dimerization interfaces. The compounds of this invention are not merely analogs of existing inhibitors but represent a fundamentally new chemical scaffold that exploits chiral control and conformational constraint to achieve unprecedented selectivity and potency against oncogenic HER2 heterodimers.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of cyclic peptidomimetic compounds that selectively inhibit the heterodimerization of HER2 with EGFR and HER3 by binding with high affinity to domain IV of the HER2 extracellular domain, thereby blocking the initiation of downstream oncogenic signaling pathways. The compounds are characterized by a symmetric, macrocyclic backbone structure that incorporates two identical pharmacophoric units derived from the sequence Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp, linked via a central Pro-Pro dipeptide motif that enforces a specific β-turn conformation critical for optimal spatial orientation of the side chains. Each pharmacophoric unit contains a positively charged arginine residue, a hydrophobic β-naphthylalanine (Anapa) residue, a phenylalanine residue, and an aspartic acid residue, collectively mimicking key residues found at the protein-protein interaction interface of HER2 with its dimerization partners. The stereochemistry of the β-amino acid Anapa and the Pro-Pro sequence is precisely controlled to ensure that the β-naphthyl groups are positioned to engage a hydrophobic pocket on HER2 domain IV that is essential for dimerization, while the arginine and aspartic acid residues form stabilizing electrostatic interactions with conserved residues such as Glu544, Asp570, and Gly581. The most preferred compound, designated herein as Compound 18, possesses an L-Pro-D-Pro sequence and an (R)-configured Anapa residue, which together confer a right-handed β-hairpin fold that optimally presents the pharmacophoric groups to the dimerization interface. This compound exhibits potent antiproliferative activity in HER2-overexpressing cancer cell lines, including SKBR-3 and Calu-3, with IC50 values in the low nanomolar range, while showing minimal activity against HER2-negative cell lines such as MCF-7 and MCF-10A, thereby demonstrating exceptional target selectivity. The compound also demonstrates significant inhibition of HER2 phosphorylation, suppression of HER2-HER3 and HER2-EGFR heterodimerization in cellular assays, and induction of apoptosis in HER2-positive cancer cells. In vivo studies in a xenograft model of HER2-positive breast cancer show that Compound 18 significantly delays tumor growth following intratumoral administration, with a magnitude of effect comparable to the clinically used kinase inhibitor lapatinib. Importantly, Compound 18 retains its activity in EGFR-mutated lung cancer cell lines harboring the T790M resistance mutation and exhibits a synergistic effect when co-administered with the TKI erlotinib, suggesting potential for combination therapy in resistant disease. The cyclic structure of the compound confers remarkable stability in mouse serum, with no detectable degradation observed over 48 hours, a marked improvement over linear analogs that are rapidly cleaved within hours. Structural analysis by NMR and molecular modeling confirms that the chiral configuration of the Anapa residue and the Pro-Pro sequence dictates the three-dimensional orientation of the β-naphthyl groups, with only the (R)-Anapa/L-Pro-D-Pro isomer positioning both hydrophobic moieties to simultaneously engage the critical hydrophobic residues Pro543, Phe573, Pro584, and Pro590 on HER2 domain IV. Other diastereomers, even those with favorable docking energies, fail to inhibit dimerization because their altered stereochemistry causes misalignment of the pharmacophores relative to the dimerization interface, resulting in binding to non-functional regions of the protein. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these compounds in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, methods of treating cancer by administering an effective amount of the compound to a subject in need thereof, and diagnostic kits for identifying patients likely to respond to such therapy based on HER2 overexpression or dimerization status. The compounds of this invention represent a breakthrough in the field of targeted cancer therapeutics by providing the first small-molecule, orally bioavailable, metabolically stable, and highly selective inhibitors of HER2 heterodimerization that operate through a mechanism distinct from kinase inhibition or antibody-mediated blockade.

### Definitions/General Discussion

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “peptidomimetic” refers to a synthetic molecule designed to mimic the structural and functional features of a natural peptide while possessing enhanced stability, bioavailability, and target specificity. In the context of this invention, the peptidomimetics are macrocyclic compounds that replicate the spatial arrangement of key amino acid side chains involved in the protein-protein interaction between HER2 and its dimerization partners, particularly EGFR and HER3, without being susceptible to proteolytic cleavage. The term “HER2” refers to the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2, also known as ERBB2, a member of the HER family of receptor tyrosine kinases that lacks a known ligand but serves as the preferred dimerization partner for other HER receptors. The term “extracellular domain” or “ECD” refers to the portion of the HER2 protein that resides outside the cell membrane and is composed of four distinct domains (I–IV), with domain IV being the primary site of interaction for dimerization partners and the binding site for the compounds of this invention. The term “heterodimerization” refers to the physical association between two different HER family members, such as HER2 and EGFR or HER2 and HER3, which results in trans-autophosphorylation and activation of downstream signaling pathways that promote cell proliferation and survival. The term “β-amino acid” refers to an amino acid in which the amino group is attached to the β-carbon rather than the α-carbon, as in standard amino acids; in this invention, the β-amino acid is β-naphthylalanine (Anapa), which provides a bulky hydrophobic moiety that mimics the side chain of tryptophan found at protein-protein interfaces. The term “Pro-Pro sequence” refers to a dipeptide unit composed of two proline residues, whose conformational rigidity and ability to induce turns in peptides are exploited in this invention to enforce a specific β-hairpin geometry. The stereochemistry of the proline residues is denoted as L-Pro or D-Pro, where L-Pro refers to the naturally occurring (S)-configured proline and D-Pro refers to the unnatural (R)-configured proline; the combination L-Pro-D-Pro and D-Pro-L-Pro produce distinct turn geometries that are critical for biological activity. The term “chiral switch” refers to the phenomenon wherein a change in the stereochemistry of one or more chiral centers in the molecule results in a dramatic alteration in biological activity, as observed in this invention where only specific diastereomers of Compound 18 exhibit potent inhibition of HER2 dimerization. The term “IC50” refers to the concentration of a compound required to inhibit 50% of a biological activity, such as cell proliferation or receptor phosphorylation, and is used herein to quantify the potency of the compounds. The term “Kd” refers to the dissociation constant, a measure of binding affinity between the compound and its target protein, determined by surface plasmon resonance and shown to be in the submicromolar range for the preferred compound. The term “dimerization interface” refers to the specific region on the surface of HER2 domain IV that physically contacts EGFR or HER3 during heterodimer formation, and which is occupied by the compounds of this invention to prevent dimerization. The term “pharmacophore” refers to the ensemble of steric and electronic features of a molecule that are necessary for its biological activity, and in this invention includes the arginine side chain, the β-naphthyl group, the phenylalanine side chain, and the aspartic acid side chain arranged in a specific three-dimensional orientation. The term “metabolic stability” refers to the resistance of a compound to enzymatic degradation in biological fluids, particularly serum, and is demonstrated herein by the absence of detectable degradation over 48 hours in mouse serum.
