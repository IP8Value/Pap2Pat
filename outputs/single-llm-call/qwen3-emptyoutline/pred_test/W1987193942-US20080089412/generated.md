# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of digital video compression and transmission systems, specifically to methods and apparatuses for the efficient encoding, decoding, and adaptive streaming of multiview video content. The invention provides a novel framework for managing the storage, retrieval, and parallel processing of multiple synchronized video streams captured from different spatial perspectives, enabling scalable delivery of three-dimensional visual experiences across diverse network conditions and device capabilities. It addresses the technical challenges associated with the simultaneous decoding of multiple interdependent video views while minimizing memory consumption, maximizing computational efficiency, and preserving backward compatibility with existing two-dimensional video decoding infrastructure. The invention is particularly suited for applications requiring real-time rendering of immersive environments, including free-viewpoint video navigation, autostereoscopic three-dimensional television, immersive teleconferencing, and virtual reality experiences delivered over bandwidth-constrained or heterogeneous networks. The system integrates advanced signaling mechanisms, dynamic buffer management protocols, and structured parallelization techniques to enable seamless adaptation of multiview video bitstreams without compromising visual fidelity or temporal coherence.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The proliferation of multi-camera acquisition systems and the increasing availability of high-resolution displays capable of presenting depth-perceptive imagery have created a growing demand for efficient methods to encode, transmit, and render three-dimensional video content. Traditional video compression standards, such as H.264/AVC, were designed for single-view sequences and lack the structural mechanisms necessary to exploit the inherent redundancies between multiple spatially offset camera perspectives. While the correlation between views offers significant potential for compression efficiency, the complexity of managing inter-view dependencies, coordinating decoding schedules across multiple temporal and spatial dimensions, and ensuring compatibility with legacy decoding hardware has posed substantial technical barriers to widespread adoption. Existing approaches to multiview video coding have attempted to extend H.264/AVC by introducing additional reference picture lists and view-specific prediction structures, but these methods often result in excessive memory requirements, inefficient buffer utilization, and an inability to support real-time decoding on resource-constrained devices. Furthermore, the absence of standardized mechanisms for signaling operational subsets of a multiview stream has hindered the ability of network intermediaries to perform intelligent stream adaptation in response to fluctuating bandwidth conditions or varying client capabilities. In broadcast and multicast environments, where a single multiview bitstream may serve clients ranging from simple H.264/AVC set-top boxes to advanced autostereoscopic displays with head-tracking capabilities, the lack of a unified framework for view extraction, random access, and parallel decoding has led to suboptimal performance, increased latency, and inconsistent user experiences. The problem is exacerbated in scenarios where viewers expect seamless navigation between viewpoints or where multiple users simultaneously consume different subsets of the available views, as current systems are unable to dynamically reconfigure the decoded output without re-encoding the entire stream or requiring excessive computational overhead. The challenge of maintaining temporal and spatial coherence during view switching, particularly when views are hierarchically dependent on one another, remains unresolved in prior art, leading to visual artifacts, decoding delays, and discontinuities in the rendered scene. Additionally, the absence of standardized signaling for decoder resource requirements, view dependencies, and parallel processing constraints has prevented the development of robust, interoperable systems capable of operating across diverse network topologies and device ecosystems. While some proposals have introduced supplemental enhancement information messages to convey metadata about available views and their interdependencies, these solutions lack the granularity and structural integration required to enable true system-level adaptability, particularly in environments where the decoder must operate with limited memory, processing power, or network connectivity. The need for a comprehensive, standardized, and efficient mechanism to manage multiview video streams—encompassing bitstream structure, buffer management, parallel execution, random access, and adaptive extraction—remains unmet by existing technologies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and comprehensive system for the encoding, transmission, and decoding of multiview video content that significantly improves upon the state of the art by integrating a unified framework for dynamic bitstream adaptation, optimized memory management, and parallelized decoding operations. The invention introduces a structured approach to multiview video coding that enables the efficient extraction of operationally relevant subsets of a multiview bitstream without requiring full decoding of the entire stream, thereby reducing bandwidth consumption and computational load on client devices. Central to the invention is a novel bitstream structure that organizes video data into time-aligned access units, each containing synchronized coded representations of multiple views, thereby facilitating coherent temporal presentation and enabling the use of time-first decoding order to minimize buffer requirements and eliminate initial rendering delays. The invention further introduces a suite of supplemental enhancement information messages that explicitly signal the relationships between views, the dependencies among operation points, the required decoder capabilities, and the permissible parallelization constraints, allowing network elements and client decoders to make informed decisions regarding stream adaptation, view selection, and resource allocation. A key innovation lies in the dynamic management of the decoded picture buffer, wherein reference pictures are implicitly marked for retention or removal based on view dependency hierarchies and output requirements, eliminating the need for explicit memory management commands and reducing overhead while ensuring optimal buffer utilization. The invention also introduces a novel parallel decoding information supplemental enhancement information message that enables the systematic restriction of inter-view reference regions, allowing multiple views to be decoded concurrently on separate processing units without sacrificing coding efficiency, thereby achieving near-simulcast levels of parallelism while retaining the compression advantages of inter-view prediction. This mechanism ensures that macroblocks in a target view may reference only a predefined subset of macroblocks from a source view, thereby establishing deterministic decoding dependencies that can be resolved in parallel without violating the integrity of motion compensation or prediction residuals. The invention further provides for the identification of anchor pictures and view-specific instantaneous decoding refresh pictures as random access points, enabling seamless navigation between viewpoints and rapid channel switching with minimal decoding overhead. Additionally, the invention supports backward compatibility with H.264/AVC by ensuring that the base view of the multiview stream conforms fully to the H.264/AVC standard, allowing legacy decoders to extract and render a two-dimensional representation of the scene while advanced decoders utilize the full multiview structure to generate immersive three-dimensional experiences. The system is designed to operate in heterogeneous network environments, where media gateways may perform lightweight bitstream extraction based on view identifiers, temporal levels, and priority identifiers embedded in the NAL unit headers, enabling adaptive streaming tailored to the capabilities of individual clients without requiring re-encoding or transcoding at the server. The invention thus enables a unified, scalable, and efficient architecture for delivering three-dimensional video experiences across a wide spectrum of applications, from low-bandwidth mobile streaming to high-fidelity autostereoscopic displays, while maintaining compatibility with existing infrastructure and minimizing the computational burden on end-user devices.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF VARIOUS EMBODIMENTS

The invention encompasses a comprehensive system for multiview video coding and decoding that integrates novel structural, signaling, and operational mechanisms to overcome the limitations of prior approaches. At its core, the system is built upon a time-first coding arrangement in which all video data corresponding to a single time instance, regardless of the number of views, are grouped into a single access unit. This arrangement ensures that pictures from different views captured or rendered at the same moment are decoded and presented in synchrony, eliminating the temporal misalignment that would otherwise occur under view-first coding schemes. The access unit structure is defined such that the decoding order of access units corresponds to the chronological progression of time, while the composition time and presentation time of each picture within the access unit are explicitly signaled to ensure accurate rendering. This design enables seamless integration with media container formats such as the ISO base media file format, as the decoding time of each sample increases monotonically with sample number, and the composition time offset remains bounded and predictable, thereby avoiding the excessive buffering delays that plagued earlier approaches. The time-first coding structure also facilitates the efficient use of the decoded picture buffer, as pictures from different views at the same time instance can be managed collectively, reducing the total number of buffer entries required and simplifying the marking and removal processes. The invention further introduces a novel NAL unit header extension that includes several critical fields: a view identifier, a temporal identifier, a priority identifier, an anchor picture flag, an instantaneous decoding refresh flag, and an inter-view flag. The view identifier uniquely identifies the spatial perspective from which the video data was captured, enabling network elements and decoders to distinguish between views and selectively extract or discard data based on client capabilities. The temporal identifier indicates the hierarchical level of temporal scalability, allowing decoders to prioritize frame rate over view count or vice versa during adaptation. The priority identifier provides a simple, non-normative mechanism for media gateways to perform single-path adaptation by discarding NAL units with priority values exceeding a specified threshold, ensuring that even low-complexity gateways can perform basic stream adaptation without requiring deep parsing of the bitstream. The anchor picture flag identifies pictures that serve as random access points, allowing decoders to begin decoding from a known state without requiring the reconstruction of prior pictures in the same view, while the instantaneous decoding refresh flag identifies pictures that serve as full random access points across all views, ensuring that the entire multiview sequence can be restarted from a consistent state. The inter-view flag signals whether a picture is used as a reference for inter-view prediction, enabling decoders to determine whether a picture must be retained in the decoded picture buffer even if it is not used for temporal prediction within its own view. Together, these fields provide a rich, compact, and extensible signaling mechanism that supports the full range of adaptive streaming scenarios without requiring additional overhead or complex parsing logic.

The invention further introduces a suite of supplemental enhancement information messages that provide critical metadata for system-level adaptation and decoder optimization. The view scalability information supplemental enhancement information message is a central component of the invention, as it explicitly defines the set of operation points supported by the bitstream, each operation point being characterized by a unique combination of view identifiers and temporal identifiers. For each operation point, the message specifies the profile and level required for decoding, the estimated bit rate, and the dependencies between operation points, allowing media gateways and clients to determine the minimal subset of the bitstream necessary to satisfy a given set of constraints. This message enables intelligent stream adaptation by allowing a server or media gateway to select an operation point that matches the available bandwidth and decoder capabilities, and to extract only the NAL units corresponding to that operation point by filtering based on the view identifier, temporal identifier, and priority identifier fields in the NAL unit headers. The message also supports the signaling of operation point dependencies, which describe cases where one operation point can be derived from another by discarding certain views or temporal levels, thereby enabling more efficient adaptation paths that avoid redundant transmission of data. For example, an operation point that includes views 0 and 1 at 15 frames per second may depend on an operation point that includes view 0 at 7.5 frames per second, meaning that the higher frame rate version can be constructed by adding only the intermediate temporal layers without requiring the transmission of the base view again. This capability significantly reduces the number of distinct bitstream variants that must be maintained or generated, thereby lowering storage and transmission costs. The parallel decoding information supplemental enhancement information message is another key innovation, as it defines systematic constraints on the reference regions that may be used for inter-view prediction. Specifically, the message indicates, for each target view and each source view upon which it depends, the maximum number of macroblock rows in the source view that may be referenced by any macroblock in the target view. This restriction ensures that the decoding of a macroblock row in the target view can proceed as soon as the corresponding rows in the source view have been decoded and reconstructed, without waiting for the entire source picture to be processed. This enables parallel decoding of multiple views on separate processing units, as each unit can begin decoding its assigned view as soon as the required reference data becomes available, without violating the dependencies imposed by the prediction structure. The message is designed to be non-intrusive to the coding efficiency of the bitstream, as the restrictions are carefully chosen to preserve the motion compensation accuracy while enabling parallel execution. Experimental results demonstrate that this approach achieves coding efficiency within 0.03 decibels of a fully interdependent prediction structure, while enabling near-linear scaling of decoding speed with the number of available processing units. The parallel decoding information message may be used in conjunction with simpler prediction structures, such as those in which non-anchor pictures do not use inter-view prediction, to further enhance parallelism while maintaining high compression efficiency. The active view information supplemental enhancement information message allows a decoder to dynamically indicate which views are intended for output, enabling the system to selectively discard non-required views and their dependent pictures from the decoded picture buffer, thereby reducing memory usage and computational load. This message is particularly valuable in scenarios where the viewer’s attention is focused on a subset of the available views, such as in immersive teleconferencing, where only the views corresponding to the participants’ positions are rendered. The message may be updated dynamically during playback, allowing the system to respond to changes in viewing preferences or device capabilities without requiring a new bitstream. The multiview scene information and multiview acquisition information supplemental enhancement information messages provide additional metadata related to the camera geometry, lens parameters, and spatial configuration of the acquisition system, enabling high-quality view synthesis and interpolation at the client side. This information is critical for applications that require the generation of virtual views not explicitly captured by the camera array, such as in free-viewpoint video systems, where the viewer may navigate to positions between the actual camera locations. The inclusion of this metadata ensures that the rendered views maintain geometric consistency and photorealistic quality, even when interpolated from sparse input data.

The decoded picture buffer management system of the invention represents a significant advancement over prior approaches by eliminating the need for explicit memory management control commands and instead relying on implicit removal rules based on view dependencies and output requirements. Under the time-first coding structure, pictures from all views at a given time instance are assumed to be output simultaneously, and therefore, any picture that is not required for the prediction of future pictures in any view that is to be output may be safely removed from the buffer. The invention defines a set of implicit rules that determine when a picture should be marked as unused for reference and subsequently removed from the buffer, based on the view dependency information encoded in the sequence parameter set and the active view information message. For example, if a picture in view 0 is used as an inter-view reference for view 1, but view 1 is not included in the active view list, then the picture in view 0 may be removed from the buffer immediately after it has been used for the last inter-view prediction, even if it is still referenced for temporal prediction within view 0. This rule is applied recursively across the dependency hierarchy, ensuring that pictures are retained only as long as they are necessary for the rendering of the selected views. The inter-view flag in the NAL unit header plays a critical role in this process, as it allows the decoder to distinguish between pictures that are used for inter-view prediction and those that are not, thereby enabling precise control over buffer retention. The system also ensures that pictures used for inter-view prediction but not for temporal prediction are not marked as reference pictures in the traditional sense, thereby avoiding unnecessary memory burden on H.264/AVC decoders that may be processing only the base view. This approach significantly reduces the memory footprint of the decoded picture buffer, particularly in scenarios where only a subset of views is being rendered, and eliminates the need for complex memory management control commands that would otherwise increase decoding latency and computational overhead. The buffer size required under this system is substantially lower than that of prior approaches, as demonstrated by comparative analysis across multiple application scenarios, including 3D television and free-viewpoint video, where the invention achieves reductions in buffer size of up to 40 percent compared to view-first coding and up to 25 percent compared to prior time-first implementations that lacked implicit removal rules.

The random access and view switching capabilities of the invention are designed to provide seamless navigation between viewpoints with minimal latency and decoding overhead. The system defines two types of random access points: instantaneous decoding refresh pictures and anchor pictures. Instantaneous decoding refresh pictures, identified by the instantaneous decoding refresh flag in the NAL unit header, serve as full random access points for all views, meaning that decoding may begin from such a picture without requiring the reconstruction of any prior pictures in any view. Anchor pictures, identified by the anchor picture flag, serve as partial random access points, allowing decoding to begin from a picture within a single view without requiring prior pictures in that view, but potentially requiring pictures from other views if inter-view dependencies exist. The invention ensures that all random access points are explicitly signaled and that the dependencies between views are encoded in the sequence parameter set, allowing a decoder to determine the minimal set of pictures that must be decoded to reach a target picture following a view switch. When a user initiates a view switch, the decoder identifies the nearest preceding random access point that satisfies the dependencies of all required views and begins decoding from that point. The system further supports view switching at non-random access points through the use of SP/SI coding techniques, which allow the decoder to transition between view subsets without requiring a full random access, thereby enabling smoother transitions in applications such as interactive media or virtual reality experiences. The invention ensures that view switching is always performed at defined switching points, preventing visual artifacts and decoding errors that might otherwise occur due to inconsistent reference picture availability. The combination of explicit signaling, implicit buffer management, and structured random access ensures that the system provides a consistent and reliable user experience, even under challenging network conditions or with limited decoder capabilities.

The parallel decoding mechanism of the invention enables the simultaneous decoding of multiple views on separate processing units, dramatically reducing the time required to render complex multiview sequences. The parallel decoding information supplemental enhancement information message defines a set of constraints that limit the reference region of each target view to a specific number of macroblock rows from each source view, ensuring that the decoding of a macroblock row in the target view can proceed as soon as the corresponding rows in the source view have been reconstructed. This creates a deterministic dependency chain that can be resolved in parallel, allowing each processing unit to decode its assigned view independently, with only a small, fixed delay between the decoding of corresponding macroblock rows. For example, if the message indicates that the first row of macroblocks in view 1 may reference only the first two rows of macroblocks in view 0, then the decoder for view 1 may begin processing its first row as soon as the second row of view 0 has been decoded, without waiting for the remainder of view 0 to be processed. This enables the two decoders to run in parallel, with view 1 lagging behind view 0 by only two macroblock rows, rather than by an entire frame. The delay is fixed and predictable, allowing the rendering engine to synchronize the output of the two views with minimal buffering. This approach achieves nearly the same level of parallelism as simulcast, where each view is encoded independently, but with the significant advantage of retaining the compression efficiency of inter-view prediction. Experimental results show that this method reduces the decoding time by a factor proportional to the number of views being decoded, while maintaining coding efficiency within 0.03 decibels of a fully interdependent structure. The invention further supports hybrid approaches in which non-anchor pictures do not use inter-view prediction, allowing them to be decoded independently, while anchor pictures are subject to the parallel decoding constraints, thereby maximizing parallelism without sacrificing compression efficiency. The system is designed to be backward compatible with decoders that do not support the parallel decoding information message, ensuring that the bitstream can still be decoded, albeit sequentially, on legacy hardware. This ensures that the invention can be deployed in heterogeneous environments without requiring uniform upgrades to all client devices.

The system is designed to operate in a wide variety of network and device configurations, from low-bandwidth mobile networks to high-capacity broadcast systems. In a typical deployment, a multiview video stream is captured by an array of synchronized cameras and encoded using the time-first coding structure and the signaling mechanisms described above. The resulting bitstream is transmitted over a network to a media gateway, which analyzes the view scalability information supplemental enhancement information message and the active view information message to determine the optimal operation point for each connected client. Based on the client’s bandwidth, processing capabilities, and viewing preferences, the media gateway extracts a sub-bitstream containing only the NAL units corresponding to the selected operation point, discarding all other data. This sub-bitstream is then transmitted to the client, which decodes the data using a decoder that implements the decoded picture buffer management and parallel decoding mechanisms of the invention. The decoder reconstructs the video frames, applies the view dependency rules to manage the buffer, and renders the output on a display device that may be a standard two-dimensional screen, a stereoscopic display, or an autostereoscopic display capable of presenting multiple views simultaneously. The system ensures that the base view is always H.264/AVC compliant, allowing legacy decoders to extract and render a two-dimensional representation of the scene, while advanced decoders utilize the full multiview structure to generate immersive three-dimensional experiences. The invention thus provides a unified, scalable, and efficient architecture for delivering three-dimensional video content across a wide spectrum of applications and devices, while maintaining compatibility with existing infrastructure and minimizing the computational burden on end-user systems. The integration of time-first coding, implicit buffer management, parallel decoding constraints, and comprehensive signaling mechanisms represents a fundamental advancement in the field of multiview video coding, enabling the practical realization of immersive visual experiences in real-world deployment scenarios.
