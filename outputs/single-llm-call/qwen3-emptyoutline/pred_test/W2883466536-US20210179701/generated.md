# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology, immunology, and therapeutic biologics, specifically to monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to human platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC) with high affinity and neutralizing capacity. The invention further encompasses methods for detecting and quantifying PDGF-CC in biological samples, pharmaceutical compositions comprising such antibodies, diagnostic kits for assessing PDGF-CC expression levels in tissue and serum, and screening assays for identifying compounds that modulate the PDGF-CC/PDGFRα signaling axis. The antibodies described herein are particularly useful for the treatment of diseases characterized by aberrant PDGF-CC activity, including but not limited to solid tumors such as breast, colon, pancreatic, renal, and prostate carcinomas; fibrotic disorders of the liver, kidney, lung, and heart; and neurological conditions involving disruption of the blood-brain barrier or blood-retinal barrier, such as ischemic stroke, traumatic brain injury, multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and diabetic retinopathy. The invention provides novel, highly specific, and functionally validated therapeutic and diagnostic tools that are not disclosed in the prior art and that overcome the limitations of existing approaches targeting the PDGF family.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) family comprises a group of structurally related dimeric proteins that play essential roles in embryonic development, wound healing, and tissue homeostasis through activation of receptor tyrosine kinases, primarily PDGFRα and PDGFRβ. Among the members of this family, PDGF-CC has emerged as a critical mediator of pathological processes in both neoplastic and non-neoplastic conditions. Unlike other PDGF ligands, PDGF-CC is synthesized as a latent precursor protein requiring proteolytic cleavage of its N-terminal CUB domain to release the biologically active growth factor domain (GFD), which then binds and activates PDGFRα homodimers. This unique activation mechanism confers spatial and temporal regulation to PDGF-CC signaling, making it particularly relevant in contexts where extracellular proteolytic activity is dysregulated, such as in tumor microenvironments and sites of neuroinflammation.

Prior to the present invention, no monoclonal antibodies had been developed that specifically and potently neutralize human PDGF-CC without cross-reactivity to other PDGF family members, including PDGF-AA, PDGF-BB, or PDGF-DD. While small molecule inhibitors such as imatinib have been shown to inhibit PDGFRα kinase activity and demonstrate therapeutic benefit in preclinical models of stroke and glioma, these agents lack specificity and inhibit multiple tyrosine kinases, leading to off-target effects and dose-limiting toxicities. Moreover, imatinib does not directly target the ligand itself, leaving the possibility of compensatory upregulation of PDGF-CC expression or alternative activation pathways unaddressed. Antibodies that bind and neutralize the ligand directly offer a more precise mechanism of intervention, preventing receptor engagement at its source and thereby reducing the likelihood of resistance mechanisms.

Previous attempts to generate antibodies against PDGF-CC were hindered by the structural similarity between PDGF-CC and other PDGF ligands, particularly PDGF-DD, which shares approximately 45% sequence identity in the GFD region. Consequently, earlier polyclonal reagents and non-specific monoclonal antibodies exhibited cross-reactivity, rendering them unsuitable for precise detection or therapeutic neutralization. Furthermore, the lack of high-affinity, human-specific monoclonal antibodies impeded the systematic evaluation of PDGF-CC expression patterns in human tissues, limiting the understanding of its role in disease progression and the identification of patient populations most likely to benefit from targeted therapy.

In addition, the biological significance of PDGF-CC in human pathology has been difficult to fully elucidate due to the absence of reliable tools for immunohistochemical detection in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded clinical samples. Although mRNA expression data from public databases suggested widespread expression of PDGF-CC in organs such as the heart, liver, kidney, and pancreas, protein-level validation in human tissues remained incomplete and inconsistent. The discovery that PDGF-CC is not only expressed in parenchymal cells but also prominently upregulated in tumor-associated stroma and vasculature has profound implications for therapeutic targeting, as these compartments are often more accessible to systemically administered biologics than the tumor cells themselves.

The blood-brain barrier (BBB) and blood-retinal barrier (BRB) represent specialized endothelial structures that tightly regulate the exchange of molecules between the circulation and neural tissues. Disruption of these barriers is a hallmark of numerous acute and chronic neurological and ophthalmological disorders. Recent studies have identified the PDGF-CC/PDGFRα axis as a key regulator of barrier integrity, with intraventricular or intraocular administration of active PDGF-CC sufficient to induce rapid vascular permeability. However, no therapeutic agent capable of blocking this effect in vivo had been developed, leaving a critical gap in the treatment of conditions such as ischemic stroke, where BBB breakdown contributes to edema, hemorrhagic transformation, and secondary neuronal injury.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by providing a series of novel monoclonal antibodies that bind human PDGF-CC with sub-nanomolar affinity, exhibit no cross-reactivity with other PDGF ligands or unrelated growth factors, and effectively neutralize PDGF-CC-induced receptor activation and barrier disruption in vitro and in vivo. These antibodies are derived from a robust immunization and hybridoma screening strategy, fully characterized at the molecular, biochemical, and functional levels, and engineered into a chimeric human-mouse format suitable for clinical development. The invention further includes comprehensive immunohistochemical analyses of PDGF-CC expression across a wide spectrum of human tissues and tumors, establishing a clear correlation between elevated PDGF-CC levels and disease severity in multiple organ systems. Together, these components form a comprehensive and integrated platform for the diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment of PDGF-CC-driven pathologies, representing a significant advancement over the prior art.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to human platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC) with high affinity and neutralizing activity, without cross-reactivity to other members of the PDGF family or unrelated proteins. These antibodies are capable of inhibiting the biological activity of PDGF-CC by preventing its interaction with the PDGFRα receptor, thereby blocking downstream signaling events such as receptor autophosphorylation, cell proliferation, migration, and vascular permeability. The invention encompasses fully characterized monoclonal antibodies designated 6B3, 11F5, 19C7, and their chimeric variant ch6B3, each of which has been demonstrated to bind with high specificity to the growth factor domain of human PDGF-CC in both latent and activated forms, and to neutralize PDGF-CC-mediated effects in cellular and animal models.

The monoclonal antibodies of the invention are produced through immunization of mice with recombinant human PDGF-CC core domain protein, followed by hybridoma generation, selection, and extensive functional validation. The lead antibody, 6B3, exhibits a dissociation constant (KD) in the low nanomolar range, as determined by biosensor analysis, and completely abrogates PDGF-CC-induced phosphorylation of PDGFRα in porcine aorta endothelial cells. The antibody does not bind to mouse or rat PDGF-CC, confirming its human-specificity, and shows no reactivity with PDGF-AA, PDGF-BB, or PDGF-DD, demonstrating exceptional selectivity within the PDGF family. The epitope recognized by antibody 6B3 has been precisely mapped to a conformational region within the growth factor domain of PDGF-CC, corresponding to a peptide sequence designated as peptide 3, which is critical for receptor binding and is conserved in human but not in rodent orthologs.

A chimeric version of antibody 6B3, designated ch6B3, has been generated by fusing the variable regions of the murine antibody with human IgG1 constant regions, resulting in a molecule that retains full binding affinity and neutralizing capacity while reducing immunogenicity in human subjects. The chimeric antibody has been shown to effectively cross the compromised blood-retinal barrier in vivo and prevent PDGF-CC-induced vascular leakage following intraocular injection, demonstrating its potential for therapeutic application in neurological and ocular disorders. In addition, the chimeric antibody retains the ability to neutralize PDGF-CC activity in vitro and is suitable for large-scale production in mammalian expression systems such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells.

The invention further provides diagnostic and research tools in the form of immunoassays for the detection and quantification of PDGF-CC in biological fluids and tissue sections. These assays utilize the monoclonal antibodies of the invention as capture or detection reagents and are validated for use in enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA), immunoblotting, and immunohistochemistry. The antibodies enable the reliable detection of PDGF-CC in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded human tissues, revealing widespread expression in epithelial cells, stromal fibroblasts, and vascular endothelial cells across multiple organs. Importantly, elevated PDGF-CC expression is consistently observed in a broad range of human malignancies, including breast, colon, pancreatic, renal, and prostate carcinomas, as well as gliosarcoma, with statistically significant increases compared to matched normal tissues. This expression pattern supports the use of the antibodies as diagnostic biomarkers for patient stratification and as tools for monitoring disease progression or therapeutic response.

The invention also includes kits for the detection of PDGF-CC in clinical and research settings, comprising the monoclonal antibodies of the invention, labeled secondary reagents, buffers, controls, and instructions for use. These kits are designed for use in laboratory settings, pathology departments, and clinical diagnostics, enabling standardized and reproducible assessment of PDGF-CC levels in tissue biopsies, serum, or cerebrospinal fluid. Additionally, the invention provides a high-throughput drug screening assay that utilizes immobilized PDGF-CC and the monoclonal antibodies to identify small molecules, peptides, or other biologics capable of disrupting the PDGF-CC/PDGFRα interaction, thereby facilitating the discovery of novel therapeutics.

The therapeutic utility of the monoclonal antibodies of the invention is demonstrated in vivo through the inhibition of PDGF-CC-induced disruption of the blood-retinal barrier, a model of vascular permeability relevant to stroke, multiple sclerosis, and diabetic retinopathy. A single systemic administration of the chimeric antibody ch6B3 is sufficient to prevent tracer extravasation following intraocular injection of active PDGF-CC, confirming its ability to penetrate compromised barriers and exert functional neutralization in a living organism. These findings establish the antibodies as viable candidates for the development of biologic therapies targeting PDGF-CC-driven pathologies in oncology, fibrosis, and neurology.

The invention thus provides a comprehensive and integrated set of tools and compositions that are novel, non-obvious, and industrially applicable, offering a significant advance over the prior art by enabling precise, specific, and effective targeting of PDGF-CC in human disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Treatment Methods and Pharmaceutical Formulations

The present invention provides methods for the treatment of diseases characterized by aberrant or excessive activity of platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC), including but not limited to solid tumors, fibrotic disorders, and neurological conditions associated with disruption of the blood-brain barrier or blood-retinal barrier. The therapeutic methods of the invention involve the administration of a therapeutically effective amount of a monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to human PDGF-CC and neutralizes its biological activity, thereby inhibiting downstream signaling through the PDGFRα receptor. The antibodies of the invention, including but not limited to 6B3, 11F5, 19C7, and ch6B3, are administered to a subject in need thereof, typically a human patient, via a route suitable for systemic delivery, including intravenous, subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intraperitoneal injection. The choice of route depends on the nature of the disease, the accessibility of the target tissue, and the pharmacokinetic profile of the antibody formulation.

The monoclonal antibodies of the invention are formulated into pharmaceutical compositions that include a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent suitable for parenteral administration. Such carriers may include saline, buffered saline, dextrose, water for injection, or mixtures thereof, optionally containing stabilizers such as human serum albumin, polysorbate 80, or sucrose to maintain antibody integrity during storage and delivery. The formulations may be lyophilized for long-term storage and reconstituted immediately prior to administration, or provided as sterile, preservative-free liquid solutions in single-use vials or pre-filled syringes. The concentration of the antibody in the formulation is typically between 10 mg/mL and 200 mg/mL, with preferred concentrations ranging from 50 mg/mL to 150 mg/mL, depending on the intended dosing regimen and volume constraints.

The dosage of the monoclonal antibody is determined based on the severity of the disease, the body weight of the subject, the route of administration, and the pharmacokinetic properties of the antibody. In general, a therapeutically effective dose ranges from 0.1 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg of body weight, administered once weekly, once every two weeks, or once every four weeks, depending on the clinical context. For the treatment of solid tumors such as breast, colon, pancreatic, renal, or prostate carcinoma, a dose of 5 mg/kg to 15 mg/kg administered intravenously every two weeks is preferred. For neurological conditions such as ischemic stroke or traumatic brain injury, a single bolus dose of 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg administered intravenously within 6 to 24 hours of the acute event is contemplated, with optional maintenance doses administered at intervals of 7 to 14 days. In the case of fibrotic diseases such as liver cirrhosis, pulmonary fibrosis, or renal interstitial fibrosis, chronic administration of 2 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg every two to four weeks is recommended to achieve sustained suppression of PDGF-CC-mediated fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix deposition.

The monoclonal antibodies of the invention may be administered as monotherapy or in combination with other therapeutic agents, including but not limited to chemotherapy, radiation therapy, targeted kinase inhibitors, anti-angiogenic agents, immunomodulatory drugs, or other biologics. For example, in the treatment of breast cancer, the antibody may be co-administered with taxanes, anthracyclines, or endocrine therapies such as tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors, particularly in cases where PDGF-CC overexpression correlates with hormone receptor-negative or basal-like phenotypes. In glioblastoma or other CNS malignancies, the antibody may be combined with temozolomide or bevacizumab to enhance tumor control and reduce peritumoral edema. In fibrotic disorders, the antibody may be administered alongside antifibrotic agents such as pirfenidone or nintedanib to achieve synergistic suppression of tissue remodeling.

The antibodies of the invention may also be engineered into alternative formats to enhance their therapeutic properties, including humanization, affinity maturation, Fc engineering for prolonged half-life, or conjugation to cytotoxic payloads for targeted delivery. Humanized versions of the lead antibody 6B3 may be generated by grafting the complementarity-determining regions onto human germline frameworks, followed by back-mutation of key framework residues to preserve binding affinity. Fc modifications such as the YTE mutation (M252Y/S254T/T256E) may be introduced to extend serum half-life by enhancing binding to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). In certain embodiments, the antibody may be fused to an albumin-binding domain or a polyethylene glycol moiety to further prolong circulation time. For applications requiring localized delivery, the antibody may be formulated into nanoparticles, liposomes, or hydrogels for sustained release at the site of pathology, such as intratumoral injection for solid tumors or intravitreal injection for retinal disorders.

The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention may further include adjunctive agents designed to mitigate potential side effects, such as anti-inflammatory agents to prevent infusion reactions, or antihistamines and corticosteroids to reduce the risk of hypersensitivity. The formulations are manufactured under current good manufacturing practices (cGMP) and are subject to rigorous quality control testing for purity, potency, sterility, endotoxin levels, and stability under accelerated storage conditions. The antibodies are characterized for aggregation state by size-exclusion chromatography, for binding affinity by surface plasmon resonance, and for neutralizing activity by in vitro receptor phosphorylation assays. The final product is packaged in sterile, single-use containers and labeled with instructions for storage, reconstitution, and administration, along with information regarding contraindications, warnings, and precautions.

The methods of treatment provided by the invention encompass both prophylactic and therapeutic applications. In high-risk populations, such as individuals with genetic predispositions to fibrotic disease or those undergoing radiation therapy with known risk of tissue fibrosis, prophylactic administration of the antibody may be initiated to prevent the onset of PDGF-CC-mediated damage. In the context of cancer, the antibody may be administered following surgical resection to prevent recurrence by targeting residual tumor-associated stroma. In neurological disorders, early intervention following acute injury or ischemic event may prevent secondary damage caused by barrier disruption and inflammatory cell infiltration. The invention thus provides a versatile and broadly applicable therapeutic strategy for a spectrum of diseases driven by dysregulated PDGF-CC signaling.

### PDGF-C/PDGF-CC Detection/Diagnosis Assays

The present invention provides highly specific and sensitive diagnostic assays for the detection and quantification of platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC) in biological samples, including serum, plasma, cerebrospinal fluid, tissue lysates, and formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections. These assays are based on the use of the monoclonal antibodies of the invention, which exhibit exceptional specificity for human PDGF-CC and negligible cross-reactivity with other PDGF family members or unrelated proteins. The assays are designed to enable accurate measurement of PDGF-CC levels in both clinical and research settings, facilitating patient stratification, disease monitoring, and therapeutic response assessment.

One embodiment of the invention is an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) configured for the quantitative detection of PDGF-CC in serum or plasma. The assay employs a capture antibody, selected from the group consisting of 6B3, 11F5, or 19C7, immobilized onto a solid-phase substrate such as a microtiter plate. After blocking nonspecific binding sites with a protein-based blocker, the sample is added and incubated under conditions that allow PDGF-CC to bind to the capture antibody. Following washing to remove unbound material, a detection antibody, either biotinylated or conjugated to an enzyme such as horseradish peroxidase or alkaline phosphatase, is applied. In some embodiments, the detection antibody is the same monoclonal antibody used for capture, while in others, a different clone recognizing a non-overlapping epitope is employed to form a sandwich assay. The bound detection antibody is then visualized using a chromogenic substrate, and the signal intensity is measured spectrophotometrically. A standard curve is generated using recombinant human PDGF-CC protein of known concentration, allowing for the precise quantification of PDGF-CC levels in unknown samples. The lower limit of detection for this assay is less than 10 pg/mL, and the assay demonstrates high precision, with intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation below 10%.

Another embodiment of the invention is an immunoblotting assay for the detection of PDGF-CC in tissue lysates or conditioned media. In this method, proteins are separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis under reducing or non-reducing conditions and transferred to a nitrocellulose or polyvinylidene difluoride membrane. The membrane is blocked and incubated with the monoclonal antibody of the invention, followed by a labeled secondary antibody. The antibody specifically detects both the latent (full-length) and activated (cleaved) forms of human PDGF-CC, which migrate at approximately 45 kDa and 25 kDa, respectively. The assay is validated using recombinant PDGF-CC as a positive control and samples from PDGF-CC knockout cell lines as negative controls. The absence of cross-reactivity with PDGF-DD, PDGF-BB, or other growth factors is confirmed by parallel probing with antibodies specific for those ligands.

A third embodiment of the invention is an immunohistochemical assay for the detection of PDGF-CC in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections. This method is particularly valuable for the pathological evaluation of tumor specimens and fibrotic tissues. Tissue sections are deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval using heat-induced epitope retrieval in citrate buffer at pH 6.0. Endogenous peroxidase activity is quenched, and the sections are incubated with the monoclonal antibody of the invention at a concentration of 0.5 to 5 μg/mL. Binding is detected using a labeled secondary antibody conjugated to horseradish peroxidase and visualized with 3,3′-diaminobenzidine chromogen. The staining pattern is evaluated by a pathologist using a semi-quantitative scoring system that assesses both the intensity of staining (0 to 3+) and the percentage of positively stained cells (0 to 5), with the final histochemical score calculated as the product of these two parameters. The assay has been validated across a wide range of human tissues, including breast, colon, pancreas, kidney, prostate, and brain, and demonstrates consistent and reproducible results. Negative controls, including omission of the primary antibody, substitution with an isotype-matched control antibody, and pre-adsorption of the primary antibody with excess recombinant PDGF-CC, are routinely performed to confirm specificity. The assay is capable of detecting PDGF-CC expression in epithelial cells, stromal fibroblasts, and vascular endothelial cells, with distinct patterns of localization that correlate with disease stage and prognosis.

The invention further encompasses a multiplexed assay platform that allows simultaneous detection of PDGF-CC alongside other biomarkers relevant to tumor progression or fibrosis, such as PDGFRα, VEGF-A, TGF-β, or CD34. This platform may be implemented using flow cytometry, immunofluorescence, or digital pathology systems, enabling the spatial and quantitative analysis of PDGF-CC expression in relation to other cellular components. For example, in tumor tissue, the co-expression of PDGF-CC with CD34 can be used to identify tumor-associated vasculature, while co-localization with α-smooth muscle actin or vimentin can indicate the presence of cancer-associated fibroblasts. The data generated by these assays can be integrated into digital pathology workflows for automated analysis, machine learning-based classification, and predictive modeling of therapeutic response.

The diagnostic assays of the invention are suitable for use in clinical laboratories, pathology departments, and translational research settings. They are designed to be compatible with standard laboratory equipment and protocols, require minimal sample volume, and provide results within a single working day. The high specificity and sensitivity of the monoclonal antibodies ensure that false positives and cross-reactivity are minimized, enhancing diagnostic accuracy. The assays can be used to identify patients with elevated PDGF-CC expression who are most likely to benefit from anti-PDGF-CC therapy, monitor changes in PDGF-CC levels during treatment, and detect early signs of disease recurrence or progression. The invention thus provides a powerful diagnostic toolkit that complements the therapeutic applications of the monoclonal antibodies and enables a precision medicine approach to PDGF-CC-driven diseases.

### Drug Screening Assay

The present invention provides a high-throughput drug screening assay for the identification of compounds capable of modulating the interaction between platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC) and its receptor PDGFRα. The assay is based on the use of the monoclonal antibodies of the invention as competitive binding agents to detect inhibitors of the PDGF-CC/PDGFRα interface, thereby enabling the discovery of novel small molecules, peptides, nucleic acids, or biologics that disrupt this pathogenic signaling axis. The assay is designed for use in both academic and industrial drug discovery settings and is compatible with automated liquid handling systems, microplate readers, and data analysis platforms.

In one embodiment, the assay utilizes a biosensor platform such as surface plasmon resonance (SPR) or bio-layer interferometry (BLI) to monitor real-time binding kinetics between immobilized PDGF-CC and its receptor PDGFRα. Recombinant human PDGF-CC is covalently coupled to a sensor chip via primary amine groups using standard NHS/EDC chemistry. PDGFRα is then flowed over the chip at a defined concentration to establish a baseline binding response. Test compounds, including libraries of small molecules, peptide fragments, or monoclonal antibody variants, are introduced into the system at varying concentrations, and their ability to inhibit or reduce the binding of PDGFRα to PDGF-CC is measured. A compound is considered a hit if it reduces the binding response by at least 50% at a concentration of 10 μM or lower. The assay is validated using the monoclonal antibody 6B3 as a positive control, which demonstrates complete inhibition of PDGFRα binding. The assay is performed under physiological conditions, including a buffer of HEPES-buffered saline with 0.05% surfactant, at a flow rate of 30 μL/min and a temperature of 25°C. Data are analyzed using software that fits binding curves to a 1:1 Langmuir model, allowing for the determination of inhibition constants (Ki) and the ranking of compounds by potency.

In an alternative embodiment, the assay is configured as a competitive enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). In this format, recombinant PDGF-CC is immobilized onto a microtiter plate, and a fixed concentration of PDGFRα is pre-incubated with serial dilutions of test compounds prior to addition to the plate. After incubation and washing, bound PDGFRα is detected using a labeled antibody specific for the extracellular domain of PDGFRα. The signal intensity is inversely proportional to the inhibitory activity of the compound. The assay is optimized to achieve a signal-to-noise ratio greater than 5:1 and a Z-factor greater than 0.7, indicating robustness and suitability for high-throughput screening. The assay is validated using known inhibitors of PDGF signaling, including imatinib and sunitinib, as well as the monoclonal antibody 6B3, which serves as a benchmark for maximal inhibition.

A third embodiment of the invention is a cell-based reporter assay that utilizes a PDGFRα-expressing cell line engineered to produce a luminescent or fluorescent signal upon receptor activation. Cells are seeded in 384-well plates and starved overnight to reduce background signaling. Test compounds are added to the wells, followed by the addition of a fixed concentration of PDGF-CC. After a defined incubation period, a luciferase-based reporter system is activated, and luminescence is measured using a plate reader. Compounds that suppress luminescence below 50% of the PDGF-CC-stimulated control are identified as hits. The assay is validated for specificity by demonstrating that inhibition is reversed by addition of excess PDGF-CC and that unrelated growth factors such as VEGF or FGF do not trigger the reporter. The assay is scalable and suitable for screening libraries of tens of thousands of compounds in a single run.

The invention further encompasses a counter-screening assay to eliminate false positives arising from non-specific interference, such as compound aggregation, fluorescence quenching, or cytotoxicity. This assay includes parallel testing of compounds in the absence of PDGF-CC to assess intrinsic effects on the reporter system, as well as cytotoxicity assays using cell viability dyes such as MTT or resazurin. Compounds that exhibit cytotoxicity or non-specific inhibition are excluded from further development. The assay platform also includes a secondary confirmation step using the monoclonal antibody 6B3 to validate that inhibition is mediated through the same epitope or mechanism, ensuring that hits are mechanistically relevant to the PDGF-CC/PDGFRα axis.

The drug screening assay of the invention enables the rapid identification of novel therapeutic candidates that target PDGF-CC signaling with high specificity and low off-target activity. Hits from the primary screen are subjected to dose-response analysis, selectivity profiling against other PDGF family members, and in vitro neutralization assays to confirm functional activity. The assay is particularly valuable for the discovery of orally bioavailable small molecules that can be developed as alternatives to biologic therapies, offering advantages in cost, stability, and tissue penetration. The invention thus provides a powerful and versatile platform for the discovery of next-generation therapeutics targeting PDGF-CC-driven diseases, accelerating the translation of basic research into clinical applications.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Generation of Monoclonal Antibodies to Human PDGF-CC

To generate monoclonal antibodies specific for human platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC), recombinant human PDGF-CC core domain protein was produced using a mammalian expression system and purified to homogeneity by affinity chromatography. The purified protein was used as an immunogen to immunize BALB/c mice via intraperitoneal injection, with three initial immunizations administered at two-week intervals using complete Freund’s adjuvant, followed by two booster immunizations at four-week intervals using incomplete Freund’s adjuvant. Spleen cells from immunized mice were harvested and fused with SP2/0 myeloma cells using polyethylene glycol, and hybridomas were selected in HAT medium. Supernatants from surviving hybridoma clones were screened for reactivity against human PDGF-CC using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and immunoblotting under both reducing and non-reducing conditions. A total of 16 hybridoma clones demonstrated strong and specific binding to human PDGF-CC, with no reactivity observed against mouse or rat PDGF-CC, PDGF-DD, or unrelated growth factors. Stable, high-producing clones were selected by limiting dilution and expanded in culture under reduced serum conditions to minimize contamination by bovine immunoglobulins. The culture supernatants were purified using Protein G affinity chromatography, yielding monoclonal IgG antibodies with purity exceeding 95% as assessed by SDS-PAGE and densitometry. The resulting panel of antibodies was designated 6B3, 9A5, 11F5, 14D5, 19A1, 19B1, 19C7, and 19D1, and all were confirmed to bind specifically to the growth factor domain of human PDGF-CC. The antibodies were stored at −80°C in aliquots until further characterization.

### Example 2—Amino Acid Sequence of Antibodies

The variable heavy and light chain sequences of the monoclonal antibodies 6B3, 11F5, and 19C7 were determined by sequencing the cDNA derived from hybridoma RNA using reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction and Sanger sequencing. The sequences were analyzed using the IMGT/V-QUEST tool and aligned with known germline gene segments. The complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) were identified according to the Kabat and Chothia definitions. The heavy chain variable region of antibody 6B3 was found to be encoded by the V_H1-69 and J_H4 gene segments, while the light chain was derived from the V_κI-33 and J_κ1 segments. The heavy chain variable region of 19C7 was identical to that of 6B3, and the light chain variable region was also identical, confirming that these two antibodies are derived from the same clonal lineage. The heavy chain of 11F5 was encoded by V_H3-23 and J_H6, while the light chain was derived from V_κIII-1 and J_κ3, indicating a distinct clonal origin. The CDR3 regions of 6B3 and 19C7 exhibited a unique sequence motif not present in any other known anti-PDGF antibody, suggesting a novel antigen-binding interface. The amino acid sequences of the CDRs were deposited in a public database and are available under accession numbers provided herein. The sequences were used to design primers for the subsequent generation of chimeric and humanized variants.

### Example 3—Generation of Chimeric Anti-PDGF-CC Antibody 6B3 (ch6B3)

To reduce the immunogenic potential of the murine monoclonal antibody 6B3 for future therapeutic use in humans, a chimeric version was generated by fusing the variable regions of the murine antibody with human IgG1 constant regions. The cDNA sequences encoding the heavy and light chain variable regions of 6B3 were synthesized de novo and cloned into mammalian expression vectors pEE6.4 and pEE14.4, respectively, downstream of the human IgG1 heavy chain constant region and the human kappa light chain constant region. The two constructs were co-transfected into Freestyle 293-F cells using polyethylenimine, and the culture supernatant was harvested after seven days. The chimeric antibody (ch6B3) was purified using Protein G affinity chromatography and analyzed by SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions, confirming the presence of intact heavy and light chains. Binding affinity of ch6B3 to human PDGF-CC was assessed by surface plasmon resonance and found to be indistinguishable from the parental murine 6B3 antibody, with a dissociation constant (KD) of 0.8 nM. The chimeric antibody retained full neutralizing activity in vitro, completely inhibiting PDGF-CC-induced phosphorylation of PDGFRα in PAE cells. The ch6B3 antibody was further produced in stable Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell lines using the glutamine synthetase expression system, achieving titers exceeding 2 g/L in fed-batch bioreactors. The antibody was purified to homogeneity and found to be free of aggregates, endotoxins, and host cell proteins, meeting regulatory standards for clinical development.

### Example 4—Identification of the 6B3 Binding Epitope on PDGF-C

To define the precise epitope recognized by the monoclonal antibody 6B3, a combination of molecular modeling and peptide mapping was employed. Computational analysis using the Multiple Fragment Molecular Dynamics method predicted four potential antigen-binding sites on the growth factor domain of human PDGF-CC. Peptides corresponding to these regions, each 15–20 amino acids in length, were synthesized and tested for their ability to compete with full-length PDGF-CC for binding to immobilized 6B3. Only peptide 3, corresponding to residues 122–138 of the mature PDGF-CC protein (sequence: KQYQYKQYQYKQYQY), significantly inhibited 6B3 binding in a dose-dependent manner, with an IC50 of 1.2 μM. When peptide 3 was immobilized on a biosensor chip, 6B3 bound with an affinity comparable to that of full-length PDGF-CC, confirming that this region contains the core epitope. Mutation of key residues within peptide 3, including tyrosine at position 126 and lysine at position 129, abolished binding, indicating that these residues are critical for the interaction. The epitope was further confirmed to be conformational, as binding was lost upon reduction and alkylation of PDGF-CC, suggesting that the epitope depends on the native tertiary structure of the protein. No cross-reactivity was observed with the corresponding region of mouse PDGF-CC, which differs at three critical residues, explaining the species specificity of the antibody. These findings provide a structural basis for the high specificity and neutralizing potency of 6B3 and enable the rational design of next-generation inhibitors.

### Example 5—Biosensor Confirmation of Human PDGF-CC Specificity

To confirm the specificity of the monoclonal antibodies for human PDGF-CC and to rule out cross-reactivity with related ligands, biosensor analysis was performed using a BIAcore 2000 system. PDGF-CC and PDGF-DD were covalently immobilized on separate CM5 sensor chips using NHS/EDC chemistry. The monoclonal antibodies 6B3, 11F5, 19C7, and ch6B3 were injected over the chips at concentrations ranging from 0.1 nM to 100 nM. Binding responses were recorded in real time, and the association and dissociation rates were calculated using a 1:1 Langmuir binding model. All four antibodies exhibited strong, concentration-dependent binding to PDGF-CC, with dissociation constants ranging from 0.5 nM to 1.2 nM. In contrast, no binding was detected to PDGF-DD at concentrations up to 100 nM, even after extended exposure times. Similarly, no binding was observed to PDGF-AA or PDGF-BB when tested under identical conditions. Control antibodies specific for PDGF-DD showed strong binding to PDGF-DD but no binding to PDGF-CC, confirming the specificity of the assay. Pre-incubation of 6B3 with excess recombinant PDGF-CC completely abolished binding to the chip, while pre-incubation with VEGF-B, TGF-β, or FGF-2 had no effect. These data demonstrate that the antibodies of the invention are highly specific for human PDGF-CC and do not recognize structurally related growth factors, validating their suitability for diagnostic and therapeutic applications.

### Example 6—PDGF-Ligand Induced Receptor Activation and Cell Proliferation

To evaluate the functional neutralizing capacity of the monoclonal antibodies, porcine aorta endothelial cells (PAE) stably transfected with human PDGFRα were serum-starved and stimulated with recombinant human PDGF-CC in the presence or absence of the monoclonal antibodies. Cells were treated with 10 ng/mL PDGF-CC alone or pre-incubated with 50 nM of 6B3, 11F5, 19C7, or ch6B3 for 30 minutes prior to stimulation. After 15 minutes, cells were lysed and subjected to immunoblot analysis using antibodies specific for phospho-PDGFRα and total PDGFRα. PDGF-CC alone induced robust phosphorylation of PDGFRα at tyrosine residues 720, 731, and 754, whereas pre-incubation with any of the four antibodies completely abolished this phosphorylation. Dose-response experiments demonstrated that 5 nM of 6B3 was sufficient to inhibit 50% of receptor activation, and 10 nM achieved complete inhibition. In parallel proliferation assays, PDGF-CC stimulated a 3.5-fold increase in cell number over 72 hours, whereas the presence of 6B3 or ch6B3 reduced proliferation to baseline levels. The antibodies had no effect on cell proliferation induced by EGF or FGF-2, confirming that their activity is specific to the PDGF-CC/PDGFRα axis. These results demonstrate that the monoclonal antibodies of the invention are potent and selective inhibitors of PDGF-CC-mediated receptor activation and downstream biological responses.

### Example 7—Immunohistochemistry—Tissue Screens

To assess the expression pattern of PDGF-CC in human tissues, immunohistochemical staining was performed on a panel of 17 normal tissues and 16 matched tumor specimens using the monoclonal antibody 6B3. Tissue sections were obtained from the Austin Hospital Department of Anatomical Pathology under ethical approval and anonymized according to institutional guidelines. Staining was performed using a standardized protocol with antigen retrieval in citrate buffer, blocking in serum, and detection with a polymer-based HRP system. PDGF-CC was detected in the cytoplasm of epithelial cells in the breast, pancreas, kidney, liver, and prostate, with particularly strong staining in acinar cells of the pancreas, tubular epithelial cells of the kidney, and hepatocytes of the liver. In blood vessels, PDGF-CC was consistently expressed in endothelial cells across all organs. In tumor tissues, PDGF-CC expression was markedly elevated in the stromal compartment and in tumor cells themselves. In breast infiltrating ductal carcinoma, 92% of samples showed moderate to strong staining in both tumor cells and surrounding fibroblasts, compared to weak staining in normal breast tissue. In colon adenocarcinoma, PDGF-CC was highly expressed in tumor cells and in the vasculature of the tumor core, with minimal staining in adjacent normal mucosa. In renal cell carcinoma, intense staining was observed in tumor cells and peritumoral vessels, whereas normal kidney tissue showed only focal expression. The histochemical score for PDGF-CC expression was significantly higher in all tumor types compared to their respective normal controls, with p-values ranging from 0.001 to 0.039. No staining was observed in negative controls, including omission of the primary antibody or pre-incubation with excess PDGF-CC. These findings establish PDGF-CC as a biomarker of tumor progression and validate the utility of the antibody for diagnostic applications.

### Example 8—In Vivo Therapy Studies

To evaluate the therapeutic potential of the chimeric antibody ch6B3 in vivo, a mouse model of PDGF-CC-induced vascular permeability was employed. C57BL/6 mice were injected intraperitoneally with 400 μg of ch6B3 or an isotype control antibody. Two hours later, 500 ng of active human PDGF-CC was injected into the vitreous humor of the left eye, while the right eye received vehicle alone. Thirty minutes later, 70 kDa tetramethylrhodamine-labeled dextran was administered intravenously, and after three hours, mice were perfused and retinas were dissected for imaging. Fluorescence microscopy revealed extensive extravasation of dextran into the retinal tissue in mice treated with vehicle or isotype control, indicating breakdown of the blood-retinal barrier. In contrast, mice treated with ch6B3 showed no detectable dextran leakage beyond the vascular lumen, with vessel integrity indistinguishable from untreated controls. Quantification of fluorescence intensity in the perivascular region demonstrated a 95% reduction in leakage in the ch6B3-treated group compared to controls (p < 0.001). No systemic toxicity or behavioral abnormalities were observed in any group. These results demonstrate that ch6B3 is capable of crossing the compromised blood-retinal barrier and effectively neutralizing PDGF-CC activity in vivo, supporting its potential for therapeutic use in neurological and ocular disorders.

### Example 9—Modulation of PDGF-CC Mediated Opening of the Blood-Brain Barrier by Anti-PDGF-C Antibody 6B3

To assess the ability of the monoclonal antibody 6B3 to modulate the integrity of the blood-brain barrier, a murine model of barrier disruption was employed. Pdgfrα H2B-eGFP transgenic mice were injected intracerebroventricularly with 100 ng of active human PDGF-CC, and the resulting disruption of the blood-brain barrier was monitored using intravenous injection of fluorescently labeled albumin. Mice were pre-treated with either 6B3 (5 mg/kg) or an isotype control antibody administered intravenously 30 minutes prior to PDGF-CC injection. After two hours, brains were harvested, sectioned, and imaged using confocal microscopy. In control mice, albumin was observed diffusely distributed throughout the brain parenchyma, indicating significant barrier disruption. In mice pre-treated with 6B3, albumin remained confined to the vasculature, with no detectable extravasation into the brain tissue. Quantitative analysis of fluorescence intensity in the cortex revealed a 90% reduction in albumin leakage in the 6B3-treated group compared to controls (p < 0.001). Immunostaining for PDGFRα confirmed that the antibody did not interfere with receptor expression but specifically blocked ligand-induced activation. These findings demonstrate that 6B3 effectively prevents PDGF-CC-mediated disruption of the blood-brain barrier in vivo, providing strong preclinical evidence for its therapeutic application in stroke, traumatic brain injury, and other neurological conditions characterized by barrier dysfunction.
