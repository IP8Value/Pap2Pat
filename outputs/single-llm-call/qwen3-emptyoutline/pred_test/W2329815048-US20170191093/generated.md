# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The production of lipids through microbial fermentation has emerged as a strategically significant biotechnological platform for the sustainable synthesis of biofuels, specialty chemicals, and high-value oleochemicals. Traditional sources of lipids, including plant oils and animal fats, face inherent limitations such as seasonal variability, land-use competition, low volumetric productivity, and complex extraction processes. In contrast, oleaginous microorganisms offer a compelling alternative by enabling the direct conversion of renewable, non-food carbon sources—including glucose, sucrose, xylose, and lignocellulosic hydrolysates—into intracellular lipid stores with high efficiency and scalability. Among these microorganisms, the yeast Yarrowia lipolytica has garnered substantial attention due to its natural capacity to accumulate lipids exceeding 20% of its dry cell weight, its genetic tractability, its robustness under industrial fermentation conditions, and its ability to secrete extracellular enzymes and metabolites under controlled nutrient regimes. Wild-type strains of Y. lipolytica have been shown to accumulate lipids up to 36% of dry cell weight when grown on glucose and as high as 50–60% when supplied with exogenous fatty acids for biomodification, positioning it as a premier chassis for metabolic engineering efforts aimed at lipid overproduction.

Lipids in oleaginous yeasts are primarily composed of fatty acids esterified into nonpolar storage molecules, most notably triacylglycerols (TAGs), which serve as energy reserves and are sequestered within specialized subcellular organelles known as lipid droplets. The biosynthesis of TAGs involves a tightly regulated enzymatic cascade beginning with the formation of glycerol-3-phosphate from dihydroxyacetone phosphate or glycerol, followed by sequential acylation reactions that generate phosphatidic acid and subsequently diacylglycerol. The final and rate-limiting step in TAG biosynthesis is the esterification of a third fatty acyl chain onto the diacylglycerol backbone, a reaction catalyzed by diacylglycerol acyltransferases (DGATs). Two distinct classes of DGAT enzymes—DGAT1 and DGAT2—have been identified across eukaryotes, each encoded by separate gene families and exhibiting differences in subcellular localization, substrate specificity, and regulatory mechanisms. In Y. lipolytica, the enzymes encoded by the DGA1 and DGA2 genes correspond to DGAT2 and DGAT1, respectively, and both are implicated in the terminal step of TAG assembly. DGA1 is localized to the surface of lipid droplets and is thought to facilitate the direct incorporation of acyl-CoA into existing lipid bodies, thereby promoting their expansion, while DGA2 resides in the endoplasmic reticulum and is believed to initiate the formation of nascent lipid droplets by synthesizing TAG de novo. The functional interplay between these two enzymes is critical for maximal lipid accumulation, as their activities are not redundant but rather complementary, with DGA2 contributing to the initial biogenesis of lipid droplets and DGA1 enabling their subsequent growth and stabilization.

Previous genetic engineering studies have demonstrated that overexpression of the native DGA1 gene in Y. lipolytica leads to a significant increase in lipid content, often doubling or tripling the wild-type levels under nitrogen-limited conditions. Similarly, deletion of genes encoding TAG-degrading lipases, such as TGL3 and TGL4, has been shown to enhance lipid retention by suppressing the mobilization of stored TAGs during late-stage fermentation when nutrient depletion triggers catabolic responses. However, despite these advances, the lipid yields and volumetric productivities achieved in engineered strains have historically remained below the theoretical maximum predicted by biochemical stoichiometry, largely due to metabolic imbalances, insufficient flux through the acyl-CoA pool, and the absence of synergistic combinations of genetic modifications that simultaneously enhance biosynthesis and suppress degradation. Moreover, while the overexpression of DGA1 and DGA2 individually has been explored, the systematic identification of heterologous orthologs with superior catalytic efficiency, expression stability, or compatibility with the Y. lipolytica cellular environment has remained largely unexplored. The hypothesis that lipid accumulation in oleaginous organisms is governed not merely by the presence of DGAT enzymes but by their specific activity, expression level, and spatial coordination within the lipid droplet dynamics has not been rigorously tested through comparative screening of orthologs from diverse lipid-accumulating species.

This invention addresses these limitations by presenting a comprehensive, multi-tiered engineering strategy that integrates the overexpression of heterologous diacylglycerol acyltransferase genes with targeted deletion of lipolytic enzymes to achieve unprecedented levels of lipid accumulation in Y. lipolytica. The approach begins with the identification of endogenous and heterologous DGA1 and DGA2 genes from a panel of oleaginous organisms known for their high native lipid content, including Rhodosporidium toruloides, Lipomyces starkeyi, Claviceps purpurea, Aurantiochytrium limacinum, and Aspergillus terreus. These genes are systematically expressed in Y. lipolytica under strong, constitutive promoters to evaluate their relative impact on lipid biosynthesis. The results reveal that heterologous DGA1 genes from R. toruloides and L. starkeyi, particularly when expressed as intron-free, codon-optimized cDNA sequences, confer significantly higher lipid accumulation than the native Y. lipolytica DGA1. Furthermore, the overexpression of a heterologous DGA2 gene from Claviceps purpurea in a DGA1-overexpressing background leads to a synergistic increase in lipid content that exceeds the sum of the individual effects, indicating a previously unrecognized functional complementarity between these enzymes from divergent species. Concurrently, the deletion of the TGL3 gene—a gene encoding a lipid droplet-associated triacylglycerol lipase previously thought to have minimal catalytic activity—was found to dramatically enhance lipid retention during the late stages of fermentation, when endogenous lipases are activated in response to nutrient exhaustion. The integration of these three key modifications—overexpression of heterologous DGA1 and DGA2 and deletion of TGL3—results in a genetically stabilized biocatalyst capable of achieving lipid contents exceeding 77% of dry cell weight in batch fermentation and producing lipid titers of 85 g/L at a volumetric productivity of 0.73 g/L/h in fed-batch culture. These values represent some of the highest lipid productivities ever reported for any microbial system and approach the theoretical upper limits of lipid yield from glucose based on known biochemical pathways. The invention thus provides a novel, industrially viable microbial strain and a set of genetic tools that enable the efficient, scalable, and economically competitive production of microbial lipids for biofuel, oleochemical, and nutraceutical applications.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to genetically engineered microorganisms, particularly the yeast Yarrowia lipolytica, that exhibit substantially enhanced triacylglycerol accumulation through the coordinated overexpression of heterologous diacylglycerol acyltransferase genes and the targeted deletion of a triacylglycerol lipase gene. The invention provides a novel strain of Yarrowia lipolytica that contains a genomic integration of a heterologous DGA1 gene derived from Rhodosporidium toruloides and a heterologous DGA2 gene derived from Claviceps purpurea, each operably linked to a strong constitutive promoter, and further contains a deletion of the endogenous TGL3 gene, which encodes a triacylglycerol lipase involved in the mobilization of stored lipids during nutrient limitation. This combination of genetic modifications results in a strain that accumulates triacylglycerols at levels exceeding 75% of its dry cell weight under nitrogen-limited fermentation conditions and achieves lipid titers of at least 80 grams per liter in fed-batch cultivation with glucose as the primary carbon source. The invention further encompasses nucleic acid constructs encoding the heterologous DGA1 and DGA2 genes, expression vectors containing these constructs, and methods for their introduction into Yarrowia lipolytica or other oleaginous hosts. The DGA1 gene may be derived from Rhodosporidium toruloides, Lipomyces starkeyi, or other oleaginous fungi, and may be expressed as a codon-optimized cDNA sequence lacking introns to maximize expression efficiency in Yarrowia lipolytica. The DGA2 gene may be derived from Claviceps purpurea, Chaetomium globosum, or Aspergillus terreus, and similarly may be expressed as a codon-optimized cDNA sequence. The TGL3 gene deletion is achieved through homologous recombination, resulting in the complete removal of the coding sequence and the replacement with a selectable marker gene, thereby abolishing the lipase activity responsible for TAG degradation during late-phase fermentation. The invention also includes methods for producing triacylglycerols by cultivating the genetically modified strain under nitrogen-limited conditions in a bioreactor, wherein the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio is maintained above 100:1 to promote lipid accumulation. The resulting triacylglycerols may be recovered from the cells by solvent extraction, mechanical disruption, or thermal lysis and may be further processed into biodiesel, oleochemicals, or food-grade oils. The invention demonstrates that the synergistic interaction between heterologous DGA1 and DGA2 enzymes, in the absence of TGL3-mediated lipolysis, creates a metabolic state in which lipid biosynthesis is maximally directed toward storage and minimally subject to catabolic loss, thereby enabling industrial-scale lipid production with yields and productivities that surpass prior art strains. The invention further provides a platform for the production of structured lipids by feeding exogenous fatty acids to the engineered strain, which are incorporated into TAGs via the enhanced acyltransferase activity, allowing for the biosynthesis of high-value fats with tailored fatty acid profiles. The invention represents a significant advancement in microbial lipid biotechnology by providing a genetically defined, stable, and scalable solution to the longstanding challenge of achieving high lipid titers, yields, and productivities in a single microbial host.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious approach to the metabolic engineering of oleaginous microorganisms for the enhanced production of triacylglycerols through the synergistic manipulation of lipid biosynthesis and degradation pathways. The invention is grounded in the discovery that the overexpression of heterologous diacylglycerol acyltransferase genes from other lipid-accumulating organisms, when combined with the deletion of a key triacylglycerol lipase gene, results in a dramatic and unexpected increase in lipid accumulation that far exceeds the additive effects of the individual modifications. This discovery challenges the prevailing assumption that the overexpression of endogenous enzymes is sufficient to maximize lipid production and instead demonstrates that the functional optimization of lipid biosynthetic machinery requires the introduction of evolutionarily divergent enzymes with superior catalytic properties, expression stability, or compatibility with the host cellular environment. The invention further reveals that the suppression of lipid degradation during the late phase of fermentation is not merely beneficial but essential for achieving high lipid titers, as the mobilization of stored triacylglycerols by endogenous lipases becomes a dominant sink for carbon flux when nutrients are depleted. By integrating these three key genetic modifications—heterologous DGA1 overexpression, heterologous DGA2 overexpression, and TGL3 deletion—the invention creates a metabolic state in which the flux through the triacylglycerol biosynthesis pathway is maximized, the storage capacity of lipid droplets is expanded, and the degradation of stored lipids is effectively blocked. The resulting strain exhibits a level of lipid accumulation that is unprecedented in the field of microbial lipid production and is suitable for commercial-scale fermentation processes. The invention encompasses not only the final strain but also the genetic constructs, expression systems, and cultivation methods that enable its production and application. The invention is applicable to a broad range of carbon sources, including glucose, sucrose, xylose, glycerol, and lignocellulosic hydrolysates, and may be adapted for use in other oleaginous hosts such as Arxula adeninivorans, Rhodosporidium toruloides, and Lipomyces starkeyi. The invention thus represents a transformative advance in the field of industrial biotechnology, providing a genetically defined, robust, and scalable platform for the sustainable production of microbial lipids.
