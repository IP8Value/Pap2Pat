# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of regenerative medicine and cellular therapy, particularly as applied to the treatment of insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus. More specifically, the invention relates to a method for the sustained reversal of hyperglycemia in diabetic subjects through the administration of human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) that have been genetically modified to express vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF). The invention further encompasses compositions comprising such VEGF-expressing hBMSCs, methods for their preparation, and their use in promoting endogenous β-cell regeneration via activation of the insulin/insulin-like growth factor (IGF) receptor signaling pathway, rather than relying solely on direct differentiation of transplanted cells into functional insulin-producing cells. The disclosed therapeutic approach leverages the dual capacity of VEGF-expressing hBMSCs to both engraft within the pancreatic tissue of diabetic subjects and to create a pro-regenerative microenvironment that supports the survival, proliferation, and functional recovery of the subject’s own residual or damaged pancreatic β-cells. This invention thus represents a significant advancement over prior cell-based therapies for diabetes, which have often yielded transient or inconsistent clinical outcomes due to poor cell engraftment, limited differentiation efficiency, or failure to address the underlying pathophysiological barriers to β-cell regeneration in the diabetic pancreas.

## BACKGROUND

Diabetes mellitus, particularly type 1 diabetes and advanced type 2 diabetes, is characterized by an absolute or relative deficiency of insulin, a hormone essential for the regulation of glucose homeostasis. Current standard-of-care for insulin-dependent diabetes involves exogenous insulin administration, which, despite its life-saving benefits, is inherently non-physiological and often fails to achieve strict euglycemia. This suboptimal glycemic control is a major contributor to the development of debilitating and life-threatening secondary complications, including nephropathy, retinopathy, neuropathy, and cardiovascular disease. As a result, there has been a long-standing pursuit of more definitive therapeutic strategies that can restore endogenous insulin production.

Pancreas transplantation and islet of Langerhans transplantation have demonstrated the physiological superiority of replacing insulin-producing cells over exogenous insulin delivery. However, these approaches are severely limited by the scarcity of donor organs, the necessity for lifelong immunosuppression with its associated risks, and, in the case of islet transplantation, issues related to graft survival and function. Consequently, significant research efforts have focused on identifying alternative, renewable sources of insulin-producing cells.

Stem cell therapy has emerged as a promising avenue. Among various stem cell types, human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) have garnered considerable attention due to their relative ease of isolation from a patient’s own body (autologous source), thereby circumventing the risk of allograft rejection, and their proven safety profile in numerous clinical trials. Early studies suggested that BMSCs might possess a degree of plasticity, potentially differentiating into endocrine pancreatic lineages, including insulin-producing β-cells, both in vitro and in vivo. However, subsequent investigations revealed that the direct differentiation of unmodified hBMSCs into functional, glucose-responsive β-cells is highly inefficient and insufficient to reverse diabetes in animal models.

To enhance the therapeutic potential of hBMSCs, researchers have explored genetic modification strategies. A key target has been the pancreatic and duodenal homeobox 1 (PDX1) gene, a master regulator of pancreatic development and β-cell identity. Forced expression of PDX1 in hBMSCs has been shown to activate a suite of genes associated with β-cell development and function, leading to the production of insulin in vitro. While this approach demonstrates the principle that hBMSCs can be directed toward a β-cell fate, its efficacy in a living organism for achieving sustained normoglycemia remains questionable.

Concurrently, another critical aspect of pancreatic islet biology has been recognized: the intimate relationship between islets and their vascular niche. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), particularly the VEGF-A isoform, is a potent angiogenic factor that is constitutively expressed by β-cells throughout life. It is essential for the development and maintenance of the dense, fenestrated capillary network that surrounds and permeates pancreatic islets. This vascular network is not merely a conduit for oxygen and nutrients but also serves as a critical signaling platform that directly influences β-cell gene expression, proliferation, and survival. In the context of diabetes, whether induced by autoimmune destruction or chemical agents like streptozotocin (STZ), there is a well-documented loss of intra-islet vascularization. This vascular regression is thought to create a hostile microenvironment that actively impedes any potential for endogenous β-cell regeneration or repair.

Previous attempts to use unmodified hBMSCs for diabetes therapy have yielded inconsistent results, with some studies reporting partial amelioration of hyperglycemia while others, including the foundational work described herein, found no significant benefit. These discrepancies likely stem from variations in experimental models, cell sources, and protocols. The central unresolved question has been the primary mechanism by which any observed therapeutic effect occurs: is it due to the direct contribution of transplanted stem cells that have differentiated into new β-cells, or is it due to a paracrine or trophic effect that stimulates the recovery of the host’s own endogenous β-cells?

The present invention was developed to directly address this fundamental question and to establish a superior, reliable, and sustained therapeutic strategy for insulin-dependent diabetes. By comparing the effects of hBMSCs engineered to express either PDX1 (a driver of β-cell differentiation) or VEGF (a driver of islet vascularization and a known modulator of β-cell health), the inventors have made a critical discovery that shifts the paradigm of stem cell therapy for diabetes away from a focus on direct cell replacement and toward a focus on microenvironmental engineering and endogenous regeneration.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and effective method for the treatment of insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus in a subject in need thereof. The method comprises administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) that have been genetically modified to express a vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF). In a preferred embodiment, the VEGF is VEGF-A, and more specifically, the VEGF165 isoform.

A key aspect of the invention is the discovery that the primary therapeutic mechanism of these VEGF-expressing hBMSCs is not the direct differentiation of the administered cells into a large number of new, functional insulin-producing β-cells. Instead, the invention is based on the finding that VEGF-expressing hBMSCs, upon systemic administration, efficiently engraft within the pancreas of a diabetic subject and create a pro-regenerative microenvironment. This is achieved through two principal, synergistic actions: (1) the differentiation of a portion of the administered hBMSCs into vascular structures, thereby restoring the critical intra-islet vascular niche that is lost in diabetes; and (2) the secretion of VEGF and other factors that systemically activate the host’s endogenous insulin/IGF receptor signaling pathway within the residual pancreatic β-cells.

This activation of the insulin/IGF receptor signaling pathway, which proceeds through the PI3-Kinase (PI3K)/AKT axis, leads to a cascade of beneficial effects in the endogenous β-cells. These effects include the upregulation and nuclear localization of the transcription factor PDX1, which is essential for β-cell identity and function; the downregulation of the cell cycle inhibitor p27Kip1, which promotes β-cell proliferation; and the suppression of apoptotic pathways, as evidenced by a reduction in cleaved caspase-3, thereby enhancing β-cell survival. The net result is a robust and sustained regeneration and functional recovery of the subject’s own endogenous β-cell mass, which is sufficient to restore normoglycemia.

In contrast, the invention demonstrates that hBMSCs genetically modified to express PDX1, while capable of differentiating into insulin-producing cells and producing detectable levels of human insulin in a diabetic host, provide only a transient amelioration of hyperglycemia. This transient effect is attributed to the poor engraftment and survival of these PDX1-expressing cells, which lack the vascular support necessary for long-term function in the damaged diabetic pancreas. Furthermore, unmodified hBMSCs, without any genetic enhancement, are shown to be ineffective in reversing diabetes, highlighting the necessity of the VEGF-mediated mechanism.

Therefore, the invention provides a composition for use in a method of treating diabetes, the composition comprising VEGF-expressing hBMSCs. The invention also provides a method for preparing such a composition, which involves isolating hBMSCs from a human donor, and transducing the cells with a nucleic acid vector encoding a VEGF polypeptide, wherein the vector is configured to drive expression of the VEGF polypeptide in the hBMSCs. The invention further provides a method for promoting endogenous β-cell regeneration in a diabetic subject, which involves administering the VEGF-expressing hBMSCs to the subject, thereby activating the insulin/IGF receptor/PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in the subject’s endogenous β-cells.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is predicated on a series of rigorous experimental findings that elucidate a previously unknown and highly effective mechanism for treating insulin-dependent diabetes using genetically modified stem cells. The core of the invention is the use of human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) that have been engineered to express vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF). The inventors have discovered that this specific modification confers upon the hBMSCs a unique therapeutic capacity that is distinct from and superior to other genetic modifications, such as the expression of PDX1, or the use of unmodified hBMSCs.

The hBMSCs used in the invention are multipotent stromal cells that can be isolated from the bone marrow of a human donor. They are characterized by their ability to adhere to plastic culture surfaces, their expression of a specific set of cell surface markers (e.g., CD44, CD105), and their lack of expression of hematopoietic markers (e.g., CD31, CD34). Furthermore, they must be capable of differentiating into osteogenic and adipogenic lineages in vitro, which confirms their mesenchymal nature. For the purposes of the invention, the hBMSCs are preferably used at a low passage number (e.g., between passage 3 and passage 7) to ensure their proliferative and differentiation potential has not been compromised by extended in vitro culture.

The genetic modification of these hBMSCs is a critical step. The cells are transduced with a vector, such as an adenoviral vector, that contains a nucleic acid sequence encoding a VEGF polypeptide. The vector is constructed such that the VEGF coding sequence is operably linked to a promoter, for example, a cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter, which drives high-level expression of the VEGF protein in the transduced hBMSCs. The resulting cells, referred to as hBMSCs-VEGF, are then expanded and prepared for administration. The VEGF polypeptide is preferably a human VEGF-A, and most preferably the VEGF165 isoform, which is the predominant and most biologically active form in humans.

The method of administration is also a key feature of the invention. The hBMSCs-VEGF are administered systemically, for example, via intracardiac injection, which allows the cells to enter the arterial circulation and be distributed throughout the body, including to the pancreas. This route of administration has been shown to be effective for delivering the cells to the target organ.

The therapeutic efficacy of the invention was demonstrated in a well-established animal model of insulin-dependent diabetes. Diabetes was induced in immunodeficient NOD/SCID mice using multiple low-dose injections of streptozotocin (STZ), a compound that selectively ablates pancreatic β-cells. This model creates a state of severe, persistent hyperglycemia and high mortality, closely mimicking the challenges of human type 1 diabetes. The diabetic mice were then treated with a single injection of either unmodified hBMSCs, hBMSCs-PDX1, or hBMSCs-VEGF.

The results were unequivocal. Mice treated with hBMSCs-VEGF showed a high rate of sustained reversion to normoglycemia, which was maintained for the entire six-week duration of the study. These mice also exhibited a dramatically improved survival rate and weight gain, comparable to healthy, non-diabetic controls. In stark contrast, mice treated with hBMSCs-PDX1 showed only a temporary improvement in blood glucose, which relapsed after 2-3 weeks. Mice treated with unmodified hBMSCs showed no therapeutic benefit whatsoever, with their condition and survival rate being identical to untreated diabetic controls.

To understand the underlying mechanism, the inventors conducted a comprehensive analysis of the pancreatic tissues. Histological examination revealed that the pancreatic islet architecture and insulin content in the hBMSCs-VEGF-treated mice were remarkably preserved and similar to those of healthy controls. In the other groups, the islets were severely atrophied and depleted of insulin. Critically, the analysis of serum insulin levels provided the key insight: while both the hBMSCs-VEGF and hBMSCs-PDX1 groups produced detectable levels of human insulin (confirming that the transplanted cells could differentiate into insulin-producing cells), the mice that achieved sustained normoglycemia (the hBMSCs-VEGF group) had significantly higher levels of *mouse* insulin. This finding strongly indicates that the primary driver of the sustained cure was the recovery of the mouse’s own endogenous β-cells, not the transplanted human cells.

Further investigation into the molecular mechanisms revealed that the hBMSCs-VEGF treatment led to a profound activation of the insulin/IGF receptor signaling pathway in the endogenous β-cells. This was evidenced by a PCR array showing the upregulation of key genes in this pathway, and by confocal microscopy showing the restoration of AKT protein expression and its translocation to the cytoplasm, a hallmark of pathway activation. Downstream of this activated pathway, the transcription factor PDX1 was restored to the nucleus of β-cells, and the cell cycle inhibitor p27Kip1 was downregulated, creating a cellular state conducive to proliferation and survival. Concurrently, markers of apoptosis, such as cleaved caspase-3, were significantly reduced.

The inventors also confirmed that the hBMSCs-VEGF successfully engrafted in the pancreas and contributed to the formation of new vascular structures, as demonstrated by co-staining for human-specific markers and vascular smooth muscle actin. This restoration of the vascular niche is believed to be a crucial component of the pro-regenerative microenvironment that enables the endogenous β-cell recovery.

In summary, the present invention provides a powerful and reliable method for treating diabetes by leveraging the ability of VEGF-expressing hBMSCs to orchestrate the regeneration of a patient’s own insulin-producing cells, offering a path toward a definitive cure for this chronic disease.

## EXAMPLES

### Cell Isolation and Culture

The human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) utilized in the practice of the present invention were obtained from a commercial source, AllCells, LLC (Emeryville, California, USA; catalog number ABM005). The initial isolation and expansion protocol was designed to ensure the procurement of a pure, viable, and multipotent population of mesenchymal stem cells suitable for subsequent genetic modification and therapeutic application. The process began with the plating of all nucleated cells from the bone marrow aspirate into a basal culture medium. This medium was composed of Alpha Minimum Essential Medium (Alfa-MEM), supplemented with 17% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 2 mM L-glutamine, 50 U/L penicillin, and 25 µg/L streptomycin, with all components sourced from Invitrogen. The cells were incubated under standard conditions of 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. After a 24-hour period, the non-adherent cells, which primarily consist of hematopoietic lineage cells, were removed from the culture vessel by aspiration of the supernatant. The remaining adherent cell population, which represents the mesenchymal stem cell fraction, was then washed thoroughly with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to remove any residual non-adherent cells and debris. Fresh complete medium was then added to the culture vessel, and the cells were allowed to proliferate for a period of 5 to 7 days. Once the adherent cell monolayer reached approximately 80% confluence, the cells were harvested using a standard trypsin/EDTA solution. The detached cells were then counted, and a defined number of cells were re-plated into new culture dishes to initiate the first passage (P1). This expansion process was repeated for a total of two additional passages to generate sufficient cell numbers for the experimental procedures. To ensure long-term viability and to create a master cell bank, aliquots of the cells were cryopreserved in a freezing medium consisting of 90% FBS and 10% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and stored at -80°C. Throughout the expansion process, the cells maintained a consistent doubling time of approximately 48 hours up to passage number 6 or 7. All experimental work described in the present invention was performed using a single batch of hBMSCs derived from a single donor to eliminate inter-donor variability. The cells were used for all experiments within passage number 7 to ensure their phenotypic and functional stability. To confirm the identity and purity of the hBMSC population, flow cytometry analysis was performed on cells from passages 3 to 6. The analysis confirmed the positive expression of canonical mesenchymal stem cell markers, including CD44 and CD105, and the negative expression of hematopoietic and endothelial markers, such as CD31 and CD34. Furthermore, to validate the multipotent differentiation capacity of the hBMSCs, in vitro differentiation assays were conducted. The cells were successfully induced to differentiate into both adipogenic and osteogenic lineages under appropriate culture conditions, as confirmed by standard staining methods (e.g., Oil Red O for adipocytes and Alizarin Red for osteoblasts), thereby fulfilling the minimal criteria for defining human mesenchymal stem cells.

### Cell Culture and Differentiation

Following the initial isolation and expansion of the hBMSCs, the cells were subjected to genetic modification to create the therapeutic cell products of the present invention. The genetic modification was achieved through adenoviral transduction. The cDNAs encoding for human PDX1 and mouse VEGF165 were each subcloned into the Adeno-X viral DNA vector (BD Biosciences Clontech) following the manufacturer's protocol. The cytomegalovirus (CMV) immediate-early promoter was used to drive the expression of the transgenes in the target cells. The recombinant adenoviral vectors, designated Ad-PDX1 and Ad-VEGF, were generated through homologous recombination in HEK293 cells, a human embryonic kidney cell line that is permissive for adenovirus replication. The viral particles were then amplified and purified according to the standard procedures outlined in the ViraPower Adenoviral Expression System manual from Invitrogen. The titer of the final viral stocks was determined to ensure consistent and efficient transduction. For the preparation of the therapeutic cell products, hBMSCs at an appropriate passage number were seeded into culture dishes and allowed to reach 60-70% confluence. The cells were then transduced with either the Ad-PDX1 or Ad-VEGF virus at a predetermined multiplicity of infection (MOI) to achieve high transduction efficiency while maintaining cell viability. The transduction was carried out in a reduced-serum medium for a period of 24 hours, after which the viral supernatant was removed and replaced with fresh complete medium. The cells were then cultured for an additional 24 hours to allow for robust transgene expression before they were harvested for transplantation. The successful expression of the PDX1 and VEGF transgenes in the transduced hBMSCs (designated hBMSCs-PDX1 and hBMSCs-VEGF, respectively) was confirmed at both the mRNA and protein levels. Reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was used to detect the presence of the transgene-specific mRNA transcripts, and Western blotting was performed to verify the production of the corresponding PDX1 and VEGF proteins. The cells were then prepared for in vivo administration by detaching them from the culture surface with trypsin/EDTA, washing them extensively in sterile PBS, and resuspending them at a concentration of 1x10^6 cells per 150 µl of PBS. The cell suspension was kept on ice and used for transplantation within one hour to ensure maximum cell viability. The entire process of cell culture, genetic modification, and preparation for transplantation was conducted under strict aseptic conditions in a certified biosafety cabinet to prevent contamination.

### Quantitative Real-Time PCR

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was employed in the present invention as a highly sensitive and specific analytical tool to assess several key parameters. First, it was used to confirm the successful genetic modification of the hBMSCs by detecting the mRNA transcripts of the PDX1 and VEGF transgenes. Second, and more importantly, qRT-PCR was used to analyze the gene expression profiles in the pancreatic tissues of the experimental animals to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the therapeutic effect. For the analysis of pancreatic tissue, the organs were harvested at the endpoint of the study (6 weeks post-transplantation) and immediately preserved in RNA Later (Invitrogen) to stabilize the RNA and prevent degradation. The samples were stored at -20°C until processing. Total RNA was extracted from the pancreatic tissues using the RNeasy Minikit (Qiagen) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The quality and quantity of the extracted RNA were assessed using a spectrophotometer. The RNA was then reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using the RT2 First Strand Kit (SABiosciences, Frederick, USA). This cDNA served as the template for the qRT-PCR reactions. For the broad assessment of changes in the insulin signaling pathway, a commercially available RT2 Profiler PCR Array for the Mouse Insulin Signaling Pathway (SABiosciences, Cat#PAMM-030-F) was used. The qRT-PCR reactions were performed on a Roche LightCycler 480 instrument using the RT2 SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (SABiosciences). The data from the PCR arrays were analyzed using the online data analysis tool provided by the manufacturer, which calculates the fold-changes in gene expression and determines statistical significance. Only results with a p-value of less than 0.05 were considered significant. In addition to the PCR array, a specific qRT-PCR assay was developed to detect and quantify human genomic DNA in the mouse tissues, which served as a measure of cell engraftment. Genomic DNA was extracted from frozen mouse tissues (pancreas, kidney, liver, etc.) using the DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen). A human-specific primer pair, designed to amplify a unique and conserved region of the human β-actin gene, was used. To control for the amount of input DNA and to account for potential variations in sample collection, the endogenous mouse GAPDH gene was amplified in parallel as an internal control. The qRT-PCR was performed on a Bio-Rad iCycler using the QuantiFast SYBR Green PCR kit (Qiagen). Absolute quantification was achieved by generating standard curves for both the human β-actin and mouse GAPDH targets. The human standard curve was created by serially diluting known amounts of human genomic DNA into a fixed amount of mouse genomic DNA. The amount of human DNA detected in the mouse tissue samples was then expressed as a percentage of the total human DNA that was originally infused into the animal. All qRT-PCR assays were performed in triplicate and repeated at least three times to ensure the reliability and reproducibility of the results.

### Immunocytochemistry

Immunocytochemistry and immunohistochemistry were critical techniques used in the present invention to visualize the localization, engraftment, and differentiation of the transplanted cells, as well as to assess the morphological and molecular state of the endogenous pancreatic tissue. At the conclusion of the in vivo study (6 weeks post-transplantation), the mouse pancreatic tissues were harvested and immediately fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at 4°C for 24 hours to preserve cellular architecture and antigenicity. The fixed tissues were then processed for paraffin embedding through a series of dehydration steps in graded ethanol, clearing in xylene, and infiltration with molten paraffin. The paraffin-embedded tissues were sectioned at a thickness of 5 micrometers using a microtome. For routine histological assessment, the sections were stained with Harris hematoxylin and eosin Y (H&E) to evaluate the overall morphology of the pancreatic islets and the surrounding exocrine tissue. For immunohistochemical and immunofluorescent analyses, the paraffin sections were first deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through a descending series of ethanol baths to water. Antigen retrieval was performed to unmask epitopes that may have been obscured by the fixation process; this was accomplished by heating the slides in a DAKO antigen retrieval buffer at 90°C for a specified time. The sections were then blocked with a protein-blocking solution to reduce non-specific binding of the primary antibodies. A panel of primary antibodies was used to probe for specific cellular markers. To identify the endocrine cell types, antibodies against insulin (to label β-cells) and glucagon (to label α-cells) were used. To assess the state of the transplanted human cells, a rabbit polyclonal antibody against human β2-microglobulin was employed, which is a highly specific marker for human cells in a murine host. To investigate the molecular pathways involved in β-cell survival and proliferation, antibodies against key signaling proteins were used, including anti-PDX1, anti-p27Kip1, anti-AKT, and anti-cleaved caspase-3. For the detection of VEGF expression, a specific anti-VEGF antibody was used. The primary antibodies were diluted in a blocking buffer and incubated with the tissue sections overnight at 4°C. Following several washes in PBS, the bound primary antibodies were detected using species-specific secondary antibodies conjugated to either a chromogenic enzyme (for light microscopy) or a fluorescent dye (for fluorescence microscopy). For chromogenic detection, a biotinylated secondary antibody followed by a streptavidin-alkaline phosphatase complex was used, and the signal was visualized with a new fuchsin substrate. For fluorescent detection, secondary antibodies conjugated to Alexa Fluor 488 or Alexa Fluor 568 (Invitrogen) were used. To perform double or triple labeling and to definitively determine if a human cell (β2-microglobulin-positive) had differentiated into a specific lineage (e.g., insulin-positive or α-smooth muscle actin-positive), a sequential staining protocol was employed. This involved first staining for the lineage marker and its corresponding fluorescent secondary antibody, and then, after thorough washing, staining for the human-specific marker with a secondary antibody conjugated to a different fluorophore. Finally, the cell nuclei were counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) to provide a clear cellular context. The stained slides were then imaged using either a Zeiss upright fluorescent microscope equipped with a digital camera for wide-field fluorescence or a Leica Microsystems SP5 laser scanning confocal microscope for high-resolution, optical sectioning and 3D reconstruction. The images were analyzed to count the number of insulin-positive cells, to assess the co-localization of markers, and to evaluate the subcellular localization of signaling proteins like AKT and PDX1.

### Example 5

The in vivo efficacy of the VEGF-expressing hBMSCs was rigorously evaluated in a preclinical mouse model of insulin-dependent diabetes. The model was established by administering three consecutive intraperitoneal injections of streptozotocin (STZ) at a dose of 50 mg/kg to 6-8 week old NOD/SCID mice. STZ is a glucosamine-nitrosourea compound that is selectively taken up by pancreatic β-cells via the GLUT2 glucose transporter, where it causes DNA alkylation and subsequent cell death, thereby inducing a state of insulin deficiency and hyperglycemia. One week after the final STZ injection, mice with non-fasting blood glucose levels exceeding 250 mg/dl were considered diabetic and were enrolled in the treatment study. The diabetic mice were randomly assigned to one of four treatment groups: a sham control group that received an injection of vehicle alone; a group that received an injection of unmodified hBMSCs; a group that received an injection of hBMSCs-PDX1; and a group that received an injection of hBMSCs-VEGF. Each mouse received a single intracardiac injection of approximately 1x10^6 cells suspended in 150 µl of PBS. The intracardiac route was chosen to deliver the cells directly into the arterial circulation, facilitating their distribution to the pancreas. An additional control group consisted of healthy, non-diabetic mice that received no treatment. The primary outcome measures were blood glucose levels, body weight, and survival. Blood glucose was monitored daily for the first week and then twice weekly for the remainder of the six-week study period using a One Touch Ultra Meter and test strips. Body weight was recorded at the time of cell transplantation and at the study endpoint. All animals were euthanized at the six-week mark, and terminal blood samples were collected for serum insulin measurements. The pancreas and other organs were harvested for histological and molecular analyses. The results of this study were striking. The group treated with hBMSCs-VEGF demonstrated a high rate of sustained normoglycemia, with 5 out of 9 mice achieving and maintaining blood glucose levels below 200 mg/dl for the entire study duration. These "rescued" mice also exhibited a 100% survival rate and gained weight at a rate comparable to the healthy control group. In contrast, the group treated with hBMSCs-PDX1 showed only a transient benefit, with 4 out of 8 mice experiencing a temporary drop in blood glucose that subsequently relapsed to severe hyperglycemia. Although these mice survived, their long-term glycemic control was poor. The group treated with unmodified hBMSCs showed no therapeutic benefit, with their blood glucose, survival, and weight profiles being indistinguishable from the sham-treated diabetic controls. The serum analysis confirmed that the sustained cure in the hBMSCs-VEGF group was associated with a significant increase in endogenous (mouse) insulin, while the transient effect in the hBMSCs-PDX1 group was associated with a short-lived contribution from transplanted (human) insulin. The comprehensive histological and molecular analyses, as detailed in the preceding examples, confirmed that the hBMSCs-VEGF treatment led to the restoration of the islet vascular niche, the activation of the insulin/IGF-1/PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, and the consequent proliferation and survival of the host's own β-cells. This example conclusively demonstrates that the administration of hBMSCs genetically modified to express VEGF is a highly effective and durable strategy for reversing insulin-dependent diabetes in a preclinical model, providing a strong foundation for its translation into human clinical therapy.
