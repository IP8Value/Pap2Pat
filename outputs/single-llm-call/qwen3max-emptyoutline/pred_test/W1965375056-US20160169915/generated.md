## BACKGROUND

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) represents a complex and heterogeneous group of neurodevelopmental conditions characterized by persistent deficits in social communication and interaction across multiple contexts, as well as restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities. The prevalence of ASD has increased substantially over recent decades, with current estimates indicating that approximately one in every 68 children in the United States receives an ASD diagnosis. Despite advances in clinical understanding and diagnostic criteria, the average age at which children receive a definitive ASD diagnosis remains around four years, significantly limiting the window for early intervention during critical periods of neurodevelopment. Early intensive behavioral interventions have been consistently demonstrated to improve long-term outcomes in children with ASD, including enhanced cognitive functioning, improved social skills, better communication abilities, and reduced family burden. However, the current diagnostic paradigm relies heavily on behavioral observation, developmental history, and comprehensive psychological assessments, which are time-consuming, resource-intensive, and often inaccessible to many families, particularly those in underserved communities.

The etiological basis of ASD remains incompletely understood, with evidence pointing toward a multifactorial origin involving complex interactions between genetic predisposition and environmental influences. While genomic studies have identified hundreds of genetic variants associated with ASD, these collectively account for only approximately 20% of diagnosed cases, highlighting the limitations of purely genetic approaches to diagnosis and risk assessment. Furthermore, genomic analyses fail to capture the dynamic influence of environmental factors, epigenetic modifications, and gene-environment interactions that may significantly contribute to disease manifestation. The complexity of ASD genetics is further compounded by questions regarding potential confounding factors in genomic studies, including population stratification and ancestral biases that may affect the validity and generalizability of predictive models derived from such data.

In response to these limitations, there has been growing interest in alternative diagnostic approaches that can capture the integrated effects of both genetic and environmental influences on biological systems. Metabolomics, the comprehensive analysis of small molecule metabolites within biological systems, offers a particularly promising avenue for ASD biomarker discovery. Unlike genomic approaches that provide static information about genetic potential, metabolomics reflects the dynamic, functional state of biological systems and captures the downstream effects of genetic, epigenetic, environmental, and microbial influences. The metabolome represents the end product of cellular processes and provides a direct readout of physiological status, making it particularly well-suited for identifying disease-associated biochemical signatures.

Previous metabolomic studies in ASD have primarily focused on urine samples, identifying alterations in various metabolic pathways including tryptophan/nicotinic acid metabolism, sulfur metabolism, amino acid metabolism, and microbial-derived metabolites. While these findings have provided valuable insights into potential pathophysiological mechanisms, urine-based metabolomic profiles may not fully reflect systemic metabolic alterations or brain-specific metabolic changes due to differences in metabolite transport, accumulation, and excretion patterns between biological compartments. Blood plasma, by contrast, represents a more direct reflection of systemic metabolic status and may provide superior biomarkers for clinical diagnostic applications.

However, the development of reliable blood-based metabolomic biomarkers for ASD faces significant technical challenges. The human metabolome encompasses thousands of chemically diverse small molecules spanning a wide range of concentrations, polarities, and chemical properties, making comprehensive metabolomic profiling technically demanding. No single analytical platform can capture the full breadth of metabolomic diversity, necessitating the use of orthogonal analytical approaches that combine different separation techniques, ionization methods, and detection technologies. High-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) has emerged as a particularly powerful tool for metabolomic analysis due to its exceptional sensitivity, accurate mass measurement capabilities, and ability to distinguish between isobaric compounds with identical nominal masses but different elemental compositions. When combined with complementary chromatographic separation techniques such as liquid chromatography (LC) and gas chromatography (GC), HRMS enables comprehensive metabolomic profiling with enhanced metabolite identification confidence.

Despite these technological advances, the successful development of clinically useful metabolomic biomarkers for ASD requires not only comprehensive analytical coverage but also sophisticated computational approaches for data analysis, feature selection, and model validation. The high dimensionality of metabolomic data, with thousands of measured features relative to relatively small sample sizes, creates significant challenges for statistical analysis and model development, including risks of overfitting and false discovery. Robust analytical pipelines must therefore incorporate rigorous quality control measures, appropriate statistical filtering methods, and independent validation strategies to ensure the reliability and generalizability of identified biomarker signatures.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a comprehensive, multi-platform metabolomic profiling approach specifically optimized for the discovery and validation of blood-based biomarkers for ASD. The invention combines orthogonal analytical methodologies—including hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (HILIC-HRMS), reversed-phase liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (C8-HRMS), and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS)—to achieve broad metabolomic coverage across diverse chemical classes. This analytical framework is coupled with sophisticated computational methods for data preprocessing, feature selection, and classification modeling that incorporate nested cross-validation, recursive feature elimination, and independent validation to ensure robust biomarker discovery and minimize overfitting. The resulting metabolic signature demonstrates superior diagnostic performance compared to existing approaches, providing a foundation for the development of clinically useful blood-based diagnostic tests for ASD that can facilitate earlier intervention and improved outcomes for affected children.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods, systems, and compositions for diagnosing autism spectrum disorder (ASD) through comprehensive metabolomic profiling of blood plasma samples. The invention is based on the discovery that specific metabolic signatures in blood plasma can reliably distinguish between children with ASD and typically developing (TD) children with high accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. These metabolic signatures comprise panels of metabolites representing diverse biochemical classes including amino acids, organic acids, fatty acids, sterols, and other small molecule metabolites that reflect underlying pathophysiological processes associated with ASD.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for diagnosing ASD in a subject comprising: (a) obtaining a blood plasma sample from the subject; (b) analyzing the blood plasma sample using a combination of orthogonal metabolomic analytical platforms, wherein the orthogonal metabolomic analytical platforms comprise hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (HILIC-HRMS) in both positive and negative ionization modes, reversed-phase liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectetry (C8-HRMS) in both positive and negative ionization modes, and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS); (c) generating a metabolic profile comprising abundance measurements for a plurality of metabolites detected across the orthogonal analytical platforms; (d) comparing the metabolic profile to a reference metabolic signature associated with ASD; and (e) diagnosing the subject as having ASD when the metabolic profile matches the reference metabolic signature above a predetermined threshold.

In another aspect, the invention provides a diagnostic system for ASD comprising: (a) an analytical module configured to perform orthogonal metabolomic analysis of blood plasma samples using HILIC-HRMS, C8-HRMS, and GC-MS; (b) a data processing module configured to generate metabolic profiles from the analytical data; (c) a classification module configured to compare metabolic profiles to a reference ASD metabolic signature and generate diagnostic predictions; and (d) a reporting module configured to output diagnostic results to a user interface.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a reference metabolic signature for ASD comprising a panel of metabolites selected from the group consisting of homocitrulline, aspartate, glutamate, dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEAS), citric acid, succinic acid, methylhexadecanoic acid, tetradecanoic acid, heptadecanoic acid, isoleucine, glutaric acid, 3-aminoisobutyric acid, creatinine, p-hydroxyphenyllactate, and indoleacetate, wherein the reference metabolic signature comprises relative abundance patterns of these metabolites that distinguish ASD subjects from typically developing subjects.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit for diagnosing ASD comprising: (a) reagents for blood plasma sample preparation; (b) chromatographic columns for HILIC and C8 separations; (c) internal standards for metabolomic analysis; and (d) instructions for performing orthogonal metabolomic analysis and interpreting results according to the methods described herein.

The invention also provides computer-readable media containing instructions that, when executed by a processor, cause the processor to perform the diagnostic methods described herein. Additionally, the invention encompasses databases containing reference metabolic signatures for ASD that can be used for diagnostic comparison and machine learning model training.

A key feature of the invention is the identification of homocitrulline as a novel and highly discriminative biomarker for ASD. Homocitrulline, a mitochondrial metabolite formed from lysine and carbamoyl phosphate, was found to be significantly decreased in the blood plasma of children with ASD compared to typically developing children. This finding is particularly significant because homocitrulline had not been previously reported as an ASD biomarker in metabolomic studies, and its identification through the comprehensive analytical approach described herein demonstrates the power of the invention's methodology for novel biomarker discovery.

The invention's diagnostic performance has been validated through rigorous statistical analysis using both training and independent validation datasets. Classification models developed using support vector machine (SVM) and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) algorithms achieved prediction accuracies exceeding 80% in independent validation sets, with area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) values ranging from 0.81 to 0.84. Importantly, the invention demonstrates that optimal diagnostic performance can be achieved with a reduced panel of 80-160 metabolites, rather than requiring analysis of the full metabolomic dataset, thereby facilitating the development of more practical and cost-effective diagnostic assays.

The metabolic signatures identified by the invention reflect multiple pathophysiological pathways implicated in ASD, including mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, energy metabolism alterations, and potential gut microbiome influences. This multi-pathway representation enhances the diagnostic robustness of the invention and provides insights into the underlying biological mechanisms of ASD that may inform future therapeutic development.

The invention represents a significant advance over existing diagnostic approaches for ASD by providing an objective, blood-based diagnostic method that can complement current behavioral assessment protocols. By enabling earlier and more accessible diagnosis, the invention has the potential to facilitate timely intervention during critical periods of neurodevelopment, ultimately improving long-term outcomes for children with ASD and reducing the burden on families and healthcare systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS

The present invention provides comprehensive methods and systems for diagnosing autism spectrum disorder (ASD) through metabolomic profiling of blood plasma samples. The invention is based on extensive research demonstrating that specific metabolic signatures in blood plasma can reliably distinguish between children with ASD and typically developing (TD) children. These metabolic signatures reflect underlying pathophysiological processes associated with ASD and provide a foundation for objective, blood-based diagnostic testing that can complement current behavioral assessment protocols.

The core methodology of the invention involves the application of orthogonal metabolomic analytical platforms to achieve comprehensive coverage of the blood plasma metabolome. This approach recognizes that no single analytical technique can capture the full chemical diversity of the human metabolome, which encompasses thousands of small molecules with varying polarities, molecular weights, and chemical properties. By combining multiple complementary analytical techniques, the invention achieves superior metabolomic coverage and enhanced biomarker discovery capability compared to single-platform approaches.

The primary analytical platforms employed in the invention include hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (HILIC-HRMS), reversed-phase liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (C8-HRMS), and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS). Each platform contributes unique capabilities to the overall analytical strategy. HILIC-HRMS is particularly effective for the separation and detection of polar and hydrophilic metabolites, including amino acids, organic acids, nucleotides, and other water-soluble compounds. The invention employs HILIC-HRMS in both positive and negative electrospray ionization (ESI) modes to maximize detection coverage across different metabolite classes. C8-HRMS, utilizing a reversed-phase chromatographic column with medium hydrophobicity, provides complementary separation capabilities for moderately polar to non-polar metabolites, including fatty acids, sterols, and lipid-derived compounds. Like HILIC-HRMS, C8-HRMS is operated in both positive and negative ESI modes to enhance metabolite detection. GC-MS provides additional coverage for volatile and semi-volatile compounds, as well as metabolites that can be derivatized to enhance volatility, including various organic acids, sugars, and amino acids.

The high-resolution mass spectrometry component of the invention utilizes quadrupole time-of-flight (QTOF) mass spectrometers capable of accurate mass measurement with mass accuracy typically better than 5 parts per million (ppm). This high mass accuracy enables confident metabolite identification through molecular formula determination and distinguishes between isobaric compounds that would be indistinguishable using lower-resolution mass spectrometry. The invention further incorporates tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) capabilities for structural confirmation of key metabolites through fragmentation pattern analysis and comparison with reference spectral libraries.

Sample preparation protocols are carefully optimized to preserve metabolite integrity and minimize analytical artifacts. Blood plasma samples are collected following standardized protocols that include overnight fasting and immediate processing to minimize metabolic changes post-collection. Plasma samples are stored at -80°C until analysis and undergo protein precipitation using cold methanol-water mixtures containing appropriate internal standards for quality control and normalization. The extraction protocol is designed to recover a broad range of metabolite classes while minimizing matrix effects and ion suppression during mass spectrometric analysis.

Data analysis represents a critical component of the invention's methodology, incorporating sophisticated computational approaches to handle the high-dimensional nature of metabolomic data while minimizing risks of overfitting and false discovery. The analytical pipeline begins with rigorous quality control procedures, including assessment of chromatographic performance, internal standard consistency, and sample integrity. Raw mass spectrometric data undergo preprocessing steps including peak detection, retention time alignment, and feature extraction using established algorithms such as XCMS. Features are defined by their mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) and chromatographic retention time, creating a comprehensive feature matrix for subsequent statistical analysis.

Statistical analysis follows a systematic approach designed to identify robust metabolic signatures while maintaining statistical rigor. Initial univariate analysis using Welch's t-tests identifies features with statistically significant abundance differences between ASD and TD groups. These features undergo careful quality review, including examination of extracted ion chromatograms for integration consistency, peak shape quality, and minimum abundance thresholds. False discovery rate correction using the Benjamini-Hochberg method controls for multiple testing effects and enhances the reliability of identified differential features.

Classification modeling employs both support vector machine (SVM) and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) algorithms to develop robust diagnostic models. The invention incorporates nested cross-validation procedures with multiple resampling iterations to ensure model stability and generalizability. Recursive feature elimination systematically identifies the minimal set of metabolites required for optimal diagnostic performance, balancing model complexity with predictive accuracy. Independent validation using held-out sample sets provides unbiased assessment of model performance and guards against overoptimistic performance estimates.

Metabolite identification follows a tiered approach that combines accurate mass measurement, retention time characteristics, fragmentation pattern analysis, and database matching. Putative identifications are initially assigned based on accurate mass matches to chemical databases within specified mass error tolerances (typically 20 ppm). These putative assignments undergo further validation using the "Find by Formula" algorithm, which evaluates the consistency between observed mass spectra and theoretical spectra for proposed molecular formulas. Confirmed metabolites undergo additional structural verification through MS/MS analysis and comparison with reference standards when available.

The invention has identified a comprehensive panel of metabolites that form the basis of its diagnostic signature for ASD. Key metabolites include homocitrulline, which represents a novel ASD biomarker discovered through the invention's comprehensive analytical approach. Homocitrulline, a mitochondrial metabolite formed from lysine and carbamoyl phosphate, was found to be significantly decreased in ASD subjects and demonstrated the highest discriminatory power among all identified features. Other important metabolites in the diagnostic signature include amino acids such as aspartate, glutamate, and isoleucine; organic acids including citric acid, succinic acid, and glutaric acid; fatty acids such as methylhexadecanoic acid, tetradecanoic acid, and heptadecanoic acid; and other compounds including dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEAS), creatinine, p-hydroxyphenyllactate, and indoleacetate.

The diagnostic performance of the invention has been rigorously validated using both training and independent validation datasets. SVM models utilizing an optimized panel of 80 metabolites achieved prediction accuracy of 81%, sensitivity of 85%, specificity of 75%, and AUC of 0.84 in independent validation. PLS-DA models using 160 metabolites achieved comparable performance with 81% accuracy, 92% sensitivity, 63% specificity, and AUC of 0.81. These performance metrics demonstrate the clinical utility of the invention's approach while acknowledging the need for further validation in larger, more diverse populations.

The metabolic signatures identified by the invention reflect multiple pathophysiological pathways implicated in ASD pathogenesis. Alterations in amino acid metabolism, particularly involving glutamate and aspartate, may reflect disruptions in neurotransmitter balance and excitatory-inhibitory equilibrium in the brain. Changes in organic acid levels, including citric acid and succinic acid, suggest mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired energy metabolism, consistent with emerging evidence linking mitochondrial abnormalities to ASD. Fatty acid alterations may indicate membrane lipid composition changes or peroxisomal dysfunction. The presence of microbiome-associated metabolites such as p-hydroxyphenyllactate supports the growing recognition of gut-brain axis involvement in ASD pathophysiology.

The invention's methodology is designed to be adaptable and scalable for clinical implementation. While the initial discovery phase utilized comprehensive multi-platform analysis, the identification of optimized metabolite panels containing 80-160 features enables the development of more targeted, cost-effective diagnostic assays suitable for routine clinical use. The invention contemplates various implementation strategies, including laboratory-developed tests (LDTs) in specialized metabolomics laboratories, commercial diagnostic kits for broader distribution, and integrated diagnostic systems combining analytical hardware, software, and reference databases.

Quality control and standardization represent essential aspects of the invention's clinical implementation strategy. The methodology incorporates multiple levels of quality control, including internal standards for analytical performance monitoring, batch effect correction through normalization procedures, and rigorous sample handling protocols to minimize pre-analytical variability. The invention also contemplates the establishment of reference ranges and diagnostic thresholds through large-scale validation studies in diverse populations, accounting for potential confounding factors such as age, sex, ethnicity, medication use, and dietary restrictions.

The invention's diagnostic approach offers several advantages over existing ASD diagnostic methods. Unlike purely behavioral assessments, which can be subjective and require specialized expertise, the invention provides an objective, quantitative measure of biological status. Compared to genomic approaches, which capture only genetic risk factors and miss environmental influences, the invention's metabolomic approach integrates the effects of both genetic and environmental factors on biological function. The blood-based nature of the test makes it more accessible than cerebrospinal fluid-based approaches, while providing more direct information about systemic metabolic status than urine-based methods.

Future developments of the invention contemplate expansion to include additional metabolite classes, integration with other omics data types (such as genomics, proteomics, and microbiomics), and refinement of diagnostic signatures for specific ASD subtypes or endophenotypes. The invention also provides a foundation for monitoring treatment response and disease progression through serial metabolomic profiling, potentially enabling personalized therapeutic approaches based on individual metabolic profiles.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Subject Recruitment and Sample Collection**

A cohort of 104 children aged 4-6 years was recruited through the UC Davis M.I.N.D. Institute Clinic, Regional Centers, clinician referrals, school districts, and community support groups. The study included 69 children with ASD and 35 typically developing (TD) children. All participants provided written informed consent through their parents or guardians, and the study was approved by the University of California at Davis Institutional Review Board. Children with ASD met strict diagnostic criteria based on DSM-IV criteria confirmed by experienced neuropsychologists, with additional corroboration through Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule-Generic (ADOS-G) and Autism Diagnostic Interview-Revised (ADI-R) assessments. TD children were screened using the Social Communication Questionnaire (SCQ) to ensure absence of ASD symptoms. Exclusion criteria included Fragile X syndrome, serious neurological or psychiatric conditions, known medical conditions such as autoimmune disease or inflammatory bowel disease, and major organ system diseases. All participants underwent overnight fasting prior to blood collection, and morning blood draws were performed between 8-10 AM using standardized protocols. Blood was collected in EDTA Vacutainer tubes, immediately mixed, placed on ice, and processed for plasma separation within two hours of collection. Plasma samples were aliquoted, labeled with barcodes, and shipped on dry ice to the analytical laboratory, where they were stored at -80°C until analysis.

**Example 2: Orthogonal Metabolomic Analysis Platform**

Blood plasma samples underwent comprehensive metabolomic analysis using three orthogonal analytical platforms. For LC-HRMS analysis, 50 µL plasma aliquots were extracted using 450 µL of 8:1 methanol:water solution containing internal standards. Extracts were centrifuged twice at 18,400×G for 20 minutes at 4°C, and supernatants were evaporated to dryness and reconstituted in 45 µL of 50:50 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile:0.1% formic acid containing internal standards. Four separate LC-HRMS analyses were performed using an Agilent G6540 QTOF system: HILIC chromatography in positive ion mode, HILIC in negative ion mode, C8 chromatography in positive ion mode, and C8 in negative ion mode. HILIC separations used a Waters Acquity UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1×150 mm, 1.7 µm) with a 29-minute gradient at 0.5 mL/minute flow rate. C8 separations used an Agilent Zorbax Eclipse Plus C8 column (2.1×100 mm, 1.8 µm) with a 50-minute gradient at 0.5 mL/minute flow rate. GC-MS analysis was performed at the West Coast Metabolomics Center using an Agilent 6890 gas chromatograph coupled to a LECO Pegasus IV TOF mass spectrometer, with metabolite identification based on comparison to a database of over 1,000 reference compounds.

**Example 3: Data Preprocessing and Quality Control**

Raw mass spectrometric data underwent rigorous quality control and preprocessing. Initial quality assessment included evaluation of total ion chromatogram quality, internal standard performance, and sample integrity. Five samples showing overt hemolysis were excluded from analysis. Raw data files were converted to mzData format and processed using XCMS for peak detection and feature extraction. Retention time alignment was performed using the obiwarp algorithm, and missing features were integrated using iterative peak filling based on retention time and mass range. Data filters removed features with low abundance, background noise characteristics, non-biological mass defects, and known contaminants. Batch effects were corrected by normalizing feature abundances to the median area of spiked-in internal standards across all samples. The final preprocessed dataset contained 4,572 features from 82 samples (52 ASD, 30 TD) that passed all quality control criteria.

**Example 4: Statistical Analysis and Feature Selection**

The preprocessed dataset was divided into a training set (61 samples: 39 ASD, 22 TD) and an independent validation set (21 samples: 13 ASD, 8 TD), with randomization ensuring similar distributions of diagnosis, IQ, and gender across sets. Univariate analysis using Welch's t-tests identified 389 features with statistically significant abundance differences between ASD and TD groups (p < 0.05). Extracted ion chromatograms for these features underwent quality review for integration consistency, peak shape, and minimum peak height (>3000), resulting in 179 high-quality features for classification modeling. False discovery rate correction using the Benjamini-Hochberg method controlled for multiple testing effects. Classification modeling employed both SVM with linear kernel and PLS-DA algorithms using the R caret package, with nested cross-validation (100 resamples, 80:20 split) and recursive feature elimination (20-feature steps) to identify optimal feature subsets and robust variable importance scores.

**Example 5: Metabolite Identification and Structural Confirmation**

Metabolite identification followed a tiered approach combining accurate mass measurement, retention time characteristics, and database matching. Putative identifications were assigned based on m/z matches to chemical databases within 20 ppm mass error tolerance. The "Find by Formula" algorithm in Agilent MassHunter Qualitative Analysis software evaluated spectral consistency with proposed molecular formulas, with features requiring median FBF scores ≥70, retention time differences ≤35 seconds, and presence in ≥50% of data files for inclusion. Seven key metabolites underwent structural confirmation through LC-HRMS-MS analysis using collision-induced dissociation and comparison with reference spectra from METLIN, MassBank, and SteminaMetDB databases. GC-MS metabolites were identified through direct comparison with reference standard spectra acquired using identical analytical methods.

**Example 6: Diagnostic Model Performance**

Classification models demonstrated robust diagnostic performance in both training and validation datasets. In the 61-sample training set, SVM models achieved average accuracy of 86% and AUC of 0.95 (95% CI 0.94-0.96), while PLS-DA models achieved 84% accuracy and AUC of 0.92 (95% CI 0.91-0.94). Permutation testing confirmed that these results were not due to chance, with randomized models achieving AUC values near 0.5. Feature optimization identified 80-feature SVM models as optimal, achieving 90% accuracy, 92% sensitivity, 87% specificity, and AUC of 0.95 in cross-validation. In the independent 21-sample validation set, the 80-feature SVM model achieved 81% accuracy, 85% sensitivity, 75% specificity, and AUC of 0.84, while the best PLS-DA model (160 features) achieved 81% accuracy, 92% sensitivity, 63% specificity, and AUC of 0.81. Performance analysis indicated that at least 40 features were required to achieve 70% accuracy, with optimal performance achieved using 80-160 features.

**Example 7: Key Metabolite Biomarkers**

The invention identified a comprehensive panel of metabolite biomarkers spanning multiple biochemical classes. Homocitrulline emerged as the most discriminative biomarker, showing significant decrease in ASD subjects and ranking highest in both SVM and PLS-DA variable importance scores. Other key biomarkers included amino acids (aspartate, glutamate, isoleucine), organic acids (citric acid, succinic acid, glutaric acid, 3-aminoisobutyric acid), fatty acids (methylhexadecanoic acid, tetradecanoic acid, heptadecanoic acid), sterols (DHEAS), and other compounds (creatinine, p-hydroxyphenyllactate, indoleacetate). Several of these metabolites represent novel ASD biomarkers not previously reported in metabolomic studies, while others confirm findings from previous research. The diverse biochemical representation of the biomarker panel reflects multiple pathophysiological pathways implicated in ASD, including mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, energy metabolism alterations, and gut microbiome influences.

**Example 8: Biological Pathway Implications**

The identified metabolic signature provides insights into multiple biological pathways potentially involved in ASD pathogenesis. Decreased homocitrulline levels suggest alterations in mitochondrial metabolism and urea cycle function, consistent with emerging evidence linking mitochondrial dysfunction to ASD. Changes in amino acid levels, particularly glutamate and aspartate, may reflect disruptions in neurotransmitter balance and excitatory-inhibitory equilibrium in neural circuits. Alterations in citric acid cycle intermediates (citric acid, succinic acid) further support mitochondrial involvement and impaired energy metabolism. Fatty acid changes may indicate membrane lipid composition alterations or peroxisomal dysfunction. The presence of microbiome-associated metabolites such as p-hydroxyphenyllactate supports the gut-brain axis hypothesis in ASD, suggesting that microbial metabolism may influence systemic metabolic status and potentially neurodevelopmental outcomes.
