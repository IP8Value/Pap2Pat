## BACKGROUND

The modeling of multivariate event stream data represents a critical challenge in numerous scientific and industrial domains, including but not limited to social network analysis, biochemical pathway modeling, electronic health record interpretation, and computer system monitoring. In these contexts, events of multiple types occur at irregular intervals on a shared timeline, creating complex temporal patterns that require sophisticated analytical approaches to understand and predict. Traditional statistical methods for modeling such data have relied heavily on parametric assumptions about the underlying stochastic processes, which often fail to capture the rich diversity of temporal dependencies observed in real-world systems. The mathematical framework of multivariate point processes and conditional intensity functions provides a theoretically sound basis for analyzing event stream data, where the instantaneous arrival rate of each event type depends on the complete history of all previous events. However, the practical implementation of this framework has been constrained by the necessity of making strong assumptions about the functional form of the conditional intensity functions.

Historical approaches to event stream modeling have included graphical event models such as proximal graphical event models, forest-based point processes, piecewise-constant conditional intensity models, and Poisson networks. These methods, while theoretically elegant, impose rigid structural constraints that may not align with the actual data-generating processes in complex real-world scenarios. For instance, piecewise-constant models assume that intensity functions remain constant over predefined intervals, while Poisson-based approaches often assume independence between events or specific decay patterns that may not reflect reality. The fundamental limitation of these parametric approaches lies in their inability to adapt flexibly to the diverse temporal patterns that emerge in different application domains without extensive domain-specific knowledge and manual tuning.

More recently, researchers have turned to sequential deep learning techniques to address the limitations of traditional parametric models. Methods such as recurrent marked temporal point processes (RMTPP) and neural Hawkes processes have demonstrated promising results by using recurrent neural networks to capture history-dependent arrival rates. These semi-parametric approaches represent a significant advancement over purely parametric methods, as they can learn complex patterns from data without requiring explicit specification of the underlying functional forms. However, even these deep learning approaches retain certain restrictive assumptions about how historical events influence future intensities. Specifically, many existing neural point process models incorporate exponential decay mechanisms inspired by classical Hawkes processes, where each historical event exerts an additive influence on the conditional intensity that decays exponentially over time. This assumption, while mathematically convenient, fails to capture more complex temporal dependencies that may be present in real-world data.

Several important limitations persist in current state-of-the-art approaches to event stream modeling. First, most existing methods do not adequately account for the information contained in inter-event intervals—the periods between consecutive events where no events occur. This "negative evidence" is crucial for accurate modeling, as the absence of events during certain time periods provides valuable information about the underlying intensity functions. Second, real-world event streams often exhibit multiple time scales, where different event types may operate on different temporal horizons or respond to historical events with varying delays. Traditional recurrent neural networks with fixed-length historical windows struggle to capture these multi-scale dynamics effectively. Third, complex history dependencies involving delayed excitation or inhibition effects, where events of one type influence the occurrence of other types after significant time lags, are not satisfactorily addressed by existing models. Finally, the mutual influence between different event types—where the occurrence of one event type affects the intensity of others in label-specific ways—is often oversimplified in current approaches.

The need for a more flexible, non-parametric approach to event stream modeling has become increasingly apparent as applications demand more accurate and adaptable models. Such an approach should be able to learn history-dependent conditional intensity functions in a fully data-driven manner, without imposing restrictive assumptions about their functional forms. It should effectively incorporate both positive evidence (actual event occurrences) and negative evidence (inter-event intervals) into the modeling process. Furthermore, it should be capable of capturing multi-scale temporal dynamics and complex inter-type dependencies that characterize real-world event streams. The present invention addresses these unmet needs by introducing a novel multi-channel neural graphical event model that combines the theoretical foundation of graphical event models with the representational power of deep neural networks, augmented with innovative mechanisms for handling continuous-time dynamics and complex temporal dependencies.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a non-parametric deep learning approach for modeling multivariate event stream data in continuous time, specifically designed to overcome the limitations of existing parametric and semi-parametric methods. The core innovation lies in a multi-channel neural graphical event model (MCN-GEM) that learns history-dependent conditional intensity functions in a fully data-driven manner, without requiring assumptions about their functional forms beyond the network architecture and activation functions. This approach fundamentally differs from prior art by incorporating three key innovations that work synergistically to achieve superior modeling performance.

First, the invention introduces a novel mechanism for capturing the continuous-time variation of historical influence on conditional intensity through the exploitation of negative evidence from inter-event durations. Rather than assuming specific decay functions or treating inter-event intervals as periods of static influence, the present invention introduces "fake epochs" within each inter-event interval to reinforce the negative evidence of no observable events during these periods. These fake epochs, represented by an auxiliary event label, participate in the recurrent neural network computations just like real events, allowing the internal states to evolve continuously throughout the entire timeline rather than only at discrete event occurrences. This approach eliminates the need for parametric assumptions about how influence decays over time and enables the model to learn arbitrary temporal patterns directly from data.

Second, the invention develops an efficient multi-scale, multi-channel internal state representation that aligns naturally with the framework of graphical event models. By partitioning the hidden state vector into label-specific sub-vectors, the model maintains separate representations for each event type, enabling it to capture label-specific dynamics and mutual influences between different event types. This multi-channel architecture allows the model to handle the varying base rates and temporal characteristics of different event types while maintaining computational efficiency. The dimensionality of the hidden state is carefully chosen as an integer multiple of the number of event labels plus one (to accommodate the fake label), ensuring that each channel receives adequate representational capacity.

Third, the invention incorporates a spatiotemporal attention mechanism that dynamically determines which historical events and which points in time are most influential for determining the instantaneous arrival rate of any given event type. This attention mechanism operates over a memory bank containing the most recent J event arrivals across all event types, computing weighted combinations of historical hidden states that emphasize the most relevant past events. The spatial component of attention captures inter-label dependencies (which event types influence which others), while the temporal component captures lagged dependencies (how far back in time relevant influences extend). This dual attention mechanism effectively performs structure learning analogous to that in graphical modeling literature, but in a continuous, differentiable manner that can be optimized through standard gradient-based methods.

The combination of these three innovations results in a model that demonstrates significant improvements over state-of-the-art approaches in terms of log-likelihood performance on both synthetic and real-world datasets. The invention has been validated on diverse datasets including synthetic data generated from proximal graphical event models, political news events from the ICEWS dataset, and electronic health records from the MIMIC-II database. In all cases, the proposed MCN-GEM outperforms existing methods, with particularly notable gains when fake epochs are incorporated into the modeling process. The model's ability to learn complex temporal patterns without parametric assumptions makes it particularly well-suited for applications where the underlying data-generating process is unknown or highly complex.

Furthermore, the attention mechanism provides additional interpretability benefits by enabling visualization of the learned relationships between event types as a graph. The attention weights can be aggregated over time to reveal which event types consistently influence others, providing insights into the underlying causal or associative relationships in the data. This interpretability feature distinguishes the present invention from purely black-box deep learning approaches and provides valuable diagnostic information for domain experts.

The present invention represents a significant advancement in the field of event stream modeling by providing a flexible, non-parametric framework that can adapt to diverse temporal patterns without requiring domain-specific knowledge or restrictive assumptions. Its ability to incorporate both positive and negative evidence, handle multi-scale dynamics, and capture complex inter-type dependencies makes it particularly well-suited for real-world applications where event streams exhibit rich and varied temporal structures.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive system and method for modeling multivariate event stream data using a non-parametric deep learning approach that overcomes the limitations of existing parametric and semi-parametric methods. The core of the invention is the Multi-Channel Neural Graphical Event Model (MCN-GEM), which integrates three key innovations: continuous-time modeling with negative evidence reinforcement, multi-channel internal state representation, and spatiotemporal attention mechanisms. These components work together to create a flexible, data-driven framework for learning history-dependent conditional intensity functions without requiring assumptions about their functional forms.

The system begins with the representation of event stream data as a temporally ordered sequence D = {(l_i, t_i)}^N_{i=1}, where t_i represents the occurrence time of the i-th event (t_i ∈ R+), and l_i represents the event label corresponding to a finite set L of possible labels (types), where |L| = M. The system assumes a time-ordered dataset where at most one event occurs at any point in continuous time, with t_i < t_j for i < j, initial time t_0 = 0 ≤ t_1, and end time t_{N+1} = T ≥ t_N, where T represents the total observation period. The strict history relative to any point t in continuous time is denoted as H_t and defined as the sequence of event epochs before time t, i.e., {(t_i, l_i)|t_i < t}. The instantaneous conditional intensity of event type k in label set L at time t is denoted as λ_k(t|H_t), which governs the history-dependent instantaneous rate of occurrence of event type k at time t.

The first major component of the invention involves continuous-time modeling with negative evidence reinforcement. Traditional recurrent neural network approaches to event stream modeling suffer from the limitation that internal states evolve only at discrete event occurrences, remaining static during inter-event intervals. This approach fails to capture the crucial information contained in the absence of events during these intervals. The present invention addresses this limitation by introducing "fake epochs" within each inter-event interval to reinforce the negative evidence of no observable events. Specifically, an auxiliary (M+1)-th label is introduced into the label set L and referred to as the "fake label." Within each inter-event interval, a predetermined number K of fake epochs (with label M+1) are interjected, spread uniformly in time over the interval. These fake epochs participate in the recurrent neural network computations just like real events, allowing the internal states to evolve continuously throughout the entire timeline.

The implementation of this continuous-time modeling component utilizes a recurrent neural network with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) cells. The sequence of tokens fed into the LSTM network corresponds to the temporally ordered event sequence D, where each token represents an event arrival with its associated label and timestamp. Each token is represented in the raw input as a concatenation of its one-hot encoded event label (1-out-of-M) and its continuous-valued timestamp. The internal states of the recurrent LSTM cell evolve in response to each current raw input, including both real and fake epochs. For computational efficiency, the LSTM parameters are shared among different event labels, though the hidden state representation is partitioned to maintain label-specific information.

The second major component of the invention is the multi-channel internal state representation. Recognizing that different event types may have different arrival rates and may mutually influence each other in label-specific ways, the system associates each label with a corresponding partition of a single hidden state vector. This is achieved by choosing the hidden state dimension to be an integer multiple of the number of labels plus one (to accommodate the fake label), i.e., m(M+1) for some positive integer m. This effectively realizes an m-dimensional hidden state for each label in L, enabling the model to maintain separate representations for each event type while sharing computational resources efficiently. These label-specific sub-vectors are selectively channeled for computing the label-specific rates through the network layer, providing the foundation for capturing complex inter-type dependencies.

The third major component of the invention is the spatiotemporal attention mechanism, which enables the model to dynamically determine which historical events and which points in time are most influential for determining the instantaneous arrival rate of any given event type. The system maintains a memory bank M of historical label-specific hidden states that span the most recent J event arrivals. Given the multi-channel view, this memory bank contains a total of M×J hidden state sub-vectors—one for each real label and for each timestamp corresponding to the most recent J event arrivals. These raw hidden states in the memory bank are combined using an attention mechanism into a net hidden state that becomes input for the lambda-network responsible for producing conditional intensities.

The attention mechanism computes a context vector c_i at each epoch i as a weighted average of the raw hidden states in the memory bank M_i. The weighting is determined by an alignment vector α_k_i, which compares the current raw hidden state h_k_i to each raw hidden state h_m in the memory bank at time i. The attention weight α_k_im is derived through a comparison function that evaluates the relevance of each historical hidden state to the current prediction task. This dual attention mechanism—spatial attention across event types and temporal attention across time points—effectively performs structure learning by identifying which event types influence which others and how far back in time relevant influences extend.

The training process for the MCN-GEM involves several key components. The system uses two feed-forward layers to learn the intensity rate λ_k(t_{i+1}) given the net hidden state h_net,k_i and the time interval Δt_{i+1} = t_{i+1} - t_i. The first feed-forward layer employs a ReLU activation function, while the second uses a softplus activation function to ensure positive conditional intensities, which is a standard requirement for point process models. The primary training objective is based on the log-likelihood function commonly used in point process modeling, with the assumption of constant intensity between consecutive events (real or fake). This leads to a computable objective function that can be optimized using standard gradient-based methods.

To address the increased flexibility and potential overfitting risks associated with the non-parametric nature of the model, the training process incorporates two regularization terms. First, a target prediction loss L_p is added, which represents the cross-entropy loss between the ground truth next event label l_{i+1} and the softmax of the predicted intensity vector λ(t_{i+1}). This classification loss serves dual purposes: it provides additional supervision signal for training and emphasizes the adversarial nature between real and fake epochs. Second, an L2-norm penalty L_w is applied to the weights of the feed-forward layers to prevent overfitting and encourage generalization.

The system's architecture is designed to handle the computational challenges associated with continuous-time modeling while maintaining the flexibility to capture complex temporal patterns. The introduction of fake epochs transforms the originally continuous-time problem into a discrete sequence modeling problem that can be efficiently handled by standard recurrent neural network architectures. The number of fake epochs K serves as a hyperparameter that controls the trade-off between computational complexity and modeling accuracy. Empirical studies have shown that even a single fake epoch per inter-event interval provides substantial improvements over models without fake epochs, while additional fake epochs provide diminishing returns and may even degrade performance due to class imbalance between real and fake epochs.

The multi-channel architecture provides several advantages over single-channel approaches. By maintaining separate hidden state representations for each event type, the model can capture the distinct temporal characteristics and base rates of different event types. This is particularly important in real-world scenarios where different event types may operate on different time scales or respond to historical events with varying delays. The multi-channel approach also enables more precise modeling of inter-type dependencies, as the influence of one event type on another can be modeled through the interactions between their respective hidden state channels.

The spatiotemporal attention mechanism provides both modeling and interpretability benefits. From a modeling perspective, attention allows the system to focus on the most relevant historical events when making predictions, effectively implementing a form of dynamic feature selection that adapts to the current context. From an interpretability perspective, the attention weights can be aggregated over time to reveal the learned relationships between event types, providing insights into the underlying causal or associative structures in the data. This interpretability feature is particularly valuable in scientific and medical applications where understanding the relationships between different event types is as important as making accurate predictions.

The system has been validated on diverse datasets spanning multiple domains. On synthetic data generated from proximal graphical event models, the MCN-GEM demonstrates superior performance compared to both parametric GEM learners and neural baselines, often achieving 20-35% better log-likelihood scores. On real-world political news data from the ICEWS dataset, the system shows consistent improvements of 5-15% over competing methods, with the exception of cases where train-test distribution shifts occur. On healthcare data from the MIMIC-II database, the system demonstrates its ability to handle complex medical event sequences and provides interpretable insights into patient care patterns.

Ablation studies confirm the importance of each component of the invention. The multi-channel architecture consistently outperforms single-channel variants, demonstrating the value of maintaining separate representations for different event types. The attention mechanism provides significant improvements over models without attention, with optimal performance achieved at moderate memory bank sizes (typically J=3). The introduction of fake epochs provides the most substantial gains, with even a single fake epoch per inter-event interval leading to dramatic improvements in modeling performance.

The system's design also considers practical implementation considerations. The choice of hyperparameters such as the number of fake epochs K, memory bank size J, and hidden state dimension m can be guided by empirical studies and domain knowledge. The system is implemented using standard deep learning frameworks such as PyTorch, making it accessible to practitioners and researchers. The computational complexity is manageable for real-world applications, with training times comparable to other neural point process models.

In summary, the present invention provides a comprehensive solution for modeling multivariate event stream data that addresses the key limitations of existing approaches. By combining continuous-time modeling with negative evidence reinforcement, multi-channel internal state representation, and spatiotemporal attention mechanisms, the system achieves superior modeling performance while maintaining interpretability and computational efficiency. The non-parametric nature of the approach makes it particularly well-suited for applications where the underlying data-generating process is unknown or highly complex, and the ability to incorporate both positive and negative evidence ensures robust performance across diverse domains.
