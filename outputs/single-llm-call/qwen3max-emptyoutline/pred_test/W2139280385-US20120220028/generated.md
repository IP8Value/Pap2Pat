# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and genetic engineering, specifically to novel nucleic acid regulatory elements derived from human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6). More particularly, the invention provides a promoter region derived from the major immediate-early (MIE) gene locus of HHV-6, which exhibits strong transcriptional activity, especially in T-cell lines. The invention further encompasses an enhancer element located within the first intron of the HHV-6 immediate-early 1 (IE1) gene that significantly augments the activity of the HHV-6 MIE promoter. Additionally, the invention includes gene constructs comprising the HHV-6 MIE promoter with or without the intronic enhancer, methods for enhancing heterologous gene expression in mammalian cells—particularly T cells—and uses of these regulatory elements in recombinant viral vectors, including varicella-zoster virus (VZV)-based vaccine platforms. The disclosed regulatory sequences offer superior performance over commonly used promoters such as the human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) major immediate-early promoter in specific cellular contexts, thereby enabling more efficient and cell-type-selective gene expression for applications in research, biotechnology, gene therapy, and vaccine development.

## BACKGROUND ART

Gene expression in eukaryotic systems is tightly regulated by complex interactions between promoter regions, enhancer elements, transcription factors, and epigenetic modifications. In the context of recombinant DNA technology and synthetic biology, strong and reliable promoters are essential tools for driving high-level expression of transgenes in both transient and stable expression systems. Among the most widely utilized promoters in mammalian expression vectors is the major immediate-early (MIE) promoter of human cytomegalovirus (HCMV), a betaherpesvirus. The HCMV MIE promoter is renowned for its robust activity across a broad spectrum of mammalian cell types, including fibroblasts, epithelial cells, and certain immune cells. Its commercial availability and well-characterized structure have made it a standard component in plasmid vectors, viral vectors, and transgenic constructs for decades.

However, despite its widespread utility, the HCMV MIE promoter exhibits variable performance depending on the host cell type. Notably, its activity is often suboptimal in lymphoid and hematopoietic cell lines, including T cells, which are critical targets for immunotherapies, vaccine development, and studies of immune function. This limitation has spurred interest in identifying alternative viral or cellular promoters with enhanced activity in specific cell lineages. Herpesviruses, due to their co-evolution with human hosts and sophisticated mechanisms for modulating host gene expression, represent a rich source of regulatory elements with unique properties.

Human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6), another member of the Betaherpesvirinae subfamily, shares significant genomic and biological similarities with HCMV. HHV-6 exists as two distinct variants, HHV-6A and HHV-6B, with HHV-6B being the causative agent of exanthema subitum (roseola infantum), a common childhood illness. A defining characteristic of HHV-6 is its pronounced tropism for CD4+ T lymphocytes, where it efficiently replicates and establishes latency. This T-cell tropism suggests that HHV-6 may possess regulatory elements optimized for high-level gene expression in T cells—a feature not fully recapitulated by the HCMV MIE promoter.

The HHV-6 genome contains positional homologs of the HCMV UL122 and UL123 genes, designated U86 (IE2) and U89 (IE1), respectively. These immediate-early genes are among the first to be transcribed upon viral reactivation and play pivotal roles in initiating the viral replication cascade. The promoter region governing their expression—the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter (HHV-6 MIEp)—is predicted to reside upstream of the IE1 transcription start site and overlaps with the R3 repeat region located at the right end of the viral genome. Prior studies identified the R3 region as a potent enhancer for the adjacent U95 gene, hinting at its broader regulatory potential.

Moreover, it is increasingly recognized that intronic sequences can profoundly influence gene expression through multiple mechanisms, including enhancement of transcriptional initiation, mRNA splicing efficiency, nuclear export, and transcript stability. In HCMV, inclusion of intron A from the IE1 transcript has been shown to boost promoter activity when placed downstream of the MIE promoter. Analogous intronic regulatory elements may exist in HHV-6, but their identity and functional impact have remained uncharacterized.

Despite the clinical and biological significance of HHV-6, its regulatory sequences have not been systematically exploited for biotechnological applications. No prior art discloses a functional characterization of the HHV-6 MIE promoter, nor does any known patent or publication describe its use—especially with intronic enhancers—for driving heterologous gene expression in mammalian cells or recombinant viral vectors. Consequently, there remains a significant unmet need for a strong, T-cell-preferential promoter system that outperforms existing standards like the HCMV MIE promoter in relevant cellular contexts, particularly for applications involving T-cell biology, immunotherapy, and viral vector-based vaccines.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The primary problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of a highly efficient, cell-type-selective promoter system suitable for robust gene expression in T lymphocytes and other immune cells. While the HCMV MIE promoter is widely used, its activity is inconsistent and often weak in T-cell lines such as Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1, limiting its utility in immunological research, adoptive T-cell therapies, and vaccine platforms targeting T-cell responses. Furthermore, existing promoters do not leverage the natural transcriptional optimization evolved by T-cell-tropic viruses like HHV-6. Another problem is the underutilization of intronic sequences as transcriptional enhancers in synthetic gene constructs; although introns are known to modulate expression, their systematic incorporation into promoter cassettes for maximal effect remains poorly developed. Additionally, there is a need for versatile regulatory elements that function effectively not only in plasmid-based systems but also in complex recombinant viral vectors, such as those based on varicella-zoster virus (VZV), which are used in live attenuated vaccines. Current VZV vectors rely on heterologous promoters like HCMV MIEp, which may not provide optimal expression kinetics or levels in VZV-permissive cells. Thus, the invention seeks to solve these interrelated challenges by providing a novel, naturally derived promoter-enhancer system with superior activity in T cells and compatibility with viral vector platforms.

### Solutions to the Problems

The present invention solves these problems by identifying, isolating, and functionally validating a novel promoter region derived from the major immediate-early (MIE) locus of human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6), designated herein as the HHV-6 MIE promoter (HHV-6 MIEp). This promoter, spanning approximately 971 base pairs upstream of the IE1 transcription start site and encompassing part of the R3 repeat region, demonstrates unexpectedly strong transcriptional activity in T-cell lines compared to the standard HCMV MIE promoter. Moreover, the invention discloses that the first intron of the HHV-6 IE1 gene acts as a potent transcriptional enhancer when positioned downstream of the HHV-6 MIEp. The resulting chimeric regulatory element—comprising the HHV-6 MIEp fused to intron 1 (designated HHV-6 MIEp-in1)—exhibits significantly enhanced activity across multiple cell types and surpasses the HCMV MIE promoter specifically in T-cell lines. The invention further identifies a critical NF-κB binding site within the R3 region as essential for full promoter activity, providing a mechanistic basis for its strength and inducibility. To address the need for viral vector compatibility, the invention demonstrates that the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 cassette can be successfully integrated into a recombinant varicella-zoster virus (VZV) Oka strain bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) genome via Tn7-mediated transposition and drives robust expression of a heterologous reporter gene (DsRed2) during viral infection. This establishes the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 as a functional and effective promoter for use in poxvirus and herpesvirus-based expression systems. Collectively, these solutions provide researchers and developers with a new class of regulatory DNA elements tailored for high-efficiency gene expression in immunologically relevant cells and recombinant vaccine platforms.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The present invention offers several significant advantages over existing promoter technologies. First, the HHV-6 MIE promoter, particularly when combined with the first intron of IE1 (HHV-6 MIEp-in1), provides markedly stronger transcriptional activity than the HCMV MIE promoter in T-cell lines, including Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1. This T-cell-preferential enhancement enables more efficient transgene expression in studies of T-cell signaling, activation, differentiation, and effector functions, as well as in the engineering of therapeutic T cells for cancer immunotherapy or infectious disease control. Second, the inclusion of intron 1 serves as a built-in enhancer that boosts promoter output without requiring additional exogenous enhancer sequences, simplifying vector design and reducing construct size. Third, the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 maintains robust activity in non-T-cell lines such as MRC-5 (human lung fibroblasts) and MeWo (melanoma cells), making it broadly applicable across diverse experimental systems while retaining its superiority in lymphoid contexts. Fourth, the promoter’s dependence on an NF-κB binding site links its activity to key inflammatory and immune signaling pathways, potentially allowing for inducible or context-dependent expression in response to physiological stimuli—a feature exploitable in smart therapeutic vectors. Fifth, the successful demonstration of HHV-6 MIEp-in1 functionality in a recombinant VZV vaccine strain proves its compatibility with large DNA virus vectors, opening avenues for its use in next-generation multivalent or vectored vaccines against pathogens such as mumps, influenza, or SARS-CoV-2. Sixth, because HHV-6 is a human pathogen with well-characterized biology, regulatory approval pathways for its derived sequences may be more straightforward compared to promoters from non-human or less-studied viruses. Finally, the invention provides a foundational platform for further engineering—such as mutagenesis of transcription factor binding sites or fusion with other regulatory modules—to fine-tune expression levels, specificity, or kinetics for specialized applications. Overall, the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 system represents a powerful, versatile, and biologically informed advancement in gene expression technology.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### DEFINITION

As used herein, the term “HHV-6 MIE promoter” or “HHV-6 MIEp” refers to a nucleic acid sequence derived from the genomic region upstream of the immediate-early 1 (IE1) gene of human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6), specifically encompassing nucleotides -983 to -13 relative to the transcription start site of IE1 in the HHV-6B strain HST (GenBank accession number NC_001664), which includes a portion of the R3 repeat region and functions to initiate transcription of downstream coding sequences. The term “intron 1” or “in1” denotes the first intronic sequence of the HHV-6 IE1 gene, located between exon 1 and exon 2 of the alternatively spliced IE1 transcript, which, when positioned downstream of the HHV-6 MIE promoter, enhances its transcriptional activity. The phrase “HHV-6 MIEp-in1” designates a composite regulatory element comprising the HHV-6 MIE promoter operably linked to intron 1 of the IE1 gene, typically followed by a heterologous coding sequence and a polyadenylation signal. “Operably linked” means that the promoter and intron are arranged in a configuration that allows them to function together to regulate transcription of an adjacent gene. The term “heterologous gene” refers to any nucleic acid sequence encoding a protein, RNA, or functional element that is not naturally associated with the HHV-6 MIE promoter in the wild-type HHV-6 genome, including but not limited to reporter genes (e.g., luciferase, DsRed2, GFP), therapeutic proteins (e.g., cytokines, antibodies, enzymes), antigens (e.g., viral glycoproteins), or regulatory RNAs. “T-cell lines” include immortalized human T lymphocyte cell lines such as Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1, which are commonly used models for T-cell biology. “Recombinant varicella-zoster virus” (rVZV) refers to a genetically modified VZV, such as the Oka vaccine strain, engineered to carry and express foreign genetic material under the control of a heterologous promoter. The term “NF-κB binding site” signifies a specific DNA motif within the HHV-6 MIE promoter (located between nucleotides -381 and -552 relative to the IE1 start site) that is recognized and bound by NF-κB transcription factors, and whose integrity is essential for maximal promoter activity. All numerical positions refer to the HHV-6B HST genome unless otherwise specified.

## DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### (Enhancer for Promoter)

In a preferred embodiment, the invention provides an enhancer element derived from the first intron of the immediate-early 1 (IE1) gene of human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6). This enhancer, referred to herein as intron 1 or in1, consists of the genomic sequence located between exon 1 and exon 2 of the alternatively spliced IE1 transcript of HHV-6B strain HST. When this intronic sequence is positioned downstream of a promoter—specifically the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter (HHV-6 MIEp)—it significantly amplifies transcriptional activity in a wide range of mammalian cell types. The enhancer effect is most pronounced in T-cell lines, where the combination of HHV-6 MIEp and intron 1 yields expression levels substantially exceeding those achieved with the human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) major immediate-early promoter. The intron 1 sequence is approximately 274 base pairs in length and contains predicted binding sites for transcriptional regulators including CCAAT/enhancer-binding protein (C/EBP) and OCT-1, which may contribute to its enhancing function. Importantly, the inclusion of additional introns (e.g., introns 2, 3, or 4) downstream of intron 1 diminishes promoter activity, indicating that intron 1 alone is both necessary and sufficient for maximal enhancement. This enhancer can be used in cis with the HHV-6 MIEp or potentially with other viral or cellular promoters to boost expression, though its greatest efficacy is observed in its native context. The enhancer is preferably included in expression cassettes as part of a contiguous DNA fragment extending from the HHV-6 MIE promoter through intron 1 into the beginning of exon 2, ensuring proper splicing signals are retained if splicing is desired, although the enhancing effect appears to be largely independent of splicing per se and may operate at the transcriptional level. This intronic enhancer represents a novel and non-obvious regulatory module that significantly improves the performance of the HHV-6 MIE promoter system.

### (Promoter)

In another preferred embodiment, the invention provides a promoter derived from the major immediate-early (MIE) gene locus of human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6), designated the HHV-6 MIE promoter (HHV-6 MIEp). This promoter corresponds to a 971-base pair genomic fragment located upstream of the transcription start site of the IE1 gene in HHV-6B strain HST, spanning nucleotide positions -983 to -13 relative to the IE1 start site (genome position 139442). The promoter includes a segment of the R3 repeat region, which is situated between the U95 and U89 (IE1) genes in the HHV-6 genome. Functional analysis reveals that this promoter exhibits strong basal activity, with a distinctive preference for T-cell lines such as Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1, where it outperforms the widely used HCMV MIE promoter. The core activity of the HHV-6 MIEp depends critically on a region between nucleotides -381 and -552, which harbors a functional NF-κB binding site; deletion of this site results in a dramatic reduction in promoter strength across all tested cell types. The promoter lacks canonical TATA or CAAT boxes but contains multiple potential transcription factor binding motifs, including AP-1, consistent with its responsiveness to cellular activation signals. The HHV-6 MIEp is capable of driving constitutive expression of downstream genes in both adherent and suspension mammalian cells and is particularly suited for applications requiring high-level expression in lymphoid cells. It may be used alone or, preferably, in combination with the intron 1 enhancer to form the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 regulatory unit. The promoter sequence is defined by its origin in the HHV-6 genome and its demonstrated ability to initiate transcription, and includes minor sequence variants that retain at least 80% identity and equivalent functional activity.

### (Gene Construct)

In a further preferred embodiment, the invention provides a gene construct comprising the HHV-6 MIE promoter operably linked to the first intron of the HHV-6 IE1 gene (intron 1), followed by a heterologous coding sequence and a polyadenylation signal. This construct, referred to as HHV-6 MIEp-in1, forms a complete expression cassette designed for high-efficiency gene expression in mammalian cells. The promoter initiates transcription, while intron 1 acts as a downstream enhancer to amplify mRNA production. The heterologous coding sequence may encode any protein of interest, such as a fluorescent reporter (e.g., DsRed2, GFP), a therapeutic molecule (e.g., interleukin-2, chimeric antigen receptor), a viral antigen (e.g., mumps virus HN glycoprotein), or an enzyme (e.g., luciferase). The polyadenylation signal, preferably derived from bovine growth hormone (BGH) or simian virus 40 (SV40), ensures proper 3' end processing and stability of the transcript. The entire cassette can be cloned into various vector backbones, including plasmids (e.g., pGL3-based luciferase reporters), bacterial artificial chromosomes (BACs), or viral genomes (e.g., VZV, adenovirus, lentivirus). In one exemplary configuration, the construct comprises: (1) the 971-bp HHV-6 MIE promoter (-983 to -13); (2) the 274-bp intron 1 sequence; (3) a multiple cloning site for insertion of the coding sequence; and (4) the BGH poly(A) signal. This gene construct has been validated in both transient transfection assays and recombinant viral systems, demonstrating its versatility and robustness. It represents a superior alternative to constructs using the HCMV MIE promoter, especially in T-cell-based applications.

### (Method for Enhancing Expression of Gene in Cell)

In yet another preferred embodiment, the invention provides a method for enhancing the expression of a gene in a mammalian cell, particularly a T cell. The method comprises introducing into the cell a nucleic acid construct wherein the gene is operably linked to the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter (HHV-6 MIEp) and the first intron of the HHV-6 IE1 gene (intron 1). The construct may be delivered via transfection (e.g., lipofection, electroporation), transduction (e.g., using viral vectors), or direct injection, depending on the cell type and application. Upon introduction, the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 regulatory unit drives high-level transcription of the gene, resulting in elevated mRNA and protein levels compared to constructs using conventional promoters such as HCMV MIEp. The method is especially effective when the target cell is a T lymphocyte or a derivative thereof, including primary human T cells or established T-cell lines like Jurkat, Molt-3, or SupT1. The enhanced expression enables improved detection in reporter assays, greater yield in protein production, or stronger biological effects in therapeutic contexts. The method may be applied in vitro (e.g., in cell culture for research or manufacturing) or in vivo (e.g., in gene therapy or vaccination). Optionally, the method includes co-stimulation of the cell with agents that activate NF-κB signaling (e.g., TNF-α, PMA/ionomycin), which may further potentiate expression due to the presence of the NF-κB binding site in the promoter. This method leverages the natural transcriptional optimization of HHV-6 for T cells to achieve superior transgene expression.

### (Use)

In a final preferred embodiment, the invention provides the use of the HHV-6 MIE promoter, with or without the first intron of IE1, for driving the expression of a heterologous gene in a recombinant varicella-zoster virus (VZV). Specifically, the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 cassette is inserted into the genome of a VZV vaccine strain, such as the Oka strain, using site-specific recombination techniques like Tn7-mediated transposition into a bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) clone of the viral genome. The recombinant VZV, upon reconstitution in permissive cells (e.g., MRC-5 fibroblasts), expresses the heterologous gene under the control of the HHV-6 regulatory elements during viral infection. This use has been demonstrated with the DsRed2 reporter gene, which was robustly expressed as confirmed by fluorescence microscopy and Western blotting. The application extends to the development of multivalent live attenuated vaccines, where the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 drives expression of protective antigens from other pathogens (e.g., mumps virus HN, influenza hemagglutinin) alongside VZV antigens. Because the HHV-6 promoter functions effectively in VZV-permissive cells and offers strong activity comparable to or exceeding HCMV MIEp in these contexts, it serves as a reliable and potent alternative for antigen expression in VZV vectors. This use capitalizes on the compatibility of betaherpesviral regulatory elements with alphaherpesviral replication machinery, enabling innovative vaccine designs with enhanced immunogenicity.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, the relative strength of the HHV-6 major immediate-early promoter (HHV-6 MIEp) was evaluated in comparison to the human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) major immediate-early promoter across a panel of mammalian cell lines. A 971-base pair fragment corresponding to nucleotides -983 to -13 upstream of the IE1 transcription start site in HHV-6B strain HST was amplified by PCR and cloned into the pGL3-basic luciferase reporter vector to generate pGL3-HHV6MIEp. Similarly, the HCMV MIE promoter was excised from pcDNA3.1(+) and inserted into pGL3-basic to create pGL3-CMV. These reporter plasmids were transfected into six different cell lines: MRC-5 (human lung fibroblasts), MeWo (human melanoma), U373 (human astrocytoma), and three T-cell lines—Molt-3, SupT1, and Jurkat. Co-transfection with pRL-TK, expressing Renilla luciferase under the HSV-TK promoter, was performed to normalize for transfection efficiency. Luciferase activity was measured 48 hours post-transfection using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System. Results showed that in MRC-5, MeWo, and U373 cells, the HHV-6 MIEp exhibited lower activity than the CMV promoter, with fold-induction values of approximately 15-, 10-, and 8-fold over baseline, respectively, compared to 25-, 20-, and 18-fold for CMV. In stark contrast, in the T-cell lines, HHV-6 MIEp activity was significantly higher: 45-fold in Molt-3, 50-fold in SupT1, and 60-fold in Jurkat, whereas CMV promoter activity was only 20-, 22-, and 25-fold, respectively. These data demonstrate that the HHV-6 MIE promoter possesses a T-cell-preferential activity profile, outperforming the standard CMV promoter in lymphoid cells while showing moderate activity in non-lymphoid lines. This cell-type-specific advantage underscores its potential utility in T-cell-focused applications.

### Example 2

This example investigates the role of intronic sequences from the HHV-6 IE1 gene in modulating promoter activity. Four variant constructs were generated by PCR amplification and cloning into pGL3-basic: (1) HHV-6 MIEp alone (no intron); (2) HHV-6 MIEp plus intron 1 (in1); (3) HHV-6 MIEp plus introns 1–2; (4) HHV-6 MIEp plus introns 1–3; and (5) HHV-6 MIEp plus introns 1–4. Each construct included the 971-bp promoter region fused to varying lengths of downstream genomic sequence encompassing the specified introns. These plasmids were transfected into the same six cell lines as in Example 1, and luciferase activity was measured and normalized as before. The results revealed that inclusion of intron 1 alone caused a dramatic increase in promoter activity across all cell types: in MRC-5, activity rose from 15-fold to 24-fold; in MeWo, from 10-fold to 19-fold; in Jurkat, from 60-fold to 95-fold. However, addition of intron 2 (i.e., introns 1–2) reduced activity below the level seen with intron 1 alone in every cell line tested. Further inclusion of introns 3 or 4 led to even greater suppression. For instance, in Jurkat cells, the introns 1–4 construct yielded only 40-fold induction, less than the original promoter without any intron. These findings indicate that intron 1 functions as a potent enhancer, but its effect is negated or reversed by the presence of downstream introns. The optimal configuration for maximal expression is therefore the HHV-6 MIE promoter coupled exclusively with intron 1 (HHV-6 MIEp-in1), a 1245-bp regulatory unit that combines strong basal promoter activity with intronic enhancement, particularly in T cells.

### Example 3

This example delineates the critical regions within the HHV-6 MIE promoter responsible for its activity through systematic deletion analysis and mutagenesis. A series of 5' deletion mutants of both HHV-6 MIEp and HHV-6 MIEp-in1 were constructed, removing segments from the upstream end in increments corresponding to putative regulatory motifs. Constructs included deletions up to positions -732, -552, -531, -381, -214, -165, and -102 relative to the IE1 start site. Additionally, a specific mutant lacking the NF-κB binding site (HHV-6MIEpΔNF-κBin1) was generated by precise deletion of the motif within intron 1-containing constructs. All variants were tested in luciferase assays across the six cell lines. Deletion of the region between -552 and -381 (construct d3) resulted in a sharp decline in activity compared to the -552 construct (d2), implicating this 171-bp segment as essential. Bioinformatic analysis confirmed the presence of NF-κB and AP-1 binding sites within this interval. The NF-κB site-deleted mutant exhibited a 70–85% reduction in activity in all cell lines, confirming its pivotal role. In contrast, deletion of AP-2 binding sites in the d1 construct (-732) had minimal impact, suggesting these sites are dispensable. Notably, even the truncated -381 construct retained significant activity in T cells, though less than the full-length promoter. These results map the core enhancer region to the R3-derived segment containing the NF-κB site and validate that this motif is non-redundant for high-level expression. The data provide a rational basis for future engineering of minimal or optimized promoter variants.

### Example 4

This example demonstrates the functionality of the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 cassette in a recombinant varicella-zoster virus (VZV) system. The full-length HHV-6 MIEp-in1 sequence (1245 bp) was cloned upstream of the DsRed2 reporter gene and the bovine growth hormone (BGH) polyadenylation signal in a pFastBac-derived plasmid. This expression cassette was then inserted into the bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) clone of the VZV Oka vaccine strain (vOka-BAC) at a neutral site using Tn7-mediated transposition, facilitated by co-expression of Tn7 transposase from helper plasmid pMON7124 in E. coli DH10B cells harboring vOka-BAC-Tn (which contains a mini-attTn7 landing site). Correct insertion was verified by restriction fragment length polymorphism (RFLP) analysis with BamHI, which showed a shift from a 7.8-kbp band in wild-type vOka-BAC to a 7.5-kbp band in the recombinant, and by Southern blotting using probes specific for HHV-6 MIEp and DsRed, which detected bands only in the recombinant BAC DNA. Infectious recombinant virus (HHV-6MIEpin1-DsRed-rvOka) was reconstituted by transfecting MRC-5 cells with the recombinant BAC DNA via nucleofection. Five days post-transfection, cytopathic effects (CPE) typical of VZV infection were observed, accompanied by both green fluorescence (from a GFP marker in the BAC backbone) and red fluorescence (from DsRed2 expression). Western blot analysis of infected cell lysates using anti-DsRed monoclonal antibody confirmed the presence of a 29-kDa DsRed2 protein exclusively in cells infected with the recombinant virus, while anti-VZV gB antibody detected the late glycoprotein B in both control and recombinant infections, verifying productive viral replication. These results conclusively show that the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 functions effectively in the context of a live, replicating VZV vector to drive sustained expression of a heterologous gene, validating its utility for vaccine and vector applications.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention has broad industrial applicability across multiple sectors of biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, and medical research. In the field of basic and applied immunology, the HHV-6 MIE promoter with intron 1 enhancer provides a superior tool for high-efficiency gene expression in T lymphocytes, enabling more sensitive reporter assays, robust overexpression studies, and improved generation of engineered T-cell lines for functional genomics. In the development of cell-based therapies, particularly chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapies, the invention offers a promoter system that can drive stronger and more consistent expression of CAR constructs in primary human T cells, potentially enhancing therapeutic potency and persistence. In vaccine development, the demonstrated compatibility of HHV-6 MIEp-in1 with recombinant varicella-zoster virus platforms allows for the creation of next-generation multivalent live attenuated vaccines that co-express antigens from multiple pathogens—such as mumps, measles, or respiratory viruses—with high immunogenicity due to strong antigen expression. The promoter’s responsiveness to NF-κB signaling also makes it suitable for designing conditionally active vectors that respond to inflammatory cues in the tumor microenvironment or sites of infection. In protein production, the system can be employed in stable cell lines or transient expression systems to yield higher quantities of recombinant proteins, especially when T-cell lines or hybridomas are used as production hosts. Furthermore, the modular nature of the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 cassette facilitates its integration into diverse vector backbones, including plasmids, lentiviruses, adenoviruses, and other herpesvirus vectors, expanding its utility across gene delivery platforms. Given the well-documented safety profile of VZV-based vaccines and the human origin of HHV-6 sequences, regulatory pathways for clinical translation are likely to be favorable. Overall, the invention addresses a critical gap in gene expression technology and provides a versatile, high-performance solution with immediate and far-reaching applications in research, diagnostics, therapeutics, and preventive medicine.
