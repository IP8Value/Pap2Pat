# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for extending lifespan and improving healthspan in animals, particularly through the modulation of mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestine. More specifically, the invention provides methods for activating mitochondrial uncoupling, either genetically or pharmacologically, to achieve beneficial effects including extended lifespan, improved gut homeostasis, reduced reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and suppression of age-related intestinal dysfunction. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising mitochondrial uncoupling agents and methods for their administration to treat or prevent age-related decline in intestinal barrier function, stem cell overproliferation, and associated pathologies. The discovery that loss of the circadian clock repressor Period (Per) in the intestine leads to constitutive upregulation of the mitochondrial uncoupling protein UCP4C, which is both necessary and sufficient for lifespan extension, forms the basis of this invention. This finding reveals a novel pathway for longevity intervention that operates independently of canonical dietary restriction and insulin-like signaling mechanisms, opening new avenues for therapeutic development targeting mitochondrial metabolism and intestinal health.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Aging is a complex biological process characterized by the progressive decline of physiological functions and increased susceptibility to disease and death. Extensive research has focused on identifying genetic and environmental factors that influence lifespan and healthspan, with particular attention to metabolic regulation and cellular homeostasis. The circadian clock, an endogenous timekeeping system that synchronizes physiological processes to the 24-hour day-night cycle, has emerged as a critical regulator of metabolism, aging, and longevity. In Drosophila melanogaster, the circadian clock consists of transcriptional feedback loops involving activators such as Clock and Cycle, and repressors including Period (Per) and Timeless (Tim). Disruption of circadian rhythms has been associated with various age-related pathologies, and organisms typically lose circadian rhythmicity with advancing age, suggesting a potential link between circadian regulation and aging processes.

Previous studies investigating the relationship between circadian clock components and lifespan have yielded conflicting results. Disruption of circadian transcriptional activators like Clock or Cycle has consistently been shown to cause metabolic dysfunction and shortened lifespan in Drosophila. However, the effects of disrupting circadian repressors such as Per and Tim on longevity have been more controversial. While some studies reported detrimental effects of Per loss on lifespan, others suggested potential beneficial metabolic effects, including increased metabolic rate and reduced fat storage. These contradictory findings highlight the complexity of circadian regulation in aging and suggest that the impact of circadian disruption on longevity may depend on specific genetic contexts, environmental conditions, and tissue-specific effects.

Mitochondrial function plays a central role in aging and longevity, with mitochondrial uncoupling emerging as a potential mechanism for lifespan extension. Mitochondrial uncoupling involves the dissipation of the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane without ATP synthesis, leading to increased respiration and heat production. This process can reduce the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which are known to contribute to cellular damage and aging. In mammals, mitochondrial uncoupling proteins (UCPs) mediate this process, with UCP1 being well-characterized in brown adipose tissue for thermogenesis. Drosophila possesses homologous UCP proteins, including UCP4A, UCP4B, and UCP4C, which may serve similar functions in regulating mitochondrial metabolism and ROS production.

The intestine has recently been recognized as a critical organ in aging and longevity regulation. Age-related decline in intestinal barrier function, characterized by increased permeability and inflammation, is a major predictor of mortality in Drosophila. This intestinal dysfunction is often accompanied by stem cell overproliferation and tissue dysplasia, which can be driven by elevated ROS levels. Intestinal stem cells (ISCs) and their progeny, enteroblasts (EBs), are particularly sensitive to oxidative stress, and their dysregulation contributes significantly to age-related intestinal pathology. Maintaining intestinal homeostasis through the regulation of ROS production and stem cell proliferation represents a promising strategy for extending healthspan and lifespan.

Pharmacological approaches to modulating mitochondrial function have been explored for their potential anti-aging effects. Compounds such as 2,4-dinitrophenol (2,4-DNP) and beta-hydroxytoluene (BHT) can induce mitochondrial uncoupling, but their use has been limited by safety concerns at high doses. However, recent evidence suggests that low-dose administration of these compounds may provide beneficial metabolic effects without severe side effects. The identification of specific molecular pathways and tissue targets for mitochondrial uncoupling could enable the development of safer and more effective therapeutic interventions for age-related decline.

Despite significant advances in understanding the roles of circadian regulation, mitochondrial function, and intestinal homeostasis in aging, the precise mechanisms linking these processes remain unclear. The discovery that loss of the circadian repressor Per specifically in the intestine leads to constitutive upregulation of UCP4C, resulting in mitochondrial uncoupling, reduced ROS production, and extended lifespan, represents a novel and unexpected finding that addresses this gap in knowledge. This invention provides the first demonstration that targeted activation of mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestine can extend lifespan independently of canonical longevity pathways, offering new opportunities for therapeutic intervention in age-related diseases and conditions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

As used herein, the term "mitochondrial uncoupling" refers to the process by which the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane is dissipated without the concomitant synthesis of ATP, leading to increased oxygen consumption and heat production. This process can be mediated by mitochondrial uncoupling proteins (UCPs) or induced by chemical uncoupling agents such as 2,4-dinitrophenol (2,4-DNP).

The term "UCP4C" refers to the Drosophila melanogaster mitochondrial uncoupling protein 4C, which is encoded by the Ucp4C gene and functions to mediate mitochondrial uncoupling in intestinal tissues. UCP4C is distinct from other UCP family members such as UCP4A and UCP4B, and exhibits specific regulatory properties and tissue expression patterns.

"Lifespan extension" as used herein refers to the increase in the median or maximum survival time of an organism compared to control animals under identical experimental conditions. Lifespan extension may be measured as a percentage increase in median lifespan or as a statistically significant shift in survival curves.

"Gut homeostasis" encompasses the maintenance of normal intestinal structure and function, including proper barrier integrity, regulated stem cell proliferation, appropriate differentiation of intestinal epithelial cells, and balanced immune responses. Disruption of gut homeostasis is characterized by increased intestinal permeability, stem cell overproliferation, tissue dysplasia, and chronic inflammation.

"Intestinal stem cells (ISCs)" are undifferentiated cells located in the intestinal epithelium that have the capacity for self-renewal and differentiation into various intestinal cell types, including enterocytes and enteroendocrine cells. ISCs play a crucial role in maintaining intestinal tissue integrity and responding to injury or stress.

"Enteroblasts (EBs)" are immediate progeny of intestinal stem cells that represent an intermediate stage in the differentiation pathway toward mature intestinal epithelial cells. EBs retain some proliferative capacity but are committed to differentiation along specific lineages.

"Reactive oxygen species (ROS)" refers to chemically reactive molecules containing oxygen, including superoxide anion, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radical. ROS are produced as natural byproducts of mitochondrial metabolism and can cause cellular damage when present in excessive amounts, contributing to aging and various pathological conditions.

"Therapeutically effective amount" means an amount of a compound or composition that produces a desired therapeutic effect in a subject without causing unacceptable levels of toxicity or adverse effects. The therapeutically effective amount may vary depending on the specific compound, route of administration, subject characteristics, and intended therapeutic outcome.

"Pharmaceutically acceptable carrier" refers to any substance that can be used to deliver a therapeutic agent to its target site in the body without causing significant adverse effects. Carriers may include excipients, diluents, solubilizers, stabilizers, and other formulation components that enhance the stability, bioavailability, or delivery of the active ingredient.

"Circadian clock repressor" refers to proteins that negatively regulate the circadian transcriptional feedback loop, including Period (Per) and Timeless (Tim) in Drosophila. These proteins inhibit the activity of circadian transcriptional activators such as Clock and Cycle, thereby controlling the rhythmic expression of clock-controlled genes.

"Age-related intestinal dysfunction" encompasses the spectrum of pathological changes that occur in the intestine during aging, including increased permeability, chronic inflammation, stem cell dysregulation, tissue dysplasia, and impaired barrier function. These changes contribute to systemic inflammation and increased mortality in aging organisms.

### Activation of Mitochondrial Uncoupling has Beneficial Effects

The present invention demonstrates that activation of mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestine confers multiple beneficial effects that contribute to extended lifespan and improved healthspan. Mitochondrial uncoupling, characterized by the dissipation of the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane without ATP synthesis, leads to increased oxygen consumption and heat production while simultaneously reducing the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS). This reduction in ROS production is particularly significant in the context of aging, as excessive ROS accumulation contributes to cellular damage, stem cell dysregulation, and tissue dysfunction.

In Drosophila melanogaster, the activation of mitochondrial uncoupling specifically in the intestine results in several key physiological benefits. First, it maintains intestinal barrier integrity by preventing age-related increases in intestinal permeability. This preservation of barrier function is critical for preventing the translocation of harmful substances and microorganisms from the gut lumen into the systemic circulation, which can trigger chronic inflammation and contribute to systemic aging. Second, mitochondrial uncoupling suppresses age-related stem cell overproliferation in the intestine. Intestinal stem cells (ISCs) and their progeny, enteroblasts (EBs), are particularly sensitive to oxidative stress, and their dysregulated proliferation contributes to tissue dysplasia and functional decline. By reducing ROS production, mitochondrial uncoupling helps maintain appropriate stem cell quiescence and prevents excessive proliferation.

Third, mitochondrial uncoupling improves overall metabolic health by creating a state of increased energy expenditure without compromising essential physiological functions. Animals with activated mitochondrial uncoupling exhibit higher metabolic rates, increased respiration, and enhanced cold tolerance, all of which contribute to improved physiological resilience. Importantly, these metabolic benefits are achieved without the negative consequences typically associated with caloric restriction or other extreme metabolic interventions.

The beneficial effects of mitochondrial uncoupling extend beyond normal aging processes to include protection against pathological conditions such as tumor formation. In models of genetically induced intestinal tumors, activation of mitochondrial uncoupling through pharmacological agents significantly delays tumor progression and extends survival. This anti-tumor effect is likely mediated through the combined actions of reduced ROS production, suppressed stem cell proliferation, and improved tissue homeostasis.

The tissue-specific nature of these benefits is particularly noteworthy. The invention demonstrates that activation of mitochondrial uncoupling specifically in the intestine, rather than systemically, is sufficient to achieve significant lifespan extension and healthspan improvement. This finding suggests that the intestine serves as a critical regulatory hub for systemic aging processes, and that targeted interventions in this tissue can have far-reaching effects on overall organismal health.

Moreover, the beneficial effects of mitochondrial uncoupling are independent of canonical longevity pathways such as dietary restriction and insulin-like signaling. This independence provides a novel therapeutic approach that can be combined with existing interventions to achieve additive or synergistic benefits. The discovery that mitochondrial uncoupling operates through a distinct mechanism opens new possibilities for developing targeted therapies that address specific aspects of aging without interfering with other essential physiological processes.

The magnitude of these beneficial effects is substantial. In experimental models, activation of mitochondrial uncoupling through genetic manipulation of UCP4C expression or pharmacological administration of uncoupling agents results in lifespan extensions of 15-20% compared to control animals. This level of extension is comparable to or exceeds that achieved by many other established longevity interventions, highlighting the therapeutic potential of this approach.

### Methods and Compositions for Activating Mitochondrial Uncoupling

The present invention provides multiple methods and compositions for activating mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestine to achieve beneficial effects on lifespan and healthspan. These approaches can be broadly categorized into genetic methods, pharmacological methods, and combination strategies that leverage both approaches.

Genetic methods for activating mitochondrial uncoupling focus on the targeted manipulation of UCP4C expression specifically in intestinal tissues. This can be achieved through the use of tissue-specific promoters and gene expression systems that allow precise control over UCP4C expression levels and timing. For example, the use of intestinal-specific GAL4 drivers such as NP3084-GAL4 or esg-GAL4 enables the selective overexpression of UCP4C in intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts. This targeted approach ensures that the beneficial effects of mitochondrial uncoupling are achieved while minimizing potential systemic effects that might result from ubiquitous expression.

The genetic manipulation can be implemented using various vector systems and delivery methods. In Drosophila models, the UAS-GAL4 system provides a powerful tool for tissue-specific gene expression, allowing researchers to control both the spatial and temporal aspects of UCP4C expression. For potential applications in mammalian systems, viral vectors such as adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) or lentiviruses can be engineered to carry UCP4C expression cassettes under the control of intestinal-specific promoters. These vectors can be delivered directly to the intestinal tissue through oral administration, enema, or other localized delivery methods.

Pharmacological methods for activating mitochondrial uncoupling involve the administration of compounds that either directly induce mitochondrial uncoupling or upregulate the expression and activity of endogenous uncoupling proteins. The invention identifies several classes of compounds that are effective for this purpose. Proton ionophores such as 2,4-dinitrophenol (2,4-DNP) directly dissipate the proton gradient across the mitochondrial membrane, leading to immediate uncoupling effects. Fatty acid derivatives such as stearic acid can activate endogenous UCP proteins through post-translational mechanisms, providing a more physiological approach to uncoupling activation.

Antioxidant compounds such as beta-hydroxytoluene (BHT) represent another class of effective uncoupling agents. These compounds not only induce mitochondrial uncoupling but also provide additional protective effects through their antioxidant properties. The dual action of these compounds makes them particularly attractive for therapeutic applications, as they address both the energetic and oxidative stress aspects of aging.

The invention also encompasses methods for optimizing the dosing and administration of these compounds to maximize therapeutic benefits while minimizing potential adverse effects. Low-dose regimens have been found to be particularly effective, providing sufficient uncoupling activity to achieve beneficial effects without causing significant toxicity. The timing of administration can also be optimized based on circadian rhythms and feeding patterns to enhance efficacy and reduce side effects.

Combination strategies that integrate both genetic and pharmacological approaches offer additional advantages. For example, animals with genetically enhanced UCP4C expression may require lower doses of pharmacological uncoupling agents to achieve optimal effects, reducing the risk of adverse reactions. Conversely, pharmacological agents can be used to complement genetic interventions, providing additional layers of control and flexibility in treatment protocols.

The invention further includes methods for monitoring and adjusting the level of mitochondrial uncoupling to ensure optimal therapeutic outcomes. Biomarkers such as oxygen consumption rates, mitochondrial membrane potential, and ROS production levels can be used to assess the effectiveness of uncoupling activation and guide dose adjustments. Non-invasive imaging techniques and biochemical assays can be employed to monitor these parameters in real-time, allowing for personalized treatment approaches.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

### Loss of the Repressive Arm of the Transcriptional Circadian Clock Extended Lifespan Independent of Dietary Protein Restriction and Reduced Insulin-like Signaling

The experiments described in this example demonstrate that loss of the circadian clock repressor Period (Per) extends lifespan in male Drosophila melanogaster through mechanisms independent of canonical dietary restriction and insulin-like signaling pathways. Male flies carrying the per01 null mutation exhibited a significant 15-20% increase in median lifespan compared to wild-type controls across multiple dietary conditions, including standard, low-protein, and high-protein diets. This lifespan extension was specific to males and was not observed in female per01 mutants under the same experimental conditions.

To confirm that the observed lifespan extension was due specifically to the loss of Per protein, rescue experiments were conducted using the UAS-GAL4 system to restore Per expression in the per01 null background. Expression of either of two independent period transgenes using either the timeless-GAL4 driver or the ubiquitin-GAL4 driver successfully reverted the per01 mutant lifespan to that of control animals, confirming that the absence of Per protein is responsible for the longevity phenotype.

The independence of this lifespan extension from dietary restriction mechanisms was demonstrated through comprehensive dietary analysis. While dietary restriction typically extends lifespan by reducing protein intake, per01 mutants actually exhibited hyperphagia, consuming more food than control animals. Furthermore, when subjected to varying concentrations of yeast extract (0.01%, 0.5%, 3%, and 10%), per01 mutants showed similar responses to dietary restriction as control animals, with both genotypes exhibiting maximal lifespan extension at the 0.5% yeast concentration. Crucially, per01 mutants maintained their lifespan advantage over controls across all dietary conditions except the highest yeast concentration (10%), where both genotypes experienced reduced lifespan due to the detrimental effects of excessive protein intake.

To investigate the relationship between per01-mediated lifespan extension and insulin-like signaling (ILS), genetic manipulations were performed to test whether inhibition of ILS contributes to the longevity phenotype. Partial genetic ablation of insulin-producing cells in the fly brain using the proapoptotic gene reaper extended the lifespans of both control animals and per01 mutants to a similar magnitude, indicating that the per01 longevity phenotype is independent of ILS inhibition. Similarly, tests of canonical longevity hallmarks revealed that per01 males did not exhibit the expected reductions in insulin signaling (measured by decreased phosphorylation of Akt protein) or TOR signaling (measured by decreased phosphorylation of S6K) that are typically associated with long-lived mutants. In fact, at certain points in the circadian cycle, per01 mutants exhibited the opposite phenotype, with increased rather than decreased signaling activity.

Additional experiments confirmed that the lifespan extension observed in per01 mutants is not dependent on TOR signaling inhibition. Ubiquitous suppression of TORC1 signaling in adulthood through inducible overexpression of a dominant-negative form of the downstream kinase S6K extended the lifespans of both control animals and per01 mutants to a similar extent, demonstrating that the per01 longevity mechanism operates independently of TORC1 inhibition.

These findings collectively establish that the lifespan extension observed in per01 mutants represents a novel longevity pathway that functions independently of the well-established dietary restriction and insulin-like signaling mechanisms. This discovery opens new avenues for understanding the complex relationships between circadian regulation, metabolism, and aging, and provides a foundation for developing targeted interventions that operate through distinct molecular pathways.

### Period Mutants Exhibited a High Metabolic Rate Due to Mitochondrial Uncoupling

This example demonstrates that per01 mutant flies exhibit a highly active metabolic state characterized by increased mitochondrial uncoupling, which underlies their extended lifespan phenotype. Comprehensive metabolic characterization revealed that per01 mutants display several hallmarks of elevated metabolic activity, including hyperphagia (increased feeding rate), decreased starvation resistance, reduced baseline lipid storage, and accelerated lipid utilization during starvation compared to control animals.

Direct measurement of metabolic rate through CO2 production analysis confirmed that per01 mutants exhibit significantly higher respiration rates throughout the circadian cycle compared to controls. This elevated metabolic output was not attributable to increased mitochondrial ATP synthesis, as treatment with mitochondrial complex inhibitors (rotenone for complex I and oligomycin for complex V) reduced CO2 production in both genotypes to similar degrees, yet per01 mutants still maintained higher respiration rates than controls.

The evidence for mitochondrial uncoupling in per01 mutants was further strengthened through in vitro mitochondrial function assays. Purified mitochondria from per01 mutants exhibited increased oxygen consumption when initiated through electron transport chain complexes I, II, and IV, without corresponding increases in mitochondrial electron transport chain protein abundance or enzymatic activity. Two critical hallmarks of mitochondrial uncoupling were observed: increased leak respiration (higher oxygen consumption after oligomycin treatment) and decreased mitochondrial membrane potential (disrupted proton gradient) as measured by JC-1 staining.

Pharmacological validation of the mitochondrial uncoupling hypothesis was achieved through treatment with known uncoupling compounds. Administration of 2,4-dinitrophenol (2,4-DNP) or stearic acid to control flies increased their respiration rates to levels comparable to those of per01 mutants, but did not further increase respiration in per01 mutants, suggesting that these mutants are already operating at maximal viable uncoupling levels. Additionally, per01 mutants exhibited faster recovery from cold shock compared to controls, consistent with increased heat production through mitochondrial uncoupling.

The specificity of the mitochondrial uncoupling phenotype to per01 mutants was confirmed through rescue experiments. Ubiquitous overexpression of Per protein during adulthood in per01 mutants reverted their elevated respiration rates back to control levels, demonstrating that the loss of Per is directly responsible for the mitochondrial uncoupling phenotype.

These findings establish that per01 mutants exhibit a genuine state of mitochondrial uncoupling that accounts for their elevated metabolic rate and contributes to their extended lifespan. The demonstration that this uncoupling occurs through classic mitochondrial UCP mechanisms, as evidenced by palmitate stimulation and GTP inhibition of respiration, provides a clear molecular basis for the observed metabolic phenotype.

### Endogenous Uncoupling Protein UCP4C was Necessary for Period Mutant Lifespan Extension and Sufficient to Extend Wild-Type Lifespan

This example provides definitive evidence that the mitochondrial uncoupling protein UCP4C is both necessary for the lifespan extension observed in per01 mutants and sufficient to extend lifespan in wild-type animals. Gene expression analysis revealed that Ucp4B and Ucp4C transcripts are normally circadian-regulated in control flies, with peak expression occurring during the night phase. In contrast, per01 mutants exhibited constitutively high expression of both Ucp4B and Ucp4C throughout the circadian cycle, suggesting that Per normally represses the expression of these uncoupling proteins during the day phase.

Genetic disruption of Ucp4B/C expression in per01 mutants using a piggyBac transposon insertion in the intergenic region between these closely linked genes completely abolished the mitochondrial uncoupling phenotype and reverted lifespan to control levels. Specifically, the disruption eliminated the increased leak respiration, restored normal mitochondrial membrane potential, normalized cold shock recovery rates, and eliminated the lifespan extension observed in per01 mutants. Orthogonal validation using RNAi-mediated knockdown of UCP4B or UCP4C in adulthood throughout the whole body similarly reverted per01 lifespan to control levels, while knockdown of UCP4A had no effect, confirming the specific requirement for UCP4B/C in mediating the per01 longevity phenotype.

The sufficiency of UCP4C for lifespan extension was demonstrated through conditional overexpression experiments using the GeneSwitch system. Ubiquitous overexpression of UCP4C during adulthood in otherwise wild-type flies recapitulated the mitochondrial uncoupling phenotype observed in per01 mutants, including increased leak respiration, decreased mitochondrial membrane potential, elevated CO2 production, and faster cold shock recovery. Most importantly, this UCP4C overexpression extended the lifespan of wild-type flies to the same extent as observed in per01 mutants (15-20% increase in median lifespan), without providing additional lifespan extension in per01 mutants, indicating that UCP4C functions in the same pathway as Per to mediate longevity.

These findings establish UCP4C as the critical molecular mediator linking Per loss to mitochondrial uncoupling and lifespan extension. The demonstration that UCP4C overexpression alone is sufficient to extend lifespan in wild-type animals provides strong evidence for the therapeutic potential of targeting this pathway for longevity intervention.

### Lifespan Extension was Mediated by Loss of Per Specifically in the Intestine

This example demonstrates that the lifespan extension observed in per01 mutants is mediated specifically by the loss of Per function in the intestinal tissue, rather than in other organs or the central nervous system. Tissue-specific rescue experiments using the UAS-GAL4 system revealed that ubiquitous expression of Per protein during adulthood in per01 mutants successfully reverted their lifespan to control levels, confirming that the absence of Per is responsible for the longevity phenotype. However, neuronal expression of Per using the elav-GeneSwitch driver did not affect the extended lifespan of per01 mutants, indicating that the central circadian clock in the brain is not involved in mediating this longevity effect.

In contrast, intestinal expression of Per using either the NP3084-GAL4 driver (targeting the whole intestine) or the esg-GAL4 driver (specifically targeting intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts) completely reverted the per01 lifespan extension to control levels. This finding was further confirmed using developmental and adult-specific rescue paradigms, demonstrating that Per function in the intestine is required throughout life to maintain normal lifespan regulation.

Direct evidence for the sufficiency of intestinal Per loss in mediating lifespan extension was obtained through CRISPR-mediated gene disruption experiments. Ubiquitous deletion of per using the daughterless GeneSwitch driver extended lifespan in otherwise wild-type flies but provided no additional extension in per01 mutants, confirming that per loss is the causative factor. More importantly, tissue-specific CRISPR-mediated disruption of per specifically in the whole intestine or in intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts during either development or adulthood was sufficient to extend lifespan to the same extent as observed in per01 mutants.

Control experiments ruled out potential confounding factors. CRISPR-mediated disruption of an unrelated gene (acp98AB) using the same tissue-specific drivers did not affect lifespan, confirming that the observed effects are specific to per disruption. Additionally, RU486 feeding alone had no influence on lifespan in control or per01 mutants lacking UAS transgenes, confirming that the GeneSwitch system itself does not contribute to the longevity phenotype. Molecular validation confirmed that CRISPR-targeted disruption of per in intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts resulted in >90% reduction in per transcript levels in the intestine after 30 days of induction.

These findings establish the intestine as the critical tissue mediating the lifespan extension observed in per01 mutants. The demonstration that loss of Per specifically in intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts is sufficient to extend lifespan highlights the importance of these progenitor cell populations in regulating systemic aging processes and provides a precise cellular target for therapeutic intervention.
