# DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to a novel class of microelectrode arrays for bidirectional neural interfacing, specifically engineered using three-dimensionally nanostructured boron-doped diamond (BDD) electrodes. These microelectrode arrays are designed to overcome the longstanding limitations associated with conventional neural electrode materials, particularly those concerning high impedance, low charge injection capacity, and insufficient signal-to-noise ratio, which collectively impede sensitive neural recording and efficient electrical stimulation. The invention provides a robust, biocompatible, and electrochemically stable platform that enables high-fidelity detection of low-amplitude neural signals and safe delivery of electrical stimuli at the cellular level, thereby significantly advancing the capabilities of neural prostheses, brain-machine interfaces, and fundamental neuroscientific research tools.

Neural interfaces rely on the intimate electrical coupling between an electrode and excitable neural tissue to either record endogenous bioelectric activity or deliver exogenous electrical stimulation. The performance of such interfaces is critically dependent on the physicochemical properties of the electrode material, especially when miniaturized to achieve high spatial resolution through increased electrode density. As electrode dimensions are reduced to micrometer or even sub-micrometer scales, the geometric surface area available for charge transfer diminishes, leading to a concomitant increase in interfacial impedance and a reduction in double-layer capacitance. This results in elevated thermal and 1/f noise, which obscures small-amplitude neural signals such as action potentials from individual neurons or local field potentials from neural ensembles. Simultaneously, the charge injection capacity—the maximum amount of charge that can be safely delivered per phase without inducing irreversible Faradaic reactions or water electrolysis—decreases, limiting the efficacy of electrical stimulation. Traditional electrode materials like platinum, iridium oxide, or titanium nitride have been optimized through various nanostructuring approaches to mitigate these issues, but they often suffer from long-term instability, chemical degradation, or mechanical fragility, particularly in chronic implantation scenarios.

Boron-doped diamond has emerged as a promising alternative due to its exceptional combination of biocompatibility, chemical inertness, mechanical hardness, and wide electrochemical potential window in aqueous environments. However, conventional planar BDD electrodes exhibit inherently low double-layer capacitance and high impedance, rendering them suboptimal for sensitive neural recording. To address this fundamental limitation, the present invention introduces a three-dimensional nanostructured architecture wherein vertically aligned carbon nanotubes (VACNTs) serve as a conductive scaffold that is fully encapsulated between two layers of boron-doped diamond. This innovative design dramatically increases the effective electrochemical surface area while maintaining the superior bulk properties of diamond. The VACNT interlayer acts as a highly conductive current collector, ensuring efficient electron transport throughout the porous structure, while the outer BDD layers provide a chemically stable, biocompatible interface that prevents any direct exposure of the carbon nanotubes to the biological milieu. This configuration not only enhances the capacitive behavior of the electrode but also preserves the wide potential window characteristic of BDD, thereby enabling safe and efficient charge injection at high current densities.

The fabrication process of the inventive microelectrode arrays begins with a fused silica substrate to eliminate electrical cross-talk between adjacent electrodes. Detonation nanodiamond particles are first deposited onto the substrate to serve as nucleation sites for subsequent diamond growth. In the case of conventional BDD microelectrodes, a photolithographic process defines aluminum hard masks in the desired electrode pattern, followed by reactive ion etching to remove exposed diamond nanoparticles. Boron-doped diamond is then grown via microwave plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (MPECVD) to form planar electrodes of approximately 500 nm thickness. For the 3D-nanostructured variant, after the initial BDD layer is grown, a thin nickel catalyst layer is selectively deposited only at the intended electrode locations. This catalyst facilitates the growth of vertically aligned carbon nanotubes via plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition, resulting in a forest-like structure approximately 3 micrometers in height. The VACNTs are then thoroughly cleaned to remove residual catalyst particles and subsequently coated with a dense layer of nanodiamond seeds via electrostatic grafting. A second, thinner BDD layer (approximately 50 nm) is then grown over the entire structure, fully embedding the VACNT scaffold within a continuous diamond matrix. Both electrode types are then individually contacted with chromium/gold metal tracks using a lift-off process, and a thick SU-8 photoresist layer is applied to insulate the metal traces, leaving only the 20-micrometer-diameter electrode sites exposed to the electrolyte.

Electrochemical characterization of the resulting microelectrodes demonstrates a remarkable improvement in performance for the 3D-nanostructured BDD variant. Cyclic voltammetry measurements in lithium perchlorate reveal a double-layer capacitance of approximately 3 millifarads per square centimeter, representing a 43-fold increase compared to conventional BDD electrodes (70 microfarads per square centimeter). This translates into a charge storage capacity of 10 millicoulombs per square centimeter within a 3.3-volt potential window, substantially exceeding that of titanium nitride and approaching values reported for conducting polymers, albeit with far greater chemical stability. Impedance spectroscopy confirms a 40-fold reduction in impedance modulus at 1 kHz, with median values of approximately 50 kiloohms for the 20-micrometer-diameter 3D-nanostructured electrodes—comparable to state-of-the-art neural electrode materials. Critically, this low impedance directly correlates with a significantly reduced intrinsic noise level: the median noise for 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrodes is measured at 3.1 microvolts, markedly lower than the 10.7 microvolts observed for conventional BDD and even surpassing the performance of standard platinum electrodes (6.9 microvolts), though slightly higher than friable black platinum (2.0 microvolts). Importantly, the mechanical robustness of the 3D-nanostructured BDD electrodes is exceptional; repeated cleaning with standard laboratory detergents and soft brushes does not degrade their electrochemical performance, confirming the structural integrity of the encapsulated VACNT scaffold.

Biological evaluation further validates the utility of the inventive material. Primary cultures of embryonic hippocampal and spinal cord neurons are successfully established on both conventional and 3D-nanostructured BDD substrates without the need for additional extracellular matrix coatings, demonstrating inherent biocompatibility. Neurons adhere, survive, and extend neurites equally well on both surfaces, expressing the neuron-specific marker beta-tubulin III, indicating that the nanostructured topography does not adversely affect neuronal health or morphology. This is particularly significant given that the VACNTs are completely encapsulated, eliminating potential cytotoxicity concerns associated with free carbon nanotubes. Functional testing in two distinct neural preparations—acute embryonic mouse hindbrain-spinal cord explants and long-term hippocampal cell cultures—confirms the superior recording and stimulation capabilities of the 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrodes. In the immature spinal cord preparation, which generates low-amplitude spikes (10–20 microvolts) due to high membrane impedance, only the 3D-nanostructured electrodes reliably detect both local field potentials and individual action potentials propagating along the rostrocaudal axis. Electrical stimulation through these electrodes effectively triggers rhythmic bursting activity, demonstrating bidirectional functionality. Similarly, in mature hippocampal cultures, the electrodes capture spontaneous and evoked multiunit spiking activity with high fidelity, with active recording sites correlating spatially with regions of dense neuronal coverage.

The invention thus provides a comprehensive solution to the key challenges in neural interface engineering: it achieves low impedance and high signal-to-noise ratio through nanostructured surface area enhancement, maintains high charge injection capacity via the wide potential window of diamond, ensures long-term stability through chemical inertness and mechanical robustness, and supports healthy neuronal integration through biocompatible surface chemistry. The fully encapsulated VACNT-BDD architecture represents a critical advancement over prior attempts to combine carbon nanomaterials with diamond, as it eliminates the risk of nanotube delamination or release into surrounding tissue. This makes the technology particularly suitable for chronic implantation applications in neural prostheses for sensory restoration (e.g., retinal or cochlear implants), motor rehabilitation (e.g., cortical or spinal cord stimulators), and closed-loop brain-machine interfaces. Moreover, the compatibility of the fabrication process with standard microfabrication techniques enables scalable production of high-density arrays tailored to specific anatomical targets, paving the way for next-generation neurotechnologies that demand both high spatial resolution and long-term reliability. The present invention therefore constitutes a significant leap forward in the field of neural engineering, offering a versatile and high-performance platform that bridges the gap between the demanding electrochemical requirements of neural interfacing and the stringent biocompatibility standards of clinical translation.
