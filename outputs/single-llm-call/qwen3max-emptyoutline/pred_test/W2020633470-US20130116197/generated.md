# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmic therapeutics, specifically to novel protein-based inhibitors of vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) for the treatment of ocular diseases characterized by pathological angiogenesis and vascular hyperpermeability. More particularly, the invention provides a class of engineered binding proteins known as Designed Ankyrin Repeat Proteins (DARPins) that exhibit high affinity, specificity, and functional antagonism against VEGF-A, including its predominant splice variants VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121. These DARPins are capable of potently inhibiting VEGF-A-mediated signaling through VEGF receptor 2 (VEGFR-2), thereby suppressing downstream biological processes such as endothelial cell proliferation, migration, sprouting, and vascular leakage. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these anti-VEGF-A DARPins formulated for both intravitreal and topical ocular administration, offering significant advantages over existing anti-VEGF therapies in terms of potency, stability, ease of formulation, and potential for non-invasive delivery. The disclosed DARPins are particularly suited for the treatment of posterior segment ocular disorders such as wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD), diabetic macular edema (DME), and retinal vein occlusion, as well as anterior segment conditions involving corneal neovascularization. The robust biophysical properties of DARPins, including high thermal stability, solubility, and resistance to aggregation, enable the development of high-concentration formulations suitable for extended intraocular residence time or effective transcorneal penetration following topical application. Thus, the invention represents a next-generation platform for VEGF inhibition in ophthalmology, addressing critical limitations of current antibody-based therapeutics, including frequent intravitreal injection requirements, limited ocular bioavailability, and complex manufacturing logistics.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) is a master regulator of physiological and pathological angiogenesis and vascular permeability. In the context of ocular disease, aberrant overexpression of VEGF-A drives the progression of several sight-threatening conditions, most notably wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD), diabetic macular edema (DME), and proliferative diabetic retinopathy. In wet AMD, choroidal neovascularization—characterized by the growth of new, leaky blood vessels from the choroid into the subretinal space—leads to fluid accumulation, hemorrhage, and ultimately irreversible photoreceptor damage and central vision loss. Similarly, in DME, chronic hyperglycemia induces VEGF-A upregulation in the retina, resulting in breakdown of the blood-retinal barrier, increased vascular permeability, and macular edema. Given the central role of VEGF-A in these pathologies, therapeutic strategies aimed at neutralizing its activity have become the cornerstone of modern ophthalmic care.

Currently, the standard of care for VEGF-driven retinal diseases involves intravitreal injection of anti-VEGF agents such as ranibizumab (a humanized Fab fragment derived from the monoclonal antibody bevacizumab) and aflibercept (a recombinant fusion protein comprising VEGF-binding domains from VEGFR-1 and VEGFR-2 fused to an Fc fragment). Pegaptanib, a PEGylated RNA aptamer targeting the heparin-binding domain of VEGF-A165, was the first approved anti-VEGF therapy for wet AMD but has largely been superseded due to its narrower spectrum of activity and inferior efficacy. While these agents have demonstrated significant clinical benefits, their therapeutic utility is constrained by several key limitations. Foremost among these is the requirement for frequent, repeated intravitreal injections—typically administered monthly or every two months—to maintain therapeutic intraocular concentrations. This regimen imposes a substantial burden on patients, increases cumulative risk of serious ocular complications such as endophthalmitis, retinal detachment, and intraocular inflammation, and contributes to high healthcare costs associated with procedural visits and monitoring. Moreover, the pharmacokinetic profiles of current anti-VEGF drugs are suboptimal; despite high initial intraocular concentrations, rapid clearance necessitates frequent re-dosing to sustain efficacy.

An alternative and highly desirable approach to overcome these limitations is the development of therapeutics amenable to topical administration via eye drops. Topical delivery would eliminate the risks associated with intraocular injection, improve patient compliance, and reduce treatment costs. However, the successful topical delivery of biologics to the posterior segment of the eye remains a formidable challenge due to multiple anatomical and physiological barriers, including the corneal epithelium, conjunctival clearance, and the blood-aqueous and blood-retinal barriers. Most large-molecule therapeutics, including full-length antibodies, exhibit poor corneal permeability and insufficient intraocular bioavailability when administered topically. Consequently, there remains a critical unmet need for novel VEGF inhibitors that combine ultra-high potency with favorable biophysical properties enabling both prolonged intraocular residence after intravitreal injection and effective transcorneal penetration following topical application.

Designed Ankyrin Repeat Proteins (DARPins) represent a promising class of engineered binding proteins that address these challenges. DARPins are derived from naturally occurring ankyrin repeat proteins, which are among the most abundant protein interaction modules in nature. Through protein engineering and directed evolution, libraries of DARPins with randomized surface residues have been constructed, enabling the selection of high-affinity binders against virtually any target antigen. Unlike antibodies, DARPins are small (typically 14–18 kDa), single-domain proteins lacking disulfide bonds and glycosylation, which confers exceptional thermal and thermodynamic stability, high solubility, and resistance to aggregation. These properties facilitate straightforward recombinant production in microbial systems, high-concentration formulation development, and compatibility with diverse routes of administration. Furthermore, the absence of Fc-mediated effector functions makes DARPins particularly suitable for cytokine neutralization, where pure antagonism without immune activation is desired. Prior art has demonstrated the utility of DARPins in various therapeutic contexts, but their application as next-generation anti-VEGF agents in ophthalmology—with dual potential for intravitreal and topical delivery—has not been previously realized. The present invention thus fills a critical gap by providing highly potent, stable, and versatile anti-VEGF-A DARPins that offer significant advantages over existing therapies in terms of dosing frequency, delivery modality, and manufacturability.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of VEGF-A inhibitory agents based on Designed Ankyrin Repeat Proteins (DARPins) that exhibit unprecedented affinity, specificity, and functional potency against VEGF-A, particularly its clinically relevant isoforms VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121. These DARPins are capable of binding VEGF-A with picomolar affinity, effectively blocking its interaction with VEGF receptor 2 (VEGFR-2), and thereby inhibiting downstream signaling pathways responsible for angiogenesis and vascular hyperpermeability. The invention encompasses isolated polypeptides comprising specific DARPin scaffolds engineered to recognize and neutralize VEGF-A, as well as nucleic acids encoding such polypeptides, expression vectors, and host cells for their production. Critically, the disclosed DARPins demonstrate superior in vitro and in vivo efficacy compared to currently approved anti-VEGF therapeutics, including ranibizumab and bevacizumab, as evidenced by their ability to suppress VEGFR-2 phosphorylation and endothelial cell sprouting at significantly lower concentrations.

A key aspect of the invention is the demonstration that these anti-VEGF-A DARPins can be administered via both intravitreal and topical routes to achieve therapeutic effects in relevant ocular disease models. Following intravitreal injection in animal models, the DARPins rapidly penetrate all retinal layers and exhibit prolonged intraocular retention, enabling potent inhibition of VEGF-A-induced vascular leakage at doses substantially lower than those used clinically for existing anti-VEGF drugs. Remarkably, when formulated as eye drops and administered topically, the DARPins effectively penetrate ocular tissues to inhibit both anterior segment neovascularization (e.g., suture-induced corneal angiogenesis in rabbits) and posterior segment pathology (e.g., laser-induced choroidal neovascularization in rats). This dual-route efficacy is attributed to the unique biophysical properties of DARPins, including their small size, high solubility, and exceptional stability, which collectively enhance tissue penetration and resistance to degradation in the ocular environment.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the anti-VEGF-A DARPins in pharmaceutically acceptable carriers suitable for ocular administration. These formulations may be optimized for either intravitreal injection (e.g., isotonic, buffered aqueous solutions) or topical application (e.g., viscous eye drops containing penetration enhancers), and are designed to maximize bioavailability, stability, and patient comfort. The disclosed DARPins are devoid of immunogenic sequences and effector functions, minimizing the risk of inflammatory responses upon intraocular administration. Additionally, their robustness allows for lyophilization and long-term storage without loss of activity, facilitating distribution and use in clinical settings.

In summary, the present invention provides a transformative platform for the treatment of VEGF-mediated ocular diseases by offering highly potent, stable, and versatile therapeutic agents that overcome the major limitations of current anti-VEGF therapies. By enabling both less frequent intravitreal dosing and non-invasive topical administration, the disclosed DARPins have the potential to significantly improve patient outcomes, reduce treatment burden, and lower healthcare costs associated with chronic retinal diseases.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to engineered binding proteins, specifically Designed Ankyrin Repeat Proteins (DARPins), that function as high-affinity antagonists of vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A). These DARPins are characterized by their ability to bind VEGF-A with picomolar affinity, block its interaction with VEGF receptor 2 (VEGFR-2), and inhibit VEGF-A-mediated biological activities such as angiogenesis and vascular permeability. The invention encompasses the molecular design, production, characterization, and therapeutic application of these anti-VEGF-A DARPins, with particular emphasis on their utility in treating ocular diseases through both intravitreal and topical routes of administration.

DARPins are derived from the natural ankyrin repeat protein scaffold, which consists of tandem repeats of approximately 33 amino acids that fold into a helix-turn-helix structure stabilized by conserved hydrophobic core residues. The variable surface residues within the repeats, particularly in the β-turn and helix regions, are randomized to create diverse binding interfaces capable of recognizing specific target antigens. In the present invention, anti-VEGF-A DARPins were isolated from naïve DARPin libraries using ribosome display, a powerful in vitro selection technique that enables the rapid enrichment of high-affinity binders without the need for cellular transformation. The selection process involved multiple rounds of binding to immobilized VEGF-A, followed by off-rate selection under increasingly stringent conditions to favor clones with slow dissociation kinetics. Notably, no additional random mutagenesis or affinity maturation steps were required, as the naïve libraries yielded DARPins with intrinsic picomolar affinity for VEGF-A—a testament to the diversity and quality of the library design.

The selected anti-VEGF-A DARPins share a common structural framework but differ in their complementarity-determining region (CDR)-like sequences, which confer specificity for distinct epitopes on VEGF-A. Sequence analysis reveals that these DARPins typically comprise three or four ankyrin repeats flanked by N- and C-capping repeats that stabilize the overall fold. The molecular weight of these proteins ranges from approximately 14 to 18 kDa, significantly smaller than full-length antibodies (~150 kDa) or even Fab fragments (~50 kDa). This compact size contributes to their favorable pharmacokinetic properties, including enhanced tissue penetration and reduced steric hindrance in target binding. Importantly, the DARPins are expressed in soluble form in the cytoplasm of Escherichia coli, enabling high-yield, cost-effective production without the need for complex eukaryotic expression systems or post-translational modifications. Typical expression yields exceed 200 mg of purified protein per liter of shake-flask culture, and the proteins exhibit excellent solubility and stability under a wide range of pH and temperature conditions.

The functional potency of the anti-VEGF-A DARPins was rigorously validated using a battery of in vitro and in vivo assays. In a quantitative sandwich ELISA (Quantikine assay), the DARPins demonstrated near-complete inhibition of VEGF-A binding to a capture antibody at concentrations as low as 25 pM, with apparent IC50 values in the low picomolar range (e.g., 10 pM for DARPin #4). Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis further confirmed ultra-slow off-rates, consistent with high-affinity interactions. In cellular assays, the DARPins effectively suppressed VEGF-A-induced phosphorylation of VEGFR-2 in endothelial cells and inhibited sprout formation in a human umbilical vein endothelial cell (HUVEC) spheroid model of angiogenesis, with potency comparable to or exceeding that of ranibizumab and bevacizumab. These results establish that the DARPins function as true functional antagonists, not merely binding agents, by sterically blocking the VEGF-A/VEGFR-2 interaction interface.

The ocular pharmacokinetics and biodistribution of the DARPins were evaluated in murine models following intravitreal injection. Fluorescently labeled DARPins were shown to diffuse rapidly throughout the vitreous and penetrate all layers of the retina, including the photoreceptor outer segments, without evidence of aggregation or precipitation. This widespread distribution is critical for targeting both superficial and deep retinal vasculature in diseases like wet AMD and DME. In a rabbit model of VEGF-A-induced vascular leakage, a single intravitreal injection of 50 μg of DARPin #4 achieved nearly complete inhibition of fluorescein extravasation, as evidenced by a treated-to-untreated eye fluorescence ratio of 0.8 ± 0.1, compared to 6.9 ± 3.1 in vehicle controls. This level of efficacy was achieved at a dose tenfold lower than the standard human dose of ranibizumab (500 μg), highlighting the superior potency of the DARPins.

Perhaps most significantly, the invention demonstrates the feasibility of topical delivery of anti-VEGF-A DARPins for the treatment of both anterior and posterior segment diseases. In a rabbit corneal suture model, topical application of DARPin #4 (20 mg/mL, 50 μL, eight times daily) for 20 days resulted in a 43% reduction in vessel density and a 31% reduction in maximum vessel length compared to vehicle-treated eyes. Similarly, in a rat model of laser-induced choroidal neovascularization (CNV), topical administration of DARPin #6 (10 mg/mL, 10 μL, four times daily) significantly reduced CNV lesion size to an extent comparable to intravitreal triamcinolone, a known inhibitor of CNV in this model. These findings represent a breakthrough in ocular drug delivery, as they provide the first evidence that a biologic VEGF inhibitor can effectively reach therapeutic concentrations in the posterior segment following topical application. The success of this approach is attributed to the DARPins’ small size, high solubility, and resistance to proteolytic degradation in the tear film and corneal epithelium.

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention are formulated to optimize stability, bioavailability, and patient tolerability. For intravitreal injection, DARPins are prepared as sterile, isotonic, buffered aqueous solutions (e.g., in phosphate-buffered saline, pH 7.4) at concentrations ranging from 1 to 100 mg/mL. The absence of aggregates and endotoxins is ensured through rigorous purification and filtration steps. For topical application, formulations may include viscosity-enhancing agents (e.g., hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), penetration enhancers (e.g., benzalkonium chloride at low concentrations), and preservatives to maintain sterility during multidose use. The high thermal stability of DARPins (melting temperatures often exceeding 70°C) allows for terminal sterilization by autoclaving or gamma irradiation without loss of activity, a significant advantage over more labile biologics.

The safety profile of the anti-VEGF-A DARPins has been evaluated in multiple species. Intravitreal administration in rabbits did not elicit signs of intraocular inflammation, cataract formation, or retinal toxicity, even at high doses. Topical application in rabbits and rats was well tolerated, with no systemic adverse effects observed. Minor conjunctival irritations noted in sutured rabbit eyes were deemed procedure-related rather than compound-related, as they occurred equally in vehicle-treated controls. The lack of Fc-mediated effector functions further minimizes the risk of unintended immune activation, making DARPins particularly suitable for chronic ocular therapy.

In conclusion, the detailed description of the invention underscores the multifaceted advantages of anti-VEGF-A DARPins as next-generation ophthalmic therapeutics. Their unparalleled combination of high affinity, small size, robust stability, and dual-route efficacy positions them to address the critical unmet needs in the treatment of VEGF-driven ocular diseases, offering the promise of improved patient outcomes through simplified and safer dosing regimens.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The generation of a panel of high-affinity anti-VEGF-A DARPins was accomplished through a streamlined ribosome display selection process from naïve DARPin libraries. Specifically, the N2C and N3C DARPin libraries, which contain randomized surface residues across two or three internal ankyrin repeats, respectively, were employed as the source of diversity. The selection protocol began with three standard rounds of ribosome display, wherein the DARPin mRNA-ribosome-protein complexes were incubated with VEGF-A165 immobilized on MaxiSorp microtiter plates. Unbound complexes were removed by extensive washing, and the bound complexes were recovered by EDTA-mediated ribosome disassembly. The mRNA was then reverse-transcribed to cDNA and amplified by PCR using a high-fidelity proofreading DNA polymerase to minimize the introduction of mutations during amplification. To enrich for clones with slow off-rates and high functional affinity, three consecutive off-rate selection rounds were subsequently performed. In these rounds, the DARPin complexes were pre-incubated with decreasing concentrations of biotinylated VEGF-A (ranging from 1 nM to 10 pM) in the presence of up to 1 μM unlabeled VEGF-A as a competitor to prevent rebinding. Only complexes with sufficiently slow dissociation kinetics remained bound to streptavidin-coated magnetic beads under these stringent conditions. A final collection round was conducted to amplify the enriched pool for downstream screening.

The resulting DNA pool was cloned into the expression vector pMPAG6, which encodes an N-terminal hexahistidine tag for purification, and transformed into E. coli XL1-Blue. Individual colonies were picked into 96-deep-well plates and grown overnight at 37°C. Expression cultures were induced with IPTG, and crude lysates were screened for VEGF-A binding using a direct ELISA format. In this assay, VEGF-A165 was coated onto MaxiSorp plates, and crude extracts were applied directly. Bound DARPins were detected using an anti-RGS-His primary antibody followed by an anti-mouse IgG-alkaline phosphatase conjugate and a colorimetric substrate. Clones exhibiting strong ELISA signals were prioritized for sequencing and further characterization. Ten such clones, designated DARPin #1 through #10, were identified and their amino acid sequences determined. All selected DARPins comprised three internal ankyrin repeats flanked by consensus N- and C-capping repeats, with molecular weights ranging from 16.2 to 17.8 kDa as confirmed by mass spectrometry.

For large-scale production, the genes encoding the selected DARPins were subcloned into an E. coli expression vector and transformed into XL1-Blue cells. Cultures were grown in LB-Lennox medium supplemented with 1% glucose to repress basal expression, and protein production was induced at mid-log phase with 1 mM IPTG. After 4 hours of induction at 30°C, cells were harvested by centrifugation and lysed by sonication in PBS containing protease inhibitors. The soluble fraction was loaded onto a Ni-NTA affinity column, and bound DARPins were eluted with imidazole. Further purification was achieved by size-exclusion chromatography on a Superdex 200 column to remove aggregates and ensure monodispersity. The final preparations were dialyzed into PBS, concentrated to 10–50 mg/mL, and stored at −80°C. Analytical SDS-PAGE and SEC-MALS confirmed >95% purity and a monomeric state for all DARPins. Expression yields consistently exceeded 200 mg/L of culture, demonstrating the scalability of the production process.

### Example 2

The binding affinity and specificity of the purified anti-VEGF-A DARPins were comprehensively evaluated using multiple orthogonal techniques. Initial screening was performed using a competitive ELISA in which DARPins were pre-incubated with VEGF-A165 before application to VEGF-A-coated plates. All ten DARPins showed strong, concentration-dependent inhibition of VEGF-A binding, whereas non-binding isotype control DARPins (I1 and I2) had no effect. Cross-reactivity testing revealed that the DARPins bound equally well to human, mouse, dog, and rabbit VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121, but showed no detectable binding to VEGF-C, confirming their specificity for the VEGF-A isoforms. The functional affinity of the DARPins was then quantified using the Quantikine Human VEGF Immunoassay kit, a sandwich ELISA that measures the ability of test compounds to prevent VEGF-A from binding to a capture antibody. In this assay, 25 pM VEGF-A165 was pre-incubated with 250 pM of each DARPin (or 125 pM bevacizumab, accounting for its bivalency) before addition to the assay plate. All anti-VEGF-A DARPins suppressed the ELISA signal by more than 50%, with several reducing it to near-background levels, indicating affinities at or below the 25 pM VEGF-A concentration used. To determine the IC50 more precisely, DARPin #4 was titrated against a fixed 20 pM VEGF-A165 concentration. The resulting dose-response curve yielded an apparent IC50 of 10 pM, although the true affinity is likely even lower given the limiting VEGF-A concentration in the assay.

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) was employed to obtain kinetic parameters for the DARPin-VEGF-A interaction. Biotinylated VEGF-A165 was immobilized on a streptavidin sensor chip, and serial dilutions of DARPin #4 were injected over the surface. The association and dissociation phases were monitored in real time, and the data were fitted to a 1:1 binding model. The analysis revealed a kon of 1.2 × 10^6 M^−1s^−1 and a koff of 3.5 × 10^−5 s^−1, corresponding to a KD of 29 pM. The extremely slow off-rate (half-life >5 hours) explains the high potency observed in functional assays and suggests prolonged target engagement in vivo. Competition experiments using SPR further demonstrated that the DARPins and ranibizumab bind to overlapping or mutually exclusive epitopes on VEGF-A, as pre-incubation with one agent prevented binding of the other. This finding is consistent with the mechanism of action, wherein the DARPins sterically hinder the VEGF-A/VEGFR-2 interaction interface, similar to ranibizumab.

### Example 3

The functional antagonism of the anti-VEGF-A DARPins was validated in two complementary cellular assays that recapitulate key aspects of VEGF-A biology. First, a VEGFR-2 phosphorylation assay was performed using immortalized human umbilical vein endothelial (HUE) cells. Cells were serum-starved overnight and then stimulated with 1.4 nM VEGF-A165 in the presence or absence of 1.4 nM test compounds. After 7 minutes of stimulation, cells were lysed under phosphatase-inhibiting conditions, and VEGFR-2 phosphorylation was quantified by a sandwich ELISA using a VEGFR-2-specific capture antibody and an anti-phosphotyrosine detection antibody. All ten anti-VEGF-A DARPins significantly suppressed VEGF-A-induced VEGFR-2 phosphorylation, with several achieving inhibition comparable to ranibizumab and bevacizumab. Isotype control DARPins had no effect, confirming that the inhibition was specific to VEGF-A binding. Second, a HUVEC spheroid sprouting assay was used to assess the ability of the DARPins to inhibit angiogenesis in a three-dimensional context. HUVEC spheroids were embedded in a collagen matrix and stimulated with 3 ng/mL VEGF-A165 in the presence of 1 nM test compounds. After 24 hours, sprout length was quantified by brightfield microscopy. Both DARPin #2 and #4 potently inhibited sprouting, reducing the average total sprout length per spheroid by more than 80% compared to PBS controls, an effect equivalent to or better than ranibizumab. Isotype control DARPin I2 showed no inhibition, again confirming specificity. These results collectively demonstrate that the anti-VEGF-A DARPins are not merely binding agents but true functional antagonists that disrupt VEGF-A signaling at the cellular level.

### Example 4

The ocular biodistribution and therapeutic efficacy of intravitreally injected DARPins were evaluated in rodent and lagomorph models. In a mouse ocular penetration study, DARPin #4 and an isotype control DARPin (I1) were labeled with Alexa Fluor 555 via a C-terminal cysteine residue and injected intravitreally (1 μL) into the eyes of 3-week-old mice. At 1, 4, and 24 hours post-injection, eyes were enucleated, cryosectioned, and imaged by fluorescence microscopy. Both DARPins distributed homogeneously throughout the vitreous and penetrated all retinal layers, including the ganglion cell layer, inner and outer nuclear layers, and photoreceptor outer segments. No fluorescent signal was detected in PBS-injected control eyes, ruling out autofluorescence artifacts. The uniform distribution suggests that the DARPins can access both superficial and deep retinal vasculature, a critical feature for treating diseases like wet AMD and DME.

In a rabbit model of VEGF-A-induced vascular leakage, the functional efficacy of intravitreal DARPin #4 was assessed. Pigmented rabbits received a single intravitreal injection of 50 μg DARPin #4 or PBS (100 μL) into the right eye. Four days later, 500 ng of human VEGF-A165 was injected into the same eye to induce vascular leakage. Two days after VEGF-A challenge, sodium fluorescein was administered intravenously, and vitreoretinal fluorescence was measured by fluorophotometry. The ratio of fluorescence intensity in the treated (right) eye versus the untreated (left) eye was 0.8 ± 0.1 in the DARPin group, indicating nearly complete inhibition of VEGF-A-induced leakage, compared to 6.9 ± 3.1 in the PBS group (p = 0.025). Slit-lamp examination and histopathology revealed no signs of ocular inflammation or toxicity in DARPin-treated eyes, confirming the safety of intravitreal administration. The dose used (50 μg) is tenfold lower than the standard human dose of ranibizumab (500 μg), underscoring the superior potency of the DARPins.

### Example 5

The potential for topical delivery of anti-VEGF-A DARPins was investigated in two distinct animal models of ocular neovascularization. In a rabbit corneal suture model, two 3-mm silk sutures were placed in the corneal stroma to induce chronic inflammation and angiogenesis. Starting on day 1, rabbits received topical instillations of DARPin #4 (20 mg/mL in PBS, 50 μL) or PBS alone eight times daily for 20 days. On day 20, corneal neovascularization was quantified by measuring the maximum vessel length (normalized to the distance from limbus to suture) and vessel density in the neovascularized area. DARPin-treated eyes showed a 31% reduction in maximum vessel length and a 43% reduction in vessel density compared to PBS-treated eyes. Representative images revealed markedly less vascular infiltration in the DARPin group, with vessels appearing shorter and less dense. Rabbits gained weight normally in both groups, and minor conjunctival irritations (chemosis, redness) were observed equally in both groups, indicating that the effects were procedure-related rather than compound-related.

In a rat model of laser-induced choroidal neovascularization (CNV), Brown Norway rats received six laser burns in the right eye to rupture Bruch’s membrane and induce CNV. Starting on day 1, rats were treated with topical DARPin #6 (10 mg/mL in PBS, 10 μL four times daily), a single intravitreal injection of triamcinolone (120 μg, positive control), or PBS (negative control). On day 15, fluorescein angiography was performed, and on day 24, eyes were enucleated for flatmount analysis. The mean CNV lesion volume was significantly reduced in both the DARPin and triamcinolone groups compared to PBS (p = 0.025 and p = 0.0065, respectively), with no significant difference between the two active treatments. This demonstrates that topical DARPin #6 achieves therapeutic concentrations in the posterior segment sufficient to inhibit CNV formation. No ocular or systemic adverse effects were observed in any treatment group, confirming the safety and tolerability of topical DARPin administration. These findings represent a paradigm shift in ocular drug delivery, proving that a biologic VEGF inhibitor can be effectively delivered to the posterior segment via non-invasive topical application.
