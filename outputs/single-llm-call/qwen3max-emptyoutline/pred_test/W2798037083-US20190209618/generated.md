# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of hematopoietic stem cell biology and regenerative medicine, specifically to methods and compositions for the ex vivo expansion of human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) through the modulation of transcriptional regulators of the Musashi-2 (MSI2) gene. More particularly, the invention provides novel methods for enhancing the self-renewal capacity of HSPCs by co-overexpression or activation of upstream transcription factors USF2 and PLAG1, which act cooperatively to drive MSI2 expression. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising expanded HSPCs, methods for culturing leukemic cells that rely on the same regulatory axis, and screening platforms for identifying small molecules or agents that modulate the USF2–PLAG1–MSI2 pathway. This invention is applicable to clinical settings requiring robust HSPC expansion, including cord blood transplantation, gene therapy, and hematopoietic reconstitution following myeloablative conditioning, as well as to the development of therapeutic strategies targeting leukemias characterized by dysregulated MSI2 expression.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) represent the foundational cellular reservoir of the adult blood system, possessing the dual capacity for long-term self-renewal and multipotent differentiation into all mature blood lineages. These properties render HSCs indispensable for lifelong hematopoiesis and central to numerous clinical applications, including bone marrow transplantation, gene therapy for monogenic blood disorders, and immune reconstitution in oncology and immunodeficiency settings. Despite their therapeutic promise, the clinical utility of HSCs—particularly those derived from umbilical cord blood (CB)—is severely limited by their scarcity in available grafts. A single CB unit typically contains only 1–5 × 10⁵ CD34⁺ HSPCs, which is often insufficient to achieve rapid and durable engraftment in adult recipients. Consequently, extensive efforts have been directed toward developing ex vivo culture systems that can expand functional HSCs without compromising their stemness or inducing malignant transformation.

A major breakthrough in this endeavor was the identification of the RNA-binding protein Musashi-2 (MSI2) as a critical regulator of HSC self-renewal. MSI2 is highly expressed in primitive HSCs and its expression declines progressively with lineage commitment. Functional studies in both murine and human systems have demonstrated that loss of MSI2 impairs HSC reconstitution capacity, while moderate overexpression significantly enhances HSC competitiveness and ex vivo expansion. In human cord blood-derived HSPCs, MSI2 overexpression leads to a marked increase in CD34⁺ cell numbers and improved engraftment in immunodeficient mouse models. Mechanistically, MSI2 exerts its pro-self-renewal effects largely through post-transcriptional repression of key differentiation-promoting transcripts, including those encoding components of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) signaling pathway, such as cytochrome P450 1B1 (CYP1B1). However, despite the clear importance of MSI2 in HSC biology, the upstream transcriptional mechanisms governing its expression in primitive hematopoietic cells have remained elusive.

Prior to the present invention, no endogenous transcription factors were known to directly regulate MSI2 expression in human HSPCs. While MSI2’s role as a downstream effector of stem cell maintenance was established, the lack of knowledge regarding its transcriptional control hindered the development of targeted, non-viral, or pharmacologically tractable strategies to modulate MSI2 levels for therapeutic benefit. Existing HSPC expansion protocols have relied on cytokine cocktails, small molecule inhibitors (e.g., UM171, SR1), or forced overexpression of individual genes such as HOXB4 or MSI2 itself. However, these approaches often suffer from limitations including incomplete mechanistic understanding, variable efficacy, potential genotoxicity (in the case of viral overexpression), or off-target effects. Moreover, the molecular basis for the restricted expression of MSI2 to primitive HSPCs—and its downregulation upon differentiation—remained unexplained.

Separately, the transcription factors upstream stimulatory factor 2 (USF2) and pleomorphic adenoma gene-like 1 (PLAG1) have been studied in other biological contexts but not in relation to HSC regulation. USF2, a member of the basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper (bHLH-Zip) family, is ubiquitously expressed and known to bind E-box motifs (CANNTG) in promoter regions to regulate diverse processes including metabolism, stress response, and cell cycle control. In hematopoiesis, USF2 has been implicated in the regulation of HOXB4, another HSC self-renewal gene, but its broader role in stem cell fate was unclear. PLAG1, a C2H2 zinc finger protein, was originally identified as a proto-oncogene in salivary gland tumors and is known to bind G-rich bipartite DNA sequences. Although PLAG1 has been associated with developmental growth and tumorigenesis, its expression and function in normal hematopoiesis were unknown. Critically, neither USF2 nor PLAG1 had been linked to MSI2 regulation, and the possibility that they might cooperate to control a master stem cell regulator was entirely unanticipated.

The absence of a defined transcriptional circuitry for MSI2 represented a significant gap in the field. Without knowledge of the factors that naturally maintain MSI2 at high levels in HSCs, it was impossible to rationally design interventions that mimic physiological regulation. Furthermore, the potential for combinatorial transcription factor activity—where two or more factors act synergistically—had not been explored in the context of HSPC expansion. Such cooperative mechanisms are common in developmental biology but remain underexploited in regenerative medicine due to the difficulty of identifying relevant factor pairs. The present invention addresses this fundamental gap by identifying, for the first time, that USF2 and PLAG1 are necessary and jointly sufficient for robust MSI2 transcription in human HSPCs, and that their co-expression recapitulates the functional benefits of direct MSI2 overexpression, including enhanced CD34⁺ cell output and repression of differentiation drivers like CYP1B1.

This discovery not only elucidates a previously unknown regulatory axis in hematopoietic stem cell biology but also provides a novel platform for therapeutic HSPC expansion. Unlike direct MSI2 overexpression, which may carry risks of excessive self-renewal or leukemogenesis given MSI2’s association with aggressive leukemia, modulating upstream transcription factors offers a more nuanced and potentially safer approach to fine-tune MSI2 levels within a physiological range. Additionally, the identification of specific binding sites within the MSI2 minimal promoter enables the development of reporter-based screening assays for small molecules that enhance USF2/PLAG1 activity or stabilize their interaction with the promoter. Thus, the background of the invention highlights an unmet need for mechanistically grounded, scalable, and clinically translatable methods to expand HSPCs, which the present invention fulfills through the unexpected discovery of the USF2–PLAG1–MSI2 regulatory module.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for the ex vivo expansion of human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) by co-modulating the activity of two transcription factors, upstream stimulatory factor 2 (USF2) and pleomorphic adenoma gene-like 1 (PLAG1), which together function as essential and cooperative regulators of Musashi-2 (MSI2) gene expression. The invention is based on the discovery that USF2 and PLAG1 bind directly to a minimal promoter region of the MSI2 gene located approximately 588 base pairs upstream of the translational start site, and that their combined action is required for maximal MSI2 transcription in primitive HSPCs. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that while USF2 is ubiquitously expressed across the hematopoietic hierarchy, PLAG1 exhibits a restricted expression pattern, being highly enriched in the most primitive CD34⁺Lin⁻ HSCs and declining sharply upon differentiation. This complementary expression profile ensures that high-level MSI2 transcription occurs only in the stem cell compartment, where both factors are present.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for expanding HSPCs comprising co-overexpressing USF2 and PLAG1 in a population of human HSPCs, thereby increasing MSI2 expression and promoting the maintenance and proliferation of primitive CD34⁺ cells during ex vivo culture. This co-overexpression results in a synergistic upregulation of MSI2—exceeding the effect of either factor alone—and leads to a significant increase in both the frequency and total number of CD34⁺ cells after seven days of culture, without inducing aberrant differentiation or loss of stemness. In a preferred embodiment, the PLAG1 isoform used is PLAG1-S (the short isoform initiating at methionine 100), which is the predominant form expressed in primary HSPCs and is functionally equivalent to PLAG1-B in driving MSI2 transcription.

In another aspect, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising HSPCs expanded according to the disclosed method, which are suitable for use in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, gene therapy, or immune reconstitution. These expanded HSPCs retain their multilineage differentiation potential and engraftment capacity, as demonstrated by their ability to repopulate immunodeficient mouse models and generate all major blood lineages.

The invention further provides a method for identifying agents that modulate HSPC expansion by screening for compounds that enhance the activity of USF2, PLAG1, or their cooperative binding to the MSI2 promoter. This includes high-throughput screening platforms using reporter constructs containing the wild-type MSI2 minimal promoter or mutated versions lacking USF2 or PLAG1 binding sites, enabling the identification of specific activators of this regulatory axis.

Additionally, the invention encompasses methods for culturing leukemic cells that depend on the USF2–PLAG1–MSI2 pathway for survival or proliferation. Given that MSI2 is overexpressed in aggressive forms of acute myeloid leukemia (AML), chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), and myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), and correlates with poor prognosis, the invention provides a means to model such leukemias in vitro by maintaining leukemic stem cells through USF2/PLAG1 co-expression, or conversely, to identify therapeutic agents that disrupt this axis for anti-leukemic purposes.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides nucleic acid constructs encoding USF2 and PLAG1, optionally linked by a self-cleaving peptide sequence (e.g., P2A) to ensure co-expression from a single transcript, as well as vectors (e.g., lentiviral, retroviral) for delivering these constructs to HSPCs. The invention also includes dominant-negative forms of PLAG1 (lacking transactivation domains) for experimental or therapeutic suppression of MSI2 expression.

Collectively, the invention establishes a new paradigm for HSPC expansion based on the physiological transcriptional regulation of a master stem cell factor, offering a more precise, effective, and potentially safer alternative to existing methods. The core insight—that cooperative transcription factor activity can be harnessed to amplify stem cell output—has broad implications for regenerative medicine beyond hematopoiesis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Methods of Expanding HSPCs

The present invention provides robust and reproducible methods for the ex vivo expansion of human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) by co-modulating the expression or activity of upstream transcription factors USF2 and PLAG1. These methods are predicated on the discovery that USF2 and PLAG1 function cooperatively to drive transcription of the MSI2 gene, a master regulator of HSC self-renewal, and that their combined action is both necessary and sufficient to enhance the maintenance and proliferation of primitive CD34⁺ cells in culture. The methods involve introducing nucleic acid constructs encoding USF2 and PLAG1 into a population of HSPCs, followed by culture under conditions that support stem cell viability and expansion. The HSPCs may be derived from any suitable source, including umbilical cord blood, mobilized peripheral blood, or bone marrow, with cord blood being particularly advantageous due to its high frequency of primitive HSCs and lower immunogenicity.

In a preferred embodiment, the method comprises isolating Lin⁻CD34⁺ cells from human cord blood using standard immunomagnetic separation techniques. The isolated cells are then transduced with a recombinant viral vector—such as a lentiviral or retroviral vector—encoding both USF2 and a short isoform of PLAG1 (designated PLAG1-S, which initiates translation at methionine 100 and lacks the first 17 N-terminal residues of PLAG1-B). The vector is engineered to express both transgenes from a single transcript, separated by a self-cleaving peptide sequence (e.g., P2A or T2A) to ensure stoichiometric co-expression, and includes a fluorescent reporter (e.g., mTagBFP) to enable tracking of transduced cells. Transduction is carried out in the presence of cytokines known to support HSPC survival, such as stem cell factor (SCF), thrombopoietin (TPO), and FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 ligand (Flt3L), typically at concentrations of 100 ng/mL each.

Following transduction, the cells are cultured in serum-free medium supplemented with the same cytokine cocktail for a period of 3 to 14 days, with 7 days being optimal for achieving maximal expansion while preserving stemness. During this period, the co-overexpression of USF2 and PLAG1-S leads to a synergistic increase in endogenous MSI2 mRNA and protein levels—up to 4.5-fold higher than in control cells transduced with empty vector. This elevated MSI2 expression, in turn, results in the post-transcriptional repression of differentiation-promoting genes such as CYP1B1, thereby preventing premature lineage commitment and promoting self-renewal divisions. At the end of the culture period, the frequency and absolute number of CD34⁺ cells are significantly increased, with reported enhancements of 5-fold and 6-fold, respectively, compared to controls.

The method is highly specific to the cooperative action of USF2 and PLAG1; overexpression of either factor alone yields only modest increases in MSI2 and CD34⁺ cell output, underscoring the necessity of their combined activity. Furthermore, the method does not require the addition of exogenous small molecules or complex cytokine mixtures beyond the standard supportive factors, making it simpler and more cost-effective than many existing expansion protocols. The expanded HSPCs retain their functional integrity, as evidenced by their ability to engraft and reconstitute multilineage hematopoiesis in immunodeficient mouse models such as NSG (NOD-scid IL2Rγnull) mice.

Alternative embodiments include the use of non-viral delivery methods, such as electroporation of mRNA encoding USF2 and PLAG1-S, which offers transient expression and reduced risk of insertional mutagenesis. Additionally, the method may be adapted for use with induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived HSPCs, providing a renewable source of cells for expansion. The invention also contemplates the use of small molecule activators of USF2 or PLAG1, once identified through screening assays described herein, as a non-genetic means to achieve the same expansion effect.

### Expanded HSPCs for Therapeutic Use

The HSPCs expanded according to the methods of the present invention possess enhanced therapeutic potential due to their increased numbers, preserved stemness, and maintained differentiation capacity. These cells are particularly valuable in clinical scenarios where HSPC dose is a limiting factor, such as in umbilical cord blood transplantation for adult patients, where a single cord unit often contains insufficient cells for reliable engraftment. By expanding the CD34⁺ cell content ex vivo, the invention enables the use of smaller or lower-quality cord units, thereby increasing the availability of grafts and reducing the time to neutrophil and platelet recovery post-transplant.

The expanded HSPCs are suitable for autologous or allogeneic transplantation and can be cryopreserved using standard protocols for later infusion. Prior to administration, the cells may be formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as saline with human serum albumin, and administered intravenously. The dosage will vary depending on the patient’s weight and condition but typically ranges from 1 × 10⁵ to 5 × 10⁶ CD34⁺ cells per kilogram of body weight.

Beyond transplantation, the expanded HSPCs serve as an ideal substrate for gene therapy applications. Their enhanced proliferative capacity allows for efficient genetic modification—via viral vectors or gene editing tools such as CRISPR-Cas9—followed by expansion to therapeutic doses. For example, in the treatment of hemoglobinopathies like sickle cell disease or β-thalassemia, HSPCs can be edited to correct the defective β-globin gene or induce fetal hemoglobin expression, then expanded using the USF2/PLAG1 method to ensure sufficient cell numbers for durable engraftment. Similarly, in immunodeficiencies such as severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID), corrected HSPCs can be expanded to restore immune function.

The safety profile of the expanded cells is favorable. Because the method relies on physiological transcription factors rather than oncogenes or potent signaling inhibitors, the risk of malignant transformation is minimized. Moreover, the transient nature of ex vivo culture limits the potential for epigenetic drift or accumulation of mutations. Quality control measures, including flow cytometric analysis for CD34 expression, colony-forming unit (CFU) assays to assess progenitor function, and karyotyping to rule out genomic instability, ensure that only therapeutically competent cells are administered.

### Compositions

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising HSPCs expanded by co-overexpression of USF2 and PLAG1, as well as nucleic acid constructs and vectors for implementing the expansion method. The HSPC compositions consist of a population of human CD34⁺ cells that have been cultured ex vivo under conditions that promote self-renewal through the USF2–PLAG1–MSI2 axis. These cells are suspended in a biocompatible solution, such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 2–5% human serum albumin, and may be provided in frozen or fresh form. The composition is sterile, pyrogen-free, and meets regulatory standards for cellular therapeutics.

In addition, the invention provides expression vectors encoding USF2 and PLAG1 isoforms. A preferred vector is a lentiviral construct based on the pSMALB backbone, which features a bidirectional promoter driving expression of the transgene(s) and a fluorescent marker (e.g., mTagBFP). In the co-expression vector, the USF2 open reading frame is linked to the PLAG1-S ORF via a P2A self-cleaving peptide sequence, ensuring that a single transcript produces both proteins in equimolar amounts. The vector includes standard elements for lentiviral packaging, such as the ψ packaging signal, RRE, and cPPT, and is pseudotyped with VSV-G envelope for broad tropism.

Also provided are dominant-negative PLAG1 constructs, wherein the transactivation domain is deleted (e.g., PLAG1-B-DN or PLAG1-S-DN), useful for experimental knockdown of MSI2 expression or potential therapeutic suppression in MSI2-dependent leukemias. These constructs may be delivered via the same vectors as above.

The compositions may further include kits for HSPC expansion, comprising the expression vector, transduction reagents (e.g., polybrene), culture medium, cytokines (SCF, TPO, Flt3L), and instructions for use. Such kits facilitate standardized implementation of the method in clinical or research laboratories.

### Methods of Culturing Leukemic Cells

The USF2–PLAG1–MSI2 regulatory axis is not only operative in normal HSPCs but also hijacked in certain leukemias, where aberrantly high MSI2 expression drives disease progression. The present invention therefore provides methods for culturing leukemic cells that depend on this pathway, enabling the study of leukemia biology and drug screening. Primary leukemic blasts or established cell lines (e.g., K562, which expresses MSI2) can be maintained in culture by ensuring adequate USF2 and PLAG1 activity. In cases where PLAG1 expression is low, exogenous PLAG1-S may be introduced to sustain MSI2 levels and prevent differentiation.

Conversely, the method can be used to model leukemogenesis by overexpressing USF2 and PLAG1 in normal HSPCs, which, when combined with additional oncogenic hits (e.g., BCR-ABL in CML), may accelerate transformation. This provides a platform for studying the cooperative effects of transcription factor dysregulation and oncogene activation.

For drug discovery, leukemic cells cultured via this method can be screened against libraries of compounds to identify inhibitors of the USF2–PLAG1 interaction, MSI2 expression, or downstream effectors like CYP1B1. Such inhibitors may have therapeutic value in MSI2-high leukemias.

### Example 1: Inhibition of Cytochrome P450 1B1 Oxidase Causes Expansion of Hematopoietic Stem and Progenitor Cells

Although the primary focus of the invention is on transcriptional regulation of MSI2, it is noted that MSI2 functions partly by repressing CYP1B1, an enzyme in the AHR pathway that promotes differentiation. Therefore, direct inhibition of CYP1B1—using small molecules such as tetramethoxystilbene (TMS)—can phenocopy MSI2 overexpression and expand HSPCs. This example demonstrates that CYP1B1 inhibition is a viable alternative strategy, and importantly, that combining CYP1B1 inhibitors with USF2/PLAG1 co-expression may yield additive or synergistic effects, as both converge on the same downstream pathway.

### Results & Discussion

The experimental results confirm that USF2 and PLAG1 are direct, cooperative regulators of MSI2. ChIP-qPCR shows enrichment of both factors at the MSI2 promoter in CD34⁺ cells, and co-overexpression leads to synergistic MSI2 upregulation and HSPC expansion. The lack of physical interaction by co-IP suggests their cooperation is mediated through chromatin remodeling or sequential binding, possibly with PLAG1 acting as a pioneer factor. Genome-wide ChIP-seq reveals co-occupancy at promoters of other HSC regulators (e.g., DNMT3A, HOXA1), indicating a broader role in stem cell maintenance. These findings redefine our understanding of HSC transcriptional networks and provide a foundation for novel therapies.

### Example 2: CYPB1 Knockdown Increases the Number of CD34 Marked HSPCs

Knockdown of CYP1B1 using shRNA in cord blood HSPCs results in increased CD34⁺ cell numbers, confirming that CYP1B1 repression is sufficient for expansion. This validates the MSI2–CYP1B1 axis as a druggable target and supports the use of CYP1B1 inhibitors in combination with transcription factor modulation.

### Example 3: High Throughput Screen to Test CYP Family Inhibitors

A high-throughput screen of CYP family inhibitors identifies TMS as a potent and selective CYP1B1 inhibitor that expands HSPCs. This screen can be adapted to find compounds that enhance USF2/PLAG1 activity using MSI2 promoter reporter assays.

### Example 3: Combinatorial HSPC Promoting Effects of SR1 with TMS

The aryl hydrocarbon receptor antagonist StemRegenin 1 (SR1) and CYP1B1 inhibitor TMS act on related pathways. Combining SR1 and TMS with USF2/PLAG1 co-expression yields greater HSPC expansion than any single agent, demonstrating the power of combinatorial approaches targeting multiple nodes of the self-renewal network.
