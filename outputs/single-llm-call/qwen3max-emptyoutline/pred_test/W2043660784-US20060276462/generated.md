# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cognitive enhancement and neuropharmacology, particularly to the use of ampakines—positive allosteric modulators of α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptors—for the treatment and mitigation of cognitive impairments resulting from prolonged sleep deprivation. More specifically, the invention provides methods for restoring and enhancing performance in complex cognitive tasks, such as delayed-match-to-sample (DMS) memory tasks, in subjects experiencing significant sleep loss, through the administration of a therapeutically effective amount of an ampakine compound, exemplified by CX717. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising such ampakines, dosing regimens optimized for cognitive rescue under sleep-deprived conditions, and the identification of key neural substrates—particularly the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DPFC), dorsal striatum (DStr), medial temporal lobe (MTL), and precuneate cortex—whose metabolic activity is modulated by ampakines in a manner that correlates with behavioral recovery. The invention is especially relevant for applications in military, emergency response, medical, and industrial settings where individuals are required to maintain high levels of cognitive function despite extended periods without sleep.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Electrophysiology of Sleep and Sleep-Deprivation

Sleep is a fundamental biological process governed by intricate electrophysiological mechanisms that regulate transitions between wakefulness, non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep, and rapid eye movement (REM) sleep. These states are characterized by distinct patterns of electroencephalographic (EEG) activity, which reflect synchronized neuronal oscillations across cortical and subcortical networks. During wakefulness, the EEG is dominated by low-amplitude, high-frequency beta (15–25 Hz) and gamma (>30 Hz) oscillations, indicative of active information processing and attentional engagement. As sleep onset occurs, the EEG shifts toward slower, higher-amplitude rhythms, beginning with theta (4–8 Hz) activity in stage N1, followed by the emergence of sleep spindles (12–15 Hz) and K-complexes in stage N2, and culminating in the high-power delta (0.5–5 Hz) waves that define slow-wave sleep (SWS or N3). REM sleep is marked by a return to low-voltage, mixed-frequency EEG resembling wakefulness, often accompanied by bursts of beta activity and ponto-geniculo-occipital (PGO) waves.

Prolonged sleep deprivation disrupts this finely tuned electrophysiological architecture, leading to homeostatic imbalances that manifest as increased sleep pressure and compensatory neural phenomena. One hallmark of sleep deprivation is the elevation of delta power during wakeful periods, reflecting the brain’s attempt to initiate restorative slow-wave processes even while behaviorally awake. This phenomenon is often accompanied by transient episodes of microsleep—brief, involuntary lapses into sleep-like states lasting seconds—during which delta and theta activity intrudes into ongoing cognitive tasks. Additionally, sleep deprivation can paradoxically increase beta band power during wakefulness, possibly due to heightened arousal efforts or dysregulation of thalamocortical circuits. These electrophysiological aberrations correlate strongly with impaired vigilance, slowed reaction times, and deficits in executive function and working memory. Traditional countermeasures, such as psychostimulants (e.g., amphetamines, modafinil), may suppress subjective sleepiness but often fail to fully restore normal EEG dynamics or prevent microsleep intrusions, thereby offering only partial cognitive protection. The present invention addresses this gap by leveraging ampakines, which not only enhance cognitive performance but also normalize sleep-deprivation-induced EEG abnormalities, suggesting a more integrative restoration of neural network stability.

### Neuroanatomical Substrates of Sleep Deprivation

The cognitive impairments induced by sleep deprivation are not uniformly distributed across the brain but rather arise from dysfunction in specific, interconnected neural circuits that support attention, working memory, and executive control. Functional neuroimaging studies in humans and non-human primates have consistently implicated the prefrontal cortex—particularly the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DPFC)—as a region highly vulnerable to sleep loss. The DPFC is essential for maintaining goal-directed behavior, manipulating information in working memory, and inhibiting prepotent responses; its hypoactivation during sleep deprivation directly correlates with errors in tasks requiring sustained attention and cognitive flexibility. Concurrently, the dorsal striatum (DStr), which integrates sensorimotor and cognitive loops with the prefrontal cortex, exhibits reduced metabolic activity under sleep-deprived conditions, contributing to slowed response initiation and impaired procedural learning.

In contrast, certain limbic and parietal regions show paradoxical hyperactivation during sleep deprivation. The medial temporal lobe (MTL), including the hippocampus and surrounding entorhinal and perirhinal cortices, often displays elevated glucose metabolism, possibly reflecting compensatory recruitment or inefficient processing due to disrupted top-down control from the prefrontal cortex. Similarly, the precuneus—a midline parietal region involved in self-referential thought, episodic memory retrieval, and default mode network activity—may exhibit increased metabolic rates, potentially indicating a failure to suppress internally directed cognition during externally demanding tasks. The thalamus, a critical relay and gating structure for sensory and cortical information, typically shows reduced metabolic activity during sleep deprivation, impairing signal-to-noise ratios in cortical processing and contributing to attentional lapses. These region-specific alterations create a state of neural imbalance: hypoactive frontostriatal circuits fail to maintain task engagement, while hyperactive limbic and parietal regions may promote mind-wandering or maladaptive compensatory strategies. The present invention demonstrates that ampakines like CX717 selectively reverse these pathological metabolic patterns, restoring a functional balance that supports accurate and efficient task performance.

### Role of Stress in Sleep Deprivation and Cognitive Performance

Sleep deprivation is not merely a passive state of rest loss but an active physiological stressor that engages the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and sympathetic nervous system, leading to elevated levels of cortisol, catecholamines, and pro-inflammatory cytokines. This stress response can independently impair cognitive function, particularly in domains mediated by the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus, which are rich in glucocorticoid receptors and thus highly sensitive to stress-induced modulation. Chronic or acute stress exacerbates the cognitive deficits of sleep deprivation by promoting dendritic atrophy in the prefrontal cortex, suppressing neurogenesis in the hippocampus, and altering glutamatergic and GABAergic transmission. Moreover, the subjective experience of fatigue, irritability, and reduced motivation during sleep deprivation may further degrade performance through top-down motivational and emotional interference.

However, the cognitive benefits observed with ampakine administration in sleep-deprived subjects cannot be attributed solely to stress reduction. Unlike conventional stimulants that may amplify sympathetic activation and exacerbate anxiety, ampakines such as CX717 act directly on AMPA receptors to enhance fast excitatory neurotransmission without significantly elevating heart rate, blood pressure, or cortisol levels. The present invention demonstrates that CX717 improves performance even in the presence of ongoing sleep deprivation stress, suggesting that its mechanism operates downstream or independently of HPA axis activation. By potentiating glutamatergic signaling in key cognitive circuits, ampakines may effectively “override” the disruptive effects of stress on synaptic efficacy, thereby preserving information processing fidelity. This distinction is critical for real-world applications where stress and sleep loss co-occur, as it indicates that ampakines offer a more targeted and physiologically compatible intervention than broad-spectrum stimulants.

### Effects of Sleep-Deprivation on Memory

Memory processes, particularly those dependent on short-term retention and active manipulation of information, are exquisitely sensitive to sleep deprivation. Working memory—the ability to hold and update information over seconds to minutes—is among the most consistently impaired cognitive functions following even modest sleep loss. In delayed-match-to-sample (DMS) tasks, which require subjects to encode a sample stimulus, retain it over a variable delay, and then select it from an array of distractors, sleep deprivation produces dose-dependent declines in accuracy, especially as delay duration and memory load (number of distractors) increase. This impairment reflects a failure in the maintenance phase of working memory, likely due to weakened recurrent excitation in prefrontal microcircuits and reduced synchrony between the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus.

Long-term memory consolidation, which typically occurs during post-encoding sleep, is also disrupted by sleep deprivation, but the present invention focuses on the acute, online effects of sleep loss on memory performance during wakefulness. Under sleep-deprived conditions, the fidelity of memory traces degrades rapidly, leading to increased susceptibility to interference and accelerated forgetting. This is evident in the disproportionate decline in DMS performance on long-delay, high-load trials, where the cognitive demand exceeds the compromised capacity of sleep-deprived neural networks. Importantly, these memory deficits are not simply due to reduced alertness or motor slowing, as response latencies often remain within normal ranges despite significant accuracy losses, indicating a specific disruption in mnemonic processing. The present invention reveals that ampakines like CX717 not only restore baseline memory performance under sleep deprivation but can even enhance it beyond normal levels, particularly on challenging trials. This suggests that AMPA receptor potentiation strengthens the synaptic basis of memory maintenance, possibly by increasing the signal-to-noise ratio in recurrent cortical networks or by enhancing the gain of hippocampal-prefrontal interactions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

As used herein, the term “ampakine” refers to a class of compounds that function as positive allosteric modulators of AMPA-type glutamate receptors. These compounds bind to sites distinct from the glutamate-binding site and enhance receptor function by slowing desensitization and/or deactivation kinetics, thereby increasing the amplitude and duration of postsynaptic currents. The term includes, but is not limited to, benzamide derivatives such as CX717, CX516, and CX614, as well as structurally related molecules exhibiting similar pharmacological profiles. “CX717” specifically denotes the compound (R)-N-[2-(3,4-dichlorophenoxy)-5-fluorophenyl]-2-oxo-1-imidazolidinecarboxamide, or any pharmaceutically acceptable salt or solvate thereof.

The term “sleep deprivation” refers to a state of prolonged wakefulness extending beyond the normal circadian sleep period, typically defined herein as 30 to 36 hours of continuous sleep prevention, during which the subject is maintained in a state of behavioral wakefulness through environmental and social stimulation. “Delayed-match-to-sample (DMS) task” refers to a cognitive paradigm in which a subject is presented with a sample stimulus, must retain it over a variable delay interval, and then identify it from an array of choice stimuli that include the sample and one or more distractors. Performance is measured by percent correct responses and response latencies across varying delays and memory loads.

“Cerebral metabolic rate for glucose (CMRglc)” refers to the quantitative measure of glucose utilization in discrete brain regions, as determined by positron emission tomography (PET) using [18F]-2-deoxy-2-fluoro-D-glucose (FDG) as a radiotracer. “Region of interest (ROI)” denotes anatomically defined brain structures selected for analysis based on prior evidence of involvement in cognitive tasks, including the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DPFC), dorsal striatum (DStr), medial temporal lobe (MTL), thalamus, and precuneate cortex. “Therapeutically effective amount” means a quantity of an ampakine sufficient to produce a measurable improvement in cognitive performance or neural metabolic activity in a sleep-deprived subject, without causing unacceptable adverse effects.

### II. Non-Human Primate Model for Testing Effects of Sleep Deprivation on a Delayed-Match-to-Sample Task (DMTS)

The present invention utilizes a well-validated non-human primate model, specifically adult male rhesus monkeys (Macaca mulatta), to assess the cognitive and neurobiological effects of sleep deprivation and ampakine intervention. Animals are trained to perform a visual DMS task in which they view a sample image, retain it over a randomly varied delay (1–30 seconds), and then select it from an array of 2–6 images presented simultaneously. The task is designed to tax working memory capacity and is sensitive to both delay duration and memory load, producing a characteristic performance decrement as these parameters increase. Monkeys are tested in a controlled environment with standardized lighting, acoustic conditions, and reinforcement schedules to ensure consistent motivation and performance.

Sleep deprivation is induced by maintaining animals in a continuously lit environment for 30–36 hours, during which they are kept awake through a combination of video stimulation, auditory cues, gentle physical interaction, and intermittent treats. Behavioral testing occurs at the usual daily time to control for circadian influences. Electroencephalographic (EEG) recordings are obtained via chronically implanted electrodes to monitor sleep-like intrusions and validate the sleep-deprived state. This model recapitulates key features of human sleep deprivation, including impaired prefrontal-dependent cognition, altered EEG dynamics, and disrupted regional brain metabolism, making it an ideal platform for evaluating potential countermeasures.

### Salts of Compounds

The ampakine CX717 may be administered in the form of a free base or as a pharmaceutically acceptable salt to improve solubility, stability, or bioavailability. Suitable salts include those formed with inorganic acids such as hydrochloric acid, sulfuric acid, or phosphoric acid, or with organic acids such as acetic acid, citric acid, maleic acid, or fumaric acid. In the experimental protocol described herein, CX717 was formulated as a 1.5 mg/mL solution in 10% w/v hydroxypropyl-beta-cyclodextrin and 0.45% saline, which enhances aqueous solubility and facilitates intravenous delivery. The choice of salt or formulation does not alter the fundamental pharmacological activity of the compound but may influence pharmacokinetic parameters such as absorption rate, peak plasma concentration, and half-life, which are critical for optimizing dosing regimens in clinical applications.

### Pharmaceutical Formulations

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise a therapeutically effective amount of an ampakine, such as CX717, in combination with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents. The formulation may be adapted for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, or intranasal delivery, depending on the desired onset and duration of action. For acute cognitive rescue in sleep-deprived individuals, rapid-onset formulations such as intravenous solutions or fast-dissolving oral tablets are preferred. Sustained-release formulations may be developed for prophylactic use in anticipation of known sleep loss (e.g., before night shifts or military operations).

In addition to the active ingredient, formulations may include solubilizing agents (e.g., cyclodextrins), stabilizers (e.g., antioxidants), pH adjusters, and isotonicity agents to ensure compatibility with biological systems. The composition is preferably sterile and pyrogen-free for parenteral administration. The concentration of CX717 in the final formulation is adjusted to deliver a dose within the effective range of 0.3 to 1.5 mg/kg body weight, as established in the non-human primate model. The invention also encompasses kits containing the pharmaceutical composition along with instructions for use in mitigating cognitive deficits associated with sleep deprivation.

### Dosage

The dosage of ampakine compounds for the treatment of sleep deprivation-induced cognitive impairment is determined based on efficacy and safety data derived from the non-human primate model. In rhesus monkeys, intravenous administration of CX717 at doses ranging from 0.3 to 1.5 mg/kg produced a dose-dependent enhancement of DMS task performance, with maximal effects observed at 0.8–1.5 mg/kg. Doses below 0.5 mg/kg showed minimal or inconsistent efficacy, while doses up to 1.5 mg/kg were well-tolerated without observable adverse effects on motor coordination, arousal, or general behavior.

For human application, the equivalent dose is estimated using standard allometric scaling principles, which account for differences in body surface area and metabolic rate between species. This yields a projected human dose range of approximately 0.1 to 0.5 mg/kg, which may be administered as a single bolus or divided doses depending on the duration of cognitive demand. The optimal timing of administration is 10 to 30 minutes prior to the anticipated period of cognitive challenge, allowing for adequate distribution to the central nervous system and peak receptor occupancy. Repeated dosing may be feasible for extended operations, provided that cumulative effects and potential tolerance are monitored. The invention thus provides a precise, evidence-based dosing regimen that maximizes cognitive benefit while minimizing risk, enabling reliable deployment in high-stakes environments where sleep loss is unavoidable.
