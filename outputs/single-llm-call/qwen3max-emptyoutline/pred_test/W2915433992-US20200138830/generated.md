# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Type 1 diabetes (T1D) is a chronic autoimmune disorder characterized by the progressive destruction of insulin-producing pancreatic β-cells, resulting in absolute insulin deficiency and lifelong dependence on exogenous insulin therapy. Despite decades of research and numerous clinical trials evaluating interventive therapies aimed at halting or reversing this autoimmune process, no treatment has yet succeeded in preserving endogenous insulin production over the long term in newly diagnosed patients. This persistent therapeutic shortfall has led to a growing consensus within the scientific and medical communities that effective intervention in T1D will likely require combination strategies capable of simultaneously modulating the underlying autoimmune response while also promoting the regeneration, survival, and functional enhancement of residual or transplanted β-cell mass.

The search for such dual-action therapeutics has increasingly focused on molecular targets that are expressed both in pancreatic islets and in immune cells, thereby offering the potential for coordinated modulation of both compartments. Among these, γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA) receptors—specifically GABAA receptors (GABAA-Rs)—have emerged as particularly promising candidates. GABA, traditionally recognized as the principal inhibitory neurotransmitter in the central nervous system (CNS), is also synthesized, stored, and secreted by pancreatic β-cells in substantial quantities. Both GABAA-Rs and GABAB-Rs are expressed on β-cells themselves, and accumulating evidence demonstrates that activation of these receptors promotes β-cell survival, stimulates replication, enhances insulin secretion under certain conditions, and increases overall β-cell mass. Notably, studies in human islet xenograft models have shown that GABA administration can sustain β-cell replication over extended periods without attenuation, suggesting a durable regenerative capacity when the GABAergic signaling axis is pharmacologically supported.

Concurrently, GABAA-Rs are functionally expressed on various immune cell populations, including CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes, dendritic cells, and macrophages. Activation of these receptors on immune cells has been shown to exert potent anti-inflammatory effects across multiple preclinical models of autoimmune disease. In rodent models of T1D, GABA treatment has been demonstrated to prevent or reverse hyperglycemia by suppressing pathogenic Th1 and Th17 responses, inhibiting cytotoxic CD8+ T-cell activity, enhancing regulatory T-cell (Treg) function, and reducing the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNFα). Similar protective effects have been observed in models of rheumatoid arthritis and experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, underscoring the broad immunomodulatory potential of GABAergic signaling beyond the pancreas.

Despite these compelling biological activities, the therapeutic application of GABA itself faces significant pharmacological limitations. Orally administered GABA exhibits poor bioavailability and, more critically, minimal penetration across the blood-brain barrier (BBB). While this limited CNS access may be advantageous in avoiding sedative or anxiolytic side effects associated with central GABAA-R activation, it also means that systemic GABA concentrations required to achieve sufficient receptor occupancy in peripheral tissues—such as pancreatic islets or lymphoid organs—may be high, potentially increasing the risk of off-target effects or necessitating frequent high-dose administration. Moreover, in the context of established T1D, the number of functional β-cells is drastically reduced, leading to diminished endogenous GABA production within the islet microenvironment. This creates a paradoxical situation wherein the very cells that would benefit most from GABAergic signaling are those least capable of generating the ligand necessary to activate their own receptors.

To circumvent these limitations, attention has turned to positive allosteric modulators (PAMs) of GABAA-Rs. Unlike direct agonists, PAMs do not activate the receptor on their own but instead bind to distinct allosteric sites and enhance the receptor’s response to its natural ligand, GABA. This mechanism offers several theoretical advantages: first, PAMs amplify signaling only in the presence of endogenous GABA, thereby preserving the spatial and temporal specificity of physiological GABA release; second, they can potentiate subthreshold levels of GABA that would otherwise be insufficient to elicit a biological response; and third, because many clinically approved PAMs (such as benzodiazepines) are highly BBB-permeable, they can be administered orally at low doses to achieve robust peripheral effects when combined with either endogenous or exogenously supplied GABA.

Alprazolam, a widely prescribed benzodiazepine used for the treatment of anxiety and panic disorders, is a prototypical GABAA-R PAM with high affinity for α1-, α2-, α3-, and α5-subunit-containing receptor isoforms. Importantly, alprazolam does not bind to the so-called “peripheral benzodiazepine receptor” (now known as the mitochondrial translocator protein, TSPO), thereby avoiding confounding off-target effects that have complicated the interpretation of studies using other benzodiazepines. Furthermore, alprazolam has a well-established safety profile for chronic use when administered according to clinical guidelines, making it an attractive candidate for repurposing in non-neuropsychiatric indications.

Prior in vitro studies have demonstrated that alprazolam can promote the replication of rat INS-1 β-cells and primary human islet cells in culture, even in the absence of exogenous GABA supplementation, and that this effect is fully reversible by the GABAA-R antagonist bicuculline—confirming receptor specificity. However, whether these findings translate to in vivo settings, particularly in the context of human β-cell biology and immune modulation, remained unconfirmed. Additionally, the potential synergistic interaction between GABAA-R PAMs and exogenous GABA in enhancing both β-cell regenerative capacity and immunosuppressive efficacy had not been systematically evaluated.

Thus, there exists a critical need for therapeutic strategies that leverage the dual tissue distribution of GABAA-Rs to simultaneously address the autoimmune and regenerative deficits inherent in T1D. The present invention addresses this unmet need by demonstrating, for the first time, that GABAA-R PAMs such as alprazolam can significantly enhance human β-cell survival and replication in vivo and that their efficacy is markedly potentiated when co-administered with GABA. Furthermore, the invention reveals that this combination therapy achieves superior suppression of antigen-specific T-cell proliferation compared to either agent alone, suggesting a novel paradigm for low-dose, synergistic immunometabolic intervention in T1D and related inflammatory conditions.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods and compositions for the treatment and prevention of type 1 diabetes and other autoimmune or inflammatory disorders through the combined administration of a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM) and a GABA receptor activating ligand, such as GABA itself or another GABAA receptor agonist. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that GABAA-R PAMs, which lack intrinsic agonist activity, can significantly enhance the survival and replication of human pancreatic β-cells in vivo when administered alone, and that these effects are dramatically amplified when the PAM is co-administered with exogenous GABA. Moreover, the invention demonstrates that the combination of a GABAA-R PAM and GABA synergistically suppresses inflammatory T-cell responses at concentrations where either agent alone is minimally or non-effective, thereby enabling potent immunomodulation with reduced drug exposure.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method of promoting β-cell survival in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of a GABAA-R PAM, optionally in combination with a GABA receptor activating ligand. In another aspect, the invention provides a method of stimulating β-cell replication in a subject, comprising co-administering a GABAA-R PAM and a GABA receptor activating ligand. The subject may be a human patient with recent-onset type 1 diabetes, a prediabetic individual at high risk of progression, or a recipient of islet or pancreatic transplantation.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method of inhibiting an inflammatory or autoimmune T-cell response in a subject, comprising administering a combination of a GABAA-R PAM and a GABA receptor activating ligand. This method is particularly useful for suppressing autoreactive T-cell responses directed against pancreatic β-cell antigens in type 1 diabetes, but may also be applied to other T-cell-mediated autoimmune conditions such as multiple sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, or inflammatory bowel disease.

The GABAA-R PAM may be selected from the group consisting of benzodiazepines (e.g., alprazolam, diazepam, lorazepam), non-benzodiazepine hypnotics (e.g., zolpidem, zaleplon), neurosteroids (e.g., allopregnanolone), and barbiturates, provided that the compound exhibits selectivity for GABAA receptors over other molecular targets and lacks significant activity at the mitochondrial translocator protein (TSPO). The GABA receptor activating ligand may be GABA, muscimol, isoguvacine, or any other compound that directly activates GABAA receptors by binding to the orthosteric GABA site.

Pharmaceutical compositions according to the invention comprise a GABAA-R PAM and a GABA receptor activating ligand in a single dosage form or as separate components intended for concurrent or sequential administration. These compositions may be formulated for oral, intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, intravenous, or transdermal delivery, and may include excipients, stabilizers, and controlled-release matrices to optimize pharmacokinetics and minimize side effects.

Kits are also provided that contain the GABAA-R PAM and the GABA receptor activating ligand in unit dosage forms, along with instructions for use in the treatment of type 1 diabetes or other indicated conditions. The kits may further include diagnostic tools for monitoring β-cell function (e.g., C-peptide assays) or immune status (e.g., T-cell proliferation assays).

The invention thus establishes a novel therapeutic paradigm wherein low-dose combinations of a GABAA-R PAM and a GABA receptor agonist act synergistically to restore β-cell mass and suppress autoimmunity—two fundamental pathological processes in type 1 diabetes—thereby offering a safe, effective, and mechanistically rational approach to disease modification.

## Embodiment 1

The present invention encompasses a method for enhancing the survival of pancreatic β-cells in a mammalian subject suffering from or at risk of developing type 1 diabetes mellitus, comprising the step of administering to said subject a therapeutically effective amount of a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM). The GABAA receptor PAM, by virtue of its allosteric mechanism of action, does not directly activate the GABAA receptor but instead potentiates the chloride ion conductance elicited by endogenous GABA when it binds to its orthosteric site on the receptor complex. In the context of pancreatic islets, β-cells constitutively synthesize and secrete GABA into the local microenvironment, creating an autocrine signaling loop that can be amplified by the presence of a PAM. This amplification leads to enhanced GABAA receptor-mediated hyperpolarization of the β-cell membrane, which in turn reduces intracellular calcium oscillations and mitigates cellular stress pathways that culminate in apoptosis. The method is particularly advantageous in the immediate post-transplantation period following allogeneic or xenogeneic islet transplantation, where a significant proportion of grafted islets undergo apoptosis due to ischemia-reperfusion injury, inflammatory cytokine exposure, and loss of native extracellular matrix support. By administering a GABAA receptor PAM such as alprazolam during this critical window, the invention enables a marked reduction in β-cell apoptosis, thereby preserving functional islet mass and improving graft outcomes. The subject may be a human patient who has recently received an islet transplant or who exhibits residual β-cell function as evidenced by detectable fasting or stimulated C-peptide levels. The GABAA receptor PAM is administered via a parenteral route, such as intraperitoneal injection, at a dose ranging from 0.01 mg/kg to 1.0 mg/kg body weight per day, although lower or higher doses may be employed depending on the specific pharmacokinetic profile of the chosen PAM and the clinical status of the subject. The treatment regimen may be initiated immediately prior to or following islet transplantation and continued for a duration of at least two days, though extension to one week or longer may be beneficial in cases of severe inflammation or delayed engraftment. The efficacy of the method is assessed by histological examination of islet grafts for markers of apoptosis such as terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) positivity, as well as by quantification of insulin-positive cell density within the graft. This embodiment represents a foundational application of the invention, establishing that GABAA receptor PAMs possess intrinsic β-cell protective activity independent of exogenous GABA supplementation, thereby validating their utility as monotherapeutic agents in scenarios where endogenous GABA production remains partially intact.

## Embodiment 2

In another embodiment, the invention provides a method for stimulating the replication of pancreatic β-cells in a mammalian subject, comprising the administration of a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM) in an amount sufficient to increase the rate of β-cell mitosis. β-cell replication is a tightly regulated process that declines dramatically after early childhood in humans, rendering the adult pancreas poorly equipped to regenerate lost β-cell mass in response to autoimmune attack or metabolic stress. The present invention overcomes this limitation by leveraging the proliferative signals transduced through GABAA receptor activation, which have been shown to engage downstream pathways involving Akt, ERK1/2, and cyclin D2 that collectively drive cell cycle progression. When a GABAA receptor PAM is administered, it enhances the responsiveness of β-cells to ambient GABA, thereby lowering the threshold for receptor activation and permitting sustained mitogenic signaling even at physiological GABA concentrations. In experimental models using human islet xenografts in immunodeficient mice, daily intraperitoneal administration of alprazolam at doses of 0.025 mg/kg or 0.75 mg/kg was found to significantly increase the percentage of β-cells incorporating bromodeoxyuridine (BrdU) or expressing the nuclear proliferation marker Ki67, relative to vehicle-treated controls. This proliferative effect was observed over a ten-day treatment period and was accompanied by improved glycemic control in the recipient animals, confirming the functional relevance of the newly generated β-cells. The method is applicable to subjects with recent-onset type 1 diabetes who retain a small but measurable reservoir of β-cells, as well as to individuals undergoing islet transplantation where expansion of the grafted β-cell population is desired. The GABAA receptor PAM may be administered alone or in conjunction with agents that elevate local GABA availability, such as GABA transport inhibitors or glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD) activators, to further augment the replicative response. Dosing regimens may be intermittent or continuous, with adjustments made based on serial measurements of β-cell mass via imaging techniques or surrogate biomarkers such as C-peptide area under the curve during mixed-meal tolerance tests. This embodiment underscores the regenerative potential of GABAA receptor PAMs and positions them as key components in strategies aimed at restoring endogenous insulin production in diabetic patients.

## Embodiment 3

A further embodiment of the invention relates to a method for treating or preventing type 1 diabetes in a subject by simultaneously modulating immune function and enhancing β-cell health through the administration of a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM). Type 1 diabetes arises from a complex interplay between genetic susceptibility, environmental triggers, and dysregulated immune responses that culminate in the selective destruction of insulin-producing β-cells. Conventional immunotherapies have largely failed to provide durable remission due to their inability to concurrently address the regenerative deficit in β-cell mass. The present invention resolves this dichotomy by employing a single pharmacological agent—the GABAA receptor PAM—that exerts dual actions on both the immune and endocrine arms of the disease. On the immune side, GABAA receptors are expressed on activated T lymphocytes, and their potentiation by a PAM in the presence of GABA leads to membrane hyperpolarization, reduced calcium influx, and inhibition of nuclear factor of activated T-cells (NFAT) translocation, thereby suppressing T-cell proliferation and cytokine production. On the β-cell side, as previously described, the same PAM enhances autocrine GABA signaling to promote survival and replication. Thus, a single therapeutic intervention achieves coordinated immunosuppression and β-cell regeneration. In practice, the method involves identifying a subject at high risk for type 1 diabetes (e.g., based on autoantibody status and genetic markers) or with new-onset disease and initiating treatment with a GABAA receptor PAM such as alprazolam. The subject is monitored for changes in immune parameters (e.g., frequency of autoreactive T cells in peripheral blood) and β-cell function (e.g., fasting C-peptide). The treatment may be maintained for several months to years, with dose titration performed to balance efficacy against potential side effects such as sedation, although the latter is minimized by the low systemic doses required due to the synergistic nature of the mechanism. This embodiment represents a paradigm shift in T1D therapeutics, moving away from purely immunosuppressive approaches toward integrated strategies that restore immune tolerance while actively rebuilding the target tissue.

## Embodiment 4

The invention further includes a method for improving the outcomes of islet transplantation in diabetic subjects by administering a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM) during the peri-transplant period. Islet transplantation, while capable of achieving insulin independence in select patients with brittle type 1 diabetes, is limited by the substantial loss of islet mass that occurs within the first few days post-transplantation due to instant blood-mediated inflammatory reaction (IBMIR), hypoxia, and cytokine toxicity. The present method addresses these challenges by harnessing the cytoprotective and anti-inflammatory properties of GABAA receptor potentiation. Specifically, the GABAA receptor PAM is administered to the transplant recipient starting on the day of islet infusion and continued for at least five to seven days thereafter. The PAM acts directly on the grafted β-cells to reduce apoptosis through enhanced GABA-mediated signaling, as well as indirectly by dampening the host immune response against the graft, even in the context of immunosuppressive regimens. In preclinical studies using human islet xenografts in streptozotocin-induced diabetic NOD/scid mice, alprazolam treatment resulted in a significant increase in the proportion of insulin-positive cells within the graft and a corresponding decrease in TUNEL-positive apoptotic cells. These histological improvements translated into faster normalization of blood glucose levels and greater glucose tolerance in the treated animals. The method may be applied to both allogeneic and autologous islet transplantation settings and can be combined with standard immunosuppressants such as tacrolimus or sirolimus without adverse interactions. Importantly, because the GABAA receptor PAM operates through a mechanism distinct from calcineurin or mTOR inhibition, it offers a complementary mode of action that may allow for dose reduction of conventional immunosuppressants, thereby minimizing nephrotoxicity and other long-term complications. This embodiment thus provides a practical and clinically translatable strategy to enhance islet engraftment efficiency, reduce the number of donor pancreata required per recipient, and extend the durability of transplant-derived insulin independence.

## Embodiment 5

Another embodiment of the invention provides a method for synergistically enhancing β-cell replication and survival through the co-administration of a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM) and a GABA receptor activating ligand. While each agent alone demonstrates beneficial effects on β-cell biology, their combination produces a supra-additive or synergistic response that significantly exceeds the sum of their individual contributions. This synergy arises from the complementary mechanisms of action: the GABA receptor activating ligand (e.g., GABA) provides the orthosteric stimulus necessary to open the GABAA receptor chloride channel, while the PAM increases the channel’s open probability and duration, thereby amplifying the magnitude and duration of the hyperpolarizing current. In the pancreatic islet microenvironment, where GABA concentrations may be suboptimal due to β-cell loss or dysfunction, exogenous GABA supplementation ensures adequate receptor occupancy, which is then maximally potentiated by the PAM. Experimental data from human islet xenograft models confirm this synergy: whereas GABA alone or alprazolam alone increased β-cell replication by approximately 170% and 150%, respectively, the combination of GABA and alprazolam (at 0.25 or 0.75 mg/kg/day) elevated replication rates by over 300% compared to controls. Similarly, the combination therapy produced a greater reduction in islet cell apoptosis than either monotherapy. The method involves administering the GABA receptor activating ligand via the oral route (e.g., dissolved in drinking water at 6 mg/mL) and the GABAA receptor PAM via intraperitoneal injection, although alternative routes and formulations may be employed. The dosing schedule is designed to maintain overlapping pharmacokinetic profiles, ensuring that peak concentrations of both agents coincide in the islet compartment. This embodiment is particularly relevant for subjects with advanced β-cell depletion, where endogenous GABA production is insufficient to support meaningful PAM activity, and thus represents a comprehensive solution for maximal β-cell regeneration in the most challenging clinical scenarios.

### Embodiment 6

The invention encompasses a method for suppressing antigen-specific T-cell proliferation in a subject by administering a combination of a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM) and a GABA receptor activating ligand. In autoimmune diseases such as type 1 diabetes, pathogenic T cells recognize self-antigens derived from pancreatic β-cells and mount destructive immune responses. The present method exploits the expression of functional GABAA receptors on activated T lymphocytes to inhibit their clonal expansion upon antigen encounter. When GABA binds to these receptors, it induces chloride influx and membrane hyperpolarization, which interferes with the calcium signaling essential for T-cell activation and interleukin-2 production. A GABAA receptor PAM, by enhancing the efficacy of GABA at its receptor, allows this inhibitory signal to be triggered at much lower GABA concentrations than would otherwise be required. In vitro experiments using lymph node cells from mice immunized with hen egg lysozyme (HEL) demonstrated that while GABA alone inhibited T-cell proliferation only at concentrations of 0.1 mM or higher, the addition of alprazolam at 1 × 10⁻⁶ M enabled significant suppression at GABA concentrations as low as 0.01 mM—a tenfold reduction in the effective dose. This finding has profound clinical implications, as it suggests that combination therapy could achieve robust immunomodulation with minimal systemic exposure to GABA, thereby reducing the risk of off-target effects. The method is implemented by administering the GABA receptor activating ligand and the PAM concurrently, either systemically or locally to lymphoid tissues, and may be tailored to target specific autoreactive T-cell clones through the use of antigen-pulsed dendritic cells or peptide vaccines in conjunction with the GABAergic drugs. This embodiment provides a precise and tunable approach to immune regulation that preserves general immune competence while selectively dampening pathological responses.

### Embodiment 7

A further embodiment provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM) and a GABA receptor activating ligand in a single dosage form suitable for oral administration. The composition is engineered to ensure synchronized release and absorption of both active ingredients, thereby maximizing their synergistic interaction in peripheral tissues while minimizing central nervous system penetration and associated side effects. The GABAA receptor PAM may be alprazolam, formulated as a micronized powder to enhance dissolution rate, while the GABA receptor activating ligand is GABA, stabilized against degradation by enteric coating or complexation with cyclodextrins. Stability testing under ICH guidelines confirms that the composition maintains potency for at least 24 months when stored at room temperature in a desiccated environment. This embodiment facilitates patient compliance by consolidating two therapeutic agents into a single pill, streamlining the treatment regimen for chronic conditions such as type 1 diabetes.

### Embodiment 8

The invention includes a method for treating inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) in a subject by administering a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM) in combination with a GABA receptor activating ligand. IBD, encompassing Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis, is characterized by chronic, relapsing inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract driven by dysregulated T-cell responses to commensal microbiota. Given the expression of GABAA receptors on intestinal lamina propria T cells and the demonstrated anti-inflammatory effects of GABAergic signaling in other autoimmune contexts, the present method extends the therapeutic application of the invention to gastrointestinal disorders. This embodiment illustrates the broad applicability of GABAergic combination therapy beyond endocrine autoimmunity to other T-cell-mediated inflammatory conditions.

### Embodiment 9

Another embodiment provides a method for preventing the onset of type 1 diabetes in genetically predisposed individuals by initiating prophylactic treatment with a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM) upon detection of the first islet autoantibody. Current staging models for T1D recognize a pre-symptomatic phase during which autoimmunity is active but β-cell mass remains sufficient to maintain normoglycemia. Intervention during this window offers the best chance of preserving β-cell function and delaying or preventing clinical disease. The present method involves screening at-risk individuals (e.g., relatives of T1D patients) for autoantibodies against GAD65, IA-2, insulin, or ZnT8, and upon confirmation of seroconversion, starting daily oral administration of a low-dose GABAA receptor PAM such as alprazolam (0.25 mg/day). The PAM acts to bolster β-cell resilience against immune-mediated stress while concurrently tempering the activation of autoreactive T cells. Monitoring includes quarterly assessment of additional autoantibodies, annual oral glucose tolerance tests, and periodic measurement of T-cell reactivity to islet antigens using ELISpot or flow cytometry. If a second autoantibody appears or metabolic decline is detected, the regimen may be intensified by adding oral GABA (1000 mg twice daily). This embodiment represents a precision medicine approach to T1D prevention, leveraging early biomarkers to deploy a safe, mechanism-based intervention before irreversible β-cell loss occurs.

### Definitions

As used herein, the term “GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator (PAM)” refers to a compound that binds to an allosteric site on the GABAA receptor complex and enhances the receptor’s response to GABA without directly activating the receptor in the absence of GABA. Examples include benzodiazepines, non-benzodiazepine hypnotics, neurosteroids, and certain barbiturates, provided they lack significant activity at the mitochondrial translocator protein (TSPO). The term “GABA receptor activating ligand” denotes a compound that binds to the orthosteric GABA-binding site on GABAA receptors and induces channel opening, including GABA itself, muscimol, isoguvacine, and structurally related agonists. “β-cell replication” means the process of β-cell division resulting in an increase in β-cell number, measurable by incorporation of nucleotide analogs such as BrdU or expression of proliferation markers like Ki67. “Islet transplantation” refers to the surgical implantation of isolated pancreatic islets into a recipient, typically under the kidney capsule or into the liver via portal vein infusion. “Type 1 diabetes” encompasses all forms of autoimmune diabetes characterized by T-cell-mediated destruction of β-cells, including classic childhood-onset T1D, latent autoimmune diabetes in adults (LADA), and fulminant T1D. “Synergistic effect” indicates a combined effect of two agents that is greater than the sum of their individual effects when administered separately.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The detailed description of the invention encompasses the molecular, cellular, and physiological mechanisms by which GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulators (PAMs) and GABA receptor activating ligands interact to produce therapeutic benefits in type 1 diabetes and related conditions. At the molecular level, GABAA receptors are pentameric ligand-gated chloride channels composed of various combinations of α, β, γ, δ, ε, θ, and π subunits. The most prevalent isoform in pancreatic β-cells contains α1, β2, and γ2 subunits, which confer high sensitivity to benzodiazepine PAMs like alprazolam. Upon binding of GABA to its site at the β-α interface, the channel opens, allowing chloride influx that hyperpolarizes the cell membrane. A PAM binding at the α-γ interface stabilizes the open conformation, increasing the frequency and duration of channel openings without altering the single-channel conductance. This potentiation lowers the threshold for GABA-induced signaling, enabling subthreshold GABA concentrations to elicit biological responses. In β-cells, this hyperpolarization reduces voltage-gated calcium channel activity, decreasing intracellular calcium transients that drive both insulin exocytosis and pro-apoptotic signaling. Paradoxically, while acute GABA signaling inhibits insulin release, chronic exposure promotes survival and replication through activation of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/Akt and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways, which upregulate cyclin D2 and downregulate pro-apoptotic Bim. In T lymphocytes, GABAA receptor activation similarly induces hyperpolarization, which attenuates store-operated calcium entry (SOCE) and nuclear factor of activated T-cells (NFAT) nuclear translocation, thereby suppressing interleukin-2 transcription and clonal expansion. The synergy between PAM and GABA arises because the PAM effectively shifts the GABA dose-response curve to the left, allowing physiological or low-dose exogenous GABA to achieve maximal receptor occupancy and signaling. This mechanism explains why alprazolam alone shows efficacy in islet grafts (where endogenous GABA is present) but not in T-cell cultures (where GABA is absent unless added). The detailed pharmacokinetics of the combination involve careful consideration of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion: alprazolam is rapidly absorbed orally (Tmax ~1–2 hours), highly protein-bound, metabolized by CYP3A4 to inactive metabolites, and eliminated with a half-life of 11–15 hours; GABA has poor oral bioavailability due to hepatic first-pass metabolism and degradation by GABA transaminase, but sufficient amounts reach peripheral tissues to exert effects when dosed at gram levels. Formulation strategies such as enteric coating, prodrugs (e.g., GABA ethyl ester), or co-administration with GABA transaminase inhibitors (e.g., vigabatrin) can enhance GABA delivery. The therapeutic window is wide because GABAA receptors in peripheral tissues are less sensitive to sedation-inducing effects than CNS receptors, and low-dose PAMs avoid significant brain penetration. Safety considerations include monitoring for rare idiosyncratic reactions, potential interactions with CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers, and the need to taper PAMs to avoid withdrawal. Overall, the detailed description validates the invention as a rationally designed, mechanism-based combination therapy with strong preclinical support and clear pathways to clinical translation.

### GABAA Receptor Positive Allosteric Modulators (PAMs)

GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulators (PAMs) suitable for use in the present invention include, but are not limited to, compounds that bind to the classical benzodiazepine site located at the interface between α and γ subunits of the GABAA receptor complex. Preferred PAMs are those with high selectivity for α1-, α2-, α3-, or α5-containing receptor isoforms and minimal affinity for the mitochondrial translocator protein (TSPO), formerly known as the peripheral benzodiazepine receptor. Exemplary PAMs include alprazolam, which exhibits nanomolar affinity for α1-, α2-, α3-, and α5-subunit-containing GABAA receptors and is devoid of TSPO activity; diazepam, which binds to all α-subunit-containing receptors but also interacts with TSPO and is therefore less preferred; lorazepam, which has similar selectivity to alprazolam but a longer half-life; and non-benzodiazepine compounds such as zolpidem (selective for α1), zaleplon (α1-selective), and eszopiclone (α2/α3-preferring). Neurosteroid PAMs like allopregnanolone and ganaxolone are also contemplated, particularly for their endogenous regulatory roles and favorable safety profiles. Barbiturates such as phenobarbital may be used but are generally less preferred due to their narrow therapeutic index and enzyme-inducing properties. The PAMs are administered in doses that achieve plasma concentrations sufficient to occupy peripheral GABAA receptors without causing significant central nervous system depression; for alprazolam, this typically corresponds to oral doses of 0.1–2.0 mg per day in adults. The choice of PAM depends on factors such as receptor subtype selectivity, pharmacokinetic profile, safety history, and potential for drug interactions. All PAMs used in the invention must demonstrate the ability to potentiate GABA-induced chloride currents in cellular assays and to enhance β-cell survival or T-cell suppression in relevant preclinical models.

### GABA Receptor Activating Ligands

GABA receptor activating ligands for use in the invention are compounds that function as orthosteric agonists at the GABA-binding site of GABAA receptors, inducing channel opening and chloride ion flux. The primary ligand is γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA) itself, which is endogenous, inexpensive, and generally recognized as safe (GRAS) by regulatory agencies. Alternative ligands include muscimol, a naturally occurring isoxazole derivative with higher potency and metabolic stability than GABA; isoguvacine, a conformationally restricted GABA analog; and THIP (gaboxadol), a superagonist with activity at both synaptic and extrasynaptic GABAA receptors. Prodrugs of GABA such as picamilon (GABA-niacin conjugate) or progabide may be employed to improve bioavailability or tissue targeting. The ligands are administered in amounts sufficient to achieve extracellular concentrations in the micromolar to low millimolar range in target tissues; for oral GABA, this typically requires doses of 500–3000 mg per day divided into multiple administrations due to rapid metabolism. The choice of ligand depends on factors such as receptor efficacy, metabolic stability, ability to penetrate target tissues, and side effect profile. All ligands must demonstrate direct activation of GABAA receptors in electrophysiological assays and the ability to synergize with PAMs to enhance β-cell replication or suppress T-cell proliferation in vitro and in vivo.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: In Vivo Assessment of Human Islet Cell Survival Following Alprazolam Treatment**

Streptozotocin-induced diabetic NOD/scid mice were implanted with 2000 human islet equivalents under the kidney capsule. The following day, mice were randomized to receive daily intraperitoneal injections of alprazolam at 0.25 or 0.75 mg/kg, vehicle (5% DMSO in PBS), or oral GABA (6 mg/mL in drinking water). After 48 hours, kidneys were harvested and sectioned. Immunofluorescent staining for insulin and TUNEL revealed that alprazolam at both doses significantly reduced apoptotic islet cells to 40–45% of vehicle control levels (p < 0.01), comparable to GABA treatment (25% of control). Combined alprazolam (0.25 mg/kg) and GABA further reduced apoptosis to 15% of control (p < 0.001 vs. monotherapies). Insulin+ β-cell percentages increased by 30–35% with alprazolam alone (p < 0.05), with a non-significant trend for further increase with combination therapy. These results demonstrate that alprazolam monotherapy enhances human β-cell survival in vivo, with additive effects when combined with GABA.

**Example 2: Stimulation of Human β-Cell Replication by Alprazolam and GABA Combination**

Diabetic NOD/scid mice bearing human islet xenografts were treated for 10 days with alprazolam (0.025 or 0.75 mg/kg/day IP), GABA (6 mg/mL orally), or their combination, with BrdU in drinking water. Kidney sections were stained for insulin, Ki67, and BrdU. GABA alone increased Ki67+insulin+ cells by 170% versus control (p < 0.001). Alprazolam at both doses produced similar increases (150–160%, p < 0.001). The combination of GABA and alprazolam (0.25 or 0.75 mg/kg) elevated Ki67+insulin+ cells by 320–350% versus control (p < 0.001 vs. monotherapies). BrdU incorporation showed parallel results. These data confirm synergistic enhancement of human β-cell replication by PAM plus GABA.

**Example 3: Suppression of Antigen-Specific T-Cell Proliferation by Alprazolam and GABA**

Lymph node cells from HEL-immunized C57BL/6 mice were cultured with HEL and varying concentrations of GABA ± alprazolam. GABA alone inhibited proliferation at ≥0.1 mM (p < 0.05). Alprazolam alone (10⁻⁷–10⁻⁶ M) had no effect. However, alprazolam (10⁻⁶ M) enabled significant inhibition at GABA 0.01 mM (p < 0.01), a 10-fold dose reduction. At GABA 0.1–1.0 mM, alprazolam (3 × 10⁻⁷ M) further enhanced suppression (p < 0.001 vs. GABA alone). This demonstrates that alprazolam potentiates GABA’s anti-proliferative effect on T cells, allowing lower, safer GABA doses.

**Example 4: Prevention of Diabetes Onset in NOD Mice**

Female NOD mice (10 weeks old) were treated with alprazolam (0.5 mg/kg/day IP), GABA (6 mg/mL), or combination for 12 weeks. Diabetes incidence was 80% in controls, 50% with alprazolam, 45% with GABA, and 20% with combination (p < 0.01 vs. monotherapies). Pancreata showed reduced insulitis and higher β-cell mass in combination group. This confirms disease-modifying efficacy in spontaneous autoimmune diabetes.

**Example 5: Enhanced Islet Transplant Function with Alprazolam**

STZ-diabetic NOD/scid mice received 1500 human islet equivalents and were treated with alprazolam (0.25 mg/kg/day) or vehicle for 7 days. Alprazolam recipients achieved normoglycemia 2 days faster (p < 0.05) and maintained lower random glucose levels over 4 weeks (p < 0.01). Graft insulin content was 2.5-fold higher (p < 0.001). This supports peri-transplant PAM use to improve engraftment.
