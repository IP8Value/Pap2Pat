# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of assisted reproductive technology (ART), and more particularly to methods and systems for predicting the pregnancy potential of human oocytes based on gene expression profiles in cumulus cells. The invention provides a robust, clinically applicable framework for selecting the single most viable embryo for transfer during in vitro fertilization (IVF) procedures, thereby enhancing the likelihood of achieving a successful live birth while minimizing the risks associated with multiple gestations. Specifically, the invention leverages quantitative measurements of mRNA expression levels of a defined panel of genes—namely ephrin-B2 (EFNB2), glutathione S-transferase alpha 4 (GSTA4), progesterone receptor (PGR), glutathione peroxidase 3 (GPX3), and glutathione S-transferase alpha 3 (GSTA3)—in cumulus cells that are physically and functionally coupled to individual oocytes during folliculogenesis and ovulation. These cumulus cells, which are routinely removed during standard intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) procedures, serve as a non-invasive biological proxy for oocyte competence. The invention further incorporates patient-specific clinical parameters—including maternal age, duration of ovarian stimulation, and relative serum estradiol (E2) levels—to refine predictive accuracy. The resulting multivariable algorithm generates a quantitative score representing the probability of live birth following single embryo transfer (SET), enabling embryologists and clinicians to make data-driven decisions regarding embryo selection. This approach is particularly valuable in the context of elective SET policies, where the identification of the single highest-quality embryo is critical to maintaining high cumulative live birth rates without resorting to multiple embryo transfers. The invention thus bridges molecular diagnostics with clinical reproductive decision-making, offering a novel, evidence-based tool to improve outcomes in human infertility treatment.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Assisted reproductive technologies have revolutionized the management of human infertility, with in vitro fertilization (IVF) and its derivative techniques now accounting for a significant proportion of births in many developed nations. A central challenge in contemporary IVF practice is the optimization of embryo selection to maximize the chance of a healthy singleton live birth while avoiding the well-documented complications of multiple pregnancies, including preterm delivery, low birth weight, preeclampsia, and neonatal intensive care unit admissions. In response, single embryo transfer (SET) has emerged as the gold standard in many jurisdictions, particularly for patients with a favorable prognosis. However, the success of SET hinges critically on the ability to accurately identify the single embryo with the highest developmental and implantation potential from among those available in a given cycle. Traditional embryo selection relies predominantly on morphological assessment—evaluating features such as cell number, symmetry, fragmentation, and blastocyst expansion—performed at discrete time points during in vitro culture. While widely used due to its simplicity and non-invasiveness, morphological grading suffers from significant limitations, including inter-observer variability, static snapshots that fail to capture dynamic developmental kinetics, and an inability to discern chromosomal normality or metabolic fitness. Consequently, even embryos classified as “high quality” by morphological criteria may fail to implant or result in early pregnancy loss, underscoring the need for more objective, biologically grounded selection criteria.

In recent years, attention has turned to the molecular microenvironment surrounding the oocyte as a source of predictive biomarkers. Cumulus cells (CCs), which form a multilayered complex around the oocyte and remain in intimate contact throughout follicular development and ovulation, are uniquely positioned to reflect oocyte health and competence. These somatic cells engage in bidirectional communication with the oocyte via gap junctions and paracrine signaling, exchanging metabolites, ions, and regulatory molecules essential for coordinated maturation. Importantly, CCs are routinely aspirated and discarded during the denudation step preceding ICSI, rendering them an ethically and practically accessible source of biological material without compromising the oocyte itself. Numerous studies have explored the correlation between CC gene expression and various endpoints of ART success, including embryo morphology, blastocyst formation, chromosomal euploidy, and clinical pregnancy. Early investigations identified candidate genes—such as hyaluronan synthase 2 (HAS2), versican (VCAN), and syndecan 4 (SDC4)—whose expression levels appeared associated with oocyte quality. However, replication across independent cohorts has proven challenging, with inconsistent findings attributed to heterogeneity in patient populations, ovarian stimulation protocols, culture media formulations, and analytical methodologies. For instance, HAS2 was reported to be upregulated in CCs associated with good-quality embryos in some studies but showed no differential expression in others, highlighting the vulnerability of single-gene biomarkers to confounding variables.

This lack of reproducibility has spurred interest in multivariable modeling approaches that integrate multiple gene transcripts to enhance robustness and predictive power. Such models aim to capture the complexity of oocyte-cumulus interactions by considering synergistic or compensatory effects among biological pathways. Prior work by the inventors established a three-gene signature—comprising ephrin-B2 (EFNB2), calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase ID (CAMK1D), and stanniocalcin 1 (STC1)—that demonstrated promising accuracy in predicting pregnancy outcomes in a specific patient cohort undergoing ICSI with a standardized GnRH antagonist protocol. However, the generalizability of this model remained unconfirmed, and opportunities existed to refine the gene panel by incorporating novel candidates emerging from high-throughput genomic screens. Moreover, existing studies have been limited to inter-patient comparisons—contrasting CC profiles between women who achieved pregnancy versus those who did not—without addressing the more clinically relevant scenario of intra-patient embryo ranking. In real-world SET practice, clinicians must choose among multiple embryos derived from the same oocyte retrieval cycle, necessitating a method capable of discriminating relative oocyte quality within an individual patient, where systemic factors such as age, hormonal milieu, and stimulation response are held constant. To date, no study has prospectively validated a CC-based gene expression model in such an intra-patient context, representing a critical gap in translational applicability.

Compounding these challenges is the influence of exogenous and endogenous variables on CC transcriptomes. Ovarian stimulation protocols—differing in gonadotropin type, dose, and duration—can modulate gene expression patterns in follicular cells. Similarly, variations in culture media composition across IVF laboratories may introduce batch effects that obscure true biological signals related to oocyte competence. Additionally, patient characteristics such as body mass index (BMI), infertility etiology, and parity may indirectly affect CC biology. A truly robust predictive model must either demonstrate insensitivity to such confounders or explicitly account for them through statistical adjustment. The prior art lacks a comprehensive framework that integrates molecular biomarkers with key clinical covariates to produce a calibrated probability estimate of live birth, tailored to the individual patient’s context. Furthermore, the functional relevance of proposed biomarker genes remains poorly understood, limiting mechanistic insights and hindering rational refinement of the gene panel. For example, while EFNB2 has been implicated in luteinization and vascular remodeling, and glutathione-related enzymes (e.g., GPX3, GSTA3/4) in oxidative stress defense, their precise roles in human oocyte competence are speculative. Without validation in controlled experimental settings or animal models, the biological plausibility of candidate genes remains uncertain.

In summary, the background art reveals a field characterized by fragmented discoveries, limited reproducibility, and insufficient clinical validation. There exists a clear and unmet need for a standardized, multivariable diagnostic method that: (1) utilizes a minimal yet highly informative gene panel derived through iterative validation; (2) demonstrates consistent performance across independent patient cohorts, even when culture conditions vary; (3) enables accurate intra-patient ranking of oocyte potential—a prerequisite for effective SET; and (4) optionally incorporates readily available clinical parameters to further enhance predictive precision. The present invention directly addresses these shortcomings by introducing a rigorously validated five-gene signature, augmented with three clinical variables, that achieves high accuracy in both inter- and intra-patient prediction of live birth following SET. By transforming discarded cumulus cells into a rich source of prognostic information, the invention offers a practical, non-invasive solution to one of the most persistent challenges in modern reproductive medicine.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for predicting the likelihood of live birth following single embryo transfer in a human subject undergoing assisted reproductive technology, the method comprising: (a) obtaining cumulus cells associated with a human oocyte retrieved during an in vitro fertilization cycle; (b) quantifying the expression levels of a panel of genes in the cumulus cells, wherein the panel consists of ephrin-B2 (EFNB2), glutathione S-transferase alpha 4 (GSTA4), progesterone receptor (PGR), glutathione peroxidase 3 (GPX3), and glutathione S-transferase alpha 3 (GSTA3); (c) optionally determining one or more clinical parameters of the subject selected from the group consisting of maternal age, duration of ovarian stimulation, and relative serum estradiol level; and (d) calculating a predictive score using a multivariable regression model that incorporates the gene expression levels and, when determined, the clinical parameters, wherein the predictive score represents the probability of live birth upon transfer of an embryo derived from the oocyte. In a preferred embodiment, the gene expression levels are normalized to the geometric mean of beta-2-microglobulin (B2M) and ubiquitin C (UBC) reference genes. The method may be applied to select the single oocyte or embryo with the highest predicted probability of live birth from among multiple candidates available in a single retrieval cycle, thereby facilitating optimal single embryo transfer. The invention further encompasses a diagnostic kit comprising reagents for quantifying the expression of the five-gene panel, including sequence-specific primers and probes for quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), as well as software or computational means for executing the predictive algorithm. Additionally, the invention includes a system for implementing the method, comprising a sample processing module for cumulus cell isolation and RNA extraction, a nucleic acid amplification and detection module, and a data analysis module configured to apply the regression model and output a probability score. The predictive model has been validated in both inter-patient and intra-patient analyses, demonstrating high accuracy (up to 93%), positive predictive value (93%), negative predictive value (93%), and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC ≥ 0.93). The invention overcomes limitations of prior art by employing a cascade validation strategy to identify the most robust predictive genes, confirming performance across different culture media, and demonstrating the first successful intra-patient ranking of oocyte potential using cumulus cell gene expression. This enables clinicians to make informed, individualized decisions in single embryo transfer scenarios, ultimately improving live birth rates while adhering to best practices for minimizing multiple pregnancies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a novel and non-obvious method for predicting the pregnancy potential of human oocytes based on the quantitative expression of a specific panel of genes in cumulus cells, optionally combined with key clinical parameters. This method represents a significant advancement over existing embryo selection techniques by providing an objective, molecularly grounded assessment of oocyte competence that can be integrated into routine assisted reproductive technology (ART) workflows. The foundation of the invention lies in the recognition that cumulus cells, which are in direct physical and biochemical communication with the oocyte throughout its final stages of maturation, harbor transcriptional signatures reflective of the oocyte's intrinsic developmental capacity. By analyzing these signatures, it is possible to generate a probabilistic forecast of whether an embryo derived from a given oocyte will result in a live birth following single embryo transfer (SET).

The method begins with the collection of cumulus cells associated with individual human oocytes during a standard in vitro fertilization (IVF) or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) cycle. Following transvaginal oocyte retrieval, typically performed approximately 36 hours after administration of a human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) trigger, each oocyte is surrounded by a cumulus-oocyte complex (COC). During the denudation step, which is necessary to visualize the oocyte for ICSI, the cumulus cells are enzymatically and mechanically removed. In accordance with the invention, these cumulus cells are not discarded but instead collected individually for each oocyte, ensuring traceability between the cellular sample and its corresponding oocyte. The cumulus cells are immediately stabilized, preferably by rapid freezing in liquid nitrogen or immersion in an RNA-preserving reagent, to prevent degradation of RNA and preserve the native gene expression profile at the time of retrieval.

Subsequently, total RNA is extracted from the cumulus cell sample using methods known in the art, such as column-based purification kits (e.g., RNeasy Micro Kit, Qiagen) that include rigorous DNase treatment steps to eliminate genomic DNA contamination. The integrity and quantity of the extracted RNA may be assessed using standard techniques, although the small cell numbers typical of individual cumulus complexes often necessitate proceeding directly to reverse transcription without extensive quality control. Complementary DNA (cDNA) is then synthesized from the RNA template using a reverse transcriptase enzyme and random or oligo-dT primers, as per manufacturer protocols (e.g., iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit, Bio-Rad). Appropriate negative controls, such as reactions omitting either the reverse transcriptase enzyme or the RNA template, are included to monitor for contamination or spurious amplification.

The core of the invention involves the quantitative measurement of mRNA expression levels for a defined panel of five genes: ephrin-B2 (EFNB2), glutathione S-transferase alpha 4 (GSTA4), progesterone receptor (PGR), glutathione peroxidase 3 (GPX3), and glutathione S-transferase alpha 3 (GSTA3). These genes were identified through a systematic, multi-stage validation process involving consecutive patient cohorts and represent the most robust predictors of live birth outcome. Quantification is preferably performed using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), a highly sensitive and specific technique well-suited for low-abundance transcripts in limited samples. Gene-specific primer pairs, designed to span exon-exon junctions to avoid amplification of genomic DNA, are employed in multiplex or singleplex reactions. Amplification is carried out in a thermal cycler equipped with fluorescence detection capabilities, and cycle threshold (Ct) values are determined for each target gene.

To account for technical variability in RNA input, cDNA synthesis efficiency, and PCR amplification, the raw Ct values for the target genes are normalized to the expression of one or more stable reference genes. Through empirical validation, beta-2-microglobulin (B2M) and ubiquitin C (UBC) have been identified as suitable normalization controls in cumulus cells under the conditions described herein. Normalization is preferably performed by calculating the geometric mean of the B2M and UBC Ct values for each sample and subtracting this mean from the Ct value of each target gene, yielding ΔCt values. Alternatively, relative expression levels may be calculated using the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method, though the use of ΔCt values directly in the regression model is equally valid and often more straightforward.

In addition to the molecular measurements, the method may incorporate certain clinical parameters of the patient from whom the oocyte was retrieved. These parameters include maternal age (in years), the total number of days of ovarian stimulation with exogenous gonadotropins, and the relative serum estradiol (E2) level, which is calculated as the peak E2 concentration divided by the number of mature oocytes retrieved. These variables are readily obtainable from standard IVF cycle records and have been shown to provide incremental predictive value when combined with the gene expression data. Notably, other common clinical factors such as body mass index (BMI), infertility diagnosis, or progesterone levels at trigger did not significantly improve model performance in the validation cohort and are therefore not required.

The predictive score is generated by applying a multivariable linear regression model to the normalized gene expression values and, optionally, the clinical parameters. The mathematical formulation of the model is as follows:

Predictive Score = a + b₁(EFNB2) + b₂(GSTA4) + b₃(PGR) + b₄(GPX3) + b₅(GSTA3) + c₁(Age) + c₂(Stimulation Days) + c₃(Relative E2)

where:
- a is the intercept constant,
- b₁ through b₅ are the regression coefficients for the respective gene expression values (expressed as ΔCt or normalized relative quantities),
- c₁ through c₃ are the regression coefficients for the clinical parameters,
- and all coefficients are derived from training on a representative patient cohort using stepwise regression with predefined entry and removal criteria (e.g., type III p-value < 0.3 for variable inclusion).

In a preferred embodiment, the full model incorporating both genes and clinical parameters is used, as it achieves the highest accuracy (93%) and area under the curve (AUC = 0.95). However, a gene-only model (comprising the same five genes) also demonstrates excellent performance (accuracy = 81%, AUC = 0.93) and may be employed when clinical data is unavailable or when seeking a purely molecular assessment. The output of the model is a continuous numerical score that can be transformed into a probability of live birth using logistic regression or empirically derived calibration curves. Clinically, this score enables direct comparison between multiple oocytes or embryos from the same retrieval cycle, allowing the selection of the single candidate with the highest predicted success rate for transfer.

The invention further contemplates the use of this method in both fresh and frozen-thawed embryo transfer cycles. Validation studies have confirmed that the gene expression signature remains predictive even when embryos are cryopreserved and transferred in a subsequent frozen cycle, indicating that the molecular imprint in cumulus cells reflects inherent oocyte quality rather than transient culture conditions. Moreover, the method has been successfully applied in an intra-patient context—comparing cumulus cells from sibling oocytes within the same woman—demonstrating its utility for real-world embryo ranking decisions. This intra-patient validation is a critical advance, as it mirrors the actual clinical scenario where choices must be made among genetically and environmentally matched embryos.

From a practical standpoint, the method can be implemented in a diagnostic laboratory setting using standard molecular biology equipment. The entire workflow—from cumulus cell collection to predictive score generation—can be completed within 24 to 48 hours, allowing results to inform embryo transfer decisions in fresh cycles or to prioritize embryos for cryopreservation in freeze-all protocols. The invention also encompasses kits containing all necessary reagents for RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and qPCR amplification of the five-gene panel, along with instructions for use and access to the predictive algorithm via software or web-based platforms. Additionally, automated systems integrating sample processing, nucleic acid analysis, and data interpretation may be developed to streamline implementation in high-volume IVF centers.

In summary, the detailed description outlines a comprehensive, clinically actionable method that transforms cumulus cells—a previously discarded byproduct of ICSI—into a powerful diagnostic tool for oocyte selection. By combining a rigorously validated gene expression signature with simple clinical metrics, the invention provides unprecedented accuracy in predicting live birth potential, thereby supporting the global shift toward safer, more effective single embryo transfer practices.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, the predictive model of the present invention was validated using an independent cohort of 47 patients undergoing intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) with a standardized GnRH antagonist stimulation protocol. All patients provided written informed consent, and the study was approved by the Ethical Committee of UZ Brussel. The patient population included individuals with diverse infertility etiologies, including male factor (n=19), female factor (n=5), combined male and female factor (n=3), and idiopathic infertility (n=20). All participants underwent single embryo transfer (SET), with 20 patients receiving day 3 embryo transfers and 27 receiving day 5 blastocyst transfers, as determined by clinical protocol prior to oocyte retrieval. Critically, all pregnancies resulting from the study culminated in live births, providing a definitive endpoint for model validation.

Cumulus cells were collected individually from each oocyte selected for embryo transfer. Following oocyte retrieval 36 hours post-hCG trigger, cumulus-oocyte complexes were denuded using Cumulase enzyme in HTF-SSS medium, and the liberated cumulus cells were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Micro Kit with dual DNase treatments, and cDNA was synthesized using the iScript kit. Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) was performed for the five-gene panel (EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, GSTA3) along with reference genes B2M and UBC, using 10 µl reaction volumes and previously published or newly designed primer sequences. Gene expression values were normalized to the geometric mean of B2M and UBC.

The inter-patient analysis compared cumulus cells from oocytes that resulted in live birth (n=19) versus those that did not (n=28). Univariate t-tests revealed that EFNB2 was significantly upregulated in the pregnant group (p<0.0042 after Bonferroni correction), while CAMK1D, GSTA4, and GSR showed trends toward higher expression. Stepwise multiple regression analysis was then employed to construct predictive models. A five-gene model incorporating EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3 yielded a model p-value of <0.0001, with 78% positive predictive value (PPV), 83% negative predictive value (NPV), 81% accuracy, and an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.93. When clinical parameters—maternal age, days of stimulation, and relative estradiol—were added to the model, performance improved markedly: PPV, NPV, and accuracy all reached 93%, with an AUC of 0.95. The full regression equation was:

Predictive Score = 12.34 – 1.87(EFNB2) – 1.52(GSTA4) – 1.21(PGR) – 0.98(GPX3) – 0.89(GSTA3) + 0.15(Age) + 0.32(Stimulation Days) – 0.41(Relative E2)

(Note: Coefficients are illustrative; actual values derived from data.) This example demonstrates that the five-gene signature, especially when augmented with clinical data, provides highly accurate prediction of live birth in an independent patient cohort, confirming the robustness of the invention across different culture media (SAGE sequential media in this study versus earlier media formulations).

### Example 2

This example illustrates the intra-patient validation of the predictive model, a critical test of its clinical utility for embryo ranking within a single oocyte retrieval cycle. From the original cohort of 47 patients, seven individuals were identified who had undergone both a fresh SET cycle (resulting in no pregnancy) and a subsequent frozen single embryo transfer (FRET) cycle using a sibling embryo from the same retrieval, which resulted in a live birth. For these patients, cumulus cells from both the non-pregnant (fresh transfer) and pregnant (frozen transfer) oocytes were available for analysis, enabling paired comparisons under identical genetic and hormonal conditions.

RNA was extracted and qPCR performed as in Example 1 for seven genes: the five core genes of the invention plus CAMK1D and GSR, which showed strong trends in inter-patient analysis. Paired t-tests revealed that EFNB2 exhibited a statistically significant increase in expression in cumulus cells associated with the pregnant oocyte (p=0.03), while CAMK1D, GSR, PGR, and GSTA4 showed upward trends (p<0.10). Average fold-changes ranged from 1.1 to 2.5, indicating consistent directional differences. When individual genes were used to predict which oocyte would lead to pregnancy (based on higher expression in the pregnant sample), correct predictions were made in 71% to 88% of cases.

More importantly, the multivariable models developed in the inter-patient analysis (Examples 1 and 3) were applied to rank the two cumulus cell samples per patient. Since all clinical parameters are identical within a patient, only the gene-only models (three-gene and five-gene versions) were used. Remarkably, in six out of seven patients, the model correctly assigned a higher predictive score to the cumulus cells from the oocyte that ultimately resulted in live birth. The single misclassification occurred in Patient 4, where four of the five individual genes correctly predicted the outcome, suggesting that non-oocyte factors—such as endometrial receptivity during the fresh transfer—may have contributed to the initial failure. This example provides the first evidence that cumulus cell gene expression can reliably rank sibling oocytes by pregnancy potential, fulfilling a key requirement for clinical adoption in SET programs.

### Example 3

This example details the stepwise development and comparative performance of multiple predictive models using the 47-patient cohort. Initially, a previously published three-gene model (CAMK1D, EFNB2, STC1) was tested on the current dataset, yielding 62% PPV, 86% NPV, and 72% accuracy—confirming partial reproducibility but indicating room for improvement. Next, all 12 candidate genes (the original three plus nine new candidates from unpublished array data) were screened via univariate analysis, identifying EFNB2 as the only significantly differentially expressed gene.

Four distinct regression models were then constructed:
- Model 1: Restricted to three genes from the 12 candidates. Selected genes: EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR. Performance: 68% PPV, 79% NPV, 73% accuracy, AUC=0.82.
- Model 1bis: Model 1 plus clinical parameters. No improvement observed, indicating the three-gene set was minimally confounded by patient factors.
- Model 2: Allowed inclusion of >3 genes if they improved model p-value. Selected genes: EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, GSTA3. Performance: 78% PPV, 83% NPV, 81% accuracy, AUC=0.93.
- Model 3: Model 2 plus clinical parameters (age, stimulation days, relative E2). Performance: 93% PPV, 93% NPV, 93% accuracy, AUC=0.95.

Statistical analysis confirmed that gene expression variables consistently had lower type III p-values than clinical parameters, underscoring their primary contribution to predictive power. Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves visually demonstrated the superior discrimination of Model 3. This example highlights the iterative refinement process that led to the final five-gene plus clinical parameter model, showcasing its superiority over simpler or prior-art models.

### Example 4

This example explores the biological plausibility and functional implications of the five-gene signature. EFNB2, a member of the ephrin family of membrane-bound ligands, is implicated in cell-cell communication, angiogenesis, and tissue patterning. In the ovary, B-class ephrins have been proposed to regulate luteinization, and EFNB2 receptors show differential expression in cumulus cells surrounding euploid versus aneuploid oocytes, suggesting a role in chromosomal integrity. Progesterone receptor (PGR) mediates the effects of progesterone, a key hormone in ovulation and luteal function. Its presence in predictive models aligns with evidence linking cumulus cell steroidogenic capacity to oocyte competence; notably, PGR strongly correlated with CAMK1D (p<0.0001), a kinase involved in steroidogenesis pathways.

The remaining three genes—GSTA3, GSTA4, and GPX3—are all involved in glutathione metabolism and oxidative stress defense. Glutathione S-transferases (GSTs) conjugate glutathione to electrophilic toxins, while glutathione peroxidase (GPX) reduces hydrogen peroxide and lipid hydroperoxides. Oxidative stress is a known detriment to oocyte quality, causing DNA damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis in both cumulus cells and oocytes. Higher expression of these detoxifying enzymes in cumulus cells associated with successful pregnancies suggests enhanced protection against reactive oxygen species (ROS), potentially preserving oocyte viability during in vitro culture. This mechanistic insight not only validates the biological relevance of the gene panel but also opens avenues for therapeutic interventions aimed at modulating oxidative stress in ART.

Furthermore, the consistency of EFNB2 and GSTA4 across multiple models (present in all three inter-patient models with type III p-values <0.01 in two of three) underscores their reliability as core biomarkers. The fact that these genes retained significance despite changes in culture media (from prior studies to the current SAGE media) indicates resilience to common laboratory variables. This example thus bridges molecular diagnostics with reproductive physiology, reinforcing the invention’s foundation in sound biological principles while highlighting its potential to guide both diagnostic and therapeutic strategies in infertility treatment.
