# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Literature

Human endogenous retroviruses (HERVs) constitute a substantial fraction of the human genome, representing approximately 8.29% of genomic DNA. These sequences are remnants of ancient retroviral infections that integrated into the germ line of ancestral primates and have been vertically transmitted through generations as inherited genetic elements. Over evolutionary time, most HERV sequences have accumulated mutations, deletions, and epigenetic silencing mechanisms that render them transcriptionally and translationally inactive under normal physiological conditions. Nevertheless, certain HERV families retain open reading frames capable of encoding proteins or protein fragments, and under specific pathological or immunological contexts, these elements may be reactivated and expressed at detectable levels.

The expression of HERVs has been implicated in a variety of disease states, including autoimmune disorders, neurological conditions, and cancers. However, their role in infectious diseases, particularly those caused by exogenous retroviruses such as human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1), has only recently begun to attract scientific attention. HIV-1 infection profoundly alters the intracellular environment of host cells, disrupting normal regulatory pathways that maintain HERV quiescence. For instance, the HIV-1 accessory protein Vif counteracts the antiviral activity of APOBEC3 family proteins, which are known to restrict both exogenous retroviruses and endogenous retroelements. This suppression of intrinsic immunity may inadvertently facilitate the transcription and potential translation of HERV sequences that would otherwise remain silenced.

Moreover, HIV-1 employs the Rev protein to mediate nuclear export of unspliced and partially spliced viral RNAs via the Rev-responsive element (RRE). Interestingly, some HERV transcripts contain sequences structurally or functionally analogous to the RRE, enabling them to hijack the HIV-1 Rev-mediated export machinery. Consequently, in HIV-1-infected cells, HERV-derived RNAs may accumulate in the cytoplasm at levels significantly higher than in uninfected cells, thereby increasing the likelihood of translation into polypeptides that can be processed and presented by major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I molecules—also known as human leukocyte antigen (HLA) class I in humans.

The presentation of HERV-derived peptides on the surface of HIV-1-infected cells creates a unique immunological scenario: while these peptides originate from self-genomic elements, their aberrant expression in the context of viral infection may break immune tolerance and elicit CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) responses. Such responses could theoretically target any cell expressing HERV antigens, including HIV-1-infected cells, thereby contributing to viral control. This concept challenges the traditional view that antiviral immunity is solely directed against foreign pathogen-derived epitopes and suggests that endogenous retroviral elements may serve as surrogate targets for immune surveillance during retroviral infections.

Prior studies have detected HERV RNA in the plasma of HIV-1-infected individuals, with levels correlating with markers of disease progression. Additionally, reconstructed infectious HERV-K particles have demonstrated that at least some HERV families retain functional coding capacity. Despite these findings, the immunogenicity of HERV-encoded polypeptides and the potential for HERV-specific T cell responses to influence HIV-1 pathogenesis remained largely unexplored until recently. The present invention builds upon emerging evidence that HERV expression is upregulated during HIV-1 infection and that this upregulation can lead to the generation of antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses capable of recognizing and eliminating infected cells.

Critically, the distinction between cross-reactive T cell responses—those recognizing epitopes shared between HIV-1 and HERVs—and truly independent HERV-specific responses is essential for understanding the breadth and specificity of the anti-retroviral immune response. While sequence homology between HIV-1 and certain HERV proteins exists in discrete regions, particularly within conserved domains such as reverse transcriptase (RT) and Gag, many HERV epitopes are unique and do not share significant identity with HIV-1. The ability of the immune system to mount responses against these unique HERV epitopes suggests that HERVs may represent a previously underappreciated component of the host’s defense against HIV-1.

Furthermore, phenotypic analyses of virus-specific CD8+ T cells have revealed that effective control of chronic viral infections, such as cytomegalovirus (CMV), is associated with terminally differentiated effector memory T cells expressing CD45RA and lacking CCR7 (CCR7−CD45RA+), whereas HIV-1-specific CD8+ T cells often exhibit an exhausted or dysfunctional phenotype characterized by intermediate differentiation states and reduced proliferative and cytotoxic capacity. The observation that HERV-specific CD8+ T cells more closely resemble CMV-specific than HIV-1-specific T cells in their surface marker expression profile provides compelling evidence that these cells are not merely bystanders but may be functionally competent effectors capable of contributing to viral containment.

In summary, the literature establishes that HERVs are normally silent genomic elements that can be reactivated during HIV-1 infection due to viral interference with host restriction mechanisms and RNA trafficking pathways. This reactivation leads to the production of HERV-derived RNA and potentially polypeptides that are processed and presented via HLA class I, thereby exposing the immune system to self-antigens in an inflammatory context conducive to breaking tolerance. The resulting CD8+ T cell responses against HERV epitopes—both cross-reactive and unique—may play a previously unrecognized role in modulating HIV-1 disease progression, offering novel avenues for therapeutic intervention and vaccine design.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides isolated polypeptides derived from human endogenous retroviruses (HERVs), compositions comprising said polypeptides, and methods of using these polypeptides to elicit or modulate immune responses, particularly CD8+ T cell responses, in the context of HIV-1 infection and related conditions. Specifically, the invention discloses that HERV polypeptides, including those derived from HERV-L, HERV-H, HERV-K, and other HERV families, are expressed at elevated levels in HIV-1-infected individuals and can serve as targets for cytotoxic T lymphocytes. These HERV-derived polypeptides, when presented by HLA class I molecules on the surface of infected cells, are recognized by CD8+ T cells, leading to the lysis of target cells and contributing to the control of HIV-1 viremia.

The invention further encompasses fusion proteins comprising HERV polypeptides linked to heterologous protein domains to enhance stability, immunogenicity, or delivery. Multimerized forms of HERV polypeptides are also provided, wherein multiple copies of a HERV epitope or full-length polypeptide are arranged to increase antigen density and T cell receptor engagement. Additionally, the invention includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising HERV polypeptides in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers and, optionally, adjuvants that potentiate the immune response.

A key aspect of the invention is the demonstration that CD8+ T cell responses against HERV polypeptides are inversely correlated with HIV-1 plasma viral load, suggesting a protective role for these responses in controlling viral replication. Moreover, HERV-specific CD8+ T cells exhibit a phenotypic profile consistent with functional effector memory cells, resembling those induced by controlled chronic infections such as CMV, rather than the exhausted phenotype typical of HIV-1-specific T cells. This indicates that HERV-targeted immunity represents a robust and potentially therapeutically exploitable arm of the antiviral immune response.

The invention also provides methods for producing HERV polypeptides recombinantly in prokaryotic or eukaryotic expression systems, as well as synthetic methods for generating HERV-derived peptides corresponding to defined T cell epitopes. Immunogenic compositions comprising these polypeptides or peptides are useful as components of prophylactic or therapeutic vaccines against HIV-1, designed to broaden the immune response beyond traditional HIV-1 antigens to include conserved, host-encoded HERV targets that cannot mutate to escape immune recognition.

In one embodiment, the invention relates to a method of treating or preventing HIV-1 infection in a subject comprising administering an immunogenic composition comprising a HERV polypeptide, thereby inducing or enhancing a CD8+ T cell response against HERV-expressing cells, including HIV-1-infected cells. In another embodiment, the invention provides diagnostic methods for assessing immune competence or disease progression in HIV-1-infected individuals by measuring the magnitude and functionality of HERV-specific T cell responses.

Overall, the invention redefines the scope of relevant antigens in HIV-1 immunity by establishing HERV polypeptides as legitimate and therapeutically valuable targets for cellular immune responses, offering a novel strategy for durable and mutation-resistant control of HIV-1 infection.

## DEFINITIONS

As used herein, the term “human endogenous retrovirus” or “HERV” refers to any retroviral sequence that has integrated into the human germ line and is inherited in a Mendelian fashion as part of the human genome. HERVs are classified into families based on sequence homology to exogenous retroviruses, such as HERV-K (related to betaretroviruses), HERV-W and HERV-H (related to gammaretroviruses), and HERV-L (an ancient family with no close exogenous counterpart). A HERV sequence may be full-length, containing gag, pro, pol, and env genes flanked by long terminal repeats (LTRs), or it may be truncated, solo LTR, or contain only partial open reading frames.

The term “HERV polypeptide” denotes any peptide or protein encoded by a HERV sequence, whether derived from a full-length provirus or a fragmented genomic insertion. This includes complete structural or enzymatic proteins (e.g., Gag, Pol, Env) as well as shorter peptide fragments generated through alternative splicing, premature stop codons, or proteolytic processing. A HERV polypeptide may be naturally occurring or synthetically produced.

An “isolated HERV polypeptide” is a HERV polypeptide that has been separated from its natural genomic and cellular context, purified to a degree sufficient for pharmaceutical or diagnostic use, and is free from other cellular proteins, nucleic acids, or contaminants that would interfere with its intended application.

A “fusion protein” as used herein refers to a chimeric polypeptide comprising a HERV polypeptide operably linked to a heterologous amino acid sequence, which may confer enhanced solubility, stability, immunogenicity, half-life, or targeting properties. Examples include fusions with Fc regions of immunoglobulins, albumin, or bacterial toxin domains.

“Multimerized HERV polypeptides” are constructs in which two or more copies of a HERV polypeptide or epitope are covalently or non-covalently linked to form dimers, trimers, or higher-order oligomers, designed to increase avidity for T cell receptors or improve antigen presentation.

A “carrier” refers to any molecule or particle capable of delivering a HERV polypeptide to antigen-presenting cells, including but not limited to liposomes, nanoparticles, virus-like particles, dendritic cells, or biodegradable polymers.

A “composition” encompasses any mixture containing a HERV polypeptide in combination with one or more additional components, such as buffers, stabilizers, preservatives, or excipients, suitable for administration to a subject.

An “immunogenic composition” is a formulation designed to provoke an adaptive immune response, typically comprising a HERV polypeptide as the antigenic component along with optional adjuvants, delivery vehicles, or immune potentiators.

An “adjuvant” is any substance that enhances, modulates, or prolongs the immune response to a co-administered antigen, including aluminum salts, toll-like receptor agonists, saponins, cytokines, or emulsion-based systems.

The term “subject” refers to a human or non-human primate, preferably a human, who is at risk of, infected with, or being treated for HIV-1 infection.

“HLA” or “human leukocyte antigen” denotes the human major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I and class II molecules responsible for presenting peptide antigens to T cells. HLA class I molecules (e.g., HLA-A, -B, -C) present endogenously synthesized peptides to CD8+ T cells.

A “CD8+ T cell response” refers to the activation, proliferation, cytokine secretion (e.g., interferon-γ, TNF-α, IL-2), and/or cytotoxic activity of CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes specific for a given peptide-HLA complex.

“Inverse correlation” between a HERV-specific T cell response and HIV-1 viral load means that higher magnitudes of the T cell response are statistically associated with lower levels of circulating HIV-1 RNA, as determined by non-parametric correlation analysis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Isolated HERV Polypeptides

The present invention provides isolated polypeptides derived from human endogenous retroviruses (HERVs), which are distinct from exogenous viral proteins and represent endogenous genomic elements that become immunologically relevant during HIV-1 infection. These isolated HERV polypeptides are obtained through purification from biological sources or, more commonly, through recombinant or synthetic production methods that yield material substantially free of other cellular components. The isolation process ensures that the polypeptides are suitable for use in pharmaceutical, diagnostic, or research applications without interference from contaminants that could elicit non-specific immune reactions or compromise stability. Isolated HERV polypeptides encompass full-length proteins encoded by intact HERV open reading frames, such as those found in certain HERV-K proviruses, as well as truncated or fragmented polypeptides resulting from incomplete transcription, alternative splicing, or the presence of stop codons in older HERV insertions like HERV-L or HERV-H. Importantly, even polypeptides derived from defective HERV loci can contain immunogenic epitopes capable of binding to HLA class I molecules and stimulating CD8+ T cell responses, as demonstrated by the detection of T cell reactivity against peptides such as HERV-L IQ10 in HIV-1-infected individuals. The isolation of these polypeptides enables their characterization, standardization, and incorporation into therapeutic formulations, ensuring batch-to-batch consistency and regulatory compliance. Furthermore, isolated HERV polypeptides can be engineered to enhance their solubility, stability, or resistance to proteolytic degradation, for example, by introducing conservative amino acid substitutions or N- or C-terminal modifications that do not alter the core T cell epitope. The availability of isolated HERV polypeptides is foundational to the development of vaccines and immunotherapies that harness the immune system’s ability to recognize and eliminate cells expressing these endogenous retroviral antigens, particularly in the context of HIV-1 infection where such expression is pathologically upregulated.

### HERV Polypeptides

HERV polypeptides according to the invention are derived from any member of the diverse HERV families present in the human genome, with particular emphasis on those shown to elicit CD8+ T cell responses in HIV-1-infected individuals. Exemplary HERV polypeptides include, but are not limited to, those encoded by HERV-K (HML-2 subgroup), HERV-H, HERV-W, and HERV-L. These polypeptides may correspond to structural proteins such as Gag (group-specific antigen), enzymatic proteins such as Pol (polymerase, including reverse transcriptase, integrase, and protease domains), or envelope glycoproteins (Env). However, given the fragmented nature of most HERV loci, the invention also contemplates polypeptides that represent only portions of these canonical retroviral proteins. For instance, the HERV-L IQ10 peptide originates from an open reading frame within a HERV-L insertion and represents a decameric epitope (ten amino acids in length) that is presented by HLA class I molecules and recognized by cytotoxic T cells. Similarly, other HERV polypeptides may be nonamers, decamers, or longer sequences that are naturally processed by the proteasome and loaded onto HLA molecules in infected cells. The invention includes both naturally occurring HERV polypeptide sequences, as deposited in public databases such as NCBI or HERVd, and variants thereof that retain the ability to bind HLA and stimulate T cell responses. Variants may include conservative amino acid substitutions that do not disrupt T cell receptor recognition, or modifications designed to improve binding affinity to specific HLA alleles, thereby broadening population coverage. The identification of immunogenic HERV polypeptides is achieved through a combination of bioinformatic prediction using epitope prediction algorithms, sequence alignment with known HIV-1 epitopes to assess potential cross-reactivity, and empirical validation using assays such as ELISPOT, intracellular cytokine staining, and cytotoxicity assays. The resulting HERV polypeptides are not only tools for understanding immune responses in HIV-1 infection but also serve as active ingredients in immunogenic compositions designed to prevent or treat retroviral disease by redirecting immunity toward stable, non-mutating host-encoded targets.

### Fusion Proteins

The invention further provides fusion proteins comprising a HERV polypeptide operably linked to a heterologous protein domain that confers advantageous properties for therapeutic or diagnostic use. These fusion proteins are engineered to enhance the pharmacokinetics, immunogenicity, stability, or delivery of the HERV antigenic component. In one embodiment, the HERV polypeptide is fused to the Fc region of a human immunoglobulin, such as IgG1, to create an Fc-HERV fusion protein. The Fc domain facilitates dimerization, extends serum half-life through interaction with the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), and may promote uptake by antigen-presenting cells via Fc gamma receptors, thereby enhancing cross-presentation and CD8+ T cell priming. In another embodiment, the HERV polypeptide is fused to albumin or an albumin-binding domain to similarly prolong circulation time and improve bioavailability. Alternatively, fusion partners may include molecular adjuvants such as cytokines (e.g., IL-2, IL-12, GM-CSF) or chemokines that recruit and activate dendritic cells at the site of administration, creating a local immunostimulatory microenvironment. Bacterial or viral protein domains, such as the translocation domain of Pseudomonas exotoxin or the VP22 protein of herpes simplex virus, may be used to promote cellular uptake or intercellular spread of the HERV antigen, increasing the number of cells that process and present the epitope. Fusion proteins may also incorporate targeting moieties, such as single-chain variable fragments (scFvs) specific for dendritic cell surface markers (e.g., DEC-205, CD40), to direct the HERV antigen precisely to professional antigen-presenting cells and maximize immune activation. The linkage between the HERV polypeptide and the fusion partner may be direct or mediated by a flexible peptide linker (e.g., (GGGGS)n) to ensure proper folding and independent function of each domain. Expression of these fusion proteins is typically achieved in mammalian cell lines (e.g., CHO, HEK293) to ensure appropriate post-translational modifications, although bacterial or yeast systems may be used for simpler constructs. The resulting fusion proteins retain the ability to stimulate HERV-specific CD8+ T cell responses while offering improved manufacturability, stability, and efficacy compared to the unfused HERV polypeptide alone.

### Multimerized HERV Polypeptides

Multimerized HERV polypeptides represent another aspect of the invention, designed to increase the density of HERV epitopes and thereby enhance T cell receptor engagement and immune activation. These constructs consist of two or more copies of a HERV polypeptide or minimal T cell epitope arranged in tandem or displayed on a multimeric scaffold. In one approach, tandem repeats of a HERV-derived peptide, such as HERV-L IQ10, are genetically fused to create linear multimers (e.g., dimers, trimers, or pentamers) that can be expressed recombinantly or synthesized chemically. The increased valency of these multimers improves their ability to cross-link T cell receptors on antigen-specific CD8+ T cells, leading to stronger signaling, greater cytokine production, and enhanced proliferative responses. In another approach, HERV epitopes are conjugated to carrier proteins such as keyhole limpet hemocyanin (KLH) or bovine serum albumin (BSA) at high density, creating particulate antigens that are efficiently taken up by dendritic cells and processed for MHC class I presentation via cross-presentation pathways. Alternatively, HERV polypeptides may be displayed on self-assembling protein nanoparticles, virus-like particles (VLPs), or synthetic lipid nanoparticles, where spatial organization mimics the repetitive structure of viral surfaces and potently stimulates innate and adaptive immunity. Multimerization may also be achieved through chemical cross-linking using bifunctional linkers or click chemistry to generate defined oligomeric states. These multimerized HERV polypeptides are particularly useful in vaccine formulations, where robust T cell priming is desired, and in diagnostic reagents, where high-avidity binding to T cell receptors improves the sensitivity of immune monitoring assays. Importantly, multimerization does not alter the specificity of the T cell response but amplifies its magnitude and functionality, making it a powerful strategy for leveraging HERV antigens in HIV-1 immunotherapy.

### Carriers

Carriers for HERV polypeptides are essential components of delivery systems that protect the antigen from degradation, facilitate uptake by antigen-presenting cells, and provide controlled release kinetics to sustain immune stimulation. The invention encompasses a wide range of carriers, including biodegradable polymeric nanoparticles composed of polylactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) or chitosan, which encapsulate HERV polypeptides and release them gradually in the cytosol of dendritic cells following endosomal escape. Liposomal carriers, comprising phospholipid bilayers that can be cationic, anionic, or neutral, offer another versatile platform for HERV polypeptide delivery; these may be further modified with targeting ligands (e.g., mannose for dendritic cell-specific uptake) or fusogenic peptides to enhance endosomal disruption. Virus-like particles (VLPs), which mimic the structure of native viruses but lack viral genetic material, serve as highly immunogenic carriers that naturally traffic to lymphoid tissues and promote cross-presentation of encapsulated or surface-conjugated HERV antigens. Dendritic cell-based carriers involve the ex vivo loading of autologous dendritic cells with HERV polypeptides followed by reinfusion into the subject, a personalized approach that ensures optimal antigen presentation in the context of the subject’s own HLA alleles. Inorganic nanoparticles, such as gold or silica nanoparticles, may also be functionalized with HERV polypeptides and used as carriers that additionally serve as adjuvants by activating inflammasome pathways. The choice of carrier depends on the desired route of administration (e.g., intramuscular, subcutaneous, intradermal), the need for lymph node targeting, and the balance between safety and immunogenicity. All carriers disclosed herein are compatible with HERV polypeptides and are selected to maximize the induction of functional, cytotoxic CD8+ T cell responses against HERV-expressing cells, including those infected with HIV-1.

### Compositions

Compositions of the invention comprise a HERV polypeptide in combination with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, buffers, stabilizers, or solubilizing agents that ensure the stability, sterility, and injectability of the final product. These compositions are formulated for parenteral administration, typically as aqueous solutions or suspensions, and may be lyophilized for long-term storage with reconstitution prior to use. The concentration of the HERV polypeptide is optimized to achieve immunogenicity without inducing tolerance or adverse reactions, generally ranging from 1 microgram to 1 milligram per dose depending on the specific polypeptide, carrier, and adjuvant used. Additional components may include isotonic agents (e.g., sodium chloride, glycerol), pH adjusters (e.g., phosphate, citrate buffers), antioxidants (e.g., methionine, ascorbic acid), and preservatives (e.g., benzyl alcohol) to maintain product integrity. In embodiments where the HERV polypeptide is part of a fusion protein or multimeric construct, the composition is tailored to preserve the higher-order structure and biological activity of the antigen. The compositions may be monovalent, containing a single HERV polypeptide, or multivalent, incorporating multiple HERV epitopes from different families (e.g., HERV-K, HERV-L, HERV-H) to broaden immune coverage across diverse HLA haplotypes and HERV expression profiles. Quality control measures ensure that each batch meets specifications for purity, potency, and absence of endotoxins or microbial contamination. These compositions are suitable for use in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings, either as standalone agents or in combination with antiretroviral therapy, and represent a critical step in translating HERV-targeted immunity into clinical practice.

### Production of HERV Polypeptides

The production of HERV polypeptides for use in the invention employs well-established recombinant DNA technology, synthetic peptide chemistry, or a combination thereof. For full-length or large HERV polypeptides, a nucleic acid sequence encoding the desired polypeptide is cloned into an expression vector under the control of a strong promoter (e.g., CMV for mammalian cells, T7 for bacterial systems). The vector is then introduced into a suitable host cell—such as Escherichia coli for cost-effective production of non-glycosylated proteins, or Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells for complex, post-translationally modified polypeptides—and cultured under conditions that induce high-level expression. The expressed polypeptide is harvested from the culture supernatant or cell lysate and purified using chromatographic techniques including affinity chromatography (e.g., His-tag/Ni-NTA), ion exchange, size exclusion, or hydrophobic interaction chromatography. For smaller HERV-derived peptides corresponding to defined T cell epitopes (e.g., 8–12 amino acids), solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) using Fmoc or t-Boc chemistry is the preferred method, allowing precise control over sequence and the incorporation of non-natural amino acids or modifications (e.g., acetylation, amidation) to enhance stability or HLA binding. Synthetic peptides are purified by reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) and characterized by mass spectrometry and analytical HPLC to confirm identity and purity (>95%). In cases where multimeric or fusion constructs are desired, genetic engineering approaches are used to create tandem repeats or chimeric genes, which are then expressed and purified as above. All production methods adhere to current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) to ensure suitability for human use, and the resulting HERV polypeptides are stored under controlled conditions (e.g., −80°C, lyophilized) to maintain long-term stability.

### Immunogenic Compositions Comprising a HERV Polypeptide

Immunogenic compositions according to the invention are specifically designed to elicit or enhance CD8+ T cell responses against HERV polypeptides and are formulated to include an effective amount of a HERV polypeptide antigen in conjunction with components that promote robust and durable immunity. These compositions may be administered as part of a prime-boost regimen, using different vectors or delivery platforms for priming and boosting to avoid anti-vector immunity and maximize T cell expansion. The HERV polypeptide may be delivered as a soluble protein, a peptide-pulsed dendritic cell, or encoded within a viral vector (e.g., adenovirus, modified vaccinia Ankara) or nucleic acid platform (e.g., mRNA, DNA plasmid) that directs endogenous expression and processing, thereby ensuring natural epitope presentation via the MHC class I pathway. When used in therapeutic settings for HIV-1-infected individuals, the immunogenic composition aims to amplify pre-existing HERV-specific T cell responses or induce de novo responses that can target and eliminate reservoir cells expressing HERV antigens. In prophylactic applications, the composition seeks to establish a pool of memory CD8+ T cells that can rapidly respond upon HIV-1 infection and subsequent HERV upregulation. The inclusion of multiple HERV epitopes restricted by common HLA alleles (e.g., HLA-A*02:01, HLA-B*57:01) ensures broad population coverage and reduces the risk of immune escape. Immunogenic compositions are tested in preclinical models for their ability to induce interferon-γ production, cytotoxic activity, and phenotypic markers of functional memory T cells before advancing to clinical trials. By focusing immunity on conserved, host-encoded targets that do not mutate, these compositions offer a promising strategy for achieving sustained control of HIV-1 replication.

### Adjuvants

Adjuvants included in the immunogenic compositions of the invention serve to enhance the magnitude, quality, and durability of the HERV-specific CD8+ T cell response. Suitable adjuvants include aluminum salts (e.g., alum), which promote antigen depot formation and Th2-skewed responses but are less effective for CD8+ T cell induction; therefore, more potent adjuvants are preferred. Toll-like receptor (TLR) agonists, such as CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (TLR9 agonist), poly(I:C) (TLR3 agonist), or resiquimod (TLR7/8 agonist), activate dendritic cells and promote cross-presentation, making them ideal for CD8+ T cell priming. Saponin-based adjuvants like QS-21, used in the AS01b system (combined with MPL and liposomes), have demonstrated strong CD8+ T cell induction in clinical vaccines. Emulsion-based adjuvants such as MF59 or AS03 enhance antigen uptake and create a local cytokine milieu conducive to T cell activation. Cytokine adjuvants, including IL-12, IL-15, or FLT3 ligand, may be co-administered to directly support T cell survival, proliferation, and effector differentiation. The choice of adjuvant is guided by the desired immune profile: for HIV-1 immunotherapy, adjuvants that promote Th1-type responses and cytotoxic T lymphocyte activity are favored. Adjuvants may be physically mixed with the HERV polypeptide, co-encapsulated in nanoparticles, or genetically fused as molecular adjuvants. Their inclusion is critical for overcoming the potential tolerogenic nature of self-antigens like HERV polypeptides and for generating robust, functional T cell responses capable of controlling HIV-1 infection.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In a representative study demonstrating the principles of the invention, peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were obtained from 29 HIV-1-positive individuals enrolled in the UCSF OPTIONS cohort, along with 13 HIV-1-negative controls and three hepatitis C virus (HCV)-positive, HIV-1-negative controls. Plasma samples from 16 untreated HIV-1-positive participants and four HIV-1-negative volunteers were analyzed for HERV RNA content using quantitative reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR). Total RNA was extracted from pelleted plasma particles following high-speed centrifugation and DNase treatment to remove genomic DNA contamination. RT-qPCR was performed using primers specific for HERV transcripts, with expression levels normalized to a standard curve generated from cDNA of HIV-1-negative donor PBMCs. Results showed significantly elevated levels of HERV RNA in the plasma of HIV-1-positive individuals compared to controls (p = 0.0160, Mann-Whitney test), confirming that HIV-1 infection induces HERV expression.

Based on sequence data from NCBI, Retrosearch, and HERVd databases, a panel of HERV-derived peptides was synthesized, including both peptides sharing ≥4 amino acids with known HIV-1 epitopes and peptides unique to HERVs (≤3 amino acids in common). These peptides, along with control HIV-1 peptides, were used in interferon-γ ELISPOT assays to measure CD8+ T cell responses in cryopreserved PBMCs. Specific IFN-γ spot-forming units (SFU) per million PBMCs were quantified after subtracting background from medium-only wells. HIV-1-positive individuals exhibited significant T cell responses to HERV peptides, whereas no responses were detected in HIV-1-negative or HCV-positive controls (p < 0.001, Mann-Whitney test). Notably, responses to unique HERV peptides (e.g., HERV-L IQ10) were of comparable magnitude to those against HIV-1-similar peptides, indicating that HERV-specific immunity is not solely due to cross-reactivity.

Longitudinal analysis of three HIV-1-positive participants revealed persistent HERV-L IQ10-specific T cell responses over time. In participant OP562, who naturally controlled HIV-1 viremia without antiretroviral therapy, the HERV-specific response remained high, while in participants OP747 and OP841, initiation of highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) led to declines in both HIV-1 viral load and HERV-specific T cell responses, demonstrating that HERV immunity is driven by ongoing HIV-1 replication.

Phenotypic characterization of HERV-specific CD8+ T cells from OP562 and OP841 using multicolor flow cytometry showed that these cells predominantly exhibited a CCR7−CD45RA+ terminally differentiated effector memory phenotype, similar to CMV-specific T cells and distinct from the CD45RA− profile of HIV-1-specific T cells. Functional assays confirmed that HERV-specific CD8+ T cells produced multiple cytokines (IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-2) and exhibited degranulation upon antigen stimulation.

Crucially, a significant inverse correlation was observed between HERV-specific T cell response magnitude and HIV-1 plasma viral load across the cohort (Spearman r = −0.49, p = 0.03), suggesting a protective role for these cells. To confirm cytotoxic functionality, CD8+ T cells from OP562 and OP841 were expanded in vitro with HERV-L IQ10 peptide and tested in a 51Chromium release assay. These cells specifically lysed autologous Epstein-Barr virus-transformed B cells pulsed with HERV-L IQ10 but not with irrelevant peptides, demonstrating antigen-specific killing. No such activity was detected in cells from HIV-1-negative donors.

This example validates the core tenets of the invention: HERV polypeptides are expressed during HIV-1 infection, elicit functional CD8+ T cell responses that correlate with viral control, and represent viable targets for immunotherapy. The HERV-L IQ10 peptide, in particular, serves as a prototype for the development of HERV-based vaccines and adoptive T cell therapies aimed at eradicating HIV-1-infected cells through recognition of stable, non-mutable endogenous antigens.
