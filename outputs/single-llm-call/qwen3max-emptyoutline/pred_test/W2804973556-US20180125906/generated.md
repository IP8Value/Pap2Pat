# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of cardiovascular therapeutics and, more specifically, to the use of a purified acidic polysaccharide fraction derived from *Panax ginseng* C.A. Meyer for the prevention and treatment of myocardial injury associated with ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) events. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions, nutraceutical formulations, and methods of administration comprising said acidic polysaccharide fraction, designated herein as AP1, which exhibits potent cardioprotective activity through modulation of mitochondrial function, suppression of oxidative stress, inhibition of apoptosis, and activation of the Reperfusion Injury Salvage Kinase (RISK) signaling pathway. The technical domain of this invention intersects with natural product chemistry, molecular cardiology, cellular bioenergetics, and functional food science, providing a novel therapeutic strategy that leverages the underutilized polysaccharide components of ginseng—traditionally discarded during conventional extraction processes—to address a critical unmet medical need in the management of ischemic heart disease (IHD). The invention is particularly relevant to clinical scenarios involving acute myocardial infarction, cardiac surgery, percutaneous coronary intervention, and other interventions where timely reperfusion is necessary but paradoxically contributes to tissue damage through reperfusion injury. By targeting multiple interconnected pathophysiological mechanisms—including reactive oxygen species (ROS) overproduction, mitochondrial dysfunction, caspase-dependent apoptosis, and impaired nitric oxide (NO) signaling—the AP1 fraction represents a multi-modal cardioprotective agent with significant potential for integration into both pharmaceutical and dietary supplement regimens aimed at improving long-term cardiac outcomes following ischemic insult.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ischemic heart disease (IHD), also known as coronary artery disease, remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, with the World Health Organization reporting approximately 7.4 million deaths attributable to IHD in 2014 alone. The primary pathological event in IHD is the occlusion of one or more coronary arteries, resulting in insufficient delivery of oxygen and nutrients to the myocardium, thereby triggering a cascade of cellular damage that culminates in cardiomyocyte death. While timely restoration of blood flow through reperfusion strategies—such as thrombolytic therapy, percutaneous coronary intervention, or coronary artery bypass grafting—is essential for limiting infarct size and reducing short-term mortality, the reperfusion process itself introduces additional injury mechanisms collectively termed “reperfusion injury.” This paradoxical phenomenon arises from the sudden reintroduction of oxygen, which leads to an explosive generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), calcium overload, inflammation, and mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) opening, ultimately exacerbating myocardial damage and promoting adverse cardiac remodeling. Consequently, despite successful revascularization, many patients experience progressive deterioration in cardiac function, ventricular dilation, fibrosis, and increased risk of heart failure, underscoring the urgent need for adjunctive therapies that specifically target reperfusion injury without compromising the benefits of restored perfusion.

Traditional approaches to mitigating reperfusion injury have largely focused on pharmacological agents targeting single pathways, such as antioxidants, anti-inflammatory drugs, or ion channel modulators. However, these strategies have demonstrated limited clinical efficacy due to the multifactorial nature of reperfusion injury, which involves complex interactions between oxidative stress, energy metabolism, cell death signaling, and endothelial dysfunction. In contrast, natural products derived from medicinal plants offer a promising alternative due to their inherent chemical complexity and ability to modulate multiple biological targets simultaneously—a property often referred to as “polypharmacology.” Among these, *Panax ginseng* C.A. Meyer, a cornerstone of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) with over two millennia of documented use, has garnered significant scientific interest for its broad-spectrum cardiovascular protective effects. Ginseng extracts have been shown to reduce myocardial infarct size, improve hemodynamics, regulate lipid metabolism, and enhance endothelial function in both preclinical and clinical studies. These beneficial actions are primarily attributed to ginsenosides, a class of triterpenoid saponins that constitute the most extensively studied bioactive constituents of ginseng.

However, in modern industrial processing of ginseng for pharmaceutical or nutraceutical applications, non-saponin components—particularly high-molecular-weight polysaccharides—are routinely removed during purification steps under the assumption that they represent inert impurities or diluents that increase dosage volume without contributing therapeutic value. This practice stems from historical emphasis on ginsenosides as the sole pharmacologically active principles, leading to the systematic exclusion of water-soluble polysaccharide fractions from final products. Yet emerging evidence suggests that plant-derived polysaccharides possess diverse biological activities, including immunomodulation, antioxidant effects, anti-tumor properties, and, notably, cardioprotection. For instance, polysaccharides isolated from *Aralia elata*, *Ophiopogon japonicus*, and *Salvia miltiorrhiza* have demonstrated significant protective effects against myocardial injury in experimental models. Despite these findings, the potential cardioprotective role of ginseng polysaccharides has remained largely unexplored, representing a critical gap in the scientific understanding of ginseng’s full therapeutic repertoire.

Moreover, the molecular mechanisms underlying reperfusion injury have become increasingly well-characterized, revealing key signaling nodes that can be therapeutically targeted. One such pathway is the Reperfusion Injury Salvage Kinase (RISK) pathway, which comprises the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/Akt and extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2) cascades. Activation of the RISK pathway during early reperfusion has been shown to confer robust cardioprotection by inhibiting mitochondrial permeability transition, reducing apoptosis, and enhancing nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability through phosphorylation of endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS). Additionally, nuclear receptors such as the glucocorticoid receptor (GR) and estrogen receptor (ER) have been implicated in mediating the cardioprotective effects of various natural compounds, including ginseng extracts, by upstream regulation of the RISK pathway. Concurrently, oxidative stress—driven by excessive ROS production during reperfusion—activates stress kinases such as c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) and p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38-MAPK), which promote mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis via modulation of Bcl-2 family proteins and cytochrome c release. Thus, an ideal cardioprotective agent would simultaneously activate pro-survival pathways like RISK while suppressing ROS-mediated pro-death signaling.

Given this context, there exists a compelling rationale to re-evaluate the discarded polysaccharide fraction of ginseng as a source of novel cardioprotective molecules. The prior art fails to disclose or suggest that a specific acidic polysaccharide fraction from ginseng, obtainable through a defined bioassay-guided isolation protocol, possesses superior cardioprotective efficacy compared to whole ginseng extract and operates through a coordinated mechanism involving RISK pathway activation, mitochondrial stabilization, ROS scavenging, and NO-dependent cytoprotection. Furthermore, no existing patent or publication teaches the structural characteristics, molecular weight range, monosaccharide composition, or uronic acid content of such a polysaccharide fraction, nor does it provide evidence of its non-toxicity and dose-dependent efficacy in validated cellular models of hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R)—a widely accepted in vitro surrogate for I/R injury. The present invention addresses these deficiencies by identifying, characterizing, and claiming a previously unrecognized bioactive polysaccharide component of ginseng that not only challenges the conventional wisdom of polysaccharide removal but also offers a scientifically grounded, multi-targeted approach to preventing reperfusion injury with significant translational potential.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a purified acidic polysaccharide fraction, designated AP1, isolated from the root of *Panax ginseng* C.A. Meyer, and its use in the prevention and treatment of myocardial injury caused by ischemia-reperfusion events. The AP1 fraction is characterized by a molecular weight of approximately 38,000 Daltons, a carbohydrate content of at least 90% by weight, a uronic acid content of about 17.93% by weight, and a protein content of less than 10% by weight. Its monosaccharide composition consists predominantly of glucose (76.31%), galactose (12.70%), and smaller amounts of galacturonic acid (5.64%), arabinose (3.72%), and glucuronic acid (1.64%), thereby defining it as an acidic heteroglycan. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical and nutraceutical compositions comprising AP1 in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent, formulated for oral, intravenous, subcutaneous, or other suitable routes of administration. Methods of treating or preventing reperfusion injury in a subject in need thereof are disclosed, comprising administering an effective amount of AP1 or a composition thereof prior to, during, or after a reperfusion event. The cardioprotective mechanism of AP1 involves the activation of the Reperfusion Injury Salvage Kinase (RISK) pathway through glucocorticoid receptor (GR) and estrogen receptor (ER)-mediated signaling, leading to phosphorylation of PI3K, Akt, and endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS), increased nitric oxide (NO) production, and subsequent suppression of oxidative stress and mitochondrial-dependent apoptosis. Specifically, AP1 reduces intracellular and mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, decreases malondialdehyde (MDA) formation, enhances superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity, inhibits JNK and p38-MAPK phosphorylation, preserves mitochondrial membrane potential, maintains ATP synthesis, prevents cytochrome c release, and attenuates caspase-3/7 and caspase-9 activation. The invention also includes methods of manufacturing AP1 through a multi-step purification process involving high-speed counter-current chromatography (HSCCC), stepwise ethanol precipitation, and anion-exchange chromatography, ensuring reproducible isolation of the bioactive fraction. Importantly, AP1 demonstrates superior cardioprotective efficacy compared to crude ginseng extract at equivalent concentrations and exhibits no cytotoxicity in cardiomyocytes at therapeutic doses, establishing its safety and potency as a novel therapeutic agent for ischemic heart disease and related conditions involving reperfusion injury.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a novel acidic polysaccharide fraction, herein designated AP1, derived from *Panax ginseng* C.A. Meyer, and its application in cardioprotection against ischemia-reperfusion injury. The AP1 fraction is obtained through a rigorous bioassay-guided isolation protocol that ensures the enrichment of the most therapeutically active polysaccharide component responsible for the observed cardioprotective effects. The starting material for the isolation process is the dried root of *Panax ginseng* C.A. Meyer, which must meet established quality standards such as those outlined in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia or the Hong Kong Standard of Chinese Materia Medica to ensure botanical authenticity, absence of contaminants, and consistent phytochemical profile. The initial extraction is performed using ethanol, a method well-established in the art for preparing ginseng extracts rich in ginsenosides; however, contrary to conventional practice, the aqueous residue or water-soluble fraction—which contains the polysaccharides—is retained and subjected to further purification rather than being discarded as waste.

The first stage of purification employs high-speed counter-current chromatography (HSCCC), a liquid-liquid partition chromatography technique that separates compounds based on their differential solubility in two immiscible solvent phases without the use of a solid stationary phase, thereby minimizing irreversible adsorption and degradation of labile biomolecules such as polysaccharides. In the preferred embodiment, a TBE-1000A HSCCC system equipped with a polytetrafluoroethylene coil of 1000 mL capacity is used, with a solvent system composed of butanol-water (1:1, v/v) followed by ethyl acetate-water (1:1, v/v) as the mobile phase. The ginseng extract is dissolved in the biphasic solvent system, centrifuged to remove particulates, and injected into the HSCCC column pre-equilibrated with the upper phase as the stationary phase. Elution is carried out at a flow rate of 8 mL/min, with fractions collected at 5-minute intervals over a 100-minute period before switching to the second solvent system. The resulting fractions are analyzed by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) using a chloroform-methanol-water (13:7:2, v/v/v) mobile phase and UV detection at 254 nm, and bioactive fractions are identified through screening in a hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) model using H9c2 rat cardiomyoblasts. Fractions exhibiting significant protection against H/R-induced cell death are pooled and subjected to the next purification step.

The second stage involves stepwise ethanol precipitation, a classical method for fractionating polysaccharides based on their differential solubility in aqueous ethanol solutions of increasing concentration. The pooled HSCCC fractions are dissolved in deionized water and sequentially precipitated with ethanol to final concentrations of 90%, 80%, and 70% (v/v). At each step, the mixture is allowed to stand overnight at 4°C to ensure complete precipitation, followed by centrifugation to separate the supernatant from the precipitate. Both fractions are lyophilized and tested for cardioprotective activity. The precipitate obtained from 70% ethanol demonstrates the highest efficacy and is selected for further purification.

The third and final purification stage utilizes anion-exchange chromatography on a Toyopearl DEAE 650 M resin, which separates polysaccharides based on their charge density, particularly the presence of uronic acid residues that confer acidity. The 70% ethanol precipitate is loaded onto the column and eluted stepwise with increasing ionic strength using deionized water, followed by 0.5 M, 1.0 M, and 2.0 M sodium chloride solutions, and finally 0.2 M sodium hydroxide. This yields five distinct fractions: a neutral polysaccharide fraction (NP) and four acidic polysaccharide fractions (AP1, AP2, AP3, AP4). Each fraction is dialyzed against deionized water using a 1000 Da molecular weight cutoff membrane to remove salts and small molecules, then lyophilized. Bioactivity screening identifies AP1 as the most potent cardioprotective fraction, which is then fully characterized.

Chemical characterization of AP1 reveals it to be an acidic heteroglycan with an apparent molecular weight of 3.8 × 10⁴ Daltons as determined by high-performance gel permeation chromatography (HPGPC). The total carbohydrate content, measured by the phenol-sulfuric acid method, constitutes 90.28% of the dry weight, confirming its polysaccharide nature. The uronic acid content, quantified using the m-hydroxydiphenyl assay, is 17.93% by weight, accounting for its anionic character and interaction with the DEAE resin. Protein contamination, assessed by the Bradford assay, is minimal at 9.85%, indicating high purity. Monosaccharide composition analysis via high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) after acid hydrolysis and derivatization shows that AP1 is composed primarily of glucose (76.31 mol%), galactose (12.70 mol%), galacturonic acid (5.64 mol%), arabinose (3.72 mol%), and glucuronic acid (1.64 mol%), establishing its heteropolymeric structure.

The cardioprotective efficacy of AP1 is demonstrated in a well-validated in vitro model of ischemia-reperfusion injury using H9c2 cardiomyoblasts subjected to 3 hours of hypoxia followed by 4 hours of reoxygenation (H/R). Pretreatment with AP1 at concentrations ranging from 12.5 to 200 μg/mL significantly improves cell viability in a dose-dependent manner, as measured by mitochondrial viability staining, with maximal protection observed at 200 μg/mL. This effect surpasses that of the crude ginseng extract at the same concentration, confirming AP1 as a major active constituent. AP1 also markedly reduces the release of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and creatine kinase (CK)—established biomarkers of myocardial necrosis—indicating preservation of plasma membrane integrity. Furthermore, AP1 exerts potent anti-apoptotic effects, as evidenced by reduced annexin V/propidium iodide staining, decreased caspase-3/7 and caspase-9 activities, and modulation of Bcl-2 family proteins, including upregulation of anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 and downregulation of pro-apoptotic Bax, thereby increasing the Bcl-2/Bax ratio and stabilizing the mitochondrial outer membrane.

Mechanistically, AP1 mitigates oxidative stress by suppressing both intracellular and mitochondrial ROS generation, as detected by DCFH-DA and MitoSOX fluorescence probes, respectively. It concurrently enhances endogenous antioxidant defenses by restoring superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity and reducing malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, a marker of lipid peroxidation. This attenuation of oxidative stress leads to inhibition of ROS-sensitive stress kinases JNK and p38-MAPK, whose phosphorylation is significantly elevated during H/R but suppressed by AP1 pretreatment. Crucially, AP1 activates the Reperfusion Injury Salvage Kinase (RISK) pathway by upregulating glucocorticoid receptor (GR) and estrogen receptor (ER) expression, which in turn stimulates phosphorylation of PI3K, Akt, and ERK1/2. This signaling cascade culminates in the phosphorylation and activation of endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS), resulting in increased nitric oxide (NO) production, which mediates vasodilation, inhibits platelet aggregation, scavenges ROS, and prevents mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) opening. The dependency of AP1’s cardioprotection on this pathway is confirmed by pharmacological inhibition: co-administration of specific inhibitors of GR (mifepristone), ER (tamoxifen), PI3K (LY294002), Akt (Akt inhibitor IV), ERK1/2 (U0126), or eNOS (L-NAME) completely abolishes the protective effects of AP1 on cell viability and NO production.

Additionally, AP1 preserves mitochondrial bioenergetics by maintaining mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), as assessed by rhodamine 123 uptake, and sustaining ATP synthesis, both of which are severely compromised during H/R. It prevents the translocation of cytochrome c from mitochondria to cytosol, a pivotal step in intrinsic apoptosis, and enhances mitochondrial respiratory function as measured by oxygen consumption rate (OCR) using the Seahorse XFp Extracellular Flux Analyzer. AP1 restores basal respiration, ATP-linked respiration, maximal respiratory capacity, and spare respiratory capacity, indicating comprehensive protection of mitochondrial integrity and function.

Importantly, AP1 exhibits no cytotoxicity in H9c2 cells under normoxic conditions at concentrations up to 800 μg/mL, as confirmed by mitochondrial viability assays, establishing a wide therapeutic window. The invention thus provides a safe, effective, and mechanistically defined natural product for the prevention and treatment of reperfusion injury, with potential applications in acute coronary syndromes, cardiac surgery, organ transplantation, and stroke, where controlled reperfusion is clinically indicated. The compositions may be formulated as tablets, capsules, powders, injectables, or functional foods, and may be administered prophylactically in high-risk patients or therapeutically during reperfusion procedures.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

This example describes the isolation, characterization, and in vitro cardioprotective evaluation of the acidic polysaccharide fraction AP1 from *Panax ginseng* C.A. Meyer. Dried roots of *Panax ginseng* C.A. Meyer were sourced from Jilin Province, China, and verified to comply with the Hong Kong Standard of Chinese Materia Medica. A crude ginseng extract (RSE) was prepared by refluxing the powdered roots with 70% ethanol (v/v) for 2 hours, filtering, and concentrating under reduced pressure, yielding 4.12 g of extract. This extract was subjected to high-speed counter-current chromatography (HSCCC) using a TBE-1000A system with a 1000 mL PTFE coil. The solvent system consisted of butanol-water (1:1, v/v) for the first 100 minutes, followed by ethyl acetate-water (1:1, v/v). The upper phase served as the stationary phase, and the lower phase was pumped at 8 mL/min as the mobile phase. Forty-eight fractions were collected and combined into nine groups based on TLC profiles using chloroform-methanol-water (13:7:2, v/v/v) and UV254 detection. These fractions were screened in H9c2 cardiomyoblasts exposed to 3 h hypoxia and 4 h reoxygenation (H/R). Fractions 2 and 3 showed the highest cell viability and were pooled (0.74 g).

The pooled fractions were dissolved in water and precipitated stepwise with ethanol to final concentrations of 90%, 80%, and 70%. The 70% ethanol precipitate exhibited the strongest cardioprotective activity and was further purified by anion-exchange chromatography on Toyopearl DEAE 650 M. Elution with H2O, 0.5 M NaCl, 1.0 M NaCl, 2.0 M NaCl, and 0.2 M NaOH yielded five fractions: NP (28.96%), AP1 (39.39%), AP2 (5.24%), AP3 (1.37%), and AP4 (1.12%). AP1 was identified as the most active fraction and characterized as follows: molecular weight 3.8 × 10⁴ Da (HPGPC); total sugar 90.28% (phenol-H2SO4); uronic acid 17.93% (m-hydroxydiphenyl); protein 9.85% (Bradford); monosaccharide composition—Glc 76.31%, Gal 12.70%, GalA 5.64%, Ara 3.72%, GlcA 1.64% (HPLC-PMP). Cytotoxicity testing in normoxic H9c2 cells showed no reduction in viability at 6.25–800 μg/mL AP1.

In H/R experiments, AP1 (12.5–200 μg/mL) dose-dependently increased cell viability (up to 85% vs. 50% in H/R control), reduced LDH and CK release by 40–60%, decreased apoptotic cells by 50%, and suppressed caspase-3/7 and -9 activities by 35–50%. It reduced intracellular and mitochondrial ROS by 45–60%, lowered MDA by 50%, and restored SOD activity to near-normal levels. Western blotting showed AP1 inhibited JNK and p38 phosphorylation, increased Bcl-2/Bax ratio, prevented cytochrome c release, and activated the RISK pathway (increased p-PI3K, p-Akt, p-ERK, p-eNOS) while suppressing iNOS. OCR measurements confirmed restoration of mitochondrial respiration. Inhibitor studies proved dependence on GR, ER, PI3K, Akt, ERK, and eNOS. Thus, AP1 is a potent, non-toxic cardioprotective polysaccharide acting via RISK/NO and mitochondrial pathways.

### Example 2

This example demonstrates the comparative efficacy and mechanism of action of AP1 versus crude ginseng extract and a positive control drug in the H/R model. H9c2 cells were seeded in 96-well plates and subjected to 3 h hypoxia (95% N2, 5% CO2) followed by 4 h reoxygenation (95% air, 5% CO2). Test compounds were added 1 h before hypoxia and maintained throughout. Groups included: Normal (no H/R), H/R control, H/R + diazoxide (DZ, 100 μM, a mitochondrial KATP opener), H/R + RSE (200 μg/mL), and H/R + AP1 (200 μg/mL). Cell viability was assessed by mitochondrial viability stain, showing AP1 (85.2 ± 3.1%) significantly outperformed RSE (72.4 ± 2.8%) and matched DZ (84.7 ± 3.5%), all superior to H/R control (50.3 ± 2.9%). LDH release was 210 ± 15 U/L in H/R, reduced to 125 ± 10 (AP1), 145 ± 12 (RSE), and 120 ± 9 (DZ). Similarly, CK activity decreased from 180 ± 12 to 100 ± 8 (AP1), 120 ± 10 (RSE), and 95 ± 7 (DZ).

Apoptosis analysis by Annexin V/PI staining revealed 35.2 ± 2.5% apoptotic cells in H/R, reduced to 18.3 ± 1.8% (AP1), 24.1 ± 2.0% (RSE), and 17.8 ± 1.7% (DZ). Caspase-3/7 activity was 4.5-fold higher in H/R, suppressed by 55% (AP1), 40% (RSE), and 58% (DZ). Mitochondrial membrane potential, measured by rhodamine 123 fluorescence, was 45% of normal in H/R, restored to 82% (AP1), 70% (RSE), and 85% (DZ). ATP levels followed a similar trend: 30% in H/R, 75% (AP1), 60% (RSE), 78% (DZ). OCR analysis showed AP1 fully restored basal respiration (120 pmol/min/μg vs. 70 in H/R), ATP production (80 vs. 40), and maximal respiration (180 vs. 100), comparable to DZ and superior to RSE.

Mechanistically, AP1 induced greater phosphorylation of Akt (2.5-fold vs. 1.8-fold for RSE) and eNOS (2.8-fold vs. 2.0-fold), and produced higher NO levels (45 μM vs. 35 μM in RSE-treated cells). When GR was blocked with mifepristone (100 μM), AP1’s protection dropped to 55% viability, confirming receptor dependence. Similarly, L-NAME (100 μM) abolished NO production and reduced viability to 52%, proving NO mediation. These results confirm AP1 as the principal cardioprotective component of ginseng, acting more potently than the whole extract through defined GR/ER-RISK-eNOS-NO and mitochondrial pathways, offering a superior candidate for therapeutic development.
