# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic agents for the treatment of autoimmune and immune-related diseases and disorders, particularly those involving inflammatory myopathies such as polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and inclusion body myositis. More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions comprising Insulin-like 6 (Insl6) or Insl6-derived agents, including Insl6-Fc fusion proteins, for modulating immune responses, reducing inflammation, and promoting muscle regeneration in subjects suffering from such conditions. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical formulations, gene therapy approaches, and diagnostic or therapeutic kits incorporating Insl6-based therapeutics for the management of T cell-mediated autoimmune pathologies affecting skeletal muscle and potentially other tissues.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Idiopathic inflammatory myositis represents a heterogeneous group of rare autoimmune disorders characterized by chronic muscle inflammation, progressive muscle weakness, elevated serum creatine kinase levels, and histological evidence of immune cell infiltration into skeletal muscle tissue. The major clinical subtypes—polymyositis (PM), dermatomyositis (DM), and inclusion body myositis (IBM)—exhibit distinct immunopathological features despite sharing common clinical hallmarks. In dermatomyositis, the primary lesion involves vascular endothelial damage with perimysial and perivascular infiltration predominantly by CD4+ helper T cells, leading to characteristic perifascicular atrophy of muscle fibers. In contrast, polymyositis is marked by endomysial invasion of activated cytotoxic CD8+ T cells that directly target and damage non-necrotic muscle fibers, with CD4+ T cells present in more peripheral regions of the inflammatory infiltrate. These pathological distinctions reflect divergent underlying immune mechanisms but converge on a final common pathway of muscle fiber destruction, impaired regeneration, and functional disability.

Current therapeutic strategies for inflammatory myopathies remain limited and often inadequate, typically relying on broad immunosuppressive agents such as corticosteroids, methotrexate, azathioprine, or intravenous immunoglobulin. While these interventions may provide symptomatic relief in some patients, they frequently fail to induce sustained remission, are associated with significant systemic toxicities, and do not address the fundamental dysregulation of muscle repair mechanisms that accompany chronic inflammation. Moreover, the precise molecular pathways governing the interplay between immune activation and muscle regeneration in these conditions remain incompletely understood, hindering the development of targeted, disease-modifying therapies.

Emerging evidence suggests that the regenerative capacity of skeletal muscle is intimately linked to the local immune microenvironment. Following acute muscle injury, a coordinated sequence of immune cell recruitment—including neutrophils, macrophages, and T lymphocytes—facilitates the clearance of necrotic debris, modulates the inflammatory response, and supports satellite cell activation and differentiation necessary for effective tissue repair. However, in chronic autoimmune settings such as inflammatory myositis, this reparative process becomes dysregulated, resulting in persistent inflammation, failed regeneration, and progressive muscle wasting. Identifying endogenous factors that can simultaneously dampen pathological immune responses while promoting muscle regeneration represents a promising therapeutic strategy for these debilitating conditions.

Insulin-like 6 (Insl6) is a member of the insulin-like/relaxin peptide hormone family, which includes structurally related proteins involved in diverse physiological processes ranging from reproduction to tissue repair. Unlike classical insulin-like growth factors that signal through tyrosine kinase receptors, most relaxin-family peptides, including Insl6, are believed to act via G protein-coupled receptors, although the specific receptor for Insl6 remains unidentified. Previous studies have demonstrated that Insl6 is upregulated in response to acute muscle injury and exhibits both anti-inflammatory and pro-regenerative properties when overexpressed in experimental models of cardiotoxin-induced muscle damage. Specifically, adenovirus-mediated Insl6 expression was shown to enhance satellite cell activation, reduce inflammatory marker expression, and accelerate functional recovery following severe muscle injury. However, the role of endogenous Insl6 in autoimmune-mediated muscle pathology and its potential therapeutic utility in chronic inflammatory conditions had not been previously explored.

The present invention builds upon these foundational observations by demonstrating, for the first time, that Insl6 plays a critical protective role in experimental autoimmune myositis, a murine model that recapitulates key immunological features of human polymyositis and dermatomyositis. Using genetic models of Insl6 deficiency and overexpression, the inventors have established that endogenous Insl6 serves as a natural modulator of T cell-mediated inflammation in skeletal muscle, with Insl6-deficient mice exhibiting exacerbated disease severity, enhanced T cell infiltration, elevated pro-inflammatory cytokine production, and significantly impaired motor function compared to wild-type controls. Conversely, transgenic overexpression of Insl6 or acute systemic delivery of Insl6-Fc fusion proteins markedly attenuated disease manifestations, reduced inflammatory markers, and preserved muscle function in the same model. Furthermore, in vitro studies revealed that Insl6 directly inhibits T cell proliferation and interleukin-2 (IL-2) production, providing a mechanistic basis for its anti-inflammatory effects. Critically, analysis of human muscle biopsy specimens from polymyositis and dermatomyositis patients demonstrated significantly reduced Insl6 transcript levels compared to healthy controls, with lower Insl6 expression correlating with greater histological evidence of muscle fiber atrophy. These findings collectively establish Insl6 as a novel endogenous regulator of immune-mediated muscle pathology and provide compelling rationale for the therapeutic application of Insl6-based agents in the treatment of inflammatory myopathies and potentially other T cell-driven autoimmune disorders.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for the treatment of autoimmune diseases and immune-related disorders, particularly inflammatory myopathies such as polymyositis and dermatomyositis, through the administration of Insulin-like 6 (Insl6) or Insl6-derived therapeutic agents. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that Insl6 functions as a potent endogenous modulator of T cell-mediated inflammation in skeletal muscle, with deficiency of Insl6 exacerbating disease severity in experimental autoimmune myositis while overexpression or exogenous administration of Insl6 confers significant protection against inflammatory damage and functional impairment.

In one aspect, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising an effective amount of an Insl6 agent, wherein the Insl6 agent is selected from the group consisting of native Insl6 polypeptide, Insl6 variants, Insl6 fragments, Insl6 fusion proteins, and nucleic acid molecules encoding any of the foregoing. Particularly preferred Insl6 agents include Insl6-Fc fusion proteins, wherein the Fc domain is derived from human immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1), which confer enhanced stability, prolonged half-life, and improved pharmacokinetic properties compared to native Insl6.

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for treating a subject suffering from an autoimmune disease or immune-related disorder, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an Insl6 agent. The autoimmune disease or immune-related disorder is preferably an inflammatory myopathy selected from polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and inclusion body myositis, although the methods may also be applicable to other T cell-mediated autoimmune conditions characterized by pathological inflammation and tissue damage.

In a further aspect, the invention provides methods for reducing inflammation in a subject suffering from an autoimmune disease, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an Insl6 agent, wherein the reduction in inflammation is evidenced by decreased infiltration of CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes into affected tissues, reduced expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), and improved histological parameters.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides methods for improving muscle function in a subject suffering from an inflammatory myopathy, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an Insl6 agent, wherein improved muscle function is assessed by enhanced performance in standardized functional tests such as rotarod and treadmill endurance assays.

The invention also encompasses gene therapy approaches for delivering Insl6 to subjects in need thereof, including viral and non-viral vectors encoding Insl6 or Insl6-Fc fusion proteins, as well as hydrodynamic delivery methods for systemic expression of Insl6 from hepatic tissue. Additionally, the invention provides diagnostic and therapeutic kits comprising Insl6 agents, pharmaceutical carriers, and instructions for their use in the treatment of autoimmune and immune-related disorders.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### DEFINITIONS

As used herein, the term "Insl6" or "Insulin-like 6" refers to the polypeptide encoded by the Insl6 gene, including naturally occurring isoforms, allelic variants, and species homologs from mammals, particularly human and murine Insl6. The term encompasses both the full-length mature protein and biologically active fragments or variants thereof that retain the ability to modulate immune responses and promote tissue regeneration as described herein. Human Insl6 has the amino acid sequence set forth in GenBank Accession No. NP_001001693.1, while murine Insl6 corresponds to GenBank Accession No. NP_032437.2.

The term "Insl6 agent" refers to any composition capable of providing Insl6 biological activity, including but not limited to: (1) native Insl6 polypeptide; (2) recombinant Insl6 polypeptide produced in prokaryotic or eukaryotic expression systems; (3) Insl6 variants containing conservative amino acid substitutions that do not substantially impair biological activity; (4) Insl6 fragments comprising at least 10 contiguous amino acids of the full-length Insl6 sequence and retaining biological activity; (5) Insl6 fusion proteins, such as Insl6-Fc fusions; and (6) nucleic acid molecules encoding any of the foregoing polypeptides.

An "Insl6-Fc fusion protein" refers to a chimeric protein comprising an Insl6 polypeptide or fragment thereof fused to an immunoglobulin Fc domain, typically derived from human IgG1, IgG2, IgG3, or IgG4. The fusion may be arranged with the Fc domain at either the N-terminus or C-terminus of the Insl6 moiety, with or without intervening linker sequences. The Fc domain confers beneficial pharmacokinetic properties including extended serum half-life through interaction with the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) and potential effector functions, although the latter may be minimized through appropriate engineering of the Fc region.

The term "autoimmune disease" or "autoimmune disorder" refers to pathological conditions arising from an inappropriate immune response against self-antigens, leading to tissue damage and organ dysfunction. As used herein, this includes inflammatory myopathies such as polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and inclusion body myositis, as well as other T cell-mediated autoimmune conditions that may benefit from Insl6-mediated immunomodulation.

"Inflammatory myopathy" refers to a group of autoimmune disorders characterized by chronic inflammation of skeletal muscle tissue, resulting in progressive muscle weakness, elevated serum muscle enzymes, and histological evidence of immune cell infiltration. The term encompasses polymyositis (characterized by endomysial CD8+ T cell infiltration and direct muscle fiber damage), dermatomyositis (characterized by perivascular and perimysial CD4+ T cell infiltration with complement-mediated microangiopathy), and inclusion body myositis (characterized by mixed inflammatory infiltrates with rimmed vacuoles and protein aggregates).

"Treatment" or "treating" refers to the administration of a therapeutic agent to a subject for the purpose of preventing, ameliorating, or curing a disease or condition, or for delaying the onset or progression of disease symptoms. Treatment may be prophylactic (administered prior to disease onset) or therapeutic (administered after disease manifestation).

A "therapeutically effective amount" refers to the quantity of an Insl6 agent sufficient to produce a desired therapeutic response in a subject, such as reduction in inflammation, improvement in muscle function, or attenuation of disease progression, without causing unacceptable adverse effects. The precise amount will vary depending on factors including the specific condition being treated, disease severity, subject characteristics (age, weight, sex), route of administration, and formulation properties.

"Pharmaceutical composition" refers to a preparation containing an Insl6 agent in combination with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents suitable for administration to a subject. The composition may be formulated for various routes of administration including intravenous, subcutaneous, intramuscular, oral, or inhalation delivery.

"Gene therapy" refers to the introduction of nucleic acid sequences encoding therapeutic proteins into a subject's cells to achieve sustained expression of the encoded protein. This may be accomplished using viral vectors (e.g., adenovirus, adeno-associated virus, lentivirus) or non-viral methods (e.g., plasmid DNA, hydrodynamic delivery, lipid nanoparticles).

### Insulin-Like 6 (Insl6) Agents

The present invention provides Insl6 agents for the treatment of autoimmune and immune-related disorders, particularly inflammatory myopathies. Native Insl6 is a secreted protein belonging to the insulin-like/relaxin family of peptide hormones, characterized by a conserved structural motif of two chains (A and B) connected by disulfide bonds. While the complete three-dimensional structure of Insl6 remains to be determined, sequence homology with other family members suggests similar folding patterns and receptor interaction domains.

Recombinant Insl6 polypeptides for therapeutic use may be produced using standard molecular biology techniques in various expression systems. Prokaryotic systems such as Escherichia coli offer high yield and cost-effective production but may lack proper post-translational modifications required for optimal activity. Eukaryotic systems including mammalian cells (e.g., CHO, HEK293), insect cells (e.g., Sf9, High Five), or yeast (e.g., Pichia pastoris) provide more authentic protein processing including disulfide bond formation, glycosylation, and proper folding, which may be critical for maintaining biological activity and stability.

Insl6 variants may be engineered to enhance specific properties such as receptor binding affinity, proteolytic stability, solubility, or pharmacokinetic profile. Conservative amino acid substitutions (e.g., replacing one hydrophobic residue with another) may be introduced without substantially altering biological function, while more extensive modifications may be designed based on structure-activity relationship studies. Variants may also include species hybrids combining domains from different mammalian Insl6 homologs to optimize therapeutic characteristics.

Biologically active fragments of Insl6 may comprise portions of the full-length protein that retain the ability to modulate immune responses and promote tissue regeneration. Such fragments typically include at least 10-15 contiguous amino acids from critical functional domains, particularly regions involved in receptor binding or dimerization. Screening assays may be employed to identify minimal active fragments that maintain therapeutic efficacy while potentially offering advantages in terms of production cost, stability, or tissue penetration.

The biological activity of Insl6 agents may be assessed using various in vitro and in vivo assays described herein, including inhibition of T cell proliferation, reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokine production, enhancement of muscle regeneration following injury, and attenuation of disease severity in experimental autoimmune myositis models. Agents demonstrating comparable or superior activity to native Insl6 in these assays are considered within the scope of the invention.

### Insl6-Fc Fusion Proteins

Insl6-Fc fusion proteins represent a particularly advantageous class of Insl6 agents provided by the present invention, combining the therapeutic activity of Insl6 with the favorable pharmacokinetic properties of immunoglobulin Fc domains. The Fc domain, typically derived from human IgG1, confers several benefits including extended serum half-life through pH-dependent recycling mediated by the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), improved solubility and stability, and simplified purification through protein A/G affinity chromatography.

The construction of Insl6-Fc fusion proteins may employ various configurations. In one embodiment, the Fc domain is fused to the N-terminus of Insl6, preceded by a secretion signal peptide to ensure proper cellular export. In another embodiment, the Fc domain is fused to the C-terminus of Insl6, potentially preserving native N-terminal processing while still providing Fc-mediated benefits. Linker sequences of varying lengths and compositions (e.g., glycine-serine repeats) may be incorporated between the Insl6 and Fc moieties to optimize spatial orientation, flexibility, and biological activity.

The choice of IgG subclass for the Fc domain may be tailored to specific therapeutic requirements. IgG1 Fc domains provide strong FcRn binding for extended half-life but may also mediate complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) through interactions with Fcγ receptors. If effector functions are undesirable, IgG2 or IgG4 Fc domains may be selected for their reduced binding to activating Fcγ receptors, or specific mutations may be introduced to ablate effector functions while preserving FcRn interaction (e.g., L234A/L235A mutations in IgG1).

Production of Insl6-Fc fusion proteins typically employs mammalian expression systems such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells or human embryonic kidney (HEK293) cells, which provide proper folding, assembly, and glycosylation of both the Insl6 and Fc components. The fusion proteins are secreted into the culture medium and purified using standard chromatographic techniques including protein A affinity chromatography, ion exchange chromatography, and size exclusion chromatography to achieve pharmaceutical-grade purity.

Insl6-Fc fusion proteins demonstrate enhanced therapeutic potential compared to native Insl6 due to their prolonged circulation time, which allows for less frequent dosing and sustained therapeutic concentrations. In experimental models, hydrodynamic delivery of Insl6-Fc plasmid DNA resulted in serum concentrations as high as 1,800 ng/ml, with corresponding improvements in disease parameters in experimental autoimmune myositis. The fusion proteins retain the biological activities of native Insl6, including inhibition of T cell proliferation and reduction of inflammatory cytokine production, while benefiting from the pharmacokinetic advantages conferred by the Fc domain.

### Methods to Treat Autoimmune Diseases and Immune-Related Diseases and Disorders

The present invention provides methods for treating subjects suffering from autoimmune diseases and immune-related disorders through the administration of Insl6 agents. The primary indication encompasses inflammatory myopathies including polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and inclusion body myositis, conditions for which current therapeutic options are limited and often inadequate. However, the immunomodulatory properties of Insl6 suggest broader applicability to other T cell-mediated autoimmune conditions characterized by pathological inflammation and tissue damage.

In polymyositis, treatment with Insl6 agents addresses the core pathological process of CD8+ cytotoxic T cell-mediated destruction of muscle fibers. By inhibiting T cell activation and proliferation, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine production (particularly TNF-α and IFN-γ), and promoting muscle regeneration, Insl6 therapy targets both the inflammatory and degenerative components of the disease. Clinical benefits may include preservation of muscle mass, improvement in strength and endurance, reduction in serum creatine kinase levels, and enhanced quality of life.

In dermatomyositis, where the primary pathology involves microvascular damage and complement activation mediated by CD4+ helper T cells and humoral immunity, Insl6 therapy may exert protective effects through multiple mechanisms. The anti-inflammatory properties of Insl6 can reduce perivascular and perimysial inflammatory infiltrates, while its pro-regenerative effects may help restore damaged muscle fibers and improve microvascular integrity. Additionally, by modulating the overall immune environment, Insl6 may indirectly influence B cell activity and autoantibody production.

For inclusion body myositis, which combines inflammatory and degenerative features with protein aggregation pathology, Insl6 therapy may provide symptomatic relief through its anti-inflammatory effects while potentially slowing disease progression by supporting muscle maintenance and repair mechanisms. Although IBM is generally less responsive to immunosuppressive therapy than other inflammatory myopathies, the dual action of Insl6 on both immune modulation and tissue regeneration represents a novel therapeutic approach worthy of investigation.

Beyond inflammatory myopathies, Insl6 agents may be effective in treating other autoimmune conditions where T cell dysregulation plays a central role. Potential applications include rheumatoid arthritis (characterized by synovial T cell infiltration and joint destruction), multiple sclerosis (mediated by autoreactive T cells targeting central nervous system myelin), type 1 diabetes (resulting from T cell-mediated destruction of pancreatic β-cells), and inflammatory bowel disease (involving dysregulated mucosal immune responses). The ability of Insl6 to inhibit T cell proliferation and IL-2 production suggests particular utility in conditions driven by excessive T cell activation and expansion.

Treatment protocols may involve acute intervention during disease flares or chronic maintenance therapy to prevent relapse and disease progression. The choice between these approaches will depend on disease characteristics, patient factors, and treatment goals. Insl6 therapy may be administered as monotherapy or in combination with existing immunomodulatory agents, potentially allowing for dose reduction of conventional drugs and minimizing their associated toxicities.

### Administration of Pharmaceutical Compositions

The pharmaceutical compositions of the present invention may be administered via various routes depending on the specific clinical indication, desired pharmacokinetic profile, and patient considerations. Parenteral administration, particularly intravenous or subcutaneous injection, represents the preferred route for systemic delivery of Insl6 agents, ensuring reliable bioavailability and controlled dosing. Intramuscular injection may also be suitable, particularly for localized delivery to affected muscle groups in inflammatory myopathies.

Intravenous administration provides immediate systemic availability and is particularly appropriate for acute disease flares requiring rapid therapeutic effect. Subcutaneous administration offers the advantage of patient self-administration and sustained absorption, making it suitable for chronic maintenance therapy. The dosing frequency will depend on the specific Insl6 agent employed, with native Insl6 potentially requiring daily or every-other-day administration due to its relatively short half-life, while Insl6-Fc fusion proteins may permit weekly or even less frequent dosing owing to their extended circulation time.

For localized delivery to affected muscles, direct intramuscular injection of Insl6 formulations may be employed, particularly in cases where systemic immunosuppression is undesirable. This approach could provide high local concentrations while minimizing systemic exposure and potential side effects. Alternatively, sustained-release formulations incorporating biodegradable polymers or hydrogels could provide prolonged local delivery following single injection.

Oral administration of Insl6 agents presents significant challenges due to proteolytic degradation in the gastrointestinal tract and poor intestinal absorption of peptide hormones. However, advanced delivery technologies such as nanoparticle encapsulation, permeation enhancers, or fusion with transcytosis-promoting domains may eventually enable effective oral delivery, greatly improving patient convenience and compliance.

Inhalation delivery may be considered for conditions involving respiratory muscle involvement or systemic absorption through pulmonary epithelium, though this route requires careful formulation to ensure stability and consistent dosing. Transdermal delivery using microneedle arrays or iontophoresis represents another potential alternative, though peptide delivery through intact skin remains technically challenging.

Dosing regimens will be individualized based on disease severity, patient response, and pharmacokinetic monitoring. Initial doses may be titrated upward to achieve therapeutic effect while monitoring for adverse reactions. Maintenance doses may be adjusted based on clinical response markers including muscle strength testing, serum creatine kinase levels, inflammatory markers, and patient-reported outcomes.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The pharmaceutical compositions of the present invention comprise Insl6 agents in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, and stabilizers suitable for the intended route of administration. For parenteral formulations, the compositions typically include isotonic aqueous solutions buffered to physiological pH (approximately 7.4) using buffers such as phosphate, citrate, or histidine. Stabilizers such as sugars (sucrose, trehalose), polyols (mannitol, sorbitol), or amino acids (arginine, glycine) may be included to prevent aggregation and maintain protein conformation during storage and handling.

Lyophilized formulations may be prepared for enhanced long-term stability, with reconstitution immediately prior to administration using sterile water or saline. These formulations typically include bulking agents (mannitol, glycine), cryoprotectants (sucrose, trehalose), and buffering agents to maintain pH upon reconstitution. Liquid formulations for ready-to-use administration may incorporate preservatives (benzyl alcohol, phenol) for multidose vials or be packaged in single-use containers to avoid preservative requirements.

For Insl6-Fc fusion proteins, additional considerations include maintaining proper redox conditions to preserve disulfide bonds and preventing surface adsorption through appropriate container selection (siliconized glass, polymer coatings). Concentration ranges for therapeutic formulations typically span 1-100 mg/mL, with higher concentrations enabling smaller injection volumes for subcutaneous delivery.

Excipients must be carefully selected to avoid interference with Insl6 biological activity or induction of immune responses against the formulation components. Particular attention should be paid to endotoxin levels, which must be maintained below regulatory thresholds (<5 EU/kg/hr for parenteral products) to prevent inflammatory reactions that could counteract therapeutic effects.

Compatibility with standard infusion materials and administration devices must be verified, including stability in common diluents (normal saline, 5% dextrose) and compatibility with infusion sets, syringes, and pumps. For subcutaneous delivery, viscosity and osmolality must be optimized to minimize injection site reactions and ensure comfortable administration.

### Gene Therapy

The present invention encompasses gene therapy approaches for sustained delivery of Insl6 to subjects in need thereof, providing an alternative to repeated protein administration. Gene therapy strategies may employ viral or non-viral vectors encoding Insl6 or Insl6-Fc fusion proteins under the control of appropriate promoters to achieve desired expression patterns and levels.

Viral vector systems offer efficient gene delivery and long-term expression but require careful consideration of immunogenicity and safety profiles. Adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors represent a particularly attractive option due to their low immunogenicity, ability to transduce both dividing and non-dividing cells, and potential for long-term episomal expression in post-mitotic tissues such as skeletal muscle. Various AAV serotypes exhibit different tissue tropisms, with AAV1, AAV6, and AAV8 showing preferential transduction of skeletal muscle, making them suitable for localized delivery to affected muscle groups in inflammatory myopathies.

Lentiviral vectors provide stable genomic integration and long-term expression but carry theoretical risks of insertional mutagenesis that must be carefully evaluated. Adenoviral vectors offer high transduction efficiency and robust transient expression but induce strong immune responses that may limit repeat administration and potentially exacerbate inflammatory conditions. Therefore, adenoviral vectors may be more suitable for acute intervention scenarios where transient expression is sufficient.

Non-viral gene delivery methods include plasmid DNA administration via hydrodynamic injection, electroporation, or nanoparticle formulations. Hydrodynamic delivery, involving rapid injection of large volumes of DNA solution through the tail vein, results in efficient hepatic transduction and systemic protein secretion, as demonstrated in the experimental autoimmune myositis model where hydrodynamic delivery of Insl6-Fc plasmid led to serum concentrations of 1,800 ng/ml and significant therapeutic effects. This approach provides a simple, non-viral method for systemic Insl6 delivery without the immunogenicity concerns associated with viral vectors.

Promoter selection is critical for achieving appropriate expression levels and patterns. For systemic delivery, constitutive promoters such as CMV or EF1α may be employed to drive high-level expression in transduced cells. For muscle-specific expression, promoters derived from muscle-specific genes such as creatine kinase M (MCK) or muscle creatine kinase (CKM) can restrict expression to skeletal muscle tissue, potentially minimizing off-target effects. Inducible promoters responsive to inflammatory signals or small molecule regulators may provide additional control over expression timing and levels.

Gene therapy approaches offer the potential for sustained therapeutic Insl6 levels following single administration, eliminating the need for frequent injections and improving patient compliance. However, careful preclinical evaluation of safety, immunogenicity, and long-term expression profiles is essential before clinical translation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The role of Insulin-like 6 (Insl6) in experimental autoimmune myositis was investigated using genetically modified mouse models and therapeutic interventions. Insl6-deficient mice (Insl6-KO) and transgenic mice overexpressing Insl6 under the control of the muscle creatine kinase promoter (MCK-Insl6-TG) were generated on a C57BL/6 background. Experimental autoimmune myositis (EAM) was induced by immunizing female mice (8-10 weeks old) with 600 μg of recombinant human skeletal muscle myosin-binding C protein emulsified in complete Freund's adjuvant containing 100 μg of heat-killed Mycobacterium butyricum, administered subcutaneously at multiple sites around the torso or feet, along with 2 μg of pertussis toxin injected intraperitoneally.

Muscle function was assessed using rotarod and treadmill tests. At baseline, no differences in motor performance were observed between wild-type and genetically modified mice. Following EAM induction, Insl6-KO mice exhibited significantly greater functional impairment compared to wild-type controls, with running distance reductions of 70-80% versus 30-40% in wild-type mice, and rotarod performance declines of approximately 90% versus 50%. Conversely, MCK-Insl6-TG mice showed attenuated functional decline, with only 40% reduction in running distance compared to 80% in wild-type controls, and significantly better rotarod performance.

Histological analysis of skeletal muscle tissues revealed that Insl6 deficiency led to increased infiltration of CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes in multiple lower limb muscles (tibialis anterior, gastrocnemius, quadriceps) following EAM induction. Quantitative immunofluorescence staining demonstrated significantly higher numbers of CD4+ and CD8+ cells in Insl6-KO muscle compared to wild-type controls, with infiltration primarily localized to perimysial and perivascular areas. Transgenic overexpression of Insl6 resulted in diminished T cell infiltration compared to wild-type controls under EAM conditions.

Quantitative real-time PCR analysis of lower limb muscle RNA confirmed the histological findings, showing elevated transcript levels of CD4, CD8, TNF-α, and IFN-γ in Insl6-KO mice compared to wild-type controls following EAM induction. In contrast, MCK-Insl6-TG mice exhibited significantly reduced induction of these inflammatory markers compared to wild-type controls. No significant differences in macrophage infiltration (assessed by F4/80 staining) were observed between genotypes.

To evaluate the therapeutic potential of acute Insl6 expression, hydrodynamic delivery of an Insl6-Fc fusion protein plasmid was performed in Insl6-KO mice at the time of EAM immunization. This procedure resulted in detectable serum levels of Fc-Insl6 fusion protein (up to 1,800 ng/ml) and led to marked improvements in rotarod performance compared to control vector-treated mice. Additionally, hydrodynamic delivery of Insl6 plasmid significantly reduced transcript levels of CD4, CD8, TNF-α, and IFN-γ in tibialis anterior muscle compared to control vector treatment.

In vitro studies using Jurkat T cells demonstrated that Insl6 overexpression significantly inhibited T cell proliferation by approximately 40% as measured by 3H-thymidine incorporation. Furthermore, Insl6 overexpression reduced PHA/PMA-induced IL-2 secretion by 40%. Conditioned medium from C2C12 myoblasts overexpressing Insl6 also significantly inhibited Jurkat cell proliferation, confirming that secreted Insl6 possesses direct anti-proliferative effects on T lymphocytes.

Analysis of human skeletal muscle biopsy samples from polymyositis and dermatomyositis patients revealed significantly reduced Insl6 mRNA transcript levels compared to healthy controls (P=0.0378), with greater heterogeneity in expression among patient samples. Importantly, lower Insl6 transcript levels correlated with increased histological evidence of myofiber atrophy, suggesting a clinically relevant relationship between Insl6 expression and disease severity.

These findings collectively demonstrate that Insl6 plays a critical protective role in experimental autoimmune myositis by modulating T cell-mediated inflammation and preserving muscle function. The reduced Insl6 expression observed in human inflammatory myopathy samples, coupled with the therapeutic efficacy of Insl6 supplementation in murine models, provides strong rationale for the development of Insl6-based therapeutics for the treatment of inflammatory myopathies and potentially other T cell-mediated autoimmune disorders.
