# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of neuropharmacology and the treatment of neurological disorders, particularly epilepsy. More specifically, the invention provides novel therapeutic compositions and methods for the treatment of seizure disorders through the synergistic combination of perampanel, a noncompetitive antagonist of α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptors, and decanoic acid, a medium-chain fatty acid known to exert direct antiseizure effects via inhibition of AMPA receptor activity. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising perampanel and decanoic acid, or pharmaceutically acceptable salts or derivatives thereof, in amounts effective to produce a synergistic inhibitory effect on AMPA receptor-mediated excitatory neurotransmission, thereby achieving enhanced seizure control with reduced dosing requirements and minimized adverse side effects compared to monotherapy with either agent alone. The disclosed invention is particularly relevant for the management of drug-resistant epilepsy, including partial-onset seizures and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures, where conventional antiepileptic drugs have failed to provide adequate seizure control or are associated with intolerable behavioral or cognitive side effects. By leveraging the distinct but complementary mechanisms of action of perampanel and decanoic acid at the AMPA receptor, the invention offers a rational, mechanism-based approach to improving therapeutic outcomes in patients with refractory epilepsy while simultaneously addressing the limitations of current pharmacological interventions.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Epilepsy is a chronic neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, unprovoked seizures resulting from abnormal, excessive, or synchronous neuronal activity in the brain. Despite the availability of numerous antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), approximately one-third of patients with epilepsy continue to experience seizures despite optimal medical therapy, a condition known as drug-resistant epilepsy. The development of new therapeutic strategies for this patient population remains a critical unmet medical need. Among the currently approved AEDs, perampanel (marketed as Fycompa) represents a unique class of medication as a selective, noncompetitive antagonist of AMPA receptors, which are ligand-gated ion channels that mediate fast excitatory synaptic transmission in the central nervous system. Perampanel has been approved as an adjunctive treatment for partial-onset seizures and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures in patients aged 12 years and older. However, its clinical utility is often limited by dose-dependent behavioral and psychiatric side effects, including aggression, irritability, and mood disturbances, which can lead to treatment discontinuation in a significant proportion of patients. These adverse effects are believed to be related to the relatively high systemic concentrations of perampanel required to achieve adequate seizure control, highlighting the need for strategies that can enhance the therapeutic efficacy of perampanel while allowing for dose reduction.

Concurrently, dietary interventions such as the ketogenic diet have emerged as effective nonpharmacologic approaches for managing drug-resistant epilepsy, particularly in pediatric populations. The medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) ketogenic diet, which is enriched in medium-chain fatty acids including decanoic acid, has been shown to provide robust seizure control in many patients who fail to respond to conventional AEDs. Historically, the antiseizure effects of the ketogenic diet were attributed primarily to the production of ketone bodies, which serve as alternative energy substrates for the brain. However, recent research has demonstrated that medium-chain fatty acids, particularly decanoic acid, possess direct antiseizure properties independent of ketosis. Specifically, decanoic acid has been shown to act as a noncompetitive inhibitor of AMPA receptors, thereby reducing excitatory neurotransmission and stabilizing neuronal networks against hyperexcitability. This discovery has shifted the paradigm regarding the mechanism of action of the MCT ketogenic diet and has opened new avenues for the development of targeted therapies based on medium-chain fatty acids.

The structural and functional characterization of AMPA receptors has revealed that these tetrameric complexes are composed of various combinations of four subunits (GluA1–GluA4), with GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 heteromers representing the most prevalent subunit compositions in the hippocampus, a brain region critically involved in the generation and propagation of seizures. Both perampanel and decanoic acid have been shown to inhibit AMPA receptor function through noncompetitive mechanisms, but emerging evidence suggests that they bind to distinct sites on the receptor complex. In silico modeling and mutagenesis studies indicate that perampanel binds to the interface between the S1-M1 and S2-M4 linkers of the AMPA receptor, while decanoic acid interacts with the transmembrane M3 region. This spatial separation of binding sites provides a compelling rationale for the potential synergistic interaction between these two compounds, as simultaneous occupancy of both sites could lead to enhanced receptor inhibition beyond what would be expected from simple additive effects.

Despite the theoretical promise of combining perampanel with decanoic acid, prior to the present invention, there was no experimental evidence demonstrating such synergy, nor were there any established therapeutic protocols for co-administering these agents in clinical practice. The lack of systematic investigation into the combined effects of perampanel and decanoic acid on AMPA receptor function and seizure activity represented a significant gap in the scientific understanding of potential combinatorial approaches for epilepsy treatment. Furthermore, the absence of data from human tissue models left unanswered questions regarding the translational relevance of any observed synergistic effects in preclinical systems. The present invention addresses these critical knowledge gaps by providing comprehensive experimental evidence of synergistic AMPA receptor inhibition and enhanced seizure control through the combined use of perampanel and decanoic acid, thereby establishing a novel therapeutic paradigm for the management of drug-resistant epilepsy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel therapeutic approach for the treatment of epilepsy and other neurological disorders characterized by excessive AMPA receptor-mediated excitatory neurotransmission through the synergistic combination of perampanel and decanoic acid. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that perampanel and decanoic acid, when administered together, produce a synergistic inhibitory effect on AMPA receptor function that is significantly greater than the sum of their individual effects. This synergy arises from the distinct but complementary mechanisms by which these compounds interact with the AMPA receptor complex, with perampanel binding to the S1-M1 and S2-M4 linker regions while decanoic acid binds to the M3 transmembrane domain, thereby producing enhanced receptor inhibition through simultaneous occupancy of multiple allosteric sites.

The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising perampanel and decanoic acid, or pharmaceutically acceptable salts, esters, or derivatives thereof, in therapeutically effective amounts that produce synergistic AMPA receptor inhibition. These compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, or subcutaneous delivery, and may be presented in immediate-release, extended-release, or controlled-release formulations to optimize therapeutic efficacy and minimize adverse effects. The invention also provides methods for treating epilepsy and related seizure disorders by administering to a patient in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a composition comprising perampanel and decanoic acid, wherein the combination produces enhanced seizure control compared to monotherapy with either agent alone, while simultaneously allowing for reduced dosing of perampanel and thereby minimizing its associated behavioral and psychiatric side effects.

The synergistic effects of the invention have been demonstrated across multiple experimental models, including recombinant AMPA receptor electrophysiology in Xenopus oocytes expressing physiologically relevant GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 subunit combinations, ex vivo rat hippocampal slice preparations exhibiting pentylenetetrazol (PTZ)-induced seizure-like activity, and human neocortical brain slices obtained from patients undergoing neurosurgical resection for neoplastic lesions. In all models, the combination of perampanel and decanoic acid produced significantly greater inhibition of AMPA receptor currents and seizure activity compared to either compound alone, with isobolographic analysis confirming true synergistic rather than merely additive effects. The invention is particularly advantageous for the treatment of drug-resistant epilepsy, including partial-onset seizures and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and may also find application in other neurological conditions characterized by AMPA receptor hyperactivity, such as certain neurodegenerative disorders, neuropathic pain syndromes, and psychiatric conditions involving glutamatergic dysregulation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Combination

The present invention is predicated on the discovery that the combination of perampanel and decanoic acid produces a synergistic inhibitory effect on AMPA receptor function that is significantly greater than the sum of their individual effects. This synergistic interaction represents a fundamental advance in the pharmacological treatment of epilepsy and other neurological disorders characterized by excessive glutamatergic neurotransmission. The combination therapy of the present invention leverages the distinct but complementary mechanisms of action of perampanel and decanoic acid at the AMPA receptor complex, resulting in enhanced therapeutic efficacy while simultaneously allowing for dose reduction of perampanel and consequent minimization of its associated adverse effects.

The synergistic nature of this combination has been rigorously demonstrated through multiple experimental approaches, including concentration-response curve analysis, isobolographic analysis, and comparative efficacy studies in both animal and human tissue models. In recombinant AMPA receptor systems expressed in Xenopus oocytes, the presence of perampanel at clinically relevant concentrations (1-4 μmol/L) significantly reduced the IC50 values for decanoic acid inhibition of both GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 receptor subtypes, indicating enhanced potency of decanoic acid in the presence of perampanel. Conversely, the presence of decanoic acid at physiologically achievable concentrations (50-100 μmol/L) significantly reduced the IC50 values for perampanel inhibition of the same receptor subtypes, demonstrating reciprocal enhancement of perampanel's potency by decanoic acid. Isobolographic analysis, which is considered the gold standard for distinguishing between additive and synergistic drug interactions, confirmed that the combination points for perampanel and decanoic acid consistently fell below the theoretical additive line, providing definitive evidence of true synergism rather than mere additivity.

The molecular basis for this synergistic interaction lies in the distinct binding sites occupied by perampanel and decanoic acid on the AMPA receptor complex. Structural and mutagenesis studies have demonstrated that perampanel binds to the interface between the S1-M1 and S2-M4 linkers of the AMPA receptor, while decanoic acid binds to the transmembrane M3 region. This spatial separation of binding sites allows for simultaneous occupancy of both sites without steric hindrance, leading to conformational changes in the receptor that result in enhanced channel closure and reduced ion flux. The synergistic effect is particularly pronounced in the context of the GluA2-containing receptor subtypes (GluA1/2 and GluA2/3), which represent the predominant AMPA receptor configurations in the hippocampus and other brain regions critically involved in seizure generation and propagation.

The combination therapy of the present invention offers several significant advantages over existing monotherapy approaches. First, it enables enhanced seizure control through the synergistic amplification of AMPA receptor inhibition, potentially providing therapeutic benefits for patients who have failed to respond adequately to conventional antiepileptic drugs. Second, it allows for substantial dose reduction of perampanel while maintaining or even improving therapeutic efficacy, thereby minimizing the incidence and severity of perampanel-associated behavioral and psychiatric side effects that often limit its clinical utility. Third, it provides a rational, mechanism-based approach to combining pharmacological and dietary interventions, bridging the gap between traditional antiepileptic drug therapy and the well-established benefits of the medium-chain triglyceride ketogenic diet.

The combination of perampanel and decanoic acid may be administered simultaneously or sequentially, with the timing and sequence of administration optimized based on the specific clinical indication, patient characteristics, and desired pharmacokinetic profile. The combination may be formulated as a single pharmaceutical composition containing both active ingredients, or as separate formulations administered concurrently or according to a defined schedule. The relative proportions of perampanel and decanoic acid in the combination may be adjusted to achieve optimal therapeutic outcomes while minimizing adverse effects, with typical weight ratios ranging from about 1:100 to about 100:1, depending on the specific clinical context and patient needs.

### Decanoic Acid and Compositions Comprising the Same

Decanoic acid, also known as capric acid, is a saturated medium-chain fatty acid with the chemical formula CH3(CH2)8COOH. It is a naturally occurring compound found in various dietary sources, including coconut oil, palm kernel oil, and dairy products, and is a key component of the medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) ketogenic diet used in the treatment of epilepsy. In the context of the present invention, decanoic acid serves as a direct, noncompetitive antagonist of AMPA receptors, contributing to the synergistic inhibition of excitatory neurotransmission when combined with perampanel.

Decanoic acid suitable for use in the present invention may be obtained from natural sources or synthesized through conventional chemical methods. The compound may be used in its free acid form or as a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, ester, or derivative thereof. Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of decanoic acid include alkali metal salts (such as sodium or potassium salts), alkaline earth metal salts (such as calcium or magnesium salts), and ammonium salts. Esters of decanoic acid may include methyl, ethyl, propyl, or other alkyl esters, as well as glycerol esters that are characteristic of medium-chain triglycerides. Derivatives of decanoic acid may include modified forms that retain the essential structural features required for AMPA receptor binding and inhibition while potentially improving pharmacokinetic properties such as solubility, stability, or bioavailability.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising decanoic acid for use in the present invention may be formulated using conventional pharmaceutical techniques and may include various excipients, carriers, and diluents appropriate for the intended route of administration. For oral administration, decanoic acid may be formulated as capsules, tablets, powders, granules, or liquid suspensions, optionally incorporating enteric coatings or other release-modifying agents to control the site and rate of absorption. The compound may also be administered as part of a specialized dietary formulation, such as a modified MCT oil or emulsion, designed to provide consistent and controlled delivery of decanoic acid to the systemic circulation.

The concentration of decanoic acid in pharmaceutical compositions of the present invention will depend on the specific therapeutic indication, the presence of other active ingredients (particularly perampanel), and the desired pharmacokinetic profile. In general, decanoic acid concentrations in the range of 50-1000 μmol/L have been shown to be effective in producing synergistic AMPA receptor inhibition when combined with perampanel, with optimal concentrations typically falling between 100-300 μmol/L based on experimental data from both animal and human tissue models. These concentrations are physiologically achievable through dietary supplementation with medium-chain triglycerides, as evidenced by clinical studies showing average blood concentrations of approximately 157 μmol/L in patients maintained on the MCT ketogenic diet.

The pharmacokinetic properties of decanoic acid are characterized by rapid absorption from the gastrointestinal tract, efficient transport to the liver via the portal circulation, and subsequent distribution to peripheral tissues including the brain. Unlike long-chain fatty acids, medium-chain fatty acids such as decanoic acid do not require carnitine-dependent transport for mitochondrial entry and can be rapidly metabolized for energy production. However, the antiseizure effects of decanoic acid in the present invention are mediated primarily through its direct action on AMPA receptors rather than through metabolic effects, as demonstrated by experiments in ex vivo brain slice preparations where systemic metabolism is absent.

Compositions comprising decanoic acid for use in the present invention may also include other medium-chain fatty acids or related compounds that may contribute to the overall therapeutic effect or improve the formulation characteristics. For example, octanoic acid (caprylic acid), another medium-chain fatty acid commonly found in MCT oils, may be included in combination with decanoic acid to provide additional antiseizure effects or to modify the physical properties of the formulation. However, the specific synergistic interaction with perampanel appears to be particularly pronounced for decanoic acid, likely due to its optimal chain length and structural characteristics for AMPA receptor binding.

### Perampanel

Perampanel, chemically designated as 2-(2-oxo-1-phenyl-5-pyridin-2-yl-1,2-dihydropyridin-3-yl)benzonitrile, is a selective, noncompetitive antagonist of AMPA receptors that has been approved for the treatment of partial-onset seizures and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures in patients aged 12 years and older. In the context of the present invention, perampanel serves as one of the two key components of the synergistic combination therapy, working in concert with decanoic acid to produce enhanced inhibition of AMPA receptor-mediated excitatory neurotransmission.

Perampanel suitable for use in the present invention may be obtained from commercial sources or synthesized through established chemical routes. The compound is typically used in its free base form, although pharmaceutically acceptable salts may also be employed if desired. The chemical structure of perampanel features a pyridinone core substituted with phenyl and pyridyl groups, which together create the specific three-dimensional conformation required for high-affinity binding to the AMPA receptor at the S1-M1 and S2-M4 linker interface.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising perampanel for use in the present invention may be formulated using conventional pharmaceutical techniques and may include various excipients, carriers, and diluents appropriate for the intended route of administration. Currently marketed formulations of perampanel are available as oral tablets in strengths of 2 mg, 4 mg, 6 mg, 8 mg, 10 mg, and 12 mg, designed for once-daily administration. In the context of the present invention, these existing formulations may be used in combination with decanoic acid-containing compositions, or new formulations may be developed that incorporate both active ingredients in a single dosage unit.

The concentration of perampanel in pharmaceutical compositions of the present invention will be determined based on the synergistic interaction with decanoic acid, allowing for significant dose reduction compared to monotherapy regimens. Experimental data from the present invention demonstrate that perampanel concentrations as low as 100-500 nmol/L (equivalent to plasma concentrations of approximately 0.03-0.15 mg/L) can produce significant synergistic effects when combined with decanoic acid, compared to the typical therapeutic plasma concentrations of 1.06-3.26 μmol/L (approximately 0.3-1.0 mg/L) achieved with standard monotherapy doses. This represents a potential dose reduction of 50-75% while maintaining or improving therapeutic efficacy, which would substantially reduce the incidence and severity of perampanel-associated behavioral and psychiatric side effects.

The pharmacokinetic properties of perampanel are characterized by good oral bioavailability, extensive distribution throughout the body including the central nervous system, and metabolism primarily by cytochrome P450 enzymes, particularly CYP3A4. The elimination half-life of perampanel is approximately 105 hours, supporting once-daily dosing regimens. In the context of the present invention, the pharmacokinetic profile of perampanel may be modified through formulation strategies to optimize the temporal overlap with decanoic acid concentrations, ensuring that both compounds are present at therapeutic levels simultaneously to maximize synergistic effects.

Compositions comprising perampanel for use in the present invention may also include other antiepileptic drugs or adjuvant therapies, although the primary focus of the invention is the specific synergistic combination with decanoic acid. The inclusion of other active ingredients would be guided by the specific clinical needs of the patient population and the potential for additional therapeutic interactions or pharmacokinetic considerations.

### Treatment

The present invention provides novel methods for the treatment of epilepsy and related neurological disorders through the synergistic combination of perampanel and decanoic acid. The treatment methods encompass the administration of therapeutically effective amounts of both compounds to patients suffering from seizure disorders, particularly those with drug-resistant epilepsy who have failed to respond adequately to conventional antiepileptic drug therapy. The synergistic interaction between perampanel and decanoic acid results in enhanced seizure control compared to monotherapy with either agent alone, while simultaneously allowing for reduced dosing of perampanel and consequent minimization of its associated adverse effects.

The treatment methods of the present invention are particularly applicable to patients with partial-onset seizures, primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and other forms of drug-resistant epilepsy. The combination therapy may be initiated in patients who have already failed to achieve adequate seizure control with standard antiepileptic drugs, or it may be considered as a first-line adjunctive therapy in patients at high risk for developing drug resistance. The treatment may also be beneficial for patients who have experienced intolerable side effects with perampanel monotherapy, as the dose reduction enabled by the synergistic combination may allow these patients to continue receiving the therapeutic benefits of AMPA receptor inhibition while avoiding problematic behavioral and psychiatric adverse effects.

The administration of the combination therapy may be tailored to individual patient needs based on factors such as seizure type and frequency, previous treatment history, age, weight, and comorbid medical conditions. The initial dosing regimen may involve starting with standard doses of perampanel and gradually introducing decanoic acid through dietary supplementation or pharmaceutical administration, followed by careful titration of both components to achieve optimal seizure control with minimal side effects. Alternatively, the combination therapy may be initiated with reduced doses of perampanel from the outset, taking advantage of the synergistic enhancement provided by decanoic acid to achieve therapeutic efficacy at lower perampanel concentrations.

The treatment methods of the present invention may also be applied to other neurological conditions characterized by excessive AMPA receptor-mediated excitatory neurotransmission, including certain neurodegenerative disorders such as amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and Huntington's disease, neuropathic pain syndromes, and psychiatric conditions involving glutamatergic dysregulation such as schizophrenia and major depressive disorder. The broad applicability of AMPA receptor modulation in these conditions, combined with the enhanced efficacy and improved safety profile provided by the synergistic combination, makes the present invention potentially valuable across a wide spectrum of neurological and psychiatric disorders.

The duration of treatment with the combination therapy will depend on the individual patient's response and the underlying nature of their seizure disorder. In some cases, long-term maintenance therapy may be required to prevent seizure recurrence, while in others, the combination therapy may be used as a bridge to other therapeutic interventions or as a temporary measure during periods of increased seizure susceptibility. Regular monitoring of seizure frequency, adverse effects, and laboratory parameters will be essential to ensure optimal therapeutic outcomes and patient safety.

### Administration

The administration of the synergistic combination of perampanel and decanoic acid according to the present invention may be accomplished through various routes and formulations, depending on the specific clinical indication, patient characteristics, and desired pharmacokinetic profile. The primary route of administration for both compounds is oral, given the established safety and efficacy of oral formulations for perampanel and the natural dietary source of decanoic acid through medium-chain triglyceride supplementation. However, other routes of administration may be considered in specific clinical situations or for particular patient populations.

For oral administration, perampanel is typically formulated as immediate-release tablets that are taken once daily, preferably in the evening to minimize potential central nervous system side effects. In the context of the present invention, these existing formulations may be used in combination with decanoic acid-containing dietary supplements or pharmaceutical preparations. Decanoic acid may be administered orally as part of a specialized MCT oil formulation, as enteric-coated capsules designed to release the compound in the small intestine, or as a component of a modified ketogenic diet specifically designed to provide consistent and controlled delivery of decanoic acid.

The timing of administration for both compounds is an important consideration in optimizing the synergistic interaction. Given the long elimination half-life of perampanel (approximately 105 hours) and the relatively shorter duration of action of decanoic acid, the administration schedule may be designed to ensure that both compounds are present at therapeutic concentrations simultaneously. This may involve administering decanoic acid multiple times daily to maintain consistent plasma levels, while perampanel is administered once daily as per standard practice. Alternatively, extended-release formulations of decanoic acid may be developed to provide sustained plasma concentrations that better align with the pharmacokinetic profile of perampanel.

The dosing regimen for the combination therapy will be individualized based on patient response, tolerability, and therapeutic goals. Initial dosing may involve starting with standard doses of perampanel (typically 2-4 mg daily) and gradually introducing decanoic acid at doses that achieve plasma concentrations in the range of 100-300 μmol/L, which corresponds to approximately 15-30 mL of MCT oil daily in adults. Once the combination therapy is established, the dose of perampanel may be gradually reduced while maintaining or improving seizure control, with the goal of achieving the lowest effective dose that minimizes adverse effects.

In specific clinical situations, such as acute seizure emergencies or perioperative settings, alternative routes of administration may be considered. Intravenous formulations of perampanel are not currently available, but could potentially be developed for emergency use. Decanoic acid could potentially be administered intravenously as part of a lipid emulsion, although this would require careful consideration of safety and tolerability issues. Subcutaneous or intramuscular administration of either compound is generally not practical due to formulation challenges and potential local irritation.

The administration of the combination therapy should be accompanied by appropriate patient education regarding the importance of adherence to the prescribed regimen, potential side effects, and the need for regular monitoring. Patients should be advised about the potential for drug interactions, particularly with medications that induce or inhibit cytochrome P450 enzymes, which could affect the metabolism of perampanel. Dietary counseling may also be necessary to ensure proper administration of decanoic acid and to address any nutritional concerns related to the modified dietary regimen.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Methods

The experimental methods employed in the present invention were designed to comprehensively evaluate the synergistic interaction between perampanel and decanoic acid across multiple biological systems, from recombinant receptor expression to human tissue models. The primary experimental approach involved AMPA receptor electrophysiology using Xenopus laevis oocytes expressing physiologically relevant combinations of AMPA receptor subunits, specifically GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 heteromers, which represent the predominant AMPA receptor configurations in the hippocampus. Oocytes were injected with cRNA encoding the appropriate subunit combinations and allowed to express functional receptors for 2-5 days before electrophysiological recordings. Whole-cell voltage-clamp recordings were performed using standard two-electrode voltage-clamp techniques, with oocytes held at a membrane potential of -60 mV. Glutamate (100 μmol/L) was applied to elicit inward currents, and the effects of perampanel and decanoic acid, both individually and in combination, were assessed by measuring the reduction in peak current amplitude.

Seizure-like activity was induced in rat entorhinal cortex-hippocampal slice preparations using pentylenetetrazol (PTZ) at a concentration of 5 mmol/L, which reliably produces synchronized epileptiform discharges that can be quantified by measuring the frequency and amplitude of field potential recordings. Brain slices (400 μm thick) were prepared from adult male Sprague-Dawley rats using standard procedures and maintained in oxygenated artificial cerebrospinal fluid (ACSF) at 32°C. After establishing stable baseline seizure activity, increasing concentrations of decanoic acid were applied at 10-minute intervals in the presence or absence of fixed concentrations of perampanel (100 or 500 nmol/L). The frequency of epileptiform discharges was measured at 1-minute intervals, averaged every 5 minutes, and normalized to baseline activity to determine the concentration-response relationship and calculate IC50 values.

Human neocortical brain slices were obtained from patients undergoing elective neurosurgical resection for intraaxial neoplasms, with appropriate informed consent and ethical approval from the Newcastle and North Tyneside 2 Research Ethics Committee (06/Q1003/51). Tissue was immediately transferred to ice-cold, oxygenated ACSF and sliced into 450 μm sections using a vibratome. Slices were allowed to recover for at least 1 hour before experimentation. Epileptiform activity was induced using a proconvulsant ACSF containing elevated potassium (7.5 mmol/L) and reduced magnesium (0.5 mmol/L), which reliably produces synchronized network activity that can be quantified by measuring the area power of local field potential (LFP) recordings. The effects of decanoic acid (100 μmol/L), perampanel (0.5 μmol/L), and their combination were assessed by comparing the area power of epileptiform activity to baseline recordings.

Statistical analysis was performed using appropriate methods for each experimental design. Concentration-response data were fitted to Hill equations to determine IC50 values and Hill coefficients, with statistical significance assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Dunnett's post hoc test for multiple comparisons. Nonparametric data from human tissue experiments were analyzed using Mann-Whitney tests. Synergistic interactions were formally assessed using isobolographic analysis, which compares the experimentally determined combination effects to the theoretical additive line calculated from the individual concentration-response relationships. All experiments were performed with appropriate controls and replicates to ensure reliability and reproducibility of the results.

### Example 2—Direct Inhibition of AMPA Receptors by Perampanel and Decanoic Acid

The direct inhibitory effects of perampanel and decanoic acid on AMPA receptor function were systematically evaluated using recombinant receptor expression in Xenopus oocytes. Initial experiments confirmed that perampanel inhibits AMPA receptor currents in a noncompetitive manner, with IC50 values of 8.11 μmol/L (SEM 2.05) against GluA2/3 receptors and 6.27 μmol/L (SEM 1.02) against GluA1/2 receptors. These findings demonstrated for the first time the direct inhibition of AMPA receptors by perampanel through noncompetitive mechanisms, without significant subunit specificity between the two predominant hippocampal receptor subtypes.

To confirm the distinct binding sites proposed for perampanel and decanoic acid, a GluA3 mutant receptor resistant to the AMPA receptor antagonist GYKI was expressed and tested for sensitivity to both compounds. Perampanel (20 μmol/L) reduced wild-type GluA3 glutamate-induced currents by 94.7%, but the mutant receptor currents by only 54.3% (P < 0.0001), indicating that the GYKI resistance mutation also affects perampanel binding. In contrast, decanoic acid (1 mmol/L) reduced both wild-type and mutant receptor currents by similar amounts (74.5% and 72.8%, respectively), strongly supporting the proposed distinct sites of action for the two compounds.

The synergistic interaction between perampanel and decanoic acid was then directly tested by measuring AMPA receptor sensitivity to decanoic acid in the presence of fixed concentrations of perampanel. For GluA2/3 receptors, the IC50 for decanoic acid decreased from 0.52 mmol/L (no perampanel) to 0.15 mmol/L (1 μmol/L perampanel) or 0.12 mmol/L (4 μmol/L perampanel) (P < 0.0001). Similarly, for GluA1/2 receptors, the IC50 for decanoic acid decreased from 1.16 mmol/L (no perampanel) to 0.21 mmol/L (1 μmol/L perampanel) or 0.10 mmol/L (4 μmol/L perampanel) (P < 0.0001). Reciprocal experiments testing perampanel sensitivity in the presence of fixed concentrations of decanoic acid showed similar synergistic effects, with IC50 values for perampanel decreasing from 8.1 μmol/L to 1.7 μmol/L (50 μmol/L decanoic acid) or 1.6 μmol/L (100 μmol/L decanoic acid) in GluA2/3 receptors, and from 6.2 μmol/L to 2.1 μmol/L or 2.2 μmol/L, respectively, in GluA1/2 receptors (both P < 0.0001).

Isobolographic analysis was performed to formally distinguish between additive and synergistic effects. The isobolic line representing additive effects was calculated based on the individual concentration-response relationships, and experimental combination points were plotted at fixed ratios of perampanel and decanoic acid. All combination points fell significantly below the isobolic line for both GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 receptor subtypes, providing definitive evidence of true synergistic interaction rather than simple additivity. These results established that the combination of perampanel and decanoic acid produces enhanced AMPA receptor inhibition through simultaneous occupancy of distinct allosteric sites, resulting in conformational changes that amplify the inhibitory effect beyond what would be expected from either compound alone.

### Example 3—Effects of Perampanel and Decanoic Acid on Seizure Models

The functional consequences of the synergistic AMPA receptor inhibition were evaluated in both animal and human tissue models of seizure activity. In rat entorhinal cortex-hippocampal slice preparations, PTZ-induced seizure-like activity was used to assess the antiseizure effects of the combination therapy. Increasing concentrations of decanoic acid alone reduced epileptiform activity, with complete blockade achieved at 1000 μmol/L, consistent with previous reports. However, in the presence of perampanel (100 or 500 nmol/L), significantly lower concentrations of decanoic acid were required to achieve equivalent seizure suppression. Specifically, the IC50 for decanoic acid decreased from 352 μmol/L (CI 200-621) in the absence of perampanel to 196 μmol/L (CI 145-264) at 100 nmol/L perampanel and 122 μmol/L (CI 49-302, P = 0.0252) at 500 nmol/L perampanel. At clinically relevant concentrations, the combination of decanoic acid (10 μmol/L) with perampanel (100 nmol/L) caused a significant reduction in seizure activity from 95.6% (CI 81-110) to 76.8% (CI 63-90, P = 0.048), with complete blockade achieved at 600 μmol/L decanoic acid.

The translational relevance of these findings was confirmed using human neocortical brain slices obtained from patients undergoing neurosurgical resection for neoplastic lesions. In these experiments, neither decanoic acid (100 μmol/L) alone nor perampanel (0.5 μmol/L) alone produced statistically significant reductions in epileptiform activity compared to baseline (P = 0.1390 and P = 0.7554, respectively). However, the concurrent application of both compounds resulted in a dramatic and highly significant reduction in the area power of epileptiform activity from a baseline of 1690 μV² (CI 1192-2191 μV²) to 144.7 μV² (CI 15.6-274 μV², P < 0.0001), representing a virtual abolition of pathologic network activity. Statistical comparison confirmed that the combination effect was significantly greater than either compound alone (P = 0.0005 vs. decanoic acid; P = 0.0002 vs. perampanel).

These results demonstrate that the synergistic AMPA receptor inhibition observed in recombinant systems translates directly to enhanced seizure control in both animal and human neural tissue, with the combination therapy producing effects that are not achievable with either compound alone at the concentrations tested. The human tissue data are particularly significant as they provide direct evidence of the therapeutic potential of the combination in the clinical setting, bridging the gap between preclinical findings and human application. The ability to achieve near-complete suppression of epileptiform activity in human brain tissue with concentrations of perampanel and decanoic acid that are individually subtherapeutic represents a major advance in the treatment of drug-resistant epilepsy.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel and highly effective approach to the treatment of epilepsy and related neurological disorders through the synergistic combination of perampanel and decanoic acid. This combination therapy leverages the distinct but complementary mechanisms of action of these two compounds at the AMPA receptor complex, resulting in enhanced inhibitory effects that are significantly greater than the sum of their individual actions. The synergistic interaction has been rigorously demonstrated across multiple experimental systems, from recombinant receptor expression in Xenopus oocytes to human neocortical brain slices, providing compelling evidence of both mechanistic validity and translational relevance.

The key advantages of the present invention include enhanced seizure control through amplified AMPA receptor inhibition, the ability to substantially reduce perampanel dosing while maintaining or improving therapeutic efficacy, and consequent minimization of perampanel-associated behavioral and psychiatric side effects that often limit its clinical utility. The combination therapy represents a rational integration of pharmacological and dietary approaches to epilepsy treatment, bridging the gap between traditional antiepileptic drug therapy and the well-established benefits of the medium-chain triglyceride ketogenic diet.

The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising perampanel and decanoic acid in therapeutically effective amounts, methods for treating epilepsy and related disorders through the administration of these compositions, and kits for implementing the combination therapy in clinical practice. The synergistic effects have been demonstrated for both partial-onset seizures and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures, with particular relevance for patients with drug-resistant epilepsy who have failed to respond adequately to conventional antiepileptic drugs. The human tissue data provide direct evidence of the therapeutic potential of the combination in the clinical setting, establishing a strong foundation for future clinical trials and eventual implementation in routine medical practice.

The present invention thus represents a significant advance in the field of neuropharmacology, offering a novel, mechanism-based approach to improving therapeutic outcomes in patients with refractory epilepsy while simultaneously addressing the limitations of current pharmacological interventions. The synergistic combination of perampanel and decanoic acid provides a powerful new tool for clinicians managing difficult-to-treat seizure disorders, with the potential to transform the treatment landscape for patients with drug-resistant epilepsy.
