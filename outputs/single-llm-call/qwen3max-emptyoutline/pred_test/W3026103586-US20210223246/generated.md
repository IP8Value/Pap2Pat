# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of intraoperative diagnostic devices and methods for the real-time detection of metastatic cancer cells in lymph nodes, particularly sentinel lymph nodes (SLNs) and auxiliary lymph nodes (ALNs), during surgical procedures for cancers such as breast carcinoma. More specifically, the invention relates to a minimally invasive, rapid, and reliable diagnostic tool that exploits the metabolic acidification of lymph node microenvironments caused by fatty acid oxidation in metastatic cancer cells. The invention provides a novel apparatus—referred to herein as the Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD)—comprising a standard medical syringe needle integrated with a pH-sensitive indicator medium, enabling immediate visual assessment of lymph node status without requiring tissue excision, histopathological processing, or complex instrumentation. This technology operates on the biochemical principle that cancer cells which have metastasized to lymph nodes undergo a metabolic shift from glycolysis to fatty acid oxidation (FAO), resulting in the localized production of carbon dioxide and subsequent generation of protons (H⁺) via hydration reactions, thereby lowering the extracellular pH of the lymphatic fluid within affected nodes. The MMLD leverages this pathophysiological hallmark of metastasis to deliver a point-of-care diagnostic readout within seconds, offering significant advantages over conventional intraoperative techniques such as frozen section analysis, which are time-consuming, operator-dependent, and prone to sampling error. The invention further encompasses methods of use, calibration protocols based on pathological correlation, and potential adaptations for integration into both surgical and interventional radiological workflows, with broad applicability across oncology disciplines where lymphatic spread is a critical prognostic and therapeutic determinant.

## BACKGROUND

The accurate intraoperative identification of lymph node metastasis is a cornerstone of cancer staging and treatment planning, particularly in solid tumors such as breast carcinoma, melanoma, and head and neck cancers. Among these, breast cancer represents one of the most prevalent malignancies worldwide, and its management is heavily influenced by the status of the sentinel lymph node—the first lymph node or group of nodes to receive lymphatic drainage from the primary tumor site. The presence of metastatic deposits in the sentinel lymph node is widely recognized as the earliest indicator of systemic disease dissemination and directly impacts decisions regarding the extent of axillary lymph node dissection, adjuvant chemotherapy, and radiation therapy. Consequently, reliable and timely assessment of sentinel lymph node involvement during surgery is essential to optimize patient outcomes while minimizing unnecessary morbidity.

Historically, the gold standard for intraoperative lymph node evaluation has been the frozen section technique, wherein a portion of the excised lymph node is rapidly frozen, thinly sliced, stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), and examined under a microscope by a pathologist. While this method provides histological confirmation of malignancy, it suffers from several well-documented limitations. First, the process is inherently time-constrained, typically requiring 15 to 30 minutes per sample, which can prolong surgical duration and increase anesthesia-related risks. Second, due to time pressures and logistical constraints, only a limited portion of the lymph node—often selected based on gross morphological features such as firmness, discoloration, or size—is subjected to analysis, leading to potential sampling errors and false-negative results when micrometastases or isolated tumor cells are present in unexamined regions. Third, the interpretation of frozen sections is highly dependent on the expertise and subjective judgment of the pathologist, introducing inter-observer variability and diagnostic uncertainty, especially in cases involving reactive hyperplasia, granulomatous inflammation, or histiocytic mimics of carcinoma. These shortcomings collectively contribute to an estimated false-negative rate of 10% to 20% in frozen section analysis, necessitating secondary surgeries or adjuvant treatments based on postoperative permanent section findings.

In response to these challenges, various alternative approaches have been explored to improve the speed, accuracy, and comprehensiveness of intraoperative lymph node assessment. One strategy involves molecular techniques such as reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) for detecting tumor-specific mRNA markers; however, these methods require specialized laboratory infrastructure, trained personnel, and extended processing times, rendering them impractical for routine intraoperative use. Another approach utilizes immunohistochemical staining for cytokeratin or other epithelial markers to enhance sensitivity, but this too adds complexity and delays diagnosis. More recently, imaging-based modalities have been investigated, including near-infrared fluorescence imaging using tumor-targeted agents such as anti-EGFR or anti-HER2 conjugates labeled with fluorescent dyes, often combined with radioactive tracers like 99mTc-phytate for dual-modality guidance. While promising in preclinical models, these techniques demand costly imaging systems, regulatory approval of contrast agents, and careful timing of tracer administration, limiting their accessibility and generalizability.

Physical sensing methods have also been proposed, such as microwave ablation followed by thermal or dielectric property measurements, or tissue impedance spectroscopy, which exploits differences in electrical conductivity between normal and malignant tissues. Although these technologies offer non-invasive or minimally invasive assessment, they typically require dedicated hardware, calibration against known standards, and data interpretation algorithms, and their clinical validation remains limited. Moreover, many of these approaches still involve indirect inference of malignancy rather than direct biochemical interrogation of the tumor microenvironment.

A fundamental biological insight underpinning the present invention is the recognition that cancer cells undergo profound metabolic reprogramming upon metastasizing to lymph nodes. Recent studies, notably by Lee et al., have demonstrated through comparative transcriptomic analyses that breast cancer cells colonizing lymph nodes shift their primary energy metabolism from aerobic glycolysis—the Warburg effect commonly observed in primary tumors—to fatty acid oxidation (FAO). This metabolic adaptation is driven by the upregulation of Yes-Associated Protein (YAP), which transcriptionally activates a suite of FAO-related genes, enabling metastatic cells to utilize lipid substrates abundant in the lymphatic milieu. The biochemical consequence of enhanced FAO is the increased production of acetyl-CoA, which enters the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, ultimately yielding carbon dioxide (CO₂) as a terminal byproduct. In the aqueous environment of lymphatic fluid, CO₂ rapidly equilibrates with water via the reversible reaction: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻, catalyzed by carbonic anhydrase enzymes expressed by both tumor and stromal cells. This reaction generates free protons (H⁺), leading to a measurable decrease in extracellular pH within metastatic lymph nodes compared to their healthy counterparts.

This acidification phenomenon presents a unique and exploitable biomarker for metastasis detection. Unlike structural or molecular markers that may vary between tumor subtypes or be obscured by inflammatory processes, the pH shift is a direct, quantifiable, and universal consequence of altered cancer cell metabolism in the lymphatic niche. However, prior to the present invention, no practical, real-time method existed to harness this biochemical signature for intraoperative decision-making. Existing pH-sensing technologies, such as microelectrodes or fiber-optic probes, are either too bulky for fine-needle applications, require external readout devices, or lack the robustness and simplicity needed in the sterile, fast-paced operating room environment.

Thus, there remains a critical unmet need for a diagnostic tool that is rapid (providing results in under one minute), minimally invasive (requiring no tissue removal), operator-independent (yielding objective, visual readouts), cost-effective (using off-the-shelf components), and grounded in a biologically validated mechanism. The present invention addresses this gap by integrating a simple, calibrated pH indicator—specifically, a commercially available litmus test strip—into a standard 18-gauge syringe needle, creating a self-contained, single-use device capable of sampling lymph node fluid and instantly revealing its acidity through a color change. This innovation not only circumvents the pitfalls of frozen section analysis but also avoids the complexity and expense of emerging molecular or imaging-based alternatives, offering a pragmatic solution that can be readily adopted in diverse clinical settings, including resource-limited hospitals.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel diagnostic apparatus and associated method for the real-time, intraoperative detection of metastatic cancer cells in lymph nodes, particularly sentinel and auxiliary lymph nodes, based on the metabolic acidification of the lymph node microenvironment. The core component of the invention is the Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD), which comprises a standard medical syringe needle—preferably of 18-gauge diameter with an inner lumen of approximately 838 micrometers—integrated with a pH-sensitive indicator medium, such as a thin strip of litmus paper (e.g., ColorpHast® pH Test Strips, Merck, Germany). The MMLD is designed to be inserted percutaneously or intraoperatively into a lymph node of interest, whereupon a small volume (e.g., 100 microliters) of sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) is injected to gently irrigate the nodal parenchyma and elute interstitial fluid. The pH-sensitive indicator, positioned at or near the needle tip, comes into contact with this fluid and undergoes a visible color change corresponding to the local pH. By calibrating the color response against a reference scale, the operator can immediately determine whether the pH falls below a predetermined threshold—typically pH 6.0—which has been empirically established as indicative of metastatic involvement due to fatty acid oxidation-driven acidification.

The method of use involves minimal procedural steps: after identifying the target lymph node (e.g., via blue dye or radiotracer mapping in sentinel node biopsy), the MMLD is introduced into the node under direct visualization or ultrasound guidance. PBS is injected to facilitate fluid exchange, and the color of the embedded indicator is observed within seconds. If the indicator registers a pH less than 6.0, the lymph node is classified as metastatic; if the pH is 7.0 or higher, it is deemed non-metastatic. To enhance reliability, the procedure may be repeated with two additional MMLD units at different sites within the same node, and a positive diagnosis is confirmed if any one of the three readings indicates acidity below the threshold. This tripartite sampling strategy mitigates the risk of false negatives due to heterogeneous tumor distribution.

The invention is predicated on the discovery that metastatic cancer cells in lymph nodes exhibit a distinct metabolic phenotype characterized by upregulated fatty acid oxidation, leading to increased CO₂ production and consequent proton release via carbonic anhydrase-mediated hydration. This results in a consistent and measurable drop in extracellular pH—from a physiological range of 7.0–10.0 in healthy nodes to below 6.0 in involved nodes—as validated in a clinical cohort of 65 lymph nodes from 25 breast cancer patients. Comparative analysis against permanent H&E histopathology (the diagnostic gold standard) demonstrated that the MMLD achieves approximately 92% sensitivity and 90% selectivity, outperforming conventional frozen section analysis, which exhibited only 83% sensitivity and selectivity in the same cohort. Notably, the MMLD correctly identified one case of micrometastasis that was missed by frozen section, underscoring its potential to reduce diagnostic errors.

Beyond its primary application in surgical oncology, the MMLD may also be adapted for use in interventional radiology, where it could assist in guiding fine-needle aspiration or core biopsies by pre-screening lymph nodes for malignancy. Furthermore, by enabling real-time, non-destructive assessment, the invention offers the possibility of preserving healthy sentinel lymph nodes that would otherwise be excised unnecessarily during standard frozen section workflows, thereby reducing the incidence of postoperative complications such as lymphedema, sensory neuropathy, and shoulder dysfunction. The device is manufactured under stringent sterility protocols (e.g., ISO/NP 22441 plasma sterilization), ensuring safety and compatibility with existing surgical practices. In summary, the present invention represents a paradigm shift in intraoperative lymph node diagnostics—transforming a complex, error-prone histological process into a simple, rapid, and biochemically grounded point-of-care test.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1: Real-Time Diagnosis of Cancer Involved Lymph Nodes

The following detailed example illustrates the construction, calibration, clinical validation, and operational protocol of the Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD) as applied to the intraoperative diagnosis of metastatic involvement in sentinel and auxiliary lymph nodes of patients undergoing surgery for invasive breast carcinoma. This example is not intended to limit the scope of the invention but rather to provide a concrete embodiment demonstrating its feasibility, efficacy, and advantages over existing methodologies.

**Device Fabrication and Composition**  
The MMLD is constructed using standard, commercially available medical components to ensure ease of manufacturing, regulatory compliance, and clinical adoption. The primary structural element is a sterile, single-use hypodermic needle of 18-gauge specification, which possesses an outer diameter of approximately 1.27 millimeters and an inner lumen diameter of 838 micrometers. This gauge is selected to balance mechanical rigidity for tissue penetration with sufficient internal volume to accommodate both fluid delivery and the pH-sensing element. Affixed within the distal end of the needle lumen—preferably extending to the very tip to maximize exposure to nodal fluid—is a thin, flexible strip of pH-sensitive indicator paper. In the preferred embodiment, this is a ColorpHast® pH test strip (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany), which provides a continuous colorimetric response across the pH range of 0 to 14, with high resolution in the acidic spectrum (pH 1–6). The strip is secured in place using medical-grade, biocompatible adhesive or mechanical retention features (e.g., micro-notches or crimping) that prevent displacement during insertion or fluid injection, while ensuring full contact with the aspirated or irrigated lymphatic fluid. The proximal end of the needle is fitted to a standard Luer-lock syringe barrel containing 100 microliters of sterile, isotonic phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), pH 7.4, which serves as the irrigation medium. The entire assembly is packaged in a sealed, sterile pouch and terminally sterilized using low-temperature plasma sterilization in accordance with ISO/NP 22441 standards, preserving the integrity of the pH-sensitive chemistry while ensuring microbial safety.

**Biological Rationale and Calibration Protocol**  
The operational principle of the MMLD is rooted in the metabolic reprogramming of cancer cells that have metastasized to lymph nodes. As established by recent genomic and metabolomic studies, breast cancer cells adapting to the lymphatic microenvironment downregulate glycolytic pathways and upregulate fatty acid oxidation (FAO) through YAP-mediated transcriptional activation of enzymes such as carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A (CPT1A) and acyl-CoA dehydrogenases. This metabolic shift enhances the β-oxidation of fatty acids, generating acetyl-CoA that feeds into the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. The oxidative decarboxylation reactions of the TCA cycle produce carbon dioxide (CO₂) as a waste product. In the aqueous phase of lymphatic fluid, CO₂ undergoes spontaneous or carbonic anhydrase-catalyzed hydration according to the equilibrium: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. The accumulation of free protons (H⁺) lowers the extracellular pH, creating a localized acidic niche that is absent in non-metastatic lymph nodes, where physiological pH typically ranges from 7.0 to 10.0 due to normal immune cell activity and fluid composition.

To translate this biochemical insight into a diagnostic threshold, a calibration study was conducted using freshly excised lymph nodes from patients undergoing axillary lymph node dissection for confirmed invasive breast carcinoma. Immediately after surgical removal and before any fixation or freezing, each lymph node was subjected to MMLD testing: the needle was inserted into the node, 100 µL of PBS was injected to mix with interstitial fluid, and the resulting color of the litmus strip was recorded photographically and matched to a standardized pH color chart. Concurrently, each node underwent comprehensive histopathological evaluation via permanent hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining—the clinical gold standard—to determine true metastatic status (positive or negative). Statistical analysis of the paired pH and pathology data revealed a bimodal distribution: all histologically confirmed metastatic nodes exhibited pH values below 6.0, whereas all non-metastatic nodes registered pH values of 7.0 or higher. A diagnostic cutoff of pH < 6.0 was thus established, providing optimal separation between the two populations with minimal overlap.

**Clinical Validation Study**  
The performance of the MMLD was prospectively evaluated in a cohort of 25 female patients diagnosed with invasive breast carcinoma, who were scheduled for sentinel lymph node biopsy and/or axillary lymph node dissection between October 30, 2018, and November 30, 2019, at accredited breast cancer centers under ethical approval (IR.TUMS.VCR.REC.1397.532). A total of 65 lymph nodes (including both sentinel and auxiliary nodes) were tested intraoperatively using the MMLD protocol immediately after excision. Each node was assessed independently by three separate MMLD units inserted at spatially distinct locations to account for potential intranodal heterogeneity. A node was classified as MMLD-positive if any one of the three readings indicated pH < 6.0. For comparison, all nodes also underwent standard intraoperative frozen section analysis by board-certified pathologists, followed by definitive diagnosis via permanent H&E sections.

The results demonstrated superior diagnostic accuracy for the MMLD relative to frozen section. Against the permanent H&E gold standard, the MMLD achieved a sensitivity of 92% (correctly identifying 23 of 25 metastatic nodes) and a selectivity (specificity) of 90% (correctly identifying 36 of 40 non-metastatic nodes), with only one false-positive and one false-negative result. In contrast, frozen section analysis yielded sensitivity and selectivity of 83% each (21/25 and 33/40, respectively), missing two metastatic cases—including one instance of micrometastasis involving less than 2 mm of tumor deposit—that were clearly detected by the MMLD. Visual documentation (e.g., Figure 5B of the research paper) confirmed that the MMLD-positive, frozen-section-negative node contained discrete clusters of carcinoma cells identifiable only upon exhaustive sectioning in permanent histology, highlighting the MMLD’s ability to sample the entire nodal volume biochemically rather than relying on limited histological slices.

**Operational Workflow in the Operating Room**  
The integration of the MMLD into standard surgical practice is straightforward and requires minimal deviation from existing sentinel lymph node biopsy protocols. After preoperative lymphoscintigraphy and/or blue dye injection to localize the sentinel node(s), the surgeon exposes the target node(s) through a standard incision. Rather than immediately excising the node for frozen section, the surgeon inserts the MMLD needle directly into the intact node under direct vision. The plunger of the attached syringe is depressed to inject 100 µL of PBS, which diffuses through the nodal parenchyma, mixing with interstitial fluid and dissolving any acidic metabolites present. Within 10 to 30 seconds, the color of the litmus strip stabilizes and is visually interpreted by comparing it to a printed or digital reference scale included in the device packaging. If the color corresponds to pH < 6.0, the node is deemed metastatic, prompting the surgeon to proceed with complete axillary lymph node dissection (typically removing at least six auxiliary nodes). If the pH is ≥7.0, the node is considered non-metastatic, and further dissection may be avoided, preserving lymphatic function and reducing morbidity.

Crucially, because the MMLD does not require tissue removal for diagnosis, a non-metastatic sentinel node can remain in situ, potentially eliminating the need for its excision altogether in future optimized protocols. This represents a significant advance over current practice, where even histologically negative sentinel nodes are routinely removed, exposing patients to risks of lymphedema (occurring in up to 20% of cases), nerve injury, seroma formation, and impaired arm mobility. By enabling real-time, non-destructive assessment, the MMLD supports a more conservative, function-preserving surgical approach.

**Advantages and Broader Applications**  
The MMLD offers multiple advantages over existing intraoperative diagnostics: it is rapid (<30 seconds per test), inexpensive (utilizing commodity materials), operator-independent (colorimetric readout requires no specialized training), and grounded in a universal metabolic biomarker rather than variable protein expression. Its simplicity makes it suitable for deployment in low-resource settings lacking advanced pathology services. Beyond breast cancer, the device may be applicable to any malignancy where lymph node metastasis induces similar metabolic acidification, including melanoma, gastric cancer, and prostate cancer. Additionally, in interventional radiology suites, the MMLD could be used percutaneously under ultrasound guidance to triage suspicious lymph nodes prior to biopsy, increasing the yield of targeted sampling. Future iterations may incorporate digital colorimetry via smartphone cameras for quantitative pH measurement or multiplexed indicators for simultaneous detection of other metabolites (e.g., lactate, glucose). However, even in its current form, the MMLD fulfills a long-standing clinical need for a reliable, real-time, and minimally invasive tool to guide lymph node management during cancer surgery, thereby improving precision oncology and patient quality of life.
