## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel human triple-negative breast cancer cell line designated DKAT, and methods of using said cell line to study epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity, tumor heterogeneity, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance in aggressive breast cancers. More specifically, the invention provides a unique in vitro and in vivo model system that recapitulates the dynamic phenotypic transitions observed in highly aggressive, treatment-resistant, metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. The DKAT cell line exhibits reversible epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) in response to defined culture conditions, expresses markers of breast cancer stem cells, and forms heterogeneous tumors in xenograft models that mirror the morphological and immunohistochemical diversity of the original patient tumor. The invention further encompasses methods for identifying molecular regulators of phenotypic plasticity, such as the transcription factor Zeb1, and for screening therapeutic agents that target or modulate EMT/MET dynamics in triple-negative breast cancer. The DKAT cell line and its derivatives thus serve as a powerful platform for investigating the biological mechanisms underlying tumor progression, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance in a clinically relevant subset of breast cancer characterized by inherent cellular plasticity rather than fixed clonal heterogeneity.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Breast cancer is a heterogeneous disease encompassing multiple molecular subtypes with distinct clinical behaviors, prognoses, and therapeutic responses. Gene expression profiling has historically classified breast cancers into five major subtypes: luminal A, luminal B, HER2-enriched, normal-like, and basal-like. Among these, the basal-like subtype is predominantly composed of triple-negative breast cancers (TNBCs), which lack expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and HER2 amplification. TNBCs represent approximately 15–20% of all breast cancers and are associated with a poor prognosis, high rates of early recurrence, and a propensity for visceral and brain metastases. They occur more frequently in younger women, particularly those of African descent, and in carriers of BRCA1 mutations. Despite initial responsiveness to chemotherapy in some cases, a significant subset of TNBCs exhibit intrinsic or rapidly acquired resistance to conventional cytotoxic agents and radiation, leading to aggressive disease progression and limited treatment options.

A growing body of evidence suggests that the aggressive behavior of certain TNBCs may be linked to the activation of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), a developmental program co-opted by cancer cells to enhance motility, invasiveness, and resistance to apoptosis. EMT is characterized by the loss of epithelial markers such as E-cadherin, claudins, and occludins, and the gain of mesenchymal markers including vimentin, N-cadherin, and smooth muscle actin (SMA), accompanied by cytoskeletal reorganization and a shift from a cobblestone to a spindle-shaped morphology. Importantly, EMT has been associated with the acquisition of stem cell-like properties, which may contribute to tumor initiation, therapy resistance, and metastatic colonization. However, the role of EMT in human breast cancer remains controversial, largely due to the difficulty in observing complete EMT in clinical specimens. Metastatic lesions often retain an epithelial phenotype similar to the primary tumor, suggesting that EMT in vivo may be partial, transient, or reversible—a concept termed epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity.

This plasticity hypothesis posits that cancer cells dynamically shift between epithelial and mesenchymal states in response to microenvironmental cues, rather than undergoing a permanent, irreversible transformation. Such phenotypic flexibility could enable cells to disseminate from the primary tumor (via EMT), survive in circulation, and then re-establish proliferative epithelial colonies at distant sites (via MET). Circulating tumor cells (CTCs) exhibiting co-expression of both epithelial and mesenchymal markers have been detected in patients with metastatic breast and prostate cancer, supporting the existence of intermediate hybrid states in vivo. Nevertheless, the study of epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity in human breast cancer has been hampered by the lack of appropriate experimental models that faithfully recapitulate this dynamic behavior.

Existing breast cancer cell lines often represent fixed phenotypic states—either stably epithelial (e.g., MCF-7, T47D) or stably mesenchymal (e.g., MDA-MB-231)—and do not readily undergo reversible EMT/MET in response to physiological stimuli. While some cell lines can be induced to undergo EMT through overexpression of transcription factors like Snail, Twist, or Zeb1, or through prolonged exposure to growth factors such as TGF-β, these models typically do not revert to an epithelial state upon withdrawal of the inducing stimulus, limiting their utility for studying plasticity. Moreover, many established models fail to capture the heterogeneity and stem-like characteristics observed in aggressive clinical TNBCs.

There is therefore a critical unmet need for a biologically relevant, experimentally tractable model system that mirrors the dynamic phenotypic transitions, tumor heterogeneity, and stem cell features of aggressive, treatment-resistant, metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. Such a model would enable mechanistic studies of the molecular drivers of plasticity, facilitate the identification of novel therapeutic targets, and provide a platform for preclinical drug screening aimed at disrupting the metastatic cascade or overcoming therapy resistance in this challenging disease subset.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel human triple-negative breast cancer cell line, designated DKAT, which was isolated from the malignant pleural effusion of a patient with rapidly progressing, treatment-resistant, metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. The DKAT cell line exhibits unprecedented phenotypic plasticity, reversibly transitioning between epithelial and mesenchymal states in response to defined culture conditions, and serves as a robust model for studying epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity, tumor heterogeneity, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance in aggressive breast cancer.

In one aspect, the invention provides the DKAT cell line, which has been deposited with the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) under Accession No. [to be assigned], and derivatives thereof. The DKAT cell line is characterized by triple-negative status (ER−/PR−/HER2-wt), expression of basal cytokeratins (CK5, CK14, CK17), luminal cytokeratin (CK18), membrane and cytoplasmic E-cadherin, phosphorylated Akt (Ser473), and a high frequency of CD44+/CD24−/low cells indicative of stem-like properties. The cell line harbors a missense mutation in TP53 (R273H) but lacks mutations in PIK3CA exons 9 and 20.

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for inducing epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in DKAT cells by culturing the cells in Stromal Cell Growth Media (SCGM) comprising insulin, basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), and serum (e.g., 5% fetal bovine serum). Under these conditions, DKAT cells rapidly downregulate epithelial markers (E-cadherin, CK5, CK17, claudin-1) and upregulate mesenchymal markers (vimentin, N-cadherin), adopt a spindle-shaped morphology, and exhibit enhanced migratory capacity.

In a further aspect, the invention provides methods for inducing mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) in DKAT cells by returning cells that have undergone EMT to Mammary Epithelial Growth Media (MEGM). Upon return to MEGM, DKAT cells reverse the EMT phenotype, re-expressing epithelial markers and downregulating mesenchymal markers, thereby demonstrating reversible phenotypic plasticity.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides clonal populations derived from single DKAT cells, which retain the ability to undergo both EMT and MET, confirming that the observed plasticity is an inherent property of individual cells rather than the result of selection of pre-existing subpopulations.

In another embodiment, the invention identifies Zeb1 as a key regulator of phenotypic plasticity in DKAT cells. Overexpression of Zeb1 in epithelial DKAT cells is sufficient to induce EMT, while stable knockdown of Zeb1 using shRNA prevents the increase in cell migration normally observed upon EMT induction. Thus, the invention provides methods for modulating EMT/MET in DKAT cells by targeting Zeb1 expression or activity.

The invention further provides in vivo models of DKAT tumorigenesis and plasticity. DKAT cells are highly tumorigenic in immunocompromised mice, forming heterogeneous tumors with regions exhibiting epithelial, mesenchymal, and myoepithelial differentiation depending on the injection site and presence of Matrigel. These xenograft tumors recapitulate the morphological and immunohistochemical diversity of the original patient tumor, including invasive growth patterns, intraductal spread, and co-expression of basal and luminal markers within the same tumor mass.

Additionally, the invention provides tumorsphere assays wherein single DKAT cells form multicellular spheres containing both epithelial (E-cadherin+/vimentin−) and mesenchymal (E-cadherin−/vimentin+) subpopulations, as well as dual-positive cells, demonstrating the multilineage differentiation potential and inherent plasticity of individual DKAT cells.

The DKAT cell line and associated methods of the invention are useful for a variety of applications, including but not limited to: (1) elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity in triple-negative breast cancer; (2) identifying and validating novel therapeutic targets involved in EMT/MET regulation; (3) screening compounds for their ability to inhibit EMT, promote MET, or block the migratory and invasive properties of plastic cancer cells; (4) modeling tumor heterogeneity and stem cell dynamics in aggressive breast cancer; and (5) developing personalized therapeutic strategies for patients with plastic, treatment-resistant TNBC.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a novel human triple-negative breast cancer cell line, DKAT, and its use as a model system for studying epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity, tumor heterogeneity, and metastasis. The DKAT cell line was established from the malignant pleural effusion of a 35-year-old Caucasian woman diagnosed with a rapidly progressing, treatment-resistant, metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. The patient’s disease exhibited remarkable phenotypic diversity across primary, recurrent, and metastatic sites, with co-existing epithelial and mesenchymal features suggestive of dynamic EMT/MET processes in vivo. The DKAT cell line faithfully recapitulates this plasticity, making it a uniquely valuable tool for cancer research.

The DKAT cell line is maintained in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media (MEGM), which supports its baseline epithelial phenotype. In MEGM, DKAT cells express a combination of basal (CK5, CK14, CK17, p63) and luminal (CK18) cytokeratins, consistent with a basal-like molecular subtype. E-cadherin is expressed both at the membrane and in the cytoplasm, and low levels of vimentin are detectable. The cells are negative for ER and PR, exhibit diploid HER2 copy number, and express EGFR, confirming their triple-negative status. DKAT cells also display constitutive activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway, as evidenced by high levels of phosphorylated Akt (Ser473), likely due to relatively low PTEN expression rather than PIK3CA mutations. Furthermore, a high percentage of DKAT cells exhibit the CD44+/CD24−/low immunophenotype, which is associated with breast cancer stem cells and EMT.

A defining feature of the DKAT cell line is its ability to undergo reversible EMT and MET in response to changes in culture media. When switched from MEGM to Stromal Cell Growth Media (SCGM)—a medium containing insulin, bFGF, and 5% fetal bovine serum—DKAT cells rapidly initiate EMT. Within two hours, cells undergo morphological changes from a cobblestone to a spindle shape, and E-cadherin is internalized from the membrane to the cytoplasm. By 24 hours, vimentin expression increases dramatically (up to 100-fold), and by 14 days, E-cadherin protein levels are significantly reduced, while mesenchymal markers such as N-cadherin and vimentin are strongly upregulated. Concurrently, epithelial markers including CK5, CK17, and claudin-1 are downregulated. These molecular and morphological changes are accompanied by a significant increase in cell migration, as demonstrated by scratch/wound healing assays.

Critically, this EMT process is reversible. When DKAT cells that have been maintained in SCGM for multiple passages (exhibiting a stable mesenchymal phenotype) are returned to MEGM, they undergo MET. Within five passages, vimentin and N-cadherin expression decrease, while E-cadherin, CK17, and claudin-1 are restored to baseline levels. This bidirectional plasticity distinguishes DKAT from other breast cancer cell lines, which typically do not revert to an epithelial state after EMT induction.

To confirm that this plasticity is not due to the outgrowth of pre-existing subpopulations, clonal cell lines were generated from single DKAT cells cultured in MEGM. These clonal lines, when exposed to SCGM, underwent EMT with kinetics identical to the parental population. Similarly, clonal lines derived from single mesenchymal DKAT cells (grown in SCGM) reverted to an epithelial phenotype upon return to MEGM, demonstrating that individual DKAT cells possess the intrinsic capacity for bidirectional phenotypic switching.

At the molecular level, the transcription factor Zeb1 has been identified as a key regulator of this plasticity. Zeb1 protein levels are markedly elevated in DKAT cells undergoing EMT in SCGM, but not in cells maintained in MEGM. Constitutive overexpression of Zeb1 in epithelial DKAT cells is sufficient to induce EMT, characterized by downregulation of E-cadherin and claudin-1 and upregulation of vimentin. Conversely, stable knockdown of Zeb1 using lentiviral shRNA constructs prevents the increase in cell migration normally observed upon EMT induction, indicating that Zeb1 is necessary for the functional consequences of EMT in this model.

In vivo, DKAT cells are highly tumorigenic. As few as 10 cells injected into the mammary fat pad of immunocompromised mice can form palpable tumors within 2–4 weeks. The resulting xenografts exhibit striking heterogeneity, mirroring the original patient tumor. Without Matrigel, DKAT tumors display infiltrative growth patterns, including cords of cells invading adipose tissue, nests surrounded by dense fibrous stroma, and intraductal or Pagetoid spread along host mammary ducts. With Matrigel, tumors form organized structures with peripheral invasive cells expressing CK5 and SMA, and central nests of epithelial cells expressing CK8/18. Three-color immunofluorescence staining using human-specific p53 confirms that both basal/myoepithelial (CK14+) and luminal (CK14−) cells within the tumor are of human origin, ruling out host cell infiltration as the source of heterogeneity.

In three-dimensional tumorsphere culture, single DKAT cells generate multicellular spheres containing mixed populations of epithelial (E-cadherin+/vimentin−), mesenchymal (E-cadherin−/vimentin+), and dual-positive cells, as well as CK5+/CK18− and CK5−/CK18+ subpopulations. This demonstrates the multilineage differentiation potential of individual DKAT cells and reinforces the model of inherent plasticity rather than clonal selection.

The DKAT cell line thus represents a paradigm-shifting model for studying the dynamic nature of aggressive breast cancer. Unlike static models that represent fixed endpoints, DKAT captures the fluidity of phenotypic states that likely underlie metastasis and therapy resistance in patients. This model enables researchers to dissect the molecular circuitry of plasticity, identify vulnerabilities in plastic cancer cells, and develop strategies to lock cells in less aggressive states or eliminate plastic subpopulations.

## DEFINITIONS

As used herein, the term “DKAT cell line” refers to the human triple-negative breast cancer cell line deposited with the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) under Accession No. [to be assigned], and any derivative thereof that retains the core characteristics of phenotypic plasticity, triple-negative status, and tumorigenicity described herein.

The term “epithelial-mesenchymal transition” or “EMT” refers to a biological process whereby cells lose epithelial characteristics—including cell polarity, cell-cell adhesion mediated by E-cadherin, and expression of cytokeratins—and acquire mesenchymal traits such as increased motility, invasiveness, expression of vimentin and N-cadherin, and a spindle-shaped morphology. In the context of the DKAT cell line, EMT is induced by culturing cells in Stromal Cell Growth Media (SCGM).

The term “mesenchymal-epithelial transition” or “MET” refers to the reverse process of EMT, wherein mesenchymal cells regain epithelial features. In the DKAT model, MET is induced by returning cells from SCGM to Mammary Epithelial Growth Media (MEGM).

“Phenotypic plasticity” means the ability of a single cell or cell population to reversibly switch between distinct phenotypic states—in this case, epithelial and mesenchymal—in response to environmental cues, without permanent genetic alteration.

“Triple-negative breast cancer” or “TNBC” refers to breast cancers that lack expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and HER2 amplification or overexpression, as determined by standard clinical diagnostic criteria.

“Mammary Epithelial Growth Media” or “MEGM” refers to a serum-free medium optimized for the growth of human mammary epithelial cells, typically containing bovine pituitary extract, insulin, human recombinant epidermal growth factor, and hydrocortisone.

“Stromal Cell Growth Media” or “SCGM” refers to a medium containing insulin, basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), and serum (e.g., 5% fetal bovine serum), used herein to induce EMT in DKAT cells.

“Tumorsphere” refers to a non-adherent, spherical cluster of cells formed in ultra-low attachment culture conditions supplemented with growth factors, used to assess self-renewal and stem-like properties.

“CD44+/CD24−/low” refers to a cell surface marker profile associated with breast cancer stem cells, characterized by high expression of CD44 and low or absent expression of CD24.

“Zeb1” refers to zinc finger E-box binding homeobox 1, a transcription factor that represses E-cadherin and other epithelial genes and is a key regulator of EMT.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Establishment and Characterization of the DKAT Cell Line**

The DKAT cell line was established from malignant pleural effusion obtained from a 35-year-old female patient with biopsy-confirmed triple-negative breast cancer. The patient’s primary tumor was ER−/PR−/HER2-wt, CK5+, and EGFR+. Despite initial treatment with cyclophosphamide, methotrexate, and 5-fluorouracil, the disease recurred within the chest wall radiation field at seven months and metastasized widely to lung, pleura, liver, and bone by ten months. The patient succumbed to disease at twelve months post-diagnosis. Pleural fluid was centrifuged, and the cell pellet was resuspended in MEGM. Cells began proliferating within 48 hours and have been maintained continuously for over 70 passages. Spectral karyotyping revealed a hyperdiploid modal chromosome number of 56, with clonal abnormalities including double minutes. Sequencing identified a TP53 R273H mutation but no PIK3CA mutations in exons 9 or 20. Immunophenotyping confirmed triple-negative status (ER−/PR−/HER2-wt), expression of basal (CK5, CK17, p63) and luminal (CK18) cytokeratins, membrane and cytoplasmic E-cadherin, phosphorylated Akt (Ser473), and a high frequency of CD44+/CD24−/low cells. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of transcript expression placed DKAT within the basal subtype, closely related to SUM-149 and HMEC lines.

### Example 2

**In Vitro Induction of EMT in DKAT Cells**

DKAT cells cultured in MEGM exhibit an epithelial phenotype with strong E-cadherin and CK5 expression and weak vimentin staining. To induce EMT, cells were switched to SCGM (containing insulin, bFGF, and 5% FBS). Within 2 hours, cells adopted a spindle-shaped morphology and E-cadherin redistributed from the membrane to the cytoplasm. Western blotting showed a 100-fold increase in vimentin by 1 hour and a significant decrease in E-cadherin by day 14. Immunofluorescence confirmed loss of membranous E-cadherin and gain of vimentin. Concurrently, CK5 and CK18 were downregulated. In scratch assays, DKAT-SCGM cells closed wounds nearly 100% by 12 hours, compared to 60% for DKAT-MEGM cells, demonstrating enhanced migration. Microarray analysis revealed downregulation of epithelial genes (KRT5, KRT6C, TP63, CLDN1) and upregulation of mesenchymal and pro-invasive genes (TGFA, LOX, ALDH1A3, CDH2, FGF2).

### Example 3

**Reversibility of EMT: Induction of MET in DKAT Cells**

DKAT cells maintained in SCGM for 10 passages exhibited a stable mesenchymal phenotype. Upon return to MEGM, these cells underwent MET. After five passages in MEGM, western blotting showed decreased vimentin and N-cadherin and increased E-cadherin, CK17, and claudin-1, returning to baseline levels. Morphologically, cells reverted to a cobblestone appearance. This demonstrates that EMT in DKAT cells is fully reversible and not a permanent state, highlighting the dynamic plasticity of this model.

### Example 4

**Clonal Analysis Confirms Intrinsic Plasticity**

To rule out selection of pre-existing subpopulations, single DKAT cells were isolated in MEGM and expanded into clonal lines. One clone with low vimentin and high E-cadherin underwent EMT in SCGM with identical kinetics to the parental line. Conversely, a clonal line derived from a single mesenchymal DKAT cell (grown in SCGM) was switched to MEGM. After 14 days, the culture contained both cobblestone, E-cadherin+/vimentin− cells and spindle-shaped, E-cadherin−/vimentin+ cells, demonstrating that a single cell can give rise to both phenotypes. This confirms that plasticity is an inherent property of individual DKAT cells.

### Example 5

**Specificity of DKAT Plasticity Compared to Other Breast Cancer Lines**

To test whether media-induced plasticity is unique to DKAT, other breast cancer lines were exposed to MEGM or SCGM. MDA-MB-231 (mesenchymal) showed no change in E-cadherin or vimentin when cultured in MEGM. MCF-7, SUM-149, and SUM-190 (epithelial) showed no EMT when cultured in SCGM. hTERT-immortalized HMECs showed slight E-cadherin loss and vimentin gain in SCGM but failed to proliferate due to serum-induced arrest. Thus, reversible EMT/MET is a distinctive feature of the DKAT cell line.

### Example 6

**Role of Zeb1 in Regulating DKAT Plasticity**

Western blotting showed that Zeb1, but not Snail1 or Twist, was upregulated in DKAT-SCGM cells. To test sufficiency, Zeb1 was overexpressed in DKAT-MEGM cells via retroviral transduction. This induced EMT, with decreased E-cadherin and claudin-1 and increased vimentin. To test necessity, two independent shRNAs against Zeb1 were introduced. Upon EMT induction, Zeb1-knockdown cells failed to increase migration in scratch assays, unlike control cells. Thus, Zeb1 is both necessary and sufficient for EMT and associated migration in DKAT cells.

### Example 7

**In Vivo Tumorigenicity and Heterogeneity of DKAT Xenografts**

Limiting dilution assays showed that 10 DKAT cells injected into the mammary fat pad of NOD/SCID mice formed tumors in 2/8 mice, demonstrating high tumorigenicity. Tumors exhibited three morphological patterns: (1) infiltrating cords without fibrous stroma, (2) nests with dense fibrous matrix, and (3) intraductal/Pagetoid spread. With Matrigel, tumors formed organized structures with CK5+/SMA+ peripheral cells and CK8/18+ central cells. Human-specific p53 staining confirmed that both basal and luminal cells were of tumor origin, not host contamination. This in vivo heterogeneity mirrors the original patient tumor and validates DKAT as a model of plasticity.

### Example 8

**Tumorsphere Formation and Multilineage Differentiation**

Single DKAT cells were plated in ultra-low attachment plates with MEGM supplemented with B27, heparin, EGF, and bFGF. After 10–14 days, solid tumorspheres formed with 3–10% efficiency. Immunofluorescence revealed co-existing E-cadherin+/vimentin− and E-cadherin−/vimentin+ cells within the same sphere, as well as CK5+/CK18− and CK5−/CK18+ populations. This demonstrates that a single DKAT cell can generate both epithelial and mesenchymal progeny, supporting the stem-like and plastic nature of the cell line.

### Example 9

**Therapeutic Screening Platform Using DKAT Cells**

The DKAT model is used to screen compounds for anti-plasticity activity. Candidate agents are added to DKAT cells undergoing EMT in SCGM. Inhibition of vimentin upregulation, prevention of E-cadherin loss, or blockade of migration in scratch assays indicates anti-EMT activity. Conversely, compounds that promote MET in mesenchymal DKAT cells are identified by restoration of E-cadherin and loss of vimentin upon treatment in MEGM. Hits are validated in tumorsphere and xenograft models. This platform enables discovery of drugs targeting the plasticity axis in TNBC.
