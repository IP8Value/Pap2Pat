# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of photovoltaic devices, and more particularly to perovskite-based solar cells incorporating a multi-layered hole transport layer (HTL) architecture engineered through controlled vapor-phase doping. Specifically, the invention relates to an organometal halide perovskite solar cell comprising a sequentially deposited n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD (2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene) hole transport layer fabricated via high-vacuum thermal evaporation. The invention further encompasses methods for precisely tuning the energy level alignment between the perovskite absorber and the HTL by modulating the vapor pressure of a molecular n-type dopant—dimethylcyclopropenium (DMC)—during deposition, thereby optimizing hole extraction efficiency while simultaneously enhancing device stability against ambient environmental degradation. The disclosed technology addresses critical limitations in conventional perovskite solar cells, including interfacial energy mismatches, pinhole-induced perovskite decomposition, and dopant-induced instability arising from solution-processed organic hole transport materials. By leveraging a pinhole-free, amorphous, vacuum-deposited HTL with spatially graded doping profiles, the invention enables long-term operational stability under both ambient air and high-vacuum conditions without sacrificing power conversion efficiency. The technical scope further extends to the integration of in situ ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) as a process control tool for real-time monitoring and calibration of energy level alignment during HTL fabrication, ensuring reproducible device performance across large-area substrates. This invention thus represents a significant advancement in the commercialization pathway for perovskite photovoltaics by reconciling the traditionally opposing demands of high efficiency and environmental robustness.

## BACKGROUND ART

Perovskite solar cells have emerged as a disruptive photovoltaic technology due to their rapid efficiency improvements—from 3.8% in 2009 to over 25% in recent years—alongside advantages such as low-temperature solution processability, tunable bandgaps, and high absorption coefficients. Among the various architectures, the n-i-p (normal) configuration, typically comprising a fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) transparent conductive oxide, a compact titanium dioxide (TiO₂) electron transport layer, a methylammonium lead halide perovskite absorber (e.g., MAPbI₃), a hole transport layer (HTL), and a metal back contact (e.g., Au), has been widely adopted. In this structure, the HTL plays a pivotal role in extracting photogenerated holes from the perovskite and transporting them to the anode while blocking electrons to minimize recombination losses. Spiro-OMeTAD has become the de facto standard HTL material owing to its suitable highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level (~5.2 eV below vacuum), reasonable hole mobility, and optical transparency in the visible spectrum. However, pristine spiro-OMeTAD exhibits intrinsically low conductivity and hole mobility, necessitating chemical doping to render it functional in high-performance devices.

Conventional doping strategies involve solution-processing spiro-OMeTAD with lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP), followed by exposure to ambient air to oxidize the spiro-OMeTAD and generate hole carriers. While effective in boosting initial efficiency, this approach suffers from severe drawbacks. First, the hygroscopic nature of Li-TFSI attracts moisture, accelerating perovskite decomposition under ambient conditions. Second, the solution-coated HTL often contains pinholes and morphological defects due to poor film-forming properties, creating direct pathways for oxygen and water vapor to penetrate and degrade the underlying perovskite layer. Third, the uncontrolled and non-uniform oxidation process leads to batch-to-batch variability and long-term instability, as the dopants continue to migrate or react over time. These issues collectively result in rapid performance decay—often exceeding 50% loss in power conversion efficiency (PCE) within days or weeks under ambient storage—posing a major barrier to commercial deployment.

Alternative approaches have explored vapor-phase doping to circumvent the limitations of solution processing. For instance, co-evaporation of spiro-OMeTAD with strong p-type dopants such as F4-TCNQ (2,3,5,6-tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8-tetracyanoquinodimethane) has yielded dense, pinhole-free films with enhanced conductivity. However, excessive p-doping shifts the Fermi level too close to the HOMO, creating a large energy offset (>2 eV) between the perovskite valence band maximum (VBM, typically ~5.4 eV below vacuum) and the HTL HOMO. This mismatch impedes efficient hole extraction, increasing series resistance and reducing open-circuit voltage (Voc) and fill factor (FF). Moreover, even vacuum-deposited p-doped layers alone do not fully address interfacial recombination or provide adequate environmental sealing.

Recent studies have hinted at the potential of n-type doping in organic semiconductors for energy level engineering, but its application in perovskite HTLs remains largely unexplored. Dimethylcyclopropenium (DMC) salts have been identified as strong molecular n-dopants capable of donating electrons to organic hosts, thereby shifting the Fermi level toward the conduction band and effectively raising the HOMO level relative to the Fermi energy. However, prior art lacks a systematic methodology for integrating n-doping into perovskite device architectures in a controlled, reproducible manner that simultaneously optimizes energy alignment and encapsulation functionality.

Furthermore, existing background art fails to recognize the synergistic benefits of a vertically graded doping profile—specifically, a tri-layer structure comprising n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped sublayers—within a single HTL stack. Such a design could theoretically create a built-in electric field that facilitates directional hole transport while acting as a diffusion barrier against both external contaminants and internal ion migration. Yet, no prior patent or publication discloses a practical implementation of this concept using vacuum evaporation with precise dopant vapor pressure control, nor does it demonstrate the resulting improvements in both efficiency and long-term stability.

In summary, the background art is characterized by a fundamental trade-off: solution-processed doped HTLs offer reasonable initial efficiency but poor stability, whereas vacuum-deposited p-doped HTLs improve morphology but introduce energy level mismatches. There exists a critical unmet need for a hole transport architecture that harmonizes optimal energy alignment, defect-free morphology, and environmental resilience—requirements that the present invention uniquely fulfills through a novel n-i-p doped spiro-OMeTAD stack fabricated under controlled vacuum conditions with calibrated DMC vapor pressure.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a perovskite solar cell comprising a hole transport layer with a vertically graded doping profile, specifically configured as a sequential stack of an n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD sublayer, an intrinsic (undoped) spiro-OMeTAD sublayer, and a p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD sublayer, all deposited via high-vacuum thermal evaporation. The n-type doping is achieved using dimethylcyclopropenium (DMC) as a molecular dopant, wherein the vapor pressure of DMC during co-evaporation with spiro-OMeTAD is precisely controlled within the range of 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ Torr to 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr to tune the position of the HOMO level of the n-doped sublayer relative to the Fermi level. An optimal DMC vapor pressure of 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr is identified, which aligns the HOMO level of the n-doped spiro-OMeTAD with the valence band maximum of the underlying perovskite absorber, thereby minimizing the hole injection barrier and maximizing extraction efficiency. The intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD sublayer, interposed between the n-type and p-type regions, serves a dual function: it prevents inter-diffusion of opposing dopants that could neutralize each other and degrade performance, and it contributes to the formation of a smooth, continuous, pinhole-free film that acts as an effective barrier against environmental ingress. The p-type doping is implemented using F4-TCNQ at a concentration of 2 weight percent, co-evaporated with spiro-OMeTAD to achieve a HOMO level positioned 0.3 eV below the Fermi level, consistent with strong p-type behavior.

The invention further encompasses a method for fabricating such a solar cell, which includes the steps of: (a) preparing a substrate comprising a transparent conductive oxide layer; (b) depositing a compact electron transport layer of TiO₂ thereon; (c) forming a perovskite absorber layer by solution processing followed by thermal annealing in an inert atmosphere; (d) transferring the substrate into a high-vacuum chamber with a base pressure of 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ Torr or lower; (e) evaporating spiro-OMeTAD and DMC simultaneously under a controlled DMC vapor pressure of 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr to form the n-type doped sublayer with a thickness of approximately 30 nm; (f) continuing spiro-OMeTAD evaporation without DMC to deposit the intrinsic sublayer of approximately 20 nm thickness; (g) transferring the substrate to a second vacuum chamber and co-evaporating spiro-OMeTAD and F4-TCNQ to form the p-type doped sublayer of approximately 30 nm thickness; and (h) depositing a metal back contact, such as gold, to complete the device. Critically, the entire HTL deposition sequence is performed without breaking vacuum between the n-type and intrinsic layers, ensuring interfacial cleanliness and preventing contamination.

A key aspect of the invention is the use of in situ ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) during or immediately after each HTL sublayer deposition to measure the work function and HOMO position relative to the Fermi level, enabling real-time feedback for process optimization and quality control. This analytical capability allows for precise matching of energy levels across the perovskite/HTL interface, which is directly correlated with enhanced device performance metrics including open-circuit voltage, fill factor, and photocurrent density.

The resulting solar cell exhibits exceptional environmental stability, maintaining or even improving its power conversion efficiency after prolonged exposure to ambient air (relative humidity ~50%) for up to 890 hours, in stark contrast to reference devices with conventional solution-processed HTLs that degrade to less than 60% of their initial efficiency under identical conditions. The stability enhancement is attributed to the pinhole-free morphology of the vacuum-evaporated HTL, confirmed by atomic force microscopy and scanning electron microscopy, which effectively seals the perovskite layer from oxygen and moisture penetration. Additionally, the amorphous nature and high glass transition temperature (Tg = 121 °C) of spiro-OMeTAD contribute to the mechanical and thermal robustness of the encapsulating layer.

Moreover, the invention demonstrates a non-monotonic evolution of device performance during aging, wherein the efficiency of the n-i-p structured HTL devices increases during the first 300 hours of ambient storage due to beneficial doping effects in the intrinsic layer induced by ambient exposure or dopant redistribution, leading to reduced series resistance and improved Voc and FF. This self-optimizing behavior, combined with stable photocurrent over extended periods, underscores the dynamic adaptability and reliability of the disclosed architecture.

In essence, the invention resolves the longstanding dichotomy between efficiency and stability in perovskite photovoltaics by introducing a rationally designed, multi-functional HTL that integrates energy-level engineering, morphological perfection, and environmental protection into a single, manufacturable process. The disclosed technology is scalable, compatible with industrial vacuum deposition tools, and applicable to a broad range of perovskite compositions and device architectures beyond the specific embodiment described herein.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

The present invention will now be described in detail with reference to specific embodiments, which are provided for illustrative purposes and should not be construed as limiting the scope of the claims. It will be apparent to those skilled in the art that various modifications and variations can be made without departing from the spirit and scope of the invention.

In one embodiment, a perovskite solar cell is fabricated on a patterned fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrate having a sheet resistance of approximately 7 Ω per square. The substrate is first cleaned using a mixture of hydrochloric acid and zinc powder to remove organic and metallic contaminants, followed by thorough rinsing with deionized water and drying under a nitrogen stream. A compact electron transport layer (ETL) of titanium dioxide (TiO₂) is then deposited onto the FTO surface by spray pyrolysis. The precursor solution for the TiO₂ layer consists of titanium(IV) isopropoxide, acetylacetone, and anhydrous ethanol mixed in a volumetric ratio of 3:3:2. The spray pyrolysis is carried out at a substrate temperature of approximately 450 °C, resulting in a uniform TiO₂ film with a thickness of about 100 nanometers. Subsequently, the TiO₂-coated substrate undergoes a post-annealing treatment on a hotplate at 480 °C for 30 minutes to enhance crystallinity and remove residual organics, yielding a dense, pinhole-free compact layer that serves as an effective electron collector and hole blocker.

Following ETL formation, the perovskite absorber layer is prepared by solution processing. A precursor solution is formulated by dissolving methylammonium iodide (MAI) and lead chloride (PbCl₂) in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) at a molar ratio of 2.5:1, corresponding to concentrations of 2.2 M MAI and 0.88 M PbCl₂. This solution is spin-coated onto the TiO₂/FTO substrate at a rotational speed of 2000 revolutions per minute (rpm) for 45 seconds inside a nitrogen-filled glovebox with oxygen and moisture levels maintained below 0.1 parts per million (ppm). Immediately after spin-coating, the wet film is transferred to a hotplate and thermally annealed at 100 °C for 45 minutes to induce crystallization into the desired perovskite phase, specifically methylammonium lead iodide chloride (MAPbI₃₋ₓClₓ). X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis of the resulting thin film confirms the presence of characteristic perovskite peaks at 2θ angles of 14.2° and 28.5°, corresponding to the (110) and (220) crystallographic planes, respectively, indicative of a well-ordered tetragonal perovskite structure.

For comparative purposes, a reference solar cell is fabricated using a conventional solution-processed hole transport layer. In this reference device, a solution containing 59 mM spiro-OMeTAD, 172 mM 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP), and 32 mM lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) in chlorobenzene is spin-coated onto the perovskite layer at 2000 rpm for 60 seconds, followed by storage in ambient air for several hours to allow oxidative doping. However, the primary focus of the invention lies in the novel hole transport layer architecture described below.

The inventive hole transport layer is constructed as a tri-layer stack comprising, in sequence from the perovskite outward: (1) an n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD sublayer, (2) an intrinsic (undoped) spiro-OMeTAD sublayer, and (3) a p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD sublayer. All three sublayers are deposited by thermal evaporation under high-vacuum conditions to ensure purity, uniformity, and absence of pinholes. The deposition is performed in a multi-chamber vacuum system with a base pressure of 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ Torr or lower to minimize contamination from residual gases.

The n-type doping is accomplished using dimethylcyclopropenium (DMC) as the molecular dopant. Prior to evaporation, 5 milligrams of DMC powder are loaded into a glass ampule inside a nitrogen glovebox to prevent oxidation, and the ampule is sealed with an all-metal leak valve. The ampule is then mounted onto a heating stage within the vacuum chamber. During deposition, the ampule is heated to approximately 100 °C using a resistive heating tape, causing the DMC to sublime and generate a controlled vapor pressure in the chamber. The vapor pressure of DMC is regulated by adjusting the temperature of the ampule and the aperture of the leak valve, and is monitored in real time using a calibrated pressure gauge. A series of preliminary experiments are conducted by varying the DMC vapor pressure from 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ Torr to 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr while co-evaporating spiro-OMeTAD at a constant rate of 0.5 Ångströms per second (Å/s), as measured by a quartz crystal microbalance (QCM). After each deposition, in situ ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) is performed using a He I discharge lamp (photon energy = 21.2 eV) and an EA125 hemispherical energy analyzer to determine the work function and the position of the HOMO edge relative to the Fermi level (E_F).

The UPS measurements reveal a systematic shift in the electronic structure of spiro-OMeTAD with increasing DMC vapor pressure. At 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ Torr, the work function is 3.5 eV and the HOMO edge lies 1.5 eV below E_F. As the pressure increases to 5.0 × 10⁻⁶ Torr, 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr, and 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr, the work function decreases to 3.4 eV, 3.0 eV, and 1.8 eV, respectively, while the HOMO edge moves progressively closer to E_F, reaching 1.9 eV, 2.5 eV, and 4.6 eV below E_F. This trend confirms the n-type doping behavior of DMC, as electron donation from DMC raises the Fermi level toward the conduction band, effectively increasing the energy of the HOMO relative to E_F. Crucially, when the DMC vapor pressure is set to 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr, the HOMO level of the n-doped spiro-OMeTAD aligns with the valence band maximum (VBM) of the perovskite layer, which is independently measured by UPS to be 2.6 eV below E_F. This energy matching minimizes the hole injection barrier at the perovskite/HTL interface, facilitating efficient extraction of photogenerated holes.

Under the optimized DMC vapor pressure of 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr, the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD sublayer is deposited to a thickness of 30 nanometers. Immediately thereafter, without venting the chamber or exposing the sample to air, the DMC source is isolated and pure spiro-OMeTAD is evaporated at the same rate of 0.5 Å/s to form the intrinsic sublayer with a thickness of 20 nanometers. This undoped layer serves as a diffusion barrier that prevents the n-type dopant (DMC) and the subsequently deposited p-type dopant (F4-TCNQ) from intermixing and compensating each other, which would otherwise degrade the built-in electric field and increase recombination. The intrinsic layer also ensures a smooth, continuous interface between the doped regions, contributing to the overall pinhole-free morphology of the HTL stack.

Following the deposition of the intrinsic layer, the substrate is transferred under vacuum to a second evaporation chamber dedicated to p-type doping. In this chamber, spiro-OMeTAD and F4-TCNQ are co-evaporated from separate sources. The spiro-OMeTAD is deposited at 0.5 Å/s, while F4-TCNQ is deposited at 0.1 Å/s, resulting in a nominal doping concentration of 2 weight percent, which has been shown in prior studies to yield optimal p-type conductivity without excessive aggregation. UPS analysis of this p-doped layer confirms a HOMO position of 0.3 eV below E_F, consistent with strong p-type behavior where the Fermi level is pinned near the HOMO due to high doping efficiency. The p-type sublayer is grown to a final thickness of 30 nanometers, completing the n-i-p HTL stack with a total thickness of 80 nanometers.

Finally, a 60-nanometer-thick gold back contact is deposited by thermal evaporation through a shadow mask to define the active area of the solar cell, typically 0.1 cm². The completed device thus has the full architecture: FTO / compact TiO₂ (100 nm) / perovskite (~500 nm) / n-doped spiro-OMeTAD (30 nm) / intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD (20 nm) / p-doped spiro-OMeTAD (30 nm) / Au (60 nm).

The performance of the inventive solar cells is evaluated under standard AM1.5G illumination (100 mW/cm²) using a solar simulator and a source meter for current-voltage characterization. Statistical analysis is performed on at least six independently fabricated devices to account for process variations. The results demonstrate that the n-i-p structured HTL devices exhibit comparable or superior initial efficiency to the reference devices, with average power conversion efficiencies exceeding 18%. More significantly, the stability of these devices is dramatically enhanced. When stored in ambient air with ~50% relative humidity, the reference devices degrade to approximately 50% of their initial PCE within 300 hours, primarily due to a collapse in fill factor and photocurrent caused by perovskite decomposition through pinholes in the solution-processed HTL. In contrast, the inventive devices show a remarkable improvement in PCE during the first 300 hours of aging, with the efficiency increasing by up to 10% relative to the initial value. This enhancement is attributed to the gradual doping of the intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer by either residual F4-TCNQ or DMC dopants that diffuse over time, or by interaction with ambient oxygen and moisture, which reduces the series resistance and improves both Voc and FF. After 890 hours of continuous air exposure, the inventive devices maintain PCE values that are higher than their initial measurements, while the photocurrent density (Jsc) remains virtually unchanged, confirming the integrity of the perovskite layer.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the vacuum-evaporated HTL confirm the absence of pinholes and the smooth, amorphous morphology of the spiro-OMeTAD films. The high glass transition temperature (Tg = 121 °C) of spiro-OMeTAD further ensures that the HTL remains dimensionally stable under operational thermal stresses, acting as an effective gas barrier that protects the perovskite from environmental degradation. Control experiments with devices fabricated using only undoped spiro-OMeTAD also show performance improvements after brief ambient exposure, supporting the hypothesis that mild environmental interaction can beneficially dope the HTL without harming the perovskite—provided that the HTL is pinhole-free.

In summary, the disclosed embodiment exemplifies a holistic approach to perovskite solar cell design, where materials selection, deposition methodology, doping strategy, and interfacial engineering are synergistically integrated to overcome the historical limitations of the technology. The invention is not restricted to the specific materials or dimensions described; for instance, alternative n-type dopants, perovskite compositions (e.g., formamidinium-based or mixed-cation variants), electron transport layers (e.g., SnO₂), or substrate types (e.g., flexible plastics) may be employed without departing from the core principles of the invention. The key innovation resides in the deliberate creation of a vertically graded doping profile within a vacuum-deposited, pinhole-free HTL, calibrated through in situ energy level monitoring to achieve simultaneous optimization of charge extraction and environmental stability.
