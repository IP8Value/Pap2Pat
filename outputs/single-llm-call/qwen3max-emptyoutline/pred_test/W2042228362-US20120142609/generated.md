# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmology and molecular biology, specifically to non-human animal models that recapitulate key pathophysiological features of human retinal diseases characterized by dysfunction of Müller glial cells. More particularly, the invention provides a genetically modified non-human model, preferably murine, in which the expression of dystrophin isoform Dp71 is ablated or significantly reduced, thereby leading to mislocalization or downregulation of potassium channel Kir4.1 and aquaporin-4 (AQP4) in Müller cells, impaired potassium and water homeostasis, increased susceptibility to osmotic stress-induced Müller cell swelling, and compromised integrity of the blood-retinal barrier (BRB). The invention further encompasses diagnostic methods for detecting early-stage retinal pathologies associated with Müller cell dysfunction, therapeutic strategies targeting the Dp71-dependent signaling axis to restore BRB integrity and fluid homeostasis, and pharmaceutical compositions designed to modulate inflammatory mediators or adenosine signaling pathways that contribute to pathological Müller cell swelling. The disclosed non-human model serves as a robust preclinical platform for evaluating novel therapeutics aimed at treating retinal edema, diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, and other conditions involving glial-mediated disruption of retinal homeostasis.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The vertebrate retina is a highly organized neural tissue responsible for converting light into electrical signals that are transmitted to the brain via the optic nerve. Its structural and functional integrity depends critically on the coordinated activity of neurons and glial cells, particularly Müller cells—the principal macroglia of the retina. Müller cells span the entire thickness of the neural retina, forming intimate contacts with photoreceptors, bipolar cells, amacrine cells, ganglion cells, and retinal blood vessels. These cells perform a multitude of essential functions, including neurotransmitter recycling (notably glutamate and GABA), maintenance of extracellular ionic balance, regulation of pH, provision of metabolic support to neurons, and participation in the formation and maintenance of the blood-retinal barrier (BRB). Central to these roles is the polarized distribution of specific ion channels and transporters within distinct subcellular domains of Müller cells, especially at their endfeet adjacent to the inner limiting membrane and perivascular processes ensheathing retinal capillaries.

Among the most critical proteins localized in these specialized membrane domains are the inwardly rectifying potassium channel Kir4.1 and the water channel aquaporin-4 (AQP4). Kir4.1 mediates spatial buffering of potassium ions released during neuronal activity, facilitating their redistribution from sites of high concentration (e.g., synaptic layers) to areas of lower concentration such as the vitreous humor and intraretinal blood vessels. AQP4, in turn, enables rapid transmembrane water movement, allowing Müller cells to regulate cellular volume and participate in the clearance of excess fluid from the retinal parenchyma—a process vital for preventing edema. Both Kir4.1 and AQP4 exhibit a highly polarized expression pattern in healthy retina, being enriched predominantly in Müller cell endfeet and perivascular processes. This precise subcellular localization is not merely coincidental but is functionally indispensable; disruption of this polarization leads to impaired potassium siphoning, defective water drainage, and ultimately, neuronal dysfunction and degeneration.

It has been established that the correct targeting and stabilization of Kir4.1 and AQP4 in Müller cell membranes depend on a macromolecular protein complex anchored by dystrophin isoform Dp71, a product of the Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) gene. Dp71, unlike the full-length dystrophin expressed in muscle, lacks the actin-binding N-terminal domain but retains the cysteine-rich and C-terminal domains necessary for interaction with dystrophin-associated proteins (DAPs), including β-dystroglycan and syntrophins. In the normal retina, Dp71 colocalizes precisely with Kir4.1 and AQP4 at Müller cell endfeet and around blood vessels, forming a scaffold that restricts lateral diffusion of these channels and ensures their retention in functionally relevant microdomains. Genetic ablation of Dp71 in mice results in profound mislocalization of both Kir4.1 and AQP4—Kir4.1 becomes diffusely distributed along Müller cell fibers rather than confined to endfeet, while AQP4 expression is significantly downregulated. These molecular alterations translate into measurable physiological deficits: reduced potassium conductance, depolarization of Müller cell membrane potential, impaired glutamate uptake, and increased vulnerability to ischemic injury.

Retinal pathologies such as diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, and retinal detachment are frequently accompanied by reactive gliosis—a response wherein Müller cells undergo morphological and biochemical changes, including upregulation of glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP). Importantly, even before overt vascular abnormalities manifest in diseases like diabetic retinopathy, early alterations in Müller cell function have been observed, notably the mislocalization of Kir4.1 and downregulation of AQP4. These changes precede clinical signs of edema or neovascularization, suggesting that glial dysfunction may be a primary driver rather than a secondary consequence of vascular pathology. However, the upstream molecular triggers initiating this cascade of events remain poorly understood. Previous studies using experimental models of retinal detachment in rabbits and pigs have demonstrated loss of Kir4.1 polarization and reduced potassium currents in Müller cells, but the role of Dp71 in mediating these responses had not been systematically investigated until recently.

Moreover, the integrity of the BRB—comprising tight junctions between retinal endothelial cells and supported by pericytes and Müller cell endfeet—is crucial for maintaining the immune-privileged status of the retina and preventing leakage of plasma proteins and fluid into the neural tissue. Compromise of the BRB underlies the development of macular edema, a leading cause of vision loss in diabetic patients. While vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is widely recognized as a key mediator of BRB breakdown, emerging evidence implicates Müller cell dysfunction as an equally important contributor. Given that Dp71 is prominently expressed in Müller cell processes surrounding retinal vessels, its potential role in regulating BRB permeability represents a significant gap in current understanding.

Despite advances in identifying molecular components involved in retinal homeostasis, there remains a critical need for predictive animal models that faithfully reproduce the early glial-centric pathophysiology of human retinal diseases. Existing models often focus on late-stage vascular complications or rely on acute insults that do not reflect the chronic, progressive nature of conditions like diabetic retinopathy. Furthermore, therapeutic development has been hampered by the lack of models that isolate glial dysfunction as the primary defect, independent of systemic metabolic disturbances. The identification of Dp71 as a master regulator of Müller cell channel localization and BRB integrity thus presents a unique opportunity to address these limitations. However, prior to the present invention, no comprehensive characterization of Dp71-null mice in the context of retinal injury models had been performed, nor had the utility of such animals as platforms for drug discovery been explored.

Additionally, while pharmacological agents targeting VEGF have shown efficacy in reducing edema, they do not address the underlying glial dysfunction that may persist despite anti-angiogenic therapy. There is therefore an unmet medical need for therapies that restore Müller cell-mediated fluid clearance and BRB stability through mechanisms independent of VEGF inhibition. Potential targets include inflammatory pathways known to exacerbate Müller cell swelling, such as those involving phospholipase A2 and cyclooxygenase, as well as purinergic signaling via adenosine receptors. However, without a validated model system demonstrating the causal link between Dp71 deficiency, inflammatory mediator production, and pathological swelling, rational drug design in this area has been hindered.

In summary, the background art reveals a fragmented understanding of how glial cytoskeletal proteins govern retinal homeostasis, a paucity of models capturing early glial dysfunction preceding vascular pathology, and a therapeutic void in addressing non-VEGF-mediated mechanisms of edema formation. The present invention directly addresses these gaps by providing a genetically defined non-human model that isolates Dp71-dependent glial dysfunction as a core pathogenic mechanism, thereby enabling mechanistic studies, diagnostic innovation, and targeted therapeutic development for a spectrum of retinal disorders rooted in Müller cell failure.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a genetically engineered non-human animal model, preferably a mouse, in which the expression of dystrophin isoform Dp71 is selectively ablated or substantially diminished in Müller glial cells, resulting in a constellation of phenotypic features that mirror key aspects of human retinal diseases characterized by glial dysfunction and blood-retinal barrier (BRB) compromise. Specifically, the invention discloses that Dp71-null mice exhibit mislocalization of the inwardly rectifying potassium channel Kir4.1 and downregulation of the water channel aquaporin-4 (AQP4) in Müller cells, leading to impaired potassium buffering, defective osmotic regulation, and increased susceptibility to pathological Müller cell swelling. Moreover, these animals display a four-fold elevation in BRB permeability under baseline conditions, establishing a direct causal relationship between Dp71 deficiency in Müller cells and vascular leakage.

The invention further demonstrates that experimental retinal detachment in wild-type mice induces a rapid and significant reduction in both Dp71 mRNA and protein levels within 24 hours, concomitant with Kir4.1 mislocalization, AQP4 downregulation, and reactive gliosis marked by GFAP upregulation. Crucially, retinal detachment fails to induce additional alterations in Kir4.1 or AQP4 localization in Dp71-null mice, indicating that Dp71 depletion is epistatic to—and likely upstream of—the channel redistribution observed in injury models. This positions Dp71 downregulation as a pivotal early event in the signaling cascade of reactive gliosis, preceding and potentially driving subsequent functional impairments.

Based on these findings, the invention provides several embodiments. First, it offers a non-human model (e.g., Dp71-null mouse) for studying the pathogenesis of retinal edema, diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, and other conditions involving Müller cell dysfunction. Second, it enables diagnostic methods for detecting early retinal pathology by assessing Dp71 expression levels or the subcellular distribution of Kir4.1 and AQP4 in ocular tissues or biofluids. Third, it supports therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring Dp71 function or compensating for its loss through modulation of downstream effectors, including administration of adenosine receptor agonists or inhibitors of inflammatory enzymes such as phospholipase A2 and cyclooxygenase, which were shown to prevent pathological Müller cell swelling in Dp71-deficient retinas. Fourth, it encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising such active agents, formulated for intraocular or systemic delivery, for the treatment or prevention of retinal edema and BRB breakdown.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method of screening candidate compounds for their ability to ameliorate Müller cell dysfunction by administering the compound to the disclosed non-human model and evaluating improvements in Kir4.1/AQP4 localization, reduction in Müller cell swelling under osmotic challenge, or restoration of BRB integrity. In another aspect, the invention includes a diagnostic assay comprising detecting reduced Dp71 expression or aberrant Kir4.1/AQP4 distribution in a retinal sample as an indicator of incipient glial pathology, even in the absence of overt vascular changes. The invention thus establishes Dp71 as a central node in retinal homeostasis and offers a multifaceted platform for advancing both basic science and clinical translation in ophthalmology.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Non Human Model According to the Invention

The present invention provides a non-human animal model, preferably a mammalian model and more specifically a murine model, in which the expression of dystrophin isoform Dp71 is genetically ablated or significantly reduced, thereby producing a phenotype that recapitulates critical features of human retinal diseases associated with Müller glial cell dysfunction. The model is generated through targeted disruption of the Dp71-specific exon within the Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) gene locus, ensuring that other dystrophin isoforms (e.g., Dp427 in neurons) remain unaffected. In a preferred embodiment, the Dp71-null mouse is created by homologous recombination-mediated replacement of the first and unique exon of Dp71, along with a portion of its first intron, with a β-galactosidase-neomycin resistance (β-geo) cassette, as previously described in the art. This genetic modification abolishes Dp71 protein expression without interfering with the transcription or translation of other DMD gene products, thereby isolating the functional consequences of Dp71 loss specifically in tissues where it is normally expressed, including the retina.

In the retina of the Dp71-null mouse, Müller glial cells—which are the primary site of Dp71 expression—exhibit a characteristic suite of molecular, cellular, and physiological abnormalities that constitute the core phenotype of the model. Immunohistochemical analysis reveals that in wild-type retinas, Dp71 is strongly localized to Müller cell endfeet at the inner limiting membrane and to perivascular processes ensheathing retinal blood vessels, colocalizing precisely with Kir4.1 potassium channels and AQP4 water channels. In contrast, Dp71-null retinas show complete absence of Dp71 immunoreactivity, accompanied by a dramatic redistribution of Kir4.1 from its normal polarized domains to a diffuse pattern along the entire length of Müller cell fibers. Concurrently, AQP4 expression is significantly downregulated, with reduced immunolabeling intensity at endfeet, perivascular regions, and the outer plexiform layer. These alterations are not due to changes in total Kir4.1 protein levels, as confirmed by Western blotting, but rather reflect a failure in proper membrane targeting and retention, underscoring the scaffolding role of Dp71 in organizing the dystrophin-associated protein complex (DAPC) that anchors these channels in specific membrane microdomains.

Electrophysiological recordings from acutely isolated Müller cells of Dp71-null mice demonstrate that while the total number of functional Kir4.1 channels may remain unchanged, their mislocalization results in a marked reduction in potassium current density—defined as current amplitude normalized to membrane capacitance—due to an increase in membrane surface area associated with cellular hypertrophy. This reduction in current density impairs the capacity of Müller cells to mediate spatial potassium buffering, a process essential for preventing extracellular potassium accumulation and consequent neuronal hyperexcitability or depolarization block. Furthermore, the depolarized resting membrane potential observed in Dp71-deficient Müller cells compromises the driving force for electrogenic glutamate and GABA uptake, thereby disrupting neurotransmitter recycling and glia-neuron communication.

A hallmark feature of the Dp71-null model is its heightened susceptibility to osmotic stress. When retinal slices from untreated Dp71-null mice are exposed to hypotonic solution (60% of control osmolarity), Müller cell somata undergo significant swelling, increasing in cross-sectional area by approximately 18% compared to baseline. This contrasts sharply with wild-type Müller cells, which remain volume-stable under identical conditions unless potassium channels are pharmacologically blocked with barium ions. The pathological swelling in Dp71-null mice is mediated by inflammatory pathways, as evidenced by the ability of adenosine (10 µM), phosphollipase A2 inhibitor 4-bromophenacyl bromide (300 µM), and cyclooxygenase inhibitor indomethacin (10 µM) to completely prevent osmotic swelling. These findings indicate that Dp71 deficiency induces a state of chronic low-grade inflammation in Müller cells, characterized by upregulated production of arachidonic acid and prostaglandins, which sensitizes the cells to volume dysregulation.

Critically, the Dp71-null model exhibits a four-fold increase in BRB permeability under baseline, non-injured conditions, as quantified by Evans blue dye extravasation. This finding establishes, for the first time, a direct causal link between Müller cell-specific Dp71 expression and BRB integrity. Given that Müller cell endfeet form an integral component of the BRB by ensheathing retinal capillaries and secreting factors that influence endothelial tight junctions, the loss of Dp71 likely disrupts the structural and/or signaling interface between glia and vasculature. Notably, this BRB defect occurs in the absence of overt retinal degeneration or visual impairment in unchallenged Dp71-null mice, suggesting the presence of compensatory mechanisms that maintain gross retinal structure but fail to preserve fine-tuned homeostatic functions.

The utility of the Dp71-null model is further enhanced by its responsiveness to experimental retinal injury. When subjected to experimental retinal detachment—a well-established model of acute retinal trauma—wild-type mice rapidly downregulate Dp71 expression at both mRNA and protein levels within 24 hours, mirroring the molecular phenotype of the genetically modified model. This downregulation precedes and correlates with Kir4.1 mislocalization, AQP4 downregulation, and reactive gliosis (as indicated by GFAP upregulation). Strikingly, retinal detachment in Dp71-null mice does not produce additional alterations in Kir4.1 or AQP4 distribution, confirming that Dp71 loss is sufficient to induce the full channelopathy phenotype and that injury-induced changes in wild-type animals are mediated through Dp71 suppression. Thus, the Dp71-null mouse serves as both a genetic model of chronic glial dysfunction and a sensitized background for studying the consequences of acute retinal insults.

In summary, the non-human model according to the invention is a Dp71-deficient mouse that exhibits: (i) mislocalization of Kir4.1 and downregulation of AQP4 in Müller cells; (ii) impaired potassium buffering and osmotic regulation; (iii) chronic inflammatory activation contributing to pathological swelling; and (iv) baseline BRB hyperpermeability. These features collectively make the model uniquely suited for investigating the glial origins of retinal edema and for developing therapies that target Müller cell-mediated homeostasis rather than solely vascular endpoints.

### Uses of the Non Human Model According to the Invention

The non-human model according to the invention, particularly the Dp71-null mouse, finds extensive utility in both basic research and translational applications related to retinal physiology and disease. One primary use is as a platform for elucidating the molecular mechanisms by which Müller glial cells maintain retinal homeostasis and how their dysfunction contributes to the pathogenesis of various ocular disorders. By isolating Dp71 deficiency as the sole genetic perturbation, the model allows researchers to dissect the specific contributions of glial channel mislocalization, osmotic dysregulation, and BRB compromise to overall retinal health, independent of confounding systemic factors such as hyperglycemia or hypertension that are present in other disease models.

A key application of the model is in the study of early events in retinal disease progression. Since Dp71-null mice exhibit BRB leakage and Müller cell swelling in the absence of overt neurodegeneration or vascular pathology, they provide a unique window into the initial stages of glial dysfunction that may precede clinical manifestations in conditions like diabetic retinopathy. This enables the identification of biomarkers—such as reduced Dp71 expression or altered Kir4.1 distribution—that could serve as early diagnostic indicators before irreversible damage occurs. Furthermore, the model can be used to test the hypothesis that glial dysfunction is a primary driver rather than a secondary consequence of vascular pathology, thereby shifting therapeutic focus toward glia-targeted interventions.

Another significant use of the Dp71-null model is in preclinical drug screening and validation. The model’s reproducible phenotype—including measurable endpoints such as Müller cell swelling under osmotic challenge, BRB permeability via Evans blue assay, and electrophysiological parameters like potassium current density—provides quantitative readouts for assessing the efficacy of candidate therapeutics. For instance, compounds that restore Kir4.1/AQP4 localization, inhibit inflammatory mediators (e.g., phospholipase A2 or cyclooxygenase inhibitors), or activate protective signaling pathways (e.g., adenosine receptor agonists) can be evaluated for their ability to reverse or prevent the pathological features of the model. This is particularly valuable for developing treatments for retinal edema that operate through non-VEGF mechanisms, addressing an unmet need in patients who respond poorly to current anti-angiogenic therapies.

The model also serves as a tool for investigating the interplay between genetic susceptibility and environmental stressors. By subjecting Dp71-null mice to additional challenges—such as hyperglycemia, hypoxia, or light damage—researchers can model gene-environment interactions that may underlie variable disease penetrance in human populations. Conversely, crossing the Dp71-null line with other transgenic models (e.g., diabetic or age-related macular degeneration models) allows for the dissection of synergistic or antagonistic effects between different pathogenic pathways.

Moreover, the Dp71-null model is instrumental in validating the role of Dp71 as a master regulator of the dystrophin-associated protein complex in Müller cells. Through rescue experiments—such as viral-mediated reintroduction of Dp71 or overexpression of compensatory proteins like utrophin—scientists can confirm the specificity of the observed phenotypes and explore potential gene therapy approaches. Although utrophin is upregulated in the inner limiting membrane of detached wild-type retinas and in Dp71-null mice, the present invention demonstrates that this endogenous compensation is insufficient to restore normal channel localization or BRB function, highlighting the irreplaceable role of Dp71 in this context.

In addition to mechanistic and therapeutic studies, the model has diagnostic relevance. Tissues or biofluids derived from the model can be used to develop and calibrate assays for detecting Dp71-related pathology in human patients. For example, antibodies or imaging probes targeting Dp71, Kir4.1, or AQP4 distribution patterns could be validated in the model before clinical deployment. Similarly, the model can inform the interpretation of optical coherence tomography (OCT) or adaptive optics imaging data by correlating structural changes (e.g., retinal thickening indicative of edema) with underlying glial dysfunction.

Finally, the Dp71-null model contributes to the broader understanding of glial biology beyond the retina. As Müller cells share functional and molecular similarities with astrocytes in the central nervous system, insights gained from this model may have implications for neurological disorders involving astrocytic dystrophinopathies or channelopathies. Thus, the model serves not only as a specialized tool for ophthalmic research but also as a paradigm for studying glia-neuron-vascular interactions in neural tissues generally.

### Diagnostics Methods According to the Invention

The present invention provides diagnostic methods for the early detection and monitoring of retinal pathologies associated with Müller glial cell dysfunction, based on the identification of Dp71 deficiency or its downstream molecular consequences as key indicators of incipient disease. These methods are predicated on the discovery that downregulation or loss of Dp71 expression in Müller cells precedes and drives the mislocalization of Kir4.1 potassium channels and downregulation of AQP4 water channels, leading to impaired potassium and water homeostasis, pathological Müller cell swelling, and increased BRB permeability—features that are detectable before overt vascular or neuronal damage occurs in many retinal diseases.

In one embodiment, the diagnostic method comprises obtaining a biological sample from a subject suspected of having or being at risk for a retinal disorder, such as diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, or uveitis, and analyzing the sample for reduced expression levels of Dp71 mRNA or protein. The biological sample may be a retinal biopsy obtained during vitreoretinal surgery, although less invasive alternatives are preferred. In a more clinically feasible approach, the sample may consist of aqueous humor or vitreous fluid, in which Dp71 fragments or associated proteins may be shed and detectable by sensitive immunoassays. Alternatively, circulating exosomes derived from retinal glial cells, which can be isolated from peripheral blood, may carry Dp71 or its interacting partners and serve as liquid biopsy markers.

Detection of Dp71 expression can be performed using standard molecular techniques. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) with primers specific to the Dp71 transcript (e.g., sense: 5′-ACAACCATGAGGGAACACCT-3′; antisense: 5′-TCTGGAGCCTTCTGAGCTTC-3′) allows for precise measurement of mRNA levels, normalized to housekeeping genes such as β-actin. Protein levels can be assessed by Western blotting or enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) using monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies that specifically recognize Dp71 without cross-reacting with other dystrophin isoforms. Immunohistochemistry on retinal sections, if available, provides spatial information, revealing loss of Dp71 immunoreactivity specifically in Müller cell endfeet and perivascular processes—a pattern that distinguishes true Dp71 deficiency from generalized protein degradation.

In another embodiment, the diagnostic method focuses on the downstream effects of Dp71 loss, namely the aberrant subcellular distribution of Kir4.1 and AQP4. In healthy retina, Kir4.1 is tightly localized to Müller cell endfeet and perivascular domains; its diffuse redistribution along Müller cell fibers is a sensitive indicator of Dp71 dysfunction. Similarly, reduced AQP4 immunolabeling intensity at these sites correlates with impaired water transport. These changes can be detected in retinal samples using confocal microscopy combined with specific antibodies against Kir4.1 and AQP4. Advanced imaging techniques, such as super-resolution microscopy or proximity ligation assays, may further quantify the degree of channel mislocalization or disruption of the Dp71-DAPC-Kir4.1/AQP4 complex.

For non-invasive diagnostics, the invention contemplates the development of imaging probes that bind to Dp71 or its associated complexes and can be visualized using optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) or fluorescence lifetime imaging ophthalmoscopy (FLIO). Such probes, once validated in the Dp71-null model, could enable in vivo assessment of Müller cell health in human patients during routine eye exams.

Additionally, functional readouts may complement molecular diagnostics. For example, electrophysiological testing such as the electroretinogram (ERG) may reveal subtle changes in the slow PIII component, which reflects Müller cell potassium buffering capacity and is known to be altered in Kir4.1 channelopathies. While not specific to Dp71 alone, when combined with molecular markers, ERG abnormalities can strengthen the diagnostic inference.

The diagnostic methods of the invention are particularly valuable for identifying patients in the preclinical or early clinical stages of retinal disease, when intervention is most likely to preserve vision. By detecting glial dysfunction before vascular leakage or neuronal loss becomes evident on standard imaging, clinicians can initiate targeted therapies aimed at restoring Müller cell homeostasis, potentially halting disease progression. Furthermore, these methods can stratify patients based on underlying pathophysiology—distinguishing those with primary glial defects from those with predominantly vascular etiologies—thereby enabling personalized treatment approaches.

### Therapeutic Methods and Pharmaceutical Compositions According to the Invention

The present invention provides therapeutic methods and corresponding pharmaceutical compositions for the treatment or prevention of retinal disorders characterized by Müller glial cell dysfunction, BRB breakdown, and retinal edema, based on the modulation of pathways downstream of Dp71 deficiency. These therapies are designed to compensate for the loss of Dp71 function by targeting the inflammatory and osmotic mechanisms that drive pathological Müller cell swelling and vascular leakage, as elucidated through studies in the Dp71-null mouse model.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method of treating retinal edema comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of an adenosine receptor agonist. Adenosine, a purine nucleoside, was shown in the Dp71-null model to completely prevent osmotic Müller cell swelling when applied at a concentration of 10 µM during hypotonic challenge. This effect is likely mediated through A1 or A2A adenosine receptors expressed on Müller cells, which upon activation suppress inflammatory signaling and stabilize volume regulatory pathways. Suitable adenosine receptor agonists include but are not limited to regadenoson, capadenoson, or selective A1 agonists such as CCPA (2-chloro-N6-cyclopentyladenosine). The pharmaceutical composition may be formulated as an intravitreal injection, sustained-release implant, or topical eye drop, depending on the pharmacokinetic properties of the agent and the desired duration of action.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating retinal edema comprising administering an inhibitor of phospholipase A2 (PLA2). PLA2 catalyzes the release of arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids, a rate-limiting step in the production of pro-inflammatory eicosanoids. In the Dp71-null model, the PLA2 inhibitor 4-bromophenacyl bromide (4-BPB) at 300 µM prevented pathological Müller cell swelling, implicating PLA2-derived mediators in the volume dysregulation phenotype. Pharmaceutical compositions may include small-molecule PLA2 inhibitors such as varespladib, AX059, or newer generation compounds with improved selectivity and safety profiles. These agents may be delivered via intravitreal injection or encapsulated in nanoparticles for prolonged intraocular residence.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method of treating retinal edema comprising administering a cyclooxygenase (COX) inhibitor. COX enzymes convert arachidonic acid into prostaglandins, which are potent mediators of inflammation and vascular permeability. The COX inhibitor indomethacin (10 µM) was shown to block Müller cell swelling in Dp71-null retinas, supporting the role of prostaglandins in this process. While traditional non-selective NSAIDs like indomethacin or diclofenac may be used, selective COX-2 inhibitors such as celecoxib or etoricoxib are preferred to minimize gastrointestinal side effects, especially if systemic administration is required. Formulations may include intravitreal suspensions, biodegradable microparticles, or topical solutions with penetration enhancers.

The invention also contemplates combination therapies that simultaneously target multiple nodes in the inflammatory-osmotic cascade. For example, co-administration of an adenosine agonist and a COX inhibitor may produce synergistic effects by both suppressing mediator production and enhancing endogenous anti-inflammatory signaling. Such combinations can be incorporated into a single pharmaceutical composition or administered as separate formulations.

Pharmaceutical compositions according to the invention comprise a therapeutically effective amount of the active agent(s) together with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier suitable for ocular delivery. Carriers may include isotonic saline, balanced salt solutions, hyaluronic acid-based gels, or biodegradable polymers such as polylactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) for sustained release. Excipients may be added to enhance stability, solubility, or bioavailability. The compositions are sterile and pyrogen-free, conforming to regulatory standards for intraocular use.

Therapeutic methods of the invention are indicated for a range of conditions where Müller cell dysfunction contributes to pathology, including but not limited to diabetic macular edema, retinal vein occlusion, uveitic macular edema, and post-surgical cystoid macular edema. Importantly, these therapies offer a mechanism distinct from VEGF inhibition, providing an alternative for patients who are refractory to anti-VEGF agents or who experience tachyphylaxis with prolonged use.

In addition to small molecules, the invention encompasses gene therapy approaches aimed at restoring Dp71 expression or enhancing compensatory pathways. For instance, adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors engineered to express Dp71 under the control of a Müller cell-specific promoter (e.g., Glutamine Synthetase or RLBP1 promoter) could be delivered intravitreally to rescue channel localization and BRB integrity. Alternatively, overexpression of utrophin—a dystrophin homolog that shows partial compensatory upregulation in Dp71-null retinas—may be attempted, although the present data suggest its natural induction is insufficient, necessitating supraphysiological expression levels.

In summary, the therapeutic methods and pharmaceutical compositions of the invention represent a paradigm shift from purely anti-angiogenic strategies to glia-centric interventions that address the root causes of retinal edema and BRB failure, offering new hope for patients with currently untreatable forms of vision loss.

## EXAMPLE

The following example illustrates the construction and characterization of the Dp71-null mouse model and its application in studying retinal pathophysiology, as described in the research paper forming the basis of the present invention.

Dp71-null mice were generated by homologous recombination in embryonic stem cells, wherein the first and unique exon of the Dp71 isoform, along with a portion of the first intron, was replaced by a β-galactosidase-neomycin resistance (β-geo) cassette. This genetic modification specifically ablates Dp71 expression without affecting other dystrophin isoforms encoded by the DMD gene. Age-matched C57BL/6J mice served as wild-type controls. All animal procedures were approved by institutional ethics committees and conducted in accordance with national guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals.

To assess the baseline retinal phenotype, eyes from adult Dp71-null and wild-type mice were enucleated, fixed, and processed for immunohistochemistry. Retinal cryosections were labeled with antibodies against Dp71, Kir4.1, AQP4, β-dystroglycan, utrophin, and GFAP. In wild-type retinas, Dp71 immunoreactivity was strongly localized to Müller cell endfeet at the inner limiting membrane and to perivascular processes, colocalizing with Kir4.1 and AQP4. In Dp71-null retinas, Dp71 signal was absent, Kir4.1 was diffusely distributed along Müller cell fibers instead of being confined to endfeet, and AQP4 immunolabeling intensity was markedly reduced. GFAP expression was restricted to astrocytes in both genotypes, indicating no spontaneous reactive gliosis in untreated Dp71-null mice.

Western blot analysis of retinal lysates confirmed the absence of Dp71 protein in knockout mice and revealed a 30% reduction in AQP4 levels, while Kir4.1 protein abundance remained unchanged. Real-time PCR showed no difference in Dp71 mRNA in wild-type versus knockout, as expected.

To evaluate functional consequences, Müller cells were acutely isolated from retinas and subjected to whole-cell patch-clamp recording. Inward potassium currents evoked by hyperpolarizing steps were similar in amplitude between genotypes, but membrane capacitance—a proxy for cell surface area—was significantly increased in Dp71-null Müller cells, indicating cellular hypertrophy. Consequently, current density (current amplitude divided by capacitance) was reduced by approximately 25%, reflecting fewer functional channels per unit membrane area.

Osmotic swelling assays were performed on retinal slices loaded with MitoTracker Orange to label Müller cell somata. Perfusion with hypotonic solution (60% osmolarity) induced significant swelling in Dp71-null Müller cells (118% of baseline) but not in wild-type cells (102%). Co-application of 1 mM Ba2+ (a Kir4.1 blocker) induced swelling in wild-type cells (111.5%) but did not further exacerbate swelling in Dp71-null cells, suggesting maximal channel dysfunction already present. Adenosine (10 µM), 4-BPB (300 µM), or indomethacin (10 µM) each prevented swelling in Dp71-null cells, implicating inflammatory mediators in the phenotype.

BRB integrity was assessed by Evans blue dye extravasation. Dp71-null mice exhibited a four-fold increase in retinal vascular permeability compared to wild-type controls (p<0.05), confirming a critical role for Müller cell Dp71 in BRB maintenance.

In a separate experiment, experimental retinal detachment was induced in wild-type mice by subretinal injection of sodium hyaluronate. Twenty-four hours post-surgery, detached retinas showed a 50% reduction in Dp71 protein and mRNA, Kir4.1 mislocalization, 30% AQP4 downregulation, and three-fold GFAP upregulation—hallmarks of reactive gliosis. When the same procedure was performed in Dp71-null mice, no additional changes in Kir4.1 or AQP4 were observed, demonstrating that Dp71 loss is epistatic to injury-induced channel alterations.

These results collectively validate the Dp71-null mouse as a model of chronic Müller cell dysfunction and provide a foundation for the diagnostic and therapeutic applications disclosed herein.
