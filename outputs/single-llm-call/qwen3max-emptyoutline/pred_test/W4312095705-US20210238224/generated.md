# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of targeted protein degradation therapeutics, and more specifically to a novel class of heterobifunctional molecules known as Proteolysis Targeting Chimeras (PROTACs) that are designed to simultaneously degrade Smad3 and stabilize hypoxia-inducible factor 2 alpha (HIF-2α) in renal tissues. The invention provides a rationally engineered PROTAC molecule comprising a Smad3-binding small molecule compound (SMC) linked via a succinic acid-based linker to a von Hippel–Lindau (VHL)-binding SMC, thereby enabling dual therapeutic effects through the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway. This bifunctional molecule exploits the natural substrate specificity of the VHL E3 ubiquitin ligase complex to achieve selective degradation of Smad3—a central mediator of renal fibrosis—while concurrently stabilizing HIF-2α by competitively occupying VHL and preventing its normal role in HIF-2α ubiquitination and degradation. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing this PROTAC, methods for its synthesis, and its use in the treatment of chronic kidney disease (CKD), particularly conditions characterized by renal fibrosis, impaired renal function, and renal anemia. The technical domain spans medicinal chemistry, molecular pharmacology, nephrology, and drug delivery, with specific relevance to the development of event-driven, catalytic therapeutics that operate through protein homeostasis modulation rather than classical occupancy-based inhibition.

## BACKGROUND ART

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) represents a global health burden affecting millions worldwide, often progressing to end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis or transplantation. A hallmark pathological feature of CKD is renal fibrosis, characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM), tubular atrophy, interstitial inflammation, and loss of functional nephrons. Among the signaling pathways implicated in fibrogenesis, the transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1)/Smad3 axis has emerged as a central driver. Smad3, a transcription factor phosphorylated upon TGF-β1 receptor activation, translocates to the nucleus and promotes the expression of profibrotic genes including collagen I, α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), and other mediators of epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and ECM accumulation. Genetic ablation or pharmacological inhibition of Smad3 has consistently demonstrated antifibrotic effects in preclinical models, validating it as a high-value therapeutic target. However, conventional small-molecule inhibitors of Smad3 typically function by blocking its phosphorylation or DNA-binding activity, necessitating sustained high systemic concentrations to maintain efficacy. Such approaches are prone to off-target effects and may disrupt the pleiotropic physiological roles of Smad3 in immune regulation, cell cycle control, and tissue homeostasis, raising concerns about long-term safety.

In parallel, the hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) pathway plays a critical adaptive role in renal physiology and pathology. HIF is a heterodimeric transcription factor composed of an oxygen-labile α-subunit (HIF-1α or HIF-2α) and a constitutively expressed β-subunit. Under normoxic conditions, HIF-α subunits are hydroxylated by prolyl hydroxylase domain enzymes (PHDs), leading to their recognition and ubiquitination by the VHL E3 ubiquitin ligase complex and subsequent proteasomal degradation. In hypoxia or under PHD inhibition, HIF-α accumulates and activates genes involved in erythropoiesis (e.g., erythropoietin, EPO), angiogenesis (e.g., vascular endothelial growth factor, VEGF), and iron metabolism (e.g., duodenal cytochrome b, Dcytb; divalent metal transporter 1, DMT1; ferroportin 1, FPN1). Notably, HIF-1α and HIF-2α exhibit distinct cellular distributions and functions in the kidney: HIF-1α is predominantly expressed in tubular epithelial cells and has been associated with pro-fibrotic responses, whereas HIF-2α is mainly localized in renal interstitial fibroblasts and endothelial cells and exerts renoprotective effects, including suppression of fibrosis, promotion of peritubular capillary density, and stimulation of erythropoiesis. Stabilization of HIF-2α, therefore, represents a promising strategy for treating CKD complications such as renal anemia and microvascular rarefaction.

Recent advances in targeted protein degradation have introduced PROTACs as a revolutionary therapeutic modality. Unlike traditional inhibitors, PROTACs are heterobifunctional molecules consisting of three elements: (i) a ligand that binds the protein of interest (POI), (ii) a ligand that recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase, and (iii) a chemical linker connecting the two ligands. By bringing the POI into proximity with the E3 ligase, PROTACs facilitate the transfer of ubiquitin moieties onto the POI, marking it for degradation by the 26S proteasome. This “event-driven” mechanism allows a single PROTAC molecule to catalyze the degradation of multiple POI molecules, enabling potent effects at substoichiometric concentrations and potentially overcoming limitations of occupancy-driven pharmacology. Several PROTACs targeting oncogenic proteins have entered clinical trials, demonstrating the feasibility of this approach. However, a recognized challenge in PROTAC design is the potential for “E3 ligase hijacking,” wherein the sequestration of the E3 ligase by the PROTAC may impair the degradation of its endogenous natural substrates (ELNS), leading to unintended biological consequences. While this phenomenon is often viewed as a liability, the present invention strategically leverages it as a therapeutic advantage.

Prior art includes general PROTAC scaffolds utilizing VHL or cereblon as E3 ligase recruiters, and Smad3-targeting strategies based on kinase inhibitors or direct binding molecules. However, no prior art discloses a PROTAC specifically engineered to simultaneously degrade Smad3 and stabilize HIF-2α by exploiting the shared dependence of both proteins on the VHL-mediated degradation pathway. Existing Smad3 inhibitors do not induce protein degradation and lack the dual functionality described herein. Similarly, HIF-PHD inhibitors non-selectively stabilize both HIF-1α and HIF-2α, potentially exacerbating fibrosis through HIF-1α activation. The unique insight of the present invention—that a VHL-recruiting, Smad3-targeting PROTAC would not only degrade Smad3 but also stabilize HIF-2α due to competitive inhibition of VHL’s natural function—represents a novel and non-obvious therapeutic paradigm. Furthermore, the optimization of linker chemistry and stereochemistry to maximize degradation efficiency and selectivity, as detailed in the experimental data, distinguishes the claimed invention from generic PROTAC constructs.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel heterobifunctional PROTAC molecule designed to achieve dual therapeutic effects in the treatment of chronic kidney disease by simultaneously degrading Smad3 and stabilizing HIF-2α. The PROTAC comprises a Smad3-binding small molecule compound (SMC) covalently linked via a succinic acid-derived linker [(CH₂COOH)₂] to a VHL-binding SMC. This specific molecular architecture enables the PROTAC to recruit the VHL E3 ubiquitin ligase complex to Smad3, resulting in the polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of Smad3. Concurrently, by occupying the VHL binding site, the PROTAC prevents the recognition and ubiquitination of HIF-2α, leading to its stabilization and accumulation. Critically, the invention exploits the differential degradation mechanisms of HIF-1α and HIF-2α: while HIF-2α degradation is exclusively VHL-dependent, HIF-1α can be degraded via alternative E3 ligases such as RACK1, HAF, and CHIP, which are highly expressed in renal tubular cells. Consequently, the PROTAC selectively stabilizes HIF-2α without affecting HIF-1α levels, thereby avoiding the pro-fibrotic effects associated with HIF-1α accumulation.

The invention further discloses that the efficacy of the PROTAC is highly dependent on linker composition and the stereochemistry of the Smad3-binding moiety. Through systematic optimization, it was determined that a succinic acid-based linker [(CH₂COOH)₂] provides superior degradation efficiency compared to a polyethylene glycol (PEG) linker, likely due to optimal spatial orientation for ternary complex formation. Additionally, the S-enantiomer of the chiral Smad3-binding SMC (designated PROTAC-S) demonstrated significantly greater potency in both Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization than the R-enantiomer (PROTAC-R). Thus, the preferred embodiment of the invention is the S-configured PROTAC with a succinic acid linker, referred to herein as “the PROTAC.”

Pharmacological evaluation demonstrates that the PROTAC effectively degrades Smad3 and stabilizes HIF-2α in vitro in renal fibroblast (NRK-49F) and tubular epithelial (HK2) cell lines, with an effective concentration as low as 25 nM. In vivo, the PROTAC attenuates renal fibrosis, reduces inflammation, restores peritubular capillary density, preserves renal function, and ameliorates renal anemia in murine models of unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) and 5/6 nephrectomy (5/6Nx). These multifaceted benefits arise from the composite action of Smad3 degradation—suppressing ECM deposition, EMT, and macrophage infiltration—and HIF-2α stabilization—enhancing VEGF-mediated angiogenesis and EPO-driven erythropoiesis with improved iron utilization. Importantly, toxicity studies in mice reveal a wide therapeutic window, with no significant adverse effects on body weight, organ histology, or hematological parameters at therapeutically relevant doses.

The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the PROTAC in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for administration via subcutaneous, intraperitoneal, or other parenteral routes. Methods of treating CKD, particularly conditions involving renal fibrosis, impaired renal function, or renal anemia, are provided by administering an effective amount of the PROTAC to a subject in need thereof. The dual-mechanism action of the PROTAC represents a significant advancement over existing monotherapies, offering a synergistic approach that targets both the fibrotic and reparative arms of CKD pathophysiology.

## EMBODIMENTS

### Example 1

The present example details the synthesis, characterization, and biological evaluation of the novel VHL-recruiting, Smad3-targeting PROTAC of the invention. The PROTAC was constructed by linking a previously identified Smad3-binding small molecule compound (SMC) to a VHL-binding SMC via a succinic acid-based linker [(CH₂COOH)₂], with optimization performed to select the most efficacious stereochemical configuration and linker type.

**Chemical Synthesis and Structural Confirmation:**  
The Smad3-binding SMC used as the POI ligand was a chiral molecule identified through a combination of computer-aided virtual screening and surface plasmon resonance (SPR) validation, demonstrating specific binding to Smad3 without inhibiting its phosphorylation—a critical distinction from conventional Smad3 inhibitors, as Smad3 must be phosphorylated to serve as a substrate for ubiquitination. The VHL-binding SMC was selected from known ligands that specifically engage the VHL E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. Two distinct PROTAC variants were initially synthesized using different linkers: (i) a polyethylene glycol (PEG)-based linker providing greater flexibility and length, and (ii) a succinic acid [(CH₂COOH)₂]-based linker offering a shorter, more rigid connection. Additionally, due to the chirality of the Smad3-binding SMC, both R- and S-enantiomeric forms of each linker variant were prepared, yielding four candidate molecules: PROTAC-PEG-R, PROTAC-PEG-S, PROTAC-(CH₂COOH)₂-R, and PROTAC-(CH₂COOH)₂-S. Chemical synthesis was carried out by Ontores (Zhejiang, China) following standard peptide coupling and purification protocols. The purity of each synthesized PROTAC was confirmed to exceed 95% by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), and the molecular structures were verified by mass spectrometry (MS), which matched the theoretical molecular weights. The final selected PROTAC, designated herein as “the PROTAC,” was the S-enantiomer with the succinic acid linker, exhibiting a molecular weight of 750.8 Da and presenting as a white lyophilized powder.

**In Vitro Degradation and Stabilization Assays:**  
Initial in vitro screening was performed using lysates from human renal carcinoma ACHN cells, which express high endogenous levels of pVHL protein. Recombinant Smad3 protein was incubated with increasing concentrations of the PROTAC (0, 1, 5, 10 μg) in ACHN lysates at 37°C for 30 minutes. Western blot analysis revealed a dose-dependent reduction in Smad3 protein levels, confirming PROTAC-mediated degradation. To verify involvement of the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway (UPP), the proteasome inhibitor MG132 was co-administered. In the presence of MG132, Smad3 degradation was rescued, and higher-molecular-weight smears corresponding to polyubiquitinated Smad3 species (consistent with the addition of 8 kDa ubiquitin moieties) were detected using an anti-ubiquitin antibody, providing direct evidence of UPP-dependent degradation.

Subsequent cellular assays utilized rat renal interstitial fibroblasts (NRK-49F) and human renal proximal tubule cells (HK2). NRK-49F cells were chosen because they express high levels of VHL and represent a key cell type in renal fibrosis where HIF-2α is physiologically active. HK2 cells were selected for tubular studies due to their high VHL expression among human, mouse, and rat tubular cell lines (confirmed by immunoblotting). Treatment of NRK-49F cells with 25 nM of either PROTAC-PEG or PROTAC-(CH₂COOH)₂ demonstrated that the succinic acid-linked variant induced more pronounced Smad3 degradation at lower concentrations. Further comparison of the R- and S-enantiomers of the succinic acid-linked PROTAC showed that PROTAC-S achieved superior Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization compared to PROTAC-R. Time-course experiments in NRK-49F cells treated with 25 nM PROTAC-S revealed transient Smad3 downregulation (peaking at 4–8 hours) and sustained HIF-2α upregulation (persisting beyond 24 hours). Dose-response curves established an effective concentration (EC₅₀) of approximately 25 nM (~2 ng/mL) for both effects. In HK2 tubular cells, PROTAC-S treatment similarly induced dose-dependent Smad3 degradation but did not stabilize HIF-1α, consistent with the high expression of non-VHL E3 ligases (RACK1, HAF, CHIP) in these cells that mediate HIF-1α degradation independently of VHL.

**In Vivo Pharmacokinetics and Toxicity:**  
Pharmacokinetic (PK) studies in ICR mice evaluated multiple administration routes. Intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of 5 mg/kg yielded a peak plasma concentration (Cₘₐₓ) of 94.1 ng/mL but rapid clearance (half-life T₁/₂ = 0.481 h). Subcutaneous (s.c.) administration of the same dose produced a higher Cₘₐₓ (221 ng/mL) and prolonged exposure (T₁/₂ = 1.58 h), maintaining plasma levels well above the efficacious concentration (2 ng/mL) for an extended duration. Tissue distribution analysis after s.c. dosing (5 mg/kg) showed peak PROTAC concentrations in the kidney, liver, and heart at 4 hours post-administration, confirming renal bioavailability. Subacute and chronic toxicity assessments involved daily s.c. injections of vehicle or PROTAC at 50, 150, or 300 mg/kg for 7 days, followed by a 7-day recovery period. No behavioral abnormalities, changes in body weight trajectory, or alterations in hematological or serum biochemical parameters were observed across all dose groups. Organ-to-body weight ratios for heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney remained unchanged. Histopathological examination of liver, kidney, and spleen revealed only a mild increase in nucleated cells in the spleen at the highest dose, without splenomegaly or peripheral blood cytopenias, indicating a favorable safety profile and a wide therapeutic window.

**Therapeutic Efficacy in Murine Models of CKD:**  
Two established murine models were employed: (i) unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) in C57BL/6J mice to study acute interstitial fibrosis, and (ii) 5/6 nephrectomy (5/6Nx) to model chronic renal failure with fibrosis, functional decline, and anemia. In the UUO model, mice received i.p. PROTAC-S (90 mg/kg twice daily) starting 5 days before surgery and continuing for 9 days post-surgery. Western blot and immunohistochemical analyses of obstructed kidneys showed ~40% reduction in Smad3 protein and ~2-fold increase in HIF-2α, with no change in HIF-1α. Masson’s trichrome staining demonstrated significant attenuation of collagen deposition, while immunostaining for α-SMA and F4/80 indicated reduced myofibroblast activation and macrophage infiltration, respectively. CD34 immunostaining revealed restoration of peritubular capillary density, correlating with elevated serum VEGF levels. In the 5/6Nx model, mice received s.c. PROTAC-S (24 mg/kg every 2 days) for 5 weeks. Treated mice exhibited preserved renal function, as evidenced by significantly lower serum cystatin C, creatinine, and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels compared to vehicle controls from week 3 onward. Renal anemia was markedly improved, with hemoglobin and red blood cell counts normalizing by week 5. This was accompanied by increased serum EPO and enhanced iron mobilization, reflected in reduced hepcidin and elevated serum Fe²⁺ and ferritin. Real-time qPCR confirmed upregulation of HIF-2α target genes involved in intestinal iron absorption (Dcytb, DMT1, FPN1) in the duodenum, without affecting HIF-1α targets in the liver.

**Mechanistic Insights and Therapeutic Advantages:**  
The dual functionality of the PROTAC arises from its strategic engagement of the VHL pathway. By degrading Smad3, it directly inhibits the core fibrotic signaling cascade. Simultaneously, VHL occupancy stabilizes HIF-2α, promoting tissue repair through angiogenesis and erythropoiesis. The selectivity for HIF-2α over HIF-1α is ensured by the cell-type-specific expression of alternative HIF-1α E3 ligases in tubules, preventing unintended pro-fibrotic HIF-1α accumulation. Moreover, the PROTAC’s activity is context-dependent: it preferentially acts in injured kidneys where Smad3 is upregulated, minimizing effects on healthy tissue. This targeted, catalytic mechanism offers advantages over traditional inhibitors, including lower dosing requirements, reversibility, and reduced risk of chronic pathway blockade. The invention thus provides a first-in-class therapeutic agent that integrates antifibrotic and regenerative actions into a single molecule, representing a paradigm shift in the treatment of complex, multifactorial diseases like CKD.
