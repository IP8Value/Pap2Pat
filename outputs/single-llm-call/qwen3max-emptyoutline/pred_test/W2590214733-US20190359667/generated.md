## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of protein engineering and therapeutic biologics, and more specifically to engineered variants of transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) that function as dominant negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling. The invention provides novel monomeric TGF-β polypeptides that lack the capacity to signal through the canonical TGF-β receptor complex but retain high-affinity binding to the type II TGF-β receptor (TβRII), thereby competitively inhibiting endogenous TGF-β ligands from initiating downstream signaling cascades. These engineered monomers represent a new class of highly specific, small-molecule-sized protein therapeutics for the treatment of diseases characterized by pathological overactivation of TGF-β signaling, including fibrosis, cancer progression, metastasis, and immune suppression.

## BACKGROUND

Transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) is a pleiotropic cytokine belonging to the larger TGF-β superfamily of structurally related signaling molecules that includes bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs), activins, and growth and differentiation factors (GDFs). In mammals, three primary isoforms—TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3—are encoded by distinct genes and exhibit overlapping yet non-redundant biological functions. All TGF-β isoforms are synthesized as precursor proteins comprising an N-terminal latency-associated peptide (LAP) and a C-terminal mature domain. Following proteolytic cleavage, the mature domains dimerize via an intermolecular disulfide bond formed between conserved cysteine residues (typically Cys-77 in human TGF-β1) to yield biologically active homodimers or, less commonly, heterodimers. These dimers adopt a characteristic “hand-like” tertiary structure stabilized by an intramolecular cystine knot motif within each monomer, consisting of four antiparallel β-strands forming the “fingers” and a central α-helix (α3, also referred to as the “heel helix”) that mediates critical intermolecular contacts in the dimeric state.

Canonical TGF-β signaling is initiated when the dimeric ligand simultaneously engages two copies each of the type II (TβRII) and type I (TβRI, also known as ALK5) serine/threonine kinase receptors, forming a heterotetrameric (TβRI–TβRII)₂ signaling complex. Structural studies have revealed that TβRII binds exclusively to the “fingertip” region of a single TGF-β monomer, whereas TβRI binds to a composite interface formed by both monomers of the dimer as well as TβRII. This cooperative assembly triggers transphosphorylation of TβRI by TβRII, leading to activation of downstream Smad-dependent and Smad-independent signaling pathways that regulate diverse cellular processes including proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, migration, and extracellular matrix production.

Dysregulation of TGF-β signaling is implicated in a wide spectrum of human pathologies. In early-stage tumors, TGF-β acts as a tumor suppressor by inducing cell cycle arrest and apoptosis. However, in advanced cancers, tumor cells often become resistant to these effects and instead co-opt TGF-β signaling to promote epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis, and immunosuppression within the tumor microenvironment. Moreover, excessive TGF-β activity drives the pathogenesis of fibrotic disorders affecting the lung (idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis), liver (cirrhosis), kidney (glomerulosclerosis), and heart (myocardial fibrosis). Despite decades of research and numerous clinical trials, no TGF-β-targeted therapy has yet received regulatory approval, largely due to challenges associated with achieving sufficient target specificity, tissue penetration, and therapeutic index.

Existing strategies to inhibit TGF-β signaling include neutralizing monoclonal antibodies, soluble TβRII-Fc fusion proteins, antisense oligonucleotides, and small-molecule kinase inhibitors targeting TβRI. While these approaches have demonstrated preclinical efficacy, they suffer from significant limitations. Monoclonal antibodies and Fc fusions are large (~150 kDa and ~100 kDa, respectively), which restricts their ability to penetrate dense tumor stroma or fibrotic tissues. Furthermore, pan-TGF-β antibodies may disrupt homeostatic functions of TGF-β in normal tissues, leading to dose-limiting toxicities such as cardiac valvulopathy and inflammation. Small-molecule kinase inhibitors, while orally bioavailable and capable of deep tissue penetration, often lack selectivity for TβRI over other kinases in the same family (e.g., ALK1, ALK2), resulting in off-target effects. Additionally, because TGF-β signals through multiple parallel pathways, complete pathway blockade may require combination therapies, further complicating safety profiles.

Monomeric forms of TGF-β, generated by mutation of the interchain cysteine (e.g., C77S in TGF-β1), have been previously described. Contrary to initial expectations, these monomers retain significant signaling activity due to their ability to reversibly dimerize in solution and assemble functional receptor complexes. This residual activity renders them unsuitable as inhibitors. Attempts to generate true dominant negative TGF-β variants have thus far been unsuccessful, primarily because disrupting dimerization without compromising structural integrity and receptor binding has proven technically challenging. The heel helix (α3) represents a key structural element that contributes both to monomer–monomer interactions and to the TβRI binding interface; however, its deletion was presumed to destabilize the entire fold or abolish TβRII binding, given the compact and interdependent nature of the TGF-β scaffold.

Accordingly, there remains a critical unmet need for a TGF-β inhibitor that combines high specificity for the TGF-β/TβRII interaction, small molecular size for enhanced tissue penetration, intrinsic stability conferred by disulfide bonding, and minimal off-target effects. The present invention addresses this need by providing engineered TGF-β monomers that lack the heel helix and are further optimized to maintain high-affinity TβRII binding while being completely incapable of recruiting TβRI or assembling a functional signaling complex, thereby acting as potent and selective dominant negative inhibitors of all three major TGF-β isoforms.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides engineered monomeric polypeptides derived from transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) that function as dominant negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling. These polypeptides, referred to herein as mini-monomeric TGF-β (mmTGF-β) variants, are characterized by the deletion of a segment corresponding to α-helix 3 (the “heel helix”) and substitution of the cysteine residue normally involved in intermolecular disulfide bond formation with a non-cysteine amino acid, typically serine. In a preferred embodiment, the polypeptide is derived from TGF-β2 and further comprises one or more amino acid substitutions in the TβRII-binding interface that confer high-affinity binding to the type II TGF-β receptor (TβRII), comparable to that of native TGF-β1 or TGF-β3.

Specifically, the invention encompasses an isolated polypeptide comprising an amino acid sequence that is at least 85% identical to a reference sequence selected from the group consisting of: (a) a sequence corresponding to residues 1–51 and 72–112 of human TGF-β2, wherein Cys-77 is substituted with Ser, and wherein residues Lys-25, Ile-92, and Asn-94 are substituted with Arg, Val, and Arg, respectively; and (b) a sequence corresponding to residues 1–51 and 72–112 of human TGF-β2, wherein Cys-77 is substituted with Ser, and wherein residues Lys-25, Ile-92, Asn-94, Arg-26, Leu-89, Thr-95, and Ile-98 are substituted with Arg, Val, Arg, Lys, Val, Lys, and Val, respectively. In certain embodiments, the polypeptide lacks residues 52–71 of wild-type TGF-β2. The polypeptide retains the four intramolecular disulfide bonds characteristic of the TGF-β monomer fold and adopts a stable tertiary structure in which the region corresponding to the deleted α-helix 3 is replaced by a flexible loop connecting β-strand 4 and β-strand 5.

The engineered mmTGF-β polypeptides of the invention bind TβRII with high affinity (KD in the low nanomolar range) but are incapable of binding or recruiting TβRI, either as monomers or as transient dimers. Consequently, they do not activate TGF-β signaling pathways and instead act as competitive antagonists by occupying TβRII and preventing engagement by endogenous dimeric TGF-β ligands. Functional assays demonstrate that these polypeptides inhibit TGF-β1-, TGF-β2-, and TGF-β3-induced signaling with IC₅₀ values ranging from approximately 20 to 70 nanomolar. Importantly, the polypeptides exhibit significantly improved solubility at physiological pH compared to wild-type TGF-β dimers or full-length monomeric TGF-β variants, which precipitate under the same conditions.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a therapeutically effective amount of an mmTGF-β polypeptide as described herein, in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. Such compositions may be formulated for parenteral, subcutaneous, intravenous, intratumoral, or inhalation administration, depending on the intended therapeutic application. The compositions are particularly suited for the treatment of diseases mediated by aberrant TGF-β signaling, including but not limited to cancer (e.g., metastatic carcinoma, glioblastoma), fibrotic disorders (e.g., pulmonary fibrosis, liver cirrhosis, renal fibrosis), autoimmune diseases, and conditions involving pathological tissue remodeling.

Additionally, the invention includes methods of inhibiting TGF-β signaling in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a composition containing an mmTGF-β polypeptide of the invention. The methods may be employed to reduce tumor growth, inhibit metastasis, attenuate fibrosis, modulate immune responses, or enhance the efficacy of other therapeutic agents such as chemotherapeutics, immunotherapies, or radiation. The small size (~10 kDa) and high stability of the mmTGF-β polypeptides confer advantages in terms of tissue penetration, manufacturing scalability, and resistance to proteolytic degradation, making them attractive candidates for clinical development.

### II. Terms and Methods

As used herein, the term “engineered TGF-β monomer” or “mini-monomeric TGF-β (mmTGF-β)” refers to a recombinant polypeptide derived from a naturally occurring TGF-β isoform (e.g., TGF-β1, TGF-β2, or TGF-β3) that has been modified to remove the structural elements necessary for dimerization and TβRI recruitment while preserving the structural integrity required for high-affinity TβRII binding. Key modifications include deletion of the amino acid segment corresponding to α-helix 3 (residues 52–71 in human TGF-β2) and substitution of the conserved cysteine involved in interchain disulfide bonding (Cys-77 in TGF-β2) with a non-reactive residue such as serine. Additional substitutions may be introduced in the TβRII-binding interface to enhance affinity, particularly when the parent isoform is TGF-β2, which natively exhibits weak TβRII binding.

The term “dominant negative inhibitor” refers to a molecule that interferes with the function of a wild-type protein by forming non-functional complexes or by sequestering essential interaction partners. In the context of TGF-β signaling, an mmTGF-β polypeptide acts as a dominant negative by binding TβRII with high affinity but failing to recruit TβRI, thereby preventing the assembly of a functional signaling complex and blocking signal transduction initiated by endogenous TGF-β ligands.

“High-affinity binding to TβRII” is defined herein as a dissociation constant (KD) of less than 200 nanomolar, as measured by surface plasmon resonance (SPR) or an equivalent biophysical method under physiological buffer conditions (e.g., HBS-EP: 10 mM Hepes, pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 3 mM EDTA, 0.005% surfactant P-20). Preferably, the KD is in the range of 10 to 100 nanomolar.

“Solubility at physiological pH” refers to the ability of the polypeptide to remain in solution without significant precipitation when diluted into phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) at concentrations of at least 10 micromolar, as assessed by light scattering at 340 nm and measurement of supernatant absorbance at 280 nm after centrifugation.

The term “isolated polypeptide” means a polypeptide that has been separated from its natural environment and is at least partially purified, i.e., present in a form that is substantially free of other cellular components such as nucleic acids, lipids, and unrelated proteins. The polypeptide may be produced by recombinant expression in a host cell (e.g., E. coli, CHO, yeast) followed by purification using chromatographic techniques such as ion exchange, affinity, or size exclusion chromatography.

“Pharmaceutically acceptable carrier” encompasses any excipient, diluent, or delivery system that does not interfere with the activity of the active ingredient and is compatible with the route of administration. Examples include sterile aqueous solutions, buffered saline, liposomes, nanoparticles, and biodegradable polymers.

Methods for producing the mmTGF-β polypeptides involve standard molecular biology techniques. DNA sequences encoding the desired variants are cloned into expression vectors under the control of inducible promoters (e.g., T7/lac in E. coli). Proteins are expressed as inclusion bodies, solubilized in denaturants (e.g., guanidine hydrochloride), and refolded by rapid dilution into redox-shuffling buffers containing reduced and oxidized glutathione to facilitate correct disulfide bond formation. Refolded proteins are purified by cation-exchange chromatography (e.g., Source Q resin) and characterized by mass spectrometry, circular dichroism, NMR, and X-ray crystallography to confirm identity, purity, and structural integrity.

Binding affinities are determined by SPR using Biacore instruments. Ligands (TGF-β variants) are immobilized on sensor chips either directly via amine coupling or indirectly via biotin-streptavidin capture if N-terminally tagged with an AviTag. Analytes (receptor ectodomains) are injected at varying concentrations, and kinetic parameters (ka, kd, KD) are derived by fitting sensorgrams to a 1:1 Langmuir binding model.

Functional activity is assessed using HEK293 cells stably transfected with a luciferase reporter gene under the control of a CAGA₁₂ promoter, which is responsive to Smad2/3 phosphorylation. Cells are treated with test compounds alone or in combination with sub-saturating concentrations of TGF-β ligands, and luciferase activity is measured after 16 hours. Dose-response curves are fitted to calculate EC₅₀ (for agonists) or IC₅₀ (for antagonists) values using software such as GraphPad Prism.

Structural analyses are performed by X-ray crystallography. Crystals are grown by vapor diffusion, cryo-cooled, and diffraction data collected at synchrotron sources. Structures are solved by molecular replacement using known TGF-β or receptor structures as search models and refined iteratively with programs such as PHENIX and COOT.

### III. Overview of Several Embodiments

In a first embodiment, the invention provides an isolated mmTGF-β2 polypeptide comprising the amino acid sequence of human TGF-β2 with the following modifications: (i) deletion of residues 52 to 71; (ii) substitution of Cys-77 with Ser; and (iii) substitution of Lys-25 with Arg, Ile-92 with Val, and Asn-94 with Arg. This variant, designated mmTGF-β2-3M, exhibits improved TβRII binding relative to unmodified mmTGF-β2 but may still display suboptimal affinity in certain contexts.

In a second, preferred embodiment, the mmTGF-β2 polypeptide further includes four additional substitutions: Arg-26 to Lys, Leu-89 to Val, Thr-95 to Lys, and Ile-98 to Val, resulting in a total of seven amino acid changes relative to wild-type TGF-β2. This variant, designated mmTGF-β2-7M, binds TβRII with high affinity (KD ≈ 30–50 nM) comparable to that of TGF-β1 and TGF-β3, as confirmed by SPR and co-crystal structures of the mmTGF-β2-7M•TβRII complex.

In a third embodiment, analogous mmTGF-β variants are constructed based on TGF-β1 or TGF-β3 backbones. Although initial attempts to express mmTGF-β1 and mmTGF-β3 yielded poor folding and low solubility, it is contemplated that further optimization—such as introduction of solubilizing mutations, use of alternative expression hosts, or co-expression with chaperones—could render these variants viable. The core design principle—deletion of α-helix 3 and C77S substitution—remains applicable across isoforms.

In a fourth embodiment, the mmTGF-β polypeptides are fused to half-life extension moieties such as albumin-binding domains, Fc fragments, or polyethylene glycol (PEG) chains to increase plasma residence time for chronic indications like fibrosis. Alternatively, for applications requiring rapid clearance (e.g., imaging), the polypeptides may be left unmodified or conjugated to small tags.

In a fifth embodiment, the polypeptides are formulated as lyophilized powders for reconstitution prior to injection, or as stable liquid formulations containing stabilizers (e.g., sucrose, trehalose) and surfactants (e.g., polysorbate 80) to prevent aggregation during storage.

In a sixth embodiment, the mmTGF-β polypeptides are used in combination with other therapeutic agents. For example, in oncology, they may be co-administered with immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., anti-PD-1 antibodies) to reverse TGF-β-mediated immunosuppression in the tumor microenvironment. In fibrosis, they may be combined with antifibrotic agents such as pirfenidone or nintedanib to achieve synergistic effects.

### IV. Administration of Engineered TGF-β Monomers

The engineered mmTGF-β monomers of the invention are administered to a subject in a therapeutically effective amount to inhibit pathological TGF-β signaling. The route of administration depends on the disease indication and desired pharmacokinetic profile. For systemic diseases such as metastatic cancer or diffuse fibrosis, intravenous or subcutaneous injection is preferred. For localized conditions—for instance, intraocular fibrosis or solid tumors—direct intratumoral, intravitreal, or inhalation delivery may be employed to maximize local concentration while minimizing systemic exposure.

Dosage regimens are determined empirically based on preclinical efficacy and toxicity studies. Typical doses range from 0.1 to 10 mg/kg body weight, administered once daily to once weekly. Due to their small size and lack of Fc-mediated effector functions, mmTGF-β polypeptides are expected to have shorter half-lives than antibody-based inhibitors, necessitating more frequent dosing unless modified for half-life extension.

Pharmaceutical compositions are prepared by mixing the purified mmTGF-β polypeptide with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. For parenteral administration, the composition is formulated as a sterile, isotonic solution in PBS or saline, optionally containing stabilizers (e.g., 5% mannitol, 0.01% polysorbate 20) and adjusted to pH 6.0–7.4. Lyophilized formulations may include bulking agents (e.g., glycine) and cryoprotectants (e.g., sucrose) to maintain stability during freeze-drying and storage.

Safety and tolerability are monitored through standard clinical assessments, including hematological, hepatic, and renal function tests. Given the high specificity of mmTGF-β for TβRII and the absence of kinase inhibition, the risk of off-target toxicities is expected to be low. However, potential effects on wound healing or immune homeostasis are evaluated in long-term studies.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: An Engineered TGF-β Monomer that Functions as a Dominant Negative to Block TGF-β Signaling

#### BACKGROUND

Transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) signaling plays a dual role in cancer, acting as a tumor suppressor in early stages but promoting metastasis and immunosuppression in advanced disease. Despite extensive efforts, no TGF-β inhibitor has achieved clinical approval, largely due to the limitations of existing modalities—antibodies are too large for optimal tissue penetration, and kinase inhibitors lack specificity. Monomeric TGF-β variants (e.g., C77S mutants) were previously thought to be inactive but were later shown to signal via reversible dimerization. Thus, a true dominant negative TGF-β inhibitor—one that binds TβRII but cannot recruit TβRI—remained elusive. This example describes the rational design, biophysical characterization, and functional validation of mmTGF-β2-7M, an engineered monomer that fulfills these criteria.

#### Discussion

The design of mmTGF-β2-7M was guided by structural insights into TGF-β/receptor interactions. The heel helix (α3) was identified as a critical mediator of both monomer–monomer association and TβRI binding. Deletion of residues 52–71, encompassing α3 and flanking regions, was predicted to disrupt dimerization and TβRI recruitment while sparing the distal TβRII-binding fingertips. To compensate for TGF-β2’s naturally weak TβRII affinity, seven residues in the binding interface were mutated to match TGF-β1/β3.

Expression in E. coli yielded soluble mmTGF-β2-7M after refolding, in contrast to mmTGF-β1/β3, which aggregated. NMR and X-ray crystallography confirmed that mmTGF-β2-7M adopts a native-like fold, with the deleted helix replaced by a flexible loop. SPR showed high-affinity TβRII binding (KD ~40 nM), and co-crystal structures verified preservation of key interactions (e.g., Arg-25/Glu-119 salt bridge). Crucially, mmTGF-β2-7M failed to recruit TβRI in SPR and TR-FRET assays, confirming loss of signaling competence.

In cellular assays, mmTGF-β2-7M exhibited no agonist activity up to 20 nM and potently inhibited TGF-β1/2/3 signaling with IC₅₀ values of 20–70 nM. AUC revealed weakened self-association (KD dimerization ~40 μM vs. ~4 μM for mTGF-β3), explaining the lack of functional dimers. The improved solubility at pH 7.4—unlike wild-type TGF-β, which precipitates—enhances developability.

These data establish mmTGF-β2-7M as a first-in-class dominant negative TGF-β inhibitor. Its small size (~10 kDa), high specificity, and stability offer advantages over existing approaches. Clinical translation could address unmet needs in oncology and fibrosis, where TGF-β blockade holds promise but has been hampered by drug limitations.
