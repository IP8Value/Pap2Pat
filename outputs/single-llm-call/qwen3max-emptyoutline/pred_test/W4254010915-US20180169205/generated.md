# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of vaccine development and immunology, particularly to the design and use of novel protein-based vaccine scaffolds capable of eliciting robust humoral immune responses in both immunocompetent and immunocompromised individuals. More specifically, the invention provides a recombinant hybrid flagellin protein engineered to display conformationally intact heterologous antigenic domains on its surface in a polymeric filamentous structure, thereby enabling efficient B cell receptor (BCR) crosslinking and stimulation of T cell-independent (TI) antibody responses. The invention is particularly directed toward the use of bacterial flagellin—specifically from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium—as a structural scaffold for the presentation of viral envelope proteins, such as the Dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2) envelope glycoprotein (E), in a repetitive, high-avidity array that mimics natural pathogen-associated molecular patterns. This platform technology is applicable to the development of vaccines against a wide range of infectious agents, especially in contexts where conventional T cell-dependent (TD) immune responses are impaired or absent, including but not limited to human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection, congenital immunodeficiencies, chemotherapy-induced immunosuppression, aging, and early infancy.

### Related Art

The adaptive immune system relies heavily on coordinated interactions between T helper (Th) cells and B lymphocytes to generate high-affinity, class-switched antibody responses against pathogens. In classical T cell-dependent (TD) immunity, B cells recognize specific antigens via their surface B cell receptors (BCRs), internalize and process the antigen, and present peptide fragments on major histocompatibility complex class II (MHC-II) molecules to cognate CD4+ T helper cells. This cognate interaction provides critical co-stimulatory signals—such as CD40L-CD40 engagement and cytokine secretion—that drive B cell proliferation, somatic hypermutation, affinity maturation, class switch recombination (CSR), and differentiation into long-lived plasma cells and memory B cells. However, this pathway is fundamentally compromised in individuals with T cell deficiencies, such as those suffering from acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS), DiGeorge syndrome, or iatrogenic immunosuppression due to chemotherapy or organ transplantation. In such settings, the host remains vulnerable to infections that would otherwise be controlled by robust antibody-mediated immunity.

Fortunately, the immune system possesses an alternative, evolutionarily conserved mechanism known as T cell-independent (TI) antibody responses, which are primarily mediated by specialized subsets of innate-like B cells, including marginal zone (MZ) B cells in the spleen and B1 B cells in the peritoneal and pleural cavities. These B cells can be activated directly by repetitive, polymeric antigens—such as bacterial capsular polysaccharides or viral capsid proteins—without requiring T cell help. The key biophysical requirement for TI activation is the ability of the antigen to induce extensive crosslinking of BCRs on the B cell surface. It has been established that at least 10 to 20 BCRs must be simultaneously engaged and clustered to trigger downstream signaling cascades leading to B cell activation, proliferation, and antibody secretion. On resting B cells, BCRs are spaced approximately 35 nanometers (nm) apart in the plasma membrane, necessitating that effective TI antigens possess epitopes arranged in a spatially repetitive manner with inter-epitope distances of 5–10 nm and overall molecular dimensions exceeding 500 nm in length to facilitate sufficient multivalent binding.

Historically, many successful TI vaccines have been based on bacterial polysaccharides, such as those in the pneumococcal and meningococcal vaccines. However, these formulations often fail to induce robust or long-lasting immunity in young children and immunocompromised adults, and they do not support affinity maturation or memory formation. Protein-based TI antigens are less common, largely because most soluble proteins lack the necessary size and repetitive epitope architecture. Bacterial flagellin, the primary structural component of the flagellar filament, represents a promising candidate for TI vaccine design due to its inherent polymeric nature. Flagellin from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium (FliC) self-assembles into helical filaments up to 15 micrometers in length, composed of 11 protofilaments arranged with 11-fold symmetry. Each FliC monomer consists of four structural domains (D0, D1, D2, and D3), with D0 and D1 forming the inner core responsible for polymerization and stability, while D2 and D3 are more surface-exposed. Notably, the D3 domain is fully solvent-exposed in the assembled filament and is spaced approximately 5 nm apart from adjacent D3 domains along the filament axis—precisely matching the optimal spacing for BCR crosslinking.

Moreover, FliC is a well-characterized pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMP) that activates innate immunity through Toll-like receptor 5 (TLR5) and the Nod-like receptor CARD domain-containing protein 4 (NLRC4) inflammasome, both of which recognize conserved motifs in the D1 domain. This intrinsic adjuvant activity enhances the immunogenicity of flagellin-based constructs without requiring external adjuvants. Previous studies have explored the use of FliC as a fusion partner for vaccine antigens, typically by appending foreign epitopes to the N- or C-terminus. However, such approaches often result in steric hindrance, misfolding, or poor surface exposure of the antigen, especially in the polymeric state. Furthermore, one study suggested that flagellin-based immunogens require T cell help for antibody production, though that investigation did not assess IgM or IgG3—key isotypes associated with TI responses—thereby potentially overlooking a significant TI component.

The present invention addresses these limitations by exploiting the unique structural biology of the FliC filament. Specifically, the D3 domain, which is dispensable for filament assembly and stability, is entirely replaced with a conformationally intact, full-length viral envelope protein—here exemplified by the DENV2 E protein—flanked by flexible glycine-serine (GS) linkers. This design ensures that the inserted antigen is displayed as an independent, properly folded domain on the outer surface of the polymerized filament, with high density and optimal spatial orientation for BCR engagement. The resulting hybrid flagellin (hFliC) retains the ability to form long filaments, presents the heterologous antigen in a repetitive array, and leverages the innate immune-stimulating properties of flagellin to drive potent humoral immunity—even in the absence of functional T cell help.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel recombinant protein scaffold for the development of T cell-independent (TI) vaccines, comprising a hybrid flagellin protein wherein the native D3 domain of bacterial flagellin (FliC) is replaced with a heterologous antigenic domain, such that upon polymerization, the heterologous antigen is displayed in a repetitive, high-density, and solvent-exposed array on the surface of a filamentous structure. The invention further encompasses methods of producing, purifying, and polymerizing said hybrid flagellin, as well as pharmaceutical compositions and vaccine formulations containing the polymerized hybrid flagellin for administration to a subject to elicit a protective antibody response, particularly in individuals with impaired T cell function.

In one embodiment, the hybrid flagellin comprises a first polypeptide segment corresponding to the N-terminal portion of FliC (residues 1–184), a second polypeptide segment corresponding to the C-terminal portion of FliC (residues 285–494), and a heterologous antigenic domain inserted between said first and second segments, wherein the heterologous antigenic domain is flanked by flexible linker sequences comprising glycine and serine residues. The heterologous antigenic domain may be a full-length viral envelope glycoprotein, such as the Dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2) envelope protein, or any other conformationally dependent antigen that requires proper folding for immunogenicity.

The hybrid flagellin protein is capable of self-assembling into filaments under physiological or near-physiological conditions, such as in the presence of high molar concentrations of ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate. These filaments exhibit a diameter of approximately 35 nm, consistent with computational modeling predictions, and display the heterologous antigen on their outer surface in a spatial arrangement that facilitates efficient crosslinking of B cell receptors. The invention further demonstrates that the hybrid filaments retain the antigenicity of the inserted domain, as confirmed by binding to conformation-specific monoclonal antibodies, and that they induce robust humoral immune responses—including IgM, IgG3, and IgA—depending on the route of administration and the immunological status of the host.

Critically, the invention shows that the hybrid flagellin filaments can stimulate significant antibody production in a T cell repertoire-restricted transgenic mouse model (6.5-TCR), in which nearly all CD4+ T cells are specific for an irrelevant influenza hemagglutinin peptide and thus cannot provide cognate help to B cells responding to the DENV2 antigen. In this model, intraperitoneal immunization with polymeric hFliC induced strong IgM and IgG3 responses—hallmarks of TI immunity—demonstrating the scaffold’s capacity to bypass T cell dependence. In contrast, monomeric forms of the same protein failed to elicit comparable responses, underscoring the necessity of the polymeric architecture for TI activation.

The invention also provides methods for using the hybrid flagellin scaffold to develop multivalent or tetravalent vaccines against pathogens with multiple serotypes, such as Dengue virus, by replacing the D3 domain with envelope proteins from each of the four serotypes and co-polymerizing them into mixed filaments with defined stoichiometry. This approach may overcome the imbalanced immunogenicity observed in current tetravalent Dengue vaccine candidates, which often elicit dominant responses to one or two serotypes while providing inadequate protection against others.

In summary, the invention establishes a versatile, modular, and highly effective platform for the design of next-generation vaccines that function independently of T cell help, offering a promising solution for protecting vulnerable populations who are poorly served by conventional TD vaccines.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1

The construction and characterization of a hybrid flagellin protein (hFliC) designed to display the Dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2) envelope glycoprotein (E) as a conformationally intact, surface-exposed antigen on a polymeric filament scaffold is described herein. The native FliC protein from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium was selected as the scaffold due to its well-defined structural organization, intrinsic ability to polymerize into long filaments, and established role as a potent innate immune activator via TLR5 and NLRC4 pathways. The FliC monomer comprises four structural domains: D0 and D1 form the inner core and are essential for polymerization and stability, while D2 and D3 are located on the outer surface. Structural studies have shown that the D3 domain (residues 185–285) is fully solvent-exposed in the assembled filament and is dispensable for filament integrity, making it an ideal site for antigen insertion.

To engineer the hFliC construct, the DNA sequence encoding the D3 domain of FliC was deleted and replaced with the full-length coding sequence of the DENV2 E protein, which mediates viral attachment and fusion with host cells and is a major target of neutralizing antibodies. To preserve the conformational independence of the DENV2 E domain and prevent steric interference with FliC folding or polymerization, flexible glycine-serine (GS) linkers with the sequence GGGSGGGS were introduced at both the N- and C-termini of the DENV2 E insert. A C-terminal hexahistidine (His6) tag was also appended to facilitate purification. The final gene construct was cloned into the pENTR plasmid vector for subsequent expression in a baculovirus-insect cell system.

Recombinant hFliC protein was expressed in SF21 and SF9 insect cells using standard baculovirus protocols. The native FliC signal peptide was retained to ensure efficient secretion of the hybrid protein into the culture supernatant, and a leader peptide was added to the DENV2 E sequence to promote its extracellular localization. The secreted protein was purified from the culture medium by ammonium sulfate precipitation followed by immobilized metal affinity chromatography (IMAC) using cobalt resin. Analysis by SDS-PAGE and Coomassie staining revealed a major band at approximately 85 kDa, consistent with the predicted molecular weight of hFliC, although this band represented only about 15% of the total protein, indicating the presence of degradation products. Western blotting using an anti-His tag antibody confirmed the identity of the full-length protein and revealed a doublet, suggesting minor proteolytic cleavage near the N-terminus, which did not appear to compromise overall functionality.

To assess the secondary structural integrity of the hFliC protein, circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy was performed. The CD spectrum of wild-type FliC exhibited a characteristic α-helical signature, with experimentally determined α-helix content of 71.1%, consistent with its predominantly helical D0 and D1 domains. In contrast, the DENV2 E protein displayed a spectrum dominated by β-sheet and random coil features, with only 2.0% α-helix and 39.2% β-sheet, aligning with its known β-barrel fold. The hFliC hybrid showed a composite spectrum, with 56.0% α-helix and 18.4% β-sheet, indicating that both the FliC backbone and the DENV2 E insert retained their native secondary structures within the chimeric protein. This finding demonstrates that large, conformationally complex antigens can be successfully integrated into the FliC scaffold without disrupting the structural integrity of either component.

The ability of hFliC to form filaments was then evaluated. Purified hFliC was incubated in 1.5 M ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate—conditions known to promote FliC polymerization—and the resulting assemblies were analyzed by negative stain transmission electron microscopy (TEM). TEM images revealed long, filamentous structures with a diameter of approximately 35 nm, significantly larger than the ~20 nm diameter of wild-type FliC filaments. This increase in diameter is consistent with the computational model of the hFliC filament, which predicts that the DENV2 E domains project outward from the filament core, adding substantial bulk to the structure. The inner core of the hFliC filaments displayed high electron density, similar to that of Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV), while the outer regions appeared less dense, suggesting that the DENV2 E domains possess a degree of rotational freedom at the filament surface—a feature that may enhance antigen accessibility and BCR avidity.

To confirm that the DENV2 E domain retained its native conformation and antigenicity in the context of the hFliC filament, binding assays were performed using a recombinant monoclonal IgA antibody derived from the well-characterized 4G2 hybridoma, which specifically recognizes a conformational epitope in domain II of the DENV2 E protein. An ELISA demonstrated that hFliC, but not wild-type FliC, bound strongly to the 4G2 IgA, confirming that the inserted antigen was properly folded and accessible. Furthermore, immunogold labeling of hFliC filaments with 4G2 IgG followed by TEM visualization showed gold particles localized exclusively to the outer surface of the filaments, with no labeling observed on wild-type FliC filaments. This spatial distribution confirms that the DENV2 E domains are indeed displayed on the exterior of the polymerized scaffold, where they can engage BCRs.

Finally, the biological activity of hFliC was assessed in vitro using a B cell crosslinking assay. The 4G2 B cell hybridoma, which expresses surface Ig receptors specific for DENV2 E, was incubated with hFliC filaments. Confocal microscopy of cytospin preparations revealed that unstimulated cells displayed a diffuse, random distribution of surface Ig, whereas cells exposed to hFliC filaments exhibited a striking reorganization of BCRs into circumferential clusters around the cell periphery. Co-staining with an anti-His tag antibody confirmed that the hFliC filaments were in direct contact with the B cells and were responsible for the observed BCR clustering. This experiment provides direct evidence that the hFliC filament can crosslink BCRs above the threshold required for TI activation, fulfilling a key mechanistic requirement for T cell-independent immunogenicity.

### Example 2

The in vivo immunogenicity of the hFliC filament was evaluated in both immunocompetent and T cell-restricted mouse models to determine its capacity to elicit humoral immune responses under conditions of limited or absent T cell help. Two immunization protocols were employed, differing in the route of the priming dose: intraperitoneal (IP) or intranasal (IN). All mice received three booster doses via the IN route over a five-week period. Wild-type C57BL/6J mice were first used to establish baseline immunogenicity. Mice immunized with polymeric hFliC via IP priming developed the highest serum IgG and IgM titers, while those receiving IN priming and boosting exhibited the strongest serum IgA response, consistent with the mucosal tropism of the IN route. Isotyping analysis revealed that IP immunization favored a mixed Th1/Th2 profile, with robust IgG1, IgG2a, and IgG2b responses, whereas IN immunization skewed toward a Th2-type response, characterized by high IgG1 and IgA but low IgG2a/b. Monomeric hFliC, in contrast, induced significantly weaker responses across all isotypes, highlighting the critical role of the polymeric structure in enhancing immunogenicity.

To specifically test the T cell-independent potential of the hFliC filament, immunization experiments were conducted in 6.5-TCR transgenic mice, which express a monoclonal T cell receptor specific for an influenza hemagglutinin peptide presented on I-E^d MHC class II molecules. Due to allelic exclusion, these mice lack a diverse T cell repertoire and cannot provide cognate help to B cells responding to unrelated antigens such as DENV2 E or FliC. B10.D2 mice, the genetic background of the 6.5-TCR strain, were used as wild-type controls. Remarkably, 6.5-TCR mice immunized with polymeric hFliC via IP injection mounted a significant serum IgM response, comparable in magnitude to that of B10.D2 controls, and produced detectable levels of IgG3—the isotype most strongly associated with TI responses. No IgA was detected in any 6.5-TCR group, consistent with the T cell dependence of mucosal IgA class switching. In contrast, monomeric hFliC failed to induce any measurable antibody response in the T cell-restricted model.

These findings demonstrate that the hFliC filament can drive a bona fide TI antibody response, likely by engaging innate-like B1 or marginal zone B cells in the peritoneum, which are abundant in this compartment and responsive to polymeric antigens. The strong IgM and IgG3 responses in the absence of T cell help confirm that the repetitive, high-avidity display of the DENV2 E antigen on the hFliC filament is sufficient to trigger BCR crosslinking and plasma cell differentiation without T cell co-stimulation. This property makes the hFliC platform particularly valuable for vaccinating individuals with T cell deficiencies, where conventional TD vaccines are ineffective.

Furthermore, the modular nature of the hFliC scaffold allows for the development of multivalent vaccines. For example, to address the challenge of Dengue virus, which exists as four distinct serotypes (DENV1–4), separate hFliC constructs could be generated by inserting the E proteins from each serotype into the D3 locus. These four variants could then be co-expressed and co-polymerized into mixed filaments with equimolar ratios, ensuring balanced presentation of all four serotypes and potentially eliciting broadly neutralizing antibodies that protect against all forms of the virus. This strategy may overcome the serotype imbalance that has plagued existing tetravalent Dengue vaccines, which often induce dominant responses to one or two serotypes while leaving others inadequately covered.

In conclusion, the hFliC platform represents a significant advance in vaccine design, combining the structural advantages of a self-assembling polymeric scaffold with the immunostimulatory properties of flagellin and the precise antigen display of conformationally intact viral proteins. Its ability to function in T cell-deficient settings opens new avenues for protecting some of the most vulnerable members of the population against infectious diseases.

## CONCLUSIONS

The present invention establishes a groundbreaking platform for the development of T cell-independent vaccines through the rational engineering of bacterial flagellin into a versatile antigen-display scaffold. By replacing the dispensable D3 domain of *Salmonella* FliC with a conformationally intact heterologous antigen—exemplified here by the Dengue virus serotype 2 envelope protein—and preserving the protein’s ability to polymerize into long filaments, the invention achieves a high-density, repetitive, and solvent-exposed presentation of the target antigen that is ideally suited for B cell receptor crosslinking. Comprehensive biophysical, structural, and immunological analyses confirm that the resulting hybrid flagellin (hFliC) retains the secondary structural features of both parent proteins, assembles into filaments of predictable morphology, displays the inserted antigen in its native conformation on the filament surface, and induces robust humoral immune responses in vivo. Critically, the hFliC filament elicits significant IgM and IgG3 antibody production even in a T cell repertoire-restricted mouse model, demonstrating its capacity to function independently of T cell help—a feature of immense therapeutic value for immunocompromised individuals. The modular design of the scaffold further enables the creation of multivalent or universal vaccines against pathogens with multiple serotypes or variants. Thus, the invention provides a powerful, adaptable, and clinically relevant strategy for next-generation vaccine development that addresses a major unmet need in global public health.
