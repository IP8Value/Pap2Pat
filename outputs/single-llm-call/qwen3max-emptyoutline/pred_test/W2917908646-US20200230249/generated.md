# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of peptide therapeutics and, more specifically, to novel hybrid molecules comprising segments of natural peptide sequences integrated with oligourea foldamer segments. These hybrid molecules are designed to retain or enhance the biological activity of native peptides while simultaneously improving their pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties, particularly proteolytic stability, plasma half-life, and in vivo duration of action. The invention is particularly directed toward glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists for the treatment of metabolic disorders such as type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), cardiovascular diseases, and cognitive disorders. The disclosed compounds represent a new class of bioactive molecules that combine the structural and functional fidelity of α-helical peptides with the enhanced biostability conferred by oligourea foldamers, thereby overcoming key limitations associated with conventional peptide-based therapeutics.

## BACKGROUND

Peptide therapeutics have emerged as a promising class of pharmaceutical agents due to their high specificity, potency, and favorable safety profiles relative to small molecule drugs. Peptides can effectively modulate protein–protein interactions (PPIs), many of which are mediated by α-helical secondary structures and are considered “undruggable” by traditional small molecules. However, the clinical utility of native peptides is severely limited by inherent physicochemical and pharmacological drawbacks. These include poor membrane permeability, rapid enzymatic degradation by proteases in plasma and tissues, short systemic half-lives, and consequently low oral bioavailability. As a result, most peptide drugs require frequent parenteral administration, which diminishes patient compliance and limits their therapeutic potential.

To address these challenges, extensive research has been devoted to the development of peptidomimetics and stabilized peptide analogues. Strategies employed include N-terminal acetylation, C-terminal amidation, D-amino acid substitution, cyclization via disulfide bridges or side-chain cross-linking, lipidation for albumin binding, PEGylation, and fusion to large protein scaffolds such as Fc domains or albumin. While these approaches have yielded clinically approved drugs—such as exenatide, liraglutide, semaglutide, and dulaglutide for the treatment of type 2 diabetes—they often involve complex synthetic procedures, may alter receptor binding affinity, or introduce immunogenicity risks.

An alternative and increasingly explored strategy involves the use of foldamers—synthetic oligomers that adopt well-defined secondary structures analogous to those of natural peptides but are built from non-natural backbones. Among these, oligoureas have garnered significant attention due to their ability to form stable helical conformations that closely mimic the geometry of α-helices found in bioactive peptides. Oligoureas are composed of repeating ureido units derived from diamino acids and are resistant to proteolytic degradation because they lack the amide bonds targeted by endogenous peptidases. Importantly, oligourea helices can be seamlessly integrated into peptide sequences without disrupting the overall helical architecture, enabling the replacement of specific peptide segments while preserving critical side-chain orientations necessary for receptor engagement.

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) is a 31-amino acid incretin hormone that plays a central role in glucose homeostasis by stimulating insulin secretion in a glucose-dependent manner, suppressing glucagon release, slowing gastric emptying, and promoting satiety. GLP-1 exerts its effects through activation of the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R), a class B G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) expressed predominantly in pancreatic β-cells but also in the brain, heart, and gastrointestinal tract. Despite its potent therapeutic effects, native GLP-1 has an extremely short plasma half-life of 2–3 minutes due to rapid cleavage by dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) at the Ala²–Glu³ bond and further degradation by neutral endopeptidase 24.11 (NEP 24.11, also known as neprilysin) at multiple internal sites. Consequently, unmodified GLP-1 is not viable as a drug, necessitating the development of stabilized analogues.

Current GLP-1R agonists overcome this limitation through various modifications: exenatide incorporates a Gly⁸ substitution to resist DPP-4; liraglutide and semaglutide feature fatty acid chains attached to lysine residues to promote reversible albumin binding, thereby extending half-life; and semaglutide additionally includes an Aib (α-aminoisobutyric acid) residue at position 2 to block DPP-4 cleavage. Although effective, these strategies do not fully address susceptibility to NEP-mediated degradation or enable oral delivery, which remains a major goal in the field.

The integration of oligourea segments into peptide backbones offers a novel approach to enhance proteolytic resistance while maintaining or even improving receptor affinity. Prior structural studies have demonstrated that a triad of oligourea residues (Xu₃) can effectively replace four consecutive α-amino acid residues (X₄) in an α-helix, with minimal perturbation to the overall helical geometry and side-chain projection. This geometric compatibility arises because the oligourea helix exhibits a similar pitch and hydrogen-bonding pattern to the α-helix, albeit with a slightly larger diameter per turn. However, the precise spatial orientation of side chains on oligourea residues differs from that of natural amino acids, necessitating careful design to ensure that key pharmacophoric elements are preserved.

Despite the theoretical promise of peptide–oligourea hybrids, their practical application in vivo had remained unproven prior to the present invention. In particular, it was unknown whether such hybrids could retain high affinity for complex receptors like GLP-1R, exhibit improved stability in biological matrices, and demonstrate prolonged pharmacological activity in animal models. Furthermore, the impact of oligourea insertion on the conformational dynamics of full-length peptide hormones and their interaction with multi-domain GPCRs was not predictable from in vitro structural data alone. Thus, there existed a critical need for a systematic, structure-guided methodology to design, synthesize, and evaluate peptide–oligourea hybrids with optimized therapeutic profiles.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel peptide–oligourea hybrid molecules that exhibit enhanced proteolytic stability, prolonged plasma half-life, and extended in vivo duration of action while maintaining or improving receptor binding affinity and functional activity. Specifically, the invention discloses a series of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) analogues in which one or more segments of four consecutive natural amino acid residues within the α-helical domain of GLP-1 are replaced by a triad of oligourea residues. These oligourea triads are designed to mimic the three-dimensional structure and side-chain topology of the native α-helix, thereby preserving critical interactions with the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R).

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these hybrid molecules, as well as methods for their use in the treatment of diseases mediated by GLP-1R activation, including type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), cardiovascular disorders, and neurodegenerative conditions such as Alzheimer’s disease. In exemplary embodiments, the hybrids retain nanomolar potency in functional assays measuring cAMP production in GLP-1R-expressing cells, demonstrate significantly improved resistance to degradation by neutral endopeptidase 24.11 (NEP 24.11) and mouse plasma proteases, and exhibit prolonged glucose-lowering effects in murine models following intravenous or intraperitoneal administration.

A key aspect of the invention is the strategic placement of oligourea triads within regions of the GLP-1 sequence that are tolerant to substitution, as determined by alanine scanning and structural modeling. Preferred insertion sites include residues 14–17, 18–21, and 28–31, where replacement with oligourea segments yields hybrids with both high receptor affinity and enhanced stability. Notably, hybrid compounds GG2[YuEuAu]₁₄–₁₇ (compound 9), GG2[AuAuAu]₁₈–₂₁ (compound 14), and GG2[AuAuAu]₂₈–₃₁ (compound 22) demonstrate superior in vivo efficacy compared to the reference peptide GLP-1-G2, with sustained blood glucose control for up to six hours post-dosing.

In another embodiment, the invention extends the oligourea hybrid strategy to clinically approved GLP-1 analogues such as semaglutide. By replacing the C-terminal tetrapeptide (residues 28–31) of semaglutide with an oligourea triad, the resulting hybrid (compound 24) maintains equivalent in vitro potency and in vivo activity while exhibiting markedly improved stability in pancreatin degradation assays—a surrogate for gastrointestinal protease resistance. This finding suggests that the oligourea hybrid approach may facilitate the development of orally bioavailable peptide therapeutics, a long-standing challenge in the field.

The invention also provides methods for synthesizing these hybrid molecules using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques, with microwave-assisted coupling to ensure efficient incorporation of oligourea monomers. The disclosed compounds are characterized by analytical and functional assays, including LC-MS for purity and stability assessment, cAMP accumulation assays for receptor activation, and in vivo pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic studies in mice.

In summary, the present invention establishes a generalizable platform for enhancing the pharmaceutical properties of α-helical peptide therapeutics through the rational incorporation of oligourea foldamer segments. This approach overcomes the limitations of native peptides and existing peptidomimetics by combining structural fidelity with intrinsic protease resistance, thereby enabling the development of next-generation peptide drugs with improved dosing regimens, expanded therapeutic indications, and potential for non-parenteral administration.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that selective replacement of four consecutive α-amino acid residues in the α-helical domain of a bioactive peptide with a triad of oligourea residues can yield hybrid molecules that retain high receptor affinity while exhibiting significantly improved proteolytic stability and prolonged in vivo activity. This strategy leverages the structural similarity between the α-helix and the oligourea helix, which share comparable hydrogen-bonding patterns and helical pitches, allowing for seamless integration of non-natural segments into native peptide scaffolds. The invention is exemplified herein with respect to glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), but the underlying principles are broadly applicable to other α-helical peptide therapeutics targeting protein–protein interactions.

GLP-1 is a 31-residue peptide hormone that adopts a predominantly α-helical conformation between residues 9 and 31 upon binding to its receptor, GLP-1R. This helical segment makes critical contacts with both the extracellular domain and the transmembrane domain of the receptor, with key residues such as Asp⁹, Tyr¹³, Glu¹⁵, Phe²², and Ile²³ playing essential roles in receptor activation. The native peptide is rapidly degraded in vivo by DPP-4, which cleaves between Ala² and Glu³, and by NEP 24.11, which cleaves at multiple sites including Phe²²–Ile²³ and Val²⁷–Lys²⁸. To circumvent DPP-4 degradation, the reference peptide used in this study, GLP-1-G2 (compound 1), incorporates a Gly substitution at position 2, a modification known to confer resistance to DPP-4 without compromising receptor affinity.

Building upon this foundation, the inventors systematically replaced segments of four consecutive amino acids within the helical domain (residues 9–31) with oligourea triads. Structural modeling indicated that a triad of oligourea residues (Xu₃) optimally mimics the spatial arrangement of four α-amino acid residues (X₄) in an α-helix, with the terminal atoms of the hybrid segment aligning closely with those of the native sequence. However, the side-chain projection angles differ between the two backbones, necessitating careful selection of side-chain identities and attachment points on the oligourea scaffold. In particular, the inventors identified that side chains attached to the α-carbon of the oligourea monomer (denoted Xuα) can better replicate the orientation of certain native amino acid side chains, especially when the native residue occupies the first or fourth position of the replaced tetrapeptide.

Initial screening was performed using homooligourea triads composed of alanine-derived ureido units (AuAuAu), which served as a neutral scaffold to assess the impact of backbone modification independent of side-chain effects. These “Alau scan” hybrids were synthesized via solid-phase synthesis on Sieber amide resin using microwave-assisted coupling protocols, with Fmoc-protected oligourea monomers activated by PyBOP and DIEA. Following cleavage and purification by reverse-phase HPLC, the compounds were evaluated in a functional cAMP accumulation assay using βTC6 cells expressing the human GLP-1R. Many of the Alau hybrids exhibited substantial agonist activity, confirming that the oligourea backbone does not disrupt the overall helical conformation required for receptor engagement.

Subsequently, native side chains were reintroduced into the most promising oligourea triads to restore key pharmacophoric interactions. For example, the hybrid GG2[YuEuAu]₁₄–₁₇ (compound 9) incorporates tyrosine, glutamic acid, and alanine side chains at positions corresponding to Tyr¹³, Glu¹⁵, and Ala¹⁶ of native GLP-1, yielding a compound with an EC₅₀ of 0.18 nM—superior to that of the reference peptide GLP-1-G2 (0.24 nM). Similarly, GG2[AuAuAu]₁₈–₂₁ (compound 14) and GG2[AuAuAu]₂₈–₃₁ (compound 22) demonstrated EC₅₀ values of 4.1 nM and 13 nM, respectively, representing only modest reductions in potency despite the extensive backbone modification.

Crucially, these active hybrids also exhibited enhanced stability in enzymatic and plasma degradation assays. In the presence of NEP 24.11, compound 22 displayed a half-life of 4.5 hours, compared to 3.6 hours for GLP-1-G2, while in mouse plasma, its half-life exceeded 60 minutes—more than 3.8-fold longer than the reference peptide’s 16 minutes. Compounds 14 and 23 (a double-triad hybrid spanning residues 14–21) similarly showed prolonged plasma half-lives of 38 and 39 minutes, respectively. These improvements in stability directly translated into extended pharmacological effects in vivo. In blood glucose studies in normal C57BL/6J mice, compounds 14, 22, and 23 induced a rapid and sustained reduction in blood glucose levels that persisted for up to 4–6 hours post-injection, whereas GLP-1-G2 lost efficacy within 1 hour. Intraperitoneal glucose tolerance tests (IPGTTs) conducted at 2 and 4 hours after dosing confirmed that these hybrids remained fully active during the glucose challenge, unlike the native peptide, which was already inactive by 60 minutes.

Pharmacokinetic analysis further validated these findings, with compound 22 exhibiting a significantly longer systemic half-life than GLP-1-G2 following intravenous administration. This prolonged exposure is attributed primarily to enhanced resistance to proteolytic degradation, although contributions from altered distribution or clearance cannot be ruled out.

To demonstrate the versatility of the approach, the inventors applied the oligourea hybrid strategy to semaglutide, a clinically approved GLP-1 analogue featuring a C18 fatty acid chain linked to Lys²⁰ via a PEG spacer. The resulting hybrid, compound 24, replaces the C-terminal tetrapeptide (residues 28–31) of semaglutide with an AuAuAu triad. In vitro, compound 24 matched semaglutide’s potency in cAMP assays. In vivo, both compounds showed comparable glucose-lowering effects in db/db mice over 48 hours. However, compound 24 exhibited dramatically improved stability in pancreatin degradation assays (half-life of 3.2 hours vs. 0.63 hours for semaglutide), suggesting potential for oral delivery—a major advancement in peptide therapeutics.

The synthesis of all disclosed compounds follows a robust and scalable solid-phase protocol. Oligourea monomers are prepared as Fmoc-protected building blocks with side chains appropriately functionalized (e.g., as azides for later reduction to amines). Coupling is performed under microwave irradiation (70°C, 50 W) to ensure high efficiency, and azide reductions are carried out using trimethylphosphine in dioxane/water. Final cleavage from the resin employs a standard TFA cocktail, and purification is achieved by semi-preparative HPLC on C18 columns. Analytical characterization by LC-MS confirms identity and purity (>95% in all cases).

In summary, the detailed description demonstrates that the peptide–oligourea hybrid strategy is a powerful and generalizable method for enhancing the drug-like properties of α-helical peptide therapeutics. By rationally replacing protease-sensitive segments with protease-resistant oligourea mimics, the invention achieves a favorable balance of potency, stability, and duration of action—key attributes for next-generation peptide drugs.

### Examples

**Example 1: Synthesis of GLP-1-Oligourea Hybrids**

Compounds 1–24 were synthesized using solid-phase peptide synthesis on Sieber amide resin (loading 0.62 mmol/g). The synthesis was automated using a Liberty Blue™ microwave peptide synthesizer. Standard Fmoc-α-amino acids (5 equiv) were coupled with PyBOP (5 equiv) and DIEA (10 equiv) in DMF. Fmoc deprotection was performed with 20% piperidine in DMF. Oligourea monomers (3 equiv) were coupled twice under microwave irradiation (70°C, 50 W, 20 min) using DIEA (10 equiv). Azide-containing side chains were reduced by treatment with 1 M PMe₃ in THF/dioxane/water (7:3 v/v) under microwave irradiation (50°C, 50 W, 30 min). After chain assembly, the resin was washed with DMF, CH₂Cl₂, and Et₂O, then cleaved with 95% TFA, 2.5% triisopropylsilane, and 2.5% water for 2 hours. The crude product was precipitated in diethyl ether, triturated, and purified by semi-preparative HPLC on a C18 column (10 × 250 mm, 5 µm) using a water/acetonitrile gradient with 0.1% TFA. All compounds were obtained in >95% purity as confirmed by LC-MS.

**Example 2: In Vitro Functional Assay**

The agonist activity of compounds 1–23 was evaluated in βTC6 cells expressing the human GLP-1R using a cAMP HTRF assay (Cerep S.A.). Cells were seeded at 1.5 × 10⁴ cells/well in HBSS buffer with 20 mM HEPES and 500 µM IBMX. After 10 min incubation with test compounds, cells were lysed and cAMP levels were quantified by fluorescence resonance energy transfer. Data were normalized to the response of 10 nM GLP-1. EC₅₀ values were calculated by non-linear regression using GraphPad Prism. Compound 9 (GG2[YuEuAu]₁₄–₁₇) exhibited an EC₅₀ of 0.18 nM, superior to GLP-1-G2 (0.24 nM). Compounds 14 and 22 showed EC₅₀ values of 4.1 nM and 13 nM, respectively.

**Example 3: Enzymatic and Plasma Stability Studies**

Stability in NEP 24.11 was assessed by incubating compounds (304 µM) with enzyme (20 µg/mL) in HEPES buffer at 37°C. Aliquots were quenched with TFA at 1, 2, 4, 6, and 22 h and analyzed by HPLC. Half-lives were determined from peak area decay. In mouse plasma, compounds (5 µM) were incubated in plasma/PBS (1:1) at 37°C, and samples were quenched with acetonitrile at 20 and 60 min, then analyzed by LC-MS. Compound 22 showed a NEP 24.11 half-life of 4.5 h and a plasma half-life >60 min, compared to 3.6 h and 16 min for GLP-1-G2.

**Example 4: In Vivo Pharmacodynamics in Mice**

Male C57BL/6J mice (20–25 g) were injected i.v. with 5 µg of compound. Blood glucose was monitored for 6 h. Compounds 14, 22, and 23 induced a sustained glucose-lowering effect lasting 4–6 h, whereas GLP-1-G2 was active for only 1 h. IPGTTs performed 2–4 h post-dosing confirmed prolonged activity of the hybrids.

**Example 5: Semaglutide-Oligourea Hybrid (Compound 24)**

Compound 24 was synthesized by replacing residues 28–31 of semaglutide with an AuAuAu triad. It matched semaglutide’s in vitro potency (EC₅₀ ~0.1 nM) and in vivo efficacy in db/db mice. However, its half-life in pancreatin was 3.2 h vs. 0.63 h for semaglutide, indicating enhanced gastrointestinal stability.

These examples collectively demonstrate the feasibility, efficacy, and therapeutic potential of the peptide–oligourea hybrid approach disclosed herein.
