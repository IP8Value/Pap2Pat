# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of lipid metabolism and developmental biology, specifically to the biosynthesis, regulation, and physiological roles of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) in eukaryotic organisms. More particularly, the invention provides novel enzymes, regulatory pathways, and genetic components essential for the de novo synthesis of C15ISO and C17ISO mmBCFAs in the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans, and demonstrates their indispensable role in larval development and growth. The invention further encompasses methods for modulating mmBCFA levels through dietary supplementation, genetic manipulation, or pharmacological intervention, with implications for understanding conserved metabolic pathways in higher eukaryotes, including mammals. The identification of ELO-5 and ELO-6 as elongation enzymes specific to mmBCFA biosynthesis, and their regulation by the sterol regulatory element-binding protein homolog LPD-1, establishes a foundational framework for elucidating the molecular mechanisms governing mmBCFA homeostasis. Additionally, the invention reveals a critical developmental checkpoint at the first larval stage (L1) that is gated by C17ISO availability, thereby linking lipid metabolism to developmental progression. The disclosed findings have broad relevance to human health, given the evolutionary conservation of mmBCFA biosynthetic pathways and their association with metabolic disorders, neurological conditions, and cancer.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Fatty acids (FAs) are fundamental biomolecules that serve structural, energetic, and signaling roles across all domains of life. While straight-chain saturated and unsaturated fatty acids have been extensively characterized, branched-chain fatty acids—particularly monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs)—remain less understood despite their widespread presence in nature. mmBCFAs are distinguished by a single methyl group attached to the penultimate (iso) or antepenultimate (anteiso) carbon of the aliphatic chain. These molecules are abundant in bacterial membranes, where they modulate fluidity, permeability, and adaptation to environmental stressors such as temperature extremes. In eukaryotes, mmBCFAs have been detected sporadically in fungi, plants, and animals, including humans, where they are found in skin, sebaceous glands, hair, brain tissue, blood, and certain cancer cells. Notably, C21anteISO is a major covalently bound FA in mammalian hair, contributing to hydrophobicity, and C17anteISO esterified to cholesterol has been shown to activate protein biosynthesis enzymes. Moreover, C15ISO has been reported to selectively induce apoptosis in human cancer cells, suggesting potential therapeutic relevance.

Despite these observations, the biosynthetic pathways for mmBCFAs in eukaryotes were historically unknown. In bacteria, mmBCFA synthesis initiates from branched-chain amino acids (leucine, isoleucine, valine), which are transaminated to branched-chain α-keto acids and then decarboxylated by the branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase (BCKAD) complex to yield branched-chain acyl-CoA primers. These primers substitute for acetyl-CoA in fatty acid synthase (FAS)-mediated elongation. However, no eukaryotic enzymes had been definitively linked to mmBCFA production in vivo prior to the present work. The absence of such knowledge hindered efforts to understand the physiological significance of mmBCFAs in higher organisms and their potential roles in disease.

In mammals, indirect evidence supports the existence of conserved mmBCFA biosynthesis. Patients with maple syrup urine disease (MSUD), caused by mutations in BCKAD subunits, exhibit drastically reduced mmBCFA levels in hair, implying that the same initial enzymatic steps operate in humans. Nevertheless, the downstream elongation machinery remained unidentified. Furthermore, the functional consequences of mmBCFA deficiency in eukaryotes were unclear, as no genetic model had demonstrated an essential requirement for these lipids.

The nematode Caenorhabditis elegans emerged as a promising system to address these gaps. C. elegans can grow in chemically defined axenic media lacking exogenous mmBCFA precursors, suggesting de novo synthesis capability. Additionally, its well-annotated genome, genetic tractability, and transparent body facilitate molecular and phenotypic analyses. Prior studies identified eight genes in C. elegans homologous to yeast fatty acid elongation enzymes (ELO family), but their substrate specificities were largely uncharacterized. RNA interference (RNAi) screening revealed that most ELO genes affect straight-chain FA metabolism, but two—elo-5 and elo-6—were implicated in branched-chain FA production based on altered GC profiles upon knockdown. Concurrently, the worm’s ability to synthesize mmBCFAs de novo raised questions about the origin of primers and the regulatory networks governing this pathway.

Compounding the mystery was the observation that mmBCFA levels correlate with developmental stages and disease states, yet no mechanistic link between mmBCFA abundance and organismal physiology had been established. The lack of a clear phenotype associated with mmBCFA deficiency further obscured their biological importance. Thus, a critical need existed to: (1) identify the enzymatic components of eukaryotic mmBCFA biosynthesis; (2) determine whether mmBCFAs are essential for viability; (3) elucidate the regulatory mechanisms controlling their production; and (4) establish a model system to probe their physiological functions. The present invention fulfills these needs by demonstrating that C. elegans synthesizes C15ISO and C17ISO mmBCFAs via a conserved BCKAD-dependent pathway and two novel elongation enzymes, ELO-5 and ELO-6, and that these lipids are absolutely required for larval development. The invention further reveals that the SREBP homolog LPD-1 transcriptionally regulates elo-5 and elo-6, integrating mmBCFA biosynthesis into broader lipid homeostasis networks, and identifies a developmental checkpoint responsive to C17ISO levels.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive characterization of the biosynthesis, regulation, and essential physiological functions of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs), specifically C15ISO and C17ISO, in the eukaryotic model organism Caenorhabditis elegans. The invention discloses that C. elegans synthesizes mmBCFAs de novo using a pathway that begins with branched-chain amino acid-derived primers generated by the branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase (BCKAD) complex, followed by elongation mediated by two previously uncharacterized fatty acid elongation enzymes, ELO-5 and ELO-6. ELO-5 is essential for the production of both C15ISO and C17ISO, while ELO-6 specifically elongates C15ISO to C17ISO. Loss of ELO-5 function, either through RNA interference or a null mutation, results in severe developmental defects, including complete larval arrest at the first larval stage (L1) in the progeny of affected animals, demonstrating that mmBCFAs are indispensable for post-embryonic development.

The invention further establishes that the deficiency of C15ISO and C17ISO is solely responsible for the observed phenotypes, as dietary supplementation with C17ISO or C17anteISO—but not other fatty acids, including structurally similar mmBCFAs or straight-chain species—fully rescues the developmental arrest and growth defects. This specificity underscores the non-redundant and essential role of 17-carbon mmBCFAs in C. elegans physiology. The reversibility of the L1 arrest upon C17ISO supplementation indicates that the developmental block is a direct consequence of mmBCFA insufficiency rather than irreversible damage, revealing a nutrient-sensitive checkpoint at the L1 stage governed by C17ISO availability.

Moreover, the invention identifies the C. elegans homolog of sterol regulatory element-binding protein (SREBP), designated LPD-1, as a key transcriptional regulator of mmBCFA biosynthesis. LPD-1 controls the expression of elo-5 and elo-6, as evidenced by reduced promoter activity of these genes in lpd-1(RNAi) animals and concomitant decreases in C15ISO and C17ISO levels. This regulatory relationship integrates mmBCFA production into the broader lipid homeostasis network, as LPD-1 also governs the expression of enzymes involved in straight-chain fatty acid synthesis. Intriguingly, the invention reveals a reciprocal regulatory loop: mmBCFA deficiency upregulates lpd-1 expression in specific neuronal cells, suggesting a feedback mechanism to restore lipid balance.

The invention also discloses a coordinated adjustment in fatty acid composition in response to mmBCFA depletion, characterized by a negative correlation between C17ISO and vaccenic acid (C18:1 n7) and a positive correlation with linoleic acid (C18:2 n6). This cross-regulation implies compensatory mechanisms to maintain membrane properties and highlights potential shared regulatory nodes or functional interactions between mmBCFAs and polyunsaturated fatty acids.

Additionally, through microarray analysis and functional screening, the invention identifies four candidate genes—pnk-1, nhr-49, acs-1, and C27H6.2—that are differentially expressed under mmBCFA-deficient conditions and influence mmBCFA or related fatty acid metabolism. These genes, which include a pantothenate kinase, a nuclear hormone receptor, a very-long-chain fatty acid CoA ligase, and a RuvB-like DNA-binding protein, represent novel components of the mmBCFA homeostasis network and may interface with insulin signaling and other metabolic pathways.

In summary, the invention provides the first definitive evidence of de novo mmBCFA biosynthesis in a eukaryote, identifies the core enzymatic and regulatory machinery required, and establishes mmBCFAs as essential metabolites for development. The disclosed findings offer a paradigm for understanding conserved lipid metabolic pathways in higher eukaryotes and open avenues for therapeutic interventions targeting mmBCFA-related disorders.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans synthesizes monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs), specifically C15ISO and C17ISO, through a dedicated biosynthetic pathway that is essential for larval development and growth. This pathway involves the generation of branched-chain acyl-CoA primers from branched-chain amino acids via the branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase (BCKAD) complex, followed by sequential elongation by two fatty acid elongation enzymes, ELO-5 and ELO-6. The invention further reveals that this biosynthetic process is transcriptionally regulated by LPD-1, the C. elegans homolog of mammalian sterol regulatory element-binding protein (SREBP), and that mmBCFA levels are monitored during development to gate progression through a critical checkpoint at the first larval stage (L1). The detailed elucidation of this pathway, its regulation, and its physiological consequences constitutes a significant advance in lipid biology and developmental genetics.

Central to the invention is the identification of ELO-5 and ELO-6 as the key elongation enzymes for mmBCFA biosynthesis. Through systematic RNA interference (RNAi) screening of the eight C. elegans ELO family members, it was found that knockdown of elo-5 dramatically reduces both C15ISO and C17ISO levels, whereas elo-6 RNAi specifically diminishes C17ISO without affecting C15ISO. This substrate specificity indicates that ELO-5 catalyzes an early step in mmBCFA elongation, likely producing C15ISO, which is then extended to C17ISO by ELO-6. Both enzymes are expressed predominantly in the intestine—the primary site of lipid metabolism in C. elegans—with additional expression of elo-5 in head neurons and elo-6 in pharyngeal and vulval muscles, suggesting tissue-specific roles or regulatory inputs. The absolute requirement for ELO-5 function is underscored by the phenotype of elo-5(gk208), a null mutant allele that phenocopies the RNAi-induced L1 arrest, confirming that the developmental defects are due to loss of gene function rather than off-target RNAi effects.

The origin of the branched-chain primers for mmBCFA synthesis was traced to the BCKAD complex, mirroring the bacterial pathway. RNAi of Y39E4A.3, encoding the E1α subunit of BCKAD, resulted in significant reductions in C15ISO and C17ISO, establishing that C. elegans utilizes the same initial enzymatic steps as bacteria to generate mmBCFA precursors. This finding is corroborated by clinical observations in humans with maple syrup urine disease (MSUD), where BCKAD mutations lead to mmBCFA deficiency, indicating evolutionary conservation of this pathway from bacteria to mammals.

The physiological indispensability of mmBCFAs is demonstrated by the severe developmental consequences of their depletion. Progeny of parents subjected to elo-5 RNAi at the L1 stage arrest uniformly at the L1 stage, maintaining morphological integrity but failing to progress to subsequent larval stages. This arrest is completely reversible upon dietary supplementation with C17ISO or C17anteISO, but not with other fatty acids, including C15ISO, shorter or longer mmBCFAs, straight-chain saturated or unsaturated FAs, or phytanic acid. The inability of C15ISO to rescue, despite being a precursor to C17ISO, is attributed to inefficient uptake or activation of free C15ISO compared to bacterial-derived C15ISO-CoA, which can be elongated by ELO-6 even in elo-5(RNAi) animals. This distinction highlights the importance of substrate form (free FA vs. CoA ester) in metabolic utilization and suggests that dietary mmBCFAs must be activated before entering the elongation pathway.

The developmental timing of mmBCFA requirement is precisely mapped to the L1 stage. Staged fatty acid analysis reveals that C17ISO levels are low in L1 larvae and increase progressively through later stages to peak in gravid adults. Since neither elo-5 nor elo-6 is significantly expressed in embryos or L1, C17ISO is likely synthesized during oogenesis and deposited into eggs. Consequently, when parental elo-5 is knocked down at L1, insufficient C17ISO is provisioned to the next generation, triggering L1 arrest. If RNAi is initiated at later parental stages (L2–L4), enough C17ISO is maternally supplied to bypass L1 arrest, but the resulting animals exhibit gonadal degeneration and reduced fecundity, indicating ongoing requirements for mmBCFAs in adult reproductive tissues.

Regulation of mmBCFA biosynthesis is mediated by LPD-1, the sole C. elegans SREBP homolog. LPD-1 is known to regulate straight-chain FA synthesis by activating genes like acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC) and fatty acid synthase (FAS). The invention extends this role to mmBCFAs, showing that lpd-1(RNAi) reduces C15ISO and C17ISO levels and downregulates elo-5 and elo-6 promoter activity. This positions LPD-1 as a master integrator of both conventional and branched-chain FA metabolism. Intriguingly, mmBCFA deficiency reciprocally upregulates lpd-1 expression in amphid neurons, suggesting a cell-type-specific feedback loop to restore lipid homeostasis. This reciprocal regulation parallels the inhibition of mammalian SREBP-1c by polyunsaturated FAs, indicating a conserved mechanism for lipid-mediated transcriptional control.

Cross-regulation between mmBCFAs and other fatty acids is evident in the compensatory elevation of vaccenic acid (C18:1 n7) upon mmBCFA depletion, maintaining a constant saturation index and thus membrane fluidity. Conversely, C17ISO levels positively correlate with linoleic acid (C18:2 n6), hinting at shared biosynthetic regulation or functional synergy. These correlations suggest that the lipidome is dynamically balanced, with mmBCFAs acting as key nodes in this network.

Finally, the invention identifies additional genes involved in mmBCFA homeostasis through microarray profiling of elo-5(RNAi) animals. Four candidates—pnk-1, nhr-49, acs-1, and C27H6.2—are differentially expressed and functionally validated. pnk-1 and acs-1 are upregulated under mmBCFA deficiency and required for normal mmBCFA levels, while C27H6.2 knockdown increases monounsaturated FAs, potentially compensating for mmBCFA loss. These genes link mmBCFA metabolism to coenzyme A biosynthesis (pnk-1), nuclear hormone signaling (nhr-49), fatty acid activation (acs-1), and chromatin remodeling (C27H6.2), revealing a complex regulatory landscape that may interface with insulin signaling and stress responses.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 2

To identify the enzymatic components responsible for monomethyl branched-chain fatty acid (mmBCFA) biosynthesis in Caenorhabditis elegans, we conducted a systematic RNA interference (RNAi) screen targeting the eight genes in the worm genome that are homologous to yeast long-chain fatty acid elongation enzymes (ELO family). Wild-type N2 Bristol animals were fed Escherichia coli HT115 expressing double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) corresponding to each elo gene, and the fatty acid composition of the resulting progeny was analyzed by gas chromatography (GC). RNAi of four genes—elo-3, elo-4, elo-7, and elo-8—produced no discernible changes in fatty acid profiles, consistent with their dispensability for major lipid metabolic pathways under standard laboratory conditions. Suppression of elo-1 and elo-2, as previously reported, impaired the elongation of straight-chain saturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids, validating the efficacy of our RNAi approach. Strikingly, RNAi of elo-5 (F41H10.7) and elo-6 (F41H10.8) specifically reduced the levels of mmBCFAs. GC analysis revealed that elo-5(RNAi) animals exhibited a dramatic decrease in both C15ISO and C17ISO, whereas elo-6(RNAi) animals showed a significant reduction only in C17ISO, with C15ISO levels remaining near normal. These results suggested that ELO-5 functions upstream in the mmBCFA pathway, likely catalyzing the formation of C15ISO, while ELO-6 acts downstream to elongate C15ISO to C17ISO. To confirm the tissue-specific expression patterns of these genes, we generated transcriptional reporter constructs by fusing the promoter regions of elo-5 and elo-6 to green fluorescent protein (GFP). Microscopic examination of transgenic strains carrying elo-5Prom::GFP and elo-6Prom::GFP revealed strong expression in the intestinal cells of both constructs, consistent with the gut’s role as the primary site of lipid metabolism. Additionally, elo-5Prom::GFP fluorescence was detected in a subset of unidentified head neurons, while elo-6Prom::GFP was expressed in pharyngeal muscle, vulval muscle, and neurons, indicating potential non-intestinal roles or regulatory inputs. These expression data, combined with the biochemical phenotypes, established ELO-5 and ELO-6 as the first eukaryotic enzymes definitively linked to long-chain mmBCFA biosynthesis in vivo.

#### Example 3

To determine the origin of the branched-chain primers used in mmBCFA biosynthesis, we investigated the role of the branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase (BCKAD) complex in C. elegans. In bacteria, BCKAD converts branched-chain α-keto acids—derived from leucine, isoleucine, and valine—into branched-chain acyl-CoA primers that initiate fatty acid synthesis. We identified Y39E4A.3 as a predicted C. elegans homolog of the E1α subunit of BCKAD, based on significant sequence similarity to both Bacillus subtilis and Homo sapiens BCKAD proteins. RNAi-mediated knockdown of Y39E4A.3 in wild-type animals resulted in a substantial reduction in both C15ISO and C17ISO levels, as quantified by GC analysis. Similarly, RNAi of T05H10.6, encoding a predicted component of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex that shares structural and functional homology with BCKAD, also decreased mmBCFA production. These findings demonstrate that C. elegans employs the same initial enzymatic steps as bacteria to generate mmBCFA precursors, utilizing the BCKAD complex to produce branched-chain acyl-CoA primers from branched-chain amino acids. This conservation is further supported by clinical observations in humans with maple syrup urine disease (MSUD), a disorder caused by inherited mutations in BCKAD genes, where patients exhibit markedly reduced mmBCFA levels in hair fibers. The ability of C. elegans to grow in chemically defined axenic medium (CbMM), which lacks exogenous mmBCFA precursors, confirms that the worm can synthesize these lipids de novo, relying on endogenous BCKAD activity to supply the necessary primers for ELO-5 and ELO-6-mediated elongation.

#### Example 4

To assess the physiological consequences of mmBCFA deficiency, we examined the phenotypic effects of elo-5 and elo-6 RNAi. Animals subjected to elo-6(RNAi) displayed no obvious morphological or growth defects, consistent with the partial redundancy or non-essential nature of C17ISO under standard conditions. In contrast, elo-5(RNAi) induced severe developmental abnormalities. When wild-type eggs were placed on elo-5(RNAi) plates, the resulting adults developed normally until the second day of adulthood, at which point they exhibited an egg-laying defect. The progeny of these animals hatched on time but arrested uniformly at the first larval stage (L1), maintaining morphological integrity for up to three to four days without progressing to L2. This L1 arrest was only observed when parental RNAi was initiated at the L1 stage; if RNAi began at later larval stages (L2–L4), the progeny bypassed L1 arrest but developed progressive sickness, including gonadal degeneration characterized by vacuolization and nuclear disorganization, and severely reduced fecundity. To confirm that these phenotypes were due to loss of elo-5 function, we obtained a putative null mutant, elo-5(gk208), which carries a 245-bp deletion eliminating the predicted first exon. Homozygous elo-5(gk208) animals phenocopied the L1 arrest observed in elo-5(RNAi) progeny, providing genetic validation that ELO-5 is essential for larval development. The stage-specific severity of the phenotype—complete L1 arrest versus progressive adult sickness—correlates with the timing of maternal C17ISO provisioning, as C17ISO levels are low in L1 and increase through later stages, suggesting that sufficient maternal deposition can transiently compensate for zygotic deficiency.

#### Example 5

To test whether the developmental defects caused by elo-5(RNAi) were directly attributable to mmBCFA deficiency, we performed dietary rescue experiments. Synchronized young adult worms were placed on elo-5(RNAi) plates supplemented with various fatty acids, and their progeny were scored for developmental progression. Remarkably, supplementation with C17ISO or C17anteISO fully rescued the L1 arrest phenotype in 52 of 60 and 58 of 60 plates, respectively. Partial rescue was observed with C15ISO and C15anteISO (23 of 38 and 20 of 28 plates, respectively), but no rescue occurred with any other fatty acids tested, including straight-chain saturated (C16:0, C17:0), monounsaturated (C16:1 n7, C18:1 n7), polyunsaturated (C18:3 n6), shorter or longer mmBCFAs (C13ISO, C18ISO, C19ISO), or phytanic acid (0 of 30 plates for each). GC analysis of rescued animals confirmed that only C17ISO and C17anteISO were significantly incorporated into worm lipids, whereas C15ISO supplementation did not lead to detectable elongation to C17ISO. To investigate this discrepancy, we identified a bacterial contaminant, Stenotrophomonas maltophilia, which naturally produces high levels of C15ISO and C15anteISO but no 17-carbon mmBCFAs. When elo-5(RNAi) animals were fed S. maltophilia, they not only accumulated bacterial C15ISO and C15anteISO but also efficiently elongated them to C17ISO and C17anteISO, demonstrating that ELO-6 function remains intact in elo-5(RNAi) animals. This suggests that free C15ISO added to plates cannot be effectively activated or utilized by ELO-6, whereas bacterial-derived C15ISO-CoA (or a metabolically accessible form) can enter the elongation pathway. These results conclusively demonstrate that the elo-5(RNAi) phenotypes are solely due to C17ISO deficiency and that dietary 17-carbon mmBCFAs are both necessary and sufficient to bypass the biochemical defect.

#### Example 6

To explore the reversibility of the L1 arrest and the developmental dynamics of mmBCFA requirement, we staged wild-type animals and analyzed their fatty acid composition across life stages. GC profiling revealed that C17ISO levels are lowest in L1 larvae and increase gradually through L2, L3, L4, and young adulthood, peaking in gravid adults. Consistent with this, neither elo-5 nor elo-6 promoter activity was detectable in eggs or L1 by GFP reporter analysis or in situ hybridization, indicating that C17ISO is synthesized during oogenesis and maternally deposited into embryos. When parental elo-5 is knocked down at L1, insufficient C17ISO is provisioned to the next generation, causing L1 arrest. However, if arrested L1 larvae are transferred to plates supplemented with C17ISO or C17anteISO, approximately 50% of two-day-arrested and 1% of four-day-arrested larvae resume development and proliferate, demonstrating that the arrest is reversible and not due to irreversible damage. This establishes a nutrient-sensitive checkpoint at the L1 stage that monitors C17ISO levels to gate developmental progression. The sharp decline in rescue efficiency with prolonged arrest suggests that extended mmBCFA deprivation eventually leads to secondary defects that compromise viability. The model emerging from these data posits that C17ISO acts as a permissive signal for L1-to-L2 transition, with its threshold concentration serving as a proxy for metabolic readiness or nutritional status.

#### Example 7

To investigate potential cross-regulation between mmBCFAs and other fatty acids, we performed correlation analysis on GC data from staged wild-type animals and mixed populations. Among the numerous fatty acids detected, only two showed significant correlations with C17ISO levels: linoleic acid (C18:2 n6) and vaccenic acid (C18:1 n7). C17ISO exhibited a strong positive correlation with C18:2 n6 (r = 0.82, p < 0.001) and a negative correlation with C18:1 n7 (r = -0.76, p < 0.001). In elo-5(RNAi) animals, the loss of C15ISO and C17ISO was accompanied by a marked increase in C18:1 n7, consistent with the negative correlation. Calculation of the saturation index (SI = [saturated FA]/[mmBCFA + monounsaturated FA]) revealed no significant difference between wild-type and elo-5(RNAi) worms (SI = 0.325 ± 0.011 vs. 0.320 ± 0.032), indicating that the compensatory rise in monounsaturated FAs maintains membrane fluidity despite mmBCFA depletion. This adaptive response mirrors observations in bacteria, where monounsaturated FAs increase in response to reduced branched-chain FAs to preserve membrane properties. The positive correlation with C18:2 n6 may reflect shared regulatory mechanisms, as both mmBCFAs and polyunsaturated FAs are influenced by LPD-1, or a functional synergy in signaling or structural roles. When elo-5(RNAi) animals were fed S. maltophilia, mmBCFAs became dominant components in the 16–18-carbon FA range, displacing straight-chain species without toxicity, suggesting that mmBCFAs can effectively substitute for conventional FAs in membrane and metabolic functions.

#### Example 8

To identify transcriptional regulators of mmBCFA biosynthesis, we examined the role of LPD-1, the C. elegans homolog of mammalian sterol regulatory element-binding protein (SREBP). LPD-1 is known to regulate straight-chain FA synthesis by activating genes like ACC and FAS. RNAi of lpd-1 in wild-type animals resulted in significant reductions in C15ISO and C17ISO, as well as C18:2 n6, while C16:0 levels increased. This FA profile is consistent with downregulation of elo-5, elo-6 (mmBCFA elongation), elo-2 (straight-chain elongation), and Δ12-desaturase (C18:2 n6 synthesis). To test whether elo-5 and elo-6 are direct targets of LPD-1, we compared GFP expression in elo-5Prom::GFP and elo-6Prom::GFP reporter strains grown on control versus lpd-1(RNAi) plates. Both reporters showed markedly reduced fluorescence in lpd-1(RNAi) animals, indicating transcriptional downregulation. In contrast, a control reporter (kqt-1Prom::GFP) expressed in similar tissues showed no change. Disruption of FAS, a known LPD-1 target, did not alter FA composition, ruling out indirect effects via general FA synthesis impairment. These data establish that LPD-1 directly or indirectly promotes elo-5 and elo-6 expression, integrating mmBCFA biosynthesis into the global lipid regulatory network. Intriguingly, microarray analysis revealed a 1.68-fold upregulation of lpd-1 in elo-5(RNAi) animals, and lpd-1Prom::GFP reporter strains showed enhanced fluorescence specifically in amphid neurons under mmBCFA-deficient conditions, suggesting a cell-type-specific feedback loop to restore lipid homeostasis.

#### Example 9

To uncover additional genes involved in mmBCFA homeostasis, we performed DNA microarray analysis comparing gene expression in elo-5(RNAi) and wild-type animals. To filter out changes unrelated to mmBCFA deficiency, we excluded genes differentially expressed in spt-1(RNAi) animals, which exhibit similar gonadal defects but normal mmBCFA levels. This stringent comparison yielded 25 candidate genes, four of which—pnk-1, nhr-49, acs-1, and C27H6.2—were functionally validated by RNAi and GC analysis. RNAi of pnk-1 (pantothenate kinase), nhr-49 (nuclear hormone receptor), and acs-1 (very-long-chain FA CoA ligase) significantly reduced mmBCFA levels, while C27H6.2 (RuvB-like DNA-binding protein) knockdown increased C18:1 n7, consistent with its role in compensatory monounsaturation. Reporter strains for pnk-1 and acs-1 showed enhanced GFP expression in elo-5(RNAi) animals, confirming upregulation under mmBCFA deficiency. Notably, pnk-1Prom::GFP was downregulated in lpd-1(RNAi) animals, linking it to LPD-1 regulation. These genes connect mmBCFA metabolism to coenzyme A biosynthesis (pnk-1), transcriptional regulation of fat storage (nhr-49), fatty acid activation (acs-1), and chromatin remodeling (C27H6.2). Their prior association with the daf-2/daf-16 insulin signaling pathway—which governs metabolism, stress resistance, and developmental diapause—suggests that mmBCFA levels may interface with nutrient-sensing pathways to modulate growth and longevity. The L1 arrest phenotype of elo-5(RNAi) animals strikingly resembles the starvation-induced L1 diapause, raising the possibility that mmBCFAs act as metabolic signals in insulin-mediated developmental decisions.
