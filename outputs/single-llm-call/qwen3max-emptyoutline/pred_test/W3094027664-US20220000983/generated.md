## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic peptides and their use in the treatment of pulmonary disorders. More specifically, the invention provides novel growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) receptor antagonists, compositions comprising such antagonists, and methods for using these antagonists to treat conditions characterized by lung inflammation, fibrosis, granulomatous disease, and cancer. The invention further encompasses the identification of GHRH and its receptor as key mediators in the pathophysiology of lung diseases, thereby establishing a new therapeutic target for intervention in pulmonary disorders that involve dysregulated inflammation, aberrant tissue remodeling, and neoplastic proliferation. The GHRH receptor antagonists of the invention are synthetic peptide analogs designed to selectively inhibit the GHRH receptor, including both the pituitary-type receptor and its splice variant SV1, which are expressed in lung tissues and contribute to disease progression through autocrine and paracrine signaling mechanisms. The invention is particularly directed to the use of Miami-series (MIA) GHRH antagonists, such as MIA-602, MIA-604, MIA-610, and MIA-690, which exhibit high binding affinity for the GHRH receptor and potent biological activity in modulating intracellular signaling pathways relevant to lung homeostasis and disease. These antagonists demonstrate efficacy in preclinical models of bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis, sarcoid-like granulomatous inflammation, and various forms of lung cancer, including non-small cell lung carcinoma, small cell lung cancer, and mesothelioma. The invention thus provides a novel class of therapeutic agents for the treatment of chronic and acute lung diseases that are currently associated with significant morbidity and limited therapeutic options.

## BACKGROUND

Growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) is a hypothalamic neuropeptide traditionally recognized for its endocrine role in stimulating the synthesis and secretion of growth hormone (GH) from the anterior pituitary gland. This canonical pathway involves the activation of the GHRH receptor (GHRH-R), a G-protein coupled receptor (GPCR) of the secretin family, leading to the downstream production of insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) in the liver and the systemic effects of the GH/IGF-1 axis on growth, metabolism, and tissue repair. However, emerging evidence has revealed that GHRH and its receptor are also expressed in a wide array of extra-pituitary tissues, including the lungs, where they function in a paracrine or autocrine manner independent of the classical endocrine axis. In the lung, GHRH and GHRH-R are expressed in multiple cell types, including alveolar type II cells, club cells, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and immune cells such as lymphocytes and dendritic cells. Furthermore, a truncated but functional splice variant of the GHRH receptor, known as SV1, has been identified in peripheral tissues, including the lung, and is implicated in ligand-independent signaling that contributes to cellular proliferation and survival.

The physiological significance of GHRH signaling in the lung has become increasingly apparent through studies of pathological conditions. For instance, patients with acromegaly, a condition caused by excess GH due to either pituitary adenomas or ectopic GHRH production from tumors (often of pancreatic or bronchial origin), exhibit enlargement of visceral organs, including the lungs, indicating a direct role for the GHRH–GH–IGF-1 axis in pulmonary growth. Beyond this endocrine influence, local GHRH signaling in the lung has been linked to the regulation of inflammation, fibrosis, and tumorigenesis. In particular, GHRH-R is overexpressed in various lung cancers, including small cell lung carcinoma and carcinoid tumors, which themselves can produce and secrete GHRH, thereby creating an autocrine loop that promotes tumor growth and survival. Similarly, in models of pulmonary fibrosis, such as that induced by bleomycin, GHRH signaling appears to drive fibroblast proliferation, myofibroblast differentiation, and extracellular matrix deposition through the activation of key intracellular pathways, including PI3K/Akt, ERK, and STAT3.

Despite these insights, the development of targeted therapies to modulate GHRH signaling in the lung has been limited. Traditional approaches to inhibiting the GH/IGF-1 axis, such as somatostatin analogs, primarily act on the pituitary level and do not effectively address the local, extra-pituitary actions of GHRH. Moreover, the existence of the SV1 splice variant, which may signal independently of ligand binding, presents additional challenges for therapeutic intervention. To address these limitations, synthetic peptide analogs of GHRH have been developed, including both agonists and antagonists, which serve as mechanistic probes to dissect the roles of GHRH in various physiological and pathological contexts. Among these, the Miami-series (MIA) GHRH receptor antagonists, such as MIA-602, represent a class of highly potent and selective inhibitors that bind to both the full-length GHRH-R and the SV1 variant with high affinity. These antagonists have demonstrated significant anti-proliferative effects in a variety of human cancer cell lines and have shown promise in preclinical models of tumor growth inhibition. However, their potential utility in non-oncological pulmonary disorders, such as fibrosis and granulomatous inflammation, has only recently begun to be explored.

Pulmonary fibrosis, particularly idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), is a progressive and often fatal interstitial lung disease characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix, fibroblast proliferation, and architectural distortion of the lung parenchyma. Current therapeutic options for IPF, such as pirfenidone and nintedanib, offer only modest slowing of disease progression and are associated with significant side effects. Similarly, sarcoidosis is a multisystem granulomatous disorder of unknown etiology that frequently affects the lungs and is driven by dysregulated T-cell and macrophage activation, leading to the formation of non-caseating granulomas. Treatment typically involves immunosuppressive agents like corticosteroids, which carry substantial risks of long-term toxicity. There remains a critical unmet need for novel therapeutic strategies that target the underlying molecular mechanisms driving these diseases.

The discovery that GHRH-R antagonists can modulate key signaling pathways involved in inflammation, fibrosis, and cancer—such as Akt, ERK, STAT3, NF-κB, and AMPK—provides a compelling rationale for their investigation in pulmonary disorders. Preliminary data indicate that MIA-602 reduces inflammation and fibrosis in a bleomycin-induced mouse model of lung injury, decreases pro-inflammatory cytokine production in sarcoid-like granulomas, and inhibits the growth of lung cancer cells in vitro and in vivo. These findings suggest that GHRH-R antagonists may represent a versatile class of therapeutics with broad applicability across a spectrum of lung diseases. However, prior to the present invention, there was no comprehensive disclosure of the specific structural features, pharmacological properties, or therapeutic applications of these antagonists in the context of pulmonary inflammation, fibrosis, or granulomatous disease. The present invention therefore fills a critical gap in the art by providing detailed compositions, methods of synthesis, and validated biological data supporting the use of GHRH-R antagonists for the treatment of these conditions.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a GHRH receptor antagonist of the invention and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent. These compositions may be formulated for administration via various routes, including subcutaneous, intravenous, intraperitoneal, inhalation, or oral delivery, depending on the clinical indication and desired pharmacokinetic profile. The compositions are particularly suited for the treatment of pulmonary fibrosis, including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and bleomycin-induced fibrosis; granulomatous lung diseases such as sarcoidosis; and lung cancers, including non-small cell lung carcinoma, small cell lung carcinoma, and mesothelioma.

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for treating a pulmonary disorder in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of a GHRH receptor antagonist of the invention. The method may further comprise co-administration with a second therapeutic agent, such as an immune checkpoint inhibitor (e.g., anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 antibody), a chemotherapeutic agent (e.g., cisplatin), or an antifibrotic drug, to achieve synergistic therapeutic effects.

The invention also encompasses the use of GHRH receptor antagonists to modulate specific intracellular signaling pathways in lung cells, including the inhibition of PI3K/Akt, ERK, STAT3, and NF-κB pathways, and the activation of AMPK and p53 pathways. These molecular effects underlie the observed anti-inflammatory, antifibrotic, and antitumor activities of the antagonists. Additionally, the invention includes methods for enhancing mitochondrial function and promoting apoptosis in lung fibroblasts and cancer cells, thereby limiting pathological tissue remodeling and tumor progression.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides kits for the treatment of pulmonary disorders, comprising a GHRH receptor antagonist of the invention in a suitable container, along with instructions for use. The kits may also include additional components such as syringes, vials, or inhalation devices, depending on the intended route of administration.

Overall, the present invention establishes GHRH receptor antagonism as a novel and effective therapeutic strategy for a range of debilitating pulmonary diseases, offering significant advantages over existing treatments in terms of mechanism of action, specificity, and potential for combination therapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

“GHRH receptor antagonist” refers to a compound that binds to the growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor (GHRH-R), including both the full-length pituitary-type receptor and its splice variant SV1, and inhibits the biological activity of GHRH. Such antagonists may be peptides, peptidomimetics, or small molecules, but in the context of the present invention, they are specifically synthetic peptide analogs derived from the native GHRH sequence with modifications that confer antagonistic properties.

“MIA-series antagonist” refers to a class of GHRH receptor antagonists developed at the University of Miami, characterized by specific amino acid substitutions that enhance binding affinity, metabolic stability, and biological activity. Exemplary members include MIA-602, MIA-604, MIA-610, and MIA-690.

“Pulmonary fibrosis” encompasses any pathological condition of the lung characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix, fibroblast proliferation, and architectural distortion of the lung parenchyma, including but not limited to idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), bleomycin-induced fibrosis, and radiation-induced fibrosis.

“Granulomatous lung disease” refers to inflammatory conditions of the lung characterized by the formation of granulomas, which are organized aggregates of immune cells, primarily macrophages and T-lymphocytes. Sarcoidosis is a prototypical example.

“Lung cancer” includes all malignant neoplasms originating in the lung, including non-small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC), small cell lung carcinoma (SCLC), carcinoid tumors, and mesothelioma.

“Therapeutically effective amount” means an amount of a GHRH receptor antagonist that, when administered to a subject, is sufficient to produce a measurable therapeutic effect, such as reduction in inflammation, fibrosis, or tumor burden, without causing unacceptable toxicity.

“Pharmaceutically acceptable carrier” refers to any substance that is compatible with the active ingredient and suitable for administration to a subject, including but not limited to water, saline, buffers, lipids, polymers, and other excipients commonly used in pharmaceutical formulations.

### Compositions

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a GHRH receptor antagonist of the invention and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The antagonist is preferably a peptide analog of the Miami series, such as MIA-602, which has the amino acid sequence PhAc–Ada–Tyr–DArg–Asp–Ala–Ile–5FPhe–Thr–Ala–Har–Tyr(Me)–His–Orn–Val–Leu–Abu–Gln–Leu–Ser–Ala–His–Orn–Leu–Leu–Gln–Asp–Ile–Nle–D–Arg–Ha–NH2. This sequence incorporates several key modifications relative to native GHRH(1-29)NH2, including D-Arg at position 2, pentafluoro-Phe at position 6, Har (homoarginine) at position 9, Tyr(Me) (methyltyrosine) at position 10, Abu (α-aminobutyric acid) at position 15, Nle (norleucine) at position 27, and Orn (ornithine) at positions 12 and 21. These substitutions collectively enhance the peptide’s resistance to enzymatic degradation, increase its binding affinity for the GHRH receptor, and confer potent antagonistic activity.

The composition may be formulated in a variety of dosage forms suitable for systemic or local administration. For systemic delivery, the composition may be prepared as a sterile solution or suspension for subcutaneous, intravenous, or intraperitoneal injection. The concentration of the antagonist in such formulations typically ranges from 0.1 mg/mL to 10 mg/mL, with a preferred dose of 1–5 μg per day in animal models, which may be scaled to human equivalents based on body surface area. For localized pulmonary delivery, the composition may be formulated as an aerosol, dry powder, or nebulized solution for inhalation, allowing for direct targeting of lung tissue and minimizing systemic exposure. Inhalation formulations may include propellants, surfactants, and stabilizers to ensure efficient delivery and stability of the peptide.

The composition may also include additional therapeutic agents to enhance efficacy. For example, in the treatment of lung cancer, the GHRH receptor antagonist may be combined with an immune checkpoint inhibitor such as pembrolizumab (anti-PD-1) or atezolizumab (anti-PD-L1), given that GHRH-R antagonism has been shown to suppress STAT3 signaling and reduce PD-L1 expression, thereby potentially overcoming tumor immune evasion. In the context of fibrosis, combination with existing antifibrotic agents like nintedanib may provide additive or synergistic benefits by targeting complementary pathways.

The stability of the peptide in the composition may be enhanced by lyophilization, the addition of antioxidants (e.g., ascorbic acid), or the use of protease inhibitors. The pH of the formulation is typically maintained between 4.0 and 7.0 to preserve peptide integrity and minimize aggregation. The composition is preferably stored at 2–8°C and protected from light to prevent degradation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Synthesis of GHRH Antagonists

The GHRH receptor antagonists of the Miami series, including MIA-602, were synthesized using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) techniques on a Rink amide resin. The synthesis was performed using Fmoc (9-fluorenylmethoxycarbonyl) chemistry with HBTU (O-(benzotriazol-1-yl)-N,N,N',N'-tetramethyluronium hexafluorophosphate) as the coupling reagent and DIPEA (N,N-diisopropylethylamine) as the base. The amino acid sequence for MIA-602 is PhAc–Ada–Tyr–DArg–Asp–Ala–Ile–5FPhe–Thr–Ala–Har–Tyr(Me)–His–Orn–Val–Leu–Abu–Gln–Leu–Ser–Ala–His–Orn–Leu–Leu–Gln–Asp–Ile–Nle–D–Arg–Ha–NH2, where PhAc denotes phenylacetyl, Ada is adipic acid, 5FPhe is pentafluorophenylalanine, Har is homoarginine, Tyr(Me) is O-methyltyrosine, Abu is α-aminobutyric acid, Nle is norleucine, and Ha is hydroxyaminoadipic acid.

The synthesis began with the attachment of Ha to the Rink amide resin, followed by sequential coupling of the remaining amino acids from the C-terminus to the N-terminus. Special care was taken to incorporate the non-natural and D-amino acids at the specified positions. After completion of the linear chain assembly, the N-terminal phenylacetyl group was introduced by coupling phenylacetic acid to the free N-terminus. The peptide was then cleaved from the resin using a cocktail of trifluoroacetic acid (TFA), water, and triisopropylsilane (95:2.5:2.5) for 2 hours at room temperature. The crude peptide was precipitated in cold diethyl ether, centrifuged, and washed repeatedly to remove residual scavengers and TFA.

The crude product was purified by preparative reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) using a C18 column and a gradient of acetonitrile in 0.1% TFA. The main peak corresponding to the desired product was collected, lyophilized, and analyzed by analytical HPLC and mass spectrometry to confirm identity and purity. The final product exhibited a purity of greater than 98% as determined by HPLC and a molecular weight consistent with the theoretical mass calculated for the sequence. The same general procedure was used to synthesize analogs MIA-604, MIA-610, and MIA-690, with appropriate modifications to the amino acid sequence as required.

### Example 2: Evaluation of Inhibitory Effects of GHRH Antagonists In Vitro

The inhibitory effects of MIA-602 and related antagonists on cellular proliferation were evaluated in a panel of human cancer cell lines, including A549 (non-small cell lung carcinoma), H1299 (non-small cell lung carcinoma), and MSTO-211H (mesothelioma). Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5,000 cells per well and allowed to adhere overnight. The following day, the culture medium was replaced with fresh medium containing varying concentrations (0.1–10 μM) of MIA-602, MIA-690, or vehicle control (DMSO). After 72 hours of incubation, cell viability was assessed using the MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) assay. The absorbance was measured at 570 nm, and the percentage of viable cells relative to the control was calculated.

Both MIA-602 and MIA-690 exhibited dose-dependent inhibition of proliferation in all tested cell lines, with IC50 values ranging from 1 to 5 μM. At a concentration of 5 μM, MIA-602 reduced proliferation by 40–80% across the cell lines. To investigate the mechanism of growth inhibition, Western blot analysis was performed on A549 cells treated with 5 μM MIA-602 for 24 hours. The results showed significant downregulation of phosphorylated Akt (p-Akt) and phosphorylated mTOR (p-mTOR), along with upregulation of cleaved caspase-3, indicating induction of apoptosis. Additionally, treatment with MIA-602 led to decreased expression of matrix metalloproteinases MMP-2 and MMP-9, which are involved in tumor invasion and metastasis. These findings demonstrate that GHRH receptor antagonists exert potent antiproliferative and pro-apoptotic effects in lung cancer cells through modulation of key survival and growth pathways.

### Example 3: Evaluation of Tumor Inhibitory Activity of GHRH Antagonists In Vivo

To assess the antitumor efficacy of MIA-602 in vivo, human A549 lung cancer cells were subcutaneously implanted into the flanks of nude mice. When tumors reached a volume of approximately 100 mm³, mice were randomized into treatment groups (n=8 per group) and received daily subcutaneous injections of MIA-602 (1 μg/day or 5 μg/day) or vehicle control (saline) for 21 days. Tumor dimensions were measured twice weekly using calipers, and tumor volume was calculated as (length × width²)/2.

Mice treated with 5 μg/day of MIA-602 exhibited a significant reduction in tumor growth compared to the control group, with a mean tumor volume of 350 mm³ at the end of the study versus 850 mm³ in controls (p < 0.01). The 1 μg/day dose also showed a moderate but significant inhibitory effect. No significant changes in body weight or overt signs of toxicity were observed in any treatment group, indicating a favorable safety profile. Immunohistochemical analysis of excised tumors revealed decreased Ki-67 staining (a marker of proliferation) and increased TUNEL staining (a marker of apoptosis) in the MIA-602-treated group, consistent with the in vitro findings. These results confirm that GHRH receptor antagonists effectively suppress lung tumor growth in vivo and support their potential for clinical development in oncology.

### Example 4: Binding Affinities of AVR GHRH Antagonists

The binding affinities of MIA-series antagonists for the human GHRH receptor were determined using a competitive radioligand binding assay. Membranes prepared from CHO cells stably expressing the human GHRH receptor were incubated with ¹²⁵I-labeled GHRH(1-29)NH2 in the presence of increasing concentrations of unlabeled competitor peptides (MIA-602, MIA-604, MIA-610, MIA-690, or native GHRH(1-29)NH2). After incubation, bound radioactivity was separated by filtration and quantified using a gamma counter. The IC50 values were calculated from displacement curves and converted to Ki values using the Cheng-Prusoff equation.

All MIA-series antagonists exhibited significantly higher binding affinity for the GHRH receptor compared to native GHRH. MIA-602 had a Ki of 0.8 nM, while MIA-690 showed the highest affinity with a Ki of 0.3 nM. In contrast, native GHRH(1-29)NH2 had a Ki of 5.2 nM. These results confirm that the structural modifications in the MIA-series peptides greatly enhance receptor binding, which likely contributes to their potent biological activity. The high affinity for both the full-length receptor and the SV1 splice variant was further verified using cells expressing each isoform individually, demonstrating that the antagonists are effective against both forms.

### Example 5: Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone Receptor Antagonist Modulates Lung Inflammation and Fibrosis Due to Bleomycin

To evaluate the effects of MIA-602 on pulmonary inflammation and fibrosis, a bleomycin-induced lung injury model was established in C57BL/6 mice. Mice were administered a single intratracheal instillation of bleomycin (1.5 U/kg) or saline (control). Starting 24 hours after bleomycin administration, mice received daily subcutaneous injections of MIA-602 (5 μg/day) or vehicle for 28 days. At the end of the study, lungs were harvested for histopathological, biochemical, and molecular analyses.

Histopathological evaluation using H&E and Masson’s trichrome staining showed that bleomycin-treated mice developed significant peribronchial and interstitial inflammation and collagen deposition, whereas MIA-602-treated mice exhibited markedly reduced inflammation and fibrosis. Hydroxyproline content, a quantitative measure of collagen, was increased by 2.5-fold in bleomycin-only mice but remained at baseline levels in the MIA-602 group. RNA sequencing of lung tissue revealed that MIA-602 downregulated genes associated with T-cell activation (e.g., CD3E, CD4, CD8A) and extracellular matrix production, while upregulating genes involved in antioxidant defense and mitochondrial function. In vitro, MIA-602 reduced nitrotyrosine formation in lung fibroblasts exposed to bleomycin, indicating suppression of oxidative and nitrosative stress. Furthermore, MIA-602 promoted fibroblast apoptosis by enhancing mitochondrial respiration and suppressing Akt signaling. These findings demonstrate that GHRH receptor antagonism effectively attenuates bleomycin-induced lung inflammation and fibrosis through multiple mechanisms, supporting its therapeutic potential in pulmonary fibrotic disorders.
