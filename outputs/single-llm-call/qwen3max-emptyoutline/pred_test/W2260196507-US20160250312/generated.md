# DESCRIPTION

## 1 MATERIALS AND METHODS

The present invention encompasses a method for the development, formulation, and administration of novel liver-stage malaria vaccines based on Plasmodium falciparum antigens, particularly PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2, delivered via a heterologous viral vector prime-boost regimen comprising chimpanzee adenovirus 63 (ChAd63) and modified vaccinia virus Ankara (MVA). The materials and methods employed in the development and validation of these vaccine candidates are integral to the invention and are described herein in detail to enable reproducibility and industrial applicability. The vaccine constructs were designed using the 3D7 strain sequence of P. falciparum obtained from PlasmoDB, a publicly accessible genomic database for Plasmodium species. Each antigen coding sequence was subjected to bioinformatic analysis using SignalP 3.0 and TMHMM servers to predict signal peptides, transmembrane domains, and subcellular localization, thereby informing structural modifications necessary for optimal expression and immunogenicity in mammalian systems. To enhance secretion and immunogenicity, a tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) leader sequence derived from GenBank Accession K03021 was fused to the N-terminus of each antigen, with the exception of PfLSA3, PfLSAP1, and PfUIS3, which were predicted to contain two transmembrane domains and thus were likely membrane-anchored; for these, the tPA leader sequence was omitted to preserve native topology and function. Repetitive regions within PfLSA1 and PfLSA3, known to potentially interfere with immune recognition or protein folding, were truncated to retain only representative motifs—specifically, two copies of the degenerate seventeen-amino acid repeat in PfLSA1 (one most and one least representative) and a single copy in PfLSA3.

The modified antigen sequences were synthesized by GeneArt (Life Technologies, USA) with additional genetic elements to facilitate cloning and expression. These included a Kozak consensus sequence at the 5′ end to optimize translation initiation in mammalian cells, a KpnI restriction enzyme site for directional cloning, a STOP codon followed by a NotI restriction site at the 3′ end, and codon optimization tailored to human codon usage bias to maximize protein expression in human cells. Critically, all sequences were engineered to exclude the Vaccinia virus early gene transcription termination signal (5′-TTTTTNT-3′) to prevent premature termination during MVA replication. The resulting synthetic DNA constructs were then subcloned into viral vector backbones. For ChAd63, the antigen expression cassette was inserted into an entry vector containing a modified human cytomegalovirus immediate-early promoter flanked by two tetracycline repressor-operator complexes to allow for regulated expression. This entry vector was subsequently recombined into the E1 and E3 deleted ChAd63 genome at the E1 locus via site-specific recombination in T-REx™ 293 cells, yielding recombinant adenoviral particles. Viruses were purified using standard cesium chloride gradient ultracentrifugation and titrated by infectious unit (ifu) assay. For MVA, the antigen constructs were cloned into a shuttle plasmid under the control of the p7.5 early/late vaccinia promoter, enabling markerless integration into the thymidine kinase (TK) locus of the MVA genome through homologous recombination and transient-dominant selection. Recombinant MVA viruses were isolated by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) and plaque purification, followed by PCR verification of correct insertion and purity. Titers were determined by plaque-forming unit (pfu) assay.

All animal immunizations were conducted in accordance with stringent ethical and regulatory standards. Female BALB/c, C57BL/6, and CD-1 outbred mice, aged at least six weeks, were sourced from Harlan, UK. All procedures were approved under the UK Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act 1986 and overseen by the University of Oxford Animal Care and Ethical Review Committee under Project Licenses PPL 30/2414 or 30/2889. Mice received intramuscular (i.m.) injections into the musculus tibialis of 50 μl vaccine formulation in endotoxin-free Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline (D-PBS). The prime-boost regimen consisted of a ChAd63 prime at 1 × 10⁸ ifu, followed eight weeks later by an MVA boost at 1 × 10⁷ pfu for BALB/c and CD-1 mice, and 1 × 10⁶ pfu for C57BL/6 mice, reflecting strain-specific sensitivity to MVA-induced immunopathology. Chimeric Plasmodium berghei parasites expressing P. falciparum antigens were generated using the “gene insertion/marker out” (GIMO) technology. The GIMO parental line, containing a selectable marker (hdhfr::yfcu) at the neutral 230p locus, was transfected with linearized plasmids carrying the P. falciparum antigen under the control of the P. berghei upregulated in sporozoites 4 (UIS4) promoter and terminator, along with a GFP::luciferase reporter driven by the constitutive ef1α promoter. Transfected parasites were selected in vivo by administration of 5-fluorocytosine (5-FC) in drinking water, which eliminates parasites retaining the yfcu marker. Clonal parasite lines were obtained by limiting dilution, and correct genomic integration was confirmed by diagnostic PCR and pulsed-field gel electrophoresis followed by Southern blotting. Liver-stage expression of the transgenic antigens was validated by in vivo bioluminescence imaging 44 hours post-sporozoite challenge and by immunofluorescence assay using sera from vaccinated mice or monoclonal antibodies against P. falciparum CSP.

Immunogenicity was assessed using multiple complementary techniques. Intracellular cytokine staining (ICS) was performed on peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) isolated by ammonium-chloride-potassium (ACK) lysis, followed by six-hour stimulation with overlapping 20-mer peptide pools (15-mers for PfCelTOS) encompassing the full antigen sequence, in the presence of GolgiPlug™ and anti-CD107a-PE to detect degranulation. Cells were stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies against CD4, CD8α, CD16/32, TNFα, IL-2, and IFNγ, and analyzed by flow cytometry on an LSRII instrument using FlowJo software. Splenic T cell responses were quantified by ex vivo IFNγ enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISpot) assay, wherein splenocytes were stimulated for 18–20 hours with 1 μg/ml peptide pool, and spot-forming units (SFU) per million cells were enumerated after background subtraction. Humoral immunity was measured using the luminescence immunoprecipitation system (LIPS), in which antigen-Renilla luciferase fusion proteins were expressed in mammalian cells, lysed, and incubated with diluted serum; antibody-bound complexes were captured on protein A/G beads, and luminescence was quantified following addition of coelenterazine substrate. Protective efficacy was evaluated by intravenous challenge with 1,000 salivary gland sporozoites of the corresponding chimeric P. berghei line eight days post-MVA boost, coinciding with peak CD8⁺ T cell responses. Blood-stage parasitemia was monitored daily from day four onward by Giemsa-stained thin blood smears, and time to 1% parasitemia was calculated by linear regression. Sterile protection was defined as absence of blood-stage infection for 14 days post-challenge. Depletion studies utilized monoclonal antibodies GK1.5 (anti-CD4) and 2.43 (anti-CD8), administered intraperitoneally at 100 μg on days −2, −1, and 0 relative to challenge, achieving >99% depletion as verified by flow cytometry. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism v5, with log-rank (Mantel-Cox) tests for survival curves, Mann-Whitney U tests for parasitemia delay, and log₁₀ transformation of LIPS data prior to parametric testing. All methods were rigorously standardized to ensure reproducibility and relevance to human vaccine development.

## 2 ASSESSING IMMUNOGENICITY AND EFFICACY OF EIGHT CANDIDATE P. FALCIPARUM VACCINES IN MICE

The present invention provides a comprehensive assessment of the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of eight candidate Plasmodium falciparum liver-stage antigens—namely PfLSA1, PfLSA3, PfCelTOS, PfUIS3, PfFalstatin, PfLSAP1, PfLSAP2, and PfETRAMP5—delivered via a heterologous ChAd63-MVA prime-boost regimen in both inbred and outbred murine models. This evaluation forms the scientific foundation for the identification of superior vaccine candidates, particularly PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2, which demonstrate unprecedented levels of sterile protection against liver-stage malaria. The immunogenicity of these candidates was first evaluated in BALB/c mice, a widely used inbred strain with a well-characterized H-2ᵈ major histocompatibility complex (MHC) haplotype that permits robust CD8⁺ T cell responses. Mice were immunized intramuscularly with ChAd63 encoding the respective antigen, followed eight weeks later by an MVA boost, a regimen previously shown to elicit potent and durable T cell immunity. One week after the MVA boost, peripheral blood was analyzed for antigen-specific T cell responses using intracellular cytokine staining (ICS). All eight antigens induced detectable CD8⁺ T cells producing interferon-gamma (IFNγ), with the highest frequencies observed for PfLSA1 (median 6.3% of CD8⁺ cells), PfLSA3 (4.8%), and PfUIS3 (4.2%). In contrast, CD4⁺ IFNγ⁺ responses were uniformly low, generally below 0.5% of total CD4⁺ cells, indicating a dominant CD8⁺ T cell bias—a critical feature for liver-stage immunity, as CD8⁺ T cells are the primary mediators of infected hepatocyte clearance. To corroborate these findings, ex vivo IFNγ ELISpot assays were performed on splenocytes, revealing similarly high immunogenicity for PfUIS3 (median 1,200 spot-forming units per million splenocytes) and PfLSA1 (868 SFU), confirming the systemic induction of Th1-polarized cellular immunity.

Humoral responses were concurrently assessed using the luminescence immunoprecipitation system (LIPS), a highly sensitive and quantitative serological assay. Antibodies were detected against all antigens except PfLSAP1, with the strongest responses directed against PfFalstatin (1.6-fold increase over naïve controls) and PfLSAP2 (1.5-fold). Notably, the magnitude of antibody responses did not correlate with protective efficacy, underscoring the primacy of cellular immunity in this model. To account for potential MHC restriction inherent to inbred strains, the same immunization and assessment protocol was repeated in C57BL/6 mice, which express the H-2ᵇ haplotype. In this strain, CD8⁺ T cell responses were generally of higher magnitude but exhibited greater inter-individual variability. PfUIS3 and PfLSA3 again emerged as the most immunogenic, with median CD8⁺ IFNγ⁺ frequencies of 13.7% and 12.8%, respectively. However, no responses were detected against PfLSA1 or PfLSAP1, highlighting the influence of MHC polymorphism on epitope presentation. Consistent with this, ELISpot confirmed the absence of cellular responses to these two antigens in C57BL/6 mice. Antibody responses were also markedly different: only PfFalstatin elicited a detectable humoral response (1.5-fold), while PfLSAP1, PfLSAP2, and PfLSA1 remained serologically silent, further emphasizing the strain-dependent nature of B cell recognition.

Following the immunogenicity profiling, the protective efficacy of all eight candidates was evaluated in both BALB/c and CD-1 outbred mice using a stringent challenge model. Mice were infected intravenously with 1,000 sporozoites of chimeric P. berghei parasites genetically engineered to express the cognate P. falciparum antigen under the control of the UIS4 promoter, ensuring expression during both the sporozoite and liver stages. This model allows for direct assessment of antigen-specific protection in a physiological context, circumventing the limitations of rodent ortholog-based systems. Challenge was administered eight days post-MVA boost, aligning with the peak of CD8⁺ T cell expansion. Control groups received vaccines encoding PfCSP or PfTRAP, the two leading pre-erythrocytic antigens currently in clinical development. In BALB/c mice, PfCSP conferred 37.5% sterile protection (3/8 mice), while PfTRAP provided no sterile protection, though both induced significant delays in patency. Strikingly, vaccination with PfLSA1 or PfLSAP2 resulted in 87.5% sterile protection (7/8 mice for each), a statistically superior outcome (p < 0.0001) compared to both controls and unvaccinated mice. PfUIS3, PfFalstatin, and PfLSA3 provided partial protection, manifesting as significant delays in time to 1% parasitemia (medians of 1.42, 0.59, and 0.35 days, respectively), but no sterile protection beyond background levels. The remaining candidates—PfCelTOS, PfLSAP1, and PfETRAMP5—showed no evidence of protection.

To assess generalizability beyond MHC-restricted inbred strains, identical experiments were conducted in CD-1 outbred mice, which exhibit greater genetic diversity in MHC and immune response genes, thereby better modeling human population heterogeneity. In this model, PfCSP and PfTRAP protected 33.3% and 30% of mice, respectively. Remarkably, PfLSA1 maintained its high efficacy, protecting 87.5% of CD-1 mice (7/8), while PfLSAP2 protected 70% (7/10), both significantly exceeding control performance (p < 0.0001 and p = 0.0009, respectively). PfUIS3, despite strong immunogenicity in BALB/c mice, failed to protect CD-1 mice, although a non-significant trend toward delayed patency was observed. PfFalstatin induced a significant delay (0.97 days, p < 0.0001), but no sterile protection. The other candidates showed no efficacy. These results collectively demonstrate that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 are not only highly immunogenic but also capable of inducing robust, cross-strain protective immunity that transcends MHC restrictions, a critical attribute for a globally deployable malaria vaccine. The consistent superiority of these two antigens over the current clinical benchmarks underscores their novelty and utility as components of next-generation multi-antigen liver-stage vaccines.

## 3 ASSESSMENT OF PfUIS3, PfLSA1 AND PfLSAP2 AS CANDIDATES FOR A LIVER-STAGE MALARIA VACCINE

The present invention identifies PfUIS3, PfLSA1, and PfLSAP2 as leading candidates for inclusion in a liver-stage malaria vaccine, with PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 demonstrating exceptional protective efficacy that surpasses existing benchmarks. While all three antigens exhibited strong immunogenicity in initial screens, their functional outcomes diverged significantly upon challenge, revealing critical distinctions in their capacity to mediate sterile protection. PfUIS3, encoded by the upregulated in sporozoite 3 gene, is a well-conserved P. falciparum antigen localized to the parasitophorous vacuole membrane (PVM), a key interface between the parasite and host hepatocyte. In BALB/c mice, PfUIS3 vaccination induced high-frequency CD8⁺ T cell responses (median 4.2% IFNγ⁺) and significant splenic IFNγ production (1,200 SFU), comparable to PfLSA1. Upon challenge with chimeric P. berghei expressing PfUIS3, vaccination resulted in a statistically significant delay in patency (median 1.42 days, p = 0.004) and one instance of sterile protection (12.5%). However, this protection was not replicated in CD-1 outbred mice, where no sterile protection was observed and the delay in patency failed to reach statistical significance. This discrepancy suggests that PfUIS3-derived epitopes may be presented in an MHC-restricted manner, limiting its utility in genetically diverse populations. Despite its biological relevance and immunogenicity, PfUIS3’s inconsistent efficacy across mouse strains diminishes its suitability as a standalone vaccine component, though it may retain value in a multi-antigen formulation.

In stark contrast, PfLSA1 (liver-stage antigen 1) emerged as a premier candidate. PfLSA1 is abundantly expressed in the PVM during late liver-stage development and is essential for the transition to blood-stage infection. It is highly conserved among P. falciparum isolates and has been associated with protection in natural infection and irradiated sporozoite trials. In the present study, PfLSA1 vaccination induced robust CD8⁺ T cell responses in BALB/c mice (6.3% IFNγ⁺) and significant antibody production. Crucially, it conferred 87.5% sterile protection in both BALB/c and CD-1 mice—a level of efficacy unmatched by any other candidate tested and substantially exceeding that of PfCSP (37.5% in BALB/c, 33.3% in CD-1). This cross-strain consistency indicates that PfLSA1 contains multiple, promiscuous T cell epitopes capable of being presented by diverse MHC class I molecules, a vital characteristic for a global vaccine. Moreover, PfLSA1’s localization to the PVM likely facilitates efficient antigen processing and presentation via the MHC class I pathway, enabling CD8⁺ T cells to recognize and eliminate infected hepatocytes. The failure of a prior recombinant protein PfLSA1 vaccine to protect humans is now understood to stem from its inability to induce CD8⁺ T cells; the viral vector platform employed here overcomes this limitation by promoting endogenous antigen synthesis and cross-presentation, thereby unlocking PfLSA1’s full protective potential.

PfLSAP2 (liver-stage associated protein 2) represents a novel antigen with no previously reported vaccine evaluation. Like PfLSA1, it is predicted to localize to the PVM and demonstrated exceptional performance in this study. PfLSAP2 vaccination induced moderate CD8⁺ T cell responses in BALB/c mice and detectable antibodies in both BALB/c and CD-1 strains. Most importantly, it achieved 87.5% sterile protection in BALB/c mice and 70% in CD-1 mice, rivaling PfLSA1’s efficacy. Notably, neither PfLSA1 nor PfLSAP2 has a murine Plasmodium ortholog, meaning they would have been overlooked in traditional preclinical screening using rodent parasite models. Their identification was made possible only through the use of chimeric P. berghei expressing the authentic P. falciparum antigens, a method that constitutes a key aspect of the present invention. The high efficacy of PfLSAP2, despite its novelty, underscores the untapped potential of the P. falciparum liver-stage proteome and validates the chimeric parasite approach as a superior tool for antigen discovery. Together, PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 represent a new class of liver-stage vaccine antigens that induce potent, CD8⁺ T cell-mediated sterile protection across genetically diverse hosts, fulfilling a long-standing need for alternatives to PfCSP and PfTRAP. Their inclusion in a multi-antigen vaccine, either alone or in combination with other candidates, offers a promising path toward a highly effective malaria vaccine.

## 4. PROTECTIVE EFFICACY OF THE CANDIDATES VACCINES IN CD-1 OUTBRED MICE

The protective efficacy of the candidate P. falciparum liver-stage vaccines was rigorously evaluated in CD-1 outbred mice to address the critical limitation of MHC restriction inherent in inbred mouse strains and to better model the genetic heterogeneity of human populations. CD-1 mice, derived from an outbred stock, exhibit substantial diversity in MHC class I and II alleles, as well as in other immune response genes, thereby providing a more stringent and clinically relevant test of vaccine efficacy. In this model, the benchmark antigens PfCSP and PfTRAP—currently the basis of all advanced pre-erythrocytic malaria vaccines—demonstrated modest efficacy, with sterile protection rates of 33.3% (3/9) and 30% (3/10), respectively. These results align with historical data from human trials, where even the most advanced PfCSP-based vaccine, RTS,S, shows only partial efficacy that wanes over time. In marked contrast, vaccination with PfLSA1 conferred sterile protection in 87.5% (7/8) of CD-1 mice, a statistically significant improvement over both controls and unvaccinated mice (p < 0.0001). This extraordinary level of protection, matching that observed in BALB/c mice, demonstrates that PfLSA1 contains multiple T cell epitopes that are effectively presented across a broad range of MHC haplotypes, a property essential for global vaccine deployment. Similarly, PfLSAP2, a novel antigen with no prior vaccine history, protected 70% (7/10) of CD-1 mice, a result that is both statistically significant (p = 0.0009) and unprecedented for a newly identified candidate. The consistency of efficacy between inbred and outbred models strongly suggests that the protective mechanisms elicited by these vaccines are not dependent on a narrow set of immunodominant, strain-specific epitopes but rather involve a broader repertoire of T cell targets.

Other candidates showed variable performance in CD-1 mice. PfUIS3, despite inducing strong CD8⁺ T cell responses in BALB/c mice, failed to provide sterile protection in CD-1 mice, although a non-significant median delay in patency of 1.10 days was observed. This indicates that the dominant protective epitopes of PfUIS3 may be restricted to the H-2ᵈ haplotype and are not efficiently presented in the diverse MHC environment of CD-1 mice. PfFalstatin, a cysteine protease inhibitor, induced a significant delay in patency (0.97 days, p < 0.0001) but no sterile protection, suggesting a partial inhibitory effect on liver-stage development that is insufficient for complete parasite clearance. PfLSA3, PfCelTOS, PfLSAP1, and PfETRAMP5 showed no evidence of protection in CD-1 mice, consistent with their poor immunogenicity in this strain. Importantly, immunogenicity assessments in CD-1 mice revealed that PfLSA1 vaccination induced not only CD8⁺ IFNγ⁺ responses (median 1.1%) but also CD8⁺ TNFα⁺ (1.2%) and CD8⁺ CD107a⁺ (5.5%) responses, indicative of polyfunctional and cytotoxic T cell activity. Antibodies were detectable in 75% (6/8) of PfLSA1-vaccinated CD-1 mice, though their role in protection remains unclear. PfLSAP2 induced lower cellular responses but still elicited antibodies in 60% (6/10) of vaccinated mice, with no correlation between antibody levels and protection status, further supporting a T cell-mediated mechanism. The high efficacy of PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 in CD-1 mice represents a major advance in preclinical malaria vaccine development, as it demonstrates that sterile protection can be achieved in a genetically heterogeneous host—a prerequisite for success in human trials. These findings validate the use of outbred mouse models as a critical gatekeeper in the vaccine development pipeline and position PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 as leading candidates for clinical translation.

## 5. SUMMARY

The present invention discloses a novel and highly effective approach to liver-stage malaria vaccination through the identification and validation of two superior Plasmodium falciparum antigens, PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2, delivered via a heterologous chimpanzee adenovirus 63 (ChAd63) and modified vaccinia virus Ankara (MVA) prime-boost regimen. Unlike conventional pre-erythrocytic vaccine candidates such as PfCSP and PfTRAP, which have shown limited efficacy in human trials, the vaccines of the present invention induce unprecedented levels of sterile protection—87.5% in inbred BALB/c mice and 70–87.5% in outbred CD-1 mice—by eliciting robust, polyfunctional CD8⁺ T cell responses that are effective across diverse genetic backgrounds. The invention further encompasses the use of chimeric Plasmodium berghei parasites expressing authentic P. falciparum antigens under the control of the UIS4 promoter as a superior preclinical model for evaluating liver-stage vaccine efficacy, a system that enabled the discovery of PfLSAP2, a previously untested antigen with no murine ortholog, and confirmed the high potential of PfLSA1, which had previously failed in protein-based formulations due to inadequate T cell induction. Mechanistic studies demonstrate that protection is critically dependent on CD8⁺ T cells, with liver-resident effector memory CD8⁺ T cells playing a central role in rapid clearance of infected hepatocytes. The invention provides a complete platform for the development, production, and administration of these vaccines, including codon-optimized antigen sequences, viral vector constructs, immunization protocols, and analytical methods for assessing immunogenicity and efficacy. By overcoming the limitations of MHC restriction, suboptimal immunogenicity, and reliance on rodent orthologs, the present invention represents a significant advancement in the field of malaria vaccinology and offers a viable path toward a highly effective, globally deployable vaccine against Plasmodium falciparum malaria.

## 5. SPECT-1

The present invention is directed to a composition comprising a nucleic acid sequence encoding a Plasmodium falciparum liver-stage antigen 1 (PfLSA1) polypeptide or a Plasmodium falciparum liver-stage associated protein 2 (PfLSAP2) polypeptide, wherein said nucleic acid sequence is codon-optimized for expression in human cells and is operably linked to a promoter functional in a viral vector, and wherein said composition is formulated for administration as part of a heterologous prime-boost vaccination regimen using a chimpanzee adenovirus 63 (ChAd63) vector for priming and a modified vaccinia virus Ankara (MVA) vector for boosting. The PfLSA1 polypeptide comprises an amino acid sequence derived from the 3D7 strain of P. falciparum, optionally lacking repetitive regions and optionally fused to a tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) leader sequence to enhance secretion and immunogenicity. The PfLSAP2 polypeptide similarly comprises a 3D7 strain-derived sequence, codon-optimized and optionally modified for optimal expression. The invention further includes recombinant ChAd63 and MVA viral vectors containing said nucleic acid sequences, methods of producing these vectors, and methods of using them to induce sterile protection against P. falciparum liver-stage infection in a human subject. The regimen involves administering a priming dose of the ChAd63 vector encoding PfLSA1 or PfLSAP2, followed by a boosting dose of the MVA vector encoding the same antigen, with an interval of 4 to 12 weeks, preferably 8 weeks, between doses. The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these vectors in a physiologically acceptable carrier, as well as kits containing both the priming and boosting components. The vaccines of the invention are characterized by their ability to induce high-frequency, polyfunctional CD8⁺ T cell responses that mediate sterile protection in preclinical models, including outbred mice, and are suitable for clinical development as standalone or multi-antigen malaria vaccines.

## 6. RESULTS

The results of the present invention unequivocally demonstrate that vaccination with PfLSA1 or PfLSAP2, delivered via a ChAd63-MVA prime-boost regimen, induces exceptional levels of sterile protection against liver-stage Plasmodium falciparum infection in murine models, far exceeding the efficacy of current clinical candidates PfCSP and PfTRAP. In BALB/c mice, both PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 achieved 87.5% sterile protection (7/8 mice), compared to 37.5% for PfCSP and 0% for PfTRAP. In the more stringent CD-1 outbred model, PfLSA1 maintained 87.5% protection (7/8), while PfLSAP2 protected 70% (7/10), significantly outperforming PfCSP (33.3%) and PfTRAP (30%). Immunogenicity profiling revealed that protection correlated strongly with the induction of CD8⁺ T cells producing IFNγ, TNFα, and expressing the degranulation marker CD107a, indicative of cytotoxic potential. Depletion experiments confirmed that CD8⁺ T cells are essential for protection, as their removal abrogated sterile immunity in PfLSAP2-vaccinated mice and substantially reduced it in PfLSA1-vaccinated mice. Furthermore, antigen-specific CD8⁺ T cells were detected in the liver, predominantly of the effector memory phenotype (CD62L⁻CD127⁺), positioning them for rapid response upon sporozoite challenge. Notably, neither PfLSA1 nor PfLSAP2 has a murine Plasmodium ortholog, and their identification was made possible only through the use of chimeric P. berghei parasites expressing the authentic P. falciparum antigens—a method that constitutes a key innovation of this work. The high efficacy of these antigens in outbred mice, which model human genetic diversity, strongly supports their advancement to clinical trials. These results establish PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 as leading candidates for next-generation malaria vaccines and validate the chimeric parasite model as a powerful tool for antigen discovery.
