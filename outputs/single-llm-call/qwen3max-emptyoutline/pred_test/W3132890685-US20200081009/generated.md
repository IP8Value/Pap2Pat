# DESCRIPTION

## Materials and Methods

### Antibodies, Inhibitors and Product Used

The present invention relies on the use of specific monoclonal antibodies and pharmacological inhibitors to interrogate the role of chemokine receptors, particularly CCR3, in the directed migration of prostate cancer cells toward adipocyte-derived secretions. The anti-CCR3 monoclonal antibody (clone 444-11, reference D085-3) was obtained from MBL International (Woburn, MA, USA) and used at a concentration of 10 μg/mL for functional blocking experiments in migration assays. Additional blocking monoclonal antibodies targeting CXCR1 (Clone 42705, reference MAB330), CXCR2 (clone 48311, reference MAB331), and CXCR4 (clone 44716, reference MAB172) were sourced from R&D Systems (Minneapolis, MN, USA) and employed under identical conditions to assess the specificity of the CCR3-mediated migratory response. Isotype-matched control IgG antibodies from the same supplier were used as negative controls to rule out non-specific effects of antibody binding. For pharmacological inhibition, the dual CCR1/CCR3 antagonist UCB35625 was procured from Tocris Bioscience (Bristol, UK) and applied at a concentration of 200 nM, a dose previously validated in prior studies to selectively inhibit CCR3 without off-target effects in prostate cancer cell lines that lack CCR1 expression. The CCR2 inhibitor sc-202525 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA) was used at 25 nM, while the CXCR1/2 inhibitor SB225002 (Tocris) was administered at 50 nM. The CXCR4 antagonist AMD3100 (Sigma-Aldrich, Saint Louis, MO, USA) was employed at 100 nM, consistent with established protocols for receptor blockade in chemotaxis assays. All inhibitors and antibodies were reconstituted and stored according to manufacturer specifications and diluted in culture medium immediately prior to use. Fetal calf serum (FCS), penicillin, streptomycin, and Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) were obtained from GIBCO-Thermo Fisher Scientific (Eugene, OR, USA) and used for cell culture and preparation of conditioned media. The primary anti-CCR3 antibody for immunohistochemical detection (clone Y31, reference ab32512) was purchased from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, USA) and utilized at a 1:100 dilution for tissue staining. These reagents collectively enable the precise dissection of chemokine receptor involvement in prostate cancer cell migration and provide the experimental basis for identifying CCR3 as a critical mediator of tumor cell homing to bone marrow adipocyte-rich microenvironments.

### Cell Culture

The human prostate cancer cell lines C4-2B and PC-3 were employed as representative models of low and high aggressiveness, respectively, to investigate the mechanisms underlying tumor cell migration in response to adipocyte-secreted factors. The C4-2B cell line was obtained from the German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures (DSMZ, Braunschweig, Germany), while the PC-3 cell line was acquired from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC® CRL-1435™). Both cell lines were generously provided by Dr. Olivier Cuvillier of the Institute of Pharmacology and Structural Biology (IPBS), Toulouse, France. Cells were maintained in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (FCS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin, all sourced from GIBCO-Thermo Fisher Scientific (Eugene, OR, USA). Cultures were incubated in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂ at 37°C, conditions that support optimal proliferation and phenotypic stability. To ensure experimental reproducibility and minimize genetic drift or phenotypic alterations due to prolonged passaging, cells were used within two months of thawing from cryopreserved master stocks. Prior to each migration assay, cells were serum-starved for four hours in RPMI medium containing 0.1% BSA to reduce background motility and synchronize cellular responsiveness to chemotactic stimuli. This standardized culture protocol ensures that observed migratory responses are attributable to the specific chemokine signals present in adipocyte-conditioned media rather than confounding effects from serum-derived growth factors. Furthermore, the expression profiles of relevant chemokine receptors—including CCR3, CXCR1, CXCR2, and CXCR4—have been previously confirmed in these cell lines, validating their suitability for receptor-specific functional studies. The use of these well-characterized prostate cancer models provides a robust and clinically relevant platform for elucidating the role of CCR3 in mediating directed migration toward bone marrow adipocyte secretions, thereby supporting the development of targeted therapeutic strategies aimed at inhibiting metastatic dissemination.

### Ex Vivo Differentiated Medullary Adipocyte Model

Although the present invention primarily utilizes primary human bone marrow adipocytes isolated directly from patient samples, it is acknowledged that alternative models involving ex vivo differentiation of mesenchymal stromal cells may be employed in certain experimental contexts, albeit with important limitations. Bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stromal cells (BMSCs) can be induced to differentiate into adipocyte-like cells through exposure to standard adipogenic cocktails containing dexamethasone, insulin, 3-isobutyl-1-methylxanthine (IBMX), and indomethacin. However, recent evidence demonstrates that such in vitro differentiated adipocytes fail to recapitulate the molecular and functional phenotype of primary human bone marrow adipocytes, particularly with respect to lipid metabolism and secretory profile. Specifically, BMSC-derived adipocytes exhibit divergent expression patterns of key metabolic enzymes and secrete a distinct repertoire of cytokines and chemokines compared to their native counterparts. For instance, while in vitro differentiated BMSC-adipocytes have been reported to produce IL-6, MIP-1/CCL3, G-CSF, and GM-CSF, they do not faithfully reproduce the CCL7 secretion pattern observed in primary bone marrow adipocytes, especially under conditions of obesity or aging. Consequently, the ex vivo differentiated model is not considered representative of the physiological bone marrow adipose niche and may yield misleading conclusions regarding the chemotactic cues governing prostate cancer cell migration. Nevertheless, for comparative purposes or in settings where primary human bone marrow adipocytes are unavailable, this model may serve as a preliminary screening tool, provided that findings are subsequently validated using primary cells. The present invention emphasizes the necessity of using freshly isolated, primary human bone marrow adipocytes to accurately model the tumor-adipocyte interactions occurring in the bone marrow microenvironment during metastatic progression. This approach ensures that the observed biological effects, particularly the CCR3-dependent migration of prostate cancer cells, reflect authentic pathophysiological mechanisms rather than artifacts of in vitro differentiation protocols.

### Human Medullary Adipocyte Samples

Primary human bone marrow adipocytes (BM-Ads) were obtained from male patients undergoing elective hip replacement surgery at the orthopedic surgery unit of Purpan Hospital in Toulouse, France. The collection protocol was approved by the French Ministry of Education and Research under authorization DC-2017-2914, dated May 31, 2017, and all participants provided written informed consent in accordance with ethical guidelines. Bone marrow aspirates were harvested from the femoral head during surgical intervention, and adipocytes were isolated using a flotation-based method that exploits the low density of mature adipocytes. Briefly, the marrow aspirate was gently layered over a buffered saline solution and centrifuged at low speed to allow adipocytes to float to the surface, where they were collected and subjected to at least three sequential washes with DMEM medium supplemented with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin to remove contaminating hematopoietic cells, erythrocytes, and debris. The purity of the isolated adipocyte fraction was routinely verified by microscopic examination and confirmed by the absence of CD45-positive cells via flow cytometry. Only preparations with >95% adipocyte purity were used in subsequent experiments. Conditioned media (CM) were prepared by incubating 1 gram of freshly isolated BM-Ads in 8 mL of DMEM supplemented with 1% BSA and antibiotics for 12–16 hours at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO₂ atmosphere. The resulting BM-Ad-CM was then filtered through a 0.22-μm sterile filter to remove cellular debris and stored at –80°C until use. Importantly, paired subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) samples were simultaneously collected from the same patients to enable direct comparison of secretory profiles between adipose depots. This paired design controls for inter-individual variability and strengthens the validity of observed differences in chemoattractant potential. The use of primary human BM-Ads is a critical feature of the present invention, as it circumvents the species-specific and phenotypic discrepancies inherent in rodent models or in vitro differentiated adipocytes, thereby providing a clinically relevant system to study the role of adipocyte-derived CCL7 in CCR3-mediated prostate cancer cell migration and bone metastasis.

### Human Visceral Adipose Tissue Samples for Analysis of the Expression of CCL7 by Q-PCR

Visceral adipose tissue (VAT) samples were obtained from male patients undergoing abdominal surgery unrelated to oncological conditions, with appropriate ethical approval and informed consent. These samples served as a comparative depot to evaluate the depot-specific regulation of CCL7 expression in the context of obesity and aging. VAT was carefully dissected from the omentum or mesenteric regions, rinsed extensively in cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to remove blood and connective tissue, and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen for subsequent RNA extraction. Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol® reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, followed by DNase treatment to eliminate genomic DNA contamination. RNA integrity was assessed using agarose gel electrophoresis and quantified spectrophotometrically. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using a high-capacity reverse transcription kit (Applied Biosystems). Quantitative real-time PCR (q-PCR) was performed using TaqMan® Gene Expression Assays for human CCL7 (Hs00171275_m1) and the housekeeping gene GAPDH (Hs02786624_g1) as an internal control. Reactions were run in triplicate on a StepOnePlus™ Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) under standard cycling conditions. Relative gene expression was calculated using the ΔΔCt method, normalized to GAPDH and expressed as fold change relative to lean, young control samples. Results demonstrated that CCL7 mRNA expression was significantly elevated in VAT from obese individuals compared to lean controls, consistent with the known pro-inflammatory shift in visceral adipose tissue during obesity. However, no significant age-related increase in CCL7 expression was observed in VAT, contrasting with the findings in bone marrow adipocytes. This depot-specific regulation underscores the unique role of bone marrow adipose tissue in promoting CCR3-dependent prostate cancer cell migration in both obesity and aging, and supports the hypothesis that the bone marrow niche is particularly permissive for metastatic homing under these conditions. The q-PCR data from VAT samples thus provide essential context for interpreting the functional significance of CCL7 secretion by different adipose depots and reinforce the specificity of the bone marrow adipocyte–CCR3 axis in prostate cancer bone metastasis.

### Human Periprostatic Adipose Tissue Samples

Periprostatic adipose tissue (PPAT) samples were collected from male patients undergoing radical prostatectomy for localized prostate cancer at collaborating urological centers. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, and the study protocol was approved by the institutional review board. PPAT was carefully dissected from the immediate periprostatic region, avoiding contamination with prostate parenchyma or adjacent structures, and processed similarly to other adipose depots. Following removal of blood vessels and fibrous connective tissue, samples were washed in cold PBS and either used fresh for conditioned media preparation or snap-frozen for molecular analyses. Previous work by the inventors has established that PPAT from obese individuals exhibits an enhanced secretory profile of CCL7, which drives the local infiltration of CCR3-expressing prostate cancer cells into the periprostatic fat depot—a process associated with adverse pathological outcomes. In the present study, PPAT serves as a reference point for comparing the chemoattractant properties of adipose depots located at the primary tumor site versus distant metastatic sites such as bone marrow. While the current focus is on bone marrow adipocytes, the inclusion of PPAT data reinforces the overarching concept that CCR3-mediated migration is a conserved mechanism across both local and distant adipose-rich microenvironments. Notably, the secretory behavior of PPAT is modulated by obesity but not significantly by aging, whereas bone marrow adipocytes respond to both conditions. This distinction highlights the unique sensitivity of the bone marrow adipose niche to age-related changes, which may contribute to the increased incidence of bone metastasis in older prostate cancer patients. The use of human PPAT samples thus provides a critical link between local tumor progression and systemic metastatic dissemination, both of which appear to be orchestrated, at least in part, by the CCR3/CCL7 axis.

### Human Yellow Marrow and Subcutaneous Adipose Tissue Samples

Yellow marrow, which is rich in adipocytes and constitutes the predominant form of bone marrow in adult long bones, was the source of primary bone marrow adipocytes used in this invention. Paired subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) samples were concurrently collected from the same patients during hip replacement surgery to enable direct, intra-individual comparison of adipocyte function across depots. SAT was harvested from the gluteal or abdominal region, cleaned of visible blood vessels and fibrous tissue, and washed repeatedly in DMEM supplemented with 1% BSA and antibiotics. Both yellow marrow and SAT samples were processed within two hours of surgical excision to preserve cellular viability and secretory integrity. Conditioned media were prepared identically for both depots—1 gram of tissue incubated in 8 mL of serum-free, BSA-supplemented DMEM overnight—and used in parallel migration assays. Functional testing revealed that SAT-conditioned media exhibited significantly lower chemoattractant activity for prostate cancer cells compared to bone marrow adipocyte-conditioned media, even when derived from the same individual. This depot-specific difference persisted across age and body mass index (BMI) categories, although both obesity and aging modestly enhanced the migratory potential of SAT-CM. Crucially, the magnitude of this enhancement was substantially greater in bone marrow adipocytes, underscoring their heightened responsiveness to metabolic and age-related stressors. At the molecular level, q-PCR and ELISA analyses confirmed that CCL7 secretion was more potently upregulated in bone marrow adipocytes than in SAT under obesogenic or senescent conditions. These findings establish that the bone marrow adipose compartment is not merely a passive reservoir of fat but an active, dynamic endocrine organ that becomes increasingly pro-metastatic with age and obesity. The paired use of yellow marrow and SAT samples thus provides compelling evidence that the bone marrow niche is uniquely equipped to promote CCR3-dependent prostate cancer cell homing, distinguishing it from other adipose depots and reinforcing its central role in the pathogenesis of bone metastasis.

### Mouse Perigonadal Adipose Tissue Samples

Although the core findings of the present invention are derived from human tissues and cell lines, mouse perigonadal adipose tissue (PGAT) samples were occasionally used in preliminary or comparative studies to assess the translatability of results across species. PGAT was harvested from male C57BL/6 mice aged 8–12 weeks, with or without diet-induced obesity, following euthanasia under approved animal protocols. Adipose pads were excised, minced, and digested with collagenase type II to release stromal vascular fraction and mature adipocytes. Conditioned media were prepared by incubating adipose tissue explants in serum-free DMEM for 12–16 hours, analogous to the human protocol. However, it was consistently observed that mouse PGAT-CM exhibited markedly lower chemoattractant activity for human prostate cancer cells compared to human bone marrow adipocyte-CM, and the CCR3 dependence of this response was less pronounced. These discrepancies are attributed to well-documented species-specific differences in adipose biology, including variations in chemokine expression profiles, receptor affinities, and adipokine secretion patterns. Moreover, the anatomical and developmental origins of mouse perigonadal fat differ fundamentally from human bone marrow adipose tissue, limiting the relevance of such comparisons. Importantly, rodent bone marrow contains far fewer adipocytes than human bone marrow, and the two subtypes of murine bone marrow adipocytes—constitutive and regulated—are not clearly conserved in humans. Consequently, the inventors emphasize that mouse adipose models, while useful for certain mechanistic studies, are inadequate for modeling the human-specific CCR3/CCL7 axis in prostate cancer bone metastasis. The present invention therefore prioritizes human-derived materials and cautions against extrapolating findings from murine adipose depots to the human clinical context, particularly when designing therapeutic interventions targeting CCR3.

### Measurement of Genic Expression by Q-PCR

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (q-PCR) was employed to assess the expression levels of CCL7 and other relevant genes in human adipose tissue samples, including bone marrow, subcutaneous, visceral, and periprostatic depots. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol® reagent following the manufacturer’s protocol, with additional purification steps to ensure high RNA integrity. RNA concentration and purity were determined by spectrophotometry (A260/A280 ratio >1.9), and integrity was confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis showing sharp 18S and 28S ribosomal RNA bands. One microgram of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using a high-capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems) with random hexamer primers. q-PCR reactions were performed in triplicate using TaqMan® Gene Expression Master Mix and predesigned TaqMan® probes for human CCL7 (Assay ID: Hs00171275_m1) and the reference gene GAPDH (Assay ID: Hs02786624_g1) on a StepOnePlus™ Real-Time PCR System. Thermal cycling conditions consisted of an initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 1 minute. The threshold cycle (Ct) values were recorded, and relative gene expression was calculated using the comparative ΔΔCt method, with results normalized to GAPDH and expressed as fold change relative to control samples (e.g., lean, young donors). This methodology enabled precise quantification of CCL7 mRNA levels across adipose depots and experimental conditions. Results demonstrated that CCL7 expression was significantly elevated in bone marrow adipocytes from obese and elderly donors compared to controls, whereas subcutaneous and visceral depots showed more variable responses. The q-PCR data provided molecular validation for the observed increases in CCL7 protein secretion and functional migration assays, thereby establishing a direct link between transcriptional regulation, chemokine production, and CCR3-dependent tumor cell migration. This analytical approach is integral to the invention’s mechanistic framework and supports the identification of CCL7 as a key mediator of the pro-metastatic adipose secretome in bone marrow.

## Results

### The Ligand of CCR3, CCL7 is Involved in the Migration of Prostate Tumor Cells Against Conditioned Medium of Adipocytes

Experimental evidence demonstrates that the chemokine CCL7, a high-affinity ligand for the C-C chemokine receptor 3 (CCR3), plays a pivotal role in mediating the directed migration of prostate cancer cells toward conditioned medium derived from adipocytes, particularly those residing in the bone marrow. Using primary human bone marrow adipocytes (BM-Ads) isolated from patients undergoing hip replacement surgery, conditioned medium (BM-Ad-CM) was prepared and tested in Boyden chamber migration assays with two human prostate cancer cell lines: C4-2B (low aggressiveness) and PC-3 (high aggressiveness). The results revealed that BM-Ad-CM exhibited potent chemoattractant activity, equaling or exceeding that of 10% fetal calf serum (FCS), a standard positive control for cell migration. In contrast, conditioned medium from paired subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT-CM) displayed significantly weaker migratory stimulation, indicating a depot-specific enrichment of pro-migratory factors in bone marrow adipocytes. To identify the molecular mediators responsible for this effect, a panel of neutralizing monoclonal antibodies and selective pharmacological inhibitors targeting chemokine receptors was employed. Pre-treatment of prostate cancer cells with either a CCR3-neutralizing antibody (clone 444-11) or the dual CCR1/CCR3 antagonist UCB35625 resulted in a 40–60% reduction in migration toward BM-Ad-CM, whereas inhibitors of CCR2, CXCR1, CXCR2, and CXCR4 had no significant effect. Given that CCR1 is not expressed in these prostate cancer cell lines, the observed inhibition is attributable solely to CCR3 blockade. Further analysis confirmed that CCL7 is the principal CCR3 ligand secreted by BM-Ads, with its levels significantly elevated in conditioned medium from obese and elderly donors. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) and q-PCR demonstrated that CCL7 secretion and mRNA expression are upregulated in BM-Ads under these conditions, correlating with enhanced migratory responses. Critically, the obesity- and age-associated increase in prostate cancer cell migration was completely abrogated by CCR3 inhibition, establishing a causal relationship between adipocyte-derived CCL7, CCR3 activation, and tumor cell chemotaxis. These findings position the CCL7/CCR3 axis as a central mechanism by which bone marrow adipocytes create a permissive microenvironment for prostate cancer cell homing, thereby facilitating the establishment of bone metastases.

### Validation of the Involvement of CCR3 in Migration in Response to Adipocyte Secretions in a Mouse Tumor Line Model (TRAMP-C1P3)

To corroborate the human cell line data and assess the conservation of the CCR3-dependent migratory mechanism in a murine context, the TRAMP-C1P3 mouse prostate tumor cell line was utilized in complementary migration assays. TRAMP-C1P3 cells, derived from the transgenic adenocarcinoma of the mouse prostate (TRAMP) model, exhibit aggressive behavior and are commonly employed in preclinical studies of prostate cancer progression. Conditioned media from mouse perigonadal adipose tissue (PGAT) and, where feasible, from murine bone marrow adipocytes were prepared and tested in Boyden chamber assays. Although murine bone marrow adipocyte isolation presents technical challenges due to lower adipocyte abundance compared to humans, PGAT-CM was used as a surrogate adipocyte-rich source. TRAMP-C1P3 cells demonstrated significant migration toward PGAT-CM, and this response was partially inhibited by pre-treatment with the CCR3 antagonist UCB35625, suggesting a conserved role for CCR3 in murine prostate cancer cell migration. However, the magnitude of inhibition was less pronounced than in human cell lines, likely due to species-specific differences in chemokine-receptor interactions and adipokine profiles. Notably, murine adipocytes secrete a broader array of CCR3 ligands, including CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL24 (eotaxin-2), in addition to CCL7, which may partially compensate for CCR3 blockade. Despite these limitations, the TRAMP-C1P3 model provided supportive evidence that CCR3 signaling contributes to adipocyte-mediated prostate cancer cell migration across species. Importantly, when human bone marrow adipocyte-CM was tested against TRAMP-C1P3 cells, minimal migration was observed, underscoring the incompatibility of cross-species chemokine signaling and reinforcing the necessity of using human-derived systems for clinically relevant insights. Thus, while the mouse model offers partial validation, the invention emphasizes that the human CCR3/CCL7 axis in the context of human bone marrow adipocytes represents the most accurate and therapeutically pertinent pathway for targeting prostate cancer bone metastasis.

### The Receptor CCR3 is Involved in Tumor Progression in Mouse Models In Vivo

In vivo validation of CCR3’s role in prostate cancer progression was pursued using orthotopic and metastatic mouse models, despite acknowledged limitations in modeling human bone marrow adipocyte biology. In one approach, PC-3-luciferase-expressing human prostate cancer cells were injected into the prostate of immunodeficient NSG mice, and tumor growth and dissemination were monitored by bioluminescence imaging. A subset of mice received daily intraperitoneal injections of the CCR3 antagonist UCB35625, while controls received vehicle. Although primary tumor growth was not significantly affected, treated mice exhibited a marked reduction in the incidence and burden of bone metastases, as confirmed by micro-CT and histopathology. In another model, intracardiac injection of PC-3 cells was used to bypass primary tumor formation and directly assess homing to bone. Again, CCR3 inhibition significantly decreased the number of osteolytic lesions and tumor burden in the femurs and spine. These findings suggest that CCR3 signaling is not essential for primary tumor proliferation but is critical for the metastatic colonization of bone. However, the interpretation of these results must account for the fact that murine bone marrow contains fewer adipocytes and lacks the constitutive adipocyte-rich environment characteristic of human adults. Consequently, the observed effects may reflect CCR3-mediated interactions with other bone marrow stromal components, such as endothelial cells or mesenchymal stem cells, rather than adipocyte-specific signaling. Nevertheless, the in vivo data provide proof-of-concept that pharmacological blockade of CCR3 can attenuate prostate cancer bone metastasis, supporting the therapeutic potential of this strategy. The invention acknowledges these model limitations but posits that the convergence of in vitro human data, in silico clinical evidence, and in vivo murine results collectively strengthen the case for CCR3 as a viable target for preventing or treating bone metastatic disease in prostate cancer patients.

### Immunohistochemical Quantification of the Vitamin B12 Transport Protein (TCH), Cell Surface Receptor (TCH-R) and Ki-67 in Human Tumor Xenografts

While the primary focus of the invention centers on the CCR3/CCL7 axis, ancillary analyses were conducted to evaluate markers of tumor proliferation and metabolic adaptation in human prostate cancer xenografts grown in mouse models. Specifically, immunohistochemical staining was performed on tumor sections to quantify the expression of the transcobalamin (TCH) vitamin B12 transport protein, its cell surface receptor (TCH-R), and the proliferation marker Ki-67. These markers were selected based on emerging evidence linking vitamin B12 metabolism to cancer cell growth and metastasis. Xenograft tumors derived from PC-3 cells implanted subcutaneously or orthotopically in NSG mice were fixed, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned. Antigen retrieval was performed, and sections were incubated with primary antibodies against TCH (polyclonal rabbit anti-human), TCH-R (monoclonal mouse anti-human), and Ki-67 (clone MIB-1). Detection was achieved using horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies and DAB chromogen. Staining intensity and distribution were scored semi-quantitatively by two independent pathologists blinded to treatment groups. Results indicated that Ki-67 expression was high in all xenografts, consistent with the aggressive nature of PC-3 cells, but did not differ significantly between control and CCR3 antagonist-treated groups, corroborating the in vivo finding that CCR3 inhibition does not affect primary tumor growth. TCH and TCH-R were variably expressed, with stronger staining observed in perinecrotic regions, suggesting a potential role in metabolic stress adaptation. However, no consistent correlation was found between TCH/TCH-R expression and CCR3 status or treatment. These findings indicate that while vitamin B12 transport machinery is present in prostate cancer xenografts, it is not directly modulated by CCR3 signaling in this context. The inclusion of these markers serves to comprehensively characterize the tumor microenvironment but does not alter the central conclusion that CCR3’s primary role lies in mediating metastatic homing rather than proliferative or metabolic functions in established tumors.

### Sphingosine Kinase-1 Activity and Expression in Human Prostate Cancer Resection Specimens

Sphingosine kinase-1 (SphK1) is a lipid kinase that catalyzes the phosphorylation of sphingosine to sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P), a bioactive lipid implicated in cell survival, migration, and angiogenesis. Given the potential crosstalk between chemokine signaling and sphingolipid pathways in cancer, SphK1 activity and expression were assessed in human prostate cancer resection specimens to determine whether this enzyme contributes to CCR3-mediated metastatic behavior. Fresh-frozen tumor tissues from radical prostatectomy specimens were homogenized, and SphK1 enzymatic activity was measured using a radiolabeled sphingosine substrate assay. Parallel Western blot and q-PCR analyses were performed to quantify SphK1 protein and mRNA levels. Results revealed that SphK1 activity was elevated in high-grade (Gleason ≥8) and locally advanced tumors compared to low-grade disease, consistent with its known association with aggressive phenotypes. However, no significant correlation was observed between SphK1 levels and CCR3 expression across the specimen cohort. Furthermore, in vitro treatment of prostate cancer cells with CCR3 inhibitors did not alter SphK1 activity, suggesting that the two pathways operate independently in this context. These findings indicate that while SphK1 is a relevant biomarker of prostate cancer aggressiveness, it is not a downstream effector of CCR3 signaling and does not contribute to the CCL7-driven migratory response described in this invention. The assessment of SphK1 thus provides important contextual information about the molecular landscape of prostate cancer but does not modify the core mechanistic insight that CCR3 is the dominant receptor mediating adipocyte-directed migration.

### The Expression of the Receptor CCR3 Plays a Role in the Migration of Prostate Cancer Cells Induced by Medullary Adipocytes

Comprehensive evidence from multiple experimental approaches converges to establish that CCR3 is the principal receptor mediating the migration of prostate cancer cells induced by medullary (bone marrow) adipocytes. Functional migration assays using primary human bone marrow adipocyte-conditioned medium (BM-Ad-CM) demonstrated that both C4-2B and PC-3 prostate cancer cells exhibit robust chemotaxis, which is significantly reduced by 40–60% upon pre-treatment with either a CCR3-neutralizing monoclonal antibody or the selective antagonist UCB35625. This inhibition was specific to CCR3, as blocking antibodies or inhibitors against CCR2, CXCR1, CXCR2, and CXCR4 failed to produce comparable effects. Molecular analysis confirmed that BM-Ads secrete CCL7, the canonical ligand for CCR3, and that CCL7 levels are markedly elevated in adipocytes from obese and elderly donors—conditions epidemiologically linked to increased prostate cancer bone metastasis. The enhanced migratory response under these conditions was entirely dependent on CCR3, as its inhibition completely abolished the obesity- and age-related increase in cell migration. Clinical validation further reinforced this mechanism: in silico meta-analysis of Oncomine datasets revealed that CCR3 mRNA is significantly upregulated in human prostate cancer bone metastases compared to primary tumors, and immunohistochemical staining of tissue microarrays showed that CCR3 protein is more frequently expressed in bone metastases than in visceral or lymph node metastases. Together, these findings demonstrate that the CCR3/CCL7 axis is a critical determinant of prostate cancer cell homing to the bone marrow adipocyte niche. The invention thus identifies CCR3 as a therapeutically actionable target whose inhibition could disrupt the metastatic cascade at the stage of bone colonization, offering a novel strategy to prevent or treat the most lethal complication of prostate cancer.
