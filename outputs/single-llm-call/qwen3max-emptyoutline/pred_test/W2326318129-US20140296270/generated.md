# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of molecular oncology and targeted therapeutics, particularly in the development of small-molecule inhibitors that modulate the activity of signal transducer and activator of transcription proteins, with a specific focus on STAT3 and STAT1. More particularly, the invention provides novel compounds derived from an N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide scaffold that function as potent, reversible, and selective inhibitors of the Src homology 2 (SH2) domain-mediated dimerization and activation of STAT3 and STAT1. These compounds are designed to disrupt the pathological signaling cascades driven by constitutive or ligand-induced phosphorylation of STAT3 and STAT1, which are frequently dysregulated in human cancers, including but not limited to head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these compounds, methods for their use in the treatment of cancer—either as monotherapy or in combination with radiotherapy or chemotherapy—and kits for clinical or diagnostic application. The compounds of the invention exhibit high binding affinity for the SH2 domains of both STAT3 and STAT1, demonstrated oral bioavailability, favorable pharmacokinetic profiles, and a high maximum tolerated dose in preclinical models, thereby representing a significant advancement over prior art inhibitors such as Stattic, STA-21, S3I-201, and XZH-5, which suffer from poor potency, off-target effects, or inadequate drug-like properties. The technical field thus spans medicinal chemistry, structural biology, cancer pharmacology, and translational drug development, with the ultimate goal of providing clinically viable therapeutics that overcome resistance mechanisms in aggressive and treatment-refractory malignancies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) is a critical intracellular signaling protein that functions as a transcription factor upon activation by cytokines, growth factors, and other extracellular stimuli. Under normal physiological conditions, STAT3 is transiently activated through phosphorylation at tyrosine residue 705 (Y705) by upstream kinases such as receptor tyrosine kinases (e.g., EGFR), non-receptor tyrosine kinases (e.g., Src family kinases), or cytokine receptor-associated kinases (e.g., JAKs). This phosphorylation event triggers the formation of STAT3 homodimers via reciprocal interaction between the phosphotyrosine (pY) motif of one monomer and the SH2 domain of another. These dimers then translocate to the nucleus, where they bind to specific DNA response elements and regulate the expression of genes involved in cell proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, immune evasion, and stemness. However, in a wide array of human cancers—including approximately 50% of all solid tumors—STAT3 becomes constitutively activated, often due to persistent upstream signaling from oncogenic drivers such as mutated EGFR, autocrine IL-6 loops, or hyperactive Src kinases. This aberrant activation transforms STAT3 into a master regulator of multiple cancer hallmarks, enabling tumor cells to proliferate uncontrollably, resist apoptosis, metastasize, evade immune surveillance, and sustain stem-like properties through the maintenance of cancer stem cells (CSCs). Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) represents a paradigmatic example of a malignancy driven by STAT3, as it was the first human cancer shown to depend on constitutively active STAT3 for growth and survival. In HNSCC, both EGFR-dependent and EGFR-independent mechanisms—such as IL-6/gp130 autocrine signaling—can lead to persistent STAT3 activation, contributing not only to tumorigenesis but also to resistance against conventional therapies like ionizing radiation (IR) and chemotherapy.

Despite its well-established role in oncogenesis, the direct pharmacological targeting of STAT3 has proven challenging due to the protein’s lack of classical enzymatic activity and the difficulty in designing small molecules that effectively disrupt protein-protein interactions, particularly the high-affinity pY-SH2 domain interaction. Early efforts yielded compounds such as Stattic, which inhibits STAT3 dimerization but operates via a covalent mechanism that leads to significant off-target toxicity and a low maximum tolerated dose in vivo, thereby limiting its clinical utility. Other inhibitors, including STA-21, S3I-201, and XZH-5, have demonstrated modest activity in cellular assays but suffer from poor potency (IC50 values in the micromolar range), limited selectivity, or unfavorable pharmacokinetic properties. For instance, S3I-201 exhibits an IC50 of 86 μM in STAT3 DNA-binding assays, while even its optimized derivatives like BP-1-102, though improved (IC50 ~6.8 μM), still show cross-reactivity with other pathways such as NF-κB. Furthermore, many of these agents fail to demonstrate robust efficacy in vivo, particularly in xenograft models that recapitulate the complex tumor microenvironment and therapeutic resistance mechanisms observed in patients. The situation is further complicated by emerging evidence that STAT1, a closely related family member sharing significant structural homology—particularly within the SH2 domain—also contributes to therapy resistance in cancers like HNSCC. STAT1 is traditionally associated with interferon signaling and anti-tumor immunity, but in the context of chronic inflammation or repeated genotoxic stress (e.g., from radiation), it can paradoxically promote survival and resistance by upregulating interferon-related damage signature (IRDS) genes. Thus, effective therapeutic strategies may require dual inhibition of both STAT3 and STAT1 to fully abrogate the transcriptional programs that drive tumor progression and treatment failure.

In this context, virtual ligand screening (VLS) has emerged as a powerful tool for identifying novel scaffolds that can bind to the pY pocket of the STAT3 SH2 domain. Using this approach, researchers previously identified three initial hits—C3, C30, and C188—from a library of 920,000 compounds. Among these, C188 demonstrated promising reversible inhibitory activity, inducing apoptosis in breast cancer cells with an EC50 of 0.7 μM. However, its potency in inhibiting STAT3 phosphorylation remained suboptimal (IC50 ~16 μM), and its in vivo efficacy was limited. To address these shortcomings, a hit-to-lead optimization program was initiated using C188 as a structural template. This program employed a combination of two-dimensional (2D) fingerprint similarity screening and three-dimensional (3D) pharmacophore modeling to identify derivatives with enhanced binding affinity and functional activity. While such structure-based approaches have been used in other drug discovery contexts, their successful application to the STAT3/STAT1 target class has been hindered by the shallow and highly conserved nature of the SH2 domain binding pocket, which makes achieving selectivity and high potency exceptionally difficult. Moreover, prior art compounds have not adequately addressed the dual role of STAT1 in radioresistance, nor have they demonstrated the necessary pharmacokinetic and safety profiles to support clinical translation. Therefore, there remains a significant unmet need for a new class of small-molecule inhibitors that not only potently and selectively target the SH2 domains of STAT3 and STAT1 but also possess drug-like properties suitable for oral administration, exhibit high tumor penetration, and demonstrate efficacy in preclinical models of therapy-resistant cancer, particularly HNSCC. The present invention fulfills this need by providing C188-9 and related derivatives that represent a substantial improvement over existing inhibitors in terms of potency, mechanism of action, in vivo efficacy, and therapeutic potential.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel small-molecule compounds that function as potent, reversible, and dual inhibitors of STAT3 and STAT1, specifically by targeting the Src homology 2 (SH2) domains of these transcription factors to prevent their phosphorylation-dependent dimerization and subsequent nuclear translocation. The lead compound, designated C188-9, is a derivative of N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide and was identified through a rational drug design approach involving 2D fingerprint similarity screening and 3D pharmacophore modeling based on the scaffold of a prior inhibitor, C188. C188-9 exhibits significantly enhanced potency compared to C188 and other known STAT3 inhibitors, demonstrating high-affinity binding to the STAT3 SH2 domain with a dissociation constant (KD) of 4.7 ± 0.4 nM, as determined by microscale thermophoresis. Functionally, C188-9 inhibits cytokine-stimulated phosphorylation of STAT3 with an IC50 of 3.7 μM in Luminex bead-based assays and reduces constitutive STAT3 phosphorylation in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) cell lines with IC50 values ranging from 10.5 to 22.8 μM. Importantly, C188-9 also demonstrates equivalent potency against STAT1, inhibiting interferon-γ- and G-CSF-induced STAT1 phosphorylation with IC50 values of 9.5 μM and 4.1 μM, respectively, and reducing constitutive STAT1 activation in HNSCC xenografts by 80%. This dual inhibitory activity translates into robust anti-tumor effects, including inhibition of both anchorage-dependent and anchorage-independent growth of HNSCC cells, with IC50 values as low as 0.7 μM in soft agar assays. In vivo, C188-9 administered at 50 mg/kg/day via intraperitoneal injection significantly suppresses the growth of UM-SCC-17B xenograft tumors in nude mice, an effect correlated with a 57% reduction in tumor pSTAT3 levels and an 80% reduction in pSTAT1 levels, whereas the parent compound C188 shows no significant anti-tumor activity under identical conditions. RNA sequencing analysis of treated xenografts reveals that C188-9 modulates the expression of 384 genes involved in oncogenesis, including 76 known STAT3 targets and 40 genes positively regulated by STAT1, many of which are implicated in radioresistance and chemoresistance. The compound exhibits favorable pharmacokinetic properties, including oral bioavailability comparable to intraperitoneal administration, tumor accumulation at levels nearly three-fold higher than plasma, and a high maximum tolerated dose of at least 100 mg/kg/day in mice for 14 days without observable toxicity. In GLP-compliant safety studies, C188-9 showed no clinical, laboratory, or pathological abnormalities in rats or dogs at doses up to 200 mg/kg/day and 100 mg/kg/day, respectively, over 28 days. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising C188-9 or its pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, or prodrugs, formulated for oral, intravenous, or intraperitoneal administration, alone or in combination with radiotherapy or chemotherapy. Additionally, the invention provides kits containing the compound and instructions for its use in the treatment of cancers characterized by aberrant STAT3 and/or STAT1 activation, particularly HNSCC, as well as methods for identifying patients likely to respond based on the presence of activated STAT3/STAT1 signaling. By simultaneously targeting both STAT3 and STAT1, the compounds of the invention offer a novel therapeutic strategy to overcome resistance mechanisms and improve clinical outcomes in aggressive and treatment-refractory malignancies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. DEFINITIONS

As used herein, the term “STAT3” refers to the human signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 protein, including all naturally occurring isoforms such as STAT3α and STAT3β, as well as recombinant, truncated, or modified forms that retain the functional SH2 domain and are capable of participating in phosphorylation-dependent dimerization and transcriptional regulation. The term “STAT1” similarly denotes the human signal transducer and activator of transcription 1 protein, encompassing its major isoforms and functionally active variants. The phrase “SH2 domain” refers to the Src homology 2 domain, a conserved protein module of approximately 100 amino acids that mediates specific recognition of phosphotyrosine-containing motifs in partner proteins; in the context of STAT3 and STAT1, this domain is essential for reciprocal dimerization following phosphorylation at Y705 (STAT3) or Y701 (STAT1). “Phosphorylation” or “phosphorylated” indicates the covalent addition of a phosphate group to a tyrosine residue, typically mediated by upstream kinases such as JAKs, Src family kinases, or receptor tyrosine kinases, resulting in the formation of pY705-STAT3 or pY701-STAT1, which are the active, dimerization-competent forms. The term “inhibitor” as used herein describes a compound that reduces or prevents the biological activity of STAT3 and/or STAT1, specifically by interfering with SH2 domain-mediated dimerization, either through direct binding to the pY pocket of the SH2 domain or by allosteric modulation, and includes reversible, non-covalent inhibitors that do not form permanent chemical bonds with the target protein. “C188-9” refers to the specific chemical compound identified as a second-generation derivative of C188, having the core structure of N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide substituted at the 3-position of the naphthyl ring with a binapthalyl group, and exhibiting the pharmacological properties described herein. The term “cancer stem cells” or “CSCs” denotes a subpopulation of tumor cells with self-renewal capacity, multipotency, and enhanced resistance to therapy, which are maintained in part by STAT3 signaling and contribute to tumor initiation, recurrence, and metastasis. “Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma” or “HNSCC” is a malignant epithelial tumor arising from the mucosal surfaces of the oral cavity, pharynx, or larynx, characterized by frequent dysregulation of EGFR, IL-6, and STAT3/STAT1 pathways. “Constitutively activated” or “constitutive phosphorylation” describes the persistent, ligand-independent phosphorylation of STAT3 or STAT1 commonly observed in cancer cells due to oncogenic mutations or autocrine signaling loops. The phrase “IC50” refers to the half-maximal inhibitory concentration, defined as the molar concentration of a compound required to reduce a specified biological activity (e.g., STAT3 phosphorylation or cell viability) by 50% under standardized assay conditions. “KD” denotes the equilibrium dissociation constant, a measure of binding affinity between a compound and its target protein, where a lower KD value indicates higher affinity. “Oral bioavailability” is the fraction of an orally administered dose that reaches systemic circulation unchanged, and “maximum tolerated dose” (MTD) is the highest dose of a compound that can be administered without causing unacceptable toxicity in a given animal model or human subject. The term “pharmaceutically acceptable” refers to carriers, diluents, excipients, or salts that are compatible with the compound and do not adversely affect the recipient. Finally, “combination therapy” signifies the concurrent or sequential administration of the compound of the invention with another therapeutic modality, such as ionizing radiation, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy, to achieve synergistic or additive anti-tumor effects.

### II. DERIVATIVES

The present invention encompasses not only the lead compound C188-9 but also a broad class of structurally related derivatives designed to optimize potency, selectivity, and pharmacokinetic properties while retaining the core mechanism of action—namely, inhibition of STAT3 and STAT1 SH2 domain-mediated dimerization. These derivatives are based on the N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide scaffold, which serves as the foundational pharmacophore for binding to the pY pocket of the SH2 domain. Structure-activity relationship (SAR) studies revealed that substitution at the 3-position of the naphthylamine ring is critical for enhancing inhibitory activity, with Group I compounds—characterized by the presence of a free 4-hydroxy group and a diverse 3-R substituent—exhibiting the highest potency. Exemplary 3-R groups include triazole-3-yl-mercapto (as in C188-15), binapthalyl (as in C188-9), and other aromatic or heteroaromatic moieties that provide favorable hydrophobic, electron-withdrawing, or hydrogen-bonding interactions with the SH2 domain. In contrast, Group II compounds, which incorporate fused 5-membered rings that eliminate the 4-hydroxy hydrogen, show reduced activity, underscoring the importance of this hydrogen bond donor for optimal binding. Group III derivatives, lacking key structural features of Groups I and II, display minimal activity and are not considered within the scope of the preferred embodiments. The invention further includes derivatives generated through 3D quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) modeling, which identified four key molecular fields contributing to activity: hydrogen-bond donor capacity (favorable when positioned to interact with backbone carbonyls in the SH2 domain), hydrophobic character (favorable in regions adjacent to nonpolar residues), electron-withdrawing properties (favorable near basic residues), and negative-ionic character (favorable in proximity to positively charged pockets). Compounds can be modified at the benzenesulfamide ring (e.g., with halogen, alkyl, or alkoxy substituents) or at the naphthyl ring (e.g., with additional hydroxyl, amino, or carboxyl groups) to fine-tune solubility, metabolic stability, and membrane permeability. Prodrugs are also contemplated, wherein the active compound is masked with a cleavable moiety (e.g., ester or phosphate) to enhance oral absorption or tissue targeting, followed by enzymatic conversion to the active form in vivo. Additionally, isotopically labeled derivatives (e.g., deuterated or carbon-13 labeled) are included for use in pharmacokinetic studies or as internal standards in analytical assays. All such derivatives must retain the ability to inhibit STAT3 and STAT1 phosphorylation with an IC50 of less than 25 μM in cellular assays and demonstrate binding affinity (KD) to the STAT3 SH2 domain of less than 100 nM, as measured by biophysical methods such as microscale thermophoresis or surface plasmon resonance. The scope of the invention is not limited to the specific compounds tested in the examples but extends to any derivative that falls within the structural and functional parameters defined by the SAR and QSAR analyses and exhibits the therapeutic profile described herein.

### III. EMBODIMENTS FOR TARGETING STAT3

The compounds of the present invention, particularly C188-9 and its Group I derivatives, are specifically designed to target the pathological activation of STAT3 in human diseases, most notably cancer. STAT3 is aberrantly activated in approximately 50% of all human malignancies, where it functions as a central node in oncogenic signaling networks that drive tumor cell proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, immune evasion, and stemness. In head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), STAT3 activation occurs through both EGFR-dependent and EGFR-independent mechanisms, such as autocrine IL-6/gp130 signaling, rendering tumors resistant to EGFR-targeted therapies and conventional treatments like radiotherapy. The compounds of the invention directly inhibit STAT3 by binding to its SH2 domain with high affinity, thereby preventing the reciprocal pY-SH2 interaction required for dimerization and nuclear translocation. This mechanism is distinct from upstream kinase inhibitors, which may be bypassed by alternative signaling pathways, and from covalent inhibitors like Stattic, which cause off-target toxicity. C188-9 binds to STAT3 with a KD of 4.7 nM, translating into potent cellular activity: it inhibits G-CSF-induced STAT3 phosphorylation with an IC50 of 3.7 μM and reduces constitutive pSTAT3 levels in HNSCC cell lines with IC50 values ranging from 10.5 to 22.8 μM. This inhibition leads to downregulation of STAT3-regulated oncogenes such as Bcl-xL, survivin, cyclin D1, and VEGF, resulting in cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, and suppression of tumor growth. In vivo, C188-9 administered at 50 mg/kg/day significantly inhibits the growth of UM-SCC-17B xenografts, a radioresistant HNSCC model, correlating with a 57% reduction in tumor pSTAT3 levels. The compound also targets cancer stem cells (CSCs), which rely on STAT3 for self-renewal and are responsible for tumor recurrence and metastasis; by disrupting STAT3 signaling, C188-9 reduces the CSC population and sensitizes tumors to eradication. Importantly, the inhibition is reversible and non-covalent, minimizing the risk of idiosyncratic toxicity. The high tumor-to-plasma concentration ratio (nearly 3-fold) ensures effective target engagement within the tumor microenvironment. These properties make C188-9 and its derivatives ideal candidates for treating STAT3-driven cancers, either as monotherapy or in combination with other modalities, and represent a significant advance over prior art inhibitors that lack sufficient potency, selectivity, or drug-like characteristics for clinical development.

### IV. TARGETING CANCER STEM CELLS

A critical aspect of the present invention is its ability to target and eradicate cancer stem cells (CSCs), a subpopulation of tumor cells that possess self-renewal capacity, multipotency, and enhanced resistance to conventional therapies, thereby driving tumor initiation, maintenance, relapse, and metastasis. CSCs are maintained in part by constitutive activation of STAT3, which regulates key stemness genes such as Nanog, Oct4, and Sox2, and promotes survival under stress conditions such as hypoxia or genotoxic insult. In head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), CSCs have been implicated in radioresistance and disease recurrence, and their persistence after treatment is a major cause of therapeutic failure. The compounds of the present invention, particularly C188-9, effectively disrupt STAT3 signaling in CSCs, leading to loss of self-renewal capacity and induction of differentiation or apoptosis. Unlike many conventional chemotherapeutics that preferentially kill bulk tumor cells while sparing CSCs, C188-9 penetrates the tumor niche and achieves high intratumoral concentrations, ensuring exposure to the CSC compartment. In preclinical models, treatment with C188-9 reduces the frequency of ALDH-positive or CD44-high CSCs in HNSCC cell lines and impairs sphere-forming ability in vitro, a hallmark of stemness. Moreover, in vivo studies demonstrate that C188-9 not only inhibits primary tumor growth but also prevents the outgrowth of recurrent tumors after cessation of treatment, suggesting durable eradication of the CSC pool. The dual inhibition of STAT3 and STAT1 by C188-9 is particularly advantageous in this context, as both transcription factors contribute to the survival and stress adaptation of CSCs. For example, STAT1 activation in response to radiation or interferon signaling can upregulate DNA repair genes and anti-apoptotic proteins in CSCs, and simultaneous blockade of both pathways by C188-9 overcomes this compensatory mechanism. This comprehensive targeting of CSCs addresses a fundamental limitation of current therapies and positions the compounds of the invention as potential curative agents for aggressive and refractory malignancies.

### V. COMBINATION THERAPY

The compounds of the present invention are particularly well-suited for use in combination therapy, where they can synergize with existing modalities such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy to overcome resistance and improve clinical outcomes. In head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), resistance to ionizing radiation (IR) is a major clinical challenge, often mediated by persistent activation of STAT3 and STAT1, which upregulate DNA repair genes, anti-apoptotic proteins, and interferon-related damage signature (IRDS) genes. C188-9, by simultaneously inhibiting both STAT3 and STAT1, sensitizes HNSCC cells to IR, as demonstrated by the significant reduction in tumor growth and pSTAT3/pSTAT1 levels in xenograft models treated with C188-9 plus radiation compared to either modality alone. Similarly, combination with chemotherapy agents such as cisplatin or 5-fluorouracil is expected to enhance cytotoxicity by suppressing STAT3-mediated survival signals that protect tumor cells from drug-induced apoptosis. The favorable safety profile of C188-9, with no observed toxicity in rodents or dogs at high doses, supports its compatibility with combination regimens. Additionally, given the role of STAT3 in immune evasion—through suppression of dendritic cell maturation, promotion of regulatory T cells, and inhibition of cytotoxic T-cell function—combining C188-9 with immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., anti-PD-1 or anti-CTLA-4 antibodies) may reverse the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and enhance anti-tumor immunity. The invention thus provides methods for administering the compound concurrently or sequentially with radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy, with dosing schedules optimized to maximize synergy while minimizing overlapping toxicities. Such combinations represent a rational and clinically translatable strategy to address the multifactorial nature of treatment resistance in advanced cancers.

## EXAMPLES

### Exemplary Materials and Methods

The experimental procedures employed in the development and characterization of the compounds of the present invention were conducted in accordance with established protocols in molecular biology, cell culture, and pharmacology. Human head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) cell lines—including SCC-9, SCC-15, HN5, UM-SCC-1, SCC-61, SQ-20B, SCC-35, UM-SCC-17B, HN30, and HN31—were obtained from academic collaborators and authenticated by short tandem repeat (STR) profiling at 14 loci. Cells were maintained in appropriate media (DMEM/F12 or DMEM) supplemented with 10–20% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and incubated at 37°C in 5–7% CO2, depending on the line. The primary human esophageal epithelial cell line (HEEpiC) served as a non-tumor control and was cultured in Epithelial Cell Medium-2 (EpiCM-2) for no more than one month post-thawing. For virtual ligand screening, the C188 scaffold was used as a reference structure to perform 2D fingerprint similarity screening against the Life Chemicals database (490,000 compounds) using Unity/Sybyl/Tripos software with a 100% Tanimoto coefficient cutoff, yielding 207 hits. These were converted to 3D structures and subjected to pharmacophore sorting based on distance bins of molecular features, with the top 39 compounds (pharmacophore score >70) selected for purchase and testing. Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assays were performed using a Biacore 3000 biosensor to measure STAT3 binding to biotinylated EGFR pY1068 peptides in the presence or absence of test compounds. Phosphoflow cytometry was conducted on Kasumi-1 cells stimulated with G-CSF, followed by intracellular staining for pSTAT3, pSTAT1, and pSTAT5 and analysis on a BD LSR2 flow cytometer. Luminex bead-based assays quantified pSTAT1, pSTAT3, and GAPDH levels in cell lysates using fluorescently labeled antibodies. Microscale thermophoresis (MST) determined binding affinities (KD) by titrating compounds against fluorescently labeled STAT3β (127–722) and measuring thermophoretic movement under an infrared laser. Anchorage-dependent and -independent growth assays used MTT staining in 96-well plates (with or without ultra-low attachment coating) to assess cell viability after 48–72 hours of compound exposure. UM-SCC-17B xenografts were established by injecting 1.5 × 106 cells into the tongues of athymic nude mice, and tumor volumes were monitored twice weekly after randomization to treatment groups (DMSO, C188, or C188-9 at 50 mg/kg/day IP, 5 times/week). RNA sequencing was performed on cryosliced tumor tissues using Illumina TruSeq kits, with RNA integrity verified by Agilent Bioanalyzer (RIN >9.0). Pharmacokinetic studies in C57BL/6 and nude mice assessed plasma and tumor concentrations of C188-9 after oral or IP administration via LC-MS/MS. Statistical analysis employed Student’s t-test or paired t-test, with significance set at p < 0.05.

### Identification by VLS of Compounds that Blocked Stat3 Binding to its Phosphopeptide Ligand and Inhibited IL-6-Mediated Phosphorylation of STAT3

Virtual ligand screening (VLS) was employed to identify novel STAT3 inhibitors by docking 920,000 small molecules from eight chemical libraries into the pY-binding pocket of the STAT3 SH2 domain. This approach yielded three initial hits—C3, C30, and C188—that demonstrated reversible inhibitory activity in both a STAT3-phosphopeptide binding assay and a ligand-induced STAT3 phosphorylation assay. C188, in particular, exhibited an EC50 of 0.7 μM in inducing apoptosis in the breast cancer cell line MDA-MB-468, establishing it as a promising scaffold for further optimization. To enhance potency, a hit-to-lead program was initiated using C188 as the reference structure. A 2D fingerprint similarity screen of 490,000 compounds in the Life Chemicals database, using a 100% Tanimoto coefficient cutoff, identified 207 structurally similar molecules. These were then subjected to 3D pharmacophore analysis, which ranked compounds based on the spatial arrangement of key molecular features. The top 39 compounds with pharmacophore scores exceeding 70 were acquired and tested in STAT3 inhibition assays. Of these, nine derivatives—designated C188-1, C188-7, C188-8, C188-9, C188-15, C188-16, C188-17, C188-18, and C188-19—showed improved inhibition of STAT3 binding to pY-peptide compared to C188, as evidenced by left-shifted binding curves in SPR assays. Notably, none of the compounds ranked below position 21 in the pharmacophore list demonstrated activity comparable to or better than C188, validating the predictive power of the combined 2D/3D screening approach. These results confirmed that strategic modification of the C188 scaffold could yield second-generation probes with enhanced STAT3 inhibitory activity, setting the stage for further characterization of the most promising candidates.

### Compound-Mediated Inhibition of Ligand-Stimulated Phosphorylation of STAT3 is Specific for Stat3 Vs. Stat1

To evaluate the specificity of the C188 derivatives, their ability to inhibit ligand-stimulated phosphorylation of STAT3 versus STAT1 was assessed using phosphoflow cytometry and Luminex bead-based assays. Kasumi-1 cells were serum-starved and pre-treated with increasing concentrations of each compound before stimulation with G-CSF, a cytokine known to activate both STAT3 and STAT1. Phosphoflow analysis revealed that four compounds—C188-7, C188-8, C188-9, and C188-15—inhibited G-CSF-induced pSTAT3 with IC50 values ranging from 3.3 to 10.5 μM, significantly more potent than C188 (IC50 = 16.8 μM). Surprisingly, C188-9 also demonstrated potent inhibition of G-CSF-induced pSTAT1, with an IC50 of 4.1 μM in Luminex assays, indicating dual activity. In contrast, C188-7, C188-8, and C188-15 showed toxicity in normal murine bone marrow colony formation assays, whereas C188-9 did not, highlighting its superior selectivity for malignant over normal cells. Additional experiments with IFN-γ stimulation confirmed that C188-9 inhibited STAT1 phosphorylation with an IC50 of 9.5 μM, further supporting its dual inhibitory profile. These findings were unexpected, as the initial design focused on STAT3 selectivity, but proved advantageous given the emerging role of STAT1 in therapy resistance. The specificity of C188-9 for STAT3/STAT1 over other STAT family members (e.g., STAT5) was confirmed by the lack of effect on pSTAT5 levels in the same assays, demonstrating that the compound’s activity is confined to the intended targets. This dual specificity, combined with a lack of off-target toxicity, positions C188-9 as a uniquely valuable tool for targeting both arms of the STAT signaling axis in resistant cancers.

### Sequence Analysis and Molecular Modeling of the Interaction of Each Compound with the Stat3 Vs. Stat1 SH2 Domain

To understand the molecular basis for the dual inhibitory activity of C188-9, sequence alignment and molecular modeling studies were performed comparing the SH2 domains of STAT3 and STAT1. The SH2 domains share 62% amino acid identity, with near-complete conservation of residues lining the pY-binding pocket, including Arg609, Ser611, Ser613, and Glu614 in STAT3 (corresponding to Arg602, Ser604, Ser606, and Glu607 in STAT1). This high degree of homology explains why compounds designed to fit the STAT3 pY pocket can also engage STAT1. Docking simulations of C188-9 into both SH2 domains revealed that the binapthalyl group at the 3-position of the naphthyl ring occupies a hydrophobic cleft adjacent to the pY site, forming van der Waals contacts with conserved residues such as Leu612 (STAT3) / Leu605 (STAT1). The 4-hydroxy group forms a hydrogen bond with the backbone carbonyl of Glu614 (STAT3) / Glu607 (STAT1), a interaction critical for high-affinity binding, as evidenced by the reduced activity of Group II compounds lacking this hydrogen bond donor. The sulfonamide moiety engages in electrostatic interactions with the conserved arginine residue (Arg609/Arg602), mimicking the phosphate group of pY. These complementary interactions account for the nanomolar binding affinity of C188-9 for both STAT3 (KD = 4.7 nM) and STAT1, as confirmed by MST. The modeling also rationalized the SAR observations: bulkier 3-R substituents like binapthalyl enhance hydrophobic packing, while polar groups disrupt the binding interface. This structural insight guided the design of future derivatives and confirmed that dual inhibition is an inherent property of the optimized scaffold, rather than an off-target effect.

### Example 5

In a comprehensive in vivo efficacy study, the anti-tumor activity of C188-9 was evaluated in a xenograft model of radioresistant head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). UM-SCC-17B cells, derived from a patient who failed multiple rounds of radiotherapy, were implanted into the tongues of athymic nude mice, and once tumors were established (average volume ~15–20 mm³), mice were randomized to receive daily intraperitoneal injections of vehicle (DMSO), C188 (50 mg/kg), or C188-9 (50 mg/kg) five times per week. Tumor volumes were measured twice weekly, and after 21 days of treatment, animals were euthanized for tissue analysis. Mice treated with C188-9 exhibited marked suppression of tumor growth compared to vehicle controls (p = 0.027), with average tumor volumes reduced by over 60%. In contrast, C188 showed no significant anti-tumor effect, consistent with its lower potency in cellular assays. Immunoblotting of tumor lysates revealed that C188-9 treatment reduced pSTAT3 levels by 57% (p = 0.017) and pSTAT1 levels by 80% (p = 0.000003), whereas C188 had no significant impact on either phosphoprotein. RNA sequencing of tumor tissues identified 384 differentially expressed genes in C188-9-treated xenografts, including 76 known STAT3 targets and 40 STAT1-regulated genes, many of which are implicated in radioresistance (e.g., OASL, IFIT3, MX2, IRF7). Notably, C188-9 downregulated 24 of 38 STAT3-upregulated oncogenes and 14 of 14 STAT3-downregulated/STAT1-upregulated genes, confirming dual pathway modulation. Pharmacokinetic analysis showed that C188-9 accumulated in tumors at concentrations nearly three-fold higher than in plasma, ensuring effective target engagement. These results demonstrate that C188-9 is a potent, dual STAT3/STAT1 inhibitor with robust in vivo efficacy against therapy-resistant HNSCC, supporting its advancement to clinical development.
