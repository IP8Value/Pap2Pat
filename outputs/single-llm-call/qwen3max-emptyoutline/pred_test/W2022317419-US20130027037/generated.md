# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging has emerged as a powerful and non-invasive modality for the comprehensive assessment of cardiac structure, function, and pathology. Among its many applications, real-time cine CMR plays a critical role in the evaluation of myocardial wall motion under conditions of physiological stress, particularly during or immediately following exercise. Unlike conventional segmented cine acquisitions that rely on electrocardiographic (ECG) gating and patient breath-holding to achieve high spatial and temporal fidelity, real-time cine imaging operates without such constraints, thereby enabling dynamic visualization of the beating heart in free-breathing subjects. This capability is especially valuable in the context of exercise stress testing, where rapid acquisition is essential to capture transient wall motion abnormalities that may vanish within seconds after cessation of physical exertion.

Despite these advantages, real-time cine CMR faces significant technical challenges that compromise image quality. The primary limitations stem from the inherent trade-offs between spatial resolution, temporal resolution, and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). In conventional real-time imaging protocols, the need to maintain a high frame rate—typically exceeding 30 frames per second to adequately resolve cardiac motion at elevated heart rates—necessitates substantial undersampling of k-space. This undersampling, while enabling faster data acquisition, introduces aliasing artifacts and reduces SNR, both of which degrade diagnostic utility. Moreover, the absence of breath-holding during post-exercise imaging exacerbates motion-related artifacts, including ghosting caused by respiratory and chest wall movement, further complicating image interpretation.

To address these challenges, parallel imaging techniques have been widely adopted to accelerate data acquisition while preserving image quality. Among these, the Generalized Autocalibrating Partially Parallel Acquisitions (GRAPPA) method and its variants have become standard tools in clinical CMR. GRAPPA reconstructs missing k-space data by estimating linear convolution kernels from autocalibration signal (ACS) lines, leveraging the spatial sensitivity profiles of multiple receiver coils. However, conventional GRAPPA implementations assume static coil sensitivity maps, which may not hold true in dynamic imaging scenarios involving significant physiological motion. This limitation becomes particularly pronounced in real-time exercise stress cine, where rapid changes in heart position, respiration, and blood flow can cause temporal variations in coil sensitivities that are not captured by static calibration.

More recently, the SPIRIT (Iterative Self-Consistent Parallel Imaging Reconstruction) framework has been introduced as an advanced alternative to GRAPPA. SPIRIT formulates image reconstruction as an iterative optimization problem that enforces consistency between measured k-space data and a model of coil sensitivity-encoded image content. By incorporating a self-consistency condition across all coils and time frames, SPIRIT can achieve higher reconstruction accuracy and better noise suppression than traditional linear methods. Nevertheless, even the original SPIRIT approach relies on fixed or slowly varying coil sensitivity estimates, which may be insufficient for capturing the dynamic nature of post-exercise cardiac imaging.

In light of these limitations, there remains a pressing need for a reconstruction methodology that explicitly accounts for the temporal evolution of coil sensitivities during real-time exercise stress cine imaging. Such a method must not only improve SNR but also mitigate motion-induced artifacts without compromising temporal fidelity. Furthermore, it should be computationally feasible for clinical deployment and compatible with existing hardware and acquisition protocols. The present invention addresses these unmet needs by introducing a novel extension of the SPIRIT framework—termed Temporal SPIRIT (TSPIRIT)—that integrates temporal coil sensitivity estimation with spatial regularization to significantly enhance image quality in real-time exercise stress cardiac cine imaging. This innovation enables robust, high-fidelity visualization of myocardial wall motion under stress conditions, thereby improving the diagnostic accuracy and clinical utility of CMR in the evaluation of ischemic heart disease and other cardiac pathologies.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel method and system for reconstructing high-quality real-time cardiac cine images from undersampled magnetic resonance (MR) data acquired during or immediately following physical exercise stress. Specifically, the invention introduces an enhanced image reconstruction algorithm—referred to herein as Temporal SPIRIT (TSPIRIT)—that extends the conventional SPIRIT (Iterative Self-Consistent Parallel Imaging Reconstruction) framework by incorporating two key innovations: (1) temporal estimation of coil sensitivity profiles that evolve over time in response to physiological motion, and (2) spatial regularization to suppress noise and artifacts while preserving anatomical detail. This dual enhancement enables significant improvements in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) without introducing or exacerbating ghosting artifacts commonly associated with respiratory and chest wall motion in free-breathing subjects.

In accordance with the invention, raw k-space data are acquired using a time-interleaved undersampling pattern during real-time exercise stress cine imaging, typically employing a balanced steady-state free precession (bSSFP) sequence with high acceleration factors (e.g., R = 4). The acquired data include autocalibration signal (ACS) lines derived from temporal averaging of interleaved k-space frames, which serve as the basis for initial coil sensitivity estimation. A preliminary reconstruction is performed using a conventional parallel imaging technique such as GRAPPA to generate full k-space estimates for each temporal frame. These estimates are then refined through Karhunen-Loève Transform (KLT) filtering to enhance the stability and accuracy of coil sensitivity maps across time.

The core of the TSPIRIT algorithm involves an iterative nonlinear reconstruction process that jointly optimizes the image series and the time-varying coil sensitivity profiles. At each iteration, the algorithm enforces data consistency with the acquired undersampled k-space measurements while simultaneously updating the coil sensitivity estimates based on the current image reconstruction. This self-consistent loop ensures that the reconstructed images are consistent not only with the measured data but also with the dynamically evolving sensitivity characteristics of the multi-channel receiver coil array. To further improve image quality, a spatial regularization term—such as total variation (TV) or wavelet-based sparsity—is incorporated into the optimization objective. This regularization penalizes spatially incoherent noise and artifacts while preserving edges and fine anatomical structures, thereby enhancing both visual clarity and quantitative accuracy.

The resulting TSPIRIT-reconstructed images exhibit substantially higher SNR compared to conventional reconstruction methods such as TGRAPPA, with empirical studies demonstrating an average SNR gain of 38.2% in healthy volunteers undergoing post-exercise real-time cine imaging. Importantly, this improvement is achieved without increasing ghosting artifacts, as confirmed by quantitative analysis of peak spatial cross-correlation ratios along the phase-encoding direction. The method is fully compatible with standard clinical MR scanners, multi-channel coil arrays, and existing real-time acquisition protocols, requiring no modifications to hardware or pulse sequences. Consequently, the invention provides a practical and effective solution for improving the diagnostic quality of exercise stress cardiac cine imaging, facilitating more reliable detection of stress-induced wall motion abnormalities and supporting better clinical decision-making in the management of cardiovascular disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS AND PRESENTLY PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a system and method for reconstructing high-fidelity real-time cardiac cine images from undersampled magnetic resonance (MR) data acquired during or immediately following physical exercise stress. The invention is particularly suited for use in cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging environments where rapid heart rates, irregular breathing, and motion artifacts pose significant challenges to conventional image reconstruction techniques. Below is a detailed description of the presently preferred embodiments of the invention, including the underlying methodology, computational architecture, and integration with existing MR imaging systems.

The invention is implemented within a standard clinical MR imaging workflow, beginning with data acquisition on a commercially available MR scanner equipped with a multi-channel phased-array receiver coil. In the preferred embodiment, imaging is performed on a 1.5 Tesla (T) whole-body MR system (e.g., Siemens Avanto) using a 32-channel cardiac coil (e.g., Rapid Biomedical GmbH). Subjects undergo a standardized exercise protocol using an MR-compatible treadmill system positioned adjacent to the scanner bore, allowing immediate transition from exercise to imaging. Following peak exertion, subjects are rapidly moved into the scanner for real-time cine imaging without requiring breath-holding or ECG gating, thereby capturing the transient hemodynamic and mechanical effects of exercise stress.

Image acquisition employs a balanced steady-state free precession (bSSFP) sequence, which is well-suited for real-time cardiac imaging due to its high signal efficiency and inherent contrast between myocardium and blood pool. The sequence parameters are optimized for speed and compatibility with high acceleration factors: repetition time (TR) of 1.09 milliseconds, echo time (TE) of 0.9 milliseconds, flip angle of 58 degrees, field of view yielding an in-plane resolution of 2.44 × 2.44 mm², and receiver bandwidth of 1420 Hz. To achieve the necessary temporal resolution—typically 30–50 frames per second—k-space is undersampled at an acceleration factor of 4 using a time-interleaved sampling pattern. This pattern acquires different subsets of k-space lines in successive temporal frames, ensuring that each frame contains complementary information while maintaining sufficient temporal coverage for dynamic reconstruction.

A critical aspect of the acquisition protocol is the inclusion of autocalibration signal (ACS) lines. In the preferred embodiment, ACS lines are not acquired as separate central k-space samples but are instead generated by temporally averaging the undersampled k-space frames. Because the time-interleaved sampling ensures that all k-space locations are eventually sampled over multiple frames, averaging across time yields a fully sampled central region of k-space that serves as a robust estimate of the ACS data. This approach eliminates the need for dedicated ACS acquisitions, thereby maximizing the efficiency of the real-time sequence and minimizing dead time between frames.

Following data acquisition, the raw k-space data are processed using the TSPIRIT reconstruction algorithm, which constitutes the core innovation of the present invention. The reconstruction pipeline begins with an initial estimate of the image series using a conventional parallel imaging method, such as TGRAPPA (Temporal GRAPPA). In this step, GRAPPA kernels are calibrated from the temporally averaged ACS lines and applied to each undersampled k-space frame to synthesize the missing k-space data. The resulting full k-space datasets are then inverse Fourier transformed to produce a preliminary time series of complex-valued images.

To improve the accuracy of coil sensitivity estimation, the preliminary image series undergoes Karhunen-Loève Transform (KLT) filtering. KLT is a data-driven dimensionality reduction technique that decomposes the temporal image series into a set of orthogonal eigenimages ranked by their temporal energy content. By retaining only the dominant eigenimages—which correspond to coherent cardiac motion—and discarding higher-order components dominated by noise and incoherent motion, KLT effectively denoises the image series and stabilizes the subsequent sensitivity estimation. The filtered image series is then used to compute time-varying coil sensitivity maps via element-wise division of individual coil images by a combined reference image (e.g., the square root of the sum of squares across coils).

With these refined sensitivity estimates, the TSPIRIT reconstruction proceeds to its iterative optimization phase. The algorithm formulates the reconstruction problem as a constrained minimization of a cost function comprising three main components: (1) a data fidelity term that enforces consistency between the reconstructed images and the acquired undersampled k-space data, (2) a self-consistency term that couples the image series with the time-varying coil sensitivity profiles, and (3) a spatial regularization term that promotes smoothness or sparsity in the image domain.

Mathematically, the optimization problem can be expressed as:

minimizeₓ,ₛ ∑ₜ ‖Eₜ(xₜ) − yₜ‖² + λ₁ ∑ₜ ‖Sₜ xₜ − zₜ‖² + λ₂ R(x)

where xₜ denotes the image at time frame t, yₜ is the corresponding undersampled k-space data, Eₜ is the encoding operator that includes Fourier transform and coil sensitivity modulation, Sₜ represents the estimated coil sensitivity map at time t, zₜ is an intermediate image estimate (e.g., from GRAPPA), R(x) is the spatial regularization functional (e.g., total variation), and λ₁, λ₂ are weighting parameters that balance the contributions of the self-consistency and regularization terms.

The optimization is solved using a nested iterative scheme. An outer loop updates the coil sensitivity maps based on the current image estimate, while an inner loop solves for the image series given fixed sensitivities. The inner loop employs a nonlinear conjugate gradient (NCG) solver, which efficiently handles the large-scale nature of the problem and converges rapidly due to the smoothness imposed by regularization. The outer loop iterates until convergence criteria are met, typically defined by a small change in the image norm or a maximum number of iterations (e.g., 5–10 outer loops).

In the preferred embodiment, spatial regularization is implemented using isotropic total variation (TV), which penalizes the L1 norm of the image gradient magnitude. This choice effectively suppresses noise and incoherent artifacts while preserving sharp edges and fine anatomical details—critical for accurate assessment of endocardial borders and wall motion. Alternative regularization strategies, such as wavelet sparsity or non-local means, may also be employed depending on the specific clinical application and desired trade-off between noise suppression and structural fidelity.

The final output of the TSPIRIT algorithm is a time-resolved series of high-SNR, artifact-suppressed cardiac cine images suitable for clinical interpretation. Quantitative evaluation in a cohort of 10 healthy volunteers demonstrated that TSPIRIT achieves an average SNR gain of 38.2% ± 17.8% compared to conventional TGRAPPA reconstruction, with statistical significance (p < 1×10⁻⁵). Importantly, ghosting artifacts—quantified by the peak spatial cross-correlation ratio along the phase-encoding direction—showed no significant increase (p = 0.960), confirming that the temporal sensitivity modeling does not introduce spurious motion-related artifacts. Visual inspection by experienced cardiologists corroborated these findings, with TSPIRIT images consistently rated superior in terms of endocardial definition, myocardial texture, and overall diagnostic confidence.

The computational implementation of TSPIRIT is designed for clinical feasibility. The algorithm is coded in a high-performance computing environment (e.g., MATLAB or C++ with CUDA acceleration) and executed on a workstation equipped with a multi-core CPU and GPU. Reconstruction times for a typical 30-frame cine series (160×80 matrix, 3 slices) range from 2 to 5 minutes, which is acceptable for offline processing in a stress CMR workflow. Future implementations may leverage real-time GPU processing to enable near-immediate reconstruction and display, further enhancing clinical utility.

In summary, the presently preferred embodiment of the invention provides a complete and practical solution for high-quality real-time exercise stress cardiac cine imaging. By integrating temporal coil sensitivity estimation with spatial regularization within an iterative SPIRIT framework, the method overcomes the fundamental limitations of conventional parallel imaging in dynamic, motion-prone scenarios. The result is a robust, clinically deployable reconstruction technique that significantly improves SNR and image quality without compromising temporal fidelity or introducing additional artifacts, thereby advancing the state of the art in cardiovascular magnetic resonance imaging.
