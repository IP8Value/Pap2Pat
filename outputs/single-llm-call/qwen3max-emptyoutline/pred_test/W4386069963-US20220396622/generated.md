# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic antibodies, specifically engineered immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibodies with silenced or substantially reduced effector functions while maintaining favorable biophysical properties and antigen-binding capabilities. More particularly, the invention provides novel IgG1 Fc variants comprising specific combinations of amino acid substitutions in the lower hinge and CH2 domain that effectively abrogate binding to activating Fc gamma receptors (FcγRs) and complement component C1q, thereby eliminating antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). These engineered Fc variants retain critical functional attributes such as binding to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) for extended serum half-life and binding to Protein A for efficient purification during manufacturing. Furthermore, the disclosed variants exhibit biophysical stability profiles comparable to wild-type IgG1, including resistance to acid-induced aggregation—a critical parameter for large-scale antibody production and formulation. The invention is particularly relevant for the development of T cell-engaging bispecific antibodies, where unintended immune activation through Fc-mediated effector functions can lead to severe adverse effects, including cytokine release syndrome and off-target cytotoxicity. By providing a robust platform for generating “silent” IgG1 antibodies with superior developability characteristics, the invention addresses a long-standing need in the biopharmaceutical industry for safer, more manufacturable, and clinically effective therapeutic antibodies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Therapeutic monoclonal antibodies have become a cornerstone of modern medicine, with applications spanning oncology, autoimmune disorders, infectious diseases, and inflammatory conditions. Among the immunoglobulin subclasses, IgG1 is the most commonly used scaffold for therapeutic antibodies due to its high expression yield, favorable pharmacokinetics, and well-established manufacturing protocols. However, the native Fc region of IgG1 engages multiple components of the human immune system, including activating Fc gamma receptors (FcγRI/CD64, FcγRIIa/CD32a, FcγRIIc/CD32c, and FcγRIIIa/CD16a) and the complement cascade via C1q binding. While these effector functions are desirable in certain contexts—such as in anti-cancer antibodies that rely on ADCC or CDC for tumor cell killing—they are often detrimental in other therapeutic settings. For example, in T cell-engaging bispecific antibodies designed to redirect cytotoxic T cells to tumor cells, Fc-mediated binding to FcγR-expressing immune cells (e.g., monocytes, macrophages, NK cells) can trigger uncontrolled immune activation, leading to cytokine release syndrome, systemic inflammation, and off-target tissue damage. Similarly, in antibodies targeting soluble antigens or non-cellular epitopes, effector functions provide no therapeutic benefit and only increase the risk of adverse events.

To mitigate these risks, significant efforts have been devoted to developing “effector-silenced” or “Fc-silenced” antibody variants. Early approaches involved switching the constant region from IgG1 to IgG2 or IgG4, which naturally exhibit reduced FcγR and C1q binding. However, these subclasses suffer from inherent biophysical liabilities: IgG2 is prone to structural heterogeneity due to disulfide bond scrambling, and both IgG2 and IgG4 display increased susceptibility to acid-induced aggregation during downstream processing, particularly during the low-pH viral inactivation step that is standard in antibody manufacturing. Moreover, IgG4 undergoes Fab-arm exchange in vivo, potentially leading to loss of target specificity and formation of inactive or cross-reactive species. These drawbacks have motivated the engineering of IgG1-based Fc variants that retain the superior biophysical and pharmacokinetic properties of IgG1 while eliminating unwanted effector functions.

Structure-function studies have elucidated the molecular determinants of Fc interactions. The primary binding site for FcγRs resides in the lower hinge region (residues 233–238, EU numbering) and the proximal CH2 domain, with key residues L234, L235, and P329 playing critical roles. Similarly, C1q binding overlaps with this region, involving residues D270, K322, P329, and P331. In contrast, FcRn binding occurs at the CH2-CH3 interface and is pH-dependent, enabling IgG recycling and long serum half-life. Protein A binding, essential for purification, also localizes to the CH2-CH3 junction. Rational engineering strategies have thus focused on mutating residues in the lower hinge and CH2 domain to disrupt FcγR and C1q engagement while preserving FcRn and Protein A interactions.

Several mutation sets have been reported in the literature and clinical pipelines. The L234A/L235A (LALA) double mutation was among the first to reduce, but not eliminate, FcγR binding. Subsequent combinations, such as LALA-P329G (LALA-PG), achieved near-complete silencing of effector functions. Other variants include N297A/G (which removes the conserved N-linked glycan and abolishes FcγR/C1q binding but may compromise stability), L234F/L235E-P331S (LFLE-PS), and L234F/L235E-D265A (LFLE-DA). Despite these advances, many existing Fc-silencing mutations introduce biophysical liabilities, particularly reduced thermal stability of the CH2 domain and increased aggregation propensity under acidic conditions. For instance, D265A and P331S, when combined with lower hinge mutations, have been shown to significantly lower the melting temperature (Tm) of the CH2 domain and exacerbate acid-induced aggregation—critical concerns for process development and product shelf life.

Moreover, the sensitivity of binding assays greatly influences the assessment of “complete” silencing. Standard cell-based assays using monomeric antibodies may fail to detect residual FcγR binding that becomes apparent under high-avidity conditions, such as when antibodies are pre-complexed with multivalent antigen. Such residual binding could still trigger effector functions in vivo, especially in tissues with high antigen density or immune cell infiltration. Therefore, there remains a pressing need for Fc variants that demonstrate truly undetectable binding to FcγRs and C1q even under highly sensitive assay conditions, while maintaining wild-type-like biophysical properties, including resistance to low-pH stress, low self-interaction, and high monomer content.

The present invention addresses these unmet needs by identifying and characterizing novel combinations of lower hinge and CH2 mutations—specifically, S267K, P329G, and P329A in combination with LALA or LALE (L234A/L235E)—that achieve complete abrogation of FcγR and C1q binding across multiple orthogonal assay formats, including surface plasmon resonance (SPR), biolayer interferometry (BLI), and highly avid cell-based and bead-based assays. Critically, these variants exhibit CH2 melting temperatures and acid-induced aggregation profiles indistinguishable from or superior to wild-type IgG1, thereby offering a robust platform for the development of next-generation therapeutic antibodies with enhanced safety and manufacturability.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides engineered immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibodies and Fc-containing polypeptides comprising specific combinations of amino acid substitutions that effectively silence effector functions while preserving essential biophysical and functional properties. In particular, the invention discloses IgG1 variants that incorporate mutations in the lower hinge region—specifically L234A/L235A (LALA) or L234A/L235E (LALE)—in combination with one or more mutations in the CH2 domain selected from the group consisting of S267K, P329G, and P329A (EU numbering). These combinations result in antibodies that exhibit no detectable binding to activating Fc gamma receptors (FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa) and complement component C1q under highly sensitive assay conditions, including high-avidity cell-based and bead-based formats, thereby eliminating antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC).

Remarkably, these Fc-silenced variants retain wild-type-like binding to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) at acidic pH, ensuring normal serum half-life and pharmacokinetics, and maintain strong affinity for Protein A, enabling efficient purification during manufacturing. Furthermore, the variants demonstrate superior biophysical stability compared to previously reported Fc-silencing mutations: they exhibit CH2 domain melting temperatures (Tm) comparable to wild-type IgG1 and show minimal or no increase in acid-induced aggregation following exposure to pH 3.5—a condition that mimics the low-pH viral inactivation step in antibody production. This resistance to acid stress is a critical advantage over variants containing D265A or P331S, which display significantly reduced CH2 Tm and elevated aggregation under identical conditions.

The invention encompasses full-length IgG1 antibodies, antigen-binding fragments (e.g., Fab, scFv) fused to the engineered Fc, and bispecific or multispecific constructs incorporating the disclosed Fc variants. The antibodies may be directed against any therapeutic target, with particular utility in T cell-engaging bispecific antibodies where elimination of Fc-mediated off-target immune activation is essential for safety. The invention also includes nucleic acids encoding the engineered heavy chains, expression vectors, host cells for production, and pharmaceutical compositions comprising the antibodies. Methods of treating diseases—such as cancer, autoimmune disorders, and inflammatory conditions—using the disclosed antibodies are further provided. By combining complete effector function silencing with excellent developability characteristics, the invention offers a transformative platform for the next generation of safer, more manufacturable, and clinically effective antibody therapeutics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that specific combinations of amino acid substitutions in the lower hinge and CH2 domain of human IgG1 Fc confer complete abrogation of effector functions without compromising biophysical stability or key functional properties such as FcRn and Protein A binding. The engineered Fc variants described herein represent a significant advancement over prior art, as they address the dual challenges of achieving true “silencing” under physiologically relevant, high-avidity conditions and maintaining robustness during manufacturing and storage.

The core of the invention lies in the strategic pairing of lower hinge mutations with select CH2 domain mutations. The lower hinge mutations include L234A/L235A (LALA) and L234A/L235E (LALE), both of which reduce FcγR binding but do not fully eliminate it, particularly under high-avidity conditions. When combined with CH2 mutations S267K, P329G, or P329A, however, the resulting variants exhibit no detectable binding to FcγRI, FcγRIIa, or FcγRIIIa in both surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and highly sensitive cell-based assays using THP-1 monocytes pre-complexed with multivalent CD3ε-BSA conjugates. Similarly, these combinations abolish C1q binding in bead-based assays that detect complement activation even when SPR fails to distinguish subtle differences. This complete silencing is critical for applications such as T cell-engaging bispecifics, where residual FcγR binding could lead to cytokine release or off-target cytotoxicity.

Importantly, the disclosed variants preserve binding to FcRn, as demonstrated by SPR kinetics showing association and dissociation rates comparable to wild-type IgG1. FcRn binding is pH-dependent, occurring in the acidic environment of endosomes (pH ~6.0) and releasing in the neutral pH of blood, thereby mediating IgG recycling and long serum half-life. Retention of this interaction ensures that the engineered antibodies will have pharmacokinetic profiles similar to conventional IgG1 therapeutics. Additionally, all variants maintain strong affinity for Protein A, a bacterial protein used in the first step of antibody purification via affinity chromatography. This is essential for scalable, cost-effective manufacturing, as loss of Protein A binding would necessitate alternative, less efficient purification strategies.

Biophysical characterization reveals that the S267K, P329G, and P329A combinations with LALA or LALE exhibit thermal stability and resistance to acid-induced aggregation that are either equivalent to or better than wild-type IgG1. Differential scanning fluorescence (DSF) measurements show CH2 domain melting temperatures (Tm) within 1–2°C of wild-type (67.5°C), whereas variants containing D265A or P331S exhibit Tm reductions of 4–7°C. This stability directly translates to performance in the pH 3.5 stress assay, a surrogate for the low-pH hold step used in viral inactivation during manufacturing. After one hour at pH 3.5, the S267K, P329G, and P329A variants show minimal increases in aggregate formation (Δ% monomer < 2%), while D265A and P331S variants exhibit substantial aggregation (Δ% monomer > 9%). This resistance to acid stress is a major advantage, as aggregation can lead to product loss, immunogenicity, and regulatory hurdles.

The invention encompasses full-length IgG1 antibodies comprising two identical heavy chains, each containing the engineered Fc region, and two light chains. The variable regions may be derived from any antigen-specific antibody, including human, humanized, or chimeric sequences. The antibodies may be monospecific or part of bispecific or multispecific constructs, such as those employing CrossMab, knobs-into-holes, or other heterodimerization technologies. The Fc variants may also be incorporated into Fc-fusion proteins or other Fc-containing scaffolds.

Nucleic acid molecules encoding the engineered heavy chains are provided, along with expression vectors and host cells (e.g., CHO, HEK293) for recombinant production. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the antibodies in pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, buffers, and excipients are also disclosed, suitable for intravenous, subcutaneous, or other routes of administration. Methods of treating diseases—including hematologic malignancies, solid tumors, autoimmune diseases (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, lupus), and chronic inflammatory conditions—are enabled by the improved safety profile of the effector-silenced antibodies.

In summary, the invention provides a class of IgG1 Fc variants that achieve the long-sought goal of complete effector function silencing without sacrificing developability, manufacturability, or pharmacokinetics. These variants represent a superior platform for next-generation antibody therapeutics, particularly in contexts where immune activation must be strictly avoided.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Antibodies

The anti-CD3ε monoclonal antibody ADI-26906, an IgG1 subclass antibody, was used as the parental scaffold for engineering Fc variants. The variable heavy (VH) and variable light (VL) domains were derived from a humanized antibody specific for human CD3ε. The constant regions comprised human IgG1 heavy chain and human kappa light chain. Wild-type (WT) ADI-26906 and its Fc variants were produced by transient transfection of HEK293 cells using standard protocols. Briefly, plasmids encoding the heavy and light chains were co-transfected using polyethyleneimine (PEI). Culture supernatants were harvested 5–7 days post-transfection and clarified by centrifugation and filtration. Antibodies were purified using MabSelect SuRe Protein A resin (Cytiva) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Eluted antibodies were buffer-exchanged into HEPES-buffered saline (HBS: 25 mM HEPES, 150 mM NaCl, pH 7.3) using tangential flow filtration or size-exclusion chromatography. Purity and concentration were assessed by UV absorbance at 280 nm and confirmed by SDS-PAGE and size-exclusion chromatography (SEC). The Fc variants generated included lower hinge mutants (L234A/L235A [LALA], L234A/L235E [LALE], L234F/L235E [LFLE], and others) and combinations thereof with CH2 domain mutations (S267K, P329G, P329A, D265A, P331S, etc.), as detailed in Tables 1 and 2 of the research paper. All variants retained the ability to bind human CD3ε with affinity comparable to WT, as confirmed by biolayer interferometry (BLI) using Octet HTX, indicating that Fc engineering did not perturb the antigen-binding fragment (Fab). The antibodies were stored at 4°C or −80°C in HBS and used within three months for all assays to ensure consistency.

### Example 2: THP-1 Cell Binding and C1q Binding to Antibodies

Binding of Fc variants to Fcγ receptors on human monocytic THP-1 cells was assessed under both standard and high-avidity conditions. THP-1 cells (DSMZ ACC 16) were cultured in RPMI 1640 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and maintained at 37°C in 5% CO₂. For standard binding assays, 200,000 cells per well were incubated with 100 nM of each IgG variant for 30 minutes on ice. Cells were washed twice with PBSF (PBS + 0.1% BSA) and stained with goat anti-human IgG R-PE (Southern Biotech) for 20 minutes at room temperature. Median fluorescence intensity (MFI) was measured by flow cytometry on a BD FACSCanto II and analyzed using FCS Express software. For high-avidity conditions, variants were pre-complexed with CD3ε-BSA conjugate (New England Peptide), prepared by conjugating CD3ε peptide (residues 21–47) to bovine serum albumin at a ratio of approximately 12 peptides per BSA molecule. Pre-complexing was performed by mixing 100 nM antibody with 10 nM CD3ε-BSA for 20 minutes at room temperature, followed by addition to THP-1 cells and processing as above. This format mimics immune complex formation in vivo and enhances sensitivity to detect low-affinity FcγR interactions. Results showed that while LALA and LALE alone reduced binding to near-background levels under standard conditions, significant residual binding was observed under high-avidity conditions. In contrast, variants incorporating S267K, P329G, or P329A with LALA or LALE exhibited no detectable binding above background in both assay formats.

For C1q binding, a bead-based assay was developed. Streptavidin-coated Dynabeads were loaded with biotinylated CD3ε peptide for 15 minutes at room temperature, washed, and saturated with excess antibody. Beads were then incubated with 100 nM human C1q (Sigma) ± 100 µL human complement (containing C3) for 25 minutes at room temperature. After washing, beads were stained with anti-C1q APC and anti-C3 PE antibodies and analyzed by flow cytometry. This assay detected C1q deposition and downstream complement activation. WT IgG1 showed strong C1q and C3 binding, while LALA and LALE alone abolished C1q binding. However, among CH2 variants, only S267K, P329G, P329A, D270A, and K322A completely eliminated C1q binding, consistent with their role in disrupting the C1q interface.

### Example 3: FcγR Binding by Antibodies

FcγR binding kinetics were quantified using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) on a Biacore 8K+ instrument. Recombinant human FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIa (CD32a), and FcγRIIIa (CD16a) were obtained from commercial sources. A CM5 sensor chip was activated with EDC/NHS, and goat anti-human Fc antibody (Jackson ImmunoResearch) was immobilized via amine coupling to flow cells 1 (reference) and 2. Antibody variants (~400–425 response units, RU) were captured on flow cell 2. FcγRs were injected at 100 nM (for FcγRI) or 1000 nM (for FcγRIIa/IIIa) in HBS-EP+ running buffer (10 mM HEPES pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 3 mM EDTA, 0.05% Surfactant P20) at 30 µL/min for 180 seconds, followed by 300 seconds of dissociation. Surfaces were regenerated with 10 mM glycine pH 1.5. Data were processed by double reference subtraction and fitted to a 1:1 binding model using Biacore Insight Evaluation software. WT IgG1 showed high-affinity binding to FcγRI (KD ~10–100 nM) and weaker but detectable binding to FcγRIIa/IIIa. LALA and LALE reduced FcγRI binding by ~10-fold. Variants with S267K, P329G, or P329A in combination with LALA or LALE showed no detectable binding above background at the concentrations tested, confirming complete abrogation of FcγR engagement. Biolayer interferometry (BLI) on Octet HTX yielded consistent results, with no binding observed for these variants even at higher concentrations.

### Example 4: Developability Assays

A panel of developability assays was employed to assess biophysical properties. Polyspecificity was measured using a polyspecificity reagent (PSR) binding assay: CHO-derived soluble membrane and cytosolic proteins were biotinylated and incubated with yeast-displayed IgGs. Binding was detected with ExtrAvidin-R-PE and normalized to a 0–1 score using control antibodies. All variants scored between 0.33–0.43, indicating low polyspecificity. Self-interaction propensity was evaluated by affinity-capture self-interaction nanoparticle spectroscopy (AC-SINS): gold nanoparticles coated with anti-human Fc were incubated with IgGs, and wavelength shift (Δλmax) was measured. Most variants showed Δλmax < 2 nm, comparable to WT; exceptions included LALA-D270A and LALAGA-P329G, which showed elevated shifts. Hydrophobicity was assessed by hydrophobic interaction chromatography (HIC) on a Sepax Proteomix HIC butyl column using a 1.8 M ammonium sulfate gradient. Retention times were unchanged across all variants, indicating no increase in surface hydrophobicity. Thermal stability was measured by differential scanning fluorescence (DSF): samples (1 mg/mL) mixed with SYPRO Orange were heated from 40–95°C at 0.5°C/min, and CH2 Tm was determined from the first derivative. WT Tm was 67.5°C; S267K, P329G, and P329A variants ranged from 66.0–67.0°C, while D265A and P331S variants dropped to 60.5–63.0°C. Acid-induced aggregation was tested by pH 3.5 stress SEC: antibodies (15 mg/mL) were incubated in 200 mM acetic acid/50 mM NaCl, pH 3.5, for 1 hour at room temperature, then diluted to 1 mg/mL in PBS and analyzed by SEC. Monomer loss (Δ% monomer) was minimal for S267K/P329G/P329A variants (<2%) but severe for D265A/P331S (>9%).

### Example 5: ADCC Activity Assay

Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) was evaluated using a luciferase-based reporter assay. Target cells (PC3 prostate carcinoma, Sigma-Aldrich) were engineered to stably express firefly luciferase. Effector cells were human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) isolated from healthy donors. PC3 cells (10,000/well) were seeded in 96-well plates and incubated with serial dilutions of IgG variants (0.001–100 nM) for 1 hour at 37°C. PBMCs (100,000/well) were added, and co-cultures were incubated for 6 hours. Luciferase activity was measured using Bright-Glo reagent (Promega); luminescence inversely correlated with target cell lysis. WT IgG1 induced potent ADCC with EC50 ~1 nM. LALA and LALE variants showed >100-fold reduction in potency. Variants with S267K, P329G, or P329A in combination with LALA or LALE showed no detectable ADCC activity even at 100 nM, confirming complete silencing of FcγRIIIa-mediated NK cell activation.

### Example 6: Complement Activity Assay

Complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) was assessed using a calcein-AM release assay. Target cells (Raji B lymphoma, ATCC) were labeled with calcein-AM (5 µM, 1 hour, 37°C), washed, and seeded (20,000/well) in 96-well plates. Antibody variants (0.01–1000 nM) were added, followed by 10% human complement serum (Cedarlane) for 2 hours at 37°C. Fluorescence (excitation 485 nm, emission 530 nm) was measured; release of calcein indicated membrane damage. WT IgG1 induced CDC with EC50 ~10 nM. LALA and LALE variants showed no CDC activity, consistent with their inability to bind C1q. All CH2 variants, including S267K, P329G, and P329A, also showed no CDC, confirming that hinge mutations alone are sufficient to ablate complement activation in this context. The bead-based C1q binding assay (Example 2) further validated that these variants do not initiate the classical complement pathway.
