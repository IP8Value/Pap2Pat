# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Disclosure

The present invention relates generally to the field of machine learning and natural language processing, particularly to systems and methods for efficient training of deep neural network models for text classification tasks. More specifically, the invention pertains to active learning techniques that enable the creation of compact, representative surrogate datasets from large unlabeled data pools, which can be used to train deep text classification models with significantly reduced computational resources while maintaining or even improving upon the performance achievable with full training datasets. The invention further relates to the identification and utilization of favorable sampling properties inherent in uncertainty-based query strategies when applied to deep neural network models, particularly demonstrating robustness against various forms of sampling bias and algorithmic factors that have historically plagued active learning approaches.

### Related Art

The development of deep neural networks (DNNs) has revolutionized natural language processing (NLP), enabling state-of-the-art performance across various text classification tasks. However, the practical deployment of these models faces significant challenges related to the substantial computational resources required for training on large datasets and the considerable time and cost associated with obtaining labeled data. Traditional approaches to addressing these challenges have included active learning techniques, which aim to construct smaller, representative datasets by strategically selecting the most informative samples from an unlabeled pool for labeling and subsequent model training.

Early work in active text classification, such as that by Lewis and Gale (1994), established the foundation for greedy query generation using label uncertainty as a means to efficiently create representative samples. This approach demonstrated that uncertainty-based sampling could achieve similar test accuracy compared to using the full training dataset, thereby reducing the labeling burden. However, subsequent research raised concerns about sampling bias inherent in these uncertainty-based approaches, particularly regarding the potential for redundant sampling and lack of diversity in the selected samples. These concerns led to the development of more sophisticated acquisition functions that explicitly incorporated diversity measures, such as those proposed by Brinker (2003) and Hoi et al. (2006), or employed ensemble approaches to implicitly improve diversity, as suggested by Liere and Tadepalli (1997) and McCallum and Nigam (1998).

The adaptation of active learning frameworks to deep learning contexts has introduced additional complexity and uncertainty regarding the transferability of principles established with shallow models. While some researchers, such as Sener and Savarese (2018) and Ducoffe and Precioso (2018), found that uncertainty-based strategies performed no better than random sampling even when using ensembles, others, including Beluch et al. (2018) and Gissin and Shalev-Shwartz (2019), reported that uncertainty measures computed with ensembles outperformed diversity-based approaches. Furthermore, studies by Gal et al. (2017), Beluch et al. (2018), and Siddhant and Lipton (2018) indicated that ensemble-based uncertainty measures surpassed those computed using single models. A recent empirical study by Siddhant and Lipton (2018) investigating active learning in NLP suggested that Bayesian active learning outperformed classical uncertainty sampling across all settings, though these approaches were limited to relatively small datasets.

Despite these advances, significant gaps remain in the understanding of how sampling bias and sample efficiency manifest in deep active learning contexts, particularly when dealing with large-scale datasets that are characteristic of modern deep learning applications. The literature lacks comprehensive empirical investigation into the stability of actively collected query and train sets, the impact of various algorithmic factors on sampling properties, and the generalizability of findings across different deep learning architectures. Moreover, there remains ambiguity regarding what constitutes a "good" sample in the context of deep active learning and whether the theoretical and empirical evidence regarding sampling efficiency and bias translates effectively from shallow to deep models.

Existing approaches have typically focused on either expensive diversity measures or complex ensemble methods to address perceived limitations of uncertainty-based sampling. However, these solutions often introduce significant computational overhead that may offset the benefits of reduced dataset size. Additionally, the relationship between actively acquired samples and fundamental concepts in machine learning, such as support vectors in support vector machines, has not been thoroughly explored in the context of deep learning models. The current state of the art thus presents a fragmented understanding of active learning in deep text classification, with conflicting results and limited empirical validation on large-scale datasets that reflect real-world applications.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides systems and methods for efficient training of deep neural network models for text classification through the strategic application of uncertainty-based active learning techniques. The invention demonstrates that utilizing uncertainty query strategies with deep models, particularly FastText.zip (FTZ), enables the creation of compact, representative surrogate datasets that maintain the essential characteristics of the full training dataset while achieving significant compression ratios of 5x-40x. This approach facilitates the training of large, deep text classification models, such as ULMFiT, to state-of-the-art accuracy with speedups ranging from 25x to 200x compared to conventional training on full datasets.

A key aspect of the invention is the discovery that uncertainty-based sampling using deep models exhibits remarkably favorable sampling properties that address longstanding concerns about sampling bias in active learning. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that entropy-based query strategies applied to deep models like FTZ produce query and train sets with negligible class bias, even when using large query sizes. This finding contradicts previous assumptions that greedy uncertainty-based approaches would lead to disproportionate representation from certain classes, thereby developing lopsided representations in each query.

Furthermore, the invention reveals that uncertainty sampling with deep models exhibits a desirable form of feature bias, preferentially selecting samples that lie near class boundaries rather than introducing undesirable redundancy or outlier selection. This behavior is evidenced by the surprisingly high overlap between actively acquired samples and the support vectors of a support vector machine (SVM) trained on the same dataset. This favorable bias toward class boundary samples contributes to the effectiveness of the resulting surrogate datasets in maintaining classification performance while significantly reducing dataset size.

The invention also establishes the robustness of uncertainty-based sampling with deep models to various algorithmic factors that have historically affected active learning performance. These factors include initial set selection, query size, and specific query strategy implementation. The experimental results demonstrate that samples obtained using FastText are largely independent of the initial random seed, exhibit stable performance across different query sizes, and show high degrees of overlap regardless of variations in the specific uncertainty query strategy employed (e.g., Least Confidence versus Entropy).

An additional significant contribution of the invention is the demonstration that single deep models used for querying provide sample sets comparable to more expensive approaches utilizing ensembles of models. This finding challenges the prevailing assumption that ensemble methods are necessary to achieve effective uncertainty estimation in active learning contexts. The invention shows that the computational overhead associated with ensemble approaches offers little additional benefit compared to using a single FTZ model with posterior uncertainty as the query function.

The invention further provides a novel, state-of-the-art baseline for active text classification that outperforms recent work in the field. By leveraging the favorable sampling properties of uncertainty-based strategies with deep models, the invention achieves superior performance using substantially less training data—specifically, 4x less data compared to previous approaches. This baseline also outperforms diversity-based query strategies, such as coreset sampling, at all training data sizes, despite the latter's reliance on expensive diversity measures.

In practical applications, the invention enables the fast bootstrapping of training for very large models like ULMFiT by utilizing the compact surrogate datasets generated through the disclosed active learning approach. This capability addresses the significant impediment posed by the cost and time required to obtain and label vast amounts of data for training large DNNs, thereby facilitating the development and deployment of new and improved models in resource-constrained environments.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive framework for efficient training of deep neural network models for text classification through the strategic application of uncertainty-based active learning techniques. At the core of this invention is the discovery that deep models, particularly FastText.zip (FTZ), when employed with uncertainty-based query strategies, exhibit remarkably favorable sampling properties that address longstanding concerns about sampling bias and inefficiency in active learning. This section provides a detailed description of the invention's methodology, experimental validation, and practical applications.

The invention operates within the standard active learning paradigm, where a labeled training set is incrementally constructed from a pool of unlabeled data through sequential selection and labeling of informative samples. The process begins with an initial randomly drawn sample from the unlabeled pool, which serves as the starting point for model training. Subsequently, at each iteration, the current model is trained on the existing labeled dataset, and a model-dependent query strategy is employed to select new samples from the unlabeled pool for labeling and addition to the training set. This iterative process continues until the desired training set size is achieved or performance metrics indicate sufficient convergence.

A critical innovation of the present invention lies in the identification and exploitation of the favorable sampling properties exhibited by deep models when used with uncertainty-based query strategies. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that utilizing the entropy query strategy with a deep model like FastText.zip (FTZ) produces query and train sets with exceptional sampling characteristics. These characteristics include negligible class bias, desirable feature bias toward class boundaries, and remarkable robustness to various algorithmic factors that have historically affected active learning performance.

The experimental validation of the invention involved conducting over 2,300 experiments across eight large, representative datasets widely used for text classification: AG-News (AGN), DBPedia (DBP), Amazon Review Polarity (AMZP), Amazon Review Full (AMZF), Yelp Review Polarity (YRP), Yelp Review Full (YRF), Yahoo Answers (YHA), and Sogou News (SGN). These datasets range in size from 120K to 3.6M samples, representing a two-order-of-magnitude increase compared to datasets used in previous studies. The experimental setup evaluated two representative text classification models—Multinomial Naive Bayes (MNB) with TF-IDF features and FastText.zip (FTZ)—along with four acquisition functions: Least Confidence (LC) and Entropy (Ent), each computed using both single models and ensembles of five models.

The investigation of class bias revealed that while traditional models like MNB exhibited significant class bias in individual queries, leading to lopsided representations from certain classes, the deep model FTZ demonstrated balanced representation across all classes when using the entropy query strategy. This balance was maintained even with large query sizes, contradicting previous assumptions about the limitations of greedy uncertainty-based approaches. Importantly, the resulting sample sets from both models showed no evidence of class bias, indicating that the cumulative effect of multiple queries effectively mitigates any temporary imbalances that might occur in individual queries.

Regarding feature bias, the invention uncovered a surprising and beneficial characteristic of uncertainty sampling with deep models: a strong preference for selecting samples that lie near class boundaries. This behavior was demonstrated through direct comparison with support vectors from a support vector machine (SVM) trained on the same dataset. The high percentage overlap between actively acquired samples and SVM support vectors indicates that the sampling process is indeed biased, but in a desirable manner that focuses on the most informative samples for classification performance. This finding aligns with recent research suggesting that deep classification networks may naturally focus on a subset of data closer to class boundaries, resembling support vectors in their importance to model performance.

The robustness of the invention's approach to various algorithmic factors was thoroughly investigated through systematic experimentation. With respect to initial set selection, the results demonstrated that samples obtained using FastText were largely independent of the initial random seed, showing consistent overlap across different initializations. In contrast, samples obtained with Naive Bayes exhibited significant dependence on the initial seed, highlighting the superior stability of deep models in active learning contexts. Regarding query size, the invention showed that FastText maintained stable performance across different query sizes (0.25%, 0.5%, 1%, and 2% of the dataset), while MNB exhibited more erratic behavior. This stability extends to the actual samples selected, with high overlap observed across different query sizes when using FastText.

The investigation of query strategy variations revealed that different uncertainty-based approaches—Least Confidence versus Entropy, with or without deletion of least uncertain samples—produced highly overlapping sample sets when applied to FastText. This finding indicates that the approach is robust to specific implementation details of the query strategy. Perhaps most significantly, the comparison between single models and ensembles of five models showed little to no difference in sample overlaps, demonstrating that the computationally expensive ensemble approach offers minimal benefit over using a single FTZ model with posterior uncertainty as the query function.

The practical applications of the invention extend beyond theoretical insights into active learning. The compact surrogate datasets generated through the disclosed approach can be effectively utilized for training large, deep text classification models like ULMFiT. Experimental results demonstrate that these surrogate datasets, compressed by factors of 5x-40x compared to the original datasets, enable training of ULMFiT to state-of-the-art accuracy with speedups ranging from 25x to 200x. This dramatic reduction in computational requirements addresses the significant barrier posed by the cost and time needed to obtain and label vast amounts of data for training large DNNs.

The invention also establishes a new state-of-the-art baseline for active text classification that outperforms recent work in the field. When compared to Bayesian active learning approaches like BALD (Siddhant and Lipton, 2018), the invention achieves superior performance using only 12% of the data compared to the 50% required by previous methods. Additionally, the invention outperforms diversity-based query strategies, such as coreset sampling (Sener and Savarese, 2018), at all training data sizes, despite the latter's reliance on expensive diversity measures. This performance advantage is attributed to the favorable sampling properties of uncertainty-based strategies with deep models, combined with the superior representational capabilities of models like FastText compared to the simpler architectures used in previous studies.

The implementation details of the invention ensure reproducibility and practical applicability. The FastText model was implemented using the original implementation with zipped version optimization for memory usage without loss of accuracy or speed. The ULMFiT model was trained using default hyperparameters from the official implementation, with minor adjustments to batch size for computational efficiency. The Multinomial Naive Bayes implementation utilized scikit-learn's default parameters with appropriate feature engineering through TF-IDF vectorization. Support vector machines were implemented using ThunderSVM, a fast GPU-accelerated library, ensuring accurate computation of support vectors for comparison purposes.

The invention's methodology includes careful consideration of experimental design to ensure valid conclusions. Multiple random initial sets and multiple runs per seed were employed to account for stochasticity in model training, particularly for the FastText model. Query sizes were carefully selected based on dataset characteristics, with smaller percentages used for larger datasets to maintain computational feasibility while ensuring meaningful comparisons. The evaluation metrics included not only classification accuracy but also measures of sampling bias, sample overlap, and computational efficiency, providing a comprehensive assessment of the approach's performance.

The theoretical implications of the invention challenge several long-standing assumptions in the active learning literature. The finding that uncertainty-based sampling with deep models exhibits negligible class bias contradicts previous concerns about greedy query strategies leading to lopsided representations. The discovery of favorable feature bias toward class boundaries suggests that deep models may possess inherent mechanisms for identifying informative samples that align with fundamental principles of statistical learning theory. The robustness to algorithmic factors indicates that deep models may provide more stable and reliable active learning performance compared to traditional models, potentially simplifying the design and implementation of active learning systems.

In practical terms, the invention enables organizations to significantly reduce the costs and time required for developing and deploying deep text classification models. By generating compact surrogate datasets that maintain the essential characteristics of the full training data, the invention allows for rapid prototyping, experimentation, and deployment of models in resource-constrained environments. The ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance with dramatically reduced computational requirements makes advanced text classification capabilities accessible to a broader range of applications and users.

The invention's approach also opens new avenues for research and development in related areas. The demonstrated transferability of surrogate datasets to other deep models suggests potential applications in hyperparameter optimization, architecture search, and transfer learning. The favorable sampling properties observed with FastText raise questions about whether similar characteristics exist in other deep learning architectures and tasks, potentially leading to generalized principles for efficient active learning across different domains. The connection between actively acquired samples and support vectors provides a bridge between classical statistical learning theory and modern deep learning practices, offering opportunities for theoretical unification and practical innovation.

The experimental validation of the invention included comprehensive testing across various dimensions of active learning performance. The investigation of class bias utilized Kullback-Leibler divergence and label entropy measures to quantify the balance of class representation in both individual queries and final sample sets. Feature bias was assessed through direct comparison with SVM support vectors, providing a concrete measure of the samples' proximity to class boundaries. Algorithmic factor robustness was evaluated through systematic variation of initial set selection, query size, and query strategy, with sample overlap serving as the primary metric for stability assessment.

The invention's performance was benchmarked against multiple baselines and state-of-the-art approaches. Random sampling served as the basic baseline for comparison, while more sophisticated approaches like BALD and coreset sampling represented the current state of the art in active learning for text classification. The results consistently demonstrated superior performance of the invention's approach across all datasets and evaluation metrics, establishing its position as a new standard for active text classification.

The computational efficiency of the invention was carefully documented and validated. The entire experimental suite required three weeks of running time on a high-performance AWS instance with Intel Xeon E7-8880 v3 processors and 1TB RAM, reflecting the scale and comprehensiveness of the validation effort. The speedup factors achieved in training large models like ULMFiT were measured relative to conventional training on full datasets, taking into account both reduced dataset size and fewer training epochs required to achieve comparable performance.

The invention's methodology includes provisions for handling the stochastic nature of deep learning training. Multiple random initializations and multiple runs per seed were employed to ensure that the observed results were not artifacts of specific random conditions. The reporting of mean and standard deviation values across runs provides a robust characterization of the approach's performance and reliability. The careful documentation of hyperparameters and implementation details ensures that the results are reproducible and can be extended to other contexts and applications.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a comprehensive solution to the challenges of efficient training of deep neural network models for text classification. Through the strategic application of uncertainty-based active learning techniques with deep models, the invention enables the creation of compact, representative surrogate datasets that maintain high classification performance while dramatically reducing computational requirements. The discovery of favorable sampling properties in deep models addresses longstanding concerns about sampling bias and establishes a new foundation for active learning in deep text classification contexts.
