# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of neurodegenerative disease therapeutics, specifically to the treatment and prevention of Alzheimer’s disease (AD). More particularly, the invention provides methods and compositions for reducing amyloid-β (Aβ) peptide production by modulating the interaction between transient receptor potential canonical 6 (TRPC6) and the amyloid precursor protein (APP), or its C-terminal fragment C99. The invention further encompasses the use of TRPC6-derived peptides, especially those corresponding to the second transmembrane domain (TM2) of TRPC6, as therapeutic agents that selectively inhibit γ-secretase-mediated cleavage of APP without affecting the processing of other γ-secretase substrates such as Notch. The disclosed technology represents a novel approach to Aβ-lowering therapy that circumvents the adverse effects associated with broad-spectrum γ-secretase inhibitors, thereby offering a safer and more targeted strategy for the management of Alzheimer’s disease and related amyloidopathies.

## BACKGROUND

Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by cognitive decline, memory loss, and behavioral abnormalities. A central pathological hallmark of AD is the accumulation of extracellular amyloid plaques in the brain, primarily composed of amyloid-β (Aβ) peptides. These peptides are generated through the sequential proteolytic cleavage of the amyloid precursor protein (APP) by β-secretase (BACE1) and γ-secretase. The γ-secretase complex, comprising presenilin (PS1 or PS2), nicastrin, Aph-1, and Pen-2, catalyzes the final step in Aβ production by cleaving the C99 fragment of APP to release Aβ peptides of varying lengths, including the highly aggregation-prone Aβ42 species. While Aβ accumulation is widely believed to initiate the pathogenic cascade leading to synaptic dysfunction, neuronal loss, and dementia, therapeutic strategies aimed at reducing Aβ have faced significant challenges.

Historically, efforts to target Aβ production have focused on inhibiting γ-secretase activity. However, clinical trials of pan-γ-secretase inhibitors have been largely unsuccessful due to severe side effects, most notably gastrointestinal toxicity and impaired Notch signaling, which is essential for cell differentiation, immune function, and vascular development. These adverse outcomes stem from the fact that γ-secretase processes over 90 known substrates, including Notch, N-cadherin, and ErbB4, making non-selective inhibition therapeutically untenable. Consequently, there has been a concerted effort to develop γ-secretase modulators (GSMs) that selectively reduce Aβ42 production without inhibiting overall γ-secretase activity or affecting the cleavage of other substrates. Despite progress in small-molecule GSMs, their efficacy and long-term safety remain uncertain, and no such agent has yet achieved regulatory approval for AD treatment.

In parallel, research has identified endogenous proteins that can influence γ-secretase substrate selectivity. For instance, GPR3, Aph1B/C, GSK3α, and CK1 have been shown to enhance Aβ production, while TMP21 was reported to suppress Aβ generation without altering Notch processing. However, TMP21 overexpression in mice leads to severe neurological deficits and premature death, limiting its therapeutic utility. Thus, there remains an unmet need for endogenous or exogenous modulators that can safely and specifically reduce Aβ production by interfering with APP-γ-secretase interaction without disrupting the physiological functions of γ-secretase.

Transient receptor potential canonical 6 (TRPC6) is a member of the TRP channel family, traditionally known as a nonselective cation channel involved in calcium signaling, neuronal development, and synaptic plasticity. TRPC6 has been implicated in various physiological processes, including dendritic growth, fear memory, and protection against excitotoxicity. Recent studies have also suggested a role for TRPC6 in neuroprotection, but its involvement in APP metabolism or Aβ regulation had not been previously established. Notably, all known functions of TRPC6 have been attributed to its ion channel activity, mediated by ligands such as hyperforin or diacylglycerol analogs like OAG.

The present invention arises from the unexpected discovery that TRPC6 regulates Aβ levels independently of its canonical channel function. Instead, TRPC6 acts as a selective endogenous modulator of γ-secretase by directly interacting with the C99 fragment of APP, thereby preventing its association with the γ-secretase complex and subsequent cleavage into Aβ peptides. This interaction is mediated by a specific structural domain within TRPC6—the second transmembrane domain (TM2)—and does not affect the processing of other γ-secretase substrates such as Notch or E-/N-cadherin. Furthermore, a synthetic cell-permeable peptide mimicking the TM2 domain of TRPC6, designated TAT-TM2, recapitulates the Aβ-lowering effect both in vitro and in vivo, demonstrating therapeutic potential. These findings redefine the functional repertoire of TRPC6 and provide a novel mechanistic basis for the development of substrate-selective Aβ-lowering therapies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for reducing amyloid-β (Aβ) peptide production in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering an effective amount of a composition comprising a TRPC6 polypeptide or a functional fragment thereof that includes the second transmembrane domain (TM2) of TRPC6. In particular, the invention discloses that TRPC6 reduces Aβ levels not through its ion channel activity, but by selectively interacting with the C99 fragment of the amyloid precursor protein (APP), thereby preventing its cleavage by γ-secretase. This interaction is specific to APP and does not interfere with the γ-secretase-mediated processing of other substrates, such as Notch, thus avoiding the toxic side effects associated with conventional γ-secretase inhibitors.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising a cell-permeable peptide derived from the TM2 domain of TRPC6, such as TAT-TM2, which comprises amino acids 487–508 of human or mouse TRPC6 fused to a protein transduction domain (e.g., the HIV TAT peptide). This peptide effectively inhibits Aβ production in cultured cells and in animal models of Alzheimer’s disease without affecting Notch signaling. The C-terminal 11 amino acids of the TM2 domain are identified as critical for the inhibitory activity, and mutations within this region abolish the Aβ-lowering effect.

Additionally, the invention provides methods for treating or preventing Alzheimer’s disease or other Aβ-related disorders by enhancing the expression or activity of TRPC6 in the brain, either through genetic means (e.g., viral vector-mediated gene delivery) or pharmacological upregulation. Transgenic mice overexpressing TRPC6 in forebrain neurons exhibit significantly reduced Aβ plaque burden, improved synaptic integrity, and enhanced spatial learning and memory when crossed with APP/PS1 AD model mice, confirming the therapeutic relevance of TRPC6 modulation.

The invention also includes diagnostic and prognostic applications, such as assessing TRPC6 expression levels in patient samples as a biomarker for AD susceptibility or progression. Moreover, screening assays are provided to identify compounds that mimic or enhance the TRPC6-APP interaction, thereby promoting selective inhibition of Aβ production.

In summary, the invention establishes TRPC6 as a novel endogenous γ-secretase modulator with substrate specificity for APP, reveals a non-channel function of TRPC6 in Aβ regulation, and provides a foundation for the development of safe and effective Aβ-lowering therapeutics based on the TM2 domain of TRPC6.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### EXAMPLE 1

The present invention begins with the identification of TRPC6 as a key regulator of Aβ production. Initial experiments were conducted in primary cultured rat cortical neurons to assess the role of various calcium channels in Aβ metabolism. Using RNA interference, several calcium channel proteins—including Cav1.2, Cav3.1, Cav3.3, TRPC5, and TRPC6—were downregulated. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) analysis revealed that only the knockdown of TRPC6 resulted in a significant increase in both Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels in the culture medium, whereas suppression of other channels had no effect. This finding indicated that TRPC6 uniquely suppresses Aβ accumulation in neurons. To determine whether this effect was dependent on TRPC6’s ion channel activity, neurons were treated with OAG, a TRPC channel activator, or SKF96365, a TRPC channel blocker. Neither compound altered Aβ levels, suggesting that TRPC6’s regulation of Aβ is independent of its canonical channel function. This observation was further corroborated in HEK293APP stable cells, a well-established model for studying Aβ production. Overexpression of full-length TRPC6 in these cells led to a marked reduction in Aβ40, Aβ42, Aβ38, and total Aβ1-x levels in the culture medium, while overexpression of the closely related TRPC5 had no effect. Importantly, the Aβ-lowering effect of TRPC6 persisted in the presence of SKF96365, and even a dominant-negative mutant of TRPC6 (DN-TRPC6), which abolishes channel activity, still reduced Aβ levels. These results collectively demonstrate that TRPC6 inhibits Aβ production through a mechanism that does not require its ion conductance properties, revealing a novel non-channel function of this protein.

### EXAMPLE 2

To elucidate the mechanism by which TRPC6 reduces Aβ, the effects of TRPC6 on APP processing pathways were systematically investigated. First, the levels of full-length APP, soluble APPα (sAPPα), and the expression of key secretases—ADAM10 (α-secretase) and BACE1 (β-secretase)—were examined in HEK293APP cells overexpressing TRPC6. No significant changes were observed, indicating that TRPC6 does not alter APP expression or the initial α- or β-cleavage steps. In vitro enzymatic assays using fluorogenic substrates confirmed that neither α- nor β-secretase activity was affected by TRPC6 overexpression. Next, the possibility that TRPC6 enhances Aβ clearance was tested by measuring the expression of Aβ-degrading enzymes such as insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) and neprilysin, as well as the chaperone clusterin, which facilitates Aβ efflux. None of these proteins showed altered expression in TRPC6-expressing cells. Furthermore, an Aβ clearance assay—where exogenous Aβ42 was added to cells and its degradation or cellular uptake was monitored—revealed no difference between control and TRPC6-expressing HEK293 cells. These data ruled out alterations in Aβ production via α/β-secretase or in Aβ clearance as mechanisms underlying TRPC6’s effect. Instead, attention turned to γ-secretase, the final enzyme in Aβ generation. TRPC6 overexpression in COS7 cells stably expressing C99 (the direct substrate of γ-secretase) dramatically reduced Aβ40 and Aβ42 in the medium. Similarly, in HEK293C99 cells, TRPC6 decreased the generation of the APP intracellular domain (AICD), a co-product of γ-secretase cleavage alongside Aβ. However, the levels of C-terminal fragments (CTFs) of APP remained unchanged, and the expression of core γ-secretase components (PS1, PS2, nicastrin, Pen-2) was unaffected. Critically, in vitro γ-secretase activity assays using a fluorogenic substrate showed no change in enzymatic activity, indicating that TRPC6 does not inhibit the catalytic function of the γ-secretase complex per se. These findings pointed toward a selective interference with γ-secretase’s access to or processing of APP-C99.

### EXAMPLE 3

To confirm the specificity of TRPC6’s action on APP processing, the effect of TRPC6 on other γ-secretase substrates was evaluated. Notch is a critical developmental receptor whose cleavage by γ-secretase releases the Notch intracellular domain (NICD), essential for transcriptional regulation. HEK293 cells expressing NotchΔE (a constitutively active form of Notch that serves as a direct γ-secretase substrate) were co-transfected with TRPC6. Western blot analysis showed no change in NICD levels, indicating that TRPC6 does not inhibit Notch cleavage. Similarly, the processing of E-cadherin and N-cadherin—two adhesion molecules cleaved by γ-secretase to release CTF2 fragments—was unaffected by TRPC6, as evidenced by unchanged CTF2/CTF1 ratios in transfected cells. These results demonstrate that TRPC6 selectively targets APP-C99 without disrupting the physiological processing of other γ-secretase substrates, thereby avoiding the toxicities associated with global γ-secretase inhibition. This substrate selectivity is a key feature that distinguishes TRPC6 from conventional γ-secretase inhibitors and positions it as a promising candidate for therapeutic development.

### EXAMPLE 4

The physical basis for TRPC6’s selective effect on APP was investigated through co-localization and co-immunoprecipitation studies. Because commercially available anti-TRPC6 antibodies were unsuitable for immunofluorescence, HEK293 cells were co-transfected with HA-tagged TRPC6 and Myc-tagged APP or C99. Confocal microscopy revealed partial co-localization of TRPC6 and APP/C99 in intracellular compartments, suggesting potential interaction. Subcellular fractionation of mouse brain lysates using iodixanol gradient centrifugation showed that endogenous TRPC6 and APP CTFs co-sedimented in the same fractions, providing further anatomical support for their association. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments in mouse brain, HEK293 cells, and primary cortical neurons demonstrated that TRPC6 co-precipitated with APP when using an anti-APP antibody, but not when using antibodies against PS1, Notch, or APLP2 (a homolog of APP). Reciprocal immunoprecipitation with anti-TRPC6 antibody pulled down APP but not NotchΔE. Moreover, TRPC6 interacted with familial AD-linked APP mutants (I716F and V717F), which harbor alterations near the γ-secretase cleavage site, indicating that the interaction is robust across pathogenic variants. In contrast, TRPC5 did not co-precipitate with APP under identical conditions. These findings establish that TRPC6 selectively and directly interacts with APP, particularly its C99 fragment, forming a molecular complex that excludes other γ-secretase components and substrates.

### EXAMPLE 5

To determine how TRPC6 binding to APP affects γ-secretase engagement, the interaction between C99 and PS1 was examined. In HEK293C99 cells, reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation confirmed that C99 and PS1 form a complex. However, when TRPC6 was co-expressed, this interaction was significantly diminished, indicating that TRPC6 binding to C99 sterically or allosterically hinders its association with the γ-secretase complex. This mechanism explains the selective reduction in Aβ without affecting γ-secretase activity toward other substrates. Additional experiments confirmed that APP does not modulate TRPC6 channel function: hyperforin-induced Ca²⁺ influx in HEK293TRPC6 cells was unchanged by APP co-expression. Thus, the relationship is unidirectional—TRPC6 modulates APP processing, but APP does not influence TRPC6’s ion channel activity. This asymmetry reinforces the notion that TRPC6’s role in Aβ regulation is structurally mediated rather than signaling-dependent.

### EXAMPLE 6

To map the domain of TRPC6 responsible for Aβ inhibition, a series of truncation and point mutation constructs were generated. TRPC6 contains six transmembrane domains (TM1–TM6) and cytoplasmic N- and C-termini. Initial tests of isolated N- (1–405 aa) and C-termini (725–930 aa) showed no effect on Aβ, ruling out involvement of cytoplasmic domains. Systematic truncation identified a minimal region (437–508 aa) encompassing TM1, the first extracellular loop, and TM2 that retained Aβ-inhibitory activity. Further mutagenesis focused on TM2 (approximately 487–508 aa). Four distinct TM2 mutants were created: two point mutations (C6-mut1, C6-mut2), a domain replacement (C6-mut3), and a sequence reversal (C6-mut4). All failed to reduce Aβ levels when expressed in HEK293APP cells. Co-immunoprecipitation revealed that C6-mut1 did not bind APP, confirming that TM2 mediates the TRPC6-APP interaction. Importantly, immunofluorescence showed that C6-mut1 retained proper subcellular localization and co-localized with APP similarly to wild-type TRPC6, indicating that the loss of function was due to disrupted binding, not mislocalization. These results definitively identify TM2 as the essential domain for TRPC6’s Aβ-lowering effect.

### EXAMPLE 7

Leveraging the identification of TM2 as the functional domain, a cell-permeable peptide, TAT-TM2, was synthesized by fusing the TM2 sequence (487–508 aa of mouse TRPC6) to the HIV TAT protein transduction domain. This peptide was tested in HEK293APP cells and primary cortical neurons. TAT-TM2, but not control peptides (TAT-TM1 or mutated TAT-TM2-mut1/2), dose-dependently reduced Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels. Truncation analysis showed that the C-terminal 11 amino acids of TM2 (TAT-TM2-C) were sufficient for activity, while the N-terminal 10 residues (TAT-TM2-N) were inactive. Point mutations in TAT-TM2-C abolished its effect, confirming sequence specificity. Biotinylated TAT-TM2 (TAT-TM2-biotin) bound to APP in pull-down assays, and this binding was competed by excess unlabeled TAT-TM2, demonstrating direct and specific interaction. In vivo, intraperitoneal injection of TAT-TM2 into APP/PS1 mice resulted in a significant reduction in brain Aβ levels, as measured by ELISA in a double-blinded manner. These results validate TAT-TM2 as a functional mimetic of full-length TRPC6 and a promising therapeutic lead.

### EXAMPLE 8

To confirm that TAT-TM2 acts by inhibiting γ-secretase cleavage of C99, an in vitro reconstituted assay was performed. Purified C99-His protein was incubated with γ-secretase-enriched membrane fractions from HEK293 cells in the presence or absence of test peptides. TAT-TM2, but not its mutants or TAT-TM1, dose-dependently inhibited Aβ production in this cell-free system, proving that its mechanism is direct and does not require cellular signaling or trafficking. In cellular models, TAT-TM2 suppressed Aβ secretion from COS7-C99 cells and reduced AICD generation in HEK293C99 cells, consistent with inhibition of γ-secretase cleavage. Crucially, TAT-TM2 did not affect NICD production from NotchΔE, reaffirming its substrate selectivity. These data establish that TAT-TM2 functions as a selective γ-secretase modulator by blocking the APP-γ-secretase interface, offering a precise molecular tool for Aβ reduction.

### EXAMPLE 9

The therapeutic potential of TRPC6 was validated in vivo using a genetic approach. TRPC6 transgenic mice, which overexpress TRPC6 specifically in forebrain excitatory neurons, were crossed with APP/PS1 mice—a well-characterized AD model that develops robust Aβ plaques and cognitive deficits. At 6–11 months of age, APP/PS1/TRPC6 double-transgenic mice exhibited significantly reduced amyloid plaque burden in the cortex and hippocampus, as visualized by 6E10 and MOAB-2 Aβ immunostaining and thioflavin S staining. Biochemical analysis of brain homogenates confirmed reductions in both soluble and insoluble Aβ40 and Aβ42, as well as total Aβ extracted with guanidine-HCl. Synaptic integrity was assessed by Golgi staining of dendritic spines in the hippocampus: APP/PS1 mice showed significant spine loss compared to wild-type controls, but APP/PS1/TRPC6 mice were protected from this degeneration. Behavioral testing in the Morris water maze revealed that APP/PS1/TRPC6 mice exhibited improved spatial learning and memory—finding the hidden platform faster during training and crossing the target quadrant more frequently during probe trials—compared to APP/PS1 littermates. Notably, TRPC6 overexpression alone enhanced cognitive performance in young mice but not in aged ones, suggesting its effects are most pronounced in the context of Aβ pathology. These findings demonstrate that elevating TRPC6 in the brain mitigates Aβ accumulation, preserves synaptic structure, and rescues cognitive deficits in an AD mouse model, providing strong preclinical evidence for TRPC6-based therapeutics.
