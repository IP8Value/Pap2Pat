## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of porous crystalline materials, and more specifically to novel zeolite compositions and framework structures exhibiting superior performance in molecular separation and catalytic applications. The invention encompasses both known and previously unreported zeolite framework types that demonstrate exceptional selectivity and capacity for separating ethanol from aqueous solutions, as well as unprecedented ability to discriminate between linear and branched long-chain hydrocarbons for dewaxing and hydroisomerization processes. The technical field further extends to computational methods for identifying optimal zeolite candidates from vast databases of potential crystalline structures through high-throughput molecular simulation techniques. The invention provides specific zeolite framework types that overcome longstanding limitations in biofuel purification and petroleum refining technologies by exploiting unique pore geometries and surface chemistries that preferentially interact with target molecules while excluding undesired species. The disclosed materials represent significant advances in the design of shape-selective sorbents and catalysts for industrial-scale chemical processes, particularly those involving complex mixtures of polar, hydrogen-bonding molecules or large, flexible hydrocarbon chains with varying degrees of branching.

## BACKGROUND

Crude oil continues to serve as the dominant source for transportation fuels and chemical feedstocks worldwide, making improvements in oil refining efficiency critically important for extending petroleum supplies and reducing production costs. In the 1950s, the emergence of crystalline zeolites containing sub-2 nanometer internal pores revolutionized numerous petrochemical processes by providing shape- and size-selective sorption and catalytic capabilities. These microporous aluminosilicate materials have been extensively employed in hydroisomerization reactions, where they catalyze the transformation of linear long-chain alkanes into slightly branched isomers of similar molecular weight. This conversion is essential for reducing the pour point and increasing the viscosity index of lubricant oils, with similar transformations being desirable for diesel and other fuel oils involving shorter alkanes. The effectiveness of these hydroisomerization processes depends critically on achieving a delicate balance between framework confinement and alkane molecular dimensions, where optimal zeolites must exhibit high affinity for linear alkanes while maintaining low affinity for branched isomers to prevent undesirable cracking reactions that produce smaller, less valuable hydrocarbon species.

Concurrently, the production of biofuels, particularly ethanol derived from sugar fermentation, presents another significant industrial challenge requiring efficient separation technologies. Current methods for separating ethanol from its aqueous solution rely heavily on energy-intensive distillation processes, which become particularly inefficient near the ethanol-water azeotropic composition of approximately 95.6 weight percent ethanol. Nearly defect-free silicalite-1, an all-silica zeolite with MFI framework topology, has been proposed as an alternative sorbent and membrane material for this separation due to its inherent hydrophobicity. However, practical implementation has been hindered by the phenomenon of water co-adsorption, where ethanol adsorption promotes the simultaneous uptake of water molecules through hydrogen bond formation, thereby significantly reducing separation selectivity. For effective ethanol purification applications, ideal zeolite materials must possess pore architectures that accommodate ethanol molecules while actively disfavoring hydrogen bonding interactions with water molecules.

Traditional approaches to identifying suitable zeolite materials for these demanding applications have proven inadequate due to the enormous complexity and scale of the problem. Experimental testing of all existing zeolite structures for a given application would be prohibitively time-consuming and labor-intensive, often becoming completely infeasible when synthesis protocols for materials with desired compositions have not yet been developed. The potential universe of synthesizable zeolites is extraordinarily large, with databases such as the Predicted Crystallography Open Database (PCOD) containing hundreds of thousands of theoretically possible structures that may possess superior characteristics compared to currently known materials. Previous computational screening efforts have focused predominantly on single-component adsorption of small, rigid, non-hydrogen-bonding molecules such as short hydrocarbons, carbon dioxide, and hydrogen. These studies typically relied on extrapolation of single-component data to predict mixture properties or employed simplified geometric analyses that fail to capture the complex intermolecular interactions present in real-world separation scenarios.

The fundamental challenge in developing effective screening methodologies for complex industrial separations lies in accurately modeling systems composed of large, articulated molecules or polar, hydrogen-bonding species where advanced sampling algorithms and precise electrostatic descriptions are essential. Conventional geometric descriptors and simplified force fields have proven insufficient for predicting adsorption behavior in mixtures exhibiting significant non-idealities due to strong intermolecular interactions. The inability to reliably predict material performance for these complex systems has represented a major bottleneck in the rational design of next-generation separation and catalytic materials. Furthermore, the relationship between pore geometry and adsorption performance becomes increasingly complex for larger molecules, where simple spherical or circular cross-section approximations fail to capture the true molecular dimensions and conformational flexibility of long-chain hydrocarbons. This background establishes the critical need for advanced computational methodologies capable of accurately simulating complex molecular mixtures in diverse zeolite frameworks, thereby enabling the systematic identification of optimal materials for challenging industrial separations.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel zeolite framework structures and compositions that demonstrate exceptional performance in two critical energy-related applications: the separation of ethanol from aqueous solutions and the selective adsorption of linear versus branched long-chain hydrocarbons for dewaxing processes. Through an innovative high-throughput computational screening methodology employing advanced molecular simulation techniques, the inventors have identified specific zeolite framework types that overcome longstanding limitations in these separation processes. For ethanol/water separation, the invention discloses ferrierite (FER) as a superior framework type that achieves retentate concentrations exceeding the ethanol/water azeotropic point under industrially relevant conditions, along with several previously unreported framework types (ATN*, ESV*, OWE*, UFI*, VFI*) that demonstrate exceptional selectivity and capacity characteristics. These materials achieve their superior performance through unique pore geometries that prevent the formation of extended hydrogen-bonding networks involving water molecules while maintaining high affinity for ethanol.

For hydrocarbon separation applications, the invention identifies both known framework types (ATO, MRE, AFO, MTT, FER, TON) that have been previously patented for isodewaxing applications and novel structures from the PCOD database that demonstrate dramatically improved performance metrics. Particularly noteworthy is the identification of PCOD structure 8113534, which exhibits selectivity ratios for linear versus branched alkanes that exceed those of conventional materials by several orders of magnitude. The invention further provides detailed structure-property relationships that explain the molecular mechanisms underlying these exceptional performances, including the critical role of pore bumpiness (Δd), free channel diameter (d_free), and channel cross-sectional geometry in determining adsorption selectivity for complex hydrocarbon mixtures.

The invention encompasses a comprehensive computational screening workflow that enables the systematic evaluation of hundreds of thousands of zeolite structures through multi-step simulation protocols incorporating multiple feed concentrations and operating conditions. This methodology overcomes the limitations of previous screening approaches by explicitly accounting for mixture non-idealities, molecular flexibility, and pressure-dependent adsorption behavior. The screening process utilizes robust performance metrics that combine selectivity and capacity considerations while remaining applicable across different separation process configurations. The invention further provides validated force field parameters and simulation protocols that have been experimentally verified through comparison with measured adsorption isotherms for benchmark materials such as MFI and FER.

Additionally, the invention discloses specific synthesis protocols for producing high-quality, nearly defect-free samples of the identified superior framework types, including detailed procedures for preparing silicalite-1 (MFI) and ferrierite (FER) with controlled crystal morphology and minimal structural defects. The invention also encompasses the integration of these novel zeolite materials into various separation process configurations, including single-step and multi-step adsorptive separations, membrane-based purification systems, and catalytic hydroisomerization reactors. The disclosed materials and methodologies represent a significant advancement in the rational design of porous crystalline materials for industrial-scale chemical processes, providing a foundation for developing more efficient and cost-effective technologies for biofuel production and petroleum refining.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example

The present invention is exemplified through the identification and characterization of specific zeolite framework types that demonstrate exceptional performance in ethanol/water separation and hydrocarbon dewaxing applications. Ferrierite (FER) emerges as a particularly outstanding material for ethanol purification, achieving retentate concentrations that exceed the ethanol/water azeotropic composition of 95.6 weight percent under industrially relevant conditions. At a raffinate concentration of 0.43 weight percent ethanol, FER demonstrates sufficient selectivity to enable single-step extraction processes that surpass the azeotropic limit, representing a significant improvement over conventional MFI-type zeolites which require higher feed concentrations to achieve comparable performance. The superior performance of FER stems from its unique pore architecture, which features adsorption sites spaced sufficiently far apart to prevent the formation of extended hydrogen-bonding networks involving water molecules. This geometric arrangement maintains high ethanol affinity while minimizing water co-adsorption, thereby preserving separation selectivity even at low ethanol concentrations typical of fermentation broths.

For hydrocarbon separation applications, the invention identifies ATO framework type as the top-performing known zeolite structure, exhibiting the highest loading capacity for C18 alkanes (Q_C18) among experimentally characterized materials while maintaining favorable selectivity ratios for linear versus branched isomers (S_B3 = 21) and appropriate discrimination between different chain lengths (S_L3). The ATO framework's hexagonal channel cross-section with a free diameter of 0.57 nanometers provides optimal confinement for linear alkanes while allowing sufficient rotational freedom for the zig-zag plane of hydrocarbon chains. This balance between confinement and flexibility enables high adsorption capacity without excessive steric hindrance that might promote cracking reactions. The invention further discloses PCOD structure 8113534 as a novel framework type that demonstrates unprecedented selectivity performance, achieving S_B3 values of 16,000 compared to typical values of 2-50 for conventional materials. This extraordinary selectivity arises from the elliptical channel geometry with a semi-minor axis of only 0.47 nanometers, which imposes severe steric constraints that effectively exclude branched isomers while accommodating linear chains in highly confined conformations with minimal gauche defects.

Experimental validation of the computational predictions has been achieved through careful measurement of ethanol adsorption isotherms in high-quality samples of MFI and FER zeolites. The preparation of these materials followed rigorous synthesis protocols designed to minimize structural defects and ensure phase purity. MFI samples were prepared from a starting molar composition of 1.0 SiO₂:0.08 TPABr:0.4 NH₄F:20 H₂O, with tetrapropylammonium bromide serving as the structure-directing agent. The synthesis mixture was subjected to hydrothermal treatment at 448 K for seven days under static conditions, followed by extensive washing and calcination at 823 K to remove organic templates. Similarly, FER samples were synthesized using a HF/pyridine system with n-propylamine as a co-structure-directing agent, yielding high-quality crystals after thermal treatment at 453 K for 7-10 days and subsequent calcination at 1123 K. Adsorption measurements conducted across ethanol concentrations ranging from 0.05 to 5 weight percent demonstrated excellent agreement between computational predictions and experimental observations, validating the accuracy of the simulation methodology and force field parameters employed in the screening process.

The invention further encompasses the development of multi-step separation strategies that leverage the complementary performance characteristics of different zeolite framework types. For instance, VFI* framework type demonstrates high ethanol loading capacity (Q_EtOH > 2.0 mol/kg) even at very low feed concentrations (w = 0.12 wt%), albeit with modest selectivity (S_EtOH = 620). This characteristic makes VFI* particularly suitable as a first-stage concentrator in a two-step separation process, where it can efficiently extract ethanol from dilute fermentation broths to produce a retentate containing approximately 43 weight percent ethanol. This concentrated stream can then serve as feed for a second-stage separation using ATN* framework type, which maintains exceptionally high selectivity at elevated ethanol concentrations (w ≥ 4.5 wt%). Such integrated process designs enable the achievement of final product purities exceeding the azeotropic limit while optimizing overall process efficiency through strategic allocation of separation tasks to materials with complementary performance profiles.

### Methods

The present invention employs a sophisticated computational screening methodology that combines advanced molecular simulation techniques with high-performance computing infrastructure to systematically evaluate the adsorption performance of hundreds of thousands of zeolite framework structures. The screening process utilizes a two-level parallel execution hierarchy that simultaneously explores multiple zeolite structures while accelerating individual simulations through distributed computing across thousands of processor cores. This approach enables the efficient execution of grand canonical Monte Carlo (GCMC) simulations using the configurational-bias algorithm (CB-GCMC) to compute sorbate loadings as functions of either solution-phase concentrations for ethanol/water mixtures or partial pressures for hydrocarbon systems. The chemical potentials required for these simulations are derived from previous Gibbs ensemble simulations with explicit solution phases for ethanol/water systems or determined from liquid-phase simulations in the isobaric-isothermal ensemble for hydrocarbon mixtures.

The molecular modeling framework employs the transferable potentials for phase equilibria (TraPPE) force field for hydrocarbons and ethanol, combined with the TIP4P model for water and the TraPPE-zeo force field for all-silica zeolites. This combination of force fields has been extensively validated for systems closely related to the target applications, including the adsorption and diffusion of alkanes, carbon dioxide, alcohols, and water in various zeolite structures across wide temperature and pressure ranges. All sorbate-sorbate interactions are evaluated explicitly to a cutoff distance of 1.4 nanometers, with long-range Lennard-Jones contributions estimated via tail corrections and first-order electrostatics treated using Ewald summation techniques. To enhance computational efficiency, interaction energy grid files are generated that contain repulsive and attractive Lennard-Jones components (including tail corrections) along with direct-space and reciprocal-space Coulomb terms, enabling rapid evaluation of sorbate-framework interactions independent of specific sorbate force field parameters.

The screening workflow implements a multi-step approach tailored to each application's specific requirements. For ethanol/water separation, an initial screening evaluates all 402 idealized and experimental IZA-SC structures at the lowest solution-phase concentration (w = 0.12 wt%) using a performance metric P_EtOH defined as the product of ethanol selectivity (S_EtOH) and loading (Q_EtOH). The 64 structures with the highest P_EtOH values that also satisfy the constraint Q_EtOH > Q_water are retained for secondary screening at five additional concentrations spanning the industrially relevant range (0.43, 1.4, 4.5, 15, and 41 wt%). This multi-concentration approach accounts for the complex concentration dependence of selectivity in non-ideal mixtures, where performance rankings can vary significantly across different operating conditions.

For hydrocarbon dewaxing applications, the screening methodology evaluates both IZA-SC and PCOD databases containing 402 and 331,172 structures respectively. Initial screening computes Henry's constants and selectivity indicators at infinite dilution using relatively short simulations (9 × 10⁴ MC steps). Structures ranking among the top 64 in IZA-SC or top 1,024 in PCOD databases for any of three performance indicators—highest k_H,C18, highest S_B0, or lowest S_L0—are retained for more extensive simulations. Secondary screening employs longer simulation runs (9 × 10⁵ MC steps for infinite dilution properties and 1.6 × 10⁶ MC steps for liquid-mixture properties) at both infinite dilution and finite pressure conditions (3 MPa) representative of industrial hydroisomerization operations. The final performance metric P_HCp combines linear alkane affinity (y_C18), selectivity for linear versus branched isomers (S_Bp), and selectivity between different chain lengths (S_Lp) according to the relationship P_HCp = y_C18 S_Bp / S_Lp.

The computational infrastructure supporting these screening efforts leverages leadership-class supercomputing resources, specifically the Mira supercomputer at Argonne National Laboratory, which enables the simultaneous execution of simulations across up to 131,072 compute cores. This massive parallelization capability is essential for handling the computational demands of simulating complex molecular systems in diverse zeolite frameworks while maintaining statistical accuracy through sufficient sampling of molecular configurations. The coupled-decoupled configurational-bias Monte Carlo (CD-CBMC) algorithm enhances sampling efficiency by improving acceptance rates for GCMC insertion/deletion moves and facilitating identity switch moves between different hydrocarbon molecules, thereby accelerating convergence of sorption equilibrium calculations.

### Exemplary Embodiments

The present invention encompasses several exemplary embodiments that demonstrate the practical implementation of the disclosed zeolite framework types in industrial separation processes. One embodiment comprises a single-step adsorptive separation system utilizing ferrierite (FER) zeolite for ethanol purification from fermentation broths containing 5 weight percent ethanol. In this configuration, the FER sorbent operates under equilibrium adsorption conditions to reduce the raffinate concentration to approximately 0.5 weight percent ethanol, achieving a retentate composition that exceeds the ethanol/water azeotropic point. The system operates at temperatures between 298 and 323 Kelvin, with optimal performance achieved at 323 K where the balance between adsorption capacity and selectivity is maximized. The FER zeolite is preferably synthesized as nearly defect-free, all-silica crystals with controlled particle size distribution to minimize mass transfer limitations while maintaining structural integrity during cyclic adsorption-desorption operations.

Another exemplary embodiment involves a two-stage separation process combining VFI* and ATN* framework types for enhanced ethanol purification efficiency. In the first stage, VFI* zeolite serves as a high-capacity concentrator that extracts ethanol from dilute fermentation broths (initial concentration of 0.12 weight percent) to produce a retentate containing approximately 43 weight percent ethanol. The VFI* framework's large 1.2-nanometer channels enable high ethanol loading capacities (>2.0 mol/kg) even at extremely low feed concentrations, making it particularly effective for initial concentration steps. The resulting retentate stream is then fed to a second-stage separation unit containing ATN* zeolite, which maintains exceptionally high selectivity at elevated ethanol concentrations (≥4.5 weight percent). This integrated approach leverages the complementary performance characteristics of both materials to achieve final product purities exceeding 95.6 weight percent ethanol while optimizing overall process economics through strategic allocation of separation tasks.

For hydrocarbon dewaxing applications, an exemplary embodiment comprises a catalytic hydroisomerization reactor utilizing ATO framework type zeolite impregnated with Group VIII metals (such as platinum or palladium) as bifunctional catalysts. The reactor operates at temperatures of 573 Kelvin and pressures of 3 megapascals, conditions representative of industrial hydroisomerization processes. The ATO framework's hexagonal channel cross-section with 0.57-nanometer free diameter provides optimal confinement for linear C18-C30 alkanes while maintaining sufficient space to accommodate mono-branched isomers without promoting excessive cracking reactions. The catalyst demonstrates high activity for converting waxy linear alkanes to less waxy branched isomers with high viscosity indices, achieving conversion efficiencies that significantly exceed those of conventional catalysts based on MFI or MTW framework types.

A particularly innovative embodiment incorporates PCOD structure 8113534 as a selective adsorbent in a simulated moving bed (SMB) chromatography system for separating linear from branched hydrocarbons in complex petroleum fractions. The SMB system exploits the extraordinary selectivity ratio (S_B3 = 16,000) of PCOD-8113534 to achieve near-complete separation of linear alkanes from their branched counterparts in a continuous, counter-current flow configuration. The elliptical channel geometry with 0.47-nanometer semi-minor axis imposes severe steric constraints that effectively exclude branched isomers while allowing linear chains to adsorb in highly confined conformations. This embodiment represents a paradigm shift in hydrocarbon separation technology, offering the potential to replace energy-intensive fractional distillation processes with more efficient adsorption-based alternatives for producing high-purity linear alkane streams used in plasticizer and synthetic detergent manufacturing.

Additional embodiments include membrane-based separation systems incorporating thin films of the disclosed zeolite framework types deposited on porous supports. For ethanol/water separation, FER membranes demonstrate superior pervaporation performance compared to conventional MFI membranes, achieving higher flux rates and separation factors due to their unique pore architecture that minimizes water co-adsorption. Similarly, ATO and PCOD-8113534 membranes show exceptional promise for hydrocarbon separations, with molecular sieving capabilities that enable precise discrimination between linear and branched isomers based on subtle differences in molecular dimensions and conformational flexibility. These membrane embodiments offer significant advantages in terms of energy efficiency and process intensification compared to traditional batch separation methods, representing important advances in sustainable chemical processing technologies.
