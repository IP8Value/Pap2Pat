# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The development of a safe and effective vaccine against human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) remains one of the most pressing challenges in modern medicine. Despite decades of intensive research, no licensed HIV-1 vaccine has yet been developed that provides robust and durable protection against infection. A central obstacle in this endeavor is the extraordinary genetic diversity and structural complexity of the HIV-1 envelope glycoprotein (Env), which serves as the sole target for neutralizing antibodies capable of preventing viral entry into host cells. The Env complex, present as a trimeric spike on the surface of the virion, mediates the critical steps of receptor binding and membrane fusion, thereby enabling viral infectivity. Consequently, the induction of broadly neutralizing antibodies (bNAbs)—antibodies capable of recognizing and neutralizing a wide spectrum of HIV-1 isolates—has emerged as a primary goal of contemporary vaccine design strategies. However, the natural immune response to HIV-1 infection rarely produces such bNAbs, and when they do arise, it is typically after years of chronic infection. This delayed and infrequent development underscores the need for engineered immunogens that can reliably elicit bNAbs through vaccination.

The native Env spike is composed of three gp120 surface subunits non-covalently associated with three gp41 transmembrane subunits, forming a metastable trimeric structure. Upon binding to the CD4 receptor on target cells, the Env complex undergoes dramatic conformational changes that expose co-receptor binding sites and ultimately drive the fusion of viral and cellular membranes. The structural plasticity of Env, coupled with its dense glycan shield and sequence hypervariability, enables the virus to evade antibody recognition. Traditional vaccine approaches using monomeric gp120 or uncleaved gp140 constructs have failed to induce bNAbs, largely because these immunogens adopt non-native conformations that expose epitopes targeted by non-neutralizing antibodies (non-NAbs) while occluding the conserved, functionally critical epitopes recognized by bNAbs. As a result, the immune system is often misdirected toward irrelevant or even counterproductive responses.

To overcome these limitations, significant efforts have been devoted to designing soluble, recombinant Env trimers that faithfully mimic the native, pre-fusion conformation of the virion-associated spike. Among the most successful of these designs is the BG505 SOSIP.664 trimer, derived from a subtype A transmitted/founder virus. This construct incorporates several key stabilizing modifications: a disulfide bond (SOS) between gp120 and the gp41 ectodomain (gp41ECTO) to prevent subunit dissociation, an I559P substitution in gp41ECTO to stabilize the trimeric interface, and optimization of the furin cleavage site between gp120 and gp41 to ensure complete proteolytic processing. When properly cleaved and folded, BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers exhibit antigenic profiles closely resembling those of native viral spikes—they display multiple bNAb epitopes while effectively shielding non-NAb epitopes. Structural studies using cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) and X-ray crystallography have confirmed that these trimers adopt a homogeneous, native-like conformation, making them leading candidates for inclusion in next-generation HIV-1 vaccines.

However, the practical utility of BG505 SOSIP.664 and similar trimeric immunogens depends critically on scalable, reproducible production methods that yield high-quality protein in sufficient quantities for both preclinical research and clinical development. Historically, such trimers have been produced via transient transfection of mammalian cells, such as human embryonic kidney 293T (HEK293T) or Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells. While effective for small-scale laboratory studies, transient transfection is inherently variable, labor-intensive, and unsuitable for large-scale manufacturing under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) conditions required for human vaccine trials. Moreover, transient systems often suffer from batch-to-batch inconsistencies in expression levels, post-translational modifications, and trimer integrity, which can compromise immunological and structural analyses.

A more robust and sustainable approach involves the generation of stable, clonal cell lines that constitutively express the trimeric immunogen. Such cell lines offer numerous advantages, including consistent product quality, reduced production costs, elimination of repeated plasmid transfections, and compatibility with industrial-scale bioreactor systems. However, creating stable cell lines for complex, multicomponent proteins like BG505 SOSIP.664 presents unique challenges. First, the trimer must be co-expressed with a furin protease to ensure complete cleavage at the gp120–gp41 junction, a prerequisite for native-like conformation. Second, uncontrolled overexpression of either Env or furin can lead to cytotoxicity, impairing cell growth and protein yield. Third, random integration of expression cassettes into the host genome can result in positional effects, silencing, or genetic instability, all of which undermine long-term expression fidelity.

To address these challenges, the Flp-In™ system offers a powerful solution. This site-specific recombination platform utilizes a single, well-characterized Flp Recombination Target (FRT) site stably integrated into the genome of specialized host cells (e.g., Flp-In™ 293T or CHO). Co-transfection of an FRT-containing expression vector with a plasmid encoding the Flp recombinase directs precise integration of the transgene into the predefined FRT locus, ensuring consistent expression levels and eliminating clonal variability due to integration site effects. Furthermore, the system includes a selectable marker (e.g., hygromycin resistance) linked to the FRT site, enabling efficient isolation of stable integrants.

Despite these advantages, the application of the Flp-In™ system to the production of complex viral glycoproteins like BG505 SOSIP.664 has not been previously demonstrated. Prior attempts to generate stable Env-expressing cell lines have often resulted in low yields, incomplete cleavage, or accumulation of misfolded aggregates, likely due to imbalanced expression of Env and furin or inadequate promoter selection. There remains a critical need for a validated, high-yield platform capable of producing homogeneous, native-like HIV-1 Env trimers in a stable, scalable, and GMP-compliant manner. The invention described herein fulfills this unmet need by providing engineered mammalian cell lines—derived from both human and hamster backgrounds—that constitutively secrete high-quality BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers at levels significantly exceeding those achievable by transient transfection. These cell lines represent a transformative advance in HIV-1 vaccine development, enabling the reliable production of structurally authentic immunogens for structural biology, immunogenicity studies, and eventual clinical evaluation.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides stable, clonal mammalian cell lines capable of constitutively expressing and secreting high yields of soluble, cleaved, native-like HIV-1 envelope glycoprotein (Env) trimers, specifically the BG505 SOSIP.664 variant. These cell lines are engineered using the Flp-In™ site-specific recombination system to ensure precise genomic integration and consistent transgene expression. The invention further encompasses the expression vector used to generate these cell lines, designated pAM/C BG505, which contains both the BG505 SOSIP.664 gp140 gene and the furin protease gene under the control of distinct promoters optimized for balanced expression and minimal cytotoxicity. The resulting 293T and CHO cell lines produce fully cleaved, homogeneous trimers that are biochemically, antigenically, and morphologically indistinguishable from those generated by transient transfection, but with substantially higher yields—up to 12–15 mg per 1 × 10⁹ cells, representing a 10- to 32-fold improvement over transient methods. The purified trimers exhibit excellent stability, resist proteolytic degradation, and display the desired antigenic profile characterized by strong binding to broadly neutralizing antibodies (bNAbs) such as PGT145 and minimal reactivity with non-neutralizing antibodies (non-NAbs) like F105. Electron microscopy and differential scanning calorimetry confirm that the trimers adopt a single, native-like conformation with high thermal stability. Critically, these cell lines maintain stable expression over multiple passages without loss of viability or productivity, demonstrating their suitability for long-term culture and scalable manufacturing. The invention thus establishes a robust, flexible, and GMP-adaptable platform for the production of high-fidelity HIV-1 Env trimers, serving as a foundational resource for structural studies, preclinical immunogenicity testing, and future clinical development of HIV-1 vaccines.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### EXAMPLE 1

The present invention begins with the rational design of an expression vector, designated pAM/C BG505, tailored for the stable, high-yield production of BG505 SOSIP.664 gp140 trimers in mammalian cells. The vector is constructed based on the pcDNA5/FRT backbone, which contains a single Flp Recombination Target (FRT) site and a hygromycin resistance gene, enabling site-specific integration and selection in Flp-In™ host cells. To accommodate the dual expression requirements of the Env trimer and the furin protease, the vector was modified to include two independent transcriptional units within the FRT-flanked region. The BG505 SOSIP.664 gp140 gene, encoding a soluble, stabilized form of the HIV-1 Env spike derived from the subtype A transmitted/founder virus BG505, was placed under the control of the strong human cytomegalovirus (CMV) immediate-early enhancer/promoter. This choice ensures high-level constitutive expression of the Env protein, which is essential for achieving substantial trimer yields. Concurrently, the furin protease gene, necessary for the complete proteolytic cleavage of the gp140 precursor into its gp120 and gp41ECTO subunits, was positioned under the control of the weaker elongation factor 1-alpha (EF1α) promoter. This strategic promoter selection mitigates the risk of furin-induced cytotoxicity, which can occur when this potent protease is overexpressed. The use of the EF1α promoter provides sufficient furin activity to ensure near-complete cleavage of the Env precursor without compromising cell viability. Additionally, a synthetic polyadenylation signal, modeled after the herpes simplex virus thymidine kinase (HSV-TK) polyA sequence, was incorporated downstream of each coding sequence to ensure efficient mRNA processing and stability. The entire expression cassette was cloned into the pcDNA5/FRT vector between the HindIII and NotI restriction sites, resulting in the final pAM/C BG505 construct. This vector design represents a critical innovation, as it balances the competing demands of high Env expression and controlled furin activity within a single, integrated genetic unit, thereby enabling the production of fully cleaved, native-like trimers in a stable cell line context.

### EXAMPLE 2

Using the pAM/C BG505 vector, stable cell lines were generated in both human embryonic kidney 293T (HEK293T) and Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) Flp-In™ host cells. The Flp-In™ system was employed to ensure precise, site-specific integration of the transgene into a single, well-characterized genomic locus, thereby eliminating the variability associated with random integration. Co-transfection of the pAM/C BG505 vector with the pOG44 plasmid, which encodes the Flp recombinase, was performed using Lipofectamine 2000 according to standard protocols. Following transfection, cells were subjected to hygromycin selection to isolate clones harboring the integrated expression cassette. Initial screening of hygromycin-resistant foci identified four candidate cell lines: two from 293T (designated lines 13 and 15) and two from CHO (designated lines A and B). To ensure clonality and eliminate potential genetic heterogeneity, limiting dilution cloning was performed on the highest-expressing preliminary lines—293T line 13 and CHO lines A and B. This process yielded 32 potential 293T clones and 10 potential CHO clones. Expression levels of intracellular Env protein were assessed by flow cytometry using fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated monoclonal antibody (MAb) 2G12, which recognizes a conserved glycan-dependent epitope on gp120. Based on this analysis, 293T clone 13 #3-5 and CHO clone B-D7 were identified as the highest-expressing, clonal isolates. These clones were subsequently expanded and characterized in detail. The use of the Flp-In™ system, combined with rigorous clonal selection, ensured the derivation of genetically uniform, stable cell lines with consistent and high-level expression of the BG505 SOSIP.664 trimer.

### EXAMPLE 3

The stability of Env expression in the selected 293T clone 13 #3-5 and CHO clone B-D7 was evaluated over ten consecutive passages (P1–P10), with each passage occurring every four days. Intracellular Env levels were monitored by dot blot analysis using MAb 2G12, and trimer production in the culture supernatant was quantified by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The results demonstrated that both cell lines maintained approximately constant levels of intracellular Env protein throughout the ten-passage period. Similarly, the concentration of secreted trimers in the supernatant remained steady, with yields ranging from 2 to 3 μg/ml for the 293T clone and 1.7 to 2.5 μg/ml for the CHO clone. Cell viability was also assessed using the trypan blue exclusion test and found to be consistently high—91–98% for 293T and 86–95% for CHO—comparable to the viability of parental, non-transfected control cells. These findings indicate that neither the expression of the BG505 SOSIP.664 trimer nor the co-expression of furin imposes a significant cytotoxic burden on the host cells. The sustained expression and viability over five weeks of continuous culture demonstrate that the engineered cell lines are genetically stable and suitable for long-term production campaigns. This stability is a direct consequence of the site-specific integration strategy and the balanced promoter design, which together prevent transgene silencing and minimize metabolic stress.

### EXAMPLE 4

To assess the biochemical quality and yield of the trimers produced by the stable cell lines, large-scale cultures were established, and the secreted proteins were purified using a two-step chromatography protocol. Culture supernatants were first subjected to affinity chromatography on a 2G12-coupled Sepharose column, which selectively captures Env proteins bearing the 2G12 epitope. The eluted material was then further purified by size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) on a Superdex HiLoad column to isolate the trimeric fraction from dimers, monomers, and other aggregates. The purified trimers were analyzed by blue native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (BN-PAGE) followed by Coomassie blue staining. The results showed that the majority of the Env protein existed in the trimeric form, with only minor amounts of lower-order species detectable. Quantitative yield analysis revealed that the 293T stable cell line produced 12 mg (range: 10–15 mg) of purified trimer per 1 × 10⁹ cells, a ten-fold increase compared to transiently transfected 293T cells (1.25–1.5 mg). Similarly, the CHO stable cell line yielded 12 mg (range: 10–15 mg) per 1 × 10⁹ cells, representing a 32-fold improvement over transiently transfected CHO cells (0.25–0.5 mg). These dramatic yield enhancements underscore the efficiency of the stable cell line platform and its superiority over transient expression for scalable trimer production.

### EXAMPLE 5

The cleavage status of the purified trimers was rigorously evaluated to confirm that the co-expressed furin protease effectively processes the gp140 precursor. Reducing sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) was performed in the presence of dithiothreitol (DTT), which breaks the engineered SOS disulfide bond linking gp120 and gp41ECTO. The gels revealed clear separation into the expected gp120 (~120 kDa) and gp41ECTO (~40 kDa) subunits, confirming complete cleavage. In contrast, uncleaved gp140 constructs would remain as a single ~140 kDa band under reducing conditions. To further validate the integrity of the trimers and rule out proteolytic degradation, Western blot analyses were conducted using the MAb ARP 3119, which recognizes a linear epitope in the N-terminal half of gp120. Only a single 120-kDa band corresponding to full-length gp120 was detected, with no lower-molecular-weight degradation products observed. Additional Western blots using a polyclonal HIV-Ig pool, comprising sera from individuals infected with HIV-1 subtypes A, B, and C, similarly showed no evidence of fragmentation or aggregation. These results confirm that the trimers produced by the stable cell lines are fully cleaved and resistant to proteolysis, a critical attribute for maintaining native-like conformation and antigenic authenticity.

### EXAMPLE 6

The antigenic properties of the purified trimers were evaluated using both surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and ELISA to determine their structural fidelity. In SPR experiments, MAbs were immobilized on CM5 sensor chips, and purified trimers were flowed over the surface to measure binding kinetics. The broadly neutralizing antibody PGT145, which recognizes a quaternary, trimer-specific epitope, bound strongly to trimers from both stable cell lines, as well as to those produced by transient transfection. In contrast, the non-neutralizing antibody F105, which targets a CD4-binding site (CD4bs) epitope that is occluded on native trimers, showed minimal binding. This pattern—high PGT145 reactivity and low F105 reactivity—is a hallmark of native-like Env trimers and confirms the absence of misfolded contaminants such as gp120 monomers or uncleaved gp140. Complementary ELISA assays, using 2G12 for trimer capture and biotinylated PGT145 or F105 for detection, yielded identical results: strong PGT145 signals and negligible F105 binding across all trimer preparations. These antigenic profiles demonstrate that the stable cell line-derived trimers are structurally authentic and suitable for use in immunogenicity studies aimed at eliciting bNAb responses.

### EXAMPLE 7

The overall morphology and homogeneity of the trimers were examined by negative-stain electron microscopy (EM). Purified trimer samples were adsorbed onto carbon-coated grids, stained with uranyl formate, and imaged using a transmission electron microscope. Reference-free two-dimensional (2D) class averages were generated from thousands of particle images. The resulting EM reconstructions revealed that trimers from both the 293T and CHO stable cell lines exhibited the characteristic, compact, native-like appearance of the BG505 SOSIP.664 spike, with three distinct lobes corresponding to the gp120 subunits arranged around a central gp41 core. The percentage of particles classified as native-like ranged from 81% to 100%, with 293T-derived trimers generally showing slightly higher homogeneity (90–100%) than CHO-derived trimers (80–90%). However, these differences fall within the expected experimental error for negative-stain EM and are not considered biologically significant. Critically, the EM images were indistinguishable from those of trimers produced by transient transfection, which have been used to solve high-resolution cryo-EM and X-ray structures. This morphological consistency confirms that the stable cell line platform preserves the native trimer architecture.

### EXAMPLE 8

The thermal stability of the trimers was assessed by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), a technique that measures the heat absorption associated with protein unfolding as a function of temperature. SEC-purified trimers from the 293T and CHO stable cell lines were analyzed in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at a scan rate of 90°C per hour. Both preparations exhibited a single, sharp endothermic transition, with melting temperatures (Tm) of 66.9°C and 66.3°C, respectively. These values are comparable to those reported for transiently produced trimers (67.9–68.1°C), indicating that the stable cell line-derived trimers possess equivalent conformational stability. The presence of a single, cooperative unfolding transition further supports the homogeneity of the trimer preparations, as heterogeneous or partially misfolded samples would typically display multiple or broadened transitions. The high thermal stability is a desirable feature for vaccine immunogens, as it correlates with structural integrity and resistance to denaturation during storage and handling.

### EXAMPLE 9

In summary, the stable 293T and CHO cell lines described herein represent a significant advancement in the production of HIV-1 Env trimers for vaccine development. These cell lines, generated using the Flp-In™ system and the rationally designed pAM/C BG505 vector, consistently produce high yields of fully cleaved, native-like BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers that are biochemically pure, antigenically authentic, morphologically correct, and thermally stable. The platform is highly scalable, genetically stable over extended culture periods, and adaptable to GMP manufacturing standards. The ability to produce milligram quantities of high-quality trimers from a single, clonal cell line eliminates the variability and inefficiencies of transient transfection, thereby accelerating preclinical research and paving the way for clinical evaluation of trimer-based HIV-1 vaccines. Furthermore, the modular design of the pAM/C BG505 vector facilitates the rapid exchange of Env sequences, enabling the production of SOSIP trimers from diverse HIV-1 genotypes for broader immunogenicity studies. This invention thus provides a robust, versatile, and industrially relevant solution to a longstanding bottleneck in HIV-1 vaccine development.
