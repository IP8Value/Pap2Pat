# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present disclosure relates to novel peptide compounds that function as high-affinity inhibitors of Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1), a non-tyrosine kinase receptor critically involved in pathological angiogenesis, tumor progression, and metastasis. More specifically, the disclosure provides branched and linear peptidic ligands incorporating a C-terminal arginine-containing motif conforming to the C-end rule (CendR) and further modified by the strategic incorporation of a cysteine residue at or near the N-terminus. These compounds exhibit significantly enhanced binding affinity for the b1 domain of NRP-1 compared to their unmodified counterparts, thereby potently inhibiting the interaction between vascular endothelial growth factor A165 (VEGF-A165) and NRP-1. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these peptides, methods for their synthesis, and their use in the treatment of diseases mediated by aberrant NRP-1 signaling, including but not limited to various forms of cancer, ocular neovascularization, and other angiogenesis-dependent pathologies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) is a transmembrane glycoprotein that functions as a co-receptor for multiple extracellular ligands, most notably class 3 semaphorins and several members of the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) family, including VEGF-A165, placental growth factor (PlGF), and others. Unlike classical receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), NRP-1 lacks intrinsic enzymatic activity; however, it plays a pivotal role in modulating signal transduction by forming complexes with primary signaling receptors such as VEGFR-2. This cooperative enhancement of RTK signaling is central to both physiological processes like neuronal development and pathological conditions, particularly tumor angiogenesis and cancer cell survival, migration, and invasion. Elevated expression of NRP-1 on the surface of numerous cancer cell types has been consistently correlated with poor clinical prognosis, making it an attractive therapeutic target for oncology and anti-angiogenic drug development.

The molecular basis for NRP-1 ligand recognition is governed by a well-established principle known as the C-end rule (CendR). This rule dictates that high-affinity binding to the b1 domain of NRP-1 requires a C-terminal motif of the general structure R/KXXR/K, where the ultimate C-terminal arginine (or, less commonly, lysine) is absolutely essential. The binding pocket of the NRP-1 b1 domain is shallow and sterically constrained, allowing only the terminal basic residue to insert deeply into a specific pocket formed by conserved aromatic and acidic residues. Any modification that blocks the free α-carboxylate of this C-terminal arginine—such as amidation or extension of the peptide chain—completely abolishes binding. This stringent structural requirement has been observed across a diverse array of natural and synthetic NRP-1 ligands, including the C-terminus of VEGF-A165 (CDKPRR), the tumor-penetrating peptide iRGD (CRGDK/RGPD/EC), the heptapeptide A7R (ATWLPPR), and even certain viral capsid proteins.

Despite the clear importance of the CendR motif, research has indicated that additional structural elements can profoundly influence binding affinity and functional activity. For instance, the native VEGF-A165 C-terminal hexapeptide CDKPRR contains two invariant cysteine residues that form critical disulfide bonds within the full-length protein. Studies have demonstrated that synthetic peptides mimicking this sequence lose significant NRP-1 binding affinity when these cysteines are substituted or deleted, suggesting a role for the thiol-containing side chains beyond mere structural stabilization. The chemical nature of the cysteine residue—its ability to act as a hydrogen bond donor or acceptor via its thiol group, engage in S-π interactions with aromatic residues in the receptor, or potentially participate in transient covalent interactions—has been hypothesized to contribute to the stability of the ligand-receptor complex.

Building upon this knowledge, prior art has described the development of short, branched peptidomimetics based on the CendR principle. One such scaffold, H-Lys(hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg-OH (where hArg is homoarginine, Dab is 2,4-diaminobutyric acid, and Dhp is 3,4-dehydroproline), was shown to inhibit VEGF-A165/NRP-1 complex formation with a micromolar IC50 value. While a significant improvement over early linear peptides like A7R, there remained a clear need to further enhance potency to achieve therapeutically relevant concentrations. The present inventors recognized a critical gap in the understanding of how proximal amino acid residues, particularly those with unique chemical functionalities like cysteine, modulate the interaction between CendR peptides and NRP-1. Previous efforts had not systematically explored the effect of adding a cysteine residue either alone or as part of a dipeptide fragment (e.g., Cys-Asp) to the N-terminus of high-affinity branched scaffolds or to established linear NRP-1 ligands. Furthermore, the potential trade-off between increased binding affinity and decreased metabolic stability—a common challenge in peptide-based drug design—had not been adequately addressed for such cysteine-modified constructs. The background art thus lacked a rational design strategy that could simultaneously leverage the CendR motif and the unique physicochemical properties of cysteine to create a new generation of NRP-1 inhibitors with sub-micromolar, even nanomolar, potency and acceptable pharmacokinetic profiles.

A key aspect of the invention is the discovery of a “Cysteine Rule” (CysR), which complements the established C-end rule (CendR). The CysR posits that the addition of a cysteine residue at the N-terminus of a CendR-compliant peptide ligand for NRP-1 results in a dramatic and unexpected increase in binding affinity for the NRP-1 b1 domain. This enhancement is observed irrespective of whether the core peptide scaffold is branched or linear, and it is not merely a consequence of extending the peptide chain but is specifically attributable to the unique chemical properties of the cysteine thiol group.

The compounds of the disclosure are potent inhibitors of the VEGF-A165/NRP-1 protein-protein interaction. In a competitive binding assay, the branched compounds of Formula (I) exhibit IC50 values in the range of 60 to 80 nanomolar, representing a 50- to 70-fold improvement in potency over the parent compound H-Lys(hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg-OH (IC50 = 4.3 μM). Similarly, the linear compounds of Formula (II) show substantial improvements in affinity, with Compound 7 (a derivative of the VEGF-A165 C-terminus) achieving an IC50 of 190 nM.

Furthermore, the compounds of the disclosure demonstrate favorable enzymatic stability in human plasma. While the addition of the N-terminal cysteine does lead to some susceptibility to proteolytic cleavage, the half-lives of the lead compounds (e.g., Compound 4) are measured in tens of hours, which is a significant improvement over many natural, unmodified bioactive peptides whose half-lives are often only minutes. This balance of high potency and reasonable metabolic stability makes these compounds promising candidates for therapeutic development.

The disclosure also provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a compound of Formula (I) or (II), or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof, and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. These compositions are useful for the treatment of a disease or condition in a subject wherein inhibition of NRP-1 activity is beneficial. Such diseases include, but are not limited to, solid tumors, hematological malignancies, age-related macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, rheumatoid arthritis, and psoriasis.

Methods for preparing the compounds of the disclosure are also provided, which generally involve standard solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) techniques using Fmoc chemistry on a preloaded resin, followed by cleavage, purification, and characterization.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSURE

### Compounds of the Disclosure

The present invention is directed to a novel class of peptidic compounds that are potent and selective inhibitors of the Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) receptor. These compounds are designed based on the fundamental C-end rule (CendR) for NRP-1 binding but are uniquely modified by the strategic incorporation of a cysteine residue to achieve unprecedented levels of affinity. The compounds can be broadly categorized into two structural classes: branched peptidomimetics and linear peptides.

The first class comprises branched compounds of Formula (I):
Lys(X)-AA2-AA3-Arg.
In this formula, the lysine (Lys) residue serves as the central branching point. The alpha-amino group of this lysine forms the N-terminus of the main peptide chain, which is linked to AA2 and then to AA3, terminating in the essential C-terminal arginine (Arg). Simultaneously, the epsilon-amino group of the lysine side chain is substituted with a group X. The identity of X is critical to the compound’s activity. In the parent compound, X is homoarginine (hArg), a longer-chain analog of arginine that provides an additional basic center. The present invention discloses that replacing this simple hArg with a more complex moiety containing cysteine leads to a dramatic boost in potency. Specifically, X can be Cys-hArg, where a cysteine is linked via its carboxyl group to the alpha-amino group of hArg, effectively placing the cysteine on the side chain of the central lysine. Alternatively, the entire branched structure can be preceded by a cysteine on the main chain N-terminus, which is represented by the compound where the main chain is Cys-Lys(hArg)-... In this case, X remains hArg, but the N-terminus of the main chain is now a cysteine. The amino acids AA2 and AA3 are selected from a small set of non-natural amino acids known to confer conformational rigidity and metabolic stability. AA2 is preferably 2,4-diaminobutyric acid (Dab) or its homolog 2,5-diaminopentanoic acid (Dap). AA3 is preferably 3,4-dehydroproline (Dhp), a proline analog that introduces a double bond into the pyrrolidine ring, further restricting backbone flexibility and potentially pre-organizing the peptide into a bioactive conformation.

The second class of compounds are linear peptides of Formula (II):
Cys-Y-Z-Z-Arg.
These compounds are derived from known, naturally occurring or previously reported NRP-1 ligands. The core innovation is the addition of a single cysteine residue to the N-terminus of these active sequences. The variables Y and Z represent the amino acids that constitute the original active core of the parent ligand. For example, in the case of tuftsin, the active core is TKPR (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg), so Y would be Lys and Z would be Pro, yielding the inventive compound Cys-Lys-Pro-Arg (Compound 6). Similarly, for the VEGF-A165-derived sequence DKPRR, the core is shortened to preserve the CendR motif while adding the N-terminal Cys, resulting in Cys-Asp-Lys-Pro-Arg-Arg (Compound 7). The key feature of all Formula (II) compounds is the presence of the N-terminal Cys immediately preceding a sequence that terminates in the required C-terminal Arg, thereby creating a Cys-X-X-X-Arg motif that embodies both the CendR and the newly discovered CysR principles.

All compounds of the disclosure may exist in their free acid form (with a C-terminal -COOH) or as a pharmaceutically acceptable salt. Suitable salts include those formed with inorganic bases such as sodium, potassium, or ammonium, or with organic bases such as triethylamine or arginine. The peptides are typically synthesized with a free C-terminus to comply with the CendR, as any modification like amidation would destroy activity.

### Methods for Preparing Compounds of Formula (I)

The compounds of the present disclosure are prepared using well-established techniques of solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS), specifically employing the 9-fluorenylmethoxycarbonyl (Fmoc) strategy. This method allows for the stepwise, controlled assembly of the peptide chain from the C-terminus to the N-terminus on an insoluble polymeric support, which greatly simplifies purification after each coupling step.

The synthesis is initiated on a preloaded resin to ensure the correct C-terminal arginine is in place. A suitable starting material is Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin, where the arginine side chain is protected with the 2,2,4,6,7-pentamethyldihydrobenzofuran-5-sulfonyl (Pbf) group, and the C-terminal carboxyl is attached to the Wang linker on the resin. The first step involves deprotection of the Fmoc group from the N-terminus of the resin-bound arginine using a solution of 20% piperidine in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF). This liberates the alpha-amino group for the next coupling reaction.

For the synthesis of the branched compounds of Formula (I), the next amino acid to be coupled is a suitably protected lysine derivative. To build the main chain, Fmoc-Lys(Mtt)-OH or Fmoc-Lys(Alloc)-OH is used, where Mtt (4-methyltrityl) or Alloc (allyloxycarbonyl) is an orthogonal protecting group on the epsilon-amino side chain of lysine. This orthogonal protection allows the main chain to be extended while keeping the side chain protected. The coupling is performed using a standard activating agent such as O-(7-azabenzotriazol-1-yl)-N,N,N',N'-tetramethyluronium hexafluorophosphate (HATU) in the presence of a base like N,N-diisopropylethylamine (DIPEA) in DMF. After coupling, the Fmoc group is removed, and the cycle of deprotection and coupling is repeated to add AA2 (e.g., Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH or Fmoc-Dap(Boc)-OH, where Boc is tert-butyloxycarbonyl) and then AA3 (e.g., Fmoc-Dhp-OH).

Once the main chain Lys-AA2-AA3-Arg is complete on the resin, the orthogonal protecting group (Mtt or Alloc) on the lysine side chain is removed. If Alloc is used, deprotection is achieved with tetrakis(triphenylphosphine)palladium(0) in the presence of a scavenger like phenylsilane in dichloromethane (DCM). This step reveals the epsilon-amino group of the lysine, which is now ready for branching. The group X is then coupled to this side chain amine. If X is hArg, Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH is coupled. If X is Cys-hArg, the dipeptide Fmoc-Cys(Trt)-hArg(Pbf)-OH (where Trt is trityl, a common thiol protecting group) is coupled in a single step. After this side chain coupling, the final Fmoc group is removed from the N-terminus of the main chain.

For compounds where the cysteine is on the main chain N-terminus (e.g., Compound 3), the synthesis diverges slightly. After the main chain Lys(hArg)-AA2-AA3-Arg is assembled, the final Fmoc deprotection is followed by the coupling of Fmoc-Cys(Trt)-OH to the N-terminus. This places the cysteine at the very beginning of the peptide sequence.

Following the complete assembly of the protected peptide on the resin, the final product is cleaved from the solid support and all remaining side-chain protecting groups are removed simultaneously. This is accomplished using a cleavage cocktail, for example, a mixture of trifluoroacetic acid (TFA), dithiothreitol (DTT) as a scavenger for cationic species and to reduce any disulfide bonds, water, and triisopropylsilane (TIS) in a ratio of 88:5:5:2 (v:w:v:v). The cleavage reaction is typically allowed to proceed for 2-4 hours at room temperature. The crude peptide is then precipitated by the dropwise addition of the cleavage mixture into cold diethyl ether, centrifuged, and the pellet is washed with ether to remove residual TFA and organic byproducts.

The crude peptide is dissolved in water/acetonitrile and purified to homogeneity using preparative reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC). A C12 or C18 column is typically used with a gradient of water (containing 0.1% TFA) and acetonitrile (containing 0.1% TFA). The pure fractions are pooled and lyophilized to yield the final peptide as a white to off-white powder. The identity and purity of the final product are confirmed by analytical RP-HPLC and high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS).

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The compounds of the present disclosure can be formulated into a variety of pharmaceutical compositions for administration to a subject in need of NRP-1 inhibition. A pharmaceutical composition of the invention comprises a therapeutically effective amount of at least one compound of Formula (I) or (II), or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof, in combination with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, diluents, or excipients.

The choice of carrier and the overall formulation will depend on the intended route of administration. For systemic delivery, particularly for the treatment of cancer, parenteral formulations are preferred. These include sterile aqueous or non-aqueous solutions, suspensions, or emulsions. A typical intravenous (IV) formulation would contain the peptide dissolved in sterile saline (0.9% NaCl) or a buffered solution such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at a pH of approximately 7.4. To enhance solubility and stability, co-solvents like ethanol, propylene glycol, or polyethylene glycol may be added in small amounts. The concentration of the active compound in a parenteral composition will typically range from about 0.1 mg/mL to about 100 mg/mL, depending on the dose and volume constraints.

For localized delivery, such as in the treatment of ocular diseases like age-related macular degeneration, the compounds can be formulated into ophthalmic solutions, suspensions, or gels. These formulations would include appropriate tonicity agents (e.g., sodium chloride, glycerin), preservatives (e.g., benzalkonium chloride, though preservative-free formulations are preferred for chronic use), and viscosity-enhancing agents (e.g., hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, hyaluronic acid) to prolong residence time on the eye.

The pharmaceutical compositions may also be designed for sustained or controlled release. This can be achieved by incorporating the peptide into biodegradable polymeric matrices or microspheres made from materials like polylactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA). Such depot formulations can provide a slow, continuous release of the active agent over days or weeks, reducing the frequency of administration.

In all cases, the pharmaceutical composition must be sterile and stable under the conditions of manufacture and storage. The composition should be fluid enough to be easily administered by the chosen route and should be isotonic with the blood in the case of parenteral administration to avoid local irritation or tissue damage. The skilled formulator can select from a wide array of known excipients and techniques to produce a composition that meets these criteria for the specific peptide and its intended therapeutic application.

### Therapeutic Uses

The primary therapeutic utility of the compounds and compositions of the present disclosure lies in their ability to potently and selectively inhibit the interaction between VEGF-A165 and its co-receptor NRP-1. By disrupting this key protein-protein interaction, these agents interfere with a critical signaling axis that drives pathological angiogenesis and tumor progression. Consequently, the invention provides a method for treating a disease or condition in a subject that is mediated by NRP-1 activity.

The most prominent application is in oncology. The compounds can be used for the treatment of a wide range of solid tumors, including but not limited to carcinomas of the breast, lung, prostate, colon, ovary, and pancreas, as well as glioblastoma and melanoma. In these cancers, NRP-1 is often overexpressed on both tumor cells and tumor-associated endothelial cells. Inhibition of NRP-1 can therefore exert a dual anti-tumor effect: it can directly suppress tumor cell survival, migration, and invasion, and it can indirectly starve the tumor by blocking the formation of new blood vessels (angiogenesis). The compounds may be administered as a monotherapy or, more advantageously, in combination with other anti-cancer agents such as conventional chemotherapeutics, radiation therapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors, or other targeted therapies like VEGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors.

Beyond oncology, the anti-angiogenic properties of these NRP-1 inhibitors make them valuable for treating other angiogenesis-dependent diseases. A major area is ophthalmology, where they can be used to treat neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration (AMD), diabetic retinopathy, and retinal vein occlusion. In these conditions, abnormal blood vessel growth in the retina leads to vision loss, and current anti-VEGF therapies are the standard of care. An NRP-1 inhibitor offers a complementary mechanism of action that could be used alone or in combination with existing anti-VEGF drugs to improve efficacy or overcome resistance.

Other potential therapeutic applications include the treatment of inflammatory diseases where angiogenesis plays a role, such as rheumatoid arthritis and psoriasis. In rheumatoid arthritis, the inflamed synovium is highly vascularized, and inhibiting this process can reduce joint destruction. In psoriasis, the characteristic skin plaques are supported by a dense network of new blood vessels.

The method of treatment involves administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a compound of the disclosure. The effective amount will vary depending on the specific compound, the disease being treated, its severity, the route of administration, and the individual patient's characteristics. For systemic anti-cancer therapy, a typical daily dose for an adult human might range from about 0.1 mg/kg to about 50 mg/kg of body weight, administered intravenously over a period of time. For localized ocular delivery, much lower doses, perhaps in the microgram range per eye, would be sufficient. The dosing regimen can be adjusted by the attending physician to achieve the desired therapeutic outcome while minimizing potential side effects.

## EXAMPLES

### Example

The following examples are provided to illustrate the preparation and biological evaluation of representative compounds of the present disclosure. These examples are not intended to limit the scope of the invention in any way.

**Example 1: Synthesis of H-Cys-Lys(hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg-OH (Compound 3)**

Compound 3 was synthesized on a 0.5 g scale of Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin (loading 0.39 mmol/g) using manual Fmoc-SPPS. The resin was first treated with 20% piperidine in DMF (2 x 10 mL, 5 min each) to remove the Fmoc group. The main chain was then elongated by sequential couplings of Fmoc-Dhp-OH, Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH, and Fmoc-Lys(Alloc)-OH using HATU (2 eq) and DIPEA (5 eq) in DMF. After each coupling, the Kaiser test confirmed completion. The Alloc group on the lysine side chain was removed by treatment with a solution of tetrakis(triphenylphosphine)palladium(0) (0.1 eq) and phenylsilane (20 eq) in DCM for 1 hour. The side chain was then acylated with Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH using the standard HATU/DIPEA protocol. Finally, the N-terminal Fmoc group was removed, and Fmoc-Cys(Trt)-OH was coupled to the main chain N-terminus. The fully protected peptide-resin was washed thoroughly with DMF and DCM and then cleaved with a cocktail of TFA:DTT:H2O:TIS (88:5:5:2, 5 mL) for 3 hours. The cleaved peptide was precipitated in cold diethyl ether, centrifuged, and the pellet was washed with ether. The crude product was purified by preparative RP-HPLC on a Phenomenex Jupiter Proteo C12 column using a water/acetonitrile gradient with 0.1% TFA. Lyophilization afforded Compound 3 as a white powder. Analytical HPLC showed >95% purity, and HRMS confirmed the expected mass ([M+H]+ calc. for C33H60N14O9S: 821.4521; found: 821.4518).

**Example 2: Synthesis of H-Lys(Cys-hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg-OH (Compound 4)**

Compound 4 was synthesized similarly to Compound 3, with a key difference in the order of assembly. After building the main chain Fmoc-Lys(Alloc)-Dab(Boc)-Dhp-Arg-resin, the Alloc group was removed from the lysine side chain. Instead of coupling Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH directly, the pre-formed, side-chain protected dipeptide Fmoc-Cys(Trt)-hArg(Pbf)-OH was coupled to the lysine epsilon-amine. Subsequent Fmoc deprotection of the main chain N-terminus yielded the final linear sequence on the resin. Cleavage and purification were performed as in Example 1 to afford Compound 4 as a white powder (>95% purity by HPLC; HRMS [M+H]+ calc. for C33H60N14O9S: 821.4521; found: 821.4525).

**Example 3: Competitive NRP-1 Binding Assay**

The ability of the synthesized compounds to inhibit the binding of biotinylated VEGF-A165 (bt-VEGF-A165) to recombinant human NRP-1 was assessed using a chemiluminescent ELISA-like assay. A 96-well plate was coated overnight at 4°C with 200 ng/well of recombinant human NRP-1 in PBS. The wells were blocked with 0.5% BSA in PBS for 1 hour at room temperature. Test peptides were serially diluted in PBS and mixed 1:1 with a solution of bt-VEGF-A165 (400 ng/mL) and heparin (4 µg/mL). This mixture (100 µL) was added to the wells and incubated for 2 hours at room temperature. After washing, streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase conjugate (1:8000 in PBS) was added for 45 minutes. Following a final wash, a chemiluminescent substrate was added, and luminescence was measured immediately. The percent inhibition was calculated relative to a positive control (bt-VEGF-A165 alone) and a negative control (no NRP-1 coating). The IC50 values were determined by nonlinear regression analysis. **Example 4: Enzymatic Stability in Human Plasma**

The metabolic stability of Compounds 3 and 4 was evaluated by incubating them in freshly drawn human plasma at 37°C. Each peptide was added to plasma to a final concentration of ~1 μM. At predetermined time points (0, 1, 4, 24, 48, 72, 96 hours), aliquots were removed, and proteins were precipitated by adding an equal volume of 98% ethanol. After centrifugation, the supernatants were analyzed by RP-HPLC to quantify the remaining intact peptide. The results, shown in Figure 2A, indicate that both compounds are relatively stable. After 24 hours, approximately 50% of each peptide remained intact. Over the full 96-hour period, Compound 4 showed superior stability, with ~40% remaining, compared to ~20% for Compound 3. This suggests that the site of cysteine attachment influences susceptibility to proteolysis, with the side-chain attachment in Compound 4 conferring greater resistance, likely due to steric hindrance around the scissile amide bond. These half-lives, on the order of tens of hours, are considered favorable for a peptide therapeutic and represent a viable profile for further preclinical development.
