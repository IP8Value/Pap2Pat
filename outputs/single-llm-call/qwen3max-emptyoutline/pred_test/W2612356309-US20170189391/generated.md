# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of oncology and targeted cancer therapeutics, and more specifically to the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) through the inhibition of protein kinase C (PKC) signaling pathways. In particular, the invention provides methods and pharmaceutical compositions for selectively inhibiting the proliferation of TNBC cells by administering chelerythrine or other PKC inhibitors that target PKN2 (also known as PRK2 or protein kinase N2), a member of the PKC superfamily that is overexpressed in TNBC relative to non-TNBC subtypes. The invention further encompasses combination therapies involving chelerythrine and conventional chemotherapeutic agents such as taxol, which demonstrate synergistic or additive anti-proliferative effects against TNBC cell lines both in vitro and in vivo. Additionally, the invention includes diagnostic and prognostic applications based on the differential expression of PKN2 in TNBC versus other breast cancer subtypes, enabling patient stratification for targeted therapy. The therapeutic strategy disclosed herein addresses an unmet medical need in the clinical management of TNBC, a highly aggressive and treatment-refractory form of breast cancer for which no approved targeted therapies currently exist.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women globally, with an estimated 1.67 million new cases and over 500,000 deaths reported in 2012 alone. Clinically, breast cancer is classified into molecular subtypes based on the expression status of three key biomarkers: estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). This classification yields three primary categories: hormone receptor-positive (ER+ and/or PR+), HER2-positive (HER2-overexpressing), and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), which is defined by the absence of ER and PR expression and lack of HER2 amplification or overexpression. TNBC accounts for approximately 10–15% of all breast cancer cases and is distinguished by its aggressive biological behavior, high rates of early recurrence, and propensity for visceral metastases—particularly to the lungs and brain. Unlike hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive breast cancers, which benefit from endocrine therapies and HER2-targeted agents respectively, TNBC lacks established molecular targets for precision medicine, rendering standard cytotoxic chemotherapy the only systemic treatment option. Although TNBC initially demonstrates responsiveness to conventional chemotherapy regimens, patients frequently experience rapid disease relapse and develop resistance, leading to poor overall survival outcomes compared to other breast cancer subtypes.

In light of these challenges, extensive research efforts have been directed toward identifying novel therapeutic targets in TNBC. Numerous signaling pathways implicated in tumorigenesis—including those involving poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP), epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), angiogenesis, mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), proteasome, cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs), histone deacetylases (HDACs), Src kinase, Wnt signaling, and phosphatidylinositide 3-kinases (PI3K)—have been evaluated in clinical trials for TNBC. Despite promising preclinical data, none of these targeted approaches has yet yielded an FDA-approved therapy for TNBC, underscoring the urgent need for innovative strategies grounded in a deeper understanding of TNBC-specific molecular vulnerabilities.

One such potential vulnerability lies within the protein kinase C (PKC) family, a group of serine/threonine kinases that regulate diverse cellular processes including proliferation, survival, migration, and apoptosis. PKC enzymes are categorized into four subfamilies—conventional (cPKC: α, βI, βII, γ), novel (nPKC: δ, ε, η, θ), atypical (aPKC: ζ, ι/λ), and protein kinase N (PKN: PKN1, PKN2, PKN3)—based on structural and regulatory differences. Accumulating evidence suggests a critical role for PKC signaling in breast cancer pathogenesis. Notably, an inverse correlation between ER expression and PKC activity has been consistently observed in both breast cancer cell lines and patient tumor specimens, with PKC levels elevated in malignant compared to normal breast tissue. Among PKC isoforms, PRKCA (encoding PKCα) has been particularly implicated in breast cancer aggressiveness and resistance to antiestrogen therapies. Moreover, PRKCA has been identified as a central node in the signaling network of breast cancer stem cells, which share phenotypic and functional similarities with TNBC cells. Recent studies have further demonstrated direct associations between PRKCA overexpression and TNBC in both preclinical models and clinical samples, positioning PKC as a compelling candidate for therapeutic intervention in this recalcitrant disease subtype.

Despite these insights, no prior studies have systematically evaluated the efficacy of PKC inhibitors specifically in the context of TNBC. Chelerythrine, a natural benzophenanthridine alkaloid originally isolated from *Chelidonium majus*, represents one of the most potent and selective small-molecule inhibitors of PKC activity described to date. It functions by competitively binding to the catalytic domain shared across multiple PKC isozymes, thereby blocking their kinase activity. Chelerythrine has exhibited broad-spectrum anticancer properties in various malignancies, including lymphoma, osteosarcoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma, primarily through the induction of apoptosis. However, its potential utility in TNBC—and the molecular basis for any observed selectivity—has remained unexplored until now.

Compounding the therapeutic challenge is the heterogeneity of TNBC itself, which comprises multiple molecularly distinct subtypes with varying dependencies on specific signaling pathways. This complexity necessitates not only the identification of actionable targets but also the development of companion diagnostics to guide patient selection. Furthermore, given the limitations of monotherapy in advanced cancers, rational combination strategies that enhance efficacy while mitigating toxicity are essential. In this regard, the integration of targeted agents like PKC inhibitors with standard chemotherapeutics holds significant promise, particularly if synergistic interactions can be demonstrated.

Thus, there exists a critical gap in the art: a lack of effective, mechanism-based therapies for TNBC that exploit the unique molecular dependencies of this disease. The present invention addresses this gap by revealing, for the first time, that chelerythrine exerts selective anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic effects against TNBC cell lines in vitro and in vivo, and that this selectivity is mediated—at least in part—through the inhibition of PKN2, a PKC-related kinase that is consistently overexpressed across diverse TNBC models. These findings establish PKN2 as a novel therapeutic target in TNBC and position chelerythrine and related PKC inhibitors as promising candidates for clinical translation, either as single agents or in combination with existing chemotherapies such as taxol.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) comprising administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a protein kinase C (PKC) inhibitor, wherein said PKC inhibitor selectively targets PKN2 (protein kinase N2), a member of the PKC superfamily that is overexpressed in TNBC cells relative to non-TNBC breast cancer cells. In a preferred embodiment, the PKC inhibitor is chelerythrine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, ester, or derivative thereof. The invention further discloses that chelerythrine exhibits selective anti-proliferative activity against TNBC cell lines—including MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, and MDA-MB-468—with IC50 values ranging from 2.6 μM to 4.2 μM, while demonstrating significantly reduced potency against non-TNBC cell lines such as MCF7, ZR-75-1, SK-BR-3, and MDA-MB-453, for which IC50 values exceed 10 μM or show no inhibition even at the highest tested concentrations. This selectivity is dose- and time-dependent and is corroborated by colony formation assays, which reveal near-complete abrogation of clonogenic survival in TNBC cells following brief exposure to chelerythrine, whereas non-TNBC cells retain substantial colony-forming capacity even after prolonged treatment.

The invention further establishes that the selective anti-proliferative effect of chelerythrine in TNBC is primarily mediated through the induction of apoptosis, rather than cell cycle arrest. Morphological hallmarks of apoptosis—including chromatin condensation and nuclear fragmentation—are observed exclusively in TNBC cells upon chelerythrine treatment, as visualized by Hoechst 33258 staining. Biochemical confirmation is provided by the detection of cleaved poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (cPARP), a canonical marker of caspase-mediated apoptosis, in TNBC but not non-TNBC cell lines following chelerythrine exposure. Quantitative flow cytometric analysis using Annexin V/propidium iodide double staining demonstrates that chelerythrine induces apoptosis in 22.2% to 67.8% of TNBC cells depending on the cell line, while non-TNBC cells remain largely viable under identical conditions. This apoptotic response is time-dependent, with increasing fractions of apoptotic cells observed over 24 hours of treatment.

A key mechanistic insight of the invention is the identification of PKN2 as a critical mediator of TNBC cell survival and a primary target underlying chelerythrine’s selectivity. Gene expression profiling of 56 human breast cancer cell lines from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) database reveals that among the 12 PKC isozymes, PKN2 mRNA is significantly elevated in TNBC cell lines (n=26) compared to non-TNBC counterparts (n=30). This finding is validated at the protein level in the panel of eight breast cancer cell lines used in the study, where PKN2 expression is consistently higher in all four TNBC lines than in non-TNBC lines. In contrast, PRKCA (PKCα), previously associated with TNBC, is overexpressed only in a subset of TNBC lines (MDA-MB-231 and BT-549), indicating that PKN2 represents a more universally relevant target across TNBC subtypes.

Functional validation is achieved through lentivirus-mediated shRNA knockdown of PKN2, which results in a significant reduction in colony formation specifically in TNBC cell lines, with no appreciable effect on non-TNBC cells. This dependency is further confirmed in vivo using xenograft models: knockdown of PKN2 in MDA-MB-231 (TNBC) cells markedly suppresses tumor growth in nude mice, whereas the same genetic manipulation in ZR-75-1 (non-TNBC) cells does not alter xenograft formation. These data collectively demonstrate that PKN2 is essential for TNBC cell proliferation and tumorigenicity, thereby explaining the selective efficacy of pan-PKC inhibitors like chelerythrine in this context.

Moreover, the invention discloses a synergistic or additive therapeutic interaction between chelerythrine and the chemotherapeutic agent taxol in TNBC. Combination treatment with suboptimal doses of chelerythrine (2 μM) and taxol (10 nM) results in significantly greater inhibition of TNBC cell proliferation than either agent alone across all four tested TNBC lines. Combination index (CI) analysis confirms synergy (CI < 0.9) in HCC1937 cells and additive effects (0.9 < CI < 1.1) in the remaining lines, suggesting that PKC inhibition potentiates the cytotoxic activity of microtubule-targeting agents. This finding supports the clinical development of combination regimens incorporating PKC inhibitors with standard chemotherapy to improve outcomes in TNBC patients.

In addition to its therapeutic implications, the invention provides diagnostic and prognostic utilities based on PKN2 expression levels. The consistent overexpression of PKN2 in TNBC versus non-TNBC subtypes enables its use as a biomarker for patient stratification, allowing selection of individuals most likely to benefit from PKC inhibitor therapy. Assays for detecting PKN2 mRNA or protein in tumor biopsies—including quantitative real-time PCR, immunohistochemistry, and Western blotting—are encompassed within the scope of the invention.

Finally, the invention contemplates broader applications beyond TNBC, given the emerging links between PKC signaling and comorbidities prevalent in breast cancer survivors. Notably, TNBC patients exhibit a higher incidence of metabolic syndrome and obesity—established risk factors for cardiovascular disease (CVD)—and PKC activation is implicated in CVD pathogenesis. Chelerythrine has been shown to ameliorate endothelial dysfunction in diabetic models, suggesting that its use in TNBC may confer dual benefits: direct antitumor activity and mitigation of CVD-related complications. This multifaceted therapeutic profile enhances the clinical value proposition of PKC inhibitors in the TNBC setting.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to methods and compositions for the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) through the selective inhibition of protein kinase C (PKC) signaling, with particular emphasis on targeting PKN2 (protein kinase N2), a PKC-related kinase that is differentially overexpressed in TNBC cells. The following detailed description sets forth specific embodiments of the invention, including pharmaceutical formulations, dosing regimens, combination therapies, and diagnostic applications, all of which are supported by robust experimental evidence demonstrating the selective anti-tumor activity of chelerythrine and related PKC inhibitors in preclinical models of TNBC.

In accordance with the invention, a therapeutically effective amount of a PKC inhibitor is administered to a subject diagnosed with TNBC. The term “therapeutically effective amount” refers to a quantity of the PKC inhibitor sufficient to inhibit tumor growth, reduce tumor burden, induce tumor regression, prolong progression-free survival, or otherwise provide a measurable clinical benefit without causing unacceptable toxicity. The PKC inhibitor may be selected from a class of compounds capable of inhibiting the kinase activity of one or more PKC isozymes, with preference given to those exhibiting selectivity for PKN2 or pan-PKC inhibitors that effectively engage PKN2 among other targets. In a preferred embodiment, the PKC inhibitor is chelerythrine, a naturally occurring benzophenanthridine alkaloid with well-documented PKC-inhibitory properties. Chelerythrine may be used in its native form or as a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, ester, prodrug, or derivative designed to improve solubility, stability, bioavailability, or pharmacokinetic profile. Alternative PKC inhibitors encompassed by the invention include, but are not limited to, Gö6976, rottlerin, sotrastaurin, enzastaurin, and midostaurin, provided they demonstrate activity against PKN2 or induce comparable selective cytotoxicity in TNBC models.

The administration route may be intravenous, intraperitoneal, oral, subcutaneous, or topical, depending on the formulation and clinical context. In preclinical xenograft studies described herein, chelerythrine was administered intraperitoneally at a dose of 5 mg/kg every 3–4 days, resulting in significant suppression of TNBC tumor growth without overt toxicity. Translating this to human dosing would involve standard allometric scaling, yielding an estimated human equivalent dose (HED) of approximately 0.4 mg/kg, though actual clinical dosing would require careful phase I safety and pharmacokinetic evaluation. Pharmaceutical compositions may comprise the PKC inhibitor in combination with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents, formulated as solutions, suspensions, tablets, capsules, liposomes, nanoparticles, or sustained-release depots to optimize delivery and minimize off-target effects.

A critical aspect of the invention is the recognition that PKN2 serves as a key molecular determinant of TNBC sensitivity to PKC inhibition. PKN2, encoded by the *PKN2* gene, belongs to the protein kinase N subfamily of the PKC superfamily and shares structural homology with other PKC isozymes, particularly in the catalytic domain. Unlike conventional and novel PKCs, PKN2 is regulated by Rho GTPases and contains N-terminal HR1 domains that mediate protein-protein interactions. Its biological functions include regulation of actin cytoskeleton dynamics, cell adhesion, migration, and apoptosis. The present inventors have discovered that PKN2 is consistently overexpressed at both mRNA and protein levels across multiple TNBC cell lines—including MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, and MDA-MB-468—whereas non-TNBC lines (MCF7, ZR-75-1, SK-BR-3, MDA-MB-453) exhibit markedly lower expression. This differential expression pattern was confirmed through analysis of the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) database, which revealed significantly higher *PKN2* transcript levels in a cohort of 26 TNBC cell lines compared to 30 non-TNBC lines. Importantly, functional studies using lentiviral shRNA-mediated knockdown demonstrated that PKN2 is essential for TNBC cell proliferation and tumorigenicity: silencing *PKN2* drastically reduced colony formation in vitro and impaired xenograft growth in vivo, with no comparable effects observed in non-TNBC models. These findings establish PKN2 as a bona fide therapeutic target in TNBC and provide a mechanistic rationale for the selective efficacy of pan-PKC inhibitors like chelerythrine, which inhibit PKN2 alongside other PKC isozymes.

While PKN2 emerges as a central player, the invention does not exclude contributions from other PKC family members. For instance, PRKCA (PKCα) was found to be overexpressed in two TNBC lines (MDA-MB-231 and BT-549) and its knockdown impaired colony formation in those specific contexts. This suggests that TNBC may harbor heterogeneous dependencies on distinct PKC isozymes, and optimal therapeutic strategies might involve either pan-inhibition (as achieved by chelerythrine) or tailored combinations targeting multiple relevant isoforms. However, the universal overexpression of PKN2 across all tested TNBC lines positions it as a more reliable and broadly applicable target than PRKCA, which is variably expressed.

The anti-tumor mechanism of chelerythrine in TNBC is predominantly apoptotic. Treatment with chelerythrine (5 μM) induced hallmark morphological changes of apoptosis—chromatin condensation and nuclear fragmentation—in TNBC cells, as visualized by Hoechst 33258 fluorescence microscopy, while non-TNBC cells remained morphologically intact. Biochemically, this was accompanied by robust cleavage of PARP, a substrate of executioner caspases, detectable by Western blot only in TNBC lines. Flow cytometric quantification using Annexin V/propidium iodide staining revealed that 22.2% to 67.8% of TNBC cells underwent apoptosis after 24 hours of chelerythrine exposure, depending on the cell line, whereas non-TNBC cells showed minimal apoptotic induction. Time-course experiments further demonstrated that apoptosis increased progressively over 24 hours, confirming a dynamic and sustained response. In contrast, cell cycle analysis showed only modest and variable effects—such as G0/G1 accumulation in HCC1937 or G2/M arrest in MDA-MB-468—indicating that cell cycle perturbation is not the primary driver of growth inhibition. This apoptotic selectivity aligns with prior reports of chelerythrine’s pro-death mechanisms in other cancers, which include Bcl-2 inhibition, ERK/Akt dephosphorylation, p53 activation, HSF1/hsp70 suppression, and reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation. Future studies will elucidate whether PKN2 inhibition specifically engages any of these pathways in TNBC, particularly given that PKN2 is cleaved by caspase-3 during apoptosis and its C-terminal fragment can modulate Akt activity, potentially amplifying pro-apoptotic signals.

An additional embodiment of the invention involves combination therapy with standard chemotherapeutic agents. Given that chemotherapy remains the backbone of TNBC treatment, enhancing its efficacy through rational drug combinations is clinically imperative. The inventors evaluated the interaction between chelerythrine and taxol (paclitaxel), a microtubule-stabilizing agent commonly used in breast cancer. In vitro SRB assays showed that co-administration of low-dose chelerythrine (2 μM) and taxol (10 nM) produced significantly greater anti-proliferative effects than either drug alone across all four TNBC lines. Combination index (CI) analysis using Compusyn software classified the interaction as synergistic (CI = 0.75) in HCC1937 cells and additive (CI = 1.00–1.14) in the others, indicating that PKC inhibition sensitizes TNBC cells to taxol-induced cytotoxicity. This synergy may arise from complementary mechanisms: while taxol causes mitotic arrest and subsequent apoptosis, chelerythrine directly triggers apoptotic pathways and may disrupt survival signals that confer chemoresistance. Such combinations could allow dose reduction of taxol, thereby mitigating its neurotoxic and myelosuppressive side effects while improving tumor control.

Diagnostic applications are integral to the invention’s implementation. The consistent overexpression of PKN2 in TNBC provides a biomarker for patient selection. Tumor biopsies can be analyzed for *PKN2* mRNA levels via quantitative real-time PCR or for PKN2 protein expression via immunohistochemistry (IHC) or Western blotting. Patients whose tumors exhibit high PKN2 expression would be prioritized for PKC inhibitor therapy, enabling a precision medicine approach. Assay kits comprising primers, probes, or antibodies specific for PKN2 detection are contemplated as part of the invention’s commercial embodiment.

Safety considerations are addressed by the observed selectivity of chelerythrine for TNBC over non-TNBC cells, which suggests a favorable therapeutic window. Moreover, emerging evidence links PKC inhibition to cardiovascular benefits: TNBC patients have elevated rates of metabolic syndrome and obesity—key CVD risk factors—and chelerythrine has been shown to improve endothelial function in diabetic models. Thus, unlike many cancer therapies that exacerbate CVD risk (e.g., anthracyclines, HER2 inhibitors), chelerythrine may offer dual oncologic and cardioprotective advantages, particularly relevant given that CVD is the leading cause of non-cancer mortality in breast cancer survivors.

In summary, the preferred embodiments of the invention encompass: (1) monotherapy with chelerythrine or other PKN2-targeting PKC inhibitors for TNBC; (2) combination regimens pairing PKC inhibitors with taxol or other chemotherapies; (3) diagnostic methods for PKN2-based patient stratification; and (4) pharmaceutical formulations optimized for clinical delivery. These embodiments are supported by comprehensive in vitro and in vivo data demonstrating selective TNBC growth inhibition, apoptosis induction, PKN2 dependency, and chemosensitization, thereby providing a robust foundation for clinical translation in a disease area of high unmet need.
