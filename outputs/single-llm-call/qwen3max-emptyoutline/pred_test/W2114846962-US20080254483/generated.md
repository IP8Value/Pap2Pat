# DESCRIPTION

## 1. FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of diagnostic medicine and, more specifically, to methods and compositions for the detection, diagnosis, prognosis, and monitoring of renal cell carcinoma (RCC) in human subjects. The invention provides a novel and clinically significant biomarker—pro-form matrix metalloproteinase-7 (pro-MMP-7)—that is detectable in serum and exhibits markedly elevated concentrations in patients diagnosed with RCC compared to healthy individuals or those suffering from non-malignant or other malignant conditions. The invention further encompasses monoclonal antibodies specifically reactive with pro-MMP-7 and mature MMP-7, as well as immunoassay systems utilizing these antibodies for the quantitative determination of MMP-7 levels in biological fluids, particularly human serum. Additionally, the invention includes diagnostic kits, prognostic tools, and therapeutic monitoring platforms based on the measurement of serum pro-MMP-7, thereby offering a non-invasive, sensitive, and reliable approach for managing RCC across diverse clinical contexts, including early detection in high-risk populations, post-surgical surveillance for recurrence, and evaluation of response to targeted therapies. The identification of pro-MMP-7 as an immunogenic protein uniquely recognized by autoantibodies in the sera of RCC patients represents a significant advancement in tumor immunology and serological biomarker discovery, enabling the development of companion diagnostics that may improve patient outcomes through timely intervention and personalized treatment strategies.

## 2. BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) constitutes the most prevalent form of kidney cancer in adults, accounting for approximately 90% of all malignant renal tumors. Despite advances in imaging modalities such as computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), a substantial proportion of RCC cases are still diagnosed at advanced stages due to the absence of specific symptoms in early disease. Consequently, there remains an unmet clinical need for reliable, non-invasive biomarkers capable of facilitating early detection, accurate prognosis, and effective monitoring of therapeutic response in RCC patients. Historically, efforts to identify such biomarkers have been hindered by the heterogeneity of RCC subtypes, the lack of validated circulating markers with sufficient sensitivity and specificity, and the limitations of conventional approaches that rely on tissue-based analyses rather than accessible biofluids like serum or plasma.

Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are a family of zinc-dependent endopeptidases involved in the degradation of extracellular matrix components and play critical roles in various physiological and pathological processes, including embryogenesis, wound healing, angiogenesis, and cancer progression. Among these, MMP-7 (also known as matrilysin-1) has garnered attention due to its overexpression in multiple epithelial malignancies, including colorectal, breast, lung, and ovarian cancers. In the context of RCC, prior studies have demonstrated elevated MMP-7 expression in tumor tissues, correlating with higher tumor grade, advanced stage, and poor prognosis. However, these findings were largely confined to immunohistochemical assessments of resected specimens and did not translate into a practical, blood-based diagnostic tool.

Serological proteome analysis (SERPA), which combines two-dimensional electrophoresis (2DE) with immunoblotting using patient sera, has emerged as a powerful strategy for identifying tumor-associated antigens that elicit humoral immune responses. This approach leverages the body’s own immune system as a biosensor for aberrantly expressed or modified proteins in cancer cells. While SERPA and related techniques such as Proteomex and SEREX have successfully uncovered autoantibody targets in several cancers, their application to RCC had not yielded a clinically actionable serum marker prior to the present invention. Notably, existing literature lacked evidence of circulating pro-MMP-7 as an immunogenic entity or its utility as a quantitative serum biomarker for RCC.

Conventional immunoassays for MMPs, including enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs), often suffer from cross-reactivity with other MMP family members, inability to distinguish between latent (pro-) and active forms, and insufficient sensitivity for low-abundance analytes in complex biological matrices. Moreover, many commercial assays target total MMP-7 without differentiating isoforms, potentially obscuring biologically relevant information. The development of a homogeneous, dual-monoclonal antibody-based fluoroimmunoassay using time-resolved amplified cryptate emission (TRACE) technology addresses these shortcomings by enabling rapid, specific, and highly sensitive quantification of both pro- and mature MMP-7 in serum, with minimal sample preparation and reduced interference from matrix effects.

Prior to this invention, no validated serum biomarker existed for the routine clinical management of RCC. Although vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and other cytokines have been explored, their performance has been inconsistent across studies, limiting their adoption in practice. The absence of a reliable humoral marker has impeded the implementation of cost-effective screening programs for high-risk cohorts—such as patients with von Hippel-Lindau syndrome, long-term hemodialysis recipients, or renal transplant recipients—and has left clinicians without objective tools to assess residual disease after nephrectomy or to monitor response to emerging targeted therapies like tyrosine kinase inhibitors and immune checkpoint modulators.

Thus, there exists a compelling need for a novel, robust, and clinically translatable biomarker that can be measured in serum to support the full spectrum of RCC care—from early detection in asymptomatic individuals to dynamic assessment of disease burden during treatment. The present invention fulfills this need by establishing pro-MMP-7 as a previously unrecognized immunogenic protein secreted by RCC cells and demonstrating its significantly elevated concentration in the serum of RCC patients, thereby providing the foundation for a new generation of diagnostic, prognostic, and theranostic applications in renal oncology.

## 3. SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive solution for the detection, diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic monitoring of renal cell carcinoma (RCC) through the identification and utilization of pro-matrix metalloproteinase-7 (pro-MMP-7) as a novel and clinically valuable serum biomarker. In one aspect, the invention discloses that pro-MMP-7, a 29 kDa latent precursor form of MMP-7 with an isoelectric point (pI) of approximately 7.7, is secreted by RCC cells and is specifically recognized by immunoglobulin G (IgG) autoantibodies present in the sera of RCC patients but not in healthy controls or patients with non-RCC conditions. This immunogenic property was discovered through an integrated proteomic and serological screening approach involving two-dimensional electrophoresis (2DE) of secreted proteins from the human RCC cell line CAL 54, followed by immunoblotting with pooled sera from RCC patients and healthy donors, leading to the selective identification of pro-MMP-7 as a differentially reactive antigen.

In another aspect, the invention provides monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to epitopes present on both pro-MMP-7 and mature (active) MMP-7. These antibodies, designated herein as clones 01S and 29S, were generated by immunizing BALB/c mice with recombinant human MMP-7 and subsequent hybridoma technology. Both clones exhibit high affinity and specificity for MMP-7 isoforms, as confirmed by Western blot analysis of CAL 54 cell supernatants and recombinant standards. Notably, while all four tested clones (01S, 04S, 21S, and 29S) recognize pro-MMP-7, only clones 04S, 21S, and 29S detect the mature 19 kDa form, indicating differential epitope recognition; however, the combination of 01S and 29S was selected for assay development due to optimal signal-to-noise characteristics and compatibility with homogeneous immunoassay formats.

A further aspect of the invention is a homogeneous, dual-monoclonal sandwich fluoroimmunoassay based on time-resolved amplified cryptate emission (TRACE) technology, which enables rapid, sensitive, and quantitative measurement of total MMP-7 (including both pro- and mature forms) in human serum. In this assay, the monoclonal antibody of clone 01S is conjugated to europium cryptate, serving as the donor fluorophore, while the antibody of clone 29S is labeled with AF647, acting as the acceptor fluorophore. Upon binding of both antibodies to MMP-7 in a sample, energy transfer occurs, generating a measurable fluorescent signal proportional to the analyte concentration. The assay demonstrates excellent analytical performance, including a linear calibration curve (r² > 0.99) over a range of 0.5 to 40 µg/L, intra-assay coefficient of variation (CV) of 4.2%, and mean recovery of 96% upon spiking of recombinant MMP-7 into serum, confirming accuracy and precision suitable for clinical use.

Critically, the invention establishes that serum concentrations of MMP-7 are significantly elevated in patients with RCC compared to healthy individuals and disease controls. In a cohort of 30 RCC patients, median serum MMP-7 was 7.56 µg/L (range: 3.12–30.5 µg/L), whereas healthy controls (n=30) exhibited a median of 2.13 µg/L (range: 0.17–3.5 µg/L), with a p-value <0.0001 by Mann-Whitney U test. The assay achieved a sensitivity of 93% (95% CI: 78%–99%) at a specificity of 75% (95% CI: 59%–87%) for distinguishing RCC from non-RCC conditions, underscoring its diagnostic potential. Importantly, Western blot analysis of human serum samples revealed the exclusive presence of the 29 kDa pro-MMP-7 isoform, with no detectable mature MMP-7, suggesting that pro-MMP-7 is the predominant circulating form in both health and disease, possibly due to stabilization via complexation with serum proteins or polymerization, as previously hypothesized.

The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits comprising the disclosed monoclonal antibodies, assay buffers, calibrators traceable to recombinant human MMP-7, and instructions for use in quantifying serum MMP-7 levels. These kits are intended for in vitro diagnostic use in clinical laboratories and may be adapted for automation on platforms such as the Brahms Kryptor system. Additionally, the invention provides methods for diagnosing RCC in a subject by measuring serum MMP-7 concentration and comparing it to a predetermined threshold (e.g., >3.5 µg/L), wherein a value above the threshold indicates a high likelihood of RCC. Similarly, the invention enables prognostic stratification by correlating baseline or serial MMP-7 measurements with disease stage, risk of recurrence, or overall survival, although larger longitudinal studies are needed to fully define these relationships.

Moreover, the invention supports therapeutic monitoring by allowing clinicians to track changes in serum MMP-7 levels during treatment with targeted agents or immunotherapies, where decreasing concentrations may indicate response, while rising levels could signal progression or resistance. The high sensitivity of the assay makes it particularly suited for detecting minimal residual disease after nephrectomy, potentially reducing reliance on frequent imaging studies. Finally, the discovery of pro-MMP-7 as an immunogenic tumor-associated antigen opens avenues for vaccine development or antibody-based therapeutics targeting this neoepitope, especially given the observed IgG reactivity specific to the pro-form, which may reflect exposure of unique N-terminal prodomain sequences in RCC cells.

In summary, the present invention transforms the scientific observation of pro-MMP-7 immunogenicity into a clinically actionable platform for RCC management, offering a non-invasive, reproducible, and informative serological test that addresses longstanding gaps in renal oncology diagnostics and care.

## MMP-7

Matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP-7), also known as matrilysin-1, is a member of the matrix metalloproteinase family of zinc-dependent endopeptidases that play essential roles in the remodeling of the extracellular matrix (ECM) during both physiological and pathological processes. Unlike many other MMPs that are membrane-bound or require activation by other proteases, MMP-7 is secreted as a soluble zymogen (pro-MMP-7) and can autoactivate under certain conditions, making it uniquely capable of initiating proteolytic cascades. The human MMP-7 gene is located on chromosome 11q22.3 and encodes a pre-proenzyme of 198 amino acids, which includes a 19-amino acid signal peptide, an 80-amino acid prodomain containing a conserved cysteine residue that maintains latency through interaction with the catalytic zinc ion (the “cysteine switch” mechanism), and a 172-amino acid catalytic domain. Upon removal of the prodomain—typically by proteolytic cleavage mediated by plasmin, trypsin, or other MMPs—the mature, enzymatically active form of MMP-7 (approximately 19 kDa) is generated, exhibiting broad substrate specificity against ECM components such as fibronectin, laminin, elastin, and proteoglycans, as well as non-matrix proteins including E-cadherin, Fas ligand, and pro-tumor necrosis factor-alpha.

In normal physiology, MMP-7 is expressed at low levels in tissues such as the uterus, prostate, and mammary gland, where it contributes to tissue repair, epithelial cell migration, and innate immune defense. However, aberrant overexpression of MMP-7 has been consistently documented in a wide array of epithelial malignancies, including colorectal, gastric, pancreatic, breast, lung, and ovarian cancers, where it promotes tumor invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis, and evasion of apoptosis. In renal cell carcinoma (RCC), prior immunohistochemical studies have shown that MMP-7 is upregulated in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal kidney, with higher expression correlating with advanced Fuhrman nuclear grade, larger tumor size, venous invasion, and reduced overall survival. Despite these associations, the presence and clinical relevance of circulating MMP-7—particularly its latent pro-form—in the serum of RCC patients had not been established prior to the present invention.

The current invention reveals that pro-MMP-7 (29 kDa, pI ~7.7) is secreted by RCC cells, as demonstrated by proteomic analysis of the CAL 54 cell line supernatant, and serves as an immunogenic antigen capable of eliciting a humoral immune response in RCC patients. This finding is significant because it identifies pro-MMP-7 not merely as a passive byproduct of tumor metabolism but as a molecule that interacts with the host immune system, potentially exposing unique conformational or sequence-specific neoepitopes within the prodomain that are absent or masked in healthy tissues. The exclusive reactivity of patient-derived IgG with pro-MMP-7—and not with the mature form—in immunoblotting experiments suggests that the immunodominant epitope(s) reside within the N-terminal proregion, which is cleaved off during activation. Such neoepitopes may arise from post-translational modifications, altered folding in the tumor microenvironment, or dysregulated processing pathways characteristic of RCC.

Furthermore, the invention confirms that pro-MMP-7 is the predominant, if not sole, circulating isoform in human serum, as evidenced by Western blot analyses of both healthy and RCC patient sera, which consistently showed a single band at ~29 kDa with no detectable mature MMP-7 (~19 kDa), even when using antibodies capable of recognizing both forms. This observation aligns with earlier reports suggesting that pro-MMP-7 circulates in serum as high-molecular-weight complexes or polymers stabilized by interactions with α2-macroglobulin or other serum proteins, which may protect it from premature activation or degradation. The inability to detect active MMP-7 in serum likely reflects either extremely low concentrations below the limit of detection (<0.7 µg/L in the described system) or rapid inhibition by endogenous tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs), particularly TIMP-1, which binds tightly to active MMP-7.

From a diagnostic standpoint, the quantification of total MMP-7—which, in serum, effectively measures pro-MMP-7 due to the absence of the mature form—provides a robust surrogate for tumor burden or activity in RCC. The developed TRACE-based immunoassay, utilizing monoclonal antibodies 01S and 29S, captures both isoforms but, in practice, reports pro-MMP-7 levels in clinical samples. The structural integrity and antigenicity of pro-MMP-7 in serum render it an ideal analyte for immunoassay development, as it is stable, abundant relative to other potential markers, and differentially expressed in disease versus health. The molecular weight and isoelectric point determined by 2DE (29 kDa, pI 7.7) match theoretical predictions for human pro-MMP-7 (UniProt accession P09237), and mass spectrometric sequencing of tryptic peptides (FYLYDSETK and VIEIMQKPR, corresponding to residues 42–50 and 78–86) provided definitive confirmation of identity.

Thus, MMP-7, particularly in its pro-form, emerges from this invention as a central player in RCC biology and a linchpin for a new class of liquid biopsy-based diagnostics. Its dual role—as both a functional mediator of tumor progression and an immunogenic trigger of autoantibody production—underscores its biological significance and validates its selection as a biomarker candidate through the integrated proteomics-serology pipeline employed herein.

## 4. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION AND EXAMPLES

The following experimental section details the methodologies, materials, and representative examples that underpin the invention, providing reproducible protocols for the identification of pro-MMP-7 as an RCC-associated immunogenic protein, the generation and characterization of specific monoclonal antibodies, the development and validation of a homogeneous fluoroimmunoassay, and the clinical evaluation of serum MMP-7 as a diagnostic biomarker.

**Example 1: Cell Culture and Secretome Preparation**

The human renal cell carcinoma cell line CAL 54, originally derived from a malignant pleural effusion of an RCC patient, was obtained from the German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures (DSMZ GmbH). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum, 2 mmol/L L-glutamine, 100 kU/L streptomycin, 400 kU/L penicillin, 400 µg/L hydrocortisone, and 100 mmol/L sodium pyruvate, maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. For secretome collection, cells were seeded at a density of 4 × 10⁹ cells per liter and allowed to adhere overnight. The culture medium was then replaced with serum-free DMEM, and cells were incubated for 48 hours to allow accumulation of secreted proteins. Supernatants were harvested, centrifuged at 2000g for 15 minutes at 4°C to remove cellular debris, filtered through a 0.2-µm pore-size membrane (Millipore), and dialyzed against 20 mmol/L Tris-HCl buffer (pH 6.8). Proteins were precipitated by addition of ice-cold ethanol to a final concentration of 80% (v/v), incubated for 1 hour at -20°C, and pelleted by centrifugation at 38,000g for 25 minutes at 4°C. The resulting precipitate was washed once with 80% ethanol, air-dried, and stored at -80°C until use.

**Example 2: Two-Dimensional Electrophoresis and Immunoblotting**

Total secreted proteins from CAL 54 (100 µg for analytical gels, 300 µg for preparative gels) were solubilized in isoelectric focusing (IEF) buffer containing 8 mol/L urea, 20 g/L CHAPS, 20 mL/L Triton X-100, 8 g/L Pharmalyte pH 3–10, 100 mmol/L dithiothreitol, 2 mL/L Tergitol NP7, and traces of bromophenol blue. Samples were loaded onto 18-cm nonlinear immobilized pH gradient (IPG) strips (pH 3–10) and subjected to IEF using an IPGphor apparatus (Amersham Biosciences) according to standard protocols. Following IEF, IPG strips were equilibrated sequentially in SDS-containing buffers with dithiothreitol (reducing agent) and iodoacetamide (alkylating agent), then placed atop 12.5% polyacrylamide SDS-PAGE gels (20 × 20 cm) for the second dimension. Gels were silver-stained using the method of Shevchenko et al. and scanned for image analysis. For immunoblotting, proteins were transferred to nitrocellulose membranes, blocked with 3% gelatin in Tris-buffered saline (TBS), and probed with pooled human sera (1:100 dilution): one pool comprised equal volumes from six RCC patients (stages II–IV), and another from ten healthy donors. Bound IgG was detected using alkaline phosphatase-conjugated anti-human IgG and visualized with NBT/BCIP chromogenic substrate. Immunoreactive spots unique to the RCC serum blot were identified by computer-assisted subtraction using Melanie II software.

**Example 3: Protein Identification by Mass Spectrometry**

Immunoreactive protein spots of interest were excised from preparative 2DE gels, destained, and digested in-gel with sequencing-grade trypsin. Resulting peptide mixtures were analyzed by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry on a BIFLEX III instrument (Bruker-Daltonik). Peptide mass fingerprints were searched against the Swiss-Prot database using Mascot software, with mass tolerance set to ±50 ppm. Tandem MS (MS/MS) was performed on selected ions using a QSTAR quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer (Applied Biosystems) to confirm sequence assignments. Spot S4, exhibiting a molecular weight of 29 kDa and pI of 7.7, yielded peptide masses matching human pro-MMP-7 (Swiss-Prot P09237), with MS/MS confirming sequences FYLYDSETK (residues 42–50) and VIEIMQKPR (residues 78–86).

**Example 4: Generation and Characterization of Anti-MMP-7 Monoclonal Antibodies**

Four female BALB/c mice (6 weeks old) were immunized subcutaneously with 26 µg of recombinant human MMP-7 (R&D Systems) emulsified in complete Freund’s adjuvant, followed by booster injections in incomplete Freund’s adjuvant at 4-week intervals. Three months post-primary immunization, splenocytes were fused with P3-X63-Ag8.653 mouse myeloma cells using polyethylene glycol. Hybridoma supernatants were screened by homogeneous fluoroimmunoassay for reactivity against recombinant MMP-7. Four positive clones—01S, 04S, 21S, and 29S—were expanded and antibodies purified by protein A affinity chromatography. Western blot analysis using CAL 54 supernatant and recombinant standards confirmed that all four clones recognized pro-MMP-7 (29 kDa), while clones 04S, 21S, and 29S also detected mature MMP-7 (19 kDa), indicating differential epitope specificity.

**Example 5: Development of Homogeneous Fluoroimmunoassay for MMP-7**

A sandwich TRACE immunoassay was developed using monoclonal antibodies 01S and 29S. Antibody 01S was conjugated to europium cryptate (Cis Bio International), and antibody 29S to AF647 fluorophore (Molecular Probes), following manufacturer protocols. Conjugates were diluted in assay buffer (100 mmol/L sodium phosphate, 1 g/L bovine serum albumin, 600 mmol/L KF, 0.2 g/L nonspecific mouse IgG, pH 7.1) to final concentrations of 0.35 mg/L (cryptate) and 5 mg/L (AF647). Calibrators (0.5–40 µg/L) were prepared by spiking CAL 54 supernatant (containing natural pro-MMP-7) into newborn calf serum, with concentrations assigned by comparison to purified recombinant MMP-7. Assays were performed on a Brahms Kryptor analyzer: 50 µL sample/calibrator, 50 µL each antibody conjugate were mixed and incubated at 37°C for 50 minutes. Fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) signals were measured and converted to MMP-7 concentrations via a 4-parameter logistic calibration curve.

**Example 6: Clinical Validation of Serum MMP-7 as an RCC Biomarker**

Serum samples were collected from 30 histologically confirmed RCC patients (prior to surgery or therapy), 40 control patients with benign or other malignant conditions, and 30 healthy donors, in compliance with ethical guidelines and informed consent. Samples were processed within 30 minutes of venipuncture, centrifuged at 2000g for 15 minutes at 4°C, and stored at -20°C. MMP-7 concentrations were measured in duplicate using the validated TRACE assay. Statistical analysis (GraphPad Prism 4.0) revealed significantly higher MMP-7 in RCC patients (median 7.56 µg/L) versus healthy controls (median 2.13 µg/L; P < 0.0001, Mann-Whitney U test). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.89, with 93% sensitivity at 75% specificity using a cutoff of 3.5 µg/L. Western blot of representative sera confirmed the presence of only pro-MMP-7 (29 kDa), with no mature form detected.

These examples collectively demonstrate the feasibility, specificity, and clinical utility of pro-MMP-7 as a serum biomarker for RCC, supported by rigorous analytical and clinical validation, thereby enabling its translation into diagnostic practice.
