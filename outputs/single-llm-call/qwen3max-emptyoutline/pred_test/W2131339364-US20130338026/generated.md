# DESCRIPTION

## PRIOR ART

Endometrial carcinoma represents the most prevalent malignancy of the female pelvic gynecologic system, with an estimated lifetime risk among women ranging from 2% to 3%. While approximately 75% of cases are diagnosed at a localized stage and are amenable to surgical resection, a significant proportion—15% to 20%—of these ostensibly early-stage tumors experience disease recurrence. Moreover, tumors presenting with metastatic spread at diagnosis, or those that recur after initial treatment, exhibit poor responsiveness to conventional chemotherapeutic regimens and radiation therapy, resulting in a grim prognosis and high mortality rates. This clinical reality underscores a critical unmet need for robust biomarkers capable of identifying patients at heightened risk of relapse, as well as for the development of novel therapeutic strategies tailored to aggressive or metastatic endometrial cancer.

Despite the substantial burden of this disease, research efforts in endometrial cancer have historically lagged behind those dedicated to other major malignancies such as breast and ovarian cancers. One promising avenue for addressing this gap is the identification of somatically altered genes that function as drivers of tumor progression. Such genetic alterations can serve a dual purpose: they can act as prognostic or predictive biomarkers to stratify patient risk, and the genes themselves can represent viable targets for the development of new, mechanism-based therapies.

A well-established genomic feature in many human cancers is the amplification of chromosomal region 8q24. This locus is not only the most frequently amplified region across a broad spectrum of cancer types but also serves as a negative prognostic indicator in several of them. The *MYC* proto-oncogene, located within this amplicon, has long been recognized as a potent driver of oncogenesis due to its central role in regulating cell proliferation, metabolism, apoptosis, and differentiation. Consequently, *MYC* has been widely presumed to be the primary target of the 8q24 amplification event. However, the functional consequences of this amplification in the specific context of endometrial carcinoma have remained largely uncharacterized. Furthermore, it is increasingly appreciated that focal amplifications in cancer genomes often encompass multiple genes, and the selective pressure driving the amplification may act on more than one of these co-amplified genes. This phenomenon, known as oncogene cooperation, has been documented in other cancer types, where neighboring genes within an amplicon can functionally interact to enhance tumorigenic potential.

In the vicinity of *MYC* on 8q24 lies the gene *ATAD2* (ATPase family AAA domain-containing protein 2), which encodes a bromodomain-containing protein. Prior research in other cancer contexts, notably breast, lung, and prostate cancers, has linked *ATAD2* overexpression to a poor clinical prognosis. Functionally, ATAD2 has been shown to act as a transcriptional co-regulator for MYC, binding directly to the MYC protein and to the E-box regions of MYC target genes, thereby potentiating MYC-dependent transcription. This established biological relationship suggests that *ATAD2* could be a co-target of the 8q24 amplification, working in concert with *MYC* to drive a more aggressive cancer phenotype. However, prior to the present invention, there was no direct evidence linking 8q24 amplification to *ATAD2* overexpression in endometrial cancer, nor was there a comprehensive analysis demonstrating that *ATAD2* expression, rather than *MYC* expression alone, is the dominant molecular correlate of MYC pathway activation and poor clinical outcomes in this disease. The prior art, therefore, fails to recognize the critical and independent role of *ATAD2* as both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target in endometrial carcinoma and other MYC-driven cancers, a gap that the present invention decisively addresses.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for the prognosis and treatment of endometrial carcinoma and other MYC-dependent cancers by identifying and targeting the *ATAD2* gene and its protein product. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that in endometrial cancer, the 8q24 amplification, while encompassing the well-known *MYC* oncogene, exerts its primary oncogenic effect through the co-amplification and consequent overexpression of the *ATAD2* gene. Contrary to the prevailing assumption that *MYC* is the sole or primary driver of this amplification, the inventors have demonstrated that *ATAD2* expression is a far stronger correlate of both 8q24 amplification and the downstream activation of the MYC signaling pathway than is *MYC* expression itself. This finding is pivotal, as it shifts the focus from the notoriously "undruggable" MYC protein to its co-regulator, ATAD2, which presents a more tractable target for therapeutic intervention.

The invention establishes that high levels of *ATAD2* expression, whether driven by genomic copy number gain, transcriptional upregulation by E2F factors, or other mechanisms, are strongly associated with aggressive disease features in endometrial cancer, including high tumor grade, non-endometrioid histological subtype, estrogen receptor (ER) negativity, metastasis, and, most critically, reduced progression-free survival and increased disease-specific mortality. Importantly, *ATAD2* expression was found to be an independent predictor of poor prognosis, even after adjusting for established clinical variables such as ER status. This positions *ATAD2* as a powerful new prognostic biomarker that can be used to identify a high-risk patient population that would benefit from more aggressive or targeted therapeutic strategies.

Furthermore, the invention extends beyond prognosis to provide a clear therapeutic rationale. Through functional studies in endometrial and breast cancer cell lines, the inventors have shown that cancer cells exhibiting dependence on MYC for their survival and proliferation are equally dependent on ATAD2. Knockdown of *ATAD2* using RNA interference resulted in a significant reduction in cell viability that was highly correlated with the effects of *MYC* knockdown. This demonstrates that ATAD2 is not merely a passive marker of MYC activity but is a critical, non-redundant component of the MYC oncogenic program. Consequently, the invention provides a method for treating a MYC-dependent cancer in a patient by administering a therapeutically effective amount of an agent that inhibits the expression or activity of ATAD2. Such agents include, but are not limited to, small molecule inhibitors of the ATAD2 bromodomain, antisense oligonucleotides, siRNA, shRNA, or other nucleic acid-based therapeutics designed to silence *ATAD2* gene expression.

The invention also reveals that the dependency on ATAD2 in MYC-driven cancers can be exploited using existing classes of drugs. Specifically, the inventors found that sensitivity to the histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor Trichostatin-A was strongly correlated with MYC and ATAD2 dependency in both endometrial and breast cancer cell lines. This suggests that HDAC inhibitors, or more selective bromodomain inhibitors, may be particularly effective in the subset of patients whose tumors are characterized by high *ATAD2* expression or 8q24 amplification. In summary, the present invention provides a comprehensive framework for improving the management of endometrial and other MYC-driven cancers by offering a novel prognostic biomarker (*ATAD2*) and a validated therapeutic target (ATAD2 protein), thereby enabling a more precise and effective approach to patient care.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

### Patient Series

The foundation of the present invention is a meticulously curated and clinically annotated series of endometrial carcinoma specimens, comprising both primary tumors and matched metastases, collected from patients treated at Haukeland University Hospital in Norway. This patient series was assembled under strict ethical oversight, with approval from the Norwegian Data Inspectorate, the Norwegian Social Sciences Data Services, and the Regional Research Ethics Committee in Medicine, Western Norway. All participants provided written informed consent, ensuring the highest standards of ethical conduct and patient autonomy. The series is divided into distinct cohorts for discovery and validation purposes. The primary investigation series consists of 82 endometrial cancer samples, for which comprehensive genome-wide copy-number data (from 70 of these tumors) and gene expression profiles were available from prior work. A subset of 57 tumors from this series had been previously profiled using Agilent 21k and 22k oligoarrays, providing a robust baseline for integrative genomic analysis. For validation, an internal validation series was generated, comprising 40 primary and 19 metastatic endometrial cancers, all recruited from the same geographical region to minimize population-based confounding factors. This internal validation series was analyzed using Agilent 44K expression arrays to ensure consistency and comparability with the primary investigation series. An external validation series was also employed, consisting of 111 previously published endometrial tumor expression profiles, which served to test the generalizability of the findings beyond the institution of origin. To further solidify the clinical correlations, two additional focused assay series were utilized: a quantitative PCR (qPCR) validation series of 162 endometrial cancer samples, which allowed for precise, high-throughput measurement of *ATAD2* and *MYC* transcript levels, and a fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) series of 399 formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor samples, which enabled the direct visualization and quantification of 8q24 copy number alterations at the single-cell level within the tissue architecture. All patients in these series were followed prospectively from the time of their primary surgery until either death or October 2010, whichever came first, generating a rich dataset of long-term clinical outcomes, including progression-free survival and disease-specific survival. The detailed patient characteristics and histopathological variables for all internal datasets, including age, tumor stage (FIGO), grade, histological subtype, and estrogen receptor status, have been comprehensively documented and are integral to the multivariate statistical analyses that underpin the prognostic claims of the invention. The inclusion of paired primary and metastatic samples in the FISH and internal validation series was particularly crucial, as it allowed for a direct comparison of molecular features between the primary site and its metastatic progeny, revealing the dynamic evolution of 8q24 amplification and *ATAD2* expression during disease progression. This well-characterized patient series, with its multi-layered molecular and clinical data, provides the essential empirical basis for the novel insights and methods disclosed herein.

### RNA Analysis

The RNA analysis central to the present invention was performed to comprehensively assess the global gene expression landscape of endometrial carcinomas and to specifically quantify the transcript levels of key genes of interest, namely *ATAD2* and *MYC*. Total RNA was extracted from frozen tumor specimens using standardized protocols to ensure high integrity and purity. This RNA was then hybridized to high-density Agilent 44K oligonucleotide microarrays (Catalog No. G4112F), following the manufacturer’s instructions and established laboratory procedures. The use of this platform allowed for the simultaneous measurement of the expression levels of over 41,000 transcripts, providing a genome-wide view of the transcriptional state of each tumor. The raw signal intensities from the microarrays were processed and analyzed using the BRB-ArrayTools software suite developed by the National Cancer Institute, a widely accepted and robust bioinformatics platform for microarray data analysis. A critical step in the data processing pipeline was batch median normalization, which was applied to correct for any systematic technical variations that might have arisen between different experimental batches or array runs, thereby ensuring that the observed differences in gene expression were biologically meaningful and not artifacts of the experimental process. From this global expression data, the inventors were able to derive a quantitative measure of "MYC signaling strength" for each tumor. This was achieved by applying Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) to a pre-defined signature of 68 genes that have been consistently shown to be upregulated by MYC across multiple biological contexts and experimental assays. The GSEA algorithm calculates an enrichment score that reflects the degree to which this MYC target gene set is coordinately overexpressed in a given sample, providing a more robust and biologically relevant readout of MYC pathway activity than the expression level of *MYC* alone. In addition to the primary 68-gene signature, the inventors also evaluated five additional, independently derived MYC activation signatures obtained from the Gene Set Enrichment Database, to confirm the robustness of their findings across different definitions of MYC activity. The correlation between the expression of individual genes, particularly *ATAD2* and *MYC*, and this composite measure of MYC signaling strength was a key analytical step that led to the core discovery of the invention. To validate the microarray findings with an orthogonal and highly sensitive technique, a subset of samples was also analyzed by real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR). For this, cDNA was synthesized from 1 µg of total RNA using High Capacity RNA-to-cDNA kits. The expression of *ATAD2* and *MYC* was then quantified using TaqMan Gene Expression Assays (Hs00204205 and Hs00905030, respectively) on microfluidic cards, with *GAPDH* (Hs99999905_m1) serving as the endogenous control for normalization. All qPCR reactions were run in triplicate, and the data were analyzed using RQ Manager software to ensure precision and reliability. This multi-faceted RNA analysis strategy, combining genome-wide profiling with targeted, high-sensitivity validation, provided the rigorous and reproducible data necessary to establish the central role of *ATAD2* in MYC pathway activation in endometrial cancer.

### DNA Analysis

The DNA analysis performed in the course of the present invention was designed to characterize the genomic landscape of endometrial carcinomas, with a specific focus on identifying and quantifying the 8q24 amplification event and its relationship to gene expression. Genome-wide copy-number profiles for the primary investigation series of 70 tumors were generated using high-resolution single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) arrays, a technology that allows for the detection of copy number gains, losses, and amplifications across the entire genome. This analysis confirmed that 8q24 is a frequent site of amplification in endometrial cancer, with 23% of the tumors in this cohort exhibiting this alteration. The inventors noted that these amplifications were typically of low-level, with copy numbers generally not exceeding 4.7, and that the amplicon size was significantly larger than the typical distribution of amplification sizes observed in endometrial cancer, suggesting a strong selective pressure to maintain a large genomic segment. To complement the genome-wide SNP array data and to provide a direct, visual assessment of the 8q24 copy number in a much larger, independent cohort of FFPE tumor samples, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) was employed. Tissue microarrays (TMAs) were constructed from the 399-patient FISH series, with cores taken from the highest-grade areas of each tumor to ensure the analysis captured the most aggressive clone. These TMAs were hybridized with a dual-probe FISH kit: the MYC Spectrum Orange FISH probe, which targets the 8q24 locus, and the Chromosome Enumeration Probe 8 (CEP8), which targets the centromere of chromosome 8 and serves as a reference for ploidy. Hybridization was performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol, and signals were counted in 40 to 60 non-overlapping nuclei within areas of optimal tissue digestion. An amplification was defined as a MYC/CEP8 ratio greater than 1.0, a standard threshold that accounts for polysomy of chromosome 8. This FISH analysis not only confirmed the presence of 8q24 amplification in endometrial cancer but also allowed for a direct correlation between the genomic alteration and long-term clinical outcomes in a large, clinically annotated patient cohort. The DNA analysis, by integrating high-resolution genome-wide data with targeted, clinically applicable FISH, provided the definitive evidence that 8q24 amplification is a recurrent and clinically relevant genomic event in endometrial cancer, setting the stage for the subsequent identification of its key target genes.

### Statistics

The statistical analyses underpinning the present invention were designed to rigorously test the hypotheses generated from the integrated genomic and clinical data and to establish the robustness and independence of the observed associations. The relationship between molecular variables (e.g., 8q24 amplification status, *ATAD2* expression, *MYC* expression, MYC signaling strength) and categorical clinical or pathological variables (e.g., tumor grade, histological subtype, ER status) was assessed using Pearson’s chi-squared (χ²) test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate for the data distribution. The association between continuous molecular variables was evaluated using two-sided Student’s t-tests for group comparisons and linear regression analysis for correlation. A key statistical approach was the use of multivariate linear regression to model the determinants of *ATAD2* expression. This analysis included *ATAD2* copy number, *ESR1* (estrogen receptor alpha) expression, and the expression of various E2F transcription factors as predictor variables. The model was refined to identify the combination of predictors that best explained the variance in *ATAD2* expression, with the final model including *ATAD2* copy number, *ESR1* expression, and *E2F1* expression, which together explained 77% of the variation in endometrial cancer. Survival analysis was a cornerstone of the statistical work. Univariate survival analysis was performed using the log-rank test to compare progression-free survival and disease-specific survival between groups defined by molecular markers (e.g., high vs. low *ATAD2* expression, presence vs. absence of 8q24 amplification). To determine whether a molecular marker was an independent predictor of outcome, multivariate survival analysis was conducted using the Cox proportional hazards (Mantel-Cox) model. This model adjusted for established clinical and pathological prognostic factors, such as ER status, to isolate the unique contribution of the novel marker (e.g., *ATAD2* expression) to patient risk. The strength of the association was reported as a Hazard Ratio (HR) with a corresponding p-value. For the functional studies in cell lines, the correlation between the effects of *ATAD2* knockdown and *MYC* knockdown on cell viability was assessed using linear regression, with the coefficient of determination (R²) and p-value reported to quantify the strength and significance of the relationship. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The consistent application of these rigorous statistical methods across multiple independent datasets provided the high level of confidence required to support the novel claims of the invention regarding the prognostic and therapeutic significance of *ATAD2* in endometrial cancer.

### Results and Discussion

The results of the integrated genomic and functional analyses form the core of the present invention and reveal a paradigm-shifting understanding of the 8q24 amplification in endometrial cancer. The initial assessment of *MYC* as a target of 8q24 amplification confirmed that both *MYC* mRNA and a composite MYC signaling signature were elevated in amplified tumors. However, a critical and unexpected finding was that the variation in *MYC* expression itself accounted for only a small fraction (5%) of the variation in MYC signaling strength across all datasets. This disconnect between the canonical oncogene’s expression and its functional output strongly suggested that other factors within the 8q24 amplicon were playing a dominant role in driving the MYC transcriptional program. A systematic evaluation of all 26 genes in the peak region of the 8q24 amplification identified *ATAD2* as the gene whose expression was most strongly correlated with the amplification event. More importantly, *ATAD2* expression was found to be the single strongest molecular correlate of MYC signaling strength, explaining nearly half (48%) of its variation—a correlation that was more than nine times stronger than that of *MYC* expression itself and was observed even in tumors without 8q24 amplification. This finding was robustly validated in multiple independent endometrial cancer datasets and extended to other major cancer types (breast, ovarian, and glioblastoma) using data from The Cancer Genome Atlas, establishing a general principle that *ATAD2* is a key modulator of MYC activity across diverse malignancies.

The clinical translation of this molecular insight is profound. The invention demonstrates that 8q24 amplification, as detected by FISH, is a powerful negative prognostic marker, associated with a stark reduction in 5-year survival (64% vs. 85%). Crucially, high *ATAD2* expression was shown to be an independent predictor of both disease-specific death and disease progression, even after adjusting for ER status. The most lethal subgroup was identified as ER-negative tumors with high *ATAD2* expression, which exhibited a dramatically elevated hazard ratio for death. This prognostic power was further confirmed in the qPCR validation series, solidifying *ATAD2* as a novel and clinically actionable biomarker. The analysis of paired primary and metastatic samples provided direct evidence that the 8q24/*ATAD2*/MYC axis is dynamically selected for during disease progression, with metastases showing significantly higher rates of 8q24 amplification, *ATAD2* expression, and MYC signaling strength than their matched primary tumors.

The functional studies provide the mechanistic and therapeutic rationale for the invention. The demonstration that knockdown of *ATAD2* in endometrial and breast cancer cell lines results in a loss of viability that is highly correlated with the effects of *MYC* knockdown proves that ATAD2 is not just a correlative marker but a critical, non-redundant dependency in MYC-driven cancers. This establishes ATAD2 as a bona fide therapeutic target. The finding that sensitivity to the HDAC inhibitor Trichostatin-A is also correlated with MYC/ATAD2 dependency suggests a potential repurposing strategy for existing drugs and provides a proof-of-concept for targeting the bromodomain function of ATAD2. In summary, the present invention moves beyond the prior art’s narrow focus on *MYC* to reveal *ATAD2* as the central node linking the 8q24 amplification to aggressive disease biology and poor clinical outcomes. It provides a complete solution for endometrial cancer management, offering a novel method for identifying high-risk patients via *ATAD2* assessment and a clear path forward for developing targeted therapies against the ATAD2 protein, thereby fulfilling a long-standing and critical unmet medical need.
