# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of vaccinology and immunology, specifically to the development of a bivalent oral vaccine composition comprising outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) derived from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi and *Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A. The invention provides a novel acellular vaccine formulation that induces robust mucosal and systemic immune responses against both typhoidal and paratyphoidal Salmonellae, thereby offering broad-spectrum protection against enteric fever caused by these pathogens. The OMV-based vaccine of the present invention is designed for oral administration and leverages the natural immunostimulatory properties of bacterial outer membrane components—including lipopolysaccharide (LPS), outer membrane proteins (OMPs), and other pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs)—to elicit long-lasting humoral and cell-mediated immunity without the need for exogenous adjuvants. This invention addresses a critical unmet medical need for a safe, effective, and broadly protective vaccine against both *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A, particularly in light of the rising global incidence of paratyphoid fever and the limitations of currently available typhoid vaccines, which offer little or no cross-protection against paratyphoidal strains.

## BACKGROUND AND PRIOR ART OF THE INVENTION

Enteric fever, a life-threatening systemic illness caused primarily by *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi (*S*. Typhi) and *Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A (*S*. Paratyphi A), remains a significant public health burden, particularly in low- and middle-income countries with inadequate sanitation and limited access to clean water. Globally, *S*. Typhi alone is estimated to infect over 21 million individuals annually, resulting in approximately 200,000 deaths. While historically *S*. Typhi has been the dominant etiological agent, recent epidemiological trends indicate a marked increase in the proportion of enteric fever cases attributable to *S*. Paratyphi A, especially across South and Southeast Asia. This shift in disease epidemiology raises serious concerns that *S*. Paratyphi A may soon emerge as the predominant cause of enteric fever, further complicating control efforts due to the absence of any licensed vaccine specifically targeting this pathogen.

Currently, three vaccines are globally licensed for the prevention of typhoid fever: (1) Ty21a, a live-attenuated oral vaccine derived from a galactose epimerase (galE)-deficient mutant of the wild-type *S*. Typhi strain Ty2; (2) the parenteral Vi polysaccharide vaccine, composed of the purified capsular Vi antigen; and (3) a more recently developed Vi-tetanus toxoid conjugate vaccine. Although Ty21a demonstrates partial cross-protection against paratyphoid fever in some settings, this effect is inconsistent and insufficient for reliable clinical use. Critically, the Vi-based vaccines are entirely ineffective against *S*. Paratyphi A because this organism lacks the Vi capsular polysaccharide. Consequently, there exists an urgent and unmet need for a new generation of vaccines capable of conferring simultaneous and durable protection against both *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A.

Outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) represent a promising platform for next-generation acellular vaccine development. Naturally secreted by Gram-negative bacteria during normal growth, OMVs are spherical, nano-sized (typically 20–300 nm in diameter) proteoliposomes that bud from the outer membrane. They encapsulate a diverse array of bacterial components, including LPS, integral and peripheral outer membrane proteins, periplasmic proteins, phospholipids, nucleic acids, and virulence factors. Importantly, OMVs inherently possess potent immunostimulatory properties due to their enrichment in PAMPs, which engage pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) such as Toll-like receptors (TLRs) on innate immune cells, thereby triggering robust inflammatory and adaptive immune responses. This intrinsic adjuvant activity eliminates the requirement for external adjuvants, enhancing both safety and simplicity of formulation.

The only licensed OMV-based vaccine to date is the Neisseria meningitidis serogroup B (MenB) vaccine, such as MeNZB or VA-MENGOC-BC, which demonstrated high efficacy (approximately 80%) during outbreaks in Norway and New Zealand. These successes have spurred interest in adapting the OMV platform to other Gram-negative pathogens. Prior work by the inventors and others has shown that OMVs derived from *Vibrio cholerae* and *Shigella* species can induce protective immunity in preclinical models. However, despite the clear rationale, no OMV-based vaccine against typhoidal or paratyphoidal Salmonellae has been developed or clinically evaluated.

Several challenges have hindered progress in this area. First, the immunological correlates of protection against enteric fever are incompletely understood, though evidence strongly supports the necessity of both mucosal and systemic immunity, involving antibodies (particularly IgA and opsonizing IgG subclasses) as well as Th1/Th17-polarized T-cell responses. Second, while whole-cell killed or live-attenuated vaccines can induce broad immune responses, they often suffer from reactogenicity, inconsistent immunogenicity, or biosafety concerns. Third, subunit vaccines based on single antigens—such as Vi polysaccharide—fail to elicit comprehensive immunity due to their T-cell-independent nature, resulting in poor immunological memory and limited efficacy in young children. The OMV approach circumvents these limitations by presenting multiple native conformational antigens in their natural context alongside built-in adjuvants, thereby mimicking natural infection without the risk of disease.

Prior art includes methods for OMV isolation from various bacteria, typically involving culture, centrifugation, filtration, and ultracentrifugation. However, these protocols are not standardized across species and may yield preparations with variable purity, composition, and immunogenicity. Furthermore, no prior art discloses a bivalent OMV formulation combining *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A OMVs for oral delivery, nor does it demonstrate the induction of dual-specific protective immunity against both pathogens in a relevant animal model. The present invention fills this critical gap by providing a well-characterized, orally administered bivalent OMV vaccine that elicits strong humoral and cellular immune responses, significantly reduces bacterial colonization in systemic organs following challenge, and operates through multiple protective mechanisms—including bacterial agglutination, inhibition of motility and mucin penetration, and enhanced opsonophagocytosis.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

It is a primary objective of the present invention to provide a novel, safe, and efficacious bivalent vaccine composition capable of inducing protective immunity against both *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi and *Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A. Another objective is to develop an acellular vaccine formulation that does not require the addition of external adjuvants, leveraging instead the intrinsic immunostimulatory properties of naturally derived outer membrane vesicles (OMVs). A further objective is to create a vaccine suitable for oral administration, thereby stimulating both mucosal and systemic arms of the immune system, which are essential for preventing intestinal colonization and systemic dissemination of typhoidal Salmonellae. It is also an objective of the invention to provide a vaccine that elicits long-lasting immune responses, as evidenced by sustained antibody titers and memory cell activation for at least 90 days post-immunization in preclinical models. Additionally, the invention aims to overcome the limitations of existing typhoid vaccines by offering cross-protection against paratyphoid fever, for which no licensed vaccine currently exists. Another objective is to define the immunological mechanisms underlying the protective efficacy of the bivalent OMV vaccine, including the roles of specific antibody isotypes (e.g., IgG1, IgG2a, IgG3, IgA), T-helper cell polarization (Th1/Th17), dendritic cell activation, and functional effector mechanisms such as bacterial agglutination, motility inhibition, mucin barrier penetration blockade, and macrophage-mediated opsonophagocytosis. It is a further objective to standardize methods for the reproducible production, purification, and characterization of OMVs from *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A clinical isolates, ensuring batch-to-batch consistency in protein and O-antigen content. The invention also seeks to demonstrate, in a validated murine model of systemic salmonellosis, that immunization with the bivalent OMV formulation results in significantly improved survival rates and reduced bacterial loads in spleen, liver, and intestine following lethal or sub-lethal challenge with heterologous virulent strains. Moreover, it is an objective to establish through adoptive transfer experiments that both humoral (antibody-mediated) and cellular (T-cell-mediated) immune components are necessary and sufficient for full protective efficacy. Finally, the invention aims to provide a foundation for future clinical development by establishing proof-of-concept in a relevant animal model, thereby enabling progression to Phase I human trials.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Bacterial Strains and Culture Conditions

The present invention utilizes specific clinical isolates of *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi and *Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A, designated herein as *S*. Typhi C-6953 and *S*. Paratyphi A C-6915, respectively, for the production of outer membrane vesicles (OMVs). These strains were obtained from the culture bank of the National Institute of Cholera and Enteric Diseases (NICED), Kolkata, India, and have been fully characterized both phenotypically and genotypically to confirm their identity and virulence potential. For challenge studies, additional virulent strains—*S*. Typhi C-6.946 and *S*. Paratyphi A BCR 148—were employed, also sourced from the NICED repository. All bacterial strains are maintained in brain heart infusion broth (BHI; Difco, USA) supplemented with 20% (v/v) glycerol and stored at −80°C to ensure long-term viability and genetic stability. Prior to experimental use, each strain is revived by streaking onto Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA; Difco, NJ, USA) plates and incubating at 37°C for 18–24 hours. Single colonies are then inoculated into Tryptic Soy Broth (TSB; Difco, USA) and grown under aerobic conditions at 37°C with shaking at 100 revolutions per minute (rpm) until the culture reaches mid-exponential phase, typically corresponding to an optical density at 600 nm (OD600) of approximately 0.6–0.8. This growth phase is critical, as OMV production is maximized during active bacterial replication. The culture conditions—medium composition, temperature, aeration, and growth phase—are strictly controlled to ensure consistent OMV yield and composition across batches. Antibiotic susceptibility profiling is performed using the Kirby-Bauer disk diffusion method in accordance with Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) and World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines, utilizing a panel of 17 antibiotics including ampicillin (AMP), cefotaxime (CTX), cefazolin (CFZ), cefuroxime (CFX), ceftriaxone (CRO), chloramphenicol (CHL), imipenem (I), nalidixic acid (NAL), colistin (CO), tetracycline (TET), trimethoprim (TMP), sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim (SXT), levofloxacin (LO), azithromycin (AZ), ciprofloxacin (CIP), streptomycin (STR), and kanamycin (KAN). Serotyping is conducted using the White-Kauffmann-Le Minor scheme with commercially available anti-O and anti-H antisera, while genotypic confirmation is achieved via polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification of serovar-specific flagellin genes (*fliC-d* for *S*. Typhi and *fliC-a* for *S*. Paratyphi A) and the genus-specific *prtA* gene, using primers as specified in the research documentation. These rigorous characterization steps ensure that only authentic, virulent, and antibiotic-resistant-profile-defined strains are used in OMV production and immunological evaluation.

### Preparation of OMVs

The preparation of outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) from *S*. Typhi C-6953 and *S*. Paratyphi A C-6915 is carried out according to a standardized protocol adapted from established methodologies with minor modifications to optimize yield and purity for vaccine applications. Briefly, bacterial cultures are grown in Tryptic Soy Broth (TSB) at 37°C under shaking conditions (100 rpm) until they reach mid-exponential phase, as determined by OD600 measurement. The cultures are then subjected to centrifugation at 8,000 revolutions per minute (rpm) for 40 minutes at 4°C to pellet bacterial cells, effectively separating them from the culture supernatant containing secreted OMVs. The supernatant is carefully decanted and passed through a 0.22 μm pore-size sterile bacterial filter (Millipore, USA) to remove any residual whole cells or large debris. The filtrate, now enriched in OMVs, is subsequently subjected to ultracentrifugation using a Sorvall T-865 rotor at 140,000 × g for 4 hours at 4°C. This high-speed centrifugation step pellets the OMVs, which are then gently resuspended in sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) to a final volume suitable for downstream applications. The protein concentration of the purified OMV preparation is quantified using a modified Lowry protein assay kit (Pierce, USA), while the amount of O-antigen polysaccharide associated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is determined by the phenol-sulfuric acid method originally described by Dubois et al. Each batch of OMVs consistently yields a protein concentration in the range of 400–440 μg/mL and an O-antigen content of approximately 250 μg/mL. These quantitative metrics are rigorously assessed prior to each immunization or in vitro experiment to ensure dosing accuracy and reproducibility. The final OMV suspensions are aliquoted and stored at −80°C until use, with care taken to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles that could compromise vesicle integrity. For the bivalent vaccine formulation, equal volumes of purified *S*. Typhi OMVs and *S*. Paratyphi A OMVs are mixed in a 1:1 ratio immediately before administration, resulting in a combined dose containing 25 μg of total OMV protein per immunization.

### Negative Staining of OMVs and OMV-Secreting Bacteria

To confirm the morphology, size distribution, and structural integrity of the isolated outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) as well as to visualize OMV secretion from live bacteria, negative staining transmission electron microscopy (TEM) is employed. For OMV visualization, a 5 μL aliquot of the purified OMV suspension is applied onto a carbon-coated copper grid and allowed to adsorb for 1 minute at room temperature. Excess liquid is gently blotted away using filter paper, and the grid is washed twice with drops of Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) to remove salts and contaminants. The sample is then stained with a 2% (w/v) aqueous solution of uranyl acetate for 1 minute, after which excess stain is removed by blotting. For imaging OMV-secreting bacteria, log-phase bacterial cultures are processed similarly: a 5 μL drop of live bacterial suspension is placed on a carbon-coated grid, allowed to adhere, washed with Tris-HCl buffer, and negatively stained with uranyl acetate. Both types of samples are examined using a Tecnai 12 Bio Twin transmission electron microscope (FEI, the Netherlands) operated at an accelerating voltage of 80 kV. Micrographs are captured digitally and analyzed to determine vesicle size, shape, and homogeneity. TEM analysis confirms that both *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A secrete spherical OMVs during exponential growth, with diameters ranging from 130 to 300 nanometers. Notably, *S*. Typhi-derived OMVs tend to be slightly larger on average than those from *S*. Paratyphi A, a morphological distinction that may reflect differences in outer membrane composition or biogenesis mechanisms between the two serovars. The presence of intact, non-lysed bacterial cells with visible budding vesicles further validates that OMV production occurs naturally during active growth and is not an artifact of cell lysis during purification.

### Animals

All in vivo experiments described herein are conducted using seven-week-old female BALB/c mice, obtained from the Animal Resource Division of the National Institute of Cholera and Enteric Diseases (NICED), Kolkata, India. Mice are housed in individually ventilated cages under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions at a controlled ambient temperature of 25°C and relative humidity of 75%. They are provided with autoclaved rodent chow and sterile drinking water ad libitum throughout the study period. To minimize stress and circadian variation, all procedures are performed during the same time window each day. Groups of ten mice are assigned to each experimental condition, including immunized, non-immunized control, and phosphate-buffered saline (PBS)-treated groups, with randomization used to ensure balanced distribution of baseline characteristics. All animal handling, immunization, challenge, and euthanasia procedures are performed in strict compliance with the guidelines set forth by the Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA), Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, Government of India. The experimental protocol has been reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Ethical Committee (IAEC) of NICED under project approval number PRO/117/June, 2015–June, 2018. Humane endpoints are predefined and include a ≥20% reduction in body weight compared to baseline, severe lethargy, hunched posture, piloerection, or a rectal temperature drop of ≥3°C below normal; animals meeting these criteria are promptly euthanized via intraperitoneal injection of ketamine hydrochloride to prevent unnecessary suffering. This rigorous ethical framework ensures that animal welfare is prioritized while generating scientifically valid data on vaccine immunogenicity and efficacy.

### Collection of Serum and Stool

Serial collection of serum and stool samples is performed to longitudinally assess systemic and mucosal immune responses following oral immunization with the bivalent OMV vaccine. Blood is drawn from the lateral tail vein of each mouse at predetermined time points: day 0 (pre-immunization), and days 14, 21, 28, 35, 78, and 90 post-first immunization. Approximately 100–150 μL of blood is collected per time point into BD Microtainer serum separator tubes (BD, NJ, USA), allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 minutes, and then centrifuged at 1,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C. The resulting serum is carefully aspirated, aliquoted, and stored at −20°C until use in serological assays such as ELISA and immunoblotting. Concurrently, stool pellets are aseptically collected from individual mice at the same time intervals. Each stool sample is weighed and homogenized in ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) containing a protease inhibitor cocktail (MP Biomedicals, USA) at a ratio of 100 mg stool per 1 mL buffer to prevent degradation of secretory immunoglobulins. The homogenates are centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 minutes at 4°C to remove particulate matter, and the clarified supernatants are transferred to fresh tubes and stored at −20°C. These stool supernatants serve as the source of mucosal antibodies, particularly secretory IgA (sIgA), for subsequent analysis. The paired collection of serum and stool enables comprehensive evaluation of both systemic (IgG, IgM) and mucosal (IgA, sIgA) humoral immunity over an extended period, providing critical insights into the durability and compartmentalization of the vaccine-induced response.

### SDS-PAGE and Immunoblot

Sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and subsequent immunoblotting (Western blotting) are employed to characterize the protein and lipopolysaccharide (LPS) composition of the OMV preparations and to identify immunodominant antigens recognized by vaccine-induced antibodies. For protein profiling, purified OMVs, isolated outer membrane proteins (OMPs), and whole-cell lysates (WCL) are mixed with SDS-PAGE sample buffer, boiled for 5 minutes, and loaded onto 12% polyacrylamide gels. Electrophoresis is performed at a constant voltage of 100 V using an AE-6530 SDS-PAGE apparatus (ATTO Corporation, Japan). Gels are stained with either Coomassie Brilliant Blue or silver stain to visualize total protein bands. For LPS analysis, OMVs are first treated with Proteinase K (200 μg/mL) in the presence of 0.5% SDS at 60°C for 2 hours to degrade proteins and enrich for LPS, while mature LPS is extracted directly from bacterial pellets using a hot phenol-water method. Both OMV-associated and native LPS samples are prepared in LPS-specific sample buffer, heated at 100°C for 10 minutes, and resolved on 12% SDS-PAGE gels under similar conditions. Following electrophoresis, proteins or LPS are transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes using a semi-dry blotting apparatus (AE 6687, ATTO Corporation, Japan). The membranes are blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) in Tris-buffered saline (TBS) containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBST) to prevent non-specific binding. Primary antibodies—either polyclonal sera from OMV-immunized mice or convalescent sera from mice infected with wild-type *S*. Typhi or *S*. Paratyphi A—are diluted in blocking buffer and incubated with the membranes overnight at 4°C. After washing, membranes are probed with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody (Sigma, USA). Immunoreactive bands are visualized using either BCIP/NBT alkaline phosphatase substrate (MP Biomedicals, USA) for colorimetric detection or Amersham™ ECL™ Prime Western Blotting Detection Reagent (GE Healthcare, UK) for chemiluminescence, with images captured using a digital imaging system. This approach reveals a complex array of immunogenic proteins in the 20–90 kDa range within OMVs, as well as reactivity against both OMV-associated and native LPS structures, confirming the antigenic breadth of the vaccine formulation.

### Dot Blot Assay

Dot blot analysis is utilized as a rapid and semi-quantitative method to assess the reactivity of vaccine-induced antibodies against purified lipopolysaccharide (LPS) antigens from *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A. Purified LPS preparations are serially diluted in PBS to concentrations ranging from 100 ng to 1 μg per spot. Aliquots of 2 μL from each dilution are directly applied onto a nitrocellulose membrane using a micropipette, allowing the samples to air-dry completely. The membrane is then blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) in Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBST) for 1 hour at room temperature to minimize non-specific binding. Following blocking, the membrane is incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibody—specifically, heat-inactivated serum collected from mice immunized with the bivalent OMV formulation. After thorough washing with TBST, the membrane is probed with HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody (Sigma, USA) for 1 hour at room temperature. Bound antibodies are detected by chemiluminescence using Amersham™ ECL™ Prime reagent, and the signal intensity is captured using a CCD-based imaging system. The dot blot assay confirms that antibodies generated by OMV immunization specifically recognize LPS from both *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A in a dose-dependent manner, even though the OMV-associated LPS exhibits structural differences from native, mature LPS (as evidenced by altered migration patterns in SDS-PAGE). This cross-reactivity underscores the ability of the bivalent vaccine to elicit antibodies against conserved or serovar-specific O-antigen epitopes, which are critical for protective immunity.

### ELISA

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) are performed to quantitatively measure antigen-specific immunoglobulin levels in serum and stool samples collected from immunized and control mice. Polystyrene microtiter plates (Nunc, Denmark) are coated overnight at 4°C with 5 μg per well of either *S*. Typhi OMVs, *S*. Paratyphi A OMVs, or purified Vi polysaccharide (Batch number: T030516, Bio-Med, India) diluted in carbonate-bicarbonate coating buffer (pH 9.6). Plates are washed three times with PBS containing 0.5% Tween-20 (PBS-T) and blocked with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) in PBS for 1 hour at 37°C. Serial two-fold dilutions of serum or stool supernatant samples are added to the wells and incubated for 1 hour at 37°C. After washing, HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies specific for mouse IgG, IgG1, IgG2a, IgG3, IgA, secretory IgA (sIgA), or IgM (all from SouthernBiotech or Sigma-Aldrich, USA) are added and incubated for another hour at 37°C. Following a final wash, the chromogenic substrate o-phenylenediamine dihydrochloride (OPD) is added, and the enzymatic reaction is allowed to proceed for 10 minutes at room temperature in the dark. The reaction is stopped by adding 100 μL of 2 N sulfuric acid, and the optical density (OD) is measured at 492 nm using a microplate reader. Endpoint titers are calculated as the reciprocal of the highest serum dilution yielding an OD value at least twice that of naïve control sera. All assays are performed in triplicate, with samples from individual mice tested separately to account for biological variability. ELISA results demonstrate that oral immunization with the bivalent OMV formulation induces significantly elevated levels of all measured immunoglobulin isotypes against both OMV components, with peak responses observed between days 28 and 35 post-primary immunization and sustained detectable titers up to day 90. Notably, the IgG3/IgG1 ratio exceeds unity, indicating a Th1-biased response, while robust mucosal IgA and sIgA responses are detected in stool, confirming successful stimulation of gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT).

### Ex Vivo Studies on Isolated Dendritic Cells

To elucidate the cellular mechanisms underlying the vaccine-induced immune response, ex vivo functional assays are conducted using bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) and splenic lymphocytes isolated from immunized and naïve mice. BMDCs are generated by culturing bone marrow cells harvested from femurs and tibias of naïve BALB/c mice in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 20 ng/mL recombinant mouse granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) for 7–10 days, with fresh medium and cytokine added every 2–3 days. Immature BMDCs are then stimulated with 10 μg/mL of the bivalent OMV formulation for 24 hours in a humidified 37°C, 5% CO₂ incubator. Culture supernatants are collected and analyzed by cytokine-specific ELISA kits (Invitrogen, USA) for the production of interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-12p70 (IL-12p70), and interleukin-23 (IL-23)—cytokines critical for Th1 and Th17 differentiation. In parallel, splenic cells are isolated from immunized mice one week after the final immunization and re-stimulated in vitro with 10 μg/mL bivalent OMVs for 24 hours; supernatants are assayed for interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), IL-6, and IL-17. Additionally, co-culture experiments are performed by mixing purified CD4⁺ T cells (isolated from splenocytes using magnetic bead-based negative selection) with mature BMDCs at a 4:1 ratio in the presence of bivalent OMVs. After 8 hours of co-culture, supernatants are analyzed for Th1 (IFN-γ) and Th17 (IL-17) polarizing cytokines. Flow cytometric analysis of splenocytes further reveals significant upregulation of CD4⁺, CD8⁺, and CD19⁺ cell populations in immunized mice compared to controls, indicating activation of both helper T cells, cytotoxic T cells, and B cells. Collectively, these ex vivo studies demonstrate that the bivalent OMV vaccine potently activates dendritic cells to secrete Th1/Th17-polarizing cytokines, drives antigen-specific T-cell responses, and enhances adaptive immune cell proliferation—key features of a protective vaccine against intracellular pathogens like *Salmonella*.

## The Non-Limiting Advantages are Given Below:

The bivalent outer membrane vesicle (OMV) vaccine of the present invention offers numerous non-limiting advantages over existing typhoid and paratyphoid prevention strategies. Firstly, it provides simultaneous protection against both *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi and *Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A, addressing a critical gap in current vaccinology where no licensed vaccine exists for paratyphoid fever and existing typhoid vaccines lack reliable cross-protection. Secondly, the vaccine is acellular and derived from naturally secreted vesicles, eliminating risks associated with live-attenuated or whole-cell killed vaccines, such as reversion to virulence or excessive reactogenicity. Thirdly, the OMV formulation possesses intrinsic adjuvant properties due to its enrichment in pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) like lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and outer membrane proteins, thereby obviating the need for external adjuvants that can complicate manufacturing, increase cost, and raise safety concerns. Fourthly, the vaccine is designed for oral administration, which not only improves patient compliance—especially in pediatric and mass vaccination campaigns—but also stimulates mucosal immunity in the gut, the primary site of *Salmonella* invasion, thereby blocking initial colonization and translocation. Fifthly, the vaccine induces a balanced and robust immune response encompassing both humoral and cell-mediated arms: it generates high-titer, long-lasting antibodies (including opsonizing IgG subclasses and mucosal IgA) and promotes Th1/Th17-polarized T-cell responses, which are essential for clearing intracellular bacterial reservoirs in systemic organs. Sixthly, mechanistic studies confirm that vaccine-induced antibodies mediate multiple protective functions, including bacterial agglutination, inhibition of flagellar motility, blockade of mucin layer penetration, and enhancement of macrophage opsonophagocytosis—thereby attacking the pathogen at multiple stages of infection. Seventhly, the production process is scalable and reproducible, utilizing well-defined culture conditions and purification protocols that yield consistent OMV batches with standardized protein and O-antigen content. Eighthly, preclinical data in a stringent murine challenge model demonstrate high efficacy, with 80–90% survival against lethal doses and significant reductions in bacterial burden in spleen, liver, and intestine. Ninthly, adoptive transfer experiments prove that both antibodies and T cells contribute to protection, suggesting durable and multifaceted immunity. Finally, the vaccine platform is adaptable; the same methodology could be extended to include OMVs from *S*. Paratyphi B or C, paving the way for a trivalent or quadrivalent formulation against all major paratyphoidal serovars. These advantages collectively position the bivalent OMV vaccine as a transformative candidate for the global control of enteric fever.
