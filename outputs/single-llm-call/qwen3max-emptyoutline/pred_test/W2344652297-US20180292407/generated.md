# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for the high-throughput production of antigen-specific monoclonal antibodies, particularly those that recognize post-translationally modified epitopes such as phosphorylation sites on proteins. More specifically, the invention provides an integrated platform combining chemical fixation of antibody-secreting cells (ASCs), intracellular staining with fluorescently labeled antigens, fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), and single-cell reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) to isolate rare ASCs expressing desired immunoglobulins and to recover their full-length variable region genes for recombinant monoclonal antibody expression. The method is especially suited for generating monoclonal antibodies against low-abundance or structurally similar epitopes—such as phospho-specific peptides—that are difficult to distinguish using conventional hybridoma or phage display technologies. The invention further encompasses optimized protocols for RNA preservation, crosslink reversal, and cDNA synthesis from fixed and permeabilized single cells, enabling robust amplification of full-length immunoglobulin heavy and light chain variable (VH and VL) genes even after paraformaldehyde fixation and intracellular immunostaining. This technical approach addresses longstanding challenges in the field of antibody discovery, including the inability to reliably isolate rare ASCs specific for post-translational modifications, the degradation of RNA during live-cell sorting procedures, and the inefficiency of recovering paired VH–VL sequences from single ASCs. The disclosed methodology thus constitutes a significant advancement in biotechnology, immunology, and diagnostic reagent development, with broad applicability in research, clinical diagnostics, and therapeutic antibody engineering.

## BACKGROUND ART

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) have become indispensable tools in biomedical research, diagnostics, and therapeutics due to their high specificity and affinity for target antigens. Traditional methods for mAb generation, such as hybridoma technology developed by Köhler and Milstein in 1975, involve fusing antibody-producing B cells from immunized animals with immortal myeloma cells to create stable hybrid cell lines that secrete a single antibody species. While effective for many applications, this approach suffers from several limitations: it is labor-intensive, time-consuming, inefficient for isolating rare antigen-specific B cells, and often fails when targeting highly conserved or post-translationally modified epitopes that elicit weak immune responses. Moreover, hybridoma fusion efficiency varies significantly across species, making it particularly challenging to generate mAbs from non-murine hosts such as guinea pigs or rabbits, which are frequently used for immunization with complex or self-like antigens due to their more diverse immune repertoires.

Alternative strategies, including phage display and single B cell cloning, have been developed to circumvent some of these issues. Phage display allows in vitro selection of antibody fragments from large synthetic or immune libraries but often yields antibodies with suboptimal affinity or stability due to the lack of natural affinity maturation. Single B cell approaches, which involve isolating individual B cells or plasma cells (PCs) and amplifying their immunoglobulin genes for recombinant expression, offer a more physiological route to mAb discovery. However, these methods rely heavily on the ability to identify and sort antigen-specific cells with high fidelity—a task complicated by the low surface expression of immunoglobulins on terminally differentiated PCs, which primarily retain antibodies intracellularly within the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus.

To address this challenge, researchers have developed techniques that exploit intracellular immunoglobulin content as a proxy for antigen specificity. One such method, termed Endoplasmic Reticulum-based Intracellular Antigen-binding Assay (ERIAA), uses live-cell staining with ER-tracker dyes and fluorescent antigens to identify PCs based on co-localization of antigen binding and ER markers. While useful, ERIAA is limited by high background noise, autofluorescence, and the vulnerability of cellular RNA to degradation during prolonged live-cell handling and sorting, especially in the presence of exogenous RNases. Furthermore, live-cell staining requires careful maintenance of cell viability, restricts the use of harsh permeabilization agents, and often results in poor signal-to-noise ratios when detecting rare antigen-specific populations—particularly those recognizing subtle epitopic differences such as single phosphorylation events.

Another critical bottleneck in single-cell antibody cloning is the recovery of full-length variable region genes from sorted cells. Paraformaldehyde (PFA), a common fixative used to preserve cellular morphology and inactivate RNases, induces RNA-protein and RNA-RNA crosslinks that impede RNA extraction and reverse transcription. Previous attempts to amplify immunoglobulin transcripts from PFA-fixed cells have largely been restricted to short amplicons (<100 bp), rendering them unsuitable for recovering the full-length VH and VL domains (~350–400 bp) required for functional antibody reconstruction. Although protease treatment and heat-mediated reversal of crosslinks have been explored, consistent and efficient amplification of full-length V genes from fixed, stained, single cells has remained elusive.

Compounding these technical hurdles is the growing demand for modification-specific antibodies, particularly those that distinguish between phosphorylated and unphosphorylated forms of signaling proteins like p53, CHK2, or transcription factors such as RORγt. These epitopes are often poorly immunogenic, and the resulting ASCs are extremely rare in lymphoid tissues. Conventional screening methods struggle to differentiate between antibodies that bind the modified epitope versus those recognizing the unmodified backbone or cross-reactive isoforms (e.g., RORγ vs. RORγt). Without a subtraction strategy to eliminate cross-reactive clones, the isolation of truly specific mAbs becomes a needle-in-a-haystack problem.

In light of these challenges, there exists a clear and unmet need for a robust, high-sensitivity platform capable of (1) preserving RNA integrity during antigen-specific cell identification, (2) enabling precise discrimination of rare ASCs based on intracellular antigen binding, (3) facilitating efficient recovery of paired VH–VL genes from single fixed cells, and (4) supporting the generation of high-affinity, modification-specific mAbs from diverse animal species. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing a novel method—termed FIXAA (Fixation-based Intracellular Antigen-binding Assay)—that integrates optimized fixation, dual-color intracellular staining with modified and unmodified antigens, stringent RNase control, and a tailored single-cell RT-PCR workflow to achieve unprecedented sensitivity and specificity in mAb discovery.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive and highly efficient method for the generation of antigen-specific monoclonal antibodies, with particular emphasis on antibodies that recognize post-translationally modified epitopes such as phosphorylation sites. The core of the invention lies in a novel cell processing and sorting strategy—designated FIXAA—that enables the identification, isolation, and genetic characterization of rare antibody-secreting cells (ASCs) based on their intracellular binding to fluorescently labeled antigens, while simultaneously preserving RNA integrity for downstream molecular cloning. Unlike conventional live-cell approaches, FIXAA employs controlled chemical fixation to inactivate endogenous and exogenous RNases, thereby preventing RNA degradation during the staining and sorting procedures. This fixation step is carefully optimized to maintain both the antigen-binding capacity of intracellular immunoglobulins and the reversibility of RNA-protein crosslinks, allowing subsequent recovery of full-length immunoglobulin variable region transcripts.

### Solution to Problem

The primary technical problem addressed by the present invention is the inability of existing methodologies to reliably isolate rare ASCs that produce antibodies against structurally nuanced or low-immunogenicity epitopes—especially phosphorylation site-specific epitopes—and to efficiently recover their complete VH and VL gene pairs for recombinant expression. Conventional hybridoma technology is ill-suited for such targets due to low fusion efficiency and poor representation of rare clones. Live-cell single B cell sorting methods suffer from RNA degradation, high background, and insufficient resolution to distinguish between closely related epitopes (e.g., phosphorylated vs. unphosphorylated peptides or splice isoforms like RORγ and RORγt).

The present invention solves these problems through a multi-step integrated protocol that begins with the fixation of lymph node or spleen-derived ASCs using a precisely timed paraformaldehyde (PFA) treatment under cold conditions. This fixation preserves cellular morphology, inactivates RNases, and stabilizes intracellular immunoglobulins without irreversibly destroying their antigen-binding function. Following fixation, cells are permeabilized and subjected to intracellular staining with two key reagents: (1) a fluorescently labeled antigen of interest (e.g., a biotinylated phospho-peptide conjugated to DyLight 488-streptavidin) and (2) a differentially labeled version of a related but undesired antigen (e.g., the corresponding unmodified peptide conjugated to DyLight 550-streptavidin). This dual-staining strategy enables a subtraction-based gating approach during FACS: only cells that are positive for the target antigen and negative for the cross-reactive or unmodified antigen are selected, thereby enriching for truly specific ASCs.

To further enhance purity and reduce background, the staining cocktail includes DAPI for nuclear identification and DsRed protein to label damaged or sticky cells that non-specifically bind reagents; these DsRed-positive cells are excluded from sorting. The entire staining and sorting process is conducted under RNase-free conditions, including pretreatment of FACS fluidics with sodium hypochlorite and RNase-free water, use of RNase inhibitors (e.g., RNaseOUT), and preparation of antigens in diethylpyrocarbonate (DEPC)-treated buffers.

Following high-purity single-cell sorting into 96-well plates, the fixed cells undergo a controlled reversal of PFA-induced crosslinks via incubation with Protease K at 50°C for one hour. This step liberates RNA from protein adducts while maintaining sufficient integrity for reverse transcription. The mRNA is captured on oligo(dT) magnetic beads directly in the lysis buffer, and a streamlined single-cell cDNA synthesis protocol—including 3′ homopolymer tailing—is employed to generate templates suitable for 5′ RACE PCR. This specialized PCR strategy successfully amplifies full-length VH and VL genes from individual fixed and stained ASCs with high efficiency (up to 87% success rate in spiked hybridoma models and 82% in phospho-specific ASCs).

The amplified V genes are then assembled into full-length immunoglobulin heavy and light chain expression cassettes using template-switching PCR (TS-jPCR) or homologous recombination, and co-transfected into mammalian cells (e.g., Expi293 or 293FT) for transient antibody production. The resulting recombinant mAbs are screened for specificity and affinity, with demonstrated success in generating high-affinity (KD < 1 nM), phosphorylation site-specific antibodies against p53-pT18 and CHK2-pT68, as well as isoform-specific antibodies against RORγt that do not cross-react with RORγ.

Thus, the invention provides a complete, reproducible, and scalable solution to the long-standing challenges in modification-specific mAb generation, offering superior sensitivity (detection limit <0.01% ASC frequency), specificity (via dual-antigen subtraction gating), and molecular recovery efficiency compared to prior art methods.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENT

### (1) Fixing Step

The fixing step is a foundational element of the present invention, designed to simultaneously preserve cellular RNA integrity, inactivate ribonucleases, and maintain the antigen-binding functionality of intracellular immunoglobulins within antibody-secreting cells (ASCs). In this step, freshly isolated lymph node or spleen cells from an immunized animal—typically a guinea pig, rat, or rabbit—are washed in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and resuspended in ice-cold 2% paraformaldehyde (PFA) in PBS at a density of 1–10 × 10⁶ cells per milliliter. The suspension is incubated on ice for a precisely controlled duration ranging from 8 to 30 minutes, with 8–15 minutes being optimal for most applications. This short, cold fixation minimizes excessive protein-RNA crosslinking while effectively denaturing endogenous RNases and preventing RNA degradation during subsequent permeabilization and staining steps. Longer fixation times (e.g., 30 minutes) may be used when enhanced morphological preservation is required, but they necessitate more aggressive crosslink reversal later. The PFA concentration is critical: concentrations below 1% fail to adequately inactivate RNases, while those above 4% cause irreversible RNA damage and loss of immunoglobulin antigen-binding capacity. After fixation, cells are pelleted by centrifugation at 700 × g for 2 minutes at 4°C, and the supernatant is aspirated. The cell pellet is then either processed immediately or stored short-term at 4°C in PBS for up to 2 hours without significant RNA degradation. Importantly, all solutions and tubes used in this step are certified RNase-free, and operators wear gloves and use aerosol-resistant tips to prevent exogenous RNase contamination. This fixing protocol represents a deliberate balance between RNA preservation and retention of biological functionality, enabling downstream antigen-binding assays that would otherwise be compromised by RNA decay in live-cell methods.

### (2) Cell Membrane Lysis Step

Following fixation, the cell membrane lysis (permeabilization) step is performed to render the plasma and organelle membranes permeable to large fluorescent probes and antibodies, thereby exposing the abundant intracellular immunoglobulins retained within the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus of plasma cells. This step is carried out by resuspending the fixed cell pellet in an intracellular staining solution containing 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS (PBST), which solubilizes lipid membranes while preserving protein epitopes and RNA integrity. The permeabilization occurs concurrently with the staining reaction, eliminating the need for a separate lysis step that could increase handling time and RNase exposure. The staining solution is prepared fresh and includes 0.01% diethylpyrocarbonate (DEPC) to ensure RNase-free conditions, along with 400 units of recombinant RNase inhibitor (e.g., RNaseOUT) per 250 µL reaction. Cells are incubated in this solution on ice for 15 minutes, a duration sufficient for complete permeabilization without causing excessive RNA fragmentation. The low temperature further suppresses any residual RNase activity that may have survived fixation. Notably, alternative permeabilizing agents such as saponin or methanol are avoided, as they either fail to provide uniform access to intracellular compartments or cause irreversible RNA precipitation. The integration of permeabilization into the staining buffer streamlines the workflow and minimizes cell loss, which is crucial when working with rare ASC populations that may constitute less than 0.01% of total lymphocytes.

### (3) Staining Step

The staining step is a sophisticated multiplexed intracellular labeling procedure that enables the specific identification of ASCs based on their cognate antigen recognition profile. In this step, the permeabilized cells are incubated with a cocktail containing three to four key components: (1) a fluorescently labeled antigen of interest, (2) a differentially labeled related antigen used for subtraction, (3) a fluorescent secondary antibody against the host species’ immunoglobulin, and (4) viability and nuclear markers. For example, when isolating ASCs specific for a triphosphorylated p53 peptide (pS15/pT18/pS20), the antigen of interest is a biotinylated triphospho-p53 peptide pre-conjugated to DyLight 488-streptavidin (emission ~518 nm), while the subtraction antigen is the identical but unmodified p53 peptide conjugated to DyLight 550-streptavidin (emission ~576 nm). Both peptide-streptavidin complexes are prepared at 0.4 µM in PBST containing 10 µM free biotin to block non-specific streptavidin binding, and are mixed with DyLight 650-conjugated goat anti-guinea pig IgG (for host immunoglobulin detection), DsRed protein (0.1 µg/mL, to label damaged or sticky cells), and DAPI (1 µM, for nuclear staining). The final staining volume is typically 250 µL per 1–10 million cells, and incubation proceeds on ice for 15 minutes to minimize internalization artifacts and preserve RNA. The use of chemically synthesized, biotinylated peptides ensures batch-to-batch consistency and compatibility with RNase-free preparation. The dual-antigen design is critical: only cells exhibiting high signal in the target channel (e.g., FL1 for DyLight 488) and low/no signal in the subtraction channel (e.g., FL2 for DyLight 550) are considered specific. The anti-IgG signal confirms the cell is an ASC, DAPI identifies nucleated cells, and DsRed negativity excludes debris and non-specifically adherent cells. All fluorescent reagents are diluted in DEPC-treated PBST with RNase inhibitor, and light exposure is minimized to prevent fluorophore bleaching.

### (4) Cell Isolation Step

The cell isolation step utilizes high-speed fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) to physically separate single antigen-specific ASCs from the heterogeneous lymphocyte population based on the multiparametric staining profile established in the previous step. Sorting is performed on a flow cytometer equipped with an automatic cell deposition unit (e.g., JSAN or equivalent) capable of index sorting into 96-well PCR plates. Prior to sample loading, the fluidic system is rigorously decontaminated by flushing with 0.1% sodium hypochlorite followed by multiple rinses with RNase-free water to eliminate environmental RNases. The stained cell suspension is diluted to 1–5 × 10⁶ cells/mL in ice-cold PBS containing 1 µM DAPI and filtered through a 35-µm mesh to remove aggregates. During acquisition, gates are set to exclude doublets (via FSC-A vs. FSC-H), dead cells (DAPI-positive), and DsRed-positive events. The target population—defined as anti-IgGhigh, antigen-of-interesthigh, subtraction-antigennegative—is identified in a bivariate plot (e.g., FL1 vs. FL2) and appears as a discrete cluster well-separated from background. Single cells from this gate are sorted directly into individual wells of a U-bottom 96-well plate pre-filled with 20 µL of Cell Lysis/Binding buffer containing 30 µg of oligo(dT)₂₅ magnetic beads and 100 µg/mL Protease K. The deposition accuracy is verified by post-sort reanalysis or imaging, and plates are immediately sealed and stored at −80°C or processed directly for cDNA synthesis. This step achieves exceptional purity and sensitivity, routinely isolating ASCs present at frequencies as low as 1 in 10,000 total cells.

### (6) Antigen Specific Monoclonal Antibody Preparing Step

The antigen-specific monoclonal antibody preparing step encompasses the molecular cloning, assembly, and recombinant expression of full-length immunoglobulin genes derived from the single sorted ASCs. Immediately after cell sorting, the 96-well plates undergo reverse crosslinking by incubating at 50°C for 1 hour, during which Protease K digests crosslinked proteins and liberates RNA for capture by the oligo(dT) beads. The beads are then magnetically concentrated, and a single-tube cDNA synthesis reaction is performed directly on-bead, incorporating a template-switching oligo that adds a universal 5′ sequence during reverse transcription. This enables 5′ RACE PCR using a constant 5′ primer and isotype-specific 3′ primers to amplify full-length VH and VL genes. The PCR products are verified by gel electrophoresis and purified, then joined to constant region cassettes via TS-jPCR to generate linear heavy and light chain expression constructs. These linear DNAs are co-transfected into Expi293 cells using standard protocols, and supernatants are harvested 72 hours later. Antibody binding is confirmed by ELISA against the immunizing antigen, and specificity is validated by competition assays, Western blotting, and immunofluorescence using modified vs. unmodified targets. High-affinity clones (KD < 1 nM) are scaled up and purified via protein A chromatography. This step transforms the genetic information from a single fixed cell into a functional, recombinant monoclonal antibody with defined specificity, completing the pipeline from immunized animal to characterized mAb in under two weeks.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In Example 1, the inventors established the foundational protocol of FIXAA using the OKT10 hybridoma cell line, which secretes a mouse IgG1 monoclonal antibody against human CD38. To determine the optimal fixative, OKT10 cells were treated with various aldehydes (2% PFA, 0.5% glutaraldehyde, or methanol) for 30 minutes at 4°C, permeabilized, and stained intracellularly with DyLight 488-labeled recombinant CD38 and DyLight 594-conjugated anti-mouse IgG. Flow cytometry revealed that only PFA-fixed cells retained CD38-binding activity, while all fixatives except glutaraldehyde preserved detectable intracellular IgG. Next, RNA was extracted from PFA-fixed, stained cells, and RT-PCR targeting a 71-bp fragment of the mouse IgG1 constant region yielded a product, albeit at reduced intensity compared to unfixed controls, confirming partial RNA preservation. Critically, when single OKT10 cells were fixed for 10, 15, or 30 minutes, stained, and sorted, 5′ RACE PCR failed to amplify the full-length VH gene unless the cells were treated with Protease K at 50°C for 1 hour post-sorting. This crosslink reversal step increased the amplification success rate from 0% to over 80%, demonstrating that controlled proteolysis enables full-length V gene recovery from fixed cells. This example validated the core hypothesis that PFA fixation, when combined with optimized crosslink reversal, supports both antigen-binding detection and RNA-based cloning.

### Example 2

Example 2 evaluated the sensitivity and lower detection limit of FIXAA using artificial mixtures of OKT10 hybridomas spiked into Jurkat T cells at ratios of 1:100, 1:1,000, and 1:10,000. The mixtures were fixed with 2% PFA for 8 minutes, stained intracellularly with anti-mouse IgG (DyLight 650), DAPI, and DsRed, and analyzed by FACS. The target population—defined as IgGhigh and DsRednegative—formed a distinct cluster even at the 1:10,000 dilution. Single cells from this gate were sorted into 96-well plates, and 5′ RACE PCR yielded full-length VH genes with success rates of 87% (1:100), 72% (1:1,000), and 47% (1:10,000). Sequencing confirmed all amplicons matched the known OKT10 VH sequence. This experiment demonstrated that FIXAA can detect and clone ASCs present at frequencies below 0.01%, far exceeding the sensitivity of conventional methods.

### Example 3

In Example 3, FIXAA was applied to generate a monoclonal antibody specific for the RORγt isoform, which differs from RORγ only in its N-terminal 21 amino acids. Guinea pigs were immunized with a KLH-conjugated RORγt-specific peptide (MRTQIEVIPC). Lymph node cells were fixed, stained intracellularly with DyLight 488-conjugated RORγt peptide, DyLight 550-conjugated RORγ peptide, anti-guinea pig IgG (DyLight 650), DAPI, and DsRed. FACS analysis revealed a narrow R4-gated population (RORγthigh, RORγnegative, IgGhigh) comprising ~0.05% of lymphocytes. Single-cell 5′ RACE PCR from 40 R4 cells yielded 38 paired VH–VL amplicons (95% success). Transient transfection of these pairs into 293 cells produced mAbs, 19 of 20 of which bound RORγt but not RORγ in Western blots. This example proved FIXAA’s ability to isolate ASCs specific for minimally divergent protein isoforms through subtraction staining.

### Comparative Example 1 (Method in NPL 4)

Comparative Example 1 contrasted FIXAA with ERIAA (Endoplasmic Reticulum-based Intracellular Antigen-binding Assay), a live-cell method described in prior literature (NPL 4). Guinea pigs immunized with triphospho-p53 peptide were used as the source of ASCs. In ERIAA, live lymph node cells were stained with ER-Tracker Blue-White DPX, TP-p53 (DyLight 488), UM-p53 (DyLight 550), and anti-IgG. The target population (ERhigh, TP-p53medium, UM-p53negative, IgGmedium) appeared diffuse with high background. Single-cell sorting yielded only 34% paired V gene amplification success, and just 16% of expressed mAbs bound TP-p53. In stark contrast, FIXAA on the same samples produced a discrete R4 gate (IgGhigh, TP-p53high, UM-p53negative), 82% amplification success, and 96% functional mAbs. This comparison unequivocally demonstrated FIXAA’s superior signal-to-noise ratio, RNA preservation, and cloning efficiency.

### Example 3 (Continued)

Continuing from Example 3, the RORγt-specific mAbs generated by FIXAA were further characterized for cross-reactivity. Clone #7 was tested by ELISA against a panel of nuclear receptors and showed no binding to RORα, RORβ, or unrelated proteins. Immunofluorescence on Th17-differentiated CD4+ T cells revealed nuclear staining that co-localized with RORγt but not in RORγt-knockout cells. The dissociation constant (KD) was measured at 0.5 nM by BLItz assay. These data confirmed that FIXAA produces high-affinity, isoform-selective mAbs suitable for functional studies.

### Example 4

Example 4 focused on the generation of phosphorylation site-specific mAbs against p53-pT18, an epitope for which no commercial antibodies existed. Guinea pigs immunized with triphospho-p53 peptide were processed by FIXAA using TP-p53 (DyLight 488) and UM-p53 (DyLight 550). From 96 sorted ASCs, 79 yielded paired VH–VL genes. Transient expression produced 48 mAbs, 46 of which bound TP-p53. Among these, five clones showed exclusive reactivity to pT18 in peptide ELISA. Clone #23 was selected for validation: it stained nuclei of etoposide-treated 293FT cells, and this signal was abolished by λ-phosphatase treatment or pre-incubation with pT18 peptide—but not with other phospho-peptides. In Saos-2 cells expressing wild-type p53, #23 labeled nuclei, but no signal was seen with the T18A mutant. Western blotting confirmed a single band at 53 kDa in DNA-damaged cells, lost upon phosphatase treatment. The KD for pT18 peptide was 0.2 nM. Similarly, FIXAA generated an anti-CHK2-pT68 mAb (#34) with KD = 0.08 nM. These results established FIXAA as a robust platform for producing high-quality, phospho-specific mAbs against previously intractable epitopes.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention has extensive industrial applicability across multiple sectors, including biopharmaceutical development, diagnostic assay manufacturing, academic research reagent production, and clinical biomarker validation. In the biopharmaceutical industry, the ability to rapidly generate high-affinity, modification-specific monoclonal antibodies accelerates target validation, mechanism-of-action studies, and companion diagnostic development—particularly for signaling pathways involving phosphorylation, acetylation, or ubiquitination. Diagnostic companies can leverage FIXAA to produce highly specific capture and detection antibodies for immunoassays (e.g., ELISA, Luminex, or lateral flow tests) that distinguish disease-associated phospho-forms of proteins such as tau, α-synuclein, or oncoproteins like EGFR or HER2. Academic and core facilities benefit from a turnkey platform to generate custom antibodies against novel post-translational modifications without relying on commercial vendors or hybridoma core services. Moreover, because FIXAA works efficiently in guinea pigs, rats, and rabbits—species with broader immune repertoires than mice—it expands the range of druggable epitopes, including those that are poorly immunogenic in murine systems. The method’s high throughput (96-well format), reproducibility, and compatibility with synthetic peptide antigens make it ideal for large-scale antibody discovery campaigns. Finally, the recombinant nature of the output ensures batch-to-batch consistency and facilitates humanization for therapeutic use. Thus, the invention represents a transformative tool with immediate and wide-ranging utility in both research and commercial settings.
