## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccine development, specifically to multivalent glycoconjugate vaccines for the prevention of bacterial infections caused by Gram-negative pathogens. More particularly, the invention provides a quadrivalent conjugate vaccine composition comprising four distinct Klebsiella pneumoniae O-polysaccharide (OPS) antigens chemically conjugated to Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins, designed to elicit protective immune responses against both Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections. The invention further encompasses methods of making such conjugate vaccines, genetically engineered bacterial strains optimized for antigen production, pharmaceutical compositions containing the conjugate vaccine, and methods of using the conjugate vaccine for the prevention and treatment of nosocomial infections caused by these multidrug-resistant bacterial pathogens.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nosocomial infections, also known as healthcare-associated infections, represent a significant clinical burden worldwide, contributing to increased morbidity, mortality, prolonged hospital stays, and substantial healthcare costs. Among the most problematic causative agents of these infections are Gram-negative bacterial pathogens, particularly Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (PA), which have been identified by both the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the World Health Organization (WHO) as priority pathogens due to their rapidly increasing antimicrobial resistance profiles. The emergence of resistance to last-resort antibiotics such as carbapenems and colistin has created an urgent need for alternative therapeutic strategies that can circumvent the molecular mechanisms of antibiotic resistance.

Traditional antibiotic therapy faces significant challenges due to the sophisticated resistance mechanisms employed by these pathogens, including enzymatic degradation of antibiotics, efflux pumps, and modification of antibiotic targets. In contrast, vaccine-based approaches target conserved surface antigens essential for bacterial virulence or survival, thereby providing protection that is not susceptible to conventional antibiotic resistance mechanisms. Despite decades of research, no licensed human vaccines currently exist for either KP or PA, leaving vulnerable patient populations without effective prophylactic options.

For Pseudomonas aeruginosa, flagellin proteins have emerged as promising vaccine candidates due to their essential role in bacterial motility and virulence. Pathogenic PA strains express a single polar flagellum composed primarily of flagellin protein, with two major serotypes designated FlaA and FlaB. Antibodies directed against flagellin have been shown to inhibit bacterial motility and provide protection in animal models of infection. However, immunity to flagellin is serotype-specific, necessitating the inclusion of both FlaA and FlaB antigens in any broadly protective PA vaccine formulation.

Klebsiella pneumoniae presents a more complex challenge for vaccine development due to its extensive capsular diversity, with over 80 known K-antigen serotypes that show significant geographical variation in prevalence. This diversity has historically made capsule-based vaccine approaches impractical for broad protection. However, KP also expresses lipopolysaccharide-associated O-polysaccharides (OPS) on its surface, with only nine distinct OPS structures identified to date. Importantly, four of these OPS types—O1, O2, O3, and O5—account for more than 80% of clinical isolates worldwide, making them attractive targets for vaccine development. Unlike capsular polysaccharides, OPS antigens have been shown to be accessible to antibodies even in the presence of the capsule, and anti-OPS antibodies have demonstrated protective efficacy in animal models through opsonophagocytic killing mechanisms.

A fundamental limitation of bacterial polysaccharide antigens is their poor immunogenicity when administered alone. Pure polysaccharides are T-cell-independent antigens that fail to induce immunological memory, class switching, or affinity maturation, resulting in weak and short-lived immune responses, particularly in high-risk populations such as infants, elderly individuals, and immunocompromised patients. This limitation has been successfully overcome in existing licensed vaccines through the chemical conjugation of polysaccharides to protein carriers, which converts the immune response to a T-cell-dependent pathway, resulting in enhanced antibody titers, immunological memory, and improved protection across diverse age groups.

Current carrier proteins used in licensed conjugate vaccines, such as tetanus toxoid and diphtheria toxoid derivatives, are already included in routine childhood immunization schedules, leading to high levels of pre-existing anti-carrier immunity in the population. This pre-existing immunity can result in epitopic suppression, where the immune response to the polysaccharide component is diminished due to immune focusing on the carrier protein. Therefore, there exists a need for novel carrier proteins that can provide dual functionality—serving as both an effective carrier for polysaccharide conjugation and as a protective antigen against a second pathogen.

The present invention addresses these unmet needs by providing a rationally designed quadrivalent conjugate vaccine that combines the four predominant KP OPS antigens with PA flagellin proteins, creating a single vaccine formulation that provides broad protection against both KP and PA infections. This approach leverages the proven effectiveness of conjugate vaccine technology while simultaneously targeting two high-priority nosocomial pathogens with a single immunization strategy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel quadrivalent conjugate vaccine composition comprising four distinct Klebsiella pneumoniae O-polysaccharide (OPS) antigens—specifically O1, O2, O3, and O5 serotypes—each chemically conjugated to Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins. The conjugate vaccine is designed to elicit robust, protective immune responses against both Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections, addressing the critical need for effective prophylaxis against these multidrug-resistant nosocomial pathogens.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a conjugate comprising at least one Klebsiella pneumoniae O-polysaccharide selected from the group consisting of O1, O2, O3, and O5 serotypes, covalently linked to a Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein selected from the group consisting of FlaA and FlaB serotypes. The conjugation is achieved through a stable thioether linkage formed between the reducing end of the OPS molecule and lysine residues on the flagellin protein via aminooxy-thiol chemistry, preserving the structural integrity of both the polysaccharide and protein components while ensuring optimal immunogenic presentation.

The invention further provides a multivalent vaccine composition comprising a combination of four distinct conjugates: O1-FlaB, O2-FlaA, O3-FlaB, and O5-FlaA, formulated to provide comprehensive coverage against the predominant clinical isolates of both pathogens. This specific combination has been optimized based on extensive immunogenicity studies demonstrating equivalent carrier functionality between FlaA and FlaB proteins, while ensuring balanced immune responses to all four OPS serotypes.

Additionally, the invention encompasses genetically engineered bacterial strains specifically designed for the efficient production and purification of vaccine antigens. For Klebsiella pneumoniae, these include attenuated strains with deletions in guaBA genes (rendering them guanine auxotrophic) and wzabc genes (eliminating capsule production), which facilitate safe, large-scale fermentation and simplified OPS purification. For Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin production, the invention provides recombinant expression systems in Escherichia coli utilizing codon-optimized flagellin genes cloned into expression vectors, enabling high-yield production of pure, endotoxin-free flagellin proteins.

The invention also provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the conjugate vaccine in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, diluents, and adjuvants suitable for human administration. These compositions are formulated for various routes of administration, including intramuscular, subcutaneous, and intradermal injection, and may include additional components to enhance stability, immunogenicity, or delivery.

Furthermore, the invention provides methods for preventing and treating bacterial infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa by administering the conjugate vaccine to subjects in need thereof. The vaccine induces protective immune responses characterized by high-titer IgG antibodies against both OPS and flagellin antigens, which mediate bacterial clearance through opsonophagocytic uptake and motility inhibition mechanisms, respectively.

The invention additionally provides methods for manufacturing the conjugate vaccine, including processes for the production and purification of individual antigens, chemical conjugation procedures, and quality control measures to ensure consistent product characteristics. These methods are scalable and suitable for commercial manufacturing under current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP).

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Conjugate

The present invention provides a novel conjugate comprising at least one Klebsiella pneumoniae O-polysaccharide (OPS) antigen covalently linked to a Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein. The conjugate is specifically designed to overcome the limitations of traditional polysaccharide vaccines by converting the immune response to a T-cell-dependent pathway, thereby enhancing immunogenicity, inducing immunological memory, and providing robust protection against bacterial infection.

The Klebsiella pneumoniae OPS antigens utilized in the conjugate are selected from the four predominant serotypes that account for more than 80% of clinical isolates worldwide: O1, O2, O3, and O5. These OPS structures have been extensively characterized and validated as protective antigens capable of eliciting functional antibodies that mediate opsonophagocytic killing. The O1 and O2 serotypes are polygalactan structures, while O3 and O5 are composed primarily of mannose residues, providing structural diversity that ensures broad coverage against circulating KP strains.

The Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins serve as both carrier proteins and protective antigens in the conjugate. Two major serotypes are utilized: FlaA and FlaB, which represent the only two known flagellin serotypes expressed by pathogenic PA strains. The flagellin proteins are produced as recombinant proteins in Escherichia coli expression systems, incorporating a 6xHis tag for purification purposes while maintaining their structural integrity and immunogenic properties. Importantly, the conjugation process has been optimized to preserve the protective epitopes of both the OPS and flagellin components while ablating the Toll-like receptor 5 (TLR5) agonist activity of flagellin, which could otherwise contribute to excessive inflammatory responses upon vaccination.

The chemical conjugation between the OPS and flagellin components is achieved through a sophisticated two-step process utilizing aminooxy-thiol chemistry. In the first step, the reducing end of the OPS molecule, which contains a 2',5'-anhydromannose aldehyde structure, is reacted with O-(3-mercaptopropyl)-hydroxylamine to introduce a thiol-reactive aminooxy group. This reaction is carried out under controlled conditions at room temperature for 12-18 hours in sodium acetate buffer at pH 5. Following this modification, the aminooxy-functionalized OPS is stabilized by reduction with sodium cyanoborohydride to prevent hydrolysis of the oxime linkage.

In parallel, the flagellin protein is derivatized with N-γ-maleimidobutyryl-oxysuccinimide ester (GMBS) to introduce maleimide groups that react specifically with thiol groups. This reaction is performed at room temperature for one hour in sodium phosphate buffer at pH 7.4, followed by extensive diafiltration to remove unreacted reagents. The maleimide-derivatized flagellin is then combined with the aminooxy-thiol-functionalized OPS at a 1:1 weight ratio and incubated for 24-48 hours at 4°C with gentle tumbling to facilitate conjugation through the formation of stable thioether linkages.

The resulting conjugate maintains the structural integrity of both the OPS and flagellin components, with the polysaccharide chain extending from the protein surface in a manner that preserves accessibility of protective epitopes. Analytical characterization by high-performance liquid chromatography with size exclusion chromatography (HPLC-SEC) demonstrates that the conjugates exhibit a relatively narrow size distribution with consistent molecular weight profiles, indicating homogeneous conjugation products. The polysaccharide-to-protein ratio in the purified conjugates is carefully controlled to ensure optimal immunogenicity, typically ranging from 0.8:1 to 1.2:1 by weight.

The conjugation process effectively ablates the TLR5 agonist activity of the flagellin carrier protein, as demonstrated by in vitro assays using HEK293-Luc cells stably expressing firefly luciferase under the control of an NF-κB-dependent promoter. While native recombinant flagellin proteins exhibit strong TLR5 activation with EC50 values in the low nanogram range, the conjugated forms show negligible TLR5 activity, indicating that the conjugation process sterically blocks or alters the TLR5 recognition domain while preserving other immunogenic epitopes.

### Compositions

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the conjugate vaccine in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, diluents, and adjuvants suitable for human administration. These compositions are formulated to ensure stability, safety, and optimal immunogenicity while maintaining the structural integrity of the conjugate components.

The vaccine compositions may contain one or more of the individual conjugates described herein, with the preferred embodiment being a quadrivalent formulation comprising four distinct conjugates: O1-FlaB, O2-FlaA, O3-FlaB, and O5-FlaA. Each conjugate is present in the composition at concentrations ranging from 1 to 20 micrograms of OPS per dose, with a preferred concentration of 5 micrograms of each OPS type per dose. This specific combination has been optimized based on extensive immunogenicity studies demonstrating balanced immune responses to all four OPS serotypes and both flagellin types.

The pharmaceutical compositions are prepared in aqueous buffers compatible with parenteral administration, typically phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at pH 7.4 containing 5 mM EDTA to prevent metal-catalyzed oxidation. Additional stabilizers such as sucrose, trehalose, or human serum albumin may be included to enhance long-term stability during storage. The compositions are sterile-filtered through 0.2-micron filters and filled into appropriate containers under aseptic conditions.

Adjuvants may be included in the compositions to enhance immunogenicity, particularly in populations with reduced immune responsiveness. Suitable adjuvants include aluminum salts (such as aluminum hydroxide or aluminum phosphate), oil-in-water emulsions (such as MF59 or AS03), toll-like receptor agonists (such as CpG oligonucleotides or monophosphoryl lipid A), or saponin-based formulations (such as QS-21). The choice of adjuvant depends on the intended route of administration, target population, and desired immune response profile.

The compositions may be formulated as liquid solutions for immediate use or as lyophilized powders for reconstitution prior to administration. Lyophilized formulations typically include cryoprotectants such as sucrose or mannitol to maintain conjugate stability during freeze-drying and storage. The reconstitution solution is provided separately and may contain additional stabilizers or buffers to optimize the final formulation.

Quality control measures are implemented throughout the manufacturing process to ensure consistent product quality. These include testing for residual endotoxin levels (typically less than 0.1 endotoxin units per microgram of protein), residual host cell proteins (less than 1% by weight), residual nucleic acids (absorbance at 260 nm below specified thresholds), and sterility testing. The final product is characterized by multiple analytical methods including SDS-PAGE with Coomassie staining, HPLC-SEC with refractive index detection, and specific assays for polysaccharide and protein content.

### Methods of Treatment

The present invention provides methods for preventing and treating bacterial infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa by administering the conjugate vaccine to subjects in need thereof. The vaccine is particularly indicated for the prevention of nosocomial infections in high-risk populations, including hospitalized patients, immunocompromised individuals, elderly patients, and those with underlying medical conditions that increase susceptibility to Gram-negative bacterial infections.

The method of treatment involves administering an effective amount of the conjugate vaccine composition to a subject, typically through intramuscular injection, although other routes including subcutaneous, intradermal, or intranasal administration may also be employed. The dosing regimen typically consists of a primary series of two to three doses administered at intervals of 2-4 weeks, followed by booster doses as needed to maintain protective immunity. In high-risk populations, such as patients scheduled for major surgery or those with prolonged hospital stays, the vaccine may be administered prophylactically prior to anticipated exposure to nosocomial pathogens.

The conjugate vaccine induces protective immune responses characterized by high-titer IgG antibodies against both the OPS and flagellin components. Anti-OPS antibodies mediate protection against Klebsiella pneumoniae through opsonophagocytic uptake and killing by phagocytic cells, as demonstrated in vitro using J774 mouse macrophage cells and in vivo in mouse models of systemic infection. These antibodies bind to the OPS antigens on the bacterial surface, even in the presence of the capsule, and facilitate recognition and engulfment by phagocytic cells, leading to bacterial clearance and protection against disseminated infection.

Anti-flagellin antibodies provide protection against Pseudomonas aeruginosa through multiple mechanisms, including inhibition of bacterial motility and interference with flagellin-mediated virulence functions. Motility inhibition assays demonstrate that vaccine-induced antibodies can significantly reduce the swarming motility of PA strains, thereby limiting their ability to spread and establish infection at mucosal surfaces. Additionally, anti-flagellin antibodies may interfere with flagellin-mediated activation of host inflammatory pathways, potentially reducing tissue damage associated with infection.

The vaccine provides broad protection against diverse clinical isolates of both pathogens, as demonstrated by cross-reactivity studies showing that anti-OPS antibodies induced by the conjugate vaccine bind effectively to encapsulated KP strains representing various capsule types, while anti-flagellin antibodies show serotype-specific protection against corresponding PA strains. The quadrivalent formulation ensures coverage against the vast majority of clinically relevant KP isolates (greater than 80% based on OPS typing) while providing protection against both major PA flagellin serotypes.

The method of treatment is particularly valuable in the context of antimicrobial resistance, as the vaccine-induced immune responses are not affected by the molecular mechanisms of antibiotic resistance that render conventional therapies ineffective. By preventing infection rather than treating established disease, the vaccine also reduces the selective pressure for resistance development and decreases the overall burden of antibiotic use in healthcare settings.

### Methods of Making the Conjugate

The present invention provides detailed methods for manufacturing the conjugate vaccine, encompassing the production and purification of individual antigens, chemical conjugation procedures, and quality control measures to ensure consistent product characteristics suitable for commercial manufacturing under current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP).

The production of Klebsiella pneumoniae OPS antigens begins with the cultivation of genetically engineered reagent strains in large-scale fermentation systems. The preferred strains are attenuated derivatives of clinical isolates with deletions in guaBA genes (rendering them guanine auxotrophic for safety) and wzabc genes (eliminating capsule production to simplify purification). Fermentation is conducted in chemically defined medium supplemented with guanine for guaBA-deficient strains, using controlled conditions of temperature (37°C), agitation (400 rpm), aeration (4 LPM ambient air), and pH (maintained at 7.0 with ammonium hydroxide).

Following fermentation, the OPS antigens are released from the bacterial cells through nitrous acid deamination, a process that cleaves the OPS from the lipid A core while preserving the polysaccharide structure. The crude extract is clarified by centrifugation and microfiltration, followed by concentration and diafiltration using tangential flow filtration (TFF) with 10 kDa molecular weight cutoff membranes. Further purification is achieved through anion exchange chromatography in negative mode using Sartobind Q capsules, followed by ammonium sulfate precipitation to remove residual contaminants. The final purification step involves TFF with 5 kDa membranes and extensive diafiltration with deionized water, yielding highly purified OPS with low levels of residual protein (<1%), endotoxin (<0.01 EU/μg), and nucleic acids.

Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins are produced as recombinant proteins in Escherichia coli BL21 cells transformed with expression plasmids containing codon-optimized flagellin genes. Fermentation is conducted in Terrific Broth medium supplemented with carbenicillin for plasmid maintenance, using controlled conditions of temperature (30°C during growth, 37°C during induction), agitation (250 rpm), aeration (4 LPM ambient air), and pH (maintained at 7.0 with ammonium hydroxide). Expression is induced with isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) when the culture reaches an OD600 of approximately 3.0.

The recombinant flagellin proteins are extracted from the soluble fraction using B-PER reagent and purified through a multi-step process beginning with immobilized metal affinity chromatography (IMAC) using Ni-Sepharose resin to capture the 6xHis-tagged proteins. This is followed by sequential TFF, cation exchange, and anion exchange membrane chromatography steps to achieve high purity. The final product is characterized by SDS-PAGE with Coomassie staining, endotoxin testing, and concentration determination by UV absorbance at 280 nm using calculated extinction coefficients.

The chemical conjugation process involves two parallel modification steps followed by coupling. The OPS is first functionalized with O-(3-mercaptopropyl)-hydroxylamine at the reducing end under controlled pH and temperature conditions, followed by stabilization with sodium cyanoborohydride. Simultaneously, the flagellin protein is derivatized with GMBS to introduce maleimide groups reactive with thiol functionalities. The modified components are then combined at a 1:1 weight ratio and incubated under gentle mixing conditions to facilitate conjugation through thioether bond formation.

The conjugation reaction is monitored by SDS-PAGE and HPLC-SEC to ensure complete reaction and proper conjugate formation. The final purification step involves size exclusion chromatography using Superdex 200 columns to separate the conjugate from unreacted starting materials and side products. The purified conjugate is concentrated by TFF, sterile-filtered, and stored under appropriate conditions to maintain stability.

Throughout the manufacturing process, rigorous quality control measures are implemented, including identity testing by monosaccharide composition analysis (HPAEC-PAD), molecular size determination (HPLC-SEC), purity assessment (SDS-PAGE), endotoxin testing (LAL assay), and quantification of polysaccharide and protein content using specific analytical methods.

### Genetically Engineered Strains

The present invention provides genetically engineered bacterial strains specifically designed for the efficient and safe production of vaccine antigens. These strains have been optimized through targeted genetic modifications to enhance antigen yield, simplify purification processes, and ensure biosafety during large-scale manufacturing operations.

For Klebsiella pneumoniae OPS production, the invention provides attenuated reagent strains derived from clinical isolates through the deletion of specific genes involved in virulence and metabolic pathways. The key genetic modifications include deletion of the guaBA operon, which encodes enzymes essential for guanosine biosynthesis, rendering the strains guanine auxotrophic and significantly attenuated in virulence. This attenuation is demonstrated by a dramatic increase in the intraperitoneal LD50 from approximately 10^4 CFU in wild-type strains to greater than 10^9 CFU in the engineered derivatives, ensuring safe handling during manufacturing.

Additional genetic modifications include deletion of the wzabc gene cluster, which is responsible for capsule biosynthesis. Elimination of capsule production serves multiple purposes: it simplifies the purification process by removing a major contaminant that would otherwise co-purify with OPS, it enhances OPS accessibility for chemical modification during conjugation, and it may further contribute to attenuation by removing a key virulence factor. The absence of capsule is confirmed by negative staining with India ink, which shows the characteristic absence of the white halo surrounding encapsulated bacteria.

The specific reagent strains provided by the invention include CVD 3001, derived from KP B5055 (O1:K2) through deletion of both guaBA and wzabc genes; CVD 3002, derived from KP 4425/51 (O5:K57) through deletion of wzabc genes; and utilization of naturally non-encapsulated strains such as KP 7380 (O2) without additional modification. For strains that are refractory to genetic engineering, such as KP 390 (O3:K11), the invention provides methods for direct use of the wild-type strain with appropriate safety considerations.

For Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin production, the invention provides recombinant expression systems in Escherichia coli rather than direct modification of PA strains. This approach eliminates the need to handle pathogenic PA strains during manufacturing and allows for high-level expression of pure flagellin proteins. The expression system utilizes codon-optimized synthetic genes encoding FlaA and FlaB flagellin proteins, cloned into the pTrc-His-TOPO expression vector under the control of the trc promoter. The synthetic genes incorporate sequences encoding a 6xHis tag at the N-terminus to facilitate purification while maintaining the structural integrity of the flagellin protein.

The recombinant E. coli strains, designated BL21/pTrcHis-flaAopt and BL21/pTrcHis-flaBopt, are specifically engineered for high-yield flagellin production under controlled fermentation conditions. The use of E. coli as the production host provides several advantages, including well-established fermentation protocols, absence of endogenous flagellin that could contaminate the product, and the ability to produce non-glycosylated flagellin proteins that may present different immunogenic profiles compared to native PA flagellin.

These genetically engineered strains represent a critical component of the invention's manufacturing platform, enabling the production of high-quality, well-characterized antigens suitable for human vaccine development while maintaining appropriate biosafety standards throughout the manufacturing process.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Construction and Characterization of Genetically Engineered Klebsiella pneumoniae Reagent Strains for OPS Production

Klebsiella pneumoniae strain B5055 (O1:K2) was used as the parental strain for the construction of reagent strain CVD 3001 through sequential genetic modifications. The guaBA operon was deleted using lambda red-mediated recombination with a linear DNA cassette constructed by overlapping PCR, as described by Chalker et al. and adapted by Tennant et al. The deletion construct included a kanamycin resistance gene flanked by FLP recognition target (FRT) sites for subsequent removal. Following transformation and selection on kanamycin-containing plates, successful deletion of guaBA was confirmed by PCR amplification across the deletion site and sequencing of the scar region remaining after FLP-mediated excision of the antibiotic resistance marker.

The guanine auxotrophy phenotype of the guaBA deletion mutant was confirmed by comparing growth on chemically defined medium (CDM) with and without guanine supplementation. While the wild-type B5055 strain grew robustly on CDM lacking guanine, the guaBA mutant showed no growth unless supplemented with 0.01% guanine, confirming the expected metabolic phenotype.

Subsequently, the wzabc gene cluster responsible for capsule biosynthesis was deleted from the guaBA mutant using the same lambda red-mediated recombination approach. The resulting double mutant, designated CVD 3001, was confirmed for capsule deficiency by negative staining with India ink. Microscopic examination at 1000X magnification revealed that while wild-type B5055 displayed a prominent white halo indicative of capsule presence, CVD 3001 showed no such halo, confirming successful capsule elimination.

The attenuation phenotype of CVD 3001 was evaluated in a mouse model of intraperitoneal infection. Groups of six-to seven-week-old female Crl:CD-1 mice were infected with serial dilutions of either wild-type B5055 or CVD 3001, and mortality was monitored for one week. The LD50 for wild-type B5055 was determined to be approximately 10^4 CFU, while CVD 3001 showed no mortality even at the highest dose tested (10^9 CFU), demonstrating a greater than 10^5-fold increase in attenuation.

Similar genetic engineering strategies were applied to construct reagent strain CVD 3002 from KP strain 4425/51 (O5:K57) through deletion of the wzabc genes. KP strain 7380, which was naturally non-encapsulated and of O2 serotype, was used directly as a reagent strain without genetic modification. KP strain 390 (O3:K11) was found to be refractory to genetic engineering attempts and was therefore used in its native form for OPS production, with appropriate biosafety considerations.

These genetically engineered reagent strains were then used for large-scale OPS production in 4-liter fermentation cultures under controlled conditions. The strains demonstrated robust growth in chemically defined medium supplemented with guanine (for CVD 3001), and OPS yields were comparable to those obtained from wild-type strains, confirming that the genetic modifications did not adversely affect OPS biosynthesis. The elimination of capsule production in CVD 3001 and CVD 3002 significantly simplified the downstream purification process, as evidenced by reduced contamination with high-molecular-weight capsular material during initial clarification steps.

The OPS antigens purified from these reagent strains were characterized by multiple analytical methods. High-performance liquid chromatography with size exclusion chromatography (HPLC-SEC) revealed relatively homogeneous molecular size distributions for all four OPS types (O1, O2, O3, and O5), with similar elution profiles indicating consistent molecular weight ranges. Monosaccharide composition analysis by high-performance anion exchange chromatography with pulsed amperometric detection (HPAEC-PAD) confirmed that O1 and O2 were polygalactan structures, while O3 and O5 were composed primarily of mannose residues, consistent with established structural data.

Residual contaminant levels in the purified OPS preparations were consistently low, with protein content below 1% by Bradford assay, endotoxin levels below 0.01 endotoxin units per microgram by limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) assay, and nucleic acid contamination indicated by absorbance at 260 nm below 0.1. These quality parameters demonstrate that the genetically engineered reagent strains enable the production of high-purity OPS antigens suitable for human vaccine development.

The successful construction and characterization of these reagent strains represent a critical advancement in the manufacturing platform for the conjugate vaccine, providing safe, efficient, and scalable sources of the key OPS antigens required for broad protection against Klebsiella pneumoniae infections.
