## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of metabolic engineering and industrial biotechnology, and more specifically to recombinant filamentous fungi engineered for the biosynthesis of 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) from renewable carbon sources. The invention further encompasses methods for producing 3HP using genetically modified strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus that have been optimized through targeted gene deletions and heterologous pathway expression to enhance yield, titer, and productivity of 3HP under acidic fermentation conditions. Additionally, the invention provides compositions comprising such recombinant fungi, as well as kits and bioprocess protocols for scalable microbial production of 3HP—a valuable platform chemical used in the synthesis of biodegradable polymers, acrylic acid, and carbon fiber precursors.

## BACKGROUND

The global chemical industry has long relied on petroleum-derived feedstocks for the production of a vast array of commodity and specialty chemicals. However, the finite nature of fossil resources, coupled with growing environmental concerns regarding greenhouse gas emissions and ecological degradation associated with extraction and refining processes, has catalyzed a paradigm shift toward sustainable alternatives rooted in biological systems. This transition envisions a bioeconomy wherein renewable biomass—such as lignocellulosic sugars, agricultural residues, and waste streams—is converted into fuels, materials, and chemicals via engineered microorganisms. Central to this vision is the development of robust microbial cell factories capable of efficiently channeling carbon flux from inexpensive, non-food feedstocks into high-value products under industrially relevant conditions.

Among the target molecules driving innovation in industrial biotechnology is 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP), a three-carbon organic acid with significant commercial potential. 3HP serves as a direct monomer for the synthesis of poly(3-hydroxypropionate), a biodegradable polyester, and can be copolymerized with other hydroxy acids—such as 3-hydroxybutyric acid, 4-hydroxybutyric acid, or lactic acid—to tailor polymer properties like flexibility, thermal stability, and degradation rate. Moreover, 3HP can be chemically dehydrated to acrylic acid, a key intermediate in the production of superabsorbent polymers, paints, adhesives, and coatings. Given the multi-billion-dollar market for acrylates, biobased routes to acrylic acid via 3HP represent a compelling opportunity for decarbonizing existing petrochemical supply chains. Additionally, 3HP can be converted to acrylonitrile—a precursor to polyacrylonitrile—and subsequently to carbon fibers used in lightweight composites for aerospace, automotive, and wind energy applications.

Despite its promise, large-scale biological production of 3HP remains economically challenging due to limitations in host organism performance, pathway efficiency, product toxicity, and downstream processing costs. Naturally, 3HP is synthesized only by certain autotrophic bacteria and archaea via specialized CO₂ fixation cycles (e.g., the 3-hydroxypropionate bicycle in Chloroflexi or the 3HP/4-hydroxybutyrate cycle in Crenarchaeota). Consequently, synthetic biology approaches have been employed to reconstruct 3HP biosynthetic pathways in heterologous hosts. Three primary routes have been explored: (1) the glycerol pathway, which converts glycerol to 3HP via 3-hydroxypropionaldehyde; (2) the malonyl-CoA pathway, which reduces malonyl-CoA to 3HP; and (3) the β-alanine pathway, which decarboxylates aspartate to β-alanine, transaminates β-alanine to malonate semialdehyde, and then reduces malonate semialdehyde to 3HP.

Each pathway presents distinct advantages and challenges. The glycerol route has achieved high yields in bacteria like Escherichia coli and Corynebacterium glutamicum but is limited by substrate availability unless integrated with biodiesel production. The malonyl-CoA route suffers from cofactor imbalance and competition with fatty acid biosynthesis. The β-alanine pathway, while thermodynamically favorable and compatible with glucose metabolism, requires precise balancing of nitrogen flux and is susceptible to side reactions and product degradation.

To date, most engineering efforts have focused on model organisms such as Saccharomyces cerevisiae and E. coli. While these hosts offer well-characterized genetics and established tools, they often lack the physiological traits necessary for cost-effective industrial fermentation—particularly the ability to thrive at low pH and secrete organic acids in their protonated (free-acid) form, which simplifies recovery and reduces neutralization costs. Filamentous fungi of the genus Aspergillus, in contrast, are renowned for their natural capacity to produce organic acids—most notably citric acid and itaconic acid—at titers exceeding 100 g/L under highly acidic conditions (pH 2–3). Aspergillus niger and Aspergillus terreus have been used commercially for decades, demonstrating exceptional tolerance to low pH, high osmolarity, and organic acid stress.

Aspergillus pseudoterreus, a close relative of A. terreus, shares these advantageous traits and has been shown to naturally produce itaconic acid—a structural analog of 3HP—via the decarboxylation of cis-aconitate by the enzyme cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (CadA). This native metabolic node presents both an opportunity and a challenge: while the high glycolytic flux and acidogenic metabolism provide a strong foundation for organic acid production, endogenous itaconic acid synthesis competes directly with any introduced pathway for carbon derived from the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. Therefore, strategic redirection of metabolic flux away from itaconate and toward 3HP is essential.

Prior attempts to produce 3HP in fungi have been limited to yeasts such as Pichia pastoris and Schizosaccharomyces pombe, with modest success. No reports exist of 3HP production in filamentous fungi, despite their superior acid tolerance and secretion capabilities. Furthermore, the metabolic complexity of filamentous fungi—characterized by compartmentalization, morphological heterogeneity, and intricate regulatory networks—has historically hindered rational engineering. Recent advances in multi-omics technologies (proteomics, metabolomics, fluxomics) now enable system-wide interrogation of engineered strains, facilitating data-driven strain improvement even in non-model organisms.

A critical barrier to efficient 3HP accumulation is its susceptibility to catabolism. In bacteria and some yeasts, 3HP can be degraded oxidatively back to malonate semialdehyde and then to acetyl-CoA via malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase—an enzyme typically involved in valine and isoleucine catabolism. If such a pathway exists in Aspergillus species, it would act as a significant sink, reversing the final step of the β-alanine pathway and diverting carbon away from 3HP. Identifying and eliminating this competing reaction is therefore crucial for maximizing yield.

In summary, there remains a pressing need for a filamentous fungal host engineered to produce 3HP at high yield and titer under acidic, industrially relevant conditions. Such a host must combine (i) deletion of native organic acid pathways that compete for carbon, (ii) stable integration and expression of a heterologous 3HP biosynthetic pathway, (iii) elimination of endogenous 3HP degradation routes, and (iv) compatibility with low-pH fermentation to facilitate product recovery. The present invention addresses these needs through the development of recombinant ΔcadA strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus expressing a codon-optimized β-alanine pathway and further engineered with a deletion in a putative malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase gene, resulting in significantly enhanced 3HP production.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides recombinant strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus engineered for the efficient production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) from renewable carbon sources under acidic fermentation conditions. These strains are characterized by the disruption or deletion of the endogenous cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cadA) gene, which abolishes native itaconic acid production and redirects metabolic flux toward heterologous pathways. Additionally, the strains stably integrate and express a synthetic β-alanine biosynthetic pathway comprising three heterologous enzymes: aspartate-1-decarboxylase (PAND), β-alanine-pyruvate transaminase (BAPAT), and 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase (HPDH), each codon-optimized for expression in Aspergillus species and operably linked to strong, constitutive fungal promoters. In a preferred embodiment, the invention further comprises a deletion of a gene encoding a putative malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Apald6), which prevents the catabolism of malonate semialdehyde—the immediate precursor to 3HP—thereby significantly increasing 3HP yield and titer.

The invention also encompasses methods for producing 3HP using these recombinant fungi. The methods involve cultivating the engineered Aspergillus pseudoterreus strains in a fermentation medium containing a fermentable carbon source—such as glucose, xylose, or sucrose—at a pH maintained between approximately 2.0 and 3.5, preferably around 2.8, under aerobic conditions with controlled agitation and aeration. Under these conditions, the fungus secretes 3HP predominantly in its protonated free-acid form, which simplifies downstream separation and purification. The methods may be carried out in shake flasks, benchtop bioreactors, or at industrial scale in stirred-tank reactors, with process parameters optimized to maximize growth-linked 3HP production during the exponential phase.

Furthermore, the invention provides methods for producing 3HP that include the additional step of deleting or inactivating the Apald6 gene to block the oxidative degradation of malonate semialdehyde to acetyl-CoA. This genetic modification results in a greater than three-fold increase in 3HP yield compared to strains lacking this deletion, as demonstrated in both shake-flask and small-scale bioreactor experiments.

The invention additionally includes compositions comprising the recombinant Aspergillus pseudoterreus strains described herein, as well as kits for 3HP production that contain such strains along with instructions for their use in fermentation. These kits may further include culture media formulations, pH control reagents, and analytical standards for monitoring 3HP production.

In one aspect, the invention provides a recombinant filamentous fungus of the species Aspergillus pseudoterreus, wherein the fungus comprises (a) a disruption or deletion of the cadA gene encoding cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase; (b) at least one integrated copy of a nucleic acid construct encoding a β-alanine pathway for 3HP biosynthesis, said construct comprising nucleotide sequences encoding PAND, BAPAT, and HPDH enzymes codon-optimized for Aspergillus, each operably linked to a promoter functional in Aspergillus; and (c) optionally, a disruption or deletion of the Apald6 gene encoding a malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of producing 3HP, comprising: (i) providing a recombinant Aspergillus pseudoterreus strain as described above; (ii) culturing the strain in a fermentation medium comprising a carbon source at a pH of 2.0 to 3.5; and (iii) recovering 3HP from the culture broth.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a composition comprising a recombinant Aspergillus pseudoterreus strain engineered to produce 3HP, wherein the strain exhibits a 3HP titer of at least 0.8 g/L in a controlled bioreactor fermentation at pH 2.8 after seven days of cultivation.

These and other embodiments of the invention enable the sustainable, cost-effective microbial production of 3HP—a key building block for green polymers and chemicals—from renewable feedstocks under industrially favorable acidic conditions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention is directed to the metabolic engineering of Aspergillus pseudoterreus for the biosynthesis of 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP), a valuable platform chemical with applications in biodegradable plastics, acrylic acid production, and carbon fiber manufacturing. The core strategy involves the creation of recombinant fungal strains that combine three key genetic modifications: (1) deletion of the native cadA gene to eliminate itaconic acid production and free up metabolic flux from the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle; (2) stable chromosomal integration and expression of a heterologous β-alanine pathway for 3HP biosynthesis, composed of codon-optimized genes encoding aspartate-1-decarboxylase (PAND), β-alanine-pyruvate transaminase (BAPAT), and 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase (HPDH); and (3) deletion of a putative malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase gene (Apald6) to prevent the catabolic conversion of malonate semialdehyde—the direct precursor to 3HP—into acetyl-CoA, thereby locking carbon flux toward 3HP accumulation.

These engineered strains are cultivated under acidic fermentation conditions (pH 2.0–3.5), leveraging the natural acid tolerance of Aspergillus pseudoterreus to produce 3HP predominantly in its protonated free-acid form. This physicochemical property significantly reduces the cost and complexity of downstream processing, as the product can be recovered without extensive neutralization or salt formation steps. Multi-omics analyses—including global and targeted proteomics, intracellular and extracellular metabolomics, and flux balance modeling—were instrumental in identifying metabolic bottlenecks, off-target effects, and competing degradation pathways, enabling a rational, data-driven approach to strain optimization. The resulting strains demonstrate substantially improved 3HP yields compared to initial constructs, with the Apald6 deletion conferring a greater than three-fold increase in titer under controlled bioreactor conditions. The invention thus provides a robust, scalable platform for the sustainable production of 3HP from renewable sugars.

### Recombinant ΔcadA Fungi

The recombinant fungi of the present invention are derived from Aspergillus pseudoterreus, a filamentous fungus closely related to Aspergillus terreus and known for its natural ability to produce itaconic acid at high titers under acidic conditions. The parental strain used in this work is Aspergillus pseudoterreus ATCC® 32359™. A critical first step in engineering this host for 3HP production was the disruption or deletion of the endogenous cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cadA) gene, which encodes the enzyme responsible for converting cis-aconitate—a TCA cycle intermediate—into itaconic acid. In wild-type A. pseudoterreus, this pathway diverts substantial carbon flux away from central metabolism and toward itaconate secretion, particularly during the late exponential and stationary phases of growth under acidic, nutrient-limited conditions.

By deleting cadA, the engineered strains lose the capacity to produce itaconic acid, as confirmed by metabolite profiling in both shake-flask and bioreactor cultures. This genetic lesion serves two primary purposes: first, it eliminates a major competing product that would otherwise contaminate the desired 3HP output and complicate purification; second, it redirects metabolic flux from cis-aconitate back into the TCA cycle or toward alternative biosynthetic routes, including the introduced 3HP pathway. The cadA locus was specifically chosen as the site for integration of the heterologous 3HP biosynthetic construct, ensuring that the deletion and pathway insertion occur simultaneously through homologous recombination. This strategy not only knocks out itaconate production but also provides a defined genomic location for stable, single-copy integration of the transgene cassette, reducing positional effects and enhancing genetic stability.

The resulting ΔcadA strains serve as the foundational chassis for all subsequent engineering. They retain the desirable physiological traits of the parent strain—including high glycolytic flux, broad substrate utilization (glucose, xylose, etc.), and tolerance to low pH—but no longer secrete itaconic acid. This clean metabolic background allows for unambiguous attribution of any observed organic acid production to the introduced pathway. Furthermore, the absence of itaconate accumulation prevents potential feedback inhibition or redox imbalances that might otherwise interfere with 3HP biosynthesis. The ΔcadA mutation is stable through multiple generations and does not impair growth under standard fermentation conditions, confirming that the loss of itaconate production is not detrimental to cellular fitness in the context of engineered metabolism.

### Methods of Producing Aconitic Acid

Although the primary objective of the present invention is the production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid, it is instructive to note that the parental Aspergillus pseudoterreus strain naturally produces aconitic acid—as well as its isomer itaconic acid—as a major fermentation product under acidic conditions. Aconitic acid exists in two forms: cis-aconitate and trans-aconitate, with cis-aconitate being the direct intermediate in the TCA cycle between citrate and isocitrate. In wild-type A. pseudoterreus, cis-aconitate accumulates when the activity of aconitase—the enzyme that converts cis-aconitate to isocitrate—is downregulated under low-pH, high-glucose conditions, leading to its partial secretion into the culture medium. Additionally, a fraction of cis-aconitate is decarboxylated by CadA to itaconic acid.

The methods for producing aconitic acid using wild-type or non-engineered A. pseudoterreus involve cultivating the fungus in a minimal medium containing a high concentration of glucose (e.g., 100 g/L) at an initial pH of approximately 3.4, which spontaneously drops to below 3.0 during active growth due to organic acid production. Cultivation is carried out aerobically at 30°C with sufficient agitation (e.g., 200 rpm in shake flasks or controlled impeller speed in bioreactors) and aeration (e.g., 0.4 vessel volumes per minute of air sparging). Under these conditions, aconitic acid begins to accumulate during the mid-to-late exponential phase and continues to increase into the stationary phase, often reaching titers of several grams per liter alongside itaconic acid.

However, in the context of the present invention, aconitic acid production is not desired, as it represents a diversion of carbon away from the target product, 3HP. Indeed, experimental data from bioreactor scale-up revealed that in early engineered strains lacking the Apald6 deletion, the onset of aconitic acid (and other TCA-derived organic acids such as citric, isocitric, and α-ketoglutaric acids) coincided with a decline in 3HP titer, suggesting a metabolic shift from growth-linked 3HP production to overflow metabolism typical of organic acid-producing Aspergillus species. This observation underscores the importance of not only introducing the 3HP pathway but also managing the host’s native metabolic responses to prevent competition. Consequently, the recombinant strains of the present invention—particularly those with the Apald6 deletion—are cultivated under conditions that favor sustained glycolytic flux and minimize the transition to TCA-cycle overflow, thereby suppressing aconitic acid production and stabilizing 3HP accumulation.

### Methods of Producing 3-HP

The methods of producing 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) according to the present invention involve the cultivation of recombinant Aspergillus pseudoterreus strains that have been genetically modified to express a heterologous β-alanine biosynthetic pathway and to lack competing metabolic routes. The process begins with the preparation of a spore inoculum from a glycerol stock of the engineered strain, which is streaked onto complete medium agar plates and incubated at 30°C for five days to induce conidiation. Conidia are harvested by washing the plates with a sterile solution of 0.4% Tween 80, and a defined number of spores (typically 2 × 10⁶) are used to inoculate a fermentation medium.

The fermentation medium is a minimal medium formulated to support robust growth while minimizing the production of unwanted byproducts. A representative composition includes 100 g/L glucose as the primary carbon source, 2.36 g/L (NH₄)₂SO₄ as the nitrogen source, and a balanced mixture of salts and trace elements: 0.11 g/L KH₂PO₄, 2.08 g/L MgSO₄·7H₂O, 0.13 g/L CaCl₂·2H₂O, 74 mg/L NaCl, 1.3 mg/L ZnSO₄·7H₂O, 0.7 mg/L MnCl₂·4H₂O, 5.5 mg/L FeSO₄·7H₂O, and 0.2 mg/L CuSO₄·5H₂O. The initial pH of the medium is adjusted to 3.4 using 5 M KOH, though it rapidly declines during active metabolism.

Fermentation is conducted under aerobic conditions at 30°C. In shake-flask cultures, 50 mL of medium in 250 mL flasks is agitated at 200 rpm. For controlled bioreactor cultivations, the process is scaled to either 0.5 L or 20 L working volumes in stirred-tank reactors. Agitation is initially set to a low rate (e.g., 100–200 rpm) to support mycelial growth without excessive shear, and is increased (to 400–600 rpm) once the pH drops to 2.8 to ensure adequate oxygen transfer. The pH is actively maintained at 2.8 throughout the fermentation by automated addition of 5 M KOH, although in some embodiments, pH control may be omitted to allow natural acidification, which favors 3HP in its free-acid form.

Air is sparged continuously at a rate of 0.4 vessel volumes per minute to maintain dissolved oxygen levels above critical thresholds. Samples are periodically withdrawn for analysis of biomass (via dry cell weight), residual glucose, and extracellular metabolites, including 3HP. 3HP is quantified using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) or high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) with appropriate calibration standards.

The engineered strains produce 3HP via the β-alanine pathway: aspartate is decarboxylated by PAND to β-alanine; β-alanine undergoes transamination with pyruvate, catalyzed by BAPAT, to yield malonate semialdehyde and alanine; and malonate semialdehyde is reduced by HPDH using NADPH to form 3HP. This pathway is growth-linked, with 3HP production initiating early in the exponential phase and peaking before the onset of overflow metabolism. In the optimized strain lacking Apald6, 3HP accumulates steadily to a final titer of 0.88 ± 0.11 g/L in 0.5 L bioreactors after seven days, representing a 3.3-fold improvement over the parent strain. The product is secreted into the culture broth and can be recovered by standard techniques such as filtration, extraction, or electrodialysis, taking advantage of its low pKa (4.5) and predominance in the protonated form at pH 2.8.

### Example 1

In this example, the initial construction of recombinant Aspergillus pseudoterreus strains capable of producing 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) is described. The parental strain, Aspergillus pseudoterreus ATCC® 32359™, was genetically modified to disrupt the native cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cadA) gene and simultaneously integrate a synthetic β-alanine pathway for 3HP biosynthesis. The pathway consists of three heterologous enzymes: aspartate-1-decarboxylase from Tribolium castaneum (TcPAND), β-alanine-pyruvate transaminase from Bacillus cereus (BcBAPAT), and 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase from Escherichia coli (EcHPDH). Each coding sequence was codon-optimized for expression in Aspergillus species to enhance translational efficiency.

The DNA construct was assembled using Gibson Assembly cloning. The TcPAND gene was placed under the control of the strong, constitutive gpdA promoter from Aspergillus niger, while BcBAPAT and EcHPDH were co-expressed from a bidirectional promoter system utilizing the eno1 promoter from A. niger. Transcriptional termination was ensured by the elf3 terminator from A. niger. This entire cassette was flanked by homologous sequences corresponding to the 5′ and 3′ regions of the cadA locus in A. pseudoterreus, enabling targeted integration via homologous recombination. The construct was cloned into the pBlueScript SK(–) vector and designated pZD4025.

A second plasmid, pZD4028, was constructed to include a pyrithiamine resistance marker (ptrA) from Aspergillus oryzae for selection, along with additional regulatory elements. This plasmid was linearized with EcoRV and used to transform A. pseudoterreus protoplasts via a chemical-mediated transformation protocol. Transformants were selected on minimal medium containing pyrithiamine and screened by PCR to confirm correct integration at the cadA locus.

Two strains were isolated: ABF_004528_2, which contained a single copy of the 3HP pathway integrated at the cadA locus (designated cad:3HP+), and ABF_004528_6, which contained one copy at cadA and an additional ectopic integration elsewhere in the genome (designated cad:3HP+, 3HP+). Both strains were confirmed to be ΔcadA by the absence of itaconic acid production in shake-flask cultures. Shake-flask fermentations in minimal medium with 100 g/L glucose at 30°C and 200 rpm revealed that strain ABF_004528_6 produced nearly twice the amount of extracellular 3HP compared to ABF_004528_2 after eight days, demonstrating a gene dosage effect. This example establishes the feasibility of 3HP production in A. pseudoterreus and provides the foundational strains for subsequent optimization.

### Example 2

This example details the multi-omics characterization of the 3HP-producing strains generated in Example 1 to identify metabolic perturbations and potential bottlenecks. Strains ABF_004528_2 (single-copy), ABF_004528_6 (double-copy), and a ΔcadA control strain (ABF_002234) were cultivated in shake flasks and sampled every two days over an eight-day period. Samples were processed for global proteomics, targeted proteomics of central carbon metabolism enzymes, intracellular metabolomics, and extracellular metabolite quantification.

Global proteomics detected 4,674 proteins, while targeted proteomics quantified 79 key metabolic enzymes. Extracellular analysis identified nine organic acids, and intracellular metabolomics detected 320 metabolites, of which 122 were structurally annotated. As expected, the three heterologous enzymes (TcPAND, BcBAPAT, EcHPDH) were detected at 1.66-fold higher levels in the double-copy strain, correlating with its higher 3HP titer. Intracellular pools of β-alanine and 3HP were elevated in both engineered strains but did not differ significantly between them, suggesting that transport or downstream reactions were not limiting.

Notably, the intracellular concentration of 2,4-diaminobutanoate—a non-canonical metabolite not previously reported in Aspergillus—was found to be dramatically increased (41- to 131-fold) in the engineered strains. Proteomic analysis revealed strong upregulation of Apuga1, a 4-aminobutyrate aminotransferase. It was hypothesized that Apuga1 acts promiscuously on aspartate-semialdehyde (derived from aspartate) to produce 2,4-diaminobutanoate, effectively creating a nitrogen sink that could compete with the 3HP pathway. Additionally, the putative malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase Apald6 (encoded by gene g153) was significantly upregulated in the 3HP-producing strains, suggesting a role in 3HP catabolism.

Extracellular metabolite profiling in bioreactor scale-up (20 L) revealed that 3HP production peaked at 0.69 g/L at hour 67 but then declined to 0.17 g/L by the end of the run. Control experiments confirmed that 3HP was stable in acidified medium in the absence of cells, implicating biotic degradation. The timing of 3HP loss coincided with the onset of aconitic acid and other TCA-derived organic acid production, indicating a metabolic shift that activates 3HP degradation pathways. Flux balance analysis using the Aspergillus niger model iJB1325, augmented with the β-alanine pathway, predicted 3HP secretion but also suggested unmodeled catabolic routes. This example highlights the power of multi-omics to uncover hidden metabolic interactions and guides the next round of engineering.

### Example 3

In this example, the hypothesis that Apald6 mediates 3HP degradation was tested by constructing a deletion mutant. The Apald6 gene (g153), encoding a putative malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase homologous to bacterial and yeast enzymes known to catabolize 3HP, was targeted for knockout in the high-producing strain ABF_004528_6 (cad:3HP+, 3HP+). Upstream and downstream flanking regions of Apald6 were amplified from A. pseudoterreus genomic DNA, and the hygromycin phosphotransferase (hph) resistance cassette was used as a selectable marker. These fragments were assembled with the pRF-HU2 vector backbone using NEBuilder HiFi DNA Assembly and transformed into ABF_004528_6 via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation.

The resulting strain, ABF_008339 (cad:3HP+, 3HP+; ΔApald6), was confirmed by PCR and subjected to shake-flask fermentation under identical conditions as the parent strain. After eight days, the ΔApald6 mutant produced 3.4 times more extracellular 3HP than the parent strain, confirming that Apald6 functions as a malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase that diverts flux away from 3HP and toward acetyl-CoA. This result validates the oxidative 3HP degradation pathway in A. pseudoterreus and demonstrates that blocking this route is a highly effective strategy for improving yield.

The metabolic model iJB1325 was updated to include the Apald6-catalyzed reaction (malonate semialdehyde + NAD⁺ + H₂O → acetyl-CoA + CO₂ + NADH + H⁺). Parsimonious flux balance analysis with this revised model better recapitulated experimental observations, including the absence of unexplained metabolite drains. This example illustrates how iterative cycles of omics-guided hypothesis generation and genetic validation can rapidly optimize strain performance.

### Example 4

This example evaluates the performance of the optimized ΔApald6 strain in controlled bioreactor conditions. Strain ABF_008339 (ΔcadA, ΔApald6, double-copy 3HP pathway) and its parent ABF_004528_6 were cultivated in 0.5 L Sixfors bioreactors with 0.45 L working volume of minimal medium containing 100 g/L glucose. Temperature was maintained at 30°C, pH was controlled at 2.8 via automated KOH addition, and agitation was increased from 200 to 600 rpm once pH dropped below 2.8. Air was sparged at 0.4 vvm.

Over seven days, the parent strain accumulated 0.27 ± 0.10 g/L 3HP, while the ΔApald6 mutant reached 0.88 ± 0.11 g/L—a 3.3-fold improvement. Critically, 3HP titers remained stable throughout the fermentation in the mutant, with no evidence of degradation, unlike the decline observed in the 20 L bioreactor with the non-ΔApald6 strain. Additionally, the major organic acids seen in larger-scale runs (e.g., aconitic acid) were absent in the 0.5 L bioreactors, suggesting that reactor hydrodynamics and oxygen transfer influence the metabolic shift to overflow metabolism.

The ΔApald6 strain also exhibited altered byproduct profiles, accumulating higher levels of glycerol and erythritol and showing slower CO₂ evolution, indicative of redirected redox and carbon flux. These results confirm that deletion of Apald6 not only blocks 3HP degradation but also reshapes central metabolism to favor product retention. This example demonstrates the scalability and robustness of the engineered strain and establishes a benchmark for future process optimization aimed at achieving industrially relevant 3HP titers.
