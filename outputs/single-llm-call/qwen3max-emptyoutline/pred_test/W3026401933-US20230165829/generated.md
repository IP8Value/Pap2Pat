# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of oncology and molecular pharmacology, specifically to the development of therapeutic agents for the treatment of cancers driven by RAS oncogenes. More particularly, the invention relates to a novel class of compounds derived from flavaglines—natural products isolated from plants of the genus *Aglaia*—that selectively inhibit the activation of RAS GTPases, with a pronounced effect on KRAS isoforms. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these compounds, methods for their use in inhibiting RAS signaling, and their application in the treatment of RAS-mutant cancers, including but not limited to pancreatic, lung, and colorectal carcinomas. The compounds of the invention act through a previously unrecognized mechanism involving the disruption of plasma membrane-associated prohibitin complexes, thereby interfering with the nanoclustering and GTP-loading of RAS proteins, particularly KRAS4B and KRAS4A, which are critical for oncogenic signal transduction. This represents a significant advancement over existing RAS-targeted therapies, which are often limited to specific point mutations such as G12C and do not address the broader spectrum of RAS-driven malignancies or the compensatory reactivation of wild-type RAS observed in clinical settings.

## BACKGROUND ART

RAS proteins constitute a family of small GTPases that function as pivotal molecular switches in intracellular signal transduction pathways governing cell proliferation, differentiation, survival, and metabolism. The three major human RAS isoforms—HRAS, NRAS, and KRAS (which exists as two splice variants, KRAS4A and KRAS4B)—are encoded by distinct genes but share high sequence homology, particularly within their catalytic G domains. Their activity is tightly regulated by the cyclical binding and hydrolysis of guanosine nucleotides: in the inactive state, RAS is bound to GDP, whereas upon stimulation by extracellular signals such as growth factors, guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) catalyze the exchange of GDP for GTP, resulting in the active conformation that engages downstream effectors like RAF kinases, PI3K, and RalGDS. GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) accelerate the intrinsic GTP hydrolysis rate, returning RAS to its inactive GDP-bound state. Oncogenic mutations in RAS, most commonly occurring at codons 12, 13, and 61, impair GAP-mediated hydrolysis, leading to constitutive activation and uncontrolled signaling that drives tumorigenesis. KRAS mutations are especially prevalent, found in approximately 90% of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas, 40% of colorectal cancers, and 30% of non-small cell lung cancers (NSCLC), making KRAS one of the most frequently mutated oncogenes in human cancer.

Historically, RAS proteins were considered "undruggable" due to their picomolar affinity for GTP/GDP, the absence of deep hydrophobic pockets suitable for small-molecule binding, and the high intracellular concentration of GTP that outcompetes potential inhibitors. This paradigm began to shift with the discovery that the KRAS G12C mutant, which substitutes glycine with cysteine at position 12, creates a unique druggable pocket adjacent to the nucleotide-binding site in the GDP-bound state. Covalent inhibitors such as sotorasib (AMG 510) and adagrasib (MRTX849) exploit this cysteine residue to trap KRAS G12C in its inactive conformation, leading to significant clinical responses in subsets of NSCLC patients. However, these agents are mutation-specific and ineffective against other common KRAS mutants like G12D, G12V, or Q61H, which collectively represent the majority of KRAS-driven cancers. Moreover, emerging resistance mechanisms—including secondary RAS mutations, amplification of receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), and feedback reactivation of wild-type RAS—limit their long-term efficacy. Consequently, there remains an urgent need for therapeutic strategies that can broadly inhibit multiple RAS isoforms and mutants, irrespective of the specific amino acid substitution.

Flavaglines are a class of naturally occurring cyclopenta[b]benzofuran compounds originally isolated from *Aglaia* species, known for their potent anti-tumor, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective activities. Early studies identified eukaryotic initiation factor 4A (eIF4A), an RNA helicase involved in cap-dependent translation initiation, as a primary target of flavaglines such as rocaglamide and silvestrol. These compounds stabilize eIF4A in a conformation that enhances its binding to polypurine sequences in mRNA 5′-UTRs, thereby blocking ribosomal scanning and suppressing the translation of oncogenic mRNAs, including those encoding cyclin D1, Mcl-1, and Bcl-2. More recently, prohibitins (PHB1 and PHB2)—evolutionarily conserved scaffold proteins localized to mitochondria, the nucleus, and the plasma membrane—were identified as additional high-affinity targets of flavaglines. Prohibitins form ring-like oligomeric complexes in the inner mitochondrial membrane that are essential for cristae morphogenesis and mitochondrial function. At the plasma membrane, however, prohibitins have been implicated in organizing signaling platforms by interacting with various receptors and kinases, including CRAF (RAF1). It was previously demonstrated that flavaglines disrupt the PHB1–CRAF interaction, thereby inhibiting CRAF activation and downstream MAPK signaling in KRAS-mutant cells. Notably, this anti-tumor effect could be bypassed by oncogenic BRAF (e.g., V600E), which activates MEK/ERK independently of upstream RAS and CRAF regulation, suggesting that the primary site of action might lie upstream of CRAF.

Despite these advances, the precise molecular mechanism by which flavaglines exert their anti-RAS effects remained unclear. While inhibition of eIF4A or CRAF could contribute to reduced tumor cell viability, neither fully explains the rapid and direct suppression of RAS-GTP loading observed in cellular assays. Furthermore, not all flavaglines exhibit equivalent anti-RAS activity, implying that structural features beyond the core cyclopenta[b]benzofuran scaffold dictate functional specificity. The differential post-translational modifications and membrane localization patterns of RAS isoforms—such as the polybasic farnesylated tail of KRAS4B versus the dual palmitoylation of HRAS—also suggest that isoform-selective regulatory mechanisms may exist at the plasma membrane, potentially involving lipid microdomains and scaffolding proteins like prohibitins. Prior art lacks a comprehensive understanding of how flavagline structure correlates with RAS inhibitory potency, whether prohibitins serve as direct mediators of RAS activation, and whether flavagline-induced disruption of membrane nanodomains constitutes a viable therapeutic strategy for pan-RAS inhibition. The present invention addresses these gaps by elucidating a novel mechanism wherein a defined subset of flavaglines selectively targets plasma membrane prohibitin complexes to prevent RAS nanoclustering and GTP-loading, thereby offering a mutation-agnostic approach to RAS-driven oncology.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel therapeutic strategy for the inhibition of RAS GTPase activation through the targeted disruption of plasma membrane-associated prohibitin complexes by a structurally defined subset of flavagline compounds. Contrary to prior assumptions that flavagline-mediated anti-tumor effects are solely attributable to eIF4A inhibition or CRAF dissociation, the inventors have discovered that certain flavaglines directly interfere with the spatial organization of RAS proteins at the plasma membrane, specifically by preventing the formation of nanoclusters essential for efficient GTP loading and effector engagement. This mechanism is mediated through the high-affinity binding of these flavaglines to prohibitin 1 (PHB1) and prohibitin 2 (PHB2), which function as lipid scaffolds that facilitate the segregation of phosphatidylserine (PS) and phosphatidic acid (PA) into microdomains required for KRAS activation. The invention demonstrates that only flavaglines possessing specific side chain substitutions on the cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core exhibit potent anti-RAS activity, with structural features such as the presence of bromine or chlorine at position 4′, a dimethylcarboxamide or methyl ester at position 1, and a fluorine at position 3′ being critical determinants of efficacy. Notably, these compounds inhibit GTP loading across multiple KRAS mutants—including G12D, G12V, and G12C—as well as wild-type KRAS, and show activity against NRAS and HRAS in biochemical assays, although nanoclustering inhibition is predominantly observed for KRAS isoforms.

The invention further discloses that the anti-RAS activity of these flavaglines is independent of eIF4A inhibition and CRAF binding, as evidenced by the use of flavagline analogs like FL42, which lacks eIF4A-inhibitory properties yet retains full RAS-inhibitory potency. Through a combination of NanoBiT-based real-time RAS activation assays, fluorescence lifetime imaging–Förster resonance energy transfer (FLIM-FRET), electron microscopy, and nanobar curvature-sensing platforms, the inventors establish that effective flavaglines disrupt the co-localization of KRAS with PS and PA at the plasma membrane, thereby preventing the conformational changes necessary for stable GTP binding and effector recruitment. Cellular thermal shift assays (CETSA) confirm direct engagement of PHB1/2 by active flavaglines, while siRNA-mediated depletion of PHB1 phenocopies the RAS-inhibitory effects of flavagline treatment, underscoring the essential role of prohibitins in this process. In vivo validation in autochthonous KRAS G12D-driven non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) mouse models demonstrates significant tumor regression upon rocaglamide administration, without observable hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity at therapeutically effective doses.

Accordingly, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a flavagline compound selected from the group consisting of rocaglamide, FL3, FL10, FL13, FL15, FL19, FL23, FL32, FL37, FL40, and FL42, or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt or solvate thereof, in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. These compounds are characterized by the general structure of a cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core with specific substituents that confer high-affinity binding to prohibitins and consequent inhibition of RAS nanoclustering. The invention also encompasses methods of treating a RAS-driven cancer in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a composition as described herein. The cancers amenable to treatment include, but are not limited to, pancreatic adenocarcinoma, colorectal carcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer, melanoma, and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, particularly those harboring mutations in KRAS, NRAS, or HRAS at codons 12, 13, or 61. The disclosed compounds exhibit IC50 values in the low nanomolar range (6–25 nM) for inhibition of RAS-GTP loading and tumor cell proliferation, and are effective against both mutant and wild-type RAS, thereby overcoming limitations of current allele-specific inhibitors.

### R8 is —H, —OCH3, —Br, —F, —Cl;

The invention further defines a critical structure-activity relationship wherein the substituent at position 8 of the flavagline scaffold modulates anti-RAS potency. Specifically, the R8 group may be selected from hydrogen (—H), methoxy (—OCH3), bromo (—Br), fluoro (—F), or chloro (—Cl). Experimental data demonstrate that the presence of a methoxy group at R8 is highly detrimental to RAS inhibitory activity, as exemplified by the inactivity of flavaglines such as FL1 and FL6, which bear this substitution. In contrast, replacement of the methoxy with halogen atoms—particularly bromine or chlorine—significantly enhances the ability of the compound to suppress KRAS activation. This effect is attributed to the increased lipophilicity and steric profile conferred by halogen substituents, which likely improve membrane permeability and optimize interactions with the prohibitin-lipid interface. Fluorine, due to its small size and strong electronegativity, may either enhance or diminish activity depending on the electronic and steric context of adjacent substituents, particularly at positions 1′ and 4′. Hydrogen at R8 is permissive for activity, as seen in rocaglamide and several active analogs, suggesting that the absence of a bulky or polar group at this position allows for optimal conformational flexibility or binding orientation within the prohibitin complex. Thus, the selection of R8 as —H, —Br, —F, or —Cl—while excluding —OCH3—is a key determinant of the compound’s efficacy in disrupting RAS nanoclustering and represents a novel and non-obvious feature of the claimed invention.

### R8 is —H, —OCH3, —Br, —F, —Cl;

Reiterating for emphasis and completeness in the context of patent claim drafting, the R8 substituent on the cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core of the flavagline compounds of the invention is definitively selected from the group consisting of —H, —OCH3, —Br, —F, and —Cl. However, it is critically established that when R8 is —OCH3, the compound exhibits substantially reduced or negligible activity in inhibiting RAS GTP loading and subsequent downstream signaling. Therefore, while the chemical scope includes —OCH3, the functional and therapeutic embodiments of the invention specifically exclude compounds wherein R8 is —OCH3, or alternatively, require that R8 be —H, —Br, —F, or —Cl to achieve the desired biological effect. This distinction is supported by comparative NanoBiT assays showing that flavaglines with R8 = —OCH3 (e.g., FL1, FL6) fail to inhibit KRAS activation even at high concentrations, whereas those with R8 = —Br or —Cl (e.g., FL23, FL42) exhibit potent inhibition with IC50 values below 50 nM. The inclusion of —F at R8 is conditionally active, with efficacy dependent on synergistic interactions with other ring substituents, as detailed in the Modes for Carrying Out the Invention. This refined definition of R8 ensures that the claimed compounds possess the requisite specificity for prohibitin-mediated RAS inhibition, distinguishing the invention from prior art flavaglines that may target eIF4A but lack anti-RAS activity.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The present invention may be carried out using a variety of flavagline compounds that share a common cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core but differ in their side chain substitutions, which critically determine their ability to inhibit RAS activation via prohibitin targeting. Representative active compounds include rocaglamide, FL3, FL10, FL13, FL15, FL19, FL23, FL32, FL37, FL40, and FL42, all of which have been synthesized according to established protocols involving regioselective functionalization of the flavagline scaffold. The synthesis typically begins with the preparation of the tricyclic benzofuran intermediate, followed by introduction of substituents at positions 1, 3′, 4′, and 8 through electrophilic aromatic substitution, nucleophilic acyl substitution, or transition-metal-catalyzed cross-coupling reactions. For instance, bromination at position 4′ can be achieved using N-bromosuccinimide (NBS) under radical conditions, while esterification or amidation at position 1 employs acid chlorides or activated carboxylic acids in the presence of coupling agents like HATU or EDCI. The final compounds are purified by silica gel chromatography or preparative HPLC and characterized by NMR and high-resolution mass spectrometry to ensure structural fidelity.

In vitro, the anti-RAS activity of these compounds is evaluated using a NanoBiT complementation assay, wherein KRAS (wild-type or mutant) is fused to the large bioluminescent fragment (LgBiT) and the RAS-binding domain (RBD) of CRAF is fused to the small fragment (SmBiT). Upon GTP loading, KRAS undergoes a conformational change that enables RBD binding, bringing LgBiT and SmBiT into proximity to reconstitute NanoLuc luciferase activity, which is quantified as relative luminescence units (RLU). Treatment with active flavaglines (e.g., 100 nM rocaglamide for 4 hours) results in a dose-dependent reduction in RLU, with IC50 values calculated using nonlinear regression analysis. This assay is validated using known KRAS inhibitors like ARS-1620, which shows mutant-specific inhibition, in contrast to the pan-KRAS inhibition observed with rocaglamide. Additional confirmation is obtained through traditional RAS-GTP pulldown assays using GST-CRAF-RBD beads, followed by Western blotting with pan-RAS antibodies.

For mechanistic studies, FLIM-FRET is employed to assess RAS nanoclustering in live cells. HEK293 cells are co-transfected with GFP- and mCherry-tagged RAS constructs, and donor fluorescence lifetime is measured before and after flavagline treatment. A decrease in FRET efficiency indicates disruption of nanocluster formation. Similarly, immunogold electron microscopy of plasma membrane sheets from BHK cells expressing GFP-RAS mutants reveals reduced gold particle clustering upon rocaglamide treatment, specifically for KRAS but not HRAS or NRAS. Nanobar assays further demonstrate that active flavaglines reverse the curvature-sensing preference of oncogenic KRAS, redistributing it from bar ends to centers, consistent with loss of nanoclustering.

In vivo, efficacy is tested in autochthonous mouse models of KRAS-driven NSCLC. SP-C/rtTA; TetO-KRASG12D mice are induced with doxycycline for two months to initiate tumor formation, followed by intraperitoneal administration of rocaglamide (2.5 mg/kg, three times weekly) for six weeks. Tumor burden is quantified by counting lung surface nodules, and histopathological analysis confirms reduced tumor size and proliferation. Toxicity is assessed by measuring serum levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and creatinine in treated mice, with no significant elevations observed.

Pharmaceutical compositions are prepared by dissolving the flavagline compound in a mixture of ethanol, Cremophor EL, and saline (1:1:8, v/v/v) for intravenous or intraperitoneal injection, or by formulating into oral tablets with microcrystalline cellulose, lactose, and magnesium stearate. Dosage regimens range from 1 to 10 mg/kg body weight, administered once daily or intermittently based on pharmacokinetic profiles. The compounds are stable under standard storage conditions and exhibit favorable solubility and metabolic stability in preclinical models.

In summary, the invention provides a robust platform for the development of next-generation RAS inhibitors that operate through a novel membrane-targeted mechanism, offering broad applicability across RAS-mutant cancers and overcoming the limitations of current mutation-specific therapies.
