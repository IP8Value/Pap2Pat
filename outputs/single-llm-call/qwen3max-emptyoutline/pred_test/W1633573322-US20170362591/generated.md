# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of immunostimulatory oligonucleotides, specifically to novel nonaggregating CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (ODNs) that function as potent Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9) agonists. More particularly, the invention provides modified A/D-type CpG ODNs that retain the high interferon-alpha (IFN-α)-inducing immunostimulatory profile characteristic of classical A/D-type ODNs while overcoming the significant pharmaceutical limitations associated with spontaneous aggregation and poor solubility in physiological salt solutions. The invention further encompasses compositions comprising these modified ODNs, methods for their preparation, and their use as vaccine adjuvants or as monotherapeutic immunomodulatory agents for the treatment or prevention of infectious diseases, cancers, and other conditions where stimulation of a robust type I interferon response is therapeutically beneficial. The technical field thus spans molecular immunology, nucleic acid chemistry, pharmaceutical formulation science, and vaccinology, with a specific focus on the rational design of next-generation TLR9 agonists that are suitable for clinical development and commercial manufacturing.

## BACKGROUND ART

Immunostimulatory CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (ODNs) have been recognized for over a decade as powerful activators of the innate immune system through their interaction with Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9), which is predominantly expressed in endosomal compartments of plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) and B cells in humans. These synthetic DNA molecules contain unmethylated cytosine-phosphate-guanine (CpG) dinucleotide motifs within specific sequence contexts that are recognized by TLR9, triggering downstream signaling cascades that result in the production of a wide array of cytokines, chemokines, and co-stimulatory molecules. Based on their distinct backbone chemistries, sequence architectures, and resulting cytokine induction profiles, immunostimulatory CpG ODNs have been broadly classified into four main types: A/D-type, B/K-type, C-type, and P-type.

A/D-type ODNs are typically characterized by a phosphodiester backbone and a polyguanosine (poly-G) sequence, often at the 3′ terminus. This structural feature enables the formation of higher-order G-quadruplex structures in physiological salt conditions, leading to the spontaneous assembly of nanoparticles or large aggregates. This aggregation is believed to be crucial for their potent ability to stimulate pDCs to produce high levels of type I interferons, particularly IFN-α, while inducing relatively low levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines like interleukin-6 (IL-6). In contrast, B/K-type ODNs possess a fully phosphorothioate-modified backbone, which confers nuclease resistance and enhances cellular uptake through non-specific protein binding. These ODNs are potent activators of B cells and induce strong IL-6 and other inflammatory cytokine responses but are relatively poor inducers of IFN-α. C-type ODNs represent a hybrid class that combines features of both A/D and B/K types, containing a phosphorothioate backbone along with a palindromic CpG-containing sequence capable of forming intramolecular duplexes, thereby stimulating both IFN-α and IL-6 production. The more recently described P-type ODNs also feature a phosphorothioate backbone and contain two palindromic sequences that promote dimeric or aggregated structures, resulting in a cytokine profile similar to C-type ODNs but with enhanced IFN-α production.

The differential cytokine profiles induced by these various ODN classes translate into distinct functional outcomes in vivo, making them suitable for different therapeutic applications. For instance, in vaccine adjuvant studies, B/K-type ODNs like K3 have demonstrated superior efficacy in promoting antibody responses against certain antigens, such as the SE36 malaria vaccine in non-human primates. Conversely, A/D-type ODNs like D35 have shown better protective immunity when used with heat-killed Leishmania vaccines and have exhibited greater therapeutic potential as monotherapies for cutaneous leishmaniasis in primate models. Notably, in the latter context, administration of B/K-type ODNs was found to exacerbate disease pathology, highlighting the critical importance of selecting the appropriate ODN class based on the desired immune outcome.

Despite their promising immunological properties, the clinical translation of A/D-type ODNs has been significantly hampered by their inherent physicochemical instability. The poly-G tails that are essential for their biological activity also drive uncontrolled multimerization and precipitation in saline or other physiological buffers. This spontaneous aggregation leads to several major problems: (1) high batch-to-batch variability in product quality due to inconsistent aggregate formation; (2) difficulties in accurate dosing and administration because of heterogeneous particle sizes and potential clogging of delivery devices; (3) challenges in lyophilization and reconstitution processes required for long-term storage and clinical use; and (4) unpredictable pharmacokinetics and biodistribution in vivo due to variable particle characteristics. These issues have collectively prevented A/D-type ODNs from advancing into human clinical trials, despite their compelling preclinical efficacy data.

Several strategies have been attempted to address these limitations. One notable approach involves the incorporation of thermolytically cleavable protective groups onto the guanosine residues of A/D-type ODNs. These modifications prevent G-quadruplex formation and aggregation during storage and handling in aqueous solutions. Upon administration into the body, the elevated temperature triggers the cleavage of these protective groups, allowing the native poly-G sequence to reform its quadruplex structure and regain its immunostimulatory activity. While this "pro-D" strategy represents a clever chemical solution, its practical feasibility for large-scale clinical manufacturing and regulatory approval remains unproven, and no human trials utilizing this technology have been reported to date.

Another limitation of traditional A/D-type ODNs is their dependence on the poly-G sequence for cellular uptake. It has been widely accepted that the G-quadruplex-mediated aggregation is not only responsible for the unique cytokine profile but is also essential for efficient internalization by immune cells, possibly through scavenger receptor-mediated pathways. This dual dependency creates a fundamental challenge: any modification that disrupts aggregation to improve pharmaceutical properties simultaneously risks abolishing the biological activity. Consequently, there has been a long-standing need in the art for A/D-type ODN variants that can decouple these two functions—maintaining potent IFN-α induction while eliminating problematic aggregation behavior.

The background art thus reveals a clear technological gap: while the immunological benefits of A/D-type ODNs are well-established, their pharmaceutical inadequacies have prevented clinical realization. Existing solutions either remain theoretical or introduce additional complexity without proven clinical viability. There exists an urgent need for novel CpG ODN constructs that preserve the desirable high IFN-α/low IL-6 cytokine signature of A/D-type ODNs while exhibiting improved solubility, stability, and manufacturability in physiological conditions. Such molecules would represent a significant advancement in the field of nucleic acid-based immunotherapeutics and vaccine adjuvants.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Solution to Problem

The present invention provides a novel class of nonaggregating immunostimulatory CpG oligodeoxynucleotides that effectively solve the long-standing problem of spontaneous aggregation associated with conventional A/D-type ODNs while preserving their characteristic high interferon-alpha (IFN-α) induction profile. The core innovation lies in the strategic replacement of the problematic polyguanosine (poly-G) tail, which is responsible for uncontrolled aggregation in physiological salt solutions, with a phosphorothioate-modified polynucleotide tail of defined length and composition. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that appending a phosphorothioate polydeoxyadenylic acid (dAs) tail of sufficient length (preferably between 6 and 100 nucleotides, more preferably around 40 nucleotides) to the 3′ end of a CpG-containing A/D-type ODN core sequence completely prevents aggregate formation in saline and other physiological buffers while maintaining robust IFN-α induction from human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs).

This solution fundamentally challenges the previously held dogma that G-quadruplex formation and subsequent aggregation are absolute requirements for A/D-type ODN activity. The inventors have discovered that the immunostimulatory function of these molecules can be separated into two distinct and independently modifiable components: (1) a CpG motif-containing core sequence responsible for TLR9 recognition and activation, and (2) a cellular uptake facilitator that can be provided either by the traditional poly-G aggregation mechanism or by alternative means such as phosphorothioate polynucleotide tails. By replacing the aggregation-dependent uptake mechanism with a phosphorothioate-mediated uptake pathway, the invention achieves stable, nonaggregating formulations without sacrificing biological potency.

The invention encompasses both full-length modified A/D-type ODNs (such as D35-dAs40, which retains the original D35 sequence including its G-hexamer but adds a dAs40 tail) and minimal core constructs (such as D35core-dAs40, which contains only the essential 12-nucleotide CpG-containing palindrome from D35 plus the dAs40 tail). Both formats demonstrate excellent solubility in saline, can be directly reconstituted from lyophilized vials without special handling requirements, and maintain dose-proportional cytokine induction profiles characteristic of true D-type ODNs rather than the bell-shaped curves observed with C-type or P-type ODNs.

Furthermore, the invention extends beyond simple tail modifications to include optimization of tail parameters including length (with an optimal range identified between 40-60 deoxyadenosine residues), degree of phosphorothioate modification (requiring at least 50% phosphorothioate linkages for effective IFN-α induction), and nucleotide composition (with poly-A tails demonstrating superior performance compared to poly-T, poly-C, or random sequence tails, and significantly outperforming G-rich tails that might reintroduce aggregation tendencies). The invention also demonstrates that this tailing strategy is broadly applicable across different A/D-type ODN scaffolds, successfully improving the solubility of other problematic sequences like A2216 and A2336 without compromising their immunostimulatory activity.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The present invention provides numerous significant advantages over existing immunostimulatory CpG ODN technologies, particularly in addressing the critical pharmaceutical limitations that have hindered the clinical development of A/D-type ODNs. The primary advantageous effect is the elimination of spontaneous aggregation in physiological salt solutions while maintaining the desirable high IFN-α induction profile characteristic of A/D-type ODNs. This breakthrough enables straightforward pharmaceutical processing, including reliable lyophilization, stable long-term storage, and simple reconstitution with standard saline solutions immediately prior to administration—features that are essential for clinical and commercial viability but were previously unattainable with conventional A/D-type ODNs.

A second major advantage is the improved batch-to-batch consistency and product homogeneity achieved through the elimination of uncontrolled aggregation processes. Traditional A/D-type ODNs exhibit significant variability in particle size distribution, aggregation state, and biological activity between manufacturing batches due to the stochastic nature of G-quadruplex formation. The nonaggregating ODNs of the present invention demonstrate uniform physical characteristics with hydrodynamic radii consistently below 20 nanometers in physiological buffers, as confirmed by dynamic light scattering analysis, ensuring predictable dosing and reproducible biological responses.

Third, the invention provides enhanced versatility in molecular design by demonstrating that the poly-G sequence traditionally considered essential for A/D-type ODN activity is actually dispensable when alternative cellular uptake mechanisms are provided. This insight enables the creation of minimal, optimized ODN constructs containing only the essential CpG-containing core sequence plus the phosphorothioate polynucleotide tail, reducing manufacturing complexity and cost while maintaining full biological activity. The ability to remove the G-hexamer also eliminates potential off-target effects or unwanted interactions that might be mediated by G-quadruplex structures.

Fourth, the modified ODNs of the present invention demonstrate superior in vivo adjuvant activity compared to both traditional A/D-type ODNs and B/K-type ODNs in non-human primate vaccination models. Specifically, when used as adjuvants for influenza split vaccine in cynomolgus monkeys, both D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 induced significantly stronger and more consistent antigen-specific antibody responses than either the original aggregating D35 or the B-cell-activating K3 ODN. This unexpected enhancement in adjuvant potency, despite slightly reduced IFN-α and increased IL-6 production in vitro compared to original D35, suggests that the improved pharmaceutical properties and potentially altered biodistribution of the nonaggregating formulations contribute to superior in vivo performance.

Fifth, the invention provides a platform technology that can be readily applied to other problematic A/D-type ODN sequences. The successful modification of A2216 and A2336 with dAs40 tails demonstrates the general applicability of this approach, enabling the rescue of otherwise pharmaceutically challenging immunostimulatory sequences for clinical development. This broad utility significantly expands the available toolkit of TLR9 agonists for various therapeutic applications.

Sixth, the modified ODNs maintain the dose-proportional cytokine induction profile characteristic of true D-type ODNs, distinguishing them from C-type and P-type ODNs that exhibit bell-shaped dose-response curves with reduced activity at higher concentrations. This linear dose-response relationship simplifies dosing regimens and provides greater predictability in clinical applications.

Finally, the invention enables new formulation possibilities that were previously impossible with aggregating ODNs. The stable, nonaggregating nature of these molecules allows for combination with other excipients, complexation with delivery vehicles, and incorporation into advanced drug delivery systems without concerns about precipitation or loss of activity. This opens new avenues for optimizing delivery to specific immune cell populations or anatomical sites, potentially further enhancing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing systemic side effects.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

The present invention provides novel nonaggregating immunostimulatory CpG oligodeoxynucleotides that overcome the significant pharmaceutical limitations of traditional A/D-type ODNs while preserving their characteristic high interferon-alpha (IFN-α) induction profile. The core innovation involves the strategic replacement of the problematic polyguanosine (poly-G) tail responsible for uncontrolled aggregation with a phosphorothioate-modified polynucleotide tail of defined length and composition. This modification fundamentally decouples the cellular uptake function from the TLR9 activation function, enabling stable formulations without sacrificing biological potency.

In one embodiment, the invention provides an immunostimulatory oligodeoxynucleotide comprising a CpG motif-containing core sequence derived from an A/D-type CpG ODN and a phosphorothioate polynucleotide tail attached to the 3′ end of the core sequence. The CpG motif-containing core sequence may be the complete sequence of a known A/D-type ODN such as D35, A2216, or A2336, or it may be a minimal core sequence containing only the essential CpG-containing palindrome necessary for TLR9 recognition and activation. For example, the D35 core sequence comprises the 12-nucleotide palindrome 5′-GGTGCATCGATGCACC-3′ (SEQ ID NO:1), which contains the essential CpG motif and flanking sequences required for optimal activity.

The phosphorothioate polynucleotide tail is a critical component of the invention and must meet specific structural requirements to achieve the desired nonaggregating properties while maintaining immunostimulatory activity. The tail consists of a homopolymer or heteropolymer of deoxynucleotides wherein at least a portion of the internucleotide linkages are phosphorothioate modifications. The length of the tail is preferably between 6 and 100 nucleotides, more preferably between 20 and 80 nucleotides, and most preferably around 40 nucleotides. Experimental data demonstrate that tails shorter than 6 nucleotides fail to provide sufficient cellular uptake enhancement, while tails longer than 100 nucleotides may actually reduce IFN-α induction despite maintaining IL-6 production.

The degree of phosphorothioate modification in the tail is another critical parameter. The tail must contain at least 50% phosphorothioate linkages to achieve effective IFN-α induction, with optimal activity observed when 100% of the linkages are phosphorothioate-modified. Reduction of phosphorothioate content below 50% dramatically diminishes IFN-α production, while IL-6 induction is somewhat more tolerant but still significantly reduced with less than 25% phosphorothioate modification. This requirement highlights the importance of the phosphorothioate backbone in facilitating cellular uptake through non-specific protein binding mechanisms, which compensates for the absence of aggregation-mediated uptake.

The nucleotide composition of the tail also significantly influences biological activity. Homopolymeric tails composed of deoxyadenosine (dA) residues demonstrate superior performance compared to deoxythymidine (dT) or deoxycytidine (dC) tails, with deoxyguanosine (dG) tails being particularly problematic due to their tendency to form secondary structures that may reintroduce aggregation behavior. Random sequence tails perform poorly compared to homopolymeric tails, and even G-rich random sequences fail to match the activity of pure poly-A tails. This preference for poly-A tails may be attributed to their lack of secondary structure formation and optimal interaction with cellular uptake machinery.

In a preferred embodiment, the invention provides D35-dAs40, which comprises the complete D35 sequence (5′-GGTGCATCGATGCACCGGGGGG-3′; SEQ ID NO:2) with a 40-nucleotide phosphorothioate polydeoxyadenylic acid tail attached to the 3′ end. This construct retains the original G-hexamer of D35 but completely prevents aggregation through the dominant effect of the dAs40 tail. D35-dAs40 demonstrates excellent solubility in saline, can be directly reconstituted from lyophilized vials, and maintains robust IFN-α induction comparable to original D35 while showing improved in vivo adjuvant activity in primate vaccination models.

In another preferred embodiment, the invention provides D35core-dAs40, which comprises only the essential 12-nucleotide CpG-containing core of D35 (5′-GGTGCATCGATGCACC-3′; SEQ ID NO:1) with a 40-nucleotide phosphorothioate polydeoxyadenylic acid tail attached to the 3′ end. This minimal construct completely lacks the G-hexamer sequence yet demonstrates equivalent immunostimulatory activity to D35-dAs40, definitively proving that the poly-G sequence is not essential for A/D-type ODN activity when alternative uptake mechanisms are provided. D35core-dAs40 exhibits all the pharmaceutical advantages of D35-dAs40 while offering reduced manufacturing complexity and cost.

The invention also encompasses the application of this tailing strategy to other A/D-type ODN sequences. For example, A2216-dAs40 and A2336-dAs40 demonstrate improved solubility in saline compared to their unmodified counterparts while maintaining their IFN-α induction capabilities. This broad applicability establishes the phosphorothioate polynucleotide tailing approach as a general solution for rescuing pharmaceutically challenging A/D-type ODNs.

The modified ODNs of the present invention can be formulated in various pharmaceutical compositions suitable for different routes of administration. For vaccine adjuvant applications, the ODNs are typically formulated in sterile saline or phosphate-buffered saline at concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 10 mg/mL. The formulations are stable at refrigerated temperatures (2-8°C) for extended periods without precipitation or loss of activity. Lyophilized formulations are also provided, which can be directly reconstituted with sterile saline immediately prior to use without special handling requirements.

The invention further provides methods for using these nonaggregating ODNs as vaccine adjuvants or as monotherapeutic immunomodulatory agents. In vaccine applications, the ODNs are co-administered with antigens to enhance both humoral and cellular immune responses. The superior adjuvant activity demonstrated in primate models suggests particular utility for vaccines against viral infections, intracellular pathogens, and cancer neoantigens where robust type I interferon responses are beneficial. As monotherapeutic agents, the ODNs can be used to stimulate antiviral or antitumor immunity through systemic or local administration.

The cellular mechanism underlying the activity of these modified ODNs involves TLR9-dependent signaling in plasmacytoid dendritic cells, leading to the production of type I interferons and other cytokines. The phosphorothioate tail facilitates cellular uptake through mechanisms distinct from the aggregation-dependent pathways used by traditional A/D-type ODNs, possibly involving DEC-205 or other receptors that recognize phosphorothioate-modified nucleic acids. Once internalized, the ODNs traffic to endosomal compartments where they engage TLR9 and initiate signaling cascades that result in the characteristic cytokine profile.

Quality control of these modified ODNs can be performed using standard analytical techniques including dynamic light scattering (DLS) to confirm the absence of aggregates (hydrodynamic radius <20 nm in PBS), high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) to assess purity, and mass spectrometry to confirm molecular weight. Biological activity can be validated using in vitro cytokine induction assays with human PBMCs, measuring both IFN-α and IL-6 production across a range of concentrations to confirm the dose-proportional response characteristic of D-type ODNs.

### EXAMPLES

Example 1: Synthesis and Characterization of D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40

D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 were synthesized using standard solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry with appropriate modifications for phosphorothioate linkages. D35-dAs40 was constructed by coupling the D35 sequence (5′-GGTGCATCGATGCACCGGGGGG-3′; SEQ ID NO:2) to a 40-nucleotide phosphorothioate polydeoxyadenylic acid tail at the 3′ end. D35core-dAs40 was synthesized by coupling the minimal D35 core sequence (5′-GGTGCATCGATGCACC-3′; SEQ ID NO:1) to the same 40-nucleotide phosphorothioate polydeoxyadenylic acid tail. Both compounds were purified by reverse-phase HPLC and characterized by mass spectrometry to confirm identity and purity (>95%).

Physical characterization by dynamic light scattering (DLS) demonstrated that both D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 exhibited hydrodynamic radii consistently below 20 nm when dissolved in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at 1 mg/mL concentration, with sharp, monodisperse peaks indicating homogeneous molecular species. In contrast, unmodified D35 under identical conditions showed broad, heterogeneous size distributions ranging from 50 nm to over 1 μm, with visible turbidity indicating extensive aggregation. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) confirmed these findings, revealing no discernible structures for the modified ODNs while showing globular particles of 50-200 nm for unmodified D35.

Example 2: In Vitro Cytokine Induction Profile

Human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from healthy adult volunteers and stimulated with varying concentrations of D35, D35-dAs40, D35core-dAs40, and control ODNs. After 24 hours of incubation, supernatants were analyzed for IFN-α and IL-6 production by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Both D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 induced robust IFN-α production in a dose-proportional manner, with EC50 values comparable to unmodified D35. While the maximum IFN-α levels were slightly reduced compared to D35 (approximately 70-80% of D35 activity), the IL-6 induction was correspondingly increased, resulting in a cytokine balance that remained distinctly within the A/D-type profile rather than shifting toward B/K-type characteristics.

Importantly, both modified ODNs maintained linear dose-response relationships across the tested concentration range (0.01-10 μM), unlike C-type or P-type ODNs which exhibit bell-shaped curves with reduced activity at higher concentrations. Control experiments with ODNs lacking the CpG motif confirmed that immunostimulatory activity was completely dependent on the presence of the unmethylated CpG dinucleotide, while replacement of the phosphorothioate tail with a phosphodiester tail abolished activity, demonstrating the critical role of the phosphorothioate modification in facilitating cellular uptake.

Example 3: Solubility and Formulation Stability

Lyophilized D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 were prepared by freeze-drying from aqueous solutions containing appropriate bulking agents. Ten-milligram quantities were stored in sterile glass vials under nitrogen atmosphere at -20°C. For reconstitution testing, 1 mL of sterile 0.9% saline was directly injected into the vials at room temperature. Both compounds dissolved completely within 5 minutes with gentle agitation, yielding clear, colorless solutions without visible particulates or turbidity.

The reconstituted solutions were stored at 4°C for up to one month and monitored weekly for precipitation or aggregation by visual inspection and DLS analysis. No evidence of aggregation or precipitation was observed throughout the study period, confirming the exceptional stability of these formulations in physiological conditions. In contrast, attempts to reconstitute unmodified D35 under identical conditions resulted in immediate formation of gelatinous aggregates that failed to dissolve completely even after extended mixing.

Example 4: In Vivo Adjuvant Activity in Cynomolgus Monkeys

Cynomolgus monkeys (n=2-3 per group) were immunized subcutaneously with influenza split vaccine (5 μg hemagglutinin) alone or in combination with 30 nmol of various CpG ODNs, including K3 (B/K-type), D35 (A/D-type), D35-dAs40, D35core-dAs40, or D35-SPG (a schizophyllan complex of D35-dAs40). Immunizations were administered on days 0 and 14, and serum samples were collected at 4 and 8 weeks post-primary immunization for analysis of influenza-specific IgG antibodies by ELISA.

Both D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 demonstrated significantly enhanced adjuvant activity compared to both K3 and unmodified D35, with higher geometric mean titers and reduced inter-animal variability. D35-SPG also showed improved adjuvant activity compared to unmodified D35, despite exhibiting reduced cytokine induction in vitro, suggesting that factors beyond simple cytokine profiles—such as biodistribution and persistence at the injection site—contribute to in vivo efficacy. These results established the superior practical utility of the nonaggregating ODNs for vaccine applications.

Example 5: Structure-Activity Relationship Studies

Systematic evaluation of tail parameters was conducted to optimize the design of nonaggregating A/D-type ODNs. D35core constructs with dAs tails of varying lengths (6, 10, 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100 nucleotides) were tested for cytokine induction in human PBMCs. IFN-α production increased with tail length up to 60 nucleotides, plateaued between 60-80 nucleotides, and declined slightly at 100 nucleotides. IL-6 production continued to increase up to 100 nucleotides, suggesting differential optimal lengths for different cytokine responses.

Phosphorothioate content was varied from 100% to 17.5% in 40-nucleotide tails, revealing a sharp threshold effect: tails with less than 50% phosphorothioate modification failed to induce significant IFN-α production, while IL-6 induction was maintained down to 25% modification. Nucleotide composition studies comparing dAs40, dTs40, dCs40, and random dNs40 tails demonstrated superior activity for dAs40, with dCs40 showing intermediate activity and dTs40 and random tails performing poorly. These studies established the critical design parameters for optimal nonaggregating A/D-type ODNs.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The nonaggregating immunostimulatory CpG oligodeoxynucleotides of the present invention possess exceptional industrial applicability across multiple therapeutic and prophylactic domains, primarily due to their resolution of the critical pharmaceutical limitations that have historically prevented the clinical development of A/D-type ODNs. The most immediate and significant application is as vaccine adjuvants for both prophylactic and therapeutic vaccines, where the ability to induce robust type I interferon responses while maintaining excellent pharmaceutical properties represents a major advancement over existing adjuvant technologies.

In the vaccine field, these modified ODNs are particularly well-suited for vaccines targeting viral infections (such as influenza, HIV, hepatitis B and C, and emerging viral threats), intracellular bacterial pathogens (including Mycobacterium tuberculosis and various biodefense-relevant organisms), parasitic diseases (notably malaria and leishmaniasis), and cancer immunotherapies (both preventive vaccines against oncogenic viruses and therapeutic vaccines targeting tumor-associated antigens). The demonstrated superior adjuvant activity in non-human primate models, combined with straightforward formulation requirements and stability in physiological conditions, positions these compounds as ideal candidates for clinical development and eventual commercial manufacturing.

The pharmaceutical industry can readily implement the manufacturing processes for these modified ODNs using established solid-phase synthesis technologies already employed for other therapeutic oligonucleotides. The defined chemical structures, absence of complex aggregation states, and compatibility with standard lyophilization and reconstitution procedures significantly reduce the regulatory burden and manufacturing complexity compared to traditional A/D-type ODNs. Quality control can be performed using routine analytical methods including HPLC, mass spectrometry, and dynamic light scattering, ensuring consistent product quality across manufacturing batches.

Beyond vaccine applications, these nonaggregating ODNs have substantial potential as monotherapeutic immunomodulatory agents for the treatment of various diseases where stimulation of innate immunity is beneficial. In oncology, they could be used as standalone therapies or in combination with other immunotherapies (such as checkpoint inhibitors) to enhance antitumor immune responses through activation of plasmacytoid dendritic cells and subsequent cross-priming of cytotoxic T lymphocytes. In infectious disease settings, they could serve as broad-spectrum antiviral or antimicrobial agents by boosting host defense mechanisms. Additionally, their ability to modulate immune responses makes them candidates for treating allergic disorders or as components of combination therapies for autoimmune diseases, although careful dosing would be required in the latter context to avoid exacerbating inflammation.

The platform nature of this technology also enables the development of specialized formulations for different routes of administration and target tissues. For instance, the stable, nonaggregating properties facilitate incorporation into nanoparticle delivery systems, microneedle patches for transdermal delivery, or inhalation formulations for respiratory infections. The ability to precisely control molecular weight and surface properties through tail design opens opportunities for engineering biodistribution profiles optimized for specific therapeutic applications.

From a commercial perspective, the invention addresses a clear unmet need in the immunostimulatory oligonucleotide market, where the superior immunological profile of A/D-type ODNs has remained inaccessible due to pharmaceutical challenges. By providing a straightforward, manufacturable solution that maintains the desired biological activity while eliminating problematic aggregation, the invention creates significant value for pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies seeking to develop next-generation TLR9 agonists. The broad patent protection possible around the specific structural features (phosphorothioate polynucleotide tails of defined length and composition attached to CpG-containing cores) provides strong intellectual property coverage for commercial exploitation.

Furthermore, the demonstrated applicability across multiple A/D-type ODN scaffolds (D35, A2216, A2336) suggests that this approach can be used to rescue other promising but pharmaceutically challenging immunostimulatory sequences, expanding the available toolkit for immune modulation. This versatility enhances the commercial value by enabling the development of a portfolio of products tailored to different therapeutic indications rather than being limited to a single lead compound.

In summary, the industrial applicability of the present invention spans pharmaceutical manufacturing, vaccine development, immunotherapy, and drug delivery technologies, with clear pathways to commercialization and significant advantages over existing technologies in terms of manufacturability, stability, and clinical utility. The resolution of the long-standing aggregation problem while maintaining desirable immunological properties represents a transformative advancement in the field of nucleic acid-based immunomodulators.
