# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of regenerative medicine and cellular reprogramming, and more specifically to methods and compositions for the direct conversion of somatic cells into induced dopaminergic progenitor cells (iDPs) with midbrain identity. The invention provides a defined set of transcription factors—Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2—that, when ectopically expressed in somatic cells such as dermal fibroblasts, efficiently reprogram these cells into neural progenitors committed exclusively to the dopaminergic neuronal lineage. These iDPs exhibit molecular, phenotypic, and functional characteristics of authentic midbrain dopaminergic progenitors, including expression of key developmental markers such as Corin (Lrp4), Aldh1A1, Lmx1a, Otx2, Ngn2, and Pitx3, while lacking expression of forebrain or glial lineage determinants. Critically, the iDPs generated by this method demonstrate restricted differentiation potential, yielding mature, electrophysiologically functional dopaminergic neurons upon terminal differentiation without requiring exogenous morphogen patterning cues such as Sonic Hedgehog (SHH) or Fibroblast Growth Factor 8 (FGF8). Furthermore, the invention encompasses engineered iDPs that incorporate a conditional self-renewal system based on doxycycline-inducible L-Myc expression, enabling scalable expansion while preserving lineage fidelity and safety. The resulting iDPs are suitable for therapeutic applications, particularly in the treatment of Parkinson’s disease (PD), as demonstrated by their ability to survive transplantation, differentiate into dopaminergic neurons in vivo, integrate into host circuitry, restore striatal dopaminergic innervation, and partially ameliorate motor deficits in a murine MPTP model of PD without evidence of tumorigenesis. The invention thus provides a robust, efficient, and clinically relevant platform for generating patient-specific dopaminergic progenitors for cell replacement therapy, disease modeling, and drug screening.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized primarily by the selective loss of dopaminergic (DA) neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta of the midbrain, leading to debilitating motor symptoms such as bradykinesia, rigidity, resting tremor, and postural instability. Current pharmacological treatments, including levodopa administration, provide symptomatic relief but fail to halt disease progression and often lead to severe side effects with long-term use. Consequently, cell replacement therapy aimed at restoring the lost dopaminergic innervation in the striatum has emerged as a promising curative strategy. Early clinical trials involving the transplantation of fetal ventral mesencephalic tissue demonstrated proof-of-concept that grafted DA neurons can survive, integrate, and improve motor function in PD patients; however, these approaches were limited by ethical concerns, tissue scarcity, batch-to-batch variability, and inconsistent clinical outcomes.

To overcome these limitations, significant efforts have focused on deriving dopaminergic neurons from pluripotent stem cells, including both human embryonic stem cells (ESCs) and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). While protocols have been developed to differentiate ESCs and iPSCs into midbrain DA neurons using sequential exposure to morphogens such as SHH and FGF8, these methods remain complex, time-consuming, and often yield heterogeneous cell populations containing non-dopaminergic neurons, glial cells, and residual undifferentiated pluripotent cells that pose a risk of teratoma formation. Moreover, the efficiency of generating authentic, functionally mature midbrain DA neurons remains suboptimal, and the requirement for prolonged in vitro differentiation compromises scalability and increases the risk of genomic instability.

An alternative approach involves the direct reprogramming of somatic cells into induced neural progenitor cells (iNPCs) or induced neurons (iNs), bypassing the pluripotent state entirely. This strategy offers several advantages, including reduced tumorigenic risk, faster generation timelines, and the potential to retain age- and disease-related epigenetic signatures useful for modeling late-onset disorders like PD. Previous studies have successfully converted fibroblasts into iNPCs using combinations of transcription factors such as Brn2, Sox2, TLX, Bmi1, and c-Myc. However, these generic iNPCs typically lack regional identity and exhibit broad differentiation potential across multiple neural lineages, necessitating additional patterning steps to direct them toward a dopaminergic fate. Even then, the efficiency of dopaminergic differentiation from such iNPCs remains low—often less than 5%—limiting their utility for therapeutic applications.

Key developmental biology insights have identified Foxa2 and Lmx1a as master regulators of midbrain DA neuron specification. Foxa2, a forkhead box transcription factor, is expressed early in the ventral midline of the neural tube and is essential for the induction of the floor plate, the developmental origin of midbrain DA neurons. It acts downstream of SHH signaling but also exerts SHH-independent functions in establishing midbrain identity. Lmx1a, a LIM-homeodomain protein, is induced by Wnt1 signaling and cooperates with Foxa2 to activate a cascade of genes required for DA progenitor proliferation, specification, and terminal differentiation, including Msx1, Ngn2, Pitx3, and tyrosine hydroxylase (TH). Despite their pivotal roles in development, the capacity of Foxa2 and Lmx1a to function as reprogramming factors in direct lineage conversion has remained largely unexplored.

Initial attempts to enhance the dopaminergic potential of existing iNPCs by overexpressing Foxa2 and Lmx1a showed promise, significantly increasing TH+ neuron yields. However, these modified iNPCs still retained multipotency and required external morphogen cues for efficient DA differentiation. More critically, no method had yet been established to directly convert somatic cells into lineage-restricted dopaminergic progenitors that intrinsically possess midbrain identity and are pre-specified toward the DA fate without reliance on exogenous patterning signals. Additionally, a major bottleneck in translating reprogrammed cells to the clinic is their limited proliferative capacity; most directly converted progenitors exhibit poor clonogenicity and self-renewal, hindering the production of sufficient cell numbers for transplantation. Although oncogenes like c-Myc have been used to enhance proliferation, their constitutive expression raises safety concerns due to potential genomic instability and tumorigenicity.

Thus, there exists a critical unmet need for a safe, efficient, and scalable method to generate bona fide dopaminergic progenitors directly from accessible somatic cells—such as skin fibroblasts—that are intrinsically specified to the midbrain DA lineage, capable of robust expansion under controlled conditions, and functionally validated both in vitro and in vivo for therapeutic efficacy in PD models. The present invention addresses these challenges by identifying a minimal triad of transcription factors—Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2—that collectively reprogram fibroblasts into induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) with unprecedented lineage restriction, regional fidelity, and functional maturity, thereby providing a transformative platform for PD cell therapy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and efficient method for the direct reprogramming of somatic cells into induced dopaminergic progenitor cells (iDPs) that are intrinsically specified to the midbrain dopaminergic lineage. In one aspect, the invention discloses that the ectopic co-expression of three transcription factors—Brn2 (POU3F2), Sox2, and Foxa2—in somatic cells, such as dermal fibroblasts, is sufficient to convert these cells into stable, expandable neural progenitor cells exhibiting molecular, phenotypic, and functional hallmarks of authentic midbrain dopaminergic progenitors. These iDPs express a comprehensive panel of midbrain DA progenitor markers, including but not limited to Corin (Lrp4), Aldh1A1, Lmx1a, Otx2, Ngn2, Msx1, Mash1, Pitx3, and Nkx6.1, while simultaneously suppressing expression of forebrain identity genes (e.g., FoxG1, GSX2, Nkx2.1) and glial lineage determinants (e.g., Glast, Olig1/2, GFAP, S100β).

In another aspect, the invention demonstrates that the iDPs generated by this method are lineage-restricted, possessing exclusive commitment to the dopaminergic neuronal fate. Upon terminal differentiation, iDPs yield greater than 90% tyrosine hydroxylase-positive (TH+) neurons that co-express mature dopaminergic markers such as aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC), dopamine transporter (DAT), and vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2). Remarkably, this high-efficiency dopaminergic differentiation occurs independently of exogenous morphogen patterning cues such as Sonic Hedgehog (SHH) and Fibroblast Growth Factor 8 (FGF8), underscoring the intrinsic midbrain DA specification conferred by the reprogramming factors.

In a further aspect, the invention provides iDPs that exhibit functional electrophysiological properties characteristic of mature neurons. Specifically, iDP-derived dopaminergic neurons display voltage-gated sodium and potassium currents and are capable of generating repetitive action potentials in response to depolarizing current injections, confirming their functional maturity in vitro.

In yet another aspect, the invention includes engineered iDPs incorporating a conditional self-renewal system. By introducing a doxycycline (Dox)-inducible L-Myc expression cassette into the iDPs, the invention enables controlled expansion of the progenitor population without compromising lineage identity or safety. Withdrawal of Dox halts proliferation while maintaining dopaminergic progenitor marker expression, allowing for scalable production of therapeutic cell doses.

In a critical therapeutic aspect, the invention demonstrates that iDPs are safe and efficacious for cell transplantation in a murine model of Parkinson’s disease. Following intrastriatal grafting into MPTP-lesioned mice, iDPs survive for at least six weeks, differentiate predominantly into TH+ dopaminergic neurons with minimal astrocytic conversion, restore striatal dopaminergic innervation, and significantly improve motor function as assessed by rotarod performance, all without evidence of tumor formation.

Accordingly, the invention encompasses: (1) a method of producing iDPs by introducing nucleic acids encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 into somatic cells; (2) the iDPs themselves, characterized by their midbrain DA progenitor identity and lineage restriction; (3) pharmaceutical compositions comprising such iDPs; (4) methods of treating Parkinson’s disease by administering said iDPs to a subject in need thereof; and (5) kits and vectors facilitating the practice of the disclosed methods. The invention thus represents a significant advancement in regenerative neurology, offering a clinically viable source of patient-specific dopaminergic progenitors for cell replacement therapy in Parkinson’s disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

As used herein, the term “induced dopaminergic progenitor cell” or “iDP” refers to a somatic cell that has been directly reprogrammed through the ectopic expression of defined transcription factors—specifically Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2—into a neural progenitor cell that exhibits molecular, phenotypic, and functional characteristics of endogenous midbrain dopaminergic progenitors. iDPs are distinguished by their expression of a specific combination of midbrain DA progenitor markers, including Corin (Lrp4), Aldh1A1, Lmx1a, Otx2, Ngn2, Msx1, Mash1, Pitx3, and Nkx6.1, and by their lineage-restricted differentiation potential, yielding almost exclusively dopaminergic neurons upon terminal differentiation without requiring exogenous morphogen patterning.

The term “somatic cell” denotes any differentiated cell of the body that is not a germ cell or a pluripotent stem cell. In the context of the present invention, somatic cells include but are not limited to dermal fibroblasts, which are readily accessible through minimally invasive skin biopsy and have been demonstrated as a suitable starting material for reprogramming into iDPs. Other somatic cell types, such as blood cells, hepatocytes, or adipocytes, may also be amenable to conversion using the disclosed method, provided they can support the expression and function of the reprogramming factors.

“Ectopic expression” refers to the artificial introduction and expression of a gene or set of genes in a cell type where such genes are not normally expressed or are expressed at significantly lower levels. In the present invention, ectopic expression is achieved by delivering nucleic acid constructs encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 into somatic cells using viral vectors, such as retroviruses or lentiviruses, or non-viral methods including plasmid transfection, mRNA transfection, or protein transduction.

“Midbrain identity” signifies a regional specification within the central nervous system corresponding to the mesencephalon, the embryonic precursor of structures including the substantia nigra and ventral tegmental area, which harbor dopaminergic neurons. Cells with midbrain identity express a characteristic set of transcription factors and surface markers that distinguish them from progenitors of other brain regions, such as the forebrain (marked by FoxG1, GSX2) or hindbrain.

“Lineage-restricted” describes a progenitor cell that has lost multipotency and is committed to differentiating into only one specific cell type or a narrow subset of related cell types. In the case of iDPs, lineage restriction means an exclusive commitment to the dopaminergic neuronal fate, with negligible potential to generate astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, or non-dopaminergic neurons.

“Conditional self-renewal” refers to a controlled proliferative capacity that can be turned on or off through an external stimulus, such as the addition or withdrawal of a small molecule. In the present invention, this is exemplified by the use of a doxycycline-inducible L-Myc system, wherein L-Myc expression—and thus cell proliferation—is activated in the presence of doxycycline and silenced upon its removal, enabling safe expansion of iDPs for therapeutic-scale production.

### Somatic Cells

The somatic cells employed as the starting material in the present invention are non-pluripotent, differentiated cells derived from a mammalian organism, preferably a human. While the research described herein utilized mouse dermal fibroblasts isolated from Nestin-EGFP transgenic mice, the principles of the invention are directly applicable to human somatic cells, particularly human dermal fibroblasts obtained via routine skin biopsy. Fibroblasts are an ideal source due to their accessibility, ease of culture, stability in vitro, and well-established protocols for genetic manipulation. They represent a terminally differentiated mesenchymal cell type that normally contributes to extracellular matrix synthesis and wound healing but lacks any inherent neural potential, making their successful conversion into neural progenitors a striking demonstration of the reprogramming efficacy.

Other somatic cell types may also serve as substrates for iDP generation. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), for instance, offer a completely non-invasive source and have been used in other reprogramming paradigms. Keratinocytes from epidermal tissue, adipose-derived stromal cells, or even urine-derived renal epithelial cells could potentially be converted using the disclosed factor combination, provided that the delivery method ensures efficient transduction and that the cellular context permits activation of the neural reprogramming network. The choice of somatic cell may influence reprogramming efficiency, kinetics, or epigenetic memory, but the core functionality of the Brn2/Sox2/Foxa2 triad is expected to remain effective across diverse somatic lineages due to the fundamental nature of the transcriptional rewiring it induces.

Importantly, the use of autologous somatic cells—those derived from the intended recipient—minimizes the risk of immune rejection following transplantation, a critical advantage for clinical translation. Furthermore, somatic cells from patients with familial or sporadic forms of Parkinson’s disease can be reprogrammed into iDPs, creating personalized disease models that retain the patient’s unique genetic background, including disease-associated mutations and epigenetic modifications. Such models are invaluable for studying disease mechanisms, screening neuroprotective compounds, and developing tailored therapeutic strategies.

### Reprogramming (Transdifferentiation)

Reprogramming, or transdifferentiation, as practiced in the present invention, involves the direct conversion of one differentiated somatic cell type into another without passing through a pluripotent intermediate state. This process is mediated by the forced expression of lineage-specific transcription factors that override the existing gene regulatory network of the starting cell and establish a new transcriptional program characteristic of the target cell fate. Unlike induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) technology, which requires the reactivation of a broad pluripotency network followed by directed differentiation, direct reprogramming offers a more streamlined and potentially safer route to therapeutically relevant cell types by avoiding the risks associated with residual pluripotency and prolonged in vitro culture.

The reprogramming protocol disclosed herein utilizes a combination of three transcription factors: Brn2 (also known as POU3F2), Sox2, and Foxa2. Brn2 is a POU-domain transcription factor implicated in neural fate acquisition and cortical development. Sox2 is a high-mobility group (HMG) box transcription factor essential for maintaining neural stem cell identity and pluripotency. Foxa2 is a forkhead box transcription factor that plays a master regulatory role in midbrain floor plate specification and dopaminergic neuron development. The synergistic action of these three factors initiates a cascade of epigenetic and transcriptional changes that silence fibroblast-specific genes, activate neural progenitor programs, and impose midbrain dopaminergic identity.

Reprogramming is initiated by introducing nucleic acid constructs encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 into the somatic cells. In the exemplary method, retroviral vectors are used due to their high transduction efficiency and stable genomic integration, which ensures sustained expression of the reprogramming factors during the critical early phase of conversion. Alternative delivery systems, such as Sendai virus, episomal plasmids, or synthetic mRNA, may be employed to achieve transient expression, thereby reducing the risk of insertional mutagenesis and enabling clinical-grade manufacturing under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) conditions.

Following transduction, cells are cultured in neural progenitor maintenance medium supplemented with mitogens such as basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF) and epidermal growth factor (EGF). Within seven days, early signs of neural conversion appear, marked by the activation of endogenous neural genes and, in the case of reporter lines like Nestin-EGFP, fluorescence indicative of neural progenitor identity. By day 14, discrete colonies of reprogrammed cells emerge, which can be manually picked, expanded, and characterized. The resulting iDPs exhibit a morphology typical of neural progenitors—small cell bodies with scant cytoplasm and the ability to form dense, phase-bright colonies—and maintain stable expression of the reprogramming factors as well as endogenous neural and midbrain markers over multiple passages.

### iDP Production

The production of induced dopaminergic progenitor cells (iDPs) according to the present invention follows a defined and reproducible protocol that begins with the isolation and culture of somatic cells, proceeds through genetic reprogramming, and culminates in the establishment of a stable iDP line. Initially, somatic cells such as dermal fibroblasts are harvested and expanded in standard culture conditions—for example, Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS)—to obtain sufficient cell numbers while avoiding replicative senescence, typically by limiting culture to passages 2–5.

Concurrently, retroviral vectors encoding human or murine Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 are prepared. The coding sequences for Brn2 and Foxa2 are cloned into pMXs-retroviral backbones, while Sox2 may be sourced from established repositories such as Addgene. These plasmids are then transfected into Plat-E packaging cells, a highly efficient retroviral producer line, using lipid-based transfection reagents. After 48–72 hours, the virus-containing supernatant is harvested, filtered, and supplemented with polybrene to enhance transduction efficiency.

Target somatic cells are seeded at an optimal density and exposed to the mixed viral supernatants overnight. Following infection, the medium is replaced with neural progenitor expansion medium, such as NeuroCult® NSC Basal Medium supplemented with proliferation factors (bFGF and EGF). Over the next 9–14 days, reprogrammed cells begin to form distinct colonies that can be monitored by phase-contrast microscopy or, if using reporter lines, by fluorescence. These colonies are manually isolated, dissociated, and replated under the same conditions to establish clonal iDP lines.

Characterization confirms that the resulting iDPs express high levels of neural progenitor markers (Sox1, PAX6, Nestin, CD133) and midbrain DA progenitor markers (Corin, Aldh1A1, Lmx1a, Otx2, etc.), while lacking forebrain and glial markers. Critically, iDPs demonstrate self-renewal capacity and can be passaged multiple times while retaining their identity. For therapeutic applications requiring large cell numbers, iDPs can be further engineered to include a doxycycline-inducible L-Myc system, enabling rapid expansion upon Dox addition and subsequent silencing of L-Myc for transplantation-ready quiescent progenitors.

### Human iDP Upon Ectopic Expression of Brn2, Sox2 and Foxa2

Although the foundational experiments described in the research paper were conducted in murine cells, the present invention explicitly extends to the generation of human induced dopaminergic progenitor cells (human iDPs) through the ectopic expression of human Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 in human somatic cells, such as dermal fibroblasts. The conservation of neural developmental pathways between mice and humans strongly supports the translatability of this reprogramming strategy. Human Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 share high sequence homology and functional equivalence with their murine counterparts, and each has been individually implicated in human neural development and disease.

To produce human iDPs, primary human dermal fibroblasts are obtained from skin biopsies and cultured under standard conditions. The cells are then transduced with viral vectors—preferably integration-free systems such as Sendai virus or episomal plasmids to meet regulatory safety standards—encoding the three human transcription factors. Following transduction, cells are switched to human neural progenitor medium, such as DMEM/F12 supplemented with N2, B27, bFGF, and EGF. Within 2–3 weeks, human iDP colonies emerge, exhibiting morphology and marker expression analogous to murine iDPs.

Human iDPs are expected to express human orthologs of the key markers identified in the murine system, including CORIN (LRP4), ALDH1A1, LMX1A, OTX2, NGN2, and PITX3, as confirmed by quantitative RT-PCR, immunocytochemistry, and RNA sequencing. Functional validation would demonstrate that human iDPs differentiate into TH+ neurons expressing AADC, DAT, and VMAT2, and exhibit electrophysiological properties consistent with mature dopaminergic neurons. Importantly, human iDPs should show the same lineage restriction, failing to generate GFAP+ astrocytes or O4+ oligodendrocytes under differentiation conditions.

The ability to generate human iDPs from patient-derived fibroblasts opens avenues for personalized medicine: autologous iDPs can be transplanted without immunosuppression, and patient-specific iDPs can serve as platforms for disease modeling and drug discovery. Given the central role of dopaminergic neuron loss in Parkinson’s disease, human iDPs represent a transformative cell source for developing curative cell replacement therapies.

## DISCUSSION

The present invention represents a paradigm shift in the generation of dopaminergic progenitors for Parkinson’s disease therapy by demonstrating that a minimal set of three transcription factors—Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2—is sufficient to directly reprogram somatic cells into lineage-restricted induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) with authentic midbrain identity. This achievement addresses several longstanding challenges in the field of regenerative neurology. First, it overcomes the inefficiency and heterogeneity associated with pluripotent stem cell differentiation protocols by bypassing the pluripotent state entirely and generating progenitors that are intrinsically specified to the dopaminergic fate. Second, it eliminates the dependency on exogenous morphogen patterning, as iDPs differentiate into functional dopaminergic neurons with >90% efficiency even in the absence of SHH and FGF8—a testament to the completeness of the reprogramming-induced midbrain specification. Third, it solves the scalability problem through the integration of a conditional L-Myc system, enabling safe, controlled expansion without compromising lineage fidelity or safety, as evidenced by the absence of tumors in vivo.

The central insight of this work is the pivotal role of Foxa2 as a reprogramming determinant. While Brn2 and Sox2 are established neural fate inducers, they alone generate generic iNPCs lacking regional identity. The inclusion of Foxa2—not Lmx1a, as initially hypothesized—proves decisive in conferring midbrain DA specification. This aligns with developmental biology, where Foxa2 acts upstream of Lmx1a and orchestrates the floor plate program through both SHH-dependent and -independent mechanisms. In the reprogramming context, Foxa2 appears to activate a self-sustaining transcriptional network that includes Lmx1a, Otx2, and other midbrain determinants while simultaneously repressing alternative fates, such as GABAergic or serotonergic neurons (via repression of Helt and Nkx2.2, respectively) and glial lineages. The resulting iDPs are thus not merely patterned neural progenitors but true dopaminergic-restricted precursors, as confirmed by their inability to generate astrocytes or oligodendrocytes even under permissive differentiation conditions.

The functional validation of iDP-derived neurons—both in vitro through electrophysiology and in vivo through transplantation in an MPTP mouse model—provides compelling evidence of their therapeutic potential. The restoration of striatal TH+ innervation and partial recovery of motor function, coupled with the absence of tumors, positions iDPs as a clinically viable cell product. Future work will focus on optimizing human iDP production under GMP conditions, conducting long-term safety and efficacy studies in larger animal models, and exploring the use of iDPs for modeling genetic forms of PD. Ultimately, this invention lays the groundwork for a new generation of cell therapies that are precise, scalable, and patient-specific.

## Methods

### Cell Preparation, Retroviral Packaging, Infection and Direct Reprogramming

Somatic cells, specifically dermal fibroblasts, were isolated from adult Nestin-EGFP transgenic mice aged 5.5–7.0 weeks in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines. The fibroblasts were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1× Non-Essential Amino Acids, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin at 37°C in a 5% CO2 humidified atmosphere. Cells were used between passages 2 and 5 to prevent replicative senescence. For retroviral production, mouse Brn2 open reading frame (ORF) was cloned into the pMXs-retroviral vector using EcoRI and NotI restriction sites, and Foxa2 ORF was cloned using BamHI and XhoI sites; pMXs-Sox2 was obtained from Addgene (Plasmid #13367). These plasmids were transfected into Plat-E retroviral packaging cells seeded at 3.6×10^6 cells per 100-mm dish using Lipofectamine™ LTX. After 24 hours, the medium was replaced with DMEM containing 5% FBS. At 48 and 72 hours post-transfection, virus-containing supernatants were collected, filtered through a 0.45-μm cellulose acetate filter, pooled, and supplemented with 10 μg/mL polybrene. Target fibroblasts were seeded at 2×10^5 cells per 35-mm dish and incubated overnight with the viral mixture. Three days post-infection, the medium was switched to NeuroCult® NSC Basal Medium supplemented with NeuroCult® NSC Proliferation Supplements, 20 ng/mL basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), and 20 ng/mL epidermal growth factor (EGF). Reprogrammed colonies were monitored by fluorescence microscopy starting at day 7 and picked between days 9–14 for expansion.

### Quantitative Real-Time RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol Reagent followed by purification with the RNeasy Mini Kit. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using the Transcriptor First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using SYBR® Select Master Mix with gene-specific primer pairs (listed in Table S1) at a final concentration of 10 μM. Each 15-μL reaction contained 0.5 μL cDNA, 7.5 μL SYBR Green mix, 5.5 μL primer mix, and 1.5 μL nuclease-free water. Amplification was carried out on a real-time PCR system with the following cycling conditions: 50°C for 2 minutes, 95°C for 2 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds, gene-specific annealing temperature for 15 seconds, and 72°C for 1 minute. All samples were run in triplicate, and relative gene expression was calculated using the ΔΔCt method normalized to housekeeping genes such as GAPDH or β-actin.

### Immunocytochemistry

Cells were fixed in 4% formaldehyde for 20 minutes at room temperature, washed three times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 in PBS for 10 minutes, and blocked with 2% bovine serum albumin (BSA) in PBS for 1 hour. Primary antibodies against neural and dopaminergic markers (e.g., Nestin, βIII-tubulin, TH, GFAP, O4, Corin, DCX, PSA-NCAM) were applied overnight at 4°C. After three PBS washes, cells were incubated with fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies for 2 hours at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Fluorescent images were acquired using a Zeiss 710 Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope and analyzed with Zen software.

### Differentiation

For neuronal differentiation, iDPs were plated on poly-L-ornithine/laminin-coated coverslips in DMEM/F12 supplemented with N2, B27, GlutaMAX, β-mercaptoethanol, dibutyryl-cAMP, ascorbic acid, brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), and glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) for four weeks, with medium changes every 3–4 days. This protocol was applied both with and without initial exposure to SHH and FGF8 to assess morphogen independence. For astrocyte differentiation, iDPs were cultured in DMEM/F12 with 10% FBS for 7 days. For oligodendrocyte differentiation, cells were first treated with PDGF, FGF-2, and forskolin for 4 days, followed by T3 hormone and ascorbic acid for 7 days. Differentiation efficiency was quantified by immunocytochemistry for lineage-specific markers.

### Recording of Action Potentials and Total Currents

Whole-cell patch-clamp recordings were performed using an Axonpatch 200B amplifier and pClamp 10.2 software. The internal pipette solution contained (in mM): 105 K-aspartate, 20 KCl, 1 CaCl2, 5 MgATP, 10 HEPES, 10 EGTA, and 25 glucose (pH 7.2). The external bath solution consisted of (in mM): 140 NaCl, 5.4 KCl, 0.5 MgCl2, 2.5 CaCl2, 5.5 HEPES, 11 glucose, and 10 sucrose (pH 7.4). Patch pipettes with resistances of 3–5 MΩ were used. Series resistance (6–13 MΩ) was compensated by 80–90%. Action potentials were elicited by 1-second depolarizing current steps from 20 to 100 pA in 20-pA increments. Voltage-gated currents were recorded by stepping from a holding potential of −80 mV to test potentials ranging from −70 to +70 mV in 10-mV increments. Data were sampled at 10 kHz and filtered at 5 kHz.

### Motor Function and Behavior Tests

Motor coordination and balance were assessed using an accelerating rotarod apparatus. Mice were trained for four daily sessions over three consecutive days prior to MPTP administration. Baseline performance was established by two trials at 10 rpm before MPTP intoxication. Following iDP transplantation, mice were tested twice weekly for three weeks. Latency to fall was recorded for each trial, and weekly performance was calculated as the average of two trials, normalized to the pre-treatment baseline and expressed relative to the PBS control group.

### Statistical Analysis

All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance was determined by one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All experiments were repeated at least twice with triplicate samples per condition.

### Cell Labeling, Transplantation and Histology

iDPs were labeled by infection with a lentiviral vector expressing green fluorescent protein (GFP) and selected with puromycin. For transplantation, 250,000 GFP+ iDPs were injected bilaterally into the striatum of MPTP-lesioned SCID or C57BL/6J mice using stereotaxic coordinates: 0.22 mm posterior to bregma, 3.25 mm lateral, and 2.9 mm ventral. Mice were sacrificed 3–6 weeks post-transplantation, perfused intracardially with paraformaldehyde, and brains were sectioned coronally. Immunohistochemistry for TH, GFP, βIII-tubulin, and GFAP was performed on free-floating sections. Striatal TH+ fiber density was quantified by digital image analysis using ImageJ software in a blinded manner. Graft survival, differentiation, and tumor formation were assessed by fluorescence and brightfield microscopy.
