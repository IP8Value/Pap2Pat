## BACKGROUND

Chromatin is the complex of DNA and proteins that packages eukaryotic genomes within the nucleus. The fundamental repeating unit of chromatin is the nucleosome, which consists of approximately 147 base pairs of DNA wrapped around an octamer of core histone proteins—two copies each of H2A, H2B, H3, and H4. Nucleosomes serve not only as structural scaffolds but also as dynamic regulators of genomic processes such as transcription, replication, repair, and recombination. The stability, positioning, and composition of nucleosomes are tightly controlled through post-translational modifications of histones, ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling, and incorporation of histone variants. Disruption of these regulatory mechanisms can lead to aberrant gene expression and genome instability, both hallmarks of cancer.

In recent years, a new class of driver mutations in cancer has emerged: mutations in the genes encoding core histones themselves. These so-called “oncohistones” are typically heterozygous missense substitutions that occur at highly conserved residues within the histone fold domain (HFD), the region responsible for mediating histone-histone and histone-DNA interactions essential for nucleosome integrity. Notably, oncohistone mutations do not act alone; rather, they function synergistically with other oncogenic lesions to promote tumorigenesis by altering the epigenetic landscape. For example, the H2B-E76K substitution, found in various malignancies, reduces nucleosome stability in vitro and enhances cellular transformation when co-expressed with activated PI3KCA. Similarly, recurrent mutations in canonical H2A—such as R29Q and E121Q—disrupt critical contacts with DNA or perturb inter-nucleosomal interfaces, particularly within the acidic patch, thereby compromising chromatin architecture.

Concurrently, a distinct class of naturally occurring histone variants known as short H2A (sH2A) variants has been identified in mammals. These variants—including H2A.B (encoded by H2AFB1, H2AFB2, and H2AFB3), H2A.P (HYPM), and H2A.Q—are predominantly expressed during spermatogenesis and exhibit striking structural divergence from canonical H2A. sH2As possess truncated C-terminal tails, altered HFD sequences, and modified acidic patches, all of which contribute to the formation of unstable nucleosomes that wrap only ~120 base pairs of DNA. This inherent nucleosome destabilization is thought to facilitate rapid chromatin remodeling during meiosis and transcriptional activation in male germ cells. Moreover, H2A.B has been shown to interact directly with RNA and splicing factors, influencing alternative splicing patterns in testis. Germline deletion of H2A.B in mice results in defective chromatin organization and aberrant splicing, underscoring its physiological importance in gametogenesis.

Despite these well-characterized roles in normal development, the potential involvement of sH2As in oncogenesis has remained unexplored. Given that the defining molecular features of sH2As—nucleosome destabilization, altered DNA wrapping, and splicing modulation—overlap significantly with the phenotypic consequences of canonical oncohistone mutations, it is plausible that ectopic expression of sH2As in somatic tissues could recapitulate oncohistone activity. Indeed, preliminary evidence suggests that H2A.B transcripts are detectable in Hodgkin’s lymphoma, where their presence correlates with accelerated cell proliferation. However, no systematic investigation has been conducted to determine whether sH2As are broadly reactivated across human cancers, whether their expression associates with specific molecular subtypes or transcriptional programs, or whether they induce cancer-relevant phenotypes such to splicing dysregulation or immune recognition.

The absence of such knowledge represents a significant gap in our understanding of chromatin-driven oncogenesis. While canonical oncohistone mutations are relatively rare and often restricted to pediatric or rare adult cancers, sH2As are encoded by intact, wild-type genes that may be aberrantly activated through epigenetic or transcriptional mechanisms common in many malignancies. If sH2As function as endogenous oncohistones, their reactivation could represent a widespread, previously unrecognized mechanism by which diverse cancers achieve a permissive chromatin state conducive to transformation. Furthermore, because sH2As are normally restricted to immune-privileged germ cells, their ectopic expression in tumors might generate tumor-specific antigens amenable to immunotherapeutic targeting.

Thus, there exists a compelling need to define the role of sH2A variants—particularly H2A.B—in human cancer, to elucidate the molecular consequences of their expression in non-germline contexts, and to evaluate their potential as diagnostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets. The present invention addresses these needs by demonstrating that sH2As inherently possess oncohistone-like features, are aberrantly expressed across a broad spectrum of malignancies, associate with cancer-testis antigen programs, and drive distinct splicing alterations consistent with nucleosome instability. These findings establish sH2As as a novel class of endogenous oncohistones and open new avenues for cancer detection, stratification, and treatment.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods and compositions related to the identification, diagnosis, and therapeutic targeting of cancers characterized by the aberrant expression of short histone H2A (sH2A) variants, particularly H2A.B isoforms encoded by the H2AFB1, H2AFB2, and H2AFB3 genes. It has been discovered that sH2A variants, which are normally restricted to testicular tissue during spermatogenesis, inherently possess structural and functional features analogous to those of canonical oncohistones—mutant histones that drive cancer through nucleosome destabilization. Specifically, wild-type sH2A proteins contain amino acid substitutions and C-terminal truncations at positions that correspond precisely to recurrent cancer-associated mutations in canonical H2A, including R29Q/F and loss of E121. These alterations are evolutionarily conserved across primates, indicating strong selective pressure and functional significance in germ cell biology. However, when expressed ectopically in somatic tissues, these same features confer oncohistone-like activity by reducing nucleosome stability, perturbing chromatin architecture, and facilitating oncogenic transcriptional and post-transcriptional programs.

The invention further demonstrates that H2A.B paralogs are aberrantly reactivated in a wide array of human malignancies, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), Hodgkin’s lymphoma, uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC), urothelial bladder carcinoma (BLCA), cervical carcinoma, and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL). This reactivation is highly specific to tumor tissue, as H2A.B expression is virtually absent in adjacent normal tissues and non-testicular somatic tissues in healthy individuals. Importantly, H2A.B expression occurs independently of global X-chromosome dysregulation or gene amplification, suggesting locus-specific transcriptional activation driven by cancer-associated epigenetic or regulatory mechanisms.

Moreover, the invention reveals that H2A.B-expressing cancers exhibit distinct molecular signatures. Although H2AFB1 and H2AFB2/3 encode nearly identical proteins, their independent reactivation in different tumor specimens is associated with overlapping global gene expression changes, indicating shared functional consequences. H2A.B-positive tumors frequently co-express cancer-testis antigens (CTAs), a class of immunogenic proteins normally silenced in adult somatic tissues but reactivated in cancer. This association is statistically significant in multiple cancer types, including UCEC, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), and B-ALL, suggesting coordinated derepression of germline-specific gene programs.

Critically, the invention establishes that H2A.B expression drives widespread alterations in RNA splicing. Analysis of transcriptomic data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and independent B-ALL cohorts shows that H2A.B-positive tumors exhibit reduced inclusion of alternative cassette exons and decreased usage of proximal alternative polyadenylation sites—splicing features known to be sensitive to RNA polymerase II elongation rates. In B-ALL, H2A.B expression is additionally associated with decreased intron retention. These splicing changes are consistent with a model in which H2A.B-induced nucleosome destabilization accelerates transcriptional elongation, thereby reducing the time available for spliceosome assembly on weak splice sites. Such splicing dysregulation may enhance oncogene expression or generate novel protein isoforms that contribute to tumor progression.

Additionally, the invention identifies sH2A-derived peptides that are predicted to bind with high affinity to common human leukocyte antigen (HLA) alleles, suggesting that H2A.B-expressing tumors may present tumor-specific neoantigens recognizable by cytotoxic T cells. This finding supports the development of immunotherapies targeting sH2A epitopes.

Accordingly, the invention provides diagnostic methods for detecting H2A.B expression in biological samples to identify patients with sH2A-driven cancers, prognostic tools based on H2A.B-associated splicing or CTA signatures, and therapeutic strategies including small molecules that disrupt H2A.B incorporation into chromatin, antisense oligonucleotides or RNA interference agents that suppress H2A.B expression, and vaccines or adoptive T-cell therapies directed against sH2A-derived HLA-presented peptides. The invention thus establishes sH2As as a novel class of endogenous oncohistones and provides a foundation for precision oncology approaches targeting this previously unrecognized axis of chromatin dysfunction in cancer.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is based on the unexpected discovery that short histone H2A (sH2A) variants—proteins normally restricted to male germ cells—possess intrinsic structural features that mirror those of canonical oncohistones and, when aberrantly expressed in somatic tissues, drive molecular phenotypes characteristic of cancer. Unlike classical oncohistones, which arise from somatic mutations in histone genes, sH2As are encoded by wild-type genomic loci that have evolved nucleosome-destabilizing properties essential for spermatogenesis. However, when reactivated in non-germline contexts, these same properties confer oncogenic potential by perturbing chromatin dynamics, altering gene expression, and inducing splicing dysregulation. This section provides a comprehensive description of the invention, including the molecular characteristics of sH2As, their expression patterns in cancer, associated transcriptional and post-transcriptional programs, and implications for diagnosis and therapy.

Short H2A variants constitute a rapidly evolving subclass of histone H2A that includes H2A.B (with paralogs H2A.B.1.1/H2AFB2, H2A.B.1.2/H2AFB3, and H2A.B.2/H2AFB1), H2A.P (HYPM), and H2A.Q. These variants are encoded by five X-linked genes in humans and are predominantly expressed during meiosis in the testis. Structurally, sH2As differ from canonical H2A in three key respects: (1) they harbor multiple amino acid substitutions within the histone fold domain (HFD), particularly at residues that contact DNA or mediate inter-histone interactions; (2) they possess a truncated C-terminal tail that lacks the docking domain required for stable interaction with H2B and proper nucleosome assembly; and (3) they exhibit alterations in the acidic patch, a surface-exposed region critical for higher-order chromatin folding and binding of chromatin-associated proteins. Collectively, these features result in the formation of unstable nucleosomes that wrap only ~120 base pairs of DNA—significantly less than the canonical 147 bp—and display increased dynamics in vivo.

Through comparative sequence analysis, it has now been established that many of the amino acid changes naturally present in wild-type sH2As correspond exactly to recurrent somatic mutations observed in canonical H2A across diverse human cancers. For instance, the arginine at position 29 (R29) in canonical H2A is a hotspot for mutation in cancer, with R29Q being the second most frequent H2A oncohistone substitution identified in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). Remarkably, all human sH2A variants naturally encode either glutamine (Q) or phenylalanine (F) at this position, effectively mimicking the R29Q/F oncohistone mutation without requiring any genetic alteration. Similarly, the glutamic acid at position 121 (E121)—the most commonly mutated residue in canonical H2A—is entirely absent in sH2As due to C-terminal truncation. Phylogenetic analysis across primates confirms that these “oncohistone-like” features are highly conserved, despite the otherwise rapid evolution of sH2A sequences, indicating that they are under positive selection for functional roles in germ cell biology. Thus, sH2As represent a unique class of endogenous proteins that have evolved oncohistone properties as part of their normal physiological function.

The invention further demonstrates that these germline-encoded oncohistone mimics are aberrantly reactivated in a broad spectrum of human malignancies. Analysis of RNA sequencing data from TCGA reveals that H2A.B paralogs are expressed above a threshold of 1.5 transcripts per million (TPM) in numerous tumor types, including 50% of diffuse large B-cell lymphomas (DLBCL), 9.5% of uterine corpus endometrial carcinomas (UCEC), 4.7% of bladder carcinomas (BLCA), and 4.5% of cervical carcinomas. Critically, H2A.B expression is undetectable or extremely rare (<1.5%) in non-testicular normal tissues from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project and is absent in adjacent normal tissue from TCGA patients, confirming that its presence in tumors reflects pathological reactivation rather than contamination or baseline expression. Similar findings are observed in independent cohorts of B-acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL), where 6–13% of cases show H2A.B upregulation, and in the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE), where 70% of Hodgkin’s lymphoma and 25% of non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma cell lines express H2A.B. These data establish H2A.B as a widely reactivated oncohistone mimic across both hematologic and solid tumors.

Importantly, the reactivation of H2A.B is not attributable to global X-chromosome aneuploidy or broad epigenetic derepression. Comparative analysis of X-linked versus autosomal transcript levels shows no significant difference between H2A.B-positive and H2A.B-negative tumors. Furthermore, examination of genomic regions surrounding the H2AFB loci reveals that upregulation is confined to the H2A.B genes themselves, without concomitant activation of neighboring genes. This locus-specific induction suggests that H2A.B reactivation is driven by precise regulatory mechanisms—potentially involving promoter demethylation, enhancer hijacking, or transcription factor activation—rather than stochastic chromosomal instability.

Despite near-identical protein sequences, H2AFB1 and H2AFB2/3 are independently reactivated in different tumor specimens. Nevertheless, comparative transcriptome analysis shows that tumors expressing either paralog exhibit highly correlated gene expression changes, with thousands of genes commonly up- or downregulated across multiple cancer types (e.g., UCEC, HNSC, LUSC, BLCA). This convergence implies that the functional impact of H2A.B is determined primarily by its protein structure rather than paralog-specific regulatory elements. Among the 146 genes consistently upregulated in H2A.B-positive cancers, 12 are classified as cancer-testis antigens (CTAs)—proteins normally expressed only in germ cells but frequently reactivated in tumors and recognized by the immune system. To quantify this association, a composite CTA score was calculated for each tumor based on the aggregate expression of the top 40 most variably expressed CTAs. H2A.B-positive tumors in UCEC, HNSC, LUSC, and B-ALL show significantly elevated CTA scores compared to H2A.B-negative counterparts, indicating coordinated derepression of germline-specific gene programs. Notably, this association is not universal; DLBCL and sarcoma (SARC) tumors do not show significant CTA enrichment, suggesting context-dependent regulation.

To assess whether H2A.B expression might be confounded by immune cell infiltration—given that CTAs can attract lymphocytes—immune marker gene expression was analyzed across tumor types. No consistent enrichment of B-cell, T-cell, NK cell, monocyte, or macrophage transcripts was observed in H2A.B-positive tumors. In fact, UCEC tumors showed reduced expression of perforin (PRF1) and myeloid markers, possibly reflecting an immunosuppressive microenvironment that permits sustained H2A.B expression. The detection of H2A.B in cancer cell lines, which lack stromal components, further supports tumor-intrinsic expression.

A central aspect of the invention is the demonstration that H2A.B expression induces widespread splicing alterations consistent with nucleosome instability. H2A.B has been previously shown to bind RNA and interact with splicing factors in testis, and its incorporation into chromatin accelerates RNA polymerase II elongation by reducing nucleosomal barriers. Faster elongation is known to impair the recognition of weak splice sites, leading to exon skipping and altered polyadenylation. In agreement with this model, H2A.B-positive TCGA tumors exhibit significant reductions in the inclusion of alternative cassette exons and usage of proximal alternative polyadenylation (APA) sites, particularly in BLCA, SARC, and UCEC. These changes, while modest in magnitude at individual loci, affect thousands of events genome-wide, indicating a systemic impact on RNA processing. In B-ALL—a cancer type not typically associated with splicing factor mutations—H2A.B expression is additionally linked to decreased intron retention, a feature that may reflect altered kinetics of co-transcriptional splicing. These findings position H2A.B as a driver of splicing dysregulation in cancers lacking canonical splicing mutations, thereby expanding the mechanistic repertoire of chromatin-based oncogenesis.

Finally, the invention identifies potential immunotherapeutic opportunities arising from H2A.B expression. Using the NetPanMHCBA4.0 algorithm and other prediction tools from the Immune Epitope Database (IEDB), multiple 8–11 amino acid peptides derived from H2A.B.1.1, H2A.B.1.2, H2A.B.2, H2A.P, and H2A.Q were found to bind with high affinity (IC50 < 300 nM) to common HLA class I alleles, including HLA-A*0201, A*0301, B*0702, and B*4402. Because sH2As are absent from normal somatic tissues, these peptides represent tumor-specific neoantigens that could be targeted by vaccines, T-cell receptor-engineered lymphocytes, or checkpoint blockade combinations. Moreover, the global splicing alterations induced by H2A.B may generate additional immunogenic neoepitopes through frameshifts or cryptic splice junctions.

In summary, the detailed description establishes that sH2As are endogenous oncohistones whose germline-evolved nucleosome-destabilizing features become oncogenic upon ectopic expression in cancer. Their reactivation defines a molecular subset of tumors with distinct transcriptional, splicing, and immunological characteristics, providing a rationale for novel diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic strategies.

### Additional Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below:

“Short H2A variant” or “sH2A” refers to a naturally occurring histone H2A variant characterized by a truncated C-terminal tail, amino acid substitutions in the histone fold domain, and reduced nucleosome stability. In humans, sH2A variants include H2A.B (encoded by H2AFB1, H2AFB2, and H2AFB3), H2A.P (HYPM), and H2A.Q.

“H2A.B” refers collectively to the protein products of the H2AFB1, H2AFB2, and H2AFB3 genes, which share >95% amino acid identity and form unstable nucleosomes that wrap approximately 120 base pairs of DNA.

“Oncohistone” refers to a histone protein—either mutant or wild-type—that promotes oncogenesis through disruption of nucleosome stability or chromatin function. Canonical oncohistones arise from somatic mutations (e.g., H2A-R29Q), whereas sH2As represent endogenous oncohistones encoded by wild-type genes.

“Aberrant expression” means expression of an sH2A variant in a tissue or cell type where it is not normally expressed, particularly in non-germline somatic tissues. In the context of cancer, aberrant expression is defined as RNA transcript levels exceeding 1.5 transcripts per million (TPM) as measured by RNA sequencing.

“Cancer-testis antigen (CTA)” refers to a protein normally expressed only in germ cells of the testis but reactivated in various cancers. CTAs are often immunogenic and include members of the MAGE, NY-ESO-1, and SSX families.

“Splicing dysregulation” encompasses any deviation from normal RNA splicing patterns, including altered cassette exon inclusion, intron retention, alternative 5′ or 3′ splice site usage, and alternative polyadenylation.

“Nucleosome destabilization” refers to a reduction in the thermodynamic stability or structural integrity of the nucleosome core particle, resulting in increased DNA unwrapping, histone exchange, or reduced protection of DNA from enzymatic or transcription factor access.

“HLA-presented peptide” means a short peptide (typically 8–11 amino acids) derived from intracellular protein degradation and displayed on the cell surface in complex with a human leukocyte antigen (HLA) class I molecule, where it can be recognized by CD8+ T cells.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Identification of Oncohistone Features in sH2A Variants**

Human sH2A protein sequences (H2A.B.1.1, H2A.B.1.2, H2A.B.2, H2A.P, H2A.Q) were aligned with canonical H2A using ClustalW. The alignment revealed that all sH2As naturally encode glutamine or phenylalanine at position 29, corresponding to the R29Q/F oncohistone mutation frequently observed in TCGA cancers. Additionally, all sH2As lack the C-terminal residues beyond position ~115, resulting in the absence of E121—a residue mutated in ~8% of H2A-mutant cancers. Phylogenetic analysis of primate sH2A sequences confirmed conservation of these features over millions of years of evolution, supporting their functional importance.

**Example 2: Detection of H2A.B Expression in Human Cancers**

RNA-seq data from TCGA (n=10,000+ tumors) were analyzed for H2A.B transcript levels. Tumors with >1.5 TPM of any H2AFB paralog were classified as H2A.B-positive. Results showed 50% of DLBCLs, 9.5% of UCECs, 4.7% of BLCA, and 4.5% of cervical cancers were positive. No expression was detected in GTEx non-testis tissues (n=7,000+ samples). Independent validation in B-ALL cohorts (Liu et al. 2016, Qian et al. 2017, Yasuda et al. 2016) confirmed 6–13% positivity.

**Example 3: Association with Cancer-Testis Antigens**

A CTA score was computed for each TCGA tumor as the sum of Z-normalized log-expression values for the top 40 most variable CTAs. Mann–Whitney U tests revealed significantly higher CTA scores in H2A.B-positive vs. negative tumors in UCEC (p<0.01), HNSC (p<0.05), LUSC (p<0.05), and B-ALL (p<0.01), but not in DLBCL or SARC.

**Example 4: Splicing Alterations in H2A.B-Positive Tumors**

Alternative splicing events were quantified using MISO v2.0. H2A.B-positive tumors showed significant reductions in alternative cassette exon inclusion (ΔΨ > 0.1, p<0.05) in BLCA (n=1,200 events), UCEC (n=950), and SARC (n=800). In B-ALL, decreased intron retention was the dominant feature (n=600–900 events across four datasets).

**Example 5: Prediction of Immunogenic sH2A Peptides**

Using NetPanMHCBA4.0 and consensus IEDB algorithms, 23 sH2A-derived peptides were predicted to bind strongly (IC50 < 300 nM) to at least one common HLA allele. For example, the H2A.B.2 peptide “LLQKFLKK” (SEQ ID NO:1) binds HLA-A*0201 with IC50 = 45 nM, making it a candidate for T-cell-based immunotherapy.
