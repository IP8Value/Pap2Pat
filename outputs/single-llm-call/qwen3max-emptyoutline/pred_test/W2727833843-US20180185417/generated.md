# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of regenerative medicine has witnessed significant advances in the use of cell-based therapies to treat a wide range of conditions, including ischemic vascular disease, critical limb ischemia, myocardial infarction, and impaired bone healing. In particular, autologous cell therapies—wherein cells are harvested from a patient, processed, and reintroduced into the same individual—have emerged as a promising strategy to promote tissue repair and regeneration without the immunological risks associated with allogeneic transplantation. However, the clinical translation of such therapies is often hindered by stringent regulatory requirements, particularly those imposed by agencies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA). These regulations distinguish between minimally manipulated and more than minimally manipulated cellular products, with the latter classified as advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs), which require extensive preclinical and clinical validation, Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) compliance, and substantial financial investment.

One major challenge in the development of intraoperative cell therapies—procedures wherein cells are harvested, processed, and re-administered during a single surgical session—is achieving sufficient cell numbers and purity without resorting to ex vivo expansion or chemical modification. Ex vivo expansion, while effective in increasing cell yield, introduces variability, contamination risks, and regulatory complexity. Similarly, cytokine-based mobilization strategies, such as the use of granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) to mobilize hematopoietic stem cells, are associated with systemic side effects and logistical burdens, including multiple hospital visits for injections and cell collection. Moreover, these approaches often fail to deliver consistent cell populations with predictable regenerative potential.

In contrast, intraoperative cell enrichment offers a streamlined, point-of-care alternative that aligns with the principles of minimal manipulation. By isolating therapeutically relevant cell subsets directly from a patient’s own blood or bone marrow during surgery, clinicians can bypass the need for culture expansion and reduce procedural time and cost. This approach is particularly attractive in orthopedic and cardiovascular applications, where timely delivery of pro-angiogenic and pro-osteogenic cells can significantly improve outcomes. For instance, in the context of bone fracture repair, approximately 7.9 million fractures occur annually in the United States, with 5–10% progressing to non-union or delayed healing. Early intervention with enriched regenerative cell populations may mitigate these complications, reduce hospital stays, and lower healthcare expenditures.

Current methods for cell isolation in clinical settings predominantly rely on immunomagnetic separation using antibody-conjugated magnetic beads. While effective, these techniques result in cells that remain bound to antibodies or beads post-isolation. Regulatory authorities in both the United States and Europe consider such modifications to constitute more than minimal manipulation, thereby triggering ATMP classification. This regulatory burden limits the widespread adoption of intraoperative cell therapies, especially in resource-constrained or ambulatory surgical settings. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need for a cell enrichment platform that yields “untouched” cells—free of exogenous ligands, beads, or chemical residues—while maintaining high viability, purity, and functional potency.

Aptamers—single-stranded oligonucleotides that fold into specific three-dimensional structures capable of binding molecular targets with high affinity and specificity—offer a compelling alternative to antibodies for cell isolation. Unlike protein-based reagents, aptamers are chemically synthesized, exhibit excellent batch-to-batch reproducibility, are stable at room temperature, and can be reversibly dissociated from their targets under mild physical conditions. The first FDA-approved aptamer, pegaptanib, an RNA aptamer targeting vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)-165, was approved in 2004 for the treatment of neovascular age-related macular degeneration, demonstrating the clinical viability of aptamer-based therapeutics. More recently, DNA aptamers have been explored for diagnostic and cell-sorting applications due to their nuclease resistance, ease of modification, and lower immunogenicity.

Despite these advantages, existing aptamer-based cell separation systems often require enzymatic digestion (e.g., DNase I), competitive displacement with complementary strands, or high concentrations of polyanionic agents like dextran sulfate to release captured cells. These chemical interventions introduce contaminants that may compromise cell function or necessitate additional purification steps, thereby undermining the goal of minimal manipulation. Furthermore, microfluidic platforms that exploit temperature- or shear-dependent aptamer binding have been reported but remain complex, low-throughput, and unsuitable for intraoperative use.

Thus, the art lacks a simple, scalable, and regulatory-compliant system for the rapid, bead-free, and chemical-free enrichment of therapeutically relevant cell populations from whole blood or other biological fluids. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a novel device and method that leverages the reversible binding properties of aptamers under biophysically induced shear stress to isolate target cells without residual ligands or beads. This platform not only meets the criteria for minimal manipulation but also enables the intraoperative generation of enriched cell populations with enhanced angiogenic and osteogenic potential, thereby facilitating their immediate therapeutic application in regenerative medicine.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel, minimally manipulative system and method for the enrichment of target cells from a mixed cell population, particularly from whole blood or peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), without the use of exogenous ligands, magnetic beads, or chemical elution agents in the final cell product. The invention is based on the discovery that certain aptamers, when immobilized on solid-phase supports, can selectively bind target cells expressing a specific surface marker and subsequently release those cells upon application of controlled mechanical shear forces, yielding a purified, bead-free, and ligand-free cell population suitable for immediate therapeutic use.

In one aspect, the invention relates to a device for enriching CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population. The device comprises a column body defining a fluid flow path, a porous filter disposed at the distal end of the column body having a pore size sufficient to retain agarose-based beads but permit passage of leukocytes, and a packed bed of neutravidin-functionalized agarose beads within the column body. At least a portion of the agarose beads is coated with a biotinylated aptamer specific for human CD31 (Platelet/Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule-1, PECAM-1). The aptamer is selected for its high specificity and moderate affinity, enabling robust capture under static or low-flow conditions and efficient release under shear-induced resuspension without enzymatic or chemical intervention.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for enriching CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population. The method involves introducing a cell suspension containing CD31+ and CD31− cells into the device described above under binding conditions that promote aptamer-mediated adhesion of CD31+ cells to the aptamer-coated beads. Unbound cells are then washed away, and the captured CD31+ cells are released by mechanically resuspending the bead bed in a buffer solution, thereby applying shear forces sufficient to disrupt the aptamer-CD31 interaction. The released cells are collected by centrifugation, resulting in a highly enriched, viable, and functionally active CD31+ cell population free of beads, antibodies, or chemical residues.

In yet another aspect, the invention encompasses kits for performing the enrichment method. The kit includes the aforementioned device, a vial of biotinylated anti-CD31 aptamer, binding buffer, wash buffer, and instructions for use. The kit may further include syringes, tubing, and connectors compatible with standard infusion pumps, enabling integration into intraoperative workflows.

The invention also provides methods of treatment utilizing the enriched CD31+ cell populations. Specifically, the enriched cells or their conditioned medium may be administered to a patient to promote angiogenesis, osteogenesis, or tissue repair in conditions such as bone non-union, critical limb ischemia, myocardial infarction, or chronic wounds. The cells may be delivered directly to the site of injury, incorporated into biomaterial scaffolds, or used in combination with other therapeutic agents.

A key advantage of the invention is its compliance with regulatory definitions of minimal manipulation. Because the final cell product contains no residual beads, antibodies, or chemical eluents, it avoids classification as an ATMP in many jurisdictions, thereby simplifying regulatory approval and enabling point-of-care use. Additionally, the system is scalable, cost-effective, and adaptable to other cell surface markers by substituting the CD31-specific aptamer with an aptamer targeting a different antigen, thus serving as a platform technology for both positive and negative selection strategies.

The enriched CD31+ cell population exhibits enhanced regenerative potential, as demonstrated by in vitro assays showing increased tube formation in endothelial cell cultures and enhanced mineralization in mesenchymal stromal cell cultures. Flow cytometric analysis confirms significant enrichment of monocytes (CD14+) and naïve T cells (CD3+CD31+), both of which contribute to angiogenic and osteogenic signaling through paracrine mechanisms. The system achieves a CD31+ purity of at least 85%, with cell viability exceeding 95%, and a recovery yield of approximately 25% from PBMCs.

Overall, the invention represents a paradigm shift in intraoperative cell therapy by providing a closed, rapid, and regulatory-friendly method to generate therapeutically potent cell populations at the point of care, thereby overcoming the limitations of conventional cell isolation technologies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. CD31+ Cells

CD31, also known as Platelet/Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule-1 (PECAM-1), is a 130-kDa transmembrane glycoprotein belonging to the immunoglobulin superfamily. It is constitutively expressed on the surface of endothelial cells, platelets, monocytes, neutrophils, and a subset of lymphocytes, including naïve T cells. CD31 plays a critical role in cell-cell adhesion, leukocyte transendothelial migration, angiogenesis, and signal transduction. In the context of regenerative medicine, CD31+ cells have been shown to possess multifaceted therapeutic properties, including the ability to promote vascularization, modulate immune responses, and support bone formation.

Within peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), CD31 is expressed on approximately 70–78% of viable lymphocytes and monocytes, with expression levels largely preserved across age and gender. Notably, monocytes exhibit the highest density of CD31 expression among PBMC subsets, followed by naïve T cells. The CD31+ monocyte population is particularly enriched in pro-angiogenic and pro-osteogenic factors, such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), interleukin-8 (IL-8), and matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9), which contribute to tissue repair through paracrine signaling. Similarly, CD31+ T cells, upon stimulation, secrete elevated levels of granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) and other cytokines that enhance endothelial cell proliferation and vessel maturation.

The therapeutic relevance of CD31+ cells has been demonstrated in multiple preclinical and clinical studies. For example, infusion of CD31+ cells has been shown to improve perfusion in models of hindlimb ischemia and to accelerate bone healing in non-union fracture models. In humans, CD31+ cell therapy has been explored in patients with critical limb ischemia and myocardial infarction, with promising results in terms of safety and efficacy. Importantly, the regenerative capacity of CD31+ cells appears to be independent of their differentiation into endothelial or osteoblastic lineages; rather, their primary mechanism of action is through the secretion of trophic factors that recruit endogenous progenitor cells and modulate the local microenvironment.

Given their abundance in peripheral blood, stability across demographic variables, and potent regenerative functions, CD31+ cells represent an ideal target for intraoperative enrichment. The present invention capitalizes on this by providing a method to isolate these cells rapidly and without residual reagents, enabling their direct use in surgical settings. The enriched CD31+ population retains full functionality, as evidenced by enhanced angiogenic and osteogenic activity in vitro, and is suitable for autologous transplantation without the need for ex vivo expansion or genetic modification.

### II. Aptamers

Aptamers are short, single-stranded oligonucleotides—typically 20–100 nucleotides in length—that fold into defined three-dimensional structures capable of binding specific molecular targets with high affinity and selectivity. They are generated through an in vitro selection process known as Systematic Evolution of Ligands by Exponential Enrichment (SELEX), which iteratively isolates nucleic acid sequences with desired binding properties from a large random library. Aptamers can be developed against a wide range of targets, including proteins, small molecules, and even whole cells.

The aptamer employed in the present invention is a DNA-based oligonucleotide specifically selected to bind human CD31 with high specificity. It is commercially available and has been validated for its ability to recognize CD31+ cells in PBMC populations. The aptamer is biotinylated at its 5' or 3' terminus, enabling immobilization onto neutravidin-coated solid supports via the ultra-high-affinity biotin-neutravidin interaction (Kd ≈ 10⁻¹⁴ M). This immobilization strategy ensures stable anchoring of the aptamer while preserving its binding conformation and accessibility to CD31 on the cell surface.

A critical feature of the aptamer is its moderate binding affinity for CD31 (Kd ≈ 1.14 × 10⁻⁹ M), which is significantly lower than that of typical monoclonal antibodies. This property is advantageous for reversible cell capture: while the avidity effect from multiple aptamer-cell interactions enables efficient retention under low-shear binding conditions, the individual binding events are sufficiently weak to be disrupted by mechanical shear forces during the release step. This balance allows for high capture efficiency without requiring harsh chemical or enzymatic elution methods.

The aptamer exhibits high sensitivity and specificity for CD31+ cells, as demonstrated by flow cytometry. At a concentration of 5 µg/mL, it achieves a sensitivity of 99.0 ± 1.3% and a specificity of 96.9 ± 4.1% in PBMCs. Importantly, the aptamer does not cross-react with CD31− cells, ensuring minimal contamination of the enriched product. Furthermore, because aptamers are chemically synthesized, they offer superior batch-to-batch consistency compared to biological reagents like antibodies, which are subject to lot-to-lot variability due to differences in host cell expression and purification.

The use of aptamers in the present invention aligns with the goal of minimal manipulation. Unlike antibodies, which are proteins that may trigger immune responses or denature under storage conditions, aptamers are non-immunogenic, stable at room temperature, and can be easily modified with functional groups (e.g., biotin, fluorophores) without affecting their binding properties. The bi-labeled version of the aptamer, conjugated with both biotin and Cy5, has been used to confirm that no residual aptamer remains on the released cells, as the fluorescence signal is absent in the final product, indicating complete dissociation.

### III. Devices for Enrichment of Cells

The device of the present invention is a gravity- or pump-driven column system designed for the rapid, closed, and sterile enrichment of CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population. The device comprises a cylindrical column body, typically constructed from medical-grade polypropylene or polycarbonate, with a volume capacity of 1–2 mL. The column is fitted with a Luer-lock connector at the proximal end for attachment to syringes or infusion lines and a porous filter at the distal end.

The filter is fabricated from a 20 µm mesh polyethylene or nylon membrane, which serves a dual function: it retains the agarose-based capture beads within the column while permitting the passage of leukocytes, which have diameters ranging from 6 to 10 µm. This size-exclusion design ensures that the final cell product is completely free of beads, addressing a major regulatory concern associated with conventional magnetic bead systems. The filter is securely affixed to the base of the column using a biocompatible cyanoacrylate adhesive, forming a leak-proof seal that withstands operational pressures.

Packed within the column is a bed of neutravidin-functionalized agarose beads, with particle diameters ranging from 45 to 165 µm. Prior to packing, the beads are filtered through a 100 µm strainer to remove fines and ensure uniform flow characteristics. The beads are then incubated with the biotinylated anti-CD31 aptamer, allowing covalent immobilization via the biotin-neutravidin linkage. To optimize performance and prevent clogging, the column contains a mixture of aptamer-coated and uncoated agarose beads in a 1:1 ratio. The uncoated beads act as spacers, reducing cell-bead interactions outside the capture zone and facilitating bead resuspension during the release step.

The total bead volume is optimized to 800 µL (400 µL coated + 400 µL uncoated), which provides saturation-level capture capacity for typical PBMC inputs (e.g., 2.5 × 10⁷ cells/mL). The column is pre-equilibrated with binding buffer and can be stored at 4°C for up to 72 hours prior to use. During operation, the device is mounted vertically on a syringe pump, and cell suspensions are loaded at a controlled flow rate of 50 µL/min to maximize contact time and binding efficiency. After washing, the column is disconnected, and the beads are manually resuspended using a standard 1 mL syringe with an 18-gauge needle, generating shear forces sufficient to release captured cells. The eluate is then centrifuged to pellet the cells, which are resuspended in saline or culture medium for immediate use.

The device is designed for single-use, disposable application, ensuring sterility and eliminating cross-contamination risks. It is compatible with standard operating room equipment and can be integrated into existing surgical workflows without requiring specialized training or infrastructure.

### IV. Methods of Enriching Cells from a Mixed Cell Population

The method of the present invention for enriching CD31+ cells comprises the following steps: (a) preparing a cell suspension from a biological sample such as whole blood or bone marrow aspirate; (b) loading the suspension onto the aptamer-functionalized column under binding conditions; (c) washing away unbound cells; (d) releasing captured CD31+ cells by mechanical resuspension of the bead bed; and (e) collecting the released cells by centrifugation.

In step (a), PBMCs are isolated from whole blood using standard density gradient centrifugation (e.g., Ficoll-Paque) or commercial kits (e.g., SepMate). The cells are resuspended in binding buffer—a physiological saline solution containing calcium and magnesium ions to support CD31 conformation—at a concentration of 2.5 × 10⁷ cells/mL.

In step (b), the cell suspension is introduced into the column at a flow rate of 50 µL/min using an infusion pump. The slow flow rate ensures adequate contact time between cells and aptamer-coated beads, promoting high capture efficiency. The column is maintained in a vertical orientation to prevent cell sedimentation and channeling.

In step (c), after the entire sample has passed through, the column is rinsed with 3 mL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at 300 µL/min to remove non-specifically bound or trapped cells.

In step (d), the column is detached from the pump, and 1 mL of PBS is drawn into a syringe attached to the column outlet. The plunger is then repeatedly depressed and withdrawn 10–15 times to vigorously resuspend the bead bed. This action generates localized shear forces that disrupt the aptamer-CD31 bonds, releasing the captured cells into suspension. No enzymes, denaturants, or competing ligands are used.

In step (e), the eluate is transferred to a microcentrifuge tube and centrifuged at 300 × g for 3 minutes to pellet the cells. The supernatant is discarded, and the cell pellet is resuspended in an appropriate carrier solution. The final product exhibits >85% CD31+ purity, >95% viability, and is free of beads and aptamers.

This method is completed within 30–45 minutes, making it suitable for intraoperative use. It can be adapted for negative selection by using aptamers against undesirable markers (e.g., CD8) and collecting the flow-through fraction.

### V. Kits

The invention further provides kits for performing the enrichment method. Each kit includes: (i) one or more pre-packed enrichment columns containing aptamer-coated and uncoated agarose beads; (ii) a vial of lyophilized or liquid biotinylated anti-CD31 aptamer (for user-coating if desired); (iii) binding buffer and wash buffer; (iv) sterile syringes and Luer-lock connectors; and (v) detailed instructions for use. The kit components are sterilized by gamma irradiation or ethylene oxide and packaged in a sealed, tamper-evident container. Optional components may include cell collection tubes, viability dyes, and quality control reagents.

The kit is designed for use by clinicians or surgical staff with minimal training. All reagents are ready-to-use, and the procedure requires no specialized equipment beyond a standard syringe pump. The kit supports processing of 10–100 mL of whole blood, yielding 10–20 million enriched CD31+ cells—sufficient for most regenerative applications.

### VI. Methods of Treatment

The enriched CD31+ cell populations produced by the invention may be used in a variety of therapeutic contexts. In one embodiment, the cells are administered directly to a site of tissue injury, such as a bone fracture non-union, myocardial infarct zone, or ischemic limb, via injection or topical application. In another embodiment, the cells are seeded onto biocompatible scaffolds (e.g., collagen, hydroxyapatite) and implanted surgically. Alternatively, the conditioned medium from the enriched cells—which contains secreted regenerative factors—may be used as a cell-free therapeutic agent.

The treatment is particularly indicated for patients at risk of impaired healing, including diabetics, elderly individuals, or those with vascular insufficiency. Clinical protocols may involve a single intraoperative dose or repeated administrations, depending on the condition. Because the cells are autologous and minimally manipulated, they are exempt from many regulatory hurdles, enabling rapid clinical adoption.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and methods

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from leukoreduction filters or fresh whole blood using Ficoll-Paque density gradient centrifugation or SepMate kits. Cells were resuspended in PBS with 2% fetal calf serum at 2.5 × 10⁷ cells/mL. Enrichment columns were assembled by packing 800 µL of agarose beads (400 µL aptamer-coated at 5 µg/mL, 400 µL uncoated) into microcentrifuge tubes fitted with 20 µm filters. PBMCs were loaded at 50 µL/min, washed with 3 mL PBS, and cells released by syringe-mediated bead resuspension. Viability was assessed using Muse® Cell Analyzer. Flow cytometry used antibodies against CD31, CD14, CD3, CD19, and CD45. Angiogenic potential was tested via HUVEC tube formation assay on Matrigel; osteogenic potential via Alizarin Red staining of MSCs treated with conditioned medium.

### Aptamer Selection and Validation

A commercially available biotinylated DNA aptamer against CD31 was validated by flow cytometry. PBMCs incubated with aptamer showed dose-dependent binding, with 99% sensitivity and >96% specificity at 5 µg/mL. Co-staining with anti-CD31 antibody confirmed co-localization. Magnetic bead isolation using the aptamer yielded CD31+ purity comparable to antibody-based methods, confirming suitability for cell enrichment.

### Development of a CD31+ Cell Enrichment System

Columns were optimized by varying aptamer concentration (0.5–20 µg/mL), bead composition (coated vs. mixed), and bead volume (200–800 µL). A 5 µg/mL aptamer concentration with 50:50 coated:uncoated beads and 800 µL total volume provided optimal yield (25%), purity (87%), and viability (97%). Clogging was eliminated by inclusion of uncoated beads. Microscopy confirmed absence of beads in eluate.

### CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations

System validation using fresh PBMCs showed enrichment of CD31+ cells from 64% to 87%. Subpopulation analysis revealed significant increases in CD14+ monocytes and CD3+CD31+ naïve T cells, both known to secrete regenerative factors. No residual aptamer or beads were detected in the final product.

### Angiogenic and Osteogenic Potential of CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations

Conditioned medium from enriched cells increased HUVEC tube length by 1.9-fold versus control. In MSC cultures, it enhanced mineralization by 1.8-fold, as measured by Alizarin Red quantification. These results confirm the functional superiority of the enriched population for regenerative applications.
