# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of vaccinology, specifically to recombinant poxvirus-based vaccine vectors and their use in the prevention of infectious diseases. More particularly, the invention provides a novel recombinant Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV) vaccine platform derived from the Copenhagen strain of vaccinia virus (VACV), engineered to be replication-defective in mammalian cells through targeted deletion of the D13L gene encoding an essential viral assembly protein. The invention further encompasses multivalent SCV vaccines capable of expressing heterologous antigens from multiple pathogens within a single construct, thereby enabling simultaneous immunization against distinct viral agents such as Zika virus (ZIKV) and chikungunya virus (CHIKV). The invention also includes methods for the production of these recombinant SCV vaccines in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells stably expressing the D13 assembly protein and the host range factor CP77, which support high-yield manufacturing under serum-free, protein-free conditions compatible with current good manufacturing practices (cGMP). Additionally, the invention provides methods of inducing protective immune responses in non-human primates and, by extension, in humans, through administration of SCV-based vaccines either as a single dose or in a homologous prime-boost regimen, without reliance on heterologous vector systems. The invention further relates to the induction of broadly neutralizing antibody responses against diverse genotypes of CHIKV and robust neutralizing antibody responses against ZIKV, sufficient to confer protection against viremia following viral challenge. Moreover, the invention contemplates the potential utility of SCV vectors as smallpox vaccines due to their capacity to elicit anti-VACV neutralizing antibodies comparable in magnitude to those induced by established Modified Vaccinia Ankara (MVA) and ACAM2000 vaccines.

## BACKGROUND

Poxvirus-based vaccine vectors have long been recognized for their exceptional utility in vaccine development due to several advantageous biological and practical characteristics. Among these are their large genomic capacity—capable of accommodating at least 25,000 base pairs of foreign DNA—which enables the co-expression of multiple antigens from different pathogens within a single viral construct. This feature is particularly valuable for the development of combination vaccines targeting co-circulating arboviruses such as ZIKV and CHIKV, which share overlapping geographic distributions, mosquito vectors, and potential for human co-infection. Furthermore, poxvirus vectors exhibit remarkable thermostability, allowing for cold chain-independent distribution—a critical advantage for deployment in resource-limited settings where refrigerated transport and storage infrastructure may be lacking. Importantly, poxviruses replicate exclusively in the cytoplasm of infected cells and do not integrate into the host genome, thereby eliminating concerns regarding insertional mutagenesis that can arise with retroviral or lentiviral vector systems. Additionally, certain poxvirus vectors, including Modified Vaccinia Ankara (MVA), have demonstrated compatibility with needle-free delivery methods, enhancing patient compliance and reducing risks associated with sharps.

Historically, numerous poxvirus vector platforms have been developed and clinically evaluated, including NYVAC, ALVAC, fowlpox, and MVA. Of these, MVA has emerged as a leading candidate due to its extensive safety profile, having been administered to approximately 120,000 individuals during the final stages of the global smallpox eradication campaign without significant adverse events. MVA is currently marketed as IMVANEX/IMVAMUNE for smallpox and mpox prophylaxis. However, a well-documented limitation of MVA and other replication-defective poxvirus vectors is their suboptimal immunogenicity when used as standalone vaccines in naïve individuals. While highly effective as boosters in heterologous prime-boost regimens—such as DNA prime followed by MVA boost—these vectors often fail to induce robust primary immune responses sufficient for protection against challenging pathogens like ZIKV or CHIKV. This necessitates complex vaccination schedules involving multiple platforms, increasing logistical burden, cost, and potential regulatory hurdles.

A major constraint in the manufacturing of traditional poxvirus vaccines, including MVA, NYVAC, and ALVAC, is their dependence on primary chicken embryo fibroblasts (CEFs) for viral propagation. CEFs are not only biologically variable between batches but also pose challenges in scalability, standardization, and compliance with modern biopharmaceutical manufacturing standards. Although alternative cell lines such as EB66 (a duck embryo-derived line) have been explored, they remain avian in origin and do not fully address the need for a mammalian, cGMP-compatible production system.

In response to these limitations, the Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV) platform was developed as a next-generation poxvirus vaccine vector based on the Copenhagen strain of VACV. Unlike MVA, which was attenuated through over 500 serial passages in CEFs resulting in numerous genomic deletions and mutations, SCV is rationally engineered through precise deletion of the D13L gene, which encodes the D13 scaffold protein essential for virion morphogenesis. This single genetic modification renders SCV incapable of producing infectious progeny in standard mammalian cells, thereby ensuring safety while preserving the full coding capacity and immunostimulatory properties of the parental VACV strain. Critically, SCV can be efficiently propagated in genetically modified Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells that stably express both D13 and the host range protein CP77, enabling high-titer production under defined, serum-free, protein-free culture conditions. CHO cells are a well-established, FDA-approved platform for the manufacture of therapeutic proteins and monoclonal antibodies, offering significant advantages in terms of scalability, consistency, and regulatory acceptance.

Recent preclinical studies have demonstrated the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of a bivalent SCV vaccine (SCV-ZIKA/CHIK) encoding the structural polyprotein cassette of CHIKV (from the 2006 Indian Ocean lineage isolate 06–021) and the prME envelope glycoproteins of ZIKV (from the 2015 Brazilian isolate SPH2015). In mouse models, a single dose of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK conferred protection against lethal challenge with both viruses, highlighting the potential of this platform for rapid, single-dose immunization. However, translation to higher species requires validation in non-human primates (NHPs), which more closely recapitulate human immune responses and disease pathogenesis. Prior to the present invention, no data existed on the immunogenicity, safety, or protective efficacy of SCV-based vaccines in NHPs, nor on their ability to induce cross-neutralizing antibodies against diverse CHIKV genotypes or to protect against ZIKV viremia—a key correlate of congenital transmission risk.

Moreover, while combination vaccines offer clear public health benefits—including reduced shot burden, improved compliance, and lower costs—their development is often hindered by antigenic competition, formulation complexity, and manufacturing challenges. The demonstration that a single SCV construct can simultaneously express two large antigen cassettes and elicit robust, protective immune responses against both targets would represent a significant advance in multivalent vaccine design.

Finally, the potential for antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) of dengue virus (DENV) infection following ZIKV vaccination remains a theoretical safety concern, particularly given structural similarities between flaviviruses. Although natural ZIKV infection has not been conclusively linked to ADE of DENV in epidemiological studies, poorly designed vaccines expressing immature or misfolded envelope proteins could theoretically increase this risk. Thus, any novel ZIKV vaccine platform must be evaluated for ADE potential, ideally in relevant animal models.

The present invention addresses these unmet needs by providing comprehensive evidence from an NHP study demonstrating that SCV-ZIKA/CHIK is safe, immunogenic, and protective against ZIKV challenge, induces broadly neutralizing antibodies against multiple CHIKV genotypes, and elicits anti-VACV responses suggestive of potential utility as a smallpox vaccine—all achievable through simple homologous vaccination regimens without the need for heterologous priming.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a recombinant Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV) vaccine platform and its application in the prevention of infections caused by Zika virus (ZIKV), chikungunya virus (CHIKV), and variola virus (the causative agent of smallpox). The SCV vector is derived from the Copenhagen strain of vaccinia virus (VACV) and is rendered replication-defective in mammalian cells through a targeted deletion of the D13L gene, which encodes the essential viral assembly protein D13. Recombinant SCV vaccines according to the invention are produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells that have been genetically modified to stably express both D13 and the host range protein CP77, enabling high-yield, scalable, and cGMP-compliant manufacturing under serum-free and protein-free conditions.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a multivalent SCV vaccine comprising a single recombinant viral construct encoding at least two heterologous antigen expression cassettes: (i) a chikungunya virus (CHIKV) structural polyprotein cassette comprising the nucleotide sequence encoding the C-E3-E2–6K-E1 polyprotein from a 2006 Indian Ocean lineage (IOL) isolate (strain 06–021, GenBank: AM258992); and (ii) a Zika virus (ZIKV) prME cassette comprising the nucleotide sequence encoding the prM and E envelope glycoproteins from a 2015 Brazilian isolate (ZikaSPH2015, GenBank: KU321639). This bivalent vaccine, designated SCV-ZIKA/CHIK, is capable of eliciting robust humoral immune responses against both target pathogens following either a single immunization or a homologous prime-boost regimen.

The invention further provides methods of immunizing a subject against ZIKV and CHIKV, comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine. In a preferred embodiment, the subject is a human, and the vaccine is administered intramuscularly in a dose ranging from 10⁷ to 10⁸ plaque-forming units (pfu). The invention demonstrates that a single dose of 10⁷ pfu induces detectable neutralizing antibodies against ZIKV and CHIKV, while two doses of 10⁸ pfu administered three to five weeks apart significantly enhance neutralizing titers and confer near-sterilizing immunity against ZIKV challenge in non-human primates.

Additionally, the invention shows that SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccination induces broadly neutralizing antibodies against multiple CHIKV genotypes, including West African (WA), East/Central/South African (ECSA), Indian Ocean lineage (IOL), and Asian genotypes. Notably, while early antibody responses (day 35 post-vaccination) show preferential neutralization of IOL and WA strains—correlating with sequence identity in the immunodominant linear E2EP3 epitope—later responses (day 70) exhibit significant epitope spreading, resulting in equivalent neutralization across all tested genotypes. This time-dependent broadening of the humoral response underscores the capacity of the SCV platform to drive maturation of cross-reactive B-cell clones.

The invention also establishes that SCV-based vaccines elicit anti-VACV neutralizing antibodies and IgG responses comparable in magnitude to those induced by licensed MVA and ACAM2000 smallpox vaccines. Following two immunizations with 10⁸ pfu of SCV, non-human primates achieved mean reciprocal PRNT50 titers of 79 ± 24 against VACV Western Reserve strain, suggesting potential utility of SCV as a next-generation smallpox/mpox vaccine.

Furthermore, the invention includes data demonstrating the absence of antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) of dengue virus disease in an IFNAR1−/− mouse model following SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccination, alleviating a key safety concern associated with flavivirus vaccine development.

In summary, the present invention provides a safe, manufacturable, and highly immunogenic poxvirus vector platform capable of delivering multivalent protection against emerging arboviruses and orthopoxviruses through simplified vaccination regimens, thereby addressing critical gaps in global vaccine preparedness.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Construction of Vaccines

The recombinant Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV) vaccines described herein were constructed using molecular cloning techniques to insert heterologous antigen expression cassettes into the genome of a replication-defective vaccinia virus (VACV) Copenhagen strain backbone. The parental SCV vector was engineered through precise deletion of the D13L open reading frame (ORF), which encodes the D13 scaffold protein essential for the formation of immature virions during poxvirus morphogenesis. This deletion renders the virus incapable of completing its replication cycle in standard mammalian cells, as the absence of D13 prevents proper virion assembly and egress. However, the vector retains full capacity for gene expression and immunogen presentation due to preservation of all other viral genes involved in transcription, translation, and immune modulation.

To enable propagation of the D13L-deleted SCV, a complementing cell line was developed by stably transfecting Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO-S) cells with expression plasmids encoding both the VACV D13 protein and the host range factor CP77 (also known as C7L homolog). CP77 is required to overcome intrinsic cellular restrictions in CHO cells that would otherwise block VACV replication. The resulting CHO-D13/CP77 cell line supports high-titer production of infectious SCV particles while maintaining the replication defect in non-complementing cells, including human and non-human primate tissues.

For the construction of the bivalent SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine, two independent antigen expression cassettes were inserted into non-essential loci of the SCV genome using homologous recombination or CRISPR/Cas9-mediated targeted integration. The first cassette comprises the full-length structural polyprotein gene of chikungunya virus (CHIKV), derived from the 2006 Indian Ocean lineage (IOL) isolate 06–021 (GenBank accession AM258992). This cassette encodes the C-E3-E2–6K-E1 polyprotein (1249 amino acids, 3747 bp), which is proteolytically processed during viral maturation to yield the individual structural proteins necessary for virion assembly and infectivity. The second cassette encodes the prM and E envelope glycoproteins of Zika virus (ZIKV), derived from the 2015 Brazilian epidemic strain ZikaSPH2015 (GenBank accession KU321639). This prME cassette (692 amino acids, 2067 bp) directs the synthesis of a precursor polyprotein that is cleaved by host furin-like proteases to generate mature M and E proteins, which form the surface spikes of the flavivirus particle and are the primary targets of neutralizing antibodies.

Each expression cassette was placed under the control of a strong synthetic early/late VACV promoter to ensure high-level transcription during both phases of the viral life cycle. Flanking sequences homologous to the intended insertion sites in the SCV genome were included to facilitate precise integration via homologous recombination. Following transfection of the parental SCV into CHO-D13/CP77 cells along with the donor plasmids containing the antigen cassettes, recombinant viruses were isolated by plaque purification under selective conditions. PCR analysis confirmed the correct insertion of both CHIKV and ZIKV cassettes and the absence of wild-type revertants or unintended genomic rearrangements.

A control SCV vector (SCV-control) was constructed in parallel, containing no heterologous inserts but otherwise identical in genetic background and production methodology. Both SCV-ZIKA/CHIK and SCV-control vaccines were produced in serum-free, protein-free media using the CHO-D13/CP77 cell line. Infected cells were harvested 48–72 hours post-infection, subjected to multiple freeze-thaw cycles to release cell-associated virus, and clarified by low-speed centrifugation. The viral supernatant was then purified by ultracentrifugation through a 36% sucrose cushion to remove cellular debris and concentrate infectious particles. The final vaccine preparations were resuspended in Tris-buffered saline (10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 150 mM NaCl), aliquoted into single-use vials at concentrations of 10⁸ pfu per 500 µl, and stored at −80°C. Sterility testing was performed according to FDA-CBER guidelines (21 CFR 610.12), and mycoplasma contamination was ruled out using a commercial PCR-based detection kit. Titers were confirmed by plaque assay on complementing CHO-D13/CP77 cells, and stability during simulated transport on dry ice was verified.

This rational, modular design allows for straightforward incorporation of additional antigen cassettes or modifications (e.g., fusion loop mutations to mitigate ADE risk) without compromising vector integrity or manufacturability, establishing SCV as a flexible platform for rapid response to emerging infectious disease threats.

### Example 2

In this example, the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine were evaluated in a non-human primate (NHP) model using Indian rhesus macaques (Macaca mulatta). Sixteen healthy, ZIKV- and CHIKV-naïve animals (8 males, 8 females; aged 1–6 years; weight 2.0–8.0 kg) were randomized into four groups based on gender and weight. Group 1 (n=5) received a single intramuscular (i.m.) injection of 10⁷ plaque-forming units (pfu) of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK in the right quadriceps. Group 2 (n=5) received two i.m. injections of 10⁸ pfu of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK, administered on days 0 and 35. Group 3 (n=4) served as a vector control and received two doses of 10⁸ pfu of SCV-control (lacking ZIKV and CHIKV inserts) on the same schedule. Group 4 (n=2) received a positive control vaccine consisting of formalin-inactivated ZIKV PRVABC59 administered i.m. on days 0 and 35.

All procedures were conducted under Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee approval, with animals housed in AAALAC-accredited facilities and monitored twice daily for clinical signs. No fever, weight loss, or vaccine-related adverse events were observed, except for one animal in the SCV-control group that developed a bilateral inguinal rash attributed to environmental factors rather than vaccination.

Serum samples were collected on days 0, 20, 35, 40, 70, and 77 for immunological and virological analyses. On day 70, all animals were challenged subcutaneously in the left forearm with 10⁵ pfu of wild-type ZIKV strain PRVABC59. Post-challenge viremia was monitored by quantitative reverse-transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR) of serum RNA, with a lower limit of reliable quantification (LLoRQ) established at 860 ZIKV RNA copies/ml.

Anti-ZIKV neutralizing antibody responses were measured using a 50% Focus Reduction Neutralization Test (FRNT50) against the homologous PRVABC59 strain. All animals in Groups 1 and 2 developed detectable neutralizing antibodies by day 35, with significantly higher titers in Group 2 (mean FRNT50 = 1:1280) compared to Group 1 (mean FRNT50 = 1:160). A second immunization in Group 2 resulted in a 5.5-fold increase in neutralizing titers by day 70 (p=0.009), whereas titers in Group 1 remained stable. In contrast, Group 3 showed no significant neutralizing activity, confirming the specificity of the response to the ZIKV insert.

Following ZIKV challenge, both SCV-ZIKA/CHIK groups exhibited significantly reduced viremia compared to controls, as determined by area under the curve (AUC) analysis of serum RNA copies over time (p<0.01, Kolmogorov–Smirnov test). Notably, none of the animals in Group 2 had detectable viremia above the LLoRQ at any time point, indicating near-sterilizing immunity. In Group 1, two animals (5550 and 5552) showed transient low-level viremia (1–3 days above LLoRQ), and these individuals also had the lowest neutralizing titers on day 70. A strong inverse correlation was observed between pre-challenge FRNT50 titers and post-challenge viral load (Spearman’s r = −0.89, p<0.001), supporting a protective role for neutralizing antibodies. The positive control group (Group 4) also showed complete suppression of viremia, validating the challenge model.

These results demonstrate that SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccination induces dose-dependent neutralizing antibody responses that correlate with protection against ZIKV viremia in NHPs, with two doses of 10⁸ pfu providing optimal efficacy.

### Example 3

This example details the evaluation of anti-chikungunya virus (CHIKV) humoral immune responses induced by SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccination in non-human primates. Given the genetic diversity of CHIKV, which is classified into three major genotypes—West African (WA), East/Central/South African (ECSA), and Asian—with the Indian Ocean lineage (IOL) representing a subclade of ECSA, it was critical to assess the breadth of neutralizing activity across representative isolates.

Serum samples from vaccinated NHPs (Groups 1 and 2 from Example 2) were tested in a 50% Focus Reduction Neutralization Test (FRNT50) against four CHIKV strains: (i) LR2006-OPY1 (IOL, Reunion Island, 2006); (ii) 37997 (WA, Senegal, 1983); (iii) R99659 (Asian, British Virgin Islands, 2014); and (iv) AF15561 (Asian, Thailand, 1962). The vaccine antigen is derived from the IOL strain 06–021, which shares 100% identity in the E2EP3 linear neutralizing epitope with LR2006-OPY1 and 37997, but contains one or two non-conservative substitutions in this region relative to the Asian strains.

On day 35 post-vaccination, animals in both groups exhibited significantly higher neutralizing titers against IOL and WA strains compared to Asian strains (mean 5.6-fold higher in Group 1; 30-fold higher in Group 2, p<0.001). This pattern is consistent with an early, E2EP3-focused antibody response that preferentially recognizes conserved epitopes. However, by day 70, neutralizing titers against Asian strains had increased dramatically—5.2-fold in Group 1 (p=0.009) and 44-fold in Group 2 (p=0.005)—while responses to IOL/WA strains showed modest increases (7.1-fold in Group 2). As a result, by day 70, neutralizing titers against all four genotypes were not significantly different within each group.

The mean reciprocal FRNT50 titers on day 70 were 59.4 ± 8.9 (range 28–93) in Group 1 and 736 ± 187 (range 90–3090) in Group 2, well above the putative correlates of protection established for related alphaviruses (e.g., ≥10 for Ross River virus; ≥35 in mouse models). These findings indicate that SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccination not only induces potent neutralizing antibodies but also drives epitope spreading over time, resulting in broad cross-genotype reactivity. This temporal evolution of the humoral response suggests that even a single dose may provide durable, broad protection, though a booster enhances magnitude and accelerates breadth.

### Conclusion

The data presented herein conclusively demonstrate that the SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine is safe, immunogenic, and protective in a non-human primate model. A single immunization induces detectable neutralizing antibodies against both ZIKV and CHIKV, while a homologous prime-boost regimen significantly enhances titers and confers near-complete protection against ZIKV viremia. The vaccine elicits broadly neutralizing antibodies against diverse CHIKV genotypes through a time-dependent process of epitope spreading, overcoming initial immunodominance of conserved linear epitopes. Furthermore, SCV vaccination generates anti-VACV responses comparable to licensed smallpox vaccines, suggesting dual utility. Critically, the platform obviates the need for heterologous prime-boost strategies, simplifying clinical deployment. These attributes position SCV as a versatile, next-generation vector for multivalent vaccine development against emerging infectious diseases.

### Example 4

This example describes the assessment of anti-vaccinia virus (VACV) immune responses induced by SCV vaccination in non-human primates. Since SCV is derived from the Copenhagen strain of VACV, it retains numerous native VACV antigens that can stimulate cross-reactive immunity. To evaluate this, serum samples from NHPs vaccinated with SCV-ZIKA/CHIK (Group 2, n=5) or SCV-control (Group 3, n=4) were analyzed for VACV-specific neutralizing antibodies and total IgG.

Neutralizing activity was measured using a 50% Plaque Reduction Neutralization Test (PRNT50) against the VACV Western Reserve strain. Following two immunizations with 10⁸ pfu, both groups achieved comparable mean reciprocal PRNT50 titers of 79 ± 24 by day 70. In contrast, a single dose of 10⁷ pfu (Group 1) induced minimal neutralizing activity, detectable in only one animal. Total anti-VACV IgG was quantified by endpoint ELISA, yielding mean reciprocal titers of 800 ± 163 across both groups by day 70, with kinetics paralleling neutralizing responses.

These titers are comparable to those reported in human phase III trials: MVA vaccination (two doses of 10⁸ TCID₅₀) yielded mean PRNT50 of 94, while ACAM2000 (single dose) produced 65.6. Although correlates of protection against smallpox are not fully defined, neutralizing antibodies against both intracellular mature virions (MVs) and extracellular enveloped virions (EVs) are considered important. While the assay used MVs, the robust response suggests potential efficacy. These findings support the repurposing of SCV as a safer, replication-defective smallpox/mpox vaccine, particularly given its favorable safety profile in SCID mice and NHPs.

### Example 5

This example addresses the safety concern of antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) of dengue virus (DENV) infection following ZIKV vaccination. Due to structural homology in the flavivirus envelope protein, cross-reactive antibodies induced by ZIKV vaccines could theoretically enhance DENV entry into Fc receptor-bearing cells, exacerbating disease.

To evaluate this risk, sera from SCV-ZIKA/CHIK-vaccinated mice were tested in an established IFNAR1−/− mouse model of ADE. In this model, passive transfer of subneutralizing anti-ZIKV antibodies prior to DENV challenge leads to enhanced vascular leakage, cytokine storm, and mortality. Mice receiving immune sera from SCV-ZIKA/CHIK-vaccinated donors showed no increase in DENV-induced morbidity or mortality compared to controls receiving naïve sera. Histopathological and virological analyses confirmed absence of enhanced DENV replication or tissue damage.

These results indicate that SCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccination does not induce ADE-prone antibodies, likely due to proper folding and presentation of prME antigens in the context of the poxvirus vector, which favors conformational neutralizing epitopes over fusion loop-directed cross-reactive ones. This finding alleviates a major regulatory concern for ZIKV vaccine development.

### Example 6

This example outlines the manufacturing process for SCV vaccines under conditions suitable for clinical translation. SCV-ZIKA/CHIK and SCV-control were produced in a research-grade BSL-2 facility using the CHO-D13/CP77 cell line in serum-free, protein-free medium. Cells were infected at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.01–0.1, and cultures were maintained for 48–72 hours until >90% cytopathic effect was observed.

Harvested cells were lysed by three freeze-thaw cycles in Tris-buffered saline, and debris was removed by centrifugation at 2,000 × g for 10 minutes. The supernatant was layered onto a 36% sucrose cushion and ultracentrifuged at 25,000 rpm for 90 minutes in a SW28 rotor. Pelleted virions were resuspended in formulation buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 150 mM NaCl), sterile-filtered (0.22 µm), and aliquoted into cryovials at 10⁸ pfu/500 µl.

Quality control included: (i) PCR confirmation of insert integrity and absence of wild-type virus; (ii) sterility testing in thioglycollate and soybean-casein media; (iii) mycoplasma testing by PCR ELISA; and (iv) endotoxin testing (<1 EU/ml). Stability studies showed <0.5 log₁₀ loss in titer after 72 hours on dry ice, supporting global distribution. This process is readily scalable to cGMP bioreactors, leveraging established CHO cell infrastructure.

### Example 7

This example discusses the implications of the NHP data for human vaccine development. The observation that two doses of 10⁸ pfu SCV-ZIKA/CHIK conferred near-sterilizing immunity against ZIKV viremia suggests that similar regimens could prevent maternal-fetal transmission in humans. Although sterilizing immunity was not absolute, the strong inverse correlation between neutralizing titers and viral load implies that even partial suppression may reduce congenital Zika syndrome (CZS) risk, analogous to rubella vaccination.

The induction of broadly neutralizing CHIKV antibodies supports coverage against global circulating strains, including recent ECSA introductions in the Americas. The homologous prime-boost efficacy eliminates reliance on DNA or adenoviral primes, streamlining clinical protocols. Moreover, the anti-VACV responses open avenues for dual-purpose vaccines against mpox and arboviruses.

Limitations include the lack of CHIKV challenge data (due to BSL-3 constraints) and T-cell assessments. Future studies will evaluate pregnant NHP models, T-cell responses, and cGMP-manufactured lots in Phase I trials. Nevertheless, the current data robustly support advancement of SCV-ZIKA/CHIK as a promising multivalent vaccine candidate.
