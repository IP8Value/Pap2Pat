# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by cognitive decline, memory impairment, and behavioral disturbances. It is the most common cause of dementia among older adults and represents a major public health challenge due to its increasing prevalence in aging populations worldwide. The neuropathological hallmarks of AD include extracellular amyloid plaques composed primarily of aggregated amyloid-β (Aβ) peptides, intracellular neurofibrillary tangles formed by hyperphosphorylated tau protein, synaptic dysfunction, neuronal loss, and neuroinflammation. Among these features, the accumulation of Aβ peptides—particularly those derived from the amyloid precursor protein (APP)—has been central to the amyloid cascade hypothesis, which posits that Aβ aggregation initiates a cascade of events leading to neurodegeneration and clinical symptoms.

Amyloid-β peptides are generated through sequential proteolytic cleavage of APP by β- and γ-secretases. The most abundant Aβ species are Aβ1-40 and Aβ1-42, with the latter being more hydrophobic and prone to aggregation. In addition to full-length Aβ peptides, N-terminally truncated and post-translationally modified variants have been identified in the brains of AD patients. One such variant is pyroglutamate-modified Aβ starting at position 3 (AβpE3), which results from the cyclization of the N-terminal glutamate residue to form pyroglutamate. This modification enhances the peptide’s resistance to degradation, increases its hydrophobicity, and promotes its aggregation into stable oligomeric and fibrillar structures. AβpE3 has been shown to be highly abundant in amyloid plaques and cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) in both sporadic and familial AD cases, and it is considered a key contributor to disease pathogenesis.

Over the past two decades, significant efforts have been directed toward developing therapeutic strategies targeting Aβ. These include active and passive immunotherapies using antibodies designed to recognize various epitopes of Aβ. While several anti-Aβ antibodies have demonstrated efficacy in reducing plaque burden and improving cognitive outcomes in preclinical models, their translation to human clinical trials has yielded mixed results. Some antibodies, such as aducanumab and lecanemab, have shown modest clinical benefits but are associated with adverse effects like amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA). A critical limitation of many existing antibodies is their lack of specificity for the most pathogenic Aβ species. For instance, antibodies targeting generic Aβ epitopes may bind to monomers, oligomers, and fibrils indiscriminately, potentially neutralizing beneficial forms or failing to engage the most toxic aggregates.

Emerging evidence suggests that soluble oligomers of Aβ—rather than insoluble fibrils or plaques—are the primary mediators of synaptic toxicity and cognitive dysfunction in early AD. Among these, low-molecular-weight (LMW) oligomers, including dimers, trimers, and tetramers, have been shown to impair long-term potentiation, induce synaptic loss, and trigger neuroinflammatory responses. However, the heterogeneity of Aβ oligomers, their transient nature, and the lack of conformation-specific detection tools have hindered the development of targeted therapies. Moreover, the presence of multiple Aβ isoforms, including N-terminally truncated and modified species like AβpE3, adds complexity to the identification of the most relevant therapeutic targets.

In this context, the discovery of AβpE3-specific oligomers and the development of antibodies capable of selectively recognizing these pathogenic conformers represent a significant advancement. Prior art includes antibodies such as 2-48, which recognizes the N-terminal pyroglutamate residue of AβpE3, but these reagents often fail to distinguish between monomeric, oligomeric, and fibrillar forms of AβpE3. Consequently, there remains a critical unmet need for antibodies that specifically target the oligomeric state of AβpE3, which is believed to be particularly neurotoxic and relevant to early disease mechanisms. Such antibodies could serve dual purposes: as diagnostic biomarkers for detecting pathogenic Aβ species in biological fluids and as therapeutic agents for selectively neutralizing toxic oligomers without disrupting physiological Aβ functions.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel monoclonal antibody, designated 9D5, that exhibits high specificity for low-n oligomeric forms of AβpE3-42 while showing no cross-reactivity with full-length Aβ1-42 or other Aβ variants. This unique binding profile enables the selective detection and neutralization of pathogenic AβpE3 oligomers, offering a precision approach to AD diagnosis and treatment. The background of the invention thus lies at the intersection of molecular neuroscience, immunology, and biotechnology, leveraging advances in antibody engineering and Aβ biochemistry to develop a targeted intervention for a complex and multifactorial disease.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel monoclonal antibody, designated 9D5, that specifically recognizes low-molecular-weight oligomeric forms of pyroglutamate-modified amyloid-β peptide starting at position 3 (AβpE3-42), without cross-reacting with full-length Aβ1-42 or other Aβ isoforms. This antibody demonstrates exceptional selectivity for oligomeric AβpE3-42 species ranging from tetramers to decamers, as confirmed by size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) coupled with dot blot analysis and Western blotting under both native and denaturing conditions. The 9D5 antibody was generated by immunizing Balb/c mice with synthetic AβpE3-38 peptide, followed by hybridoma technology and rigorous screening for clones exhibiting specific binding to AβpE3-42 and AβpE3-38, but not to AβpE1-42 or unmodified Aβ1-42.

The invention further encompasses the use of the 9D5 antibody as a diagnostic tool for the detection of AβpE3 oligomers in biological samples, including human plasma and brain tissue. Notably, an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) developed using 9D5 as the capture antibody revealed significantly reduced levels of AβpE3 oligomers in the plasma of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) patients compared to healthy controls, suggesting a potential biomarker application for early disease detection and monitoring. This finding is consistent with the hypothesis that AβpE3 oligomers are sequestered in the brain during AD progression, leading to their depletion in peripheral circulation.

Additionally, the invention provides a therapeutic method for treating Alzheimer’s disease and related disorders by administering the 9D5 antibody to subjects in need thereof. Passive immunization of 5XFAD transgenic mice—a well-established model of AD—with 9D5 resulted in significant reductions in amyloid plaque burden, including total Aβ, Aβ42, Aβ40, and AβpE3 deposits in both the hippocampus and cortex. Furthermore, 9D5 treatment led to a marked decrease in intraneuronal AβpE3 oligomer accumulation and improved behavioral outcomes, as evidenced by stabilization of performance in the elevated plus maze test, a measure of anxiety and exploratory behavior. These therapeutic effects are attributed to the antibody’s ability to selectively bind and neutralize neurotoxic AβpE3 oligomers, thereby inhibiting their aggregation into higher-order structures and preventing downstream pathological consequences.

In vitro studies further support the therapeutic potential of 9D5. Thioflavin T (ThT) aggregation assays demonstrated that 9D5 effectively suppresses the formation of higher-order aggregates of AβpE3-42 without interfering with the initial rapid formation of low-n oligomers, indicating that the antibody acts by stabilizing or sequestering these intermediate species. Moreover, cytotoxicity assays using SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells showed that 9D5 completely abrogates the toxic effects of AβpE3-42, while having no impact on the toxicity of Aβ1-42, underscoring its specificity for the AβpE3 isoform.

The invention also includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the 9D5 antibody in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for systemic or central nervous system administration. These compositions may be formulated for intravenous, intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, or intranasal delivery, depending on the desired pharmacokinetic profile and blood-brain barrier penetration. The antibody may be used alone or in combination with other therapeutic agents targeting different aspects of AD pathology, such as tau aggregation inhibitors, anti-inflammatory drugs, or neuroprotective compounds.

In summary, the present invention offers a highly specific, conformation-selective antibody that targets a pathologically relevant subset of Aβ oligomers implicated in the early stages of Alzheimer’s disease. By enabling precise detection and neutralization of AβpE3 oligomers, the 9D5 antibody represents a significant advancement in both diagnostic and therapeutic approaches to AD, with potential applications in patient stratification, disease monitoring, and targeted immunotherapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a monoclonal antibody, designated 9D5, that exhibits high specificity for low-molecular-weight oligomeric forms of pyroglutamate-modified amyloid-β peptide starting at position 3 (AβpE3-42). This antibody was developed through a rigorous process of immunization, hybridoma generation, and epitope-specific screening, resulting in a reagent with unique binding properties that distinguish it from previously described anti-Aβ antibodies. The detailed characterization of 9D5, along with its diagnostic and therapeutic applications, is described herein.

The 9D5 antibody is a mouse monoclonal IgG2b antibody produced by the hybridoma cell line PG3-38 9D5H6, which was deposited in accordance with the Budapest Treaty. The antibody was generated by immunizing three Balb/c mice with synthetic AβpE3-38 peptide, a fragment encompassing residues 3 to 38 of Aβ with an N-terminal pyroglutamate modification. Following immunization, lymph node cells were harvested and fused with the myeloma cell line P3-X63-Ag8 to generate hybridomas. The resulting supernatants were screened by ELISA using a panel of Aβ peptides, including AβpE3-38, AβpE3-42, and AβpE1-42. Clones that exhibited strong reactivity with AβpE3-38 and AβpE3-42 but no signal with AβpE1-42 were selected for subcloning and further characterization. The 9D5 clone was ultimately chosen based on its superior specificity and affinity for oligomeric AβpE3-42.

To confirm the oligomer-specific binding of 9D5, size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) was performed on freshly dissolved synthetic Aβ1-42 and AβpE3-42 peptides under native conditions. The eluted fractions were analyzed by dot blot using either the generic Aβ antibody W0-2 (which recognizes residues 5–8 of Aβ) or the 9D5 antibody. SEC of Aβ1-42 revealed a distribution of aggregates including low-n oligomers (4–6×), higher oligomers (16–20×), and some monomeric/dimeric species. In contrast, AβpE3-42 exhibited a distinct aggregation profile with prominent peaks corresponding to smaller assemblies (1–2×) and low-n oligomers (4–6×), but reduced levels of higher-order aggregates. Dot blot analysis with W0-2 detected all Aβ1-42 and AβpE3-42 fractions, confirming the presence of Aβ across the elution profile. However, 9D5 exclusively recognized low-n oligomeric fractions (4–10×) of AβpE3-42 and showed no reactivity with any Aβ1-42 fractions, demonstrating its strict dependence on both the pyroglutamate modification and the oligomeric conformation.

Under denaturing conditions, Western blot analysis of AβpE3-42 revealed a single low-molecular-weight band detected by 9D5, consistent with monomeric or dimeric species, while no signal was observed for Aβ1-42. This indicates that the 9D5 epitope is conformation-dependent and is lost upon denaturation of higher-order aggregates, further supporting its specificity for soluble oligomeric forms. The molecular weight standards used for SEC calibration—including blue dextran (>200 kDa), bovine serum albumin (67 kDa), ovalbumin (43 kDa), chymotrypsinogen (25 kDa), RNaseA (13.7 kDa), aprotinin (6.5 kDa), and vitamin B12 (1.35 kDa)—allowed for accurate stoichiometric estimation of the oligomeric states, confirming that 9D5 binds species in the range of approximately 16–40 kDa, corresponding to tetramers through decamers of AβpE3-42.

The functional consequences of 9D5 binding were evaluated in vitro using Thioflavin T (ThT) aggregation assays and neurotoxicity studies. In ThT assays, AβpE3-42 exhibited rapid formation of intermediate oligomeric assemblies without a pronounced lag phase, in contrast to Aβ1-42, which showed a typical lag phase followed by fibril elongation. When 9D5 was added to AβpE3-42 at a molar ratio of 1:76 (antibody:peptide), the formation of higher aggregates beyond the inflection point (~200 minutes) was significantly inhibited, while the initial rapid oligomerization remained unaffected. This suggests that 9D5 does not prevent the nucleation of oligomers but rather blocks their further assembly into larger, potentially fibrillar structures.

In parallel, the neurotoxicity of Aβ peptides was assessed using SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells. Incubation with 2 μM AβpE3-42 resulted in significant cell death, as measured by MTS assay, whereas co-administration of 9D5 (1 ng/μL) completely abolished this toxic effect. In contrast, Aβ1-42-induced toxicity was unaffected by 9D5, confirming the antibody’s isoform and conformation specificity. These findings indicate that 9D5 exerts a protective effect by neutralizing the toxic oligomeric species of AβpE3-42, likely through sequestration or conformational stabilization.

The diagnostic utility of 9D5 was demonstrated in human plasma samples from AD patients and healthy controls. An ELISA was developed using 9D5 as the capture antibody and biotinylated 2-48 (an antibody against the N-terminal pyroglutamate of AβpE3) as the detection reagent. Plasma from AD patients showed a 46% reduction in AβpE3 oligomer levels compared to healthy controls (p < 0.05), suggesting that these oligomers are depleted from circulation as they accumulate in the brain. This observation aligns with previous reports of reduced autoantibodies against AβpE3 in AD plasma and supports the use of 9D5-based assays as a non-invasive biomarker for AD.

Immunohistochemical analysis of post-mortem human brain tissue further validated the specificity of 9D5. In non-demented controls, no 9D5-positive plaques were observed, although some cases showed weak vascular staining. In contrast, both sporadic and familial AD cases exhibited strong 9D5 immunoreactivity in intraneuronal compartments and cerebral amyloid angiopathy, with minimal overlap with 4G8 (an antibody against Aβ17–24) staining patterns. Notably, cases with presenilin-1 mutations displayed prominent intraneuronal 9D5 staining, highlighting the relevance of AβpE3 oligomers in genetic forms of AD.

In transgenic mouse models, 9D5 immunoreactivity was age-dependent. No signal was detected in 3-month-old 5XFAD mice, but robust intraneuronal staining emerged in the subiculum by 6 months and intensified by 12 months. Co-localization studies revealed that 9D5-positive aggregates were internalized by microglia (as indicated by Iba-1 co-staining) but not by astrocytes (GFAP-negative), suggesting a role for microglial clearance in AβpE3 oligomer dynamics. Intraneuronal 9D5 staining was also observed in spinal cord motor neurons of aged APP/PS1KI mice, corroborating the neuron-specific accumulation of these oligomers.

Therapeutically, passive immunization of 4.5-month-old 5XFAD mice with 250 μg of purified 9D5 administered intraperitoneally once weekly for six weeks resulted in significant reductions in amyloid plaque load in both hippocampus and cortex. ELISA of brain homogenates confirmed decreased levels of AβpE3 in both TBS-soluble and SDS-soluble fractions. Behaviorally, treated mice showed stabilized performance in the elevated plus maze, indicating mitigation of anxiety-related deficits. These results establish 9D5 as a potent therapeutic agent capable of modifying AD pathology and improving functional outcomes.

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise the 9D5 antibody in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), with optional stabilizers, surfactants, or preservatives. The antibody may be administered via intravenous, intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, or intranasal routes, with dosing regimens tailored to achieve sufficient central nervous system exposure. Given its high specificity, 9D5 is expected to minimize off-target effects and reduce the risk of adverse events such as ARIA, which have been associated with less selective anti-Aβ antibodies.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Generation and Characterization of 9D5 Antibody**

Balb/c mice were immunized with synthetic AβpE3-38 peptide. Hybridomas were generated by fusing lymph node cells with P3-X63-Ag8 myeloma cells. Supernatants were screened by ELISA against AβpE3-38, AβpE3-42, and AβpE1-42. Clone 9D5 was selected for its specific reactivity with AβpE3 peptides and lack of cross-reactivity with AβpE1-42. Isotyping confirmed 9D5 as an IgG2b antibody.

**Example 2: Size-Exclusion Chromatography and Dot Blot Analysis**

Synthetic Aβ1-42 and AβpE3-42 were monomerized and subjected to SEC on a Superdex 75 column. Fractions were spotted on nitrocellulose and probed with W0-2 or 9D5. 9D5 detected only low-n oligomeric fractions (4–10×) of AβpE3-42, confirming its oligomer specificity.

**Example 3: Thioflavin T Aggregation Assay**

AβpE3-42 (55 μM) was incubated with or without 9D5 (1:76 molar ratio). ThT fluorescence was monitored over time. 9D5 inhibited the formation of higher aggregates after 200 minutes, demonstrating its ability to block oligomer maturation.

**Example 4: Neurotoxicity Assay**

SH-SY5Y cells were treated with Aβ1-42 or AβpE3-42 ± 9D5. Cell viability was assessed by MTS assay. 9D5 completely rescued AβpE3-42-induced toxicity but had no effect on Aβ1-42 toxicity.

**Example 5: Plasma ELISA for AD Diagnosis**

Plasma from AD patients and healthy controls was analyzed using a 9D5-capture/2-48-detection ELISA. AβpE3 oligomer levels were 46% lower in AD patients (p < 0.05), supporting diagnostic utility.

**Example 6: Immunohistochemistry in Human Brain**

Post-mortem brain sections from AD and control cases were stained with 9D5. Strong intraneuronal and CAA staining was observed in AD, but not in controls, confirming disease-specific accumulation.

**Example 7: Passive Immunization in 5XFAD Mice**

4.5-month-old 5XFAD mice received weekly intraperitoneal injections of 250 μg 9D5 for 6 weeks. Treated mice showed reduced plaque load, lower AβpE3 levels in brain lysates, and improved behavior in the elevated plus maze.

These examples collectively demonstrate the novelty, specificity, and therapeutic potential of the 9D5 antibody, establishing it as a valuable tool for the diagnosis and treatment of Alzheimer’s disease.
