# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of fluid dynamics and heat transfer engineering, specifically to engineered surfaces for simultaneous enhancement of convective heat transfer and reduction of hydrodynamic drag in fluid systems. More particularly, the invention pertains to liquid-infused surfaces (LIS) comprising transverse grooves filled with a liquid infusing phase that is immiscible with the external working fluid, wherein the thermal conductivity of the solid substrate forming the grooves is comparable to or less than that of the infusing liquid. The invention further encompasses methods for designing such surfaces based on dimensionless parameters governing the dispersive convection within the surface texture, as well as systems incorporating these surfaces for applications including but not limited to heat exchangers, microfluidic cooling devices, electronic thermal management systems, and energy-efficient fluid transport conduits. The invention exploits the coupling between momentum slip induced by the liquid-infused interface and thermally driven recirculation within the textured cavities to achieve a synergistic improvement in thermal performance without proportional increase in pumping power, thereby overcoming the classical trade-off between heat transfer augmentation and pressure drop penalty that has historically constrained the design of compact thermal systems.

## BACKGROUND

The pursuit of enhanced heat transfer with minimal hydraulic resistance has long represented a fundamental challenge in thermal-fluid engineering. Traditional approaches to augmenting heat transfer—such as surface roughening, extended surfaces (fins), or internal flow disruption via inserts—typically incur significant drag penalties due to increased momentum exchange at the fluid-solid interface. This adverse coupling arises because geometric features that disrupt the thermal boundary layer also disturb the velocity profile, leading to higher wall shear stress and consequently greater pumping power requirements. In laminar flows, where heat transfer is primarily conductive near the wall, surface modifications must penetrate deep into the thermal boundary layer to be effective, inevitably increasing friction. In turbulent flows, while eddy diffusivity enhances bulk heat transfer, surface textures can either suppress or amplify turbulence depending on their geometry, scale, and orientation, making predictive design difficult. The canonical Reynolds analogy, which posits a fixed relationship between momentum and heat transfer coefficients for smooth walls at unit Prandtl number, further underscores the intrinsic linkage between drag and heat flux in conventional systems, suggesting that decoupling these phenomena requires breaking the assumptions underlying this analogy.

Superhydrophobic surfaces (SHS), which trap air pockets in microstructured textures to create composite solid-gas interfaces, have demonstrated drag reduction through apparent slip at the liquid-air interface. However, their application to heat transfer enhancement has been limited by the extremely low thermal conductivity of air, which creates insulating regions that impede conductive heat flux from the solid substrate into the fluid. While longitudinal grooves aligned with the flow direction can mitigate this issue by preserving streamwise continuity of the solid, transverse configurations—which are more effective for certain manufacturing processes or structural considerations—suffer from severe thermal bottlenecks unless the trapped phase possesses high thermal conductivity. Liquid-infused surfaces (LIS) offer a compelling alternative by replacing the gas phase with a liquid that is immiscible with the external working fluid yet exhibits favorable thermal properties. The infusing liquid conforms to the texture, forming a dynamic interface that can support both momentum slip and thermal conduction, provided the liquid’s thermal conductivity is sufficiently high relative to the solid substrate.

Prior art in LIS for thermal applications has largely assumed isothermal solid textures or infinite thermal conductivity of the solid phase, simplifying the analysis but neglecting realistic material constraints. In practice, many promising substrate materials—particularly polymers used in lightweight or flexible electronics—exhibit thermal conductivities comparable to or even lower than common liquids such as water or organic solvents. For instance, polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS), a widely used elastomer in microfluidics, has a thermal conductivity of approximately 0.15 W/m·K, which is lower than that of water (0.6 W/m·K). Similarly, emerging applications in stretchable electronics employ liquid metal alloys like gallium-based eutectics (e.g., EGaIn) with thermal conductivities (~25–30 W/m·K) that rival those of stainless steel (~16 W/m·K), enabling matched-conductivity LIS designs. Despite these material possibilities, the role of convective heat transport within the infusing liquid phase—driven by shear-induced recirculation in the textured cavities—has been either overlooked or deemed negligible in previous theoretical models and numerical studies. Analytical frameworks such as those by Hatte & Pitchumani (2021) have modeled LIS heat transfer using thermal resistance networks but have implicitly assumed purely conductive transport within the texture, assigning a Nusselt number of unity to the surface layer and thereby underestimating the total heat flux when internal convection is present.

Furthermore, existing computational investigations of LIS in turbulent flows have focused predominantly on drag reduction or have considered only idealized thermal boundary conditions that do not reflect practical material pairings. The critical insight that dispersive convection—defined as the time-averaged, spatially periodic component of convective heat flux arising from the deterministic flow structure within the texture—can significantly enhance surface heat transfer when the solid and liquid thermal conductivities are comparable has not been systematically explored or quantified. This gap is particularly pronounced for transverse groove geometries, which induce strong spanwise-uniform recirculation vortices but have been dismissed in prior work due to perceived thermal inefficiency. The absence of a predictive scaling law linking the magnitude of this dispersive convection to operational and geometric parameters has hindered the rational design of LIS for thermal applications. Consequently, there remains a pressing need for a comprehensive framework that accounts for the coupled effects of momentum slip, internal convection, and realistic material properties to enable the simultaneous optimization of heat transfer and drag reduction in both laminar and turbulent regimes.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention provides a liquid-infused surface (LIS) system and associated design methodology that achieves simultaneous enhancement of convective heat transfer and reduction of hydrodynamic drag by leveraging dispersive convection within transverse surface textures. The core innovation lies in the recognition that when the thermal conductivity of the solid substrate (κ_s) is comparable to or less than that of the infusing liquid (κ_i), the shear-driven recirculation within the textured cavities generates a net positive contribution to the total heat flux, termed dispersive convection (q_conv,d), which overcomes the thermal penalty of replacing solid material with liquid. This effect is quantified through a novel scaling relationship that expresses the relative contribution of dispersive convection to the total heat flux as a logarithmic function of a surface Péclet number (Pe_i), defined using the slip velocity at the liquid-infused interface and the groove depth. The invention further establishes that this scaling holds across both laminar and turbulent external flow regimes, enabling predictive design without recourse to computationally intensive simulations.

In one aspect, the invention comprises a heat transfer surface comprising a solid substrate defining a plurality of transverse grooves, each groove having a depth k and a pitch p, wherein the grooves are filled with an infusing liquid immiscible with an external working fluid, and wherein the thermal conductivity of the solid substrate κ_s satisfies κ_s ≤ κ_i, where κ_i is the thermal conductivity of the infusing liquid. The surface is configured such that the ratio of groove depth to total system height k/(h + 2k) is at least 0.1, and the surface Péclet number Pe_i = ρ c_p U_s k / κ_i falls within the range 10 < Pe_i < 1000, where U_s is the slip velocity at the interface, ρ is the density, and c_p is the specific heat capacity of the infusing liquid. Under these conditions, the dispersive convection contributes at least 1% to the total heat flux, resulting in a heat transfer enhancement of at least 3% compared to a smooth reference surface with equivalent thermal properties, while simultaneously achieving a drag reduction of at least 2%.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for designing a liquid-infused heat transfer surface, comprising the steps of: (a) selecting an infusing liquid and a solid substrate such that κ_s ≤ κ_i; (b) determining the operational flow conditions to compute the expected slip velocity U_s; (c) calculating the surface Péclet number Pe_i = ρ c_p U_s k / κ_i; and (d) selecting groove dimensions k and p such that Pe_i lies within the range 10 to 1000 and the solid fraction φ_s = w/p is less than or equal to 0.5, where w is the groove width. This method ensures that the dispersive convection is maximized while maintaining structural integrity and manufacturability of the surface texture.

In yet another aspect, the invention includes a thermal system incorporating the liquid-infused surface, such as a heat exchanger, microchannel cooler, or fluid transport conduit, wherein the simultaneous heat transfer enhancement and drag reduction lead to a net improvement in the heat flux to drag ratio (q/τ) of at least 10% compared to a conventional smooth-walled system operating under identical conditions. The system leverages the amplification effect of turbulent fluctuations, wherein the baseline Nusselt number of the external flow acts as a multiplier on the dispersive convection contribution, thereby extending the benefits of the invention to high-Reynolds-number applications.

The invention thus overcomes the historical trade-off between heat transfer and drag by exploiting the dual functionality of the liquid-infused interface: providing momentum slip to reduce shear stress while enabling internal convection to augment heat flux. By grounding the design in physically meaningful dimensionless parameters and validated scaling laws, the invention enables the systematic development of high-performance thermal surfaces for next-generation energy-efficient systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a liquid-infused surface (LIS) architecture and associated design principles that enable concurrent enhancement of convective heat transfer and reduction of hydrodynamic drag in fluid flow systems. The foundation of the invention rests on the discovery that transverse groove textures filled with an infusing liquid can generate beneficial dispersive convection when the thermal conductivity of the solid substrate is comparable to or less than that of the infusing liquid. This section provides a comprehensive exposition of the physical mechanisms, mathematical formulations, design criteria, and implementation strategies that constitute the invention.

The liquid-infused surface of the invention comprises a solid substrate machined, etched, molded, or otherwise fabricated to define a periodic array of transverse grooves. As used herein, "transverse" denotes that the grooves extend perpendicular to the primary direction of external fluid flow, creating spanwise-uniform cavities that induce two-dimensional recirculation patterns. Each groove is characterized by a depth k (measured from the nominal surface plane to the groove bottom), a width w (the open dimension parallel to the surface), and a pitch p (the center-to-center distance between adjacent grooves). The solid fraction φ_s is defined as the ratio of the solid land width to the pitch, φ_s = (p - w)/p, and typically ranges from 0.1 to 0.5 in preferred embodiments to balance structural robustness with fluidic and thermal performance. The grooves are completely filled with an infusing liquid that is immiscible with the external working fluid, forming a stable liquid-liquid interface at the nominal surface plane (y = 0). The infusing liquid is selected based on compatibility with the substrate material, chemical stability under operating conditions, and crucially, thermal conductivity κ_i that satisfies κ_i ≥ κ_s, where κ_s is the thermal conductivity of the solid substrate. Representative material pairings include: (1) a PDMS substrate (κ_s ≈ 0.15 W/m·K) infused with water (κ_i ≈ 0.6 W/m·K); (2) a stainless steel substrate (κ_s ≈ 16 W/m·K) infused with a gallium-indium-tin eutectic alloy (κ_i ≈ 25 W/m·K); or (3) a silicon substrate (κ_s ≈ 150 W/m·K) infused with liquid sodium (κ_i ≈ 140 W/m·K), among others.

The external working fluid flows over the liquid-infused interface under a pressure gradient or applied body force, with bulk properties characterized by density ρ_∞, dynamic viscosity μ_∞, specific heat capacity c_p,∞, and thermal conductivity κ_∞. The flow may be laminar or turbulent, with a bulk Reynolds number Re_∞ = ρ_∞ U_∞ h / μ_∞, where U_∞ is a characteristic bulk velocity and h is the channel half-height or equivalent length scale. The thermal behavior is further described by the Prandtl number Pr_∞ = c_p,∞ μ_∞ / κ_∞, which governs the relative thickness of the velocity and thermal boundary layers. At the liquid-infused interface, the external fluid experiences an apparent slip due to the reduced shear stress at the liquid-liquid boundary, characterized by a slip velocity U_s equal to the streamwise velocity of the infusing liquid at y = 0. This slip velocity depends on the viscosity ratio μ_i / μ_∞, the groove geometry, and the applied shear stress, and can be estimated from analytical models or empirical correlations for transverse LIS.

The key innovation of the invention arises from the heat transfer mechanisms within this configuration. When the external fluid and substrate are maintained at different temperatures (e.g., T_u at the upper channel wall and T_l at the lower substrate base), heat flows from the hot to the cold region through a combination of conduction and convection in all phases. Critically, the shear stress exerted by the external flow on the liquid-infused interface drives a recirculating motion within each groove—a cavity vortex—that advects thermal energy along closed streamlines. This motion gives rise to a dispersive convective heat flux, denoted q_conv,d, which represents the time-averaged, spatially periodic component of convection resulting from the deterministic flow structure imposed by the texture. Unlike random turbulent fluctuations, which average to zero over sufficiently long times, dispersive convection persists in both laminar and turbulent regimes and contributes directly to the net heat transfer across the surface.

The total surface-averaged heat flux q can be rigorously decomposed using a generalized Fukagata-Iwamoto-Kasagi (FIK) identity for the energy equation, yielding:

q = q_cond,∞ + q_cond,s + q_cond,i + q_conv,r + q_conv,d

where q_cond,∞, q_cond,s, and q_cond,i are the conductive heat flux contributions from the external fluid, solid substrate, and infusing liquid, respectively; q_conv,r is the convective heat flux from random (turbulent) fluctuations; and q_conv,d is the dispersive convective heat flux from the texture-induced recirculation. In laminar flow, q_conv,r = 0, and the heat flux simplifies to q = q_cond,∞ + q_cond,s + q_cond,i + q_conv,d. The reference heat flux q_0 for a smooth surface with the same substrate material and boundary conditions is q_0 = q_cond,∞ + q_cond,s, since there is no infusing liquid or texture-induced convection.

The invention demonstrates that when κ_s ≤ κ_i, the combined effect of conduction through the higher-conductivity infusing liquid and dispersive convection leads to q > q_0, i.e., heat transfer enhancement relative to the smooth reference. Specifically, for κ_s = κ_i, the conductive terms q_cond,s + q_cond,i equal the smooth-wall conduction q_cond,s,0, and any enhancement arises solely from q_conv,d. For κ_s < κ_i, both enhanced conduction and convection contribute to the improvement. Conversely, if κ_s >> κ_i, the replacement of highly conductive solid with less conductive liquid dominates, leading to q < q_0 despite the presence of convection.

The magnitude of the dispersive convection is governed by a surface Péclet number defined as Pe_i = ρ c_p U_s k / κ_i, where U_s is the slip velocity, k is the groove depth, and the fluid properties correspond to the infusing liquid. Through extensive numerical simulations across a wide range of parameters, the invention establishes the following scaling law for the relative contribution of dispersive convection to the total heat flux:

q_conv,d / q ≈ [k / (h + 2k)] × 0.027 ln(Pe_i) - 0.035

This relationship holds for 10 < Pe_i < 1000, 2 ≤ p/k ≤ 8, φ_s ≤ 0.5, and κ_s = κ_i = κ_∞. The prefactor k/(h + 2k) represents the geometric scaling, reflecting that the dispersive convection is confined to the groove region and its influence diminishes as the system height increases. The logarithmic dependence on Pe_i indicates that the convective enhancement grows slowly but persistently with increasing flow speed or decreasing thermal diffusivity, making it relevant across a broad operational range. For Pe_i < 10, the convection is negligible; for Pe_i > 1000, the approximation breaks down as the assumption of linear temperature profiles fails.

In turbulent external flows, the total heat flux enhancement is amplified by the presence of random fluctuations. Although q_conv,d itself remains small (typically 1–2% of q), it acts as a seed that is magnified by the baseline Nusselt number Nu_0 of the smooth-wall flow. The invention derives the following expression for the total heat transfer enhancement:

q / q_0 ≈ 1 + Nu_0 × (q_conv,d / q_0)

This shows that the same dispersive convection that provides modest gains in laminar flow can yield substantial improvements in turbulent flow, where Nu_0 may be 5–10 or higher. For example, with q_conv,d / q_0 = 0.005 and Nu_0 = 9, the enhancement reaches 4.5%, consistent with simulation results reported in the research paper.

The design methodology of the invention proceeds as follows: First, select an infusing liquid and solid substrate such that κ_i ≥ κ_s. Second, determine the expected operating conditions (flow rate, fluid properties) to estimate the slip velocity U_s, either from analytical models (e.g., Schönecker et al. for Stokes flow) or empirical correlations. Third, compute Pe_i = ρ c_p U_s k / κ_i and select k such that 10 < Pe_i < 1000. Fourth, choose p and w to satisfy 2 ≤ p/k ≤ 8 and φ_s ≤ 0.5, ensuring the scaling law validity and structural integrity. Finally, verify that the resulting surface provides the desired balance of heat transfer enhancement and drag reduction, with the latter typically ranging from 2% to 8% for transverse LIS depending on viscosity ratio and texture geometry.

The invention is implemented in various thermal systems, including plate heat exchangers where LIS plates separate hot and cold streams; microchannel coolers for high-power electronics, where the small dimensions naturally lead to high Pe_i; and pipeline transport systems, where drag reduction lowers pumping costs while maintaining or improving heat transfer for temperature-sensitive fluids. The liquid-infused interface may be stabilized by surface chemistry (e.g., preferential wetting of the substrate by the infusing liquid) or geometric confinement (e.g., re-entrant structures), and the infusing liquid may be replenished through integrated reservoirs to compensate for long-term depletion.

In summary, the invention provides a paradigm-shifting approach to thermal surface design by harnessing dispersive convection in liquid-infused textures, enabled by strategic matching of solid and liquid thermal conductivities. The predictive scaling laws and clear design criteria overcome the limitations of prior art, offering a robust pathway to energy-efficient thermal systems with simultaneous heat transfer and drag performance improvements.
