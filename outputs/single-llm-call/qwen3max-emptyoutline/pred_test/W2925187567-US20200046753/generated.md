# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of molecular therapeutics and gene silencing, specifically to a nonviral, hybrid mRNA/DNA-based system for sustained cytoplasmic expression of short hairpin RNA (shRNA) and its subsequent processing into small interfering RNA (siRNA) for prolonged RNA interference (RNAi)-mediated gene silencing. More particularly, the invention provides a T7 RNA polymerase (T7pol)-driven autogene system that enables long-term production of shRNA in the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells without requiring nuclear entry of genetic material, thereby overcoming critical limitations of conventional plasmid-based shRNA expression systems and synthetic siRNA delivery methods. The invention further encompasses a liposomal delivery vehicle, specifically composed of 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphatidylcholine (DOPC), for the systemic administration of the hybrid system to target tissues, such as solid tumors, in vivo. This technology represents a significant advancement in the development of RNAi-based therapeutics for chronic diseases, including cancer, where sustained suppression of disease-associated genes is required over extended periods without the need for frequent re-administration of the therapeutic agent.

## BACKGROUND ART

RNA interference (RNAi) is a naturally occurring, evolutionarily conserved cellular mechanism that regulates gene expression through the sequence-specific degradation of messenger RNA (mRNA). Since its discovery, RNAi has emerged as a powerful tool for both functional genomics and therapeutic intervention, offering the potential to silence virtually any disease-causing gene with high specificity. The core effector molecules of RNAi are small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), typically 21- to 23-nucleotide double-stranded RNA duplexes. When introduced into cells, these exogenous siRNAs are loaded into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), which uses one strand of the duplex as a guide to identify and cleave complementary target mRNA, thereby preventing its translation into protein. While chemically synthesized siRNAs have been widely used in research and have entered clinical trials, their therapeutic utility is severely limited by their transient nature. In standard cell culture systems, the gene silencing effect induced by a single transfection of synthetic siRNA typically lasts only 2 to 4 days due to rapid degradation by nucleases, dilution through cell division, and the non-renewable nature of the administered dose. This necessitates repeated administrations for any sustained therapeutic effect, which is not only impractical for chronic disease management but also increases the risk of off-target effects and immune activation.

To address this limitation, researchers have developed strategies for the endogenous expression of siRNA precursors, namely short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs). These shRNAs are transcribed from DNA vectors within the cell and are subsequently processed by the cellular machinery, specifically the enzyme Dicer, into mature siRNAs. The most common approach utilizes plasmid DNA vectors where the shRNA sequence is placed under the control of a RNA polymerase III (Pol III) promoter, such as the U6 or H1 promoter. These promoters are strong, constitutive, and produce transcripts with well-defined start and end points, making them ideal for shRNA expression. However, this nuclear-based system faces a formidable barrier: the inefficient delivery of plasmid DNA from the cytoplasm into the nucleus. It is estimated that less than 1% of internalized plasmid DNA successfully reaches the nucleus, a problem that is exacerbated in non-dividing or post-mitotic cells where the nuclear envelope remains intact. Consequently, the levels of shRNA expression achieved by nonviral plasmid delivery are often significantly lower than those achieved by viral vectors, which have evolved mechanisms to efficiently deliver their genetic cargo to the nucleus. Furthermore, even when nuclear entry is successful, the expression from these plasmids is often not sustained over long periods, failing to provide the durable silencing required for many therapeutic applications.

Viral vectors, including adenoviruses, lentiviruses, and adeno-associated viruses (AAVs), have proven highly effective in mediating long-term shRNA expression both in vitro and in vivo. They can achieve high transduction efficiencies and stable integration (in the case of lentiviruses) or episomal persistence, leading to gene silencing that can last for weeks or months. Despite their efficacy, the clinical translation of viral vectors for RNAi therapy is hampered by significant safety concerns. These include the potential for insertional mutagenesis, which can lead to oncogenesis, and the induction of strong, sometimes life-threatening, immune responses against the viral components or the transduced cells. Additionally, the large-scale manufacturing of high-titer, clinical-grade viral vectors is complex and expensive, further limiting their widespread use. As a result, there remains a critical unmet need for a nonviral platform that can combine the safety and manufacturability of synthetic systems with the long-lasting gene silencing capability of viral vectors.

In an attempt to circumvent the nuclear barrier, cytoplasmic shRNA expression systems have been explored. One such strategy involves a two-plasmid system where one plasmid expresses the bacteriophage T7 RNA polymerase (T7pol) under the control of a mammalian promoter like CMV, and the other plasmid contains the shRNA sequence under the control of a T7 promoter. T7pol is unique in that it can function in the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells without requiring any host cofactors. However, this system still requires the nuclear entry of the first plasmid to express T7pol, thus inheriting the same fundamental limitation of inefficient nuclear delivery. Moreover, previous attempts using this approach have not demonstrated a significant improvement in the duration of shRNA expression compared to conventional methods. The T7 autogene system, which has been successfully used for high-level protein expression, offers a more promising avenue. In this system, the T7pol gene itself is placed under the control of a T7 promoter, creating a positive feedback loop. Once an initial amount of T7pol is present, it can transcribe its own mRNA from the autogene plasmid, leading to a self-amplifying cascade of T7pol production. This results in very high levels of the polymerase, which can then drive the expression of any co-delivered T7 promoter-controlled transgene. While this system has been used for protein production, its application for the sustained cytoplasmic expression of shRNA has not been previously reported or realized.

## DISCLOSURE

### Technical Problem

The primary technical problem addressed by the present invention is the inability of existing RNAi technologies to achieve long-term, sustained gene silencing in a safe and practical manner. Conventional synthetic siRNA therapies are transient, requiring frequent and often impractical re-dosing, which can lead to cumulative toxicity and poor patient compliance. Plasmid DNA-based shRNA systems, while capable of longer expression, are critically hampered by the extremely low efficiency of nuclear delivery of the plasmid, especially in non-dividing cells and in vivo, which results in weak and inconsistent silencing. Viral vector-based shRNA systems, though potent and long-lasting, carry unacceptable risks of immunogenicity and insertional mutagenesis, along with high manufacturing costs, which have stalled their clinical progress. Existing cytoplasmic shRNA expression systems, such as the pCMV/pT7_T7pol system, fail to solve the nuclear entry problem because they still rely on a nuclear promoter (e.g., CMV) to initiate the expression of the T7 polymerase. Therefore, there exists a significant gap in the art for a nonviral, non-integrating, and safe RNAi platform that can generate therapeutic levels of siRNA for a prolonged period—on the order of weeks—without requiring the genetic material to enter the nucleus or the patient to undergo repeated injections.

### Technical Solution

The present invention provides a novel and elegant solution to this long-standing problem through the development of a T7 autogene-based hybrid mRNA/DNA system for sustained cytoplasmic shRNA expression. This system completely bypasses the need for nuclear entry of any genetic component by utilizing a carefully designed combination of three key elements: (1) an in vitro transcribed, 5′-capped T7 RNA polymerase (T7pol) mRNA, (2) a minimal, linear double-stranded DNA fragment containing a T7 promoter and a shRNA sequence (pT7/shRNA DNA fragment), and (3) a T7 autogene plasmid. The T7 autogene plasmid is engineered such that the T7pol gene is placed downstream of a T7 promoter and an Encephalomyocarditis virus (EMCV) Internal Ribosomal Entry Site (IRES) element, and upstream of a polyA sequence and a T7 terminator. The process begins with the co-delivery of these three components into the cytoplasm of a target cell. The 5′-capped T7pol mRNA is immediately translated by the host cell's ribosomes to produce the initial pool of T7pol protein. This initial T7pol then binds to the T7 promoter on the autogene plasmid and transcribes a new mRNA that encodes T7pol and is fused to the IRES element. The IRES allows this newly transcribed mRNA to be efficiently translated in the cytoplasm, independent of the standard 5′-cap-dependent translation initiation, thereby producing more T7pol protein. This creates a powerful, autocatalytic positive feedback loop that rapidly amplifies the intracellular concentration of T7pol to very high levels. A portion of this abundant T7pol then binds to the T7 promoters on the co-delivered pT7/shRNA DNA fragments, driving the robust and sustained transcription of shRNA directly in the cytoplasm. The resulting shRNA is then processed by the cell's endogenous RNAi machinery (Dicer and RISC) into mature, functional siRNA, which mediates the long-term silencing of the target gene. To enable systemic delivery, this entire hybrid system is encapsulated within a neutral liposomal vehicle composed of DOPC, which facilitates its accumulation in target tissues, such as solid tumors, via the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect.

### Advantageous Effects of the Invention

The present invention offers a multitude of significant and advantageous effects over the prior art. First and foremost, it achieves unprecedented long-term gene silencing. The inventive system has been shown to produce mature siRNA for more than 13 days in vitro and to maintain significant target gene suppression in vivo for at least 6 days following a single intravenous injection, a duration far exceeding that of conventional siRNA or plasmid-based shRNA systems. Second, the system operates entirely in the cytoplasm, eliminating the major bottleneck of nuclear delivery that plagues all other nonviral DNA-based gene expression systems. This makes it highly effective in both dividing and non-dividing cells. Third, by leveraging the autocatalytic amplification of the T7 autogene system, the invention generates a massive and sustained intracellular concentration of T7pol, which in turn drives high-level shRNA transcription, leading to a potent and durable RNAi response. Fourth, the use of a neutral DOPC liposome as a delivery vehicle minimizes cytotoxicity and innate immune activation, which are common problems with cationic lipid or polymer-based carriers. Indeed, the inventive lipoplexes showed no significant cytotoxicity in cell culture and did not induce the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-α or INF-α in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) or in mouse serum, demonstrating a favorable safety profile. Fifth, the system is nonviral and non-integrating, thereby avoiding the serious safety risks of insertional mutagenesis and strong adaptive immune responses associated with viral vectors. Sixth, the modular design of the system allows for easy retargeting; by simply changing the sequence of the pT7/shRNA DNA fragment, the system can be adapted to silence any gene of interest. Finally, the ability to achieve long-term silencing with a single administration has profound implications for clinical translation, as it would drastically reduce the dosing frequency, improve patient compliance, and lower the overall cost of therapy for chronic diseases like cancer.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Example 1. Design and Preparation of pT7/T7 Polymerase System Capable of Sustained Expression of shRNA in Cytoplasm

The foundation of the present invention is a rationally designed T7 autogene system that functions exclusively in the cytoplasm to enable sustained shRNA expression. The system was constructed from three distinct nucleic acid components, each playing a critical role in the overall mechanism. The first component is a T7 autogene plasmid. This plasmid was engineered by cloning the full-length T7 RNA polymerase (T7pol) gene into a specialized vector backbone downstream of a T7 promoter and an Encephalomyocarditis virus (EMCV) Internal Ribosomal Entry Site (IRES) element. The T7 promoter serves as the binding site for the T7pol enzyme to initiate its own transcription. The EMCV IRES element is a crucial feature, as it allows the resulting mRNA transcript to be translated by the host cell's ribosomes in a cap-independent manner, which is essential for efficient protein production in the cytoplasm where the mRNA lacks a protective 5′-cap structure. The T7pol gene is further flanked downstream by a polyadenylation (polyA) signal sequence and a T7 transcription terminator to ensure proper mRNA processing and stability. The second component is a 5′-capped T7pol mRNA. This mRNA was synthesized in vitro using a DNA template containing a T7 promoter upstream of the T7pol coding sequence. During the in vitro transcription reaction, an anti-reverse cap analog (ARCA) was incorporated at a 4:1 molar ratio to GTP to ensure that the majority of the mRNA transcripts were correctly capped at the 5′-end. This 5′-cap is vital for the initial round of translation, as it protects the mRNA from degradation and facilitates its recognition by the cellular translation machinery. This initial burst of T7pol protein is the trigger that kickstarts the entire autogene amplification cycle. The third and final component is a minimal, linear double-stranded DNA fragment, referred to as the pT7/shRNA DNA fragment. This fragment was generated using an assembly PCR method and is designed to be just 64 base pairs in length. It contains a T7 promoter sequence at its 5′-end, followed by a sequence encoding a specific shRNA. The shRNA sequence was designed to target the red fluorescence protein (RFP) gene for experimental validation and consists of a 19-nucleotide sense sequence, a 9-nucleotide loop sequence, and the reverse complement of the 19-nucleotide sequence. The first nucleotide of the transcribed RNA was deliberately designed to be a guanosine, as this is a known requirement for efficient initiation by the T7 RNA polymerase. The small size of this DNA fragment is a significant advantage, as it is more easily delivered into cells and is less prone to degradation compared to larger plasmid constructs. The combined action of these three components—the initial T7pol mRNA, the self-amplifying T7 autogene plasmid, and the minimal shRNA-encoding DNA fragment—creates a powerful, self-sustaining cytoplasmic factory for the production of shRNA, which is then processed into the active siRNA form by the cell's native RNAi machinery.

### Example 2. Preparation of DOPC Liposome Transporter Loaded with Sustained Cytoplasmic Expression System for shRNA

For the systemic delivery of the hybrid mRNA/DNA system to target tissues in vivo, a biocompatible and efficient delivery vehicle was essential. Cationic liposomes, while commonly used for nucleic acid delivery, were deemed unsuitable due to their well-documented issues with high cytotoxicity, strong interaction with serum proteins, and potential to cause unwanted immune activation. Instead, a neutral liposomal formulation based on 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphatidylcholine (DOPC) was selected. DOPC is a naturally occurring phospholipid that has been shown to be effective for the systemic delivery of oligonucleotides in preclinical models, particularly in tumor xenograft studies, due to its ability to form stable nanoparticles and exploit the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect in leaky tumor vasculature. The lipoplexes, designated as auto_shRFP@LS for the RFP-targeting system, were prepared by mixing the three nucleic acid components—pT7/shRFP DNA fragment, 5′-capped T7pol mRNA, and T7 autogene plasmid—in a fixed molar ratio of 0.4:1:1 with DOPC lipid in the presence of excess t-butanol. Tween 20 was added as a stabilizer at a weight ratio of 1:19 (Tween 20 to total oligonucleotides/DOPC). The mixture was then lyophilized to remove the organic solvent, resulting in a dry lipid film. This film was subsequently hydrated with normal 0.9% saline solution to form the liposomal nanoparticles encapsulating the hybrid system. To ensure the quality of the final product, free, unencapsulated nucleic acids were removed by filtration using an Amicon ultracentrifugal filter with a 30 kDa molecular weight cutoff. The concentration of nucleic acids that passed through the filter was measured spectrophotometrically, confirming a complexing efficiency of over 90%. The final lipoplex suspension was reconstituted in saline to a standard concentration of 150 μg/mL for use in all subsequent experiments. For tracking and biodistribution studies, a fluorescent version of the lipoplex was prepared by incorporating a Cy5.5-labeled pT7/shRFP DNA fragment, which was synthesized by including Cy5.5-dUTP during the PCR assembly process, into the formulation at a weight ratio of 0.5%.

### Example 3. Examination of Biophysicochemical Properties of DOPC Liposome Transporter Loaded with Sustained Cytoplasmic Expression System for shRNA

The biophysicochemical properties of the auto_shRFP@LS lipoplexes were rigorously characterized to ensure they were suitable for biological applications. A gel retardation assay was performed to determine the optimal complexing ratio between the nucleic acid payload and the DOPC lipid. A fixed amount of the hybrid mRNA/DNA mixture was complexed with DOPC at various weight ratios (oligonucleotides:DOPC = 1:0, 1:5, 1:7.5, 1:10). The resulting complexes were run on a 1.5% agarose gel. At a weight ratio of 1:10, the vast majority of the nucleic acids were retained in the well of the gel, indicating complete complexation and the formation of large, negatively charged or neutral lipoplexes that could not migrate through the gel matrix. This 1:10 ratio was therefore selected as the optimal formulation for all subsequent studies. The size and surface charge of the lipoplexes were then measured using dynamic light scattering (DLS). The analysis revealed that the auto_shRFP@LS lipoplexes had a narrow size distribution with a mean hydrodynamic diameter of 31.4 ± 1.2 nm, which is an ideal size for exploiting the EPR effect in solid tumors. The zeta potential of the particles was measured to be +1.1 mV, confirming their near-neutral surface charge, which is beneficial for reducing non-specific interactions with blood components and cell membranes, thereby improving circulation time and biocompatibility. The cytotoxicity of the lipoplexes was evaluated in B16F10/RFP melanoma cells using an MTT assay. Cells were treated with increasing concentrations of auto_shRFP@LS (up to 60 μg/mL) and their viability was assessed at 24 and 48 hours post-transfection. The results showed no appreciable cytotoxicity at any of the tested concentrations, demonstrating that the DOPC liposome formulation is highly biocompatible and safe for use in cell culture and in vivo. Finally, the cellular internalization of the lipoplexes was confirmed using flow cytometry and confocal microscopy. B16F10 cells were transfected with Cy5.5-labeled auto_shRFP@LS, and after 1 hour, flow cytometry analysis showed that nearly 100% of the cells were positive for the Cy5.5 signal, indicating highly efficient cellular uptake. Confocal microscopic images clearly showed that the internalized lipoplexes were localized in the cytoplasmic compartment and were excluded from the nucleus, which is a critical observation that validates the cytoplasmic mode of action of the inventive system. In stark contrast, naked Cy5.5-labeled pT7/shRNA DNA fragments showed no cellular uptake, highlighting the indispensable role of the DOPC liposome as a delivery vehicle.

### Example 4. Experiment on Sustained Expression of T7 Polymerase Using Modified pT7/T7 Polymerase System

A key hypothesis of the present invention was that the co-delivery of T7pol mRNA with the T7 autogene plasmid would lead to a more efficient and sustained production of T7pol protein compared to traditional systems that rely on a nuclear promoter for the initial expression. To test this, a direct comparison was made between the inventive system (T7pol mRNA + T7 autogene plasmid) and a conventional pCMV-triggering T7 autogene system (pCMV_T7pol plasmid + T7 autogene plasmid). In the latter, the T7pol gene is under the control of a CMV promoter, which requires the plasmid to enter the nucleus for transcription. B16F10/RFP cells were transfected with either formulation, and the levels of T7pol protein were monitored over a period of 9 days using western blotting with a T7pol-specific antibody. The results were striking. The cells transfected with the inventive T7pol mRNA/autogene system showed a dramatic and continuous increase in T7pol protein levels, which is the hallmark of a successful autocatalytic amplification loop. High levels of T7pol were maintained for the entire 9-day duration of the experiment. In contrast, the cells transfected with the pCMV_T7pol/autogene system showed a much slower and lower rate of T7pol accumulation. Quantitative analysis of the western blot band intensities revealed that the inventive system produced a 4.0-fold higher level of T7pol protein over the 9-day period compared to the pCMV-triggering system (p < 0.0001). This significant enhancement is attributed to the fact that the T7pol mRNA can be immediately translated in the cytoplasm upon delivery, providing an instant and robust trigger for the autogene plasmid without the delay and inefficiency associated with nuclear entry and nuclear transcription. This experiment conclusively demonstrated that the hybrid mRNA/DNA design is a superior strategy for achieving the high and sustained levels of T7pol necessary to drive long-term shRNA expression.

### Example 5. Examination of Long-Term Cytoplasmic Expression of shRNAs by Auto_shRFP@LS and their Conversion to siRNAs

The ultimate goal of the inventive system is not merely to produce T7pol, but to use it to drive the sustained production of shRNA, which must then be processed into the active siRNA form. To quantitatively measure the production of mature siRNA over time, a highly sensitive and specific TaqMan small RNA assay was developed. First, a standard curve was generated using serial dilutions of a chemically synthesized siRNA guide strand that targets RFP (siRFP). This assay uses a stem-loop reverse transcription (RT) primer that is specific to the 3′-end of the mature siRNA guide strand, ensuring that only the processed, functional siRNA is detected, and not the precursor shRNA or the DNA template. The RT product is then amplified by qPCR using a FAM-labeled probe specific to the siRFP sequence. This assay was validated to be highly specific, as it did not detect any signal from synthetic shRFP hairpins or the pT7/shRFP DNA fragments. Using this assay, the levels of mature siRFP were measured in B16F10/RFP cells transfected with either the full auto_shRFP@LS system or a control system lacking the T7 autogene plasmid (auto(−)_shRFP@LS). The results showed that in cells receiving the full system, the levels of mature siRFP increased exponentially over time, reaching a maximum at around day 10 post-transfection. Critically, a significant amount of mature siRFP was still detectable at day 13, confirming the long-term production capability of the system. In contrast, the control system, which relied only on the initial supply of T7pol mRNA without the self-amplifying autogene, showed a much lower and linear increase in siRFP levels, which plateaued early. This direct correlation between the high levels of self-replenished T7pol and the sustained production of mature siRNA provides strong mechanistic evidence for the efficacy of the inventive platform.

### Example 6. Examination of In Vitro Gene Silencing by Auto shRNA @LS Nanoparticles

The functional consequence of the sustained siRNA production was evaluated by measuring the silencing of the target RFP gene in B16F10/RFP cells over an extended period. The silencing effect was assessed at multiple levels: mRNA, protein, and cellular fluorescence. Using quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR), the levels of RFP mRNA were measured for up to 13 days post-transfection. Cells treated with a single dose of conventional synthetic siRFP@LS showed a transient reduction in RFP mRNA, with a minimum of 75% knockdown at day 3, but the mRNA levels were fully restored by day 7. In stark contrast, cells treated with auto_shRFP@LS showed a steady and profound decrease in RFP mRNA, reaching a minimum of 54% knockdown at day 7, and the silencing effect was still significant at day 13. The control auto(−)_shRFP@LS system showed an intermediate effect, with a continuous decrease over 10 days but at a much lower efficiency. At the protein level, western blotting confirmed these findings. The reduction in RFP protein in the auto_shRFP@LS group was significant for at least 10 days, whereas the reduction from the synthetic siRNA group lasted only 3 days. Flow cytometry, which measures the RFP fluorescence of individual cells, provided a direct and quantitative readout of the silencing effect across the entire cell population. The auto_shRFP@LS treatment led to a maximal 49% reduction in RFP-positive cells at day 7, and a 33% reduction was still evident at day 10. The synthetic siRNA and the control system showed much weaker and shorter-lived effects. Collectively, these multi-faceted analyses demonstrate that the auto_shRNA@LS nanoparticles are capable of inducing a potent and remarkably prolonged gene silencing effect in vitro, far surpassing the capabilities of current state-of-the-art RNAi technologies.

### Example 7. Examination of Cancer Accumulation Property of Auto_shRNA@LS Nanoparticles in Mouse Xenograft Model

To evaluate the potential of the inventive system for in vivo cancer therapy, its biodistribution and tumor-targeting ability were investigated in a B16F10 melanoma xenograft mouse model. Mice bearing subcutaneous B16F10 tumors were given a single intravenous injection of either Cy5.5-labeled auto_shRFP@LS or a control of free Cy5.5-labeled siRFP. After 24 hours, the mice were sacrificed, and major organs (heart, liver, spleen, lungs, kidneys) along with the tumors were excised for ex vivo near-infrared fluorescence imaging. The results were highly informative. In the control group that received free siRFP, the fluorescence signal was almost exclusively localized to the kidneys, which is the primary route of clearance for small, unformulated oligonucleotides, and no signal was detected in the tumors. In contrast, the group that received the auto_shRFP@LS nanoparticles showed a strong and clear fluorescence signal in the tumor tissue. While some signal was also detected in the liver and kidneys, the preferential accumulation in the tumor was evident. This tumor-specific accumulation is attributed to the nanoparticle's size (~31 nm), which is well within the range to exploit the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect—a phenomenon where the leaky vasculature and poor lymphatic drainage of solid tumors allow nanoparticles of this size to extravasate from the bloodstream and accumulate in the tumor interstitium. This experiment provides compelling evidence that the DOPC liposomal formulation effectively delivers the hybrid mRNA/DNA system to the tumor site following systemic administration, a critical prerequisite for its therapeutic application.

### Example 8. Examination of Gene Silencing of Auto_shRNA@LS Nanoparticles in Mouse Xenograft Model

The final and most critical test of the inventive system was to determine if the tumor-accumulated nanoparticles could mediate long-term gene silencing in vivo following a single administration. B16F10/RFP tumor-bearing mice were injected once with either auto_shRFP@LS, a scrambled control (scrambled auto_shRFP@LS), synthetic siRFP@LS, or PBS. The RFP fluorescence from the tumors was then monitored non-invasively over six days using an in vivo imaging system (IVIS). The control groups (PBS and scrambled auto_shRFP@LS) showed a steady increase in RFP signal as the tumors grew, as expected. The synthetic siRFP@LS group showed a temporary slowing of the RFP signal increase for the first three days, but the signal then sharply rose to match the control groups, confirming the transient nature of conventional siRNA therapy. Remarkably, the auto_shRFP@LS group showed a significant and sustained suppression of the RFP signal throughout the entire six-day monitoring period, despite ongoing tumor growth. At the endpoint (day 6), ex vivo analysis of the excised tumors confirmed these findings. The RFP fluorescence intensity in the auto_shRFP@LS group was 2.7-fold lower than in the PBS control group. Immunohistochemical staining with an anti-RFP antibody showed a dense presence of RFP protein in the control tumors but a marked absence in the auto_shRFP@LS-treated tumors. Finally, qRT-PCR analysis of the tumor tissue revealed a 43% reduction in RFP mRNA levels in the auto_shRFP@LS group compared to the PBS control, while the other groups showed no significant reduction. These comprehensive in vivo data unequivocally demonstrate that a single intravenous injection of the auto_shRNA@LS nanoparticles can achieve significant and long-lasting target gene silencing at the tumor site, a feat that has not been accomplished by any other nonviral RNAi platform to date. This breakthrough has profound implications for the future of RNAi-based cancer therapy and the treatment of other chronic diseases.
