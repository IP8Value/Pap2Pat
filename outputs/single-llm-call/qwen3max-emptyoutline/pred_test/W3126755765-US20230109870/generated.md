# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and newborn screening, specifically to methods for the early detection of genetic variants in the DMD gene that are amenable to exon-skipping therapies in patients with Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD). More particularly, the invention provides a multiplex quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assay configured to detect hemizygous or homozygous deletions of specific exons—namely exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54—of the human DMD gene from dried blood spot (DBS) samples. The assay is designed to identify newborns who carry deletions that render them eligible for currently approved or investigational antisense oligonucleotide-mediated exon-skipping treatments targeting exons 45, 51, or 53. The invention further encompasses kits, reagents, and protocols for performing such an assay in high-throughput newborn screening programs, as well as systems for integrating this molecular test into public health screening algorithms for neuromuscular disorders. The method enables the precise, rapid, and cost-effective identification of a therapeutically relevant subset of DMD patients at birth, thereby facilitating pre-symptomatic intervention with disease-modifying therapies and improving long-term clinical outcomes.

## BACKGROUND

Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) is a severe, X-linked recessive disorder characterized by progressive muscle degeneration and weakness, typically manifesting in early childhood and leading to premature death, often due to cardiac or respiratory failure. DMD arises from pathogenic variants in the DMD gene, which spans approximately 2.4 megabases on the X chromosome and encodes dystrophin, a critical structural protein essential for maintaining the integrity of muscle fibers during contraction. The absence of functional dystrophin disrupts the dystrophin-associated glycoprotein complex, resulting in sarcolemmal instability, chronic inflammation, fibrosis, and eventual replacement of muscle tissue with fat and connective tissue. In contrast, Becker Muscular Dystrophy (BMD), a milder allelic form of the disease, is associated with in-frame deletions or mutations that permit the production of partially functional dystrophin, thereby delaying symptom onset and slowing disease progression.

Approximately 60–70% of DMD cases are caused by large deletions involving one or more exons of the DMD gene, with duplications and point mutations accounting for most of the remaining cases. The clinical severity of DMD is largely determined by whether the mutation disrupts the translational reading frame: out-of-frame deletions typically result in complete loss of dystrophin and a DMD phenotype, whereas in-frame deletions often yield truncated but partially functional protein and a BMD phenotype. This genotype-phenotype correlation has paved the way for targeted therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring the reading frame to convert a severe DMD phenotype into a milder BMD-like condition.

In recent years, exon-skipping therapies using antisense oligonucleotides (AONs) have emerged as a promising treatment modality for a subset of DMD patients. These RNA-based therapeutics function by masking specific splice sites during pre-mRNA processing, thereby inducing the exclusion of a targeted exon from the mature transcript. When applied to out-of-frame deletions, strategic skipping of a flanking exon can restore the open reading frame, enabling the synthesis of an internally deleted yet partially functional dystrophin protein. To date, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has granted accelerated approval to eteplirsen (Exondys 51) for exon 51 skipping and golodirsen (Vyondys 53) for exon 53 skipping, with additional AONs such as viltolarsen (exon 53), casimersen (exon 45), and suvodirsen (exon 51) having undergone or currently undergoing clinical evaluation. Collectively, these therapies are estimated to benefit approximately 30% of DMD patients, with theoretical models suggesting that up to 83% of deletion mutations could be rendered amenable to single or multi-exon skipping approaches.

Despite these advances, the clinical implementation of exon-skipping therapies is critically dependent on early and accurate genotyping. Current diagnostic pathways for DMD are often delayed, with a mean age of diagnosis around 4.4 years—well after the onset of irreversible muscle damage. This diagnostic lag is attributable to the non-specific nature of early symptoms (e.g., delayed motor milestones, calf pseudohypertrophy) and the absence of routine screening. While newborn screening (NBS) programs have been proposed as a solution, existing pilot studies have relied on biochemical markers such as creatine kinase (CK) or its muscle-specific isoform CKMM measured in dried blood spots. However, these approaches suffer from significant limitations: CK levels can be elevated due to non-DMD conditions (e.g., birth trauma, hypoxia), leading to false positives, while some DMD patients exhibit normal or only mildly elevated CK, resulting in false negatives. Moreover, CK-based screening does not provide information about the underlying genetic defect, thereby failing to distinguish between patients who are eligible for exon-skipping therapy and those who are not.

Molecular methods such as multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA) and next-generation sequencing (NGS) offer comprehensive DMD genotyping but are currently impractical for population-wide NBS due to cost, complexity, and turnaround time. MLPA, while robust for detecting deletions and duplications, requires specialized equipment and interpretation expertise, and NGS remains prohibitively expensive for large-scale screening. Consequently, there exists an unmet need for a simple, scalable, and cost-effective molecular assay that can rapidly identify newborns with DMD-causing deletions amenable to existing exon-skipping therapies.

The present invention addresses this gap by leveraging the principles of real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR)—a technology already validated in NBS programs for other genetic disorders such as spinal muscular atrophy (SMA). qPCR offers high sensitivity, specificity, and throughput, with the added advantage of compatibility with standard DBS-derived DNA without the need for quantification or normalization. By focusing on a limited set of exons whose deletion status serves as a proxy for eligibility for exon 45, 51, or 53 skipping, the assay achieves both clinical relevance and technical feasibility. Specifically, deletions of exons 44, 46, 50, 52, or 54 are indicative of genomic rearrangements that, when combined with adjacent deleted exons, may create an out-of-frame context correctable by skipping the intervening target exon. For instance, a deletion spanning exons 46–49 results in an out-of-frame transcript that can be rescued by skipping exon 45, whereas a deletion of exons 45–47 is already in-frame and thus not amenable to exon 45 skipping. The assay does not attempt to characterize the full deletion boundaries but rather flags the presence of a deletion involving any of the five key exons, triggering confirmatory testing to determine therapeutic eligibility.

This targeted approach represents a paradigm shift in NBS philosophy—from universal disease detection to precision identification of treatable subtypes. It aligns with the growing trend in genomic medicine to match diagnostics with available therapeutics, ensuring that screening efforts yield actionable clinical insights. Furthermore, early identification of eligible patients enables pre-symptomatic treatment initiation, which is increasingly recognized as critical for maximizing therapeutic efficacy, particularly in the context of emerging gene therapies that may benefit from combinatorial approaches with exon skipping.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for identifying newborns with Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) who are eligible for exon-skipping therapy, comprising performing a multiplex quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assay on a dried blood spot (DBS) sample to detect hemizygous or homozygous deletions of exons 44, 46, 50, 52, or 54 of the human DMD gene. The method includes extracting genomic DNA from a single 3.1-mm punch of a DBS, amplifying the five target exons simultaneously in a single reaction tube using exon-specific primer-probe sets labeled with spectrally distinct fluorophores, and analyzing the amplification curves to determine the presence or absence of each target exon. A normalized fluorescence ratio (NFR) is calculated for each exon by dividing the endpoint fluorescence of the sample by the median endpoint fluorescence of all samples in the same run for that exon; an NFR below a predetermined threshold (e.g., 0.2) is indicative of a deletion. The assay is configured to achieve 100% sensitivity and specificity for the detection of deletions involving the five target exons in a validation cohort of 120 individuals with known DMD genotypes. Samples exhibiting no amplification for any of the five exons undergo a secondary confirmation step using duplex qPCR reactions pairing each target exon with a reference gene (e.g., RPP30) to rule out technical artifacts. Positive results are subsequently confirmed by an orthogonal method such as multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA) on a fresh EDTA blood sample, followed by RNA analysis if necessary to verify reading frame restoration potential. The invention further encompasses a kit comprising lyophilized or liquid reagents including primers and probes for the five DMD exons and optionally a reference gene, along with instructions for use in a newborn screening setting. The method is particularly suited for integration into public health NBS programs as a first-tier molecular screen for DMD patients amenable to exon 45, 51, or 53 skipping therapies, enabling early diagnosis and pre-symptomatic treatment initiation. The assay is robust, scalable, and cost-effective, with a per-sample reagent cost of less than five euros, and is compatible with existing high-throughput qPCR platforms equipped with at least five fluorescence detection channels. The invention also contemplates future expansion of the assay to include additional exons as new exon-skipping therapies targeting other regions of the DMD gene become available, thereby broadening its clinical utility.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a molecular diagnostic method for the early detection of DMD patients who are candidates for exon-skipping therapy, based on the identification of specific exon deletions in the DMD gene using a multiplex qPCR assay performed on dried blood spot (DBS) samples. In one embodiment, the method comprises the steps of: (a) obtaining a DBS sample collected from a newborn as part of a routine newborn screening program; (b) extracting genomic DNA from a 3.1-mm diameter punch of the DBS using a standard alkaline lysis or silica-column-based protocol; (c) preparing a multiplex qPCR reaction mixture containing the extracted DNA, a master mix comprising DNA polymerase, dNTPs, buffer, and stabilizers, and five pairs of exon-specific primers along with five corresponding hydrolysis probes, each labeled with a unique fluorophore selected from a panel of spectrally resolvable dyes such as FAM, Yakima Yellow, ROX, Atto 647N, and Atto 700; (d) thermocycling the reaction under conditions that permit simultaneous amplification of DMD exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54; (e) monitoring real-time fluorescence emission for each channel throughout the amplification process; (f) determining the presence or absence of each target exon based on the amplification curve characteristics and endpoint fluorescence intensity; and (g) calculating a normalized fluorescence ratio (NFR) for each exon by dividing the sample’s endpoint fluorescence by the median endpoint fluorescence of all samples in the same run for that exon, wherein an NFR value less than 0.2 is interpreted as indicative of a hemizygous or homozygous deletion of the corresponding exon.

In a preferred embodiment, the primer and probe sequences are as follows: for exon 44, forward primer 5′-TAC CTG CAG GCG ATT TGA C-3′ (SEQ ID NO:1), reverse primer 5′-CAC CCT TCA GAA CCT GAT CTT T-3′ (SEQ ID NO:2), and FAM-labeled probe 5′-AAA TTC CTG AGA ATT GGG AAC ATG-3′ (SEQ ID NO:3); for exon 46, forward primer 5′-TTT ATG GTT GGA GGA AGC AGA-3′ (SEQ ID NO:4), reverse primer 5′-AAT GGG CAG AAA ACC AAT GA-3′ (SEQ ID NO:5), and Yakima Yellow-labeled probe 5′-AAC CTG GAA AAG AGC AGC AAC T-3′ (SEQ ID NO:6); for exon 50, forward primer 5′-CTG AGT GGA AGG CGG TAA AC-3′ (SEQ ID NO:7), reverse primer 5′-TCT CAC CCA GTC ATC ACT TCA-3′ (SEQ ID NO:8), and ROX-labeled probe 5′-ACTTCAAGAGCTGAGGGCAAAG-3′ (SEQ ID NO:9); for exon 52, forward primer 5′-AAT ACA CAA CGC TGA AGA ACC C-3′ (SEQ ID NO:10), reverse primer 5′-TTG TGT GTC CCA TGC TTG TT-3′ (SEQ ID NO:11), and Atto 647N-labeled probe 5′-CGC TGC CCA AAA TTT GAA AAA-3′ (SEQ ID NO:12); and for exon 54, forward primer 5′-TCT ATA GCA GTT GGC CAA AGA C-3′ (SEQ ID NO:13), reverse primer 5′-TCA TGG TCC ATC CAG TTT CA-3′ (SEQ ID NO:14), and Atto 700-labeled probe 5′-AAT ATC AAT GCC TCT TGG AGA AGC-3′ (SEQ ID NO:15). The concentrations of primers and probes are optimized to minimize cross-reactivity and ensure balanced amplification across all targets, typically ranging from 80 to 300 nmol/L depending on the specific sequence and fluorophore.

The thermocycling conditions are standardized to 95°C for 5 minutes for initial denaturation, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 30 seconds and 61°C for 75 seconds, which allows for efficient amplification while maintaining specificity. The use of a high-fidelity DNA polymerase with hot-start capability is preferred to reduce non-specific amplification and improve assay robustness. The DNA input is not quantified prior to amplification; instead, a fixed volume (e.g., 1 µL) of crude DNA extract is used directly, leveraging the inherent normalization provided by the multiplex design and the NFR calculation.

In another embodiment, the method includes a two-tier validation protocol to address rare instances where a large deletion might span multiple consecutive target exons, potentially leading to ambiguous results. If a sample shows no amplification for any of the five exons, it is retested in a series of duplex qPCR reactions, each containing primers and probe for a single DMD exon paired with a reference gene such as RPP30 (ribonuclease P/MRP subunit p30), which serves as an internal control for DNA quality and quantity. This confirmatory step ensures that the absence of signal is due to a true genomic deletion rather than PCR inhibition or DNA degradation.

The assay is designed to be gender-neutral, meaning it is applied to both male and female newborns without prior sex determination. While DMD predominantly affects males due to its X-linked inheritance, this approach allows for the detection of rare female cases, such as those with Turner syndrome (45,X) or skewed X-chromosome inactivation leading to symptomatic carrier status. However, the assay is not intended to identify asymptomatic female carriers, as their fluorescence profiles typically fall within the normal range due to the presence of one intact allele.

Data interpretation is automated through software that calculates NFR values and applies predefined cutoffs to classify samples as “deleted” or “non-deleted” for each exon. The system generates a report indicating which exons, if any, are deleted, thereby flagging the sample for confirmatory testing. Importantly, the assay does not provide a definitive DMD diagnosis but rather identifies a genetic signature associated with eligibility for specific exon-skipping therapies. Final diagnosis and treatment decisions require follow-up with MLPA or sequencing to delineate exact deletion boundaries and assess reading frame status.

The method is particularly advantageous for integration into existing NBS infrastructure, as it utilizes the same DBS cards and DNA extraction workflows employed for other molecular screens such as SMA. Its low cost, rapid turnaround time (less than 4 hours from DNA extraction to result), and compatibility with high-throughput platforms make it suitable for population-scale implementation. Moreover, the modular design allows for easy adaptation: as new exon-skipping drugs targeting different exons (e.g., exon 50 or 55) gain regulatory approval, additional primer-probe sets can be incorporated into the multiplex panel without major protocol changes.

Ethical considerations are addressed by limiting the scope of the screen to therapeutically actionable genotypes, thereby avoiding the identification of untreatable DMD cases that could cause unnecessary parental anxiety without immediate clinical benefit. This targeted approach mirrors the successful model of SMA screening, which focuses exclusively on SMN1 homozygous deletions despite the existence of other SMA-causing variants.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—DMD Genotyping Assay

A multiplex qPCR assay was developed and validated to detect deletions of DMD exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54 in dried blood spot (DBS) samples. Genomic DNA was extracted from a 3.1-mm punch of each DBS using a previously published alkaline lysis protocol. One microliter of the crude DNA extract was added to a 25-µL reaction containing 5× Takyon No ROX Master Mix (Eurogentec), exon-specific primers at 300 nmol/L, and hydrolysis probes at concentrations ranging from 80 to 180 nmol/L as specified in Table 3 of the research paper. The reactions were run on a CFX96 Real-Time PCR System (Bio-Rad) with the following cycling conditions: 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 30 seconds and 61°C for 75 seconds. Fluorescence was monitored in five channels corresponding to FAM, Yakima Yellow, ROX, Atto 647N, and Atto 700.

The assay was tested on a cohort of 120 individuals with known DMD genotypes determined by MLPA. This cohort included 51 male DMD patients (the “deleted group”), 50 female carriers (“carrier group”), and 19 healthy controls (“control group”). Within the deleted group, 34 patients harbored deletions overlapping at least one of the five target exons, while 17 had deletions elsewhere in the DMD gene. All 34 patients with target exon deletions showed complete absence of amplification (i.e., flat amplification curves and negligible endpoint fluorescence) for the corresponding exon(s), whereas the 17 patients with non-target deletions exhibited normal amplification for all five exons. All carrier and control samples displayed robust amplification across all targets, confirming the assay’s ability to distinguish hemizygous deletions in males from heterozygous or wild-type states. Normalized fluorescence ratios (NFRs) were calculated by dividing each sample’s endpoint fluorescence by the median fluorescence of all samples in the run for that exon. An NFR threshold of 0.2 was established as the cutoff for deletion calling, yielding 100% sensitivity (34/34) and 100% specificity (86/86) in this validation set. Notably, the assay did not differentiate between female carriers and controls, as expected given the diploid nature of the X chromosome in females. These results demonstrate that the multiplex qPCR assay reliably detects hemizygous deletions of the five target exons in male DMD patients and is suitable for newborn screening applications.

### Example 2—DMD Genotyping Assay

To evaluate the clinical utility of the assay in the context of exon-skipping therapy eligibility, a retrospective analysis was performed using data from the Leiden Open Variation Database for DMD (LOVD-DMD), which contained 26,078 patient entries as of April 20, 2020. The analysis focused on patients with large deletions (≥1 exon) and assessed how many would be identified by the assay (i.e., have a deletion overlapping at least one of exons 44, 46, 50, 52, or 54) and how many of those would be amenable to exon 45, 51, or 53 skipping. Of the 16,414 patients with DMD gene deletions, 9,532 (58.1%) had deletions covering at least one flanking exon and would thus be flagged by the assay. Among these, 5,144 (54.0%) had deletion patterns that could be rescued by skipping exon 45, 51, or 53, representing 19.7% of all database entries. For example, deletions overlapping exon 50 were found in 5,494 patients, of whom 2,034 (37.0%) were rescuable by exon 51 skipping. Similarly, deletions overlapping exon 52 occurred in 3,375 patients, with 1,583 (46.9%) amenable to exon 53 skipping. In contrast, deletions overlapping exon 54 (n=1,386) were not associated with any rescuable genotypes under current therapeutic paradigms, highlighting the importance of confirmatory testing to distinguish between treatable and non-treatable cases identified by the initial screen. The analysis confirmed that screening for the five flanking exons captures the majority of patients eligible for the three approved exon-skipping therapies, providing a strong rationale for the assay’s design. When combined with the high analytical performance demonstrated in Example 1, these epidemiological data support the implementation of this qPCR assay as a first-tier molecular screen in newborn screening programs aimed at early identification of DMD patients who could benefit from pre-symptomatic exon-skipping treatment.
