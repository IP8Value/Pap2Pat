# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNOLOGY FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of oncology and molecular therapeutics, and more specifically to methods and compositions for enhancing the efficacy of radiotherapy in the treatment of malignant glioma through the targeted inhibition of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs). The invention further encompasses novel three-dimensional human brain organoid models that recapitulate the cellular composition and structural complexity of the mature human brain, which serve as a physiologically relevant platform for evaluating both the therapeutic efficacy and potential neurotoxicity of candidate radiosensitizing agents. In particular, the invention provides methods for identifying lncRNA targets whose knockdown selectively sensitizes malignant glioma cells—including both adult glioblastoma (GBM) and pediatric diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG)—to clinically relevant doses of fractionated ionizing radiation, without impairing the viability or function of normal human neural cells such as astrocytes and neurons. The invention also includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) directed against a specific primate-conserved lncRNA, herein designated lncGRS-1 (CTC-338M12.4), which is encoded on chromosome 5q35.3 and functions as a critical regulator of glioma cell proliferation and radiation resistance. Additionally, the invention discloses integrated systems for high-throughput functional genomic screening using CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) technology coupled with validation in patient-derived glioma cultures and mature brain organoid co-culture models, thereby enabling the rapid discovery and preclinical assessment of novel non-coding RNA targets for combinatorial cancer therapy.

## BACKGROUND

Malignant gliomas represent some of the most aggressive and lethal primary brain tumors in both adults and children. In adults, glioblastoma (GBM) is the most common and devastating form, with a median survival of only 14 months despite maximal surgical resection followed by adjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy. In pediatric patients, diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG) is the predominant malignant glioma, arising in the brainstem and typically diagnosed in children under ten years of age. DIPG is largely inoperable due to its critical anatomical location, and standard treatment consists almost exclusively of fractionated radiotherapy, which provides only transient symptomatic relief and extends median survival to a mere 9–10 months, with fewer than 10% of patients surviving beyond two years. Radiotherapy remains a cornerstone of treatment for nearly all malignant gliomas because it effectively reduces tumor burden and delays recurrence; however, its therapeutic utility is fundamentally constrained by the narrow therapeutic window imposed by radiation-induced damage to surrounding healthy brain tissue. Consequently, there is an urgent and unmet clinical need for agents that can selectively enhance the radiosensitivity of glioma cells while sparing normal neural cells, thereby improving tumor control without exacerbating neurotoxicity.

The human genome encodes tens of thousands of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs)—transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides that lack protein-coding potential—many of which exhibit highly cell type-specific expression patterns and play critical roles in regulating gene expression, chromatin architecture, and cellular identity. Dysregulation of specific lncRNAs has been implicated in the pathogenesis of numerous cancers, including glioma, where they can function as oncogenes or tumor suppressors. Due to their restricted expression profiles, lncRNAs represent an attractive class of therapeutic targets with the potential for high tumor selectivity and reduced off-target effects. However, the vast majority of lncRNAs remain functionally uncharacterized, and there are currently no systematic methods to predict which lncRNAs might serve as effective radiosensitizers in glioma or other cancers. Traditional approaches to drug discovery in oncology have focused predominantly on protein-coding genes, leaving the non-coding genome largely unexplored as a source of novel therapeutic vulnerabilities.

Recent advances in functional genomics, particularly CRISPR-based screening technologies, have enabled genome-scale interrogation of gene function in mammalian cells. CRISPR interference (CRISPRi), which utilizes a catalytically inactive Cas9 (dCas9) fused to transcriptional repressor domains such as KRAB, allows for reversible and specific knockdown of target genes without inducing DNA double-strand breaks. This technology has been successfully applied to screen both coding and non-coding genomic elements for roles in essential cellular processes, including proliferation, survival, and drug response. However, the application of CRISPRi to identify modifiers of radiation response—particularly in the context of clinically relevant fractionated dosing regimens—has not been systematically explored at scale. Moreover, while CRISPR screens can efficiently nominate candidate targets, their translation into viable therapeutics requires robust validation in biologically relevant models that accurately reflect both tumor biology and the normal tissue microenvironment.

Conventional preclinical models for glioma, such as two-dimensional (2D) cell cultures and murine xenografts, suffer from significant limitations. 2D cultures fail to recapitulate the three-dimensional architecture, cellular heterogeneity, and microenvironmental cues that influence tumor behavior and therapeutic response in vivo. Murine xenograft models, while useful for assessing in vivo efficacy, are often poor predictors of human toxicity, especially when the therapeutic target is not conserved between species. Notably, many human lncRNAs, including the one central to this invention (lncGRS-1), lack orthologs in rodents, rendering traditional mouse models inadequate for evaluating potential neurotoxicity of target inhibition in normal human brain tissue. Organoids—three-dimensional, self-organizing structures derived from pluripotent stem cells that mimic key aspects of organ development and function—have emerged as a promising alternative. Embryonic brain organoids have been used to model early neurodevelopment and certain aspects of gliomagenesis, but they are composed primarily of immature, proliferative neural progenitors and lack the differentiated cell types, such as mature astrocytes and neurons, that dominate the adult human brain. As such, embryonic organoids may not accurately reflect the response of mature neural tissue to radiation or targeted therapeutics, particularly those intended for use in adult or pediatric patients whose brains are already largely developed.

Thus, despite decades of research, there remains a critical gap in the ability to systematically discover and validate non-coding RNA targets that can safely and effectively enhance radiotherapy in malignant glioma. The present invention addresses this gap by integrating a high-throughput CRISPRi-based functional screen with a novel human brain organoid platform composed of mature, post-mitotic neural cells, thereby enabling the identification of glioma-selective radiosensitizers with minimal risk of neurotoxicity.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a comprehensive platform for the identification, validation, and therapeutic exploitation of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) that function as radiosensitizers in malignant glioma. In one aspect, the invention discloses a method for identifying a radiotherapy sensitizer comprising performing a CRISPR interference (CRISPRi)-based functional genomic screen in a glioma cell line engineered to stably express a dCas9-KRAB fusion protein, wherein the screen targets a library of guide RNAs (sgRNAs) directed against transcription start sites (TSS) of lncRNA loci expressed in human glioma; exposing the screened cells to a clinically relevant regimen of fractionated ionizing radiation; and identifying lncRNA targets whose knockdown results in a statistically significant reduction in cell proliferation or viability specifically in the presence of radiation, as compared to non-irradiated controls. The screen is designed to detect synergistic interactions between lncRNA knockdown and radiation, thereby prioritizing candidates that potentiate the cytotoxic effects of radiotherapy without exhibiting substantial toxicity in the absence of radiation.

In another aspect, the invention identifies a specific lncRNA, designated lncGRS-1 (corresponding to the annotated transcript CTC-338M12.4 located on human chromosome 5q35.3), as a top-tier radiosensitizer in both adult and pediatric malignant glioma. lncGRS-1 is a nuclear-enriched, spliced, polyadenylated transcript that is downregulated in response to radiation and exhibits primate-specific conservation, with no orthologs present in rodents or other commonly used laboratory animals. Knockdown of lncGRS-1 using either CRISPRi or antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) significantly inhibits the proliferation of patient-derived glioma cells—including GBM and DIPG lines—both in isolation and in combination with fractionated radiotherapy, demonstrating strong synergy between lncGRS-1 inhibition and radiation treatment. Critically, lncGRS-1 knockdown does not impair the viability, proliferation, or apoptotic profile of normal human astrocytes or neurons, indicating a glioma-selective mechanism of action.

Accordingly, the invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising ASOs specifically designed to hybridize to and induce degradation of lncGRS-1 RNA. These ASOs are chemically modified (e.g., with locked nucleic acid (LNA) residues) to enhance stability, binding affinity, and nuclease resistance, and are formulated for intrathecal or intracerebroventricular delivery to achieve therapeutic concentrations within the central nervous system. The compositions may be administered alone or in combination with standard radiotherapy regimens to treat patients diagnosed with malignant glioma.

To address the limitations of conventional preclinical models, the invention also discloses a novel three-dimensional human brain organoid system, termed “mature brain organoids” (MBOs), which are assembled from induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived mature astrocytes (iAstrocytes) and/or mature cortical neurons (i3Neurons). These MBOs closely mimic the cellular composition and structural maturity of the postnatal human brain, providing a physiologically relevant substrate for modeling glioma invasion and evaluating therapeutic responses. The invention describes methods for co-culturing fluorescently labeled glioma cells with MBOs to generate tumor-organoid chimeras, which can be treated with ASOs and/or fractionated radiation to simultaneously assess tumor growth inhibition and host organoid toxicity. Using this platform, the invention demonstrates that ASO-mediated knockdown of lncGRS-1 significantly reduces glioma tumor burden within MBOs and further enhances the anti-tumor effects of radiotherapy, without adversely affecting the size, viability, or integrity of the host organoid.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides integrated systems for high-throughput target discovery and validation, comprising: (i) a CRISPRi screening module configured to perform pooled lncRNA knockdown under fractionated radiation conditions; (ii) a validation module comprising patient-derived glioma cultures and normal human neural cells for secondary screening; and (iii) a mature brain organoid co-culture module for tertiary assessment of efficacy and safety in a 3D tissue context. This multi-tiered approach enables the rapid progression of candidate targets from initial screen hit to preclinical candidate with a well-characterized therapeutic index.

Collectively, the invention establishes a generalizable framework for leveraging functional genomics and advanced human tissue models to unlock the therapeutic potential of the non-coding genome in oncology, with immediate application to the development of safer and more effective radiotherapy combinations for malignant glioma.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### Definitions

As used herein, the term “long non-coding RNA” or “lncRNA” refers to an RNA transcript that is greater than 200 nucleotides in length and lacks a significant open reading frame capable of encoding a functional protein. lncRNAs may be spliced or unspliced, polyadenylated or non-polyadenylated, and localized to the nucleus, cytoplasm, or both. The term includes transcripts previously annotated in public databases such as GENCODE or RefSeq, as well as novel transcripts identified through experimental methods such as RNA sequencing.

The term “radiotherapy sensitizer” or “radiosensitizer” refers to a molecule, compound, or genetic element whose inhibition, knockdown, or functional blockade enhances the cytotoxic or anti-proliferative effects of ionizing radiation on cancer cells, without proportionally increasing toxicity to normal cells. A radiosensitizer may act by impairing DNA repair, inducing cell cycle arrest, promoting apoptosis, or otherwise compromising the ability of cancer cells to survive radiation-induced damage.

“Fractionated radiation” denotes a radiotherapy regimen in which the total prescribed dose of ionizing radiation is delivered in multiple smaller doses (fractions) over a period of days or weeks, as opposed to a single acute dose. In the context of malignant glioma, a typical fractionated regimen involves daily doses of approximately 1.8 to 2.0 Gray (Gy), administered five days per week, for a total cumulative dose ranging from 54 to 60 Gy.

“CRISPR interference” or “CRISPRi” refers to a gene regulation technology that uses a catalytically inactive Cas9 protein (dCas9) fused to a transcriptional repressor domain (such as KRAB) to block transcription initiation or elongation at a target genomic locus. Targeting is achieved through the co-expression of a single guide RNA (sgRNA) that directs the dCas9-repressor complex to a specific DNA sequence adjacent to a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM).

“Mature brain organoid” or “MBO” refers to a three-dimensional cellular aggregate derived from human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) that has been differentiated into post-mitotic, functionally mature neural cell types—including astrocytes and/or neurons—and assembled into a tissue-like structure that recapitulates key features of the adult or postnatal human brain, as opposed to embryonic or fetal brain organoids which are enriched for neural progenitor cells.

“Antisense oligonucleotide” or “ASO” refers to a synthetic, single-stranded nucleic acid polymer, typically 12 to 25 nucleotides in length, designed to bind to a complementary RNA target through Watson-Crick base pairing. ASOs employed in the present invention are chemically modified (e.g., with locked nucleic acid (LNA) or 2′-O-methoxyethyl (MOE) residues) to enhance binding affinity, nuclease resistance, and pharmacokinetic properties, and are capable of inducing target RNA degradation via recruitment of RNase H1 upon hybridization.

“Patient-derived glioma cells” encompass primary cell cultures established directly from tumor specimens obtained from patients diagnosed with malignant glioma, including but not limited to glioblastoma (GBM) and diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG). These cells retain key genetic, epigenetic, and phenotypic characteristics of the original tumor and are considered more representative of human disease than immortalized cell lines.

“Therapeutic index” refers to the ratio between the dose of a therapeutic agent that produces a desired anti-tumor effect and the dose that causes unacceptable toxicity to normal tissues. A high therapeutic index indicates a wide margin of safety, which is a critical attribute for any candidate radiosensitizer intended for clinical use.

### Method for Identification of a Radiotherapy Sensitizer

The present invention provides a robust and scalable method for identifying lncRNAs that function as radiotherapy sensitizers in malignant glioma. The method begins with the engineering of a glioma cell line—such as U87 MG—to stably express a dCas9-KRAB fusion protein, which serves as the effector component of the CRISPRi system. This cell line is then transduced with a pooled lentiviral library encoding tens of thousands of sgRNAs, each designed to target the transcription start site (TSS) of a specific lncRNA locus expressed in human glioma. The library is constructed to include multiple independent sgRNAs per lncRNA (typically 10) to ensure robust knockdown and to mitigate off-target effects, along with a set of non-targeting control sgRNAs to serve as internal references for normalization and statistical analysis.

Following selection of transduced cells (e.g., via puromycin resistance), the population is split into replicate cultures and subjected to a clinically relevant fractionated radiation regimen. In a preferred embodiment, cells receive a total dose of 8 Gy delivered in four fractions of 2 Gy each, administered every other day over the course of eight days. This dosing schedule approximates the biological effect of an LD50 (lethal dose for 50% of cells) in the U87 model, thereby providing sufficient dynamic range to detect both synergistic sensitizers (which enhance radiation-induced cell death) and buffering protectors (which mitigate radiation effects). Cells are propagated for a total of 12 days post-initial irradiation to allow phenotypic differences to manifest as changes in relative sgRNA abundance.

At the beginning (T0) and end (T12) of the screen, genomic DNA is harvested from aliquots of the culture, and the integrated sgRNA barcodes are amplified by PCR and subjected to high-throughput sequencing. The relative enrichment or depletion of each sgRNA is calculated as the log2 fold-change in read counts between T12 and T0. For each lncRNA, a phenotype score is derived from the average performance of its top-performing sgRNAs (e.g., the top three), and a statistical significance score (e.g., negative log10 of the Mann-Whitney U test p-value comparing sgRNA abundances at T12 vs. T0) is computed. A composite “screen score” is then generated by multiplying the phenotype score by the significance score.

To identify true radiosensitizers, the screen scores from the radiation-treated condition are compared to those obtained from a parallel screen conducted in the absence of radiation (a “growth-only” screen). lncRNAs that exhibit a higher screen score in the presence of radiation than in its absence are classified as candidate sensitizers. Additional filtering criteria may include: (i) exclusion of “neighbor hits” (lncRNAs whose TSS lies within 1 kilobase of an expressed protein-coding gene, to avoid confounding effects from proximal gene knockdown); (ii) expression in a panel of patient-derived glioma cultures (both adult GBM and pediatric DIPG); and (iii) a “sensitizer score” defined as the ratio of the radiation screen score to the growth screen score, with higher ratios indicating greater selectivity for the radiation context.

This method enables the unbiased, genome-scale discovery of lncRNA targets whose functional inhibition specifically enhances the efficacy of fractionated radiotherapy, providing a rational basis for prioritizing candidates for downstream validation and therapeutic development.

### Methods of Diagnosis and Treatment

The invention further provides methods for the diagnosis and treatment of malignant glioma based on the identification and targeting of lncGRS-1. In a diagnostic aspect, the presence and expression level of lncGRS-1 RNA in a patient’s tumor specimen may be assessed using techniques such as quantitative reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR), RNA in situ hybridization (e.g., RNAscope), or RNA sequencing. Elevated expression of lncGRS-1 relative to normal brain tissue or a predefined threshold may serve as a biomarker to identify patients who are likely to benefit from lncGRS-1-targeted therapy, either alone or in combination with radiotherapy.

In a therapeutic aspect, the invention provides a method for treating a subject diagnosed with malignant glioma, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) that specifically hybridizes to lncGRS-1 RNA, thereby inducing its degradation and inhibiting its biological function. The ASO is preferably a locked nucleic acid (LNA)-modified oligonucleotide, 16 to 20 nucleotides in length, with a sequence complementary to a unique region of the lncGRS-1 transcript (e.g., spanning an exon-exon junction to ensure specificity for spliced isoforms). The ASO may be formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier and administered via routes that achieve sufficient exposure in the central nervous system, such as intrathecal injection, intracerebroventricular infusion, or convection-enhanced delivery.

In a preferred embodiment, the ASO is administered in combination with a standard fractionated radiotherapy regimen. For example, a patient may receive daily fractions of 2 Gy, five days per week, for a total of 30 fractions (60 Gy), while concurrently receiving weekly intrathecal injections of the lncGRS-1 ASO. The timing of ASO administration is optimized to ensure target knockdown during the course of radiation treatment, thereby maximizing radiosensitization. The combined therapy is expected to result in significantly greater tumor growth inhibition and prolonged progression-free survival compared to radiotherapy alone, without increasing the incidence or severity of radiation-induced neurotoxicity.

The method may be applied to both adult patients with glioblastoma and pediatric patients with diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma, as lncGRS-1 is expressed and functionally required in both subtypes. Monitoring of treatment response may include serial imaging (e.g., MRI), assessment of neurological function, and periodic sampling of cerebrospinal fluid to measure lncGRS-1 levels as a pharmacodynamic biomarker of target engagement.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The invention includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) that specifically targets lncGRS-1 RNA for degradation. The ASO is a single-stranded oligonucleotide of 12 to 25 nucleotides in length, preferably 16 to 20 nucleotides, with a nucleotide sequence that is fully complementary to a contiguous segment of the lncGRS-1 transcript (GENCODE identifier CTC-338M12.4). In a preferred embodiment, the ASO is designed to span a splice junction of lncGRS-1 to ensure specificity for the mature, processed transcript and to avoid cross-reactivity with genomic DNA or unspliced precursors.

The ASO is chemically modified to enhance its drug-like properties. In one embodiment, the ASO incorporates locked nucleic acid (LNA) monomers at strategic positions, particularly at the 5′ and 3′ ends (a “gapmer” design), to increase binding affinity (melting temperature) and resistance to exo- and endonucleases. The central region (the “gap”) typically consists of 7 to 10 deoxynucleotides to support RNase H1-mediated cleavage of the RNA strand in the RNA-DNA heteroduplex. Alternative modifications may include 2′-O-methoxyethyl (MOE), phosphorothioate backbone linkages, or conjugation to ligands that enhance blood-brain barrier penetration or cellular uptake.

The pharmaceutical composition comprises the ASO in a concentration sufficient to achieve at least 70% knockdown of lncGRS-1 RNA in glioma cells following administration. The composition is formulated in a sterile, isotonic, buffered solution suitable for intrathecal or intracerebroventricular delivery, such as artificial cerebrospinal fluid (aCSF) or phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing a low concentration of a surfactant (e.g., polysorbate 80) to prevent adsorption to container surfaces. The composition may additionally contain preservatives (e.g., benzyl alcohol) if intended for multi-dose use, though single-use vials are preferred for CNS applications.

Dosage regimens are tailored to the clinical context but typically involve weekly or bi-weekly administrations during the course of radiotherapy. For example, a dose of 1 to 10 mg of ASO may be administered intrathecally once per week for six to eight weeks, coinciding with a standard 6-week radiotherapy schedule. The composition may be co-formulated with radioprotectants for normal tissue (e.g., amifostine) if deemed necessary, though the glioma-selective nature of lncGRS-1 knockdown is expected to obviate such measures.

### Three-Dimensional Human Brain Organoid of Malignant Glioma

A key innovation of the present invention is the development of a three-dimensional human brain organoid model that accurately recapitulates the mature cellular environment of the postnatal human brain and supports the invasive growth of malignant glioma cells. This model, termed a “mature brain organoid” (MBO), is generated by first differentiating human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)—preferably from a well-characterized, karyotypically normal line such as WTC11—into two distinct neural lineages: (i) mature astrocytes (iAstrocytes) and (ii) mature cortical neurons (i3Neurons).

iAstrocytes are produced by directing iPSCs through a neural induction protocol involving dual SMAD inhibition (using SB431542 and DMH1), followed by prolonged culture (≥180 days) in media supplemented with epidermal growth factor (EGF) and fibroblast growth factor (FGF) to promote astroglial maturation. The resulting iAstrocytes express canonical markers such as GFAP, S100β, and ALDH1L1, and exhibit morphological complexity and functional properties akin to adult human astrocytes.

i3Neurons are generated from an isogenic iPSC line engineered to carry a doxycycline-inducible NEUROG2 transgene. Upon doxycycline treatment, these cells rapidly differentiate into post-mitotic glutamatergic neurons expressing MAP2, βIII-tubulin, and synaptic markers, with electrophysiological properties consistent with cortical identity.

MBOs are assembled by combining dissociated iAstrocytes and i3Neurons in defined ratios (e.g., 1:1) and seeding them into low-adhesion, U-bottom 96-well plates at densities of 10,000 to 20,000 cells per well. Centrifugation promotes aggregation, and over 48 hours, the cells self-assemble into spherical organoids that maintain viability and structural integrity for several weeks in culture. These MBOs contain both ramified astrocytes and networked neurons, closely mimicking the cellular composition of the mature human cerebral cortex.

To model malignant glioma, patient-derived glioma cells (e.g., DIPG SF8628 or GBM U87) are first labeled with a red fluorescent protein (RFP) via lentiviral transduction. These RFP+ cells are then seeded onto the surface of pre-formed MBOs at a defined ratio (e.g., 1 tumor cell per 5 host cells). Over time, the glioma cells invade the organoid parenchyma in a manner that recapitulates the diffuse, infiltrative growth pattern characteristic of human glioma in vivo.

This MBO-glioma co-culture system serves as a powerful platform for evaluating therapeutic interventions. Test compounds (e.g., lncGRS-1 ASOs) are added to the culture medium, and tumor burden is quantified over time by measuring RFP fluorescence intensity within the organoid boundaries using automated microscopy and image analysis. Simultaneously, the health of the host organoid is assessed by monitoring overall size, morphology, and viability markers. Crucially, the system can be combined with fractionated radiation (delivered using a clinical irradiator at doses up to 54 Gy in 2 Gy fractions) to model the full standard-of-care regimen. Using this approach, the invention demonstrates that lncGRS-1 knockdown significantly reduces tumor growth and enhances radiation response without harming the host neural tissue, thereby validating both efficacy and safety in a human-relevant context.

### Systems

The invention further encompasses integrated systems for the high-throughput discovery and validation of lncRNA radiosensitizers. Such a system comprises three interconnected modules: (i) a primary screening module, (ii) a secondary validation module, and (iii) a tertiary organoid testing module.

The primary screening module includes a CRISPRi-compatible glioma cell line (e.g., U87-dCas9-KRAB), a pooled sgRNA library targeting lncRNA TSSs, a radiation delivery apparatus (e.g., a Cs-137 or X-ray irradiator with precise dose calibration), and a next-generation sequencing pipeline for sgRNA barcode quantification. This module is configured to execute the radiation modifier screen as described above, outputting a ranked list of candidate lncRNA hits.

The secondary validation module comprises a panel of patient-derived glioma cell cultures (including both adult GBM and pediatric DIPG lines) and normal human neural cells (e.g., primary astrocytes, iPSC-derived neurons). This module is used to confirm that candidate hits from the primary screen exhibit consistent radiosensitizing effects across diverse genetic backgrounds and, critically, lack toxicity in non-malignant cells. Validation assays include RT-qPCR for target knockdown, cell proliferation assays, viability/apoptosis measurements, and RNA-seq for transcriptomic profiling.

The tertiary organoid testing module consists of the mature brain organoid (MBO) platform described herein, equipped with automated imaging systems (e.g., high-content fluorescence microscopes) and image analysis software for longitudinal quantification of tumor burden and organoid health. This module enables medium-throughput testing of lead candidates under conditions that closely mimic the in vivo brain microenvironment, including the presence of fractionated radiation.

The entire system may be operated under standardized protocols and data management frameworks to ensure reproducibility and facilitate regulatory submission. Data from each module feed into a centralized bioinformatics pipeline that integrates genomic, transcriptomic, and phenotypic information to compute a composite “therapeutic index score” for each candidate, guiding final selection for preclinical development.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A CRISPRi-based radiation modifier screen was performed to identify lncRNAs that sensitize glioma cells to fractionated radiotherapy. U87 glioblastoma cells stably expressing dCas9-KRAB were transduced with a lentiviral library containing 56,890 sgRNAs targeting 5,689 lncRNA loci, along with 1,202 non-targeting control sgRNAs. Following puromycin selection, duplicate cultures were exposed to 8 Gy of ionizing radiation delivered in four 2-Gy fractions every other day, while control cultures received no radiation. Cells were propagated for 12 days, and sgRNA abundance was measured by deep sequencing at the start (T0) and end (T12) of the experiment.

Analysis identified 467 lncRNA hits that significantly altered cell growth in irradiated conditions (FDR = 0.25%). Of these, 33 exhibited stronger growth-inhibitory effects in the presence of radiation than in its absence, qualifying them as candidate radiosensitizers. These 33 hits were further filtered for expression in a panel of patient-derived glioma cultures (two DIPG and three GBM lines), resulting in nine high-confidence candidates designated lncGRS-1 through lncGRS-9. lncGRS-1 (CTC-338M12.4) emerged as the top hit based on its high “sensitizer score” (radiation screen score / growth screen score = 8.7).

Validation studies confirmed that CRISPRi-mediated knockdown of lncGRS-1 reduced U87 cell proliferation by 48% in the absence of radiation and by an additional 37% when combined with 8 Gy fractionated radiation, demonstrating synergy (p = 0.0052). Similar results were obtained in patient-derived SF10360 (GBM) and SF8628 (DIPG) cells. Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) targeting lncGRS-1 achieved >89% knockdown efficiency and inhibited proliferation by ~80% in both GBM and DIPG cultures, while showing no effect on normal human astrocytes (NHAs) or HeLa cells.

To evaluate therapeutic potential in a human-relevant tissue context, mature brain organoids (MBOs) were generated from iPSC-derived astrocytes (iAstrocytes) and co-cultured with RFP-labeled SF8628 DIPG cells. Weekly administration of lncGRS-1 ASOs reduced tumor burden by 62% compared to control ASOs (p < 0.01), without affecting organoid size or viability. When combined with fractionated radiation (18 Gy in 9 fractions), lncGRS-1 ASOs further reduced tumor burden by 81% relative to radiation alone (p < 0.001), confirming radiosensitization in a 3D model that recapitulates the mature brain microenvironment.

These results establish lncGRS-1 as a glioma-selective therapeutic target and validate the integrated screening-to-organoid platform as a powerful engine for discovering novel radiosensitizers in the non-coding genome.
