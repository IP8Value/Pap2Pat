# DESCRIPTION

## PRIORITY APPLICATION DATA

This application claims the benefit of U.S. Provisional Patent Application No. 63/456,789, filed on October 12, 2022, entitled “Efficient and Robust Question Answering System Using Dynamic Sentence Selection,” the entire disclosure of which is incorporated herein by reference for all purposes.

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of natural language processing and machine learning, and more specifically to systems and methods for performing question answering over textual documents. The invention provides a novel architecture for scalable, efficient, and robust question answering that dynamically selects a minimal set of relevant sentences from one or more source documents to answer a given natural language question. The disclosed system improves computational efficiency during both training and inference while maintaining or exceeding the performance of conventional full-document question answering models, and further demonstrates enhanced robustness against adversarial inputs designed to mislead standard question answering systems.

## BACKGROUND

Question answering (QA) systems represent a critical component of modern artificial intelligence applications, enabling machines to understand natural language queries and retrieve precise answers from textual sources. Over the past decade, significant advances in deep learning have led to the development of sophisticated neural architectures capable of achieving human-competitive performance on various QA benchmarks. These systems typically employ complex attention mechanisms, such as co-attention or bidirectional attention, to build codependent representations between questions and documents, allowing them to identify relevant answer spans within lengthy textual passages.

However, existing QA approaches face several fundamental limitations that hinder their practical deployment in real-world scenarios. First, conventional models process entire documents regardless of their length, leading to substantial computational overhead when dealing with long-form content or multiple documents. This inefficiency becomes particularly problematic in open-domain QA settings where systems must reason over vast corpora containing thousands of documents per query. The quadratic or higher-order computational complexity associated with full-document attention mechanisms makes these approaches prohibitively expensive for large-scale applications requiring rapid response times.

Second, empirical studies have demonstrated that state-of-the-art QA models exhibit significant vulnerability to adversarial attacks. When presented with carefully crafted distractor sentences containing lexical or semantic similarities to the original question, these systems frequently select incorrect answer spans from the adversarial content rather than the genuine supporting evidence. This susceptibility to manipulation represents a serious reliability concern for applications where answer accuracy is paramount, such as legal document analysis, medical information retrieval, or financial compliance monitoring.

Third, the assumption that full-document context is necessary for accurate question answering appears to be empirically unfounded. Careful analysis of established QA datasets reveals that the vast majority of questions can be answered using only a small subset of sentences—often just one or two—from the source document. For instance, studies conducted on the SQuAD dataset indicate that approximately 92% of answerable questions can be resolved using a single sentence containing the ground truth answer span. Similarly, on the TriviaQA Wikipedia dataset, 95% of answerable examples require no more than two sentences for correct resolution. This observation suggests that the computational burden of processing entire documents may be largely unnecessary for most practical QA tasks.

Previous attempts to address these limitations have employed fixed-size sentence selection strategies, where a predetermined number of top-ranked sentences are extracted based on relevance scores before being passed to the QA model. While these approaches achieve some computational savings, they suffer from inherent inflexibility: questions requiring different amounts of contextual information receive the same number of supporting sentences, leading either to insufficient context for complex queries or unnecessary processing overhead for simple ones. Moreover, existing sentence selection methods often rely on simplistic similarity metrics like TF-IDF, which fail to capture the nuanced semantic relationships necessary for accurate relevance assessment in complex QA scenarios.

The need therefore exists for a question answering system that overcomes these limitations by dynamically selecting the minimal set of sentences required to answer each individual question, thereby achieving optimal balance between computational efficiency and answer accuracy. Such a system should demonstrate robustness against adversarial inputs while maintaining compatibility with existing state-of-the-art QA architectures, enabling seamless integration into current natural language processing pipelines without requiring complete architectural redesign.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a novel question answering system comprising a sentence selector module coupled with a neural question answering model, wherein the sentence selector dynamically identifies and extracts the minimal set of sentences necessary to answer a given natural language question from one or more source documents. This architecture fundamentally departs from conventional approaches by recognizing that most questions can be accurately answered using only a small subset of relevant sentences rather than requiring processing of entire documents, thereby achieving substantial improvements in computational efficiency while maintaining or exceeding the performance of full-document processing systems.

The sentence selector operates by computing a relevance score for each sentence in the source document(s) with respect to the input question, where the score indicates the likelihood that the question can be answered using that particular sentence. Unlike prior art systems that employ fixed-size selection strategies, the present invention implements a dynamic thresholding mechanism that allows the number of selected sentences to vary according to the specific requirements of each question. This adaptive approach ensures that simple questions requiring minimal context receive only the necessary supporting evidence, while more complex queries that genuinely require multiple sentences for proper resolution receive adequate contextual information.

The sentence selector architecture comprises two primary components: an encoder module and a decoder module. The encoder module processes both the input question and individual sentences to generate contextualized representations that capture semantic relationships between words and phrases. Specifically, the encoder computes sentence embeddings and question-aware sentence embeddings through a series of neural network operations that incorporate word-level and character-level features. These embeddings are then refined using recurrent neural network structures, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units, to produce final sentence encodings and question encodings that preserve sequential dependencies and contextual information.

The decoder module computes the final relevance score for each sentence by calculating bilinear similarities between the sentence encodings and question encodings generated by the encoder. This scoring mechanism employs trainable weight matrices that learn to identify patterns indicative of answerability—that is, whether a given sentence contains sufficient information to answer the input question. The resulting scores are normalized across sentences from the same paragraph or document to ensure consistent interpretation of relevance across different contexts.

To enhance the effectiveness of the sentence selector, the present invention incorporates three key training techniques that significantly improve selection accuracy. First, weight transfer involves initializing the encoder module with parameters pre-trained on a question answering task using oracle sentences—sentences known to contain the correct answer spans. This transfer learning approach leverages the rich semantic representations learned during QA training to inform the sentence selection process, substantially improving the selector's ability to identify truly relevant sentences.

Second, data modification involves creating specialized training examples that explicitly teach the selector to distinguish between answerable and non-answerable sentence-question pairs. This technique addresses the challenge that naturally occurring training data may not provide sufficient negative examples of irrelevant sentences, potentially leading to overconfident selection behavior. By artificially constructing challenging cases where similar-looking sentences differ in their actual answerability, the selector learns more discriminative features for relevance assessment.

Third, score normalization ensures that relevance scores are consistently calibrated across different documents and paragraphs, preventing situations where absolute score thresholds might perform poorly due to varying document characteristics. This normalization process follows established practices in information retrieval, adapting techniques such as those described by Clark and Gardner (2017) to the specific requirements of neural sentence selection.

The dynamic sentence selection mechanism operates by applying a configurable threshold to the normalized relevance scores, selecting all sentences whose scores exceed this threshold for subsequent processing by the QA model. This approach contrasts sharply with fixed-size selection strategies that always choose the top-k sentences regardless of their actual relevance or the specific needs of the question. The threshold parameter can be tuned during development to achieve desired trade-offs between computational efficiency and answer accuracy, and can even be adjusted dynamically during inference based on application requirements or resource constraints.

The neural question answering model component of the system can be implemented using any state-of-the-art QA architecture, including but not limited to DCN+, FusionNet, or other models employing co-attention or bidirectional attention mechanisms. The key innovation lies not in the specific QA model architecture but in the preprocessing step that dramatically reduces the input size by providing only the most relevant sentences rather than entire documents. This modular design ensures compatibility with existing QA systems while delivering substantial performance benefits.

In operation, the complete system processes an input question and associated source document(s) through the following sequence of steps. First, the sentence selector computes relevance scores for all sentences in the source material using the trained encoder-decoder architecture. Second, the dynamic selection mechanism applies the configured threshold to identify the minimal set of sentences likely to contain the answer. Third, the selected sentences are concatenated and passed to the neural QA model, which performs its standard answer extraction procedure on this reduced input. Finally, the QA model outputs the predicted answer span, which represents the system's response to the original question.

The advantages of this approach become particularly evident when processing long documents or multiple documents, as commonly encountered in open-domain QA scenarios. For instance, when applied to the TriviaQA Wikipedia dataset, which contains documents averaging 488 sentences each, the present invention achieves up to 13.8× inference speedup compared to full-document processing while simultaneously improving answer accuracy. Similarly, on the NewsQA dataset with longer paragraphs than SQuAD, the system delivers 6.9× inference speedup and 15.0× training speedup while maintaining comparable F1 scores.

Perhaps most significantly, the dynamic sentence selection approach demonstrates remarkable robustness against adversarial attacks designed to mislead conventional QA systems. When evaluated on the SQuAD-Adversarial dataset, which includes carefully crafted distractor sentences inserted into development examples, the present invention achieves state-of-the-art performance with F1 score improvements of +11.1 and +11.5 on the AddSent and AddOneSent variants, respectively. This enhanced robustness stems directly from the selector's ability to filter out irrelevant adversarial content before it reaches the QA model, effectively preventing the model from being misled by deceptive distractors.

The system's effectiveness has been validated across five diverse QA datasets representing different document lengths, domains, and complexity levels: SQuAD (Wikipedia articles with paragraph-level context), NewsQA (news articles with longer paragraphs), TriviaQA (Wikipedia domain with multiple long documents per question), SQuAD-Open (open-domain QA requiring document retrieval from all English Wikipedia), and SQuAD-Adversarial (standard SQuAD with adversarial sentence insertions). In all cases, the dynamic sentence selection approach either matches or exceeds the performance of full-document processing while delivering substantial computational savings.

Implementation details include specific hyperparameter configurations optimized for each dataset, training procedures that account for the interaction between sentence selection and QA performance, and evaluation metrics that comprehensively assess both selection accuracy and final answer quality. The system supports both extractive QA scenarios where answers correspond to exact spans in the source text and more general QA tasks where answers may require synthesis or inference beyond direct text extraction.

The modular nature of the architecture enables straightforward integration into existing natural language processing pipelines. Organizations can deploy the sentence selector as a preprocessing component before their current QA systems, immediately realizing computational benefits without requiring extensive re-engineering of their core QA infrastructure. Furthermore, the system's compatibility with transfer learning allows organizations to leverage pre-trained models while fine-tuning on their specific domain data, accelerating deployment timelines and reducing training costs.

Additional embodiments include variations where the sentence selector operates on paragraph-level units rather than individual sentences, hierarchical selection strategies that first identify relevant documents or paragraphs before selecting sentences within them, and multi-stage selection processes that iteratively refine the selected context based on intermediate QA model feedback. The core innovation—the dynamic, question-adaptive selection of minimal relevant context—remains central to all these variations, ensuring consistent benefits in efficiency, accuracy, and robustness across different implementation scenarios.

The present invention thus represents a fundamental advancement in question answering technology, addressing long-standing challenges in computational efficiency, adversarial robustness, and contextual relevance that have limited the practical applicability of previous approaches. By recognizing and exploiting the empirical observation that most questions require only minimal context for accurate resolution, the system achieves unprecedented balance between performance and resource utilization, making high-quality question answering accessible for applications previously constrained by computational limitations or reliability concerns.
