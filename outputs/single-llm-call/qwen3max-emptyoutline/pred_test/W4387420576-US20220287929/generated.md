# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention pertains to the field of dermatological and cosmetic compositions, particularly those intended for the topical treatment of skin aging and oxidative stress-related skin conditions. More specifically, the invention relates to a novel lipid-based delivery system, designated herein as SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, which comprises engineered lipid particles formulated to encapsulate and deliver antioxidant active ingredients—such as vitamins A and C—in a manner that is highly responsive to the presence of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the skin. The invention further encompasses methods for preparing such lipid particles, topical formulations containing them, and their use in improving skin hydration, reinforcing the skin barrier function, reducing transepidermal water loss, and ameliorating visible signs of skin aging, including wrinkle depth, surface roughness, and loss of elasticity. The technology described herein represents a significant advancement in noninvasive skin bioengineering by leveraging the intrinsic chemical and physical properties of natural waxes and polyunsaturated fatty acids to create a ROS-sensitive matrix that not only protects labile antioxidants from premature degradation but also enhances their targeted release and efficacy in the presence of oxidative stress. This invention is situated at the intersection of nanotechnology, dermopharmacology, and cosmetic science, offering a scientifically grounded solution to the persistent challenges of cutaneous aging driven by both intrinsic biological processes and extrinsic environmental aggressors.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Skin aging is a complex, multifactorial process influenced by both endogenous (intrinsic) and exogenous (extrinsic) factors. Intrinsic aging is an inevitable, genetically programmed phenomenon that occurs over time, characterized by a progressive decline in cellular proliferation, reduced synthesis of extracellular matrix components such as collagen and elastin in the dermis, and increased expression of matrix metalloproteinases that degrade structural proteins. Extrinsic aging, on the other hand, is primarily driven by environmental exposures—most notably ultraviolet (UV) radiation, atmospheric pollutants, and lifestyle factors such as smoking—which accelerate the accumulation of oxidative damage in skin cells. Central to both forms of aging is the dysregulation of redox homeostasis, leading to chronic oxidative stress. Under physiological conditions, reactive oxygen species (ROS), including superoxide anion, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radicals, are generated as byproducts of normal aerobic metabolism and play essential roles in cellular signaling and immune defense. However, when ROS production exceeds the capacity of endogenous antioxidant systems—comprising enzymatic scavengers like superoxide dismutase and catalase, as well as nonenzymatic molecules such as glutathione, vitamin E, vitamin C, and vitamin A—oxidative damage ensues. This damage manifests as lipid peroxidation of cell membranes, protein carbonylation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and DNA lesions, particularly at telomeric regions, ultimately triggering cellular senescence, apoptosis, or impaired regenerative capacity.

The aesthetic consequences of this cumulative oxidative insult are well-documented: increased wrinkle formation, loss of skin firmness and elasticity, uneven pigmentation, dryness, impaired barrier integrity, and diminished hydration. These manifestations not only affect appearance but also compromise the skin’s primary protective functions. In response, the cosmetic and dermatological industries have long sought effective strategies to mitigate oxidative stress and its downstream effects. Antioxidant vitamins—particularly vitamin A (retinoids), vitamin C (ascorbic acid and its derivatives), and vitamin E (tocopherols)—have been widely incorporated into topical formulations due to their proven ability to neutralize free radicals, stimulate collagen synthesis, and support epidermal repair. However, the practical application of these actives is fraught with challenges. Vitamin C, for instance, is highly unstable in aqueous environments and prone to oxidation upon exposure to light, air, or heat, rapidly losing its efficacy. Similarly, retinoids are photosensitive and can cause significant irritation, erythema, and peeling when applied in free form, limiting patient compliance and therapeutic utility. Moreover, the hydrophilic nature of vitamin C and the lipophilic character of vitamin A present formulation difficulties in achieving co-delivery within a single stable vehicle.

To address these limitations, researchers have turned to encapsulation technologies, particularly lipid-based nanoparticles such as solid lipid nanoparticles (SLNs) and nanostructured lipid carriers (NLCs). These systems offer several advantages: they enhance the chemical stability of encapsulated actives, provide controlled or sustained release profiles, reduce skin irritation by shielding the active from direct contact with the stratum corneum, and improve penetration through the lipid-rich intercellular matrix of the epidermis due to their biomimetic composition. Lipid nanoparticles composed of natural waxes, triglycerides, and fatty acids exhibit high affinity for skin lipids—such as ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids—thereby facilitating integration into the skin barrier and promoting occlusion, which in turn enhances hydration and reduces transepidermal water loss (TEWL). Despite these benefits, conventional encapsulation systems often lack responsiveness to the specific pathological conditions they aim to treat. Most release their payload passively over time, regardless of the local oxidative environment, potentially resulting in suboptimal dosing at the site of need or premature depletion of the active ingredient.

Furthermore, some studies have reported paradoxical pro-oxidant effects when high concentrations of certain antioxidants—especially vitamin C—are applied in free form, wherein the antioxidant itself can participate in redox cycling and generate additional ROS under specific conditions, such as in the presence of transition metals like iron. This underscores the critical need for delivery systems that not only protect antioxidants but also modulate their release in a context-dependent manner. Prior art in the field includes various lipid particle systems, but none have demonstrated a mechanism whereby the release or activation of the encapsulated antioxidant is directly triggered or enhanced by elevated levels of ROS—a hallmark of aged or photo-damaged skin. Additionally, many existing systems rely on organic solvents, high-energy homogenization, or synthetic polymers, raising concerns about environmental sustainability, biocompatibility, and regulatory approval for cosmetic use.

The present invention arises from the recognition that an ideal anti-aging delivery platform should be (1) composed of natural, biocompatible, and sustainable ingredients; (2) capable of co-encapsulating both hydrophilic and lipophilic antioxidants; (3) stable during storage and upon application; (4) non-irritating and compatible with the skin’s natural barrier; and (5) functionally responsive to the oxidative microenvironment of aged skin. While earlier attempts have utilized lipid nanoparticles for cosmetic delivery, they have not achieved the synergistic combination of ROS sensitivity, dual-vitamin compatibility, process sustainability, and demonstrable clinical superiority over both free and conventionally encapsulated actives. Thus, there remains a significant unmet need for a next-generation topical delivery system that intelligently responds to the biochemical signatures of skin aging, thereby maximizing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing adverse effects. The background art fails to disclose or suggest a lipid particle system prepared from a specific blend of jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax, combined with natural polyunsaturated fatty acids from safflower oil, and engineered to exhibit heightened sensitivity to ROS through a solvent-free, low-temperature emulsification process that yields particles with a defined size distribution and superior oxidative stability, as embodied in the present invention.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel lipid particle-based delivery system, referred to as SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, designed for the topical administration of antioxidant active ingredients to combat skin aging and oxidative stress. The invention is predicated on the discovery that a specific composition of natural waxes and polyunsaturated fatty acids, when processed via a modified emulsification method under controlled thermal and mechanical conditions, yields lipid particles that exhibit exceptional sensitivity to reactive oxygen species (ROS) while simultaneously providing enhanced stability, hydration, and barrier-repairing properties. The core of the invention lies in the unique formulation and preparation process of these lipid particles, which enables the efficient co-encapsulation of both lipophilic (e.g., retinyl palmitate, a form of vitamin A) and hydrophilic (e.g., sodium ascorbyl phosphate, a stable derivative of vitamin C) antioxidants within a single, unified matrix. This matrix is not merely a passive carrier but functions as an active, responsive system that preferentially releases or activates its payload in the presence of elevated ROS levels, thereby targeting the very condition it is intended to treat.

The lipid particles are prepared by first melting a blend of jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax at a moderate temperature of approximately 55°C, followed by the incorporation of natural polyunsaturated fatty acids derived from safflower oil and other liposoluble actives. Retinyl palmitate is added just prior to emulsification to preserve its integrity. Concurrently, an aqueous phase containing decyl glucoside—a mild, nonionic surfactant—and sodium ascorbyl phosphate is prepared at a slightly lower temperature. The lipid mixture is then emulsified into the aqueous phase under high-shear mixing at 3500 rpm, yielding a fine emulsion that is subsequently filtered through a 25 μm sieve to ensure uniform particle size. Rapid cooling to 10°C solidifies the lipid core, resulting in stable microparticles that are stored at 4°C until formulation into a final gel base. Notably, this process avoids the use of organic solvents and high temperatures, aligning with green chemistry principles and enhancing product safety.

In vitro studies using human keratinocytes (HaCaT cells) challenged with menadione—a known inducer of mitochondrial ROS—demonstrate that formulations containing SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ significantly inhibit hydrogen peroxide production compared to placebo, free vitamin, and conventionally encapsulated vitamin controls. At a concentration of 5 mg/mL, the SIREN formulation reduced ROS production to 44.5% of the positive control, whereas free vitamins exhibited pro-oxidant behavior, increasing ROS levels beyond baseline. This superior antioxidant activity is attributed to the protective encapsulation that prevents premature degradation and the ROS-responsive nature of the lipid matrix, which may undergo structural changes or enhanced permeability in oxidative environments, facilitating targeted release.

In vivo clinical evaluations on 30 healthy female volunteers aged 36–58 confirm the multifunctional benefits of the invention. After four weeks of daily application, the SIREN-containing formulation significantly improved stratum corneum hydration (+20.17% over placebo), outperforming both free and encapsulated vitamin gels. It also demonstrated the greatest reduction in transepidermal water loss (TEWL), indicating superior barrier reinforcement—likely due to the occlusive film formed by the hydrophobic lipid particles that minimizes moisture evaporation. Furthermore, 3D topographical analysis of facial skin revealed statistically significant reductions in both average skin roughness (Ra) and mean depth of roughness (Rz), confirming visible improvements in skin texture and wrinkle depth. These results collectively establish that SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ is not only a more effective antioxidant delivery system but also a comprehensive anti-aging solution that addresses hydration, barrier integrity, and surface morphology.

Thus, the invention encompasses: (1) the lipid particles themselves, defined by their specific composition and preparation method; (2) topical formulations comprising these particles in a cosmetically acceptable base; and (3) methods of using said formulations to treat or prevent skin aging, reduce oxidative stress, enhance hydration, restore barrier function, and improve skin smoothness. The invention represents a paradigm shift in cosmetic delivery by integrating responsiveness, sustainability, and clinical efficacy into a single, patentable platform.

## DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a novel lipid particle system, designated SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, and its use in topical compositions for the prevention and treatment of skin aging and related conditions associated with oxidative stress. The invention is based on a scientifically validated approach that combines advanced material science with dermatological insight to create a delivery system that is not only protective and stable but also functionally intelligent—responding dynamically to the biochemical environment of aged or stressed skin. The following detailed description sets forth the composition, preparation, and functional characteristics of the invention, supported by experimental data demonstrating its superiority over existing approaches.

The lipid particles of the present invention are composed of a carefully selected blend of natural waxes and fatty acids that confer both structural integrity and functional responsiveness. Specifically, the lipid phase comprises jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax, melted together at a temperature of approximately 55°C. Jojoba wax, a liquid wax ester derived from the seeds of Simmondsia chinensis, is renowned for its similarity to human sebum, providing excellent spreadability and biocompatibility. Mimosa wax, extracted from the bark of Acacia dealbata, contributes hardness and high melting point, aiding in particle stability. Sunflower seed wax, obtained from Helianthus annuus, offers a balanced profile of saturated and unsaturated chains, enhancing flexibility and compatibility with skin lipids. This ternary wax system is uniquely advantageous because it allows particle formation at relatively low processing temperatures, avoiding thermal degradation of sensitive actives and eliminating the need for energy-intensive or solvent-based methods.

To this molten wax blend, natural polyunsaturated fatty acids derived from safflower oil (Carthamus tinctorius) are added. Safflower oil is rich in linoleic acid (C18:2), an omega-6 fatty acid known for its role in maintaining skin barrier function and modulating inflammatory responses. The incorporation of these unsaturated chains introduces sites of vulnerability to oxidation, which, contrary to being a drawback, is strategically leveraged in the present invention. Under normal conditions, these fatty acids are shielded within the solidified wax matrix. However, in the presence of elevated ROS—as found in photoaged or intrinsically aged skin—these sites undergo controlled oxidation, leading to localized disruption of the lipid structure and enhanced release of encapsulated antioxidants. This mechanism ensures that the active ingredients are delivered precisely where and when they are most needed, maximizing therapeutic impact while minimizing systemic exposure or off-target effects.

The active ingredients encapsulated within the lipid particles include both lipophilic and hydrophilic antioxidants. Retinyl palmitate, a stable ester of vitamin A, is added to the lipid phase just before emulsification to preserve its potency. In the aqueous phase, sodium ascorbyl phosphate—a water-soluble, oxidation-resistant derivative of vitamin C—is dissolved along with decyl glucoside, a mild, plant-derived surfactant that facilitates emulsification without compromising skin integrity. The emulsification step is performed at 3500 rpm for 10 minutes at 48–50°C, creating a fine oil-in-water emulsion. This high-shear mixing ensures uniform dispersion and small particle size, which is further refined by sieving through a 25 μm mesh to remove aggregates and guarantee consistency. The emulsion is then rapidly cooled to 10°C to solidify the lipid core, yielding discrete microparticles with diameters not exceeding 25 μm. These particles are stored at 4°C to maintain stability until incorporation into a final gel base, which may comprise standard cosmetic excipients such as water, glycerin, xanthan gum, and preservatives.

The resulting formulation exhibits remarkable stability, as confirmed by accelerated testing at 40°C, room temperature, and 4°C over three months, with no observed changes in color, pH, viscosity, odor, or physical separation. Oxidative stability, assessed via ASTM D1959-97 standard, reveals an iodine index below 60 g Iodine/100 g, indicating low susceptibility to rancidity. This stability is critical for shelf life and consumer safety, yet it does not preclude functional responsiveness—the particles remain inert under normal conditions but become activated in oxidative milieus.

In vitro validation was conducted using HaCaT human keratinocytes, a well-established model for skin toxicity and antioxidant studies. Cells were pretreated with test formulations at 5 and 1.25 mg/mL, then challenged with 20 μM menadione to induce ROS production. Hydrogen peroxide levels were quantified using the ROS-Glo™ H₂O₂ assay, which measures bioluminescence proportional to H₂O₂ concentration. Results showed that the SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ formulation reduced ROS production to 44.5% (at 5 mg/mL) and 78.59% (at 1.25 mg/mL) of the menadione-only control, demonstrating potent dose-dependent antioxidant activity. In stark contrast, the gel containing free vitamins increased ROS production to 95.3% at 5 mg/mL—likely due to the pro-oxidant behavior of unshielded vitamin C in the presence of cellular metal ions. The encapsulated vitamin control performed better than the free form but still lagged significantly behind SIREN, with 88.51% ROS at 5 mg/mL. These findings confirm that the invention’s matrix not only protects antioxidants but also enhances their net efficacy by preventing deleterious side reactions.

In vivo studies involved 30 healthy female volunteers aged 36–58, following a randomized, split-face, blinded design compliant with the Declaration of Helsinki and GDPR. Participants applied test products daily for four weeks, with measurements taken at baseline (T0) and after treatment (T1). Hydration was assessed via corneometry (CM 825), revealing a 20.17% increase in stratum corneum water content for the SIREN formulation over placebo—significantly higher than the +9.54% and +7.51% improvements seen with free and encapsulated vitamins, respectively. TEWL measurements using Tewameter TM 300 showed the greatest reduction with SIREN, confirming its superior barrier-repairing capability, attributed to the occlusive film formed by the lipid particles that retards water evaporation from deeper layers. Anti-aging efficacy was evaluated via 3D skin topography (dermaTOP software), which quantified reductions in Ra (average roughness) and Rz (mean depth of roughness). Statistical analysis (p < 0.05) confirmed that only the SIREN formulation produced significant improvements in both parameters, translating to visibly smoother, less wrinkled skin.

In summary, the present invention provides a breakthrough in topical anti-aging therapy by integrating a ROS-responsive lipid particle system that delivers superior antioxidant protection, hydration, and barrier support. Its solvent-free, sustainable preparation, combined with clinically proven efficacy, positions SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ as a transformative platform for next-generation cosmetic and dermatological products.
