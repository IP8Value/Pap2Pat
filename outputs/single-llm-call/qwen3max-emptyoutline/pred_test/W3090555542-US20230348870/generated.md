# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of gene therapy and, more specifically, to compositions and methods for the treatment of Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) through CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing of satellite cells in skeletal muscle. The invention provides novel adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors engineered to efficiently transduce quiescent and activated satellite cells—muscle-resident stem cells responsible for postnatal muscle growth and regeneration—and to deliver CRISPR/Cas9 components that restore functional dystrophin expression by correcting mutations in the Dmd gene. The invention further encompasses optimized AAV serotypes, muscle-specific promoters, and dual-vector systems that enable durable, long-term dystrophin production from edited satellite cells, even after episomal vector loss due to cell division or muscle injury. Additionally, the invention includes pharmaceutical formulations, kits, and therapeutic protocols for systemic or localized administration of these gene-editing constructs to patients suffering from DMD or other dystrophinopathies.

## INTRODUCTION

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a severe X-linked recessive disorder caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene (DMD), leading to the absence or dysfunction of the dystrophin protein—a critical structural component that stabilizes the sarcolemma during muscle contraction. Without functional dystrophin, skeletal and cardiac myofibers undergo repeated cycles of necrosis and incomplete regeneration, ultimately resulting in progressive muscle wasting, fibrosis, loss of ambulation, cardiomyopathy, and premature death, typically by the third decade of life. Current standard-of-care treatments, including corticosteroids and supportive therapies, only modestly slow disease progression and do not address the underlying genetic defect. Consequently, there remains an urgent unmet medical need for curative strategies that restore dystrophin expression at the genomic level.

Gene editing technologies, particularly those based on the CRISPR/Cas9 system, offer a promising path toward permanent correction of DMD-causing mutations. Unlike gene replacement approaches that deliver a truncated but functional dystrophin transgene (e.g., micro-dystrophin), CRISPR/Cas9 can precisely excise or correct mutated exons to restore the endogenous reading frame, thereby enabling the production of full-length or near-full-length dystrophin under its native regulatory elements. This approach preserves physiological spatiotemporal expression patterns and avoids potential immune responses against non-self transgene products. However, a major challenge in achieving durable therapeutic efficacy lies in the transient nature of current delivery vectors, especially adeno-associated viruses (AAVs), which predominantly exist as episomes in post-mitotic cells and are rapidly diluted upon cell division. Since skeletal muscle is continuously subjected to damage and repair throughout life, the regenerative capacity of muscle relies heavily on satellite cells—resident stem cells that proliferate, differentiate, and fuse to form new myofibers. If these satellite cells remain uncorrected, newly regenerated muscle fibers will lack dystrophin, leading to recurrent degeneration and eventual therapeutic failure.

Historically, it was believed that satellite cells were refractory to AAV transduction due to their quiescent state and low expression of AAV receptor proteins. Early studies using direct reporter expression failed to detect significant AAV-mediated transgene expression in satellite cells, casting doubt on the feasibility of targeting this critical cell population. However, more recent work employing sensitive lineage-tracing strategies has revealed that certain AAV serotypes can indeed transduce satellite cells in vivo, albeit at variable efficiencies depending on the serotype, route of administration, and disease context. Despite these advances, key questions remain unresolved: Which AAV serotypes most effectively target satellite cells following systemic delivery? Can CRISPR/Cas9 components be delivered to satellite cells to achieve functional gene correction? Do muscle-specific promoters—commonly used to restrict transgene expression to striated muscle and mitigate off-target effects—remain active in satellite cells? And critically, can gene-edited satellite cells serve as a long-term reservoir for dystrophin-positive myofiber regeneration?

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a comprehensive platform for satellite cell-directed gene editing in DMD. Through systematic profiling of AAV serotypes, promoter architectures, and delivery modalities, the inventors have identified optimal conditions for efficient transduction and genome editing of satellite cells in both healthy and dystrophic muscle environments. Furthermore, the invention demonstrates, for the first time, that CRISPR-corrected satellite cells retain their regenerative potential and can give rise to dystrophin-expressing myofibers over multiple rounds of injury-induced regeneration, even after complete loss of the AAV vector. These findings establish a foundation for next-generation DMD therapies that confer lifelong benefits by engineering the muscle stem cell compartment itself.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides compositions and methods for the durable treatment of Duchenne muscular dystrophy through CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing of muscle satellite cells. In one aspect, the invention discloses adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors of serotype AAV8 or AAV9 that exhibit superior tropism for satellite cells compared to other commonly used serotypes, enabling efficient delivery of gene-editing machinery following either local intramuscular or systemic intravenous administration. In another aspect, the invention features dual-AAV systems wherein one vector encodes a Cas9 nuclease—preferably Staphylococcus aureus Cas9 (SaCas9)—under the control of a muscle-specific promoter selected from the group consisting of MHCK7, CK8e, and SPc5-12, and a second vector encodes two guide RNAs (gRNAs) designed to excise a disease-causing exon (e.g., exon 23 in the mdx mouse model) from the Dmd gene. Surprisingly, the inventors discovered that these muscle-specific promoters, previously assumed to be inactive in satellite cells, drive sufficient Cas9 expression to mediate robust gene editing in this stem cell population, thereby offering enhanced safety by minimizing off-target editing in non-muscle tissues and reducing immunogenicity associated with constitutive promoters like CMV.

In a further aspect, the invention provides methods for quantifying gene-editing outcomes in purified satellite cells using unbiased Tn5 tagmentation-based sequencing, revealing that while total editing frequencies are lower in satellite cells than in bulk myofibers, the corrected cells retain long-term regenerative capacity. The invention also includes a serial muscle injury model demonstrating that dystrophin expression persists through multiple rounds of degeneration and regeneration despite complete clearance of the AAV vector, confirming that edited satellite cells serve as a self-renewing source of dystrophin-positive myofibers. Additionally, the invention encompasses transplantation assays proving that CRISPR-corrected satellite cells engraft into recipient muscle and differentiate into functional, dystrophin-expressing myofibers.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the disclosed AAV vectors are formulated for systemic or localized delivery and may include excipients that enhance stability, biodistribution, or cellular uptake. Kits containing the dual-AAV components, along with instructions for use in treating DMD, are also provided. The methods of the invention are applicable not only to DMD but also to other genetic myopathies where satellite cell correction could provide sustained therapeutic benefit. Collectively, these innovations represent a paradigm shift in gene therapy for muscular dystrophies by targeting the root of muscle regeneration—the satellite cell—thereby enabling potentially curative, lifelong correction of the dystrophin defect.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### 1. Definitions

As used herein, the term “satellite cell” refers to a quiescent or activated muscle stem cell located between the basal lamina and sarcolemma of skeletal muscle fibers, characterized by expression of the transcription factor Pax7 and capable of self-renewal and differentiation into myoblasts that fuse to form new myofibers or repair damaged ones. The term “dystrophin” denotes the protein product encoded by the DMD gene in humans or Dmd gene in mice, which functions as a critical link between the intracellular actin cytoskeleton and the extracellular matrix via the dystrophin-associated glycoprotein complex; “functional dystrophin” refers to a dystrophin protein that is sufficiently expressed and properly localized to stabilize the sarcolemma and prevent muscle fiber necrosis. “CRISPR/Cas9-based gene editing system” encompasses a molecular toolkit comprising a Cas9 nuclease and at least one guide RNA (gRNA) that directs Cas9 to a specific genomic locus to induce a double-strand break, which is then repaired by endogenous DNA repair mechanisms such as non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) or homology-directed repair (HDR). “Adeno-associated virus (AAV)” refers to a small, non-enveloped, single-stranded DNA parvovirus commonly used as a gene delivery vector, with “serotype” denoting variants distinguished by differences in capsid protein sequence that confer distinct tissue tropisms; exemplary serotypes include AAV1, AAV2, AAV5, AAV6.2, AAV8, and AAV9. “Muscle-specific promoter” means a nucleic acid regulatory sequence that drives gene expression preferentially in skeletal and/or cardiac muscle cells, including but not limited to MHCK7, CK8e, and SPc5-12, each derived from enhancer and promoter elements of muscle-expressed genes such as creatine kinase or myosin heavy chain. “Gene editing outcome” includes any alteration to the genomic DNA sequence at the target site, such as exon deletion, insertion or deletion (indel), inversion, or integration of vector sequences like AAV inverted terminal repeats (ITRs). “Systemic administration” refers to delivery of a therapeutic agent into the circulatory system (e.g., via intravenous injection), whereas “local administration” denotes direct injection into a specific tissue such as skeletal muscle. “Durable therapeutic effect” signifies sustained expression of the therapeutic protein (e.g., dystrophin) over an extended period, including through cycles of muscle degeneration and regeneration, attributable to correction of long-lived or self-renewing cell populations such as satellite cells.

### 2. Dystrophin Gene

The dystrophin gene (DMD in humans, Dmd in mice) is one of the largest known human genes, spanning approximately 2.4 megabases on the X chromosome and comprising 79 exons. It encodes a 427-kDa cytoskeletal protein that is essential for maintaining the structural integrity of muscle fibers during contraction. Dystrophin links the intracellular actin network to the extracellular matrix through interactions with β-dystroglycan and other components of the dystrophin-associated glycoprotein complex (DGC). Mutations in DMD—most commonly large deletions or duplications affecting one or more exons—disrupt the reading frame, leading to premature termination codons and nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, resulting in the complete absence of functional dystrophin protein. This absence renders muscle fibers highly susceptible to mechanical stress, triggering chronic inflammation, necrosis, and failed regeneration. In the widely used mdx mouse model of DMD, a point mutation in exon 23 introduces a premature stop codon, mimicking the human disease phenotype albeit with milder severity. Therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring dystrophin function must therefore address the underlying genetic lesion. Exon-skipping approaches using antisense oligonucleotides can transiently restore the reading frame at the mRNA level, but require lifelong repeated dosing. In contrast, permanent correction of the genomic DNA via gene editing offers the potential for a one-time curative intervention. The present invention focuses on CRISPR/Cas9-mediated excision of mutated exons (e.g., exon 23 in mdx mice) to reframe the transcript and enable production of internally deleted but functional dystrophin isoforms, analogous to those seen in the milder Becker muscular dystrophy.

### 3. CRISPR/Cas9-Based Gene Editing System

The CRISPR/Cas9 system employed in the present invention consists of two core components: a Cas9 endonuclease and one or more guide RNAs (gRNAs). The gRNA is a chimeric RNA molecule composed of a CRISPR RNA (crRNA) that provides sequence specificity through ~20-nucleotide complementarity to the target DNA, and a trans-activating crRNA (tracrRNA) that facilitates binding to Cas9. Upon formation of the Cas9-gRNA ribonucleoprotein complex, Cas9 scans the genome for a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) sequence adjacent to the target site; for Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9 (SpCas9), the PAM is 5′-NGG-3′, whereas for Staphylococcus aureus Cas9 (SaCas9), used herein due to its smaller size compatible with AAV packaging constraints, the PAM is 5′-NNGRRT-3′. Once bound, Cas9 induces a blunt double-strand break ~3 nucleotides upstream of the PAM. In the absence of a repair template, the break is resolved by error-prone NHEJ, often resulting in small insertions or deletions (indels) that can disrupt gene function. However, when two gRNAs are used to target sequences flanking a genomic region (e.g., introns surrounding a mutated exon), simultaneous double-strand breaks can lead to precise excision of the intervening sequence, effectively deleting the exon and restoring the reading frame if the deletion length is a multiple of three nucleotides. The present invention utilizes this dual-gRNA strategy to excise exon 23 in the mdx mouse Dmd gene, thereby converting a null allele into a Becker-like allele that produces functional dystrophin. The system is delivered via AAV vectors, with SaCas9 and the gRNA cassettes packaged separately due to the limited cargo capacity of AAV (~4.7 kb).

### 4. Genetic Constructs

The genetic constructs of the present invention are designed for efficient packaging into AAV vectors and robust expression in muscle and satellite cells. In one embodiment, a first AAV vector comprises a nucleic acid sequence encoding SaCas9 operably linked to a muscle-specific promoter selected from MHCK7, CK8e, or SPc5-12, followed by a polyadenylation signal (e.g., bovine growth hormone polyA, bGHpA). The MHCK7 promoter, in particular, contains a hybrid regulatory element combining the mouse creatine kinase (CK) enhancer and promoter with a conserved ~190-bp fragment from the alpha-myosin heavy chain (α-MHC) enhancer, including MCAT and GATA transcription factor binding sites that enhance activity in both myofibers and satellite cells. A second AAV vector encodes two gRNA expression cassettes, each driven by a human U6 promoter, targeting intronic sequences immediately upstream of exon 23 and downstream of exon 23 in the mouse Dmd gene. The gRNA sequences are optimized for high on-target activity and minimal off-target effects, with target sites selected based on proximity to the exon-intron boundaries to maximize the likelihood of precise excision. Both vectors include AAV2-derived inverted terminal repeats (ITRs) for packaging and genome circularization. Control constructs may include vectors expressing Cre recombinase under the same promoters for use in lineage-tracing studies. All constructs are validated for integrity by restriction digest (e.g., SmaI) prior to AAV production to ensure intact ITRs.

### 5. Pharmaceutical Compositions

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise the aforementioned AAV vectors suspended in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier suitable for in vivo administration. The carrier may include isotonic saline, phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), or lactated Ringer’s solution, optionally supplemented with stabilizers such as human serum albumin, surfactants like polysorbate 80, or cryoprotectants such as sucrose or trehalose to maintain vector integrity during storage and handling. The compositions are sterile-filtered (e.g., through a 0.22-μm filter) and may be lyophilized for long-term storage, with reconstitution prior to use. The titer of each AAV vector is precisely quantified by quantitative PCR (qPCR) using a plasmid standard curve to ensure consistent dosing across batches. For systemic administration, the composition is formulated at a concentration that delivers a total dose of 1×10¹² to 5×10¹² vector genomes (vg) per vector per kilogram of body weight via intravenous infusion. For local intramuscular injection, doses range from 1×10¹¹ to 1×10¹² vg per vector per injection site. The compositions may be co-administered with immunosuppressive agents (e.g., tacrolimus) to mitigate potential immune responses against the Cas9 protein or AAV capsid, particularly in repeat-dosing scenarios.

### 6. Administration

Administration of the AAV vectors may be performed via systemic or local routes, depending on the clinical indication and desired distribution. Systemic administration, typically via tail vein injection in preclinical models or peripheral vein infusion in humans, enables widespread delivery to skeletal and cardiac muscle throughout the body, which is critical for treating a systemic disease like DMD. Local administration, such as direct intramuscular injection into affected muscles (e.g., tibialis anterior, quadriceps), may be used for proof-of-concept studies or to treat focal muscle groups. The timing of administration is optimized to coincide with periods of active muscle regeneration in DMD patients, as the dystrophic environment enhances satellite cell activation and AAV transduction efficiency. Following administration, the AAV vectors transduce both mature myofibers and satellite cells; in the latter, the episomal vector is gradually lost upon cell division, but CRISPR-mediated correction of the genomic Dmd locus ensures that all progeny cells inherit the corrected allele. Patients may receive a single dose, as the self-renewing nature of satellite cells provides a persistent source of corrected myoblasts. Monitoring of therapeutic efficacy includes assessment of dystrophin expression by immunofluorescence or Western blot, functional muscle tests, and quantification of gene-editing outcomes in muscle biopsies using deep sequencing.

### 7. Kits

Kits according to the invention contain the essential components for performing the disclosed gene-editing therapy, packaged in a sterile, sealed container with appropriate labeling and instructions for use. A typical kit includes two vials: a first vial containing a lyophilized or liquid formulation of AAV9 (or AAV8) vector encoding SaCas9 under a muscle-specific promoter (e.g., MHCK7), and a second vial containing AAV9 vector encoding the dual gRNAs targeting Dmd exon 23. Each vial is labeled with vector identity, titer, expiration date, and storage conditions (e.g., −80°C). The kit further includes a diluent for reconstitution (if lyophilized), sterile syringes and needles for administration, and a detailed protocol specifying dosing, route of administration, and patient monitoring guidelines. Optional components may include immunosuppressive drugs, biopsy collection tools for post-treatment analysis, and primers/probes for PCR-based detection of exon deletion. The kits are intended for use by trained medical professionals in clinical or research settings and comply with regulatory standards for gene therapy products.

### 8. Methods

The methods of the invention encompass therapeutic, diagnostic, and research applications centered on satellite cell-directed gene editing for DMD. A primary method involves administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of the dual-AAV CRISPR/Cas9 system described herein, resulting in permanent correction of the Dmd mutation in satellite cells and sustained dystrophin expression. Another method provides for assessing the efficiency of satellite cell transduction by delivering an AAV vector encoding Cre recombinase under a test promoter to a dual-reporter animal (e.g., Pax7nGFP;Ai9 mouse) and quantifying tdTomato+ satellite cells via flow cytometry or immunofluorescence. A further method enables quantification of gene-editing outcomes in purified satellite cells by isolating genomic DNA or cDNA from FACS-sorted Pax7+ cells and performing unbiased Tn5 tagmentation-based sequencing across the target locus, allowing precise measurement of exon deletions, indels, inversions, and ITR integrations. Additionally, the invention includes a serial injury model wherein treated muscle is subjected to repeated barium chloride-induced necrosis to evaluate the durability of dystrophin expression in the absence of AAV vector persistence. Transplantation methods involve isolating CRISPR-corrected satellite cells from donor animals and engrafting them into irradiated, immunosuppressed mdx hosts to demonstrate their regenerative capacity and contribution to dystrophin+ myofiber formation. These methods collectively validate the therapeutic premise that satellite cell editing confers long-term benefit in DMD.

## 9. EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Profiling of AAV Serotypes for Satellite Cell Targeting Efficiency. To identify AAV serotypes with optimal tropism for muscle satellite cells, the inventors utilized a dual-reporter mouse model (Pax7nGFP;Ai9;mdx) in which satellite cells are permanently labeled with nuclear GFP (nGFP) due to a knock-in at the Pax7 locus, and AAV-transduced cells are irreversibly marked with tdTomato upon Cre-mediated excision of a loxP-flanked STOP cassette at the Rosa26 locus. A panel of AAV serotypes (AAV1, AAV2, AAV5, AAV6.2, AAV8, AAV9), each encoding CMV-driven Cre recombinase, was injected intramuscularly into the tibialis anterior (TA) muscle of these mice at a dose of 4.72×10¹¹ vector genomes (vg). Eight weeks post-injection, muscles were dissociated, and single-cell suspensions were analyzed by flow cytometry to quantify the percentage of nGFP+ satellite cells that co-expressed tdTomato, indicating successful AAV transduction. AAV9, AAV6.2, and AAV8 demonstrated the highest recombination efficiencies, labeling approximately 60% of satellite cells, whereas AAV1, AAV2, and AAV5 showed markedly lower transduction (<20%). To assess systemic delivery, the top four serotypes were administered via tail vein injection at 2×10¹² vg, and satellite cells isolated from various skeletal muscles (e.g., TA, gastrocnemius, quadriceps) were analyzed. AAV8 and AAV9 consistently outperformed AAV6.2 and AAV1, achieving 20–30% transduction across muscle types. Immunofluorescence staining of muscle sections confirmed colocalization of Pax7 and tdTomato in satellite cells. Notably, when AAV9-CMV-Cre was systemically delivered to wild-type (WT) versus mdx mice, significantly higher transduction was observed in mdx satellite cells across all muscles except the diaphragm, likely due to the activated state of satellite cells in the dystrophic environment. These results establish AAV8 and AAV9 as preferred serotypes for satellite cell targeting in both local and systemic contexts.

### Example 2

AAV9-CMV-CRISPR Mediates Gene Editing in Satellite Cells In Vivo. To determine whether CRISPR/Cas9 components could be delivered to satellite cells to correct the Dmd mutation, Pax7nGFP;mdx mice were injected intramuscularly with a dual-AAV9 system: one vector encoding CMV-driven SaCas9 and another encoding two gRNAs flanking exon 23. A control group received PBS. Eight weeks later, satellite cells were isolated by FACS based on nGFP expression, and genomic DNA was extracted for analysis. Nested PCR across the Dmd locus revealed a 347-bp deletion band corresponding to precise excision of exon 23 exclusively in CRISPR-treated samples. Systemic delivery of the same vectors (5×10¹² vg each) also yielded detectable deletion bands in satellite cells from hindlimb muscles and diaphragm in four out of five mice. Sanger sequencing of the deletion product confirmed perfect ligation of intron 22 to intron 23. To quantify editing outcomes comprehensively, the inventors employed an unbiased Tn5 tagmentation-based sequencing approach on satellite cell genomic DNA. This method revealed that total gene-editing events (including deletions, indels, inversions, and ITR integrations) ranged from 0.01% to 1%, with indels at single gRNA sites being most frequent. Analysis of cDNA showed that exon 23 deletion at the mRNA level ranged from 0.4% to 1%, indicating that edited transcripts escape nonsense-mediated decay. These frequencies were approximately 10-fold lower than those observed in bulk muscle, likely due to the requirement for dual-vector transduction and concurrent double-strand breaks at two sites. Nevertheless, the data conclusively demonstrate that CRISPR-mediated gene editing occurs in satellite cells in vivo.

### Example 3

Muscle-Specific Promoters Drive Gene Expression and Editing in Satellite Cells. To evaluate the activity of muscle-specific promoters in satellite cells, AAV9 vectors encoding Cre recombinase under the control of CMV, MHCK7, CK8e, or SPc5-12 promoters were injected intramuscularly into Pax7nGFP;Ai9;mdx mice at 4×10¹⁰ vg. Eight weeks later, recombination efficiency was assessed by flow cytometry. CMV drove the highest tdTomato expression (33% of satellite cells), while MHCK7 and CK8e achieved ~15.6%, and SPc5-12 reached 11.5%, confirming that these muscle-specific promoters are active in satellite cells, albeit at reduced levels compared to CMV. To compare gene-editing efficacy, SaCas9 was placed under the same promoters in the dual-AAV9-CRISPR system and delivered intramuscularly. Immunofluorescence staining of TA sections showed that MHCK7 yielded the highest percentage of dystrophin+ fibers (73%), followed by SPc5-12 (53%) and CK8e (48.3%), all surpassing CMV (35%). Unbiased sequencing of bulk muscle DNA corroborated these findings, with MHCK7 producing the highest deletion frequency. Critically, nested PCR on sorted satellite cells confirmed exon 23 deletion in all promoter groups, proving that muscle-specific promoters support CRISPR editing in satellite cells. These results advocate for the use of MHCK7 or similar promoters to restrict Cas9 expression to muscle lineages, thereby enhancing safety without compromising efficacy.

### Example 4

Sustained Dystrophin Expression After Serial Muscle Injury. To test whether CRISPR-corrected satellite cells provide long-term dystrophin production, mdx mice were injected intramuscularly with AAV9-CMV-CRISPR and subjected to a serial injury regimen starting four weeks post-injection. Barium chloride (BaCl₂) was injected into the TA every two weeks for up to three rounds, inducing >80% myofiber necrosis per injury. Western blot analysis after three injuries confirmed complete loss of SaCas9 protein, indicating AAV vector clearance. Despite this, immunofluorescence revealed persistent dystrophin expression: initially 28.3% of fibers were dystrophin+, decreasing to ~10% after two or three injuries. This maintenance of dystrophin, in the absence of vector, strongly suggests that edited satellite cells contributed to regeneration. The partial decline in dystrophin+ fibers may reflect competition between corrected and uncorrected satellite cells or selective advantages conferred by dystrophin expression itself, as dystrophin has been implicated in satellite cell maintenance. This example validates the central hypothesis that satellite cell editing enables durable therapeutic effects through repeated cycles of muscle turnover.

### Example 5

Transplantation of CRISPR-Corrected Satellite Cells Generates Dystrophin+ Myofibers. To directly demonstrate the regenerative capacity of edited satellite cells, a transplantation assay was performed. Pax7nGFP;mdx donor mice were injected with AAV9-CMV-CRISPR or PBS into the hindlimb. Eight weeks later, ~30,000 nGFP+ satellite cells were isolated by FACS and transplanted into the TA of irradiated (18 Gy), tacrolimus-immunosuppressed mdx host mice. Four weeks post-engraftment, host muscles were analyzed. Mice receiving CRISPR-corrected cells exhibited 5.95 ± 0.40 dystrophin+ fibers per mm², compared to 1.46 ± 0.41 in PBS controls (baseline revertant fibers). PCR on host genomic DNA confirmed the presence of the exon 23 deletion band only in the CRISPR group, and Sanger sequencing verified precise intron ligation. These results prove that CRISPR-edited satellite cells engraft, proliferate, and differentiate into functional, dystrophin-expressing myofibers in vivo, fulfilling a key criterion for therapeutic relevance.

### Example 6

Quantification of Gene-Editing Outcomes Using Tn5 Tagmentation Sequencing. To overcome limitations of traditional PCR-based methods, the inventors adapted a Tn5 transposase-mediated tagmentation protocol for high-throughput sequencing of CRISPR editing outcomes in satellite cells. Genomic DNA from FACS-sorted nGFP+ cells was fragmented and tagged with sequencing adapters in a single enzymatic reaction, followed by PCR amplification of the Dmd target region and Illumina sequencing. This unbiased approach quantified all editing events: deletions (precise exon excision), indels (at each gRNA site), inversions (from inverted cuts), and ITR integrations. In satellite cells from locally treated mice, total editing ranged up to 1.5%, with deletions accounting for ~0.05%. mRNA analysis showed higher edited transcript levels (0.5–1%), consistent with selective stabilization of in-frame mRNAs. This method provides a comprehensive, quantitative assessment of editing fidelity and efficiency in rare cell populations, serving as a critical tool for optimizing future gene-editing therapies.
