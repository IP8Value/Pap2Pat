# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of pharmaceutical and nutraceutical compositions for the treatment and management of type 2 diabetes mellitus and associated metabolic disorders. More specifically, the invention provides a novel aqueous extract derived from a combination of fruit and root tissues of *Curculigo latifolia* (Dryand. ex W.T. Aiton), a plant species belonging to the Hypoxidaceae family, and its use in modulating glucose and lipid metabolism in subjects suffering from or at risk of developing type 2 diabetes mellitus. The invention further encompasses methods for preparing said extract, pharmaceutical formulations containing the extract as an active ingredient, and therapeutic protocols employing the extract to ameliorate hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia, insulin resistance, and related complications such as hepatotoxicity and nephropathy. The extract is characterized by its ability to simultaneously upregulate key genes involved in insulin signaling, glucose uptake, adipokine secretion, and lipid homeostasis—including but not limited to IRS1, IGF-1, GLUT4, PPARα, PPARγ, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, LPL, leptin, and lipase—thereby exerting a multi-targeted, synergistic antidiabetic and hypolipidemic effect. Unlike conventional antidiabetic drugs that often act through a single mechanism and are associated with adverse side effects such as hypoglycemia, weight gain, gastrointestinal disturbances, or hepatotoxicity, the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract of the present invention demonstrates a favorable safety profile, improves insulin sensitivity without inducing significant weight gain, and enhances endogenous adiponectin and insulin secretion. The invention is particularly relevant to the development of natural, plant-based therapeutics that address the complex pathophysiology of type 2 diabetes mellitus, which involves both insulin deficiency and peripheral insulin resistance, and offers a cost-effective, accessible, and physiologically comprehensive alternative to existing pharmacological interventions.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a composition comprising an aqueous extract of *Curculigo latifolia* prepared from a 1:1 (w/w) mixture of fruit and root tissues of said plant, wherein the extract is effective in treating or preventing type 2 diabetes mellitus and its associated metabolic complications. The invention further provides a method of preparing said extract by separately extracting fresh fruit and dried root materials with distilled water under ambient conditions with continuous stirring for 24 hours, followed by filtration, lyophilization, and reconstitution in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The resulting extract exhibits potent antidiabetic and hypolipidemic activities, as demonstrated by its ability to significantly reduce fasting plasma glucose levels, normalize dyslipidemia (including reductions in total cholesterol, triglycerides, and low-density lipoprotein, and elevation of high-density lipoprotein), and improve markers of hepatic and renal function in a validated animal model of type 2 diabetes mellitus induced by high-fat diet feeding combined with low-dose streptozotocin administration. Mechanistically, the extract functions by upregulating the expression of a network of genes critical to glucose and lipid metabolism in insulin-sensitive tissues such as skeletal muscle and adipose tissue. These include insulin receptor substrate 1 (IRS1), insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), glucose transporter type 4 (GLUT4), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha and gamma (PPARα and PPARγ), adiponectin receptors 1 and 2 (AdipoR1 and AdipoR2), lipoprotein lipase (LPL), leptin, and lipase. The coordinated enhancement of these molecular pathways leads to improved insulin sensitivity, increased glucose uptake into peripheral tissues, enhanced fatty acid oxidation, reduced lipotoxicity, and restoration of adipokine balance, particularly through the elevation of circulating adiponectin levels. Additionally, the extract increases endogenous insulin secretion without causing hypoglycemia, suggesting a dual action involving both insulin secretagogue and insulin-sensitizing properties. Importantly, the extract does not promote weight gain—a common adverse effect of many current antidiabetic agents such as sulfonylureas and thiazolidinediones—and may even mitigate obesity-related weight accumulation in diabetic subjects. The invention also includes pharmaceutical formulations comprising the extract in combination with one or more excipients suitable for oral, parenteral, or other routes of administration, as well as methods of using the extract to treat patients diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus, prediabetes, metabolic syndrome, or related conditions characterized by insulin resistance and dyslipidemia. The therapeutic efficacy of the extract has been shown to be dose-dependent, with optimal effects observed at doses of 100–200 mg/kg body weight in preclinical models, and superior to or comparable with standard-of-care drugs such as glibenclamide in reducing hyperglycemia while offering additional benefits in lipid regulation and organ protection. By leveraging the natural phytochemical profile of *Curculigo latifolia*, rich in phenolic compounds with antioxidant activity, the invention addresses multiple facets of type 2 diabetes pathogenesis in a holistic manner, thereby providing a safer, more sustainable, and mechanistically robust therapeutic option for the growing global population affected by this chronic metabolic disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a novel composition derived from *Curculigo latifolia*, a plant native to Southeast Asia, particularly Malaysia, where it is locally known as Lemba. While the fruit of this plant has been traditionally recognized for its sweet-tasting and taste-modifying properties due to the presence of proteins such as curculin and neoculin, the therapeutic potential of *Curculigo latifolia* in the context of metabolic disease has remained unexplored until now. The inventors have discovered that a specific aqueous extract prepared from a defined ratio of fruit and root tissues of *Curculigo latifolia* possesses remarkable antidiabetic and hypolipidemic properties, acting through a unique and comprehensive mechanism that targets multiple nodes in the regulatory networks governing glucose and lipid homeostasis. The following detailed description outlines the preparation, characterization, and biological activity of the inventive extract, supported by experimental data demonstrating its efficacy in a clinically relevant animal model of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

### Example 1

The preparation of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract begins with the collection and authentication of plant material. Fresh fruits and roots of *Curculigo latifolia* were harvested from Beranang, Selangor, Malaysia, and verified by a qualified taxonomist at the Biodiversity Unit of the Institute of Bioscience, Universiti Putra Malaysia, with voucher specimen number SK 1709/09 deposited for future reference. The fruits were carefully plucked from the apex of the stem, washed thoroughly with tap water to remove surface contaminants, gently blotted dry with absorbent tissue paper, and immediately stored at −20°C to preserve their biochemical integrity until extraction. Concurrently, the roots were cleaned under running tap water, air-dried overnight in a controlled oven maintained at 40°C to prevent thermal degradation of bioactive constituents, and subsequently ground into a fine powder using an electric grinder. The powdered root material was then sealed in moisture-proof plastic bags and stored at 4°C to maintain stability. For the extraction process, fifty grams of the freshly thawed fruit material was manually mashed using a mortar and pestle to disrupt cellular structures and facilitate solvent penetration. The mashed fruit was then transferred to a container and mixed with 2000 mL of distilled water. In parallel, fifty grams of the dried root powder was added to 2000 mL of distilled water in a separate container. Both mixtures were subjected to continuous mechanical stirring at room temperature (approximately 25°C) for a duration of 24 hours to ensure exhaustive extraction of water-soluble components. Following this maceration period, each mixture was filtered through Whatman No. 1 filter paper to remove solid particulates, yielding clear aqueous filtrates. These filtrates were then frozen and lyophilized under vacuum to obtain dry, stable powders. The resulting fruit and root lyophilized extracts were stored at −80°C until further use. For all subsequent biological evaluations and therapeutic applications, the fruit and root extracts were combined in a precise 1:1 (w/w) ratio to form the final composition of the invention. This specific ratio was determined empirically to maximize synergistic interactions between the phytochemical profiles of the two plant parts, as preliminary screening indicated that neither the fruit nor the root extract alone achieved the same magnitude of metabolic modulation as the combined formulation. The 1:1 fruit:root extract thus represents a critical aspect of the invention, as it embodies a balanced phytochemical matrix that collectively contributes to the observed multi-targeted therapeutic effects.

### Example 2

To evaluate the therapeutic potential of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract, a robust and clinically translatable animal model of type 2 diabetes mellitus was employed. This model was developed by first inducing obesity and insulin resistance in male Sprague-Dawley rats through prolonged feeding of a high-fat diet (HFD), followed by a low-dose injection of streptozotocin (STZ) to partially impair pancreatic β-cell function, thereby recapitulating the dual pathophysiological features of human type 2 diabetes: peripheral insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency. The HFD was formulated according to the method of Levin et al. (1989), consisting of 50% normal rat chow pellet, 24% corn oil (Mazola brand), 20% full-cream milk powder (NESPRAY brand from Nestlé), and 6% sugar. Nutritional analysis confirmed that this diet delivered 56.9% of its total energy from fat, representing a 47.4% increase in fat-derived calories compared to the standard normal pellet diet (NPD). Forty-two male Sprague-Dawley rats, initially weighing 160–180 g, were acclimatized for one week on NPD before being randomly assigned to either a control group (n=6) maintained on NPD or an experimental cohort (n=36) fed the HFD for four weeks to induce obesity. At the end of this period, the HFD-fed rats exhibited significant weight gain (approximately 30% increase from baseline) and elevated fasting blood glucose levels, confirming the development of insulin resistance. These obese rats were then fasted overnight, anesthetized with diethyl ether, and administered a single intravenous injection of STZ at a dose of 40 mg/kg body weight. Seven days post-injection, rats with fasting blood glucose levels exceeding 170 mg/dL were classified as diabetic and randomly allocated into five treatment groups (n=6 per group): Group 3 served as the diabetic control and received no treatment; Groups 4, 5, and 6 received daily oral administrations of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract at doses of 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg body weight, respectively; and Group 7 received the standard antidiabetic drug glibenclamide at 10 mg/kg body weight as a positive control. Treatment was continued for four weeks, during which body weights were monitored weekly. At the conclusion of the study, all animals were fasted for 15 hours, anesthetized, and sacrificed via cardiac puncture for blood and tissue collection. This experimental design allowed for a direct comparison of the extract’s efficacy against both untreated diabetic pathology and a conventional therapeutic agent, while ensuring that the model accurately reflected the progressive nature of human type 2 diabetes.

### Example 3

Administration of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract resulted in significant improvements in glycemic control in diabetic rats. After four weeks of treatment, fasting plasma glucose levels were markedly reduced in all extract-treated groups in a dose-dependent manner. Specifically, rats treated with 200 mg/kg body weight of the extract (Group 6) exhibited a 64.5% reduction in blood glucose levels compared to the diabetic control group (Group 3), while those receiving 100 mg/kg (Group 5) and 50 mg/kg (Group 4) showed reductions of 54.3% and 51.6%, respectively. In contrast, glibenclamide-treated rats (Group 7) demonstrated only a 32.4% reduction, indicating that the extract was substantially more effective at lowering hyperglycemia than this commonly prescribed sulfonylurea. Notably, glucose levels in the normal control group (Group 1) remained stable throughout the study, increasing by only 2.4%, whereas both the obese (Group 2) and diabetic control (Group 3) groups experienced significant elevations of 18.6% and 15.2%, respectively, underscoring the progressive deterioration of glucose homeostasis in the absence of intervention. The superior hypoglycemic effect of the extract is attributed to its dual mechanism of action: enhancing endogenous insulin secretion and improving insulin sensitivity in peripheral tissues. Plasma insulin measurements confirmed that all extract-treated groups exhibited increased insulin levels after four weeks of therapy—16% in Group 4, 13% in Group 5, and 12% in Group 6—compared to baseline and the diabetic control. This contrasts with glibenclamide, which primarily acts by stimulating insulin release but does not address underlying insulin resistance. Furthermore, the extract’s ability to lower glucose without inducing hypoglycemia (as evidenced by the absence of dangerously low glucose readings in any treated group) highlights its physiological regulation of glucose metabolism, likely mediated through the upregulation of insulin signaling components such as IRS1 and GLUT4, as detailed in subsequent examples. These findings establish the extract as a potent antihyperglycemic agent with advantages over existing monotherapies.

### Example 4

In addition to its antidiabetic effects, the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract demonstrated pronounced hypolipidemic activity, effectively reversing the dyslipidemia characteristic of type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome. Diabetic control rats (Group 3) exhibited severe hyperlipidemia, with plasma levels of total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) elevated by 56.2%, 52.6%, and 75.4%, respectively, compared to normal controls, while high-density lipoprotein (HDL) was reduced by 51%. This pathological lipid profile was visually evident in the opacity of plasma samples, which appeared milky due to high lipid content. Following four weeks of treatment with the extract, all dosage groups showed significant normalization of lipid parameters. The highest dose (200 mg/kg, Group 6) produced the most robust effects, with substantial decreases in TC, TG, and LDL, and a concomitant increase in HDL. Critically, the extract outperformed glibenclamide, which showed minimal impact on lipid metabolism. The restoration of lipid homeostasis was accompanied by a visible clearing of plasma samples, transitioning from opaque to transparent, indicative of reduced circulating lipids. Mechanistically, this effect is linked to the extract’s ability to upregulate PPARα and PPARγ—nuclear receptors that orchestrate fatty acid oxidation, lipoprotein metabolism, and adipocyte differentiation—as well as lipoprotein lipase (LPL), which hydrolyzes circulating triglycerides for tissue uptake and storage. The activation of PPARα, in particular, promotes hepatic and muscular fatty acid β-oxidation, thereby reducing lipid accumulation and improving insulin sensitivity. Moreover, the increase in HDL, often referred to as “good cholesterol,” contributes to reverse cholesterol transport and cardiovascular protection—addressing a major comorbidity in diabetic patients. These results position the extract not merely as a glucose-lowering agent but as a comprehensive metabolic regulator capable of mitigating both hyperglycemia and dyslipidemia, two interlinked hallmarks of type 2 diabetes.

### Example 5

A pivotal discovery of the present invention is the extract’s capacity to modulate adipokine signaling, particularly through the elevation of adiponectin—a hormone with insulin-sensitizing, anti-inflammatory, and anti-atherogenic properties that is typically suppressed in obesity and type 2 diabetes. Plasma adiponectin levels were significantly lower in both obese (Group 2) and diabetic control (Group 3) rats compared to normal controls. However, treatment with the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract led to a marked and dose-dependent increase in circulating adiponectin: Group 5 (100 mg/kg) showed a 56% rise, Group 4 (50 mg/kg) a 48% increase, and Group 6 (200 mg/kg) a 41% elevation. In stark contrast, glibenclamide treatment (Group 7) failed to alter adiponectin levels, highlighting a unique advantage of the extract. The upregulation of adiponectin is mechanistically connected to the observed improvements in insulin sensitivity and lipid metabolism, as adiponectin enhances glucose uptake in muscle and suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis via AMPK activation. Furthermore, gene expression analysis revealed that the extract significantly increased mRNA levels of adiponectin receptors AdipoR1 and AdipoR2 in both adipose and muscle tissues. AdipoR1 predominantly activates AMPK, promoting fatty acid oxidation and glucose utilization, while AdipoR2 signals through PPARα to regulate lipid metabolism. The coordinated upregulation of both receptors amplifies adiponectin’s metabolic actions. Additionally, the extract increased leptin expression, which may contribute to modest appetite regulation and energy expenditure, though body weight changes were primarily influenced by lipid-lowering effects rather than caloric restriction. Importantly, the extract prevented the progressive decline in adiponectin seen in untreated diabetic rats, thereby preserving a critical endogenous defense against insulin resistance. This adipokine-modulating effect represents a sophisticated layer of metabolic regulation not achievable with conventional antidiabetic drugs and underscores the extract’s role as a functional modulator of adipose tissue biology.

### Example 6

The molecular basis of the extract’s therapeutic efficacy was elucidated through genome-wide expression profiling of key metabolic genes in insulin-responsive tissues. Using the GenomeLab GeXP system, the inventors quantified mRNA levels of a panel of genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism in white adipose tissue (retroperitoneal, subcutaneous, and epididymal depots) and skeletal muscle. In diabetic control rats, expression of IRS1, IGF-1, GLUT4, PPARα, PPARγ, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, LPL, leptin, and lipase was significantly downregulated compared to normal controls, reflecting the systemic impairment of metabolic signaling in diabetes. Remarkably, treatment with the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract reversed this suppression across all tested genes in a dose-responsive fashion. The upregulation of IRS1 and IGF-1 enhances proximal insulin signaling, facilitating the activation of downstream effectors like PI3K and Akt, which in turn promote the translocation of GLUT4 vesicles to the plasma membrane for glucose uptake—consistent with prior in vitro findings showing increased glucose uptake in 3T3 adipocytes and L6 myotubes. The induction of PPARγ, a master regulator of adipogenesis and insulin sensitivity, suggests that the extract may mimic the action of thiazolidinediones but without their associated hepatotoxicity, as evidenced by normalized liver enzyme levels (ALT and GGT) in treated rats. Similarly, PPARα activation drives fatty acid catabolism and improves lipid profiles. The concurrent upregulation of LPL and lipase supports enhanced lipid clearance from circulation and intracellular lipid turnover. Notably, the extract also ameliorated diabetic complications: plasma levels of urea and creatinine—markers of renal dysfunction—were significantly reduced, indicating protection against diabetic nephropathy, while ALT and GGT reductions pointed to hepatoprotection. These systemic benefits, coupled with the absence of toxicity or weight gain (in fact, extract-treated rats gained less weight than glibenclamide-treated ones), confirm the extract’s safety and multi-organ protective effects. Collectively, these gene expression data provide a comprehensive mechanistic framework for the extract’s antidiabetic and hypolipidemic actions, validating its status as a pleiotropic, naturally derived therapeutic agent for type 2 diabetes mellitus and its comorbidities.
