# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of nonlinear dynamical systems and classical chaos theory, and more particularly to methods and systems for inducing, detecting, and utilizing a novel form of interaction between chaotic systems known as chaotic entanglement. The invention further relates to the generation and application of controlled periodic orbits—referred to herein as cupolets—that are stabilized from otherwise unstable chaotic dynamics through information-theoretic control mechanisms. These cupolets serve as fundamental building blocks for representing the state space of chaotic systems and enable the emergence of mutually stabilizing interactions between distinct chaotic entities. The disclosed technology provides a framework for modeling classical analogs of quantum mechanical phenomena—including entanglement, wave function collapse, superposition of states, and entropy reversal—within deterministic, nonlinear classical systems. The invention is applicable to secure communications, classical memory storage, synchronization of complex networks, and the theoretical exploration of the boundary between classical and quantum physics.

## BACKGROUND

Classical chaotic systems have long been studied for their sensitivity to initial conditions, exponential divergence of nearby trajectories, and complex geometric structures such as strange attractors densely populated with unstable periodic orbits (UPOs). Despite extensive research into the statistical and topological properties of chaos, a persistent conceptual gap has existed between the deterministic unpredictability of classical chaos and the probabilistic, nonlocal features of quantum mechanics. Quantum entanglement, in particular, has been regarded as an exclusively quantum phenomenon due to its violation of Bell inequalities, instantaneous correlations across arbitrary distances, and resistance to classical explanation. However, recent theoretical and computational advances have revealed that certain classical chaotic systems can exhibit behaviors that closely parallel key aspects of quantum entanglement when subjected to specific forms of interaction and control.

Historically, efforts to stabilize UPOs within chaotic systems have relied on techniques such as the OGY method (Ott, Grebogi, and Yorke) or Newton-Raphson-based algorithms, which require precise knowledge of the system’s Jacobian and often fail to scale efficiently across large ensembles of orbits. These methods typically depend heavily on initial conditions and are computationally intensive, limiting their practical utility in applications requiring rapid orbit selection or real-time adaptation. In contrast, the Hayes-Grebogi-Ott (HGO) control scheme introduced a more robust approach by using small, targeted perturbations applied at discrete sections of the attractor—known as Poincaré surfaces—to steer trajectories toward desired symbolic sequences. This method was later refined by Parker and Short to generate a new class of stabilized periodic orbits called cupolets (Chaotic, Unstable, Periodic, Orbit-LETS), which are uniquely associated with binary control sequences and can be stabilized independently of initial conditions.

Cupolets possess several advantageous properties: they approximate true UPOs with high fidelity; they are generated via minimal informational input (e.g., short binary strings); and they support one-to-one correspondence between control sequences and resulting orbits. These features make cupolets ideal candidates for encoding, transmitting, and storing information in chaotic media. Moreover, because cupolets emerge from the intrinsic dynamics of chaotic systems rather than being externally imposed solutions, they preserve the topological structure of the underlying attractor while enabling deterministic control over otherwise unpredictable behavior.

Recent investigations have uncovered a remarkable phenomenon wherein two interacting chaotic systems, each stabilized onto a cupolet, can enter into a state of mutual stabilization that persists without external intervention. This state—termed chaotic entanglement—arises when the symbolic dynamics (e.g., visitation sequences) generated by one cupolet are processed through an exchange function and used as control instructions for the second system, and vice versa. When the emitted control sequences match the exact requirements for maintaining each partner’s periodic orbit, a self-sustaining feedback loop is established. Disturbing either system disrupts this loop and collapses the entangled state, mirroring the fragility of quantum entanglement under measurement. Furthermore, chaotic entanglement exhibits entropy-reversing characteristics: whereas free-running chaotic systems generate information at a positive Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy rate, entangled cupolets evolve periodically with zero entropy, effectively reversing the usual thermodynamic arrow of time in an information-theoretic sense.

Prior art includes studies on quantum-classical correspondence, such as the use of out-of-time-order correlators to detect quantum chaos or the observation of quantum scarring in wavefunctions localized along classical periodic orbits. Additionally, classical analogs of entanglement have been proposed using optical beams or coupled oscillators, but these lack the dynamical depth and intrinsic instability characteristic of true chaotic systems. The concept of “chaotic entanglement” as described in earlier works by the inventors represents a significant departure from these models because it emerges from the natural dynamics of low-dimensional chaotic systems (e.g., Chua’s oscillator, Lorenz system) and does not rely on linear subsystems or engineered Hamiltonians. Crucially, the present invention extends beyond simulation by providing a formalizable, algorithmic framework for inducing, verifying, and exploiting chaotic entanglement in both theoretical and potentially physical contexts.

However, existing approaches suffer from critical limitations. First, no systematic method exists for identifying pairs of cupolets capable of forming entangled states without exhaustive enumeration. Second, the role of the exchange function—as a mediator of interaction analogous to an environmental coupling—is not fully characterized, leaving open questions about the generality and physical realizability of different entanglement mechanisms. Third, while cupolets offer a basis for representing chaotic states, no coherent formalism has been developed to describe superposition, measurement-induced collapse, or Hilbert-space-like constructions within classical chaos. Finally, the potential technological applications of chaotic entanglement—such as classical memory devices, secure communication protocols resistant to eavesdropping, or synchronization primitives for neuromorphic computing—remain largely unexplored.

Thus, there exists a compelling need for a comprehensive methodology that not only defines and generates chaotic entanglement but also leverages its unique properties for practical innovation. The present invention addresses these gaps by introducing a unified framework encompassing cupolet generation, entanglement induction via programmable exchange functions, functional representation of orbits for analytical manipulation, and interpretation of chaotic dynamics through quantum-inspired concepts—all grounded in rigorous nonlinear dynamics and information theory.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel system and method for inducing, detecting, and utilizing chaotic entanglement—a self-sustaining, mutually stabilizing interaction between two or more chaotic systems that have each collapsed onto controlled periodic orbits known as cupolets. The invention establishes a classical analog to quantum entanglement by demonstrating that deterministic, nonlinear dynamical systems can exhibit correlated, non-separable states whose stability depends intrinsically on continuous bidirectional exchange of symbolic information. This correlation is fragile under perturbation, reversible upon restoration of interaction, and accompanied by a reduction in dynamical entropy, thereby exhibiting key signatures previously thought exclusive to quantum mechanics.

In one aspect, the invention discloses a method for generating cupolets in a chaotic system by applying a repeating binary control sequence through a modified Hayes-Grebogi-Ott (HGO) control scheme. The chaotic system is defined by a set of nonlinear differential equations possessing a strange attractor embedded with unstable periodic orbits. Control planes are established as Poincaré sections intersecting the attractor, each partitioned into a plurality of control bins. As the system evolves, microcontrol perturbations reset the trajectory to the center of the bin whenever it intersects a control plane, while macrocontrol perturbations—triggered by specific bits in the control sequence—are applied to induce lobe-switching behavior after a predetermined number of loops. When the control sequence is periodic, the system stabilizes onto a cupolet regardless of initial conditions, and the resulting orbit corresponds uniquely to the applied sequence.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for inducing chaotic entanglement between two chaotic systems. Each system is first stabilized onto a respective cupolet using external controls. The visitation sequence—defined as the binary record of lobe visits generated by each cupolet—is then transmitted to an exchange function, which processes the sequence according to a predefined rule (e.g., thresholding, bitwise logic, convolution) to produce an emitted sequence. This emitted sequence is applied as a control input to the partner system. If the emitted sequence from System A matches the control sequence required to maintain the cupolet of System B—and vice versa—then the external controls may be removed, and the two systems remain locked in a mutually stabilizing feedback loop. This state constitutes chaotic entanglement.

The invention further discloses a method for detecting chaotic entanglement by verifying the consistency between emitted and required control sequences. This may be performed computationally by precomputing libraries of cupolet control and visitation sequences, then testing pairwise compatibility under a given exchange function. Alternatively, in a physical implementation, entanglement may be inferred from sustained periodicity in both systems after cessation of external control, or from correlated responses to synchronized perturbations.

In yet another aspect, the invention introduces a functional representation of cupolets derived via Fourier analysis of numerically integrated trajectories. By sampling a stabilized cupolet over one period and applying a fast Fourier transform (FFT), a finite set of significant spectral coefficients is identified. These coefficients are used to construct a truncated trigonometric series that approximates the cupolet’s time evolution with high accuracy. This functional form enables symbolic manipulation, analytical integration, and embedding into larger computational frameworks, facilitating applications in modeling, control design, and theoretical analysis.

Moreover, the invention proposes a state representation for chaotic systems based on superposition over cupolet bases. The state vector of a chaotic system is defined as a weighted sum of cupolet trajectories, where the weights reflect proximity to each orbit under the natural invariant measure. This formulation supports an interpretation of free chaotic evolution as a “mixed state” continuously transitioning between local cupolet neighborhoods. Application of control sequences induces “wave function collapse” onto a single cupolet, analogous to quantum measurement.

The invention also addresses the measurement problem in classical chaos by distinguishing between knowledgeable and blind measurements. A knowledgeable measurement—one designed with full awareness of the control bin resolution and cupolet stability margins—can extract information without disrupting entanglement. In contrast, a blind measurement introduces uncontrolled perturbations that destabilize the cupolet, break the feedback loop, and destroy entanglement. This dichotomy mirrors the observer effect in quantum mechanics and enables secure information encoding: only parties possessing the control protocol can access stored data without corruption.

Additionally, the invention identifies chaotic entanglement as an entropy-reversing process. While uncontrolled chaotic systems exhibit positive Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy due to continuous generation of new symbolic sequences, entangled cupolets evolve periodically with zero entropy. This reduction violates no physical law because the system is open—the exchange function mediates energy/information flow—but it challenges conventional interpretations of the Second Law in information-theoretic contexts.

Finally, the invention contemplates physical realizations of chaotic entanglement through engineered systems such as coupled Chua circuits, optoelectronic oscillators, or neural networks implementing integrate-and-fire dynamics. Exchange functions may model physical couplings like electromagnetic fields, chemical diffusion, or synaptic transmission. The invention thus bridges abstract dynamical theory with tangible technologies, enabling new paradigms in classical information processing, secure communication, and foundational physics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present invention is directed to systems and methods for generating, entangling, and utilizing cupolets—controlled periodic orbits extracted from chaotic dynamical systems—as a foundation for classical analogs of quantum phenomena. The following detailed description provides a comprehensive exposition of the invention’s components, operational principles, mathematical formulations, and potential embodiments. It is to be understood that the invention is not limited to the specific examples or implementations described herein, but encompasses all equivalents and generalizations falling within the scope of the appended claims.

A chaotic system suitable for practicing the invention is any deterministic nonlinear dynamical system exhibiting sensitive dependence on initial conditions, a strange attractor, and a dense set of unstable periodic orbits (UPOs). Exemplary systems include Chua’s double scroll oscillator, the Lorenz system, the Rössler system, and discrete maps such as the Hénon map or logistic map in chaotic regimes. For concreteness, the invention is primarily described with reference to Chua’s circuit, governed by the following dimensionless differential equations:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot{v}_{C_1} &= \frac{G(v_{C_2} - v_{C_1}) - g(v_{C_1})}{C_1}, \\
\dot{v}_{C_2} &= \frac{G(v_{C_1} - v_{C_2}) + i_L}{C_2}, \\
\dot{i}_L &= -\frac{v_{C_2}}{L},
\end{aligned}
\]

where \( g(v) \) is a piecewise-linear function defined as:

\[
g(v) = 
\begin{cases}
m_1 v, & \text{if } |v| \leq B_p, \\
m_0(v + B_p) - m_1 B_p, & \text{if } v \leq -B_p, \\
m_0(v - B_p) + m_1 B_p, & \text{if } v \geq B_p.
\end{cases}
\]

Under standard parameter values (\( C_1 = 1/9, C_2 = 1, L = 1/7, G = 0.7, m_0 = -0.5, m_1 = -0.8, B_p = 1 \)), this system exhibits a double-scroll attractor comprising two lobes surrounding unstable fixed points. Trajectories wander aperiodically, switching unpredictably between lobes.

To generate cupolets, the invention employs an adapted HGO control scheme. Two control planes are defined as Poincaré sections: one intersecting the upper lobe, the other the lower lobe, each perpendicular to the dominant flow direction. Each control plane is partitioned into N bins (e.g., N = 2000), indexed spatially. As the system evolves, every intersection with a control plane is recorded as a binary symbol: ‘0’ for the lower lobe, ‘1’ for the upper lobe, forming a visitation sequence. Simultaneously, a microcontrol perturbation resets the state to the bin center, ensuring trajectory reproducibility. Additionally, a macrocontrol perturbation—applied only when the control sequence bit is ‘1’—is defined as the minimal displacement along the control plane necessary to alter the lobe visited N steps later in the sequence. This allows precise steering of long-term dynamics using minimal intervention.

When a periodic binary control sequence (e.g., ‘000011111’) is repeatedly applied, the system stabilizes onto a cupolet—a periodic orbit shadowing a true UPO. Critically, stabilization occurs irrespective of initial state, and each unique sequence yields a unique cupolet. Over 8800 distinct cupolets have been generated from 16-bit or shorter sequences in Chua’s system, demonstrating scalability.

Chaotic entanglement is induced as follows. Consider two identical chaotic systems, System I and System II. System I is externally controlled to stabilize onto cupolet \( \mathbf{C}_A \) using control sequence \( S_A \). As \( \mathbf{C}_A \) evolves, it generates visitation sequence \( V_A \). This sequence is input to an exchange function \( \mathcal{E} \), which outputs emitted sequence \( E_B = \mathcal{E}(V_A) \). \( E_B \) is applied as control to System II. If \( E_B = S_B \)—the control sequence for some cupolet \( \mathbf{C}_B \)—then System II stabilizes onto \( \mathbf{C}_B \). Concurrently, \( V_B \) (from \( \mathbf{C}_B \)) is processed by \( \mathcal{E} \) to yield \( E_A = \mathcal{E}(V_B) \). If \( E_A = S_A \), then the external control on System I is redundant: \( \mathbf{C}_A \) and \( \mathbf{C}_B \) now mutually sustain each other’s stability via bidirectional information exchange. This closed-loop state is chaotic entanglement.

The exchange function \( \mathcal{E} \) may be identity (pure entanglement), threshold-based (e.g., preponderance function converting runs of ‘1’s to ‘0’s based on energy thresholds), or any computable mapping. Its design determines which cupolet pairs can entangle. Libraries of compatible pairs can be precomputed by enumerating cupolets, recording \( (S, V) \) pairs, and testing \( \mathcal{E}(V_i) = S_j \) and \( \mathcal{E}(V_j) = S_i \).

Functionally, a cupolet \( s(t) \) is represented via Fourier series. After numerical integration over one period T, 1024 samples are taken. FFT yields coefficients \( C_f \). Truncating to Q significant modes (e.g., Q=11), the functional form is:

\[
s(t) = \frac{1}{P}C_0 + \frac{1}{P}\sum_{f=1}^{Q} \left[ C_f e^{i2\pi f t / T} + C_{-f} e^{-i2\pi f t / T} \right],
\]

which closely approximates the true orbit, enabling symbolic computation.

The state of a chaotic system is represented as \( \Psi(t) = \sum_k \alpha_k(t) \psi_k(t) \), where \( \psi_k \) is the k-th cupolet and \( \alpha_k \) weights reflect proximity under the natural measure. Free evolution yields a mixed state; control induces collapse to a single \( \psi_k \).

Measurement is classified as knowledgeable (non-disruptive, using sub-bin resolution sensors) or blind (disruptive). Only knowledgeable measurements preserve entanglement, enabling secure readout.

Entropy analysis shows that entangled cupolets have zero Kolmogorov entropy, reversing the information-generating tendency of chaos. This is permissible in open systems and suggests novel thermodynamic implications.

Physical embodiments include coupled electronic circuits, optical systems with nonlinear media, or biological networks where exchange functions model natural couplings. The invention thus provides a robust, scalable platform for classical information processing with quantum-like features, absent nonlocality but rich in dynamical complexity.
