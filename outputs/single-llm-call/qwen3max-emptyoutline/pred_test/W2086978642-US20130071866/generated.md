# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of medical diagnostics and prognostics, particularly in the context of critical care medicine. More specifically, the invention relates to a novel method and system for assessing the severity of illness, predicting clinical outcomes, and monitoring disease progression in critically ill patients admitted to intensive care units (ICUs). The invention provides a real-time, sensitive, and reliable biomarker-based scoring system that integrates measurements of intracellular adenosine 5′-triphosphate (ATP) levels in blood cells with serum lactate concentrations to generate a composite index—referred to herein as the ATP-lactate energy risk score (A-LES)—which serves as a dynamic prognostic indicator of patient survival, organ dysfunction, and response to therapeutic intervention. This invention further encompasses improved methodologies for the accurate and reproducible extraction and quantification of ATP from whole blood samples, especially in high-protein biological matrices, thereby overcoming limitations inherent in conventional ATP assay techniques. The technical domain thus spans clinical biochemistry, molecular diagnostics, critical care physiology, and biomedical engineering, with direct applicability to emergency medicine, sepsis management, postoperative monitoring, and the evaluation of mitochondrial dysfunction in acute and chronic critical illnesses.

## BACKGROUND ART

Critical illness, whether resulting from trauma, infection, surgical complications, or systemic inflammatory responses, imposes profound metabolic stress on the human body. A central hallmark of such conditions is the disruption of cellular bioenergetics, particularly within mitochondria, which are responsible for the majority of ATP production via oxidative phosphorylation. When mitochondrial function is compromised—as occurs in septic shock, multiple organ failure, severe hypoxia, or viral myocarditis—the cell shifts toward less efficient anaerobic metabolic pathways, leading to the accumulation of lactate and a concomitant decline in intracellular ATP stores. This energetic crisis not only impairs cellular homeostasis but also triggers cascades of inflammation, apoptosis, and immune dysregulation that can culminate in multiorgan dysfunction and death. Consequently, the ability to rapidly and accurately assess a patient’s bioenergetic status has long been recognized as a cornerstone of effective critical care management.

Historically, clinicians have relied on severity-of-illness scoring systems such as the Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) and the Simplified Acute Physiology Score II (SAPS II) to stratify risk, guide treatment decisions, and predict mortality in ICU settings. These systems integrate multiple physiological parameters—including heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, temperature, arterial pH, serum sodium and potassium, creatinine, hematocrit, white blood cell count, Glasgow Coma Scale score, age, and chronic health status—into a single numerical score that correlates with hospital mortality. While APACHE II and similar tools have demonstrated utility in epidemiological studies and quality assurance programs, they suffer from several well-documented limitations in routine clinical practice. First, their calculation is time-consuming and requires access to numerous laboratory and clinical data points, many of which may be delayed, incomplete, or subject to inter-observer variability. Second, these scores are typically assessed only once during the first 24 hours of ICU admission and are not designed to dynamically reflect changes in a patient’s condition over time. Third, they do not directly measure underlying pathophysiological processes such as mitochondrial dysfunction or cellular energy depletion, instead relying on surrogate markers that may lag behind actual biological deterioration.

In parallel, serum lactate has emerged as a widely used biomarker of tissue hypoperfusion and anaerobic metabolism. Elevated lactate levels (>2 mM) are strongly associated with poor outcomes in sepsis, shock, and other critical conditions, and serial lactate measurements are now incorporated into resuscitation protocols such as early goal-directed therapy. However, lactate alone is an imperfect indicator: it can be elevated in non-hypoxic states (e.g., due to accelerated glycolysis in cancer or liver dysfunction), and its clearance may be delayed even after hemodynamic stabilization. Moreover, lactate reflects only one aspect of metabolic derangement—the accumulation of a byproduct—without providing information about the cell’s capacity to generate energy.

Adenosine 5′-triphosphate (ATP), the primary energy currency of the cell, represents a more direct measure of bioenergetic health. Intracellular ATP levels integrate inputs from glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, and fatty acid β-oxidation, and their depletion signifies a failure of energy homeostasis. In critically ill patients, reduced ATP synthesis has been documented in skeletal muscle, myocardium, and circulating blood cells, particularly erythrocytes, which rely exclusively on glycolysis for ATP production. Despite its theoretical promise, ATP has not been widely adopted as a clinical biomarker due to significant technical challenges. Most notably, ATP is highly labile and rapidly degraded by ectonucleotidases upon release into the extracellular space, making serum ATP measurements unreliable. Furthermore, standard ATP extraction methods—such as those using trichloroacetic acid, perchloric acid, or ethylene glycol—suffer from poor recovery in high-protein samples like whole blood or tissue homogenates, where ATP co-precipitates with denatured proteins during sample preparation. This artifact leads to underestimation of true intracellular ATP concentrations and limits the reproducibility of assays.

Recent research has begun to address these limitations. Studies have shown that blood cell ATP levels, when accurately measured, correlate with disease severity in sepsis, influenza-associated encephalopathy, and mitochondrial disorders. Additionally, the concept of “alarmins”—endogenous danger signals released by damaged or stressed cells—has gained traction, with extracellular ATP now recognized as a key damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) that activates purinergic receptors and promotes inflammation. Although extracellular ATP is transient, its intracellular counterpart in circulating cells may serve as a stable proxy for systemic bioenergetic stress. Building on this foundation, there exists a compelling need for a clinically feasible, real-time biomarker system that combines the metabolic insights of ATP and lactate into a unified, interpretable metric capable of outperforming existing static scoring systems.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a real-time, sensitive, and clinically practical method for assessing the severity of critical illness, predicting patient outcomes, and monitoring therapeutic response in intensive care settings. Existing severity scoring systems such as APACHE II are static, labor-intensive, and fail to capture dynamic changes in a patient’s bioenergetic status. Meanwhile, individual biomarkers like lactate or ATP, when measured in isolation, offer incomplete or technically unreliable information. The invention addresses these shortcomings by introducing a novel composite index—the ATP-lactate energy risk score (A-LES)—that integrates two fundamental aspects of cellular metabolism: energy production (reflected by intracellular ATP levels in blood cells) and anaerobic stress (reflected by serum lactate concentration). By doing so, the invention solves the long-standing problem of lacking a direct, dynamic, and easily measurable indicator of mitochondrial dysfunction and systemic bioenergetic failure in critically ill patients. Furthermore, the invention overcomes the technical barrier of inaccurate ATP quantification in whole blood by employing an optimized phenol-based extraction methodology that ensures high recovery and reproducibility, thereby enabling reliable clinical application of ATP as a biomarker.

### Means to Solve the Object

The invention achieves its objective through a multi-faceted approach encompassing both methodological innovation and diagnostic application. First, it utilizes an improved ATP extraction protocol based on Tris-EDTA-saturated phenol (phenol-TE), which effectively solubilizes ATP from blood cells without co-precipitation with proteins, thereby yielding accurate and reproducible measurements of intracellular ATP concentration in whole blood samples. This extraction method is compatible with standard firefly luciferase-based bioluminescence assays and can be implemented in routine clinical laboratories.

Second, the invention establishes reference ranges for blood ATP and serum lactate in healthy individuals across a broad age spectrum (0 to 92 years), confirming that these values are stable with respect to gender and only mildly influenced by age, thus providing a robust baseline for clinical comparison.

Third, and most critically, the invention defines a new prognostic index—the ATP-lactate energy risk score (A-LES)—calculated as the ratio of serum lactate concentration (in millimolar, mM) to blood ATP concentration (in mM). This dimensionless ratio inherently normalizes for variations in blood cell count and hemodilution, as both numerator and denominator are affected similarly by transfusion or anemia, and it amplifies the signal of bioenergetic imbalance: low ATP and high lactate jointly drive the score upward, reflecting severe mitochondrial dysfunction.

Fourth, the invention demonstrates that A-LES can be measured at ICU admission to predict mortality with accuracy comparable to APACHE II, but unlike APACHE II, A-LES can be serially monitored throughout the ICU stay to dynamically track disease progression or recovery. The score exhibits marked divergence between survivors and non-survivors, with survivors consistently showing declining A-LES values during treatment, while non-survivors maintain or exhibit rising scores.

Fifth, the invention identifies a clinically actionable threshold—specifically, an A-LES value of 20—as a critical cutoff for survival prediction, with values above this level strongly associated with mortality in the studied cohort. This threshold enables immediate risk stratification at the bedside.

Finally, the invention validates the equivalence of A-LES measurements across different blood sampling sites (arterial, central venous, peripheral venous), ensuring flexibility in clinical implementation without compromising accuracy.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention yields several significant and unexpected advantages over the prior art. Most notably, it provides a real-time, dynamic, and biologically grounded prognostic tool that directly reflects the core pathophysiology of critical illness—namely, the collapse of cellular energy metabolism. Unlike APACHE II, which is a static snapshot requiring extensive data collection, A-LES can be determined rapidly from a single blood draw using widely available laboratory equipment, making it suitable for point-of-care or near-patient testing. Its serial measurability allows clinicians to objectively assess the effectiveness of interventions such as fluid resuscitation, vasopressor support, or antimicrobial therapy, thereby facilitating personalized, response-guided critical care.

Moreover, by combining ATP and lactate into a ratio, the invention mitigates confounding factors that plague either marker alone. For instance, anemia or recent transfusion may lower absolute ATP levels without indicating true bioenergetic failure; however, since lactate is unaffected by red blood cell count, the A-LES ratio remains a more specific indicator of metabolic stress. Conversely, conditions causing isolated lactate elevation (e.g., thiamine deficiency or metformin use) may not necessarily reflect ATP depletion, and the A-LES would remain low or moderate, preventing overestimation of risk.

Clinically, the invention enables earlier identification of high-risk patients who may benefit from escalated monitoring or experimental therapies targeting mitochondrial protection or bioenergetic support. It also offers a quantitative endpoint for clinical trials evaluating novel resuscitation strategies. From a healthcare systems perspective, the simplicity and objectivity of A-LES reduce inter-clinician variability and administrative burden, potentially improving resource allocation and outcome benchmarking.

In summary, the invention transforms ATP from a research curiosity into a clinically actionable biomarker and establishes A-LES as a superior, physiology-driven alternative to conventional severity scores for both initial risk assessment and ongoing illness monitoring in the ICU.

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The invention is carried out through a standardized clinical and laboratory protocol designed to ensure accuracy, reproducibility, and clinical utility. The process begins with the collection of a blood sample from a patient, which may be obtained from any clinically accessible vascular site, including peripheral venous, radial arterial, or central venous catheters. The choice of anticoagulant is flexible: ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) or sodium heparin may be used, as both preserve ATP stability during short-term storage. Immediately upon collection, a precise volume of whole blood—typically 0.1 mL—is transferred into a pre-prepared tube containing 1.3 mL of Tris-EDTA-saturated phenol (phenol-TE) extraction reagent. This reagent, commercially available as part of the AMERIC-ATP kit (Wako Pure Chemical Industries, Osaka, Japan), is specifically formulated to denature proteins while maintaining ATP in the aqueous phase, thereby preventing co-precipitation and maximizing recovery.

The mixture is vigorously vortexed or shaken for approximately 20 seconds to ensure complete lysis of blood cells and extraction of intracellular ATP. The sample is then stored at −20°C or lower until analysis, though immediate processing is preferred to minimize any potential degradation. For ATP quantification, the sample is thawed and centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 5 minutes at 4°C to achieve phase separation. The upper aqueous phase, which contains the extracted ATP, is carefully aspirated, avoiding the interphase and organic layer. A small aliquot—typically 50 µL—is diluted 10,000-fold with deionized water to bring the ATP concentration within the linear range of the detection assay.

Concurrently, serum lactate is measured from the same blood draw using a standard blood gas analyzer or enzymatic assay, yielding a concentration in millimolar (mM) units. The diluted ATP extract (10 µL) is then injected into a luminometer-compatible reaction mixture containing 90 µL of luciferin/luciferase reagent. The ensuing bioluminescent reaction, proportional to ATP concentration, is measured immediately using a luminometer such as the GloMax-96 Microplate Luminometer (Promega, Tokyo, Japan). The raw luminescence signal is converted to ATP concentration (mM) using a calibration curve generated from known ATP standards processed identically to patient samples.

Once both ATP and lactate values are obtained, the ATP-lactate energy risk score (A-LES) is calculated as the simple mathematical ratio: A-LES = [serum lactate (mM)] / [blood ATP (mM)]. This calculation can be performed manually, via laboratory information system integration, or through dedicated software embedded in point-of-care devices.

For clinical interpretation, the A-LES is compared against established reference ranges. In healthy individuals, the mean A-LES is approximately 2.00 (standard deviation 0.83), with values rarely exceeding 4.0. In ICU patients, an A-LES ≥ 20 at any time during admission is strongly predictive of mortality, while values consistently <5.5 at discharge are associated with survival. Serial measurements—performed daily or more frequently as clinically indicated—allow tracking of trends: a declining A-LES indicates improving bioenergetic status and favorable prognosis, whereas a rising or persistently elevated score suggests ongoing mitochondrial dysfunction and high risk of adverse outcome.

To account for potential confounders such as anemia, the invention optionally includes normalization of ATP levels to total hemoglobin (tHb) or red blood cell count, yielding an ATP/tHb ratio that further refines prognostic accuracy. However, the unadjusted A-LES has proven sufficiently robust in clinical validation due to the intrinsic compensatory relationship between ATP and lactate in the ratio format.

The entire workflow—from blood draw to A-LES reporting—can be completed within 60 minutes using existing hospital infrastructure, making it feasible for routine adoption in ICUs worldwide. Training requirements are minimal, as the procedure mirrors standard phlebotomy and laboratory practices. Quality control is maintained through the use of internal ATP standards, replicate measurements, and adherence to CLIA or equivalent regulatory guidelines.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, the invention was applied to establish normative reference values for blood ATP, serum lactate, and A-LES in a healthy population. A cohort of 155 individuals, ranging in age from 0 to 92 years and comprising both males and females, was recruited following ethical approval and informed consent. Each participant was confirmed to be free of acute or chronic illness through medical history, physical examination, and basic laboratory screening. Venous blood was drawn into EDTA-containing tubes and processed immediately using the phenol-TE extraction method described herein. ATP was quantified via firefly luciferase bioluminescence, and lactate was measured using a blood gas analyzer.

The results revealed a normal distribution of blood ATP levels with a mean of 0.62 mM and a standard deviation of 0.19 mM. No statistically significant differences were observed between genders, and while a slight downward trend in ATP was noted in individuals aged 60 years and older, this did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05). Resting serum lactate levels were uniformly low, with a mean of 1.19 mM (SD 0.50), and showed no variation by age or sex. Consequently, the A-LES in healthy subjects exhibited a mean of 2.00 (SD 0.83), with 95% of values falling below 3.6. These findings established a robust baseline for subsequent clinical comparisons and confirmed that A-LES is stable across demographic variables in the absence of disease.

### Example 2

This example demonstrates the clinical validation of A-LES in a prospective cohort of 42 consecutive patients admitted to the ICU over a one-year period. Patients represented a heterogeneous mix of critical conditions, including post-cardiovascular surgery (21.4%), septic shock (16.7%), trauma, respiratory failure, and neurological emergencies. Blood samples were collected at ICU admission and serially thereafter, with concurrent determination of APACHE II scores, blood ATP, serum lactate, and A-LES.

At admission, median ATP was 0.38 mM (IQR 0.29–0.56), significantly lower than healthy controls (p < 0.001), while median lactate was 2.72 mM (IQR 1.92–5.78), and median A-LES was 8.00 (IQR 4.74–13.37). Patients were stratified by APACHE II score (<20 vs. ≥20) and outcome (survival vs. non-survival). Severely ill patients (APACHE II ≥20) had significantly lower ATP (0.31 mM vs. 0.56 mM, p < 0.01) and higher A-LES (12.5 vs. 5.2, p < 0.01) than moderately ill patients. Among the seven patients with septic shock, ATP was markedly depressed at 0.29 mM (IQR 0.27–0.33).

Serial monitoring revealed that survivors consistently exhibited declining A-LES values during ICU stay, reaching a median of 2.7 (IQR 2.1–3.3) at discharge. In stark contrast, non-survivors showed persistently high or increasing A-LES, with a median of 38.9 (IQR 21.0–67.9) at the time of death or last measurement (p < 0.01 vs. survivors). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis at admission yielded area-under-curve (AUC) values of 0.83 for APACHE II, 0.75 for ATP, and 0.71 for A-LES, confirming comparable predictive power. However, only A-LES demonstrated dynamic responsiveness: while APACHE II scores remained relatively static in severely ill patients, A-LES clearly differentiated trajectories of recovery versus deterioration. Notably, all survivors had A-LES <5.49 at discharge, whereas all non-survivors exceeded 20 during their ICU course, establishing 20 as a critical prognostic threshold.

### Example 3

This example illustrates the technical superiority of the phenol-TE ATP extraction method over conventional chaotropic reagents in high-protein samples. Whole blood from five ICU patients was split into aliquots and subjected to ATP extraction using either trichloroacetic acid (TCA), perchloric acid (PCA), or phenol-TE. Following extraction and bioluminescence assay, ATP recovery was calculated relative to a spiked internal standard.

TCA and PCA methods yielded ATP recoveries of only 42% and 38%, respectively, due to co-precipitation of ATP with denatured hemoglobin and other blood proteins. In contrast, phenol-TE achieved 96% recovery, with excellent reproducibility (coefficient of variation <5%). When these extraction methods were used to calculate A-LES in the same patients, TCA- and PCA-derived scores were artificially elevated by 2.3- and 2.6-fold, respectively, leading to false-positive risk stratification. Only phenol-TE produced A-LES values consistent with clinical outcomes. This example underscores that the accuracy of A-LES is critically dependent on the fidelity of ATP measurement, and that the phenol-TE method is essential for reliable implementation of the invention.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention possesses broad industrial applicability across multiple sectors of the healthcare and diagnostics industry. Primarily, it is directly implementable in hospital intensive care units, emergency departments, and high-dependency wards where rapid assessment of critical illness severity is essential. The A-LES scoring system can be integrated into existing laboratory workflows with minimal modification, as it relies on standard blood collection procedures and widely available analytical platforms such as blood gas analyzers and luminometers. Diagnostic companies can commercialize the invention through the development of dedicated assay kits that combine phenol-TE extraction reagents, luciferase detection mixes, and software for automated A-LES calculation, thereby streamlining adoption in clinical laboratories worldwide.

Furthermore, the invention is amenable to point-of-care (POC) device development. Given that both lactate and ATP can be measured using miniaturized biosensors—lactate via electrochemical strips and ATP via stabilized luciferase formulations—it is feasible to engineer handheld or benchtop POC instruments that deliver A-LES results within minutes at the bedside. Such devices would be particularly valuable in resource-limited settings, ambulances, or military field hospitals where access to central laboratories is restricted.

Pharmaceutical and biotechnology firms can utilize A-LES as a pharmacodynamic biomarker in clinical trials of novel therapeutics targeting mitochondrial function, sepsis, or ischemia-reperfusion injury. By providing a real-time readout of bioenergetic recovery, A-LES enables more efficient dose-finding, patient stratification, and go/no-go decisions in drug development pipelines.

From a health informatics perspective, A-LES can be incorporated into electronic health record (EHR) systems and clinical decision support algorithms to trigger alerts for deteriorating patients, recommend interventions, or predict ICU length of stay. Its numerical simplicity facilitates machine learning applications aimed at refining mortality prediction models.

Regulatory agencies may recognize A-LES as a qualified biomarker for critical care endpoints, accelerating approval pathways for new resuscitation protocols or devices. Finally, the invention supports value-based healthcare initiatives by enabling more precise risk adjustment in ICU performance metrics, thereby promoting equitable quality assessment across institutions.

In sum, the invention bridges a critical gap between cellular pathophysiology and clinical decision-making, offering a scalable, evidence-based tool with transformative potential for global critical care practice.
