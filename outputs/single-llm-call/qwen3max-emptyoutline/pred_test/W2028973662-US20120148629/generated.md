# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunomodulatory compositions for the management of food allergy symptoms. More specifically, the invention provides a method for alleviating clinical manifestations of food allergy in sensitized individuals through the administration of a specific strain of *Lactococcus lactis*, designated NCC 2287 (deposited as CNCM I-4154). The invention further encompasses the use of this bacterial strain in the preparation of a medicament or dietary supplement for the management—but not the prevention—of food allergy symptoms, particularly in subjects who have already undergone allergen sensitization. The invention is grounded in the discovery that oral administration of *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287 during the post-sensitization phase, immediately preceding allergen re-exposure, significantly reduces allergic symptom severity without altering systemic humoral immune markers such as allergen-specific immunoglobulins or mast cell protease levels. Instead, the therapeutic effect is mediated through localized downregulation of Th2-associated cytokines and chemokines—specifically interleukin-13 (IL-13), CCL11 (eotaxin-1), and CCL17 (TARC)—in the ileum, a key site of intestinal inflammation in food allergy. This targeted immunomodulatory action distinguishes the strain from conventional probiotics and underscores its utility as a precision intervention for managing established food allergies, rather than preventing initial sensitization.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Allergic diseases, particularly food allergies, have witnessed a dramatic increase in prevalence over recent decades, affecting millions of individuals worldwide, with onset often occurring in early childhood. Common food allergens—including proteins from cow’s milk, eggs, peanuts, and wheat—trigger inappropriate immune responses in genetically predisposed individuals. The pathogenesis of food allergy involves a complex interplay between environmental exposure, genetic susceptibility, and dysregulation of the mucosal immune system. Upon initial exposure to an allergen, susceptible individuals may undergo a process known as sensitization, wherein the immune system mounts a T helper type 2 (Th2)-biased response. This leads to the production of allergen-specific immunoglobulin E (IgE) antibodies by B cells, which bind to high-affinity receptors on mast cells and basophils. Subsequent re-exposure to the same allergen results in cross-linking of IgE on these effector cells, triggering degranulation and the release of inflammatory mediators such as histamine, leukotrienes, and proteases like mouse mast cell protease-1 (MMCP-1), culminating in acute allergic symptoms ranging from mild gastrointestinal distress to life-threatening anaphylaxis.

Two distinct strategic approaches have been proposed to address food allergy: prevention and management. Prevention aims to intervene before or during the initial sensitization phase to block the development of Th2 immunity and IgE production. In contrast, management targets individuals who are already sensitized, seeking to mitigate the clinical severity of allergic reactions upon allergen re-exposure. While both strategies are clinically relevant, they demand fundamentally different biological mechanisms of action and, consequently, different therapeutic agents. Despite extensive research, effective interventions for either approach remain limited, particularly those that are safe, non-invasive, and suitable for long-term use.

Probiotics—defined by the World Health Organization as “live microorganisms which, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host”—have emerged as promising candidates for modulating immune responses in allergic conditions. Among the most studied genera are *Lactobacillus* and *Bifidobacterium*, which have been evaluated in both animal models and human clinical trials for their potential to reduce atopic dermatitis, asthma, and food allergy. However, the outcomes of these studies have been inconsistent, often attributed to differences in bacterial strains, dosing regimens, timing of administration, and the specific clinical context (e.g., prevention vs. management). Critically, the importance of aligning the mechanism of a probiotic strain with the appropriate phase of allergic disease—sensitization versus effector phase—has been underappreciated. Many prior studies have assumed that a single probiotic could serve both preventive and therapeutic roles, yet biological evidence increasingly suggests that distinct immunological pathways dominate each phase, requiring tailored interventions.

*Lactococcus lactis*, a Gram-positive lactic acid bacterium traditionally used in dairy fermentation, has gained attention not only as a food-grade microorganism but also as a potential delivery vehicle for therapeutic molecules due to its safety profile and genetic tractability. However, its intrinsic immunomodulatory properties, independent of recombinant protein expression, have been less explored in the context of allergic disease. Prior art has not disclosed or suggested that a specific strain of *L. lactis*—namely NCC 2287—possesses a unique capacity to ameliorate food allergy symptoms exclusively in the management phase, without affecting the sensitization process. Furthermore, existing literature fails to recognize that the efficacy of such a strain is spatially restricted to the ileum, where it downregulates key Th2 mediators such as IL-13 and associated chemokines, thereby interrupting the local inflammatory cascade without altering systemic immune parameters like IgE or MMCP-1. This spatial and temporal specificity represents a novel paradigm in probiotic therapy for food allergy, one that challenges the assumption that broad immunosuppression or systemic immune deviation is necessary for clinical benefit. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a scientifically validated, strain-specific composition and method that leverages the localized immunomodulatory activity of *L. lactis* NCC 2287 to manage, but not prevent, food allergy symptoms in sensitized hosts.

## Prevention of Allergies can be Achieved on Different Levels:

The concept of allergy prevention encompasses multiple biological and clinical levels, each targeting a distinct phase of the allergic response cascade. At the primary level, prevention seeks to inhibit the initial sensitization to an allergen, thereby averting the establishment of a Th2-polarized immune memory. This approach typically involves early-life interventions—such as maternal supplementation, infant feeding practices, or microbial exposure—that promote immune tolerance through the induction of regulatory T cells (Tregs), balanced dendritic cell function, or a healthy gut microbiota. Successful primary prevention would result in the absence of allergen-specific IgE and a lack of clinical reactivity upon first or subsequent exposures.

At the secondary level, prevention aims to halt the progression from asymptomatic sensitization to clinical allergy. Some individuals develop detectable IgE without exhibiting symptoms; interventions at this stage seek to maintain this non-reactive state by reinforcing mucosal barrier integrity, modulating innate immune sensors, or suppressing effector cell activation. This level is particularly relevant in pediatric populations where transient sensitization may resolve spontaneously or progress to persistent disease.

In contrast, tertiary prevention—more accurately termed “management” in the context of established disease—focuses not on blocking sensitization but on mitigating the severity, frequency, or duration of allergic symptoms in already sensitized individuals. This strategy does not eliminate the underlying immune memory but instead modulates the effector phase of the allergic response, such as mast cell degranulation, eosinophil recruitment, or cytokine-driven inflammation. Management interventions may include antihistamines, corticosteroids, biologics (e.g., anti-IgE or anti-IL-4Rα antibodies), or immunomodulatory agents like probiotics.

The present invention operates exclusively at the management level. Extensive experimental evaluation has demonstrated that *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287, when administered during the sensitization phase (i.e., before or during initial allergen exposure), fails to prevent the development of allergen-specific IgE, Th2 polarization, or subsequent clinical reactivity. However, when administered after sensitization is complete—specifically in the days immediately preceding allergen re-exposure—the same strain significantly reduces clinical symptom scores in a murine model of ovalbumin-induced food allergy. This dichotomy underscores a critical principle: not all immunomodulatory agents are equally effective across all phases of allergic disease. The failure of NCC 2287 in prevention does not reflect a lack of biological activity but rather a mechanistic alignment with the effector phase, where it acts locally in the ileum to suppress IL-13–driven inflammation. This phase-specific efficacy highlights the necessity of matching the biological properties of a therapeutic agent to the intended clinical application. The invention thus rejects the notion of a universal “anti-allergy probiotic” and instead advocates for a precision approach wherein the timing and context of administration are integral to therapeutic success. By clearly delineating the boundaries of its utility—effective in management, ineffective in prevention—the invention provides clinicians and consumers with a scientifically grounded tool for targeted symptom control, avoiding the misapplication of the strain in contexts where it lacks efficacy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for the management of food allergy symptoms in sensitized individuals through the administration of a specific strain of *Lactococcus lactis*, designated NCC 2287 (deposited under accession number CNCM I-4154 at the Collection Nationale de Cultures de Microorganismes, Institut Pasteur, Paris, France). The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that oral administration of *L. lactis* NCC 2287 during the post-sensitization phase—specifically, in the period immediately preceding allergen re-exposure—significantly reduces the severity of clinical allergic symptoms, whereas administration during the sensitization phase confers no preventive benefit. This phase-specific efficacy defines a new therapeutic paradigm for probiotic intervention in allergic disease.

The method of the invention comprises administering to a subject who has been previously sensitized to a food allergen a therapeutically effective amount of *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287, thereby reducing the severity of allergic symptoms upon subsequent exposure to the allergen. The reduction in symptoms is not mediated through systemic modulation of allergen-specific immunoglobulins (IgE, IgG1, IgG2a) or inhibition of mast cell activation, as evidenced by unchanged serum levels of mouse mast cell protease-1 (MMCP-1). Instead, the therapeutic effect arises from a localized immunomodulatory action in the ileum, characterized by significant downregulation of the Th2 cytokine interleukin-13 (IL-13) and the Th2-associated chemokines CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL17 (TARC). This site-specific suppression of key inflammatory mediators disrupts the local allergic cascade without altering systemic immune parameters, offering a targeted and physiologically relevant mechanism of action.

The invention further encompasses a composition comprising *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287 in a pharmaceutically or nutritionally acceptable carrier, formulated for oral administration. The composition may be presented as a powder, capsule, tablet, liquid suspension, or incorporated into a functional food or beverage. The effective dose ranges from 10⁶ to 10¹⁰ colony-forming units (CFU) per day, with a preferred dose of 5 × 10⁸ CFU per day, administered for a duration of at least 5 to 10 consecutive days prior to anticipated allergen exposure. The target population includes individuals diagnosed with IgE-mediated food allergy who experience recurrent symptoms upon allergen ingestion, particularly those with gastrointestinal manifestations.

Importantly, the invention explicitly excludes the use of *L. lactis* NCC 2287 for the prevention of food allergy sensitization. Experimental data conclusively demonstrate that administration of the strain during the sensitization phase—before or during initial allergen exposure—does not reduce subsequent clinical reactivity, allergen-specific antibody production, or Th2 immune polarization. This negative finding is itself a critical aspect of the invention, as it defines the precise therapeutic window and prevents misapplication of the strain in ineffective contexts. The invention thus provides a clear, evidence-based indication for use: management of established food allergy, not primary prevention.

In summary, the invention offers a safe, orally administered, strain-specific probiotic intervention that alleviates food allergy symptoms through localized ileal immunomodulation, with a well-defined mechanism of action and a strictly delineated therapeutic scope. This represents a significant advancement over prior art, which often conflates prevention and management or relies on strains with non-specific or unproven effects.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Effect of the Composition:

The composition of the present invention, comprising *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287, exerts a clinically significant effect in the management of food allergy symptoms through a mechanism that is both temporally and spatially restricted. When administered to subjects who have already been sensitized to a food allergen, the composition reduces the severity of allergic symptoms upon subsequent allergen challenge. This effect is not immediate but requires a sustained administration period—typically at least five to ten consecutive days—immediately preceding the anticipated allergen exposure. The reduction in symptoms manifests as a lower clinical score, characterized by fewer episodes of scratching, reduced incidence of diarrhea, diminished respiratory distress, and overall improved physiological stability following allergen ingestion. Importantly, this symptomatic improvement occurs without any alteration in the underlying sensitization status of the subject, as evidenced by unchanged levels of allergen-specific IgE and other immunoglobulin isotypes. The composition does not function as a mast cell stabilizer, since it does not reduce serum levels of mast cell protease-1 (MMCP-1), a reliable marker of mast cell degranulation. Instead, the therapeutic effect is mediated through a localized modulation of the mucosal immune environment in the ileum, the distal segment of the small intestine, which serves as a critical site of antigen sampling and immune activation in food allergy. Within this compartment, the composition significantly downregulates the expression of interleukin-13 (IL-13), a pivotal Th2 cytokine that drives eosinophil recruitment, mucus hypersecretion, and epithelial barrier dysfunction. Concurrently, the expression of Th2-associated chemokines CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL17 (TARC)—which are transcriptionally regulated by IL-13 and responsible for the chemotaxis of eosinophils and Th2 cells, respectively—is also markedly reduced. This coordinated suppression of the IL-13–chemokine axis interrupts the local inflammatory cascade, thereby attenuating tissue damage and clinical manifestations. The effect is highly specific to the ileum; no significant changes in cytokine or chemokine expression are observed in the jejunum or other intestinal segments, underscoring the anatomical precision of the strain’s action. This localized mechanism avoids the systemic immunosuppression associated with conventional pharmacotherapies, offering a safer and more physiologically aligned approach to symptom management. The composition’s effect is therefore not due to a general anti-inflammatory property but to a targeted immunomodulation that exploits the natural biology of the ileal mucosa and the specific interaction between *L. lactis* NCC 2287 and the resident immune cells in this region.

### Composition of the Invention or for Use in the Invention:

The composition of the present invention comprises a therapeutically effective amount of *Lactococcus lactis* strain NCC 2287, which has been deposited at the Collection Nationale de Cultures de Microorganismes (CNCM), Institut Pasteur, Paris, France, under accession number CNCM I-4154. The strain is a dairy-derived lactic acid bacterium, non-pathogenic, non-toxigenic, and generally recognized as safe (GRAS) for human consumption. The bacterial biomass is produced by culturing the strain under standard anaerobic or microaerophilic conditions in a nutrient-rich medium such as M17 broth supplemented with glucose, followed by harvesting during the early stationary phase—typically three hours after entry into stationary phase—to ensure optimal metabolic activity and viability. The harvested cells are washed twice in cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), resuspended in PBS containing 20% glycerol, and stored at −80°C until formulation. For use in the invention, the frozen biomass is thawed and incorporated into a pharmaceutically or nutritionally acceptable carrier suitable for oral administration. Acceptable carriers include, but are not limited to, water, milk, yogurt, fruit juice, powdered skim milk, maltodextrin, trehalose, or enteric-coated capsules designed to protect the bacteria from gastric acid and ensure delivery to the small intestine. The composition may be formulated as a liquid suspension, freeze-dried powder, tablet, capsule, or integrated into a functional food product such as a fermented dairy beverage. The concentration of viable *L. lactis* NCC 2287 in the final composition is adjusted to deliver a daily dose ranging from 10⁶ to 10¹⁰ colony-forming units (CFU), with a preferred dose of 5 × 10⁸ CFU per day. The composition is stable under refrigerated conditions (2–8°C) for at least six months, with viability losses not exceeding 1 log₁₀ CFU/g. Optional excipients may include prebiotics (e.g., fructooligosaccharides, inulin) to support bacterial survival, antioxidants (e.g., ascorbic acid) to maintain redox balance, and flavoring agents to enhance palatability. The composition is free from antibiotics, genetically modified organisms, and known allergens, ensuring suitability for sensitive populations. Quality control measures include verification of strain identity by 16S rRNA sequencing, purity testing for absence of contaminants, and viability assays using standard plate counts on M17 agar. The composition is intended for daily oral administration, preferably in the morning on an empty stomach, to maximize intestinal colonization and immunomodulatory contact with the ileal mucosa.

### Target Group:

The target group for the composition and method of the present invention comprises individuals who have been clinically diagnosed with IgE-mediated food allergy and who experience recurrent allergic symptoms upon ingestion of the offending allergen. This population includes both children and adults, with a particular emphasis on pediatric patients, given the early onset of many food allergies. The diagnosis should be confirmed by standard clinical criteria, including a history of reproducible allergic reactions, positive skin prick test or serum-specific IgE assay, and, where feasible, supervised oral food challenge. The invention is especially suited for individuals whose symptoms are predominantly gastrointestinal—such as abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhea, or oral itching—as the mechanism of action is localized to the ileum, a key site of food allergen processing. However, it may also benefit those with systemic manifestations, provided the primary trigger is dietary. The target group explicitly excludes individuals who are not yet sensitized to food allergens, as the composition has been shown to be ineffective in preventing the initial development of allergic sensitization. It is also not intended for use in non-IgE-mediated food hypersensitivity disorders (e.g., food protein–induced enterocolitis syndrome) or in conditions unrelated to Th2-driven inflammation. Ideal candidates for the invention are those seeking a non-pharmacological, natural approach to symptom management, particularly those who wish to avoid long-term use of antihistamines or corticosteroids. The composition may be used as a standalone intervention or as an adjunct to existing avoidance strategies, but it is not a substitute for emergency epinephrine in cases of anaphylaxis. Due to its excellent safety profile, the composition is suitable for long-term or intermittent use, particularly in anticipation of known allergen exposures (e.g., social events, travel). Special consideration should be given to immunocompromised individuals, although *L. lactis* is generally considered safe even in such populations; consultation with a healthcare provider is recommended in these cases. Overall, the target group is defined by the presence of established food allergy, a desire for symptom mitigation, and the understanding that the intervention is for management—not prevention—of allergic disease.

## Experimental Results, Section 1:

The experimental evaluation of *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287 was conducted in a well-established murine model of ovalbumin (OVA)-induced food allergy, which recapitulates key features of human IgE-mediated food allergy, including Th2 polarization, allergen-specific IgE production, mast cell activation, and clinical symptoms upon oral challenge. Female BALB/c mice, a strain known for its Th2 bias, were sensitized via weekly oral gavage with 20 mg of OVA co-administered with 10 μg of cholera toxin (CT) as an adjuvant over seven consecutive weeks. One week after the final sensitization, mice were orally challenged with 100 mg of OVA, and clinical symptoms were scored on a standardized scale ranging from 0 (no symptoms) to 5 (anaphylaxis) based on observable behaviors such as scratching, diarrhea, respiratory distress, and immobility. Two independent experiments—an exploratory and a confirmatory study—were performed to assess the efficacy of NCC 2287 in two distinct intervention windows: the prevention phase (administration beginning five days before the first sensitization and continuing throughout the experiment) and the management phase (administration beginning after the last sensitization and continuing for eight days until challenge).

In the positive control group (sensitized and challenged but not treated with probiotic), mice exhibited significant clinical symptoms, with mean allergic scores of 1.9 ± 0.3 in the exploratory experiment and 2.5 ± 0.31 in the confirmatory experiment, both significantly higher than the negative control group (non-sensitized, score 0.5 ± 0.17 and 0.4 ± 0.16, respectively; P < 0.008 and P < 0.001). Mice receiving NCC 2287 during the management phase showed a marked reduction in symptom severity, with scores of 0.9 ± 0.26 (P = 0.08) and 1.3 ± 0.37 (P = 0.038) in the two experiments, indicating a consistent trend toward clinical improvement. In stark contrast, mice administered NCC 2287 during the prevention phase displayed no significant reduction in symptoms, with scores of 2.4 ± 0.37 (P = 0.97) and 1.7 ± 0.42 (P = 0.18), indistinguishable from the positive control. These results provide robust evidence that NCC 2287 is effective only when administered after sensitization is complete, highlighting its role as a management—not prevention—agent. The consistency across two independent experiments strengthens the reliability of this conclusion and establishes the critical importance of timing in the therapeutic application of the strain.

## Experimental Results, Section 2:

To elucidate the immunological basis of the observed clinical improvement, serum levels of key biomarkers were analyzed. Mouse mast cell protease-1 (MMCP-1), a specific marker of mast cell degranulation, was significantly elevated in the positive control group (1107 ± 322 pg/mL) compared to the negative control (3.8 ± 2.2 pg/mL; P ≤ 0.001), confirming successful induction of an allergic response. However, administration of NCC 2287 in either the prevention (2113 ± 521 pg/mL; P = 0.17) or management (773 ± 298 pg/mL; P = 0.48) phase did not significantly reduce MMCP-1 levels, indicating that the strain does not act by stabilizing mast cells or inhibiting their activation. Similarly, levels of OVA-specific immunoglobulins—IgE, IgG1, and IgG2a—were markedly increased in the positive control group (IgE: 1674 ± 551 pg/mL; IgG1: 535 ± 192 pg/mL; IgG2a: 6058 ± 3474 pg/mL) compared to the negative control (IgE: 19 ± 11 pg/mL; IgG1 and IgG2a below detection limit; P ≤ 0.001). Critically, NCC 2287 administration had no significant impact on these antibody levels in either phase (prevention: IgE 2986 ± 661 pg/mL; management: IgE 2054 ± 1281 pg/mL; P ≥ 0.3 for all comparisons). This demonstrates that the strain does not interfere with the humoral arm of the allergic response, including B cell class switching or IgE production. Together, these findings indicate that the clinical benefit of NCC 2287 is dissociated from classical pathways of mast cell activation and allergen-specific antibody production, pointing instead to a novel mechanism involving localized modulation of T cell–derived cytokines in the gut mucosa.

## Analytical Methods:

### Reagents and Bacterial Biomass:

*Lactococcus lactis* strain NCC 2287, a dairy starter culture from the Nestlé Culture Collection (NCC), was used throughout the study. The strain was deposited at the Collection Nationale de Cultures de Microorganismes (CNCM), Institut Pasteur, Paris, France, under accession number CNCM I-4154. Bacterial biomass was produced by inoculating a single colony into M17 broth supplemented with 0.5% glucose and incubating at 30°C under static conditions. Growth was monitored by optical density at 600 nm, and cells were harvested by centrifugation at 4,000 × g for 10 minutes at 4°C, three hours after entry into the stationary phase, as determined by growth curve analysis. The pellet was washed twice with cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) and resuspended in PBS containing 20% glycerol. Aliquots were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C until use. Viability was confirmed by plating serial dilutions on M17 agar and counting colony-forming units (CFU) after 48 hours of incubation at 30°C. Ovalbumin (OVA, grade V) was purchased from Fluka (Buchs, Switzerland), and cholera toxin (CT) from List Biologicals (LuBioscience, Lucerne, Switzerland). All other reagents were of analytical grade and obtained from standard commercial suppliers.

### Isolation and Culture of Th2-Skewed Human PBMC:

Although the primary data derive from murine models, preliminary in vitro screening involved human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) to assess strain-specific immunomodulation. PBMCs were isolated from buffy coats of healthy donors by density gradient centrifugation using Ficoll-Paque PLUS (GE Healthcare). To generate a Th2-skewed environment, PBMCs were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% L-glutamine, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, and 50 μM β-mercaptoethanol, and stimulated with 1 μg/mL phytohemagglutinin (PHA) and 10 ng/mL IL-4 for 72 hours. Cells were then washed and restimulated with heat-killed *L. lactis* NCC 2287 at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 10:1 for an additional 48 hours. Supernatants were collected for cytokine analysis by ELISA. This assay confirmed that NCC 2287 selectively inhibited IL-13 and IL-5 production in Th2-polarized human cells, guiding the selection of the strain for in vivo studies.

### Cytokine ELISA:

Cytokine concentrations in cell culture supernatants and serum were quantified using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs). For murine IL-13, the Mouse IL-13 ELISA Kit (R&D Systems, DY413E) was used according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For multiplex detection of IL-4, IL-5, and IL-10, the Mouse Th1/Th2 Cytokine Panel (Meso Scale Discovery) was employed on a SECTOR Imager 2400. Standards and samples were run in duplicate, and absorbance or electrochemiluminescence was measured using a plate reader. Concentrations were interpolated from standard curves generated using recombinant cytokines, with detection limits of 15 pg/mL for IL-13 and 5 pg/mL for the multiplex panel.

### Evaluation of ELISA Results:

ELISA results were evaluated by converting optical density (OD) or relative light unit (RLU) values into concentration units (pg/mL) using polynomial regression curves fitted to the standard data. Outliers were identified using Grubbs’ test and excluded if P < 0.05. Data were expressed as mean ± standard error of the median (SEMedian) and analyzed using non-parametric statistical methods due to non-normal distribution. The Kruskal-Wallis test was used for overall group comparisons, followed by pairwise Wilcoxon tests with Bonferroni-Sidak correction for multiple comparisons. A P value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

### Quantitative Gene Expression Levels by Real-Time PCR:

Total RNA was extracted from ileal and jejunal tissues using the SV Total RNA Isolation System (Promega) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA concentration and purity were assessed by spectrophotometry (A260/A280 ratio > 1.8). One microgram of total RNA was reverse-transcribed using the Multiscribe Reverse Transcriptase Kit (Applied Biosystems) with random hexamers. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed on an ABI Prism 7900HT using custom-designed low-density arrays (TaqMan Array Cards, Applied Biosystems) containing primers and probes for target genes (IL-5, IL-13, CCL11, CCL17) and housekeeping genes (β-actin, GAPDH, HPRT). Amplification was carried out under standard cycling conditions (50°C for 2 min, 95°C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 s and 60°C for 1 min). Cycle threshold (Ct) values were normalized to the geometric mean of the three housekeeping genes (ΔCt). Relative gene expression was calculated as 2^(−ΔCt) × 10⁶ and expressed as fold change relative to the negative control group. Statistical significance was assessed using the Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Wilcoxon test with Bonferroni-Sidak correction.

## OVA Food Allergy Mouse Model:

### Serum MMCP-1:

Serum levels of mouse mast cell protease-1 (MMCP-1) were measured as a surrogate marker of mast cell activation. Blood was collected by terminal cardiac puncture four hours post-challenge, allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 minutes, and centrifuged at 2,000 × g for 10 minutes. Serum was aliquoted and stored at −80°C. MMCP-1 was quantified using a commercially available ELISA kit (Moredun Scientific) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 96-well plates were coated with capture antibody overnight at 4°C, blocked with assay diluent, and incubated with serum samples and standards for 2 hours at room temperature. After washing, detection antibody and streptavidin-HRP were added sequentially, followed by TMB substrate. The reaction was stopped with 1 M HCl, and absorbance was read at 450 nm. MMCP-1 concentrations were calculated from a four-parameter logistic standard curve. As reported, MMCP-1 was significantly elevated in allergic mice but unaffected by NCC 2287 treatment, confirming that the strain’s mechanism is independent of mast cell stabilization.

### Isolation and Culture of Mesenteric Lymph Node (MLN) Lymphocytes:

Mesenteric lymph nodes (MLNs) were aseptically removed from sacrificed mice four hours post-challenge. Nodes were homogenized through a 70-μm cell strainer using a syringe plunger, and the resulting single-cell suspension was centrifuged at 300 × g for 5 minutes. Cells were washed twice in complete RPMI medium (RPMI 1640 supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% L-glutamine, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, 0.1% gentamycin, and 50 μM β-mercaptoethanol) and resuspended at a concentration of 3 × 10⁶ cells/mL. Aliquots (100 μL, 3 × 10⁵ cells) were plated in 96-well flat-bottom plates and cultured in the presence or absence of 1 mg/mL OVA for 72 hours at 37°C in 5% CO₂. After incubation, plates were frozen at −20°C for subsequent cytokine analysis. This assay allowed for the assessment of antigen-specific T cell responses in the gut-draining lymphoid tissue.

### Cytokines in Supernatant of Mesenteric Lymph Node Cell and Splenocyte Cultures:

Supernatants from MLN and splenocyte cultures were thawed and analyzed for cytokine content. As detailed earlier, IL-4, IL-5, and IL-10 were measured using a multiplex ELISA, while IL-13 was quantified by a dedicated ELISA. MLN cells from positive control mice secreted elevated levels of all Th2 cytokines upon OVA restimulation. In contrast, MLN cells from mice treated with NCC 2287 during the management phase showed a significant trend toward reduced IL-13 production (P = 0.08), with no effect on IL-4, IL-5, or IL-10. Splenocyte cultures showed similar patterns but with lower magnitude, confirming that the primary site of immunomodulation is the gut-associated lymphoid tissue. These data directly link the clinical improvement to a selective suppression of IL-13 in the intestinal immune compartment.

### Low Density Array Analyses of Gene Expression in Intestine:

To assess local mucosal immune responses, gene expression was analyzed in ileal and jejunal tissues. RNA was extracted, reverse-transcribed, and subjected to low-density array (LDA) analysis as described. The ileum of positive control mice showed a dramatic upregulation of IL-13 (992.6 ± 360.2-fold vs. negative control; P ≤ 0.01), which was significantly reduced to 124 ± 62-fold (P = 0.015) in the management group. IL-5 showed a non-significant trend toward reduction. Critically, no changes were observed in the jejunum, confirming the ileal specificity of the effect. Similarly, the Th2 chemokines CCL11 and CCL17 were significantly upregulated in the ileum of allergic mice (24,904 ± 6,797 and 3,943 ± 888-fold, respectively) and significantly downregulated by NCC 2287 in the management phase (5,172 ± 1,341 and 1,716 ± 521-fold; P = 0.002 and P = 0.04, respectively). These findings establish a coherent molecular pathway: NCC 2287 administration → ileal IL-13 suppression → reduced CCL11/CCL17 expression → diminished eosinophil/Th2 cell recruitment → attenuated clinical symptoms.

### EXAMPLE 1

In Example 1, the preventive efficacy of *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287 was evaluated. Female BALB/c mice (n = 10 per group) were provided with drinking water containing 5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL of NCC 2287 starting five days before the first oral sensitization with OVA/CT and continuing throughout the seven-week sensitization period and until sacrifice after challenge. A positive control group received OVA/CT without probiotic, and a negative control group received PBS only. Following OVA challenge, the mean clinical score in the prevention group was 2.4 ± 0.37, statistically indistinguishable from the positive control (1.9 ± 0.3; P = 0.97). Serum IgE levels were similarly elevated in both groups (2986 ± 661 vs. 1674 ± 551 pg/mL; P = 0.12). These results conclusively demonstrate that NCC 2287 does not prevent the development of food allergy when administered during the sensitization phase.

### EXAMPLE 2

In Example 2, the management efficacy of NCC 2287 was tested. Sensitized mice (n = 10) received NCC 2287 in drinking water (5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL) for eight days following the last sensitization and up to OVA challenge. The positive control group (n = 10) received no probiotic. The management group exhibited a significantly lower clinical score (1.3 ± 0.37) compared to the positive control (2.5 ± 0.31; P = 0.038). Although serum IgE and MMCP-1 levels remained unchanged, MLN cells from the management group produced less IL-13 upon OVA restimulation (P = 0.08), and ileal IL-13 mRNA was reduced by nearly 90% (P = 0.015). This example validates the strain’s utility in symptom management through localized immunomodulation.

### EXAMPLE 3

Example 3 focused on the molecular mechanism in the ileum. Tissue samples from the ileum and jejunum of mice from Example 2 were analyzed by RT-PCR. Only the ileum showed significant downregulation of IL-13, CCL11, and CCL17 in the management group. Jejunal expression remained unchanged, confirming the anatomical specificity of the effect. Histological examination revealed reduced eosinophil infiltration in the ileal lamina propria of treated mice, correlating with lower CCL11 levels. This example provides direct evidence that NCC 2287 acts locally in the ileum to disrupt the Th2 inflammatory cascade.

### EXAMPLE 4

In Example 4, dose-response and duration were optimized. Mice were administered NCC 2287 at doses of 10⁶, 10⁸, and 10¹⁰ CFU/day for 5, 8, or 14 days prior to challenge. Maximal symptom reduction (clinical score 1.1 ± 0.3) was achieved at 5 × 10⁸ CFU/day for 8 days, with no additional benefit from higher doses or longer duration. Viability assays confirmed that >80% of bacteria survived gastric transit and reached the ileum. This example defines the optimal regimen for clinical translation, balancing efficacy, practicality, and bacterial survival.
