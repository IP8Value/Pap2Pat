# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER

The disclosed subject matter relates generally to the field of organ transplantation and, more specifically, to methods and systems for the extracorporeal regeneration and functional recovery of severely damaged donor lungs. The invention further encompasses an interventional cross-circulation platform that provides prolonged, normothermic, physiologically regulated support to enable therapeutic interventions, continuous monitoring, and comprehensive assessment of lung repair at molecular, cellular, tissue, and organ levels. The disclosed subject matter also includes novel diagnostic modalities for non-invasive evaluation of lung injury and recovery during extracorporeal support, including dynamic surface thermography and exosome profiling in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid. Additionally, the invention provides a framework for the salvage of donor lungs previously deemed unsuitable for transplantation due to severe acute injury, such as that caused by aspiration of gastric contents, thereby expanding the pool of viable donor organs available for life-saving transplantation procedures.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER

Organ transplantation represents one of the most significant medical advances of the past century, offering definitive therapy for end-stage organ failure. Despite substantial progress in surgical techniques, immunosuppression, and post-transplant care, the global shortage of transplantable organs remains the single greatest barrier to meeting clinical demand. This crisis is particularly acute in the context of lung transplantation, where up to eighty percent of potential donor lungs are discarded due to injury or dysfunction that renders them unsuitable for immediate transplantation. Among the most common causes of donor lung rejection are injuries sustained at or near the time of brain death, including trauma, pulmonary contusion, ventilator-associated lung injury, and aspiration of gastric contents. Aspiration pneumonitis, resulting from the inhalation of acidic gastric fluid containing hydrochloric acid, bile acids, pepsin, and particulate matter, induces severe chemical and inflammatory damage to the pulmonary epithelium and endothelium, leading to alveolar-capillary barrier disruption, pulmonary edema, impaired gas exchange, and profound respiratory insufficiency. Lungs injured by gastric aspiration are almost universally excluded from transplantation due to their poor functional status and high risk of primary graft dysfunction post-transplant.

In response to this critical unmet need, ex vivo lung perfusion (EVLP) has emerged as a promising technology to assess and potentially rehabilitate marginal donor lungs. EVLP systems typically provide normothermic perfusion for up to six hours using acellular or blood-based perfusates under controlled ventilation. While EVLP has enabled the successful transplantation of some previously rejected lungs, its utility is fundamentally constrained by the limited duration of support and the absence of systemic physiological regulation. Consequently, EVLP has proven ineffective in recovering lungs with severe injuries such as those caused by gastric aspiration, which require extended periods of support and complex, multi-modal therapeutic interventions to reverse structural and functional damage. Previous attempts to apply EVLP to aspiration-injured lungs have failed to reduce pulmonary edema, resolve inflammation, or improve histological architecture, underscoring the inadequacy of current platforms for managing profound lung injury.

Recognizing these limitations, researchers have developed alternative extracorporeal support strategies, including cross-circulation platforms that connect donor organs to a living host animal to provide systemic homeostasis, endocrine regulation, immune modulation, and metabolic support. Such platforms have demonstrated the capacity to maintain lung viability for durations exceeding thirty-six hours, far beyond the capabilities of conventional EVLP. However, prior cross-circulation systems have been primarily investigational, lacking integrated methodologies for therapeutic intervention, real-time diagnostics, and comprehensive assessment of organ regeneration. Moreover, no existing system has successfully demonstrated the functional and structural recovery of lungs with severe aspiration injury to a degree that meets established transplantation criteria.

Critical gaps remain in the art regarding the ability to not only sustain but actively regenerate severely damaged lungs outside the body. There is a pressing need for an integrated platform that combines prolonged physiological support with targeted therapeutic interventions—including airway clearance, surfactant replacement, and alveolar recruitment—and non-invasive diagnostic tools to monitor recovery in real time. Furthermore, there is a lack of validated biomarkers and imaging modalities capable of objectively quantifying the trajectory of lung repair during extracorporeal support. The development of such a system would represent a transformative advance in transplant medicine, enabling the salvage of a vast reservoir of currently discarded donor lungs and significantly increasing the number of life-saving transplants performed annually. The present invention addresses these unmet needs by providing a comprehensive interventional cross-circulation platform specifically engineered for the regeneration of severely injured donor lungs, along with associated diagnostic and analytical methodologies that establish new benchmarks for extracorporeal organ recovery.

## SUMMARY

The disclosed subject matter provides a comprehensive interventional cross-circulation platform and associated methodologies for the extracorporeal regeneration of severely damaged donor lungs, particularly those injured by aspiration of gastric contents. In one aspect, the invention comprises a method for regenerating a severely damaged lung ex vivo, the method comprising: (a) connecting the severely damaged lung to a living host via a cross-circulation circuit, wherein the cross-circulation circuit provides systemic physiological regulation including hemodynamic support, metabolic homeostasis, and immune modulation; (b) maintaining the lung under normothermic conditions with continuous mechanical ventilation; (c) performing a series of therapeutic interventions on the lung during cross-circulation, the therapeutic interventions comprising bronchoalveolar lavage to remove gastric debris and neutralize acidity, exogenous surfactant replacement to restore alveolar stability, and alveolar recruitment maneuvers to re-expand collapsed lung regions; and (d) monitoring lung recovery through functional, histological, and molecular assessments over a period of at least 36 hours. The severely damaged lung may be characterized by initial PaO2/FiO2 ratio less than 100 mmHg, extensive alveolar hemorrhage, pervasive leukocyte infiltration, disruption of type I pneumocyte membranes, and loss of epithelial and endothelial tight junctions.

In another aspect, the invention provides a cross-circulation system for extracorporeal lung support, comprising: a donor lung chamber configured to house a pair of lungs in a prone orientation within a temperature-controlled saline bath; a bio-bridge comprising a segment of aortic arch connected between the left atrial cuff of the donor lungs and the venous drainage of the cross-circulation circuit, wherein the bio-bridge enables selective cannulation of individual pulmonary veins for unilateral functional assessment; a dual-lumen endotracheal tube assembly permitting independent ventilation of left and right lungs through the use of bronchial blockers; a centrifugal pump console integrated with pressure, flow, and temperature sensors for real-time monitoring of pulmonary hemodynamics; and a data acquisition interface for recording and analyzing pressure-volume loops, dynamic compliance, and gas exchange metrics. The system further includes ports for periodic bronchoscopy, bronchoalveolar lavage, and surfactant delivery without interruption of perfusion or ventilation.

In yet another aspect, the invention discloses non-invasive diagnostic methods for assessing lung injury and recovery during extracorporeal support. One such method employs dynamic surface thermography to evaluate global and regional lung perfusion and ventilation-perfusion matching, comprising the steps of: capturing infrared thermal images of the lung surface at predetermined intervals; identifying regions of abnormal temperature heterogeneity correlated with areas of consolidation or hemorrhage; and performing a localized thermal challenge by applying a hypothermic probe to a defined area of the pleural surface and measuring the time constant of rewarming as an indicator of microvascular integrity and perfusion efficiency. Another diagnostic method involves the quantification of exosome concentration in bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid as a biomarker of lung injury severity and recovery trajectory, wherein elevated exosome levels at early time points (e.g., 12 hours of cross-circulation) correlate with active inflammation and tissue damage, while decreasing concentrations over time reflect resolution of injury and restoration of epithelial homeostasis.

Additionally, the invention encompasses a standardized protocol for the functional and structural assessment of extracorporeal lung recovery, integrating multi-scale analyses across macroscopic, microscopic, and molecular domains. This protocol includes: serial radiographic imaging to quantify aerated lung volume; pressure-volume loop analysis to assess elastic recoil and hysteresis; dynamic compliance measurements before and after therapeutic interventions; histopathological scoring based on a modified lung injury index evaluating polymorphonuclear cell infiltration, alveolar and interstitial edema, interstitial infiltrates, and apoptosis; immunohistochemical staining for key cellular markers including aquaporin 5 (type I pneumocytes), surfactant protein C (type II pneumocytes), CD31 (endothelial cells), zonula occludens-1 and -3 (tight junctions), and connexin 43 (gap junctions); and electron microscopy to evaluate ultrastructural restoration of the alveolar-capillary barrier, lamellar bodies in type II cells, and ciliated epithelial brush borders.

The disclosed subject matter further provides evidence that lungs subjected to severe aspiration injury and supported by the interventional cross-circulation platform for 36 hours demonstrate significant functional and structural regeneration, including: reduction in lung weight indicative of edema resolution; normalization of BAL fluid pH and pepsin concentration; clearance of inflammatory cytokines including IL-6, IL-8, TNFα, and IFNγ; restoration of dynamic compliance to greater than 65% of control values; improvement of PaO2/FiO2 ratio from less than 90 mmHg to over 640 mmHg; re-establishment of epithelial and endothelial tight junctions; recovery of type I and type II pneumocyte morphology and function; and regeneration of ciliated airway epithelium and mucus-secreting goblet cells. These findings collectively establish that the interventional cross-circulation platform enables not merely the maintenance but the active regeneration of severely damaged lungs to a functional state compatible with transplantation criteria.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER

The disclosed subject matter provides a transformative approach to addressing the critical shortage of transplantable donor lungs by enabling the extracorporeal regeneration of organs previously considered irreversibly damaged. Central to this innovation is an interventional cross-circulation platform that integrates prolonged physiological support with targeted therapeutic interventions and advanced diagnostic monitoring. The platform is specifically designed to manage lungs injured by aspiration of gastric contents—a common and severe form of donor lung injury that has historically rendered such organs unsuitable for transplantation. The methodology begins with the induction of a standardized, clinically relevant lung injury in a donor animal, typically through bronchoscopic delivery of pH-adjusted gastric contents into a single lung lobe. Following a defined period of in vivo injury development (e.g., six hours), the lungs are explanted and connected to a living host recipient via a specialized extracorporeal circuit that provides systemic regulation analogous to in vivo conditions.

A key technical feature of the disclosed system is the use of a vascular bio-bridge, constructed from the donor’s own aortic arch, to facilitate selective pulmonary venous drainage. This bio-bridge is sutured between the left atrial cuff of the explanted heart-lung block and the venous return line of the cross-circulation circuit. Critically, the bio-bridge allows for the placement of individual cannulas into the left and right pulmonary veins, enabling unilateral assessment of gas exchange and compliance in injured versus control lungs. This capability is essential for rigorous experimental validation and provides a powerful tool for clinical translation, where differential lung function must be evaluated. Concurrently, the trachea is intubated with a standard endotracheal tube, and bronchial blockers may be deployed under bronchoscopic guidance to isolate individual lungs for independent ventilation and functional testing. The entire lung assembly is housed within a temperature-controlled organ chamber filled with recirculating warm saline, maintaining normothermic conditions throughout the support period.

The cross-circulation circuit itself is primed with autologous blood collected during donor procurement and connected to the recipient host via bilateral internal jugular vein cannulation. The recipient, maintained under general anesthesia with full physiological monitoring, serves as a bioreactor that supplies oxygenated blood, nutrients, hormones, and immune factors while removing metabolic waste products. This systemic integration distinguishes the disclosed platform from acellular perfusion systems and provides the biological complexity necessary for organ regeneration. Continuous monitoring of circuit parameters—including pulmonary artery and vein pressures, flow rates, and temperatures—is achieved through integrated sensors linked to a clinical data acquisition system. Ventilation is managed using a standard ICU ventilator programmed with lung-protective strategies, including low tidal volumes, moderate positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), and periodic recruitment maneuvers.

Therapeutic interventions are systematically applied according to a predefined protocol optimized to address the pathophysiology of aspiration injury. Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) is performed at regular intervals (e.g., every 2–6 hours during the first 18 hours of support) using sterile saline delivered segmentally via flexible bronchoscopy. This procedure effectively removes particulate gastric debris, neutralizes residual acidity, and reduces proteolytic enzyme activity, thereby mitigating ongoing epithelial damage. Immediately following lavage, exogenous surfactant—typically a bovine lipid extract formulation—is instilled into the airways to replenish depleted endogenous surfactant pools and restore alveolar stability. Surfactant delivery is confirmed bronchoscopically and via transmission electron microscopy, which demonstrates abundant lamellar bodies within alveolar spaces. Subsequently, alveolar recruitment maneuvers are executed through strategic adjustments in ventilator settings, including transient increases in PEEP and inspiratory hold pressures, supplemented by manual compression techniques to direct airflow toward atelectatic regions. These interventions synergistically improve ventilation-perfusion matching and enhance gas exchange capacity.

Non-invasive diagnostics play a pivotal role in monitoring recovery and guiding therapeutic decisions. Dynamic surface thermography, performed using a high-resolution infrared camera, reveals spatial and temporal patterns of lung surface temperature that correlate with underlying pathology. Injured lungs initially exhibit heterogeneous, elevated surface temperatures corresponding to consolidated, perfused but poorly ventilated regions. Over time, as aeration improves and ventilation-perfusion mismatch resolves, surface temperatures homogenize and converge with those of control lungs. A novel thermal challenge test further refines this assessment: a standardized hypothermic probe is applied to a defined pleural area, and the rate of rewarming is quantified using video thermography. Protracted rewarming in injured lungs reflects impaired microvascular perfusion, while normalization of rewarming kinetics over 36 hours indicates restoration of vascular integrity.

Complementing thermal imaging, molecular diagnostics leverage bronchoalveolar lavage fluid as a rich source of biomarkers. Exosome concentration in BAL fluid emerges as a sensitive indicator of injury severity and recovery trajectory. Levels peak at 12 hours of cross-circulation—coinciding with maximal inflammatory activity—and decline steadily thereafter as epithelial homeostasis is restored. Similarly, serial measurement of inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, IL-8, TNFα) and gastric-specific markers like pepsin provides quantitative evidence of therapeutic efficacy. The pH of BAL fluid normalizes from acidic (pH ~4.5) to physiological levels (pH ~5.5) within 12 hours, confirming successful neutralization of aspirated gastric acid.

Comprehensive multi-scale assessment validates the extent of lung regeneration. Macroscopically, gross inspection and radiography demonstrate progressive resolution of consolidation and hemorrhage, with near-complete restoration of aerated lung volume by 36 hours. Functionally, pressure-volume loops evolve from severely deranged hysteresis to near-normal elastic recoil, while dynamic compliance improves more than six-fold. Gas exchange metrics show dramatic recovery, with PaO2/FiO2 ratios rising from <90 mmHg to >640 mmHg—well within acceptable transplantation thresholds. Histopathological analysis, conducted by blinded reviewers using a validated lung injury scoring system, reveals marked reductions in all injury categories: polymorphonuclear cell infiltration, alveolar and interstitial edema, and apoptosis. At the cellular level, immunohistochemistry confirms the regeneration of critical structures: aquaporin 5 expression in type I pneumocytes, surfactant protein C in type II cells, CD31-positive endothelium, and zonula occludens proteins at epithelial and endothelial tight junctions. Electron microscopy provides definitive ultrastructural evidence, showing restored alveolar-capillary barriers, intact type I cell membranes, reconstituted lamellar bodies, and functional ciliated epithelium.

The disclosed platform thus represents a paradigm shift in extracorporeal organ support, moving beyond mere preservation to active regeneration. By combining the physiological fidelity of cross-circulation with precision therapeutics and advanced diagnostics, the invention enables the salvage of lungs with injuries previously considered non-recoverable. This approach not only expands the donor pool but also establishes a new standard for evaluating organ viability, with implications extending beyond lung transplantation to other solid organs susceptible to ischemic or inflammatory injury.

## EXAMPLES

### Methods

The following examples illustrate the application of the interventional cross-circulation platform for the regeneration of severely damaged donor lungs, with particular emphasis on lungs injured by aspiration of gastric contents. All procedures were conducted in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at Columbia University. Sixteen miniature swine (eight donor–recipient pairs) from a partially inbred herd with well-characterized major histocompatibility complex (MHC) loci were utilized to minimize immunologic cross-reactivity. Animals ranged in age from five to eight months and weighed between 30.6 and 53.5 kilograms, with a median weight of 39.2 kilograms.

Standardization of the gastric aspiration injury model began with the collection of gastric contents from six additional swine of the same herd, which had been fasted for twelve hours. An orogastric tube was inserted under sedation, and gastric fluid was aspirated, strained through sterile surgical gauze to remove large particulates, and normalized to a pH of 2.00 ± 0.05 using sterile hydrochloric acid. The concentration of total non-sulfated bile acids was confirmed to be 416 ± 208 µM using a Bile Acid Assay Kit, and pepsin presence was verified via ELISA. Aliquots were stored at –80°C and thawed immediately prior to experimental use. Microbial cultures confirmed sterility of all preparations.

Donor swine underwent general anesthesia induced with Telazol (5 mg/kg) and buprenorphine hydrochloride (0.03 mg/kg), followed by continuous infusions of midazolam (1.5 mg/kg/h), fentanyl citrate (0.1 mg/kg/h), and inhaled isoflurane (1–5% in oxygen). A flexible video bronchoscope was advanced into the lower lobe of the left lung, with tip position confirmed by chest X-ray. Standardized gastric contents (2 mL/kg) were then delivered bronchoscopically while withdrawing the scope to a position approximately one centimeter distal to the carina to prevent contralateral contamination. The gastric fluid remained in situ for six hours to allow development of acute lung injury, during which time donor vitals, ventilator parameters, chest radiographs, and bronchoscopic images were recorded every two hours. Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid and blood samples were collected at baseline and six hours post-aspiration for analysis of inflammatory markers, cellular content, and hemogas values.

Following injury validation, donor lungs were procured via median sternotomy. After systemic heparinization (30,000 U), the main pulmonary artery was cannulated for autologous blood collection, which was stored in citrate-phosphate-dextrose bags at 8°C. Cardiac arrest was induced, and anterograde flush with cold Perfadex (low-potassium dextran solution) containing alprostadil (25 mg/kg) was administered via the pulmonary artery. The heart-lung block was excised en bloc, and on the back table, the heart was removed, leaving a 3–5 mm left atrial cuff. The aortic arch was dissected to serve as a bio-bridge: the left subclavian and brachiocephalic branches were stapled, and the remaining aortic segment was sutured between the left atrial cuff and the venous drainage cannula of the cross-circulation circuit. Individual 20-gauge cannulas were placed through the bio-bridge wall into the left and right pulmonary veins, with positions confirmed by X-ray and fiber-optic imaging, enabling unilateral venous blood sampling for gas exchange analysis.

Recipient swine were prepared identically to donors and anesthetized for the duration of the 36-hour cross-circulation period. Bilateral neck cut-downs exposed the internal jugular veins, which were cannulated with 16–18 Fr catheters using the Seldinger technique after systemic heparinization (15,000 U). Methylprednisolone (1 g) and calcium chloride (500 mg) were administered intravenously just prior to circuit connection. The cross-circulation circuit, primed with donor-collected autologous blood, consisted of a Jostra HL-20 pump console, Rotaflow centrifugal pump, softshell reservoir, and Smart-coated tubing. Initial flow rates were set to 5–10% of estimated cardiac output, targeting pulmonary artery pressures below 15 mmHg and pulmonary vein pressures of 3–5 mmHg. Ventilation was initiated within ten minutes of circuit start using volume control mode (respiratory rate 6–8 bpm, tidal volume 6–8 mL/kg, PEEP 5 cmH2O, FiO2 40%).

Therapeutic interventions commenced immediately upon establishment of cross-circulation and were repeated at 2, 4, 6, 12, and 18 hours. Each 30-minute intervention sequence included: (1) three cycles of bronchoalveolar lavage, wherein 5 mL of sterile saline was injected and aspirated from each of ten bronchopulmonary segments per lung (total ~50 mL per intervention, ~300 mL cumulative); (2) surfactant replacement with one vial (3 mL) of bovine lipid extract surfactant (BLES, 27 mg phospholipids/mL), delivered bronchoscopically to all lobes (total dose 12.5 mg phospholipid/kg); and (3) alveolar recruitment maneuvers involving incremental increases in PEEP (up to 12.5 cmH2O), tidal volume (15–20% increase), and inspiratory hold pressures (up to 25 cmH2O), supplemented by manual compression of aerated regions to redirect airflow to atelectatic zones. Peak inspiratory pressures were maintained below 30 cmH2O to prevent barotrauma.

Functional monitoring occurred every six hours and included: arterial and venous blood gas analysis from main and single-lung cannulas; dynamic compliance calculation (Cdyn = tidal volume / (peak inspiratory pressure – PEEP)); pressure-volume loop generation using custom Arduino-based sensors and MATLAB processing; lung weight measurement via top-loading balance within the organ chamber; and portable chest radiography. Lung performance challenges were conducted by transiently doubling minute ventilation and increasing FiO2 from 40% to 100%, with pre- and post-challenge blood gases used to calculate ΔpO2 and ΔpCO2 as indices of oxygenation and ventilation reserve.

Histopathological and molecular analyses employed systematic, randomized sampling to avoid bias. Lungs were divided into twelve anatomical regions, and a random number generator assigned sampling locations for each time point (0, 18, and 36 hours of cross-circulation). Tissue wedges were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 3–5 µm thickness. Staining included hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), pentachrome, Alcian blue, periodic acid-Schiff, silver reticulin, and immunohistochemistry for aquaporin 5, surfactant protein C, CD163, CD31, caspase 3, neutrophil elastase, zonula occludens-1 and -3, and connexin 43. Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) assays detected late apoptotic cells. All slides were randomized, coded, and reviewed by a blinded pulmonary pathologist using a modified lung injury score evaluating six categories: airway and alveolar polymorphonuclear cells, alveolar edema, interstitial infiltrate, interstitial edema, and apoptosis (combined caspase 3 and TUNEL scores).

Ultrastructural analysis utilized scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). For SEM, samples were formalin-fixed, ethanol-dehydrated, frozen, and lyophilized before imaging on a Zeiss GeminiSEM 300 at 2.5 kV. For TEM, tissues were glutaraldehyde-fixed, osmium tetroxide-postfixed, dehydrated, embedded in Lx-112 resin, ultrathin-sectioned (60 nm), stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate, and imaged on a JEOL JEM-1200 EXII microscope. Functional cellular assays included: (1) BODIPY-labeled surfactant protein B uptake to assess type II pneumocyte viability; (2) acetylated LDL and carboxyfluorescein succinimidyl ester (CFSE) uptake to evaluate endothelial function; and (3) Alamar Blue metabolic activity assays normalized to DNA content via PicoGreen quantification.

Diagnostic thermography employed a FLIR T430sc infrared camera with ResearchIR software. Global surface temperatures were averaged over isomorphic regions of interest, while localized rewarming kinetics were assessed by applying an 8-mm aluminum hypothermic probe (4°C) to the pleura for one second and recording thermal recovery at 60 frames/second. Temperature differentials between cooled and surrounding areas were quantified using ImageJ software, with time constants derived from exponential decay curves. Exosome analysis of BAL fluid utilized NanoSight Tracking Analysis to determine particle concentration, size distribution, and mean diameter.

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 7.0e. One-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc tests and Student’s t-tests compared groups, with p < 0.05 considered significant. No data were excluded, and all experiments were conducted with technical triplicates. The study design represented a proof-of-concept with n = 8, sufficient to demonstrate reproducibility and achieve statistical significance between time points, with power analysis planned for future studies.
