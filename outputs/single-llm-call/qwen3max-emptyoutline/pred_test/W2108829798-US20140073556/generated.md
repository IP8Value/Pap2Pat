# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of anti-inflammatory therapeutics, particularly to synthetic peptide-based antagonists of interleukin-1 receptor type I (IL-1RI). More specifically, the invention provides a novel ten-amino acid peptide, termed Ilantide, derived from the N-terminal domain of human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra), which functions as a competitive inhibitor of IL-1 signaling by binding to IL-1RI with high affinity. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising Ilantide in monomeric or multimeric forms, methods for the treatment of inflammatory and neuroinflammatory disorders mediated by IL-1, and formulations suitable for systemic administration that enable effective penetration of the blood–brain barrier (BBB). The disclosed compounds and methods are applicable to a broad spectrum of conditions, including but not limited to rheumatoid arthritis, type 2 diabetes mellitus, Alzheimer’s disease, multiple sclerosis, and other acute or chronic inflammatory diseases wherein IL-1 plays a pathogenic role.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

Interleukin-1 (IL-1) is a pivotal proinflammatory cytokine that orchestrates the innate immune response and contributes significantly to the pathogenesis of numerous inflammatory and autoimmune disorders. It exists in two primary bioactive isoforms, IL-1α and IL-1β, both of which signal through the interleukin-1 receptor type I (IL-1RI). Upon ligand binding, IL-1RI recruits the interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein (IL-1RAcP), forming a functional signaling complex that activates downstream pathways, notably nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK). These cascades induce the transcription of genes encoding additional cytokines, chemokines, adhesion molecules, and enzymes such as cyclooxygenase-2 and inducible nitric oxide synthase, thereby amplifying the inflammatory response. Dysregulated IL-1 activity has been implicated in a wide array of pathological conditions, including rheumatoid arthritis (RA), systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis, adult-onset Still’s disease, type 2 diabetes, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s disease (AD).

To counteract excessive IL-1 signaling, endogenous regulatory mechanisms exist, the most prominent being the interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra). IL-1Ra is a naturally occurring protein that binds to IL-1RI with high affinity but fails to recruit IL-1RAcP, thereby acting as a pure competitive antagonist without intrinsic agonist activity. Recombinant human IL-1Ra, marketed as Anakinra (Ana), has been approved for clinical use in several autoinflammatory conditions. While effective in certain peripheral inflammatory diseases, Ana exhibits significant limitations that restrict its broader therapeutic utility. Chief among these is its poor ability to cross the blood–brain barrier (BBB), which severely limits its efficacy in treating central nervous system (CNS) disorders characterized by neuroinflammation, such as AD, multiple sclerosis, and other neurodegenerative conditions. Additionally, as a recombinant protein, Ana is subject to manufacturing complexities, batch-to-batch variability, immunogenicity risks, and a relatively short plasma half-life requiring daily subcutaneous injections.

Alternative biologic strategies have been developed, including IL-1-neutralizing monoclonal antibodies (e.g., Canakinumab) and soluble decoy receptors such as Rilonacept (an IL-1 trap composed of the extracellular domains of IL-1RI and IL-1RAcP fused to the Fc portion of IgG). Although these agents demonstrate improved pharmacokinetics compared to Ana, they remain large macromolecules that do not efficiently penetrate the BBB and are associated with high production costs and potential immunogenicity. Consequently, there remains an unmet medical need for a low-molecular-weight, non-immunogenic, stable, and BBB-penetrant IL-1 antagonist capable of modulating both peripheral and central inflammation.

Peptide-based therapeutics offer a promising alternative to biologics due to their smaller size, potential for oral or systemic delivery, reduced immunogenicity, and ease of chemical synthesis with high reproducibility. Structure-based design approaches allow for the identification of minimal functional motifs within larger proteins that retain biological activity. In the case of IL-1Ra, structural studies of the IL-1Ra–IL-1RI complex (e.g., PDB:1IRA) have revealed that specific regions of IL-1Ra, particularly within its N-terminal domain, mediate critical interactions with the third immunoglobulin-like (Ig3) module of IL-1RI. These interactions are essential for high-affinity binding and competitive inhibition of IL-1. However, prior to the present invention, no synthetic peptide derived from this region had been demonstrated to possess sufficient affinity, stability, and functional antagonism to serve as a viable therapeutic agent.

Existing attempts to develop small-molecule IL-1 inhibitors have largely failed due to the large and flat protein–protein interaction interface between IL-1 and IL-1RI, which is difficult to disrupt with conventional drug-like molecules. Peptides, by contrast, can mimic secondary structural elements and engage in multivalent interactions that more closely resemble natural protein interfaces. Nevertheless, linear peptides often suffer from rapid proteolytic degradation, poor membrane permeability, and low receptor affinity when isolated from their native protein context. Strategies to overcome these limitations include cyclization, D-amino acid substitution, lipidation, and multimerization. The present invention addresses these challenges by identifying a specific decapeptide sequence from the N-terminus of IL-1Ra—SGRKSSKMQA—and demonstrating that, particularly in a tetravalent dendrimeric form (Ilantide-t), it retains high-affinity binding to IL-1RI, effectively inhibits IL-1-induced signaling, and exhibits unexpected pharmacokinetic properties, including measurable penetration into the central nervous system following systemic administration.

Thus, the background art lacks a synthetic, low-molecular-weight IL-1 antagonist that combines high target specificity, functional efficacy in both peripheral and neural tissues, favorable pharmacokinetics, and manufacturability. The present invention fulfills this gap by providing Ilantide and its derivatives as novel therapeutic agents for a wide range of IL-1-mediated diseases.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel synthetic peptide, designated Ilantide, having the amino acid sequence Ser-Gly-Arg-Lys-Ser-Ser-Lys-Met-Gln-Ala (SGRKSSKMQA; SEQ ID NO:1), which functions as a potent and specific antagonist of interleukin-1 receptor type I (IL-1RI). Ilantide is derived from the N-terminal domain of human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra) and competitively inhibits the binding of IL-1α and IL-1β to IL-1RI, thereby preventing the recruitment of the interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein (IL-1RAcP) and subsequent activation of proinflammatory signaling pathways, including nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK).

In one aspect, the invention encompasses Ilantide in its monomeric form (Ilantide-m) and in multimeric configurations, particularly a tetravalent dendrimeric form (Ilantide-t) wherein four Ilantide monomers are covalently linked to a lysine-based scaffold. The multimeric form exhibits enhanced functional potency and prolonged biological activity compared to the monomer, despite a lower molar potency, due to avidity effects and potentially improved stability.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising Ilantide or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, or derivative thereof, in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. These compositions are formulated for systemic administration, including subcutaneous, intravenous, intraperitoneal, or intranasal routes, and are designed to achieve therapeutic concentrations in both peripheral tissues and the central nervous system.

Methods of treatment are also disclosed, wherein Ilantide is administered to a subject in need thereof to prevent, ameliorate, or treat a disorder mediated by IL-1 signaling. Such disorders include, but are not limited to, rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis, adult-onset Still’s disease, type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, Alzheimer’s disease, multiple sclerosis, experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, sepsis, gout, inflammatory bowel disease, psoriasis, atherosclerosis, ischemia-reperfusion injury, and other acute or chronic inflammatory conditions.

A key advantage of Ilantide over existing IL-1 antagonists is its ability to cross the blood–brain barrier (BBB) following systemic administration, as demonstrated by detectable concentrations in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) after subcutaneous injection in rodent models. This property enables the treatment of neuroinflammatory components of diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease, where IL-1β is overproduced by activated microglia and contributes to neuronal dysfunction, synaptic loss, and cognitive decline. Ilantide has been shown to reduce social memory deficits induced by amyloid-β peptides and to attenuate lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced sickness behavior, effects that correlate with modulation of central and peripheral cytokine profiles.

Additionally, Ilantide protects pancreatic β-cells from IL-1β-induced apoptosis, a mechanism relevant to the preservation of insulin secretion in diabetes. Notably, this cytoprotective effect occurs independently of nitric oxide suppression, suggesting a distinct mechanism of action compared to full-length IL-1Ra and potentially offering a more targeted anti-apoptotic profile.

The invention also includes diagnostic and research tools comprising Ilantide for the study of IL-1 signaling pathways, receptor-ligand interactions, and inflammatory disease models. Furthermore, kits containing Ilantide and instructions for its use in therapeutic or experimental settings are provided.

In summary, the present invention offers a first-in-class, synthetic, low-molecular-weight IL-1RI antagonist with dual peripheral and central activity, overcoming critical limitations of current biologic therapies and opening new avenues for the treatment of a broad spectrum of inflammatory and neurodegenerative diseases.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Inflammation

Inflammation is a complex biological response of vascularized tissues to harmful stimuli, such as pathogens, damaged cells, or irritants. It is a protective mechanism aimed at eliminating the initial cause of cell injury, clearing out necrotic cells and tissues, and initiating tissue repair. However, when dysregulated or persistent, inflammation becomes a driver of pathology in numerous diseases. The inflammatory response involves a cascade of molecular and cellular events, including the release of cytokines, chemokines, prostaglandins, and reactive oxygen species, as well as the recruitment and activation of immune cells such as neutrophils, macrophages, and lymphocytes. Among the cytokines, interleukin-1 (IL-1) stands out as a master regulator of the acute-phase response and a key mediator in both acute and chronic inflammatory conditions. IL-1 promotes fever, leukocyte activation, endothelial adhesion molecule expression, and the production of other inflammatory mediators. In chronic settings, sustained IL-1 signaling contributes to tissue destruction, fibrosis, and organ dysfunction. The present invention targets this central node in the inflammatory network by providing a novel antagonist that specifically blocks IL-1 receptor engagement, thereby interrupting the inflammatory cascade at its source. By doing so, Ilantide offers a mechanism to suppress pathological inflammation while preserving other arms of the immune system, potentially reducing the risk of broad immunosuppression associated with corticosteroids or global cytokine inhibitors.

### Interleukins

Interleukins are a group of cytokines originally identified as being produced by leukocytes and acting on other leukocytes, though it is now known that many cell types produce and respond to interleukins. They play crucial roles in the regulation of immune responses, hematopoiesis, and inflammation. The interleukin-1 family comprises eleven members, including the agonists IL-1α and IL-1β, the natural antagonist IL-1Ra, and several other ligands with diverse functions. IL-1α is constitutively expressed and acts primarily as an alarmin upon cellular damage, while IL-1β is synthesized as an inactive precursor (pro-IL-1β) and requires cleavage by inflammasome-associated caspase-1 to become active. Both isoforms bind to IL-1RI with similar affinity, triggering identical downstream signaling. The balance between IL-1 and IL-1Ra determines the net inflammatory output of a tissue. In many diseases, this balance is skewed toward excess IL-1 activity. The invention leverages detailed structural knowledge of the IL-1/IL-1RI/IL-1Ra interaction to design a minimal peptide mimic of IL-1Ra that restores this balance. Unlike full-length IL-1Ra, which interacts with multiple domains of IL-1RI, Ilantide targets a specific hotspot within the Ig3 domain, sufficient to block IL-1 binding but potentially allowing differential modulation of downstream signals, as evidenced by its dissociation of anti-apoptotic effects from nitric oxide suppression in pancreatic islets.

### Peptide Fragments of the Present Invention

The core of the present invention is the identification and validation of a specific decapeptide fragment derived from the N-terminal region of human IL-1Ra. This fragment, Ilantide (SEQ ID NO:1: SGRKSSKMQA), corresponds to residues 4–13 of the mature IL-1Ra protein and was selected based on structural modeling of the IL-1Ra–IL-1RI complex (PDB:1IRA), which indicated that this segment makes direct contact with the Ig3 module of IL-1RI. The sequence contains a cluster of basic residues (Arg, Lys) and polar residues (Ser, Gln) that are predicted to form electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding interactions with complementary surfaces on the receptor. To confirm that the biological activity is sequence-dependent, control peptides were synthesized, including scrambled versions (e.g., KQSAGKRSMS and KASQKGMSRS) and a reversed sequence (AQMKSSKRGS). These controls showed no significant binding to IL-1RI or inhibition of IL-1 signaling, confirming that the specific linear arrangement of amino acids in Ilantide is essential for function. The peptide may be modified to enhance stability or pharmacokinetics, for example, by N-terminal acetylation, C-terminal amidation, substitution of L-amino acids with D-amino acids at non-critical positions, or incorporation of non-natural amino acids, provided that such modifications do not abolish IL-1RI binding or antagonism. The invention thus includes not only the native sequence but also conservative variants that maintain the spatial orientation and charge distribution necessary for receptor interaction.

### Synthetic Preparation

Ilantide and its derivatives are prepared using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) techniques, preferably employing the fluorenylmethyloxycarbonyl (Fmoc) protection strategy. Synthesis is carried out on a suitable resin, such as TentaGel resin, which provides a polyethylene glycol-polystyrene matrix that enhances solvation and coupling efficiency. The stepwise addition of Fmoc-protected amino acids is performed using activators such as HBTU or HATU in the presence of a base like diisopropylethylamine (DIPEA). After chain assembly, the peptide is cleaved from the resin and side-chain protecting groups are removed using a trifluoroacetic acid (TFA)-based cocktail. For the monomeric form (Ilantide-m), the crude peptide is purified by gel filtration chromatography using Sephadex G-10 or by reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) to achieve a purity of at least 85%, as confirmed by analytical HPLC and mass spectrometry. For the tetravalent dendrimeric form (Ilantide-t), a lysine dendron scaffold is first constructed on the resin, featuring four orthogonal reactive sites. Four equivalents of the Ilantide sequence are then coupled to these sites, followed by simultaneous cleavage and deprotection. The resulting multimeric compound is purified using similar chromatographic methods. Biotinylation or other labeling can be introduced during synthesis by incorporating Fmoc-Lys(Biotin)-OH at the N- or C-terminus for detection or tracking purposes. The synthetic process ensures high reproducibility, scalability, and absence of biological contaminants, distinguishing Ilantide from recombinant biologics like Anakinra.

### Compound of the Present Invention

The primary compound of the invention is Ilantide, a synthetic decapeptide with the sequence SGRKSSKMQA (SEQ ID NO:1). This compound functions as a competitive antagonist of IL-1RI, binding with an apparent dissociation constant (Kd) of approximately 2.75 × 10⁻⁸ M, which is comparable to that of full-length IL-1Ra (Kd ≈ 4.8 × 10⁻⁸ M) and significantly higher than that of IL-1β (Kd ≈ 1.71 × 10⁻⁷ M), as determined by surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis. Ilantide specifically inhibits IL-1β-induced activation of NF-κB in reporter cell lines and suppresses IL-1β-stimulated tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α) secretion from macrophages, without affecting IL-6-induced STAT3 activation, demonstrating pathway selectivity. It promotes survival and neurite outgrowth in primary cerebellar granule neurons, protects pancreatic islets from IL-1β-induced apoptosis, and reduces inflammation in animal models of arthritis and neuroinflammation. The compound is stable under physiological conditions for a duration sufficient to exert therapeutic effects, as evidenced by a plasma half-life of approximately 2 hours in rats following subcutaneous administration. Its small size (molecular weight ~1.1 kDa for the monomer) facilitates tissue distribution and, critically, enables partial penetration of the blood–brain barrier, a property not shared by current IL-1 antagonist biologics.

### Multimeric Compound

To enhance the functional potency and duration of action of Ilantide, a multimeric form, designated Ilantide-t, was developed. Ilantide-t consists of four Ilantide monomers covalently linked to a central lysine-based dendrimeric scaffold, creating a tetravalent structure. This design exploits the principle of avidity, where multiple weak interactions collectively result in stronger overall binding to the target receptor. Indeed, Ilantide-t demonstrated superior efficacy compared to the monomer in several functional assays. For instance, in the protection of rat pancreatic islets from IL-1β-induced apoptosis, Ilantide-t achieved 93% inhibition at a concentration of 8.68 × 10⁶ nM, surpassing the maximal effect of Anakinra (64% inhibition). Similarly, in vivo, Ilantide-t showed robust anti-inflammatory effects in collagen-induced arthritis and neuroinflammatory models at a dose of 10 mg/kg administered subcutaneously. The multimeric architecture may also confer resistance to proteolytic degradation, extending the peptide’s half-life in circulation. Importantly, the enhanced activity of Ilantide-t is not due to non-specific aggregation, as control multimers of scrambled peptides were inactive. The invention therefore includes not only the tetravalent form but also other multimeric configurations (e.g., dimers, trimers, or higher-order assemblies) that improve pharmacological properties while retaining specificity for IL-1RI.

### Methods

The invention provides methods for treating a subject suffering from or at risk of developing an IL-1-mediated disorder by administering a therapeutically effective amount of Ilantide or a multimeric derivative thereof. The method comprises identifying a subject with a condition characterized by excessive or dysregulated IL-1 signaling and delivering Ilantide via a route that achieves sufficient systemic exposure. Administration may be acute (e.g., in sepsis or acute gout) or chronic (e.g., in rheumatoid arthritis or Alzheimer’s disease), with dosing regimens tailored to the disease indication, severity, and patient factors. The method is particularly advantageous for disorders with a neuroinflammatory component, as Ilantide’s BBB penetration allows direct modulation of central IL-1 activity. In preclinical models, Ilantide reduced LPS-induced social withdrawal, ameliorated amyloid-β-induced memory deficits, and delayed the onset of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, supporting its use in human neurodegenerative and autoimmune CNS diseases. The method may be used alone or in combination with other anti-inflammatory agents, such as TNF-α inhibitors, corticosteroids, or disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs), to achieve synergistic effects or reduce doses of more toxic therapies.

### Pharmaceutical Formulation

Pharmaceutical formulations of Ilantide are prepared by combining the peptide with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, diluents, or excipients. For parenteral administration (subcutaneous, intravenous, or intramuscular), the formulation typically comprises Ilantide dissolved or suspended in an isotonic aqueous solution such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) or saline, optionally containing stabilizers (e.g., human serum albumin, sugars like trehalose or sucrose), surfactants (e.g., polysorbate 80), and preservatives (e.g., benzyl alcohol). Lyophilized powders may be prepared for reconstitution prior to use, enhancing shelf-life stability. For intranasal delivery, formulations may include absorption enhancers (e.g., chitosan) and mucoadhesive polymers to promote CNS uptake. Oral formulations are less preferred due to potential gastrointestinal degradation but could be developed using enteric coatings or nanoparticle encapsulation. The concentration of Ilantide in the final formulation is adjusted to deliver a therapeutically effective dose, typically ranging from 0.1 mg/kg to 50 mg/kg body weight, based on preclinical efficacy and safety data. All components are selected to ensure compatibility, sterility, and minimal irritation at the site of administration.

### Dosage

The dosage of Ilantide depends on the specific disease being treated, the route of administration, the patient’s age, weight, and overall health, and the desired therapeutic outcome. In animal studies, effective doses ranged from 1 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg administered subcutaneously once daily. Translating these findings to humans using standard allometric scaling suggests an initial human dose of approximately 0.16 mg/kg to 1.6 mg/kg. For chronic conditions like rheumatoid arthritis or Alzheimer’s disease, a maintenance dose of 1–10 mg/kg once or twice daily may be appropriate. In acute settings such as gout flares or cytokine release syndrome, higher bolus doses (e.g., 10–50 mg/kg) may be administered intravenously. Dosing frequency may be adjusted based on pharmacokinetic monitoring; given Ilantide’s plasma half-life of ~2 hours in rats, multiple daily doses or sustained-release formulations may be necessary to maintain therapeutic levels. Clinical trials would determine the optimal dose-response relationship, maximum tolerated dose, and long-term safety profile in humans.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Design and Synthesis of Ilantide**

Based on the crystal structure of the IL-1Ra–IL-1RI complex (PDB:1IRA), a ten-amino acid sequence corresponding to residues 4–13 of human IL-1Ra (SGRKSSKMQA) was identified as a candidate peptide for mimicking IL-1Ra’s receptor-binding function. This peptide, designated Ilantide, was synthesized using Fmoc solid-phase peptide synthesis on TentaGel resin. The crude product was purified by gel filtration on Sephadex G-10, yielding Ilantide-m with >85% purity as assessed by HPLC. A tetravalent dendrimeric version, Ilantide-t, was prepared by coupling four Ilantide sequences to a lysine dendron scaffold during synthesis, followed by similar purification. Control peptides, including scrambled (KQSAGKRSMS, KASQKGMSRS) and reversed (AQMKSSKRGS) sequences, were synthesized in parallel for specificity testing.

### Example 2

**Binding Affinity to IL-1RI by Surface Plasmon Resonance**

The interaction between Ilantide and the ectodomain of human IL-1RI was analyzed using a Biacore 2000 instrument. IL-1RI was immobilized on a CM4 sensor chip, and increasing concentrations of Ilantide-t, IL-1β, or IL-1Ra were injected. Ilantide-t bound to IL-1RI with an apparent Kd of 2.75 × 10⁻⁸ M, comparable to IL-1Ra (Kd = 4.8 × 10⁻⁸ M) and significantly tighter than IL-1β (Kd = 1.71 × 10⁻⁷ M). No binding was observed to a control protein (NCAM Ig1-2), confirming specificity. Competitive SPR assays showed that Ilantide-t dose-dependently inhibited IL-1β binding to IL-1RI, indicating shared epitopes.

### Example 3

**Inhibition of NF-κB Activation**

HEK-Blue IL-1β cells, engineered to express an NF-κB-inducible SEAP reporter, were stimulated with 1.2 pM IL-1β in the presence of varying concentrations of Ilantide-m, Ilantide-t, IL-1Ra, or sIL-1RI. Both Ilantide forms dose-dependently suppressed SEAP production, with Ilantide-t showing greater maximal inhibition. Scrambled peptides had no effect. In contrast, Ilantide-t did not inhibit IL-6-induced STAT3 activation in HEK-Blue IL-6 cells, demonstrating pathway selectivity for IL-1 signaling.

### Example 4

**Suppression of TNF-α Secretion from Macrophages**

Murine AMJ2-C8 macrophages were pretreated with Ilantide-m, Ilantide-t, or controls, then stimulated with IL-1β. Conditioned media were applied to L929 fibroblasts with actinomycin D, and TNF-α-induced cytotoxicity was measured by MTS assay. Ilantide-m and Ilantide-t significantly reduced TNF-α levels in a dose-dependent manner, matching the efficacy of IL-1Ra and sIL-1RI. Scrambled peptides were inactive, confirming sequence specificity.

### Example 5

**Neuroprotective Effects in Primary Neurons**

Cerebellar granule neurons (CGNs) from 7-day-old rats were cultured under low-potassium conditions to induce apoptosis. Treatment with Ilantide-m or Ilantide-t (1–10 μM) significantly increased neuronal survival, comparable to IL-1Ra and IGF-1. Additionally, Ilantide-t (0.1–1 μM) induced robust neurite outgrowth, an effect blocked by co-administration of IL-1β, indicating that Ilantide promotes neuronal differentiation by antagonizing tonic IL-1 signaling.

### Example 6

**Protection of Pancreatic Islets from Apoptosis**

Neonatal rat islets were exposed to 150 pg/ml IL-1β with or without Ilantide or Anakinra. Ilantide-t (8.68 × 10⁶ nM) reduced apoptosis by 93%, exceeding Anakinra’s maximal effect (64% at 8.68 × 10⁴ nM). Surprisingly, neither Ilantide form inhibited IL-1β-induced nitric oxide production, as measured by Griess assay, suggesting a nitric oxide-independent anti-apoptotic mechanism.

### Example 7

**Pharmacokinetics and BBB Penetration**

Adult male Wistar rats received a single subcutaneous dose of biotinylated Ilantide-t (10 mg/kg). Plasma concentrations peaked at ~6 μg/ml at 20 minutes and declined with a half-life of ~2 hours. At 30 minutes post-injection, CSF concentrations reached 125 ng/ml, yielding a plasma/CSF ratio of 55. This demonstrates that Ilantide-t crosses the BBB to a therapeutically relevant extent, unlike Anakinra.

### Example 8

**Efficacy in Collagen-Induced Arthritis**

Rats with CIA were treated daily with Ilantide-t (10 mg/kg, s.c.) from day 8 to 15 post-immunization. Ilantide-t significantly reduced clinical arthritis scores from day 10 onward compared to vehicle, as confirmed by area-under-curve analysis (P < 0.05). Histopathology revealed decreased joint inflammation and cartilage erosion, supporting its potential for rheumatoid arthritis therapy.

### Example 9

**Amelioration of Neuroinflammatory Deficits**

In LPS-treated rats, Ilantide-t (10 mg/kg, s.c.) prevented social interaction deficits and body weight loss, effects accompanied by elevated plasma IL-10. In amyloid-β-injected rats, Ilantide-t restored social recognition memory, as measured by a recognition ratio significantly below 0.5 (P = 0.002). In EAE, Ilantide-t delayed clinical onset by 1–2 days. These results confirm Ilantide’s efficacy in diverse neuroinflammatory models.
