# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL DOMAIN

The present invention resides in the field of industrial biotechnology, specifically in the domain of microbial enzyme production and bioprocess engineering. More particularly, the invention relates to genetically engineered strains of the filamentous fungus *Trichoderma reesei* capable of hyperproducing cellulolytic and hemicellulolytic enzyme cocktails, and to fermentation processes for the cost-effective production of these enzymes using low-cost, agroindustrial carbon sources. The invention further encompasses methods for the rational design and combinatorial genetic modification of fungal hosts to enhance protein secretion, relieve carbon catabolite repression, expand substrate utilization range, and improve the functional composition of secreted enzyme mixtures. The technical domain intersects with sustainable bioeconomy initiatives, lignocellulosic biorefining, and the development of green alternatives to petrochemical-based industrial processes. The invention is particularly relevant to industries involved in the production of biofuels, biochemicals, and biomaterials from non-edible plant biomass, where efficient and economical enzymatic deconstruction of lignocellulose is a critical bottleneck. The disclosed technology addresses key challenges in the field, including the high cost of enzyme production, the limited catalytic efficiency of native fungal enzyme cocktails, and the dependence on expensive inducing sugars, thereby enabling more economically viable and environmentally sustainable bioprocessing routes.

## PRIOR ART

The industrial application of enzymes as biocatalysts has grown significantly due to their specificity, efficiency, and environmental compatibility compared to traditional chemical catalysts. Among these, cellulases—enzymes capable of hydrolyzing cellulose into fermentable sugars—are of paramount importance for the emerging bioeconomy, particularly in the conversion of lignocellulosic biomass into biofuels and value-added chemicals. The filamentous fungus *Trichoderma reesei* has emerged as the dominant microbial platform for the industrial production of cellulase enzyme cocktails, accounting for an estimated 80% of formulations used in the lignocellulosic biofuels sector. This predominance stems from its natural proficiency in secreting large quantities of a synergistic set of carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes).

The foundational strain for most academic and many industrial *T. reesei* variants is the isolate QM6a, originally sourced from the Solomon Islands during World War II. In the early 1970s, a program of random mutagenesis on QM6a yielded a series of hypercellulolytic mutants, the most notable being RUT-C30. This strain, now available in the public domain, carries a significant 85-kb chromosomal deletion and a truncation in the *cre1* gene, which encodes a master repressor of cellulase gene expression under carbon catabolite repression (CCR) by glucose. The partial relief of CCR in RUT-C30 allows it to produce extracellular protein titers up to 20 g L⁻¹, a substantial improvement over its progenitor. However, this performance remains far below that of proprietary industrial strains, which are reported to achieve titers exceeding 100 g L⁻¹, though the specific genetic and process details behind these achievements are closely guarded trade secrets.

Extensive research has identified numerous genetic targets for improving *T. reesei*'s enzyme production capabilities. A central regulator is the transcription factor XYR1, which is the primary activator of cellulase and hemicellulase gene expression. Its constitutive overexpression has been shown to boost overall enzyme production and partially alleviate CCR. Further refinement of this approach involved the introduction of specific point mutations, such as V821F and A824V, into the XYR1 protein, which appear to confer a "glucose-blind" phenotype, allowing for robust enzyme expression even in the presence of repressing sugars like glucose. Another key target is the repressor ACE1; silencing or deleting this gene has been demonstrated to synergistically enhance the effects of XYR1 overexpression. Other positive regulators include ACE2, ACE3, the HAP2/3/5 complex, and the putative methyltransferase LAE1, all of which have been manipulated to varying degrees of success.

In parallel, strategies have been developed to improve the quality and stability of the secreted enzyme cocktail. A major deficiency of native *T. reesei* secretomes is their low β-glucosidase activity, which leads to the accumulation of the inhibitory disaccharide cellobiose during cellulose hydrolysis. To address this, researchers have introduced heterologous β-glucosidase genes, such as *cel3a* from *Talaromyces emersonii*, into the fungus. Additionally, the proteolytic degradation of secreted enzymes by the host's own proteases has been identified as a limiting factor. The deletion of genes encoding major extracellular proteases, such as the subtilisin SLP1 and the aspartic protease PEP1, has been shown to increase the net yield of stable, active enzyme.

A critical economic barrier to large-scale enzyme production is the cost of the culture medium, with the carbon source alone accounting for up to 60% of total expenses. Consequently, there has been a strong drive to utilize inexpensive, non-food agroindustrial residues. Sugarcane molasses, a by-product of sugar refining, is an attractive candidate due to its high concentration of sugars (primarily sucrose, glucose, and fructose), vitamins, and minerals. However, a fundamental limitation of *T. reesei* is its inability to metabolize sucrose, as it lacks the extracellular invertase enzyme necessary to cleave sucrose into its constituent monosaccharides. This deficiency has been overcome in previous studies by engineering the fungus to express the *suc1* invertase gene from *Aspergillus niger*.

Despite these individual advances, the combinatorial effect of multiple beneficial genetic modifications in a single strain has been rarely explored, largely due to the historical technical difficulties of performing sequential genetic engineering in *T. reesei*. These challenges included a scarcity of selectable markers and low transformation efficiencies. The advent of CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing technology has revolutionized this field, enabling precise, markerless, and efficient integration of multiple genetic changes. While CRISPR/Cas9 has been successfully applied to *T. reesei*, its use for the systematic, rational combination of known beneficial traits to create a publicly available, high-performance production platform has not been fully realized. Furthermore, the development of a simple, industrially compatible fermentation process that leverages the full potential of such a rationally engineered strain, using a low-cost carbon source like molasses, represents a significant unmet need in the art. The prior art lacks a comprehensive solution that integrates advanced genetic engineering with a practical, low-cost bioprocess to achieve both high enzyme titers and a functionally superior enzyme cocktail from a publicly accessible strain.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel, rationally engineered strain of *Trichoderma reesei* and a corresponding, cost-effective fermentation process for the hyperproduction of a functionally enhanced cellulase and hemicellulase enzyme cocktail. The invention overcomes the limitations of the prior art by combining a minimal set of six specific, well-characterized genetic modifications within the publicly available RUT-C30 strain background, using a highly efficient CRISPR/Cas9-based genome editing system. This engineered strain, designated herein as BR_TrR03, exhibits a suite of advantageous phenotypes: it is capable of utilizing sucrose as a carbon source, it displays a near-complete relief from carbon catabolite repression by glucose, it produces a secretome with dramatically elevated levels of β-glucosidase and other key enzymatic activities, and it achieves exceptionally high rates of protein secretion.

The core of the invention lies in the specific combination of genetic alterations. First, the gene encoding the β-glucosidase Cel3a from *Talaromyces emersonii* is inserted under the control of the strong, inducible xylanase 1 (*xyn1*) promoter into the locus of the *slp1* gene, which encodes a major extracellular subtilisin protease. This dual modification simultaneously enhances the functional composition of the enzyme cocktail by addressing the critical β-glucosidase deficiency and reduces the proteolytic degradation of secreted proteins by eliminating SLP1. Second, the native *ace1* gene, a key repressor of cellulase expression, is replaced with a cassette for the constitutive expression of a mutated allele of the master transcriptional activator *xyr1* (specifically, the *xyr1-V821F* allele) under the control of the pyruvate decarboxylase 1 (*pdc1*) promoter. This modification fundamentally reprograms the strain’s regulatory network, leading to a massive, constitutive upregulation of the entire cellulase and hemicellulase gene battery and a profound insensitivity to glucose-mediated repression. Third, the gene encoding the invertase Suc1 from *Aspergillus niger*, along with its native regulatory sequences, is inserted into the locus of the *pep1* gene, which encodes the most abundant aspartic protease in the *T. reesei* secretome. This final modification grants the strain the ability to hydrolyze and utilize sucrose, thereby enabling the use of inexpensive, sucrose-rich feedstocks like sugarcane molasses, while concurrently providing a second layer of protease reduction by eliminating PEP1.

In conjunction with the engineered strain, the invention provides a novel, industrially compatible fermentation process. This process utilizes a simple, low-cost medium based on sugarcane molasses as the primary carbon source and a novel organic nitrogen source comprising whole yeast cells that have been pre-grown on the same molasses. This integrated approach eliminates the need for expensive, purified components like yeast extract or peptone, and crucially, removes the requirement for costly inducing sugars such as lactose or microcrystalline cellulose. Using this strain and process, the invention achieves an unprecedented extracellular protein titer of 80.6 g L⁻¹ with a production rate of 0.24 g L⁻¹ h⁻¹, which is the highest experimentally supported titer reported for any publicly available *T. reesei* strain. The resulting enzyme cocktail demonstrates superior saccharification efficiency on industrially pretreated lignocellulosic biomass, performing on par with a leading commercial preparation (CTec2) and effectively preventing the accumulation of the inhibitory intermediate cellobiose. Thus, the invention provides a complete, reproducible, and economically viable solution for the production of high-performance cellulase enzymes from a renewable, non-food feedstock.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a genetically modified microorganism, specifically a strain of the filamentous fungus *Trichoderma reesei*, and a method for producing a cellulase and hemicellulase enzyme cocktail using said microorganism in a fermentation process with a low-cost, agroindustrial carbon source. The invention is predicated on the rational, combinatorial engineering of a well-characterized parental strain to integrate a specific set of genetic modifications that collectively address the major bottlenecks in industrial enzyme production: low titer, poor cocktail composition, carbon catabolite repression, and high substrate cost.

The parental strain for the invention is *Trichoderma reesei* RUT-C30 (or its equivalent from recognized strain collections such as IHEM_5652 or ATCC_56765). This strain serves as a robust starting point due to its partial relief from carbon catabolite repression, conferred by its naturally truncated *cre1* gene, and its status as a publicly available, well-studied hyperproducer. The genetic modifications are introduced into this strain in a stepwise, iterative manner using a highly efficient, single-plasmid CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing system. This system comprises a plasmid vector that includes a *Streptococcus pyogenes* Cas9 gene codon-optimized for expression in *T. reesei*, a ribozyme-flanked guide RNA (gRNA) cassette for targeting a specific genomic locus, a selectable marker (e.g., a hygromycin resistance gene, *hph*), and the AMA1 fungal replicator sequence to facilitate episomal maintenance and high transformation efficiency. The use of this system allows for precise, markerless integration of donor DNA cassettes, enabling the sequential stacking of multiple genetic changes without the depletion of selectable markers.

The first embodiment of the invention involves a first round of genetic engineering. In this step, a donor DNA cassette is constructed and integrated into the genome of the RUT-C30 strain at the locus of the *slp1* gene, which encodes a major extracellular subtilisin-like protease. The donor cassette is designed to simultaneously delete the *slp1* open reading frame and insert a heterologous gene encoding a β-glucosidase. Specifically, the β-glucosidase gene is the *cel3a* gene from the thermophilic fungus *Talaromyces emersonii*. This gene is placed under the transcriptional control of the *T. reesei* xylanase 1 (*xyn1*) promoter, which is a strong, inducible promoter known to be highly active during growth on cellulose and hemicellulose. The integration of this cassette results in a strain, designated herein as BR_TrR01, that is deficient in the SLP1 protease and constitutively expresses the *T. emersonii* Cel3a β-glucosidase in its secretome whenever the *xyn1* promoter is active. This strain exhibits a significant, several-fold increase in specific β-glucosidase activity compared to the parental RUT-C30, effectively addressing the long-standing deficiency in the native *T. reesei* enzyme cocktail.

The second embodiment of the invention involves a second round of genetic engineering performed on the BR_TrR01 strain. In this step, a second donor DNA cassette is constructed and integrated into the genome at the locus of the *ace1* gene, which encodes a key transcriptional repressor of cellulase and hemicellulase genes. The donor cassette is designed to delete the *ace1* open reading frame and replace it with a cassette for the constitutive expression of a mutated allele of the master transcriptional activator *xyr1*. The specific mutation is a valine-to-phenylalanine substitution at amino acid position 821 (V821F) of the XYR1 protein. This mutated *xyr1-V821F* allele is placed under the control of the *T. reesei* pyruvate decarboxylase 1 (*pdc1*) promoter, which is a strong, constitutive promoter that is active on a wide range of carbon sources, including glucose. The integration of this cassette results in a strain, designated herein as BR_TrR02, that is deficient in the ACE1 repressor and constitutively expresses the "glucose-blind" XYR1-V821F activator. This dual modification has a profound effect: it leads to a massive, constitutive upregulation of the entire suite of cellulase and hemicellulase genes, and it renders the strain largely insensitive to carbon catabolite repression by glucose. Consequently, the BR_TrR02 strain exhibits a dramatic, multi-fold increase in extracellular protein titer when grown on either inducing sugars like lactose or repressing sugars like glucose, and it shows a further significant enhancement in its specific β-glucosidase activity due to the global upregulation of CAZyme genes by the hyperactive XYR1.

The third and final embodiment of the invention involves a third round of genetic engineering performed on the BR_TrR02 strain. In this step, a third donor DNA cassette is constructed and integrated into the genome at the locus of the *pep1* gene, which encodes the most abundant aspartic protease in the *T. reesei* secretome. The donor cassette is designed to delete the *pep1* open reading frame and insert a heterologous gene encoding an invertase. Specifically, the invertase gene is the *suc1* gene from the filamentous fungus *Aspergillus niger*. This gene is inserted along with its native upstream and downstream regulatory sequences to ensure its proper expression and function. The integration of this cassette results in the final engineered strain, designated herein as BR_TrR03, which is deficient in both the SLP1 and PEP1 major extracellular proteases and is capable of expressing a functional invertase. The expression of the *A. niger* Suc1 invertase enables the BR_TrR03 strain to hydrolyze sucrose into glucose and fructose, thereby granting it the ability to utilize sucrose as a sole carbon and energy source for growth and enzyme production. This capability is a critical feature of the invention, as it unlocks the use of low-cost, sucrose-rich agroindustrial by-products, such as sugarcane molasses, for industrial fermentation.

In a further embodiment, the invention provides a method for producing a cellulase and hemicellulase enzyme cocktail using the BR_TrR03 strain. This method is a fermentation process conducted in a bioreactor under fed-batch mode. The fermentation medium is a simple, low-cost formulation that comprises two primary components: a carbon source and a nitrogen source. The carbon source is sugarcane molasses, a readily available, inexpensive by-product of the sugar industry that is rich in sucrose, glucose, and fructose. The nitrogen source is a novel, integrated component consisting of whole yeast cells of *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* that have been pre-grown on a medium containing the same sugarcane molasses. This "molasses-grown yeast" (MG-yeast) serves as a source of organic nitrogen, vitamins, and other micronutrients, and its use eliminates the need for expensive, commercially processed nitrogen sources like yeast extract or peptone. The fermentation process is initiated with a batch phase using a medium containing a defined amount of molasses-derived sugars and the MG-yeast, followed by a prolonged feeding phase where additional molasses is continuously or intermittently added to the bioreactor to sustain high-level enzyme production. This process is conducted under controlled conditions of pH, temperature, and dissolved oxygen, which are standard for fungal fermentations. The remarkable feature of this process is that it does not require any expensive inducing sugars; the constitutive nature of the genetic modifications in BR_TrR03, combined with the nutritional profile of the molasses/MG-yeast medium, is sufficient to drive hyperproduction of the enzyme cocktail. The fermentation can be extended for a period of up to 14 days (336 hours) to achieve a final extracellular protein titer of at least 80 g L⁻¹, with a volumetric productivity of at least 0.24 g L⁻¹ h⁻¹.

The enzyme cocktail produced by the BR_TrR03 strain via this process is itself a further embodiment of the invention. This cocktail is characterized by its high total protein concentration, its balanced and enhanced specific enzymatic activities, and its superior functional performance. It contains dramatically elevated levels of β-glucosidase activity, which prevents the accumulation of the inhibitory disaccharide cellobiose during biomass hydrolysis. It also exhibits very high levels of xylanase and other hemicellulolytic activities, as well as robust cellulolytic activities (endoglucanase, cellobiohydrolase, and filter paper activity). The cocktail is effective for the saccharification of various pretreated lignocellulosic biomasses, such as sugarcane straw, at high solids loadings, and its performance is comparable to that of leading commercial enzyme preparations. The cocktail may be used in its crude form as a whole fermentation broth, which is a significant economic advantage as it avoids the costs associated with downstream processing, purification, and storage.

### Examples

The following examples are provided to illustrate the invention in greater detail and are not intended to limit the scope of the claims in any way.

**Example 1: Construction of the CRISPR/Cas9 System and Donor Cassettes**

A single-plasmid CRISPR/Cas9 vector, pTrCas9gRNA1, was assembled for use in *T. reesei*. The plasmid backbone was derived from pUC19. The vector contained a *Streptococcus pyogenes* Cas9 gene codon-optimized for *T. reesei* and flanked by the *pdc1* promoter and terminator. The gRNA expression cassette was placed under the control of the *eno1* promoter and the *xyn1* terminator, with the gRNA sequence itself flanked by hammerhead (HH) and hepatitis delta virus (HDV) ribozymes for precise processing. The plasmid also carried the *hph* gene for hygromycin resistance and the AMA1 fungal replicator for high-copy episomal maintenance. Three specific gRNA sequences were designed to target the *slp1*, *ace1*, and *pep1* loci, respectively, and were cloned into the Bsp1407I site of pTrCas9gRNA1 to generate the final targeting vectors.

Three markerless donor cassettes were constructed for homologous recombination. The first cassette, for the *slp1* locus, comprised 1 kb of upstream and downstream homology arms flanking a fusion of the *xyn1* promoter, the *T. emersonii cel3a* coding sequence, and the *xyn1* terminator. The second cassette, for the *ace1* locus, comprised 1 kb homology arms flanking a fusion of the *pdc1* promoter, the *T. reesei xyr1-V821F* coding sequence, and the *pdc1* terminator. The third cassette, for the *pep1* locus, comprised 1 kb homology arms flanking the *A. niger suc1* gene with its native 1.5 kb upstream and 1.0 kb downstream regulatory regions. These cassettes were assembled in *S. cerevisiae* via in vivo homologous recombination and then PCR-amplified to generate linear DNA fragments for fungal transformation.

**Example 2: Generation and Characterization of Engineered Strains**

The RUT-C30 strain was transformed sequentially with the CRISPR/Cas9 vectors and the corresponding linear donor cassettes. Protoplasts were generated and transformed using standard protocols. Transformants were selected on hygromycin-containing minimal media. Correct integration events were verified by PCR and Sanger sequencing across the modified loci. The CRISPR/Cas9 plasmid was subsequently lost by streaking on non-selective media, yielding marker-free, homokaryotic strains: BR_TrR01 (*slp1*::Pxyn1-cel3a), BR_TrR02 (*slp1*::Pxyn1-cel3a; *ace1*::Ppdc1-xyr1-V821F), and BR_TrR03 (*slp1*::Pxyn1-cel3a; *ace1*::Ppdc1-xyr1-V821F; *pep1*::AnSuc1).

Shake flask cultivations were performed in a medium containing 50 g L⁻¹ of lactose, glucose, or sucrose. The parental RUT-C30 and BR_TrR01 and BR_TrR02 strains were unable to grow on sucrose and produced negligible protein. In contrast, BR_TrR03 grew robustly on sucrose and produced protein titers comparable to those on lactose. On lactose and glucose, BR_TrR02 and BR_TrR03 showed a threefold increase in protein titer over BR_TrR01, and a 26-fold increase in specific β-glucosidase activity over the parental strain.

**Example 3: Bioreactor Fermentation and Process Development**

Fed-batch bioreactor fermentations were conducted. In an initial test with an inducer-rich medium (lactose, Avicel, yeast extract), BR_TrR03 produced 34.9 g L⁻¹ of protein in 168 h, a threefold increase over RUT-C30. Subsequently, a molasses-based process was developed. A fermentation with molasses and ammonium sulfate alone yielded 25.0 g L⁻¹. The addition of yeast extract to the batch medium dramatically increased the titer to 46.8 g L⁻¹. To reduce costs, yeast extract was replaced with whole yeast cells. YEPD-grown yeast yielded 41.7 g L⁻¹, and molasses-grown yeast (MG-yeast) yielded 42.9 g L⁻¹ in 168 h. Extending the MG-yeast fermentation to 336 h resulted in a final titer of 80.6 g L⁻¹ with a productivity of 0.24 g L⁻¹ h⁻¹. A control fermentation with BR_TrR02 (which lacks invertase) on acid-inverted molasses and MG-yeast yielded only 59.2 g L⁻¹, confirming the critical role of the *suc1* gene for achieving maximum productivity on raw molasses.

**Example 4: Enzyme Cocktail Characterization and Saccharification Performance**

Enzyme cocktails from the bioreactor runs were analyzed. The BR_TrR03 cocktail produced on the molasses/MG-yeast medium (MMGY) showed a 72-fold increase in specific β-glucosidase activity and a 42-fold increase in xylanase activity compared to the RUT-C30 cocktail from the inducer-rich medium. Saccharification assays on pretreated sugarcane straw at 20% solids loading showed that the BR_TrR03-MMGY cocktail released significantly more glucose and total sugars than the RUT-C30 cocktail and did not accumulate cellobiose. Its performance was equivalent to the commercial CTec2 cocktail. The whole broth from the BR_TrR03-MMGY fermentation was equally effective as the clarified supernatant, demonstrating the viability of an on-site manufacturing model without downstream processing.
