# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of quantum computing and, more specifically, to systems and methods for implementing quantum-assisted machine learning (QAML) using tensor network architectures on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices. The invention provides a complete workflow for generative modeling tasks that integrates classical data preprocessing, quantum state embedding, classical training of matrix product state (MPS) models, and hardware-aware compilation of trained models into executable quantum circuits optimized for specific NISQ hardware constraints including limited qubit connectivity, restricted gate sets, and hardware noise characteristics. The invention particularly addresses the challenge of efficiently mapping abstract tensor network representations into physical quantum operations while minimizing resource requirements and maximizing fidelity on current and near-term quantum hardware platforms.

## BACKGROUND

Quantum computing has emerged as a promising computational paradigm with potential applications across various domains including cryptography, materials science, and machine learning. However, current quantum computing platforms remain constrained by significant technical limitations that prevent the realization of large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computation. These limitations include hardware noise, limited qubit coherence times, restricted qubit connectivity, and constrained native gate sets. The current era of quantum computing is characterized as the noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) era, where devices typically contain between a few to several dozen qubits but lack the error correction capabilities necessary for executing complex quantum algorithms reliably.

Machine learning has been identified as a particularly promising application area for NISQ devices due to several inherent characteristics of machine learning algorithms that align well with NISQ constraints. Machine learning algorithms are generally robust to noise, can be designed to be highly resource-efficient in terms of qubit requirements, and can leverage the exponential expressivity of quantum models with respect to circuit depth. Quantum-assisted machine learning (QAML) represents a hybrid approach where quantum circuits are used to process data or compute features that may not be efficiently simulable classically, while classical optimization techniques are employed to train the quantum model parameters based on measurement outcomes.

Tensor networks (TNs) have emerged as a powerful mathematical framework for designing QAML algorithms that are both quantum-resource efficient and amenable to classical simulation and optimization during the development phase. Among various tensor network topologies, matrix product states (MPSs) are particularly attractive for QAML applications due to their maturity, well-understood optimization strategies, and ability to be implemented using sequential preparation schemes that require only a constant number of physical qubits independent of the input data dimension. This property makes MPS-based QAML models maximally quantum resource-efficient, requiring only O(1) physical qubits for processing classical data vectors of arbitrary length N, plus O(log₂χ) ancilla qubits where χ represents the bond dimension that encapsulates the model's expressivity.

Despite these advantages, significant challenges remain in translating theoretically optimal MPS models into practical implementations on real NISQ hardware. Traditional quantum compilation approaches, which are designed for general quantum circuits, often produce suboptimal results when applied to tensor network models due to their failure to exploit the unique structural properties and inherent freedoms present in tensor network representations. Specifically, tensor networks possess gauge freedoms that allow for equivalent representations of the same quantum state through different tensor configurations, and the ancilla degrees of freedom in sequential preparation schemes are never directly measured, providing additional flexibility in their representation. Furthermore, the isometric operations derived from tensor network models do not require exact compilation since they result from classical optimization procedures with finite tolerance, making overly precise compilation potentially counterproductive on noisy hardware where additional gates introduce more errors.

Previous approaches to implementing QAML on quantum hardware have largely treated the compilation stage as a black-box process, applying generic quantum compilation techniques without considering the specific structural properties of tensor network models. This has resulted in compiled circuits that are unnecessarily deep, contain excessive numbers of entangling gates, and fail to leverage the inherent freedoms available in tensor network representations. Additionally, most prior demonstrations have focused on supervised learning tasks or small-scale proof-of-concept implementations that do not address the practical challenges of deploying QAML models on real hardware with realistic noise characteristics and connectivity constraints.

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a comprehensive framework that integrates tensor network theory with hardware-aware compilation techniques specifically designed for QAML applications. The invention introduces novel compilation strategies that exploit the gauge freedoms inherent in tensor network representations to produce more diagonal and thus more efficiently compilable operations. It also provides greedy compilation heuristics that prioritize shallow circuit depths and minimal entangling gate counts while maintaining fidelity within the tolerance bounds established by the classical training procedure. These innovations enable the robust design and deployment of QAML models on current NISQ hardware while providing a pathway toward scaling to classically intractable regimes.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention provides a complete workflow for implementing quantum-assisted machine learning (QAML) using matrix product state (MPS) tensor network architectures on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices. The invention encompasses methods for classical data preprocessing, quantum state embedding, classical training of MPS models, and hardware-aware compilation of trained models into executable quantum circuits optimized for specific NISQ hardware constraints.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for converting classical data into quantum states through an embedding process that maps each classical data vector to an unentangled product state in a quantum Hilbert space. This embedding ensures that any entanglement present in the final quantum model arises solely from correlations in the data ensemble rather than from a priori assumptions about individual data vectors, while also enabling highly resource-efficient sequential preparation schemes.

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for training generative MPS models as Born machines using classical optimization techniques. The trained MPS model represents a quantum wave function whose squared amplitudes correspond to the probability distribution over classical data vectors according to Born's rule. The optimization procedure employs gradient descent techniques inspired by density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) algorithms to minimize the negative log-likelihood of the training data with respect to the MPS tensor parameters.

In a further aspect, the invention provides methods for converting the classically trained MPS model into a sequential preparation scheme comprising isometric operations acting on a single physical (readout) qubit coupled to ancillary quantum resources. This conversion leverages the left-canonical form of the MPS to define isometries that can be applied sequentially to generate data samples, requiring only a constant number of physical qubits independent of the input data dimension.

In yet another aspect, the invention introduces a diagonal gauge transformation that exploits the unitary freedom remaining in the left-canonical form of MPS models to optimize the representation for hardware compilation. This transformation permutes the basis states of the ancillary degrees of freedom to make the isometric operations as diagonal as possible, thereby reducing the complexity of the compiled quantum circuits.

In still another aspect, the invention provides greedy compilation heuristics specifically designed for compiling tensor network isometries into native quantum operations for target hardware architectures. These heuristics construct a search tree of potential circuit structures with continuous parameters, evaluate them against a cost function that measures fidelity to the target isometry within a specified tolerance, and prioritize solutions that minimize entangling gate counts while maintaining acceptable fidelity.

The invention also provides methods for handling cases where the bond dimension χ is not a power of two, requiring isometric completion to map the operations onto discrete qubit systems. Additionally, the invention includes techniques for filtering numerical noise from classically optimized MPS models before compilation, ensuring that compilation efforts are focused on meaningful model structure rather than optimization artifacts.

The complete workflow enables robust design and performance assessment of QAML models on NISQ devices in regimes where classical simulations are still possible, while also providing preconditioned models that can accelerate optimization when scaling to classically intractable regimes. The invention has been validated through implementation on real quantum hardware and hardware simulators, demonstrating improved performance compared to hand-compiled circuits and generic compilation approaches.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive framework for implementing quantum-assisted machine learning (QAML) using matrix product state (MPS) tensor network architectures on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices. The framework addresses the complete pipeline from classical data preprocessing through quantum circuit execution, with particular emphasis on hardware-aware compilation techniques that optimize performance on real quantum hardware subject to noise, connectivity, and gate set constraints.

The invention begins with the preprocessing of classical data, which consists of a collection of classical data vectors organized into a training set T = {xⱼ}ᴺᵀⱼ₌₁, where each element xⱼ is an N-dimensional vector. The first step in the QAML workflow is to define a mapping that embeds these classical data vectors into quantum states in a Hilbert space. In the preferred embodiment, this embedding maps each classical data vector to an unentangled product state, which provides several advantages. Unentangled states are simpler to prepare experimentally with high fidelity, enable the use of qubit-efficient sequential preparation schemes, and ensure that any entanglement present in the final quantum model arises solely from correlations in the data ensemble rather than from predetermined assumptions about individual data vectors.

For discrete binary data where each element xᵢ ∈ {0, 1}, the embedding is performed by mapping each classical bit to a corresponding qubit state |xᵢ⟩, such that the full N-dimensional classical data vector is embedded into a register of N qubits as the product state |x⟩ = ⊗ᵢ₌₀ᴺ⁻¹|xᵢ⟩. While embeddings for continuous variable data are also possible, the binary embedding is utilized in the preferred embodiments described herein due to its simplicity and compatibility with current quantum hardware architectures.

Following the quantum embedding step, the invention proceeds to learn a quantum model for the collection of embedded quantum states {|xⱼ⟩}ᴺᵀⱼ₌₁. The quantum model is defined as a collection of operations applied to quantum resources to produce a state that encodes the statistical properties of the training data ensemble. In the preferred embodiment, this quantum model takes the form of a tensor network (TN), specifically a matrix product state (MPS). Tensor networks represent high-rank tensors describing quantum wave functions as contractions over low-rank tensors, with the computational power of the approximation controlled by the maximum dimension χ of any contracted index, known as the bond dimension.

MPSs are particularly advantageous for QAML applications due to several key properties. First, they represent the most mature and well-understood tensor network topology, benefiting from decades of research in quantum many-body physics that has produced robust optimization strategies. Second, MPSs are highly quantum resource-efficient because their associated wave functions can be prepared sequentially, allowing qubits to be reused during deployment on quantum hardware. Third, every state that can be sequentially prepared can be written as an MPS, meaning that any QAML model achieving optimal O(1) scaling of qubit requirements with data vector length N using sequential preparation can be expressed as an MPS.

The invention specializes to generative modeling tasks, where the quantum model is implemented as a Born machine. In this approach, the collection of quantum data vectors is encoded into a wave function |ψ⟩ such that the probability distribution evaluated at data vector x is given by P(x) = |⟨x|ψ⟩|²/Z, where Z = ⟨ψ|ψ⟩ is a normalization factor. This corresponds directly to Born's rule for quantum measurement outcomes, hence the name "Born machine." The state |ψ⟩, or equivalently the process of generating |ψ⟩ from a known fiducial state, constitutes the quantum machine learning model.

The Born machine is parameterized as an MPS with the wave function expressed as |ψ⟩ = Σⱼ₀...ⱼₙ₋₁ Tr(Aⱼ₀[⁰]Aⱼ₁[¹]...Aⱼₙ₋₁[ᴺ⁻¹])|j₀...jₙ₋₁⟩, where Aⱼᵢ[ⁱ] are the MPS tensors. Using the binary encoding of data, the probability distribution becomes P(x) = |Tr(Aₓ₀[⁰]Aₓ₁[¹]...Aₓₙ₋₁[ᴺ⁻¹])|²/Z, with the normalization factor Z = Σₓ|Tr(Aₓ₀[⁰]Aₓ₁[¹]...Aₓₙ₋₁[ᴺ⁻¹])|².

The optimization of the Born machine is performed using a DMRG-style procedure with gradient descent. The objective function is the average negative log-likelihood of the training data, L(T) = -(1/Nᵀ)Σⱼ₌₁ᴺᵀ log P(xⱼ). The gradient is computed with respect to groups of s neighboring MPS tensors (typically s = 1 or 2), and the tensors are updated according to A → A + η∇L(T), where η is the learning rate. For the single-site algorithm (s = 1), this update preserves the bond dimension and canonical form of the MPS. For the two-site algorithm (s = 2), the updated tensor can be split using singular value decomposition (SVD), allowing the bond dimension to increase adaptively based on the data complexity. The optimization proceeds through complete sweeps over the MPS until convergence is achieved, as indicated by minimal further reduction in the negative log-likelihood.

Following classical optimization, the trained MPS model is converted into a sequential preparation scheme suitable for implementation on quantum hardware. This conversion leverages the left-canonical form of the MPS, where each MPS tensor L[ⁱ]ⱼᵢ satisfies the isometry condition Σⱼᵢ L[ⁱ]ⱼᵢ†L[ⁱ]ⱼᵢ = I. In this form, the sequential preparation procedure takes the form |ψ⟩ = L[⁰]L[¹]...L[ᴺ⁻¹]|0...0⟩, where each L[ⁱ] is an isometric operator acting on the physical qubit and ancillary resources. The sequential nature of this preparation enables highly resource-efficient implementation requiring only a single physical (readout) qubit together with log₂χ ancilla qubits, where χ is the bond dimension.

The sequential preparation scheme operates by initializing both the physical qubit and ancilla in the |0⟩ state, applying the first isometry L[ᴺ⁻¹], measuring the physical qubit in the computational basis to obtain the first data sample element xₙ₋₁, reinitializing the physical qubit to |0⟩ while leaving the ancilla unmeasured, and repeating this process with subsequent isometries to generate the remaining data elements. This procedure requires mid-circuit measurement and reset capabilities, which are available on certain quantum hardware platforms such as trapped ion systems.

A critical innovation of the present invention lies in the compilation stage, where the formal isometries derived from the MPS model are translated into executable quantum operations for target hardware architectures. Traditional quantum compilation approaches are suboptimal for tensor network models because they fail to exploit the unique structural properties and inherent freedoms present in these representations. The invention addresses this limitation through two key innovations: diagonal gauge transformation and greedy compilation heuristics.

The diagonal gauge transformation exploits the unitary freedom remaining in the left-canonical form of MPS models. While the left-canonical form ensures that each MPS tensor satisfies the isometry constraint, it still allows for the insertion of unitary matrices and their inverses on the bond bases without changing the overall quantum state or violating the isometry conditions. This freedom arises because the bond degrees of freedom mediate correlations between physical degrees of freedom but are never directly measured, leaving their basis representation unconstrained.

The invention provides a systematic procedure to exploit this freedom by defining a matrix of overlaps M[ⁱ] = Σⱼᵢ L[ⁱ]ⱼᵢ†L[ⁱ]ⱼᵢ that integrates out the physical qubit and acts only in the ancilla space. The goal is to make M[ⁱ] as diagonal as possible, which corresponds to preserving individual ancilla basis states and reducing the number of quantum operations required. Rather than using the full polar decomposition which can destroy sparsity, the invention employs a permutation-based approach that uses the unitary component from the polar decomposition to define a permutation of ancilla basis states. This operation preserves sparsity while increasing diagonal dominance.

The transformation is applied through right-moving and left-moving updates that permute adjacent bond bases, analogous to the mixed canonical form transformation in standard MPS algorithms. When applied to all tensors, the MPS is said to be in the diagonal gauge, which enforces the isometries for state preparation to be as diagonal as possible according to the specified cost function. The location of the diagonality center (analogous to the orthogonality center in mixed canonical form) can be optimized, but the invention provides a practical choice of setting it to an isometry that is initially an identity matrix, which can always be introduced by padding classical data vectors with zeros. This choice ensures that the permutation transforms the identity isometry into a permutation matrix, which is typically easier to compile with high fidelity than general nonsparse isometries.

Following the diagonal gauge transformation, the invention provides greedy compilation heuristics specifically designed for tensor network isometries. These heuristics address the unique requirements of QAML compilation, including the need to minimize entangling gate counts (due to their higher error rates compared to single-qubit gates), handle cases where the bond dimension χ is not a power of two, and maintain fidelity only within the tolerance established by the classical optimization procedure.

The greedy compilation procedure constructs a search tree of potential circuit structures, where the root node comprises single-qubit gates for each qubit, and child nodes correspond to adding entangling gates in allowed positions followed by additional single-qubit gates. The search is guided by a cost function that measures the difference between the target isometry L and candidate unitary Û over only the elements where |Lᵢ,ⱼ| > δ for some tolerance δ. This selective evaluation reduces computational complexity while maintaining accuracy.

The optimization begins by optimizing the root node parameters and checking if the cost function drops below a specified tolerance ε. If not, a queue of gates is formed by adding entangling gates and single-qubit rotations in all allowed positions, these are optimized, and their cost functions recorded. If no acceptable gate is found, a priority queue is formed by sorting gates according to their cost functions and appending additional entangling gates and rotations. To control exponential growth, the number of gates forming the starting point of the priority queue is limited to a fixed number that can vary between optimization cycles, typically allowing more gates in early cycles when circuits are shallower and optimization is faster.

The invention also incorporates problem-specific optimizations, including the use of real-valued gates when the quantum embedding produces real-valued isometries (as with binary data), and the introduction of longer gate sequence motifs such as two-qubit rotation gates Ŝ(θ, θ') and three-qubit controlled versions F that can be compiled into multiple CNOTs but enable more rapid convergence. Penalty functions can be applied to account for the different CNOT costs of these motifs when ordering the priority queue.

Additional preprocessing steps include cleaning the isometries from classical optimization noise by applying MPS compression techniques that remove elements below a specified tolerance level ε. This is accomplished through local SVD compression, where MPS tensors are decomposed and truncated to keep only the largest singular values or those above a cutoff threshold. This filtering ensures that compilation efforts focus on meaningful model structure rather than numerical artifacts from the optimization process.

The complete workflow has been validated through implementation on both real quantum hardware and hardware simulators. For exactly solvable benchmark models requiring only two qubits (one physical, one ancilla), the invention demonstrates superior performance compared to hand-compiled circuits, achieving lower Kullback-Leibler divergence between ideal and experimental probability distributions on IBM quantum devices. The automatically compiled circuits are generally shallower than hand-compiled versions, resulting in better fidelity despite being substantially different in structure.

For more complex applications using features extracted from the MNIST handwritten digit dataset, the invention demonstrates the ability to train and compile MPS models with bond dimension χ = 8 (requiring three ancilla qubits) that successfully capture the essential features of the training data. Hardware simulator studies with parametric noise models show that the compiled models maintain reasonable fidelity even under realistic depolarizing and readout noise conditions characteristic of current NISQ devices.

The invention provides several advantages over prior approaches. First, it exploits the inherent freedoms in tensor network representations to produce more efficiently compilable models. Second, it provides compilation heuristics specifically designed for the unique requirements of QAML applications, prioritizing shallow circuits and minimal entangling gate counts while maintaining sufficient fidelity. Third, it handles the practical challenges of real hardware implementation, including non-power-of-two bond dimensions, hardware connectivity constraints, and noise characteristics. Fourth, it enables detailed performance assessment in the classically simulable regime while providing a pathway toward scaling to classically intractable regimes through the use of classically trained models as preconditioners for quantum optimization.

The framework is extensible to other tensor network topologies beyond MPS, such as tree tensor networks, and to other QAML applications beyond generative modeling, including feature extraction and classification tasks. The compilation techniques can be adapted to different hardware architectures with varying connectivity patterns, gate sets, and noise characteristics by modifying the allowed entangling gate positions and cost function parameters accordingly.

In summary, the present invention provides a complete, hardware-aware framework for implementing QAML using tensor network architectures on NISQ devices. By integrating classical data preprocessing, quantum state embedding, classical MPS training, diagonal gauge optimization, and greedy compilation heuristics, the invention enables robust deployment of QAML models on current quantum hardware while providing a foundation for scaling to more complex applications in the future.
