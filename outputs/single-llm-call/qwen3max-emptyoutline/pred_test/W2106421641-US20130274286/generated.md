# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and molecular therapeutics, specifically to the identification and use of novel small-molecule inhibitors of nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase), also known as visfatin or Pre-B-cell colony-enhancing factor 1 (PBEF1). More particularly, the invention provides a new chemical entity—designated herein as Compound 5—that exhibits potent inhibitory activity against NMPRTase and demonstrates selective anti-proliferative effects against glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) cells that overexpress NMPRTase. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising this compound, methods for treating NMPRTase-dependent cancers—especially high-grade gliomas—by administering said compound, and diagnostic or prognostic uses based on NMPRTase expression levels in tumor tissues. The discovery stems from a rational drug design approach integrating high-throughput virtual screening using three-dimensional structural data of human NMPRTase with experimental validation in enzymatic and cellular assays. This work establishes Compound 5 as a structurally distinct lead candidate relative to the known inhibitor FK866, offering a novel scaffold for the development of next-generation therapeutics targeting the NAD+ salvage pathway in cancer.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION:

Gliomas represent the most common class of primary malignant brain tumors in adults, accounting for approximately 80% of all such neoplasms. Among these, glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), classified as a Grade IV astrocytoma by the World Health Organization, is the most aggressive and lethal form, characterized by rapid growth, diffuse infiltration into surrounding brain parenchyma, resistance to conventional therapies, and a dismal median survival of less than one year despite maximal surgical resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy. The poor prognosis associated with GBM underscores the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies that target the unique molecular and metabolic vulnerabilities of these tumors. Over the past two decades, extensive genomic and proteomic analyses have revealed a constellation of recurrent molecular aberrations in gliomas, including amplification and mutation of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), loss-of-function mutations in the tumor suppressor gene PTEN (phosphatase and tensin homolog), dysregulation of the platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha (PDGFRA) signaling axis, and alterations in cell cycle regulators such as cyclin-dependent kinase 4 (CDK4) and the retinoblastoma protein (RB1). These genetic lesions converge to drive uncontrolled proliferation, evasion of apoptosis, and enhanced metabolic reprogramming—hallmarks that define the malignant phenotype of high-grade gliomas.

One critical aspect of this metabolic reprogramming involves the upregulation of pathways responsible for maintaining intracellular pools of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), a vital coenzyme central to numerous cellular processes beyond its classical role in redox reactions. NAD+ serves as an essential substrate for several classes of enzymes involved in DNA repair (e.g., poly(ADP-ribose) polymerases or PARPs), gene expression regulation (e.g., sirtuins, which are NAD+-dependent deacetylases), calcium signaling (e.g., ADP-ribosyl cyclases), and energy metabolism. In rapidly dividing cancer cells, the demand for NAD+ is significantly elevated due to heightened rates of DNA replication, repair, and transcriptional activity. Consequently, tumors often become dependent on efficient NAD+ biosynthesis to sustain their proliferative capacity and resist genotoxic stress. Mammalian cells synthesize NAD+ through multiple pathways, including the *de novo* pathway from tryptophan and the salvage pathways that recycle nicotinamide (NAM) or nicotinic acid (NA). Of particular relevance to cancer biology is the NAM salvage pathway, wherein free nicotinamide is converted to nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) by the rate-limiting enzyme nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase).

NMPRTase, encoded by the *PBEF1* gene, has emerged as a key metabolic node in various malignancies. Microarray-based gene expression profiling studies comparing glioma tissues of varying grades (II to IV) with normal brain glial cells have consistently demonstrated a progressive upregulation of *PBEF1* mRNA levels correlating with tumor grade—Grade IV glioblastomas exhibit the highest expression, followed by Grade III anaplastic astrocytomas and Grade II diffuse astrocytomas. This graded increase suggests that NMPRTase activity becomes increasingly critical as gliomas acquire more aggressive phenotypes. Similar overexpression of NMPRTase has been documented in other cancers, including colorectal carcinoma, where it correlates with poor clinical outcomes. The functional significance of this overexpression is underscored by the observation that pharmacological inhibition of NMPRTase leads to a rapid depletion of intracellular NAD+ pools, resulting in energetic crisis, impaired DNA repair, and ultimately, apoptotic cell death in tumor cells, while sparing normal cells that rely less heavily on this salvage route.

To date, the most extensively studied and potent small-molecule inhibitor of human NMPRTase is FK866 (also known as APO866 or daporinad), a synthetic compound identified through phenotypic screening. FK866 binds to the active site of NMPRTase with nanomolar affinity, effectively blocking the conversion of nicotinamide to NMN and thereby depleting cellular NAD+. Preclinical studies have shown that FK866 induces apoptosis in a wide range of cancer cell lines, including glioblastoma, leukemia, and lymphoma, and exhibits antitumor activity in xenograft models. However, despite promising early results, clinical development of FK866 has been hampered by dose-limiting toxicities, particularly thrombocytopenia, and limited efficacy in solid tumors, possibly due to compensatory metabolic mechanisms or inadequate blood-brain barrier penetration. Moreover, FK866 represents a single chemical scaffold, raising concerns about the potential for resistance development and limiting opportunities for structural optimization. Therefore, there exists a compelling need to identify alternative NMPRTase inhibitors with improved pharmacological properties, novel chemotypes, and enhanced selectivity for NMPRTase-overexpressing tumors such as glioblastoma.

Recent advances in structural biology have provided atomic-resolution insights into the mechanism of NMPRTase catalysis and inhibition. Crystal structures of human NMPRTase in complex with its reaction product NMN (PDB ID: 2GVG) and with FK866 (PDB ID: 2GVJ) reveal that the functional enzyme exists as a homodimer, with each active site formed at the interface between the two subunits. Both ligands occupy a long, tunnel-like binding pocket, with partial overlap in their binding modes. A conserved feature of ligand recognition is the hydrophobic stacking interaction between an aromatic moiety of the ligand and the side chains of phenylalanine 193 (F193) from one subunit and tyrosine 18 (Y18) from the opposing subunit. Importantly, comparative analysis of the NMPRTase active site with those of related enzymes in the NAD+ biosynthetic pathway—namely nicotinate phosphoribosyltransferase (NAPRT) and quinolinate phosphoribosyltransferase (QPRT)—reveals significant divergence in amino acid composition, suggesting that highly specific inhibitors can be designed to target NMPRTase without cross-reactivity. This structural information provides a robust foundation for structure-based drug design (SBDD).

In parallel, computational methodologies, particularly molecular docking and virtual screening, have matured into powerful tools for accelerating early-stage drug discovery. By computationally predicting the binding affinity and orientation of millions of small molecules against a target protein of known structure, virtual screening enables the rapid prioritization of a manageable number of high-probability hits for experimental testing. The advent of high-performance computing platforms, such as IBM BlueGene supercomputers, has rendered large-scale virtual screening feasible even for academic laboratories. In this context, the inventors developed a parallelized implementation of the AutoDock algorithm optimized for distributed computing environments, significantly enhancing throughput and enabling the systematic interrogation of diverse chemical libraries.

Prior to the present invention, no reports existed describing the successful application of structure-based virtual screening to identify novel, non-FK866-like inhibitors of NMPRTase with validated activity in both enzymatic and cellular models of glioblastoma. While analogs of FK866 have been synthesized and tested, these efforts remain confined to modifications of the original scaffold. The identification of entirely new chemical entities capable of potently and selectively inhibiting NMPRTase would represent a significant advance in the field, offering not only new therapeutic candidates but also valuable chemical probes to dissect the biological roles of NMPRTase in health and disease. Thus, the background art highlights a clear gap: the absence of structurally diverse, experimentally validated NMPRTase inhibitors derived from rational, structure-guided approaches, particularly for the treatment of NMPRTase-dependent cancers like glioblastoma.

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION

It is therefore an object of the present invention to provide a novel small-molecule compound that acts as a potent and selective inhibitor of human nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase), thereby disrupting NAD+ biosynthesis in cancer cells that are dependent on this metabolic pathway for survival and proliferation. Another object is to identify a compound that exhibits superior or complementary properties to the known inhibitor FK866, including but not limited to a distinct chemical scaffold, improved selectivity, reduced off-target effects, or enhanced ability to inhibit the growth of glioblastoma multiforme cells. A further object is to provide a method for the treatment of cancers characterized by the overexpression of NMPRTase, particularly high-grade gliomas such as glioblastoma multiforme, by administering a therapeutically effective amount of said novel compound. Yet another object is to establish a robust platform combining high-throughput virtual screening based on the three-dimensional crystal structure of NMPRTase with experimental validation in enzymatic and cellular assays, thereby enabling the efficient discovery of new lead compounds for oncology drug development. Additionally, it is an object of the invention to provide pharmaceutical compositions comprising the novel NMPRTase inhibitor, either alone or in combination with other anticancer agents, suitable for systemic or localized administration. Finally, it is an object to utilize the differential expression of NMPRTase between tumor and normal tissues as a biomarker to stratify patients who are most likely to benefit from therapy with the disclosed compound, thereby enabling personalized treatment approaches for glioma and other NMPRTase-overexpressing malignancies.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel compound, designated herein as Compound 5, which has been identified as a potent inhibitor of human nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase). Compound 5 was discovered through a comprehensive drug discovery pipeline that integrated high-throughput virtual screening of a large, drug-like chemical library against the three-dimensional crystal structure of human NMPRTase, followed by rigorous experimental validation in both biochemical and cellular assays. Specifically, the inventors performed virtual screening of the Maybridge HitFinder™ library, containing over 13,000 compounds adhering to Lipinski’s rule of five, using a parallelized version of the AutoDock 3/4 molecular docking software executed on a high-performance computing cluster. Docking poses were evaluated based on binding energy, cluster size (indicating conformational stability), and conservation of key interactions observed in the crystal structures of NMPRTase bound to its natural product NMN and the known inhibitor FK866. From this computational screen, six top-ranking compounds were selected for purchase and experimental testing.

Biochemical assays measuring the conversion of radiolabeled nicotinamide to NAD+ in cytoplasmic extracts from the NMPRTase-overexpressing U87 glioblastoma cell line revealed that two of the six tested compounds—Compounds 4 and 5—significantly inhibited NMPRTase enzymatic activity, with Compound 5 demonstrating superior potency. Concurrently, cell viability assays (MTT assays) showed that only Compounds 1 and 5 effectively inhibited the proliferation of U87 cells, with IC50 values of 335 μM and 325 μM, respectively. Crucially, Compound 5 was the only molecule that exhibited both strong NMPRTase inhibitory activity and potent anti-proliferative effects, indicating that its mechanism of action is directly linked to NAD+ depletion via NMPRTase blockade. This conclusion was further substantiated by control experiments showing that neither FK866 nor Compound 5 inhibited the growth of U251 glioma cells, which express negligible levels of NMPRTase, thereby confirming target specificity.

Structural analysis of the docked poses of Compound 5 revealed that it binds within the same elongated tunnel-like active site of NMPRTase as NMN and FK866, adopting two predominant binding modes. In the first mode, overlapping with the NMN binding site, Compound 5 engages in hydrophobic stacking with F193 and Y18 and forms multiple hydrogen bonds with residues such as F193, G353, G384, R196, H247, R311, and Y18. In the second mode, which substantially overlaps with the FK866 binding site, it interacts with residues including Y188 and H191. Notably, Compound 5 achieved the lowest computed binding energy among all screened compounds in both binding modes and formed a greater number of specific hydrogen bonds with active site residues compared to the other tested leads, which likely accounts for its enhanced inhibitory potency. Importantly, docking simulations performed on NMPRTase pre-bound to NMN or nicotinamide confirmed that Compound 5 can still bind effectively via the second mode, suggesting it may act as a non-competitive or mixed-type inhibitor, potentially circumventing competition with high intracellular concentrations of substrate or product.

Thus, the present invention discloses Compound 5 as a novel, structurally distinct lead compound for the development of therapeutics targeting NMPRTase-dependent cancers. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising Compound 5, methods of treating glioblastoma and other NMPRTase-overexpressing malignancies by administering Compound 5, and the use of NMPRTase expression levels as a predictive biomarker for patient selection. The successful application of this integrated computational-experimental approach validates its utility for the rational discovery of new oncology drugs and provides a foundation for the optimization of Compound 5 into clinical candidates.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a novel small-molecule inhibitor of human nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase), referred to herein as Compound 5, and its use in the treatment of cancers that exhibit dependence on the NAD+ salvage pathway, particularly glioblastoma multiforme (GBM). The identification of Compound 5 represents a significant advancement in the field of targeted cancer therapy, as it arises from a rational, structure-based drug design strategy that leverages high-resolution structural data of the target enzyme and state-of-the-art computational screening technologies. Unlike prior art inhibitors such as FK866, which belong to a single chemical class, Compound 5 possesses a unique molecular scaffold, offering new opportunities for medicinal chemistry optimization and potentially overcoming limitations associated with existing compounds.

The foundation of the invention lies in the recognition that NMPRTase, the rate-limiting enzyme in the NAD+ salvage pathway, is frequently overexpressed in high-grade gliomas and other malignancies, rendering these tumors vulnerable to pharmacological inhibition of this enzyme. Depletion of intracellular NAD+ pools following NMPRTase inhibition disrupts critical cellular processes, including energy metabolism, DNA repair, and epigenetic regulation, ultimately leading to selective cancer cell death. The inventors capitalized on the availability of crystal structures of human NMPRTase in complex with both its reaction product (NMN) and a potent inhibitor (FK866) to define the geometry and key interaction motifs of the enzyme’s active site. This structural knowledge was then employed to guide a large-scale virtual screening campaign aimed at identifying novel chemical entities capable of occupying this site with high affinity.

The virtual screening process commenced with the selection of the Maybridge HitFinder™ library, a collection of 14,400 compounds curated to represent drug-like chemical diversity while adhering to established guidelines for oral bioavailability (Lipinski’s rule of five). Prior to docking, the ligand structures were prepared using Schrodinger LigPrep software to generate low-energy, three-dimensional conformers, assign appropriate protonation states at physiological pH, and remove undesirable chemical features such as excessive hydrogen bond donors or acceptors. This preprocessing step yielded a refined set of 13,214 compounds suitable for computational analysis. The target protein structure, derived from the PDB entry 2GVJ (NMPRTase–FK866 complex), was prepared by removing water molecules and co-crystallized ligands, adding polar hydrogens, and assigning Kollman united atom charges. To balance computational feasibility with biological relevance, the protein was treated as a rigid body during docking, while a sufficiently large grid box (86 × 60 × 50 points with 0.375 Å spacing) was defined to encompass the entire active site tunnel, including regions occupied by both NMN and FK866.

Docking simulations were performed using a parallelized implementation of AutoDock 3, executed across 256 processors of an IBM BlueGene supercomputer, enabling the rapid evaluation of over 13,000 compounds. Each ligand was subjected to 100 independent docking runs, and the resulting poses were clustered based on root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) to identify the most stable and reproducible binding conformations. The selection of potential lead compounds was governed by stringent criteria derived from control docking experiments with NMN and FK866: only ligands exhibiting predicted binding energies lower (more negative) than those of the reference compounds and forming clusters of sufficient size (indicating conformational convergence) were retained for further analysis. Additional filtering was applied based on the conservation of critical interactions, particularly the hydrophobic stacking between an aromatic ring of the ligand and the F193/Y18 residue pair, a hallmark of productive binding observed in both crystal structures.

This multi-tiered computational workflow successfully narrowed the initial library to 34 high-confidence hits, from which the top six purchasable compounds were selected for experimental validation. These compounds, designated Compounds 1 through 6, were acquired from commercial sources and subjected to a battery of biological assays to assess their functional activity. Initial characterization of a panel of glioma cell lines confirmed that U87 and U138 cells exhibit significantly elevated levels of both *PBEF1* mRNA and NMPRTase protein compared to normal brain tissue and other glioma lines such as U251, which served as a negative control. Cytoplasmic extracts prepared from U87 cells were used as a source of active NMPRTase in an enzymatic assay that measured the conversion of [14C]-nicotinamide to [14C]-NAD+, quantified via thin-layer chromatography and phosphorimaging. As expected, FK866 potently inhibited this reaction, while extracts from U251 cells showed minimal activity. Among the six test compounds, only Compounds 4 and 5 demonstrated significant inhibition of NMPRTase activity, with Compound 5 exhibiting the greatest effect.

Parallel MTT cell viability assays were conducted on U87 cells treated with increasing concentrations of each compound. FK866 inhibited U87 proliferation with an IC50 of 170 μM, consistent with literature reports. Compounds 1 and 5 also showed dose-dependent growth inhibition, with IC50 values of 335 μM and 325 μM, respectively. The lack of correlation between enzymatic inhibition and cellular activity for Compound 1 suggests an off-target mechanism, whereas the concordance observed for Compound 5 strongly implicates NMPRTase as its primary cellular target. This hypothesis was confirmed by testing FK866 and Compound 5 on U251 cells, which do not express detectable levels of NMPRTase; neither compound affected U251 viability, even at high concentrations, while the nonspecific cytotoxic agent adriamycin remained effective. These results collectively demonstrate that the anti-proliferative effect of Compound 5 is mediated specifically through NMPRTase inhibition.

Detailed analysis of the docked poses of Compound 5 provided mechanistic insights into its superior activity. The compound was found to adopt two major binding orientations within the NMPRTase active site. In Mode 1, which overlaps with the NMN binding locus, Compound 5 engages in π-stacking with F193 and Y18 and forms an extensive network of hydrogen bonds involving backbone and side-chain atoms of residues F193, G353, G384, R196, H247, R311, and Y18. In Mode 2, which aligns closely with the FK866 binding pose, key interactions include hydrogen bonding with Y188 and H191, along with van der Waals contacts with a hydrophobic subpocket lined by residues such as V242, I309, and I351. Notably, Compound 5 achieved the lowest AutoDock-predicted binding energy of all screened compounds in both modes, and its ability to form multiple specific hydrogen bonds—unlike the other tested leads—likely contributes to its enhanced binding affinity and inhibitory potency. Furthermore, redocking experiments using NMPRTase structures pre-bound to NMN or nicotinamide confirmed that Mode 2 remains accessible, suggesting that Compound 5 can inhibit the enzyme even in the presence of high concentrations of substrate or product, a property that may confer a therapeutic advantage in the tumor microenvironment.

In summary, the present invention discloses Compound 5 as a novel, potent, and selective inhibitor of NMPRTase with demonstrated efficacy in suppressing the growth of NMPRTase-dependent glioblastoma cells. The compound’s unique chemical structure, distinct from FK866, provides a valuable starting point for medicinal chemistry campaigns aimed at improving potency, pharmacokinetic properties, and blood-brain barrier penetration. The integrated methodology employed—combining structural bioinformatics, high-throughput virtual screening, and orthogonal biological validation—exemplifies a modern, rational approach to drug discovery and offers a blueprint for targeting other metabolic vulnerabilities in cancer.

### EXAMPLE 1

The preparation and characterization of cytoplasmic extracts from glioma cell lines for use in NMPRTase enzymatic assays were carried out as follows. Human glioblastoma-derived cell lines U87, U138, U251, U373, U138, LN229, U343, and LN18, along with control cell lines SW480 (colon carcinoma), HaCat (immortalized human keratinocytes), and HBL100 (immortalized human breast epithelial cells), were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. Cells were maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. For extract preparation, logarithmically growing U87 cells were harvested by centrifugation at 500 × g for 5 minutes, washed three times with ice-cold calcium- and magnesium-free phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and resuspended in 1 mL of 0.01 M sodium phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) per 2–3 × 10^7 cells. Cell lysis was achieved by a single freeze-thaw cycle: the suspension was frozen in liquid nitrogen and slowly thawed on ice. The lysate was then centrifuged at 23,000 × g for 90 minutes at 0°C to remove cellular debris and nuclei, yielding a clear supernatant. To further purify the cytoplasmic fraction, 70 μL of 1% protamine sulfate solution was added per milliliter of supernatant, incubated on ice for 15 minutes to precipitate nucleic acids, and centrifuged again at 23,000 × g for 30 minutes at 0°C. The final supernatant, enriched in soluble cytoplasmic proteins including NMPRTase, was aliquoted and stored at −80°C until use. Protein concentration was determined using the Bradford assay, and the integrity of the extract was verified by Western blot analysis using a rabbit polyclonal antibody against GST-PBEF1, which confirmed high expression of NMPRTase in U87 extracts compared to U251 extracts. This standardized protocol ensured a consistent and active source of NMPRTase for subsequent enzymatic inhibition studies.

### EXAMPLE 2

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) was employed to assess the relative expression levels of the *PBEF1* gene across a panel of glioma cell lines to identify models with high NMPRTase expression suitable for functional studies. Total RNA was isolated from cultured cells using TRI reagent according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA purity and concentration were determined spectrophotometrically by measuring absorbance at 260 nm and 280 nm, with samples exhibiting an A260/A280 ratio between 1.8 and 2.0 considered acceptable. RNA integrity was confirmed by electrophoresis on a 1% MOPS-formaldehyde agarose gel, visualizing sharp 28S and 18S ribosomal RNA bands. First-strand cDNA synthesis was performed using 1 μg of total RNA and the ABI PRISM cDNA Archive Kit in a 20 μL reaction volume, following the manufacturer’s protocol. Real-time PCR amplification was conducted on an ABI PRISM 7900 Sequence Detection System using SYBR Green chemistry (Dynamo HS SYBR Green qPCR Kit). Reactions contained 5 μL of 2× master mix, 0.5 μM of each *PBEF1*-specific primer, 2 μL of diluted cDNA template, and nuclease-free water to a final volume of 10 μL. Amplification conditions consisted of an initial denaturation at 95°C for 15 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 60 seconds. Melting curve analysis was performed post-amplification to confirm the specificity of the PCR products. The expression of *PBEF1* was normalized to the geometric mean of four stably expressed reference genes—*GARS*, *AGPAT1*, *ATP5G1*, and *RPL35A*—whose expression had been previously validated as invariant across glioma samples in microarray experiments. Relative quantification was calculated using the ΔΔCT method, with expression levels reported as fold change relative to the mean *PBEF1* expression in normal brain tissue samples from published datasets. This analysis revealed that U87 and U138 cell lines exhibited 3.5-fold and 2.8-fold higher *PBEF1* mRNA levels, respectively, compared to normal brain, establishing them as appropriate models for NMPRTase-dependent studies.

### EXAMPLE 3

The NMPRTase enzymatic activity assay was performed to evaluate the inhibitory potential of the six selected lead compounds. The assay measured the conversion of radiolabeled nicotinamide ([14C]-NAM) to [14C]-NAD+ in the presence of cytoplasmic extracts from U87 glioblastoma cells. The standard reaction mixture (500 μL total volume) contained 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.8), 5 mM MgCl2, 2 mM ATP, 0.5 mM phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate (PRPP), and 0.1 mM [14C]-nicotinamide (specific activity 55 mCi/mmol). Reactions were initiated by the addition of 100 μL of U87 cytoplasmic extract (containing approximately 200 μg of total protein) and incubated at 37°C for 60 minutes. Control reactions included a positive control with 10 μM FK866 and a negative control using extract from U251 cells, which lack significant NMPRTase expression. Reactions were terminated by the addition of 10 mM unlabeled nicotinamide (to prevent further enzymatic activity) followed by heating at 105°C for 2 minutes to denature proteins. Precipitated proteins were removed by centrifugation at 2,500 × g for 10 minutes at 4°C, and the supernatant containing radiolabeled metabolites was stored at −20°C. Separation of [14C]-NAM and [14C]-NAD+ was achieved by ascending thin-layer chromatography (TLC) on cellulose plates using a solvent system of 1 M ammonium sulfate:ethanol (3:7, v/v). After development, TLC plates were dried and exposed to Fuji imaging plates overnight. Radiolabeled spots corresponding to NAD+ and NAM were visualized and quantified using a Fuji FLA-5100 phosphorimager and Alpha Innotec software. The percentage of [14C]-NAM converted to [14C]-NAD+ was calculated for each sample, and inhibition was expressed as the reduction in NAD+ formation relative to the vehicle-treated control. This assay confirmed that Compounds 4 and 5 significantly reduced NAD+ synthesis, with Compound 5 exhibiting near-complete inhibition at 100 μM, comparable to the effect of FK866.

### EXAMPLE 4

Cell viability assays were conducted using the MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) reduction method to determine the anti-proliferative effects of the lead compounds on U87 glioblastoma cells. U87 cells were seeded in 96-well tissue culture plates at a density of 1,500 cells per well in 100 μL of complete DMEM medium. After 24 hours of incubation to allow cell attachment, test compounds were added in triplicate at concentrations ranging from 10 μM to 1,000 μM, dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) such that the final DMSO concentration did not exceed 0.5%. Control wells received an equivalent volume of DMSO vehicle. Cells were incubated with the compounds for 48 hours under standard culture conditions. At the end of the treatment period, 20 μL of MTT solution (5 mg/mL in PBS) was added to each well, and the plates were returned to the incubator for an additional 3 hours to allow viable cells to reduce MTT to purple formazan crystals. The culture medium was then carefully aspirated, and the formazan crystals were solubilized by adding 200 μL of DMSO per well. The plates were shaken gently for 10 minutes to ensure complete dissolution, and the absorbance was measured at 550 nm using a microplate ELISA reader. Background absorbance from blank wells (medium + MTT + DMSO without cells) was subtracted from all readings. Cell viability was expressed as a percentage of the vehicle-treated control, and dose-response curves were generated using GraphPad Prism software to calculate IC50 values. The results demonstrated that Compound 5 inhibited U87 cell proliferation with an IC50 of 325 μM, while Compound 1 showed similar potency (IC50 = 335 μM), and the other compounds exhibited minimal or no activity at the concentrations tested.

### EXAMPLE 5

To confirm the target specificity of Compound 5 and FK866, their effects on the viability of NMPRTase-negative U251 glioma cells were assessed. U251 cells, which express negligible levels of *PBEF1* mRNA and NMPRTase protein as confirmed by RT-qPCR and Western blotting, were seeded in 96-well plates at 1,500 cells per well and allowed to adhere overnight. Cells were then treated with increasing concentrations of FK866 (10–1,000 μM) or Compound 5 (10–1,000 μM) for 48 hours. As a positive control for cytotoxicity, a separate set of wells was treated with 1 μM adriamycin, a broad-spectrum chemotherapeutic agent. Following treatment, cell viability was measured using the MTT assay as described in Example 4. The results showed that neither FK866 nor Compound 5 significantly reduced the viability of U251 cells, even at the highest concentration tested (1,000 μM), with cell survival remaining above 90% of the vehicle control. In contrast, adriamycin treatment resulted in near-complete cell death, confirming the responsiveness of U251 cells to cytotoxic agents. This differential sensitivity—where U87 cells (NMPRTase-high) are killed by NMPRTase inhibitors while U251 cells (NMPRTase-low) are unaffected—provides compelling evidence that the anti-proliferative effects of Compound 5 are specifically mediated through inhibition of NMPRTase and not due to non-specific cytotoxicity. This finding underscores the potential therapeutic window for Compound 5 in treating NMPRTase-overexpressing tumors.

### EXAMPLE 6

Western blot analysis was performed to corroborate the RT-qPCR findings and directly assess NMPRTase protein levels in glioma cell lines. Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors, and total protein concentration was determined by the BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein (30 μg per lane) were separated by SDS-PAGE on a 10% polyacrylamide gel and transferred onto a nitrocellulose membrane. The membrane was blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk in Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBST) for 1 hour at room temperature, then incubated overnight at 4°C with a primary antibody: either a rabbit polyclonal antibody against GST-PBEF1 (1:1,000 dilution) or a mouse monoclonal anti-α-tubulin antibody (1:5,000 dilution) as a loading control. After washing with TBST, the membrane was incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies (anti-rabbit or anti-mouse IgG, 1:5,000 dilution) for 1 hour at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents and exposure to X-ray film. Densitometric analysis of the bands confirmed that U87 and U138 cells exhibited markedly higher levels of NMPRTase protein compared to U251 and other glioma lines, consistent with the mRNA expression data. This protein-level validation was critical for selecting U87 as the primary source of enzyme for the NMPRTase activity assays and as the cellular model for compound testing.

### Inhibition of NMPRTase Activity by Selected Lead Compounds

The six lead compounds identified through virtual screening were rigorously evaluated for their ability to inhibit NMPRTase enzymatic activity using the radiolabeled assay described in Example 3. Cytoplasmic extracts from U87 cells, which express high levels of NMPRTase, were used as the enzyme source. Each compound was tested at a final concentration of 100 μM in the reaction mixture. As shown in the experimental results, FK866 (positive control) reduced [14C]-NAD+ formation by approximately 90% compared to the vehicle control, confirming the assay’s sensitivity. Among the test compounds, Compound 4 inhibited NAD+ synthesis by about 60%, while Compound 5 exhibited even greater efficacy, reducing NAD+ production by over 85%, approaching the level of inhibition achieved by FK866. Compounds 1, 2, 3, and 6 showed minimal or no inhibitory activity, with NAD+ levels remaining above 80% of the control. These data clearly demonstrate that Compound 5 is a potent inhibitor of NMPRTase, validating the predictions made by the virtual screening and docking analysis. The strong correlation between the computed binding energy (lowest among all compounds) and the experimental inhibitory potency further supports the reliability of the computational model used in this study.

### EXAMPLE 8

Molecular modeling and docking studies were refined to investigate the binding mode of Compound 5 in the context of substrate-bound NMPRTase. To simulate physiological conditions where the enzyme may be occupied by its natural substrate nicotinamide or product NMN, the inventors performed additional docking experiments using the crystal structure of NMPRTase (PDB 2GVG) with NMN retained in the active site. The grid box was redefined to focus on the region adjacent to the NMN-binding site, and Compound 5 was docked using AutoDock 4 with the same parameters as before. Remarkably, the second binding mode of Compound 5—overlapping with the FK866 site—was consistently reproduced, with a binding energy only slightly less favorable than in the apo-enzyme. This indicates that Compound 5 can bind to NMPRTase even when NMN is present, suggesting a non-competitive or mixed inhibition mechanism. This property is therapeutically advantageous because it implies that Compound 5 would remain effective in the tumor microenvironment, where intracellular concentrations of nicotinamide and NMN may be elevated due to high metabolic flux. The ability to inhibit the enzyme without direct competition with substrate enhances the likelihood of achieving sustained NAD+ depletion and robust anti-tumor effects *in vivo*. These refined modeling studies provide a structural rationale for the potent cellular activity of Compound 5 and inform future optimization efforts aimed at maximizing occupancy of this secondary inhibitory site.
