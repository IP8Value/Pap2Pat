# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular therapeutics for the treatment of fibrotic skin disorders, particularly hypertrophic scarring. More specifically, the invention provides a novel dual-targeted small interfering RNA (siRNA)-based therapeutic composition comprising siRNA molecules directed against both transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) mRNA, formulated in a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) nanoparticle delivery system. This composition is designed for intradermal or intrascar administration to achieve simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 gene expression in human fibroblasts and myofibroblasts within hypertrophic scar tissue. The invention further encompasses methods for reducing the size and severity of hypertrophic scars and preventing excessive fibrosis in human skin by modulating key pro-fibrotic pathways through the synergistic inhibition of TGF-β1 and COX-2. The therapeutic approach disclosed herein leverages a newly discovered mechanism of action wherein the co-silencing of these two targets induces selective apoptosis of activated fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, thereby disrupting the pathological cycle of extracellular matrix overproduction and tissue contraction that characterizes hypertrophic scarring. Additionally, the invention includes pharmaceutical formulations suitable for clinical use, methods of manufacturing such formulations, and their application in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings for patients suffering from dermal fibrosis, including post-surgical, post-traumatic, or burn-related hypertrophic scars.

## BACKGROUND

Hypertrophic scarring represents a significant clinical challenge in dermatology, plastic surgery, and wound care, affecting millions of individuals worldwide each year. Unlike normal wound healing, which results in a thin, flat, and pliable scar, hypertrophic scars are characterized by excessive deposition of collagen and other extracellular matrix (ECM) components, leading to raised, rigid, erythematous, and often painful or pruritic lesions that can impair function and cause psychological distress. The pathophysiology of hypertrophic scarring involves a dysregulated wound healing process marked by prolonged inflammation, hypercellularity due to persistent fibroblast proliferation, aberrant differentiation of fibroblasts into contractile myofibroblasts expressing α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), and an imbalance between ECM synthesis and degradation. Despite decades of research, current therapeutic options remain limited in efficacy and often associated with significant side effects or recurrence rates. Conventional treatments such as intralesional corticosteroid injections, surgical excision, pressure therapy, silicone gel sheeting, laser therapy, and cryotherapy provide only partial improvement and do not address the underlying molecular drivers of fibrosis.

Transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) has long been recognized as a central mediator of fibrosis across multiple organ systems, including the skin. In the context of dermal wound healing, TGF-β1 is secreted by platelets, macrophages, and fibroblasts during the inflammatory phase and promotes fibroblast recruitment, proliferation, and differentiation into myofibroblasts. It also potently stimulates the synthesis of collagen types I and III, fibronectin, and other ECM proteins while simultaneously inhibiting matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) responsible for ECM degradation. Elevated levels of TGF-β1 have been consistently documented in hypertrophic scar tissue compared to normal skin, and numerous preclinical studies have demonstrated that antagonizing TGF-β1 signaling can attenuate fibrosis. However, monotherapy targeting TGF-β1 alone has shown limited success in clinical translation, likely due to the redundancy and complexity of fibrotic pathways.

Cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), the inducible isoform of the cyclooxygenase enzyme responsible for prostaglandin synthesis during inflammation, presents a more nuanced role in fibrosis. While traditionally viewed as a pro-inflammatory mediator, some in vitro studies have suggested that COX-2-derived prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) may exert anti-fibrotic effects by counteracting TGF-β1 signaling in certain cell types. However, these findings are largely derived from experiments using terminally differentiated or senescent cells and may not reflect the dynamic interplay of these pathways in the early and active phases of wound healing in vivo. Importantly, fetal wound healing—which occurs without scarring—is associated with low levels of both PGE2 and COX-2, implying that elevated COX-2 activity in adult wounds may contribute to pathological scarring. Moreover, recent evidence indicates that COX-2 can modulate the canonical TGF-β/SMAD signaling pathway, suggesting a functional crosstalk between these two targets that has not been therapeutically exploited.

The advent of RNA interference (RNAi) technology has opened new avenues for precise gene silencing in fibrotic diseases. Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) can be designed to specifically degrade target mRNAs, offering high selectivity and potency. However, the clinical application of siRNA therapeutics for skin disorders has been hampered by formidable delivery challenges. Naked siRNA molecules are rapidly degraded by nucleases in the extracellular environment, exhibit poor cellular uptake due to their negative charge and large size, and cannot efficiently penetrate the dense, avascular structure of mature scar tissue. Consequently, effective delivery systems are essential to protect siRNA payloads, facilitate cellular internalization, and enable endosomal escape to reach the cytoplasmic RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC).

To address this, various nanocarriers have been explored, including liposomes, polymers, and peptides. Among these, histidine-lysine co-polymers (HKPs) have emerged as promising biodegradable vectors for siRNA delivery. HKPs are synthetic polypeptides composed of repeating histidine and lysine residues, which confer pH-dependent buffering capacity (due to histidine imidazole groups) that promotes endosomal disruption and siRNA release, along with strong electrostatic binding to nucleic acids (via lysine amine groups). Previous studies have demonstrated the safety and efficacy of HKP-formulated siRNAs in preclinical models of cancer and fibrosis. Nevertheless, no prior art has described the simultaneous targeting of TGF-β1 and COX-2 using HKP-delivered siRNAs for the treatment of hypertrophic scarring, nor has the synergistic apoptotic effect of dual silencing on fibroblasts been elucidated.

Existing clinical-stage oligonucleotide therapeutics for scarring, such as EXC001 (a TGF-β1 antisense oligonucleotide) and RXI-109 (a TGF-β1 siRNA), require relatively high doses and have shown modest efficacy. The lack of a multi-targeted approach that addresses the interconnected nature of pro-fibrotic signaling networks represents a critical unmet need. The present invention overcomes these limitations by providing a rationally designed, dual-gene silencing strategy that not only suppresses key fibrotic mediators but also triggers a novel mechanism of action—selective apoptosis of pathogenic fibroblasts—thereby offering a more comprehensive and durable therapeutic response for hypertrophic scar reduction.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel therapeutic composition and method for the treatment of hypertrophic scarring and other fibrotic skin disorders through the simultaneous silencing of transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) gene expression using small interfering RNA (siRNA) molecules delivered via a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) nanoparticle system. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that co-inhibition of TGF-β1 and COX-2, but not inhibition of either target alone, induces robust apoptosis in human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts (HSFs) and myofibroblasts, leading to a significant reduction in scar tissue mass and fibrotic markers in vivo. This synergistic effect arises from the functional interdependence of the TGF-β1 and COX-2 signaling pathways, wherein each pathway modulates the activity of the other, and their concurrent suppression disrupts the survival signals that maintain the activated, pro-fibrotic phenotype of fibroblasts in pathological scars.

The therapeutic composition of the invention comprises at least two distinct siRNA duplexes: a first siRNA specifically targeting the mRNA of human TGF-β1, and a second siRNA specifically targeting the mRNA of human COX-2. These siRNAs are formulated with a histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) to form stable, nanoscale particles suitable for intradermal or intrascar injection. The HKP serves as a non-viral delivery vehicle that protects the siRNA from degradation, enhances cellular uptake through endocytosis, and facilitates endosomal escape via the proton sponge effect mediated by the imidazole groups of histidine residues at acidic pH. The resulting nanoparticles exhibit a particle size of approximately 100 to 200 nanometers in diameter, preferably about 150 nanometers, and a zeta potential of about +30 to +50 millivolts, which promotes interaction with negatively charged cell membranes and stability in aqueous suspension.

In a preferred embodiment, the siRNA sequences are 25-mer blunt-ended duplexes designed to target conserved regions of human TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA with high specificity and minimal off-target effects. The TGF-β1 siRNA is selected to hybridize with a sequence within the coding region of the human TGF-β1 mRNA (e.g., GenBank accession NM_000660), while the COX-2 siRNA targets a sequence within the coding region of the human COX-2 mRNA (e.g., GenBank accession NM_000963). The siRNA duplexes are chemically synthesized and may include modifications such as 2'-O-methyl or phosphorothioate linkages to enhance nuclease resistance and reduce immunogenicity, provided that such modifications do not compromise RNAi activity.

The method of treatment according to the invention involves administering the HKP-formulated TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNA composition directly into hypertrophic scar tissue or into a site at risk of developing hypertrophic scarring, such as a fresh surgical incision or burn wound. Administration is typically performed via intradermal or intrascar injection, with the dose ranging from 5 to 50 micrograms of total siRNA per square centimeter of scar tissue, preferably about 20 micrograms per cm³. The treatment regimen may include multiple injections administered at intervals of 3 to 7 days, for example, three injections spaced five days apart, to sustain target gene silencing over the critical period of scar maturation.

The therapeutic efficacy of the invention is manifested through several interconnected biological effects. First, the dual siRNA treatment achieves significant knockdown of both TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA and protein levels in scar fibroblasts, as confirmed by quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) and Western blotting. Second, this knockdown leads to the downregulation of downstream pro-fibrotic markers, including α-SMA, collagen type I alpha 1 (Col1A1), collagen type III alpha 1 (Col3A1), and plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1). Third, and most critically, the co-silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 triggers intrinsic apoptotic pathways in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, as evidenced by increased caspase activation, phosphatidylserine externalization (detected by Annexin V staining), DNA fragmentation (detected by TUNEL assay), and characteristic morphological changes observed under electron microscopy. This selective induction of apoptosis reduces the population of ECM-producing cells within the scar, thereby decreasing overall tissue cellularity and collagen content.

The invention is further supported by in vivo data demonstrating that intrascar injection of the HKP(TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNA) formulation—referred to herein as STP705—into human hypertrophic scar tissue implanted subcutaneously in immunodeficient mice results in a statistically significant reduction in scar volume (approximately 40–45%) compared to untreated controls or scrambled siRNA controls. Similar efficacy is observed in a human skin graft model subjected to full-thickness wounding, where STP705 treatment prevents the development of hypertrophic-like features and reduces graft thickness. Histological analyses reveal decreased collagen density (by Masson’s trichrome staining), reduced α-SMA-positive myofibroblasts, diminished vascularization (by CD31 and VEGF staining), and increased apoptotic cells in treated tissues.

Importantly, the invention discloses a previously unrecognized mechanistic link between TGF-β1 and COX-2 signaling in fibroblasts. Silencing of COX-2 leads to reduced phosphorylation of SMAD2/3, the canonical downstream effectors of TGF-β receptor activation, indicating that COX-2 activity is required for full TGF-β1 signaling. Conversely, TGF-β1 knockdown reduces COX-2 expression, suggesting a positive feedback loop. This bidirectional regulation explains why dual targeting is necessary to achieve the apoptotic effect: single-gene silencing leaves the other pathway intact, allowing compensatory survival signaling. Only when both nodes are suppressed is the pro-survival network sufficiently disrupted to commit fibroblasts to apoptosis.

The pharmaceutical composition of the invention is stable, scalable, and suitable for clinical use. The HKP-siRNA nanoparticles can be lyophilized into a powder for long-term storage and reconstituted in sterile aqueous solution immediately before administration. The formulation exhibits no significant toxicity in preclinical models and demonstrates superior delivery efficiency compared to naked siRNA or other carriers, as shown by prolonged retention of fluorescently labeled siRNA within scar tissue for up to 48 hours post-injection.

In summary, the invention provides a first-in-class, mechanism-based therapeutic strategy for hypertrophic scarring that combines rational target selection, advanced delivery technology, and a novel biological insight into fibroblast apoptosis. By simultaneously silencing TGF-β1 and COX-2, the invention addresses the multifactorial nature of fibrosis and offers a potent, targeted, and potentially curative approach to a condition that has long resisted effective medical intervention.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions

The present example demonstrates the design, selection, and in vitro validation of siRNA sequences targeting human TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNAs, and confirms that co-transfection of these siRNAs results in efficient and specific knockdown of both target genes in human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts (HSFs). Initially, eight candidate siRNA duplexes were designed for each target gene using bioinformatic algorithms to identify 25-nucleotide sequences within the coding regions of human TGF-β1 (GenBank NM_000660) and COX-2 (GenBank NM_000963) that exhibited high predicted silencing efficiency and low off-target potential. These siRNAs were synthesized as blunt-ended duplexes and screened in human PC3 prostate adenocarcinoma cells, a standard model for initial siRNA efficacy testing, using Lipofectamine 2000-mediated transfection. After 48 hours, total RNA was isolated and subjected to quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) to assess target mRNA levels. The most potent siRNA for each gene—defined as the sequence achieving greater than 70% knockdown with minimal cytotoxicity—was selected for further study. The chosen TGF-β1 siRNA (SEQ ID NO: 1: 5′-GCAUCCUGCUUCCACAUUCUU-3′ and SEQ ID NO: 2: 5′-GAAUGUGGAAGCAGGAUGCCU-3′) and COX-2 siRNA (SEQ ID NO: 3: 5′-GGACUUGGCUGGGAUUUUAUU-3′ and SEQ ID NO: 4: 5′-UAAAAUCCCAGCCAAGUCCU-3′) were then used in all subsequent experiments.

Primary HSFs were isolated from surgically excised hypertrophic scar tissue obtained from consenting patients. Cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum and used between passages 3 and 6 to maintain phenotypic stability. HSFs were transfected with either TGF-β1 siRNA alone, COX-2 siRNA alone, or a combination of both siRNAs (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNA), using HKP as the delivery vehicle at a carrier-to-siRNA mass ratio of 4:1. Control cells were treated with HKP alone or HKP complexed with a non-targeting (scrambled) siRNA. At 48 hours post-transfection, total RNA was extracted and analyzed by qRT-PCR. The results showed that TGF-β1 siRNA alone reduced TGF-β1 mRNA levels by 75% ± 5%, while COX-2 siRNA alone reduced COX-2 mRNA by 72% ± 6%. Remarkably, the combination treatment achieved comparable knockdown of both targets (74% for TGF-β1 and 71% for COX-2), confirming that co-delivery does not interfere with individual silencing efficiency. Furthermore, cross-regulation was observed: COX-2 siRNA alone reduced TGF-β1 mRNA by 40% ± 8%, and TGF-β1 siRNA alone reduced COX-2 mRNA by 38% ± 7%, indicating a reciprocal positive regulatory relationship between the two genes in HSFs. Protein-level validation by Western blotting confirmed corresponding reductions in TGF-β1 and COX-2 protein expression. These findings establish that the selected siRNA sequences are highly effective and that simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 is feasible and results in mutual downregulation, laying the foundation for the synergistic biological effects described in subsequent examples.

### Example 2: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions Resulted in Activation of Apoptosis of Fibroblasts/Myofibroblasts

This example demonstrates that the co-silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2, but not the silencing of either gene alone, induces apoptosis in human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts (HSFs) and myofibroblasts. Primary HSFs were transfected with TGF-β1 siRNA, COX-2 siRNA, or the combination (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNA), all delivered via HKP nanoparticles, as described in Example 1. Control cells received HKP alone or HKP with scrambled siRNA. At 48 hours post-transfection, cells were assessed for apoptotic markers using multiple complementary techniques. First, morphological changes were examined by transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Cells treated with the combination siRNA exhibited classic apoptotic features, including chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, and plasma membrane blebbing, whereas cells treated with single siRNAs or controls maintained normal fibroblastic morphology. Second, flow cytometry analysis using Annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide (PI) staining revealed that the combination treatment significantly increased the percentage of early apoptotic (Annexin V+/PI−) and late apoptotic (Annexin V+/PI+) cells to 32% ± 4%, compared to less than 8% in all single-treatment or control groups (p < 0.001). Third, immunofluorescence staining for α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA)—a marker of myofibroblast differentiation—showed a marked reduction in α-SMA-positive cells following combination treatment, consistent with the loss of activated myofibroblasts via apoptosis. Additionally, the combination-treated cells displayed a narrowed, elongated morphology and reduced cell density in culture, further supporting a loss of viability.

To confirm the specificity of the apoptotic effect, pharmacological inhibitors were employed. HSFs were treated with Galunisertib (a TGF-β receptor type I antagonist) or Celecoxib (a COX-2 inhibitor) individually or in combination. Only the dual inhibition recapitulated the apoptotic phenotype observed with siRNA co-silencing, validating that the effect is due to functional blockade of both pathways. Moreover, overexpression of COX-2 via a pcDNA3.1-COX-2 plasmid rescued HSFs from TGF-β1 siRNA-induced apoptosis, confirming the interdependence of the two pathways. Western blot analysis of combination-treated cells showed decreased expression of phosphorylated SMAD2/3 and PAI-1—key downstream effectors of TGF-β1 signaling—further linking pathway suppression to cell death. These results collectively demonstrate that simultaneous inhibition of TGF-β1 and COX-2 disrupts critical survival signals in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, leading to selective apoptosis, a mechanism not achievable by targeting either pathway alone.

### Example 3. HKP Enhances siRNA Delivery Into Human Hypertrophic Scar

This example evaluates the efficiency and kinetics of siRNA delivery into human hypertrophic scar tissue using the HKP nanoparticle system compared to naked siRNA. HKP-siRNA nanoparticles were prepared by mixing HKP polymer (H3K4b variant) with a 1:1 molar ratio of TGF-β1 and COX-2 siRNAs at a 4:1 mass ratio (HKP:siRNA) in aqueous solution. The resulting nanoparticles had an average hydrodynamic diameter of 150 nm and a zeta potential of +40 mV, as measured by dynamic light scattering. For in vivo tracking, siRNA was labeled with Alexa Fluor 488. Human hypertrophic scar tissue fragments (2 cm²) were implanted subcutaneously into the dorsal flanks of nude mice (nu/nu Balb/c). Four weeks post-implantation, when the grafts were well-vascularized and stable, mice received a single intrascar injection of either 20 μg of naked Alexa Fluor-siRNA or 20 μg of HKP-packaged Alexa Fluor-siRNA in 50 μl volume. Scar tissue samples were harvested at 4, 24, and 48 hours post-injection and analyzed by fluorescence microscopy.

At 4 hours, both formulations showed diffuse distribution throughout the scar tissue. However, by 24 hours, the fluorescence signal from naked siRNA was nearly undetectable, indicating rapid degradation and clearance. In contrast, HKP-packaged siRNA remained clearly visible, with punctate intracellular fluorescence suggestive of endocytosed nanoparticles. At 48 hours, the HKP-siRNA signal was still robust, demonstrating sustained retention. To correlate delivery with functional gene silencing, separate groups of mice received intrascar injections of HKP(TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNA) or saline control. Scar tissue was harvested at 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours, and mRNA was analyzed by qRT-PCR. TGF-β1 knockdown peaked at 48 hours (65% reduction), while COX-2 knockdown peaked at 72 hours (60% reduction), with both effects persisting for at least 96 hours. In comparison, qRT-PCR of normal human skin versus hypertrophic scar confirmed that both TGF-β1 and COX-2 are significantly overexpressed in scar tissue (3.5-fold and 2.8-fold, respectively), justifying their selection as therapeutic targets. These data confirm that HKP nanoparticles significantly enhance the stability, cellular uptake, and duration of siRNA activity in human hypertrophic scar tissue, enabling effective target gene silencing in vivo.

### Example 4. HKP-Packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA Reduces Size of Human Hypertrophic Scar

This example demonstrates the therapeutic efficacy of HKP-packaged TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNA (STP705) in reducing the volume of established human hypertrophic scar tissue in a mouse xenograft model. Human hypertrophic scar tissue was obtained from surgical excisions, trimmed of subcutaneous fat, and cut into 2 cm² pieces. These fragments were implanted subcutaneously into the dorsal flanks of nude mice (n = 12 per group). Four weeks post-implantation, when the grafts had stabilized and exhibited histological features resembling human hypertrophic scars (including high cellularity, dense collagen, and α-SMA+ myofibroblasts), mice were randomized to receive either STP705 (20 μg siRNA in 50 μl HKP formulation per cm³ of scar) or saline control. The STP705 was administered via intrascar injection at five sites (four quadrants and center) to ensure even distribution, with three total injections given at 5-day intervals. Scar dimensions were measured weekly using calipers, and volume was calculated as (length × width × height)/2.

By day 28 post-initiation of treatment, STP705-treated scars showed a 45% ± 6% reduction in volume compared to baseline, while control scars exhibited no significant change (p < 0.001). Histological analysis of harvested tissue confirmed these findings: Masson’s trichrome staining revealed markedly reduced collagen density in treated scars, and immunohistochemistry showed significant decreases in α-SMA, TGF-β1, and COX-2 protein expression. qRT-PCR of tissue homogenates confirmed >60% knockdown of TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA, along with >50% reduction in Col1A1 and Col3A1. Hydroxyproline content—a biochemical measure of total collagen—was reduced by 48% in treated scars. Importantly, no systemic toxicity or local adverse reactions (e.g., ulceration, infection) were observed. These results provide compelling evidence that repeated intrascar administration of STP705 leads to sustained target gene silencing, reduced ECM deposition, and significant scar regression in a clinically relevant human tissue model.

### Example 5. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Reduces Size of Human Skin Grafts

This example extends the findings of Example 4 to a model of de novo hypertrophic scar formation using human skin grafts. Full-thickness human skin samples (2 cm × 1.6 cm) were obtained from women undergoing breast reduction surgery, with informed consent. These grafts were transplanted onto full-thickness excisions on the backs of nude mice, replacing the excised mouse skin. The grafts were secured with sutures and covered with sterile dressings for two weeks to allow engraftment. Four weeks post-grafting, mice were treated with either STP705 (20 μg/50 μl/cm³, three injections at 5-day intervals) or saline control, administered via intradermal injection at five sites per graft. Graft thickness was measured by caliper, and tissue was harvested at day 28 for analysis.

STP705-treated grafts showed a 40% ± 5% reduction in thickness compared to controls (p < 0.001). Histologically, treated grafts exhibited a more normalized dermal architecture with reduced cellularity, thinner collagen bundles, and fewer α-SMA+ myofibroblasts. qRT-PCR confirmed significant knockdown of TGF-β1 (62%), COX-2 (58%), α-SMA (55%), and Col1A1 (50%) in treated grafts. Hydroxyproline content was reduced by 42%. This model is particularly valuable because it recapitulates the entire process of human wound healing and scar formation, demonstrating that STP705 can prevent the development of hypertrophic features when administered during the active remodeling phase. The consistency of results with the hypertrophic scar implant model underscores the broad applicability of the dual-targeting approach across different fibrotic contexts.

### Example 6. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Demonstrates a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Mechanism of Action

This example elucidates the underlying biological mechanism responsible for the scar-reducing effects of STP705. Tissue samples from the human hypertrophic scar and skin graft models (Examples 4 and 5) were analyzed for markers of fibrosis and apoptosis. Hydroxyproline assays confirmed a significant reduction in total collagen content in STP705-treated tissues (45–48% decrease, p < 0.001). Histological staining (H&E and Masson’s trichrome) revealed not only reduced collagen but also decreased epidermal hyperplasia and dermal thickness. Immunohistochemistry showed downregulation of pro-fibrotic and pro-angiogenic factors, including α-SMA, VEGF, and CD31, indicating reduced myofibroblast presence and vascularization.

Most critically, TUNEL (terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling) assays were performed to detect apoptotic cells in situ. STP705-treated tissues exhibited a 3.5-fold increase in TUNEL-positive cells compared to controls, with the majority of apoptotic cells localized in the dermal compartment and co-staining with fibroblast markers (e.g., vimentin). Quantitative analysis confirmed that the number of apoptotic fibroblasts/myofibroblasts was significantly higher in treated groups (p < 0.001). This finding, combined with the in vitro data from Example 2, establishes a novel mechanism of action: the primary anti-fibrotic effect of STP705 is not merely the suppression of ECM production, but the active elimination of the cells responsible for pathological matrix deposition. This mechanism distinguishes the invention from conventional anti-fibrotics that only inhibit synthesis without reducing cellularity.

### Example 7. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Induced Apoptotic Activities of the Fibroblast/Myofibroblasts In Vivo

This example provides direct in vivo evidence that STP705 induces apoptosis specifically in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts within human scar tissue. Using the human hypertrophic scar implant model, mice were treated with STP705 or control as described in Example 4. At day 28, scar tissue was harvested and subjected to dual-label immunofluorescence staining for TUNEL (apoptosis) and α-SMA (myofibroblasts) or vimentin (fibroblasts). Confocal microscopy revealed extensive co-localization of TUNEL signal with α-SMA and vimentin, confirming that the apoptotic cells were indeed fibroblastic in origin. Quantification showed that 28% ± 4% of α-SMA+ cells were TUNEL-positive in treated scars, compared to less than 5% in controls. Flow cytometry of single-cell suspensions from digested scar tissue further validated these results, showing a 4-fold increase in Annexin V+ fibroblasts in the STP705 group. These data conclusively demonstrate that the dual silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 triggers apoptosis in the key effector cells of fibrosis in vivo, providing a cellular basis for the observed scar regression.

### Example 8. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Serves as a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Therapeutic Agent

This example summarizes the therapeutic profile of STP705 as a novel anti-fibrotic agent. Across multiple models—human fibroblast culture, human hypertrophic scar implants, and human skin grafts—STP705 consistently demonstrates potent target gene silencing, downregulation of fibrotic markers, induction of fibroblast apoptosis, and significant reduction in scar size and collagen content. The effective dose (20 μg/cm³) is substantially lower than those reported for other clinical-stage oligonucleotide therapeutics (e.g., EXC001, RXI-109), likely due to the synergistic dual-targeting design and the enhanced delivery efficiency of the HKP nanoparticle system. The formulation is stable, scalable, and well-tolerated, with no observed toxicity in preclinical studies. The mechanism of action—selective apoptosis of pathogenic fibroblasts—represents a paradigm shift from traditional anti-fibrotics that only modulate ECM turnover. Taken together, these data position STP705 as a first-in-class, clinically viable therapeutic for the treatment and prevention of hypertrophic scarring and other fibrotic skin disorders.

## Discussion

The present invention establishes a groundbreaking approach to the treatment of hypertrophic scarring through the simultaneous RNA interference-mediated silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2, delivered via a biodegradable HKP nanoparticle system. The core innovation lies in the discovery that dual targeting of these two interconnected pathways—not only suppresses key fibrotic mediators but also induces selective apoptosis of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, thereby addressing the cellular root of pathological scarring. This mechanism is distinct from and superior to monotherapies that merely inhibit ECM synthesis without reducing the population of ECM-producing cells. The functional crosstalk between TGF-β1 and COX-2, wherein each pathway sustains the other’s activity, explains why only co-inhibition achieves the apoptotic threshold. The HKP delivery system overcomes the historical barrier of siRNA instability and poor tissue penetration, enabling efficient and sustained gene silencing in the dense, avascular environment of scar tissue. The consistent efficacy across human cell culture, human scar xenografts, and human skin graft models—coupled with a favorable safety profile and low effective dose—strongly supports the clinical translation of this therapeutic strategy. This work not only provides a promising new treatment for a debilitating condition but also advances the fundamental understanding of fibroblast biology in fibrosis, revealing apoptosis as a therapeutically tractable endpoint in scar resolution.

## Materials and Methods

### siRNA Sequence Design and Selection

Twenty-five-mer blunt-ended siRNA duplexes targeting human TGF-β1 (GenBank NM_000660) and COX-2 (GenBank NM_000963) mRNA sequences were designed using proprietary algorithms to ensure high specificity and minimal off-target effects. Eight candidate siRNAs per gene were initially synthesized and screened in human PC3 cells. Cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 2.5×10⁵ cells/well and transfected with Lipofectamine 2000-complexed siRNAs. After 48 hours, total RNA was isolated using the RNAqueous-4 PCR kit, and qRT-PCR was performed to quantify target mRNA knockdown. The most potent siRNA for each gene—achieving >70% silencing with low cytotoxicity—was selected for all subsequent experiments. The final TGF-β1 siRNA sequences were 5′-GCAUCCUGCUUCCACAUUCUU-3′ (sense) and 5′-GAAUGUGGAAGCAGGAUGCCU-3′ (antisense); the COX-2 siRNA sequences were 5′-GGACUUGGCUGGGAUUUUAUU-3′ (sense) and 5′-UAAAAUCCCAGCCAAGUCCU-3′ (antisense). All siRNAs were purchased from Qiagen or Dharmacon.

### Growth Inhibition In Vitro

Human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts (HSFs) were isolated from patient-derived scar tissue and cultured in DMEM with 20% FBS. For transfection, cells were seeded at 10⁶ cells/well in 6-well plates and treated six hours later with HKP-formulated siRNAs (10 μg/ml HKP + 5 μg/ml siRNA). Apoptosis was assessed by Annexin V-FITC/PI staining using the Dead Cell Apoptosis Kit (Invitrogen) and analyzed by flow cytometry (EPICS Altra). Cell cycle analysis was performed by fixing cells in 70% ethanol, staining with propidium iodide, and analyzing DNA content by flow cytometry. Morphological changes were documented by phase-contrast microscopy.

### Nanoparticle Preparation

HKP-siRNA nanoparticles were prepared by mixing histidine-lysine co-polymer (H3K4b variant) with a 1:1 molar mixture of TGF-β1 and COX-2 siRNAs at a 4:1 mass ratio (HKP:siRNA) in sterile water. The mixture was vortexed for 30 seconds and incubated at room temperature for 20 minutes to allow self-assembly. The resulting nanoparticles were characterized by dynamic light scattering (size ~150 nm, zeta potential ~+40 mV) and could be lyophilized for storage. Before use, lyophilized powder was reconstituted in sterile PBS.

### Mouse

Eight-week-old male nude mice (nu/nu Balb/c) were purchased from the Center for Experimental Animals in Shanghai, China. All animal procedures were approved by the Independent Ethics Committee of Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital and conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines. Mice were housed in pathogen-free conditions with ad libitum access to food and water.

### Human Hypertrophic Scar and Skin Tissue Implant Models

Human hypertrophic scar tissue was obtained from surgical excisions with informed consent, trimmed of fat, and cut into 2 cm² pieces. These were implanted subcutaneously into the dorsal flanks of anesthetized nude mice and sutured to the deep fascia. For the skin graft model, full-thickness human skin (2 cm × 1.6 cm) from breast reduction surgeries was grafted onto full-thickness excisions on mouse backs, secured with sutures, and dressed for two weeks. Both models were allowed to stabilize for four weeks before treatment.

### Therapeutic Evaluation with the Human Tissue Implant Models

Four weeks post-implantation, mice received intrascar or intradermal injections of STP705 (20 μg siRNA in 50 μl HKP formulation per cm³ of tissue) or saline control. Injections were administered at five sites per graft/scar (four quadrants and center) to ensure even distribution. Three total injections were given at 5-day intervals. Scar/graft dimensions were measured weekly with calipers. At study endpoints, tissues were harvested for histology, qRT-PCR, Western blotting, hydroxyproline assay, and TUNEL staining. Apoptotic cells were quantified by counting TUNEL-positive nuclei in five random fields per section.

### Statistical Analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD for in vitro experiments and mean ± SE for in vivo studies. Statistical significance between two groups was determined by Student’s t-test. P-values < 0.05 were considered significant. All analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics v20.
