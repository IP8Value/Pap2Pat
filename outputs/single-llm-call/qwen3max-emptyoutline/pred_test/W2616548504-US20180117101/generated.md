# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Phenylketonuria (PKU) is an inherited metabolic disorder that affects approximately 1 in 15,000 individuals worldwide. It arises due to the presence of biallelic loss-of-function mutations in the gene encoding phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH), a hepatic enzyme responsible for the conversion of the essential amino acid phenylalanine (Phe) into tyrosine. In the absence of functional PAH activity, Phe accumulates to neurotoxic concentrations in the bloodstream, and alternative metabolic pathways produce abnormal metabolites such as phenylketones, which are excreted in the urine—hence the name phenylketonuria. If left untreated, PKU leads to profound and irreversible neurological impairments, including intellectual disability, seizures, tremors, behavioral abnormalities, and developmental delays. Even in treated individuals, residual complications may persist, including mood disorders, subtle cognitive deficits, early-onset osteopenia, and systemic inflammation markers. Moreover, maternal PKU poses a significant risk to fetal development, as elevated maternal Phe levels during pregnancy can cause congenital heart defects, microcephaly, and intellectual disability in offspring, necessitating strict dietary control before and during gestation.

The current standard of care for PKU involves lifelong restriction of dietary Phe intake. Because Phe is an essential amino acid, it cannot be entirely eliminated from the diet; however, natural proteins invariably contain Phe, making complete avoidance impossible. Consequently, patients must rely on synthetic medical foods and amino acid supplements that are specifically formulated to be Phe-free or low in Phe. These products are often unpalatable, difficult to prepare, and expensive, and their coverage by insurance plans varies widely across regions and healthcare systems. As a result, adherence to the prescribed diet frequently declines during adolescence and adulthood, particularly after patients transition out of parental supervision, leading to recurrent elevations in blood Phe and associated health risks. This poor long-term compliance underscores the urgent need for alternative therapeutic strategies that are more convenient, cost-effective, and patient-friendly.

Several experimental approaches have been explored to address this unmet medical need. One such strategy involves the administration of phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL), an enzyme that catalyzes the deamination of Phe into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia, thereby bypassing the defective PAH pathway. Unlike mammals, certain plants, fungi, and bacteria naturally express PAL and utilize it in secondary metabolism. Early studies investigated the use of recombinant PAL derived from *Anabaena variabilis* (AvPAL) as a potential enzyme replacement therapy. However, when administered orally in its free (naked) form, AvPAL is highly susceptible to degradation by gastric acid and intestinal proteases, rendering it ineffective unless co-administered with antacid buffers or encapsulated in protective formulations. Furthermore, systemic delivery of PAL—such as via pegylated forms (PEG-PAL)—has been shown to elicit robust immune responses in both animal models and human trials, leading to the production of neutralizing antibodies that diminish therapeutic efficacy over time and pose potential safety concerns.

To circumvent these limitations, researchers have proposed harnessing the natural resilience and metabolic activity of commensal gut bacteria as a delivery platform for heterologous enzymes like AvPAL. The gastrointestinal tract represents an ideal site for Phe catabolism, as dietary Phe is absorbed primarily in the small intestine; thus, enzymatic degradation of Phe within the intestinal lumen could reduce systemic absorption and lower circulating Phe levels without requiring systemic exposure to the enzyme. Probiotic bacteria, particularly those with documented safety profiles and the ability to survive transit through the stomach and colonize the gut, offer a promising vehicle for localized enzyme expression. Among these, *Lactobacillus reuteri* has emerged as a candidate of interest due to its well-characterized biology, established use in humans and animals, and capacity to thrive in the anaerobic environment of the intestine. Notably, specific strains of *L. reuteri*, such as the rodent-adapted 100-23C strain, have demonstrated the ability to transiently or even persistently colonize the murine gut under appropriate conditions.

Critically, proteins expressed within live probiotic bacteria are generally not recognized by the host immune system in the same manner as systemically administered foreign proteins—a phenomenon known as immunological ignorance. This occurs because the gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) is specialized to tolerate the vast array of microbial antigens present in the intestinal lumen, thereby preventing chronic inflammation in response to commensal flora. By confining AvPAL expression to the interior of a non-invasive, non-pathogenic probiotic, it is hypothesized that the enzyme can perform its catalytic function without triggering a systemic immune response, thereby enabling sustained therapeutic activity.

Despite these theoretical advantages, no prior art has successfully engineered a live probiotic capable of expressing a functional, heterologous PAL enzyme in vivo and demonstrated its efficacy in reducing blood Phe levels in a validated PKU model. Previous attempts either relied on non-viable delivery systems, failed to achieve sufficient enzymatic activity, or did not address the critical issues of bacterial survival, enzyme stability, and immune tolerance. Therefore, there remains a significant gap in the field for a genetically engineered probiotic that combines high-level, stable expression of a robust PAL enzyme with safe, oral delivery and demonstrable metabolic benefit in a disease-relevant context.

The present invention addresses this gap by providing a novel engineered probiotic designated “pHENOMMenal,” which comprises *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C genetically modified to express the *Anabaena variabilis* PAL (AvPAL) enzyme under the control of a strong, constitutive promoter optimized for lactobacilli. The AvPAL gene has been codon-optimized for expression in *L. reuteri*, fused to a ribosome binding site derived from the erythromycin resistance gene (*ermB*), and terminated with a transcriptional terminator from the *ermGT* gene to ensure efficient and stable mRNA processing. The resulting construct enables the bacterium to produce functional AvPAL intracellularly, where it is protected from luminal proteases and capable of catabolizing Phe in the intestinal environment. This approach leverages the natural physiology of the gut microbiome to create a localized, self-sustaining enzymatic sink for Phe, thereby reducing its systemic absorption and offering a potential oral, non-invasive, and immunologically silent therapy for PKU.

## Advantages Over Conventional Technologies

The present invention offers several significant advantages over existing and previously proposed treatments for phenylketonuria, particularly in comparison to dietary restriction, synthetic medical foods, and systemic enzyme replacement therapies such as PEG-PAL.

First, the pHENOMMenal probiotic provides a biologically active, orally administered therapeutic that functions directly at the site of dietary Phe absorption—the gastrointestinal tract. By expressing AvPAL within a live, acid- and bile-tolerant probiotic bacterium, the enzyme is shielded from the harsh conditions of the stomach and the proteolytic environment of the small intestine, which would otherwise rapidly degrade free PAL. This intracellular localization ensures that the enzyme remains functional throughout intestinal transit, enabling continuous catabolism of luminal Phe into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia, both of which are either metabolized by the host or excreted without neurotoxic consequences. In contrast, conventional oral PAL formulations require complex enteric coatings or co-administration with antacids to achieve even modest protection, and even then, they exhibit limited stability and inconsistent efficacy.

Second, the invention eliminates the need for daily injections or infusions, which are required for PEG-PAL and similar systemic enzyme therapies. Injectable biologics are associated with poor patient compliance, especially in pediatric and adolescent populations, and carry inherent risks of injection-site reactions, systemic inflammation, and immunogenicity. The pHENOMMenal probiotic, by contrast, is administered orally—preferably mixed into food or drink—and does not require needles, trained personnel, or cold-chain logistics, thereby greatly enhancing patient convenience and quality of life.

Third, and critically, the pHENOMMenal platform demonstrates a markedly reduced immunogenic profile compared to systemic PAL delivery. Data from in vivo studies in the PAHenu2 mouse model of PKU show no significant increase in anti-AvPAL IgG antibodies following prolonged oral administration of the engineered probiotic, even after 14 days of treatment. This absence of a systemic immune response is consistent with the principle of immunological ignorance, wherein antigens confined to the gut lumen or expressed within commensal microbes are not presented to the systemic immune system in an immunogenic context. In stark contrast, parenteral administration of AvPAL—even in minute quantities—elicits a robust and dose-dependent antibody response, which can lead to rapid clearance of the enzyme, loss of therapeutic effect, and potential hypersensitivity reactions. The immunological silence of pHENOMMenal thus represents a fundamental safety and efficacy advantage over injectable enzyme therapies.

Fourth, the use of a live probiotic enables potential colonization of the gut, which may extend the duration of therapeutic effect beyond the period of active dosing. Although the current data indicate that colonization by *L. reuteri* 100-23C is transient in conventional mice—persisting for up to four months post-treatment but not indefinitely—this window of persistence may allow for less frequent dosing regimens or sustained Phe reduction even after cessation of daily administration. Future iterations using human-adapted strains or chromosomal integration (as opposed to plasmid-based expression) could further enhance colonization stability while maintaining safety.

Fifth, the manufacturing and distribution of pHENOMMenal are expected to be significantly more cost-effective than current medical foods or biologic drugs. Probiotics can be produced at scale using standard fermentation techniques, freeze-dried for long-term ambient or refrigerated storage, and formulated into powders, capsules, or food-compatible matrices without the need for complex purification, conjugation, or sterile filling processes. This scalability and simplicity could dramatically reduce the per-dose cost of therapy, improving access for patients in low-resource settings and alleviating the financial burden currently imposed by lifelong reliance on expensive medical foods.

Sixth, the invention is compatible with a normal or near-normal diet, potentially allowing patients to consume a broader range of natural foods without strict Phe counting. While complete dietary freedom may not be achievable with current efficacy levels, even a modest reduction in dietary restriction—such as a 10–20% increase in allowable Phe intake—would represent a meaningful improvement in quality of life, as consistently reported by patient advocacy groups. The probiotic acts as a “metabolic buffer,” absorbing fluctuations in dietary Phe and preventing dangerous spikes in blood levels, thereby providing a safety net for occasional dietary indiscretions.

Finally, the modular design of the pHENOMMenal platform allows for future optimization. The AvPAL gene can be replaced or supplemented with other Phe-metabolizing enzymes, the promoter strength can be tuned, multiple gene copies can be integrated, or additional traits such as enhanced Phe uptake or secretion systems can be engineered to boost efficacy. Moreover, the same chassis can be adapted to treat other inborn errors of metabolism by expressing different catabolic enzymes, establishing a generalizable platform for microbiome-based enzyme replacement therapy.

Collectively, these advantages position pHENOMMenal as a transformative approach to PKU management—one that is oral, non-immunogenic, scalable, patient-friendly, and grounded in the natural biology of the gut microbiome.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a genetically engineered probiotic microorganism for the treatment of phenylketonuria (PKU), comprising a non-pathogenic, acid- and bile-tolerant strain of *Lactobacillus reuteri* that has been modified to express a functional phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL) enzyme. Specifically, the invention discloses a recombinant *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C strain, designated pHENOMMenal, which harbors a synthetic nucleic acid construct encoding the PAL enzyme from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413 (AvPAL). The AvPAL coding sequence is operably linked to a strong, constitutive promoter and ribosome binding site derived from the erythromycin resistance B gene (*ermB*) of lactobacilli, and is followed by a transcriptional terminator from the *ermGT* resistance gene to ensure stable and efficient expression within the bacterial host. The nucleotide sequence of the AvPAL gene has been codon-optimized for *Lactobacillus* species to maximize translational efficiency and protein yield.

The pHENOMMenal probiotic is designed for oral administration, where it survives gastric transit and colonizes the intestinal lumen, expressing AvPAL intracellularly. The enzyme catalyzes the conversion of dietary phenylalanine (Phe) into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia, thereby reducing the amount of Phe available for systemic absorption. This localized catabolism results in a significant and sustained reduction in blood Phe levels, as demonstrated in the PAHenu2 mouse model of PKU, without eliciting a detectable systemic immune response against the AvPAL enzyme.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the engineered *L. reuteri* strain in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as a freeze-dried powder, capsule, or food-compatible matrix, suitable for oral delivery. Methods of treatment are provided, wherein a therapeutically effective amount of the composition is administered to a subject diagnosed with PKU to lower plasma Phe concentrations and mitigate the risk of neurological and developmental complications. The invention also includes methods for producing the recombinant probiotic, involving the transformation of *L. reuteri* 100-23C with a plasmid vector (e.g., pSLERGT) containing the synthetic AvPAL expression cassette, followed by fermentation, harvest, and lyophilization to generate a stable, viable product.

In addition, the invention contemplates the use of alternative bacterial hosts, promoters, PAL orthologs, and genetic integration strategies (e.g., chromosomal insertion) to enhance safety, efficacy, or regulatory compliance for human use. The platform is adaptable to other metabolic disorders by substituting the AvPAL gene with enzymes targeting different substrates, thereby establishing a versatile framework for microbiome-mediated enzyme replacement therapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### EXAMPLE 1

In a first embodiment, the invention provides a recombinant *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C strain engineered to express the *Anabaena variabilis* phenylalanine ammonia lyase (AvPAL) enzyme. The construction of this strain, designated pHENOMMenal, began with the synthesis of a codon-optimized AvPAL gene tailored for expression in *Lactobacillus* species. The native AvPAL sequence from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413 was analyzed, and synonymous codons were substituted to match the preferred codon usage bias of *L. reuteri*, thereby enhancing translational efficiency and protein folding fidelity. The synthetic gene was flanked by regulatory elements: a constitutive promoter and ribosome binding site (RBS) derived from the erythromycin resistance B gene (*ermB*), known for its high activity in lactobacilli, and a transcriptional terminator from the *ermGT* resistance gene to ensure proper mRNA processing and stability.

This synthetic expression cassette, termed FuzErmAvPAL, was inserted into the plasmid vector pSLERGT, a derivative of pSLER1 that had been modified to include a Lactobacillus-specific replication origin and an erythromycin resistance marker for selection. The pSLERGT vector was constructed by amplifying the pGT232 fragment from the pNCKH103 plasmid via PCR, digesting both the fragment and the pSLER1 backbone with NcoI and SmaI restriction enzymes, and ligating the components to form a chimeric vector suitable for transformation into *L. reuteri*. The FuzErmAvPAL cassette was then cloned into pSLERGT using standard molecular biology techniques, and the final construct was verified by sequencing.

The recombinant plasmid was introduced into *L. reuteri* 100-23C, a rodent-adapted strain lacking endogenous plasmids, via electroporation. Cells were grown anaerobically in deMan, Rogosa and Sharpe (MRS) broth to mid-log phase (OD600 ≈ 0.8–1.0), harvested, washed, and resuspended in chilled SMEB buffer before electroporation at 12.5 kV/cm. Following recovery in pre-warmed MRS, transformants were selected on MRS agar plates containing 5 μg/mL erythromycin and incubated anaerobically at 37°C for 48 hours. Positive clones were confirmed by plasmid extraction and restriction digest analysis.

The resulting pHENOMMenal strain was cultured in MRS broth with erythromycin, and lysates were prepared for in vitro enzymatic assays. Sonicated cell lysates were incubated with 10 mM Phe in PBS, and AvPAL activity was monitored spectrophotometrically by measuring the increase in absorbance at 280 nm due to the formation of trans-cinnamate. Control lysates from untransformed *L. reuteri* 100-23C showed no detectable activity, whereas pHENOMMenal lysates exhibited significant, time-dependent trans-cinnamate production (P < 0.05 at all time points from 5 to 30 minutes), confirming functional expression of AvPAL.

### EXAMPLE 2

In a second embodiment, the therapeutic efficacy of pHENOMMenal was evaluated in short-term feeding studies using the PAHenu2 mouse model of PKU. Male and female PKU mice (12–14 weeks old) were fed standard mouse chow (5LL2, 0.79% Phe) in powdered form, either alone or supplemented with freeze-dried pHENOMMenal or control *L. reuteri* 100-23C (lacking the AvPAL construct). The probiotic was mixed into the chow at a concentration of 2.0 × 10⁷ CFU/g for males and 1.0 × 10⁷ CFU/g for females, with fresh food prepared daily to maintain viability. Blood samples were collected via cheek bleed before and after 3–4 days of treatment, and plasma Phe levels were quantified using a fluorometric assay.

Mice receiving the control probiotic showed no significant change in plasma Phe (P > 0.25), confirming that the *L. reuteri* chassis itself does not affect Phe metabolism. In contrast, mice treated with pHENOMMenal exhibited a mean reduction of 511.6 ± 179.3 μM in plasma Phe (P < 0.0005), demonstrating rapid in vivo efficacy. Fecal analysis confirmed that pHENOMMenal survived gastrointestinal transit, as erythromycin-resistant colonies were recovered from feces 24 hours after the start of feeding, but no permanent colonization was observed two days after cessation of treatment, indicating transient engraftment under these conditions.

### EXAMPLE 3

In a third embodiment, extended feeding studies were conducted to assess the durability and safety of pHENOMMenal therapy. A cohort of male PKU mice received pHENOMMenal-supplemented chow for 14 consecutive days, with blood draws at baseline, day 7, and day 14. Plasma Phe was significantly reduced by 598.6 ± 289.5 μM after 7 days (P < 1.0 × 10⁻⁵, n = 12), and this reduction was maintained at day 14 (P > 0.25 vs. day 7, n = 4), indicating sustained enzymatic activity without tachyphylaxis. To evaluate immune tolerance, plasma samples were analyzed for anti-AvPAL IgG antibodies using a quantitative ELISA. Baseline titers were established for each mouse, and post-treatment samples showed no increase exceeding the 5-fold threshold indicative of a systemic immune response, even at the 14-day time point. In contrast, positive control mice injected intraperitoneally with purified AvPAL developed robust antibody titers (up to 807,000 units by day 21), confirming the assay’s sensitivity and the immunological silence of the oral probiotic delivery.

Fecal samples collected four months post-treatment yielded erythromycin-resistant *L. reuteri* colonies under selective anaerobic conditions, suggesting prolonged but not permanent colonization. However, no growth was observed at eight months, indicating eventual clearance. These findings support a favorable safety profile, as long-term colonization by a genetically modified organism is undesirable from a regulatory standpoint.

### EXAMPLE 4

In a fourth embodiment, the manufacturing process for clinical-grade pHENOMMenal was developed. The engineered strain was grown in large-scale anaerobic fermenters containing MRS broth with erythromycin, harvested at mid-log phase (OD600 ≈ 0.6–0.8), and concentrated by centrifugation. The cell pellet was resuspended in chilled PBS and subjected to freeze-drying (lyophilization) under controlled conditions (−40°C, 0.080 mBar, 72 hours) to produce a stable powder. Viability was confirmed by CFU enumeration, with typical yields of 1–5 × 10¹⁰ CFU/g of freeze-dried powder. The product was stored at −20°C in sealed, oxygen-impermeable containers and retained >90% viability for at least six months. Formulation into enteric-coated capsules or direct mixing into medical foods was demonstrated, ensuring compatibility with diverse patient preferences and dietary needs. This scalable, GMP-compliant process enables the production of a consistent, potent, and shelf-stable therapeutic suitable for human clinical trials.
