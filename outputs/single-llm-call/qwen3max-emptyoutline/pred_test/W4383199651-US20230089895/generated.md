# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of digital advertising and, more specifically, to systems and methods for optimizing real-time bidding (RTB) in programmatic advertising environments. The invention provides a novel framework for adaptive risk-aware bidding that integrates prediction uncertainty and dynamic risk tendency within a reinforcement learning architecture to maximize key performance indicators such as total clicks or return on ad spend under budget constraints. The technical domain encompasses computational advertising, machine learning, reinforcement learning, Bayesian inference, and financial risk modeling as applied to online auction-based advertising systems. The invention is particularly relevant to demand-side platforms (DSPs) that participate in RTB auctions on behalf of advertisers, where accurate bid optimization is critical for campaign performance and competitive advantage in highly dynamic market conditions.

The invention addresses fundamental challenges in RTB systems where traditional approaches fail to account for the inherent uncertainties in user response prediction models and the dynamic risk preferences of bidders based on their current resource state (remaining budget and remaining auction opportunities). By theoretically establishing the relationship between prediction uncertainty and risk tendency through Value at Risk (VaR) analysis, the invention provides a mathematically grounded framework for modifying ad impression values that enables optimal bid price determination. The technical field further extends to the integration of expert knowledge-based risk modeling with self-supervised reinforcement learning techniques to automatically adapt risk preferences without extensive manual parameter tuning, thereby providing both interpretable and automated solutions for different operational requirements.

## BACKGROUND

Real-time bidding (RTB) has emerged as a dominant paradigm in digital advertising, representing a multi-billion dollar global market where demand-side platforms (DSPs) programmatically purchase individual ad impressions on behalf of advertisers through instantaneous auctions. The success of a DSP in this highly competitive environment critically depends on its bid optimization capability, which aims to maximize agreed-upon key performance indicators (KPIs) such as total clicks or return on ad spend while operating under strict budget constraints. Traditional bid optimization approaches typically involve two sequential steps: user response prediction and bid price determination. User response prediction models estimate the true value of potential ad impressions, commonly measured as click-through rate (CTR) or conversion rate (CVR), while bid price determination generates optimal bid prices for incoming bid requests based on these estimated values.

Despite substantial progress in developing sophisticated prediction models—including linear models, factorization machines, and deep learning architectures—these approaches consistently return point estimates that fail to capture the inherent uncertainties present in real-world advertising data. These uncertainties arise from incomplete data collection, unavoidable data noise, and the sparse nature of user interaction signals, particularly for rare feature combinations or new users. Previous research has demonstrated that explicitly accounting for prediction uncertainty can significantly improve campaign performance, yet existing bid optimization frameworks largely ignore this critical dimension.

Concurrently, the dynamic nature of RTB markets necessitates modeling the sequential dependencies between bid requests under budget constraints, considering varying levels of market competition across different time periods and user segments. Recent advances have framed the bidding process as a Markov decision process (MDP) and employed reinforcement learning techniques—both model-based and model-free—to optimize bidding strategies over sequences of auctions. However, these reinforcement learning approaches fundamentally assume that the underlying impression value estimates are accurate and deterministic, an assumption that rarely holds in practice due to the aforementioned prediction uncertainties.

The intersection of these two limitations—ignoring prediction uncertainty in value estimation and assuming perfect value knowledge in sequential decision-making—has created a significant gap in RTB bid optimization capabilities. A comprehensive solution requires simultaneous consideration of three inherently correlated components: the uncertainties associated with impression value predictions, the current state of the DSP (characterized by remaining budget and future auction opportunities), and the prevailing market competition dynamics. These components collectively determine the DSP's appropriate risk tendency, defined as its willingness to take risks by bidding more aggressively or conservatively based on its resource constraints and market conditions.

Prior art in RTB bidding includes linear bidding strategies that scale predicted CTR values by a fixed multiplier, and reinforcement learning approaches that model the sequential nature of bidding but rely solely on point estimates of impression values. While some work has attempted to incorporate risk management through Value at Risk (VaR) concepts from financial mathematics, these approaches have failed to properly model the intrinsic interactions between market environment dynamics and the bidder's internal state. Specifically, existing methods do not account for how a bidder's risk preference should dynamically adapt based on its remaining resources relative to remaining opportunities, nor do they provide a theoretically grounded mechanism for combining prediction uncertainty with dynamic risk preferences to generate modified impression values that lead to optimal bidding decisions.

Furthermore, practical implementation of risk-aware bidding strategies faces significant challenges in parameter tuning and adaptability. Expert knowledge-based approaches require careful selection of multiple hyperparameters through extensive historical data analysis, making them labor-intensive and potentially suboptimal when market conditions change. Conversely, purely data-driven approaches may lack interpretability and stability, particularly when training data is limited or when the reinforcement learning process encounters exploration-exploitation trade-offs that affect convergence.

The background art thus reveals a clear need for an integrated framework that simultaneously addresses prediction uncertainty, dynamic risk tendency, and sequential decision-making under budget constraints, while providing both theoretically sound foundations and practical implementation mechanisms that balance interpretability with automation. Such a framework would enable DSPs to make more informed bidding decisions that appropriately account for both the reliability of their prediction models and their strategic positioning within the competitive auction environment.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides an adaptive risk-aware bidding algorithm for real-time bidding (RTB) systems that simultaneously considers prediction uncertainty and dynamic risk tendency to optimize bidding performance under budget constraints. The invention establishes a novel theoretical framework that reveals the intrinsic relationship between prediction uncertainty and risk tendency through Value at Risk (VaR) analysis, enabling the formulation of modified ad impression values that lead to optimal bid prices in sequential decision-making processes.

In accordance with the invention, the value of an ad impression is decomposed into a weighted sum comprising the mean predicted click-through rate (pCTR) and a compound term that simultaneously reflects prediction uncertainty and the bidder's risk tendency. This formulation is mathematically proven to achieve the optimal VaR bid price that maximizes the Value at Risk of cumulative ad impression value over the remaining auction sequence. The risk tendency component is defined as a function of the bidder's resource state, characterized by the remaining auction number and remaining budget, and is independent of the specific bid request features, reflecting the rational bidder's strategic positioning rather than individual impression characteristics.

The invention provides two distinct instantiations for determining the risk tendency of a demand-side platform (DSP). The first instantiation leverages expert knowledge of RTB dynamics to establish three fundamental properties that any rational risk tendency function should satisfy: (1) the sufficiency of remaining budget determines the sign of risk tendency, where positive risk tendency indicates preference for winning auctions; (2) risk tendency exhibits monotonicity with respect to remaining budget and auction number, increasing with available budget and decreasing with remaining opportunities; and (3) under scenarios with large remaining resources, risk tendency depends on the ratio of budget to remaining auctions and market competition levels. These properties lead to a mathematically formulated risk tendency function that incorporates budget richness thresholds and hyperparameters controlling the slope of risk preference transitions.

The second instantiation employs a self-supervised reinforcement learning approach that automatically learns the risk tendency function from bidding experience without requiring manual parameter tuning. This method utilizes an experience buffer to store successful bidding episodes characterized by high cumulative rewards, along with their corresponding resource states and risk tendency values. A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) mapping function is trained to approximate the optimal risk tendency by minimizing mean square error between predicted and experienced risk tendencies, effectively learning from historical successes while discarding suboptimal experiences through buffer management policies.

The invention further provides a complete bid price determination mechanism that integrates the modified ad impression values into a model-based reinforcement learning framework. This framework iteratively updates cumulative reward estimates based on average impression values and market price distributions, then calculates optimal bid prices by solving for the price point that balances immediate reward against future opportunity costs. The resulting bid prices are constrained by remaining budget and optimized to maximize cumulative performance metrics over the entire auction sequence.

Experimental validation demonstrates that the adaptive risk-aware bidding algorithm significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across multiple real-world datasets and budget scenarios. Both expert knowledge-based and self-supervised instantiations achieve superior performance compared to traditional reinforcement learning bidding strategies that ignore prediction uncertainty and dynamic risk preferences. Ablation studies confirm that both prediction uncertainty modeling and dynamic risk tendency adaptation contribute meaningfully to performance improvements, with risk tendency modeling proving particularly critical for optimal bid optimization.

The invention thus provides a comprehensive solution that addresses the fundamental limitations of existing RTB bidding approaches by theoretically unifying prediction uncertainty quantification with dynamic risk preference modeling within a reinforcement learning framework. The dual instantiation approach offers flexibility for different operational requirements, providing both interpretable expert-guided solutions and automated self-learning alternatives that eliminate extensive manual tuning efforts while maintaining competitive performance.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive framework for adaptive risk-aware bidding in real-time bidding (RTB) systems, addressing the critical limitations of existing approaches that fail to simultaneously account for prediction uncertainty and dynamic risk preferences. The detailed description begins with the foundational problem formulation and proceeds through the core components of the invention, including uncertainty quantification, theoretical relationship establishment, risk tendency modeling, and bid price determination.

The RTB system operates as a sequential decision-making process where a demand-side platform (DSP) acts on behalf of an advertiser to compete for advertisement auctions generated by user visits. Each auction presents a bid request containing user and context features, and the DSP must determine an optimal bid price to maximize cumulative ad impression value while respecting budget constraints. The invention formulates this bid optimization problem as a Markov decision process (MDP) at the episode level, where each episode consists of T sequential bid auctions accompanied by a budget B. The state space is defined as S = {(t, b, x_t) | t ∈ {0, 1, ..., T}, b ∈ [0, B], x_t ∈ X}, where t represents the remaining auction number, b denotes the remaining budget, and x_t is the feature vector of the current bid request from the set X of all possible bid request features.

For each state s = (t, b, x_t), the action space A_s comprises all possible bid prices in the range {0, ..., b}, constrained by the remaining budget. The state transition dynamics account for both winning and losing scenarios in second-price auctions. When the bid price a exceeds the market price δ (representing the highest competing bid), the DSP wins the auction and transitions to state (t-1, b-δ, x_{t-1}) with probability p_x(x_{t-1}) ∫_0^a m(δ)dδ, where p_x(x_{t-1}) is the probability of observing feature vector x_{t-1} and m(δ) is the market price distribution. Conversely, when a ≤ δ, the DSP loses the auction and transitions to state (t-1, b, x_{t-1}) with probability p_x(x_{t-1}) ∫_{a+1}^∞ m(δ)dδ. The immediate reward is given by the predicted click-through rate r_mean(x_t) when winning the auction, and zero otherwise.

Central to the invention is the quantification of prediction uncertainty in user response prediction models. The preferred embodiment employs Bayesian logistic regression to explicitly measure uncertainties in predicted CTR (pCTR) values. In this approach, model weights w are treated as random variables following Gaussian distributions rather than fixed parameters. The posterior weight distribution p(w|x, y) is maximized given bid request features x and click labels y, yielding a probability distribution over predicted CTR values p(ŷ|x). From this distribution, the mean r_mean(x) = E_p(ŷ|x)[ŷ] and standard deviation r_std(x) = √Var_p(ŷ|x)[ŷ] are computed, where the standard deviation directly reflects the uncertainty associated with the pCTR prediction for feature vector x. This uncertainty quantification captures both data completeness (through feature frequency effects on weight variance) and model confidence in predictions.

The invention establishes a theoretical foundation for integrating prediction uncertainty with risk tendency through Value at Risk (VaR) analysis borrowed from financial mathematics. The core insight is that the value of an ad impression θ(t, b, x_t) should be formulated as a linear combination: θ(t, b, x_t) = r_mean(x_t) + β(t, b)r_std(x_t), where β(t, b) represents the bidder's risk tendency at resource state (t, b). This formulation is theoretically motivated by VaR principles, which estimate the potential underestimation or overestimation of predicted CTR with a given probability confidence level.

The VaR of cumulative ad impression value V_a_λ(t, b, x_t) is defined as V_a(t, b, x_t) - λ(t, b)V_a_std(t, b, x_t), where V_a(t, b, x_t) and V_a_std(t, b, x_t) represent the cumulative estimated impression value and uncertainty of winning ads under bidding strategy a(t, b, x_t), and λ(t, b) is a state-associated coefficient balancing estimated value against uncertainty across all remaining auctions. The invention proves through Cantelli's inequality that this linear combination guarantees probability bounds consistent with VaR requirements, ensuring that the modified impression value appropriately accounts for worst-case scenarios within acceptable risk tolerance levels.

The risk tendency function β(t, b) is designed to reflect the rational bidder's strategic positioning based on resource constraints. The invention identifies three essential properties that any valid risk tendency function must satisfy. First, the sign of risk tendency is determined by budget sufficiency: positive risk tendency indicates preference for winning auctions when budget is sufficient, while negative risk tendency reflects conservative bidding when resources are constrained. Second, risk tendency exhibits monotonicity: it increases with remaining budget b (∂β/∂b > 0) and decreases with remaining auction number t (∂β/∂t < 0), reflecting the natural relationship between available resources and risk-taking capacity. Third, under scenarios with large remaining resources (early campaign stages), risk tendency depends on the budget-to-auction ratio b/t and market competition levels.

To implement these properties, the invention introduces the concept of budget richness quantified through expected bid price U(t, b). Given market price distribution m(δ) and resource state (t, b), U(t, b) represents the expected bid price that would deplete the remaining budget evenly across remaining auctions, calculated by solving ∫_0^{U(t,b)} δm(δ)dδ / ∫_0^{U(t,b)} m(δ)dδ = b/t. This equation ensures that the expected actual cost per winning auction equals the allocated budget per auction under even distribution assumptions.

The expert knowledge-based risk tendency instantiation is then formulated as β(t, b) = α tanh(U(t, b) - Û), where α > 0 controls the slope of risk preference transitions, Û represents a budget richness threshold tuned from historical data, and the hyperbolic tangent function confines risk tendency within (-1, 1). This formulation satisfies all three identified properties: the sign depends on whether U(t, b) ≥ Û, monotonicity follows from U(t, b)'s dependence on b/t ratio, and the functional form naturally incorporates market competition through the market price distribution embedded in U(t, b) calculation.

The self-supervised risk tendency instantiation eliminates manual parameter tuning through a reinforcement learning approach that learns optimal risk tendencies from successful bidding experiences. This method maintains an experience buffer storing quaternary tuples B = (t, b, β(t, b), V_episode), where V_episode represents the cumulative reward achieved in the entire episode using risk tendency β(t, b) at resource state (t, b). The buffer prioritizes "good" experiences by retaining only those with higher cumulative rewards, removing lowest-performing samples when capacity is exceeded. A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) mapping function β_mlp(t, b) = MLP(t, b; W_mlp) with trainable weights W_mlp approximates the optimal risk tendency function. Training proceeds by sampling batches from the experience buffer and minimizing mean square loss between predicted and stored risk tendencies, effectively learning from historical successes while adapting to changing market conditions.

The bid price determination mechanism integrates the modified ad impression values into a model-based reinforcement learning framework. The cumulative reward V(t, b, x_t) represents the expected cumulative reward starting from state (t, b, x_t) with optimal bidding, updated according to the Bellman equation incorporating both winning and losing scenarios. The cumulative reward without observation of specific features V(t, b) is obtained by integrating over the entire feature space, yielding V(t, b) = ∫_X p_x(x_{t-1})[r_mean(x_{t-1}) + ∫_0^b g(δ)m(δ)dδ]dx_{t-1}, where g(δ) = V(t-1, b-δ) - V(t-1, b) represents the marginal value of winning versus losing at market price δ.

The optimal bid price a*(t, b, x_t) is calculated by finding the price that maximizes the immediate reward adjusted by future opportunity costs. Specifically, the bid price satisfies θ(t, b, x_t) = V(t-1, b) - V(t-1, b-a*), where θ(t, b, x_t) is the modified ad impression value incorporating both prediction uncertainty and risk tendency. Due to the monotonic properties of cumulative reward V(t, b) with respect to budget b and the decreasing nature of g(δ) with respect to δ, there exists a unique integer price A within [0, b] satisfying g(A) ≥ 0 and g(A+1) < 0, which constitutes the optimal bid price.

The complete algorithm operates as follows: for each incoming bid request with feature vector x_t at state (t, b, x_t), the system first computes the mean pCTR r_mean(x_t) and standard deviation r_std(x_t) using the Bayesian logistic regression model. The risk tendency β(t, b) is then determined either through the expert knowledge-based formula or the self-supervised MLP mapping function. The modified ad impression value θ(t, b, x_t) = r_mean(x_t) + β(t, b)r_std(x_t) is calculated and used in the bid price determination equation to compute the optimal bid price a*(t, b, x_t). After submitting the bid and observing the auction outcome, the system updates its state and continues the process until episode termination.

Experimental validation demonstrates the superiority of the adaptive risk-aware bidding framework across multiple dimensions. Comparative evaluations against state-of-the-art baselines including linear bidding strategies and traditional reinforcement learning approaches show consistent performance improvements in terms of total clicks achieved under various budget constraints. Ablation studies confirm that both prediction uncertainty modeling and dynamic risk tendency adaptation contribute significantly to performance gains, with risk tendency modeling proving particularly critical for optimal bid optimization. Hyperparameter sensitivity analyses reveal robust performance across reasonable parameter ranges, while visualization of learned risk tendencies demonstrates alignment between expert knowledge-based and self-supervised approaches, validating the theoretical foundations of the invention.

The invention thus provides a complete, theoretically grounded, and practically implementable solution for adaptive risk-aware bidding in RTB systems, addressing the fundamental limitations of existing approaches through the simultaneous consideration of prediction uncertainty and dynamic risk preferences within a reinforcement learning framework. The dual instantiation approach offers operational flexibility, enabling both interpretable expert-guided implementations and automated self-learning alternatives that maintain competitive performance while eliminating extensive manual tuning requirements.
