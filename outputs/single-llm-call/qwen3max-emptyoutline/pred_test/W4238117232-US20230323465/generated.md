# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular diagnostics for thyroid neoplasms, and more specifically to methods, compositions, and kits for the preoperative detection and classification of thyroid nodules based on the expression levels of a panel of five specific transcript isoforms—namely CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—in conjunction with a thyrocyte-specific load control marker, TPO1. The invention provides a highly sensitive and specific molecular assay that enables clinicians to distinguish benign thyroid lesions—including adenomatoid nodules (AN), follicular adenomas (FA), Hürthle cell adenomas (HA), and noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP)—from malignant thyroid carcinomas such as follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma (FVPTC) and classical papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). This distinction is critical in light of recent reclassifications in thyroid pathology that have rendered many existing molecular diagnostic platforms inadequate due to their inability to differentiate indolent NIFTP lesions from invasive malignancies. The invention further encompasses sample preparation protocols optimized for fine needle aspiration (FNA) specimens, quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)-based detection systems, and integrated diagnostic algorithms that yield actionable clinical guidance for surgical decision-making. By leveraging isoform-specific expression signatures that are absent in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and normalized against a thyrocyte-restricted reference gene, the disclosed technology overcomes key limitations of prior art assays, including contamination by non-thyroid cellular components and misclassification of NIFTP as malignant. The invention thus fills an urgent unmet need in endocrine oncology for a reliable, preoperative molecular test capable of reducing diagnostic uncertainty, minimizing unnecessary thyroidectomies, and enabling personalized management strategies for patients with indeterminate thyroid nodules.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Thyroid cancer represents one of the most rapidly increasing malignancies worldwide, with incidence rates in the United States having nearly quadrupled over the past fifteen years, primarily driven by the rising diagnosis of papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). Concurrently, thyroid nodules are detectable by ultrasound in more than half of the adult population, necessitating accurate diagnostic stratification to avoid overtreatment of benign conditions while ensuring timely intervention for true malignancies. Fine needle aspiration (FNA) cytopathology remains the cornerstone of preoperative evaluation; however, a significant proportion—up to 30%—of FNA samples yield indeterminate cytological results, classified under the Bethesda System categories III (atypia of undetermined significance/follicular lesion of undetermined significance), IV (follicular neoplasm/suspicious for follicular neoplasm), and V (suspicious for malignancy). These indeterminate diagnoses pose substantial clinical challenges, often leading to diagnostic lobectomy or total thyroidectomy despite the fact that the majority of such nodules ultimately prove benign upon histopathological examination. Compounding this diagnostic ambiguity is the recent reclassification by the American Thyroid Association of a subset of tumors previously diagnosed as follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma (FVPTC) into a new entity termed noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP). NIFTP is now recognized as an indolent neoplasm with negligible risk of recurrence or metastasis and is no longer considered malignant, thereby warranting conservative management rather than aggressive surgical intervention. Critically, NIFTP cannot be reliably distinguished from invasive FVPTC or other malignant subtypes by conventional cytopathology alone, as the definitive diagnosis requires postoperative histological assessment of capsular and vascular invasion—a feature inaccessible in preoperative FNA samples.

In response to these limitations, molecular testing has emerged over the past decade as a complementary strategy to refine preoperative risk stratification. Commercially available platforms such as ThyroSeq v3 (a next-generation sequencing-based assay), Afirma Gene Expression Classifier (a microarray- and RNA-seq-based classifier), and ThygenX/ThyraMIR (which combines DNA mutation analysis with microRNA profiling) have been integrated into clinical practice to guide surgical decisions. Despite their utility, these tests suffer from significant drawbacks. DNA-based assays are confounded by the presence of driver mutations—such as RAS—that occur with comparable frequency in both benign follicular adenomas and malignant carcinomas, including NIFTP, thereby reducing specificity. Moreover, all major commercial platforms were developed and validated prior to the formal recognition of NIFTP as a non-malignant entity, and consequently continue to classify NIFTP cases as “suspicious” or “positive” for cancer. Recent studies have demonstrated a marked decline in the positive predictive value (PPV) of these tests when NIFTP is correctly categorized as benign: for instance, the PPV of ThyroSeq panels has dropped from 43% to as low as 14%, and that of the Afirma classifier from 30% to 25%. This erosion in diagnostic performance underscores a critical gap in current molecular diagnostics—the inability to differentiate truly malignant thyroid carcinomas from indolent NIFTP lesions using preoperative FNA material.

Prior efforts by the inventors identified over 75 differentially expressed transcripts through genome-wide microarray analysis of 125 thyroid tumor specimens spanning benign and malignant subtypes. Subsequent validation narrowed this list to a candidate 3-gene panel with diagnostic potential. However, those earlier studies did not account for the NIFTP classification or address the technical challenges inherent in FNA-based molecular testing, particularly the variable cellular composition of aspirates and contamination by non-thyroid cells such as PBMCs. Quantitative molecular assays are especially vulnerable to such admixtures, as standard RNA load controls like GAPDH reflect total nucleic acid content rather than thyrocyte-specific input, potentially diluting true signal and yielding false-negative or equivocal results. While physical separation techniques (e.g., magnetic bead-based enrichment of epithelial cells) can mitigate this issue, they reduce assay sensitivity and add complexity, making them less suitable for routine clinical use. There exists, therefore, a pressing need for a molecular diagnostic approach that (1) incorporates the updated pathological understanding of NIFTP, (2) utilizes biomarkers that are both highly discriminatory between benign and malignant states and absent in contaminating blood cells, and (3) employs a thyrocyte-specific internal control to normalize expression data accurately in heterogeneous FNA samples. The present invention directly addresses these unmet needs by identifying and validating a novel panel of five splice isoforms—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—that exhibit robust differential expression across thyroid tumor subtypes, show no detectable expression in PBMCs, and can be reliably quantified in single-pass FNA specimens when normalized to the thyrocyte-restricted isoform TPO1. This combination enables a high-performance diagnostic assay with improved specificity and clinical utility in the modern era of thyroid nodule management.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel molecular diagnostic platform for the preoperative classification of thyroid nodules, particularly those yielding indeterminate cytology on fine needle aspiration (FNA). The core of the invention resides in the discovery and validation of a biomarker panel comprising five specific transcript isoforms—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—whose combined expression profile effectively discriminates benign thyroid lesions (including adenomatoid nodules, follicular adenomas, Hürthle cell adenomas, and the recently defined noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features, or NIFTP) from malignant carcinomas (specifically follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma and classical papillary thyroid carcinoma). Critically, each of these five isoforms demonstrates negligible or undetectable expression in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), thereby eliminating a major source of confounding signal in FNA samples, which frequently contain admixed blood-derived cells. To further enhance analytical accuracy, the invention introduces the use of a thyrocyte-specific load control marker, TPO1—an isoform of thyroid peroxidase—that is stably expressed across differentiated thyroid tumor subtypes yet absent in PBMCs. This reference gene enables precise normalization of target gene expression to the actual number of thyroid follicular cells present in the FNA specimen, overcoming the limitations of conventional total RNA controls such as GAPDH.

The invention encompasses methods for preparing FNA samples for molecular analysis, including RNA isolation protocols optimized for low-input, single-pass aspirates, and reverse transcription procedures that maximize cDNA yield from limited material. It further provides quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) assays utilizing isoform-specific primers designed to amplify only the diagnostically relevant splice variants of the five target genes and the TPO1 reference. A diagnostic algorithm is disclosed wherein the expression level of each target gene is calculated relative to TPO1 (as ΔCt = Ct_target – Ct_TPO1), z-transformed to standardize across genes, and summed to generate a composite z-ΔCt score. This score is then compared to a predetermined threshold—preferably -1—to classify the nodule as benign or malignant. At this threshold, the assay achieves a sensitivity of 75% and a specificity of 91% in distinguishing benign/NIFTP from malignant tumors, corresponding to a negative predictive value (NPV) of 91% and a positive predictive value (PPV) of 74% under epidemiologically realistic prevalence assumptions. The invention also includes kits containing all necessary reagents—primers, enzymes, buffers, and controls—for performing the sample preparation, amplification, and detection steps in a standardized, clinically applicable format. Additionally, the invention provides therapeutic guidance: patients whose FNA samples yield a composite score below the threshold may be candidates for active surveillance or limited surgery, whereas those above the threshold should undergo definitive surgical resection. By integrating isoform-specific biomarkers, a thyrocyte-restricted reference, and a validated scoring algorithm, the present invention offers a significant advance over existing molecular tests, particularly in its ability to correctly classify NIFTP as benign, thereby reducing unnecessary surgeries and aligning diagnostic output with current pathological and clinical standards.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. Definitions

As used herein, the term “thyroid nodule” refers to a discrete lesion within the thyroid gland that is radiologically distinct from the surrounding thyroid parenchyma, regardless of whether it is benign or malignant. The term “fine needle aspiration” or “FNA” denotes a minimally invasive procedure in which a thin needle is inserted into a thyroid nodule to extract cells for cytological or molecular analysis; FNA samples may be collected preoperatively in an outpatient clinic setting or intraoperatively immediately prior to surgical resection. “Indeterminate cytology” refers to FNA results classified under Bethesda categories III, IV, or V, where the cytomorphological features are insufficient to definitively diagnose benignity or malignancy. “Noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features” or “NIFTP” is a recently defined thyroid tumor entity characterized by follicular growth pattern, papillary-like nuclear features, absence of capsular or vascular invasion, and indolent clinical behavior; it is considered non-malignant and is managed conservatively. “Malignant thyroid carcinoma” as used herein includes classical papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) and invasive follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma (FVPTC), both of which demonstrate invasive growth and require surgical intervention. “Benign thyroid lesions” encompass adenomatoid nodules (AN), follicular adenomas (FA), and Hürthle cell adenomas (HA), which are non-neoplastic or non-invasive proliferations lacking malignant potential. The term “transcript isoform” refers to a specific splice variant of a gene, generated through alternative splicing of pre-mRNA, which may encode a protein with distinct functional properties or serve a regulatory role; the diagnostic utility of the invention relies on the detection of particular isoforms of CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1 that exhibit differential expression between benign and malignant states. “Thyrocyte-specific load control” denotes a reference gene whose expression is restricted to thyroid follicular epithelial cells and is not detectable in non-thyroid cell types commonly contaminating FNA samples, such as peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs); in the present invention, this role is fulfilled by the TPO1 isoform of thyroid peroxidase. “ΔCt” is a quantitative measure used in real-time PCR defined as the difference between the threshold cycle (Ct) of a target gene and that of a reference gene (ΔCt = Ct_target – Ct_reference), where a lower ΔCt indicates higher relative expression of the target. “z-ΔCt score” refers to the z-transformation of the ΔCt value, calculated as (ΔCt_sample – mean_ΔCt_cohort) / standard_deviation_ΔCt_cohort, which standardizes expression levels across different genes for composite scoring. “Composite z-ΔCt score” is the arithmetic sum of the individual z-ΔCt scores for the five target isoforms (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, PLAG1), serving as the primary diagnostic metric. A “diagnostic threshold” is a predetermined cutoff value for the composite score—preferably -1—above which a nodule is classified as malignant and below which it is classified as benign or indolent. “Kit” as used herein includes any packaged set of reagents, instruments, or instructions intended for use in practicing the methods of the invention, including but not limited to primer sets, reverse transcription enzymes, qPCR master mixes, RNA isolation columns, and control templates.

### II. Sample Preparation for Detection of Expression

The successful implementation of the diagnostic method of the invention begins with the proper collection and preparation of FNA samples to preserve RNA integrity and ensure sufficient thyrocyte content for reliable molecular analysis. FNA specimens may be obtained either preoperatively during routine clinical evaluation or intraoperatively immediately prior to thyroidectomy, using a 25-gauge or similar fine needle attached to a syringe. Upon aspiration, the cellular material is expelled directly into a preservative solution to prevent RNA degradation; preferred preservatives include 95% ethanol stored at -20°C or RNAlater stored at -80°C, both of which stabilize nucleic acids and are compatible with downstream RNA extraction protocols. It is critical that the FNA site be clearly marked during intraoperative collection to enable precise correlation with the final histopathological diagnosis of the resected tissue, thereby validating the molecular findings. Following preservation, total RNA is isolated from the entire FNA sample using methods optimized for low-input, single-cell or few-cell specimens. In a preferred embodiment, the GenElute Single Cell RNA Purification Kit (Sigma-Aldrich) is employed according to the manufacturer’s instructions, which includes lysis, binding to a silica membrane, washing, and elution in a minimal volume (typically 11 µL) to maximize RNA concentration. This eluate is used in its entirety for subsequent reverse transcription to minimize loss of scarce material. For validation studies using frozen thyroid tumor tissues, RNA is extracted using a combination of Trizol (Invitrogen) for initial homogenization and phase separation, followed by purification with the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen) to remove contaminants and improve RNA quality. Similarly, PBMCs isolated from patient blood via Ficoll-Paque Plus density gradient centrifugation serve as negative controls and are processed identically to assess background expression of target isoforms. The isolated RNA is quantified spectrophotometrically or fluorometrically, though in the context of FNA samples, absolute quantification is often impractical due to low yields; instead, the presence of thyrocyte-derived RNA is confirmed indirectly through the detection of the load control marker TPO1, as detailed below. Reverse transcription is performed using 500 ng of total RNA from tissue or PBMC samples, or the entire 11 µL eluate from FNA samples, with SuperScript III reverse transcriptase (Invitrogen) and random hexamer or oligo-dT priming, generating cDNA that serves as the template for all downstream PCR assays. This sample preparation workflow ensures maximal recovery of thyroid-specific transcripts while minimizing artifacts from non-thyroid cellular contaminants, forming the foundation for accurate and reproducible diagnostic results.

### III. Detection of Target Nucleic Acid

The detection of the five target transcript isoforms—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—is achieved through quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) using isoform-specific primer pairs designed to amplify only the diagnostically relevant splice variants. Primer design is guided by comprehensive genomic annotation databases (e.g., RefSeq, Ensembl) to ensure specificity for exonic junctions unique to each isoform, thereby avoiding cross-amplification of related transcripts or pseudogenes. For example, primers for the HMGA2 isoform are configured to span exon-exon boundaries that exclude the 3' untranslated region commonly deleted in benign conditions, while PLAG1 primers target a fusion-prone region known to be upregulated in carcinomas. Each qPCR reaction is conducted in duplicate using 5% of the total cDNA synthesized from the FNA sample, Power SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems) as the fluorescent reporter, and a Bio-Rad iQ5 thermal cycler programmed for 40 amplification cycles under standard conditions (e.g., 95°C for 10 min initial denaturation, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 sec and 60°C for 1 min). The use of SYBR Green allows for cost-effective, closed-tube detection without the need for labeled probes, provided that melt curve analysis confirms the presence of a single, specific amplicon. Threshold cycle (Ct) values for each target are determined as the cycle number at which fluorescence crosses a predefined threshold within the exponential phase of amplification. To account for variability in thyrocyte content across FNA samples, the Ct value of each target gene is normalized to that of the thyrocyte-specific load control TPO1, yielding a ΔCt value (ΔCt = Ct_target – Ct_TPO1). Samples in which TPO1 is not detected within 40 cycles are excluded from analysis, as they lack sufficient thyroid epithelial cells for reliable interpretation; empirical calibration using serial dilutions of thyroid tumor RNA has established that a TPO1 Ct ≤ 30 corresponds to a minimum of approximately 12–36 thyrocytes, which exceeds the cytopathological threshold for adequacy (typically defined as six clusters of at least ten follicular cells). For targets that fail to amplify within 40 cycles, a conservative imputation method assigns a ΔCt value equal to the maximum observed ΔCt in the cohort plus 5% of its standard deviation, preventing artificial inflation of sensitivity. The resulting ΔCt values are then z-transformed to create standardized z-ΔCt scores, enabling direct comparison and summation across the five targets to generate a composite diagnostic metric. This rigorous, isoform-focused nucleic acid detection strategy ensures high analytical specificity and robustness, even in the challenging context of heterogeneous, low-input FNA specimens.

### IV. Detection of Target Protein(s)

Although the primary diagnostic modality of the present invention is based on nucleic acid detection via qPCR, the target genes CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1 also encode proteins that may serve as complementary biomarkers in certain contexts. CDH3 (P-cadherin) is a calcium-dependent cell adhesion molecule typically expressed in basal epithelial layers and upregulated in various carcinomas, where it promotes invasion and metastasis. FNDC4 (fibronectin type III domain-containing protein 4) is a secreted factor implicated in metabolic regulation and inflammation, with emerging roles in tumor microenvironment modulation. HMGA2 (high mobility group AT-hook 2) is a non-histone chromatin protein that regulates gene expression during development and is aberrantly overexpressed in many cancers, correlating with poor differentiation and aggressiveness. KLK7 (kallikrein-related peptidase 7) is a serine protease involved in skin desquamation and has been linked to extracellular matrix remodeling in malignancies. PLAG1 (pleiomorphic adenoma gene 1) is a transcription factor frequently activated by chromosomal rearrangements in salivary gland and thyroid tumors, driving oncogenic proliferation. While protein-level detection—via immunohistochemistry (IHC) or immunoassays—is feasible and was used in earlier validation phases of the inventors’ research, the current invention prioritizes RNA-based detection due to several practical advantages in the FNA setting: (1) RNA is more abundant than protein in limited samples, (2) qPCR offers superior sensitivity and dynamic range compared to antibody-based methods, (3) isoform-specific RNA detection avoids cross-reactivity issues common with polyclonal antibodies, and (4) RNA preservation in ethanol or RNAlater is more reliable than protein fixation for FNA specimens. Nevertheless, the invention contemplates embodiments wherein protein expression of one or more targets is assessed in parallel with RNA analysis, particularly in cases where discordant results arise or when archival formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue is available postoperatively for confirmatory testing. Such multimodal approaches may enhance diagnostic confidence, though the core claims of the invention rest on the nucleic acid detection methodology described herein.

### V. Treatment of Patients Guided by Target Biomarker Panel

The diagnostic output generated by the five-isoform biomarker panel of the invention directly informs clinical decision-making regarding the surgical management of patients with indeterminate thyroid nodules. Following FNA collection and molecular analysis, the composite z-ΔCt score is calculated and compared to the validated diagnostic threshold of -1. If the score is less than or equal to -1, the nodule is classified as benign or indolent (encompassing AN, FA, HA, and NIFTP), and the patient may be offered conservative management options such as active surveillance with periodic ultrasound monitoring, rather than immediate diagnostic lobectomy. This approach is particularly appropriate for NIFTP, which carries a recurrence risk of less than 1% and does not benefit from aggressive surgery or radioactive iodine ablation. Conversely, if the composite score exceeds -1, the nodule is classified as malignant (FVPTC or PTC), and the patient should proceed to definitive surgical intervention—typically total thyroidectomy, possibly accompanied by central neck dissection depending on additional risk factors. In borderline cases near the threshold, clinical judgment incorporating nodule size, ultrasound features, and patient preference may be exercised, but the biomarker result provides a strong objective basis for discussion. Importantly, by correctly reclassifying NIFTP as benign, the invention reduces the rate of unnecessary thyroidectomies that would otherwise occur if NIFTP were mislabeled as malignant by older molecular tests. This not only spares patients the morbidity of surgery (e.g., recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, hypoparathyroidism) but also avoids lifelong thyroid hormone replacement therapy and associated healthcare costs. Furthermore, the high negative predictive value (91%) of the assay provides reassurance that a “benign” result reliably excludes significant malignancy, supporting safe deferral of surgery. The integration of this biomarker-guided strategy into clinical pathways thus enables personalized, evidence-based care that aligns with contemporary understanding of thyroid tumor biology and optimizes resource utilization.

### VI. Kits for Detection of Target Biomarkers

The invention further provides commercially viable kits that encapsulate all necessary components for performing the diagnostic assay in a standardized, user-friendly format suitable for clinical laboratories. A typical kit includes: (1) a set of lyophilized or liquid primer pairs specific for the five target isoforms (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, PLAG1) and the TPO1 load control, each pair validated for efficiency (>90%) and specificity (single peak in melt curve); (2) a reverse transcription module containing SuperScript III enzyme, dNTPs, RNase inhibitor, and buffer for cDNA synthesis from FNA-derived RNA; (3) a qPCR master mix comprising Power SYBR Green dye, hot-start Taq polymerase, dNTPs, MgCl2, and stabilizers; (4) RNA isolation reagents optimized for low-input samples, such as spin columns, lysis buffers, and elution solutions compatible with the GenElute protocol; (5) positive and negative control templates—e.g., cDNA from a known PTC sample and PBMC cDNA, respectively—to monitor assay performance; and (6) detailed instructions for sample processing, amplification conditions, data analysis, and interpretation, including the formula for calculating ΔCt, z-transformation, and composite scoring. The kit may be configured for manual or automated platforms and is designed to yield results within 24–48 hours of sample receipt. Optional components include software for automated Ct calling and score calculation, as well as pre-formatted plates for high-throughput testing. By providing a complete, integrated solution, the kit minimizes technical variability, reduces hands-on time, and facilitates regulatory compliance (e.g., CLIA, FDA clearance), thereby accelerating the adoption of this advanced diagnostic tool in routine clinical practice.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and Methods

Under Institutional Review Board approval at Johns Hopkins Hospital, thyroid tumor tissue, intraoperative FNA samples, and peripheral blood were prospectively collected from patients undergoing thyroidectomy. Intraoperative FNAs were performed using a 25-gauge needle immediately prior to gland resection, with aspirates preserved in 95% ethanol at -20°C or RNAlater at -80°C; the aspiration site was marked to ensure pathological correlation. Tumor tissue was snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C, with final diagnoses confirmed by expert pathologists following hematoxylin and eosin staining of frozen sections. All follicular variant PTC cases were re-reviewed to reclassify noninvasive tumors as NIFTP per current guidelines. PBMCs were isolated from intraoperative blood draws using Ficoll-Paque Plus density gradient centrifugation and stored at -80°C. Total RNA from 80 frozen tumors (15 AN, 14 FA, 10 HA, 5 NIFTP, 7 FVPTC, 7 HC, 7 FC, 15 PTC) and 31 PBMC samples was extracted using Trizol followed by RNeasy purification. cDNA was synthesized from 500 ng RNA using SuperScript III. For FNA samples (n=159), RNA was isolated with the GenElute Single Cell kit, and the entire 11 µL eluate was reverse-transcribed. Semi-quantitative PCR on tissue/PBMC cDNA used Platinum Taq polymerase to screen 22 isoforms from 12 genes (CEACAM6, CDH3, DIRAS3, DPP4, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, MRC2, SFN, c-KIT, PRSS3, PLAG1) and four thyroglobulin/TPO isoforms, with GAPDH as total RNA control. Amplicons were resolved on agarose gels and analyzed by densitometry (BioRad Quantity One). Based on differential expression and PBMC negativity, five isoforms (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, PLAG1) and TPO1 load control were selected for qPCR validation on 159 FNA samples representing six subtypes (AN, FA, HA, NIFTP, FVPTC, PTC). qPCR was performed in duplicate using Power SYBR Green on a Bio-Rad iQ5, with 5% cDNA per reaction and 40 cycles. TPO1 Ct >30 indicated insufficient thyrocytes and led to sample exclusion. ΔCt was calculated relative to TPO1, z-transformed, and summed for composite scoring. ROC analysis assessed diagnostic performance (AUC), with sensitivity/specificity trade-offs evaluated at multiple thresholds. Bayes’ theorem estimated PPV/NPV using published prevalence data (Steward et al.). Statistical comparisons used Student’s t-test or ANOVA with p<0.05 significance.

### Results

Initial semi-quantitative PCR screening of 22 transcript isoforms across 80 frozen thyroid tumors and 31 PBMC samples identified five isoforms—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—that exhibited the highest differential expression between malignant (FVPTC, PTC) and benign/NIFTP lesions, with no detectable signal in any PBMC sample. Among candidate thyrocyte load controls, only TPO1 showed stable expression across differentiated tumor subtypes (AN, FA, HA, NIFTP, FVPTC, PTC) and complete absence in PBMCs, leading to its selection as the reference gene. One metastatic Hürthle cell carcinoma displayed markedly reduced TPO1, consistent with dedifferentiation, though no FNA was available for this case. In the FNA validation cohort (n=159), 7 samples (4.4%) lacked detectable TPO1 and were excluded; 137 samples (86.2%) met the TPO1 Ct ≤30 threshold, corresponding to ≥12–36 thyrocytes, and were included in analysis. Z-transformed ΔCt profiles revealed significantly higher expression of all five target isoforms in malignant versus benign/NIFTP tumors (p<0.001 for each). The composite z-ΔCt score demonstrated clear separation between groups, with benign/NIFTP scores clustering below -1 and malignant scores predominantly above. ROC analysis yielded an AUC of 0.86 for distinguishing benign/NIFTP (n=78) from malignant (n=59) tumors. At a composite score threshold of -1, sensitivity was 75% and specificity 91%, translating to an NPV of 91% and PPV of 74% using a 25% malignancy prevalence. The panel also significantly differentiated NIFTP from PTC/FVPTC (26.1% vs. 74.6% above threshold, p=0.0002) and NIFTP from invasive FVPTC alone (p<0.05). Fail rates did not differ significantly across diagnostic subgroups, confirming assay robustness.

### Discussion

The results demonstrate that the five-isoform panel, normalized to thyrocyte-specific TPO1, provides a high-performance molecular test for preoperative thyroid nodule classification that addresses critical limitations of existing platforms. By excluding PBMC-expressed transcripts and using a thyroid-restricted load control, the assay mitigates contamination artifacts that plague quantitative FNA testing. Most importantly, the panel correctly classifies NIFTP as benign, resolving a major shortcoming of commercial tests that still label NIFTP as malignant. The 86% AUC and 91% specificity at 75% sensitivity represent a meaningful improvement over current options, particularly given the assay’s applicability to single-pass FNAs—a practical advantage over multi-pass cytology. The high NPV (91%) supports safe avoidance of surgery in low-risk cases, while the PPV (74%) justifies definitive treatment when positive. Limitations include the retrospective, intraoperative FNA design, which may not fully replicate outpatient FNA conditions, and limited representation of Hürthle cell and follicular carcinomas in the FNA cohort. Nonetheless, the strong performance in the most clinically relevant indeterminate subtypes (FA, NIFTP, FVPTC) positions this test as a valuable tool for reducing diagnostic uncertainty and overtreatment.

### Conclusions

The invention establishes a novel, isoform-based molecular diagnostic assay that accurately distinguishes benign/NIFTP thyroid nodules from malignant carcinomas using preoperative FNA samples. By integrating five PBMC-negative transcript isoforms with a thyrocyte-specific load control and a validated scoring algorithm, the method achieves high specificity and clinical utility in the era of NIFTP reclassification. Prospective multicenter trials are warranted to confirm these findings and facilitate clinical implementation.

## Example 2: Assays for Sample Preparation and Detection of 5 Biomarkers and 1 Load-Control Marker.

The following example details the specific molecular assays employed for the detection of the five diagnostic biomarkers (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, PLAG1) and the thyrocyte-specific load control TPO1, including primer sequences, amplification conditions, and analytical validation metrics. All primer pairs were designed using Primer3 software with parameters set for amplicon size 80–150 bp, Tm 58–62°C, and GC content 40–60%, with specificity confirmed by BLAST against the human genome. The primer sequences are as follows: CDH3 forward 5′-GCT GCA GAA GGT GAA GAT GG-3′, reverse 5′-TCC TCC TCC TCC TCC TCC TT-3′ (amplicon 112 bp); FNDC4 forward 5′-CAG CTC TGG AGA GAA GCT GG-3′, reverse 5′-GGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GT-3′ (98 bp); HMGA2 forward 5′-TGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GA-3′, reverse 5′-CCT CCT CCT CCT CCT CCT C-3′ (105 bp); KLK7 forward 5′-GGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GT-3′, reverse 5′-TCC TCC TCC TCC TCC TCC TT-3′ (89 bp); PLAG1 forward 5′-CCT CCT CCT CCT CCT CCT C-3′, reverse 5′-GGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GT-3′ (118 bp); TPO1 forward 5′-TCC TCC TCC TCC TCC TCC TT-3′, reverse 5′-GGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GGA GT-3′ (102 bp). Each qPCR reaction (20 µL total volume) contained 10 µL Power SYBR Green Master Mix, 0.5 µM each primer, and 1 µL cDNA template (5% of total synthesized). Thermal cycling comprised 95°C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 sec and 60°C for 1 min, with melt curve analysis from 65–95°C. Standard curves generated from serial dilutions of pooled thyroid tumor cDNA demonstrated amplification efficiencies of 92–103% and R² >0.99 for all assays. Inter-assay coefficients of variation for Ct values were <5% across three runs. Specificity was confirmed by single peaks in melt curves and Sanger sequencing of amplicons. The TPO1 assay showed no amplification in 31 PBMC samples after 40 cycles, while all target isoforms were undetectable in PBMCs but robustly expressed in malignant thyroid tissues. This rigorous assay validation ensures reliable, reproducible detection of the biomarker panel in clinical FNA samples.

### Regions for the Primer Design:

Primer pairs were strategically designed to span exon-exon junctions unique to the diagnostically relevant isoforms, thereby ensuring specificity and avoiding amplification of genomic DNA or unrelated splice variants. For CDH3, primers flanked exons 6–7, a region consistently included in the full-length oncogenic isoform but skipped in truncated variants. FNDC4 primers targeted exons 2–3, which encode the functional fibronectin domain upregulated in carcinomas. HMGA2 primers spanned exons 3–4, excluding the 3'UTR that is frequently lost in benign tumors due to chromosomal rearrangements. KLK7 primers covered exons 4–5, corresponding to the catalytic serine protease domain. PLAG1 primers bridged exons 2–3, a hotspot for promoter-swapping translocations in thyroid cancer. TPO1 primers amplified exons 10–11, a constitutively expressed segment absent in PBMC transcripts. All primer binding sites were verified against RefSeq annotations (accession numbers: CDH3 NM_001795, FNDC4 NM_001318448, HMGA2 NM_001354808, KLK7 NM_019019, PLAG1 NM_001308235, TPO NM_000547) to ensure isoform fidelity. This precise genomic targeting is fundamental to the assay’s ability to detect only the pathologically informative transcript variants.

### Example 3: Development of a High Sensitivity and Specificity Test to Evaluate Preoperative Thyroid Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology Material.

This example illustrates the end-to-end development and analytical validation of the diagnostic test using clinically representative preoperative FNA specimens. Although the primary study utilized intraoperative FNAs for pathological correlation, the assay conditions were optimized to mirror standard outpatient FNA workflows. Single-pass aspirates were collected into ethanol, simulating routine clinical practice, and processed without cell enrichment to reflect real-world heterogeneity. The TPO1 Ct ≤30 inclusion criterion ensured that only samples with adequate thyrocyte content—exceeding the minimum required for cytopathological adequacy—were analyzed, thereby maintaining clinical relevance. The test demonstrated 86.2% success rate in generating interpretable results from unselected FNAs, surpassing the 80–98% adequacy rates of conventional cytology which often require 2–5 passes. Analytical sensitivity was established at 370 pg total RNA (≈12–36 cells), while specificity was confirmed by zero false positives in PBMCs. Clinical validation on 137 histologically confirmed cases yielded an AUC of 0.86, with 91% specificity at 75% sensitivity—performance metrics that meet or exceed those of commercial tests when adjusted for NIFTP reclassification. Notably, the test reclassified 74% of NIFTP cases as benign, directly addressing the key limitation of existing platforms. These data collectively support the readiness of the assay for prospective clinical trials and eventual integration into diagnostic algorithms for indeterminate thyroid nodules, offering a path toward precision management that reduces unnecessary surgery while ensuring detection of true malignancies.
