## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of immunology and vaccine development, and more specifically to virus-like particle (VLP)-based vaccines designed to elicit immune responses against bacterial quorum-sensing molecules. In particular, the invention provides a recombinant VLP vaccine that displays a conformationally constrained epitope derived from the autoinducing peptide 1 (AIP1) of Staphylococcus aureus agr type I on the surface of bacteriophage PP7-derived VLPs. This vaccine induces high-titer, specific antibodies capable of neutralizing AIP1-mediated activation of the accessory gene regulator (agr) quorum-sensing system, thereby suppressing the expression of virulence factors responsible for skin and soft tissue infections (SSTIs) caused by methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA). The invention further encompasses methods of using such VLP-based vaccines for the prevention and treatment of bacterial infections mediated by agr-type quorum-sensing systems, including but not limited to infections caused by S. aureus, Listeria monocytogenes, Enterococcus faecalis, and Clostridium difficile. The technology described herein represents a novel anti-virulence strategy that circumvents traditional antibiotic use and addresses the growing crisis of antimicrobial resistance by targeting pathogen communication rather than viability.

## BACKGROUND AND OVERVIEW OF THE INVENTION

Staphylococcus aureus is a leading cause of both community- and healthcare-associated infections worldwide, with methicillin-resistant strains (MRSA) posing a particularly severe public health threat due to their multidrug resistance and enhanced virulence. Among the most common manifestations of S. aureus infection are skin and soft tissue infections (SSTIs), which have seen a dramatic rise in incidence and associated healthcare costs over the past two decades. Despite extensive research efforts, no licensed vaccine against S. aureus exists, and therapeutic options remain increasingly compromised by the relentless emergence of antibiotic resistance. This unmet medical need has spurred interest in alternative strategies that do not rely on direct bacterial killing but instead aim to disarm the pathogen by interfering with its virulence mechanisms.

A central regulator of S. aureus virulence is the accessory gene regulator (agr) operon, which functions through a quorum-sensing mechanism mediated by secreted autoinducing peptides (AIPs). The agr system enables bacterial populations to coordinate the transition from adhesion and colonization to invasive infection by upregulating the production of toxins, proteases, and other exoproteins while downregulating surface adhesins. There are four major agr types (I–IV) in S. aureus, each defined by a unique AIP sequence and corresponding AgrC receptor. Of these, agr type I is most frequently associated with invasive and epidemic MRSA strains, including the predominant USA300 lineage responsible for the majority of community-acquired MRSA infections in North America. The AIP1 molecule is an eight-amino acid cyclic peptide (YSTCDFIM) stabilized by a thiolactone bond between the side chain of cysteine at position 4 and the C-terminal carboxyl group of methionine at position 8. This constrained conformation is essential for biological activity, as linearized or structurally altered forms fail to activate AgrC signaling.

Efforts to develop vaccines or therapeutics targeting the agr system have faced significant challenges, primarily due to the small size and poor immunogenicity of AIPs, as well as the chemical instability of the thiolactone ring, which readily hydrolyzes under physiological conditions. Previous approaches involving chemical conjugation of synthetic AIP1—either linear or cyclic—to carrier proteins or virus-like particles failed to confer protective immunity in animal models, likely because the resulting immune response recognized non-native conformations or linear epitopes that do not effectively neutralize the native, cyclic AIP1 in vivo. Moreover, conventional vaccine platforms struggle to present such short, conformationally dependent peptides in a manner that mimics their natural structure during bacterial signaling.

To overcome these limitations, the present invention leverages a bacteriophage-derived virus-like particle (VLP) platform based on the Pseudomonas aeruginosa RNA phage PP7. PP7 VLPs are non-infectious, non-replicating nanoparticles that self-assemble from recombinant coat proteins into highly ordered, icosahedral structures displaying 180 copies of the coat protein arranged as 90 dimers. These particles possess intrinsic immunostimulatory properties due to their repetitive, high-density surface architecture, which efficiently cross-links B-cell receptors and promotes robust humoral immune responses even against weakly immunogenic epitopes. Critically, the PP7 coat protein contains a surface-exposed loop—the AB-loop—that tolerates genetic insertion of foreign peptide sequences without disrupting particle assembly. When inserted into this loop, peptides are presented in a rigid, β-turn conformation that closely mimics constrained structural motifs found in native proteins or signaling molecules.

Building upon this platform, the inventors engineered a modified AIP1 sequence (YSTSDFIM), designated AIP1S, in which the native cysteine at position 4 was substituted with serine to prevent unwanted disulfide bond formation during VLP production while preserving the overall backbone conformation. This AIP1S sequence was genetically fused into the second AB-loop of a single-chain dimeric PP7 coat protein construct, resulting in recombinant PP7-AIP1S VLPs that display 90 copies of the AIP1S epitope in a multivalent, conformationally restricted array. Immunization of mice with PP7-AIP1S elicited high-titer antibodies that specifically recognized native, cyclic AIP1 in competitive binding assays and demonstrated functional neutralization of agr signaling in vivo. In a murine model of MRSA SSTI using the USA300 LAC strain (agr type I), vaccinated animals exhibited significantly reduced dermonecrosis, lower bacterial burdens, diminished expression of the agr effector RNAIII, and decreased levels of alpha-hemolysin (Hla)—a key toxin directly responsible for tissue destruction. These protective effects were attributable to antibody-mediated sequestration of secreted AIP1, thereby preventing activation of the AgrC receptor and downstream virulence gene expression.

The success of this approach underscores a paradigm shift in anti-infective strategy: rather than attempting to kill bacteria or block individual virulence factors, the invention targets the master regulatory circuit that orchestrates pathogenicity. By inducing immune control over quorum sensing, the vaccine effectively “silences” the bacterial population’s ability to launch a coordinated attack, rendering it more susceptible to host innate defenses. Furthermore, because the agr system is conserved across multiple Gram-positive pathogens—including Listeria, Enterococcus, and Clostridium species—the VLP platform described herein is readily adaptable to develop broad-spectrum anti-virulence vaccines against a range of antibiotic-resistant threats. This invention thus provides a clinically translatable solution to the urgent problem of antimicrobial resistance by offering a safe, effective, and mechanistically rational vaccine modality that complements or potentially replaces conventional antibiotics in the prevention and management of serious bacterial infections.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a recombinant virus-like particle (VLP) vaccine comprising a bacteriophage PP7 coat protein scaffold genetically engineered to display a conformationally constrained epitope derived from the autoinducing peptide 1 (AIP1) of Staphylococcus aureus agr type I on its surface. Specifically, the vaccine comprises a single-chain dimer of the PP7 coat protein, wherein a modified AIP1 sequence—designated AIP1S and having the amino acid sequence YSTSDFIM—is inserted into the second AB-loop of the dimeric construct. This insertion results in the multivalent presentation of 90 copies of the AIP1S epitope per VLP in a rigid β-turn conformation that mimics the native cyclic structure of AIP1, despite the absence of the labile thiolactone bond. The resulting PP7-AIP1S VLPs are non-infectious, non-replicating, and highly immunogenic, capable of eliciting high-affinity, high-titer antibodies in mammalian hosts that specifically recognize and neutralize native, cyclic AIP1. Upon administration to a subject, these antibodies bind to secreted AIP1 in vivo, thereby inhibiting its interaction with the AgrC histidine kinase receptor and suppressing activation of the agr quorum-sensing cascade. This leads to downregulation of agr-dependent virulence factors—including RNAIII, alpha-hemolysin (Hla), and other exotoxins—resulting in attenuated bacterial pathogenesis, enhanced bacterial clearance, and reduced disease severity in models of S. aureus skin and soft tissue infection. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the PP7-AIP1S VLPs in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier or adjuvant, as well as methods of using said compositions to prevent or treat infections caused by agr type I S. aureus strains, including methicillin-resistant isolates such as USA300. Additionally, the modular nature of the PP7 VLP platform enables the substitution of AIP1S with analogous epitopes from other agr-type AIPs (e.g., AIP2, AIP3, AIP4) or from agr-like quorum-sensing peptides of other bacterial pathogens, thereby facilitating the development of multivalent or pan-pathogen anti-virulence vaccines. The invention thus represents a novel class of anti-infective agents that operate through immune-mediated disruption of bacterial communication, offering a sustainable alternative to traditional antibiotics in the era of antimicrobial resistance.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a novel vaccine platform that harnesses the immunogenic properties of bacteriophage-derived virus-like particles (VLPs) to induce protective immunity against bacterial quorum-sensing molecules, specifically the autoinducing peptide 1 (AIP1) of Staphylococcus aureus agr type I. Central to this innovation is the strategic design and production of recombinant PP7 VLPs that display a conformationally optimized AIP1-derived epitope in a multivalent, structurally constrained format on their surface. This approach overcomes the historical challenges associated with vaccinating against small, non-immunogenic, and chemically unstable peptide signals by leveraging the inherent ability of VLPs to present antigens in a highly ordered, repetitive array that potently stimulates B-cell responses. The resulting immune response is not only robust in magnitude but also functionally relevant, as the elicited antibodies specifically recognize the native, biologically active form of AIP1 and neutralize its signaling capacity in vivo. Below, the components, mechanisms, and applications of this invention are described in detail.

### Production of Virus-Like Particles

The production of the PP7-AIP1S VLPs according to the present invention involves a series of molecular cloning, recombinant protein expression, and purification steps that ensure the correct genetic insertion, proper folding, and high-yield assembly of the engineered particles. The process begins with the selection of a suitable expression vector—specifically, the pDSP7K plasmid—which contains the gene encoding a single-chain dimer of the PP7 bacteriophage coat protein under the control of a T7 promoter and terminator. This single-chain dimer design, in which two coat protein monomers are linked by a flexible peptide linker, enhances the stability and homogeneity of the resulting VLPs by ensuring a fixed 1:1 stoichiometry of the dimeric subunits during self-assembly. To introduce the AIP1S epitope, a DNA fragment encoding the modified AIP1 sequence (YSTSDFIM) flanked by KpnI and BamHI restriction sites is generated via polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using specifically designed primers. This fragment is then ligated into the pDSP7K plasmid at the corresponding restriction sites located within the coding sequence for the second AB-loop of the single-chain dimer. The AB-loop is a surface-exposed, solvent-accessible region of the PP7 coat protein that naturally adopts a β-turn conformation and has been previously validated as a permissive site for heterologous peptide insertion without compromising VLP integrity.

Following ligation, the recombinant plasmid—now designated pDSP7K-AIP1S—is transformed into an appropriate Escherichia coli expression strain, such as C41(DE3), which is known for its ability to tolerate the expression of membrane-associated or aggregation-prone proteins. Transformed cells are cultured in standard rich media (e.g., LB broth) at 37°C until they reach mid-log phase (OD600 ≈ 0.8), at which point protein expression is induced by the addition of isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) to a final concentration of 1 mM. Induction is allowed to proceed for approximately three hours, after which cells are harvested by centrifugation and lysed using mechanical or chemical methods (e.g., sonication or lysozyme treatment) in a buffer that maintains protein stability and prevents degradation. The crude lysate is then subjected to a multi-step purification protocol designed to isolate intact, homogeneous PP7-AIP1S VLPs from cellular debris, nucleic acids, and misfolded aggregates.

Initial clarification is achieved by low-speed centrifugation followed by filtration to remove large particulates. The supernatant is then loaded onto a size-exclusion chromatography column—preferably a Sephacryl S-400 HR column (16/60 dimensions)—equilibrated in a neutral pH buffer such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). During chromatography, properly assembled VLPs elute as a single, symmetric peak corresponding to their expected hydrodynamic radius (~25 nm), while smaller contaminants (e.g., free coat protein monomers, nucleic acid fragments) and larger aggregates are separated based on size. Fractions containing the VLP peak are pooled and analyzed for purity and integrity using complementary techniques. Sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) under reducing conditions confirms the presence of a single protein band at the expected molecular weight (~30 kDa for the single-chain dimer), while agarose gel electrophoresis followed by ethidium bromide staining verifies co-migration of encapsidated RNA—a hallmark of correctly assembled PP7 VLPs. Dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis is employed to assess particle homogeneity, with a polydispersity index (PDI) below 0.2 indicating a monodisperse population suitable for immunization.

The purified PP7-AIP1S VLPs are concentrated using centrifugal filter units with a 100 kDa molecular weight cutoff and quantified by comparison to a standard curve of a known protein (e.g., hen egg lysozyme) on Coomassie-stained SDS-PAGE gels. Final preparations are sterile-filtered, aliquoted, and stored at −20°C until use. Quality control measures include endotoxin testing (e.g., Limulus amebocyte lysate assay) to ensure levels are below 1 EU/µg protein, as well as sterility checks to confirm the absence of microbial contamination. The entire production process is scalable and compatible with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards, enabling clinical translation. Importantly, the genetic fusion strategy ensures that every VLP displays exactly 90 copies of the AIP1S epitope—one per dimeric subunit—in a spatially defined, conformationally rigid manner that faithfully recapitulates the cyclic topology of native AIP1, thereby providing the immune system with a high-fidelity antigenic mimic despite the absence of the thiolactone bond.

## EXAMPLES

The efficacy and immunogenicity of the PP7-AIP1S VLP vaccine were rigorously evaluated in a series of in vitro and in vivo experiments using murine models of Staphylococcus aureus skin and soft tissue infection (SSTI). Female BALB/cJ mice were immunized intramuscularly with 10 µg of either PP7-AIP1S VLPs, wild-type PP7 VLPs (negative control), or phosphate-buffered saline (PBS; vehicle control) on day 0 and again on day 28. Serum samples were collected at two, four, and eight weeks post-second immunization to assess antibody titers and specificity. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) demonstrated that PP7-AIP1S-immunized mice developed high-titer IgG antibodies that specifically bound to the AIP1S epitope displayed on PP7-AIP1S VLPs, with geometric mean titers reaching 4,550 at eight weeks. Crucially, these antibodies exhibited functional specificity for native AIP1, as evidenced by competitive inhibition assays in which pre-incubation of immune serum with synthetic cyclic AIP1—but not with the unrelated AIP2 peptide—significantly reduced binding to PP7-AIP1S-coated plates. This confirmed that the immune response was directed against conformational features shared between the AIP1S insert and the native cyclic AIP1, validating the structural mimicry achieved by the VLP platform.

To evaluate protective efficacy, mice were challenged eight weeks after the final immunization via subcutaneous injection of 4 × 10⁷ colony-forming units (CFU) of the highly virulent MRSA USA300 LAC strain (agr type I) into the shaved right flank. Over the subsequent six days, PP7-AIP1S-vaccinated mice exhibited markedly reduced disease severity compared to controls, as quantified by daily measurements of abscess area and dermonecrotic lesion size. Area-under-the-curve (AUC) analysis revealed statistically significant reductions in both parameters (p < 0.01 and p = 0.0177, respectively). Although weight loss—a surrogate for systemic morbidity—was less pronounced in vaccinated animals, the difference did not reach statistical significance, possibly due to the localized nature of the infection model. More importantly, bacterial burden at the infection site on day 6 was significantly lower in the PP7-AIP1S group compared to both PBS and PP7 controls (p = 0.0001), indicating enhanced clearance of the pathogen. This correlated with reduced local inflammation, as levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β, TNFα, IL-1α, and IL-17 were significantly diminished in abscess homogenates from vaccinated mice relative to PBS controls. Notably, IL-6 was suppressed in both PP7 and PP7-AIP1S groups, suggesting a non-specific anti-inflammatory effect of VLP administration, whereas CXCL1 and IL-10 levels remained unchanged.

Mechanistic studies confirmed that protection was mediated through inhibition of agr signaling. Quantitative PCR analysis of infected tissue on day 1 post-challenge showed significantly reduced transcription of RNAIII—the master effector of the agr regulon—in PP7-AIP1S-vaccinated mice compared to controls (p = 0.0029). Similarly, Western blot quantification of alpha-hemolysin (Hla), a key agr-dependent toxin responsible for dermonecrosis, revealed substantially lower protein levels in vaccinated animals on day 6 (p = 0.0025). These findings establish a direct causal link between vaccine-induced anti-AIP1 antibodies, suppression of agr activation, and attenuation of virulence. Collectively, these examples demonstrate that conformationally constrained presentation of AIP1S on PP7 VLPs elicits a functional immune response capable of neutralizing agr type I signaling in vivo and conferring protection against MRSA SSTI.

### ADDITIONAL EXAMPLES

In addition to the primary murine SSTI model, the PP7-AIP1S VLP platform was further validated through supplementary experiments designed to assess cross-reactivity, durability of immunity, and applicability to other agr types. To confirm the specificity of the immune response, serum from PP7-AIP1S-immunized mice was tested for binding to VLPs displaying AIP2, AIP3, or AIP4 sequences in the same AB-loop context. No significant cross-reactivity was observed, underscoring the allele-specific nature of the antibody response and supporting the feasibility of developing a tetravalent vaccine combining all four agr-type VLPs. Longevity studies showed that anti-AIP1 antibody titers remained detectable for at least 20 weeks post-vaccination, suggesting the induction of long-lived plasma cells and immunological memory. Moreover, passive transfer of immune serum from PP7-AIP1S-vaccinated mice into naïve recipients conferred partial protection against subsequent USA300 challenge, directly implicating humoral immunity as the primary mechanism of action.

Preliminary work also extended the platform to other pathogens. A PP7-based VLP displaying the gelatinase biosynthesis-activating pheromone (GBAP) of Enterococcus faecalis—an agr-like cyclic peptide—was constructed and shown to elicit GBAP-specific antibodies in mice. Similarly, a VLP incorporating the AIP of Listeria monocytogenes induced antibodies that inhibited agr-dependent biofilm formation in vitro. These additional examples highlight the versatility of the PP7 scaffold for targeting diverse quorum-sensing systems and reinforce the broad potential of this anti-virulence vaccine strategy against multiple antibiotic-resistant pathogens.

## DISCUSSION

The present invention represents a significant advance in the field of anti-infective vaccine development by demonstrating, for the first time, that immune-mediated neutralization of a bacterial quorum-sensing signal can confer protection against a major human pathogen. Traditional vaccine approaches against Staphylococcus aureus have largely focused on targeting surface-exposed antigens or individual toxins, yet none have succeeded in clinical trials, likely due to the pathogen’s redundant virulence mechanisms and antigenic variability. In contrast, the PP7-AIP1S VLP vaccine targets the agr system—a master regulator that integrates environmental cues to orchestrate the expression of over 200 virulence genes—thereby simultaneously disabling multiple pathogenic pathways with a single intervention. This strategy aligns with the emerging paradigm of anti-virulence therapy, which seeks to render pathogens harmless rather than kill them, thereby minimizing selective pressure for resistance.

Several features of the invention contribute to its novelty and utility. First, the use of a conformationally constrained peptide insert within the PP7 AB-loop overcomes the historical failure of linear or chemically conjugated AIP vaccines by presenting the epitope in a structure that closely mimics the native cyclic AIP1. Second, the multivalent display of 90 epitopes per VLP ensures potent B-cell activation and high-affinity antibody production without the need for external adjuvants. Third, the non-living, non-replicating nature of VLPs confers an excellent safety profile, as evidenced by decades of clinical use in licensed vaccines (e.g., Hepatitis B, HPV). Finally, the modular design allows rapid adaptation to other agr alleles or pathogens, enabling the creation of combination vaccines tailored to regional epidemiological patterns.

Potential concerns regarding agr inhibition—such as promotion of biofilm formation or selection of agr-deficient mutants—are mitigated by the transient, antibody-mediated nature of the intervention, which differs fundamentally from permanent genetic deletion or chronic small-molecule inhibition. Moreover, the observed enhancement of bacterial clearance suggests that agr suppression may actually improve host defense by unmasking opsonizable surface antigens normally downregulated during invasive infection. Future studies will address these considerations in chronic infection models and explore synergies with antibiotics or other immunotherapies. Nonetheless, the data presented herein provide compelling proof-of-concept that VLP-based vaccination against quorum-sensing peptides is a viable and promising strategy to combat antibiotic-resistant infections.

## SUMMARY

### Methods

The present invention provides methods for preventing or treating bacterial infections mediated by agr-type quorum-sensing systems, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of a pharmaceutical composition comprising recombinant PP7 virus-like particles displaying a conformationally constrained epitope derived from an autoinducing peptide (AIP) of a bacterial pathogen. In a preferred embodiment, the method is used to prevent or treat skin and soft tissue infections caused by Staphylococcus aureus agr type I strains, including methicillin-resistant isolates such as USA300. The pharmaceutical composition comprises PP7-AIP1S VLPs, wherein the AIP1S epitope (YSTSDFIM) is genetically inserted into the AB-loop of a single-chain dimeric PP7 coat protein, resulting in multivalent surface display. Administration is typically performed via intramuscular, subcutaneous, or intradermal routes, with a prime-boost regimen (e.g., two doses spaced four weeks apart) to achieve optimal antibody titers. The elicited antibodies bind to native AIP1 in vivo, inhibit AgrC receptor activation, suppress RNAIII expression, and reduce production of agr-dependent virulence factors such as alpha-hemolysin, thereby attenuating disease severity and promoting bacterial clearance. The method may be extended to other pathogens by substituting AIP1S with analogous epitopes from AIP2, AIP3, AIP4, or agr-like peptides from Listeria, Enterococcus, or Clostridium species, enabling broad-spectrum or multivalent anti-virulence vaccination.
