# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of digital health technologies, specifically in the domain of mental health risk prediction for individuals with chronic medical conditions. More particularly, the invention relates to a computer-implemented method and system for detecting potential mental health risk in individuals diagnosed with diabetes mellitus through the analysis of passive sensing signals derived from their routine interactions with diabetes management devices and digital health platforms. The invention leverages machine learning techniques to process behavioral, physiological, and demographic data collected without active participant input during normal engagement with blood glucose monitoring systems, mobile applications, web portals, and coaching services. This technical approach enables scalable, real-time assessment of mental health status without imposing additional burden on patients or requiring specialized mental health infrastructure, thereby addressing critical gaps in current healthcare delivery models for comorbid diabetes and mental health conditions.

The invention specifically addresses the intersection of chronic disease management and behavioral health monitoring through computational methods that transform routine diabetes self-management activities into predictive indicators of psychological distress. Unlike conventional mental health screening approaches that rely on periodic clinical assessments or patient-reported questionnaires, the present invention utilizes data streams generated incidentally during standard diabetes care activities. These data streams include temporal patterns of blood glucose monitoring, contextual responses provided during glucose testing, engagement metrics with digital health platforms, and communication patterns with health coaches. The technical field encompasses advanced data analytics, gradient boosting machine learning algorithms, feature engineering from heterogeneous data sources, and real-time risk stratification systems designed for integration within existing digital health ecosystems.

Furthermore, the invention operates within the broader technical domain of precision digital phenotyping, where individual behavioral signatures are extracted from digital footprints to infer clinical states. The specific application to diabetes populations represents a novel technical contribution, as previous digital phenotyping efforts have primarily focused on general populations using smartphone sensor data rather than condition-specific medical devices. The invention demonstrates that diabetes management devices, particularly cellular-enabled blood glucose meters, can serve as rich sources of behavioral and emotional data when combined with complementary digital health platform interactions. This represents a significant advancement in the technical field by expanding the scope of passive sensing beyond consumer electronics to include medical-grade devices that are already integrated into patients' daily routines for chronic disease management.

## BACKGROUND

Diabetes mellitus represents one of the most prevalent chronic conditions globally, affecting approximately 34.2 million individuals in the United States alone. A substantial proportion of this population experiences significant mental health challenges, with studies indicating that approximately 25% of individuals with diabetes exhibit clinically significant depressive symptoms, while up to 40% experience generalized anxiety disorder. The co-occurrence of diabetes and mental health conditions creates a complex clinical scenario that significantly impacts treatment adherence, disease progression, and overall health outcomes. Individuals with comorbid diabetes and mental health conditions demonstrate reduced adherence to essential diabetes management recommendations, including dietary modifications, exercise regimens, medication compliance, glucose monitoring protocols, and attendance at medical appointments. This non-adherence directly contributes to poorer glycemic control, increased risk of diabetes-related complications, and substantially higher healthcare costs, with estimates suggesting an additional economic burden of $4 billion to $9 billion annually compared to individuals with diabetes alone.

Despite clear clinical guidelines from authoritative bodies such as the American Diabetes Association and the United States Preventive Services Task Force recommending routine mental health screening for individuals with diabetes, significant gaps persist in actual clinical practice. Current data indicates that only 25% to 50% of individuals with diabetes who experience depression actually receive appropriate mental health diagnosis and intervention. This substantial care gap stems from multiple systemic barriers, including a critical shortage of qualified mental health professionals available to provide comprehensive assessment and treatment services, limited mental health training among primary care providers who typically manage diabetes care, and inadequate availability of validated mental health screening tools within routine diabetes care settings. Traditional screening approaches often require additional time, resources, and patient effort that may not be feasible within the constraints of typical clinical workflows.

Recent technological advances have introduced alternative approaches to mental health assessment through passive sensing and ecological momentary assessment (EMA) methodologies. Passive sensing refers to the automatic collection of behavioral and physiological data without requiring active participation or additional effort from the individual, while EMA involves repeated sampling of an individual's experiences and behaviors in real-time within their natural environment. These approaches offer the potential for more scalable, less burdensome, and continuous mental health monitoring compared to traditional periodic assessments. Previous research has demonstrated the utility of passive sensing using smartphone-based data, including accelerometer readings, GPS location tracking, ambient light sensor data, and call log information, for detecting mental health indicators in general populations. However, these approaches have significant limitations when applied specifically to diabetes populations, as they fail to leverage the rich behavioral and physiological data already being generated through routine diabetes management activities.

Notably absent from existing literature is any systematic investigation into the use of blood glucose monitoring devices as sources of mental health-relevant data, despite several compelling reasons why such devices should be considered ideal candidates for passive mental health assessment in diabetes populations. First, individuals with diabetes are actively encouraged to engage with blood glucose meters at regular intervals throughout their daily lives, creating frequent opportunities for data collection. Second, blood glucose monitoring behavior itself has been shown to correlate with psychological states, with both monitoring frequency and glycemic variability demonstrating associations with mood and stress levels. Third, modern blood glucose meters often incorporate interactive features that allow users to provide contextual information about their current state, including emotional well-being, at the time of testing. Finally, blood glucose meter data can be seamlessly integrated with other digital health platform interactions to create a comprehensive behavioral profile that captures multiple dimensions of an individual's daily functioning.

Existing digital health solutions for diabetes management have primarily focused on improving glycemic outcomes through education, feedback, and support mechanisms, without systematically addressing the mental health needs of this vulnerable population. While some platforms incorporate basic mood tracking features, these typically require active user input and do not leverage the rich passive data streams generated through routine device usage. Furthermore, previous attempts to apply machine learning approaches to mental health prediction in diabetes populations have been limited by reliance on traditional clinical data sources or insufficient integration of behavioral signals from digital health platforms. The current state of the art lacks a comprehensive, automated system that can continuously monitor mental health risk through analysis of naturally occurring behavioral patterns during routine diabetes self-management activities.

The technical challenges in developing such a system are substantial and include the need to handle heterogeneous data sources with varying temporal characteristics, address significant class imbalances in mental health condition prevalence, ensure model generalizability across diverse demographic groups, and maintain privacy and security of sensitive health information. Additionally, any practical implementation must demonstrate clinical validity while remaining computationally efficient enough for real-time deployment within existing digital health infrastructure. The absence of validated approaches that successfully integrate passive sensing from diabetes-specific devices with machine learning algorithms for mental health risk prediction represents a significant unmet need in digital health technology.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel computer-implemented method and system for detecting potential mental health risk in individuals with diabetes through the analysis of passive sensing signals derived from their routine interactions with diabetes management devices and digital health platforms. The invention overcomes the limitations of traditional mental health screening approaches by leveraging behavioral, physiological, and demographic data that are generated incidentally during normal diabetes self-management activities, thereby eliminating the need for additional patient burden or specialized mental health infrastructure. The core innovation lies in the recognition that diabetes management devices, particularly cellular-enabled blood glucose meters, serve as rich sources of behavioral and emotional data when combined with complementary interactions with mobile applications, web portals, and coaching services.

The method comprises collecting passive sensing signals from multiple data sources during participants' natural engagement with a digital diabetes management program. These signals include demographic information such as age, gender, ethnicity, and race; glucometer data encompassing blood glucose readings, monitoring frequency, temporal patterns of usage, and contextual responses regarding emotional state; coaching interaction data reflecting communication patterns and sociability indicators; and event data capturing engagement with digital platforms including mobile applications and web portals. The invention specifically identifies and extracts eighty-three distinct passive sensing signals that are aggregated over defined time periods to create participant-period instances suitable for machine learning analysis.

A key aspect of the invention is the development and implementation of a sophisticated machine learning model based on LightGBM gradient tree boosting algorithms. The model architecture employs an ensemble approach consisting of ten constituent LightGBM models trained on random subsets of balanced training data achieved through strategic undersampling of control instances. This ensemble methodology addresses the inherent class imbalance in mental health condition prevalence while maximizing the utilization of available training data. The final prediction is generated through soft voting, where the confidence scores from each constituent model are averaged to produce a single aggregate risk assessment.

The invention demonstrates superior performance compared to random prediction across multiple evaluation metrics, achieving area under the curve (AUC) values exceeding 0.65 on all test sets and demonstrating significant improvements in precision, recall, and F1 scores relative to baseline expectations. Feature importance analysis using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) methodology reveals that demographic factors, particularly gender and race, along with emotional state responses during blood glucose monitoring, represent the most predictive signals for mental health risk. Additional important features include interaction frequency and consistency with digital health platforms, temporal patterns of blood glucose monitoring, and glycemic variability metrics.

The system embodiment of the invention comprises a data collection module configured to extract passive sensing signals from diabetes management devices and digital platforms, a data processing module for aggregating signals over defined time windows and filtering ineligible instances, a machine learning engine implementing the ensemble LightGBM model, and an output interface for communicating mental health risk predictions to healthcare providers or integrated care systems. The invention specifically defines eligibility criteria for participant-period instances, excluding data collected prior to program enrollment and periods of extended inactivity exceeding thirty days without any platform interaction.

This technical solution represents a significant advancement in digital health technology by demonstrating that routine diabetes management activities can serve as reliable indicators of mental health status without requiring additional patient effort or specialized assessment procedures. The invention enables proactive identification of individuals at risk for mental health conditions, facilitating timely intervention and potentially improving both mental health and diabetes-related outcomes. By leveraging existing digital health infrastructure and naturally occurring behavioral data, the invention provides a scalable, privacy-conscious approach to mental health monitoring that can be readily integrated into current diabetes care delivery models.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive technical solution for detecting potential mental health risk in individuals with diabetes through the systematic analysis of passive sensing signals generated during routine engagement with digital diabetes management platforms. The detailed implementation encompasses specific data collection methodologies, signal processing techniques, machine learning model architecture, and system integration requirements that collectively enable accurate, scalable, and privacy-preserving mental health risk assessment.

The foundation of the invention rests upon the recognition that individuals with diabetes generate rich behavioral and physiological data streams through their regular interactions with diabetes management devices and digital health platforms. Unlike traditional mental health assessment approaches that require active patient participation through questionnaires or clinical interviews, the present invention leverages data that is collected incidentally during normal diabetes self-management activities. This passive data collection methodology eliminates additional patient burden while providing continuous monitoring capabilities that can detect subtle changes in behavioral patterns indicative of emerging mental health concerns.

The data collection framework encompasses four primary categories of passive sensing signals: demographic information, glucometer data, coaching interaction data, and event data from digital platform engagement. Demographic signals include age, gender, ethnicity, race, body mass index (BMI), and smoking status, all of which have established correlations with mental health outcomes in diabetes populations. Glucometer data represents the most frequent interaction point for participants and includes quantitative metrics such as blood glucose readings, monitoring frequency, and glycemic variability measures (including standard deviation of glucose values). Critically, the invention also captures qualitative contextual information provided by users during blood glucose testing, specifically responses indicating current emotional state as either wellness or unwellness. Temporal patterns of glucometer usage, including time of day and day of week when readings are taken, provide additional behavioral insights that correlate with mental health status.

Coaching interaction data serves as a proxy for sociability and social engagement, with previous research establishing relationships between communication patterns and mental health conditions. The invention captures metrics related to successful and failed contact attempts with diabetes coaches, duration of coaching interactions, and frequency of communications. Event data encompasses comprehensive engagement metrics from mobile applications and web portals, including session frequency, duration, interactivity levels, consistency of usage patterns, and timing of interactions. Additional event signals include voluntary data sharing behaviors with friends and family members, as well as responses to pop-up reminders and notifications within the digital platform.

The data processing methodology implements specific aggregation and filtering protocols to ensure data quality and model reliability. Participant-period instances are created by aggregating passive sensing signals over defined time windows, with empirical testing determining that a four-week aggregation period optimizes data availability while maintaining temporal relevance for mental health risk prediction. Two critical eligibility criteria are applied to filter out ineligible instances: first, instances collected prior to participant enrollment in the diabetes management program are excluded, and second, periods of extended inactivity defined as thirty or more consecutive days without any platform interaction are removed from analysis. This filtering approach ensures that the model operates only on complete data instances where signal absence cannot be assumed to represent zero activity, thereby maintaining the integrity of behavioral pattern analysis.

Ground truth labeling for model training and validation utilizes two complementary data sources to identify cases and controls with respect to mental health conditions. Claims data provides comprehensive information including mental health diagnoses, prescribed interventions, and pharmacy records for medications indicated for mental health conditions. Medication prescription data, available for the entire study population, contains information on mental health-related medications that were both prescribed and actually filled by participants. This dual-source approach maximizes case identification accuracy while accommodating varying data availability across the participant population.

The machine learning model architecture represents a significant technical innovation through its implementation of an ensemble LightGBM gradient tree boosting approach. The core model consists of ten constituent LightGBM models, each trained on randomly selected subsets of balanced training data. Class imbalance, a common challenge in mental health prediction where control instances typically outnumber case instances by approximately two-to-one, is addressed through strategic random undersampling of control instances to achieve equal representation of cases and controls in each training subset. This approach enables full utilization of the available training data while preventing model bias toward the majority class.

Each constituent model undergoes rigorous hyperparameter optimization using automated tuning enabled by the hyperopt Python library with five-fold cross-validation on the training set. The final prediction for any given participant-period instance is generated through soft voting, where the confidence scores (ranging from 0 to 1) produced by each of the ten constituent models are averaged to produce a single aggregate confidence score representing the predicted probability of mental health risk. This ensemble methodology enhances model robustness and generalizability while reducing the impact of any single model's potential overfitting to specific data patterns.

Comprehensive model evaluation employs seven distinct performance metrics to provide a holistic assessment of predictive capability, recognizing that total accuracy alone is insufficient for evaluating diagnostic models on imbalanced datasets. Key metrics include area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), precision, recall, F1 score, and normalized confusion matrix analysis. Performance evaluation utilizes three distinct test sets with different ground truth sources to ensure model generalizability across varying data quality and availability scenarios. Results demonstrate consistent performance across all test sets, with AUC values exceeding 0.65 and significant improvements over baseline random prediction expectations.

Feature importance analysis utilizing SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) methodology provides valuable insights into the relative contribution of individual passive sensing signals to model predictions. The analysis reveals that demographic factors, particularly gender and race, rank among the most important predictive features, consistent with established epidemiological patterns in mental health prevalence. Among behavioral signals, emotional state responses during blood glucose monitoring emerge as the most predictive passive sensing indicators, followed by interaction frequency and consistency metrics, temporal patterns of device usage, and glycemic variability measures. This hierarchical feature importance structure validates the theoretical framework underlying the invention's data collection strategy.

The system implementation comprises several integrated technical components working in concert to deliver real-time mental health risk predictions. A data ingestion module continuously collects passive sensing signals from various sources including cellular-enabled blood glucose meters, mobile applications, web portals, and coaching interaction logs. A data preprocessing pipeline applies the defined aggregation and filtering protocols to create standardized participant-period instances suitable for model input. The machine learning inference engine executes the trained ensemble model to generate risk predictions, while an alerting subsystem communicates high-risk predictions to appropriate stakeholders through secure channels integrated within existing healthcare workflows.

Privacy and security considerations are integral to the system design, with all data handling compliant with relevant healthcare regulations including HIPAA requirements. The passive nature of data collection, limited to interactions that would occur regardless of mental health monitoring purposes, enhances participant acceptance while minimizing privacy concerns compared to approaches requiring additional sensor deployment or active data collection. The invention specifically avoids collection of highly sensitive data types such as GPS location, accelerometer readings, or call content that might raise additional privacy considerations, instead focusing on behavioral patterns derived from necessary diabetes management activities.

The technical advantages of the present invention are manifold. First, it enables scalable mental health risk detection without imposing additional burden on patients or requiring specialized mental health infrastructure. Second, it leverages existing digital health platforms and devices already integrated into patients' daily routines, ensuring high adoption rates and continuous data collection. Third, the passive sensing approach provides objective behavioral indicators that complement or potentially surpass subjective self-report measures in reliability and consistency. Fourth, the ensemble machine learning architecture demonstrates robust performance across diverse demographic groups and varying data quality scenarios. Finally, the system design facilitates seamless integration with existing healthcare delivery models, enabling timely intervention and coordinated care for individuals identified as being at elevated mental health risk.

Implementation of the invention within clinical practice would involve integration with existing diabetes management programs that utilize cellular-enabled blood glucose meters and digital health platforms. Healthcare providers would receive automated alerts when participants exceed predefined risk thresholds, enabling proactive outreach and appropriate mental health referrals. The system could also support personalized intervention strategies based on specific behavioral patterns identified as predictive of mental health risk, such as decreased monitoring frequency, irregular usage patterns, or consistently negative emotional state reports. Longitudinal monitoring capabilities would allow tracking of intervention effectiveness through changes in behavioral patterns over time.

The invention represents a paradigm shift in mental health monitoring for chronic disease populations by demonstrating that routine disease management activities can serve as rich sources of behavioral and emotional data. This approach moves beyond the traditional focus on smartphones as the primary vehicle for passive sensing to embrace condition-specific medical devices as equally valuable data sources. The technical framework established by this invention could potentially be extended to other chronic conditions where regular device interactions generate similar behavioral data streams, creating new opportunities for integrated physical and mental health monitoring across diverse patient populations.
