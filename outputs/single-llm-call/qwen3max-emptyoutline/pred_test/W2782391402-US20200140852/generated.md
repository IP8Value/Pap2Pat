## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of synthetic biology and genetic code expansion, and more specifically to methods and systems for enhancing the fidelity and selectivity of orthogonal translation systems (OTS) through engineered post-translational proofreading mechanisms based on the N-end rule pathway of protein degradation. The invention further encompasses genetically engineered cells, proteins, and biocontainment strategies that leverage these enhanced OTS for improved biosafety, industrial production, and fundamental biological research.

## BACKGROUND

The genetic code, once considered immutable, has been successfully expanded in living organisms to incorporate non-standard amino acids (nsAAs) at defined positions within proteins. This expansion is typically achieved through the use of orthogonal translation systems (OTS), which consist of an orthogonal aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase (aaRS) and its cognate orthogonal tRNA that do not cross-react with the host’s endogenous translation machinery. These OTS are commonly engineered to recognize a reassigned codon, such as the amber stop codon (UAG), and charge the orthogonal tRNA with a specific nsAA. Upon encountering the reassigned codon during translation, the charged tRNA delivers the nsAA to the growing polypeptide chain, thereby site-specifically incorporating the nsAA into the protein.

Despite significant advances in OTS engineering, a persistent challenge remains: the promiscuity of many aaRS variants, which can lead to misincorporation of standard amino acids or structurally similar nsAAs in the absence of the intended nsAA. This misincorporation compromises the fidelity of genetic code expansion and can have detrimental effects in applications requiring high specificity, such as the production of therapeutic proteins containing nsAAs, the development of biocontained organisms, or the study of protein function through precise chemical modifications. Traditional methods for evaluating OTS fidelity often rely on indirect assays, such as fluorescence-based reporters or mass spectrometry, which may lack the sensitivity to detect low levels of misincorporation or fail to distinguish between different types of misincorporated residues.

The N-end rule pathway is a conserved ubiquitin-proteasome system in eukaryotes and a related ATP-dependent proteolytic system in prokaryotes that targets proteins for degradation based on the identity of their N-terminal amino acid. In Escherichia coli, the ClpAP protease complex, in conjunction with the adaptor protein ClpS, recognizes proteins bearing N-terminal destabilizing residues—such as phenylalanine (Phe), leucine (Leu), tryptophan (Trp), and tyrosine (Tyr)—and facilitates their degradation. Conversely, proteins with N-terminal stabilizing residues, such as methionine (Met), alanine (Ala), or serine (Ser), are generally resistant to this degradation pathway. This natural quality control mechanism presents a unique opportunity for engineering post-translational proofreading systems that can selectively degrade proteins resulting from erroneous nsAA incorporation, thereby enhancing the apparent fidelity of OTS.

Prior attempts to improve OTS selectivity have largely focused on directed evolution of the aaRS active site to better discriminate against standard amino acids or competing nsAAs. However, these approaches often require labor-intensive screening and may not fully eliminate misincorporation, especially when the undesired substrates are structurally similar to the target nsAA. Moreover, the impact of tRNA mutations on OTS fidelity has been underexplored, despite evidence that tRNA identity elements and interactions with elongation factors can influence incorporation efficiency and selectivity.

There remains a critical need for robust, scalable, and genetically encodable methods to enhance the fidelity of nsAA incorporation, particularly in the context of industrial biotechnology and biocontainment. The development of such methods would not only improve the reliability of genetic code expansion but also enable new applications that demand absolute specificity in amino acid identity, such as multi-nsAA incorporation, sense codon reassignment, and the creation of highly secure biocontained strains for environmental release.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel methods, systems, and compositions for enhancing the fidelity and selectivity of orthogonal translation systems (OTS) through engineered post-translational proofreading based on the N-end rule pathway of protein degradation. In one aspect, the invention discloses a genetically encoded proofreading system comprising a cleavable ubiquitin domain fused to a reporter protein, wherein a single UAG codon is positioned immediately after the ubiquitin cleavage site, such that upon translation and cleavage, the residue incorporated at the UAG site becomes the N-terminus of the reporter protein. The stability of the reporter protein is then modulated by the N-end rule pathway, allowing for real-time, in vivo assessment of OTS fidelity based on reporter signal intensity.

In another aspect, the invention provides engineered variants of the ClpS adaptor protein, particularly ClpS V65I and ClpS L32F, which exhibit altered recognition profiles for N-terminal residues, including non-standard amino acids. These engineered ClpS variants can be used to selectively stabilize proteins bearing desired nsAAs while destabilizing those containing misincorporated standard amino acids or undesired nsAAs, thereby acting as molecular filters that enhance the effective selectivity of OTS.

In yet another aspect, the invention describes a high-throughput screening platform for evolving improved OTS, which integrates the aforementioned proofreading system into a genomically recoded E. coli strain devoid of UAG codons and release factor 1 (RF1). This platform enables fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS)-based selection of OTS variants that exhibit high activity in the presence of the target nsAA and low activity in its absence, leading to the isolation of highly selective OTS with reduced promiscuity.

In a further aspect, the invention provides an optimized OTS, designated variant 10, which comprises a mutated BipARS aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase and a spontaneously evolved tRNA Tyr CUA with a G51A mutation. This variant exhibits exceptional selectivity for biphenylalanine (BipA) over standard amino acids and other nsAAs, as demonstrated by in vivo fluorescence assays, in vitro aminoacylation kinetics, and mass spectrometry.

In still another aspect, the invention demonstrates the utility of the improved OTS in synthetic auxotrophy-based biocontainment strategies. By replacing the parental BipA OTS with variant 10 in strains where essential genes have been redesigned to depend on BipA incorporation for stability, the invention achieves significantly lower escape frequencies—down to 7.4 × 10⁻⁹ over seven days—and improved cellular fitness, as evidenced by reduced doubling times. These enhancements make the biocontained strains safer and more efficient for industrial applications.

The invention further contemplates the use of the disclosed proofreading system for a wide range of applications, including but not limited to: screening natural or engineered synthetases for nsAA acceptance, reassigning sense codons, studying post-translational modifications, producing high-purity nsAA-containing biologics, and developing orthogonal biocontainment mechanisms. The modular nature of the system allows for its adaptation to different host organisms, nsAAs, and proteolytic pathways, thereby offering a versatile platform for advancing the field of synthetic biology.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### II. Removable Protecting Groups

The present invention contemplates the use of non-standard amino acids (nsAAs) that contain chemically reactive side chains, which are often protected by removable protecting groups to prevent undesired side reactions during protein synthesis or purification. In particular, the invention recognizes that the presence of such protecting groups can influence the interaction of the N-terminal residue with components of the N-end rule pathway, thereby affecting protein stability. For example, the nsAA tert-butyl-tyrosine (tBtylY) contains a bulky tert-butyl group on the phenolic oxygen of tyrosine, which renders it inert under physiological conditions. This protecting group is typically removed post-translationally using acidic conditions or specific chemical reagents to expose the reactive phenol group for downstream applications such as bioconjugation. The invention demonstrates that the variant 10 OTS exhibits high selectivity for biphenylalanine (BipA) and does not significantly incorporate tBtylY, despite the structural similarity between the two molecules. This selectivity is crucial for applications where the nsAA must remain unmodified until a specific trigger is applied, ensuring that only the desired chemical functionality is introduced at the correct time and place. The proofreading system described herein can be used to screen OTS variants for their ability to discriminate between protected and deprotected forms of nsAAs, thereby enabling the development of orthogonal pairs that respond to different chemical environments. Furthermore, the invention suggests that the size and hydrophobicity of the protecting group can be tuned to modulate N-end rule classification, allowing for the design of nsAAs that are either stabilizing or destabilizing depending on their modification state. This dynamic control over protein stability opens new avenues for regulating gene expression, controlling protein localization, and implementing logic gates in synthetic genetic circuits.

### III. Detectable Moiety

The invention utilizes a detectable moiety, specifically superfolder green fluorescent protein (sfGFP), as a reporter to monitor the fidelity of orthogonal translation systems (OTS) in real time. The sfGFP is fused downstream of a cleavable ubiquitin domain and a single UAG codon, such that successful incorporation of the target nsAA at the UAG site results in the production of a stable, fluorescent protein, whereas misincorporation of a destabilizing residue leads to rapid degradation and loss of fluorescence. The use of sfGFP offers several advantages over traditional reporters, including enhanced folding efficiency, resistance to aggregation, and high quantum yield, which collectively improve the sensitivity and dynamic range of the assay. The C-terminal His6x-tag on sfGFP facilitates purification and detection via anti-His antibodies or immobilized metal affinity chromatography, enabling both in vivo and in vitro validation of OTS performance. The invention further contemplates the replacement of sfGFP with other detectable moieties, such as luciferases, β-galactosidase, or epitope tags, depending on the specific requirements of the application. For instance, luminescent reporters may be preferred for high-throughput screening in microtiter plates, while enzymatic reporters could be used for colorimetric assays in resource-limited settings. The modular design of the reporter construct allows for easy swapping of the detectable moiety without altering the core proofreading mechanism, thereby providing flexibility in assay development. Additionally, the invention recognizes that the choice of detectable moiety can influence the kinetics of protein degradation, as larger or more stable proteins may persist longer even when bearing destabilizing N-terminal residues. Therefore, careful calibration of the reporter system is necessary to ensure accurate discrimination between stabilizing and destabilizing residues. The normalized fluorescence-to-optical density (FL/OD) ratio serves as a quantitative metric for OTS fidelity, with higher ratios indicating greater selectivity for the target nsAA.

### IV. Genetic Modifications

The invention relies on a series of precise genetic modifications to create a robust platform for OTS evaluation and evolution. Central to this platform is the use of a genomically recoded Escherichia coli strain, designated C321.ΔA, which has been engineered to lack all 321 UAG stop codons and the associated release factor 1 (RF1). This recoding eliminates competition between the orthogonal tRNA and RF1 for UAG codons, thereby increasing the efficiency of nsAA incorporation and reducing background readthrough. The C321.ΔA strain serves as the chassis for integrating the Ub-UAG-sfGFP reporter construct at a neutral genomic locus, ensuring consistent expression levels across experiments and eliminating plasmid copy number variability. Additional genetic modifications include the deletion of the native clpS gene to create a ClpS-deficient background (C321.Nend), which allows for controlled expression of wild-type or engineered ClpS variants from plasmids. This knockout prevents interference from endogenous ClpS and enables fine-tuning of proofreading stringency by modulating ClpS expression levels. The invention also incorporates a codon-optimized, N-terminally truncated form of the yeast ubiquitin cleavase UBP1, which is expressed from a separate plasmid to expose the N-terminal residue of the reporter protein immediately after translation. The truncation of UBP1 removes regulatory domains that could interfere with its activity in E. coli, while codon optimization ensures high-level expression. Furthermore, the invention utilizes MutS-deficient hosts during OTS library generation to increase the mutation rate and facilitate the emergence of rare, high-fidelity variants. This approach led to the spontaneous discovery of tRNA mutations that enhance OTS selectivity, highlighting the importance of genetic context in directed evolution experiments. Collectively, these genetic modifications create a highly controlled and sensitive environment for assessing and improving OTS fidelity, paving the way for the development of next-generation synthetic biology tools.

### V. Adapter Protein Protease Systems

The invention leverages the bacterial N-end rule pathway, specifically the ClpAP protease complex and its adaptor protein ClpS, to implement post-translational proofreading of nsAA incorporation. In wild-type E. coli, ClpS recognizes proteins bearing N-terminal destabilizing residues—such as Phe, Leu, Trp, and Tyr—and delivers them to the ClpAP protease for degradation. The invention exploits this natural quality control mechanism by engineering the system to recognize misincorporated standard amino acids or undesired nsAAs as destabilizing signals, thereby selectively degrading erroneous protein products. To enhance the versatility of this system, the invention provides engineered variants of ClpS, including ClpS V65I and ClpS L32F, which exhibit altered binding pocket geometries and thus modified recognition profiles. ClpS V65I, for instance, stabilizes proteins containing a broad range of phenyl-containing nsAAs while maintaining strong discrimination against standard amino acids, making it ideal for applications requiring high tolerance for nsAA diversity. In contrast, ClpS L32F imposes stricter selectivity, destabilizing all but the largest nsAAs, which is useful for scenarios where only a single, highly specific nsAA is desired. The invention further demonstrates that overexpression of ClpS or its variants can extend N-end rule recognition to weakly destabilizing residues like Ile and Val, although this may impose a fitness cost due to the degradation of essential native proteins. The adapter protein-protease system is modular and can be co-expressed with the Ub-UAG-sfGFP reporter and OTS components from compatible plasmids, allowing for tunable proofreading intensity. The use of artificial operons with calibrated ribosome binding sites ensures balanced expression of UBP1, ClpS, and ClpP, optimizing the efficiency of the proofreading cascade. This system represents a powerful addition to the synthetic biology toolkit, enabling real-time, in vivo quality control of genetic code expansion with unprecedented precision.

### VI. Cells

The invention is implemented in genetically engineered Escherichia coli cells that serve as a chassis for orthogonal translation and post-translational proofreading. The primary host strain is C321.ΔA, a genomically recoded organism that lacks all UAG stop codons and release factor 1 (RF1), thereby providing a clean background for amber suppression without competition from native termination machinery. This strain is further modified to integrate the Ub-UAG-sfGFP reporter construct at a defined genomic locus, ensuring stable and reproducible expression. For proofreading experiments, a ClpS-deficient derivative of C321.ΔA, termed C321.Nend, is used to eliminate background degradation mediated by endogenous ClpS and allow controlled expression of engineered ClpS variants from plasmids. The cells are cultured in defined minimal media supplemented with the target nsAA when required, and growth conditions are optimized to minimize stress responses that could interfere with translation fidelity. Notably, the invention identifies 2XYT medium as superior to LB medium for ClpS overexpression experiments, as it mitigates the growth inhibition typically observed under high ClpS expression. The cells are also engineered to express a codon-optimized, truncated form of yeast UBP1, which efficiently cleaves the ubiquitin tag from the reporter protein, exposing the N-terminal residue for N-end rule assessment. During OTS evolution, MutS-deficient versions of the host strain are employed to increase the mutational load and facilitate the discovery of rare, high-fidelity variants. The resulting biocontained strains, such as adk.d6, tyrS.d8, and adk.d6/tyrS.d8, harbor essential genes redesigned to depend on nsAA incorporation for stability, and they exhibit dramatically improved containment efficacy and fitness when equipped with the variant 10 OTS. These cells represent a significant advancement in synthetic auxotrophy-based biocontainment, offering escape frequencies below 10⁻⁸ and doubling times nearly halved compared to first-generation strains. The invention thus provides a comprehensive cellular platform for high-fidelity genetic code expansion, with applications ranging from basic research to industrial biotechnology.

### VII. Standard Amino Acid

The invention addresses the challenge of standard amino acid (AA) misincorporation by orthogonal translation systems (OTS), which can compromise the fidelity of genetic code expansion. Through systematic evaluation of the BipA OTS, the invention identifies tyrosine (Tyr), leucine (Leu), and phenylalanine (Phe) as the primary standard AAs misincorporated in the absence of the target nsAA, biphenylalanine (BipA). These residues are known N-end destabilizing signals in E. coli, making them ideal candidates for post-translational proofreading via the ClpS-ClpAP pathway. The invention demonstrates that the Ub-UAG-sfGFP reporter system, when coupled with UBP1-mediated cleavage and ClpS overexpression, effectively suppresses fluorescence in the absence of BipA, confirming that misincorporated standard AAs are recognized and degraded. Mass spectrometry analysis further validates that over 90% of the protein product in BipA-free conditions consists of Tyr, Leu, Phe, and glutamine (Gln)—the latter arising from near-cognate suppression. The engineered ClpS V65I variant maintains wild-type-level discrimination against these standard AAs, ensuring that proofreading does not inadvertently stabilize erroneous products. The invention also reveals that overexpression of ClpS or ClpS V65I can extend degradation to N-terminal isoleucine (Ile) and valine (Val), which are normally weakly destabilizing, suggesting that the proofreading system can be tuned to cover a broader spectrum of standard AAs if needed. This capability is particularly valuable for applications where even minor misincorporation events are unacceptable, such as the production of therapeutic proteins or the implementation of fail-safe biocontainment mechanisms. By converting the inherent promiscuity of OTS into a selectable trait, the invention transforms a longstanding limitation into a powerful tool for enhancing synthetic biology workflows.

### VIII. Non-Standard Amino Acid

The invention provides a comprehensive framework for evaluating and classifying non-standard amino acids (nsAAs) according to their compatibility with the N-end rule pathway. A panel of 11 phenyl-containing nsAAs, including biphenylalanine (BipA), p-iodo-phenylalanine, p-bromophenylalanine, and p-azido-phenylalanine, is systematically tested using the Ub-UAG-sfGFP reporter system in combination with wild-type and engineered ClpS variants. The results reveal a clear size-dependent trend: larger nsAAs such as p-iodo-phenylalanine and BipA are N-end stabilizing, as they are poorly recognized by ClpS and thus yield high fluorescence, whereas smaller or more polar nsAAs like p-bromophenylalanine and p-azido-phenylalanine are destabilizing, leading to reduced signal in the presence of proofreading. This finding underscores the importance of side chain bulk and hydrophobicity in determining N-end rule classification. The invention further demonstrates that the ClpS V65I variant can reclassify certain nsAAs, rendering all tested phenyl-nsAAs stabilizing while maintaining discrimination against standard AAs. Similarly, tryptophan analogs such as 5-hydroxytryptophan (5OHW) are shown to be destabilizing with wild-type ClpS but can be discriminated from tryptophan using ClpS V43I or ClpS V65I. These insights enable the rational selection of nsAAs based on their predicted stability profile, facilitating the design of orthogonal pairs for multi-nsAA incorporation. The invention also highlights the utility of the proofreading system in screening biosynthetic pathways for nsAAs, where intermediates structurally similar to the target molecule could otherwise be misincorporated by promiscuous OTS. By ensuring that only the final, fully modified nsAA is incorporated, the invention enhances the purity and yield of nsAA-containing proteins, which is critical for industrial applications. Overall, the invention establishes a new paradigm for nsAA characterization, moving beyond simple incorporation efficiency to include post-translational stability as a key parameter.

## Exemplary Degradation Materials and Methods

The invention employs a suite of molecular and cellular tools to implement and evaluate post-translational proofreading. Central to this system is the Ub-UAG-sfGFP reporter construct, which is genomically integrated into the C321.ΔA strain to ensure consistent expression. The reporter consists of a cleavable ubiquitin domain followed by a single UAG codon, a conditionally strong N-degron, and sfGFP with a C-terminal His6x-tag. Expression of the N-terminally truncated yeast ubiquitin cleavase UBP1, codon-optimized for E. coli, ensures efficient removal of the ubiquitin tag, exposing the N-terminal residue for N-end rule assessment. The ClpS adaptor protein and ClpP protease subunit are co-expressed from an artificial operon on a compatible plasmid, with expression driven by a tetracycline-inducible promoter. Engineered ClpS variants, such as ClpS V65I and ClpS L32F, are generated by site-directed mutagenesis using degenerate oligonucleotides encoding NTC or NTT codons at targeted positions. Orthogonal translation systems (OTS) are constructed using pEVOL-derived plasmids, which harbor the orthogonal tRNA Tyr CUA under constitutive control and the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase (aaRS) under arabinose-inducible regulation. For OTS evolution, error-prone PCR is used to introduce two to four mutations throughout the bipARS gene, and the resulting libraries are transformed into the C321.Nend strain for FACS-based screening. Cells are sorted in three rounds: positive selection for GFP+ in BipA+, negative selection for GFP− in BipA−, and a final positive sort to enrich high-fidelity variants. Purified OTS variants are validated by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry to confirm site-specific nsAA incorporation and absence of misincorporation products. Biocontainment efficacy is assessed by measuring escape frequencies on nonpermissive media over seven days, while fitness is evaluated by monitoring doubling times in permissive conditions. All constructs are verified by sequencing, and plasmids are maintained under appropriate antibiotic selection. This integrated methodology provides a robust and scalable platform for developing high-fidelity OTS and implementing advanced biocontainment strategies.

## Example II

### A Method of Making a Protein Having a Non-Standard Amino Acid Incorporated at its N-Terminus in an Engineered E. coli Having Orthogonal Translation Systems by Engineering Post-Translational Proofreading to Discriminate Non-Standard Amino Acids

The invention provides a method for producing a protein with a non-standard amino acid (nsAA) incorporated at its N-terminus in an engineered Escherichia coli strain, wherein the fidelity of incorporation is enhanced through engineered post-translational proofreading. The method begins by constructing a reporter plasmid encoding a fusion protein consisting of a cleavable ubiquitin domain, a single UAG codon, and a detectable moiety such as superfolder green fluorescent protein (sfGFP). This reporter is genomically integrated into a recoded E. coli strain (C321.ΔA) that lacks all UAG stop codons and release factor 1, thereby eliminating competition during amber suppression. The strain is further modified to delete the native clpS gene, creating a ClpS-deficient background (C321.Nend) that allows for controlled expression of engineered ClpS variants. An expression plasmid encoding a codon-optimized, N-terminally truncated form of yeast ubiquitin cleavase (UBP1) is introduced to ensure efficient cleavage of the ubiquitin tag, thereby exposing the N-terminal residue of the reporter protein. A second plasmid encoding an orthogonal translation system (OTS), comprising an orthogonal tRNA Tyr CUA and an aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase (aaRS) specific for the target nsAA, is co-transformed into the strain. Additionally, a proofreading plasmid expressing an engineered ClpS variant (e.g., ClpS V65I) and ClpP from an artificial operon is introduced to implement post-translational quality control. The cells are cultured in minimal media supplemented with the target nsAA, and expression of the OTS, UBP1, and ClpS is induced with appropriate inducers (e.g., arabinose and anhydrotetracycline). Upon translation, the UAG codon is suppressed by the orthogonal tRNA charged with the nsAA, resulting in full-length reporter protein. UBP1 cleaves the ubiquitin tag, exposing the nsAA as the N-terminus. If the nsAA is recognized as stabilizing by the engineered ClpS, the reporter protein accumulates and produces a detectable signal (e.g., fluorescence). If a misincorporated standard amino acid or undesired nsAA is present, the engineered ClpS directs the protein to the ClpAP protease for degradation, suppressing the signal. This method enables high-fidelity production of N-terminally modified proteins and can be adapted for any nsAA by selecting an appropriate ClpS variant that classifies the target nsAA as stabilizing and competitors as destabilizing.

### Example III

The invention further demonstrates the application of the post-translational proofreading system to evolve an improved orthogonal translation system (OTS) for biphenylalanine (BipA) incorporation. An error-prone PCR library of the bipARS gene, encoding two to four random mutations, is generated and cloned into a pEVOL-derived plasmid alongside the tRNA Tyr CUA. This library is transformed into the C321.Nend strain harboring the Ub-UAG-sfGFP reporter and the pZE/UBP1/ClpS_V65I proofreading plasmid. The cells are subjected to three rounds of fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS): first, a positive sort to select for high GFP fluorescence in the presence of BipA; second, a negative sort to eliminate cells exhibiting fluorescence in the absence of BipA, thereby enriching for variants with low promiscuity; and third, a final positive sort to recover high-performing variants. Eleven enriched variants are isolated and retransformed into a proofreading-deficient strain (C321.Ub-UAG-sfGFP) to assess intrinsic OTS activity. Variant 10, which contains multiple mutations in BipARS and a spontaneous G51A mutation in the tRNA, exhibits the highest selectivity, showing strong fluorescence in BipA+ conditions and no detectable signal in BipA− conditions. SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry confirm site-specific BipA incorporation and the absence of misincorporation products. In vitro aminoacylation assays demonstrate that both the mutated aaRS and tRNA contribute to the enhanced selectivity. This evolved OTS represents a significant improvement over the parental system and is suitable for applications requiring absolute fidelity, such as biocontainment and therapeutic protein production.

### Example IV

The invention illustrates the utility of the evolved variant 10 OTS in synthetic auxotrophy-based biocontainment. Three biocontained E. coli strains—adk.d6, tyrS.d8, and adk.d6/tyrS.d8—are engineered to depend on BipA incorporation for the stability of essential genes (adk and tyrS). The parental BipA OTS is replaced with the variant 10 OTS in each strain. Escape frequencies are measured by growing cells in permissive media containing BipA and then plating on nonpermissive media lacking BipA. Over seven days, the variant 10-containing strains exhibit escape frequencies up to two orders of magnitude lower than those with the parental OTS, with the double-engineered strain adk.d6/tyrS.d8 achieving an escape frequency of 7.4 × 10⁻⁹. Additionally, all three strains show improved fitness, with doubling times reduced by nearly twofold, likely due to decreased burden from degrading misincorporated proteins. These results demonstrate that enhancing OTS fidelity directly translates into safer and more efficient biocontained organisms, which are critical for industrial applications where environmental release must be prevented.

### Example V

The invention explores the N-end rule classification of a panel of 11 phenyl-containing non-standard amino acids (nsAAs) using the Ub-UAG-sfGFP reporter system. The Bipyridinylalanine OTS and p-acetyl-phenylalanine OTS are used to incorporate various nsAAs, and fluorescence is measured in the presence and absence of proofreading (UBP1/ClpS). Larger nsAAs such as p-iodo-phenylalanine and biphenylalanine are found to be N-end stabilizing, as their signal is unaffected by proofreading, whereas smaller or polar nsAAs like p-bromophenylalanine and p-azido-phenylalanine are destabilizing, showing significantly reduced signal when proofreading is active. This size-dependent classification is attributed to the steric constraints of the ClpS binding pocket, which tolerates bulky hydrophobic side chains but excludes smaller or polar groups. The data provide a predictive framework for selecting nsAAs based on their expected stability, facilitating the design of orthogonal systems for multi-nsAA incorporation.

### Example VI

The invention investigates the potential of engineered ClpS variants to alter N-end rule recognition of tryptophan analogs. Using the 5-hydroxytryptophan (5OHW) OTS, the invention shows that 5OHW is N-end destabilizing with wild-type ClpS, leading to reduced reporter signal. However, when ClpS V43I or ClpS V65I is expressed in a ClpS-deficient strain, the discrimination between 5OHW and tryptophan is improved, as evidenced by higher signal for 5OHW relative to tryptophan. This demonstrates that the proofreading system can be tailored to distinguish subtle chemical differences, such as the addition of a hydroxyl group to the indole ring. Such precision is invaluable for applications requiring discrimination between closely related nsAAs, such as in biosynthetic pathway engineering or the development of orthogonal signaling systems. The ability to rationally design ClpS variants for specific recognition tasks underscores the modularity and adaptability of the post-translational proofreading platform.
