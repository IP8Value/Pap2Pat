# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of gastroprotective pharmaceutical agents, specifically to a novel class of hybrid small molecules designed to prevent and treat nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)-induced gastropathy. More particularly, the invention provides a tryptamine-gallic acid conjugate, designated herein as SEGA (3a), which exhibits potent gastroprotective activity through a unique mechanism involving mitochondrial targeting, intramitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging, and iron chelation. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising this compound, methods for its synthesis, and therapeutic uses thereof in the prevention and treatment of gastric mucosal injury caused by NSAIDs, including but not limited to indomethacin, aspirin, ibuprofen, and naproxen. The invention also relates to the use of SEGA (3a) in accelerating the healing of pre-existing NSAID-induced gastric lesions, thereby offering both prophylactic and therapeutic benefits in the management of NSAID-associated gastrointestinal complications. Additionally, the invention includes diagnostic and imaging applications of radiolabeled forms of SEGA (3a), particularly those labeled with technetium-99m, for monitoring mitochondrial uptake and distribution in vivo.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are among the most widely prescribed and consumed medications globally due to their analgesic, antipyretic, and anti-inflammatory properties. However, their chronic use is frequently associated with significant gastrointestinal toxicity, collectively termed NSAID-induced gastropathy, which manifests as gastric mucosal erosion, ulceration, bleeding, and in severe cases, perforation. Traditional understanding of NSAID-induced gastric injury has largely centered on the inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes, particularly COX-1, leading to reduced prostaglandin synthesis and consequent impairment of mucosal defense mechanisms. While this COX-dependent pathway remains clinically relevant, accumulating evidence has established that NSAID-induced gastropathy involves additional COX-independent mechanisms, with mitochondrial dysfunction emerging as a critical pathophysiological component.

Mitochondria, as the primary cellular energy producers and key regulators of apoptosis, are particularly vulnerable to NSAID-induced damage. Indomethacin, a prototypical NSAID frequently used in experimental models of gastropathy, has been shown to directly interact with mitochondrial complex I of the electron transport chain, resulting in electron leakage and excessive production of superoxide anion radicals (O₂•⁻). This mitochondrial oxidative stress initiates a cascade of events collectively termed mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS), characterized by lipid peroxidation, protein carbonylation, and thiol depletion. Furthermore, ROS-mediated damage to iron-sulfur clusters in mitochondrial enzymes, such as aconitase, leads to the release of free iron within the mitochondrial matrix. This liberated iron catalyzes the Fenton reaction, converting relatively less reactive superoxide and hydrogen peroxide into highly destructive hydroxyl radicals, thereby amplifying oxidative damage in a self-perpetuating cycle.

The resultant mitochondrial dysfunction manifests as impaired respiratory function, collapse of the mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), and activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway through cytochrome c release, caspase-9 activation, and subsequent executioner caspase-3 activation. This mitochondrial pathway of apoptosis contributes significantly to gastric epithelial cell death and mucosal barrier disruption observed in NSAID-induced gastropathy. Current therapeutic strategies for preventing NSAID-induced gastropathy primarily involve co-administration of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) or histamine H2 receptor antagonists, which reduce gastric acid secretion and thereby mitigate acid-dependent mucosal damage. However, these approaches do not address the underlying mitochondrial oxidative stress and apoptosis that occur independently of gastric acid, thus providing incomplete protection against NSAID toxicity.

Various antioxidants and iron chelators have been investigated for their potential to mitigate NSAID-induced gastric injury. Compounds such as gallic acid, vitamin E, and desferrioxamine have demonstrated some protective effects in experimental models, but their efficacy is limited by poor bioavailability, inadequate mitochondrial targeting, or insufficient potency at clinically relevant doses. Gallic acid, while possessing antioxidant and iron-chelating properties, suffers from rapid metabolism and poor systemic absorption, limiting its therapeutic utility. Similarly, desferrioxamine, a potent iron chelator, has poor oral bioavailability and limited ability to penetrate cellular membranes, particularly mitochondrial membranes, thereby restricting its effectiveness in chelating intramitochondrial free iron.

The development of mitochondria-targeted therapeutic agents represents a promising strategy for addressing the root cause of NSAID-induced gastropathy. Effective mitochondrial targeting requires molecules that are sufficiently small, lipophilic, and possess a positive charge or structural features that facilitate accumulation within the negatively charged mitochondrial matrix. Tryptamine derivatives, particularly 5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin), have been recognized for their ability to accumulate in mitochondria due to their lipophilic nature and structural characteristics. However, serotonin itself exhibits toxicity at higher concentrations and lacks the necessary antioxidant and iron-chelating functionalities required for comprehensive protection against MOS.

The design of hybrid molecules that combine mitochondrial targeting moieties with potent antioxidant and iron-chelating pharmacophores represents a rational approach to developing superior gastroprotective agents. Such hybrid molecules would theoretically benefit from enhanced mitochondrial delivery of protective functionalities, thereby simultaneously addressing multiple aspects of MOS—scavenging ROS, chelating free iron, and preserving mitochondrial function. Prior art does not disclose a tryptamine-gallic acid conjugate specifically designed and demonstrated to achieve these objectives in the context of NSAID-induced gastropathy. The present invention addresses this unmet need by providing a novel compound, SEGA (3a), which integrates the mitochondrial targeting capability of 5-hydroxytryptamine with the antioxidant and iron-chelating properties of gallic acid through a stable amide linkage, resulting in a molecule with superior gastroprotective efficacy compared to existing therapeutic agents.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a novel compound, SEGA (3a), which is a hybrid molecule synthesized by conjugating 5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin) with gallic acid through an amide linkage. This compound represents a significant advancement in the field of gastroprotective agents, specifically designed to address the mitochondrial-mediated pathway of NSAID-induced gastropathy. The chemical structure of SEGA (3a) consists of a tryptamine moiety derived from 5-hydroxytryptamine, which provides mitochondrial targeting capabilities due to its lipophilic nature and structural characteristics, linked via an amide bond to a gallic acid moiety, which confers potent antioxidant and iron-chelating properties.

The synthesis of SEGA (3a) is accomplished through a well-established EDC coupling procedure, which ensures high yield and purity of the final product. In this process, 5-hydroxytryptamine is reacted with gallic acid in the presence of 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) hydrochloride as the coupling agent, 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HOBt) as an additive to prevent racemization and improve coupling efficiency, and triethylamine as a base to neutralize the hydrochloride salt and maintain optimal reaction conditions. The reaction is carried out in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) as the solvent at 0°C initially, followed by stirring at room temperature overnight until completion, as monitored by thin layer chromatography (TLC). The reaction mixture is then quenched with ice-cold water, extracted with ethyl acetate, washed with brine, dried over sodium sulfate, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product is purified by silica gel column chromatography to yield pure SEGA (3a) as a stable solid compound.

The molecular design of SEGA (3a) is based on rational considerations that integrate multiple functional requirements for effective gastroprotection against NSAID-induced injury. The tryptamine component, specifically 5-hydroxytryptamine, was selected for its demonstrated ability to accumulate in mitochondria due to its lipophilic nature and structural similarity to endogenous mitochondrial substrates. The 5-hydroxy group on the indole ring is crucial for optimal activity, as demonstrated by comparative studies with analogs lacking this substituent. The gallic acid component was chosen for its well-established antioxidant properties, including free radical scavenging and metal chelating capabilities, particularly its ability to chelate iron ions that participate in the Fenton reaction.

The amide linkage between the tryptamine and gallic acid moieties was specifically selected over alternative linkages such as ester bonds based on comparative studies that demonstrated superior stability and biological activity of the amide-linked compound. This choice was validated through the synthesis and testing of both amide-linked (SEGA, 3a) and ester-linked (GASE, 4d) conjugates, with the amide-linked compound showing significantly better antioxidant activity in both FRAP and DPPH assays. The stability of the amide linkage under physiological conditions contributes to the overall stability of SEGA (3a) in vivo, ensuring that the compound remains intact during systemic circulation and reaches its target site of action in the mitochondria of gastric mucosal cells.

The physicochemical properties of SEGA (3a) are optimized for its intended biological function. The compound exhibits sufficient lipophilicity to facilitate passive diffusion across cellular and mitochondrial membranes, while maintaining adequate solubility in aqueous environments for systemic distribution. The molecular weight of SEGA (3a) is kept within an optimal range to ensure efficient cellular uptake and mitochondrial accumulation without compromising bioavailability. The presence of multiple phenolic hydroxyl groups from the gallic acid moiety provides the necessary redox-active sites for free radical scavenging and metal chelation, while the tryptamine moiety contributes additional antioxidant potential through its indole ring system.

The invention encompasses various pharmaceutical compositions containing SEGA (3a) in therapeutically effective amounts. These compositions may be formulated for different routes of administration, including oral, intraperitoneal, intravenous, and subcutaneous delivery, depending on the specific clinical indication and desired pharmacokinetic profile. Oral formulations may include tablets, capsules, or liquid suspensions containing SEGA (3a) combined with appropriate excipients such as fillers, binders, disintegrants, and lubricants to ensure proper tablet formation and dissolution characteristics. Parenteral formulations may consist of sterile solutions or lyophilized powders for reconstitution, containing SEGA (3a) in combination with solubilizing agents, buffers, and stabilizers to maintain compound stability and compatibility with biological systems.

The dosage of SEGA (3a) for therapeutic applications is determined based on extensive preclinical studies that have established an ED50 value of 6.9 mg/kg body weight in rat models of indomethacin-induced gastropathy. This dosage translates to a human equivalent dose of approximately 8 mg/kg body weight when adjusted according to standard conversion factors, providing a basis for clinical dosing regimens. The compound demonstrates a favorable safety profile in preclinical studies, with no observed toxicity at therapeutic doses, and exhibits excellent stability in physiological conditions, maintaining its structural integrity in both saline solution and serum for up to 24 hours at 37°C.

The invention also includes radiolabeled forms of SEGA (3a), particularly those labeled with technetium-99m, which can be used for diagnostic imaging and monitoring of mitochondrial uptake in vivo. The radiolabeling is achieved through a standard stannous reduction method, where SEGA (3a) is mixed with aqueous pertechnetate (99mTcO4-) and stannous chloride at pH 3.2, followed by incubation at room temperature. The resulting radiolabeled compound demonstrates high labeling efficiency and stability, with minimal transchelation when challenged with diethylene triamine pentaacetic acid (DTPA) and maintained integrity in physiological conditions, making it suitable for in vivo imaging applications.

### Biological Effects

The biological effects of SEGA (3a) are multifaceted and specifically targeted to address the underlying mechanisms of NSAID-induced gastropathy, particularly the mitochondrial-mediated pathway of injury. The primary biological effect of SEGA (3a) is its ability to accumulate in mitochondria of gastric mucosal cells, where it exerts potent antioxidant and iron-chelating activities that prevent and reverse mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS). This mitochondrial targeting is facilitated by the lipophilic nature of the tryptamine moiety, which allows passive diffusion across mitochondrial membranes and accumulation in the mitochondrial matrix due to the negative membrane potential.

Once localized in mitochondria, SEGA (3a) effectively scavenges superoxide anion radicals (O₂•⁻) generated as a result of NSAID-induced electron leakage from the mitochondrial electron transport chain. This scavenging activity is demonstrated through fluorescence microscopy using the specific dye MitoSOX, which shows a significant reduction in mitochondrial superoxide levels in SEGA (3a)-treated cells compared to untreated controls exposed to indomethacin. The compound's ability to scavenge superoxide is attributed to the multiple phenolic hydroxyl groups present in the gallic acid moiety, which can donate electrons to neutralize free radicals and form stable phenoxyl radicals.

Concurrently, SEGA (3a) chelates free iron released from damaged iron-sulfur clusters in mitochondrial enzymes such as aconitase. This iron chelation is demonstrated through fluorescence microscopy using Phen Green SK, an iron-sensitive fluorescent probe, which shows a significant reduction in intramitochondrial free iron levels in SEGA (3a)-treated cells. The chelation of free iron prevents the Fenton reaction, thereby inhibiting the conversion of relatively less reactive superoxide and hydrogen peroxide into highly destructive hydroxyl radicals, thus breaking the cycle of oxidative amplification.

The combined effects of ROS scavenging and iron chelation result in the prevention of mitochondrial oxidative stress markers, including lipid peroxidation, protein carbonylation, and thiol depletion. Experimental measurements show that SEGA (3a) significantly prevents indomethacin-induced increases in malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, a marker of lipid peroxidation, and reduces protein carbonyl formation while maintaining normal levels of total thiols in mitochondrial fractions. These protective effects preserve mitochondrial structural integrity and functional capacity.

SEGA (3a) maintains normal mitochondrial respiratory function by preventing the indomethacin-induced decrease in the respiratory control ratio (RCR), which is the ratio of state 3 to state 4 respiration. The compound preserves complex I-mediated oxygen consumption and prevents the collapse of the mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), as measured by the JC-1 fluorescence ratio. Additionally, SEGA (3a) protects mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity, as demonstrated by the maintenance of MTT reduction capacity in treated cells, indicating preserved metabolic function.

The prevention of mitochondrial dysfunction by SEGA (3a) translates into significant antiapoptotic effects in gastric mucosal cells. The compound inhibits the activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway by preventing the indomethacin-induced activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3, as measured by specific enzymatic assays. Furthermore, SEGA (3a) modulates the expression of Bcl-2 family proteins, preventing the downregulation of antiapoptotic Bcl-2 and the upregulation of proapoptotic Bax, thereby maintaining the balance between cell survival and death signals.

These molecular effects culminate in the prevention of DNA fragmentation and apoptotic cell death, as demonstrated by TUNEL assays both in vitro and in vivo. SEGA (3a) treatment significantly reduces the number of TUNEL-positive cells in gastric mucosal tissue exposed to indomethacin, indicating protection against apoptotic cell death. Immunohistochemical studies using anti-active caspase-3 antibody further confirm the antiapoptotic effect of SEGA (3a) by showing a significant reduction in active caspase-3 immunolabeled cells in treated tissue.

Beyond its protective effects, SEGA (3a) demonstrates significant therapeutic potential in accelerating the healing of pre-existing NSAID-induced gastric lesions. In healing studies, SEGA (3a) treatment significantly reduces the injury index compared to autohealing controls, with complete restoration of normal gastric mucosal architecture observed at 20 hours post-treatment, whereas untreated controls still show significant injury. This accelerated healing is attributed to the compound's ability to create a favorable microenvironment for tissue repair by reducing oxidative stress and preventing ongoing apoptotic cell death.

The biological effects of SEGA (3a) are specific to its unique molecular structure, as demonstrated by comparative studies with structural analogs. The presence of the 5-hydroxy group on the indole ring of the tryptamine moiety is critical for optimal activity, as analogs lacking this substituent show significantly reduced gastroprotective effects. Similarly, the amide linkage between tryptamine and gallic acid is essential for stability and activity, as ester-linked analogs demonstrate inferior antioxidant properties and reduced biological efficacy.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The synthesis of SEGA (3a) was carried out according to the general EDC coupling procedure described in the detailed description. Specifically, 5-hydroxytryptamine (12 mmol) was dissolved in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) along with gallic acid (12 mmol), 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HOBt, 12 mmol), and triethylamine (72 mmol). The reaction mixture was cooled to 0°C, and 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) hydrochloride (14.4 mmol) was added dropwise with stirring. The reaction was allowed to warm to room temperature and stirred overnight until completion, as monitored by thin layer chromatography (TLC) using a solvent system of ethyl acetate:methanol:water (8:1:1). The reaction mixture was then quenched by the addition of ice-cold water (100 mL) and extracted with ethyl acetate (3 × 50 mL). The combined organic phases were washed with brine (2 × 50 mL), dried over anhydrous sodium sulfate, and concentrated under reduced pressure to yield a crude product. The crude product was purified by silica gel column chromatography using a gradient elution system of chloroform:methanol (95:5 to 90:10) to afford pure SEGA (3a) as a white solid (yield: 85%). The structure of the compound was confirmed by 1H NMR, 13C NMR, and mass spectrometry, which showed characteristic signals corresponding to the amide linkage between the tryptamine and gallic acid moieties. The compound was found to be stable when stored at -20°C under inert atmosphere and exhibited good solubility in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and moderate solubility in water, making it suitable for both in vitro and in vivo studies.

### Example 2

The in vitro antioxidant properties of SEGA (3a) were evaluated using two standard assays: the ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) assay and the 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radical scavenging assay. For the FRAP assay, SEGA (3a) was tested at concentrations ranging from 1 to 100 μM in a 96-well microplate format. The FRAP reagent was prepared fresh by mixing acetate buffer (200 mM, pH 3.6), TPTZ solution (10 mM in 40 mM HCl), and ferric chloride solution (20 mM) in distilled water, and incubated at 37°C for 1 hour. Test compounds (25 μL) were added to wells containing 175 μL of FRAP reagent, and absorbance was measured at 595 nm at 6-minute intervals up to 150 minutes. The FRAP value was calculated relative to ferrous sulfate at the same concentration. SEGA (3a) demonstrated concentration-dependent reducing activity, with a FRAP value at 6 minutes that was significantly higher than that of the ester-linked analog GASE (4d) and comparable to standard antioxidants like gallic acid. In the DPPH assay, SEGA (3a) was tested at concentrations ranging from 10 to 100 μM. The assay system contained 1 mL of test compound solution and 4 mL of DPPH solution (0.15 mM in 80% methanol/water). After 30 minutes of incubation at room temperature in the dark, absorbance was measured at 517 nm. SEGA (3a) showed concentration-dependent DPPH radical scavenging activity, with an IC50 value of 25 μM, which was significantly lower (indicating higher potency) than that of GASE (4d) (IC50 = 45 μM) and comparable to gallic acid (IC50 = 20 μM). These results confirmed that the amide linkage in SEGA (3a) provides superior antioxidant activity compared to the ester linkage in GASE (4d).

### Example 3

The iron chelating activity of SEGA (3a) was assessed using a TPTZ-based assay that measures the ability of the compound to prevent the formation of the Fe(II)-TPTZ complex. The assay system contained Fe(II) (10 μM) in 20 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.4), with SEGA (3a) tested at concentrations ranging from 500 nM to 100 μM. TPTZ (20 μM) was added to the reaction mixture, and absorbance was immediately recorded at 595 nm using a UV-visible spectrophotometer. Desferrioxamine, a known iron chelator, was used as a positive control. The results showed that SEGA (3a) effectively prevented the formation of the Fe(II)-TPTZ complex in a concentration-dependent manner, with an IC50 value of 5 μM. At concentrations above 10 μM, SEGA (3a) completely inhibited the characteristic broad peak at 595 nm associated with the Fe(II)-TPTZ complex, indicating strong iron chelating activity. This activity was comparable to desferrioxamine (IC50 = 3 μM) and significantly superior to gallic acid alone (IC50 = 15 μM), demonstrating that the conjugation of gallic acid with 5-hydroxytryptamine enhances its iron chelating properties, likely due to the additional coordination sites provided by the tryptamine moiety.

### Example 4

The gastroprotective efficacy of SEGA (3a) was evaluated in a rat model of indomethacin-induced gastropathy. Male Sprague-Dawley rats (180-220 g) were fasted for 24 hours before the experiment and divided into control, indomethacin-treated, and SEGA (3a)-pretreated groups. The indomethacin-treated group received a single oral dose of indomethacin (48 mg/kg body weight), while the SEGA (3a)-pretreated groups received intraperitoneal injections of SEGA (3a) at doses of 1, 3, 5, 10, 20, and 50 mg/kg body weight, 30 minutes before indomethacin administration. The control group received vehicle only. Four hours after indomethacin treatment, the animals were sacrificed, and stomachs were collected for macroscopic and microscopic evaluation. The severity of mucosal lesions was scored using a standardized injury index scale (0 = no pathology; 1 = small injury 1-2 mm; 2 = medium injury 3-4 mm; 4 = large injury 5-6 mm; 8 = larger injury >6 mm). SEGA (3a) demonstrated dose-dependent gastroprotective activity, with an ED50 value of 6.9 mg/kg body weight. At the highest dose tested (50 mg/kg), SEGA (3a) completely prevented the formation of gastric lesions, with an injury index comparable to the control group. Macroscopic examination revealed that SEGA (3a) pretreatment prevented the characteristic black discoloration (due to oxidized hemoglobin) and mucosal erosion observed in indomethacin-treated animals. Histological analysis confirmed the preservation of normal gastric mucosal architecture in SEGA (3a)-treated animals, with intact surface epithelium and absence of inflammatory cell infiltration, in contrast to the extensive epithelial cell shedding and inflammatory response observed in indomethacin-treated controls.

### Example 5

The ability of SEGA (3a) to prevent mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS) was investigated using primary cultures of rat gastric mucosal cells. Cells were isolated from rat stomach mucosa and cultured in Ham's F-12 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. After reaching 90% confluence, cells were divided into control, indomethacin-treated (5 mM), and SEGA (3a)-pretreated (10 μM) indomethacin-treated groups. Mitochondrial superoxide levels were measured using MitoSOX Red, a mitochondria-targeted superoxide indicator, while intramitochondrial free iron was assessed using Phen Green SK, an iron-sensitive fluorescent probe. Fluorescence microscopy revealed that indomethacin treatment significantly increased both mitochondrial superoxide (red fluorescence) and free iron (green fluorescence) levels compared to control cells. Pretreatment with SEGA (3a) completely prevented these increases, with fluorescence intensities comparable to control levels. Additionally, isolated mitochondria from these cells were analyzed for markers of oxidative stress, including lipid peroxidation (measured as malondialdehyde equivalents), protein carbonylation, and total thiol content. Indomethacin treatment resulted in a 3-fold increase in lipid peroxidation, a 2.5-fold increase in protein carbonylation, and a 60% decrease in total thiol content compared to controls. SEGA (3a) pretreatment completely prevented these changes, maintaining all parameters at control levels. These results demonstrate that SEGA (3a) effectively prevents MOS by simultaneously scavenging mitochondrial superoxide and chelating free iron.

### Example 6

The effects of SEGA (3a) on mitochondrial function were evaluated using isolated mitochondria from rat gastric mucosa. Mitochondria were isolated from control, indomethacin-treated (48 mg/kg body weight), and SEGA (3a)-pretreated (50 mg/kg body weight) indomethacin-treated rats using differential centrifugation and Percoll density gradient purification. Mitochondrial respiratory function was assessed using a Clark-type oxygen electrode to measure oxygen consumption rates. Complex I-mediated respiration was initiated by adding glutamate and malate (5 mM each), and state 3 respiration was triggered by ADP addition (1 mM). Indomethacin treatment resulted in a 70% decrease in state 3 respiration and a 30% decrease in state 4 respiration, leading to a significant reduction in the respiratory control ratio (RCR) from 4.5 in controls to 1.8 in treated animals. SEGA (3a) pretreatment completely prevented these changes, maintaining RCR at 4.3. The mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) was measured using JC-1 dye, which forms red fluorescent aggregates in polarized mitochondria and green fluorescent monomers in depolarized mitochondria. Indomethacin treatment caused a 40% decrease in the red/green fluorescence ratio, indicating ΔΨm collapse. SEGA (3a) pretreatment maintained the ratio at control levels. Mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity was assessed using the MTT reduction assay in cultured gastric mucosal cells. Indomethacin treatment reduced MTT reduction by 60%, while SEGA (3a) pretreatment completely prevented this inhibition. These results demonstrate that SEGA (3a) preserves mitochondrial functional integrity by preventing MOS-induced damage to the electron transport chain and maintaining normal bioenergetic capacity.

### Example 7

The antiapoptotic effects of SEGA (3a) were investigated using both in vitro and in vivo models of indomethacin-induced gastric injury. In cultured gastric mucosal cells, apoptosis was assessed by TUNEL assay using the APO-BrdU™ kit. Control cells showed minimal green fluorescence (Alexa Fluor 488), indicating absence of DNA fragmentation. Indomethacin treatment (5 mM, 16 hours) resulted in prominent green fluorescence co-localized with propidium iodide-stained nuclei, indicating extensive DNA fragmentation. SEGA (3a) pretreatment (10 μM) significantly reduced both the intensity and number of TUNEL-positive cells. Caspase-9 and caspase-3 activities were measured using commercial assay kits. Indomethacin treatment increased caspase-9 activity by 2-fold and caspase-3 activity by 2.5-fold compared to controls. SEGA (3a) pretreatment completely prevented these increases. Gene expression analysis by RT-PCR showed that indomethacin downregulated Bcl-2 (antiapoptotic) and upregulated Bax (proapoptotic) expression, while SEGA (3a) pretreatment maintained normal expression levels. In vivo, TUNEL staining of gastric mucosal sections from indomethacin-treated rats showed abundant dark brown-stained (TUNEL-positive) cells, while SEGA (3a)-pretreated animals showed significantly fewer positive cells. Immunohistochemistry using anti-active caspase-3 antibody confirmed these findings, with numerous caspase-3-positive cells in indomethacin-treated tissue and minimal staining in SEGA (3a)-treated tissue. These results demonstrate that SEGA (3a) prevents the activation of the mitochondrial pathway of apoptosis in gastric mucosal cells exposed to indomethacin.

### Example 8

The therapeutic potential of SEGA (3a) in accelerating the healing of pre-existing NSAID-induced gastric lesions was evaluated in a rat model. Gastric mucosal injury was first induced by oral administration of indomethacin (48 mg/kg body weight). Four hours after injury induction (designated as 0 hours of healing), animals were divided into autohealing and SEGA (3a)-treated groups. The SEGA (3a)-treated group received an intraperitoneal injection of SEGA (3a) (50 mg/kg body weight), while the autohealing group received vehicle only. Stomachs were collected at 2, 4, 8, and 24 hours post-treatment for injury index scoring and histological analysis. The autohealing group showed gradual improvement with injury indices of 52, 28, and 20 at 4, 8, and 20 hours, respectively, indicating slow natural healing. In contrast, the SEGA (3a)-treated group showed significantly accelerated healing with injury indices of 14, 8, and 0 at the same time points, achieving complete restoration of normal mucosal architecture by 20 hours. Histological examination confirmed these findings, with SEGA (3a)-treated animals showing progressive regeneration of surface epithelium, restoration of glandular architecture, and resolution of inflammatory infiltrate, while autohealing controls still exhibited significant mucosal disruption and inflammation at 20 hours. These results demonstrate that SEGA (3a) not only prevents NSAID-induced injury but also actively promotes the healing of established lesions, likely by creating a favorable microenvironment through the reduction of oxidative stress and prevention of ongoing apoptotic cell death.

### Example 9

The mitochondrial uptake and stability of SEGA (3a) were investigated using radiolabeled compound. SEGA (3a) was labeled with technetium-99m (99mTc) using a standard stannous reduction method. Briefly, aqueous 99mTcO4- (2 mCi/mL) was mixed with freshly prepared stannous chloride solution (1 mg/mL, pH 3.2) and SEGA (3a) solution (3 mg/mL). The mixture was incubated at room temperature for 20 minutes, yielding a radiolabeled product with >95% labeling efficiency as determined by ascending TLC using silica gel strips and appropriate solvent systems. Stability studies showed that the radiolabeled compound remained stable (>90% intact) in 0.9% saline and rat serum for up to 24 hours at 37°C. Transchelation studies with diethylene triamine pentaacetic acid (DTPA) at concentrations up to 50 mM showed minimal transchelation (<5%), indicating strong binding of 99mTc to SEGA (3a). For mitochondrial uptake studies, fasted Sprague-Dawley rats received intraperitoneal injections of 99mTc-SEGA (3a) (5-8 μCi in 0.03 mL), followed by oral indomethacin (48 mg/kg) 30 minutes later. Animals were sacrificed 4 hours post-injection, and mitochondria were isolated from gastric mucosa. Radioactivity was measured in a γ-scintillation counter, and results were expressed as percentage of administered dose per mg of mitochondrial protein. The data showed that 0.05% of the administered dose of SEGA (3a) accumulated in gastric mucosal mitochondria, confirming the compound's ability to reach its intended site of action. These findings support the proposed mechanism of action involving mitochondrial targeting and provide a basis for potential diagnostic applications using radiolabeled SEGA (3a).

## ADVANTAGES OF THE INVENTION

The present invention offers several significant advantages over existing approaches to preventing and treating NSAID-induced gastropathy. First and foremost, SEGA (3a) addresses the fundamental mitochondrial-mediated pathway of NSAID toxicity, which is not adequately targeted by current standard therapies such as proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) and histamine H2 receptor antagonists. While these conventional agents primarily reduce gastric acid secretion and thereby mitigate acid-dependent mucosal damage, they fail to address the COX-independent, mitochondrial oxidative stress that plays a crucial role in NSAID-induced gastric injury. By specifically targeting mitochondria and preventing mitochondrial oxidative stress through dual mechanisms of ROS scavenging and iron chelation, SEGA (3a) provides more comprehensive protection against NSAID toxicity.

Second, SEGA (3a) demonstrates superior gastroprotective efficacy compared to conventional antioxidants and iron chelators. The compound's ED50 value of 6.9 mg/kg body weight in rat models of indomethacin-induced gastropathy is significantly lower than that of gallic acid (18.9 mg/kg), vitamin E (45 mg/kg), desferrioxamine (100 mg/kg), and ranitidine (13.5 mg/kg), indicating greater potency at lower doses. This enhanced efficacy is attributed to the rational molecular design that combines mitochondrial targeting capability with potent antioxidant and iron-chelating functionalities in a single molecule, resulting in synergistic protective effects that cannot be achieved by administering these components separately.

Third, the invention provides both prophylactic and therapeutic benefits, as SEGA (3a) not only prevents NSAID-induced gastric mucosal damage but also significantly accelerates the healing of pre-existing lesions. This dual functionality is particularly valuable in clinical practice, where patients may already have established gastric injury when initiating NSAID therapy or may develop lesions despite prophylactic measures. The ability to promote healing through the creation of a favorable microenvironment by reducing oxidative stress and preventing ongoing apoptotic cell death represents a significant advancement over purely preventive approaches.

Fourth, SEGA (3a) exhibits favorable pharmacokinetic and stability characteristics that enhance its therapeutic potential. The compound demonstrates excellent stability in physiological conditions, maintaining its structural integrity in both saline solution and serum for up to 24 hours at 37°C, which ensures that it remains active during systemic circulation and reaches its target site of action. The amide linkage between tryptamine and gallic acid provides superior stability compared to ester linkages, preventing premature hydrolysis and degradation in vivo. Additionally, the compound's ability to accumulate in mitochondria, as demonstrated by radiolabeling studies showing 0.05% of the administered dose reaching gastric mucosal mitochondria, confirms its effective targeting to the site of pathological action.

Fifth, the invention offers potential diagnostic applications through the development of radiolabeled forms of SEGA (3a), particularly those labeled with technetium-99m. These radiolabeled compounds can be used for non-invasive imaging of mitochondrial uptake and distribution in vivo, facilitating personalized therapeutic approaches, monitoring treatment response, and conducting detailed pharmacokinetic studies. The high stability of the radiolabeled compound against transchelation and degradation in physiological conditions makes it suitable for clinical imaging applications.

Sixth, the molecular design of SEGA (3a) provides insights into structure-activity relationships that can guide the development of future generations of gastroprotective agents. The demonstration that the 5-hydroxy group on the indole ring of the tryptamine moiety and the amide linkage between tryptamine and gallic acid are critical for optimal activity provides valuable information for rational drug design and optimization. This knowledge can be applied to develop analogs with improved potency, selectivity, or pharmacokinetic properties.

Finally, the invention represents a paradigm shift in the approach to NSAID-induced gastropathy by focusing on the underlying mitochondrial pathology rather than merely managing symptoms or secondary consequences. This mechanistic approach has the potential to provide more effective and durable protection against NSAID toxicity, reduce the incidence of serious gastrointestinal complications, and improve the safety profile of NSAID therapy for millions of patients worldwide who require these medications for pain and inflammation management.
