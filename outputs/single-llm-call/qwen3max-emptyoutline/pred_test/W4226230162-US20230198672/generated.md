# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of digital communication systems and, more specifically, to methods and apparatuses for reliable data transmission over noisy communication channels through the use of deep learning-based channel coding. The invention introduces a novel neural network architecture and associated training methodology that integrates the principles of list decoding—long established in classical coding theory—into an end-to-end trainable autoencoder framework for channel coding. This approach enables the design of encoder-decoder pairs that can be optimized directly from empirical channel data without requiring an explicit analytical model of the channel, thereby extending the applicability of high-performance channel codes to complex or unknown channel conditions. The invention further provides a specific architectural instantiation, termed incremental redundancy autoencoder (IR-AE), which leverages a hierarchical decoding structure with non-increasing code rates across successive decoding stages to enhance error correction performance, particularly at small list sizes. The technical domain thus encompasses machine learning, information theory, wireless communications, and signal processing, with direct relevance to next-generation communication standards, including 5G and beyond, where robust, adaptive, and high-throughput coding schemes are critical.

## SUMMARY

The present invention discloses a deep learning-based channel coding system that significantly improves the reliability of data transmission over noisy channels by introducing a list autoencoder (listAE) framework. Unlike conventional autoencoder-based channel codes that produce a single decoded message estimate, the listAE generates a list of candidate message words at the decoder output, thereby emulating the well-established concept of list decoding from classical coding theory. This paradigm shift allows the system to tolerate greater uncertainty in the decoding process while preserving the possibility of correct recovery, provided the transmitted message appears anywhere within the output list. To facilitate practical implementation, the invention incorporates a cyclic redundancy check (CRC)-aided selection mechanism that replaces the theoretical genie-aided decoder during operation, enabling the system to select a valid candidate from the list based on embedded parity constraints. Critically, the invention defines a differentiable loss function that approximates the behavior of an ideal genie-aided decoder during training, thereby enabling end-to-end optimization of the encoder and decoder networks using gradient-based methods despite the inherently non-differentiable nature of discrete list membership checks. The loss function operates by computing the minimum binary cross-entropy between the transmitted message and each candidate in the list, effectively encouraging the network to bring at least one candidate close to the ground truth in the continuous probability space before hard decision.

Furthermore, the invention provides a specific and advantageous neural network architecture—termed incremental redundancy autoencoder (IR-AE)—designed to exploit the listAE framework. The IR-AE employs a multi-stage decoder composed of serially concatenated decoding blocks, each operating on a subset of the received signal corresponding to a component code of progressively lower rate. This hierarchical structure mimics the principle of incremental redundancy, where initial decoding attempts are made with higher-rate, less redundant components, and subsequent stages refine the estimate using more redundant, lower-rate components informed by the evolving list of candidates. The encoder in the IR-AE is structured analogously to a turbo encoder, producing multiple parity streams that are interleaved and power-normalized prior to transmission. The decoder iteratively processes these streams in sequence, updating the list matrix at each stage through convolutional neural network (CNN) layers, with interleavers and deinterleavers applied to maintain consistency with the encoding structure. Empirical results demonstrate that the IR-AE architecture, when integrated into the listAE framework, achieves superior block error rate (BLER) performance compared to prior deep learning-based approaches such as Turbo-AE, particularly at small list sizes (e.g., L = 8), and matches or exceeds the performance of state-of-the-art classical codes—including 3GPP New Radio (NR) polar codes—at larger list sizes (e.g., L = 64). The invention thus provides a general, trainable, and high-performance channel coding solution that bridges the gap between deep learning and classical information theory, enabling the automatic design of codes tailored to arbitrary channel conditions without reliance on human ingenuity or closed-form channel models.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention addresses a fundamental challenge in digital communications: achieving reliable data transmission over noisy channels. Traditional approaches to this problem rely on carefully designed channel codes—such as Turbo codes, low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes, and polar codes—that are constructed using mathematical analysis and information-theoretic principles, typically assuming a well-defined channel model like the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channel. While these classical codes have achieved remarkable success and are widely deployed in modern communication standards, their design process remains highly specialized, sporadic, and dependent on human insight. Moreover, their performance may degrade significantly when the actual channel deviates from the assumed model, limiting their adaptability to complex or unknown environments such as those encountered in millimeter-wave, underwater, or biological communication systems. The invention overcomes these limitations by introducing a data-driven, end-to-end trainable framework for channel coding based on deep neural networks, wherein both the encoder and decoder are learned directly from input-output examples of channel transmissions, without requiring an explicit channel model.

At the core of the invention is the list autoencoder (listAE), a novel extension of the standard autoencoder architecture used in prior deep learning-based channel coding approaches. In a conventional autoencoder for channel coding, a message word **u** ∈ {0,1}^K is encoded by a neural network f_θ(**u**) into a real-valued codeword **x** ∈ ℝ^N, which is then transmitted over a noisy channel to produce a corrupted observation **y**. The decoder network g_φ(**y**) maps **y** back to a single estimate **û** of the original message. The entire system is trained to minimize a reconstruction loss, such as binary cross-entropy (BCE), between **u** and **û**, thereby implicitly learning a code that is robust to the specific noise characteristics present in the training data. However, this single-hypothesis decoding strategy imposes a significant constraint on the decoder: it must commit to one answer, even under high uncertainty. This limitation becomes particularly acute at low signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) or with short block lengths, where the posterior distribution over possible messages may be multimodal or diffuse.

The listAE framework alleviates this constraint by allowing the decoder to output a list of L candidate message words, denoted as **u**^(list) = [**u**^(1), **u**^(2), ..., **u**^(L)]^T ∈ [0,1]^{L×K}, where each row represents a soft probability estimate for a candidate message. During training, the values in **u**^(list) are continuous in the interval [0,1], typically produced via a sigmoid activation function, and only hardened to binary values during inference. The key innovation lies in the definition of a training objective that aligns with the goal of list decoding: namely, to ensure that the true transmitted message **u** appears in the list with high probability. To this end, the invention proposes a loss function that computes the minimum BCE between **u** and any candidate in the list:

  ℒ(**u**^(list), **u**) = min_{l ∈ {1,...,L}} ρ(**u**^(l), **u**),

where ρ(·,·) denotes the average BCE over the K bits. This loss encourages the network to make at least one candidate in the list closely match the ground truth, without penalizing the others excessively. Although the min operator is non-differentiable at points where two or more candidates are equally close to **u**, such points occur with probability zero under continuous weight updates and do not impede gradient-based optimization in practice. Importantly, this loss function avoids the need to simulate the exact behavior of a genie-aided decoder—which would require hard decisions and discrete comparisons—during training, thereby maintaining differentiability and enabling efficient backpropagation.

During the testing phase, the listAE must produce a single final output. The invention supports two operational modes: genie-aided (GA) decoding and CRC-aided (CA) decoding. In GA decoding, a hypothetical oracle (the “genie”) checks whether the transmitted message **u** is present in the hardened list (i.e., after rounding each **u**^(l) to {0,1}^K). If so, the decoder outputs **u**; otherwise, it outputs a random candidate from the list. While GA decoding is useful for evaluating the theoretical upper bound of listAE performance, it is not implementable in real systems. To address this, the invention adopts CA decoding, a technique widely used in classical list decoding (e.g., in CRC-aided successive cancellation list decoding of polar codes). In this mode, Z CRC bits are appended to the original K−Z information bits before encoding, forming a length-K message **u** that satisfies a predefined CRC polynomial g(x). At the decoder, each candidate in the list is checked for CRC validity using binary Galois field arithmetic. Among all candidates that pass the CRC check, one is selected as the final output (e.g., randomly or based on a secondary metric). Notably, during training, the CRC bits are treated as ordinary information bits—their algebraic constraints are not enforced in the loss function—to preserve differentiability. This pragmatic choice simplifies training while still enabling effective CRC-based selection at test time, as demonstrated by empirical results.

The invention further provides a concrete and high-performing neural network architecture for implementing the listAE: the incremental redundancy autoencoder (IR-AE). The IR-AE is motivated by the observation that not all parts of a codeword carry equal redundancy, and that decoding can be improved by processing more reliable (i.e., lower-rate) components later in the pipeline, using refined estimates from earlier stages. Specifically, for a rate-1/3 IR-AE, the encoder produces three streams: a systematic stream **x**_1 = f_1(**u**), and two parity streams **x**_2 = f_2(π(**u**)) and **x**_3 = f_3(π'(**u**)), where π and π' are fixed interleavers and f_1, f_2, f_3 are neural network submodules (e.g., CNNs). The full codeword **x** = [**x**_1, **x**_2, **x**_3] is then power-normalized, typically using batch-wise normalization to ensure zero mean and unit variance across all symbols in a training batch. The decoder operates iteratively over I stages. In each iteration i, a series of decoding blocks processes subsets of the received signal **y** = [**y**_1, **y**_2, **y**_3]. The first block uses only **y**_1 (corresponding to a rate-1 component code), the second uses **y**_1 and **y**_2 (rate-1/2), and the third uses all three streams (rate-1/3). Each block takes the current list matrix **P**^(i) ∈ ℝ^{L×K} as input—interpreted as log-likelihood ratios (LLRs) of the message bits—and outputs an updated list matrix **P**^(i+1). Interleavers and deinterleavers are applied to the columns of the list matrix to maintain alignment with the encoding structure. After I iterations, the final list matrix is passed through a sigmoid to produce soft message estimates, which are then hardened for CRC checking.

The IR-AE architecture embodies the principle of incremental redundancy: early decoding stages operate with less redundancy but can quickly eliminate unlikely candidates, while later stages leverage full redundancy to resolve ambiguities among the remaining hypotheses. This staged refinement leads to improved performance, especially at small list sizes where the capacity to recover from early errors is limited. Experimental evaluations confirm that IR-AE outperforms the Turbo-AE architecture (which uses only two parity streams and a simpler iterative structure) at L = 8, while achieving comparable performance at L = 64. When benchmarked against classical codes—including a 3GPP NR-compliant polar code with the same block length (N = 300) and effective information length (K = 92 after 8-bit CRC)—the list IR-AE demonstrates a coding gain of up to 0.5 dB over Turbo-AE and 0.3 dB over polar codes at BLER < 0.03 under CA decoding. These gains are achieved without any handcrafted code design; instead, the entire system is learned from data using stochastic gradient descent with scheduled alternating updates of encoder and decoder weights.

Training of the listAE follows a carefully orchestrated procedure to avoid convergence to poor local minima. A large batch of random message words is generated, encoded, and corrupted with channel noise. The encoder is trained for T_enc steps while the decoder is frozen, followed by T_dec steps of decoder training with the encoder frozen. This alternating schedule, adapted from prior work on Turbo-AE, helps balance the co-adaptation of the two networks. The encoder is trained at a fixed SNR to encourage robust feature extraction, while the decoder is trained across a range of SNRs to ensure generalization. Batch sizes are kept sufficiently large, and learning rates are set low, to enable fine-tuning. Interleavers are fixed to random permutations to decouple the study from interleaver design, though the invention contemplates that optimized interleavers could further enhance performance. The use of CNNs—as opposed to fully connected or recurrent networks—is found to yield the best trade-off between performance and trainability, likely due to their ability to capture local dependencies and translation-invariant patterns in the codeword structure.

In summary, the invention provides a comprehensive and practical framework for deep learning-based channel coding that unifies the flexibility of neural networks with the rigor of classical list decoding. By enabling the decoder to output multiple hypotheses and optimizing the system to ensure the true message is among them, the listAE achieves significant performance gains over single-hypothesis approaches. The IR-AE architecture further refines this idea through a hierarchical, incremental-redundancy decoding strategy that maximizes the utility of the list at every stage. Together, these innovations represent a major step toward the vision of automatically learned, high-performance channel codes that can adapt to arbitrary channels and rival or surpass the best human-designed codes.
