# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of plant molecular biology and biotechnology, specifically to genetically modified plants exhibiting enhanced performance characteristics through the modulation of intracellular sugar compartmentalization. More particularly, the invention provides transgenic plants overexpressing a tonoplast monosaccharide transporter protein designated TMT1 (Tonoplast Monosaccharide Transporter 1), which results in improved growth rates, increased seed yield, enhanced carbon use efficiency, and altered sugar signaling responses under both standard and stress conditions. The invention further encompasses methods for producing such transgenic plants, nucleic acid constructs encoding the TMT1 protein, expression vectors comprising these constructs, and agricultural applications derived from the enhanced physiological properties conferred by TMT1 overexpression. The technical domain spans plant physiology, metabolic engineering, crop improvement strategies, and the molecular mechanisms governing sugar transport across biological membranes, with particular emphasis on the vacuolar membrane (tonoplast) as a critical regulatory interface for cellular carbohydrate homeostasis. The invention is applicable to dicotyledonous and monocotyledonous plant species, including but not limited to model organisms such as Arabidopsis thaliana and economically important crops such as wheat, rice, maize, soybean, and potato, where manipulation of vacuolar sugar storage capacity can lead to significant agronomic benefits including increased biomass production, improved seed quality, and enhanced tolerance to high exogenous sugar environments that typically inhibit plant development.

## BACKGROUND ART

Plant growth, development, and productivity are fundamentally governed by the availability, allocation, and utilization of photosynthetically fixed carbon, primarily in the form of carbohydrates such as glucose (Glc), fructose (Fru), and sucrose (Suc). Within plant cells, sugars serve not only as substrates for energy production and biosynthetic pathways but also as signaling molecules that regulate gene expression, developmental transitions, and stress responses. A critical aspect of sugar metabolism involves the spatial distribution of these metabolites among subcellular compartments, particularly between the cytosol, plastids, and the large central vacuole that occupies up to 90% of the volume in mature plant cells. The vacuole functions as a dynamic reservoir for the storage of various solutes, including inorganic ions, organic acids, secondary metabolites, and carbohydrates, thereby playing a pivotal role in maintaining cellular osmotic balance, turgor pressure, and metabolic homeostasis. The transport of sugars across the tonoplast—the membrane surrounding the vacuole—is mediated by specific carrier proteins that facilitate the movement of monosaccharides and disaccharides between the cytosol and the vacuolar lumen. Among these transporters, members of the TMT (Tonoplast Monosaccharide Transporter) family have been identified as key players in the accumulation of hexoses within the vacuole.

Prior art has established that Arabidopsis thaliana possesses three TMT isoforms—TMT1, TMT2, and TMT3—that localize to the tonoplast and are implicated in glucose uptake into the vacuole. Studies by Wormit et al. (2006) demonstrated that TMT1 and TMT2 are functionally redundant under certain conditions, as single knockout mutants exhibited minimal phenotypic alterations, whereas double mutants lacking both TMT1 and TMT2 showed impaired cold-induced accumulation of glucose and fructose, suggesting a role for these transporters in stress-responsive sugar storage. However, the precise biochemical mechanism of TMT-mediated transport remained incompletely characterized, with indirect evidence supporting a proton-coupled antiport system driven by the electrochemical gradient generated by vacuolar H+-ATPases and H+-pyrophosphatases. Despite this knowledge, the potential of manipulating TMT expression levels to enhance plant performance had not been systematically explored, and no prior art disclosed the beneficial agronomic traits resulting from constitutive overexpression of TMT1.

Conventional approaches to improving crop yield have often focused on enhancing photosynthetic efficiency, increasing nutrient uptake, or modifying sink strength through the overexpression of enzymes involved in starch or sucrose synthesis. For instance, genetic engineering of sucrose transporters such as SUC2 in Arabidopsis has been shown to influence phloem loading and long-distance sugar transport, thereby affecting source-sink relationships and ultimately seed production. Similarly, attempts to increase vacuolar storage capacity through the introduction of heterologous fructan biosynthesis pathways have demonstrated enhanced leaf carbohydrate reserves, though these strategies do not directly address the native monosaccharide transport machinery. Moreover, it has long been recognized that elevated cytosolic sugar concentrations can trigger sugar repression—a phenomenon wherein high levels of hexoses inhibit the expression of photosynthesis-related genes and suppress overall plant growth, particularly under conditions of exogenous sugar supplementation. This regulatory mechanism is thought to involve hexokinase-dependent signaling pathways that sense cytosolic glucose levels and modulate transcriptional networks accordingly. However, prior art lacked a clear understanding of how subcellular sugar partitioning, rather than total cellular sugar content, influences these signaling events and developmental outcomes.

Electrophysiological techniques such as patch-clamp analysis have been employed to study ion and metabolite transport across plant membranes, including the tonoplast. Schneider et al. (2008) reported the detection of electrogenic sugar transport currents in isolated Arabidopsis mesophyll vacuoles, providing direct evidence for carrier-mediated sugar fluxes across the tonoplast. Nevertheless, the molecular identity of the transporters responsible for these currents remained elusive, and no correlation had been established between specific TMT isoforms and measurable electrophysiological responses. Furthermore, while nonaqueous fractionation methods have enabled the quantification of subcellular metabolite distributions in plant tissues, their application to elucidate the functional consequences of altered transporter expression was limited by the absence of well-defined genetic models with targeted modifications in vacuolar sugar transport capacity.

In summary, the background art reveals a fragmented understanding of vacuolar sugar transport mechanisms and their physiological implications. Although TMT proteins were known to reside in the tonoplast and contribute to hexose accumulation, the potential of TMT1 overexpression to confer advantageous traits such as improved growth under high-sugar conditions, increased seed biomass, reduced respiratory carbon loss, and enhanced phloem export capacity had not been anticipated or demonstrated. The present invention addresses these gaps by providing comprehensive experimental evidence that constitutive overexpression of TMT1 in Arabidopsis leads to a cascade of beneficial metabolic and physiological changes, thereby establishing a novel strategy for crop improvement through the manipulation of intracellular sugar compartmentalization.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

The present invention discloses a method for enhancing plant growth, seed yield, and carbon use efficiency by genetically modifying plants to overexpress a tonoplast-localized monosaccharide transporter protein designated TMT1. The invention is based on the discovery that constitutive overexpression of TMT1 in Arabidopsis thaliana results in a significant increase in vacuolar glucose and fructose import capacity, leading to a redistribution of intracellular sugar pools that favors vacuolar accumulation over cytosolic retention. This altered subcellular sugar partitioning has profound effects on plant physiology, including reduced sensitivity to sugar-mediated growth inhibition, decreased respiratory carbon loss, enhanced phloem export of carbohydrates, and ultimately increased seed biomass and quality. The invention provides transgenic plants comprising a recombinant nucleic acid construct wherein a nucleotide sequence encoding the TMT1 protein is operably linked to a constitutive promoter, preferably the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter, thereby driving high-level expression of TMT1 in all plant tissues throughout development. The TMT1 protein encoded by the nucleotide sequence corresponds to the Arabidopsis thaliana TMT1 polypeptide (GenBank accession number Z50752; AGI locus At1g20840) or functionally equivalent homologs from other plant species that share substantial sequence identity and exhibit similar transport activity when expressed in planta.

The invention further discloses that TMT1 functions as an electrogenic proton-coupled antiporter that mediates the import of glucose into the vacuole concomitant with the export of protons from the vacuolar lumen to the cytosol. This transport mechanism is energized by the proton motive force generated across the tonoplast by vacuolar H+-ATPases and H+-pyrophosphatases, with the magnitude of sugar transport being strongly dependent on the transmembrane pH gradient. Electrophysiological analyses using whole-vacuole patch-clamp recordings have demonstrated that vacuoles isolated from TMT1-overexpressing plants exhibit significantly higher glucose-induced currents compared to wild-type controls, confirming the enhanced transport capacity conferred by TMT1 overexpression. Importantly, the invention reveals that the beneficial effects of TMT1 overexpression are not attributable to changes in total cellular sugar content but rather to the strategic sequestration of monosaccharides within the vacuole, thereby reducing cytosolic hexose concentrations that would otherwise activate sugar repression pathways.

A key aspect of the invention is the demonstration that TMT1-overexpressing plants exhibit superior growth performance under conditions of high exogenous glucose, where wild-type plants typically experience severe growth inhibition due to sugar-mediated signaling. This phenotype correlates with altered expression patterns of sugar-responsive genes: TMT1-overexpressing lines maintain higher levels of the photosynthesis-associated CAB1 gene and lower levels of the nitrate reductase NR1 gene under high-glucose conditions, consistent with reduced cytosolic sugar signaling. Additionally, the invention discloses that TMT1-overexpressing plants display reduced respiratory consumption of newly imported glucose and decreased nocturnal CO2 release, indicating improved carbon use efficiency through the conservation of photosynthetically fixed carbon for anabolic processes rather than catabolic oxidation. These metabolic advantages translate into tangible agronomic benefits, including accelerated early growth rates, increased seed weight per thousand seeds, elevated total lipid and protein content in seeds, and enhanced overall seed yield per plant.

The invention also encompasses the unexpected finding that TMT1 overexpression leads to increased expression of the phloem-specific sucrose transporter SUC2 and enhanced sugar export capacity from source leaves to developing sinks. This effect is likely mediated by the reduced cytosolic glucose concentrations in mesophyll cells, which alleviates glucose-mediated repression of SUC2 expression, thereby facilitating more efficient phloem loading and long-distance transport of carbohydrates. Consequently, developing seeds in TMT1-overexpressing plants receive greater carbon allocation, contributing to their increased biomass and nutritional quality. The invention thus establishes a novel paradigm for crop improvement wherein the manipulation of intracellular sugar compartmentalization in source tissues can simultaneously enhance both source strength (through improved carbon retention and export) and sink strength (through increased carbon delivery to reproductive organs).

In addition to the primary embodiment involving constitutive TMT1 overexpression, the invention contemplates alternative embodiments utilizing tissue-specific or inducible promoters to achieve spatially or temporally controlled expression of TMT1, thereby optimizing the balance between enhanced sugar storage and potential metabolic trade-offs. The invention is broadly applicable to diverse plant species, including both dicots and monocots, through the use of orthologous TMT1 genes or engineered variants with improved transport kinetics or substrate specificity. The disclosed technology represents a significant advancement over prior art approaches by directly targeting the native vacuolar sugar transport machinery to achieve coordinated improvements in multiple aspects of plant performance without the need for introducing heterologous metabolic pathways or disrupting essential physiological processes.

## BEST MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### Examples

The following examples illustrate the best mode for carrying out the present invention, detailing the construction of TMT1-overexpressing transgenic plants, characterization of their physiological and molecular phenotypes, and demonstration of the agronomic advantages conferred by TMT1 overexpression. While the examples focus on Arabidopsis thaliana as a model system, the principles and methodologies described are readily adaptable to other plant species through standard molecular biology and plant transformation techniques.

**Example 1: Construction of TMT1-Overexpressing Transgenic Plants**

To generate transgenic Arabidopsis plants overexpressing TMT1, the coding sequence of the Arabidopsis TMT1 gene (AGI locus At1g20840) was amplified from genomic DNA and cloned into a binary vector suitable for Agrobacterium-mediated transformation. Specifically, the TMT1 open reading frame was inserted downstream of the constitutive cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter in the pART27 vector, creating a chimeric gene construct designated 35S:TMT1. To facilitate potential protein detection, a c-Myc epitope tag (EQKLISEEDL) was introduced at the C-terminus of the TMT1 coding sequence through site-directed mutagenesis, replacing the native amino acid residues 376–383 with the corresponding codons for the epitope tag. The integrity of the final construct was verified by complete sequencing to ensure the absence of unintended mutations.

The 35S:TMT1 construct was introduced into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101 using standard electroporation protocols. Transgenic Arabidopsis plants were generated by transforming the homozygous tmt1-2::tDNA double mutant line, which lacks functional TMT1 and TMT2 isoforms and exhibits nearly identical physiological properties to the tmt1-2-3 triple mutant. Transformation was performed using the floral dip method, wherein flowering Arabidopsis plants were immersed in a suspension of Agrobacterium containing the 35S:TMT1 construct along with surfactants to facilitate bacterial entry into floral tissues. Transformed seeds were selected on Murashige and Skoog (MS) medium supplemented with appropriate antibiotics to identify primary transformants (T1 generation). Homozygous T3 lines exhibiting stable inheritance of the transgene were established through successive generations of selection and self-pollination.

From an initial pool of 25 independent transformants, three lines designated 35S:TMT1;1-2 lines 1, 4, and 10 were selected for detailed characterization based on their high levels of TMT1 mRNA accumulation as determined by quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) and northern blot analysis. Under standard growth conditions (21°C, 10-hour photoperiod), TMT1 mRNA was undetectable in both wild-type Col-0 plants and the tmt1-2::tDNA mutant background, consistent with the low basal expression of TMT1 in leaves at ambient temperature. However, upon exposure to cold stress (4°C), wild-type plants exhibited detectable TMT1 transcript levels, while the tmt1-2::tDNA mutant remained devoid of TMT1 expression, confirming the successful knockout of endogenous TMT1 and TMT2 genes. In contrast, all three selected 35S:TMT1 overexpressor lines displayed several-fold higher TMT1 mRNA levels compared to cold-treated wild-type plants, demonstrating effective constitutive expression driven by the 35S promoter.

**Example 2: Electrophysiological Characterization of Vacuolar Sugar Transport**

To directly assess the functional consequences of TMT1 overexpression on vacuolar sugar transport activity, whole-vacuole patch-clamp recordings were performed on isolated mesophyll vacuoles from wild-type, tmt1-2::tDNA mutant, and 35S:TMT1 overexpressor plants. Mesophyll protoplasts were prepared from fully expanded leaves of 5–8-week-old plants grown under controlled environmental conditions, and vacuoles were released through enzymatic digestion followed by gentle mechanical disruption. Patch pipettes were fabricated from borosilicate glass capillaries and filled with a standard bath solution containing 200 mM KCl, 1 mM CaCl2, 2 mM MgCl2, and appropriate buffering agents to establish defined pH gradients across the tonoplast.

Initial experiments were conducted under physiologically relevant conditions mimicking the natural proton gradient across the tonoplast, with the vacuolar lumen maintained at pH 5.5 and the cytosolic side at pH 7.5. Application of 50 mM glucose to the cytosolic face of the vacuole elicited robust inward currents (negative deflections at 0 mV holding potential) in wild-type vacuoles, indicative of electrogenic sugar transport coupled to proton movement. Vacuoles isolated from 35S:TMT1 line 4, which exhibited the highest TMT1 mRNA accumulation among the tested lines, displayed approximately two-fold greater glucose-induced current amplitudes compared to wild-type controls, confirming enhanced transport capacity. In stark contrast, vacuoles from the tmt1-2::tDNA double mutant showed only minimal glucose-responsive currents, representing less than 20% of wild-type activity, thereby validating the essential contribution of TMT1 and TMT2 to tonoplast monosaccharide transport.

To investigate the pH dependence of TMT1-mediated transport, additional experiments were performed under symmetrical pH conditions (pH 7.5 on both sides of the membrane). Under these conditions, glucose-induced currents in 35S:TMT1 overexpressor vacuoles were reduced by approximately 50% compared to those recorded under the physiological pH gradient, demonstrating that the proton motive force significantly enhances TMT1 transport activity. These findings collectively establish that TMT1 functions as a proton-coupled antiporter that utilizes the transmembrane pH gradient to drive glucose import into the vacuole, with overexpression leading to quantitatively greater transport capacity as evidenced by increased electrophysiological responses.

**Example 3: Growth Performance Under High-Glucose Conditions**

To evaluate the impact of TMT1 overexpression on plant growth under conditions of elevated exogenous sugar, wild-type, tmt1-2::tDNA mutant, and 35S:TMT1 overexpressor lines were cultivated in liquid culture medium supplemented with 5% (w/v) glucose. Control cultures lacking glucose supplementation served as baseline comparisons for normal growth performance. Plants were grown for 12 days under short-day conditions (10 hours light/14 hours dark) and harvested for fresh weight determination.

Under control conditions without glucose supplementation, all genotypes exhibited comparable growth rates, achieving average fresh weights of approximately 185 mg per plant after 12 days. However, in the presence of 5% glucose, wild-type plants experienced severe growth inhibition, accumulating less than 25 mg fresh weight over the same period. The tmt1-2::tDNA double mutant showed even more pronounced growth suppression, reaching only about 9 mg fresh weight, consistent with its impaired ability to sequester excess sugars into the vacuole. Remarkably, all three 35S:TMT1 overexpressor lines demonstrated superior growth performance under high-glucose conditions, with fresh weights ranging from 49 mg (line 1) to 78 mg (line 4). Notably, the magnitude of growth enhancement correlated directly with TMT1 mRNA accumulation levels, with line 4 exhibiting both the highest transcript abundance and the greatest biomass accumulation.

**Example 4: Subcellular Sugar Partitioning Analysis**

To determine whether the improved growth of TMT1 overexpressors under high-glucose conditions resulted from altered subcellular sugar distribution rather than changes in total cellular sugar content, nonaqueous fractionation was employed to quantify sugar pools in cytosolic, plastidial, and vacuolar compartments. Leaf tissue from plants grown in 5% glucose-supplemented liquid culture was freeze-dried, homogenized, and subjected to density gradient centrifugation in organic solvents to separate subcellular fractions based on buoyant density. Marker enzyme assays confirmed the purity and identity of each compartment: alkaline pyrophosphatase for plastids, UDP-glucose pyrophosphorylase (UGPase) for cytosol, and acid phosphatase for vacuoles.

Total sugar quantification revealed that all genotypes maintained nearly identical concentrations of glucose (21–23 mmol g−1 fresh weight), fructose (2–3 mmol g−1 fresh weight), and sucrose (27–31 mmol g−1 fresh weight), indicating that differences in growth performance could not be attributed to variations in overall sugar content. However, subcellular partitioning analysis uncovered dramatic shifts in compartment-specific sugar distribution. In wild-type plants, glucose was distributed relatively evenly among vacuole (40%), plastids (43%), and cytosol (17%). In contrast, 35S:TMT1 line 1 accumulated over 80% of its glucose in the vacuole, with less than 1% detectable in the cytosol. Similarly, line 4 stored 65% of glucose in the vacuole and none in the cytosol. Parallel analyses of fructose distribution showed analogous patterns, with TMT1 overexpressors sequestering up to 90% of fructose in the vacuole compared to 45% in wild-type plants. These results demonstrate that TMT1 overexpression effectively redirects monosaccharide flux toward vacuolar storage, minimizing cytosolic accumulation that would otherwise trigger sugar repression pathways.

**Example 5: Molecular Phenotyping of Sugar-Responsive Gene Expression**

To confirm that altered subcellular sugar partitioning translates into modified sugar signaling outputs, the expression of well-characterized sugar-responsive genes was analyzed in plants grown under both control and high-glucose conditions. Northern blot and qRT-PCR analyses were performed to quantify transcript levels of CAB1 (chlorophyll a/b-binding protein 1), which is repressed by glucose, and NR1 (nitrate reductase 1), which is induced by glucose.

Under control growth conditions without exogenous glucose, all genotypes exhibited similar CAB1 and NR1 mRNA levels. However, in the presence of 5% glucose, wild-type plants showed strong repression of CAB1 and induction of NR1, consistent with active sugar signaling. In contrast, 35S:TMT1 overexpressor lines maintained significantly higher CAB1 transcript levels and lower NR1 expression compared to wild-type controls under high-glucose conditions. These expression patterns were corroborated in soil-grown plants, where TMT1 overexpressors consistently displayed elevated CAB1 and reduced NR1 mRNA levels relative to wild-type plants, even in the absence of exogenous sugar supplementation. These findings provide molecular evidence that TMT1-mediated vacuolar sequestration of monosaccharides attenuates cytosolic sugar signaling, thereby preserving photosynthetic capacity and modulating nitrogen metabolism in favor of growth promotion.

**Example 6: Respiratory Carbon Use Efficiency and Gas Exchange Measurements**

To assess the impact of TMT1 overexpression on carbon use efficiency, respiratory consumption of newly imported glucose was measured using radiolabeled [14C]glucose tracer experiments. Soil-grown seedlings were incubated with 100 mM [14C]glucose in the dark for 6 hours, and the amount of 14CO2 released through respiration was quantified by scintillation counting. Wild-type plants respired approximately 18% of the imported glucose, while the tmt1-2::tDNA mutant exhibited increased respiratory loss (26%), likely due to elevated cytosolic hexose availability. In contrast, all three 35S:TMT1 overexpressor lines showed significantly reduced respiratory consumption, ranging from 13% to 14%, indicating more efficient conservation of imported carbon for biosynthetic processes.

Complementary gas exchange measurements over complete diurnal cycles revealed that while net photosynthetic rates during the light phase were largely unchanged among genotypes, nocturnal CO2 release was significantly reduced in TMT1 overexpressor lines 4 and 10 compared to wild-type and tmt1-2::tDNA controls. This reduction in nighttime respiratory carbon loss further contributes to the improved carbon balance observed in TMT1-overexpressing plants, allowing greater allocation of fixed carbon toward growth and storage.

**Example 7: Seed Yield and Composition Analysis**

To evaluate the agronomic potential of TMT1 overexpression, detailed analyses of seed properties were conducted on plants grown under standard soil conditions. Thousand-seed weight measurements revealed that tmt1-2::tDNA mutants produced seeds weighing approximately 90% of wild-type controls, while 35S:TMT1 lines exhibited significantly heavier seeds: line 1 (+13%), line 4 (+28%), and line 10 (+25%) relative to wild-type. Biochemical analyses confirmed that increased seed weight resulted from proportional increases in both lipid and protein content, with line 4 showing the most substantial enhancements (+12% lipids, +14% proteins).

Total seed yield per plant was also markedly improved in TMT1 overexpressors, with line 1 producing 218 mg seeds/plant compared to 125 mg in wild-type controls. Lines 4 and 10 yielded 178 mg and 157 mg seeds/plant, respectively, while the tmt1-2::tDNA mutant produced only 101 mg seeds/plant. Importantly, seed number per silique and total silique count per plant remained unchanged across genotypes, indicating that yield improvements stemmed exclusively from increased individual seed mass rather than altered reproductive architecture.

**Example 8: Phloem Export Capacity and SUC2 Expression**

To investigate the mechanism underlying enhanced seed yield, phloem export capacity was assessed using detached leaf exudation assays. Source leaves from 6-week-old plants were excised at the end of the light phase, and petioles were immersed in EDTA solution to prevent sieve plate occlusion. After 16 hours of dark incubation, total sugar (Suc + Glc + Fru) exported into the collection medium was quantified by HPLC. Wild-type leaves exported approximately 12.5 μmol C6 units g−1 fresh weight, while tmt1-2::tDNA mutants showed slightly reduced export (11.1 μmol). In contrast, all three 35S:TMT1 lines exhibited significantly increased export rates, ranging from 16.6 to 17.9 μmol C6 units g−1 fresh weight.

Concurrent qRT-PCR analysis revealed that SUC2 mRNA levels were elevated by approximately 50% in TMT1 overexpressor leaves compared to wild-type controls, while tmt1-2::tDNA mutants showed reduced SUC2 expression (75% of wild-type). Glucose feeding experiments confirmed that SUC2 expression is negatively regulated by cytosolic glucose, with 50% reduction after 4 hours and 90% reduction after 16 hours of glucose treatment. These findings suggest that TMT1-mediated reduction of cytosolic glucose concentrations alleviates repression of SUC2 expression, thereby enhancing phloem loading capacity and facilitating greater carbon delivery to developing seeds.

Collectively, these examples demonstrate that constitutive overexpression of TMT1 in Arabidopsis confers a suite of beneficial traits through the strategic manipulation of intracellular sugar compartmentalization, providing a robust platform for crop improvement strategies aimed at enhancing yield, quality, and stress resilience.
