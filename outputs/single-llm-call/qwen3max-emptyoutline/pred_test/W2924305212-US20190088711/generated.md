# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Technical Field

The present invention relates generally to the field of non-volatile memory devices and, more specifically, to a novel class of spintronic memory elements that utilize spin current-induced magnetization switching for data storage and retrieval. The invention further pertains to memory architectures incorporating such elements, particularly those designed for high-speed, low-power, and high-endurance applications in edge computing, artificial intelligence (AI) systems, Internet of Things (IoT) devices, and next-generation cache or embedded memory technologies. The core of the invention lies in a three-terminal memory cell architecture based on spin-orbit torque (SOT)-driven magnetization reversal in magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs), wherein the write operation is decoupled from the read path through the use of a dedicated spin current-generating layer—referred to herein as a spin current line or SOT line—typically composed of a heavy metal with strong spin Hall effect properties. This configuration enables ultrafast switching speeds, significantly reduced stress on the tunnel barrier during write operations, and demonstrably high write endurance exceeding 10¹² cycles, thereby overcoming critical limitations inherent in conventional spin-transfer torque magnetic random access memory (STT-MRAM) technologies. The invention thus provides a foundational platform for scalable, reliable, and energy-efficient memory solutions suitable for replacing static random-access memory (SRAM), embedded flash, and other volatile or semi-volatile memory types in advanced computing environments where power efficiency, speed, and longevity are paramount.

### Description of Related Art

The relentless demand for lower power consumption, higher performance, and greater integration density in modern electronic systems—particularly in the context of edge-AI and IoT applications—has intensified the search for alternatives to conventional complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS)-based memory technologies. Among emerging non-volatile memory candidates, magnetic random access memory (MRAM) has garnered significant attention due to its inherent non-volatility, fast read/write speeds, and compatibility with standard semiconductor fabrication processes. Early MRAM implementations relied on magnetic field-induced switching, which suffered from poor scalability and high power consumption. The advent of spin-transfer torque (STT) MRAM marked a substantial improvement by enabling current-driven magnetization reversal in nanoscale magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs). In STT-MRAM, a write current is passed directly through the MTJ stack, generating a spin-polarized current that exerts torque on the free layer’s magnetization, thereby switching its orientation between parallel (P) and antiparallel (AP) states relative to a fixed reference layer. This mechanism allows for high-density integration and reduced write currents compared to field-switched MRAM.

However, STT-MRAM faces fundamental challenges that limit its applicability in high-performance and ultra-low-power scenarios. A primary limitation arises from the fact that the same current path used for writing also traverses the thin insulating tunnel barrier (typically MgO) of the MTJ. Repeated high-current pulses during write operations induce electromigration, dielectric breakdown, and thermal stress in the tunnel barrier, leading to progressive degradation and eventual failure. Consequently, the write endurance of STT-MRAM is typically capped at around 10⁹ to 10¹⁰ cycles, which is insufficient for applications requiring SRAM-like endurance (>10¹² cycles), such as last-level caches (LLCs) in processors or persistent memory in AI accelerators. Moreover, as the write pulse width is reduced to achieve faster switching speeds (e.g., sub-nanosecond regimes), the required write current density increases dramatically due to the thermal activation barrier of magnetization reversal, exacerbating the stress on the tunnel barrier and further limiting endurance and reliability.

To circumvent these issues, researchers have proposed three-terminal MRAM architectures based on spin-orbit torque (SOT) effects. In SOT-MRAM, the write current flows laterally through an adjacent heavy metal layer (e.g., tungsten, tantalum, or platinum) rather than vertically through the MTJ. Due to the spin Hall effect (SHE) or Rashba-Edelstein effect (REE), this in-plane current generates a transverse spin current that injects spins with perpendicular polarization into the adjacent ferromagnetic free layer of the MTJ, thereby exerting a torque capable of switching its magnetization. Because the write current does not pass through the tunnel barrier, the MTJ is effectively isolated from electrical stress during write operations, theoretically enabling near-infinite endurance limited only by the robustness of the SOT line itself. Additionally, SOT switching can occur on picosecond to sub-nanosecond timescales, offering potential for write speeds significantly faster than STT-MRAM.

Despite these theoretical advantages, practical implementation of SOT-MRAM has been hindered by several unresolved challenges. First, early SOT devices often required an external magnetic field to break symmetry and enable deterministic switching, which is incompatible with integrated circuit design. Although field-free switching schemes have since been developed—using strategies such as exchange bias, shape anisotropy, or synthetic antiferromagnetic (SAF) pinned layers—the reliability and uniformity of such approaches across large arrays remain areas of active investigation. Second, while the decoupling of write and read paths alleviates stress on the MTJ, it introduces new failure mechanisms associated with the SOT line itself. High current densities in resistive heavy metal lines can lead to Joule heating, electromigration, void formation, and material migration, potentially causing electrical opens or shorts that render the memory cell inoperable. Critically, prior to the present invention, there had been no experimental demonstration of SOT-MRAM achieving write endurance beyond 10¹² cycles, nor conclusive evidence that the MTJ remains undamaged after such extensive cycling. Third, the integration of three-terminal cells into dense arrays presents architectural complexities, including the need for additional select lines, increased cell area, and sophisticated peripheral circuitry for write pulse generation and read sensing.

Several prior art references have explored aspects of SOT-based memory. For instance, Liu et al. (2012) demonstrated current-induced magnetization switching via the spin Hall effect in Pt/CoFeB/MgO structures but required an external magnetic field. Later works by Garello et al. (2013) and others introduced field-free switching using tilted anisotropy or interfacial Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction, yet endurance data remained scarce. U.S. Patent No. 9,812,634 B2 describes a three-terminal MRAM cell with a heavy metal write line but does not provide experimental validation of high-cycle endurance or failure mode analysis. Similarly, academic studies have reported SOT switching with pulse widths below 1 ns and current densities on the order of 10⁷–10⁸ A/cm², but without systematic endurance testing beyond 10¹⁰ cycles. Crucially, none of the prior art has conclusively identified the dominant failure mechanism in SOT-MRAM under high-cycle stress, nor has it demonstrated that the MTJ tunnel barrier remains intact after 10¹² write operations—a key requirement for commercial viability in cache and embedded memory applications.

Furthermore, while simulation studies have suggested that SOT-MRAM could offer superior energy efficiency compared to STT-MRAM for sub-nanosecond pulses, experimental validation of this claim in actual device structures with realistic materials and geometries has been lacking. The trade-off between write current density, pulse width, thermal stability, and endurance remains poorly quantified in real-world devices. Additionally, scaling SOT-MRAM to array configurations introduces variability in write current delivery, cross-talk, and yield issues that are not apparent in single-device studies. Prior attempts to fabricate small SOT-MRAM arrays have focused primarily on functionality rather than endurance or failure statistics.

In summary, the related art reveals a clear technological gap: although SOT-MRAM promises high speed, low power, and high endurance, no prior work has experimentally demonstrated write endurance exceeding 10¹² cycles in a functional device, nor has it provided a comprehensive failure mode analysis confirming that the MTJ remains undamaged while failures originate exclusively in the SOT line. The absence of such evidence has impeded the adoption of SOT-MRAM in commercial high-endurance applications. The present invention directly addresses these deficiencies by providing the first experimental proof of 10¹²-cycle endurance in a spin current-type memory cell, coupled with detailed physical characterization showing that the MTJ stack—including the critical MgO tunnel barrier—exhibits no detectable damage after extensive cycling, while all observed failures are attributable to electromigration-induced open or short circuits in the tungsten SOT line. This breakthrough establishes SOT-MRAM as a viable candidate for SRAM replacement and enables new memory architectures optimized for AI and edge computing workloads.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a spin current-type memory device and associated memory array architecture that achieves unprecedented write endurance exceeding 10¹² cycles while maintaining high-speed operation, low power consumption, and structural integrity of the magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ). The invention is based on a three-terminal memory cell design in which magnetization switching of the MTJ’s free layer is accomplished via spin-orbit torque (SOT) generated by an in-plane write current flowing through an adjacent heavy metal layer—herein referred to as the spin current line or SOT line—thereby completely decoupling the write path from the read path and eliminating electrical stress on the tunnel barrier during write operations. This fundamental architectural distinction enables the device to overcome the endurance limitations inherent in conventional spin-transfer torque (STT)-MRAM, where write current passes directly through the fragile tunnel barrier.

In one aspect, the invention discloses a spin current magnetization rotational element comprising a magnetic tunnel junction with a free layer, a tunnel barrier layer, and a pinned layer, wherein the free layer exhibits in-plane magnetization oriented orthogonally to the direction of write current flow in the SOT line. The SOT line is formed from a high-resistivity tungsten layer (approximately 400 µΩ·cm) with dimensions optimized to enhance spin Hall efficiency while maintaining thermal and electromigration stability. The pinned layer is implemented as a synthetic antiferromagnetic (SAF) structure with an IrMn antiferromagnetic pinning layer, which generates a controlled stray field that facilitates field-free magnetization switching without requiring external magnetic fields. The entire stack is fabricated using a top-pinned configuration on a silicon substrate with thermal oxide, and the MTJ is patterned into an elliptical shape (120 nm × 360 nm) to leverage shape anisotropy for stable in-plane magnetization alignment.

A key innovation of the invention is the experimental demonstration that the MTJ remains structurally and functionally intact after 10¹² write cycles, as confirmed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) cross-sectional analysis showing no degradation at the interfaces between the free layer, tunnel barrier, and pinned layer. All observed device failures during endurance testing were exclusively attributed to electromigration-induced defects in the tungsten SOT line, manifesting as either electrical-open conditions (due to void formation and line bursting) or electrical-short conditions (due to metal migration forming conductive bridges along the MTJ sidewalls). This finding conclusively establishes that the endurance bottleneck in SOT-MRAM resides not in the MTJ but in the SOT line, thereby redirecting engineering efforts toward optimizing the SOT conductor rather than reinforcing the tunnel barrier.

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for operating the spin current magnetization rotational element, including applying bipolar write pulses of alternating polarity through the SOT line to switch the free layer between parallel and antiparallel states relative to the pinned layer. The write pulses are characterized by durations ranging from 1 nanosecond to 1 microsecond and current densities between 6.7×10⁷ A/cm² and 8.3×10⁷ A/cm², with optimal endurance achieved at the lower end of this range. The read operation is performed by passing a small DC sense current vertically through the MTJ stack, independent of the SOT line, thereby enabling non-destructive readout with minimal power consumption.

Furthermore, the invention encompasses a small-scale memory array embodiment comprising an 8×8 grid of the disclosed spin current magnetization rotational elements, integrated with custom analog write/read circuitry controlled by a field-programmable gate array (FPGA). This array demonstrates the scalability of the technology and provides statistical validation of the failure mode: accelerated endurance tests at increasing write current densities (J₁ < J₂ < J₃) show a monotonic increase in the number of failed cells with higher current, consistent with electromigration-driven degradation in the SOT lines. The survival ratio of functional cells decreases predictably with write cycle count and current density, confirming the reproducibility of the failure mechanism across multiple devices.

The invention also includes design guidelines for enhancing endurance, such as reducing the resistivity of the SOT line material, minimizing the length of the SOT line segment beneath the MTJ, and engineering the SAF pinned layer to nullify stray fields that cause switching asymmetry. These strategies mitigate Joule heating and electromigration, thereby extending operational lifetime beyond 10¹² cycles. Additionally, the invention demonstrates that the threshold current density for magnetization switching increases as pulse width decreases—a behavior consistent with thermal activation models—but that SOT switching remains energetically favorable compared to STT for sub-nanosecond pulses.

In summary, the present invention represents a paradigm shift in non-volatile memory technology by providing the first experimental proof of 10¹²-cycle write endurance in a spin current-type memory device, identifying the true failure origin in the SOT line rather than the MTJ, and offering a scalable architecture suitable for high-performance, low-power applications in AI accelerators, edge computing, and next-generation cache hierarchies. The disclosed device structure, operational methodology, and failure analysis collectively establish a robust foundation for commercializing SOT-MRAM as a universal memory solution.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Spin Current Magnetization Rotational Element

The present invention is embodied in a spin current magnetization rotational element that constitutes the fundamental building block of a high-endurance, non-volatile memory cell. This element is specifically engineered to leverage spin-orbit torque (SOT) effects for ultrafast, low-power, and highly reliable magnetization switching, thereby overcoming the intrinsic limitations of prior spin-transfer torque (STT)-based magnetic memory technologies. The core of the element is a magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ) integrated with a dedicated spin current-generating layer—referred to herein as the SOT line—that enables three-terminal operation wherein write and read functions are physically and electrically separated. This architectural separation is pivotal to achieving the unprecedented write endurance of over 10¹² cycles demonstrated by the invention, as it eliminates direct current flow through the tunnel barrier during write operations, thereby preventing the electromigration, dielectric breakdown, and thermal degradation that plague conventional STT-MRAM.

The MTJ of the spin current magnetization rotational element is constructed as a top-pinned stack deposited on a thermally oxidized silicon substrate. The layer sequence, proceeding upward from the substrate, comprises a tungsten (W) SOT line, a cobalt-iron-boron (CoFeB)-based free layer, a magnesium oxide (MgO) tunnel barrier layer, a CoFeB-based synthetic antiferromagnetic (SAF) pinned layer, an iridium-manganese (IrMn) antiferromagnetic pinning layer, and a capping layer. All layers are deposited in a single sputtering process without breaking vacuum to ensure clean interfaces and optimal magnetic and transport properties. The W SOT line serves a dual purpose: it acts as the conduit for the in-plane write current and, due to its strong spin Hall effect, converts charge current into a transverse spin current that exerts torque on the adjacent free layer. The resistivity of the W layer is deliberately engineered to approximately 400 µΩ·cm through precise control of sputtering parameters, a value that enhances spin Hall efficiency while balancing conductivity and thermal stability.

The free layer is composed of CoFeB and is designed to exhibit in-plane magnetization, a critical feature that enables field-free switching when combined with the orthogonal orientation of the write current and the stray field from the SAF pinned layer. The in-plane anisotropy is achieved through a combination of material thickness and shape anisotropy; specifically, the free layer is made sufficiently thick and the MTJ is patterned into an elliptical shape measuring 120 nanometers in the minor axis and 360 nanometers in the major axis. This elliptical geometry ensures that the easy axis of magnetization aligns along the long axis of the ellipse, which is oriented perpendicular to the direction of current flow in the underlying W SOT line. As a result, the spin current generated by the SOT effect exerts a torque that efficiently rotates the magnetization vector between two stable in-plane orientations—parallel (P) and antiparallel (AP)—relative to the pinned layer’s magnetization direction.

The pinned layer is implemented as a synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF), consisting of two CoFeB layers separated by a ruthenium (Ru) coupling spacer, which are antiferromagnetically coupled to produce a net zero magnetic moment while maintaining a strong exchange bias when pinned by the adjacent IrMn layer. This SAF structure not only provides thermal stability against stray fields but also generates a controlled stray field at the free layer interface. In the disclosed embodiment, this stray field, denoted as H_shift, is measured at approximately -60 Oe and is instrumental in breaking the symmetry of the SOT switching process, thereby enabling deterministic, field-free magnetization reversal without the need for external magnetic fields or complex multi-layer engineering. The IrMn layer, with its high blocking temperature and robust exchange coupling, ensures that the SAF pinned layer remains fixed during both write and read operations.

The MgO tunnel barrier, sandwiched between the free and pinned layers, is epitaxially grown to maximize the tunnel magnetoresistance (TMR) ratio, which in the present invention exceeds 90% even after 10¹² write cycles. This high TMR ratio is essential for reliable readout, as it provides a large resistance difference between the P and AP states, enabling low-power sensing with high signal-to-noise ratio. Critically, because the write current flows laterally through the W SOT line and never traverses the MgO barrier, the tunnel barrier is shielded from electrical stress, which is the primary cause of endurance failure in STT-MRAM. This decoupling is the cornerstone of the invention’s high endurance.

Operation of the spin current magnetization rotational element involves two distinct phases: write and read. During the write phase, a bipolar current pulse is applied through the W SOT line. A positive current pulse generates a spin current that switches the free layer from the P to the AP state, while a negative current pulse reverses the process. The pulse width is tunable from 1 nanosecond to 1 microsecond, with shorter pulses requiring higher current densities due to the reduced time available for thermal activation over the energy barrier. Experimental data show that at a pulse width of 10 nanoseconds, a current density of 6.7×10⁷ A/cm² achieves nearly 100% switching probability for P-to-AP transitions, though AP-to-P transitions exhibit slightly lower probability due to the influence of the -60 Oe stray field. Increasing the current density to 8.3×10⁷ A/cm² improves switching symmetry but accelerates degradation of the W SOT line due to Joule heating.

During the read phase, a small DC voltage is applied vertically across the MTJ stack, and the resulting current is measured to determine the resistance state. Since the read current does not interact with the SOT line, the operation is non-destructive and consumes minimal power. The three-terminal architecture inherently prevents read disturb errors, as the read path is isolated from the high-current write path.

Endurance testing of the element reveals that the MTJ remains fully functional after 10¹² write cycles, with the TMR ratio sustained at approximately 90%. Cross-sectional transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis of cycled devices shows pristine interfaces at the W/free layer and free layer/MgO boundaries, confirming the absence of tunnel barrier damage. All observed failures—manifesting as either infinite resistance (electrical-open) or near-zero resistance (electrical-short)—are traced to the W SOT line. In open-failure devices, TEM images reveal voids and ruptures in the W line, indicative of electromigration under high current density. In short-failure devices, metallic filaments of W are observed bridging the MTJ sidewalls, suggesting material migration driven by Joule heating. These findings conclusively demonstrate that the endurance limit is governed by the SOT line’s electromigration resistance, not the MTJ’s integrity.

To validate scalability, an 8×8 array of these elements was fabricated with integrated analog write/read circuitry controlled by an FPGA. Accelerated endurance tests at three increasing current density levels (J₁ < J₂ < J₃) showed a clear correlation between higher current and increased failure rate, consistent with the single-element results. The survival ratio of functional cells decreased monotonically with write cycle count, confirming the reproducibility of the SOT-line-limited failure mechanism across an array.

In conclusion, the spin current magnetization rotational element of the present invention represents a transformative advance in non-volatile memory technology. By decoupling write and read paths, leveraging field-free SOT switching, and demonstrating 10¹²-cycle endurance with intact MTJ structure, the invention provides a robust, scalable, and commercially viable platform for next-generation memory applications demanding extreme reliability, speed, and energy efficiency.
