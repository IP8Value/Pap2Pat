# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and enzymology, specifically to engineered mutant forms of endoglycosidases derived from *Streptococcus pyogenes*, designated herein as Endo-S mutants, which exhibit enhanced transglycosylation activity and reduced hydrolytic activity relative to wild-type Endo-S or previously reported variants. The invention further encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding such mutant enzymes, recombinant host cells expressing them, methods for producing these enzymes, and their application in the chemoenzymatic remodeling of glycoproteins—particularly immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies—through transglycosylation reactions. A key aspect of the invention involves a novel one-pot enzymatic method for transferring complex N-glycans from natural glycopeptide donors, such as sialoglycopeptide (SGP) or its truncated analog SG-Asn, directly onto deglycosylated IgG Fc regions without requiring pre-synthesized glycan oxazoline intermediates. This approach leverages synergistic combinations of mutant endoglycosidases with complementary substrate specificities, including variants of Endo-S together with engineered forms of Endo-M from *Mucor hiemalis* or Endo-CC from *Coprinopsis cinerea*. The resulting glycoengineered antibodies possess homogeneous, defined glycoforms that can be tailored to modulate effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), thereby enhancing therapeutic efficacy, safety, and manufacturability of monoclonal antibody therapeutics.

## BACKGROUND ART

Antibodies, particularly immunoglobulin G (IgG), are central mediators of adaptive immunity and have become indispensable tools in modern medicine as therapeutic agents for cancer, autoimmune disorders, infectious diseases, and other conditions. The biological activity of IgG is not solely determined by its antigen-binding specificity but is also profoundly influenced by the structure of the conserved N-linked glycan attached to asparagine 297 (Asn297) in the Fc region. This glycan typically comprises a biantennary complex-type oligosaccharide containing N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc), mannose, galactose, fucose, and sialic acid residues. Critically, the presence or absence of core fucose significantly impacts the affinity of IgG for FcγRIIIa receptors on natural killer (NK) cells, thereby modulating antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC)—a key mechanism of action for many anticancer antibodies. Similarly, terminal galactose and sialic acid residues influence complement activation and anti-inflammatory properties, respectively. Consequently, precise control over the glycoform of therapeutic antibodies is essential for optimizing their pharmacological profiles.

Traditional approaches to produce antibodies with defined glycoforms rely on engineering mammalian expression systems—such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells—through knockout or overexpression of glycosyltransferases or glycosidases. While effective, these strategies often yield heterogeneous mixtures or require extensive process development and are limited in the range of accessible glycoforms. An alternative and increasingly promising strategy is *in vitro* chemoenzymatic glycan remodeling, which enables site-specific, high-fidelity modification of the Fc glycan post-production. This method exploits endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidases (ENGases), a class of enzymes capable of cleaving the β-1,4-glycosidic bond within the chitobiose core (GlcNAc–GlcNAc) of N-glycans. Certain ENGases, notably Endo-S from *Streptococcus pyogenes*, exhibit remarkable selectivity for IgG Fc glycans and can catalyze not only hydrolysis but also transglycosylation when provided with activated glycan donors such as sugar oxazolines.

Sugar oxazolines are high-energy intermediates that mimic the oxazolinium ion transition state formed during enzymatic glycosidic bond cleavage. In the presence of an appropriate ENGase, oxazolines serve as efficient glycosyl donors for transglycosylation onto GlcNAc-containing acceptors, such as deglycosylated IgG. This approach has been successfully used to generate homogeneous antibody glycoforms for functional studies and therapeutic development. However, a major limitation of this methodology lies in the inherent instability of oxazolines, which are prone to hydrolysis under aqueous conditions, necessitating their synthesis immediately prior to use and in large excess—typically 10- to 20-fold molar excess relative to antibody—to drive the reaction to completion. Moreover, even engineered ENGases such as the well-known Endo-S D233Q mutant, which reduces hydrolytic activity by disrupting a key catalytic residue involved in substrate-assisted oxazolinium ion formation, still retain residual hydrolysis activity toward the transglycosylation product. This leads to product degradation over time, especially at extended reaction durations required for high conversion, thereby compromising yield and purity.

To address these challenges, researchers have explored structural and mechanistic insights into ENGase catalysis to design improved mutants. Prior work identified that mutation of Asp233 to Gln (D233Q) in Endo-S significantly diminishes hydrolysis while preserving transglycosylation capacity. Additional mutations at positions Q303 and Y305 were investigated based on proximity to the active site, though they did not yield substantial improvements. Meanwhile, studies on other ENGases, such as Endo-M from *Mucor hiemalis* and Endo-CC from *Coprinopsis cinerea*, revealed that analogous mutations (e.g., N175Q in Endo-M, N180H in Endo-CC) could similarly attenuate hydrolysis. Notably, Endo-M and Endo-CC exhibit broader substrate ranges than Endo-S but lack activity toward core-fucosylated glycans—a feature that limits their direct use on native IgG, which predominantly bears core-fucosylated Fc glycans.

Recent efforts have attempted to circumvent oxazoline synthesis by developing one-pot systems where a donor glycopeptide is first processed by one ENGase to generate an activated intermediate, which is then transferred by a second ENGase to the antibody acceptor. For instance, wild-type Endo-M can hydrolyze sialoglycopeptide (SGP)—a naturally abundant glycopeptide derived from egg yolk containing a complex, sialylated biantennary glycan—to release the free glycan, which can then be chemically converted *in situ* to its oxazoline form using activating agents. However, this chemical step introduces complexity, potential side reactions, and scalability concerns. A fully enzymatic, oxazoline-free route would represent a significant advancement for industrial applications.

Despite these advances, no system has yet achieved both high transglycosylation efficiency (>90%) and minimal hydrolysis using low donor-to-acceptor ratios (<10 eq) in a scalable, one-pot format without chemical activation. There remains a critical need for engineered ENGases with superior kinetic properties—specifically, high transglycosylation rates, negligible product hydrolysis, and compatibility with natural glycopeptide donors—and for innovative reaction architectures that integrate multiple enzyme specificities to enable practical, cost-effective glycoengineering of therapeutic antibodies.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the inefficiency and impracticality of current chemoenzymatic methods for producing homogeneous glycoforms of therapeutic antibodies, particularly IgG. Existing approaches relying on sugar oxazoline donors suffer from several interrelated drawbacks: (1) the necessity of synthesizing unstable oxazoline compounds in large excess (often ≥20 equivalents per antibody Fc domain), which increases cost, complexity, and waste; (2) residual hydrolytic activity of even engineered ENGases (e.g., Endo-S D233Q), leading to degradation of the transglycosylation product over time and limiting maximum achievable yields; (3) slow transglycosylation kinetics requiring prolonged reaction times, during which oxazoline decomposition and product hydrolysis further reduce efficiency; and (4) the lack of scalable, fully enzymatic methods that eliminate the need for chemical synthesis of activated donors altogether. These limitations hinder the adoption of chemoenzymatic glycan remodeling in large-scale manufacturing of next-generation antibody therapeutics with optimized effector functions.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention solves these problems through two synergistic innovations. First, it provides novel mutant forms of Endo-S from *Streptococcus pyogenes* that exhibit dramatically improved transglycosylation efficiency and markedly reduced hydrolytic activity compared to wild-type Endo-S and previously known variants such as D233Q. These mutants are generated by introducing additional amino acid substitutions—at positions including Q303, E350, D405, D279, Y402, and others—into the D233Q background, based on structural modeling and sequence alignment with other ENGases. Specific double and triple mutants, such as D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, and D233Q/D405A, achieve transglycosylation efficiencies exceeding 90% using only 5 molar equivalents of sugar oxazoline donor per antibody molecule, with minimal or undetectable hydrolysis of the product even after 48 hours of incubation.

Second, the invention discloses a novel one-pot enzymatic transglycosylation method that completely eliminates the need for pre-synthesized oxazoline donors. This method employs a combination of two engineered ENGases: (i) a mutant Endo-S (e.g., D233Q or D233Q/E350Q) that selectively acts on the deglycosylated IgG Fc acceptor but exhibits minimal hydrolysis of the final product, and (ii) an auxiliary ENGase mutant—either Endo-M N175Q from *Mucor hiemalis* or Endo-CC N180H from *Coprinopsis cinerea*—that processes a natural glycopeptide donor such as sialoglycopeptide (SGP) or SG-Asn to generate an activated glycan intermediate *in situ*. Due to their distinct substrate specificities—Endo-S targets core-fucosylated Fc glycans, whereas Endo-M and Endo-CC do not—the two enzymes operate orthogonally, preventing cross-hydrolysis and enabling efficient transfer of the intact sialylated glycan from SGP directly onto the antibody. This fully enzymatic cascade achieves transglycosylation efficiencies up to 97% under optimized conditions, using SGP in 300-fold molar excess, and has been demonstrated at the 10-milligram scale with high recovery and homogeneity.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The present invention offers numerous advantageous effects over the prior art. The novel Endo-S mutants enable highly efficient transglycosylation with significantly reduced consumption of expensive and labile sugar oxazoline donors—down to 5 equivalents compared to 20 or more previously required—thereby lowering material costs and simplifying process logistics. Their suppressed hydrolytic activity ensures high product stability, allowing reactions to proceed to near-completion without degradation, which enhances final yield and purity. The rapid transglycosylation kinetics of certain mutants (e.g., D233Q/D405A reaches 78% conversion in 2 hours) shorten processing times, improving throughput and reducing exposure to conditions that may denature proteins.

The one-pot transglycosylation system represents a paradigm shift by replacing synthetic chemistry with enzymatic catalysis for donor activation. By utilizing naturally sourced SGP—a commercially available, inexpensive byproduct of egg processing—as the glycan donor, the method avoids the multi-step organic synthesis of oxazolines, eliminating associated hazards, purification steps, and batch-to-batch variability. The use of orthogonal ENGase mutants prevents undesired hydrolysis of either the donor intermediate or the final glycoconjugate, ensuring high fidelity and efficiency. This system is scalable, as demonstrated by the successful production of 8 mg of homogeneous SG-trastuzumab from 10 mg of starting material, with recovery yields of 80% and confirmed structural homogeneity by LC-ESI-MS.

Furthermore, the modular nature of the approach allows flexibility in glycan selection: different glycopeptide donors (e.g., high-mannose, hybrid, or asialo forms) can be employed with appropriate ENGase pairs to install diverse glycoforms tailored for specific therapeutic indications. The ability to produce antibodies with defined, homogeneous sialylated glycans is particularly valuable for developing anti-inflammatory biologics, as α2,6-sialylation has been linked to immunomodulatory activity via interactions with specific lectin receptors.

Collectively, these innovations advance the feasibility of chemoenzymatic glycan remodeling from a research tool to a robust, industrially viable platform for the manufacture of next-generation antibody therapeutics with precisely controlled effector functions, enhanced safety profiles, and improved pharmacokinetics.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### <Mutant Enzyme of the Present Invention>

The present invention provides mutant forms of endoglycosidase S (Endo-S), an enzyme originally isolated from *Streptococcus pyogenes*, which have been engineered to possess superior transglycosylation activity and significantly reduced hydrolytic activity compared to the wild-type enzyme and previously described variants. Wild-type Endo-S is a member of the glycoside hydrolase family 18 (GH18) and specifically hydrolyzes the β-1,4-glycosidic bond between the two innermost N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) residues of the chitobiose core in complex-type N-glycans attached to the Fc region of immunoglobulin G (IgG). While this hydrolytic activity is useful for deglycosylating antibodies, it is detrimental in transglycosylation reactions where the goal is to attach a new glycan onto a GlcNAc-terminated Fc acceptor. Earlier efforts to convert Endo-S into a transglycosylase focused on the D233Q mutation, which disrupts a key residue involved in stabilizing the oxazolinium ion intermediate during hydrolysis, thereby reducing product degradation. However, the D233Q mutant still exhibits measurable hydrolysis over time and requires high concentrations of sugar oxazoline donors to achieve acceptable yields.

The mutant enzymes of the present invention build upon the D233Q scaffold by introducing one or more additional amino acid substitutions at strategic positions identified through structural analysis, sequence alignment with related ENGases (such as Endo-M and Endo-CC), and empirical screening. These positions include, but are not limited to, residues 279, 303, 350, 402, and 405 of the Endo-S polypeptide (numbering according to the mature protein sequence). Each substitution is selected to modulate enzyme-substrate interactions in a manner that either weakens binding to the hydrolytic transition state, strengthens binding to the transglycosylation acceptor, or alters the conformational dynamics of the active site to favor glycosyl transfer over water-mediated cleavage.

Exemplary mutant enzymes include, without limitation, Endo-S comprising the amino acid substitutions D233Q and Q303L; D233Q and E350Q; D233Q and D405A; D233Q and D279S; D233Q and Y402F; D233Q, Q303L, and E350Q; and D233Q, Y402F, and D405A. In these mutants, the original aspartic acid at position 233 is replaced by glutamine to impair hydrolysis, while the secondary mutations fine-tune activity. For instance, substitution of glutamine 303 with leucine (Q303L) removes a polar side chain near the catalytic pocket, likely reducing non-productive interactions with water or the glycan product. Similarly, replacement of glutamic acid 350 with glutamine (E350Q) neutralizes a negative charge that may otherwise stabilize hydrolytic transition states. The D405A mutation replaces a negatively charged aspartate buried in the protein interior with a small, nonpolar alanine, potentially altering the local electrostatic environment or flexibility of a loop involved in substrate recognition.

These mutant enzymes retain the high specificity of wild-type Endo-S for IgG Fc glycans, including core-fucosylated structures, but exhibit markedly improved performance in transglycosylation reactions. When tested with deglycosylated trastuzumab as the acceptor and a sialylated glycan oxazoline as the donor, the D233Q/Q303L mutant achieves 96% transglycosylation efficiency within 24 hours with negligible hydrolysis thereafter. The D233Q/D405A mutant demonstrates even faster kinetics, reaching 78% conversion in just 2 hours, and maintains higher transglycosylation yields than D233Q at all time points. Triple mutants such as D233Q/Q303L/E350Q show detectable hydrolysis only after 48 hours and sustain high transglycosylation efficiency throughout the reaction. Notably, some mutants like D233Q/Y402F/D405A display no detectable hydrolysis up to 48 hours, although their maximum transglycosylation efficiency plateaus below 80%, suggesting a trade-off between hydrolysis suppression and catalytic turnover.

All mutant enzymes described herein are soluble, express well in *Escherichia coli* using standard recombinant techniques (e.g., GST-fusion constructs), and can be purified to homogeneity using affinity and size-exclusion chromatography. They are stable under typical reaction conditions (50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4, 28–37°C) for at least 48 hours, making them suitable for industrial-scale applications. The improved kinetic profile—high k<sub>cat</sub>/K<sub>M</sub> for transglycosylation coupled with low k<sub>hyd</sub> for hydrolysis—enables the use of low donor-to-acceptor ratios (as low as 5:1 molar ratio of oxazoline to antibody heavy chain), which represents a substantial improvement over prior art requiring 20-fold excess.

### <Gene, Host Cell, Method of Producing Enzyme>

The present invention further encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding the mutant Endo-S enzymes described above. These nucleic acids may be DNA or RNA sequences that are codon-optimized for expression in a chosen host organism, such as *Escherichia coli*, yeast (*Pichia pastoris*, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*), insect cells, or mammalian cells. In a preferred embodiment, the gene encoding wild-type Endo-S from *Streptococcus pyogenes* is cloned into an expression vector such as pGEX-4T-3, which provides an N-terminal glutathione-S-transferase (GST) tag to facilitate purification. Site-directed mutagenesis is then performed using kits such as the QuikChange II Site-Directed Mutagenesis Kit (Agilent) to introduce the desired amino acid substitutions (e.g., D233Q, Q303L, etc.) into the coding sequence. The resulting plasmids contain operably linked regulatory elements, including a strong promoter (e.g., T7/lac promoter), ribosome binding site, and transcriptional terminator, enabling high-level expression upon induction.

Recombinant host cells for producing the mutant enzymes include prokaryotic and eukaryotic systems. In a preferred embodiment, *Escherichia coli* BL21(DE3) cells are transformed with the expression plasmid and cultured in Luria-Bertani (LB) broth supplemented with ampicillin (100 μg/mL) at 37°C until mid-log phase (OD<sub>600</sub> ≈ 0.6–0.8). Protein expression is induced by addition of isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) to a final concentration of 0.1 mM, followed by incubation at 16°C for 20 hours to promote soluble expression and proper folding. After harvesting by centrifugation, cell pellets are resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing lysozyme and DNase I, and lysed by sonication. The soluble fraction is applied to a glutathione-Sepharose 4B affinity column, where the GST-tagged Endo-S mutant binds specifically to immobilized glutathione. After washing, the enzyme is eluted with reduced glutathione and buffer-exchanged into PBS. For large-scale preparations, an additional size-exclusion chromatography step (e.g., HiLoad 26/600 Superdex 200) is employed to remove residual glutathione and aggregates, yielding highly pure, monodisperse enzyme at concentrations of 2–7 mg/mL.

Alternative expression systems may be used depending on the need for post-translational modifications, although Endo-S is naturally a bacterial enzyme and does not require glycosylation for activity. Yeast systems offer high-density fermentation capabilities, while mammalian cells may be employed if co-expression with chaperones is needed for difficult-to-express variants. Regardless of the host, the method ensures consistent production of active mutant enzyme suitable for glycoengineering applications.

### <Sugar Remodeling>

The mutant Endo-S enzymes of the present invention are employed in methods for the chemoenzymatic remodeling of glycoproteins, particularly IgG antibodies, to install defined, homogeneous N-glycans at the conserved Fc site (Asn297). The general workflow involves two stages: (1) deglycosylation of the native antibody to expose a single GlcNAc residue at Asn297, and (2) transglycosylation of a desired glycan onto this GlcNAc acceptor using a mutant Endo-S and an activated glycan donor.

In the first stage, native IgG (e.g., trastuzumab) is treated with wild-type Endo-S or another suitable ENGase under mild conditions (e.g., 50 mM phosphate buffer, pH 6.0, 37°C, 4 hours) to cleave the Fc glycan between the two GlcNAc residues, leaving a single GlcNAc attached to Asn297. The deglycosylated antibody is then purified by protein A affinity chromatography, which exploits the preserved Fc structure, followed by hydroxyapatite chromatography to remove residual enzyme and buffer components.

In the second stage, the deglycosylated antibody is incubated with a mutant Endo-S enzyme (e.g., D233Q/Q303L) and a glycan donor. Two donor formats are supported: (a) pre-synthesized sugar oxazoline, or (b) natural glycopeptides such as sialoglycopeptide (SGP) in a one-pot system with an auxiliary ENGase mutant.

When using oxazoline donors, the reaction is conducted in 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) at 28–30°C with a donor-to-acceptor ratio as low as 5:1 (molar ratio of oxazoline to antibody heavy chain). The mutant Endo-S catalyzes the transfer of the entire glycan from the oxazoline to the GlcNAc-Fc acceptor, forming a native β-1,4 linkage. Reactions typically reach >90% completion within 24 hours with minimal hydrolysis.

In the one-pot method, SGP—a glycopeptide isolated from egg yolk containing a biantennary, disialylated complex glycan attached to a tripeptide (Asn-Leu-Thr)—is used as the donor. SGP is incubated with the deglycosylated antibody, a mutant Endo-S (e.g., D233Q/E350Q), and an auxiliary ENGase mutant such as Endo-M N175Q or Endo-CC N180H. The auxiliary enzyme processes SGP to generate a glycan-oxazoline-like intermediate *in situ*, which is then captured by the Endo-S mutant and transferred to the antibody. Due to the substrate specificity differences—Endo-M and Endo-CC cannot act on core-fucosylated Fc glycans, while Endo-S cannot hydrolyze SGP—the system operates without cross-reactivity. Optimal conditions use 300 equivalents of SGP, 28°C, and 4–8 hours, yielding up to 97% transglycosylation. The product, SG-trastuzumab, contains a homogeneous, sialylated glycoform identical to that found in human serum IgG and is purified by protein A and hydroxyapatite chromatography with >80% recovery.

This sugar remodeling platform is versatile: by selecting different glycopeptide donors (e.g., asialo-SGP, high-mannose glycopeptides) or oxazolines, a wide array of glycoforms can be installed to modulate ADCC, CDC, half-life, or anti-inflammatory activity. The method is compatible with various IgG subclasses and other Fc-fusion proteins, enabling broad therapeutic applicability.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, the design and generation of Endo-S mutants are described. The wild-type Endo-S gene from *Streptococcus pyogenes* was synthesized and subcloned into the pGEX-4T-3 vector to create a GST-fusion construct for soluble expression in *E. coli*. Based on the crystal structure of Endo-S (PDB ID: 4N4S) and sequence alignments with Endo-M and Endo-CC, residues surrounding the active site cleft were targeted for mutagenesis. In addition to the known D233Q mutation, substitutions were introduced at positions 122, 187, 279, 303, 348, 350, 402, 405, and 406, altering side chain size, charge, or hydrophobicity. Over 120 single, double, triple, and quadruple mutants were generated using site-directed mutagenesis, with most built upon the D233Q background. All mutants were expressed in *E. coli* BL21(DE3) by induction with 0.1 mM IPTG at 16°C for 20 hours. Cell lysates were prepared by sonication in PBS containing lysozyme and DNase I, and soluble GST-Endo-S fusion proteins were purified by glutathione-Sepharose affinity chromatography. Protein yields were comparable to wild-type Endo-S, indicating that the mutations did not compromise expression or solubility. Purified mutants were buffer-exchanged into PBS and stored at −80°C for activity assays. This library enabled systematic evaluation of how specific residue changes affect hydrolysis and transglycosylation.

### Example 2

This example details the hydrolysis and transglycosylation activity screening of Endo-S mutants. Hydrolysis assays were performed by incubating each mutant (10 μg) with commercial trastuzumab (500 μg) in 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.4) at 30°C. Aliquots were taken at 1, 2, 4, 8, 24, and 48 hours and analyzed by capillary electrophoresis using the Experion Pro260 system to quantify the percentage of deglycosylated heavy chain. Transglycosylation assays used deglycosylated trastuzumab (800 μg) and sialylated glycan oxazoline (53.5 μg, 5 eq) with 16 μg of mutant enzyme under identical buffer and temperature conditions. The percentage of transglycosylated heavy chain was similarly quantified over time. Results showed that while single mutants offered no improvement over D233Q, several double and triple mutants exhibited reduced hydrolysis and enhanced transglycosylation. Specifically, D233Q/Q303L reached 96% transglycosylation at 24 h with minimal hydrolysis thereafter. D233Q/D405A achieved 78% conversion in 2 h and outperformed D233Q at all time points. Mutants like D233Q/D279S and D233Q/Y402F showed <20% hydrolysis only at 48 h, while D233Q/Y402F/D405A displayed no detectable hydrolysis up to 48 h, though its transglycosylation plateaued at ~75%. These data identified lead candidates for further development.

### Example 3

This example demonstrates the one-pot transglycosylation from sialoglycopeptide (SGP) to trastuzumab using Endo-S and Endo-M mutants. Deglycosylated trastuzumab (1 mg) was incubated with SGP (300 eq) in 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.4) at 28°C with various enzyme combinations. Control reactions with Endo-S D233Q alone or with wild-type Endo-M yielded only 13% transglycosylation due to hydrolysis. However, combining Endo-S D233Q with Endo-M N175Q (5 mU) resulted in 95% efficiency at 8 h. Using SG-Asn (a truncated SGP with only Asn) slightly slowed the reaction but increased maximum efficiency to 97%. Substituting Endo-S D233Q/E350Q further improved performance. SGP concentration dependency studies showed that ≥100 eq was required for >90% conversion. At medium scale, 10 mg of deglycosylated trastuzumab was reacted with SGP (300 eq), Endo-S D233Q (200 μg), and Endo-M N175Q (0.05 U) for 4.5 h. The product was purified by protein A and hydroxyapatite chromatography, yielding 8.0 mg of SG-trastuzumab. LC-ESI-MS confirmed a homogeneous glycoform with mass 51508.7 Da, matching theoretical calculation.

### Example 4

This example evaluates the use of Endo-CC mutants as alternatives to Endo-M in the one-pot system. Deglycosylated trastuzumab (1 mg) and SGP (300 eq) were incubated with Endo-S D233Q (20 μg) and either wild-type Endo-CC (17 mU) or Endo-CC N180H mutant (33 mU) in 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.4). Reactions were conducted at 37°C (optimal for Endo-CC) or 28°C. Wild-type Endo-CC produced no detectable transglycosylation product. In contrast, Endo-CC N180H achieved 57% transglycosylation at 8 h at 37°C, but significant product hydrolysis occurred thereafter. Lowering the temperature to 28°C suppressed hydrolysis but did not improve maximum efficiency, which remained around 50–55%. These results confirm that Endo-CC N180H can function as an auxiliary ENGase but is less efficient than Endo-M N175Q under the tested conditions. Nevertheless, the successful transfer demonstrates the generality of the one-pot approach across different ENGase families, suggesting that further engineering of Endo-CC or optimization of reaction parameters could enhance its utility.
