## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of vector-borne disease control, specifically to the use of a novel bacterial isolate, Chromobacterium sp. strain Panamam (designated herein as Csp_P), for the suppression of mosquito populations and the inhibition of pathogen transmission by mosquitoes. More particularly, the invention provides compositions and methods for utilizing Csp_P or its bioactive metabolites to reduce infection of Anopheles and Aedes mosquitoes with Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus, respectively, and to induce mortality in both larval and adult stages of these medically important mosquito vectors. The invention further encompasses the use of Csp_P as a source of novel anti-pathogenic compounds for therapeutic and transmission-blocking drug development.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Mosquitoes are among the most significant vectors of human pathogens globally, transmitting diseases such as malaria, dengue fever, Zika virus infection, chikungunya, and yellow fever. Malaria alone, caused primarily by the protozoan parasite Plasmodium falciparum and transmitted by Anopheles mosquitoes, results in hundreds of millions of clinical cases and over half a million deaths annually, predominantly in sub-Saharan Africa. Dengue fever, caused by any of four dengue virus serotypes and transmitted by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes, affects an estimated 390 million people each year, with severe forms causing significant morbidity and mortality in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. Despite decades of vector control efforts relying on insecticides and environmental management, the global burden of these diseases remains high, exacerbated by the emergence of insecticide resistance in mosquito populations and limited availability of effective vaccines or therapeutics.

In recent years, research has increasingly focused on the role of the mosquito microbiota in modulating vector competence—the ability of a mosquito to acquire, maintain, and transmit a pathogen. Studies have demonstrated that the composition and abundance of microorganisms residing in the mosquito midgut can significantly influence the mosquito's susceptibility to infection with Plasmodium and arboviruses. For instance, certain bacterial species isolated from Anopheles mosquitoes, such as Serratia sp. and Enterobacter sp., have been shown to reduce Plasmodium infection through either direct anti-parasitic activity or indirect immune stimulation of the mosquito host. Similarly, specific bacterial isolates from Aedes aegypti have been associated with reduced dengue virus infection intensity. Moreover, antibiotic-treated mosquitoes, which have a depleted native microbiota, exhibit higher levels of Plasmodium and dengue virus infection compared to their untreated counterparts, underscoring the protective role of the gut microbiome against pathogen invasion.

The mechanisms by which mosquito-associated bacteria exert anti-pathogen effects are diverse and may include the production of antimicrobial compounds, competition for nutrients or attachment sites, modulation of the mosquito immune system, or alteration of midgut physiology. Notably, activation of the immune deficiency (IMD) pathway in Anopheles mosquitoes, a key defense mechanism against Plasmodium, has been shown to be mediated by interactions between pattern recognition receptors such as PGRP-LC and components of the midgut microbiota. Additionally, numerous bacterial species produce secondary metabolites with potent anti-pathogenic properties, including cytotoxic metalloproteases, hemolysins, antibiotics, hemagglutinins, proteases, prodigiosin pigments, and iron-chelating siderophores. These molecules can directly inhibit pathogen development or viability within the mosquito gut.

Beyond their role in pathogen blocking, certain bacteria also exhibit entomopathogenic activity, causing mortality in mosquito larvae or adults. Such bacteria hold promise as biological control agents for mosquito population suppression. For example, Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis (Bti) is widely used as a larvicide due to its production of crystal toxins that are lethal to mosquito larvae but harmless to vertebrates and most other organisms. However, concerns about the potential for resistance development and the need for more sustainable and multifunctional control strategies have spurred interest in identifying novel bacterial candidates with dual capabilities: both pathogen-blocking and mosquitocidal activities.

A critical aspect of bacterial ecology relevant to their application in vector control is their mode of growth. In natural environments, bacteria often exist not as free-floating planktonic cells but as structured communities embedded in a self-produced extracellular polymeric matrix known as biofilms. Biofilm formation confers numerous advantages to bacteria, including enhanced resistance to environmental stresses, antimicrobial agents, and host immune defenses. Importantly, bacteria in biofilms exhibit distinct gene expression profiles and metabolic activities compared to their planktonic counterparts, often resulting in the upregulation of virulence factors and secondary metabolite production. Studies in model organisms such as Drosophila melanogaster have shown that biofilm-forming bacteria like Pseudomonas aeruginosa can cause significantly higher mortality than planktonic cells, highlighting the importance of considering bacterial growth state in host-microbe interactions.

The genus Chromobacterium comprises Gram-negative, facultatively anaerobic bacteria commonly found in soil and water in tropical and subtropical regions. Members of this genus are known for their production of a wide array of bioactive compounds with antimicrobial, antiparasitic, and cytotoxic properties. The best-studied species, Chromobacterium violaceum, produces the violet pigment violacein, which has demonstrated potent activity against bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and tumor cells. Other Chromobacterium species, such as Chromobacterium subtsugae, have been evaluated for their insecticidal properties against agricultural pests. However, the potential of Chromobacterium species as agents for controlling disease-vector mosquitoes and blocking pathogen transmission has remained largely unexplored.

Given the urgent need for innovative approaches to combat mosquito-borne diseases, there exists a significant opportunity to develop novel biocontrol strategies based on bacteria that can simultaneously reduce mosquito populations and interrupt pathogen transmission cycles. The identification of a bacterial isolate capable of colonizing the mosquito midgut, exerting strong entomopathogenic effects, and producing stable metabolites that directly inhibit human pathogens would represent a major advance in the field. Such a bacterium could be deployed through baited sugar traps to target adult mosquitoes or introduced into breeding sites to control larval populations, offering a sustainable and environmentally friendly alternative or complement to conventional chemical insecticides.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel bacterial isolate, Chromobacterium sp. strain Panamam (Csp_P), which has been isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes and demonstrates exceptional utility in the control of mosquito-borne diseases. The invention encompasses compositions comprising Csp_P, as well as methods for using Csp_P to reduce infection of Anopheles gambiae mosquitoes with Plasmodium falciparum and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes with dengue virus, and to induce mortality in both larval and adult stages of these mosquito vectors.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for reducing Plasmodium falciparum infection in Anopheles gambiae mosquitoes, comprising administering to the mosquitoes a composition comprising Csp_P. In another aspect, the invention provides a method for reducing dengue virus infection in Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, comprising administering to the mosquitoes a composition comprising Csp_P. The administration can be performed through various routes, including ingestion via sugar meals or blood meals, or exposure of larvae to Csp_P in aquatic environments.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method for inducing mortality in Anopheles gambiae or Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, comprising exposing the mosquitoes to Csp_P. This method is effective against both larval and adult stages of the mosquitoes, making Csp_P a versatile agent for mosquito population suppression.

The invention also provides a method for inhibiting the development of Plasmodium falciparum in vitro, comprising contacting Plasmodium falciparum with a preparation of Csp_P grown under biofilm conditions. Similarly, the invention provides a method for inhibiting the infectivity of dengue virus in vitro, comprising contacting dengue virus with a preparation of Csp_P grown under biofilm conditions. These in vitro activities demonstrate that Csp_P produces stable secondary metabolites with direct anti-pathogenic effects, independent of the mosquito host.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a composition comprising Csp_P for use in reducing mosquito-borne disease transmission. The composition may be formulated for delivery to adult mosquitoes through baited sugar traps, or for application to mosquito breeding sites to control larval populations. The composition may comprise live Csp_P cells, Csp_P biofilms, or cell-free preparations derived from Csp_P cultures, including supernatants from biofilm cultures or purified metabolites.

The invention further encompasses the use of Csp_P or its bioactive metabolites as lead compounds for the development of therapeutic and transmission-blocking drugs. The stable secondary metabolites produced by Csp_P under biofilm conditions exhibit potent activity against both Plasmodium and dengue virus, suggesting their potential as novel anti-infective agents.

Additionally, the invention provides a method for enhancing the colonization efficiency of Csp_P in mosquito midguts, comprising subjecting Csp_P to consecutive passages through the mosquito intestine. This approach can be used to select for Csp_P variants with improved fitness and persistence in the mosquito gut environment, thereby enhancing their effectiveness as biocontrol agents.

The Csp_P strain of the present invention is characterized by its ability to rapidly colonize the midguts of both Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, its potent entomopathogenic activity against both larval and adult stages, its broad-spectrum antibacterial activity against other mosquito midgut bacteria, and its production of biofilm-associated metabolites that directly inhibit Plasmodium and dengue virus. These combined properties make Csp_P a uniquely promising candidate for the development of integrated vector management strategies aimed at both suppressing mosquito populations and blocking pathogen transmission.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. Definitions

As used herein, the term “Chromobacterium sp. strain Panamam” or “Csp_P” refers to the specific bacterial isolate described herein, which was originally isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes in Panama. Csp_P is a Gram-negative, facultatively anaerobic bacterium that belongs to the genus Chromobacterium. Molecular characterization of its 16S rRNA gene sequence reveals 98% similarity to Chromobacterium haemolyticum and Chromobacterium aquaticum, suggesting these as its closest known relatives. Csp_P is distinguishable from Chromobacterium violaceum by its inability to produce the violet pigment violacein; instead, it forms flat, tan-colored colonies on LB agar that darken with time and become opaque when exposed to light. Csp_P is capable of forming biofilms when grown under static conditions and exhibits a range of biological activities including entomopathogenicity, antibacterial activity, and anti-pathogen activity against Plasmodium and dengue virus.

The term “biofilm” as used herein refers to a structured community of bacterial cells enclosed in a self-produced polymeric matrix that is attached to a surface or interface. Biofilms are characterized by altered phenotypic properties compared to planktonic (free-floating) cells, including increased resistance to antimicrobial agents and host defenses, and differential gene expression leading to the production of specific secondary metabolites. In the context of the present invention, Csp_P biofilms are produced by culturing the bacterium in liquid medium without agitation, typically in polystyrene culture plates at room temperature for 48 hours, unless otherwise specified.

The term “planktonic” refers to the free-floating, single-cell growth state of bacteria in liquid culture, as opposed to the surface-attached, multicellular community state of biofilms. Planktonic Csp_P is typically obtained by culturing the bacterium in liquid LB broth with shaking at 30°C overnight.

The term “entomopathogenic” refers to the ability of a microorganism to cause disease or death in insects. In the present invention, Csp_P exhibits entomopathogenic activity against both larval and adult stages of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, resulting in significant mortality upon exposure or ingestion.

The term “anti-pathogen activity” refers to the ability of Csp_P or its metabolites to inhibit the development, survival, or infectivity of human pathogens, specifically Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus, either within the mosquito host or in vitro. This activity may be direct, through the production of inhibitory compounds, or indirect, through modulation of the mosquito immune system or physiology.

The term “colonization” refers to the establishment and persistence of Csp_P within the mosquito midgut following ingestion. Colonization efficiency is measured by the prevalence (percentage of mosquitoes harboring the bacterium) and bacterial load (number of colony-forming units per midgut) at specified time points post-exposure.

The term “transmission-blocking” refers to interventions that prevent the transmission of a pathogen from an infected human host to a mosquito vector, or from an infected mosquito to a human host. In the context of the present invention, Csp_P acts as a transmission-blocking agent by reducing the ability of mosquitoes to become infected with Plasmodium or dengue virus, thereby interrupting the transmission cycle.

The term “mosquitocidal” refers to the ability to kill mosquitoes, encompassing both larvicidal (killing larvae) and adulticidal (killing adult mosquitoes) activities. Csp_P demonstrates mosquitocidal activity against both life stages of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti.

The term “secondary metabolites” refers to organic compounds produced by bacteria that are not directly involved in growth, development, or reproduction, but often confer ecological advantages such as antimicrobial or antiparasitic activity. The anti-pathogen activities of Csp_P are mediated by stable secondary metabolites produced preferentially under biofilm growth conditions.

### II. Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P)

Chromobacterium sp. strain Panamam (Csp_P) is a novel bacterial isolate with exceptional properties for the control of mosquito-borne diseases. Originally isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes in Panama, Csp_P represents a unique strain within the genus Chromobacterium, distinct from previously characterized species such as Chromobacterium violaceum. While C. violaceum is known for producing the violet pigment violacein, Csp_P does not produce this compound, instead forming tan-colored colonies on LB agar that darken with prolonged incubation. Molecular phylogenetic analysis based on 16S rRNA gene sequencing places Csp_P closest to Chromobacterium haemolyticum and Chromobacterium aquaticum, with 98% sequence similarity, though it possesses distinct biological activities that set it apart from these relatives.

Csp_P is a Gram-negative, rod-shaped bacterium that grows optimally at 30°C in standard laboratory media such as Luria-Bertani (LB) broth and agar. It is capable of both planktonic growth in liquid culture with agitation and biofilm formation under static conditions. The ability to form biofilms is a critical feature of Csp_P, as many of its most potent biological activities, particularly its anti-pathogen effects, are associated with the biofilm growth state. When cultured in polystyrene 24-well plates without shaking at room temperature for 48 hours, Csp_P develops robust biofilms that produce stable secondary metabolites with direct inhibitory activity against Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus.

One of the most remarkable characteristics of Csp_P is its exceptional ability to colonize the midguts of both Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. When antibiotic-treated mosquitoes are exposed to Csp_P through a sugar meal, the bacterium rapidly establishes itself in the midgut, achieving colonization prevalences of 80% in An. gambiae and 97% in Ae. aegypti within three days post-exposure. Average bacterial loads at this time point reach approximately 10^4 CFU per midgut in An. gambiae and 10^5 CFU per midgut in Ae. aegypti, demonstrating efficient proliferation and persistence within the mosquito gut environment. Even in non-antibiotic-treated (septic) mosquitoes, Csp_P achieves high initial colonization rates, with 100% of An. gambiae and 79% of Ae. aegypti mosquitoes harboring the bacterium one day after exposure, though rapid mortality in these populations limits long-term colonization assessment.

Csp_P exhibits potent entomopathogenic activity against both larval and adult stages of the two major mosquito vectors. Larval exposure to Csp_P in breeding water results in nearly complete mortality of An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti larvae within 2-3 days, demonstrating strong larvicidal potential. In adult mosquitoes, ingestion of Csp_P through either sugar or blood meals leads to significantly reduced longevity compared to control mosquitoes. The entomopathogenic effect is dose-dependent and influenced by the route of administration, with blood meal delivery generally causing more rapid mortality than sugar feeding, likely due to the higher bacterial dose ingested in a single blood meal. This mosquitocidal activity makes Csp_P a promising candidate for both population suppression and transmission reduction strategies.

Beyond its direct effects on mosquitoes, Csp_P demonstrates broad-spectrum antibacterial activity against other bacterial species commonly found in mosquito midguts. In co-culture assays on LB agar, Csp_P produces a prominent zone of growth inhibition around its streak, effectively suppressing the growth of diverse isolates from both Ae. aegypti and An. gambiae, including Pseudomonas sp., Proteus sp., Paenobacillus sp., Comamonas sp., Acinetobacter sp., Enterobacter sp., Pantoea sp., Serratia sp., and Chryseobacterium sp. Notably, Csp_P even inhibits the growth of its close relative Chromobacterium violaceum, which is itself known for producing a variety of bioactive compounds. This competitive advantage likely contributes to Csp_P's ability to successfully colonize and persist in the complex microbial ecosystem of the mosquito midgut.

Perhaps most significantly, Csp_P exhibits powerful anti-pathogen activity against both Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus. In vivo, surviving mosquitoes that have been colonized with Csp_P show significantly reduced infection intensities with P. falciparum oocysts and dengue virus compared to control mosquitoes. This transmission-blocking effect is observed whether Csp_P is introduced prior to or concurrently with the pathogen, with greater inhibition achieved when the bacterium is delivered through the infectious blood meal itself. The anti-pathogen activity appears to be mediated, at least in part, by direct effects of Csp_P-produced metabolites, as demonstrated by in vitro assays showing that filtered preparations of Csp_P biofilms can inhibit Plasmodium development and abolish dengue virus infectivity without the presence of mosquito cells or tissues.

The anti-Plasmodium activity of Csp_P is effective against multiple life stages of the parasite, including asexual blood stages, gametocytes, and ookinetes. Filtered supernatants from 36-hour Csp_P biofilms inhibit asexual stage parasites at levels comparable to the antimalarial drug chloroquine, while 48-hour biofilm preparations strongly block ookinete development and gametocyte viability. Similarly, Csp_P biofilm preparations completely abolish dengue virus infectivity in vitro, with the activity being dependent on biofilm maturation (48-hour biofilms showing stronger activity than 24-hour biofilms) and heat-sensitive (activity lost after 24-hour incubation at 90°C). Importantly, the anti-dengue activity is not due to simple sequestration of viral particles by the biofilm matrix, nor is it mediated by pH changes in the culture medium, but rather appears to result from specific bioactive compounds produced by Csp_P during biofilm formation.

Csp_P also possesses the ability to elicit the mosquito innate immune system, though this appears to be a secondary mechanism compared to its direct anti-pathogen effects. Exposure of mosquito cell lines or whole mosquitoes to Csp_P leads to upregulation of immune-related genes, including antimicrobial peptides such as cecropins and defensins in Ae. aegypti, and trends toward increased expression of Rel2, FBN9, and cecropin genes in An. gambiae. This immune stimulation may contribute to the overall reduction in pathogen infection observed in Csp_P-colonized mosquitoes, though the potent in vitro anti-pathogen activity suggests that direct inhibition by bacterial metabolites is the primary mechanism.

The combination of these properties—efficient midgut colonization, potent entomopathogenicity, broad-spectrum antibacterial activity, and direct anti-pathogen effects against both Plasmodium and dengue virus—makes Csp_P a uniquely valuable tool for the development of integrated vector management strategies. Its ability to simultaneously suppress mosquito populations and block pathogen transmission addresses two critical components of vector-borne disease control, potentially offering greater impact and sustainability than approaches targeting only one of these aspects.

### III. Administration of Compositions Comprising Csp_P to Insects

The administration of compositions comprising Csp_P to mosquitoes can be accomplished through various routes and formulations, tailored to the specific life stage and ecological context of the target mosquito population. For adult mosquitoes, the most practical and effective delivery method is through attractive toxic sugar baits (ATSBs) or similar baited sugar traps. These devices exploit the natural sugar-feeding behavior of both male and female mosquitoes, which regularly consume plant nectar and other sugar sources for energy. By incorporating Csp_P into a sugar solution within an attractively designed trap, large numbers of mosquitoes can be exposed to the bacterium in a targeted manner, minimizing non-target effects and environmental impact.

In practice, sugar bait formulations for Csp_P delivery typically consist of a 1.5% sucrose solution containing Csp_P at concentrations ranging from 10^6 to 10^10 CFU/ml, depending on the target mosquito species and desired effect. For Anopheles gambiae, effective concentrations are typically around 10^8 CFU/ml, while for Aedes aegypti, concentrations of 10^6 CFU/ml are sufficient for colonization and pathogen blocking, though higher concentrations (10^10 CFU/ml) may be used when rapid adulticidal effects are desired. The sugar solution is presented on cotton strips or other absorbent materials within traps that may be enhanced with attractants such as fruit odors or floral scents to increase mosquito visitation rates. Mosquitoes feeding on these baits ingest Csp_P, which then colonizes their midguts and exerts its biological effects.

Alternatively, Csp_P can be administered to adult mosquitoes through blood meals, either naturally or in controlled settings. In laboratory studies, this is achieved by mixing Csp_P cultures with blood and serum and presenting the mixture through membrane feeders. While less practical for field applications, this route demonstrates that Csp_P can be effectively delivered during the blood-feeding process that is essential for female mosquito reproduction and pathogen transmission. In natural settings, this might be relevant if Csp_P were to be introduced into human or animal hosts, though this approach is not currently envisioned for field deployment.

For larval control, Csp_P can be administered directly to mosquito breeding sites such as artificial containers, puddles, or other stagnant water bodies where Ae. aegypti and An. gambiae larvae develop. Formulations for larval application typically consist of liquid cultures of Csp_P diluted to appropriate concentrations in water. In experimental settings, effective larvicidal activity is achieved by adding 50 µl of an overnight Csp_P culture (OD600 = 1.0, approximately 10^8 CFU/ml) to 5 ml of breeding water containing 2-4 day old larvae. This results in nearly complete mortality of both An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti larvae within 2-3 days. Field-applicable formulations might include dried Csp_P preparations or slow-release formulations that maintain effective concentrations over time in various aquatic environments.

The choice of Csp_P growth state for administration is critical to achieving optimal biological effects. For colonization and long-term pathogen blocking, live Csp_P cells in either planktonic or early biofilm states may be preferred, as they can establish persistent infections in the mosquito midgut. For immediate mosquitocidal or anti-pathogen effects, mature biofilm preparations (48-hour biofilms) may be more effective due to their higher production of secondary metabolites. Heat-inactivated biofilms or cell-free supernatants from biofilm cultures could also be used when the goal is to deliver the anti-pathogen metabolites without establishing live bacterial infections, potentially reducing regulatory concerns or ecological risks.

Formulations may also be optimized to enhance the stability and persistence of Csp_P in the environment. For sugar baits, the addition of preservatives or stabilizers may extend the shelf life of the bacterial preparation. For larval applications, encapsulation of Csp_P in biodegradable matrices or association with inert carriers could protect the bacteria from environmental stressors and prolong their activity in breeding sites. Additionally, Csp_P could be combined with other biocontrol agents or synergistic compounds to enhance overall efficacy or broaden the spectrum of activity.

The administration of Csp_P compositions can be integrated into existing vector control programs or deployed as standalone interventions. In areas with high malaria or dengue transmission, networks of Csp_P-baited sugar traps could be strategically placed around human dwellings to target host-seeking mosquitoes, while larval applications could be focused on identified breeding sites. The dual functionality of Csp_P—as both a mosquitocide and a transmission-blocking agent—offers the potential for significant reductions in both mosquito populations and human disease incidence, providing a powerful new tool in the fight against mosquito-borne diseases.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Chromobacterium Csp_P Reduces Malaria and Dengue Infection in Vector Mosquitoes

The ability of Chromobacterium sp. strain Panamam (Csp_P) to reduce infection of Anopheles gambiae mosquitoes with Plasmodium falciparum and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes with dengue virus was demonstrated through a series of controlled laboratory experiments. For the malaria transmission-blocking assay, female An. gambiae mosquitoes (Keele strain) were first rendered aseptic through antibiotic treatment (20 U penicillin and 20 µg streptomycin in 10% sucrose solution) from day 1 post-eclosion until 1-2 days prior to bacterial challenge. The effectiveness of antibiotic treatment was confirmed by colony-forming unit (CFU) assays showing absence of cultivable bacteria in mosquito midguts. These aseptic mosquitoes were then exposed to Csp_P through a sugar meal consisting of 1.5% sucrose solution containing 10^8 CFU/ml of Csp_P for 24 hours. Control mosquitoes received an identical sugar meal without Csp_P. Two days after Csp_P exposure, both Csp_P-exposed and control mosquitoes were allowed to feed on a P. falciparum NF54 gametocyte culture through membrane feeders for 30 minutes. The infectious blood meal contained either 10^6 CFU/ml of Csp_P (for the concurrent exposure group) or no additional bacteria (for the pre-exposure group). Unfed mosquitoes were removed immediately after the blood meal, and fed mosquitoes were maintained on 10% sucrose solution under standard insectary conditions (27°C, 95% humidity, 12-hour light/dark cycle).

Seven to eight days after the infectious blood meal, surviving mosquitoes were dissected, and their midguts were stained with 0.1% mercurochrome to visualize P. falciparum oocysts. Oocyst counts were performed under a light-contrast microscope, and the median number of oocysts per midgut was calculated for each experimental group. Results showed that mosquitoes pre-exposed to Csp_P through sugar feeding exhibited significantly reduced P. falciparum infection intensities compared to control mosquitoes (p<0.001, Mann-Whitney U test). The inhibition was even more pronounced in mosquitoes that received Csp_P concurrently with the gametocytes through the infectious blood meal, with many individuals showing complete absence of oocysts. Although Csp_P exposure caused significant mortality in adult mosquitoes (as detailed in Example 2), the surviving individuals consistently demonstrated enhanced resistance to Plasmodium infection, indicating a robust transmission-blocking effect.

For the dengue virus transmission-blocking assay, female Ae. aegypti mosquitoes (Rockefeller strain) were similarly rendered aseptic through antibiotic treatment and then exposed to Csp_P through a sugar meal containing 10^6 CFU/ml of Csp_P for 24 hours. Control mosquitoes received sugar meal without Csp_P. Two days after bacterial exposure, both groups were starved overnight and then allowed to feed on a blood meal containing dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV-2, New Guinea C strain) mixed with commercial human blood and 10% human serum. The infectious blood meal contained either 10^6 CFU/ml of Csp_P or no additional bacteria. Fed mosquitoes were maintained under standard conditions, and seven days post-infection, surviving individuals were processed for virus titration. Midguts were dissected under sterile conditions, homogenized in minimum essential medium (MEM), and serially diluted for plaque assays on C6/36 mosquito cells. Plaque formation was detected by peroxidase immunostaining using DENV-2-specific antibodies.

Results demonstrated that Csp_P-exposed Ae. aegypti mosquitoes had significantly lower dengue virus titers in their midguts compared to control mosquitoes (p<0.001, Mann-Whitney U test). Many Csp_P-exposed mosquitoes showed undetectable levels of virus, indicating complete blocking of infection in some individuals. As with the malaria assay, the concurrent delivery of Csp_P with the virus through the blood meal resulted in stronger inhibition than pre-exposure through sugar feeding alone. The consistency of these results across multiple independent replicates (n>3) confirms the reliability of Csp_P as a transmission-blocking agent against both malaria and dengue.

To investigate the mechanisms underlying this transmission-blocking activity, additional experiments were conducted to assess whether Csp_P's effects were mediated through direct anti-pathogen activity or indirect immune stimulation. In vitro assays showed that filtered preparations of Csp_P biofilms could directly inhibit P. falciparum development and abolish dengue virus infectivity without mosquito cells present, suggesting that direct anti-pathogen metabolites play a major role. However, gene expression analysis of mosquito midguts revealed that Csp_P colonization also induced upregulation of immune-related genes, including cecropins and defensins in Ae. aegypti, which may contribute to the overall reduction in pathogen infection. The combination of these direct and indirect mechanisms likely accounts for the potent transmission-blocking activity observed in vivo.

### CONCLUSION

The data presented in Example 1 conclusively demonstrate that Chromobacterium sp. strain Panamam (Csp_P) effectively reduces infection of Anopheles gambiae mosquitoes with Plasmodium falciparum and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes with dengue virus. This transmission-blocking activity is observed whether Csp_P is introduced prior to or concurrently with the pathogen, with greater inhibition achieved through concurrent delivery. The mechanism appears to involve both direct anti-pathogen effects mediated by stable secondary metabolites produced by Csp_P, particularly under biofilm growth conditions, and indirect effects through stimulation of the mosquito innate immune system. These findings establish Csp_P as a powerful tool for interrupting the transmission cycles of two of the world's most significant mosquito-borne diseases, malaria and dengue fever.

### Example 2: Live Chromobacterium Csp_P is a General Mosquitocidal, and Malaria and Dengue Transmission-Blocking Agent

The entomopathogenic and transmission-blocking properties of Chromobacterium sp. strain Panamam (Csp_P) were comprehensively evaluated through a series of experiments assessing its effects on mosquito survival, larval development, and pathogen transmission. For adult mosquito mortality assays, both antibiotic-treated (aseptic) and non-antibiotic-treated (septic) Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes were exposed to Csp_P through sugar meals or blood meals. Aseptic mosquitoes were provided with 1.5% sucrose solution containing 10^8 CFU/ml Csp_P (An. gambiae) or 10^6 CFU/ml Csp_P (Ae. aegypti) for 24 hours, while control groups received identical sugar solutions without bacteria. Survival was monitored daily, with dead mosquitoes removed and recorded. Results showed that Csp_P exposure significantly reduced the longevity of both mosquito species compared to controls (p<0.001, Log-Rank test). In septic mosquitoes, which retained their native microbiota, Csp_P exposure through sugar meals at higher concentrations (10^8 CFU/ml for An. gambiae, 10^10 CFU/ml for Ae. aegypti) caused even more rapid mortality, with nearly all An. gambiae dying within two days and approximately 70% of Ae. aegypti dying within three days. When Csp_P was introduced through blood meals containing 10^8 CFU/ml, mortality was accelerated in both species, likely due to the higher bacterial dose ingested in a single feeding event.

Larval toxicity assays were conducted by placing 2-4 day old An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti larvae in groups of 10 in 5 ml of distilled water supplemented with larval food and 50 µl of Csp_P culture (OD600=1.0, approximately 10^8 CFU/ml). Control groups received identical treatments with 1× PBS instead of bacterial culture. Mortality was monitored daily for five days. Csp_P exposure resulted in nearly complete mortality of An. gambiae larvae within three days and Ae. aegypti larvae within two days, compared to minimal mortality in control groups exposed only to normal breeding water microbiota (p<0.001, Log-Rank test). This demonstrates potent larvicidal activity that could be exploited for mosquito population suppression in breeding sites.

The transmission-blocking activity of Csp_P was further confirmed through in vitro assays demonstrating direct anti-pathogen effects. For Plasmodium inhibition, filtered preparations of Csp_P biofilms (36-48 hours old) were shown to inhibit asexual blood stages, gametocytes, and ookinetes of P. falciparum and P. berghei at levels comparable to or exceeding standard antimalarial drugs. For dengue virus, filtered preparations of 48-hour Csp_P biofilms completely abolished virus infectivity in BHK21-15 cells, with the activity being dependent on biofilm maturation and heat-sensitive. Control experiments ruled out alternative mechanisms such as viral particle sequestration or pH-mediated inactivation, confirming that specific bioactive metabolites produced by Csp_P during biofilm formation are responsible for the anti-pathogen effects.

Additional experiments demonstrated that Csp_P produces broad-spectrum antibacterial activity against other mosquito midgut bacteria, creating a prominent zone of growth inhibition around its colonies on LB agar plates. This competitive advantage likely facilitates its successful colonization of the mosquito midgut and may contribute to its overall effectiveness as a biocontrol agent by displacing other microbial species that might otherwise interfere with its activity or support pathogen development.

Collectively, these results establish Csp_P as a general mosquitocidal agent with activity against both larval and adult stages of the two most important mosquito disease vectors, as well as a potent transmission-blocking agent against both malaria and dengue. The combination of these properties in a single bacterial strain represents a significant advancement in the field of vector-borne disease control, offering the potential for integrated interventions that simultaneously suppress mosquito populations and interrupt pathogen transmission cycles.
