# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of oncology and molecular imaging, specifically relating to methods and compositions for enhancing the efficacy of phosphatidylserine (PS)-targeted cancer diagnostics and therapeutics through modulation of cell surface PS exposure using non-contact electric fields. More particularly, the invention provides a novel platform that combines low-amplitude, direct current (DC) electric fields with PS-targeting agents—such as SapC-DOPS nanovesicles, PS-specific monoclonal antibodies (e.g., bavituximab, PGN635), PS-binding peptides, or radiolabeled or optically labeled PS ligands—to improve tumor detection, monitoring, and treatment across a broad spectrum of malignancies. The invention further encompasses diagnostic imaging modalities—including optical, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), positron emission tomography (PET), and single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT)—that are augmented by pre- or co-application of controlled electric fields to increase PS externalization on tumor cells and tumor-associated vasculature, thereby amplifying signal-to-noise ratios and therapeutic selectivity. This approach leverages the biophysical response of cancer cells to sub-lethal electric fields, which modulate intracellular calcium dynamics and inhibit flippase activity, resulting in reversible translocation of endogenous PS from the inner to the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane without inducing significant cytotoxicity in normal tissues. The technical domain thus intersects biomedical engineering, cancer biology, molecular imaging, nanomedicine, and bioelectrics, offering a translatable strategy to overcome limitations in current PS-targeted platforms, including variable baseline PS expression, poor tumor penetration, and insufficient contrast in deep-tissue imaging.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer remains a leading cause of global morbidity and mortality, with conventional treatment modalities—surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy—often limited by systemic toxicity, tumor resistance, and incomplete eradication of metastatic disease. In recent years, targeted therapies and immunotherapies have emerged as promising alternatives, yet their success is frequently constrained by the heterogeneity of tumor biomarkers and the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. Among potential universal targets, phosphatidylserine (PS) has garnered significant attention due to its aberrant externalization on the surface of viable cancer cells, tumor endothelial cells, and stromal components—a phenomenon not typically observed in healthy tissues, where PS is strictly confined to the cytosolic leaflet of the plasma membrane by ATP-dependent flippases. This loss of membrane asymmetry in malignancies renders PS a nearly ubiquitous tumor-associated marker, exploitable for both diagnostic imaging and therapeutic intervention.

Despite this promise, clinical translation of PS-targeted strategies has faced several challenges. First, the degree of PS exposure varies widely among cancer types and even within individual tumors, leading to inconsistent binding and uptake of PS-directed agents such as annexin V, bavituximab, or SapC-DOPS nanovesicles. For instance, annexin V, though highly specific for PS, suffers from an extremely short plasma half-life (3–7 minutes), severely limiting its utility in clinical imaging. Engineered antibodies like PGN635 offer improved pharmacokinetics but still depend on sufficient baseline PS levels for effective tumor accumulation. Second, many PS-targeting therapeutics exhibit optimal efficacy only in tumors with high surface PS, while cancers with low PS expression remain refractory. Third, imaging modalities such as optical fluorescence are hampered by tissue autofluorescence and poor penetration depth, whereas PET and SPECT require high-affinity, stable radiotracers with minimal off-target retention. MRI-based approaches using iron oxide or gadolinium-loaded PS-targeted carriers often lack sufficient sensitivity due to the inherently low concentration of surface PS relative to total tissue mass.

Concurrently, electric field-based therapies have gained regulatory approval and clinical adoption, notably Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields) for glioblastoma. However, TTFields operate at intermediate frequencies (100–300 kHz) and primarily disrupt mitosis through dielectrophoretic forces on dividing cells, rather than modulating membrane lipid dynamics. Electroporation techniques, including irreversible electroporation (IRE) and electrochemotherapy, rely on high-voltage pulses to create permanent or transient pores in cell membranes, facilitating drug uptake but often causing significant local tissue damage and inflammation. These approaches are generally unsuitable for systemic or repeated use and do not selectively enhance PS exposure without collateral cytotoxicity.

Critically, prior art has not recognized that sub-threshold, non-contact DC electric fields—applied at low intensities (e.g., 75–150 V/cm) without direct electrode-cell contact—can reversibly and selectively increase PS externalization on cancer cells by modulating transmembrane potential and calcium influx, thereby inhibiting flippase activity without inducing apoptosis or necrosis in normal cells. This biophysical mechanism offers a previously untapped opportunity to “prime” tumors for enhanced binding of PS-targeted diagnostics and therapeutics. While it is known that chemotherapeutic agents like temozolomide or radiation can indirectly increase PS exposure via apoptosis induction, such methods are inherently cytotoxic and non-selective, damaging healthy proliferating tissues and triggering inflammatory cascades that may counteract therapeutic goals. In contrast, the electric field modality described herein operates independently of genotoxic stress, enabling safe, repeatable, and tunable modulation of PS presentation.

Moreover, no integrated system exists in the prior art that combines controlled electric field application with multimodal PS-targeted imaging (optical, PET, SPECT, MRI) or therapy (nanovesicles, antibody-drug conjugates, immune modulators) in a coordinated regimen designed to maximize tumor specificity and minimize off-target effects. Existing patents covering SapC-DOPS focus on composition and basic targeting but do not address methods to dynamically enhance PS availability. Similarly, patents on bavituximab emphasize immune activation mechanisms but overlook strategies to boost its tumor binding through physical modulation of the target antigen’s spatial distribution. The absence of a method to controllably and reversibly upregulate surface PS—without killing cells or requiring toxic adjuvants—represents a significant gap in the art, limiting the full potential of PS as a universal cancer biomarker.

Thus, there remains a compelling and unmet need for a safe, non-invasive, and broadly applicable method to enhance the exposure of phosphatidylserine on tumor cells and associated vasculature, thereby improving the sensitivity, specificity, and efficacy of PS-targeted diagnostic and therapeutic interventions across diverse cancer types. The present invention addresses this need by introducing a novel paradigm: the use of precisely calibrated, non-contact DC electric fields to transiently increase surface PS levels, creating a window of heightened susceptibility to PS-directed agents, followed by administration of imaging probes or therapeutics that capitalize on this amplified target density. This approach is compatible with existing clinical infrastructure, avoids systemic toxicity, and can be tailored to individual tumor characteristics, representing a significant advancement over current state-of-the-art methodologies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive platform for enhancing the efficacy of phosphatidylserine (PS)-targeted cancer diagnostics and therapeutics through the controlled application of non-contact direct current (DC) electric fields to modulate surface PS exposure on tumor cells and tumor-associated endothelial cells. In one aspect, the invention discloses a method for increasing the externalization of phosphatidylserine on the surface of cancer cells in a subject, comprising applying a non-contact DC electric field of 75 to 150 V/cm to a tumor site for a duration sufficient to elevate surface PS levels without inducing significant cytotoxicity in surrounding normal tissues. The electric field is generated using a parallel-plate capacitor configuration or equivalent non-invasive electrode array that maintains electrical isolation between the conductive elements and the biological tissue, thereby avoiding electrolysis, thermal damage, or direct current injection.

In another aspect, the invention provides a combined diagnostic method comprising: (a) applying a non-contact DC electric field to a suspected or confirmed tumor site in a subject; and (b) administering a PS-targeting imaging agent selected from the group consisting of fluorescently labeled SapC-DOPS nanovesicles, radiolabeled anti-PS antibodies (e.g., ⁸⁹Zr-PGN635, ⁷⁴As-bavituximab), annexin V derivatives, or PS-binding peptide constructs; wherein the electric field application precedes or coincides with imaging agent administration to enhance tumor-specific signal intensity in optical, PET, SPECT, or MRI modalities. The method enables earlier detection, more accurate staging, and real-time monitoring of treatment response with improved contrast-to-noise ratios.

In a further aspect, the invention encompasses a therapeutic regimen comprising: (a) applying a non-contact DC electric field to a tumor site; and (b) administering a PS-targeting therapeutic agent, such as SapC-DOPS nanovesicles, bavituximab, PPS1D1 peptoid hybrids, BPRDP056 conjugates, or mch1N11 antibodies, alone or in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., anti-PD-1, anti-CTLA-4), chemotherapeutics, or radiotherapy. The electric field pretreatment sensitizes PS-low tumors to these agents by increasing target density, thereby lowering the effective therapeutic dose and reducing systemic toxicity.

The invention also includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising PS-targeting agents formulated for co-administration with electric field devices, as well as kits containing instructions for synchronized field application and agent delivery. Additionally, the invention covers medical devices configured to deliver calibrated DC electric fields to superficial or deep-seated tumors via transcutaneous or implantable electrode arrays, integrated with imaging or drug delivery systems for closed-loop control.

A key advantage of the invention is its universality: because PS externalization is a hallmark of most solid tumors and hematologic malignancies, the platform is applicable across diverse cancer types—including glioblastoma, pancreatic adenocarcinoma, lung carcinoma, melanoma, breast cancer, and neuroblastoma—without requiring prior knowledge of tumor-specific protein markers. Furthermore, the electric field parameters are tunable to achieve desired levels of PS modulation without compromising cell viability, enabling repeated applications during a treatment course. Preclinical data demonstrate that DC electric field exposure increases surface PS by 2- to 5-fold in U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells, with corresponding enhancements in SapC-DOPS binding and cytotoxicity, while sparing normal astrocytes and fibroblasts.

In summary, the invention establishes a new standard of care by decoupling target availability from inherent tumor biology and instead actively engineering the tumor surface to maximize engagement with PS-directed agents. This paradigm shift—from passive targeting to active target amplification—significantly improves the therapeutic index of existing PS-based platforms and unlocks new possibilities for precision oncology.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Phosphatidylserine

Phosphatidylserine (PS) is an anionic phospholipid constituting approximately 3–10% of total cellular membrane lipids in eukaryotic cells. Under physiological conditions, PS is asymmetrically distributed, localized almost exclusively to the inner (cytosolic) leaflet of the plasma membrane through the action of ATP-dependent aminophospholipid translocases, commonly referred to as flippases. This asymmetric organization is critical for maintaining membrane integrity, regulating signal transduction pathways, and preventing unintended immune recognition. The externalization of PS to the outer leaflet is a tightly regulated process that occurs predominantly during programmed cell death (apoptosis), where it serves as an “eat-me” signal for phagocytic clearance by macrophages and dendritic cells via PS receptors such as TIM-4, BAI1, and Stabilin-2. However, in pathological states such as cancer, this asymmetry is disrupted even in viable, non-apoptotic cells due to dysregulation of flippase activity, oxidative stress, and altered lipid metabolism. Consequently, many tumor cells and tumor-associated endothelial cells exhibit constitutive PS exposure on their surface, a feature that is rare in healthy somatic tissues. This aberrant PS presentation is not merely a bystander effect but actively contributes to tumor progression by promoting immunosuppression—through the induction of anti-inflammatory M2-polarized tumor-associated macrophages and suppression of dendritic cell maturation—and facilitating angiogenesis, metastasis, and resistance to therapy. The near-universal presence of surface PS across diverse malignancies, coupled with its functional role in tumor biology, establishes PS as a compelling and broadly applicable target for both diagnostic imaging and therapeutic intervention. Unlike protein-based biomarkers that are often restricted to specific cancer subtypes (e.g., HER2 in breast cancer, EGFR in glioblastoma), PS represents a pan-cancer signature that can be exploited irrespective of genetic or histological classification, thereby enabling the development of platform technologies with wide clinical applicability.

### PS-Targeting Drugs

A variety of PS-targeting agents have been developed to exploit the externalized PS on tumor cells for therapeutic purposes. Among the most advanced are monoclonal antibodies such as bavituximab, a chimeric IgG1 antibody that binds to a complex of PS and β2-glycoprotein I (β2GP1), thereby blocking the immunosuppressive signaling of PS and engaging Fcγ receptors on immune effector cells to stimulate antitumor immunity. Bavituximab has demonstrated safety and preliminary efficacy in phase I/II trials for non-small cell lung cancer and HER2-negative breast cancer, although a phase III trial in lung cancer failed to meet its primary endpoint, possibly due to patient selection or insufficient target engagement in PS-low tumors. Another antibody, PGN635 (now known as 3G4), is a fully human monoclonal antibody with similar PS/β2GP1 specificity and has been explored in both imaging and therapeutic contexts. Beyond antibodies, protein-based agents include annexin V, a naturally occurring PS-binding protein with high affinity but poor pharmacokinetics, limiting its clinical utility. To overcome this, engineered fragments and fusion proteins have been developed, though none have achieved widespread adoption. Peptide-based PS binders, such as the dimeric peptoid hybrid PPS1D1, exhibit selective cytotoxicity toward cancer cells by disrupting membrane integrity upon PS binding, with minimal effects on normal cells. Small-molecule conjugates like BPRDP056, which fuses a zinc(II)-dipicolylamine PS-binding motif to the topoisomerase I inhibitor SN38, enable targeted drug delivery directly to PS-expressing cells, enhancing potency while reducing systemic exposure. Perhaps the most versatile platform is SapC-DOPS, a nanovesicle composed of saposin C (SapC)—a lysosomal protein with pH-dependent PS affinity—and dioleoylphosphatidylserine (DOPS). At the slightly acidic extracellular pH typical of tumors (~6.0), SapC undergoes a conformational change that enhances its binding to surface PS, enabling selective tumor homing. SapC-DOPS has demonstrated efficacy in preclinical models against glioblastoma, pancreatic cancer, lung cancer, and pediatric tumors, inducing both apoptotic and lysosomal cell death. Critically, the therapeutic efficacy of all these agents is directly proportional to the density of surface PS on target cells, creating a dependency on inherent tumor biology that limits their applicability to PS-high malignancies and reduces effectiveness in heterogeneous or PS-low tumors. The present invention overcomes this limitation by providing a method to actively increase surface PS levels prior to or during administration of these agents, thereby expanding their therapeutic window and improving clinical outcomes.

### Altering the Surface PS to Increase the Efficacy of PS Targeting Modalities

The efficacy of PS-targeting diagnostics and therapeutics is fundamentally constrained by the variable and often suboptimal levels of surface PS expression across different tumors and even within individual lesions. While some cancers exhibit robust PS externalization, others display only minimal exposure, rendering them resistant to PS-directed agents. Traditional approaches to enhance PS exposure—such as chemotherapy or radiotherapy—induce apoptosis as a side effect, thereby increasing PS as a secondary consequence. However, these methods are inherently cytotoxic, damage healthy tissues, and may trigger compensatory survival pathways that undermine therapeutic goals. The present invention introduces a novel, non-cytotoxic strategy to reversibly and selectively increase surface PS on tumor cells through the application of low-intensity, non-contact DC electric fields. This approach exploits the biophysical sensitivity of cancer cells to electric fields, which differ from normal cells in membrane composition, resting potential, and ion channel expression. When a DC electric field of 75–150 V/cm is applied across a tumor site using a parallel-plate capacitor device with insulated electrodes, it induces a transmembrane potential shift that activates voltage-gated calcium channels, leading to a transient influx of Ca²⁺ ions. Elevated intracellular calcium inhibits flippase activity—the primary mechanism maintaining PS asymmetry—thereby allowing passive diffusion of PS to the outer membrane leaflet. Importantly, this process does not require cell death and is fully reversible upon field removal, preserving tumor architecture while enhancing target availability. Preclinical studies in U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells demonstrate a 2- to 5-fold increase in surface PS within 30–60 minutes of field application, with no significant change in total cellular PS content or viability in normal astrocytes under identical conditions. This selective modulation creates a temporal window during which PS-targeting agents exhibit significantly enhanced binding, internalization, and cytotoxicity. For example, SapC-DOPS nanovesicles show a 3-fold increase in tumor cell uptake following electric field pretreatment, translating to a corresponding reduction in IC₅₀ values. Similarly, radiolabeled anti-PS antibodies achieve higher tumor-to-background ratios in PET imaging when administered post-field application. By decoupling PS exposure from apoptotic stimuli, this method enables safe, repeatable, and tunable enhancement of PS-targeted platforms, overcoming a major bottleneck in their clinical translation.

### Phosphatidylserine as a Tumor Imaging Target

Phosphatidylserine serves as a highly valuable target for non-invasive tumor imaging due to its selective externalization on malignant cells and associated vasculature, coupled with its absence on healthy tissues. A range of PS-targeting imaging agents have been developed, each adapted to specific modalities. Annexin V, labeled with technetium-99m or fluorophores, has been used in SPECT and optical imaging to detect apoptosis in response to therapy, but its rapid clearance limits utility in baseline tumor detection. Engineered antibodies like PGN635, when conjugated to near-infrared dyes (e.g., IRDye 800CW) or radionuclides (e.g., ⁸⁹Zr), provide longer circulation times and enable high-contrast imaging of PS-positive tumors in preclinical models, with peak tumor uptake observed at 24 hours post-injection. Bavituximab labeled with arsenic-74 has similarly demonstrated excellent tumor visualization in prostate cancer models via PET, achieving tumor-to-liver ratios of 22:1. Nanovesicle-based systems such as SapC-DOPS offer additional versatility: when loaded with iron oxide nanoparticles, they serve as T₂/T₂*-weighted MRI contrast agents, producing significant signal loss in tumors due to localized iron accumulation; when encapsulating gadolinium chelates, they enhance T₁-weighted MRI with tumor-specific R₁ increases of up to 9% at 10 hours post-injection; and when tagged with far-red fluorophores like CellVue Maroon, they enable real-time optical imaging of orthotopic brain and pancreatic tumors in mice. Dual-modality agents, such as iodine-125-labeled phenol-substituted dyes incorporated into SapC-DOPS, permit simultaneous optical and SPECT/PET imaging, facilitating cross-validation and quantitative biodistribution analysis. Despite these advances, the sensitivity of all PS-targeted imaging approaches is inherently limited by baseline PS density. Tumors with low PS expression yield weak signals that may be indistinguishable from background noise, particularly in deep tissues or early-stage lesions. The present invention addresses this limitation by integrating electric field-mediated PS upregulation into imaging protocols. By applying a non-contact DC field prior to probe administration, surface PS levels are transiently amplified, leading to increased probe binding and significantly improved image contrast. This strategy enables earlier detection of small or PS-low tumors, more accurate delineation of tumor margins, and enhanced monitoring of therapeutic response, thereby expanding the clinical utility of PS-targeted imaging across a broader patient population.

### Electric Field

The application of electric fields in oncology has historically focused on high-intensity modalities such as Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields) and electroporation, which exert direct cytotoxic effects through mitotic disruption or membrane poration. In contrast, the present invention utilizes low-amplitude, direct current (DC) electric fields applied in a non-contact configuration to modulate cellular physiology without inducing cell death. The electric field is generated using a pair of insulated parallel plates or conformal electrode arrays positioned externally or percutaneously around the tumor site, ensuring no direct electrical contact with tissue or bodily fluids. This design prevents electrolysis, gas formation, and thermal injury, which are common complications of direct-current applications. Field strengths are carefully calibrated between 75 and 150 V/cm—sufficient to induce measurable changes in transmembrane potential and calcium influx in cancer cells, yet below the threshold for irreversible membrane damage or significant heating. The duration of application ranges from 15 minutes to 2 hours, depending on tumor type and desired level of PS modulation. Unlike alternating fields used in TTFields, DC fields produce a unidirectional force that polarizes the cell membrane, leading to sustained activation of voltage-sensitive ion channels. Cancer cells, which often exhibit depolarized resting potentials and dysregulated calcium homeostasis, are particularly susceptible to this perturbation, resulting in selective PS externalization without affecting quiescent normal cells. The device may be integrated with real-time impedance monitoring or imaging feedback to optimize field delivery and ensure uniform coverage of the target volume. This approach represents a paradigm shift from destructive electroporation to modulatory bioelectrics, enabling safe, outpatient-compatible interventions that prime tumors for enhanced engagement with molecularly targeted agents.

### Optical Imaging

Optical imaging leverages the high sensitivity and real-time capabilities of fluorescence detection to visualize PS-expressing tumors in preclinical models and, potentially, in intraoperative settings. PS-targeting agents such as SapC-DOPS labeled with far-red fluorophores (e.g., CellVue Maroon, IRDye 800CW) accumulate selectively in tumors due to pH-dependent binding to surface PS, producing strong fluorescence signals that can be captured using rotational or planar imaging systems. Studies in orthotopic glioblastoma and pancreatic cancer models have demonstrated clear tumor delineation as early as 1 hour post-injection, with signal intensity correlating with tumor burden and PS density. However, clinical translation is hindered by limited tissue penetration of visible and near-infrared light, scattering, and autofluorescence from endogenous chromophores. The present invention mitigates these limitations by enhancing the target-to-background ratio through electric field-mediated PS upregulation. By increasing surface PS density prior to probe administration, the absolute number of bound fluorophores rises, amplifying the emitted signal and improving detectability even in deeper tissues. Furthermore, the use of far-red/near-infrared dyes minimizes interference from hemoglobin and water absorption, while advanced imaging algorithms can correct for angle-dependent signal variation. In surgical applications, this approach could guide tumor resection by highlighting PS-positive margins that might otherwise be missed. The integration of electric field priming thus transforms optical imaging from a qualitative tool into a more quantitative and sensitive modality for tumor detection and monitoring.

### PET/SPECT Imaging

Positron emission tomography (PET) and single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) offer high sensitivity and quantitative capabilities for whole-body imaging of PS-expressing tumors using radiolabeled targeting agents. Antibodies such as ⁸⁹Zr-PGN635 and ⁷⁴As-bavituximab have demonstrated excellent tumor uptake in xenograft models, with tumor-to-blood ratios exceeding 10:1 and specific accumulation in apoptotic regions. Nanovesicle-based tracers like ¹²⁵I-labeled SapC-DOPS provide an alternative with faster clearance and reduced liver uptake, achieving 4- to 8-fold higher tumor-to-brain ratios in glioblastoma models. However, the absolute signal intensity remains dependent on baseline PS levels, which can be low in certain tumors or early-stage disease. The present invention enhances PET/SPECT imaging by coupling radiotracer administration with prior electric field application. The resulting increase in surface PS leads to greater tracer binding, higher standardized uptake values (SUVs), and improved lesion detectability. Moreover, because the electric field effect is transient and reversible, imaging can be timed to coincide with peak PS exposure, maximizing contrast while minimizing background retention. This strategy is particularly valuable for monitoring early treatment response, as changes in PS density may precede morphological alterations detectable by CT or MRI. The compatibility of this approach with existing clinical PET/SPECT infrastructure ensures rapid translatability, offering a powerful tool for personalized oncology.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, the effect of non-contact DC electric fields on surface phosphatidylserine (PS) exposure in human glioblastoma cells was evaluated. U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells were cultured in standard conditions and seeded into six-well plates. A custom-built parallel-plate capacitor device, consisting of two insulated copper electrodes separated by 2 cm, was positioned above and below the culture plate without contacting the medium. Cells were exposed to DC electric fields of 75, 100, 125, or 150 V/cm for 60 minutes at 37°C in a humidified CO₂ incubator. Control cells were placed in an identical setup without field activation. Following treatment, cells were harvested and stained with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated annexin V in the presence of propidium iodide to assess surface PS and membrane integrity, respectively. Flow cytometry analysis revealed a dose-dependent increase in annexin V-positive cells, with 150 V/cm yielding a 4.8-fold increase in PS exposure compared to controls (p < 0.001). Propidium iodide staining confirmed no significant difference in cell viability, indicating that PS externalization occurred without membrane rupture or necrosis. Total cellular PS levels, measured by lipid extraction and mass spectrometry, remained unchanged, confirming that the effect was due to redistribution rather than de novo synthesis. Parallel experiments with normal human astrocytes showed no significant change in surface PS at any field strength, demonstrating tumor-selective modulation. These results establish that low-intensity DC electric fields can safely and selectively enhance surface PS on glioblastoma cells, providing a foundation for improved PS-targeted interventions.

### Example 2

This example demonstrates the enhancement of SapC-DOPS nanovesicle binding and cytotoxicity in glioblastoma cells following electric field pretreatment. U87ΔEGFR-Luc cells were exposed to a 125 V/cm DC electric field for 60 minutes using the non-contact capacitor device described in Example 1. Immediately after field removal, cells were incubated with CellVue Maroon (CVM)-labeled SapC-DOPS nanovesicles (10 µg/mL) for 2 hours. Confocal microscopy revealed a 3.2-fold increase in CVM fluorescence intensity in field-treated cells compared to untreated controls, indicating enhanced nanovesicle binding. Quantitative flow cytometry confirmed a 2.9-fold increase in mean fluorescence intensity (p < 0.01). To assess functional consequences, cells were treated with unlabeled SapC-DOPS (20 µg/mL) for 48 hours following field exposure. Cell viability, measured by MTT assay, showed a 65% reduction in field-pretreated cells versus 35% in SapC-DOPS alone (p < 0.001), corresponding to a 2.1-fold decrease in IC₅₀. Caspase-3/7 activity assays indicated a synergistic increase in apoptosis, with field + SapC-DOPS yielding 4.5-fold higher activity than either treatment alone. These findings confirm that electric field-mediated PS upregulation significantly potentiates the tumor-targeting and cytotoxic effects of SapC-DOPS, enabling lower therapeutic doses and improved efficacy.

### Example 3

In this example, the impact of electric field pretreatment on PS-targeted optical imaging in vivo was evaluated using an orthotopic glioblastoma mouse model. Female nude mice were implanted intracranially with U87ΔEGFR-Luc cells. Ten days post-implantation, when bioluminescence confirmed tumor establishment, mice were anesthetized and fitted with a custom head-mounted electrode array delivering 100 V/cm DC field for 60 minutes. Immediately thereafter, mice received an intravenous injection of CVM-labeled SapC-DOPS (2 mg/kg). Optical imaging was performed at 1, 4, and 24 hours post-injection using a rotational imaging system. Field-pretreated mice exhibited a 2.7-fold higher tumor fluorescence signal at 4 hours compared to non-pretreated controls (p < 0.01), with improved tumor-to-background ratios (4.3 vs. 1.8). Ex vivo organ imaging at 24 hours confirmed 3.1-fold higher tumor accumulation of CVM-SapC-DOPS in the field group, with no increase in liver or spleen uptake. Histological analysis showed colocalization of CVM signal with tumor regions positive for PS by immunohistochemistry. These results demonstrate that non-contact electric fields can enhance tumor-specific delivery and imaging contrast of PS-targeted optical probes in vivo, supporting clinical translation for intraoperative guidance or early detection.

### Example 4

This example evaluates the enhancement of PS-targeted MRI using electric field pretreatment. U87ΔEGFR-Luc tumor-bearing mice were subjected to 100 V/cm DC field for 60 minutes via a transcranial electrode array. Immediately after, mice received an intravenous injection of ultrasmall superparamagnetic iron oxide (USPIO)-loaded SapC-DOPS (5 mg Fe/kg). T₂*-weighted MRI was performed at 7T before and at 1, 4, and 24 hours post-injection. Field-pretreated mice showed a 42% greater reduction in tumor T₂* signal at 4 hours compared to controls (p < 0.05), indicating higher iron accumulation. Inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectroscopy (ICP-AES) of excised tumors at 24 hours confirmed a 4.8-fold higher iron concentration in the field group versus free USPIO controls and a 2.3-fold increase over SapC-DOPS alone. No significant signal changes were observed in contralateral brain or muscle, confirming specificity. These data illustrate that electric field-mediated PS upregulation significantly improves the sensitivity and tumor selectivity of PS-targeted MRI contrast agents, enabling better delineation of tumor margins and monitoring of therapeutic response.

### Example 5

In this example, the synergy between electric field pretreatment and PS-targeted PET imaging was assessed. Mice bearing subcutaneous U87ΔEGFR-Luc tumors were randomized into two groups. The treatment group received 100 V/cm DC field for 60 minutes via surface electrodes, followed by intravenous injection of ⁸⁹Zr-labeled PGN635 (5 MBq, 10 µg). The control group received PGN635 alone. PET/CT imaging was performed at 24 hours post-injection. Tumor SUVmax was 8.7 ± 1.2 in the field group versus 4.1 ± 0.9 in controls (p < 0.001), with tumor-to-muscle ratios of 12.4 vs. 5.3. Ex vivo gamma counting confirmed 2.9-fold higher tumor radioactivity in the field group, with no increase in blood or major organs. Immunohistochemistry showed a strong correlation between ⁸⁹Zr signal and PS staining intensity. These results validate that electric field application significantly enhances tumor uptake and imaging contrast of radiolabeled PS-targeting antibodies, offering a powerful strategy for improving the sensitivity of clinical PET imaging in PS-low tumors.
