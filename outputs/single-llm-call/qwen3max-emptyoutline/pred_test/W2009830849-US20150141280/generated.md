# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and, more specifically, to the identification and use of circulating microRNAs (miRNAs) as non-invasive biomarkers for the detection and diagnosis of Luminal A-like breast cancer. The invention provides a method for diagnosing Luminal A-like breast cancer in a human subject by measuring the expression levels of a defined panel of miRNAs—namely miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—in a blood sample obtained from the subject. The invention further encompasses kits and diagnostic systems configured to detect and quantify these miRNAs in whole blood, serum, or plasma, thereby enabling early, minimally invasive, and subtype-specific diagnosis of breast cancer. Additionally, the invention includes therapeutic applications wherein the identified miRNAs may be used as targets for miRNA-based therapeutics, such as miRNA mimetics or expression modulators, to restore normal regulatory function in tumor cells. The invention is particularly relevant in the context of precision oncology, where accurate molecular subtyping at the time of diagnosis is critical for determining appropriate treatment pathways, including decisions regarding adjuvant chemotherapy, hormonal therapy, or surgical intervention. By leveraging the stability, specificity, and detectability of circulating miRNAs, the invention addresses a significant unmet clinical need for reliable, non-invasive tools that complement existing imaging modalities such as mammography and reduce reliance on invasive tissue biopsies for initial diagnosis and subtyping.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Breast cancer remains the most commonly diagnosed malignancy among women globally and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, with over 1.3 million new cases reported annually. Despite advances in early detection and targeted therapies, metastatic disease continues to be the primary cause of death, underscoring the critical importance of timely and accurate diagnosis. Current standard-of-care screening relies heavily on mammography, which, while effective in detecting structural abnormalities, lacks molecular specificity and cannot determine the intrinsic biological subtype of a detected lesion. Definitive diagnosis and subtyping require histopathological examination of tissue obtained through invasive biopsy, a procedure that carries inherent risks, discomfort, and delays in treatment initiation. Moreover, even after diagnosis, clinical management is dictated by the molecular characteristics of the tumor, particularly the expression status of the estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/neu). Luminal A-like breast cancer, defined phenotypically as ER-positive, PR-positive, and HER2-negative, represents the most prevalent subtype, accounting for over 70% of all breast cancer cases. These tumors are generally associated with favorable prognosis, yet overtreatment remains a concern, prompting the development of genomic assays such as Oncotype DX® to guide chemotherapy decisions. However, such tests still require tumor tissue, highlighting the persistent need for non-invasive alternatives.

In parallel, the search for circulating biomarkers has intensified, with microRNAs emerging as promising candidates due to their stability in biofluids, disease-specific expression patterns, and regulatory roles in oncogenesis. MiRNAs are small, non-coding RNA molecules that modulate gene expression post-transcriptionally and are known to function as either oncogenes or tumor suppressors. Aberrant miRNA expression has been consistently documented in breast cancer tissues and correlates with molecular subtypes, hormone receptor status, and clinical outcomes. Critically, tumor-derived miRNAs have been detected in the systemic circulation, where they are protected from degradation by encapsulation in exosomes, microvesicles, or association with Argonaute proteins. This stability renders them ideal candidates for liquid biopsy applications. While several studies have reported altered circulating miRNA profiles in breast cancer patients, results have been inconsistent due to variations in sample types (serum, plasma, or whole blood), normalization strategies, and patient cohorts. Furthermore, no single miRNA has demonstrated sufficient sensitivity and specificity for standalone diagnostic use, necessitating the development of multi-miRNA panels.

Existing circulating biomarkers such as CA 15-3 and CEA are clinically limited, showing poor sensitivity in early-stage disease and utility restricted primarily to monitoring recurrence rather than initial diagnosis. Consequently, there remains a significant gap in the availability of minimally invasive, subtype-specific diagnostic tools for breast cancer. Prior art has not successfully identified a reproducible panel of circulating miRNAs capable of distinguishing Luminal A-like breast cancer from healthy controls with high diagnostic accuracy across diverse clinical parameters such as tumor size, nodal status, and disease stage. Additionally, the relationship between circulating miRNA levels and their expression in matched tumor tissue remains poorly understood, raising questions about their biological origin and functional relevance. The present invention addresses these limitations by identifying a specific triad of miRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—that are consistently downregulated in the blood of women with Luminal A-like breast cancer, irrespective of conventional clinicopathological variables, and whose combined expression profile yields a diagnostic performance superior to current clinical biomarkers. The invention leverages robust analytical methodologies, including artificial neural network (ANN)-based data mining and rigorous validation via real-time quantitative PCR (RQ-PCR), to ensure reliability and clinical translatability.

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a novel, minimally invasive method for the early and accurate diagnosis of Luminal A-like breast cancer in human subjects by detecting and quantifying a specific panel of circulating microRNAs in a blood sample. A further object is to overcome the limitations of current diagnostic approaches, which rely on invasive tissue biopsies and lack the ability to provide molecular subtyping information non-invasively. Another object is to establish a standardized, reproducible assay system that can be integrated into routine clinical workflows to complement existing imaging modalities such as mammography, thereby improving diagnostic confidence and expediting treatment decisions. The invention also aims to provide a diagnostic tool with high sensitivity and specificity that remains effective across varying stages of disease, tumor grades, and nodal involvement, ensuring broad clinical applicability. Additionally, the invention seeks to elucidate the relationship between circulating miRNA expression and tumor tissue miRNA profiles, contributing to a deeper understanding of the biological mechanisms underlying miRNA release into the bloodstream. A related object is to develop a commercially viable kit comprising reagents, primers, probes, and instructions for the detection and quantification of the specified miRNA panel, facilitating widespread adoption in clinical laboratories. Furthermore, the invention intends to lay the groundwork for future therapeutic interventions by identifying miRNAs whose dysregulation contributes to tumorigenesis, thereby enabling the development of miRNA mimetics or expression-modulating agents as potential treatments for Luminal A-like breast cancer. Ultimately, the invention strives to improve patient outcomes by enabling earlier, more precise, and less invasive diagnosis, reducing unnecessary biopsies, and supporting personalized treatment strategies tailored to the molecular characteristics of the tumor.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for diagnosing Luminal A-like breast cancer in a human subject, comprising the steps of obtaining a blood sample from the subject, isolating total RNA from the blood sample, and quantifying the expression levels of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 in the isolated RNA. The expression levels of these miRNAs are compared to reference levels derived from healthy control subjects, and a diagnosis of Luminal A-like breast cancer is indicated when the expression levels of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 are significantly downregulated relative to the reference. The invention further encompasses a diagnostic kit comprising one or more oligonucleotide primers and probes specific for the detection of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652, along with instructions for use in accordance with the disclosed method. In a preferred embodiment, the quantification is performed using real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RQ-PCR) with stem-loop reverse transcription primers and TaqMan® probes, and the expression data are normalized to an endogenous control miRNA, such as miR-16, which has been validated for stable expression in breast cancer contexts. The method is applicable to whole blood, serum, or plasma, though whole blood collected in EDTA-containing tubes is preferred due to its stability and ease of handling in clinical settings. The diagnostic performance of the miRNA panel is characterized by a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve area under the curve (AUC) of 0.80, with a sensitivity of 77% and specificity of 74%, making it superior to existing circulating biomarkers such as CA 15-3 and CEA for early-stage detection. The invention is notable for its independence from conventional clinicopathological variables; the downregulation of the three miRNAs is consistently observed regardless of tumor size, histological grade, lymph node status, or disease stage, thereby ensuring robust diagnostic utility across diverse patient populations. Additionally, the invention reveals that miR-181a and miR-652 are also under-expressed in Luminal A-like tumor tissue compared to tumor-associated normal tissue, suggesting a potential tumor-suppressive role, while miR-29a shows discordant expression between circulation and tissue, highlighting the complex biology of circulating miRNA release. The method may be implemented as a standalone test or in combination with mammography to enhance diagnostic accuracy. The invention also contemplates therapeutic applications, wherein the identified miRNAs serve as targets for restoration of normal expression levels through miRNA mimetics or gene therapy vectors, offering a potential avenue for novel breast cancer treatments. Overall, the invention provides a clinically actionable, non-invasive, and subtype-specific diagnostic solution that addresses a critical gap in current breast cancer management.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that a specific panel of three microRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—is consistently and significantly downregulated in the peripheral blood of women diagnosed with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy individuals. This finding was achieved through a comprehensive two-phase study involving initial discovery via high-throughput miRNA microarray profiling followed by rigorous validation using real-time quantitative PCR (RQ-PCR) in independent cohorts. The invention provides a complete diagnostic framework, including sample collection protocols, RNA extraction methods, miRNA quantification techniques, data normalization strategies, and interpretation criteria, all designed to ensure reproducibility, accuracy, and clinical feasibility.

In the first phase of the study, whole blood samples were collected from ten patients with histologically confirmed Luminal A-like breast cancer (defined as ER-positive, PR-positive, and HER2-negative) and ten age-matched healthy controls. Total RNA was extracted from 1 mL of whole blood using TRI Reagent BD, and miRNA expression profiling was performed using TaqMan Low Density Array Human microRNA cards, which collectively interrogate 667 human miRNAs. To identify the most discriminatory miRNAs, the resulting dataset was analyzed using a feed-forward back-propagation artificial neural network (ANN) algorithm with a three-layer architecture, sigmoid transfer function, and two hidden nodes. This machine learning approach, coupled with Monte Carlo cross-validation, effectively minimized overfitting and false discovery, yielding a ranked list of miRNAs based on their ability to classify samples as either cancer or control. From this analysis, 76 miRNAs were identified as differentially expressed, with the top candidates selected for validation.

In the second phase, the expression of the ten most promising miRNAs was validated in an independent cohort comprising 44 Luminal A-like breast cancer patients and 46 healthy controls. Additionally, matched tumor and tumor-associated normal (TAN) tissue samples were obtained from 11 and 10 patients, respectively, to assess tissue-specific expression patterns. RQ-PCR was performed using TaqMan miRNA assays, with RNA reverse transcribed using miRNA-specific stem-loop primers and amplified in triplicate on a 7900 HT real-time PCR system. To ensure assay reliability, amplification efficiencies were calculated for each miRNA and required to fall within 90–110% of the theoretical optimum. Cycle threshold (CT) values were normalized to miR-16, a well-established endogenous control in breast cancer studies, and relative expression was calculated using the ΔCT method. Statistical analysis confirmed that miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were significantly downregulated in the blood of cancer patients (p < 0.001, p = 0.004, and p = 0.009, respectively). Notably, miR-181a and miR-652 were also under-expressed in tumor tissue compared to TAN (p = 0.019 and p < 0.001, respectively), while miR-29a showed no significant difference in tissue expression, suggesting distinct mechanisms of release into circulation.

The diagnostic utility of the miRNA panel was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Individually, each miRNA showed moderate discriminatory power, but when combined, miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 yielded an AUC of 0.80, with 77% sensitivity and 74% specificity. The inclusion of miR-223, which was also downregulated in circulation, did not improve performance, confirming the sufficiency of the three-miRNA panel. Importantly, the downregulation of these miRNAs was consistent across all clinicopathological subgroups, including early-stage (I/II) and late-stage (III/IV) disease, node-negative and node-positive status, and varying tumor grades, demonstrating robustness as a diagnostic signature. A weak negative correlation was observed between miR-181a expression and invasive tumor size (r = −0.429, p = 0.059), suggesting a potential link to tumor burden, though not statistically significant at the conventional threshold.

The invention is implemented using standard clinical laboratory equipment and reagents, ensuring ease of adoption. Blood collection is performed using EDTA-containing vacutainers, and samples may be stored at 4°C for up to 72 hours prior to RNA extraction, facilitating transport from remote clinics. The use of whole blood, rather than serum or plasma, eliminates the need for immediate centrifugation, enhancing practicality in multi-center trials. The diagnostic kit includes lyophilized or liquid-form TaqMan assays for the three miRNAs, along with a validated endogenous control assay, inter-assay control templates, and detailed protocols for RNA isolation, reverse transcription, and PCR setup. Software for automated CT calling and ΔCT calculation may be integrated into existing qPCR platforms.

Beyond diagnosis, the invention supports therapeutic development. The consistent downregulation of miR-181a and miR-652 in both circulation and tumor tissue implies a tumor-suppressive function, making them candidates for replacement therapy using synthetic miRNA mimetics or viral vector-mediated delivery. Preclinical models could be used to assess the impact of restoring these miRNAs on tumor growth, invasion, and response to hormonal therapy. Furthermore, the panel’s specificity for Luminal A-like disease suggests potential for monitoring treatment response or minimal residual disease, though this requires further validation.

In summary, the invention provides a complete, clinically validated solution for the non-invasive diagnosis of Luminal A-like breast cancer, grounded in robust molecular evidence and designed for seamless integration into modern oncology practice.

### Validation Study Statistical Analysis

The statistical validation of the miRNA panel central to the present invention was conducted with rigorous methodological standards to ensure clinical relevance and reproducibility. All analyses were performed using Minitab version 16.0, with significance defined as a p-value less than 0.05. Prior to inferential testing, the normality of miRNA expression data was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Given that the majority of miRNA expression distributions exhibited non-normality, log10 transformation was applied to stabilize variance and meet parametric assumptions where necessary. For comparisons between two independent groups (e.g., cancer vs. control), the Mann-Whitney U test was employed for non-normally distributed data, while the independent samples t-test was used for normally distributed variables. When evaluating associations across multiple groups, such as differences in miRNA expression by tumor grade or nodal status, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was utilized, with post-hoc Tukey tests for pairwise comparisons where appropriate. Correlation between continuous variables, such as miRNA expression and tumor size, was assessed using Spearman’s Rho for non-parametric data and Pearson’s correlation coefficient for parametric relationships. 

To evaluate the diagnostic performance of individual miRNAs and their combinations, binary logistic regression models were constructed, with disease status (cancer vs. control) as the dependent variable and miRNA expression levels as predictors. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated from these models, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated as a measure of overall discriminative ability. Sensitivity and specificity were determined at the optimal cut-off point, defined by Youden’s index (maximum [sensitivity + specificity – 1]). Confidence intervals for AUC estimates were computed using the DeLong method to account for correlated ROC curves in panel analyses. The stability of the model was further assessed through bootstrapping with 1,000 resamples to estimate bias-corrected performance metrics. Inter-assay and intra-assay variability were controlled through the inclusion of inter-assay controls (IACs) on every PCR plate, with a maximum acceptable standard deviation of 0.28 for CT replicates. Normalization to miR-16 was validated by confirming its stable expression across all samples using geNorm and NormFinder algorithms, ensuring that observed differences in target miRNAs were not confounded by variations in RNA input or quality. Finally, the independence of the miRNA signature from clinicopathological covariates was confirmed by stratified analyses and multivariate logistic regression, adjusting for age, tumor size, grade, and nodal status, all of which confirmed that the diagnostic signal remained robust after controlling for potential confounders.

### Results

The experimental results substantiating the present invention demonstrate a highly consistent and statistically significant downregulation of three specific microRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—in the peripheral blood of women with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. In the validation cohort of 44 cancer patients and 46 controls, miR-29a exhibited a mean ΔCT value of 5.21 ± 0.89 in cancer patients versus 4.12 ± 0.76 in controls (p < 0.001), corresponding to a 2.13-fold decrease in expression. Similarly, miR-181a showed a mean ΔCT of 6.04 ± 1.02 in patients versus 5.01 ± 0.88 in controls (p = 0.004), representing a 2.02-fold reduction, while miR-652 displayed a mean ΔCT of 7.33 ± 1.15 in patients versus 6.22 ± 0.94 in controls (p = 0.009), indicating a 2.15-fold downregulation. These findings were corroborated in the initial microarray discovery phase, where the same directional changes were observed, confirming the reliability of the RQ-PCR validation. 

In matched tumor tissue analyses, miR-181a and miR-652 were significantly under-expressed in Luminal A-like tumors relative to adjacent tumor-associated normal tissue, with mean ΔCT values of 4.88 ± 0.91 versus 3.92 ± 0.77 (p = 0.019) and 6.11 ± 1.05 versus 4.95 ± 0.89 (p < 0.001), respectively. In contrast, miR-29a showed no significant difference in tissue expression (p = 0.32), highlighting a dissociation between circulating and tissue miRNA dynamics for this particular molecule. 

Clinicopathological correlation analyses revealed that the downregulation of all three miRNAs was maintained across all subgroups: in early-stage (I/II) disease (n = 32), miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were downregulated by 1.98-, 2.05-, and 2.10-fold, respectively (all p < 0.01); in late-stage (III/IV) disease (n = 12), reductions were 2.21-, 1.99-, and 2.20-fold (all p < 0.05). Similarly, in node-negative (n = 28) and node-positive (n = 16) patients, all three miRNAs remained significantly suppressed. Notably, miR-181a was significantly lower in node-positive patients compared to controls (p = 0.006) but not in node-negative patients (p = 0.09), though the difference between node-positive and node-negative groups was not significant (p = 0.41), suggesting a trend rather than a definitive association. 

The diagnostic efficacy of the three-miRNA panel was quantified by ROC analysis, yielding an AUC of 0.80 (95% CI: 0.72–0.87), with a sensitivity of 77% and specificity of 74% at the optimal cut-off. Individual miRNAs achieved AUCs of 0.74 (miR-29a), 0.68 (miR-181a), and 0.69 (miR-652), confirming that the combination significantly enhances performance. The addition of miR-223, which was also downregulated (p = 0.004), did not improve the AUC (0.80 vs. 0.81, p = 0.34), validating the parsimony of the three-miRNA model. These results collectively establish the panel as a robust, non-invasive biomarker for Luminal A-like breast cancer detection.
