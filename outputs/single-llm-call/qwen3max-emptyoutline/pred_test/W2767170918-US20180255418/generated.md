# DESCRIPTION

## INTRODUCTION

The field of speech enhancement has long been challenged by the complex acoustic environments encountered in real-world applications, particularly those involving multiple concurrent speakers and background noise. Traditional approaches to isolating a desired speaker from such mixtures have often fallen short when faced with the dual challenges of interference from competing talkers and contamination by ambient acoustic disturbances. In enclosed spaces—such as conference rooms, meeting halls, or vehicle cabins—these difficulties are compounded by reverberation effects that distort the spatial and spectral characteristics of speech signals. Despite decades of research into beamforming, spectral subtraction, and statistical modeling techniques, achieving robust, high-fidelity extraction of a target speaker in multi-talker scenarios remains an open problem, especially under low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and low signal-to-interference ratio (SIR) conditions.

Beamforming, as a spatial filtering technique, has emerged as one of the most effective strategies for enhancing speech captured by microphone arrays. Among the various beamformer designs, the Minimum Variance Distortionless Response (MVDR) beamformer has gained widespread adoption due to its ability to preserve the integrity of the desired signal while suppressing uncorrelated noise. However, the MVDR formulation assumes that all non-target components—including both background noise and interfering speech—are part of a single “noise” subspace. This assumption breaks down in practical settings where interfering speakers produce structured, non-stationary signals that cannot be adequately modeled as stationary noise. Consequently, MVDR-based systems often fail to sufficiently attenuate competing talkers, leading to degraded intelligibility and perceptual quality of the enhanced output.

To address this limitation, the Linearly Constrained Minimum Variance (LCMV) beamformer was introduced as a more flexible framework capable of incorporating explicit constraints for both the desired source and known interferers. By imposing linear constraints that enforce unity gain on the target speaker and null response on interfering sources, the LCMV beamformer can theoretically achieve superior interference rejection compared to MVDR. However, the practical implementation of LCMV requires accurate knowledge of the relative transfer functions (RTFs)—complex-valued frequency-domain representations that characterize the propagation path from each source to the microphone array—and a reliable estimate of the noise covariance matrix. In dynamic or unknown environments, obtaining these parameters in real time presents significant technical hurdles.

Recent advances in deep learning have offered promising tools for overcoming some of these challenges. Neural network-based voice activity detectors (VADs) and speech presence probability (SPP) estimators now enable more precise identification of speech-active frames, facilitating adaptive updates to noise models and spatial filters. Moreover, data-driven approaches allow for the construction of pre-trained libraries of RTFs corresponding to fixed speaker positions—a strategy particularly well-suited for semi-static environments like boardrooms or smart home setups where participants occupy known locations. Nevertheless, integrating these modern machine learning components with classical signal processing frameworks such as LCMV beamforming requires careful orchestration to ensure stability, consistency, and computational feasibility.

The present invention bridges this gap by introducing a novel, end-to-end multichannel speech enhancement system that synergistically combines the spatial selectivity of the LCMV beamformer with the discriminative power of deep neural networks. Central to this architecture is a dual-control mechanism that governs the adaptive updating of two critical beamformer parameters: the noise covariance matrix and the RTF matrix. A first control loop employs a neural network-derived SPP map to implement a robust VAD that identifies noise-only segments, enabling continuous refinement of the background noise model without contamination from speech activity. A second control loop introduces a Speaker Position Identifier (SPI)—a new module that leverages a pre-calibrated RTF library to classify active speech frames according to their spatial origin, thereby determining whether they originate from the desired speaker or an interferer. This classification directly informs which RTFs should be used or updated in the LCMV constraint matrix, ensuring that the beamformer maintains accurate spatial alignment with the target source even in the presence of intermittent double-talk or varying interference patterns.

Furthermore, the invention incorporates a post-processing stage based on the Neural Network Mixture-Maximum (NN-MM) algorithm, which applies soft spectral attenuation guided by phoneme-aware SPP estimates to suppress residual noise remaining after beamforming. Unlike conventional Wiener filters or binary masking techniques, the NN-MM postfilter exploits both generative and discriminative models—specifically, a phoneme-based Mixture of Gaussians (MoG) and a deep neural network (DNN) phoneme classifier—to produce time-frequency masks that preserve fine-grained spectral details while effectively attenuating non-speech components. This two-stage enhancement pipeline—comprising an adaptively controlled LCMV beamformer followed by an NN-MM postfilter—delivers substantial improvements in both objective metrics (e.g., output SNR and SIR) and subjective listening quality across a wide range of input conditions.

The system is particularly optimized for deployment in low-reverberant enclosures with fixed speaker positions, such as conference tables equipped with embedded microphone arrays. In such scenarios, a one-time calibration phase suffices to build a comprehensive RTF library mapping each physical location to its corresponding acoustic signature. During operation, the system dynamically selects and validates RTFs from this library using real-time SPI decisions, eliminating the need for online RTF estimation from potentially contaminated data. This design not only enhances robustness but also reduces computational overhead, making the solution suitable for real-time implementation on edge devices with limited processing resources.

In summary, the invention represents a significant advancement over prior art by unifying classical array signal processing with modern deep learning in a tightly integrated, feedback-controlled architecture. It solves the long-standing problem of simultaneous noise and interference suppression in multi-speaker environments through intelligent parameter adaptation driven by neural network-based scene analysis. The result is a highly effective, practical, and deployable speech enhancement system that achieves state-of-the-art performance without requiring assumptions about speaker identities, utterance content, or environmental acoustics beyond mild constraints on reverberation and positional stability.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a comprehensive multichannel speech enhancement system designed to extract a desired speaker’s voice from a noisy mixture containing one or more interfering speakers and stationary background noise, particularly in semi-static acoustic environments such as conference rooms or meeting spaces with fixed seating arrangements. The core innovation lies in a novel control architecture that enables adaptive, data-driven updates to the parameters of a Linearly Constrained Minimum Variance (LCMV) beamformer through two interdependent mechanisms: a speech-presence-probability (SPP)-based voice activity detector (VAD) for noise covariance matrix estimation and a speaker position identifier (SPI) for relative transfer function (RTF) selection and validation. These mechanisms operate in concert to ensure that the beamformer maintains optimal spatial focus on the target speaker while actively suppressing both background noise and competing talkers, even under challenging conditions of low input signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and signal-to-interference ratio (SIR).

The system begins by capturing audio signals via a fixed-array of microphones, typically arranged in a geometric configuration such as a U-shape to provide spatial diversity. In a preliminary calibration phase—conducted once during system setup—an RTF library is constructed by recording reference signals from each possible speaker position within the environment. Each entry in this library corresponds to the frequency-domain RTF between a specific source location and the microphone array, normalized with respect to a designated reference microphone. This library serves as a spatial fingerprint database that enables rapid identification of active speakers during runtime without requiring online estimation of acoustic transfer paths.

During operational use, the system processes incoming multichannel signals in the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) domain. The noise covariance matrix, essential for LCMV beamformer design, is initialized using the first few hundred milliseconds of the audio stream, assumed to contain only background noise. Subsequently, a neural network-based SPP estimator—implemented via the Neural Network Mixture-Maximum (NN-MM) algorithm—is applied to the signal from the reference microphone to generate a time-frequency speech presence probability map. This map is aggregated across frequency bins to produce a frame-level VAD decision that indicates whether any speech (from either the desired or interfering speaker) is active. When the VAD outputs a “noise-only” decision, the noise covariance matrix is recursively updated using a leaky averaging scheme; otherwise, updates are suspended to prevent speech contamination of the noise model.

Concurrently, the SPI module analyzes speech-active frames identified by the VAD to determine the spatial origin of the active speaker. For each such frame, an instantaneous RTF estimate is derived using generalized eigenvalue decomposition (GEVD) of the observed signal correlation matrix relative to the current noise covariance estimate. Under the assumption that single-speaker activity dominates most frames—a reasonable premise in conversational settings with turn-taking—the dominant eigenvector corresponds to the RTF of the active source. This estimated RTF is then compared against all entries in the pre-trained RTF library using cosine similarity as a distance metric. The library entry yielding the highest similarity score is selected as the candidate position. If the maximum similarity exceeds a confidence threshold and exhibits spectral consistency across frequency bins, the frame is classified as originating from either the desired or interfering speaker based on the known seating assignment (e.g., position #1 is always the target). This classification directly controls whether the corresponding RTF is used to update the constraint matrix of the LCMV beamformer.

The LCMV beamformer itself is formulated with two linear constraints: unity gain on the desired speaker’s RTF and zero gain on the interfering speaker’s RTF. Its weight vector is computed in real time using the latest estimates of the noise covariance matrix and the RTF constraint matrix. The resulting beamformed output is a single-channel signal in which the desired speech is preserved while interference and noise are significantly attenuated. However, residual noise often remains due to imperfect parameter estimates or model mismatches.

To address this, the invention incorporates a post-processing stage wherein the NN-MM algorithm is reapplied—not to the raw microphone signal, but to the beamformed output. This second application generates a refined SPP map that accounts for the altered spectral characteristics of the enhanced signal. A soft spectral attenuation rule, operating in the log-magnitude STFT domain, is then applied using this updated SPP map to further suppress residual noise while minimizing musical noise artifacts and speech distortion. The attenuation level is governed by a tunable parameter that balances noise reduction against speech preservation.

The entire system operates in a closed-loop fashion: VAD decisions gate noise updates, SPI decisions gate RTF updates, and both influence the beamformer weights that produce the intermediate enhanced signal fed into the postfilter. This tight integration ensures that all components remain synchronized with the evolving acoustic scene. Experimental validation conducted in a low-reverberant room with a seven-microphone U-shaped array demonstrated consistent improvements in output SNR and SIR across a wide range of input conditions. Notably, the NN-MM postfilter was found to enhance not only noise suppression but also interference rejection, likely due to its ability to exploit spectral distortions inadvertently imposed on the interferer by the beamformer.

In essence, the invention provides a complete, end-to-end solution for speaker extraction that overcomes key limitations of prior beamforming approaches by embedding deep learning–driven scene analysis into the parameter adaptation logic of a constrained optimal filter. It is particularly advantageous in applications requiring hands-free communication, voice-controlled interfaces, or remote conferencing in multi-person settings, where reliable isolation of a designated speaker is critical for downstream tasks such as automatic speech recognition or human-computer interaction.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a multichannel speech enhancement system that integrates adaptive beamforming with deep neural network–based signal analysis to extract a desired speaker’s voice from a mixture containing interfering speakers and background noise. The system is specifically engineered for deployment in environments where speaker positions are approximately fixed—such as conference tables, lecture halls, or automotive cabins—and where reverberation levels are low to moderate. The following detailed description outlines the structural components, operational principles, mathematical formulations, and control logic that constitute the invention, with reference to a preferred embodiment utilizing a seven-microphone U-shaped array, though the architecture is generalizable to other array geometries and channel counts.

The system operates on multichannel audio signals captured by an array of M omnidirectional microphones placed at fixed, known positions. In the time domain, the signal received at the m-th microphone is modeled as the convolution of the desired speech source \( s_d(n) \), the interfering speech source \( s_i(n) \), and stationary background noise \( v_m(n) \) with their respective acoustic impulse responses (AIRs) \( h_{d,m}(n) \) and \( h_{i,m}(n) \). To facilitate frequency-domain processing, the signals are transformed into the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) domain, yielding for each time frame \( l \) and frequency bin \( k \) a complex-valued observation vector \( \mathbf{z}(l,k) = [z_1(l,k), \dots, z_M(l,k)]^T \). This vector is expressed as:
\[
\mathbf{z}(l,k) = \mathbf{c}_d(l,k) s_d(l,k) + \mathbf{c}_i(l,k) s_i(l,k) + \mathbf{v}(l,k),
\]
where \( \mathbf{c}_d(l,k) \) and \( \mathbf{c}_i(l,k) \) are the relative transfer function (RTF) vectors associated with the desired and interfering sources, respectively, defined as the ratio of the acoustic transfer function (ATF) from each source to microphone \( m \) relative to a designated reference microphone (e.g., the fifth microphone in the array). The noise vector \( \mathbf{v}(l,k) \) is assumed to be zero-mean, stationary, and uncorrelated with the speech sources.

The core of the invention is an adaptively controlled Linearly Constrained Minimum Variance (LCMV) beamformer. The beamformer applies a complex weight vector \( \mathbf{w}(l,k) \in \mathbb{C}^M \) to the observation vector to produce an enhanced estimate of the desired speech:
\[
\hat{s}_d(l,k) = \mathbf{w}^H(l,k) \mathbf{z}(l,k).
\]
The weight vector is chosen to minimize the output power subject to linear constraints that enforce distortionless response on the desired source and null response on the interferer:
\[
\min_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{w}^H \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}(l,k) \mathbf{w} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{C}^H(l,k) \mathbf{w}(l,k) = \mathbf{g},
\]
where \( \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}(l,k) \) is the noise covariance matrix, \( \mathbf{C}(l,k) = [\mathbf{c}_d(l,k), \mathbf{c}_i(l,k)] \) is the RTF constraint matrix, and \( \mathbf{g} = [1, 0]^T \) specifies the desired response. The closed-form solution is given by:
\[
\mathbf{w}(l,k) = \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}^{-1}(l,k) \mathbf{C}(l,k) \left( \mathbf{C}^H(l,k) \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}^{-1}(l,k) \mathbf{C}(l,k) \right)^{-1} \mathbf{g}.
\]
Accurate computation of \( \mathbf{w}(l,k) \) hinges on reliable estimates of \( \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}(l,k) \) and \( \mathbf{C}(l,k) \), which are provided by two novel control mechanisms.

The first mechanism governs the estimation and adaptation of the noise covariance matrix. Initially, \( \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}(l,k) \) is set by averaging the outer product \( \mathbf{z}(l,k)\mathbf{z}^H(l,k) \) over the first 0.5 seconds of the audio stream, under the assumption that this segment contains only background noise. Thereafter, updates are conditionally performed based on a voice activity detector (VAD) derived from a speech presence probability (SPP) map. The SPP map \( \rho(l,k) \in [0,1] \) is generated by applying the Neural Network Mixture-Maximum (NN-MM) algorithm to the reference microphone signal \( z_{\text{ref}}(l,k) \). The NN-MM algorithm fuses a phoneme-based Mixture of Gaussians (MoG) prior with a deep neural network (DNN) phoneme classifier to produce per-time-frequency-unit probabilities of speech presence. These probabilities are summed across frequency to yield a frame-level activity score:
\[
A(l) = \sum_k \rho(l,k).
\]
A VAD decision \( V(l) \in \{0,1\} \) is then made by comparing \( A(l) \) to a threshold \( T_r = N_{\text{DFT}}/4 \), where \( N_{\text{DFT}} \) is the STFT frame length. If \( V(l) = 0 \) (noise-only), the noise covariance matrix is updated via:
\[
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}(l,k) = \alpha \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}(l-1,k) + (1 - \alpha) \mathbf{z}(l,k)\mathbf{z}^H(l,k),
\]
with \( \alpha \in (0,1) \) a forgetting factor; otherwise, no update occurs. This prevents speech leakage into the noise model.

The second mechanism addresses RTF estimation through a Speaker Position Identifier (SPI). During a one-time calibration phase, an RTF library \( \{ \mathbf{c}_s(k) \}_{s=1}^{N_s} \) is constructed by recording clean speech from each of \( N_s \) predefined positions (e.g., four chair locations around a table). Each library entry is normalized and stored as a function of frequency. At runtime, when the VAD indicates speech activity (\( V(l) = 1 \)), the system attempts to estimate the RTF of the active speaker. Assuming single-speaker dominance in the current frame, the signal correlation matrix \( \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{zz}(l,k) \) is computed over a short window of active frames. Generalized eigenvalue decomposition (GEVD) is applied to the matrix pair \( (\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{zz}, \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}) \):
\[
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{zz}(l,k) \mathbf{f}(k) = \lambda(k) \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv}(l,k) \mathbf{f}(k).
\]
The eigenvector \( \mathbf{f}(k) \) corresponding to the largest eigenvalue \( \lambda(k) \) is taken as a scaled estimate of the active source’s RTF, which is then normalized to unit norm to yield \( \hat{\mathbf{c}}(l,k) \).

This estimate is compared to each entry in the RTF library using cosine similarity:
\[
D_s(l,k) = \frac{ | \hat{\mathbf{c}}^H(l,k) \mathbf{c}_s(k) | }{ \| \hat{\mathbf{c}}(l,k) \| \| \mathbf{c}_s(k) \| }.
\]
Because the environment is low-reverberant, this measure reliably reflects spatial proximity. The position index \( I(l,k) = \arg\max_s D_s(l,k) \) is computed per frequency bin. To mitigate frequency-wise inconsistencies, a majority vote or spectral coherence check is applied across \( k \); if a single position dominates (e.g., >80% of bins), the frame is classified as originating from that location. Given that the desired speaker is assigned to a fixed position (e.g., position #1), the SPI output determines whether the estimated RTF corresponds to the target or interferer. Only frames confidently classified as single-speaker and matching the desired position are used to refresh \( \mathbf{c}_d(l,k) \); similarly for \( \mathbf{c}_i(l,k) \) if the interferer’s position is known or inferred.

The LCMV beamformer is then applied using the current \( \boldsymbol{\Phi}_{vv} \) and \( \mathbf{C} \), producing \( \hat{s}_d(l,k) \). This signal, though enhanced, still contains residual noise due to estimation errors and model imperfections. A final post-processing stage applies the NN-MM algorithm again—but now to \( \hat{s}_d(l,k) \)—to generate a refined SPP map \( \rho_{\text{post}}(l,k) \). A soft spectral attenuation rule is employed in the log-magnitude domain:
\[
\log |\hat{s}_d^{\text{final}}(l,k)| = (1 - \beta \rho_{\text{post}}(l,k)) \log |\hat{s}_d(l,k)|,
\]
where \( \beta \in (0,1] \) controls attenuation strength. The phase of \( \hat{s}_d(l,k) \) is retained to avoid distortion. This step significantly improves perceptual quality by smoothing residual artifacts.

The system was validated using real recordings in a low-echoic room with a seven-microphone U-array. Four speaker positions were defined, with the desired speaker at position #1. Scenarios included segments of solo desired speech, solo interference, silence, and double-talk, with background noise present throughout. Input SNR and SIR were varied systematically. Results showed that the proposed system consistently improved output SNR by 8–12 dB and SIR by 15–20 dB relative to the reference microphone, with the NN-MM postfilter contributing an additional 3–5 dB SNR gain. Crucially, the SPI correctly identified speaker positions in >92% of single-speaker frames, and the VAD achieved >95% accuracy in noise detection. The joint action of adaptive beamforming and neural postfiltering yielded intelligible, natural-sounding output even at input SNR = −5 dB and SIR = −5 dB.

In conclusion, the invention provides a robust, adaptive, and computationally feasible solution for speaker extraction in multi-talker environments by embedding deep learning–guided control logic into the classical LCMV beamforming framework. Its reliance on a pre-trained RTF library eliminates the need for online blind source separation, while its dual-update mechanism ensures parameter fidelity under realistic acoustic conditions. The system is readily implementable in real-time on embedded platforms and is applicable to conferencing, hearing aids, voice assistants, and any scenario requiring selective auditory attention.
