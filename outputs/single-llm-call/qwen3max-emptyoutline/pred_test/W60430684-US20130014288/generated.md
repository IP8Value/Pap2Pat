## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular diagnostics and therapeutic screening for protein trafficking disorders, and more specifically to a novel platform for the detection, quantification, and functional assessment of mutant cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) protein at the plasma membrane of living cells. The invention provides a genetically encoded, fluorogen-activating protein (FAP)-based reporter system that enables real-time, high-throughput, and quantitative analysis of CFTR trafficking, particularly for the F508del-CFTR mutant associated with cystic fibrosis. The platform facilitates the identification and evaluation of pharmacological correctors that restore proper folding, trafficking, and function of misfolded CFTR variants, and is further adaptable to other transmembrane proteins implicated in conformational diseases.

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is a life-limiting autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutations in the CFTR gene, which encodes a cAMP-regulated chloride and bicarbonate channel expressed predominantly in epithelial tissues of the lungs, pancreas, liver, and intestines. Among the over 2,000 known CFTR mutations, the deletion of phenylalanine at position 508 (F508del) is the most prevalent, occurring in at least one allele in approximately 90% of CF patients worldwide. The F508del mutation disrupts the proper folding of the CFTR protein during biosynthesis, leading to its retention in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and subsequent degradation via the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. Consequently, minimal amounts of the mutant protein reach the apical surface of epithelial cells, resulting in severely impaired anion transport and the hallmark pathophysiology of CF, including thick mucus accumulation, chronic lung infection, and progressive respiratory failure.

Therapeutic strategies aimed at correcting the underlying protein misfolding defect—known as “corrector” therapies—have emerged as a promising avenue for treating CF at its molecular root. Small-molecule correctors are designed to stabilize the nascent F508del-CFTR protein, promote its escape from ER quality control, and facilitate its trafficking through the Golgi apparatus to the plasma membrane. However, the development of effective correctors has been hampered by limitations in existing assay technologies used to screen and evaluate compound efficacy. Traditional methods for assessing CFTR surface expression, such as cell-surface biotinylation followed by immunoblotting or immunofluorescence microscopy using epitope-tagged CFTR, are labor-intensive, low-throughput, and prone to artifacts introduced by cell permeabilization, multiple washing steps, or non-specific antibody binding. Functional assays based on halide-sensitive fluorescent dyes or electrophysiological measurements, while informative, often require complex protocols, suffer from signal saturation, and depend on the rescued protein being not only present at the membrane but also functionally active—a condition that may not always correlate directly with surface abundance.

Moreover, discrepancies in corrector efficacy across different cell types (e.g., heterologous expression systems like HEK293 cells versus native human bronchial epithelial cells) have complicated the translation of preclinical findings into clinical success. This underscores the need for a robust, quantitative, and physiologically relevant assay platform that can accurately measure the amount of CFTR protein delivered to the cell surface, independent of its immediate functional status, and that can be readily adapted to high-throughput screening formats.

The present invention addresses these critical unmet needs by introducing a modular, genetically encoded reporter system based on fluorogen-activating proteins (FAPs). FAPs are engineered single-chain antibody fragments that bind with high specificity and affinity to otherwise non-fluorescent small-molecule dyes (fluorogens), inducing a dramatic increase in fluorescence quantum yield upon binding—often exceeding 15,000-fold. By fusing a FAP tag to CFTR in strategic extracellular locations, the invention enables selective, wash-free, and instantaneous visualization and quantification of CFTR at the plasma membrane of live cells using cell-impermeant fluorogens. This approach eliminates the need for fixation, permeabilization, or enzymatic treatments, thereby preserving native cellular physiology and minimizing experimental variability. The system is compatible with multiple analytical modalities, including flow cytometry, confocal microscopy, and plate-based fluorescence readers, making it ideal for both mechanistic studies and large-scale drug discovery campaigns.

Furthermore, the modular design of the FAP-CFTR platform allows for rapid adaptation to other disease-relevant transmembrane proteins that suffer from trafficking defects, such as the hERG potassium channel in long QT syndrome, the LDL receptor in familial hypercholesterolemia, or aquaporin-2 in nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. Thus, the invention not only advances the field of cystic fibrosis therapeutics but also provides a generalizable framework for accelerating the development of pharmacological chaperones for a broad class of conformational diseases.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel platform for the detection, quantification, and functional characterization of cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) protein, particularly mutant variants such as F508del-CFTR, at the plasma membrane of living cells. This platform is based on the genetic fusion of a fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) to CFTR, creating a chimeric reporter construct that enables selective, sensitive, and quantitative measurement of CFTR surface expression without the need for cell fixation, permeabilization, or multiple washing steps. The invention encompasses both N-terminal and extracellular loop (specifically, the fourth extracellular loop, EL4) fusion strategies that preserve the functional integrity of CFTR while presenting the FAP tag to the extracellular environment.

In one embodiment, the FAP is fused to the N-terminus of CFTR via an additional transmembrane segment derived from the platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR), ensuring proper orientation and extracellular exposure of the FAP domain. In another embodiment, the FAP is inserted into the fourth extracellular loop (EL4) of CFTR, between conserved glycosylation sites, to minimize disruption of protein folding and trafficking. Both constructs are shown to traffic correctly to the plasma membrane when expressed as wild-type CFTR and to exhibit the expected trafficking defect when harboring the F508del mutation, which prevents surface expression under basal conditions.

The invention further provides a method for quantifying the efficacy of pharmacological correctors—small molecules that rescue the trafficking of misfolded CFTR mutants—by measuring the fluorescence signal generated upon binding of a cell-impermeant fluorogen (e.g., MG-11p) to the FAP-tagged CFTR at the cell surface. This signal is directly proportional to the amount of CFTR present at the plasma membrane and can be measured using flow cytometry, confocal microscopy, or high-throughput plate readers. The method is highly sensitive, exhibits a large dynamic range due to the low background fluorescence of the unbound fluorogen, and correlates strongly with functional assays of CFTR activity, such as iodide efflux and short-circuit current measurements in Ussing chambers.

The invention also discloses the use of this platform to evaluate known and novel corrector compounds, including C4, CFFT-002, and C18, both individually and in combination. The data demonstrate that combinations of correctors, particularly C18 with either C4 or CFFT-002, produce synergistic or additive effects in rescuing F508del-CFTR surface expression and function, far exceeding the efficacy of single agents. Importantly, the corrector efficacy profile observed in FAP-tagged HEK293 cells closely mirrors that obtained in primary human bronchial epithelial (HBE) cells expressing endogenous F508del-CFTR, validating the physiological relevance of the platform.

Additionally, the invention provides a method for assessing the functional competence of rescued F508del-CFTR using halide-sensitive fluorescent dyes (e.g., SPQ) in conjunction with the FAP reporter, enabling simultaneous evaluation of surface abundance and channel activity. Biochemical validation via immunoblotting confirms that corrector treatment promotes the maturation of F508del-CFTR, as evidenced by the appearance of the fully glycosylated “C band,” consistent with transit through the Golgi apparatus.

Finally, the modular architecture of the FAP-CFTR platform renders it broadly applicable to other transmembrane proteins implicated in trafficking disorders. By replacing the CFTR sequence with that of another disease-relevant membrane protein, the same FAP-fluorogen detection strategy can be employed to accelerate the discovery of pharmacological chaperones for a wide range of conformational diseases.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a genetically encoded reporter system for the detection and quantification of cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) protein at the plasma membrane of living cells, with particular utility in the study and therapeutic development for cystic fibrosis (CF). The core innovation lies in the fusion of a fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) to CFTR, creating a chimeric protein that retains the biological and functional properties of native CFTR while enabling highly specific, sensitive, and quantitative detection of its surface expression through the use of cell-impermeant fluorogenic dyes.

Fluorogen-activating proteins (FAPs) are engineered single-chain variable fragments (scFvs) derived from monoclonal antibodies, selected for their ability to bind small organic molecules known as fluorogens. These fluorogens are non-fluorescent or weakly fluorescent in aqueous solution but undergo a dramatic increase in fluorescence quantum yield—often by several orders of magnitude—upon binding to their cognate FAP. This “turn-on” fluorescence mechanism provides an exceptionally low background signal, resulting in a high signal-to-noise ratio and a large dynamic range for detection. Critically, because the fluorescence is contingent upon binding, unbound fluorogen does not contribute to background, eliminating the need for extensive washing or blocking steps that are common in traditional immunofluorescence protocols.

In the context of CFTR, the invention employs two distinct fusion strategies to integrate the FAP tag into the CFTR protein while ensuring that the FAP domain is exposed to the extracellular milieu, where it can interact with cell-impermeant fluorogens. The first strategy involves the creation of an N-terminal fusion construct, herein referred to as FAP-CFTR. In this design, the FAP is positioned at the extreme N-terminus of CFTR, followed by an additional transmembrane segment derived from the platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR). This extra transmembrane helix ensures that the FAP is topologically oriented toward the extracellular space, as CFTR is a type II transmembrane protein with its N-terminus residing in the cytoplasm. Without this additional transmembrane segment, an N-terminal FAP would be sequestered intracellularly. The inclusion of the PDGFR transmembrane domain effectively reorients the N-terminus, allowing the FAP to be displayed on the cell surface.

The second strategy involves the insertion of the FAP into the fourth extracellular loop (EL4) of CFTR, generating a construct termed CFTR EL4-FAP. EL4 is the largest extracellular domain of CFTR and has been previously demonstrated to tolerate insertions of epitope tags and fluorescent proteins without abolishing channel function. Moreover, EL4 contains two conserved N-linked glycosylation sites (at asparagine residues 894 and 900), which are critical for proper protein folding and trafficking. To preserve these essential post-translational modifications, the FAP is inserted between these two glycosylation sites, specifically within the amino acid sequence KGNSTHS(FAP)RNNSY, thereby maintaining the canonical N-X-S/T glycosylation motifs. This design ensures that the FAP is presented extracellularly while minimizing interference with CFTR’s native structure and biosynthetic processing.

Both FAP-CFTR and CFTR EL4-FAP constructs, when expressed as wild-type (WT) CFTR, are efficiently trafficked to the plasma membrane, as confirmed by confocal microscopy using the cell-impermeant fluorogen MG-11p (a malachite green derivative conjugated to an 11-unit polyethylene glycol linker). MG-11p cannot cross the plasma membrane and thus only activates fluorescence when bound to FAPs exposed on the cell surface. In contrast, the cell-permeant analog MG-ester, which diffuses freely across membranes and is retained intracellularly after hydrolysis, labels both surface and intracellular pools of FAP-tagged CFTR, confirming total protein expression. When these constructs harbor the F508del mutation, they exhibit the expected trafficking defect: no surface labeling is observed with MG-11p under basal conditions, while robust intracellular labeling is detected with MG-ester, consistent with ER retention and degradation.

The functional integrity of the FAP-tagged CFTR constructs is rigorously validated using the iodide efflux assay, a standard method for assessing CFTR-mediated anion transport. In this assay, cells are loaded with the halide-sensitive fluorescent dye SPQ (6-methoxy-N-(3-sulfopropyl)quinolinium), which is quenched by intracellular iodide. Upon stimulation with forskolin (to elevate cAMP) and a CFTR potentiator (e.g., P2), functional CFTR channels mediate iodide efflux, leading to dequenching of SPQ and an increase in fluorescence. Both FAP-CFTR WT and CFTR EL4-FAP WT constructs exhibit robust iodide efflux responses comparable to untagged WT CFTR, demonstrating that the FAP fusion does not impair channel gating or conductance. Similarly, F508del-CFTR FAP constructs show no significant efflux under vehicle control but regain substantial function upon treatment with corrector compounds, confirming that rescued protein is not only present at the surface but also capable of regulated anion transport.

The invention further provides a quantitative method for evaluating corrector efficacy using flow cytometry. Stable HEK293 cell lines expressing either FAP-F508del-CFTR or F508del-CFTR EL4-FAP are treated with corrector compounds (e.g., C4, CFFT-002, C18, alone or in combination) for 24 hours. Cells are then harvested using a non-enzymatic cell dissociation reagent (e.g., Cellstripper™) to preserve surface protein integrity, incubated with MG-11p, and analyzed by flow cytometry. The mean fluorescence intensity of 10,000 cells per condition provides a direct, population-averaged measure of surface CFTR abundance. This approach reveals that C18 is a significantly more potent corrector than C4 or CFFT-002 when used alone, and that combinations of C18 with either C4 or CFFT-002 produce synergistic increases in surface expression—up to 9-fold greater than C4 alone in the N-terminal FAP construct.

Biochemical validation is performed via immunoblotting using a CFTR-specific antibody (e.g., CFFT#596). Wild-type CFTR typically migrates as two bands on SDS-PAGE: the core-glycosylated “B band” (ER-resident) and the complex-glycosylated “C band” (Golgi-processed, mature form). FAP-CFTR WT exhibits both bands, shifted upward by ~20 kDa due to the FAP tag, confirming proper maturation. In contrast, CFTR EL4-FAP WT shows less distinct banding, suggesting partial impairment of glycosylation, likely due to steric hindrance from the FAP insertion. Nevertheless, both F508del-CFTR FAP constructs show minimal C band under control conditions but acquire detectable mature forms upon corrector treatment, particularly with C18-containing combinations. This biochemical maturation correlates with surface expression and functional rescue.

Critically, the corrector efficacy profile observed in the FAP-HEK293 system is recapitulated in primary human bronchial epithelial (HBE) cells from F508del/F508del CF patients. In Ussing chamber experiments, C18 alone significantly rescues forskolin-stimulated short-circuit current, and the combination of C18 + CFFT-002 produces even greater functional correction, mirroring the trends seen in the reporter system. This cross-validation in a clinically relevant model underscores the predictive power of the FAP platform for preclinical drug development.

The invention is not limited to CFTR. The modular design—wherein a FAP is fused to an extracellular domain of a transmembrane protein—can be generalized to other trafficking-deficient membrane proteins. Examples include the hERG potassium channel (mutated in long QT syndrome), the LDL receptor (familial hypercholesterolemia), aquaporin-2 (nephrogenic diabetes insipidus), and BEST1 (vitelliform macular dystrophy). By applying the same FAP-fluorogen detection strategy, researchers can rapidly screen for correctors that restore surface expression of these disease-associated proteins, thereby accelerating therapeutic discovery across a spectrum of conformational disorders.

## EXPERIMENTAL

### EXAMPLE 1

The development and characterization of FAP-CFTR reporter constructs were carried out as follows. Two distinct fusion strategies were employed to generate chimeric CFTR proteins bearing a fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) tag in extracellular locations. First, an N-terminal fusion construct was created by inserting the FAP (dNP138, a malachite green-binding scFv) into the Sac module of the pBabeSacLac2 plasmid, followed by ligation of wild-type (WT) or F508del-CFTR into the Lac module via SfiI restriction sites. This design included an additional transmembrane segment from the platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR) to ensure extracellular orientation of the N-terminal FAP. Second, an EL4 fusion construct was generated by inserting the FAP into the fourth extracellular loop (EL4) of CFTR, between the glycosylation sites at asparagine residues 894 and 900. This was achieved using a modified CFTR plasmid containing KpnI and EcoRV sites flanking the EL4 insertion point; the FAP was amplified with compatible primers, digested, and ligated into the vector, then subcloned into a retroviral pBabe backbone for stable expression.

Stable HEK293 cell lines expressing these constructs were generated using the Phoenix Ecotropic Packaging System. Viral supernatants were produced by transfecting Phoenix-ECO cells with the respective pBabe plasmids, harvesting virus-containing medium after 48 hours, filtering, and flash-freezing. Recipient HEK293 cells were infected with viral supernatant in the presence of hexadimethrine bromide, replated, and screened for expression 48 hours post-infection. Corrector treatments were performed by adding compounds (e.g., C4, CFFT-002, C18) dissolved in DMSO to culture media at specified concentrations (typically 10 μmol/L for C4, 5 μmol/L for CFFT-002 and C18) for 24 hours prior to analysis.

Confocal microscopy was used to visualize surface and total protein expression. Cells were plated on Mattek dishes and treated with correctors or vehicle (DMSO). For surface labeling, the cell-impermeant fluorogen MG-11p (50 nmol/L) was added to live cells, and images were acquired using a Nikon Ti eclipse confocal microscope with a 640-nm laser and 700/75-nm emission filter. For total protein labeling, the cell-permeant MG-ester (50 nmol/L) was used. Representative images showed clear plasma membrane localization of FAP-CFTR WT and CFTR EL4-FAP WT with MG-11p, and predominant intracellular retention of F508del constructs under vehicle control, with rescue to the surface upon corrector treatment or low-temperature incubation (30°C).

Functional assessment was performed using the iodide efflux assay. Cells were loaded with SPQ in a hypotonic iodide solution, then perfused with nitrate buffer ± forskolin (10 μmol/L) and potentiator P2 (300 nmol/L). Fluorescence was monitored every 15 seconds using an inverted epi-fluorescence microscope. Both FAP-tagged WT constructs exhibited robust iodide efflux, while F508del constructs showed minimal response under vehicle but significant rescue with C4 + C18 corrector combination.

Flow cytometry quantification was conducted by harvesting cells with Cellstripper™, resuspending in PBS, adding MG-11p (50 nmol/L), and analyzing 10,000 events per sample on a flow cytometer equipped with a 640-nm laser and 685/35 filter. Data were normalized to the C18-treated condition and analyzed using FACS Diva software. Results demonstrated that C18 alone produced significantly higher surface expression than C4 or CFFT-002, and combinations with C18 yielded the greatest rescue.

Immunoblotting was performed on whole-cell lysates prepared in RIPA buffer. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE (3% stacking, 5% resolving gel), transferred to PVDF membranes, and probed with anti-CFTR antibody #596 (1:5,000) followed by donkey anti-mouse secondary (1:10,000). FAP-CFTR WT showed distinct B and C bands, while F508del constructs exhibited only the B band under control but acquired C band with corrector combinations, confirming biochemical maturation.

### EXAMPLE 2

The validation of the FAP-CFTR platform in a clinically relevant model was performed using primary human bronchial epithelial (HBE) cells homozygous for the F508del mutation. These cells were obtained from the Human Airway Cell Core of the University of Pittsburgh Cystic Fibrosis Research Center and cultured on collagen-coated Transwell filters until differentiation (≥3 weeks, transepithelial resistance >400 Ω/cm², presence of beating cilia). Cells were treated with correctors (5 μmol/L C18, 5 μmol/L CFFT-002, or combination) for 18–24 hours prior to functional assessment.

Ussing chamber experiments were conducted to measure transepithelial ion transport. Filters were mounted in chambers with basolateral and apical solutions gassed with 95% O₂/5% CO₂. Basolateral solution contained 120 mmol/L NaCl, 25 mmol/L NaHCO₃, 3.3 mmol/L KH₂PO₄, 0.8 mmol/L K₂HPO₄, 1.2 mmol/L CaCl₂, 1.2 mmol/L MgCl₂, and 10 mmol/L D-glucose; apical solution replaced NaCl with Na glutamate to establish a Cl⁻ gradient. Short-circuit current (I_sc) was measured continuously using a voltage-clamp. After baseline stabilization, amiloride (10 μmol/L) was added apically to inhibit ENaC, followed by forskolin (20 μmol/L) basolaterally to stimulate CFTR, then potentiator P2 (300 nmol/L) bilaterally, and finally CFTRinh-172 (10 μmol/L) apically to confirm CFTR-specific current.

Results showed that C18 alone significantly increased forskolin-stimulated I_sc compared to vehicle, and the combination of C18 + CFFT-002 produced even greater functional rescue, consistent with the trends observed in the FAP-HEK293 system. Immunoblotting of HBE lysates confirmed that the C18 + CFFT-002 combination induced the appearance of the mature C band of endogenous F508del-CFTR, whereas single agents or vehicle did not.

These data demonstrate that the FAP-CFTR reporter system accurately predicts corrector efficacy in native human airway epithelia, supporting its utility as a preclinical screening tool. The platform’s ability to quantify surface expression independently of immediate channel function, combined with its compatibility with high-throughput formats, positions it as a superior alternative to traditional assays for accelerating the development of CF therapeutics.

### Summary of Corrector Efficacy from FAP Labeling and Functional Assays

The efficacy of various corrector compounds and their combinations was systematically evaluated using the FAP-CFTR platform in HEK293 cells and validated in primary HBE cells. In HEK293 cells expressing FAP-F508del-CFTR, flow cytometry with MG-11p revealed that the reference corrector C4 (10 μmol/L) produced a modest increase in surface fluorescence (0.53-fold relative to vehicle control, normalized to C18 = 1.0), while CFFT-002 (5 μmol/L) showed similar efficacy (0.55-fold). In contrast, C18 (5 μmol/L) alone yielded a 3.5-fold improvement over C4, establishing it as the most potent single-agent corrector tested. Combination treatments dramatically enhanced rescue: C4 + C18 and CFFT-002 + C18 produced 9.3-fold and 8.6-fold increases over C4 alone, respectively, with no significant difference between the two combinations.

For the F508del-CFTR EL4-FAP construct, corrector efficacy was generally lower, reflecting the partial impairment of glycosylation and trafficking in this design. C4 and CFFT-002 alone yielded 0.51 and 0.47-fold increases, respectively, while C18 alone was again superior. Combinations C4 + C18 and CFFT-002 + C18 increased surface density to 1.29 and 1.37-fold, respectively, representing significant but less dramatic improvements than in the N-terminal construct.

Functional assays corroborated these findings. In iodide efflux experiments, only corrector combinations restored substantial anion transport in F508del-CFTR FAP-expressing HEK293 cells. In HBE cells, Ussing chamber measurements showed that C18 alone significantly rescued CFTR-mediated short-circuit current, and the C18 + CFFT-002 combination produced the greatest functional correction, aligning with the FAP-based surface expression data.

Immunoblotting confirmed that corrector combinations promoted the maturation of F508del-CFTR, as evidenced by the appearance of the fully glycosylated C band in both HEK293 and HBE cells. Notably, the C band was most prominent with C18-containing combinations, and absent in vehicle controls.

In summary, the FAP-CFTR platform provides a highly sensitive, quantitative, and physiologically relevant method for assessing corrector efficacy, with strong correlation between surface expression, biochemical maturation, and functional rescue across cell types. The data highlight C18 as a superior corrector and demonstrate the synergistic potential of combination therapies, offering a robust framework for future CF drug development.
