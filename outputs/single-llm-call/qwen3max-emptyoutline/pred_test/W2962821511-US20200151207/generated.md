# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of natural language processing (NLP) has long sought to understand the thematic structure of large-scale textual corpora, particularly those that evolve over time. Topic modeling represents a foundational approach in this endeavor, enabling the automatic discovery of latent semantic themes—commonly referred to as “topics”—that underlie collections of documents. Traditional static topic models, such as Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), have proven effective in uncovering the thematic composition of document sets by assuming that each document is a probabilistic mixture of topics and each topic is a distribution over words. However, these models are inherently limited in their ability to capture the dynamic nature of real-world text corpora, where topics emerge, fade, merge, split, or otherwise transform in response to evolving societal, scientific, or technological contexts. This limitation becomes especially pronounced when analyzing longitudinal datasets—such as decades of scientific publications, news archives, or social media streams—where temporal progression is not merely a metadata attribute but a core structural feature influencing semantic content.

To address this gap, researchers have developed dynamic topic models (DTMs) that extend static frameworks to incorporate temporal dependencies. The seminal work by Blei and Lafferty introduced a dynamic extension of LDA that models topics as evolving Gaussian distributions in a continuous latent space, with each year’s topic parameters derived from the previous year’s via a linear Gaussian transition. While this approach successfully captures smooth topic evolution, it suffers from several critical constraints. First, it assumes a fixed number of global topics that persist across all time steps, thereby precluding the emergence of genuinely novel topics that were absent in earlier periods. Second, because the model operates on the natural parameters of the multinomial word distributions rather than directly on the latent topic representations, inference becomes analytically intractable, necessitating complex variational approximations that may obscure fine-grained temporal dynamics. Third, the model does not explicitly track the popularity or salience of individual topics over time, nor does it provide a mechanism to analyze how specific words within a topic gain or lose prominence—a crucial aspect of topical trend analysis.

Concurrently, advances in neural network architectures have demonstrated remarkable success in modeling sequential data, particularly through recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their variants. These models excel at capturing long-range dependencies in time series by maintaining an internal state that accumulates information from past inputs. In parallel, energy-based models such as Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) and their extensions—including the Replicated Softmax (RSM) model—have shown promise in unsupervised representation learning for text. RSM, in particular, treats documents as binary count vectors and models their joint distribution with latent binary topic variables using an undirected graphical model, offering advantages in log-likelihood estimation and retrieval performance compared to LDA. However, standard RSM remains a static model, incapable of leveraging temporal ordering in document sequences.

Recent efforts have begun to bridge neural architectures with probabilistic topic models. For instance, the Recurrent Temporal RBM (RTRBM) and RNN-RBM frameworks have been applied to symbolic sequences like polyphonic music, where temporal dependencies among notes are modeled through recurrent connections that modulate the biases of visible and hidden units in an RBM over time. These models demonstrate that coupling deterministic recurrent dynamics with stochastic latent representations can effectively capture complex temporal structures. Yet, their application to natural language—and specifically to the problem of dynamic topic modeling in document collections—remains largely unexplored. Moreover, existing approaches often fail to reconcile the heteroscedastic nature of document collections, where both the size of individual documents and the overall volume of text can vary significantly across time steps, thereby complicating the modeling of consistent topic representations.

The need for a robust, flexible, and interpretable dynamic topic model is further underscored by practical applications in digital humanities, scientific trend analysis, intelligence monitoring, and business intelligence. Analysts require not only accurate topic detection but also the ability to trace the lifecycle of ideas—identifying when a new research direction emerges, how its vocabulary evolves, and whether it eventually supersedes or merges with existing paradigms. Current models either oversimplify these dynamics (by enforcing topic persistence) or lack the expressive power to model abrupt shifts or localized innovations. Furthermore, evaluation metrics for dynamic topic models remain fragmented: while perplexity and coherence assess generalization and interpretability, they do not fully capture the fidelity of temporal evolution or the precision of keyword-level trend tracking. There is thus a pressing need for a unified framework that integrates the representational power of neural networks with the probabilistic rigor of topic models, explicitly designed to handle variable-sized documents, support the emergence of new topics, and enable fine-grained analysis of word-level usage trends over time.

This background establishes the technical and practical motivations for the present invention: a novel neural dynamic topic model that overcomes the limitations of prior art by combining the strengths of recurrent neural networks and replicated softmax models within a generative framework that explicitly models temporal dependencies among latent topics. The resulting system provides a comprehensive solution to the triad of tasks in dynamic topic analysis—topic structure detection, topic evolution detection, and temporal topic characterization—while offering improved generalization, interpretability, and temporal fidelity compared to existing approaches.

## SUMMARY AND DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a neural dynamic topic modeling system and method, herein referred to as RNN-RSM (Recurrent Neural Network–Replicated Softmax Model), designed to analyze time-sequenced collections of documents and generate temporally coherent topical trends and automatic overview timelines. The system uniquely integrates an undirected stochastic graphical model—specifically, the Replicated Softmax (RSM) model—with a deterministic recurrent neural network (RNN) to explicitly model the temporal dependencies among latent topics across successive time steps. This architecture enables the system to perform three interrelated tasks essential to dynamic topic analysis: (1) Topic Structure Detection (TSD), which involves identifying the primary semantic themes present in a document collection at each time step; (2) Topic Evolution Detection (TED), which entails recognizing the emergence, growth, decay, or transformation of topics over time; and (3) Temporal Topic Characterization (TTC), which focuses on tracking the usage patterns of specific words within each topic across the temporal sequence, thereby enabling fine-grained analysis of keyword trends.

At the core of the invention is a generative probabilistic model defined over a sequence of document collections indexed by discrete time steps \( t = 1, 2, \dots, T \). At each time step \( t \), the observed data consist of a collection of \( N^{(t)} \) documents, each represented as a binary count vector \( \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)} \in \{0,1\}^{K \times D_n^{(t)}} \), where \( K \) is the size of the global vocabulary and \( D_n^{(t)} \) is the length of the \( n \)-th document. The model introduces two types of latent variables: a stochastic binary hidden topic vector \( \mathbf{h}^{(t)} \in \{0,1\}^F \) for each document, where \( F \) is the number of latent topics, and a deterministic RNN hidden state \( \mathbf{u}^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^H \) that encodes the temporal history of the sequence up to time \( t \). The key innovation lies in the conditional dependence structure: the biases of the RSM at time \( t \)—which govern the activation probabilities of both visible (word) and hidden (topic) units—are modulated by the RNN hidden state \( \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)} \) from the previous time step. Specifically, the visible bias \( \mathbf{b}_v^{(t)} \) and hidden bias \( \mathbf{b}_h^{(t)} \) are computed as affine transformations of \( \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)} \), i.e., \( \mathbf{b}_v^{(t)} = \mathbf{W}_{uv} \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)} + \mathbf{b}_v \) and \( \mathbf{b}_h^{(t)} = \mathbf{W}_{uh} \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)} + \mathbf{b}_h \), where \( \mathbf{W}_{uv} \) and \( \mathbf{W}_{uh} \) are trainable weight matrices and \( \mathbf{b}_v, \mathbf{b}_h \) are base biases. The RNN state itself evolves according to \( \mathbf{u}^{(t)} = \tanh(\mathbf{W}_{uu} \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)} + \mathbf{W}_{vu} \bar{\mathbf{v}}^{(t)} + \mathbf{b}_u) \), where \( \bar{\mathbf{v}}^{(t)} \) is the aggregated document representation at time \( t \) (e.g., the sum of word counts across all documents), and \( \mathbf{W}_{uu}, \mathbf{W}_{vu} \) are additional weight matrices.

This design decouples the roles of the two hidden layers: the stochastic \( \mathbf{h}^{(t)} \) captures the instantaneous topic structure of the current document collection, while the deterministic \( \mathbf{u}^{(t)} \) propagates temporal context forward, allowing the model to condition current topic distributions on the entire history of past topics. Crucially, because the RSM biases are time-dependent and influenced by the RNN, the model can dynamically activate or suppress specific topics and words in response to historical trends, thereby supporting the emergence of new topics without requiring a predefined set of persistent global topics. The joint probability distribution over the entire sequence is factorized as \( P(\mathbf{V}, \mathbf{H}) = \prod_{t=1}^T P(\mathbf{V}^{(t)} | \mathbf{h}^{(t)}) P(\mathbf{h}^{(t)} | \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)}) P(\mathbf{u}^{(t)} | \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)}, \mathbf{V}^{(t)}) \), where \( \mathbf{V} = [\mathbf{V}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{V}^{(T)}] \) and \( \mathbf{H} = [\mathbf{h}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{h}^{(T)}] \).

The model is trained using a hybrid optimization procedure that combines Contrastive Divergence (CD) for the RSM component with Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) for the RNN. At each time step, the gradient of the negative log-likelihood with respect to the RSM parameters (weights and time-dependent biases) is approximated using \( k \)-step CD, generating negative samples via Gibbs sampling. These gradients are then back-propagated through the RNN connections to update the weights \( \mathbf{W}_{uv}, \mathbf{W}_{uh}, \mathbf{W}_{vu}, \mathbf{W}_{uu} \) and biases \( \mathbf{b}_u, \mathbf{b}_v, \mathbf{b}_h \). This end-to-end training allows the model to learn both the static topic-word associations and the temporal dynamics governing their evolution.

In operation, the system processes a time-stamped document corpus by first preprocessing each document into a bag-of-words or n-gram representation, constructing a global vocabulary, and aggregating documents by time step (e.g., by year). The RNN-RSM model is then trained on this structured input. Post-training, the system performs TSD by computing the posterior probability \( P(\mathbf{v}^{(t)} | h_j^{(t)} = 1) \) for each topic unit \( j \), extracting the top-\( m \) words with highest probability as the topic signature. For TED, the system computes topic popularity trajectories by measuring the cosine similarity between discovered topic vectors and predefined key-term sets representing known research areas, revealing how these areas gain or lose prominence. Additionally, topic drift is quantified by comparing topic term sets across distant time steps. For TTC, the system generates keyword-trend sequences indicating the presence or absence of each word in the top topic terms at each time step, and computes the SPAN metric—the length of the longest contiguous interval of presence—as a measure of a keyword’s sustained relevance.

The invention offers significant advantages over prior art. Unlike DTM, it does not assume fixed global topics, enabling the detection of locally emerging themes. Its use of RSM provides better log-likelihood generalization and retrieval performance compared to LDA-based models. The explicit modeling of temporal dependencies through RNN-modulated biases allows for more accurate tracking of word-level usage trends. Evaluation on a 19-year corpus of NLP research papers demonstrates superior performance in perplexity, time-stamp prediction accuracy, topic coherence, and fidelity of topic evolution and keyword trends. The system thus provides a powerful, unified framework for generating interpretable, temporally grounded overviews of evolving thematic landscapes in longitudinal text corpora.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a computer-implemented method and system for dynamic topic modeling of time-sequenced document collections, which generates temporally coherent topical trends and automatic overview timelines by integrating a recurrent neural network (RNN) with a replicated softmax (RSM) model. The following detailed description sets forth the architecture, mathematical formulation, training procedure, and operational workflow of the RNN-RSM system, along with illustrative examples of its application and advantages over existing approaches.

The system operates on a corpus of documents that are naturally ordered in time, such as annual conference proceedings, monthly news archives, or daily social media feeds. Each document is first preprocessed to extract its lexical content, typically by tokenizing text, removing stop words, and applying stemming or lemmatization. The system then constructs a global vocabulary \( \mathcal{V} = \{w_1, w_2, \dots, w_K\} \) of size \( K \), which may include unigrams, bigrams, or other n-grams deemed relevant to the domain. Documents are aggregated into discrete time steps \( t = 1, 2, \dots, T \), where each time step \( t \) corresponds to a collection \( \mathcal{D}^{(t)} = \{d_1^{(t)}, d_2^{(t)}, \dots, d_{N^{(t)}}^{(t)}\} \) of \( N^{(t)} \) documents. Each document \( d_n^{(t)} \) is represented as a binary count matrix \( \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)} \in \{0,1\}^{K \times D_n^{(t)}} \), where \( D_n^{(t)} \) is the number of words in the document, and each column corresponds to a word occurrence encoded as a one-hot vector over the vocabulary. This representation aligns with the RSM formalism, which treats documents as replicated binary observations.

The core of the RNN-RSM model is a joint probabilistic distribution over the observed document sequences \( \mathbf{V} = [\mathbf{V}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{V}^{(T)}] \) and latent variables \( \mathbf{H} = [\mathbf{h}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{h}^{(T)}] \) and \( \mathbf{U} = [\mathbf{u}^{(0)}, \dots, \mathbf{u}^{(T)}] \), where \( \mathbf{h}^{(t)} \in \{0,1\}^F \) is a stochastic binary hidden topic vector for time step \( t \), and \( \mathbf{u}^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^H \) is a deterministic RNN hidden state. The joint distribution is defined as:

\[
P(\mathbf{V}, \mathbf{H}, \mathbf{U}) = P(\mathbf{u}^{(0)}) \prod_{t=1}^T P(\mathbf{V}^{(t)} | \mathbf{h}^{(t)}) P(\mathbf{h}^{(t)} | \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)}) P(\mathbf{u}^{(t)} | \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)}, \mathbf{V}^{(t)})
\]

Here, \( P(\mathbf{V}^{(t)} | \mathbf{h}^{(t)}) \) and \( P(\mathbf{h}^{(t)} | \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)}) \) are governed by the RSM energy function at time \( t \), while \( P(\mathbf{u}^{(t)} | \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)}, \mathbf{V}^{(t)}) \) is a deterministic RNN transition. The RSM energy for a single document \( \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)} \) and its hidden state \( \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)} \) is given by:

\[
E(\mathbf{v}_n^{(t)}, \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)}) = - (\mathbf{b}_v^{(t)})^\top \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)} - (\mathbf{b}_h^{(t)})^\top \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)} - (\mathbf{v}_n^{(t)})^\top \mathbf{W}_{vh} \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)}
\]

where \( \mathbf{W}_{vh} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times F} \) is a symmetric weight matrix shared across all time steps, and the biases \( \mathbf{b}_v^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^K \) and \( \mathbf{b}_h^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^F \) are time-dependent, computed as:

\[
\mathbf{b}_v^{(t)} = \mathbf{W}_{uv} \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)} + \mathbf{b}_v, \quad \mathbf{b}_h^{(t)} = \mathbf{W}_{uh} \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)} + \mathbf{b}_h
\]

The RNN state evolves according to:

\[
\mathbf{u}^{(t)} = \tanh\left( \mathbf{W}_{uu} \mathbf{u}^{(t-1)} + \mathbf{W}_{vu} \bar{\mathbf{v}}^{(t)} + \mathbf{b}_u \right)
\]

where \( \bar{\mathbf{v}}^{(t)} = \sum_{n=1}^{N^{(t)}} \sum_{i=1}^{D_n^{(t)}} \mathbf{v}_{n,i}^{(t)} \) is the total word count vector at time \( t \), and \( \mathbf{W}_{uv} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times H} \), \( \mathbf{W}_{uh} \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times H} \), \( \mathbf{W}_{vu} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times K} \), \( \mathbf{W}_{uu} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times H} \) are trainable weight matrices, with \( \mathbf{b}_v, \mathbf{b}_h, \mathbf{b}_u \) being bias vectors. The initial state \( \mathbf{u}^{(0)} \) is treated as a learnable parameter.

The conditional probabilities for the RSM units are derived from the energy function. For the \( j \)-th hidden unit in document \( n \) at time \( t \):

\[
P(h_{n,j}^{(t)} = 1 | \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)}) = \sigma\left( b_{h,j}^{(t)} + (\mathbf{W}_{vh}^{(:,j)})^\top \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)} \right)
\]

where \( \sigma(\cdot) \) is the logistic function. For the visible units, since the model uses replicated softmax, the probability of observing word \( k \) in position \( i \) of document \( n \) is:

\[
P(v_{n,i}^{(t)} = k | \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)}) = \frac{\exp\left( b_{v,k}^{(t)} + \mathbf{W}_{vh}^{(k,:)} \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)} \right)}{\sum_{k'=1}^K \exp\left( b_{v,k'}^{(t)} + \mathbf{W}_{vh}^{(k',:)} \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)} \right)}
\]

Training the model involves maximizing the log-likelihood of the observed data \( \log P(\mathbf{V}) \). Due to the intractability of the partition function, the gradient is approximated using Contrastive Divergence (CD). For each time step \( t \), the gradient of the negative log-likelihood with respect to the RSM parameters is:

\[
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_{vh}} \mathcal{L}^{(t)} \approx \langle \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)} (\mathbf{h}_n^{(t)})^\top \rangle_{\text{data}} - \langle \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)*} (\mathbf{h}_n^{(t)*})^\top \rangle_{\text{recon}}
\]

\[
\nabla_{\mathbf{b}_v^{(t)}} \mathcal{L}^{(t)} \approx \langle \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)} \rangle_{\text{data}} - \langle \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)*} \rangle_{\text{recon}}, \quad \nabla_{\mathbf{b}_h^{(t)}} \mathcal{L}^{(t)} \approx \langle \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)} \rangle_{\text{data}} - \langle \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)*} \rangle_{\text{recon}}
\]

where \( \langle \cdot \rangle_{\text{data}} \) denotes the expectation under the data distribution, and \( \langle \cdot \rangle_{\text{recon}} \) is estimated using \( k \)-step Gibbs sampling to generate negative samples \( \mathbf{v}_n^{(t)*}, \mathbf{h}_n^{(t)*} \).
