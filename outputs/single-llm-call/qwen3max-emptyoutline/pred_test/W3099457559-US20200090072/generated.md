# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of quantum computing and, more specifically, to quantum algorithms for solving discrete optimization problems. In particular, the invention provides novel methods and systems for implementing quantum walks derived from classical Metropolis-Hastings Markov chains without requiring the explicit construction of a walk oracle unitary, thereby enabling efficient quantum circuit implementations with reduced computational overhead. The invention further encompasses heuristic quantum algorithms based on adiabatic state preparation and unitary evolution for accelerating the search for low-energy configurations in Ising spin models, including both one-dimensional and sparse random graph topologies. The disclosed techniques are applicable to a wide range of computational tasks in statistical physics, machine learning, and combinatorial optimization where sampling from or minimizing energy functions over discrete configuration spaces is required.

## SUMMARY

The invention provides a method for implementing a quantum walk operator corresponding to a classical Metropolis-Hastings Markov chain without explicitly constructing the traditional walk oracle unitary. This is achieved by decomposing the quantum walk into a sequence of four fundamental operations—move preparation, spin flip, reflection, and Boltzmann coin—that act directly on system, move, and coin registers encoded in a unary representation. The move preparation operation generates a uniform superposition over possible spin-flip moves using a binary tree of √SWAP gates, which avoids the need for costly arbitrary rotations when the number of moves is not a power of two by padding the move set with trivial transitions. The spin flip operation conditionally applies bit flips to the system register based on the selected move and a coin qubit, implemented either sequentially with Toffoli gates or in parallel using additional scratchpad qubits to reduce circuit depth. The reflection operation implements a phase inversion about the initial state using a binary tree of Toffoli gates to compute whether all move and coin registers are in their initial states, achieving logarithmic depth in the number of moves. The Boltzmann coin operation applies conditional rotations to the coin qubit based on the energy difference between current and proposed configurations, leveraging the sparsity of the energy function to limit the number of system qubits involved in each rotation, and optionally employing quantum signal processing techniques to reduce the number of required T gates.

The invention further provides heuristic quantum algorithms for solving discrete optimization problems by preparing low-energy states of Ising models through either a Zeno-based adiabatic protocol with rewind capability or a purely unitary evolution approach. The Zeno protocol prepares the coherent stationary distribution of a target Metropolis-Hastings walk by interpolating between an easily preparable initial distribution and the target distribution through a sequence of intermediate walks, with projective measurements at each step to maintain the system in the instantaneous ground state, and incorporating a rewind mechanism that allows recovery from measurement failures without restarting from the beginning. The unitary implementation applies the sequence of quantum walk operators without intermediate measurements, relying on the inherent dynamics of the quantum system to evolve toward low-energy configurations. Both approaches demonstrate polynomial speedups over classical Metropolis-Hastings walks in numerical simulations, with the unitary implementation showing superior performance despite lacking the formal guarantees of the measurement-based approach.

The invention also discloses a parallelized classical walk that proposes simultaneous spin flips across all lattice sites with a tunable probability, which while not reversible and thus not directly amenable to Szegedy quantization, demonstrates improved classical performance and suggests a pathway toward more efficient quantum implementations if generalized quantization methods for irreversible walks can be developed. The overall architecture enables practical quantum advantage for discrete optimization problems by reducing the circuit complexity of quantum walk implementations and providing heuristic algorithms that can potentially achieve superquadratic speedups on certain problem classes, making them viable candidates for near-term quantum computing applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### I. Introduction

Quantum computing holds the promise of solving certain computational problems exponentially faster than classical computers, with applications ranging from cryptography to materials science. Among the most promising quantum algorithms for near-term applications are those based on quantum walks, which provide a natural framework for quantizing classical random walks and have been shown to offer quadratic speedups for various search and sampling problems. However, the practical implementation of quantum walks on real quantum hardware has been hindered by the high resource requirements of traditional approaches, particularly the need to implement complex oracle unitaries that encode the transition probabilities of the underlying classical walk.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel circuit implementation of quantum walks derived from Metropolis-Hastings Markov chains that circumvents the need for explicit walk oracle construction. Instead of implementing the traditional walk unitary W that maps |x⟩|0⟩ to |x⟩|w_x⟩ where |w_x⟩ encodes the transition probabilities from state x, the invention decomposes the quantum walk operator into a sequence of simpler operations that can be implemented more efficiently using the specific structure of Metropolis-Hastings transitions. This approach leverages the fact that Metropolis-Hastings walks typically involve sparse transitions (such as single-spin flips in Ising models) and that the acceptance probabilities depend only on local energy differences, allowing for significant simplifications in the quantum circuit implementation.

The invention further extends beyond mere circuit optimization to provide novel heuristic algorithms for solving discrete optimization problems. By recognizing that the primary goal in many practical applications is not to sample exactly from the Boltzmann distribution but rather to find low-energy configurations efficiently, the invention proposes two alternative approaches: a Zeno-based adiabatic protocol with rewind capability and a purely unitary evolution method. These heuristics are designed to exploit the quantum coherence of the system to accelerate the search for optimal solutions, and numerical simulations demonstrate that they can achieve polynomial speedups over classical methods, with some instances showing superquadratic improvements.

The detailed description that follows provides a comprehensive account of the technical innovations disclosed in the invention, including the specific circuit implementations of the quantum walk components, the theoretical analysis of their complexity, the design of the heuristic algorithms, and the numerical evidence supporting their effectiveness. The description is organized to first establish the mathematical foundations of the approach, then detail the circuit implementations, followed by the heuristic algorithms and their performance characteristics, and finally discuss the broader implications and potential extensions of the work.

### II. General Considerations

The foundation of the present invention lies in the quantization of classical Markov chains using Szegedy's quantum walk formalism, which provides a systematic way to construct quantum operators that preserve the spectral properties of the original classical walk while potentially offering quantum speedups. For a classical walk defined by a transition matrix W that is both irreducible and reversible (satisfying detailed balance with respect to some stationary distribution π), Szegedy's construction defines a quantum walk operator U_W whose eigenvalues are related to those of W through the transformation λ_k = cos(θ_k), where λ_k are the eigenvalues of W and e^(±iθ_k) are the corresponding eigenvalues of U_W.

The key insight that enables the practical implementation described in this invention is that while the traditional formulation of Szegedy's walk requires the implementation of a unitary W that maps |x⟩|0⟩ to |x⟩|w_x⟩, where |w_x⟩ = Σ_y √W_yx |y⟩, this unitary is not actually necessary for implementing the full quantum walk operator U_W. Instead, the invention recognizes that U_W can be constructed directly from the physical operations that would be performed in the classical Metropolis-Hastings algorithm, namely proposing a move, computing the acceptance probability, and conditionally applying the move.

This observation is particularly powerful in the context of Ising models, where the configuration space consists of n spins taking values in {+1, -1}, and the energy function E(x) is typically local, meaning that it depends only on interactions between small subsets of spins. In such cases, the Metropolis-Hastings walk usually proposes moves that flip a small, constant-bounded number of spins, and the acceptance probability depends only on the energy difference between the current and proposed configurations, which can be computed from a small subset of the system qubits due to the locality of the energy function.

The invention exploits this structure by encoding the move selection in a unary representation, where each possible move corresponds to a single qubit in the move register being set to 1 while all others are 0. This encoding, while requiring more qubits than a binary representation (N qubits instead of log N for N possible moves), enables much simpler circuit implementations because operations can be conditioned directly on individual move qubits without requiring complex decoding logic. The unary encoding is particularly advantageous for the move preparation step, where a uniform superposition over moves can be created using a binary tree of √SWAP gates, which are in the third level of the Clifford hierarchy and can be implemented with a constant number of T gates.

Another critical consideration in the invention is the handling of cases where the number of moves N is not a power of two. Traditional approaches would require arbitrary rotations to create the exact uniform superposition, which would be costly in terms of T gate count due to the need for gate synthesis. The invention circumvents this problem by padding the move set with additional trivial moves (that leave the system unchanged) to reach the next power of two, which introduces only a constant factor overhead in the walk dynamics while avoiding the expensive rotations entirely.

The reflection operation, which implements the phase inversion about the initial state |0⟩_M|0⟩_C, is another area where the invention provides significant improvements over naive implementations. A straightforward approach would use a single (N+1)-fold controlled NOT gate, which would have linear depth in N. Instead, the invention uses a binary tree structure that computes whether all move and coin registers are in their initial states using O(N) ancillary qubits and achieves logarithmic depth in N, which is crucial for maintaining the overall efficiency of the quantum walk implementation.

For the Boltzmann coin operation, which applies conditional rotations based on the energy difference, the invention leverages the sparsity of the energy function to limit the number of system qubits that need to be examined for each move. Since each move affects only a constant-bounded number of spins, and the energy function involves only local interactions, the energy difference for a given move can be computed from a constant-bounded subset of the system qubits. This means that each conditional rotation in the Boltzmann coin can be implemented with a constant number of Toffoli gates and T gates, leading to an overall complexity that scales linearly with the number of moves rather than exponentially with the system size.

The invention also considers alternative implementations of the Boltzmann coin using quantum signal processing techniques, which can potentially reduce the number of required T gates by expressing the desired rotation as a polynomial function of simpler unitaries that can be implemented more efficiently. This approach is particularly beneficial when the energy function involves many-body interactions or when high precision is required, as it can provide better scaling with the desired accuracy parameter.

### III. Example Embodiments

A first example embodiment of the invention provides a complete quantum circuit implementation for a quantum walk derived from a Metropolis-Hastings Markov chain on an Ising model with (k,d)-local interactions. The system consists of three quantum registers: a System register containing n qubits representing the Ising spin configuration, a Move register containing N qubits in unary encoding representing the possible moves, and a Coin register containing a single qubit used for the acceptance decision. The quantum walk operator is implemented as the composition U_W = R F B V, where each component is defined as follows.

The move preparation operator V creates a uniform superposition over all possible moves in the Move register. When N is a power of two, this is accomplished by starting with the state |00...01⟩_M and applying a binary tree of √SWAP gates. Each √SWAP gate acts on two qubits and creates entanglement between them, with the tree structure ensuring that after log₂ N layers of gates, all N qubits are in an equal superposition. When N is not a power of two, the invention pads the move set with (2^⌈log₂ N⌉ - N) trivial moves that correspond to no change in the system state, allowing the same binary tree construction to be used while introducing only a constant factor overhead in the walk dynamics.

The spin flip operator F conditionally applies the selected move to the System register based on the state of the Move register and the Coin register. For each move j that flips a set of spins z_j, F applies a Toffoli gate with controls on the j-th Move qubit and the Coin qubit, and targets on the System qubits corresponding to z_j. Since each move flips only a constant-bounded number of spins (by the sparsity assumption), each Toffoli gate has a constant number of targets, and the total number of Toffoli gates is O(N). To reduce circuit depth, an alternative implementation uses O(N) additional scratchpad qubits to create multiple copies of the Coin qubit in parallel, allowing all Toffoli gates to be applied simultaneously. This parallel implementation is particularly effective when the moves consist of single-spin flips, as it can achieve logarithmic depth in N using a binary tree of CNOT gates to distribute the Coin qubit state.

The reflection operator R implements a phase inversion about the state |00...0⟩_M|0⟩_C. Using standard phase kickback techniques, this is accomplished by introducing an ancillary qubit initialized to |−⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2 and applying a multi-controlled NOT gate that flips the ancillary qubit if and only if all Move and Coin qubits are in their initial states. The invention implements this multi-controlled NOT gate using a binary tree structure: pairs of qubits are combined using Toffoli gates with fresh ancillary qubits as targets, creating a hierarchy of ancillary qubits that indicate whether larger groups of qubits are in their initial states. After log₂(N+1) layers, a single ancillary qubit indicates whether all N+1 qubits are in their initial states, and this qubit is used to control the final NOT gate on the phase kickback ancillary. The ancillary qubits are then uncomputed in reverse order, resulting in a circuit with O(N) Toffoli gates and depth O(log N).

The Boltzmann coin operator B applies conditional rotations to the Coin register based on the energy difference between the current and proposed configurations. For each move j, B applies a rotation R_j(θ_j(x)) to the Coin qubit, where θ_j(x) is determined by the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability A_j(x) = min(1, e^(−βΔE_j(x))) through the relation cos(2θ_j(x)) = 1 − 2A_j(x). Due to the locality of the energy function and the sparsity of the moves, ΔE_j(x) depends only on a constant-bounded subset N_j of the System qubits, allowing each R_j to be implemented as a multi-controlled rotation with O(|N_j|) controls. The invention provides two implementation strategies: a sequential approach that applies the rotations one after another, requiring O(N log(1/ε)) T gates for accuracy ε, and a parallel approach that uses additional qubits to apply non-overlapping rotations simultaneously, reducing the circuit depth to a constant at the expense of O(N) additional qubits.

A second example embodiment of the invention provides a heuristic quantum algorithm for solving discrete optimization problems based on the Zeno effect with rewind capability. The algorithm begins with a sequence of Metropolis-Hastings walks W_0, W_1, ..., W_L = W, where W_j corresponds to inverse temperature β_j = jβ/L, ensuring that W_0 has a uniform stationary distribution that is easy to prepare. The algorithm starts in the coherent stationary distribution |π_0⟩ of W_0 and performs a sequence of projective measurements {Q_j, Q_j^⊥} where Q_j = |π_j⟩⟨π_j|. Each measurement is implemented using quantum phase estimation on the quantum walk operator U_{W_j} with sufficient precision to resolve the eigenvalue 1 from the rest of the spectrum, requiring O(1/δ_j) applications of U_{W_j} where δ_j is the spectral gap of U_{W_j}.

When a measurement yields the outcome Q_j^⊥, indicating that the system is not in |π_j⟩, the rewind mechanism allows the algorithm to recover by alternating between measurements of Q_{j-1} and Q_j until the desired outcome Q_j is obtained, rather than restarting from the beginning. This significantly reduces the expected number of quantum walk applications compared to the standard Zeno protocol, especially when the overlap between consecutive stationary distributions is high. The total time to solution is minimized by optimizing over the number of interpolation steps L, balancing the cost of each measurement against the success probability of the entire sequence.

A third example embodiment provides a purely unitary heuristic algorithm that applies the sequence of quantum walk operators U_{W_0}, U_{W_1}, ..., U_{W_L} without intermediate measurements. Starting from |π_0⟩, the algorithm evolves the state to |ψ(L)⟩ = U_{W_L}...U_{W_0}|π_0⟩ and then measures in the computational basis to obtain a candidate solution. While this approach lacks the formal guarantees of the measurement-based protocols, numerical simulations show that it often performs better in practice, possibly because the coherent evolution preserves quantum correlations that help escape local minima more effectively than the stochastic jumps induced by measurements. The total time to solution is again optimized over L, and the algorithm can be combined with the rewind mechanism by measuring the final state and repeating the entire sequence if the result is unsatisfactory.

### IV. Example Computing Environments

The invention can be implemented in various quantum computing environments, including superconducting qubit systems, trapped ion systems, photonic quantum computers, and topological quantum computers. Each platform has different characteristics that affect the practical implementation of the disclosed algorithms, particularly regarding gate fidelities, coherence times, connectivity constraints, and the availability of specific gate types.

In superconducting qubit systems, such as those developed by IBM, Google, and Rigetti, the primary considerations are the limited coherence times and the restricted connectivity between qubits. The invention's emphasis on reducing circuit depth through parallel implementations and binary tree structures is particularly beneficial in this context, as it helps minimize the impact of decoherence. The use of √SWAP gates for move preparation is well-suited to superconducting platforms, as these gates can be implemented natively or with high fidelity using microwave pulses. The requirement for O(N) additional qubits in the parallel implementations may be challenging for near-term devices with limited qubit counts, but the sequential alternatives provide viable options for smaller problem instances.

Trapped ion systems, such as those developed by IonQ and Honeywell, offer higher gate fidelities and all-to-all connectivity, which can simplify the implementation of multi-qubit gates like the Toffoli gates used in the spin flip and reflection operations. The longer coherence times in trapped ion systems make them well-suited for the more complex circuits required by the Boltzmann coin operations, particularly when high precision is needed for the conditional rotations. The ability to perform global operations on all qubits simultaneously can also be leveraged to implement the parallel versions of the algorithms more efficiently.

Photonic quantum computers, such as those based on linear optical elements, have different strengths and weaknesses. While they offer excellent coherence properties and can operate at room temperature, the probabilistic nature of photon-photon interactions makes deterministic two-qubit gates challenging to implement. However, the invention's algorithms can be adapted to measurement-based quantum computing models, where the quantum walk operations are implemented through sequences of measurements on a pre-prepared entangled state. The unary encoding of the move register may be particularly advantageous in photonic systems, as it can be implemented using path encoding where each possible move corresponds to a different optical path.

Topological quantum computers, while still largely theoretical, would provide the most robust implementation environment due to their inherent fault tolerance. The invention's careful accounting of T gate requirements and circuit depth would be directly relevant to the resource estimates for topological implementations, as the number of T gates often determines the overall complexity in these models. The use of quantum signal processing techniques for the Boltzmann coin could be particularly beneficial in topological systems, as they can provide more efficient implementations of the required rotations.

Classical computing resources are also required to support the quantum algorithms, particularly for compiling the quantum circuits, optimizing the parameters (such as the number of interpolation steps L), and post-processing the measurement results. Hybrid quantum-classical computing environments, where classical processors handle the compilation and optimization tasks while quantum processors execute the core algorithms, represent the most practical implementation scenario for the foreseeable future. The invention's modular design, with clearly defined components that can be optimized independently, facilitates this hybrid approach by allowing classical compilers to optimize each component separately before combining them into the full quantum walk operator.

### V. Further Example Embodiments

Additional embodiments of the invention extend the basic framework to handle more complex scenarios and provide enhanced functionality. One such embodiment addresses the case of non-uniform move distributions, where the probability of selecting different moves is not equal. In this case, the move preparation operator V must create a non-uniform superposition over the move register. The invention adapts the method of Grover and Rudolph for preparing arbitrary quantum states, but optimizes it for the specific case of sparse distributions that arise in practical Metropolis-Hastings implementations. By leveraging the sparsity of the move distribution (where only O(n) moves have non-zero probability), the invention reduces the number of required rotations and achieves a complexity that scales linearly with the number of non-zero probabilities rather than exponentially with the system size.

Another embodiment provides a method for handling time-dependent energy functions, where the energy landscape changes during the execution of the algorithm. This is particularly relevant for applications in machine learning, where the energy function might represent a loss function that is being optimized through gradient descent. The invention modifies the Boltzmann coin operator to account for the time dependence by incorporating additional control qubits that track the current time step, and updates the energy difference calculations accordingly. The quantum walk operators are then applied in a time-ordered sequence that respects the changing energy landscape, while maintaining the overall structure of the algorithm.

A third embodiment extends the invention to handle continuous variables by discretizing the configuration space and applying the quantum walk framework to the discretized version. This is useful for applications in quantum chemistry and materials science, where the configuration space might represent molecular geometries or electron densities. The invention provides methods for choosing appropriate discretization schemes that preserve the essential features of the energy landscape while keeping the number of discrete states manageable. The sparsity assumptions are adapted to the discretized setting by considering only local moves in the discretized space, and the unary encoding is modified to handle the larger number of possible moves that might arise in continuous variable settings.

Yet another embodiment provides a fault-tolerant implementation of the invention's algorithms, incorporating quantum error correction codes to protect against decoherence and gate errors. The invention analyzes the error propagation through the quantum walk circuit and provides guidelines for choosing appropriate error correction codes and fault-tolerant gate implementations. Particular attention is paid to the T gate requirements, as these are often the most expensive operations in fault-tolerant quantum computing. The invention's emphasis on minimizing T gate count through efficient circuit designs and the use of quantum signal processing techniques is particularly valuable in this context, as it directly reduces the overhead required for fault tolerance.

A further embodiment integrates the invention's quantum walk algorithms with classical optimization techniques to create hybrid quantum-classical optimization algorithms. In this approach, the quantum walk is used to explore the configuration space and identify promising regions, while classical optimization methods are used to refine the solutions found by the quantum algorithm. The invention provides methods for interfacing between the quantum and classical components, including techniques for extracting classical information from quantum measurements and using it to guide the classical optimization process. This hybrid approach can leverage the strengths of both quantum and classical computing to solve optimization problems more effectively than either approach alone.

### VI. Concluding Remarks

The present invention represents a significant advancement in the practical implementation of quantum walks for discrete optimization problems. By circumventing the need for explicit walk oracle construction and providing efficient circuit implementations that leverage the specific structure of Metropolis-Hastings transitions, the invention enables quantum walk algorithms to be executed with substantially reduced resource requirements compared to traditional approaches. The novel heuristic algorithms based on Zeno adiabatic preparation and unitary evolution demonstrate promising performance characteristics in numerical simulations, with some instances showing superquadratic speedups over classical methods.

The invention's modular design, with clearly defined components that can be optimized independently, facilitates implementation across different quantum computing platforms and enables straightforward integration with classical computing resources. The careful attention to circuit depth, T gate count, and qubit requirements makes the invention particularly suitable for near-term quantum computing applications, where these resources are severely constrained.

Future work will focus on extending the invention to handle more complex energy functions and move sets, developing more sophisticated heuristic algorithms that can adapt to the specific characteristics of different problem instances, and exploring the theoretical foundations of the observed superquadratic speedups. The invention's framework also provides a foundation for developing quantum algorithms for other types of Markov chain Monte Carlo methods beyond Metropolis-Hastings, potentially opening new avenues for quantum advantage in statistical sampling and inference.

The practical implications of the invention are substantial, as it provides a clear pathway toward implementing quantum walk algorithms on real quantum hardware for solving practically relevant optimization problems. By reducing the gap between theoretical quantum algorithms and their practical implementations, the invention brings us closer to realizing the potential of quantum computing for tackling some of the most challenging computational problems in science and engineering.
