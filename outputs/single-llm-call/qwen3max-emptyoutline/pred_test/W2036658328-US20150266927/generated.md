# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of biopharmaceutical manufacturing, specifically to methods for producing virus-like particles (VLPs) with enhanced structural integrity, antigenicity, and immunogenicity. More particularly, the invention relates to a novel method for cultivating recombinant Saccharomyces cerevisiae host cells expressing human papillomavirus type 16 L1 (HPV16 L1) protein under controlled carbon source concentration conditions to yield high-quality VLPs suitable for use in prophylactic and therapeutic vaccines. The invention further encompasses the resulting HPV16 L1 VLP compositions, which exhibit superior conformational epitope presentation, reduced hydrodynamic diameter, and enhanced capacity to elicit neutralizing antibody responses compared to VLPs produced under conventional fermentation conditions. This technological advancement addresses longstanding challenges in the production of recombinant subunit vaccines by optimizing intracellular assembly conditions during microbial fermentation rather than relying solely on post-purification modifications or refolding strategies.

## BACKGROUND ART

Vaccination remains one of the most effective public health interventions for preventing infectious diseases, with modern vaccine development increasingly focused on subunit and recombinant protein-based approaches that offer improved safety profiles compared to traditional whole-pathogen vaccines. Among these advanced platforms, virus-like particles (VLPs) have emerged as particularly promising vaccine candidates due to their ability to mimic the native architecture of viral pathogens while lacking infectious genetic material. VLPs are self-assembling, non-replicating macromolecular complexes composed of one or more viral structural proteins that spontaneously organize into particulate structures resembling authentic virions in size, shape, and surface topology. This structural mimicry enables VLPs to present repetitive, conformationally correct antigenic epitopes in a highly ordered array that efficiently engages B-cell receptors and stimulates robust humoral immune responses, often without requiring additional adjuvants.

The human papillomavirus (HPV) represents a paradigmatic example of successful VLP-based vaccine development, with commercially available HPV vaccines demonstrating exceptional efficacy in preventing HPV-associated cervical and other anogenital cancers. These vaccines are based on VLPs formed by the major capsid protein L1, which self-assembles into T=7 icosahedral particles when expressed in heterologous expression systems. Various expression platforms have been employed for HPV L1 VLP production, including mammalian cells, insect cells using baculovirus vectors, and yeast systems, with Saccharomyces cerevisiae emerging as a particularly attractive option due to its well-characterized genetics, established scalability, cost-effectiveness, and ability to perform eukaryotic post-translational modifications necessary for proper protein folding and assembly.

Despite these advantages, yeast-produced VLPs have historically faced challenges related to structural stability and immunogenic potency. Conventional S. cerevisiae fermentation protocols typically employ carbon source concentrations in the range of 2–4% (w/v), primarily glucose or galactose, with culture durations limited to 48–72 hours to avoid metabolic stress, ethanol accumulation, and cell viability issues associated with prolonged cultivation under high-sugar conditions. Under these standard conditions, recombinant protein expression is often suboptimal, and the resulting VLPs may exhibit incomplete assembly, conformational heterogeneity, or reduced presentation of critical neutralizing epitopes. Previous efforts to enhance VLP quality have largely focused on post-production interventions, such as redox-mediated refolding, chemical cross-linking, or optimized purification protocols designed to select for properly assembled particles. However, these approaches address symptoms rather than root causes, failing to optimize the intracellular environment where initial protein folding and assembly occur.

The relationship between culture conditions and VLP quality has received insufficient attention in the prior art, with most studies treating fermentation parameters as fixed variables rather than tunable factors influencing final product characteristics. While it is generally recognized that protein expression levels can be influenced by media composition and culture duration, the specific impact of carbon source concentration on the structural fidelity and immunological properties of assembled VLPs has not been systematically investigated. Moreover, conventional wisdom in microbial fermentation suggests that high sugar concentrations (>4%) should be avoided due to osmotic stress and metabolic burden, leading researchers to overlook potential benefits that might accrue from extended cultivation under carefully controlled high-carbon conditions. This gap in understanding has limited the ability to fully exploit the yeast expression system's potential for producing high-quality VLP vaccines with optimal immunogenic properties.

## DISCLOSURE

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention stems from the persistent challenge of producing virus-like particles with consistently high structural integrity, conformational fidelity, and immunogenic potency using conventional microbial expression systems. Specifically, HPV16 L1 VLPs produced in Saccharomyces cerevisiae under standard fermentation conditions (typically employing 2–4% carbon source concentrations and culture durations of 48–72 hours) frequently exhibit suboptimal antigenic properties, including reduced presentation of conformational neutralizing epitopes, increased hydrodynamic diameter indicative of structural heterogeneity or aggregation, and diminished capacity to elicit robust neutralizing antibody responses in vivo. These limitations arise from inadequate optimization of intracellular assembly conditions during the production phase, where the complex process of L1 protein folding, oligomerization, and VLP formation occurs. Conventional approaches that focus exclusively on post-purification modifications or refolding strategies fail to address the fundamental issue that improper initial assembly within the host cell establishes a baseline of structural defects that cannot be fully corrected downstream. Furthermore, the prevailing assumption that high carbon source concentrations (>4%) are inherently detrimental to yeast cultures has prevented systematic exploration of extended cultivation strategies that might provide sufficient time and metabolic resources for complete and correct VLP assembly. This technical problem is particularly acute in the context of developing cost-effective, scalable vaccine production platforms where both yield and quality must be simultaneously optimized to ensure clinical efficacy and commercial viability.

### Technical Solution

The present invention provides a novel solution to the aforementioned technical problem through the discovery that cultivating recombinant Saccharomyces cerevisiae host cells expressing HPV16 L1 protein in culture media containing elevated concentrations of carbon source—specifically in the range of 6% to 8% (w/v)—for extended durations of approximately six days results in the production of VLPs with significantly enhanced structural quality, antigenic properties, and immunogenic potency compared to those produced under conventional conditions. This counterintuitive finding challenges established paradigms in microbial fermentation by demonstrating that higher carbon source concentrations, when combined with appropriate culture duration, create favorable intracellular conditions that promote proper L1 protein folding, efficient self-assembly into VLPs, and preservation of conformational neutralizing epitopes. The invention specifically discloses that carbon source concentrations of 6% and 8% (comprising equal parts glucose and galactose at 3% and 4% each, respectively) enable sustained metabolic activity over extended culture periods, facilitating the accumulation of correctly folded L1 protein and providing sufficient time for complete VLP assembly within the yeast cells. This approach leverages the natural capacity of S. cerevisiae to adapt to high-osmolarity environments while maintaining cellular viability and protein production capacity over longer cultivation periods than previously considered feasible. The resulting HPV16 L1 VLPs exhibit smaller hydrodynamic diameters, indicating more homogeneous and compact particle structures, enhanced reactivity with conformation-specific neutralizing monoclonal antibodies targeting critical epitopes in the FG and HI loops of the L1 protein, and superior ability to induce high-titer neutralizing antibody responses in immunized subjects. The technical solution thus comprises both the specific cultivation method (employing 6–8% carbon source concentration for approximately six days) and the resulting VLP compositions characterized by their improved structural and immunological properties.

### Advantageous Effects

The present invention confers numerous advantageous effects that collectively address the longstanding limitations of conventional VLP production methods. First and foremost, the VLPs produced according to the disclosed method exhibit significantly enhanced structural integrity, as evidenced by their reduced hydrodynamic diameter measured by dynamic light scattering, indicating more homogeneous particle populations with minimal aggregation or misfolding. This structural improvement directly translates to superior antigenic properties, with the VLPs demonstrating markedly increased reactivity with conformation-specific neutralizing monoclonal antibodies H16.V5 and H16.E70, which recognize critical epitopes in the FG and HI loops of the HPV16 L1 protein that are essential for protective immunity. The preservation of these conformational epitopes ensures that the VLPs accurately mimic the native viral capsid structure, thereby enabling more effective B-cell receptor engagement and antibody production.

Secondly, the immunogenic potency of the VLPs is substantially enhanced, as demonstrated by their ability to induce significantly higher titers of neutralizing antibodies in vivo across multiple dose levels. In comparative immunization studies, VLPs produced under the optimized 6% and 8% carbon source conditions elicited neutralizing antibody titers that were up to nine-fold higher than those induced by VLPs produced under conventional 2% and 4% conditions, even when administered at equivalent protein doses. This enhanced immunogenicity is particularly valuable for dose-sparing strategies, potentially enabling the development of more cost-effective vaccines that require lower antigen quantities per dose while maintaining or improving protective efficacy.

Thirdly, the method achieves these quality improvements without compromising production yield, as the higher carbon source concentrations actually support increased biomass accumulation and recombinant protein expression over the extended culture period. The final yields of purified L1 protein from 8% carbon source cultures were found to be 7.6-, 2.5-, and 1.2-fold higher than those obtained from 2%, 4%, and 6% cultures, respectively, demonstrating that quality enhancement can be achieved concurrently with productivity gains. This dual benefit of improved quality and increased yield represents a significant advancement over prior art approaches that often involve trade-offs between these parameters.

Furthermore, the invention provides a robust and scalable manufacturing platform that leverages the inherent advantages of the S. cerevisiae expression system—including well-established fermentation protocols, cost-effective media components, and regulatory acceptance—while overcoming its historical limitations regarding VLP quality. The method does not require specialized equipment, complex media formulations, or post-production modifications, making it readily implementable in existing manufacturing facilities with minimal process changes. The extended culture duration of six days, while longer than conventional protocols, is offset by the substantial improvements in product quality and yield, resulting in an overall more efficient production process when considering the final product's immunological potency per unit of input material.

Finally, the discovery that intracellular assembly conditions can be optimized through controlled manipulation of carbon source concentration opens new avenues for quality-by-design approaches in VLP manufacturing. Rather than treating fermentation as a black box process focused solely on maximizing protein expression, the invention demonstrates that careful tuning of culture parameters can directly influence the structural and functional properties of the final product. This paradigm shift enables more rational process development and quality control strategies, potentially extending beyond HPV VLPs to other complex protein assemblies where proper folding and assembly are critical for biological activity.

## MODES OF THE INVENTION

The present invention may be practiced in various modes that encompass both the method of producing enhanced-quality HPV16 L1 VLPs and the resulting VLP compositions themselves. In a primary mode, the invention provides a method for producing HPV16 L1 virus-like particles comprising cultivating recombinant Saccharomyces cerevisiae host cells transformed with an expression vector encoding codon-optimized HPV16 L1 protein in a culture medium containing a carbon source concentration in the range of 6% to 8% (w/v) for a duration of approximately six days at a temperature of about 30°C with agitation at approximately 230 rpm. The carbon source preferably comprises a mixture of glucose and galactose in equal proportions, such that a 6% total carbon source concentration corresponds to 3% glucose and 3% galactose, while an 8% total concentration corresponds to 4% glucose and 4% galactose. The culture medium may be formulated as YPDG medium containing 1% yeast extract, 2% peptone, and the specified carbon source concentrations, though alternative complex or defined media formulations may be employed provided they support robust yeast growth and recombinant protein expression under the specified high-carbon conditions.

Following the cultivation period, the HPV16 L1 VLPs are recovered from the yeast cells through a series of purification steps that may include cell disruption using mechanical methods such as glass bead vortexing, clarification by centrifugation to remove cellular debris, ammonium sulfate precipitation to concentrate the L1 protein, dialysis to remove salts and small molecules, and chromatographic purification using cation-exchange resins such as phosphocellulose P-11. The purified VLPs are then formulated in an appropriate storage buffer containing physiological salt concentrations and stabilizing agents such as Tween 80 to maintain particle integrity during storage and subsequent use.

In another mode, the invention encompasses the HPV16 L1 VLP compositions produced by the aforementioned method, which are characterized by specific structural and immunological properties that distinguish them from VLPs produced under conventional conditions. These enhanced VLPs exhibit a hydrodynamic diameter that is measurably smaller than that of conventionally produced VLPs, as determined by dynamic light scattering analysis, indicating more compact and homogeneous particle structures. They demonstrate enhanced reactivity with conformation-specific neutralizing monoclonal antibodies H16.V5 and H16.E70 in enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, reflecting superior preservation of critical neutralizing epitopes in the FG and HI loops of the L1 protein. Furthermore, when administered to mammalian subjects in combination with appropriate adjuvants such as aluminum hydroxide, these VLPs induce significantly higher titers of neutralizing antibodies against HPV16 pseudovirus compared to conventionally produced VLPs, as measured by standardized neutralization assays.

The invention also includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the enhanced HPV16 L1 VLPs in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, and adjuvants suitable for administration to human or animal subjects. These compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration, including intramuscular, subcutaneous, or intradermal injection, and may contain additional HPV types or other antigens to provide broader protection against multiple HPV strains or co-administered pathogens. The VLPs may be lyophilized for long-term storage and reconstituted immediately prior to administration, or maintained in liquid formulation under appropriate refrigerated conditions.

Additionally, the invention provides methods for preventing HPV16 infection in human subjects comprising administering an effective amount of the enhanced HPV16 L1 VLPs to induce protective immunity. The vaccination regimen may involve single or multiple doses administered at appropriate intervals, with the enhanced immunogenicity of the VLPs potentially enabling reduced dosing schedules or lower antigen quantities per dose compared to conventional HPV vaccines. The invention further encompasses diagnostic applications, where the enhanced VLPs may serve as superior capture antigens in serological assays for detecting HPV16-specific antibodies in patient samples, owing to their improved conformational fidelity and epitope presentation.

The scope of the invention extends to the application of similar cultivation strategies for producing other types of VLPs or complex protein assemblies in yeast or other microbial expression systems, where extended culture under controlled high-carbon conditions may similarly enhance structural quality and biological activity. The fundamental principle of optimizing intracellular assembly conditions through careful manipulation of culture parameters represents a generalizable approach that may be adapted to various recombinant protein production scenarios where proper folding and assembly are critical for function.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the present invention and demonstrate the superior properties of HPV16 L1 VLPs produced under optimized high-carbon cultivation conditions compared to those produced under conventional conditions. These examples are provided for illustrative purposes only and should not be construed as limiting the scope of the invention.

In Example 1, recombinant Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain Y2805 was transformed with a YEGα-MCS expression vector containing a codon-optimized HPV16 L1 gene designed to minimize mRNA secondary structure and enhance translation efficiency. Transformants were selected on synthetic dropout medium lacking uracil (SD-ura) and subsequently inoculated into 150 mL of YPDG medium containing varying concentrations of carbon source: 2%, 4%, 6%, or 8% (w/v), with glucose and galactose present in equal proportions (1:1 ratio). Cultures were maintained at 30°C with shaking at 230 rpm for six days, after which cells were harvested by centrifugation. Cell pellets were disrupted using glass beads in a vortex mixer, and crude lysates were clarified by centrifugation at 14,000×g for 10 minutes. The HPV16 L1 protein was then purified through a multi-step process involving 40% saturated ammonium sulfate precipitation, dialysis against sodium phosphate buffer containing 0.15 M NaCl and 0.01% Tween 80, cold-induced precipitation of contaminating proteins at 4°C for 16 hours, and cation-exchange chromatography using phosphocellulose P-11 resin with stepwise elution using increasing NaCl concentrations (0.6–1.0 M). Final purified fractions were concentrated and dialyzed against storage buffer containing 2.68 mM KCl, 1.47 mM KH2PO4, 8.1 mM Na2HPO4, 0.325 M NaCl, and 0.01% Tween 80 at pH 7.2.

Protein concentrations were determined using the Bradford assay with bovine serum albumin as standard, while purity and identity were confirmed by SDS-PAGE and Western blot analysis using anti-HPV16 L1 antibodies. The final yields of purified L1 protein demonstrated a clear concentration-dependent increase with carbon source levels, with the 8% carbon source culture yielding 7.6-, 2.5-, and 1.2-fold more protein than the 2%, 4%, and 6% cultures, respectively. Notably, the 2% carbon source preparation exhibited lower purity (approximately 80% L1 protein by densitometric analysis) compared to the other preparations, necessitating adjustment of antigen quantities in subsequent analyses to account for this difference.

In Example 2, the structural integrity and antigenic properties of the purified VLPs were evaluated using transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA). TEM analysis confirmed that all preparations formed typical icosahedral VLP structures with diameters consistent with authentic HPV16 virions. However, dynamic light scattering (DLS) measurements revealed significant differences in hydrodynamic diameter among the preparations, with VLPs from 6% and 8% carbon source cultures exhibiting smaller particle sizes compared to those from 2% and 4% cultures, indicating more compact and homogeneous structures. Antigenic characterization was performed using conformation-specific neutralizing monoclonal antibodies H16.V5 and H16.E70, which recognize distinct epitopes in the FG and HI loops of the L1 protein. ELISA results demonstrated that VLPs produced under 6% and 8% carbon source conditions exhibited significantly higher reactivity with both monoclonal antibodies compared to those produced under 2% and 4% conditions, confirming superior preservation of critical neutralizing epitopes.

In Example 3, the immunogenic properties of the VLPs were evaluated in vivo using a murine model. Six-week-old female BALB/c mice were divided into five groups (n=8–10 per group) and immunized subcutaneously three times at two-week intervals with either phosphate-buffered saline (control) or purified HPV16 L1 VLPs (10 ng or 1000 ng per dose) formulated with aluminum hydroxide adjuvant (200 μg per dose). Serum samples were collected ten days after the third immunization and analyzed for anti-HPV16 L1 IgG titers by ELISA and neutralizing antibody activity using HPV16 pseudovirus (PsV) neutralization assays. Mice immunized with VLPs produced under 6% and 8% carbon source conditions exhibited significantly higher anti-HPV16 L1 IgG titers and neutralizing antibody activities compared to those receiving VLPs produced under 2% and 4% conditions, regardless of the administered dose level. At the 1000 ng dose level, neutralizing antibody titers induced by 6% and 8% VLPs were nine-fold and three-fold higher, respectively, than those induced by 2% and 4% VLPs. Importantly, while total IgG titers induced by 4% VLPs were comparable to those induced by 6% and 8% VLPs at the higher dose level, the corresponding neutralizing antibody titers were significantly lower, highlighting the critical importance of conformational epitope preservation for functional immune responses.

In Example 4, mechanistic investigations were conducted to understand the basis for the observed improvements in VLP quality under high-carbon cultivation conditions. Analysis of intracellular redox status revealed that prolonged culture under 6% and 8% carbon source conditions led to a shift toward a more reducing intracellular environment, characterized by decreased levels of oxidized glutathione (GSSG) relative to reduced glutathione (GSH). This altered redox state may facilitate proper disulfide bond formation and protein folding during L1 protein synthesis and VLP assembly. Additionally, the extended culture duration enabled by the high-carbon conditions appears to provide sufficient time for complete VLP assembly within the yeast cells, as evidenced by increased proportions of soluble, properly folded L1 protein in cell lysates from longer cultures. These findings suggest that the combination of elevated carbon source concentration and extended culture duration creates optimal intracellular conditions for VLP biogenesis, addressing the fundamental limitations of conventional short-duration, low-carbon fermentation protocols.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention possesses significant industrial applicability across multiple sectors of the biopharmaceutical and vaccine manufacturing industries. Most directly, the disclosed method for producing enhanced-quality HPV16 L1 VLPs provides a robust, scalable, and cost-effective platform for manufacturing next-generation HPV vaccines with superior immunogenic properties compared to current commercial products. The ability to achieve both higher yields and improved product quality using standard S. cerevisiae fermentation infrastructure makes this technology particularly attractive for large-scale vaccine production, where manufacturing efficiency and product consistency are critical economic and regulatory considerations. The enhanced immunogenicity of the VLPs may enable dose-sparing strategies that reduce the amount of antigen required per vaccine dose, potentially lowering production costs and increasing global vaccine accessibility, particularly in resource-limited settings where HPV-associated cancers represent a significant public health burden.

Beyond HPV vaccines, the fundamental principle of optimizing intracellular assembly conditions through controlled manipulation of carbon source concentration and culture duration has broad applicability to the production of other VLP-based vaccines and complex recombinant protein therapeutics. Many viral pathogens, including hepatitis B virus, norovirus, influenza virus, and HIV, have been targeted for VLP-based vaccine development, and the quality challenges associated with their production in microbial expression systems are similar to those addressed by the present invention. The discovery that extended cultivation under high-carbon conditions can enhance structural fidelity and biological activity may be adapted to these other systems, potentially improving the efficacy of multiple vaccine candidates currently in development.

The invention also has important implications for biopharmaceutical process development and quality control practices. By demonstrating that fermentation parameters can be rationally tuned to influence final product quality attributes, the invention supports a quality-by-design approach to biomanufacturing that aligns with current regulatory expectations for advanced therapy medicinal products. This paradigm shift from empirical process optimization to mechanism-based parameter selection enables more predictable and consistent production of complex biologics, reducing batch-to-batch variability and improving overall manufacturing reliability. The specific quality attributes identified in the invention—hydrodynamic diameter, conformational epitope preservation, and neutralizing antibody induction—provide measurable endpoints for process monitoring and product release testing, facilitating the implementation of robust quality control strategies.

Furthermore, the enhanced VLPs produced by the disclosed method have applications beyond prophylactic vaccination. Their superior conformational fidelity makes them valuable tools for diagnostic assay development, where accurate representation of native viral epitopes is essential for detecting clinically relevant antibody responses. The VLPs may serve as reference standards for serological testing, quality control materials for vaccine lot release, or research reagents for studying virus-host interactions and immune mechanisms. The ability to produce large quantities of high-quality VLPs also supports structure-function studies aimed at understanding the molecular basis of viral assembly and immunogenicity, potentially informing the rational design of future vaccine candidates.

From a commercial perspective, the invention addresses key market demands for more effective, affordable, and accessible vaccines. The demonstrated ability to enhance immunogenicity without increasing antigen dose or requiring complex formulation strategies provides a competitive advantage in the crowded HPV vaccine market, where differentiation based on efficacy, safety, and cost-effectiveness is increasingly important. The compatibility of the method with existing manufacturing infrastructure reduces barriers to technology transfer and commercial implementation, accelerating the path from discovery to market availability. Additionally, the potential for cross-protection against multiple HPV types through improved conformational epitope presentation may expand the clinical utility of next-generation HPV vaccines, addressing unmet medical needs in cancer prevention and treatment.

The industrial applicability of the invention extends to contract manufacturing organizations (CMOs) and biopharmaceutical companies seeking to optimize their VLP production capabilities. The straightforward nature of the process modifications—primarily involving adjustments to carbon source concentration and culture duration—makes implementation relatively simple and cost-effective, requiring minimal capital investment in new equipment or extensive process revalidation. This accessibility ensures that the benefits of the invention can be realized across the industry spectrum, from large multinational vaccine manufacturers to smaller biotechnology companies developing novel VLP-based therapeutics.

In summary, the present invention represents a significant technological advancement with far-reaching industrial implications, offering a practical solution to longstanding challenges in VLP production while opening new possibilities for vaccine development, diagnostic applications, and biopharmaceutical manufacturing innovation. The combination of scientific insight, technical feasibility, and commercial relevance positions this invention as a valuable contribution to the field of preventive medicine and biotechnology.
