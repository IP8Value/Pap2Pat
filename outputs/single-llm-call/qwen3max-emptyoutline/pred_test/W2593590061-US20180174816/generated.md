# DESCRIPTION

## MR-TOF with Frequent Pulsing

The present invention relates to a multi-reflecting time-of-flight mass spectrometer (MR-TOFMS) system that incorporates an encoded frequent pulsing (EFP) mechanism to significantly enhance analytical throughput, sensitivity, and dynamic range without compromising the high resolving power inherent to MR-TOF architectures. Traditional MR-TOFMS instruments suffer from a fundamental limitation in duty cycle due to the requirement of long ion flight paths for achieving high resolution, which necessitates infrequent ion injection events to prevent temporal overlap of successive ion packets at the detector. This constraint severely limits the instrument’s ability to efficiently sample continuous ion beams, particularly those generated by electrospray ionization (ESI), atmospheric pressure chemical ionization (APCI), or other continuous ion sources commonly employed in modern analytical chemistry. The present invention overcomes this limitation by introducing a method of frequent, non-uniform pulsing of the orthogonal accelerator (OA) coupled with a robust decoding algorithm that resolves temporally overlapped ion signals based on predetermined, unique time intervals between successive pulses.

In the disclosed system, the MR-TOFMS comprises a pair of gridless electrostatic ion mirrors arranged in a planar configuration, separated by a drift space that contains a periodic array of electrostatic lenses. Ions are introduced into the analyzer via a double orthogonal acceleration scheme, wherein a continuous ion beam is first accumulated and cooled in a radio-frequency (RF) ion guide, then orthogonally ejected into the MR-TOF drift region. The periodic lens array provides transverse confinement of the ion packet along the mean zigzag trajectory, enabling indefinite trapping and multiple reflections without significant spatial divergence. The ion mirrors are optimized for third-order time-of-flight focusing and fourth- or fifth-order energy focusing, thereby minimizing temporal aberrations and enabling resolving powers exceeding 200,000 across the full mass range, and up to 500,000 or more in a “zoom” mode where ions are trapped for multiple round trips.

The key innovation lies in the pulsing strategy of the OA. Instead of operating at a low repetition rate dictated by the total ion flight time (e.g., 1–10 kHz for flight times of 100–1000 µs), the OA is pulsed at a much higher frequency—up to 100 kHz or more—using a sequence of non-uniform, mathematically encoded time intervals. For example, the time interval \( T_i \) between the \( i \)-th and \( (i+1) \)-th pulse may be defined as \( T_i = T_D \cdot i(i-1)/2 \), where \( T_D \) is a base time unit. This encoding ensures that the time differences between any two pulses in the sequence are unique. Consequently, when multiple ion packets from different pulses arrive at the detector simultaneously, the resulting composite signal contains overlapping peaks whose relative timing encodes their origin. A dedicated decoding algorithm, implemented in the data system, leverages knowledge of the pulse sequence to deconvolve the multiplexed signal into individual mass spectra corresponding to each injection event.

This approach dramatically improves the duty cycle—from typically less than 1% in conventional MR-TOFMS to 20% or higher—thereby increasing sensitivity proportionally to the number of pulses per unit time. Crucially, because the MR-TOFMS produces high-resolution, sparsely populated spectra (especially in tandem MS or short acquisition modes), the probability of systematic peak overlap that would confound decoding is low. The algorithm evaluates candidate peak assignments based on consistency of mass, intensity, and expected time offsets, and can reject spurious signals arising from random coincidences. Experimental validation has demonstrated detection of analytes at femtogram levels in complex biological matrices, with signal-to-noise ratios improved by factors of 30 or more compared to single-pulse operation. Furthermore, the EFP method enables rapid tracking of ion flux variations on microsecond timescales, which is essential for coupling with fast separation techniques such as comprehensive two-dimensional gas chromatography (GC×GC) or ion mobility spectrometry (IMS).

The EFP-enhanced MR-TOFMS is not limited to MS-only operation; it is particularly advantageous in tandem configurations. When integrated downstream of a quadrupole, ion trap, or IMS separator, the high-speed acquisition capability of the EFP-MR-TOFMS allows full fragment spectra to be recorded for all precursor ions eluting during a wide mass window scan, effectively enabling data-independent acquisition (DIA) with high sensitivity and mass accuracy. The system maintains sub-ppm mass measurement precision, facilitating confident elemental composition assignment even for minor components in ultra-complex mixtures such as crude oil or proteomic digests. Importantly, the EFP method is compatible with existing MR-TOF hardware and requires only modifications to the pulsing electronics and data processing software, making it readily implementable in commercial instruments.

In summary, the MR-TOF with frequent pulsing represents a paradigm shift in time-of-flight mass spectrometry, decoupling sensitivity from flight time and enabling high-resolution, high-throughput analysis previously unattainable with conventional designs. By transforming the duty cycle limitation into a multiplexed advantage, the invention unlocks the full potential of MR-TOF technology for applications demanding both speed and precision, including metabolomics, petroleomics, clinical diagnostics, and environmental monitoring.

## Comprehensive MS-MS (C-MS-MS)

The present invention further encompasses a comprehensive tandem mass spectrometry (C-MS-MS) methodology that leverages the high-speed acquisition capabilities of the encoded frequent pulsing (EFP) MR-TOFMS to achieve true all-mass, parallel fragmentation analysis without the ion losses and duty cycle penalties associated with traditional scanning tandem instruments. Conventional tandem MS platforms, such as triple quadrupoles or quadrupole-time-of-flight (Q-TOF) systems, rely on sequential selection of precursor ions, which inherently discards the majority of ions during the scan cycle and limits the depth of coverage in complex samples. In contrast, the C-MS-MS approach described herein eliminates precursor ion scanning by employing either a lossless parallel separator (e.g., an ion mobility spectrometer or a high-capacity linear ion trap array) or a wide-window quadrupole operated in a continuous scan mode, coupled with the EFP-MR-TOFMS as the fragment analyzer.

In one embodiment, the C-MS-MS system utilizes a quadrupole mass filter operated with a wide transmission window of 10–30 atomic mass units (amu), which is smoothly scanned across the mass range of interest (e.g., 300–1100 amu) over a period of 0.3 to 5 seconds. During this scan, all precursor ions within the instantaneous window are simultaneously fragmented in a collision-induced dissociation (CID) cell, and the resulting fragment ions are analyzed by the EFP-MR-TOFMS at a pulsing rate of approximately 100 kHz. The high temporal resolution of the EFP acquisition—capable of resolving ion arrival profiles with microsecond precision—enables the reconstruction of fragment ion time traces that correlate precisely with the quadrupole’s scan position. By deconvolving these time-intensity profiles, the system assigns each fragment spectrum to a specific precursor mass centroid with an effective resolution of approximately 1 amu, despite the use of a 20-fold wider quadrupole window. This “two-dimensional MS-MS” (2D-MS-MS) technique provides a 10–30 fold gain in sensitivity compared to conventional stepped-window Q-TOF methods, as no ions are discarded during precursor selection.

In another embodiment, the C-MS-MS system employs a parallel ion mobility separator (IMS) as the first stage of separation. Ions are pulsed from an accumulation region into the IMS drift tube at non-uniform intervals, and the resulting mobility-separated ion packets are orthogonally injected into the EFP-MR-TOFMS. Both the IMS gate and the OA are encoded with unique pulse sequences, enabling doubly multiplexed data acquisition. The decoding algorithm first resolves the mass spectra using the OA pulse sequence, then extracts mobility profiles for each mass using the IMS gate sequence. This yields a three-dimensional dataset comprising retention time (from upstream chromatography), ion mobility, and exact mass—termed “3D-tag” analysis—which provides exceptional selectivity for compound identification in complex mixtures. When operated in MS-MS mode, the IMS serves as a lossless parent ion separator, and fragment spectra are correlated to precursors via dual time alignment in both mobility and chromatographic domains.

A more advanced implementation replaces the quadrupole or IMS with a coaxial array of 30–50 linear ion traps, each capable of storing up to 10⁹ ions. Precursor ions are mass-selectively ejected from the traps using opposing DC and RF fields, which are less susceptible to space charge effects than resonant excitation methods. The ejection occurs over a 10 ms window with moderate resolution (R = 30–50), ensuring that all ions are transmitted without loss. The EFP-MR-TOFMS then records fragment spectra for all precursors in a single, continuous acquisition. Because the trap array provides near-100% duty cycle for parent ion transmission, this configuration achieves the theoretical maximum sensitivity for all-mass MS-MS, enabling detection of low-abundance species even in the presence of overwhelming matrix interferences.

The C-MS-MS methodology is particularly powerful when combined with soft ionization sources such as conditioned glow discharge (CGD) or photoionization, which produce predominantly molecular ions with minimal fragmentation. This simplifies spectral interpretation and enhances quantitative accuracy, as ionization efficiency becomes largely independent of chemical structure. In applications such as crude oil analysis, the system has identified over 300,000 distinct species by combining GC×GC separation, CGD ionization, and C-MS-MS with EFP-MR-TOF detection. Similarly, in proteomics, the method has detected over 100 peptides from a bovine serum albumin digest at sub-micromolar concentrations in a single 5-second scan, demonstrating its suitability for high-throughput biomarker discovery.

Critically, the C-MS-MS approach is data-independent, meaning the same acquisition protocol can be applied universally regardless of sample composition. This eliminates the need for method development or dynamic exclusion lists, streamlining workflows in untargeted analysis. The high mass accuracy (<1 ppm) and resolving power (>200,000) of the MR-TOFMS ensure that fragment ions can be confidently assigned even at low ion counts, enabling de novo sequencing and structural elucidation. Together, these features establish C-MS-MS as a transformative platform for comprehensive molecular characterization across diverse fields of analytical science.

## Parallel Mass Separators

The invention further discloses a novel architecture for parallel mass separation that dramatically increases the throughput and dynamic range of mass spectrometric analysis by employing multiple, concurrently operating mass separation stages upstream of the MR-TOFMS detector. Traditional mass spectrometers process ions through a single separation channel, creating a bottleneck when analyzing samples with extreme complexity or high ion fluxes. The parallel mass separator concept addresses this limitation by distributing the ion load across an array of identical or complementary separation devices, each feeding into the same or separate MR-TOF analyzers. This multiplexing strategy exploits spatial, temporal, or functional redundancy to achieve gains in speed, sensitivity, and robustness unattainable with serial architectures.

One implementation involves a coaxial array of 30 to 50 linear ion traps, arranged concentrically around a central axis. Each trap is independently controlled and capable of storing up to 10⁹ ions, providing a collective space charge capacity orders of magnitude greater than conventional 3D ion traps. Ions are accumulated in the traps from a common source, such as an electrospray emitter, and then mass-selectively ejected using a combination of DC and RF fields. The ejection is synchronized across the array but staggered in time or mass to prevent overlap at the detector. The use of opposing DC and RF fields for mass-selective ejection minimizes space charge-induced distortions, enabling high-fidelity transmission even at high ion densities. The entire array can complete a full mass scan in 10 milliseconds with a resolving power of 30 to 50, ensuring that no precursor ions are lost during the separation process. The resulting ion packets are then fragmented and analyzed by the EFP-MR-TOFMS, which reconstructs individual MS-MS spectra based on precise timing correlations.

An alternative embodiment utilizes multiple ion mobility spectrometry (IMS) channels operating in parallel. Each IMS channel may employ a different drift gas, electric field strength, or temperature to provide orthogonal separation mechanisms. Ions are distributed among the channels via an array of ion funnels or multiplexed gates, and the mobility-separated packets are sequentially or simultaneously injected into the MR-TOFMS. The use of parallel IMS stages increases the total ion throughput and provides additional selectivity through differential mobility behavior, which can resolve isobaric species that co-elute in chromatography and have identical mass. For example, hydrocarbons and fluorinated compounds with the same nominal mass exhibit distinct drift times due to differences in collisional cross-section, enabling their discrimination in complex mixtures.

A third configuration integrates multiple chromatographic columns with a shared MR-TOFMS detector. In this setup, an array of microfluidic channels or capillary columns performs parallel liquid or gas chromatography separations, with effluents directed to individual ion sources or a multiplexed interface. The ion sources may be arranged in a circular or linear array, each coupled to a dedicated orthogonal accelerator that injects ions into a segmented MR-TOF analyzer. The MR-TOF itself may be constructed as a planar array of slots in a common electrode stack, allowing multiple ion trajectories to coexist within the same vacuum chamber while sharing high-voltage power supplies and detection electronics. This design reduces cost and footprint compared to deploying multiple standalone instruments, while multiplying analytical throughput. Applications include high-volume clinical screening, where hundreds of patient samples can be processed in parallel, or proteomics, where replicate analyses or fractionated digests are run simultaneously.

The parallel mass separator concept is synergistic with the EFP-MR-TOFMS, as the high-speed acquisition capability of the latter can keep pace with the increased data rate from multiple separators. Moreover, the multiplexed nature of EFP decoding can accommodate inputs from several sources by incorporating source-specific encoding into the pulse sequence. For instance, each ion trap in the array could be assigned a unique subset of pulse intervals, allowing the data system to attribute detected ions to their origin. This level of multiplexing not only boosts throughput but also enhances dynamic range by preventing detector saturation, as the ion load is distributed across time and space.

In summary, parallel mass separators represent a scalable solution to the growing demands of modern analytical chemistry, where sample complexity and required throughput continue to outpace the capabilities of single-channel instruments. By decoupling separation from detection and leveraging the inherent parallelism of MR-TOF technology, the invention enables unprecedented levels of performance in terms of speed, sensitivity, and information content.

## Resistive Ion Guides

The present invention incorporates advanced resistive ion guides that play a critical role in enhancing ion transmission efficiency, reducing space charge effects, and enabling stable operation under high ion flux conditions. Traditional RF ion guides, composed of discrete ring electrodes or stacked printed circuit boards, suffer from field discontinuities at the gaps between electrodes, which can cause ion scattering and losses, particularly at higher pressures where collisional cooling is essential. In contrast, the resistive ion guides disclosed herein utilize a monolithic, continuous resistive coating—such as doped glass, ceramic, or conductive polymer—applied to the inner surface of a tubular or planar structure. This design creates a smooth, quasi-DC axial field gradient when a voltage is applied across the length of the guide, while superimposed RF voltages generate a radial pseudopotential that confines ions toward the central axis.

The resistive layer serves a dual function: it acts as a voltage divider to establish a linear axial field for ion propulsion, and it dissipates charge buildup that would otherwise distort the electric field in high-current regimes. This is particularly important in applications involving intense ion beams, such as those generated by electrospray ionization of concentrated samples or MALDI of tissue sections. By preventing localized charging, the resistive guide maintains field homogeneity and ensures consistent ion transmission over extended periods. Additionally, the absence of physical gaps between electrodes eliminates sites for ion-neutral collisions that could lead to fragmentation or scattering, thereby preserving the integrity of labile ions.

In the context of the MR-TOFMS system, resistive ion guides are employed at multiple stages: as an accumulation and cooling region upstream of the orthogonal accelerator, as a transfer line between separation stages (e.g., from an IMS cell to the MR-TOF), and as an interface to the ion source. In the accumulation region, the guide operates at intermediate pressures (1–10 Torr) with a buffer gas (e.g., nitrogen or argon) to thermalize ions via collisions, reducing their kinetic energy spread and spatial divergence. The smooth axial field then gently transports the cooled ions to the OA entrance, where they are orthogonally ejected into the MR-TOF drift space. This preparation step is crucial for achieving the narrow initial ion packet width required for high-resolution TOF analysis.

When used in conjunction with the encoded frequent pulsing (EFP) method, the resistive ion guide’s ability to handle high ion currents without distortion becomes especially valuable. The EFP regime demands a continuous, stable supply of ions to the OA at rates up to 10⁹ ions per second, far exceeding the capacity of conventional segmented guides. The resistive design accommodates this load by distributing the current evenly along its length, avoiding the hot spots and arcing that can occur in discrete electrode systems. Furthermore, the guide can be dynamically biased to modulate ion velocity, enabling synchronization with the EFP pulse sequence for optimal duty cycle.

Experimental results have demonstrated that resistive ion guides improve ion transmission efficiency by 2–5 fold compared to traditional multipoles, with commensurate gains in sensitivity. They also extend the dynamic range of the instrument by delaying the onset of space charge saturation, allowing accurate quantification of both major and minor components in a single run. In tandem MS applications, the guide’s gentle transport preserves non-covalent complexes and post-translational modifications that might be disrupted by abrupt field transitions.

In summary, resistive ion guides are a foundational component of the high-throughput MR-TOFMS platform, providing the robust, high-capacity ion handling necessary to fully exploit the advantages of frequent pulsing, parallel separation, and comprehensive MS-MS. Their integration represents a significant advance in ion optics, bridging the gap between ion source and analyzer with unprecedented efficiency and reliability.

## TOF Detectors

The invention includes specialized time-of-flight (TOF) detectors engineered to meet the demanding requirements of high-resolution, high-throughput MR-TOFMS operation, particularly in the context of encoded frequent pulsing (EFP) and parallel mass separation. Conventional TOF detectors, such as microchannel plates (MCPs) with phosphor screens and photomultiplier tubes, or discrete anode arrays, often lack the dynamic range, count rate capability, or spatial resolution needed to accurately capture the dense, multiplexed ion signals generated by the disclosed system. To address these limitations, the present invention employs a hybrid detector architecture that combines high-gain secondary electron multiplication with fast, high-fidelity signal digitization and spatial encoding.

In one embodiment, the detector consists of a chevron MCP stack coupled to a delay-line anode or a pixelated anode array. The delay-line anode provides both temporal and spatial information by measuring the difference in signal arrival times at opposite ends of serpentine transmission lines, enabling reconstruction of the ion impact position with sub-millimeter precision. This spatial resolution is critical for distinguishing ions that arrive simultaneously but originate from different trajectories—for example, in a cylindrical MR-TOF analyzer where ions may follow slightly offset paths due to angular divergence. The pixelated anode, alternatively, uses a matrix of independent sensing elements (e.g., 64×64 pixels) connected to application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) that perform time-to-digital conversion (TDC) for each pixel in parallel. This design supports count rates exceeding 10⁷ ions per second per pixel, preventing saturation even under the high ion fluxes enabled by EFP and parallel separators.

The detector electronics are optimized for high bandwidth and low noise, with analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) sampling at rates of 10–40 gigasamples per second (GS/s) to resolve ion arrival times with picosecond precision. This temporal resolution is essential for deconvolving the closely spaced peaks in high-resolution MR-TOF spectra, where mass differences of less than 1 mDa must be distinguished. The digitized waveforms are processed in real time by a field-programmable gate array (FPGA) that implements baseline correction, peak detection, and preliminary decoding of EFP-encoded signals. Only validated ion events are passed to the main data system, reducing data volume and enabling sustained high-speed acquisition.

A key feature of the detector is its extended dynamic range, achieved through a combination of hardware and software techniques. At the hardware level, the MCP bias voltage can be dynamically adjusted to modulate gain, preventing saturation from intense peaks while maintaining sensitivity to weak signals. At the software level, the decoding algorithm accounts for detector nonlinearity and dead time, applying corrections based on empirical calibration curves. This ensures accurate quantification across five or more orders of magnitude in ion intensity, a necessity for analyzing complex samples with widely varying component abundances.

In configurations employing open electrostatic traps or cylindrical MR-TOF analyzers, the detector may be elongated or curved to match the ion beam profile, maximizing collection efficiency. Dual-sided detection—where ions are sensed upon both forward and return passes through the analyzer—further doubles the duty cycle and provides redundancy for error checking. The detector is also shielded against electromagnetic interference from the high-voltage pulsers and RF guides, ensuring signal fidelity even in the electrically noisy environment of a high-performance mass spectrometer.

Overall, the TOF detector disclosed herein is not merely a passive sensor but an active participant in the data acquisition chain, enabling the full exploitation of the MR-TOFMS platform’s speed, resolution, and multiplexing capabilities. Its design reflects a holistic approach to instrument engineering, where detection performance is co-optimized with ion optics, pulsing strategies, and data processing algorithms to deliver unprecedented analytical power.

## Data System

The data system of the present invention is a sophisticated, integrated computing platform specifically designed to manage the high data rates, complex signal processing, and advanced decoding algorithms required by the EFP-MR-TOFMS and its associated parallel separation technologies. Unlike conventional mass spectrometry data systems that process relatively sparse, sequentially acquired spectra, the disclosed system must handle continuous streams of multiplexed, high-resolution data at rates exceeding 100,000 spectra per second, each containing thousands of mass-resolved ion events. To meet this challenge, the data system employs a multi-tiered architecture combining real-time hardware processing, adaptive software algorithms, and scalable storage solutions.

At the core of the system is a high-performance FPGA that interfaces directly with the TOF detector’s digitizer. The FPGA executes low-level signal processing tasks—including baseline correction, noise filtering, peak picking, and initial EFP decoding—in real time, reducing the raw data stream by orders of magnitude before it reaches the main processor. This preprocessing is essential for sustaining the high acquisition rates of the EFP mode without data loss. The FPGA firmware is reconfigurable, allowing optimization for different operational modes (e.g., MS-only, MS-MS, IMS-MS) and pulse encoding schemes.

The main data processing engine runs on a multi-core CPU with access to large RAM buffers and solid-state storage. It implements advanced decoding algorithms that leverage the mathematical properties of the EFP pulse sequence to resolve overlapped peaks. For instance, when using a quadratic pulse interval encoding (\( T_i = T_D \cdot i(i-1)/2 \)), the algorithm constructs a hypothesis matrix of all possible peak assignments and iteratively refines it based on mass consistency, intensity correlation, and prior knowledge of expected isotopic patterns. Machine learning models may be employed to distinguish true signals from random overlaps, using features such as peak shape, signal-to-noise ratio, and chromatographic context.

In C-MS-MS and parallel separator modes, the data system performs multidimensional correlation analysis. For 2D-MS-MS with a scanning quadrupole, it aligns fragment ion time traces with the quadrupole’s voltage ramp to reconstruct precursor-specific spectra. For IMS-MR-TOF, it applies a two-stage decoding process: first demultiplexing the mass spectra using the OA pulse sequence, then extracting mobility profiles using the IMS gate sequence. The result is a four-dimensional dataset (retention time, mobility, mass, intensity) that can be visualized and queried through an intuitive graphical user interface.

Data storage is handled by a tiered system that archives raw waveforms for critical experiments while compressing routine data into processed peak lists. Cloud integration enables remote access, collaborative analysis, and automated database searching against spectral libraries (e.g., NIST, Wiley). The system also supports real-time feedback control, adjusting instrument parameters (e.g., EFP pulse rate, trap ejection timing) based on incoming data quality to optimize performance dynamically.

In summary, the data system is the intelligent backbone of the invention, transforming raw detector signals into actionable chemical information with speed, accuracy, and flexibility unmatched by prior art. Its tight integration with the hardware ensures that the full potential of the MR-TOF platform is realized in every analytical application.

## Conclusion

The invention represents a comprehensive advancement in time-of-flight mass spectrometry, integrating innovations in ion optics, pulsing strategies, separation science, detection technology, and data processing to achieve unprecedented levels of throughput, sensitivity, and resolution. By overcoming the historical trade-offs between these parameters, the disclosed system enables new classes of analytical challenges to be addressed—from the deep profiling of ultra-complex mixtures like crude oil to the high-throughput screening of clinical biomarkers. The modular nature of the design allows customization for specific applications, while the underlying principles of multiplexing and parallelization provide a scalable path toward even greater performance in the future.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a high-throughput mass spectrometry platform centered on a multi-reflecting time-of-flight mass spectrometer (MR-TOFMS) enhanced with encoded frequent pulsing (EFP), comprehensive tandem MS (C-MS-MS) capabilities, parallel mass separators, resistive ion guides, advanced TOF detectors, and an integrated data system. The EFP method dramatically improves duty cycle and sensitivity by pulsing the orthogonal accelerator at high frequency with unique time intervals, enabling deconvolution of overlapped spectra. C-MS-MS leverages this speed for all-mass, parallel fragmentation analysis using wide-window quadrupoles, ion mobility separators, or trap arrays as lossless parent ion selectors. Parallel mass separators—including arrays of linear ion traps, IMS channels, or chromatographic columns—multiply analytical throughput by distributing ion loads across multiple channels. Resistive ion guides ensure efficient, high-capacity ion transport with minimal losses. Specialized TOF detectors with high dynamic range and spatial resolution capture dense, multiplexed signals, while the data system performs real-time decoding and multidimensional correlation to extract chemical information. Together, these components form a unified platform capable of delivering orders-of-magnitude improvements in analytical performance for applications ranging from petroleomics to clinical diagnostics.
