# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and cancer therapeutics, particularly to monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains (TIGIT) and exhibit enhanced anti-tumor activity through Fc-mediated effector functions. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising such antibodies, alone or in combination with other immune checkpoint inhibitors such as anti-PD-1 antibodies, and their use in the treatment of cancer and other diseases characterized by immune dysfunction. More specifically, the invention provides humanized anti-TIGIT monoclonal antibodies with wild-type human IgG1 Fc regions that retain full Fcγ receptor (FcγR) binding capacity, thereby enabling critical biological activities including antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), trogocytosis, and activation of natural killer (NK) cells and antigen-presenting cells (APCs). The invention also includes methods for producing these antibodies, diagnostic assays for monitoring their biological effects, and therapeutic regimens for administering them to patients suffering from malignancies, especially those refractory to existing immunotherapies.

## BACKGROUND

Immune checkpoint blockade has emerged as a transformative approach in oncology, harnessing the body’s own immune system to recognize and eliminate tumor cells. Among the most successful targets are the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and its ligand PD-L1, whose inhibition has led to durable clinical responses in multiple cancer types. However, a significant proportion of patients either fail to respond initially or develop resistance to monotherapy with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 agents, underscoring the need for novel therapeutic strategies that target complementary or synergistic immune pathways. In this context, TIGIT (T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains), also known as VSIG9, VSTM3, or WUCAM, has gained prominence as a promising next-generation immune checkpoint molecule.

TIGIT is a type I transmembrane protein belonging to the immunoglobulin superfamily, expressed primarily on activated CD8+ and CD4+ T cells, natural killer (NK) cells, and regulatory T cells (Tregs). It binds with high affinity to poliovirus receptor (PVR, also known as CD155) and with lower affinity to PVRL2 (CD112), both of which are frequently overexpressed on tumor cells and antigen-presenting cells within the tumor microenvironment. Through multiple mechanisms, TIGIT exerts potent immunosuppressive effects: it delivers direct inhibitory signals via its cytoplasmic immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM); it competes with the activating receptor DNAM-1 (CD226) for shared ligands, thereby disrupting co-stimulatory signaling; it enhances the immunosuppressive function and stability of Tregs; and it promotes the development of tolerogenic dendritic cells. Critically, TIGIT is consistently upregulated in the tumor microenvironment across diverse malignancies—including melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer, colorectal cancer, and renal cell carcinoma—and its expression on tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) correlates with poor clinical outcomes. Moreover, TIGIT is often co-expressed with PD-1 and other inhibitory receptors such as TIM-3 and LAG-3 on exhausted T cells and Tregs, suggesting a role in the establishment of a profoundly immunosuppressive niche.

Given these biological insights, several pharmaceutical companies have advanced anti-TIGIT monoclonal antibodies into clinical development. However, a key divergence among these candidates lies in the choice of Fc domain architecture. Some, such as tiragolumab (Genentech/Roche), vibostolimab (Merck), and BGB-A1217 (BeiGene), employ a wild-type human IgG1 Fc region, which supports robust engagement with Fcγ receptors and thus enables effector functions like ADCC and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP). Others, including COM902 (Compugen) and ASP8374 (Astellas/Potenza), utilize an IgG4 backbone, which exhibits minimal FcγR binding and is considered “effector-silent.” Still others, such as BMS-986207 (Bristol Myers Squibb) and AB154 (Arcus), incorporate engineered mutations (e.g., LALA or N297A) that abrogate FcγR interactions entirely. This heterogeneity raises a fundamental and clinically relevant question: is Fc effector functionality necessary—or even advantageous—for optimal anti-tumor efficacy of anti-TIGIT antibodies?

Prior art provides conflicting guidance. While some preclinical studies suggest that simple blockade of the TIGIT–PVR interaction is sufficient to restore T cell function, emerging evidence indicates that Fc-mediated mechanisms may be indispensable for maximal therapeutic benefit. For instance, antibodies targeting other immune checkpoints like CTLA-4 (e.g., ipilimumab, an IgG1 antibody) and GITR have demonstrated that Fc-dependent depletion of intratumoral Tregs significantly contributes to anti-tumor immunity. Similarly, the density of target antigen on specific immune subsets—such as the markedly higher expression of TIGIT on tumor-infiltrating Tregs compared to conventional T cells—creates a biological rationale for selective elimination of immunosuppressive populations via ADCC. Furthermore, recent discoveries regarding FcγR-mediated trogocytosis—the transfer of membrane proteins from one cell to another following antibody bridging—suggest an additional layer of complexity, wherein surface removal of TIGIT could modulate receptor signaling dynamics beyond mere ligand blockade.

Despite these advances, no comprehensive comparative analysis had previously established the relative contributions of Fc-dependent versus Fc-independent mechanisms to the anti-tumor activity of anti-TIGIT antibodies. The lack of isogenic antibody pairs differing only in Fc functionality has hindered definitive conclusions. Consequently, there remains an unmet need for well-characterized anti-TIGIT antibodies that not only block inhibitory signaling but also actively reshape the tumor immune landscape through Fc-mediated effector functions. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a humanized anti-TIGIT monoclonal antibody, BGB-A1217, with a wild-type IgG1 Fc domain, and demonstrating through rigorous in vitro and in vivo experimentation that its Fc competence is essential for optimal anti-tumor efficacy via multiple coordinated mechanisms, including Treg depletion, NK cell activation, myeloid cell stimulation, and trogocytosis-mediated TIGIT downregulation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a humanized monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to human TIGIT with high affinity and blocks its interaction with ligands PVR and PVRL2, wherein the antibody comprises a wild-type human IgG1 Fc region that retains full binding capacity to Fcγ receptors (FcγRs). This Fc-competent configuration enables the antibody to mediate critical effector functions that significantly enhance its anti-tumor activity beyond simple receptor blockade. In particular, the invention demonstrates that the Fc domain is indispensable for the preferential depletion of regulatory T cells (Tregs) via antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), given the substantially higher surface expression of TIGIT on tumor-infiltrating Tregs compared to conventional effector T cells. Additionally, the Fc-competent anti-TIGIT antibody activates natural killer (NK) cells and myeloid lineage cells—including monocytes and dendritic cells—through co-engagement of TIGIT and FcγRs, leading to enhanced cytokine production, degranulation, and antigen-presenting cell maturation. A novel mechanism disclosed herein is FcγR-mediated trogocytosis, whereby the antibody facilitates the transfer and removal of TIGIT from the surface of T cells to FcγR-expressing acceptor cells such as monocytes and dendritic cells, thereby reducing inhibitory signaling and potentially restoring DNAM-1 homodimerization and co-stimulatory function.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the Fc-competent anti-TIGIT antibody, either as a single agent or in combination with an anti-PD-1 antibody, for the treatment of cancer. Synergistic enhancement of T cell activation and tumor growth inhibition is achieved when the two antibodies are co-administered, as demonstrated in both in vitro human immune cell assays and in vivo murine tumor models. The anti-TIGIT antibody of the invention, designated BGB-A1217, exhibits superior anti-tumor efficacy compared to an otherwise identical Fc-silent variant (BGB-A1217MF, containing ELLGG→PAAG and P329A mutations in the Fc region), which lacks binding to all classes of FcγRs. This differential activity confirms that Fc effector functions are not merely ancillary but central to the therapeutic mechanism of action.

Moreover, the invention includes methods for producing the anti-TIGIT antibody through recombinant DNA technology, involving the cloning and expression of heavy and light chain variable regions derived from a murine parent antibody (mu1217) grafted onto human IgG1 constant regions. Diagnostic methods are also provided for monitoring the biological effects of treatment, such as flow cytometric quantification of Treg frequencies in peripheral blood or tumor biopsies, measurement of TIGIT surface expression levels, and assessment of NK cell activation markers like CD107a. The invention thus establishes a new paradigm for anti-TIGIT therapy, wherein retention of wild-type IgG1 Fc functionality is essential for achieving maximal clinical benefit through a multifaceted immunomodulatory profile that integrates checkpoint blockade with active immune cell recruitment and remodeling of the tumor microenvironment.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

As used herein, the term “antibody” refers to an immunoglobulin molecule that specifically binds to a target antigen, typically comprising at least two heavy chains and two light chains interconnected by disulfide bonds. The term includes full-length antibodies as well as antigen-binding fragments such as Fab, Fab', F(ab')₂, Fv, scFv, and diabodies, provided they retain specific binding activity. The term “monoclonal antibody” denotes an antibody preparation consisting of a single molecular species, produced by a single clone of B cells or hybridoma, and exhibiting uniform binding specificity and affinity for its target epitope. “Humanized antibody” refers to a non-human antibody (e.g., murine) in which the complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) responsible for antigen recognition are grafted onto a human antibody framework, thereby reducing immunogenicity while preserving binding characteristics. The term “wild-type human IgG1 Fc region” designates the constant fragment of the human immunoglobulin G1 heavy chain without any amino acid substitutions that impair binding to Fcγ receptors or complement component C1q; it includes the canonical sequence encoded by the IGHG1 gene and supports full effector functions including ADCC, ADCP, and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). In contrast, an “Fc-silent” or “effector function-disabled” Fc region contains mutations (e.g., L234A/L235A [LALA], E233P/L234A/L235A/G236G [PAGG], or P329A) that abolish or significantly reduce interactions with FcγRs and C1q.

“TIGIT” (T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains) is a type I transmembrane protein encoded by the TIGIT gene in humans (also known as VSIG9, VSTM3, or WUCAM), comprising an extracellular immunoglobulin variable (IgV)-like domain, a transmembrane region, and a cytoplasmic tail containing an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM) and an immunoglobulin tail tyrosine (ITT)-like motif. Its primary ligands are PVR (CD155) and PVRL2 (CD112), both members of the nectin family of adhesion molecules. “Regulatory T cells” or “Tregs” are a specialized subset of CD4+ T lymphocytes characterized by high expression of CD25 and the transcription factor Foxp3, which play a critical role in maintaining immune tolerance and suppressing anti-tumor immunity. “Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity” (ADCC) is a mechanism whereby an antibody bound to a target cell engages FcγRIIIa (CD16a) on natural killer (NK) cells, triggering the release of cytotoxic granules and lysis of the target. “Trogocytosis” refers to the process by which membrane fragments, including integral proteins like TIGIT, are transferred from a donor cell to an acceptor cell following antibody-mediated bridging via FcγRs; this results in the physical removal of the target antigen from the donor cell surface. “Combination therapy” signifies the concurrent or sequential administration of two or more therapeutic agents—in this case, an anti-TIGIT antibody and an anti-PD-1 antibody—with the intent of achieving synergistic anti-tumor effects greater than those attainable with either agent alone.

### Anti-TIGIT Antibodies

The present invention provides a humanized monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to the extracellular domain of human TIGIT with high affinity and effectively blocks its interaction with ligands PVR and PVRL2. This antibody, designated BGB-A1217, was derived from a murine hybridoma clone mu1217 generated by immunizing mice with recombinant human TIGIT protein. The variable regions of the heavy and light chains of mu1217 were sequenced, and the murine framework residues were replaced with corresponding sequences from human germline immunoglobulin genes to minimize immunogenicity while preserving the original antigen-binding properties. The resulting humanized antibody comprises human IgG1 constant regions for both heavy and light chains, ensuring compatibility with standard manufacturing processes and favorable pharmacokinetic profiles in humans.

BGB-A1217 exhibits exceptional binding kinetics to human TIGIT, as determined by surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assay, with an equilibrium dissociation constant (KD) of 0.135 nM, reflecting a combination of rapid association (kon) and slow dissociation (koff) rates. In cell-based binding assays using TIGIT-overexpressing BW5147.3 cells, BGB-A1217 demonstrates an EC50 of 0.54 nM, confirming potent recognition of native, membrane-bound TIGIT in its physiological conformation. Critically, BGB-A1217 completely inhibits the binding of TIGIT to its ligands: in competition assays with HEK293 cells expressing PVR or PVRL2, it achieves IC50 values of 4.53 nM and 1.69 nM, respectively, indicating robust blockade of both high- and low-affinity interactions. This dual-ligand inhibition is essential for overcoming the redundancy in TIGIT signaling within the tumor microenvironment, where both PVR and PVRL2 are commonly upregulated on malignant and stromal cells.

Structurally, the antigen-binding site of BGB-A1217 is defined by six complementarity-determining regions (CDRs)—three from the heavy chain (HCDR1, HCDR2, HCDR3) and three from the light chain (LCDR1, LCDR2, LCDR3)—which form a precise three-dimensional interface complementary to an epitope on the IgV-like domain of TIGIT. This epitope overlaps with the ligand-binding site, thereby sterically hindering access of PVR and PVRL2. Importantly, BGB-A1217 does not cross-react with murine TIGIT, necessitating the use of human TIGIT knock-in mouse models for preclinical evaluation. However, surrogate murine antibodies (e.g., mu10A7) with analogous functional properties have been developed for studies in wild-type murine tumor models.

The therapeutic superiority of BGB-A1217 stems not only from its high-affinity blockade but also from its Fc-competent design. Unlike Fc-silent variants, BGB-A1217 engages a broad spectrum of Fcγ receptors—including FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIA/B (CD32), and FcγRIIIA (CD16a)—with high avidity, enabling a suite of effector functions that actively remodel the tumor immune landscape. This multifunctionality distinguishes BGB-A1217 from purely blocking antibodies and underpins its enhanced anti-tumor efficacy, as detailed in subsequent sections.

### Anti-PD1 Antibodies

In accordance with the invention, the anti-TIGIT antibody BGB-A1217 is administered in combination with an anti-PD-1 antibody to achieve synergistic anti-tumor effects. PD-1 (programmed cell death protein 1) is a well-established immune checkpoint receptor expressed on activated T cells, B cells, and myeloid cells, which upon binding to its ligands PD-L1 or PD-L2, delivers inhibitory signals that dampen T cell proliferation, cytokine production, and cytotoxic activity. In the tumor microenvironment, persistent antigen exposure leads to T cell exhaustion, characterized by sustained co-expression of multiple inhibitory receptors including PD-1 and TIGIT. Monotherapy with anti-PD-1 antibodies (e.g., pembrolizumab, nivolumab) can reinvigorate exhausted T cells, but response rates remain suboptimal in many indications, partly due to compensatory upregulation of alternative checkpoints like TIGIT.

The anti-PD-1 antibody employed in the present invention, designated BGB-A317, is a humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to human PD-1 and blocks its interaction with PD-L1 and PD-L2. In preclinical assays, BGB-A317 restores T cell effector functions, as evidenced by increased IFN-γ secretion and proliferation in response to antigenic stimulation. When combined with BGB-A1217, the two antibodies act cooperatively to overcome the layered immunosuppression characteristic of advanced tumors. In vitro, co-culture of pre-activated human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) with PD-L1-expressing tumor cells reveals that the combination induces significantly higher levels of IFN-γ release than either antibody alone, indicating mitigation of T cell exhaustion through dual checkpoint blockade. This synergy is further corroborated by intracellular cytokine staining, which shows enhanced frequencies of IFN-γ- and TNF-α-producing CD8+ T cells in the presence of both antibodies.

In vivo, the combination of BGB-A1217 and a surrogate anti-mouse PD-1 antibody (Ch15mt) demonstrates superior tumor growth inhibition compared to monotherapies in syngeneic mouse models of colon carcinoma (MC38) and renal cell carcinoma (Renca). Notably, the efficacy of the combination is dependent on the Fc competence of BGB-A1217, as replacement with the Fc-silent variant BGB-A1217MF abolishes the added benefit. This observation underscores that the synergy arises not merely from additive receptor blockade but from the integration of Fc-mediated mechanisms—such as Treg depletion and NK cell activation—enabled by BGB-A1217. Thus, the invention establishes a rational combination regimen wherein anti-PD-1 relieves intrinsic T cell inhibition while anti-TIGIT actively eliminates suppressive elements and enhances innate immune activation, collectively fostering a more robust and durable anti-tumor response.

### Further Alteration of the Framework of Fc Region

While the preferred embodiment of the invention utilizes a wild-type human IgG1 Fc region to preserve full effector functionality, the disclosure contemplates strategic modifications to the Fc framework to fine-tune biological activity for specific therapeutic contexts. Such alterations may include amino acid substitutions that modulate binding affinity to particular Fcγ receptor isoforms, thereby enhancing selectivity for desired effector functions while minimizing off-target effects. For example, mutations that increase affinity for FcγRIIIa (e.g., S298A/E333A/K334A [AAK]) could potentiate ADCC against TIGIT-high Tregs without exacerbating cytokine release syndrome. Conversely, mutations that reduce binding to FcγRIIb (an inhibitory receptor) might amplify activating signals in myeloid cells.

Another class of Fc engineering involves glycoform manipulation, such as afucosylation, which dramatically enhances affinity for FcγRIIIa and boosts ADCC potency. Although the exemplified antibody BGB-A1217 is produced with native glycosylation, afucosylated variants could be generated using cell lines engineered to lack fucosyltransferase activity (e.g., POTELLIGENT® technology). Such variants would be particularly advantageous in patients harboring the low-affinity F158 polymorphism of FcγRIIIa, who may exhibit suboptimal responses to standard IgG1 antibodies.

Importantly, any Fc modification must be evaluated in the context of TIGIT biology. Given that TIGIT is expressed on both effector and regulatory lymphocytes, excessive ADCC could theoretically deplete beneficial immune subsets. However, the high target density on Tregs relative to conventional T cells provides a therapeutic window for selective depletion. Therefore, Fc alterations should aim to preserve or enhance this selectivity rather than indiscriminately amplifying cytotoxicity. The invention thus provides a platform for iterative Fc optimization, guided by mechanistic insights into TIGIT expression patterns and FcγR biology in the tumor microenvironment.

### Antibody Production

The anti-TIGIT antibody of the invention is produced using standard recombinant DNA and protein expression techniques well known in the art. The nucleotide sequences encoding the heavy and light chain variable regions of the murine parent antibody mu1217 are first isolated from hybridoma mRNA via reverse transcription and PCR amplification. These sequences are then subjected to humanization by grafting the murine CDRs onto selected human germline framework regions (e.g., VH3-23 for heavy chain, VK1-39 for light chain) using molecular modeling to ensure structural compatibility. The humanized variable region sequences are cloned into mammalian expression vectors containing human IgG1 constant region genes for the heavy chain and human kappa constant region for the light chain.

The resulting plasmids are co-transfected into a suitable host cell line—typically Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells or human embryonic kidney (HEK293) cells—using methods such as electroporation or lipid-mediated transfection. Stable transfectants are selected using antibiotic resistance markers (e.g., puromycin or methotrexate) and screened for high-level antibody secretion. The lead clone is expanded in bioreactors under controlled conditions to produce large quantities of the antibody, which is then purified from culture supernatant using a multi-step chromatographic process. This typically involves protein A affinity chromatography to capture IgG, followed by ion exchange and size exclusion chromatography to remove aggregates, host cell proteins, and other impurities. The final product is formulated in a buffered solution (e.g., 20 mM histidine, 150 mM sodium chloride, pH 6.0) and sterile-filtered for clinical use.

Quality control measures include assessment of purity by SDS-PAGE and capillary electrophoresis, confirmation of identity by mass spectrometry and peptide mapping, quantification of endotoxin levels, and validation of biological activity through binding and functional assays as described herein. The production process is conducted under current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) regulations to ensure consistency, safety, and efficacy for human administration.

### Methods of Detection and Diagnosis

The invention provides diagnostic methods for monitoring the biological effects of anti-TIGIT antibody treatment in patients, enabling personalized dosing and prediction of therapeutic response. Central to these methods is multiparameter flow cytometry, which allows simultaneous quantification of TIGIT expression levels, T cell subset frequencies, and activation markers in peripheral blood or tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes. Specifically, whole blood or dissociated tumor samples are stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies against CD3, CD4, CD8, CD25, Foxp3, and TIGIT, followed by fixation and permeabilization for intracellular Foxp3 detection. The frequency of Tregs (defined as CD4+CD25highFoxp3+) within the CD3+ T cell compartment is calculated, with a significant reduction post-treatment serving as a pharmacodynamic biomarker of Fc-mediated ADCC activity.

Additionally, surface expression of TIGIT on T cells and NK cells is measured as mean fluorescence intensity (MFI), with decreases indicative of trogocytosis or internalization. Co-staining for DNAM-1 (CD226) provides complementary information, as upregulation of this activating receptor often accompanies TIGIT downregulation. NK cell activation status is assessed by measuring CD107a exposure—a marker of degranulation—following ex vivo stimulation with tumor cells in the presence of patient serum. Elevated CD107a levels correlate with functional NK engagement and predict clinical benefit.

Serum biomarkers may also be monitored, including cytokines such as IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-2, which reflect systemic immune activation. Multiplex immunoassays (e.g., Luminex) enable simultaneous quantification of multiple analytes from small sample volumes. Together, these diagnostic tools facilitate real-time assessment of drug mechanism of action and guide clinical decision-making, such as continuation of therapy or escalation to combination regimens.

### Methods of Treatment

The anti-TIGIT antibody of the invention is administered to patients suffering from cancer, particularly those with solid tumors known to express PVR/PVRL2 and harbor TIGIT+ immune infiltrates, including but not limited to non-small cell lung cancer, melanoma, colorectal cancer, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, and renal cell carcinoma. Treatment may be initiated as monotherapy or, preferably, in combination with an anti-PD-1 antibody to exploit synergistic mechanisms. The antibody is formulated for intravenous infusion, typically at doses ranging from 1 to 20 mg/kg body weight, administered every two to three weeks. Dosing regimens are optimized based on pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic modeling and patient-specific factors such as tumor burden and immune status.

In clinical practice, patients are stratified based on baseline biomarkers predictive of response, such as high TIGIT expression on TILs, elevated Treg:Teff ratios, or co-expression of PD-1 and TIGIT on exhausted T cells. Those with primary resistance to anti-PD-1 monotherapy are prime candidates for combination therapy. Treatment continues until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity, with regular monitoring of immune parameters as described above. The Fc-competent nature of the antibody ensures not only checkpoint blockade but also active remodeling of the tumor microenvironment through Treg depletion and innate immune activation, thereby converting immunologically “cold” tumors into “hot” ones amenable to immune attack.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Formulations

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise the anti-TIGIT antibody BGB-A1217 in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier suitable for parenteral administration. The antibody is formulated at concentrations of 10–100 mg/mL in an aqueous buffer system designed to maintain stability, solubility, and biological activity during storage and delivery. A typical formulation includes 20 mM histidine, 150 mM sodium chloride, 0.02% polysorbate 80, adjusted to pH 6.0 with hydrochloric acid or sodium hydroxide. Excipients such as sucrose or trehalose may be added as cryoprotectants for lyophilized products. The composition is sterile-filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane and filled into glass vials or prefilled syringes under aseptic conditions.

Stability studies demonstrate that the formulated antibody retains >95% monomeric integrity and full binding activity after 24 months at 2–8°C. The composition is compatible with standard infusion protocols, requiring no special handling beyond routine biologic drug precautions. For combination therapy, separate vials of anti-TIGIT and anti-PD-1 antibodies are administered sequentially during the same clinic visit to avoid potential incompatibilities.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Kits

The invention further provides kits for the convenient administration of the anti-TIGIT antibody, either alone or in combination with an anti-PD-1 antibody. Each kit contains one or more vials of lyophilized or liquid antibody, accompanied by diluent vials (if needed), sterile transfer devices, and detailed instructions for reconstitution and infusion. Combination kits include matched doses of both antibodies, pre-calculated based on average patient weight, to simplify dosing and reduce preparation errors. ## EXAMPLE

### Example 1: Generation of Anti-TIGIT Monoclonal Antibody

Murine monoclonal antibodies against human TIGIT were generated by immunizing BALB/c mice with recombinant human TIGIT extracellular domain (ECD) fused to a human Fc tag. Following booster immunizations, splenocytes were harvested and fused with SP2/0 myeloma cells using polyethylene glycol-mediated hybridoma technology. Resulting hybridomas were screened by ELISA for binding to human TIGIT-Fc but not to irrelevant Fc-fusion proteins. Positive clones were subcloned by limiting dilution, and clone mu1217 was selected based on its high binding affinity and ability to block TIGIT–PVR interaction in preliminary assays. The mu1217 antibody was isotyped as IgG1κ and subsequently used as the parental antibody for humanization.

### Example 2: Cloning and Sequence Analysis of TIGIT Antibodies

Total RNA was extracted from mu1217 hybridoma cells and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. The variable region genes of the heavy and light chains were amplified by PCR using degenerate primers specific for murine immunoglobulin frameworks. The amplified products were cloned into TA vectors and sequenced to determine the exact nucleotide and amino acid sequences of the variable domains. Sequence analysis revealed that the heavy chain variable region belonged to the VHJ558 family, while the light chain was of the Vκ21 family. The complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) were identified according to the Kabat numbering system, and these CDR sequences were grafted onto human acceptor frameworks (VH3-23 and VK1-39) selected for high sequence homology and structural compatibility. The resulting humanized variable region genes were synthesized and cloned into expression vectors containing human IgG1 and κ constant regions.

### Example 3: Affinity Determination of Purified Murine Anti-TIGIT Antibodies by SPR

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis was performed on a Biacore T200 instrument to determine the binding kinetics of mu1217 to human TIGIT. An anti-human Fc capture antibody was immobilized on a CM5 sensor chip, and mu1217 was captured via its Fc region. Serial dilutions of recombinant human TIGIT-His (0.078–20 nM) were injected over the chip surface, and binding responses were recorded in real time. Data were fitted to a 1:1 Langmuir binding model using Biacore Evaluation Software, yielding an association rate constant (kon) of 2.1 × 10⁵ M⁻¹s⁻¹, a dissociation rate constant (koff) of 2.8 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, and an equilibrium dissociation constant (KD) of 0.133 nM, confirming high-affinity interaction.

### Example 4: Humanization of the Murine Anti-Human TIGIT mAb mu1217

The humanized antibody was constructed by replacing the murine framework regions of mu1217 with human germline sequences while retaining the original CDRs. Several back-mutations were introduced into the human frameworks to restore critical vernier zone residues that support CDR loop conformation. The final humanized construct, designated BGB-A1217, was expressed in HEK293 cells and purified by protein A chromatography. Binding affinity to human TIGIT was confirmed by SPR (KD = 0.135 nM), and functional activity was validated in ligand blockade assays, showing complete inhibition of TIGIT–PVR binding at nanomolar concentrations.

### Example 5: Binding Activities of Different Versions of 1217 to Native TIGIT

To compare binding to native TIGIT, BW5147.3 cells stably transfected with human TIGIT were stained with BGB-A1217 (wild-type IgG1 Fc) or BGB-A1217MF (Fc-silent mutant). Both antibodies showed identical binding curves with EC50 values of approximately 0.54 nM, demonstrating that Fc mutations do not affect antigen recognition. Flow cytometry histograms confirmed equivalent mean fluorescence intensities, indicating preserved epitope specificity and affinity in the Fc-silent variant.

### Example 6: Anti-TIGIT Antibodies Block the Interactions of TIGIT with its Ligands PVR and PVR-L2

HEK293 cells expressing PVR or PVRL2 were incubated with serial dilutions of BGB-A1217 or BGB-A1217MF in the presence of a fixed concentration of TIGIT-mIgG2a fusion protein. After washing, bound TIGIT-mIgG2a was detected using an AF647-conjugated anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody. Both antibodies dose-dependently inhibited TIGIT binding to PVR (IC50 = 4.53 nM) and PVRL2 (IC50 = 1.69 nM), with no significant difference between Fc variants, confirming that ligand blockade is an Fc-independent function.

### Example 7: Activation of CMV-Specific Human T Cells by Anti-TIGIT Antibodies

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from HLA-A2.1+ donors were stimulated with CMV pp65 peptide to expand antigen-specific T cells. These effectors were co-cultured with pp65-pulsed HCT116 target cells in the presence of BGB-A1217 or BGB-A1217MF. Supernatants were harvested after 18 hours, and IFN-γ levels were measured by ELISA. Both antibodies enhanced IFN-γ production in a dose-dependent manner, with no significant difference between groups, demonstrating that T cell activation via checkpoint blockade occurs independently of Fc effector functions.

### Example 8: Anti-TIGIT Antibodies Enhanced NK Cell-Mediated Cytotoxicity

NK92MI cells engineered to express CD16a-V158 were used as effectors in an ADCC assay against BW5147.3/TIGIT target cells. Co-culture with BGB-A1217 induced potent, dose-dependent lysis of targets as measured by LDH release, while BGB-A1217MF and human IgG controls showed no activity. Flow cytometry confirmed upregulation of CD107a and perforin in NK cells only with BGB-A1217, establishing Fc-dependent NK activation.

### Example 9: Anti-TIGIT Antibodies can Reduce the Surface Expression of TIGIT Receptor Via FcγR-Mediated Trogocytosis

Jurkat/TIGIT/DNAM-1 cells labeled with CFSE were co-cultured with HEK293 cells expressing various FcγRs in the presence of CF633-labeled BGB-A1217 or BGB-A1217MF. After overnight incubation, TIGIT (CF633) MFI on Jurkat cells was measured by flow cytometry. BGB-A1217, but not BGB-A1217MF, induced significant TIGIT loss, with FcγRI showing the strongest effect. In primary cell assays, co-culture of human T cells with autologous monocytes or DCs confirmed Fc-dependent trogocytosis, which was blocked by anti-CD64 or anti-CD32 antibodies, respectively.
