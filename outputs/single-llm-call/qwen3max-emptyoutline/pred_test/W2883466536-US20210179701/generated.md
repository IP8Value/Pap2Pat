# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and therapeutic antibody development, specifically to monoclonal antibodies that selectively bind to and neutralize human platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC). The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising such antibodies, diagnostic assays for detecting PDGF-CC expression in biological samples, kits for performing said assays, and methods for screening compounds that modulate PDGF-CC activity. Additionally, the invention provides methods for treating diseases and pathological conditions associated with aberrant PDGF-CC signaling, including but not limited to various cancers, fibrotic disorders, and central nervous system (CNS) pathologies characterized by blood-brain barrier (BBB) or blood-retinal barrier (BRB) dysfunction. The antibodies described herein exhibit high specificity for human PDGF-CC without cross-reactivity to other members of the PDGF family, such as PDGF-AA, PDGF-BB, or PDGF-DD, and effectively inhibit PDGF-CC-induced activation of its cognate receptor, PDGFRα. These properties render the disclosed antibodies particularly suitable for both diagnostic and therapeutic applications in human medicine.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Platelet-derived growth factors (PDGFs) constitute a family of structurally related dimeric proteins that play critical roles in embryonic development, tissue homeostasis, wound healing, and pathological processes such as tumorigenesis, fibrosis, and vascular dysfunction. The PDGF family includes five known ligands—PDGF-AA, -BB, -AB, -CC, and -DD—which exert their biological effects by binding to and activating two receptor tyrosine kinases, PDGFRα and PDGFRβ. Among these ligands, PDGF-CC is unique in that it is synthesized as a latent precursor requiring proteolytic cleavage for activation. Full-length PDGF-CC comprises an N-terminal CUB (complement C1r/C1s, Uegf, Bmp1) domain and a C-terminal growth factor domain (GFD). Activation occurs through cleavage by specific proteases, such as tissue plasminogen activator (tPA), which releases the bioactive GFD dimer capable of binding to and dimerizing PDGFRα homodimers, thereby initiating intracellular signaling cascades that regulate cell proliferation, migration, survival, and contraction.

Dysregulation of the PDGF/PDGFR signaling axis has been implicated in a wide spectrum of human diseases. In oncology, autocrine and paracrine PDGF signaling contributes to the pathogenesis of gliomas, sarcomas, leukemias, and epithelial cancers. PDGF-CC, in particular, functions as a potent proto-oncogene and mitogen for mesenchymal cell types, including cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), smooth muscle cells, and certain stem cells. Overexpression of PDGF-CC promotes tumor growth by recruiting CAFs into the tumor stroma and enhancing angiogenesis through induction of osteopontin. Moreover, elevated PDGF-CC levels have been linked to resistance against conventional chemotherapeutics, such as cisplatin in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, and anti-angiogenic therapies targeting VEGF. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that genetic or pharmacological inhibition of PDGF-CC can reverse therapeutic resistance and suppress tumor progression.

Beyond cancer, PDGF-CC plays a pivotal role in fibrotic diseases affecting multiple organs. Transgenic mouse models overexpressing PDGF-CC develop liver steatosis, fibrosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma, while experimental models implicate PDGF-CC in renal interstitial fibrosis and fibrosis of the heart and lungs. These findings underscore the profibrotic potential of PDGF-CC and highlight its relevance as a therapeutic target in chronic organ dysfunction.

Perhaps most notably, PDGF-CC has emerged as a key regulator of vascular barrier integrity in the central nervous system. Intraventricular administration of active PDGF-CC disrupts the blood-brain barrier (BBB), leading to increased permeability and neuroinflammation. Conversely, inhibition of the PDGF-CC/PDGFRα axis using small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors, such as imatinib, has shown protective effects in animal models of stroke, spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, seizures, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. A recent phase II clinical trial in ischemic stroke patients treated with thrombolysis plus imatinib demonstrated significant neurological improvement compared to thrombolysis alone, providing clinical validation for targeting this pathway.

Despite the compelling evidence implicating PDGF-CC in diverse pathologies, there remains a critical unmet need for molecular tools that specifically and potently neutralize human PDGF-CC activity. Existing approaches rely on broad-spectrum tyrosine kinase inhibitors that lack selectivity and are associated with off-target effects. Monoclonal antibodies offer a promising alternative due to their high specificity, long half-life, and favorable safety profiles. However, prior to the present invention, no high-affinity, function-blocking monoclonal antibodies against human PDGF-CC had been reported. The absence of such reagents has hindered both mechanistic studies of PDGF-CC biology and the development of targeted therapeutics. Thus, there exists a clear and pressing need for novel antibodies capable of selectively binding human PDGF-CC, inhibiting its interaction with PDGFRα, and modulating downstream pathological processes in a clinically relevant manner.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides isolated monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to human platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC) with high affinity and neutralize its biological activity. In particular, the invention discloses murine monoclonal antibody 6B3 and its chimeric derivative ch6B3, which recognize a conformational epitope within the growth factor domain of human PDGF-CC and effectively block PDGF-CC-induced phosphorylation of PDGFRα. These antibodies do not cross-react with other PDGF family members, including PDGF-AA, PDGF-BB, or PDGF-DD, nor do they bind murine or rat PDGF-CC, making them highly specific reagents for human applications.

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the disclosed anti-PDGF-CC antibodies, formulated for administration to a subject in need thereof, for the treatment of diseases mediated by aberrant PDGF-CC signaling. Such diseases include, but are not limited to, solid tumors (e.g., glioblastoma, breast carcinoma, colorectal cancer, renal cell carcinoma, pancreatic adenocarcinoma, prostate adenocarcinoma), fibrotic disorders (e.g., hepatic fibrosis, renal fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis), and neurological conditions involving blood-brain or blood-retinal barrier disruption (e.g., ischemic stroke, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, diabetic retinopathy).

Additionally, the invention provides diagnostic methods and kits for detecting PDGF-CC expression in biological samples, such as tissue sections or bodily fluids, using the disclosed antibodies. These assays enable the identification of patients likely to benefit from anti-PDGF-CC therapy based on elevated PDGF-CC levels in tumor or stromal compartments. The invention also includes drug screening assays for identifying compounds that modulate PDGF-CC activity, either by competing with the antibody for binding or by altering PDGF-CC conformation or stability.

In one embodiment, the chimeric antibody ch6B3, comprising murine variable regions fused to human constant regions (IgG1 kappa), retains full neutralizing activity and exhibits reduced immunogenicity compared to its murine counterpart, rendering it suitable for preclinical and clinical development. The epitope recognized by 6B3 and ch6B3 has been mapped to a specific peptide sequence within the PDGF-CC growth factor domain, designated as peptide 3, enabling structure-guided optimization and engineering of next-generation antibodies.

Collectively, the antibodies, compositions, methods, and kits of the present invention represent a significant advancement in the targeted inhibition of PDGF-CC signaling, offering new avenues for diagnosis, prognosis, and therapy across a broad range of human diseases.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Treatment Methods and Pharmaceutical Formulations

The present invention provides methods for treating a subject suffering from a disease or condition mediated by aberrant PDGF-CC signaling, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an anti-PDGF-CC antibody as described herein. The term “subject” refers to a mammal, preferably a human, diagnosed with or at risk of developing a PDGF-CC-associated pathology. The diseases amenable to treatment include, without limitation, malignant neoplasms wherein PDGF-CC is overexpressed in tumor cells, stromal fibroblasts, or tumor vasculature; fibrotic diseases characterized by excessive extracellular matrix deposition in organs such as the liver, kidney, lung, or heart; and neurological or ophthalmological disorders involving pathological opening of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) or blood-retinal barrier (BRB).

Administration of the anti-PDGF-CC antibody may be performed via any clinically acceptable route, including intravenous, subcutaneous, intraperitoneal, intravitreal, or intrathecal injection, depending on the target tissue and disease indication. For systemic conditions such as metastatic cancer or multi-organ fibrosis, intravenous infusion is preferred to ensure widespread distribution. For localized CNS or ocular pathologies, direct delivery routes may enhance efficacy and reduce systemic exposure.

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise the anti-PDGF-CC antibody in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. Suitable formulations include aqueous solutions buffered to physiological pH (e.g., phosphate-buffered saline), lyophilized powders for reconstitution, or sustained-release depots. The antibody concentration typically ranges from 0.1 mg/mL to 100 mg/mL, with dosing regimens tailored to achieve therapeutic serum levels sufficient to neutralize circulating and tissue-bound PDGF-CC. Dosing may be single or repeated, with intervals determined by pharmacokinetic parameters such as half-life and clearance rate. In exemplary embodiments, the chimeric antibody ch6B3 is administered at a dose of 1–20 mg/kg body weight, once weekly or biweekly.

The anti-PDGF-CC antibodies may be used as monotherapy or in combination with other therapeutic agents. In oncology, combinations with chemotherapy (e.g., cisplatin), radiotherapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors, or anti-angiogenic drugs (e.g., bevacizumab) may yield synergistic effects by targeting complementary pathways. In fibrotic diseases, co-administration with antifibrotics such as pirfenidone or nintedanib may enhance efficacy. In neurological indications, adjunctive use with thrombolytics (e.g., tPA in stroke) or neuroprotective agents may improve outcomes.

The therapeutic efficacy of the antibodies is predicated on their ability to bind PDGF-CC with high affinity (KD in the low nanomolar range) and prevent its interaction with PDGFRα, thereby inhibiting receptor dimerization, autophosphorylation, and downstream signaling. This blockade translates into reduced tumor growth, diminished fibroblast activation, attenuated angiogenesis, and restoration of vascular barrier integrity, as demonstrated in preclinical models.

### PDGF-C/PDGF-CC Detection/Diagnosis Assays

The invention further provides diagnostic assays for detecting and quantifying PDGF-CC in biological samples, facilitating patient stratification and monitoring of therapeutic response. These assays utilize the disclosed anti-PDGF-CC antibodies as capture or detection reagents in immunoassay formats, including enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), immunohistochemistry (IHC), immunofluorescence (IF), Western blotting, and flow cytometry.

In IHC, formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections are deparaffinized, subjected to antigen retrieval, and incubated with the anti-PDGF-CC antibody (e.g., 6B3 or ch6B3) at a predetermined concentration (e.g., 1.5 μg/mL). Specific binding is visualized using enzyme-conjugated secondary antibodies (e.g., horseradish peroxidase) and chromogenic substrates (e.g., DAB), with staining intensity and distribution scored using standardized systems such as the H-score. This enables assessment of PDGF-CC expression in tumor cells, stromal compartments, and blood vessels, with elevated levels correlating with disease aggressiveness and poor prognosis in multiple cancer types.

ELISA-based assays allow quantitative measurement of soluble PDGF-CC in serum, plasma, cerebrospinal fluid, or vitreous humor. Microtiter plates are coated with a capture antibody, samples are added, and bound PDGF-CC is detected using a biotinylated or enzyme-labeled detection antibody. Standard curves generated with recombinant PDGF-CC permit absolute quantification.

The specificity of the assays is confirmed by competitive inhibition with excess recombinant PDGF-CC, which abolishes signal, whereas unrelated proteins (e.g., VEGF-B) do not interfere. Cross-reactivity testing ensures no detection of PDGF-AA, -BB, or -DD.

These diagnostic tools are integral to precision medicine approaches, identifying patients with PDGF-CC-driven pathologies who are most likely to respond to anti-PDGF-CC therapy.

### Kits

The invention includes kits for performing the aforementioned diagnostic and research assays. Each kit contains at least one vial of the anti-PDGF-CC antibody (e.g., 6B3 or ch6B3), along with ancillary reagents necessary for the intended application. For IHC kits, components may include antigen retrieval buffer, blocking serum, detection system (e.g., HRP-conjugated secondary antibody), chromogen, and counterstain. ELISA kits comprise pre-coated plates, standards, detection antibodies, wash buffer, and substrate solution. Instructions for use, including recommended dilutions, incubation times, and interpretation criteria, are provided.

Kits may also include control tissues or samples with known PDGF-CC expression status to validate assay performance. All components are optimized for compatibility and stability, ensuring reproducible results across laboratories.

### Drug Screening Assay

The invention provides a drug screening assay for identifying compounds that modulate PDGF-CC activity. The assay is based on the competition between test compounds and the anti-PDGF-CC antibody for binding to immobilized PDGF-CC. In a typical format, PDGF-CC is immobilized on a biosensor chip or microtiter plate. Test compounds are pre-incubated with the antibody or added simultaneously, and binding is measured using surface plasmon resonance (SPR), ELISA, or fluorescence polarization.

Compounds that reduce antibody binding are candidate inhibitors of PDGF-CC, either by direct competition for the 6B3 epitope or by inducing conformational changes that mask the epitope. Alternatively, the assay can be adapted to detect stabilizers or activators of PDGF-CC by monitoring enhanced binding under denaturing conditions.

This platform enables high-throughput screening of chemical libraries, natural products, or biologics for novel PDGF-CC modulators, accelerating drug discovery efforts.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Generation of Monoclonal Antibodies to Human PDGF-CC

Monoclonal antibodies against human PDGF-CC were generated by immunizing mice with recombinant human PDGF-CC core domain protein. Hybridomas were produced using standard fusion techniques and screened for reactivity to PDGF-CC by ELISA and Western blot. Sixteen hybridoma clones exhibiting strong binding were selected, expanded, and subcloned. IgG was purified from culture supernatants using Protein G affinity chromatography, yielding antibodies designated 6B3, 9A5, 11F5, 19A1, 19B1, 19C7, and 19D1. These antibodies specifically recognized human PDGF-CC in both latent and activated forms under reducing and non-reducing conditions, with no cross-reactivity to mouse or rat PDGF-CC.

### Example 2—Amino Acid Sequence of Antibodies

The complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) of the heavy and light chains of antibodies 6B3, 11F5, and 19C7 were sequenced. Notably, 6B3 and 19C7 exhibited identical CDR sequences, explaining their overlapping epitope specificity as determined by competitive binding assays. The CDR sequences are critical for antigen recognition and define the unique binding properties of each antibody clone.

### Example 3—Generation of Chimeric Anti-PDGF-CC Antibody 6B3 (ch6B3)

To reduce immunogenicity for potential human use, the murine 6B3 antibody was converted into a chimeric form by cloning its variable heavy and light chain regions upstream of human IgG1 and kappa constant regions, respectively, in glutamine synthetase expression vectors. The resulting construct, pDGVch6B3, was transfected into CHO cells, and ch6B3 was expressed and purified. The chimeric antibody retained identical antigen-binding and neutralizing properties to the parental murine 6B3, with only a single amino acid difference in the heavy chain variable region.

### Example 4—Identification of the 6B3 Binding Epitope on PDGF-C

Epitope mapping was performed using molecular modeling and peptide competition assays. Four putative binding sites on PDGF-CC were predicted, and corresponding synthetic peptides were generated. Only peptide 3 inhibited 6B3 binding to PDGF-CC in a dose-dependent manner in biosensor assays. Direct binding of 6B3 to immobilized peptide 3 confirmed that this sequence constitutes the core epitope.

### Example 5—Biosensor Confirmation of Human PDGF-CC Specificity

Surface plasmon resonance analysis demonstrated that 6B3, 11F5, 19C7, and ch6B3 bind human PDGF-CC with high affinity (KD in low nanomolar range) and show no binding to PDGF-DD or other PDGF isoforms. Competition assays confirmed distinct or overlapping epitopes among the clones, with 6B3 and 19C7 sharing an identical binding site.

### Example 6—PDGF-Ligand Induced Receptor Activation and Cell Proliferation

Neutralization assays using PAE cells overexpressing PDGFRα showed that pre-incubation of PDGF-CC with 6B3 or ch6B3 completely abolished receptor phosphorylation. Five clones (6B3, 11F5, 14D5, 19B1, 19C7) exhibited neutralizing activity, confirming their functional blockade of PDGF-CC signaling.

### Example 7—Immunohistochemistry—Tissue Screens

Immunohistochemical analysis of 17 normal and 16 tumor tissues revealed widespread PDGF-CC expression in epithelial cells, stroma, and vasculature. Tumors exhibited significantly higher PDGF-CC levels than matched controls in bladder, brain, breast, colon, kidney, pancreas, and prostate (p<0.05). Staining was specific, as confirmed by competition with PDGF-CC but not VEGF-B.

### Example 8—In Vivo Therapy Studies

In a breast cancer xenograft model, treatment with 6B3 reduced tumor size and microvascular density by approximately 30% and induced a phenotypic shift toward hormone receptor positivity, enhancing sensitivity to endocrine therapy. These results demonstrate the therapeutic potential of anti-PDGF-CC antibodies in oncology.

### Example 9—Modulation of PDGF-CC Mediated Opening of the Blood-Brain Barrier by Anti-PDGF-C Antibody 6B3

In a blood-retinal barrier leakage model, intraperitoneal administration of ch6B3 prevented PDGF-CC-induced vascular permeability following intraocular PDGF-CC injection. Control mice exhibited significant dextran extravasation, whereas ch6B3-treated mice maintained barrier integrity. This confirms the in vivo efficacy of ch6B3 in neutralizing PDGF-CC-mediated barrier disruption, supporting its use in CNS and ocular diseases.
