# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of natural product biosynthesis, particularly within the domain of microbial secondary metabolism, has long been a focal point for the discovery of novel compounds with applications in agriculture, medicine, and industry. Terpenoids, also known as isoprenoids, constitute one of the largest and most structurally diverse classes of natural products found across all domains of life. These compounds are derived from the universal five-carbon building blocks isopentenyl diphosphate (IPP) and its isomer dimethylallyl diphosphate (DMAPP), which are assembled into linear prenyl diphosphates of varying lengths—such as geranyl diphosphate (GPP, C10), farnesyl diphosphate (FPP, C15), and geranylgeranyl diphosphate (GGPP, C20)—that serve as substrates for terpene synthases (TPSs). TPSs catalyze the cyclization, rearrangement, and termination of these linear precursors to generate an immense array of carbon skeletons, which are subsequently modified by tailoring enzymes to yield functionally diverse terpenoids.

Among fungi, the phylum Basidiomycota represents a vast and underexplored reservoir of terpenoid biosynthetic potential. While Ascomycota fungi have been extensively studied and are known to produce a wide range of bioactive terpenoids—including mycotoxins, antibiotics, and immunosuppressants—the metabolic capabilities of basidiomycetes, particularly non-wood-decaying and symbiotic species, remain poorly characterized. Recent advances in genome sequencing and bioinformatic analysis have revealed that many basidiomycete genomes encode multiple putative terpene synthase genes, suggesting a significant but latent capacity for terpenoid production. Notably, sesquiterpenoids (C15) from basidiomycetes often exhibit unusual structural features and potent biological activities, such as antimicrobial, cytotoxic, and anti-inflammatory properties, making them attractive targets for biotechnological exploitation.

Phylogenetic analyses of fungal sesquiterpene synthases (STSs) have uncovered a remarkable correlation between enzyme sequence and catalytic mechanism. Specifically, STSs from Basidiomycota can be classified into distinct phylogenetic clades, each associated with a conserved initial cyclization step—such as 1,10-closure of E,E-farnesyl diphosphate (E,E-FPP) or its isomer (3R)-nerolidyl diphosphate (3R-NPP)—that dictates the resulting terpenoid scaffold. This structure-function relationship enables predictive functional annotation of uncharacterized STS genes based solely on sequence homology, thereby facilitating targeted discovery of novel terpenoid pathways.

*Serendipita indica* (formerly *Piriformospora indica*), a root-colonizing endophytic fungus belonging to the Sebacinales order within Basidiomycota, has emerged as a model organism for studying plant-microbe mutualism. This fungus establishes a biphasic symbiotic relationship with a broad host range of monocot and dicot plants, conferring benefits such as enhanced nutrient uptake, increased biomass, and improved resistance to biotic and abiotic stresses. Despite its well-documented role in modulating plant physiology and immunity, *S. indica* was previously thought to lack the genetic machinery for specialized metabolite production, as its genome appeared to be devoid of large gene clusters typically associated with secondary metabolism in other fungi. Consequently, the mechanisms by which *S. indica* mediates its beneficial interactions—particularly through the secretion of small molecules—remained incompletely understood.

The absence of detectable terpenoid production in *S. indica* cultures, coupled with the lack of canonical biosynthetic gene clusters in its genome, led to the assumption that this fungus relies primarily on proteinaceous effectors and phytohormone mimicry for host interaction. However, the possibility that *S. indica* might produce low-abundance or conditionally expressed terpenoids had not been rigorously investigated. The discovery of a single terpene synthase gene, designated *SiTPS*, in the *S. indica* genome thus represented a significant and unexpected finding, challenging the prevailing view of this endophyte as a metabolically minimalist symbiont.

Prior to the present invention, no functional terpene synthase had been identified or characterized in *S. indica*, and no sesquiterpenoid products had been attributed to its metabolism. Furthermore, while viridiflorol—a tricyclic sesquiterpene alcohol with a 7/5/3 ring system belonging to the aromadendrane family—had been reported in various plant species and occasionally as a minor component in fungal extracts, only two dedicated viridiflorol synthases had been molecularly characterized: one from plants and another from the basidiomycete *Cyclocybe aegerita*. The enzymatic origin of viridiflorol in other contexts remained speculative, and its potential ecological roles in microbial systems were largely unexplored. Critically, there was no evidence linking viridiflorol or its biosynthetic machinery to fungal defense mechanisms or plant-microbe interactions.

Thus, there existed a clear need for the identification, functional characterization, and application of novel terpene synthases from understudied symbiotic fungi, particularly those capable of producing bioactive sesquiterpenoids with agricultural or pharmaceutical relevance. The present invention addresses this need by disclosing the first terpene synthase from *S. indica*, demonstrating its specific enzymatic activity as a viridiflorol synthase, and revealing its potential role in mediating fungal-fungal antagonism—a function that may contribute to the ecological success of *S. indica* in the competitive rhizosphere environment. This discovery not only expands the known repertoire of fungal terpenoid biosynthetic enzymes but also provides a new tool for the sustainable production of viridiflorol and related compounds, as well as a genetic basis for engineering microbial consortia with enhanced biocontrol properties.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel fungal sesquiterpene synthase, designated SiTPS, encoded by the gene PIIN_06735 in *Serendipita indica*, and its use in the production of the sesquiterpene alcohol viridiflorol. The invention further encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding SiTPS, expression vectors comprising said nucleic acids, recombinant host cells engineered to express SiTPS, and methods for producing viridiflorol using these recombinant systems. Additionally, the invention discloses the role of SiTPS and its enzymatic product, viridiflorol, in conferring antifungal activity against plant-pathogenic fungi, thereby providing a basis for the development of biocontrol agents and methods for enhancing plant health through microbial-mediated defense.

In one aspect, the invention provides an isolated polypeptide having sesquiterpene synthase activity, wherein the polypeptide comprises an amino acid sequence that is at least 90% identical to the amino acid sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO: 1, and wherein the polypeptide catalyzes the conversion of E,E-farnesyl diphosphate (E,E-FPP) to viridiflorol as the predominant product. In certain embodiments, the polypeptide exhibits at least 95%, 98%, or 99% sequence identity to SEQ ID NO: 1. In further embodiments, the polypeptide is derived from *Serendipita indica* or a related *Serendipita* species.

In another aspect, the invention provides an isolated nucleic acid molecule encoding the aforementioned polypeptide, wherein the nucleic acid molecule comprises a sequence that is at least 90% identical to the nucleotide sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO: 2. In preferred embodiments, the nucleic acid molecule is codon-optimized for expression in a heterologous host cell, such as *Escherichia coli*, yeast, or filamentous fungi.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides an expression vector comprising the nucleic acid molecule of the invention operably linked to a promoter functional in a host cell. The expression vector may further comprise selectable markers, origins of replication, and other regulatory elements necessary for stable maintenance and high-level expression in prokaryotic or eukaryotic hosts.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a recombinant host cell transformed or transfected with the expression vector of the invention, wherein the host cell is capable of producing viridiflorol when cultured under appropriate conditions. Suitable host cells include, but are not limited to, *Escherichia coli*, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, *Pichia pastoris*, *Aspergillus niger*, and *Serendipita indica* itself.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for producing viridiflorol, comprising culturing a recombinant host cell expressing SiTPS in a medium containing a source of E,E-FPP or precursors thereof, and recovering viridiflorol from the culture. The method may further include co-expression of genes encoding enzymes of the mevalonate (MVA) or methylerythritol phosphate (MEP) pathway to enhance intracellular pools of FPP, thereby increasing viridiflorol yield.

In a related aspect, the invention provides a method for inhibiting the growth of a plant-pathogenic fungus, comprising contacting the pathogen with an effective amount of viridiflorol or a composition comprising viridiflorol. The plant-pathogenic fungus may be selected from the group consisting of *Colletotrichum truncatum*, *Fusarium oxysporum*, *Rhizoctonia solani*, *Botrytis cinerea*, and other economically significant phytopathogens. The method may be applied in vitro, in planta, or in the rhizosphere to protect crops from fungal diseases.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for enhancing the biocontrol capability of a beneficial microorganism, comprising introducing into the microorganism a nucleic acid encoding SiTPS, thereby enabling the microorganism to produce viridiflorol. The beneficial microorganism may be a plant growth-promoting rhizobacterium, a mycorrhizal fungus, or an endophytic fungus such as *Trichoderma* spp. or *Serendipita* spp.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a transgenic plant expressing SiTPS, wherein the plant exhibits increased resistance to fungal pathogens due to the localized production of viridiflorol in plant tissues. The transgenic plant may be generated by stable transformation with a plant expression vector comprising the *SiTPS* gene under the control of a constitutive, tissue-specific, or inducible promoter.

Collectively, these aspects of the invention provide a comprehensive platform for the biotechnological production and application of viridiflorol, leveraging the unique enzymatic properties of SiTPS to address needs in sustainable agriculture, natural product synthesis, and microbial ecology.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Synthetic Pathways

The present invention discloses a novel biosynthetic pathway for the production of viridiflorol, a tricyclic sesquiterpene alcohol, mediated by the fungal sesquiterpene synthase SiTPS from *Serendipita indica*. This pathway initiates with the universal sesquiterpene precursor E,E-farnesyl diphosphate (E,E-FPP), a linear C15 isoprenoid diphosphate synthesized via the mevalonate (MVA) pathway in fungi. In the first committed step, SiTPS catalyzes the ionization of E,E-FPP through the cleavage of the diphosphate group, generating a reactive allylic carbocation at C1. This cation undergoes a stereospecific 1,10-cyclization, forming a new carbon–carbon bond between C1 and C10, which results in the formation of a bicyclic germacradienyl cation intermediate. Subsequent intramolecular rearrangements, including hydride shifts and additional cyclizations, lead to the formation of a tricyclic eudesmyl cation. The reaction cascade is terminated by the stereoselective addition of a water molecule, quenching the final carbocation and yielding viridiflorol as the primary product. Minor side products, including ledol and other structurally related sesquiterpenes, may also be formed depending on the precise conformational dynamics of the enzyme’s active site and the availability of alternative quenching agents.

The enzymatic mechanism of SiTPS aligns with the phylogenetic classification of basidiomycete sesquiterpene synthases, wherein Clade I enzymes are defined by their ability to catalyze 1,10-cyclization of E,E-FPP. This mechanistic fidelity enables accurate prediction of product profiles based on sequence homology, facilitating the rational design of synthetic biology approaches for terpenoid production. The pathway described herein is distinct from alternative routes to viridiflorol observed in other organisms, such as the 1,10-cyclization of (3R)-nerolidyl diphosphate ((3R)-NPP) catalyzed by the viridiflorol synthase from *Cyclocybe aegerita* (AaVS), which belongs to Clade II. The existence of convergent evolutionary solutions to the biosynthesis of the same terpenoid scaffold underscores the chemical versatility of terpene synthases and highlights the importance of enzyme-specific engineering for optimal product yield and purity.

In recombinant microbial systems, the efficiency of viridiflorol production can be significantly enhanced by reconstructing the upstream isoprenoid biosynthetic pathway. For instance, in *Escherichia coli*, which naturally produces IPP and DMAPP via the methylerythritol phosphate (MEP) pathway, the introduction of a heterologous farnesyl diphosphate synthase (FPPS) gene—such as that from *Gallus gallus*—enables the condensation of IPP and DMAPP into E,E-FPP. Co-expression of *SiTPS* with *FPPS* and key MEP pathway genes (e.g., *dxs*, *idi*, *ispDF*) creates a metabolic channel that diverts carbon flux toward sesquiterpene biosynthesis. Similarly, in yeast or filamentous fungi, which utilize the MVA pathway, overexpression of rate-limiting enzymes such as HMG-CoA reductase can increase FPP availability, thereby boosting viridiflorol titers. The modular nature of this synthetic pathway allows for fine-tuning through promoter engineering, gene copy number variation, and cofactor optimization, making it highly adaptable to industrial-scale fermentation processes.

### Expression of Enzymes

The successful implementation of the viridiflorol biosynthetic pathway requires the controlled and efficient expression of the SiTPS enzyme in a suitable host organism. The invention provides multiple strategies for achieving high-level expression of functional SiTPS, tailored to the physiological characteristics of prokaryotic and eukaryotic hosts. In bacterial systems, such as *Escherichia coli*, the *SiTPS* coding sequence is typically cloned into an expression vector under the control of a strong inducible promoter, such as the T7 or lac promoter. To facilitate purification and detection, the SiTPS polypeptide may be fused to an affinity tag, such as a hexahistidine (His6) tag, at either the N- or C-terminus. Expression is induced by the addition of isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) during mid-logarithmic growth, followed by a temperature shift to 16–18°C to promote proper protein folding and solubility. The recombinant protein is then purified using immobilized metal affinity chromatography (IMAC) and desalted into an appropriate storage buffer containing glycerol, DTT, and MgCl2 to maintain enzymatic stability.

In eukaryotic hosts, such as *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* or *Aspergillus niger*, expression constructs are designed with fungal-compatible promoters and terminators. For example, in *S. cerevisiae*, the *SiTPS* gene may be placed under the control of the galactose-inducible *GAL1* promoter or the constitutive *TEF1* promoter, while in *A. niger*, strong promoters such as *glaA* or *cbh1* are employed. Codon optimization of the *SiTPS* sequence to match the preferred codon usage of the host organism is often performed to enhance translational efficiency and protein yield. In filamentous fungi, including *S. indica* itself, transformation is achieved via protoplast-mediated DNA delivery, and transformants are selected using antibiotic resistance markers such as hygromycin B. Stable integration of the expression cassette into the fungal genome ensures heritable expression of SiTPS across generations.

For in planta applications, plant expression vectors are constructed using binary vectors compatible with *Agrobacterium*-mediated transformation. The *SiTPS* gene is operably linked to a plant promoter, such as the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S (CaMV 35S) promoter for constitutive expression, or a root-specific promoter (e.g., *RB7*) to restrict viridiflorol production to the rhizosphere. Inclusion of a transit peptide may be necessary to direct subcellular localization of SiTPS to plastids or the cytosol, depending on the compartmentalization of the isoprenoid precursor pool. Transgenic plants are regenerated from transformed explants and screened for *SiTPS* integration and expression via PCR and RT-qPCR, respectively.

Regardless of the host system, the functionality of the expressed SiTPS enzyme is confirmed through in vitro or in vivo terpene synthase assays, followed by gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) analysis to verify the identity and purity of the enzymatic product. These expression platforms collectively enable the scalable and tunable production of viridiflorol for diverse applications.

### Hosts

The invention contemplates a wide range of host organisms for the expression of SiTPS and the production of viridiflorol, each offering distinct advantages in terms of genetic tractability, metabolic capacity, and scalability. Prokaryotic hosts, particularly *Escherichia coli*, are favored for rapid protein production and ease of genetic manipulation. Strains such as BL21(DE3) or C41(DE3) are commonly used due to their compatibility with T7 RNA polymerase-based expression systems and their ability to tolerate membrane-associated or toxic proteins. *E. coli*’s native MEP pathway provides a foundation for sesquiterpene biosynthesis, which can be augmented by introducing heterologous FPPS and optimizing precursor supply through plasmid-based overexpression of MEP pathway genes.

Eukaryotic microbial hosts, including the yeast *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* and the methylotrophic yeast *Pichia pastoris*, offer the benefit of post-translational modifications and a more favorable intracellular environment for the folding and activity of eukaryotic enzymes. *S. cerevisiae* possesses a robust MVA pathway that can be engineered to overproduce FPP, and its well-established genetic tools allow for precise regulation of gene expression. *P. pastoris*, on the other hand, is renowned for its high-density fermentation capabilities and strong inducible promoters (e.g., *AOX1*), making it suitable for industrial-scale production.

Filamentous fungi represent another class of hosts with inherent advantages for terpenoid biosynthesis. Species such as *Aspergillus niger* and *Trichoderma reesei* are GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe) organisms with extensive use in biotechnology, and their native secondary metabolism can be harnessed or redirected for heterologous compound production. Moreover, *Serendipita indica* itself serves as a natural host for SiTPS, and genetic engineering of this endophyte allows for the study of viridiflorol’s ecological role in a native context. Transformation of *S. indica* is achieved through PEG-mediated protoplast transformation, and selection is based on antibiotic resistance conferred by integrated marker genes.

Plant hosts are also encompassed within the scope of the invention, wherein transgenic plants expressing SiTPS can produce viridiflorol in situ, thereby acquiring enhanced resistance to fungal pathogens. Model plants such as *Arabidopsis thaliana* or crop species like tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*) and rice (*Oryza sativa*) can be transformed using standard *Agrobacterium*-mediated protocols. The choice of host depends on the intended application: microbial hosts are ideal for bulk production and purification of viridiflorol, while plant hosts are suited for in planta protection and ecological studies.

### Manufacturing

The manufacturing of viridiflorol according to the present invention involves the cultivation of recombinant host cells under controlled bioprocess conditions to maximize product yield and purity. In microbial fermentation, batch, fed-batch, or continuous culture modes may be employed depending on the host and scale of production. For *E. coli*-based systems, Terrific Broth (TB) or defined minimal media supplemented with glycerol or glucose as carbon sources are commonly used. Induction of *SiTPS* expression is typically performed at an OD600 of 0.6–0.8 with IPTG, followed by extended incubation at reduced temperatures (16–20°C) to minimize inclusion body formation and enhance soluble enzyme activity. To capture volatile terpenes, an organic overlay such as n-hexane or dodecane is added to the culture, which partitions the hydrophobic products and prevents evaporation or degradation.

In yeast fermentations, rich media such as YPD or synthetic complete media are used, and induction is triggered by carbon source shift (e.g., from glucose to galactose in *S. cerevisiae*). High-cell-density cultures in bioreactors with controlled pH, dissolved oxygen, and temperature are employed to achieve optimal productivity. For filamentous fungi, submerged liquid cultures in complex media (e.g., CM or MYP) are maintained with vigorous agitation to ensure adequate oxygen transfer and prevent mycelial clumping.

Following fermentation, the organic phase containing viridiflorol is separated by centrifugation or phase separation, and the solvent is evaporated under reduced pressure. Crude extracts are then subjected to purification using techniques such as silica gel chromatography, preparative thin-layer chromatography (TLC), or high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) to isolate viridiflorol from minor byproducts and residual media components. The identity and purity of the final product are confirmed by GC–MS, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, and comparison with authentic standards.

The manufacturing process is designed to be scalable, cost-effective, and environmentally sustainable, leveraging renewable feedstocks and minimizing waste generation. Downstream processing is optimized to recover high-purity viridiflorol suitable for pharmaceutical, cosmetic, or agricultural applications.

### Example 1: Materials and Methods

All experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with standard molecular biology, microbiology, and analytical chemistry protocols. *Serendipita indica* (DSM11827) was maintained on complete medium (CM) agar plates supplemented with 2% glucose and 1.5% agar at 28°C. Tomato seeds (*Solanum lycopersicum*, cv. Moneymaker) were surface-sterilized and germinated on sterile filter paper in a controlled growth chamber. For root inoculation, seedlings were incubated overnight in a chlamydospore suspension (300,000 spores/mL) and subsequently transferred to Murashige-Skoog (MS) agar plates. Total RNA was extracted using the Spectrum™ Plant Total RNA Kit, and cDNA was synthesized using oligo-dT primers. RT-qPCR was performed with gene-specific primers and normalized to *S. indica GAPDH*.

The *SiTPS* coding sequence was amplified from *S. indica* cDNA and cloned into the pET28b+ vector for *E. coli* expression. Protein purification was carried out using His SpinTrap columns, and enzymatic assays were performed in 50 mM HEPES buffer (pH 7.2) containing 10 mM MgCl2, 100 mM KCl, 5 mM DTT, 10% glycerol, and 5 µg of E,E-FPP. Reactions were overlaid with n-hexane and incubated at 30°C for 1 hour. For in vivo characterization, *E. coli* C41 cells were co-transformed with pACYCDuet_GgFPPS and pIRS plasmids to produce FPP, and *SiTPS* was introduced via pET28b+. Cultures were induced with IPTG and supplemented with pyruvate, and terpenes were extracted with n-hexane.

GC–MS analysis was performed on an Agilent 7890A GC with a VF-5ms column using a temperature gradient from 80°C to 325°C. Spectra were matched against the NIST17 database and verified with authentic viridiflorol and ledol standards. For *S. indica* transformation, the *SiTPS* gene was cloned into the K167 vector under the FGB1 promoter and introduced into protoplasts via PEG-mediated transformation. Transformants were selected on hygromycin-containing plates and verified by PCR. Root colonization was quantified by qPCR using fungal ITS and plant β-tubulin primers. Antifungal assays were conducted in 96-well plates with *Colletotrichum truncatum* spores and viridiflorol concentrations ranging from 50 to 500 µg/mL. Statistical analyses were performed in R using t-tests and ANOVA.

### Example 2: Phylogenetic Analysis on SiTPS

Phylogenetic analysis of SiTPS was performed to determine its evolutionary relationship with other basidiomycete sesquiterpene synthases. The amino acid sequence of SiTPS (JGI Protein ID: 77541) was aligned with functionally characterized STSs from *Coprinopsis cinerea* and *Omphalotus olearius* using the GUIDANCE2 server with CLUSTALW. A maximum-likelihood tree was constructed in MEGAX with 500 bootstrap replicates, revealing that SiTPS clusters within Clade I, which is defined by enzymes catalyzing 1,10-cyclization of E,E-FPP. A second phylogenetic tree, including putative TPSs from *Serendipita* species and *Cladonia uncialis*, confirmed that SiTPS shares highest similarity with orthologs from closely related endophytes. This phylogenetic placement provided a strong predictive basis for its enzymatic function, guiding subsequent biochemical characterization.

### Example 3: SiTPS Encodes a Viridiflorol Synthase

Functional characterization of SiTPS demonstrated that it specifically converts E,E-FPP into viridiflorol. In vitro assays with purified SiTPS-His6 protein showed activity only with E,E-FPP, not with GPP or GGPP. GC–MS analysis of the reaction products revealed a major peak with retention time and mass spectrum matching authentic viridiflorol, along with minor peaks corresponding to ledol and other sesquiterpenes. In vivo expression in an *E. coli* strain engineered to produce FPP confirmed these results, with viridiflorol as the dominant product. The proposed cyclization mechanism involves 1,10-closure of E,E-FPP, consistent with SiTPS’s placement in Clade I. This represents the first functional viridiflorol synthase identified in *S. indica* and the second in fungi.

### Example 4: SiTPS is Induced Upon Root-Colonization

RT-qPCR analysis revealed that *SiTPS* expression is significantly upregulated during symbiosis with tomato roots. Fungal biomass harvested from colonized roots at 11 days post-inoculation (dpi) showed a 3-fold increase in *SiTPS* transcript levels compared to mycelium grown on synthetic medium. Re-analysis of published microarray data from barley roots indicated that *SiTPS* is induced as early as 36–48 hours post-inoculation, particularly in living root tissue, suggesting a role in the initial stages of host interaction. This conditional expression pattern implies that viridiflorol production is tightly regulated in response to the plant environment.

### Example 5: SiTPS Implication in Colonization of Tomato Roots

To assess the role of SiTPS in root colonization, an *SiTPS*-overexpressing *S. indica* strain (ovII) was generated and compared to wild-type and empty vector controls. Quantification of fungal DNA relative to plant DNA in tomato roots at 2 and 11 dpi showed no significant difference in colonization efficiency among the strains. This indicates that viridiflorol production does not enhance the ability of *S. indica* to colonize host roots under the tested conditions. However, given the antifungal activity of viridiflorol, its primary role may lie in defense against competing microbes rather than in promoting symbiosis.
