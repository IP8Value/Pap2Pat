# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Protein structure prediction represents one of the most enduring and consequential challenges in computational biology, with implications spanning drug discovery, enzyme engineering, synthetic biology, and fundamental understanding of molecular mechanisms underlying life processes. Over several decades, numerous methodologies have been developed to address this problem, ranging from physics-based simulations grounded in molecular dynamics to knowledge-based approaches that leverage evolutionary information encoded in multiple sequence alignments (MSAs). Among these, fragment assembly has historically emerged as one of the most successful ab initio strategies for de novo protein structure modeling. In fragment assembly, short segments of known protein structures—referred to as fragments—are selected from structural databases based on sequence similarity or other criteria and assembled into full-length models through conformational search algorithms. This approach underpins widely used software platforms such as Rosetta and QUARK, which have consistently ranked among the top performers in community-wide assessments like the Critical Assessment of Structure Prediction (CASP) experiments.

The efficacy of fragment assembly critically depends on the quality and relevance of the fragment libraries employed. A high-quality fragment library contains a diverse yet accurate collection of local structural motifs that closely resemble native conformations of the target protein at each position along its sequence. Consequently, significant research efforts have been devoted to improving fragment library construction algorithms. Notable examples include NNMake, which uses neural networks to select fragments based on sequence profiles; LRFragLib, which integrates local structural propensities derived from MSAs; Flib-Coevo, which incorporates co-evolutionary signals; and more recently, DeepFragLib, a deep learning-based method that leverages both sequential and structural context to generate highly accurate fragment ensembles. These advances have substantially increased the proportion of near-native fragments recruited for each residue position, thereby enhancing the fidelity of downstream modeling pipelines.

Despite their central role in fragment assembly, fragment libraries contain rich structural information that extends far beyond their immediate utility in conformational sampling. Specifically, each fragment encodes detailed geometric features—including backbone torsion angles (ϕ and ψ), secondary structure assignments, inter-residue distances (e.g., Cα–Cα and Cβ–Cβ), and higher-order backbone angles such as θ (planar angle between three consecutive Cα atoms) and τ (dihedral angle over four Cα atoms)—that collectively reflect local three-dimensional constraints inherent to folded proteins. However, this wealth of structural data has remained largely untapped outside the domain of fragment-based modeling. Traditional protein property prediction methods—such as those predicting secondary structure, solvent accessibility, or contact maps—have predominantly relied on sequence-derived features like position-specific scoring matrices (PSSMs), hidden Markov models (HMMs), or direct coupling analysis (DCA) scores extracted from MSAs. While these approaches have achieved impressive accuracy, they inherently lack explicit access to real structural exemplars that could provide complementary, physically grounded priors.

In parallel, recent breakthroughs in end-to-end deep learning architectures—most notably AlphaFold2—have revolutionized the field by integrating co-evolutionary, sequential, and structural reasoning within unified frameworks capable of predicting atomic-level coordinates directly from amino acid sequences. Nevertheless, even these state-of-the-art systems often benefit from auxiliary structural information when available, and many intermediate tasks—such as distance map prediction or torsion angle estimation—remain critical components of hybrid modeling strategies. Gradient descent-based folding protocols, exemplified by trRosetta and SAMF, further illustrate the continued relevance of predicted structural properties: these methods optimize 3D coordinates by minimizing energy functions derived from predicted inter-residue distances, torsion angles, and other geometric restraints. The accuracy of such predictions thus directly governs the quality of the final structural models.

Given this landscape, there exists a compelling opportunity to systematically harness the structural content embedded in modern fragment libraries—not merely as templates for assembly but as sources of informative priors for both gradient-based folding and deep learning-driven property prediction. Prior work has not comprehensively evaluated the fidelity of structural properties extractable from fragment libraries nor explored their integration into alternative modeling paradigms. Moreover, existing evaluation metrics for fragment libraries—such as precision (fraction of low-RMSD fragments) and coverage (fraction of positions spanned by at least one good fragment)—fail to capture the nuanced accuracy of individual geometric attributes like torsion angles or pairwise distances. Without fine-grained assessment tools, it remains unclear whether fragment libraries can reliably inform specific structural constraints beyond coarse topological similarity.

This gap motivates the present invention, which introduces novel methodologies for extracting, evaluating, and leveraging structural information from high-quality fragment libraries across two distinct computational frameworks: (1) as differentiable, protein-specific potentials in gradient descent-based folding engines, and (2) as structured inputs to deep neural networks designed for multi-property protein structure prediction. By bridging the representational power of fragment ensembles with modern optimization and machine learning techniques, the disclosed invention enables more accurate and robust prediction of both local and global protein structural features, ultimately advancing the frontier of computational structural biology.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides systems, methods, and computer-readable media for leveraging structural information embedded in protein fragment libraries to enhance the accuracy and reliability of protein structure prediction. Specifically, the invention discloses a dual-pronged approach wherein fragment libraries—comprising ensembles of short, experimentally determined structural segments aligned to a target protein sequence—are utilized both as sources of differentiable geometric constraints in gradient descent-based folding protocols and as structured inputs to deep neural networks trained to predict multiple protein structural properties simultaneously. This dual utilization addresses key limitations in current methodologies by integrating physically grounded, local structural priors derived from real protein conformations into prediction pipelines that traditionally rely solely on sequence-derived features.

In accordance with a first aspect of the invention, a method is provided for constructing protein-specific energy potentials from fragment libraries using weighted Gaussian Mixture Models (wGMMs). The method begins by generating a high-quality fragment library for a target protein sequence using an advanced fragment recruitment algorithm such as DeepFragLib, which selects fragments based on deep learning-informed compatibility with the target’s sequence and predicted structural context. Each fragment in the library is associated with a confidence score, typically derived from a predicted root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) relative to the unknown native structure. To standardize variable-length fragments, a smoothing operation is applied whereby longer fragments are decomposed into overlapping sub-fragments of fixed length (e.g., seven residues) via a sliding window. From these normalized fragments, six categories of structural properties are extracted: backbone torsion angles ϕ and ψ, backbone angles θ and τ, and intra-fragment pairwise distances between Cα–Cα and Cβ–Cβ atoms. For each residue position and each property type, a wGMM is fitted to the distribution of observed values across all fragments covering that position, with weights assigned according to the fragment confidence scores. The resulting wGMM parameters—comprising mixture weights, means, and variances—are then converted into differentiable potential functions via negative log-likelihood formulations. These potentials are integrated into a gradient descent-based folding framework such as SAMF, where they act as position-specific restraints that guide conformational search toward regions of structural space consistent with the fragment ensemble. Empirical validation demonstrates that incorporation of these fragment-derived potentials yields statistically significant improvements in model accuracy, as measured by TM-Score, and enhances the ability of energy-based ranking to identify near-native decoys.

In accordance with a second aspect of the invention, a Fragment-Assisted Deep Neural Network (FA-DNN) is disclosed for joint prediction of one-dimensional (1D) and two-dimensional (2D) protein structural properties. FA-DNN comprises two principal modules: a fragment library encoder and a structural property predictor. The fragment library encoder processes the raw fragment ensemble—represented as a tensor encoding per-residue structural features across multiple fragments—and transforms it into a compact, position-wise embedding that captures salient geometric patterns. This is achieved through a hierarchical convolutional architecture featuring residual blocks that operate along the fragment length dimension, followed by aggregation (e.g., averaging) over fragments to produce a single vector per sequence position. The structural property predictor then combines these fragment-derived embeddings with conventional sequence-based features—including one-hot encoded amino acids, PSSMs from MSAs, and DCA-derived pairwise statistics—to predict four backbone torsion angles (ϕ, ψ, θ, τ) and Cβ–Cβ inter-residue distances. The network employs a shared 2D residual backbone with separate output branches for 1D and 2D predictions, trained end-to-end using a composite loss function that emphasizes accuracy across all target properties. Benchmarking against state-of-the-art predictors such as SPOT-1D, Spider3, RaptorX, and trRosetta reveals that FA-DNN achieves superior performance on independent test sets including CASP13 FM, CASP13 TBM, CAMEO, and CASP14 FM, particularly for targets with sparse evolutionary information where fragment-based priors compensate for limited MSA depth.

Additionally, the invention introduces a suite of novel fragment-level evaluation metrics designed to quantitatively assess the fidelity of structural properties within fragment libraries. Unlike traditional metrics that evaluate global similarity (e.g., RMSD-based precision and coverage), these new metrics operate at the level of individual geometric attributes. For secondary structure, fragment accuracy is defined as the expected agreement between fragment-assigned and native secondary structure classes (H, E, C, O) across all positions and fragments. For continuous properties like torsion angles and distances, error metrics are formulated as the expected mean absolute deviation between fragment and native values, again averaged over positions and fragments. These metrics enable rigorous comparison of fragment library construction algorithms and confirm that modern methods like DeepFragLib yield highly accurate representations of local structure, especially when augmented with confidence-weighted sampling.

Collectively, these innovations establish fragment libraries not merely as tools for fragment assembly but as versatile repositories of structural knowledge that can be systematically mined to improve diverse aspects of protein structure prediction. The invention thus provides a foundational framework for integrating empirical structural data into next-generation computational pipelines, with broad applicability in structural bioinformatics, protein design, and functional annotation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example Environment

The methods and systems described herein may be implemented within a computing environment comprising one or more processors, memory units, storage devices, and input/output interfaces, configured to execute specialized software for protein structure prediction. In a typical deployment, the invention operates on high-performance computing clusters or cloud-based infrastructure equipped with graphical processing units (GPUs) to accelerate deep learning computations and gradient-based optimization. The software stack includes scientific computing libraries such as NumPy, SciPy, and PyTorch or TensorFlow for neural network training and inference, alongside structural bioinformatics toolkits like Biopython or MDAnalysis for handling protein coordinate data. Input to the system consists of a target protein amino acid sequence, optionally accompanied by a multiple sequence alignment (MSA) generated via homology search tools such as JackHMMER or HHblits against large sequence databases (e.g., UniRef, BFD). The MSA serves as the primary source of evolutionary information for both fragment library construction and feature extraction in the FA-DNN pipeline.

Fragment libraries are constructed using DeepFragLib or equivalent state-of-the-art algorithms, which query curated structural databases (e.g., PDB70, Lib956) containing non-redundant, high-resolution protein structures. These databases are version-controlled and timestamped to ensure temporal separation from evaluation benchmarks like CASP, thereby preventing data leakage. The fragment recruitment process involves deep neural networks trained to predict structural compatibility between query sequences and candidate fragments, yielding for each residue position a ranked list of 50–200 fragments with associated confidence scores (e.g., predicted RMSD). The resulting fragment library is stored in a standardized format (e.g., NNMake-compatible) for downstream processing.

For gradient descent-based folding, the system interfaces with a protein modeling engine such as SAMF, which implements differentiable geometry operations and energy minimization routines. The engine accepts as input a set of predicted inter-residue distance maps (e.g., from trRosetta) and integrates the fragment-derived wGMM potentials as additional terms in the total energy function. Structural refinement proceeds via iterative gradient updates until convergence, producing an ensemble of decoy structures that are subsequently ranked by composite energy scores.

For deep learning-based property prediction, the FA-DNN model is instantiated as a deep residual network with custom input preprocessing layers to handle heterogeneous feature types. Training occurs on large datasets of high-resolution protein structures (e.g., HR5916), with hyperparameters optimized via validation on held-out subsets. During inference, the model processes the target sequence and its fragment library in parallel, outputting per-residue torsion angles and a full inter-residue distance matrix. Predictions are post-processed using inverse transformations (e.g., tanh inversion for distance mapping) to recover physical units.

All components are orchestrated through a modular software pipeline that ensures reproducibility, scalability, and interoperability with existing structural bioinformatics workflows. The environment supports batch processing of multiple targets, automated benchmarking against standard datasets, and export of results in common structural formats (e.g., PDB, mmCIF) for downstream analysis or visualization.

### Structural Properties of Proteins and Fragments

Proteins adopt well-defined three-dimensional conformations governed by the physicochemical properties of their constituent amino acids and the constraints imposed by the polypeptide backbone. The spatial arrangement of a protein chain can be characterized at multiple levels of granularity, from local backbone geometry to long-range tertiary contacts. Central to this description are several key structural properties that define the conformational state of each residue and its relationship to neighboring residues. These properties include backbone torsion angles, secondary structure elements, and inter-atomic distances, all of which are explicitly encoded within protein fragment libraries and form the basis for the predictive models disclosed herein.

Backbone torsion angles describe the rotational freedom around bonds in the polypeptide backbone. The two primary torsion angles are phi (ϕ) and psi (ψ), which correspond to rotations around the N–Cα and Cα–C bonds, respectively. Together, ϕ and ψ define the Ramachandran plot, a fundamental representation of allowed backbone conformations constrained by steric clashes. In addition to these classical angles, the invention also considers two higher-order backbone angles: theta (θ) and tau (τ). Theta is defined as the planar angle formed by three consecutive Cα atoms (Cα^(i−1)–Cα^i–Cα^(i+1)), capturing local chain curvature. Tau is the dihedral angle over four successive Cα atoms (Cα^(i−1)–Cα^i–Cα^(i+1)–Cα^(i+2)), reflecting the twist of the backbone beyond nearest neighbors. These angles provide complementary geometric descriptors that are particularly useful for modeling loop regions and irregular secondary structures.

Secondary structure refers to recurring local motifs stabilized by hydrogen bonding patterns, primarily alpha-helices (H), beta-strands (E), and coils (C). In the context of fragment analysis, secondary structure assignment is extended to the fragment level: a fragment is classified as H, E, or C if more than half of its residues belong to the respective class in the native structure; otherwise, it is labeled as “other” (O). This coarse-grained categorization facilitates evaluation of fragment library accuracy while preserving biological interpretability.

Inter-residue distances constitute critical long-range constraints that define protein topology. The invention focuses on two types of intra-fragment distances: Cα–Cα and Cβ–Cβ. Cα atoms mark the central point of each residue, and their pairwise distances encode the overall fold. Cβ atoms (or Cα for glycine) provide side-chain reference points, with Cβ–Cβ distances being especially informative for defining hydrophobic cores and packing interactions. Within a fragment of length *n*, there are *n(n−1)/2* unique pairwise distances, each contributing to the local structural signature.

Each of these properties is inherently present in every fragment of a library, as fragments are derived from real protein structures. When aggregated across the ensemble of fragments covering a given residue position, these properties form empirical distributions that reflect the conformational variability compatible with the local sequence context. The invention exploits these distributions by either modeling them explicitly via wGMMs for use as energy potentials or encoding them implicitly through deep neural networks for property prediction. Critically, because fragments are selected based on sequence similarity and structural compatibility, their embedded properties serve as physically plausible priors that complement predictions derived from evolutionary couplings or sequence profiles alone.

### Evaluation of Fragment Library

To rigorously assess the utility of fragment libraries as sources of structural information, the invention introduces a comprehensive suite of evaluation metrics that operate at the fragment level rather than the traditional residue or whole-protein level. These metrics enable quantitative comparison of fragment library construction algorithms and validate the fidelity of extracted structural properties.

Traditional evaluation relies on precision and coverage computed over RMSD thresholds. Precision measures the fraction of fragments in the library whose RMSD to the native structure falls below a cutoff (e.g., 2.0 Å), while coverage measures the fraction of residue positions spanned by at least one such high-quality fragment. While useful for assessing overall library quality, these metrics do not reveal how accurately specific geometric attributes are represented.

The invention therefore defines seven novel fragment-level metrics corresponding to key structural properties:

1. **Fragment Secondary Structure Accuracy (ACC_SS)**: For each fragment *f_i* at position *p_i*, an indicator function *I_SS(f_i)* equals 1 if the fragment’s secondary structure class matches that of the corresponding native segment, and 0 otherwise. The overall accuracy is the expectation over all positions and fragments:  
   *ACC_SS(FL) = E_{p_i}[E_{f_i}[I_SS(f_i)]]*.

2. **Torsion Angle Errors (ERR_ϕ, ERR_ψ)**: For a fragment *f_i*, the mean absolute angular deviation from the native is computed as  
   *err_ang(f_i, f*) = (1/N) Σ_j min(|ang_i^j − ang_*^j|, 360° − |ang_i^j − ang_*^j|)*,  
   where *ang* denotes ϕ or ψ, and *N* is the fragment length. The library-wide error is the expectation over positions and fragments:  
   *ERR_ang(FL) = E_{p_i}[E_{f_i}[err_ang(f_i, f*)]]*.

3. **Backbone Angle Errors (ERR_θ, ERR_τ)**: Defined analogously to torsion angles but for θ and τ.

4. **Distance Errors (ERR_Cα, ERR_Cβ)**: For intra-fragment pairwise distances, the mean absolute error (MAE) between fragment and native distances is computed, then averaged over all residue pairs, fragments, and positions.

These metrics were applied to fragment libraries generated by DeepFragLib, NNMake, and Flib-Coevo on CASP13 FM, CASP13 TBM, and CAMEO test sets. Results demonstrated that DeepFragLib consistently achieved the highest accuracy across all properties, with particularly strong performance in secondary structure (ACC_SS > 0.9) and low errors in torsion angles (ERR_ϕ ≈ 15°, ERR_ψ ≈ 25°). Furthermore, weighting fragments by predicted RMSD confidence (via softmax with temperature T=0.1) yielded additional accuracy gains, confirming that confidence scores effectively stratify fragment quality.

This evaluation framework establishes that modern fragment libraries contain highly accurate, multi-faceted structural information that can be reliably extracted for downstream applications. It also provides a benchmark for future fragment library algorithms, emphasizing the importance of property-level fidelity beyond global RMSD.

### Prediction of Protein Structure

The invention enhances gradient descent-based protein structure prediction by incorporating fragment-derived structural information as differentiable, protein-specific energy potentials. This approach complements existing distance-based restraints (e.g., from trRosetta) with local geometric constraints extracted from fragment libraries.

The process begins with fragment library construction using DeepFragLib, yielding for each target protein a set of fragments with associated predicted RMSD scores. To handle variable fragment lengths, a smoothing operation decomposes each fragment into overlapping 7-residue sub-fragments via a sliding window. This standardization ensures uniformity in subsequent property extraction.

Six structural properties are extracted from each sub-fragment: ϕ, ψ, θ, τ, Cα–Cα distances, and Cβ–Cβ distances. For each residue position *i* and property type, a weighted Gaussian Mixture Model (wGMM) is fitted to the distribution of observed values across all fragments covering *i*. Fragment weights are computed as  
*w_fi = exp((5.0 − predRMSD_i)/T) / Σ_fj exp((5.0 − predRMSD_j)/T)*,  
with T=0.1, emphasizing high-confidence fragments. Model selection via Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) determines an optimal component count of four for all properties.

The wGMMs are converted to energy potentials using negative log-likelihood:  
*L_ϕ(x) = −Σ_i log Σ_t w_{i,t}^ϕ p(ϕ_i; μ_{i,t}^ϕ, σ_{i,t}^ϕ)*,  
where *x* is the current protein conformation, *ϕ_i* is its i-th torsion angle, and *p* is the Gaussian PDF. Analogous potentials are defined for ψ, θ, τ, and distance properties.

These potentials are integrated into the SAMF folding framework as additive terms in the total energy:  
*L_FL(x) = w_ϕ L_ϕ + w_ψ L_ψ + w_θ L_θ + w_τ L_τ + w_Cα L_Cα + w_Cβ L_Cβ*.  
Weights are tuned on CASP12FM to maximize TM-Score.

Benchmarking on CASP13 FM/TBM and CAMEO shows consistent improvements: average TM-Score of top1 decoys increases by 8.84%, and the number of correct topologies (TM>0.5) rises from 111 to 135 across all targets. Paired t-tests confirm statistical significance (p<0.001 for top1 models). Notably, fragment potentials narrow the gap between best and top1 decoys, improving energy-based ranking.

This demonstrates that fragment-derived potentials provide valuable local constraints that refine global distance-based models, particularly in regions where evolutionary signals are weak.

### Prediction of Protein Structural Properties

The invention further discloses FA-DNN, a deep neural network that leverages fragment libraries as structured inputs to predict multiple protein structural properties with high accuracy. FA-DNN comprises two modules: a fragment library encoder and a structural property predictor.

The fragment library encoder processes an L×50×15×D input tensor, where L is protein length, 50 is the number of fragments per position, 15 is padded fragment length, and D=11 encodes per-residue features (one-hot secondary structure, sin/cos of ϕ, ψ, θ, τ). A hierarchical convolutional architecture applies eight residual blocks along the fragment length dimension, each containing two 1D convolutions (kernel=3, filters=64) with ELU activation. The first residue’s representation is extracted from each fragment (corresponding to the target position), then averaged across fragments to yield an L×64 embedding.

The structural property predictor combines this embedding with sequence-derived features: one-hot amino acids, PSSM from DeepMSA, and DCA statistics. These 1D features are tiled into 2D matrices and concatenated with DCA to form a 2D input. A 30-block 2D residual network (64 filters, 3×3 kernels, ELU, BatchNorm, dropout=0.15) processes this input. Two output branches follow: one pools to 1D and predicts ϕ, ψ, θ, τ via a fully connected layer; the other predicts Cβ–Cβ distances directly, with outputs mapped via *d’ = tanh((d−10)/2.4)* to emphasize short distances.

Trained on HR5916 (5916 high-resolution chains), FA-DNN outperforms baselines on CASP13/14 and CAMEO. For 1D properties, MAE improvements over SPOT-1D include ϕ (0.303 vs 0.312), ψ (0.429 vs 0.430), θ (0.041 vs 0.041), τ (0.418 vs 0.428). For Cβ distances, MAE is 7.766 vs 8.304 (trRosetta) and 8.636 (RaptorX). Gains are largest for targets with shallow MSAs (e.g., CASP14 T1037, T1042), where fragment priors compensate for limited evolutionary information.

This confirms that fragment libraries provide complementary structural signals that enhance deep learning predictions, especially in data-scarce regimes.

### Example Method and Example Implementations

An exemplary implementation of the invention proceeds as follows:

1. **Input Preparation**: Receive a target protein sequence. Generate an MSA using JackHMMER against UniRef30. Construct a fragment library using DeepFragLib v1.0, querying Lib956 (timestamped pre-CASP13), yielding 50–200 fragments per position with predicted RMSD scores.

2. **Fragment Processing**: Apply smoothing to convert fragments to 7-residue sub-fragments. Extract ϕ, ψ, θ, τ, Cα–Cα, and Cβ–Cβ values. Compute fragment weights via softmax over predicted RMSD (T=0.1).

3. **wGMM Construction**: For each position and property, fit a 4-component wGMM using expectation-maximization. Validate component count via BIC on CASP12FM.

4. **Potential Integration**: Implement wGMM potentials in SAMF. Tune weights on CASP12FM to maximize TM-Score. Run folding with trRosetta distance restraints ± fragment potentials. Generate 50 decoys, select top1 by energy and best by TM-Score.

5. **FA-DNN Inference**: Encode fragment library into L×50×15×11 tensor. Pass through fragment encoder to obtain L×64 embeddings. Combine with PSSM/DCA features. Run through 2D residual network. Output ϕ, ψ, θ, τ and Cβ–Cβ distances.

6. **Evaluation**: Compute MAE for 1D/2D properties on CASP13/14, CAMEO. Compare against SPOT-1D, trRosetta, etc.

This pipeline demonstrates the invention’s dual utility: fragment libraries enhance both physics-based folding and data-driven prediction, establishing a new paradigm for integrating empirical structural knowledge into computational biology.
