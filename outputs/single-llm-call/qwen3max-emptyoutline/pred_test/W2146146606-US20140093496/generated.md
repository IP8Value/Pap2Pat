# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of engineered immunoglobulins, particularly monoclonal antibodies with modified Fc regions designed to exhibit enhanced binding specificity and affinity for the inhibitory Fcγ receptor IIb (FcγRIIb) while maintaining or reducing binding to activating Fcγ receptors, especially both allotypes of FcγRIIa (FcγRIIaH131 and FcγRIIaR131). The invention provides novel amino acid substitutions within the Fc region of human IgG1 antibodies that confer this selective binding profile, which is critical for therapeutic applications where engagement of inhibitory signaling pathways is desired without the concomitant risk of platelet activation, thromboembolic events, or rapid in vivo clearance associated with increased binding to activating Fcγ receptors. The invention further encompasses the structural basis for this selective recognition, the biophysical and pharmacokinetic properties of the engineered antibodies, and their use in a broad spectrum of therapeutic contexts, including but not limited to the treatment of autoimmune diseases, allergic disorders, and cancer immunotherapy through the enhancement of agonistic activity of antibodies targeting members of the tumor necrosis factor receptor (TNFR) superfamily.

## BACKGROUND ART

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) have emerged as a cornerstone of modern therapeutics, with applications spanning oncology, autoimmune diseases, infectious diseases, and beyond. A significant portion of their therapeutic efficacy, particularly in the context of cancer, is mediated through interactions between the antibody’s constant fragment (Fc) region and a family of Fc gamma receptors (FcγRs) expressed on the surface of various immune cells. These FcγRs are broadly classified into two functional categories: activating receptors (e.g., FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa) and an inhibitory receptor, FcγRIIb. The balance between signals transduced by these opposing receptor types is a critical determinant of the overall immune response triggered by an antibody.

Historically, much of the engineering effort has been directed towards enhancing the binding affinity of the Fc region for activating FcγRs, such as FcγRIIIa, to boost antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and phagocytosis (ADCP), thereby improving the antitumor activity of therapeutic antibodies. This has been achieved through specific amino acid substitutions in the Fc region, for example, the well-known G236A/S239D/I332E (GAALIE) or S298A/E333A/K334A (AAA) variants. An alternative approach involves glyco-engineering, where the removal of fucose from the N-linked glycan at Asn297 in the Fc region results in a dramatic increase in affinity for FcγRIIIa, leading to enhanced ADCC.

However, the role of the inhibitory FcγRIIb is equally, if not more, nuanced and therapeutically relevant. FcγRIIb is the sole inhibitory FcγR in humans and is expressed on B cells, dendritic cells (DCs), and other myeloid cells. On B cells, co-engagement of FcγRIIb with the B cell receptor (BCR) by an immune complex (IC) delivers a potent inhibitory signal that suppresses B cell activation and proliferation, a mechanism that is central to maintaining B cell homeostasis and tolerance. In dendritic cells, FcγRIIb ligation can inhibit antigen processing and DC maturation, thereby promoting peripheral tolerance and suppressing T cell activation. These properties make FcγRIIb an attractive target for therapies aimed at dampening unwanted immune responses, such as in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) or other autoimmune conditions.

Furthermore, a distinct and powerful therapeutic application of FcγRIIb has been uncovered in the context of agonistic antibodies targeting the TNFR superfamily (e.g., CD40, DR5, CD137/4-1BB). For these antibodies to exert their full agonistic activity, they often require cross-linking on the surface of the target cell. FcγRIIb-expressing cells, such as certain myeloid cells, can serve as a "scaffold" by binding the Fc region of the agonist antibody, thereby facilitating its efficient cross-linking and subsequent robust signaling in the target cell. Therefore, engineering the Fc region to have enhanced affinity for FcγRIIb can significantly amplify the agonistic activity of such antibodies, making them more potent immunotherapeutic agents.

In pursuit of this goal, the S267E/L328F substitution in the human IgG1 Fc region was developed and shown to increase binding affinity for FcγRIIb by several hundred-fold. This variant has demonstrated promising therapeutic effects in preclinical models, such as suppressing B cell activation in an SLE model and enhancing the agonistic activity of anti-CD40 and anti-DR5 antibodies. However, a critical flaw in the S267E/L328F variant has been identified: it also exhibits a substantial increase in binding affinity for one of the two common allotypes of the activating receptor FcγRIIa, specifically FcγRIIaR131. This lack of selectivity is a major concern for clinical translation.

The reason for this concern lies in the expression of FcγRIIa on human platelets. Immune complexes formed by certain therapeutic antibodies (e.g., anti-CD154, anti-VEGF) have been shown to activate platelets via cross-linking of FcγRIIa, leading to platelet aggregation and, in some clinical settings, serious thromboembolic complications. Since the S267E/L328F variant enhances binding to FcγRIIaR131, it carries an inherent and potentially amplified risk of inducing such adverse events in patients who are homozygous (R/R131) or heterozygous (R/H131) for this allotype. Given that the R/R131 genotype is present in a significant portion of the global population (e.g., ~31% in Caucasians, ~6% in Japanese), this safety liability is a substantial barrier to the development of therapeutics based on this Fc variant. Moreover, enhanced binding to FcγRIIa may lead to faster clearance of the antibody from circulation, as FcγRIIa is known to mediate internalization and lysosomal degradation of immune complexes, potentially reducing its therapeutic half-life and efficacy.

Thus, there exists a clear and unmet need in the art for a novel Fc-engineered antibody that can selectively enhance binding to the inhibitory FcγRIIb over both allotypes of the activating FcγRIIa (FcγRIIaH131 and FcγRIIaR131). Such an antibody would retain the desirable therapeutic benefits of FcγRIIb engagement—such as B cell suppression, induction of tolerance, or enhanced agonism of TNFR superfamily antibodies—while simultaneously mitigating the safety risks associated with platelet activation and the potential pharmacokinetic drawbacks of rapid clearance. The present invention directly addresses this critical need by providing a new class of Fc variants that achieve this high degree of selectivity, along with a detailed understanding of the underlying structural mechanisms that enable this precise discrimination between highly homologous receptors.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The primary problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of a safe and effective Fc-engineered antibody platform that can selectively enhance binding to the inhibitory FcγRIIb receptor without concurrently increasing binding to the activating FcγRIIa receptors, particularly the FcγRIIaR131 allotype. Existing Fc variants, such as the S267E/L328F mutant, while successful in enhancing FcγRIIb affinity, suffer from a critical safety liability due to their non-selective binding profile, which includes a significant increase in affinity for FcγRIIaR131. This non-selectivity poses a substantial risk of inducing platelet activation and aggregation, potentially leading to life-threatening thromboembolic events in a large segment of the patient population. Furthermore, enhanced binding to FcγRIIa may lead to accelerated clearance of the therapeutic antibody from the bloodstream, thereby reducing its bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy. The high degree of sequence homology between FcγRIIb and FcγRIIa makes the task of engineering such a selective Fc variant exceptionally challenging, as most modifications that affect one receptor tend to affect the other in a similar manner. Thus, the invention solves the problem of achieving a high degree of functional selectivity between these two highly similar receptors, a feat that was previously considered difficult or impossible.

### Means for Solving the Problems

The invention solves the aforementioned problems by providing a novel set of amino acid substitutions within the Fc region of a human IgG1 antibody that collectively confer a unique and highly selective binding profile. The core discovery is the identification of a single substitution, Pro238Asp (P238D), which, contrary to conventional engineering approaches, enhances binding to FcγRIIb while simultaneously decreasing binding to both FcγRIIaH131 and FcγRIIaR131. This initial finding was made through a comprehensive mutagenesis screen of over 500 variants in the lower hinge and CH2 region of the Fc domain. Building upon the P238D scaffold, a second round of comprehensive mutagenesis was performed to identify additional substitutions that could further enhance FcγRIIb affinity. This iterative and structure-informed approach led to the development of a multi-substituted variant, designated herein as V12, which incorporates the following six amino acid changes: Glu233Asp, Gly237Asp, His268Asp, Pro271Gly, Tyr296Asp, and Ala330Arg, all in combination with the foundational P238D substitution. The V12 variant exhibits a remarkable >200-fold increase in binding affinity for FcγRIIb compared to wild-type IgG1, while its binding to FcγRIIaR131 remains at a level comparable to wild-type IgG1, and its binding to all other activating FcγRs (FcγRI, FcγRIIaH131, FcγRIIIa) is significantly reduced. The invention is further supported by the elucidation of the crystal structures of the Fc(P238D)/FcγRIIb and Fc(V12)/FcγRIIb complexes, which reveal the dynamic conformational changes and novel molecular interactions that underpin this unprecedented selectivity.

### Effects of the Invention

The present invention provides a powerful and versatile Fc-engineering platform with numerous beneficial effects. First and foremost, it enables the creation of therapeutic antibodies with a vastly improved safety profile. By avoiding enhanced binding to FcγRIIa, the V12-based antibodies are not expected to induce platelet activation or aggregation, thereby mitigating the risk of thromboembolic complications. Second, the invention offers a solution to a potential pharmacokinetic issue, as the selective engagement of FcγRIIb, which is known to recycle internalized immune complexes, is not expected to lead to the rapid clearance associated with FcγRIIa-mediated lysosomal degradation. Indeed, the V12 variant demonstrates a plasma half-life in human FcRn transgenic mice that is comparable to that of wild-type IgG1. Third, the invention retains and even amplifies the key therapeutic benefits of FcγRIIb engagement. The V12 variant has been shown to potently enhance the agonistic activity of an anti-CD137 antibody, a member of the TNFR superfamily, to a degree comparable to the non-selective S267E/L328F variant. This demonstrates its broad applicability for developing more potent cancer immunotherapies. Finally, the invention provides a fundamental scientific advance by revealing a novel structural mechanism—centered on a dynamic conformational change in the Fc domain—that allows for the precise discrimination between FcγRIIb and FcγRIIa, a challenge that had eluded the field for years. This knowledge will inform future generations of Fc engineering.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### <Substitution>

The present invention is embodied in a polypeptide comprising the Fc region of a human IgG1 antibody, wherein the Fc region comprises a set of specific amino acid substitutions that confer selective, high-affinity binding to human FcγRIIb. The foundational substitution is the replacement of Proline at position 238 (EU numbering) with Aspartic acid (P238D). This single substitution is sufficient to create a variant that distinguishes FcγRIIb from both allotypes of FcγRIIa. To further enhance the binding affinity for FcγRIIb, the P238D substitution is combined with one or more of the following additional substitutions: Glutamic acid at position 233 to Aspartic acid (E233D), Glycine at position 237 to Aspartic acid (G237D), Histidine at position 268 to Aspartic acid (H268D), Proline at position 271 to Glycine (P271G), Tyrosine at position 296 to Aspartic acid (Y296D), and Alanine at position 330 to Arginine (A330R). In a preferred embodiment, the Fc region comprises all seven of these substitutions, creating the variant designated as V12. The amino acid sequence of the Fc region of the V12 variant is defined by the presence of these specific residues at the indicated positions relative to the wild-type human IgG1 Fc sequence. The invention also encompasses any polypeptide that is a functional equivalent of the V12 variant, meaning it has at least 90%, 95%, or 98% sequence identity to the V12 Fc sequence and retains the key functional property of selective, high-affinity binding to FcγRIIb over FcγRIIa. The substitutions are introduced into the Fc region using standard molecular biology techniques, such as site-directed mutagenesis, and the resulting polypeptide is expressed in a suitable host cell system, such as mammalian cells (e.g., HEK293 or CHO cells), for purification and therapeutic use.

### <Antibody>

The invention provides a full-length, antigen-binding antibody molecule comprising a heavy chain and a light chain, wherein the heavy chain comprises a variable region that confers specificity for a desired target antigen and a constant region that includes the engineered Fc region as described above. The light chain can be of any suitable type (kappa or lambda) and comprises a variable region that pairs with the heavy chain variable region to form the complete antigen-binding site. The target antigen for the antibody can be any protein or other molecule for which a therapeutic effect can be achieved through the unique Fc-mediated functions enabled by the V12 variant. This includes, but is not limited to, antigens on B cells (e.g., CD19, CD20) for the treatment of autoimmune diseases like SLE; antigens on tumor cells for the purpose of delivering an Fc-engineered payload that modulates the immune microenvironment; or, most prominently, members of the TNFR superfamily (e.g., CD40, DR5, CD137/4-1BB, OX40, GITR) for cancer immunotherapy. In the latter case, the antibody acts as an agonist, and its therapeutic potency is dramatically enhanced by the V12 Fc's ability to engage FcγRIIb on accessory cells, which serves as a cross-linking scaffold. The antibody is produced by co-transfecting host cells with plasmids encoding the modified heavy chain and the appropriate light chain, followed by purification from the culture supernatant using standard chromatographic methods, such as Protein A affinity chromatography. The resulting antibody is a stable, monomeric protein suitable for pharmaceutical formulation and administration.

### <Pharmaceutical Compositions>

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of the antibody described above and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. The pharmaceutical composition is formulated for parenteral administration, which includes intravenous, subcutaneous, or intramuscular routes. The composition is typically prepared in a sterile, isotonic, buffered aqueous solution. Common buffers include histidine, acetate, or phosphate buffers at a pH ranging from approximately 5.0 to 7.0. The composition may also contain stabilizers such as sugars (e.g., sucrose, trehalose) or polyols (e.g., sorbitol, mannitol) to prevent aggregation and maintain the physical stability of the antibody during storage. Surfactants, such as polysorbate 20 or polysorbate 80, may be added at low concentrations to minimize surface adsorption and interfacial stress. The final concentration of the antibody in the composition is selected based on the intended dose and route of administration, and can range from 1 mg/mL to 200 mg/mL or higher. The composition is filled into appropriate containers, such as vials or pre-filled syringes, and is stored under refrigerated conditions (2-8°C) or, for some formulations, at room temperature. The pharmaceutical composition is intended for the treatment of a variety of diseases, including autoimmune disorders, allergic diseases, and cancer, where the selective engagement of FcγRIIb provides a therapeutic advantage.

### Asparagine: Asn (N)

In the context of the present invention, the amino acid Asparagine (Asn, N) is a naturally occurring, polar, uncharged amino acid that is a constituent of the wild-type human IgG1 Fc region. While the specific engineered variants of the invention (P238D and V12) do not involve a direct substitution of an Asn residue, the presence of Asn at position 297 (Asn297) is of critical importance. Asn297 is the site of attachment for the conserved N-linked glycan in the Fc region. This glycan is essential for maintaining the structural integrity of the Fc domain and for its interaction with FcγRs. The invention’s variants, including V12, are designed to function with the native glycan structure. The research paper notes that for crystallization studies, the FcγRIIb was deglycosylated to minimize heterogeneity, but the therapeutic antibodies of the invention are intended to be produced with their natural glycosylation pattern. The Asn297-linked glycan is therefore an integral part of the three-dimensional structure that presents the engineered amino acid side chains (e.g., Asp238, Asp237, etc.) in the correct orientation to interact with FcγRIIb. Any modification to the glycan at Asn297, such as afucosylation, is a separate engineering strategy that could, in principle, be combined with the V12 Fc mutations, although the primary focus of the present invention is on the protein sequence-based selectivity.

### Glutamine: Gln (Q)

Glutamine (Gln, Q) is a naturally occurring, polar, uncharged amino acid that is present at several positions in the wild-type human IgG1 Fc region. The engineered variants of the present invention, specifically the P238D and V12 variants, do not involve a direct substitution of a Gln residue. The amino acid substitutions that define the invention are focused on positions 233, 237, 238, 268, 271, 296, and 330, none of which are Gln in the wild-type sequence. However, Gln residues are part of the overall structural scaffold of the Fc domain. Their side chains, with their amide functional groups, can participate in hydrogen bonding networks that contribute to the stability of the CH2 domain. The conformational changes induced by the key substitutions of the invention, such as the shift of the loop around position 238, may indirectly affect the local environment and hydrogen-bonding potential of nearby Gln residues. Nevertheless, the functional properties of the invention—the selective, high-affinity binding to FcγRIIb—are primarily dictated by the novel interactions formed by the introduced Asp, Gly, and Arg residues, rather than by any direct role of the endogenous Gln residues. The invention is thus defined by the specific set of substitutions listed, and the presence of Gln at its native positions is a background feature of the IgG1 Fc scaffold.

### Glycine: Gly (G)

Glycine (Gly, G) is a unique, small, non-polar amino acid with a hydrogen atom as its side chain, which confers exceptional conformational flexibility to the polypeptide backbone. In the present invention, Glycine plays a dual role. First, it is a native residue at position 237 in the wild-type human IgG1 Fc region. In the V12 variant, this Gly237 is substituted with Aspartic acid (G237D), a change that contributes to the enhanced binding to FcγRIIb by forming new van der Waals contacts with FcγRIIb residues. Second, and more critically, Glycine is introduced as a substitution at position 271, where the wild-type Proline is replaced with Glycine (P271G). This P271G substitution is a key component of the V12 variant and is responsible for a significant portion of the affinity improvement. The rationale for this substitution is that Proline, with its rigid cyclic structure, imposes a fixed conformation on the polypeptide backbone. Replacing it with the highly flexible Glycine releases this conformational constraint, allowing the adjacent loop (residues 266-273) to adopt a new, more favorable conformation that facilitates the formation of a salt bridge between Asp270 (in the Fc) and Arg131 (in FcγRIIb). This structural rearrangement, revealed by the crystal structure of the Fc(V12)/FcγRIIb complex, is a prime example of how the introduction of a flexible Glycine residue can be a powerful tool in protein engineering to optimize binding interfaces. The P271G substitution is therefore a crucial element of the invention's means for solving the problem of achieving high-affinity, selective FcγRIIb binding.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

This example describes the initial comprehensive mutagenesis screen that led to the discovery of the P238D and L328E variants. A library of over 500 single amino acid substitution variants of the human IgG1 Fc region was generated. Each of approximately 30 residues in the lower hinge and CH2 region was systematically replaced with every naturally occurring amino acid except cysteine. The binding affinity of each variant for the extracellular domains of human FcγRIIaH131, FcγRIIaR131, and FcγRIIb was then evaluated using a high-throughput surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assay. The results were normalized to the binding of the wild-type IgG1 template. From this extensive screen, only two substitutions were identified that met the stringent criteria of enhancing binding to FcγRIIb while simultaneously decreasing binding to both FcγRIIa allotypes. These were the substitution of Proline 238 with Aspartic acid (P238D) and the substitution of Leucine 328 with Glutamic acid (L328E). In contrast, the previously reported S267E and L328F substitutions, while enhancing FcγRIIb binding, also increased binding to FcγRIIaR131, confirming their lack of selectivity. This screen established P238D as a novel and highly selective starting point for further engineering.

### Example 2

This example details the quantitative affinity analysis of the P238D and L328E variants against the full panel of human FcγRs. The dissociation constants (KD) for the interaction of wild-type IgG1, P238D, and L328E with FcγRI, FcγRIIaH131, FcγRIIaR131, FcγRIIb, and FcγRIIIa (both V158 and F158 allotypes) were determined using SPR. The results showed that the P238D variant exhibited a 12-fold increase in affinity for FcγRIIb compared to wild-type IgG1. Crucially, its affinity for FcγRIIaR131 was reduced by 15-fold, and for FcγRIIaH131 by 18-fold. Its affinity for the other activating receptors, FcγRI and FcγRIIIa, was also significantly reduced. The L328E variant showed a more modest 4-fold increase in FcγRIIb affinity and a reduction in binding to both FcγRIIa allotypes, but its overall profile was less pronounced than that of P238D. This example confirmed that the P238D substitution creates a Fc variant with a uniquely selective binding profile, enhancing the inhibitory receptor while dampening engagement with all activating receptors.

### Example 3

This example presents the determination and analysis of the crystal structure of the complex between the Fc fragment of the P238D variant (Fc(P238D)) and the extracellular domain of human FcγRIIb. The complex was crystallized, and X-ray diffraction data were collected to a resolution of 2.6 Å. The structure was solved by molecular replacement. The analysis revealed that the P238D substitution induces a significant conformational change in the Fc domain. In the wild-type Fc, Pro238 is buried in a hydrophobic core. The introduction of the hydrophilic Asp238 forces it to shift out of this core and become solvent-exposed, which in turn causes a rearrangement of the surrounding loop (residues 233-240). This dynamic change leads to the formation of two novel interactions with FcγRIIb: a hydrogen bond between the main chain of Gly237 (in Fc) and the side chain of Tyr160 (in FcγRIIb), and a salt bridge between Asp270 (in Fc) and Arg131 (in FcγRIIb). The structural comparison with the Fc(IgG1)/FcγRIIaR131 complex explains the selectivity: FcγRIIa has a Phe instead of Tyr160, preventing the hydrogen bond, and FcγRIIaH131 has a His instead of Arg131, weakening the salt bridge. This structural insight provides the mechanistic basis for the observed selectivity of P238D.

### Example 4

This example describes the second round of engineering aimed at further enhancing the FcγRIIb affinity of the P238D scaffold. Initial attempts to combine P238D with known FcγRIIb-enhancing substitutions like L328E or S267E/L328F were unsuccessful, showing no additive effect, likely due to the altered Fc interface. Therefore, a new comprehensive mutagenesis screen was performed, this time using the P238D variant as the template. Approximately 400 new single-substitution variants were generated and screened for FcγRIIb binding. Six substitutions that provided an additive benefit were identified: E233D, G237D, H268D, P271G, Y296D, and A330R. These were then combinatorially assembled. The stepwise combination of these substitutions led to progressively higher affinity for FcγRIIb, culminating in the V12 variant, which contains all six additional substitutions on top of P238D. The V12 variant demonstrated a 62-fold increase in affinity for FcγRIIb over the P238D parent, representing a total >200-fold increase over wild-type IgG1.

### Example 5

This example reports the full characterization of the binding profile of the final V12 variant. The KD values for V12 against all human FcγRs were measured by SPR. The results confirmed that V12 has a KD of 0.044 μM for FcγRIIb, a 217-fold improvement over wild-type IgG1. Critically, its binding to FcγRIIaR131 was 0.67 μM, which is essentially the same as wild-type IgG1 (0.65 μM), and its binding to FcγRIIaH131 was significantly weaker (KD > 100 μM). Binding to all other activating FcγRs (FcγRI, FcγRIIIa) was either undetectable or extremely weak. In stark contrast, the S267E/L328F variant showed a 355-fold increase for FcγRIIb but an even greater 864-fold increase for FcγRIIaR131. This example definitively establishes V12 as a purely selective FcγRIIb-enhancing variant, free from the off-target binding to FcγRIIa that plagues the prior art.

### Example 6

This example assesses the biophysical and pharmacokinetic properties of the V12 variant to ensure its suitability as a therapeutic agent. The melting temperature (TM) of the CH2 domain, measured by thermal shift assay, was 62°C for V12, which is 8°C lower than wild-type IgG1 (70°C), indicating a modest reduction in thermal stability, likely due to the disruption of the hydrophobic core by P238D. However, a real-time stability study at a high concentration of 100 mg/mL, stored at 25°C for 4 weeks, showed that the formation of high-molecular-weight (HMW) aggregates for V12 (0.33%) was comparable to that of wild-type IgG1 (0.30%), demonstrating good solution stability. The binding affinity of V12 for human FcRn at pH 6.0 was 1.4 μM, nearly identical to wild-type IgG1 (1.5 μM). Consistent with this, a pharmacokinetic study in human FcRn transgenic mice showed that the plasma half-life of V12 was 2.6 days, which is statistically indistinguishable from the 2.8-day half-life of wild-type IgG1. These results confirm that the V12 variant, despite its altered FcγR binding profile, maintains the critical pharmacokinetic property of a long serum half-life.

### Example 7

This example demonstrates the functional superiority and safety of the V12 variant in two key biological assays. First, a platelet activation and aggregation assay was performed using washed platelets from donors with either FcγRIIa R/R131 or H/H131 genotypes. Immune complexes (ICs) were formed using anti-IgE antibodies with either the S267E/L328F or V12 Fc variants. ICs with the S267E/L328F variant induced strong platelet activation (measured by CD62p and PAC-1 expression) and aggregation in R/R131 platelets, but not in H/H131 platelets. In contrast, ICs with the V12 variant did not induce any activation or aggregation in platelets from either genotype. This directly proves the safety advantage of the selective V12 variant. Second, an in vitro T-cell activation assay was performed using an agonistic anti-CD137 antibody. Co-culture of CTLL-2 (CD137+) and Raji (FcγRIIb+) cells with the anti-CD137 antibody bearing the V12 Fc resulted in a >5-fold increase in IFN-γ production compared to the wild-type IgG1 Fc, a level of enhancement equivalent to that achieved with the S267E/L328F variant. This confirms that the V12 variant fully retains the therapeutic benefit of enhanced FcγRIIb-mediated agonism.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The Fc-engineered antibody variants of the present invention, particularly the V12 variant, have broad and significant industrial applicability in the field of biopharmaceuticals. They provide a superior platform for developing next-generation therapeutic antibodies with an optimized safety and efficacy profile. The primary industrial application is in the development of safer and more potent immunotherapies for cancer. By enabling the creation of highly selective FcγRIIb-enhancing agonist antibodies against TNFR superfamily members (e.g., CD137, CD40, OX40), the invention allows for the design of drugs that can powerfully stimulate anti-tumor T-cell responses without the risk of platelet-related toxicities. A second major application is in the treatment of autoimmune and allergic diseases. Antibodies targeting B-cell antigens (e.g., CD19) or IgE, when equipped with the V12 Fc, can more effectively suppress pathogenic B-cell or mast cell activity through FcγRIIb co-engagement, again with a reduced safety risk. The invention is also applicable to any therapeutic scenario where the induction of immune tolerance or the suppression of an unwanted immune response is desired, such as in transplantation or chronic inflammatory conditions. The fact that the V12 variant maintains a normal pharmacokinetic profile and good biophysical stability further enhances its industrial utility, as it can be readily manufactured, formulated, and administered using existing industry-standard processes. In summary, the invention provides a versatile and valuable new tool for the biopharmaceutical industry to develop a new class of safer, more effective, and more targeted antibody therapeutics.
