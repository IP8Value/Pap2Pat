# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Mucosal surfaces represent the primary interface between the host and the external environment, serving as critical barriers against invading pathogens. These surfaces, which include the respiratory, gastrointestinal, and genitourinary tracts, are lined with specialized epithelial cells that secrete mucus, antimicrobial peptides, and immunoglobulins to prevent microbial colonization. Despite these innate defenses, many pathogens have evolved mechanisms to breach mucosal barriers, leading to significant global morbidity and mortality. Effective protection against such infections requires robust immune responses at the site of entry, particularly those involving cell-mediated immunity mediated by T helper 1 (Th1) lymphocytes. However, the development of vaccines capable of eliciting strong mucosal Th1 responses remains a major challenge in immunology and vaccinology.

Current vaccine strategies predominantly rely on parenteral administration, which often fails to generate adequate mucosal immunity. While systemic immunization can produce circulating antibodies and memory T cells, these effectors may not efficiently traffic to mucosal tissues or provide immediate protection at the portal of pathogen entry. In contrast, mucosal vaccination—delivered via intranasal, oral, or intravaginal routes—can stimulate local immune responses in mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT), including nasal-associated lymphoid tissue (NALT) and gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT). Importantly, due to the common mucosal immune system, stimulation at one mucosal site can lead to protective immunity at distant mucosal surfaces, a phenomenon known as the “common mucosal immune response.” This interconnectedness makes intranasal immunization an attractive strategy for inducing immunity in the female reproductive tract, for example, without direct vaginal delivery.

Despite its theoretical advantages, mucosal vaccine development has been hindered by several obstacles. First, mucosal surfaces are inherently hostile environments characterized by enzymatic degradation, low pH, and rapid clearance mechanisms, which can rapidly inactivate or eliminate conventional protein antigens before they reach underlying immune cells. Second, there is a lack of safe and effective adjuvants approved for human use that can enhance mucosal immune responses, particularly those driving Th1-type cell-mediated immunity. Most licensed adjuvants, such as aluminum salts (alum), primarily promote Th2-biased humoral responses and are ineffective at stimulating cytotoxic T lymphocytes or Th1 cells, which are essential for clearing intracellular pathogens like *Chlamydia trachomatis*. Moreover, alum and other traditional adjuvants often induce excessive inflammation, which can damage delicate mucosal tissues and contribute to immunopathology rather than protection.

*Chlamydia trachomatis* is a paradigmatic example of a mucosal pathogen that underscores the need for novel vaccine platforms. It is the most common bacterial sexually transmitted infection worldwide, with an estimated 92 million new cases annually. In women, untreated chlamydial infection can ascend from the cervix to the upper genital tract, causing pelvic inflammatory disease, tubal scarring, ectopic pregnancy, and infertility. Clearance of *C. trachomatis* is critically dependent on Th1 immune responses, particularly the production of interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) by CD4+ T cells, which activates macrophages and induces indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase to deplete tryptophan—an essential nutrient for chlamydial growth. However, despite decades of research, no licensed vaccine exists, largely due to the inability to generate durable, high-magnitude Th1 responses in the genital mucosa without triggering harmful inflammation.

The major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of *Chlamydia* has long been considered a leading vaccine candidate due to its surface exposure, conservation across serovars, and ability to elicit neutralizing antibodies and T cell responses. However, recombinant MOMP produced in standard expression systems often misfolds, losing critical conformational epitopes necessary for protective immunity. Native MOMP, when properly folded within the chlamydial outer membrane complex, can induce partial protection, but its production is technically challenging and not scalable for vaccine manufacturing. Furthermore, even when delivered with potent adjuvants, soluble MOMP typically fails to generate robust mucosal Th1 immunity, highlighting the need for advanced delivery systems that protect the antigen, target it to antigen-presenting cells, and provide appropriate immunostimulatory signals.

Nanoparticle-based delivery systems have emerged as promising solutions to these challenges. By encapsulating antigens, nanoparticles can shield them from degradation, facilitate uptake by dendritic cells (DCs)—the key initiators of adaptive immunity—and co-deliver immunomodulatory molecules. Among various nanocarriers, vault nanoparticles represent a unique and biologically inspired platform. Vaults are naturally occurring, evolutionarily conserved ribonucleoprotein particles found in nearly all eukaryotic cells, including humans. They are barrel-shaped structures approximately 700 Å in length and 400 Å in width, with a molecular mass of about 13 MDa. The vault particle is composed primarily of 96 copies of the major vault protein (MVP), which self-assembles into the characteristic hollow shell, along with minor components such as telomerase-associated protein 1 (TEP1), vault poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (VPARP), and a small untranslated RNA (vRNA). Although the precise cellular function of vaults remains incompletely understood, they have been implicated in intracellular transport, multidrug resistance, and innate immune responses, including potential roles in bacterial uptake in respiratory mucosa.

Critically, recombinant vaults can be engineered using only the MVP gene, which is sufficient to form stable, empty nanoparticles when expressed in insect cells via the baculovirus expression system. The internal cavity of these recombinant vaults is spacious enough to accommodate multiple protein antigens, making them ideal carriers for vaccine delivery. Moreover, their size—comparable to small bacteria—facilitates efficient phagocytosis by dendritic cells, a prerequisite for antigen processing and presentation to T cells. Previous studies have demonstrated that fusion of cargo proteins to the minimal interaction domain (mINT) derived from VPARP enables their specific packaging into the vault lumen during assembly, preserving antigen integrity and functionality.

To further enhance immunogenicity, vaults can be surface-modified to promote interactions with immune cells. For instance, the addition of a C-terminal peptide derived from staphylococcal protein A (termed the “z” peptide) confers the ability to bind the Fc region of immunoglobulins. This modification allows vaults to engage Fc receptors (FcRs) on dendritic cells, a pathway known to enhance antigen cross-presentation and Th1 polarization—particularly relevant for intracellular pathogens like *Chlamydia*. Indeed, FcR-mediated uptake has been shown to be essential for protective immunity against chlamydial genital infection in murine models.

Despite these advantages, the immunological properties of vault nanoparticles as mucosal vaccine adjuvants had not been systematically evaluated prior to the present invention. In particular, it was unknown whether vaults could activate dendritic cells, what signaling pathways they engage, and whether they could drive Th1-biased mucosal immunity without inducing pathological inflammation. Traditional adjuvants often activate pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) such as Toll-like receptors (TLRs), which can trigger robust cytokine production but also excessive inflammation. The discovery of alternative activation pathways—such as the inflammasome—has opened new avenues for “smart” adjuvant design that selectively promotes protective immunity while minimizing collateral damage.

The inflammasome is a multiprotein complex that forms in response to cellular stress or pathogen-associated molecular patterns, leading to the activation of caspase-1 and the maturation of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and IL-18. The NLRP3 inflammasome, activated by diverse stimuli including alum, ATP, and crystalline substances, plays a key role in bridging innate and adaptive immunity. Notably, inflammasome activation can promote Th1 and Th17 responses without necessarily inducing broad pro-inflammatory cascades associated with TLR signaling. Thus, an adjuvant that selectively engages the inflammasome—while avoiding TLR activation—could represent a significant advance in mucosal vaccine design.

In summary, the field of mucosal vaccinology has been constrained by the absence of delivery platforms that simultaneously protect antigens, target dendritic cells, and provide tailored immunostimulatory signals conducive to Th1 immunity without inflammation. The present invention addresses this unmet need through the development and application of engineered vault nanoparticles as a novel “smart adjuvant” system capable of inducing potent, protective mucosal immunity against challenging intracellular pathogens such as *Chlamydia trachomatis*.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel vaccine composition and method for inducing mucosal immune responses, particularly Th1-type cell-mediated immunity, using recombinant vault nanoparticles engineered to encapsulate immunogenic proteins and display surface modifications that enhance dendritic cell targeting and activation. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that vault nanoparticles loaded with protein antigens—specifically the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of *Chlamydia muridarum*—function as “smart adjuvants” that activate the inflammasome pathway in dendritic cells without engaging Toll-like receptors (TLRs), thereby promoting protective Th1 immunity in mucosal tissues while minimizing pro-inflammatory responses that can cause tissue damage.

In one aspect, the invention provides a recombinant vault nanoparticle comprising a self-assembled shell formed from modified major vault protein (MVP) subunits and an encapsulated immunogenic protein fused to a vault-targeting domain. The MVP is engineered to include a C-terminal Fc-binding peptide (e.g., the “z” peptide derived from staphylococcal protein A) that enables the vault to bind immunoglobulins and engage Fc receptors on antigen-presenting cells, thereby enhancing uptake and cross-presentation. The immunogenic protein is selected from pathogens requiring Th1-mediated clearance, such as *Chlamydia trachomatis*, and is preferably MOMP or an immunogenic fragment thereof. The vault-targeting domain, such as the minimal interaction domain (mINT) of VPARP, ensures efficient packaging of the antigen into the vault lumen during assembly, preserving its conformational integrity and immunogenicity.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of immunization comprising administering the recombinant vault nanoparticle to a mucosal surface of a subject, such as intranasally, to induce protective immunity at distant mucosal sites, including the genital tract. This method leverages the common mucosal immune system, wherein stimulation of nasal-associated lymphoid tissue (NALT) leads to the generation of effector T cells and antibodies that home to reproductive mucosa. The invention demonstrates that intranasal delivery of MOMP-loaded vaults significantly reduces bacterial burden following intravaginal challenge with *C. muridarum*, outperforming both negative controls and live-attenuated infection models in terms of early pathogen clearance.

A key feature of the invention is the mechanism by which vault nanoparticles activate dendritic cells. Unlike conventional adjuvants that signal through TLRs and induce broad pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6, the protein-loaded vaults of the present invention selectively activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, leading to caspase-1-dependent maturation and secretion of IL-1β and IL-18. This inflammasome activation is dependent on the presence of encapsulated protein cargo, as empty vaults do not elicit this response. Critically, this pathway promotes Th1 differentiation—as evidenced by increased IL-12p70 and CXCL10 production—without inducing neutrophil-recruiting chemokines like CXCL2 or excessive TNF-α, thereby avoiding tissue-damaging inflammation. This selective immunomodulation defines the vaults as “smart adjuvants.”

Furthermore, the invention reveals that the nature of the encapsulated antigen influences the overall immune response, including the potential to break immune tolerance to self-proteins. Specifically, while vaults containing green fluorescent protein (GL) or mCherry do not induce anti-vault antibodies, MOMP-loaded vaults unexpectedly stimulate IgG responses against the MVP shell itself. This suggests that certain antigens, possibly due to their structural or functional properties (e.g., porin activity), can enhance inflammasome activation to a degree that overcomes peripheral tolerance, providing a potential biomarker for vaccine efficacy.

The invention also includes methods for producing the recombinant vault nanoparticles using baculovirus expression systems in insect cells, wherein co-expression of cp-MVP-z (cysteine-stabilized MVP with C-terminal z peptide) and antigen-mINT fusion constructs results in self-assembled, cargo-loaded vaults that can be purified by standard chromatographic or gradient techniques. The resulting nanoparticles are stable, homogeneous, and suitable for mucosal delivery in liquid or lyophilized formulations.

In summary, the present invention provides a versatile, safe, and effective platform for mucosal vaccination against intracellular pathogens, combining antigen protection, targeted dendritic cell delivery, and selective inflammasome-mediated adjuvant activity to achieve robust Th1 immunity without pathological inflammation. This approach represents a significant advancement over existing vaccine technologies and holds promise for preventing diseases such as chlamydial infection, tuberculosis, influenza, and other mucosal pathogens requiring cell-mediated clearance.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to recombinant vault nanoparticles engineered for mucosal vaccine delivery, methods of their production, and their use in inducing protective Th1-biased immune responses in mucosal tissues without eliciting excessive inflammation. The vault platform leverages the natural architecture and biocompatibility of endogenous vault particles while incorporating strategic modifications to enhance antigen delivery, dendritic cell engagement, and tailored immunostimulation.

Vaults are large, evolutionarily conserved ribonucleoprotein complexes found in the cytoplasm of nearly all eukaryotic cells. The native vault particle is approximately 700 Å in length and 400 Å in diameter, with a molecular mass of about 13 MDa. It consists of 96 copies of the major vault protein (MVP), which self-assembles into a barrel-shaped shell with two protruding caps, along with smaller quantities of telomerase-associated protein 1 (TEP1), vault poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (VPARP), and a small untranslated RNA (vRNA). Although the physiological role of vaults remains incompletely defined, they have been implicated in nucleocytoplasmic transport, cellular stress responses, and innate immunity, including potential involvement in bacterial sensing in mucosal epithelia.

For vaccine applications, recombinant vaults are produced by expressing only the MVP gene in heterologous systems such as insect cells using the baculovirus expression vector system (BEVS). This approach yields empty, vault-like particles that retain the structural integrity of native vaults but lack the minor protein and RNA components. To enhance stability, the MVP can be engineered with a cysteine-rich tag (denoted “cp-MVP”), which promotes disulfide bond formation and improves particle rigidity under physiological conditions. These recombinant vaults possess a large internal cavity capable of accommodating multiple protein antigens, making them ideal nanocarriers for vaccine delivery.

To enable targeted delivery to antigen-presenting cells, particularly dendritic cells (DCs), the vault shell is further modified by fusing a C-terminal peptide that binds the Fc region of immunoglobulins. In a preferred embodiment, this is the 33-amino acid “z” peptide derived from staphylococcal protein A, which binds IgG with high affinity at a site distinct from the Fc receptor (FcR) binding domain. This modification, resulting in “cp-MVP-z,” allows the vault to form immune complexes with endogenous or co-administered antibodies, facilitating uptake via Fcγ receptors on DCs—a pathway known to enhance cross-presentation and Th1 polarization. Experimental data confirm that cp-MVP-z vaults exhibit significantly enhanced binding to mouse IgG compared to unmodified vaults, while showing no binding to IgA, consistent with the specificity of the z peptide.

The immunogenic cargo is encapsulated within the vault lumen by fusing the antigen to a vault-targeting domain that directs co-assembly during particle formation. The minimal interaction domain (mINT), corresponding to amino acids 1563–1724 of VPARP, is used for this purpose. The mINT domain interacts specifically with the inner surface of the MVP shell, ensuring efficient packaging of the fused antigen. In the exemplified embodiment, the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of *Chlamydia muridarum*—a well-characterized immunogen critical for protective immunity—is fused to mINT (MOMP-mINT) and co-expressed with cp-MVP-z in Sf9 insect cells. Upon co-expression, the MOMP-mINT fusion is incorporated into the assembling vaults, resulting in homogeneous nanoparticles referred to as “MOMP-vaults.” These particles are purified by sucrose gradient centrifugation or chromatography, yielding preparations that are >95% pure as assessed by SDS-PAGE and negative-stain electron microscopy, which confirms preservation of the canonical vault morphology.

The immunological properties of these engineered vaults were rigorously evaluated in vitro and in vivo. In dendritic cell uptake assays, MOMP-vaults and control GL-vaults (containing green fluorescent protein) were efficiently internalized by bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs), with kinetics indicating active phagocytosis rather than surface adhesion. Confocal microscopy confirmed intracellular localization of vaults within the cytoplasm, distinct from fluid-phase markers like FITC-dextran, which remained largely surface-bound.

More importantly, cargo-loaded vaults—but not empty vaults—induced dendritic cell maturation characterized by the secretion of Th1-promoting cytokines and chemokines. Specifically, MOMP-vaults stimulated significant production of IL-12p70, a key driver of Th1 differentiation, as well as CXCL10 (IP-10), which recruits Th1 cells to sites of infection. Notably, this response was balanced by concurrent IL-10 secretion, suggesting regulated immune activation. Crucially, vaults did not induce pro-inflammatory mediators associated with tissue damage, such as TNF-α, IL-6, or CXCL2 (MIP-2), distinguishing them from TLR-activating adjuvants.

Mechanistic studies revealed that vault-induced cytokine production occurs independently of Toll-like receptors. Using HEK293 reporter cells expressing individual murine TLRs (TLR2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9), no NF-κB activation was detected in response to empty, GL-, or MOMP-vaults, ruling out TLR engagement as the activation pathway. Instead, evidence points to inflammasome activation: cargo-loaded vaults induced robust secretion of mature IL-1β from BMDCs, a process dependent on both priming (e.g., via residual LPS or intrinsic vault properties) and inflammasome assembly. Western blot analysis showed cleavage of pro-IL-1β (37 kDa) to its mature form, and the response was comparable to that induced by LPS plus nigericin—a canonical NLRP3 inflammasome activator. This identifies the vault platform as a novel inflammasome-triggering adjuvant that operates without TLR stimulation.

In vivo, intranasal immunization of C57BL/6 mice with MOMP-vaults (200 µg per dose, three doses at two-week intervals) generated potent mucosal immunity. Following intravaginal challenge with *C. muridarum*, MOMP-vault-immunized mice exhibited significantly reduced bacterial burden in vaginal swabs compared to GL-vault controls and, remarkably, achieved faster clearance than mice previously infected with live *Chlamydia*—a stringent benchmark for vaccine efficacy. Protection correlated with enhanced Th1 responses in iliac lymph nodes and genital tract tissues, including elevated frequencies of IFN-γ-producing CD4+ T cells and increased levels of IFN-γ, IL-1α, and CXCL10 in oviduct homogenates.

An unexpected finding was that MOMP-vault immunization broke immune tolerance to the self-protein MVP, inducing anti-vault IgG antibodies, whereas GL-vaults or mCherry-vaults did not. This suggests that the immunostimulatory potency of MOMP—possibly related to its porin structure or ability to enhance pro-IL-1β synthesis—can overcome peripheral tolerance mechanisms, offering a potential correlate of protection.

Formulations of the invention are suitable for mucosal administration, particularly intranasal delivery, which exploits the common mucosal immune system to generate distal genital tract immunity. The vault nanoparticles are stable in aqueous buffers, can be lyophilized for storage, and are compatible with standard vaccine excipients. Dosage ranges from 10 µg to 500 µg of total vault protein per administration, with antigen content typically 0.1–1% by weight, though optimization may vary by pathogen.

In summary, the detailed description encompasses the molecular design, production, characterization, and immunological mechanism of action of recombinant vault nanoparticles as a next-generation mucosal vaccine platform. The invention provides a rational, tunable system that combines antigen protection, targeted delivery, and selective inflammasome activation to achieve protective Th1 immunity without inflammatory pathology.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Recombinant vault nanoparticles containing the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of *Chlamydia muridarum* were engineered and produced using the baculovirus expression system in Sf9 insect cells. The major vault protein (MVP) was modified by the addition of a cysteine-rich stabilization tag (cp-MVP) and a C-terminal 33-amino acid “z” peptide derived from staphylococcal protein A to confer Fc-binding capability, resulting in the construct cp-MVP-z. The MOMP antigen, comprising amino acids 1–366 (excluding the signal sequence), was fused to the minimal interaction domain (mINT; amino acids 1563–1724 of VPARP) via PCR ligation to create the MOMP-mINT fusion construct. Both cp-MVP-z and MOMP-mINT were cloned into the pFastBac vector using BamHI and XhoI restriction sites. Recombinant baculoviruses were generated using the Bac-to-Bac system according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Sf9 cells were co-infected at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.01 with baculoviruses encoding cp-MVP-z and MOMP-mINT. After 65 hours of incubation at 27°C, cells were harvested by centrifugation, lysed on ice in buffer A (50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 75 mM NaCl, 0.5 mM MgCl2) containing 1% Triton X-100, 1 mM dithiothreitol, 0.5 mM PMSF, and a protease inhibitor cocktail, and clarified by centrifugation. The lysate was mixed with an equal volume of lysate from cells expressing cp-MVP-z alone and incubated on ice for 30 minutes to allow packaging of MOMP-mINT into pre-formed vault shells. The mixture was then layered onto a 40–45% sucrose gradient and centrifuged at 100,000 × g for 16 hours at 4°C. Vault-containing fractions at the 40/45% interface were collected, dialyzed against phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and concentrated using centrifugal filters. The final product, termed MOMP-vaults, was analyzed by SDS-PAGE with Coomassie staining, which revealed bands corresponding to cp-MVP-z (~97 kDa) and MOMP-mINT (~75 kDa), and by negative-stain electron microscopy, which confirmed the presence of intact, barrel-shaped particles indistinguishable from native vaults. Protein concentration was determined by BCA assay, and endotoxin levels were measured by LAL assay to be <0.1 EU/µg protein, ensuring suitability for in vivo use.

### Example 2

The immunological activity of MOMP-vaults was evaluated in vitro using bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) from C57BL/6 mice. Immature BMDCs were generated by culturing bone marrow precursors in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 50 µM β-mercaptoethanol, and 20 ng/mL granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) for six days, with fresh medium added on days 3 and 6. Cells were enriched to >94% purity by positive selection using anti-CD11c magnetic beads. For uptake assays, 1×10^6 BMDCs were incubated with 500 µg of GL-vaults (containing green fluorescent protein as a control cargo) or 250 µg of FITC-dextran for 30 or 60 minutes at 37°C. Cells were then stained with PE-conjugated anti-CD11c antibody and analyzed by flow cytometry. GL-vaults showed time-dependent uptake, with fluorescence intensity approaching that of FITC-dextran at 60 minutes. Confocal microscopy of fixed cells confirmed intracellular localization of GL-vaults within CD11c+ cells, in contrast to the predominantly surface-bound FITC-dextran. For cytokine profiling, BMDCs were stimulated with 10 µg/mL of MOMP-vaults, GL-vaults, empty cp-MVP-z vaults, or 100 ng/mL lipopolysaccharide (LPS) for 24 hours. Supernatants were analyzed using a Milliplex Mouse Cytokine/Chemokine Immunoassay. MOMP-vaults induced significant secretion of IL-12p70 (120 ± 15 pg/mL) and CXCL10 (850 ± 90 pg/mL), comparable to LPS, but did not induce TNF-α (<10 pg/mL) or CXCL2 (<20 pg/mL), which were robustly produced by LPS. Additionally, MOMP-vaults stimulated IL-1β secretion (200 ± 25 pg/mL) after 6 hours, which was absent in empty vault-treated cells. Western blot analysis of cell lysates using anti-IL-1β antibody confirmed cleavage of pro-IL-1β (37 kDa) to mature forms, indicative of inflammasome activation. These results demonstrate that protein-loaded vaults selectively activate dendritic cells via an inflammasome-dependent, TLR-independent pathway that promotes Th1 immunity without pro-inflammatory cytokine release.

### Example 3

The in vivo efficacy of MOMP-vaults was tested in a murine model of genital chlamydial infection. Female C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks old) were anesthetized with ketamine/xylazine and immunized intranasally with 200 µg of MOMP-vaults in 30 µL PBS on days 0, 14, and 28. Control groups received either 200 µg of GL-vaults or a single intranasal inoculation of 10^3 inclusion-forming units (IFU) of live *C. muridarum* (Live-CM group). Two weeks after the final immunization (or four weeks after Live-CM infection), all mice were synchronized with 2.5 mg medroxyprogesterone acetate subcutaneously to render the genital tract susceptible to infection. One week later, mice were challenged intravaginally with 1.5×10^5 IFU of *C. muridarum*. Vaginal swabs were collected every three days post-challenge, and chlamydial burden was quantified by determining IFU on McCoy cell monolayers. MOMP-vault-immunized mice exhibited a 1.5-log reduction in peak bacterial load on day 3 compared to GL-vault controls (p<0.001) and cleared the infection by day 15, whereas GL-vault mice required until day 21. Remarkably, MOMP-vault mice achieved faster clearance than Live-CM mice, who still harbored detectable bacteria on day 15. Serum collected two weeks post-immunization was analyzed by Western blot against *C. muridarum* lysate, revealing MOMP-specific IgG in MOMP-vault and Live-CM groups, but not in GL-vault mice. ELISA using purified cp-MVP-z vaults as antigen showed that only MOMP-vault-immunized mice developed anti-vault IgG, indicating broken tolerance to the self-protein MVP. These findings confirm that intranasal MOMP-vault immunization induces potent, protective mucosal immunity superior to natural infection.

### Example 4

The mechanism of vault-induced immunity was further dissected by analyzing T-cell responses in lymphoid and genital tissues. On days 7 and 15 post-challenge, iliac lymph nodes (ILNs) were harvested from immunized mice, and single-cell suspensions were stimulated ex vivo with PMA/ionomycin in the presence of brefeldin A. Cells were stained for CD3, CD4, and intracellular IFN-γ and IL-4, and analyzed by flow cytometry. MOMP-vault-immunized mice showed a 3-fold increase in IFN-γ+ CD4+ T cells in ILNs on day 7 compared to GL-vault controls (p<0.01), with a further increase on day 15, indicating active Th1 cell recruitment and expansion. In contrast, Live-CM mice exhibited stable Th1 frequencies across time points. The IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio exceeded 10:1 in all immune groups, confirming Th1 bias. Genital tract tissues (cervix-vagina, uterine horns, oviducts) were homogenized, and supernatants were analyzed by Luminex. MOMP-vault mice had significantly higher levels of IFN-γ (450 ± 50 pg/mL) and CXCL10 (1200 ± 150 pg/mL) in oviducts on day 7 compared to Live-CM mice (p<0.05), correlating with accelerated bacterial clearance. Additionally, IL-1α—a marker of T-cell infiltration—was elevated in oviducts of MOMP-vault mice (300 ± 40 pg/mL vs. 150 ± 20 pg/mL in Live-CM, p<0.01). To confirm the absence of TLR activation, vault preparations were tested in HEK293 reporter cells expressing murine TLR2, 3, 4, 5, 7, or 9. No NF-κB-driven SEAP activity was detected above background for any vault type, even at 10 µg/mL, while positive controls (e.g., LPS for TLR4) induced strong responses. Collectively, these data establish that MOMP-vaults function as smart adjuvants by activating the inflammasome to drive Th1 immunity in mucosal tissues without TLR-mediated inflammation.
