# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) is a zinc metalloprotease that plays a central role in the catabolism of multiple bioactive peptides, including insulin, glucagon, amylin, and amyloid-beta (Aβ). IDE is ubiquitously expressed in mammalian tissues, with particularly high levels found in the liver, kidney, brain, and muscle. Its primary physiological function is to regulate the extracellular and intracellular concentrations of these peptides by catalyzing their hydrolysis, thereby modulating signaling pathways critical for glucose homeostasis, energy metabolism, and neurodegeneration. Given its broad substrate specificity and involvement in multiple disease pathways, IDE has emerged as a compelling therapeutic target for conditions such as type 2 diabetes, Alzheimer’s disease, and metabolic syndrome.

Despite decades of research, the development of potent, selective, and drug-like small-molecule inhibitors of IDE has remained a significant challenge. Early inhibitors, such as bacitracin and N-ethylmaleimide, suffer from poor selectivity, low potency, and non-specific mechanisms of action, rendering them unsuitable for clinical use. Subsequent efforts have yielded peptide-based inhibitors and substrate mimetics, but these often exhibit poor pharmacokinetic properties, including rapid proteolytic degradation, limited cell permeability, and inadequate bioavailability. Moreover, many of these compounds bind within the catalytic chamber of IDE, competing directly with endogenous substrates—a mechanism that may limit their therapeutic utility due to potential interference with the natural turnover of multiple peptide hormones.

The structural complexity of IDE further complicates inhibitor design. IDE exists as a dynamic homodimer that undergoes large conformational changes during substrate binding and catalysis. The enzyme features a unique “clamshell” architecture, wherein two domains enclose a large catalytic chamber capable of accommodating substrates up to ~70 amino acids in length. This chamber contains the catalytic zinc ion coordinated by conserved histidine and glutamate residues, along with exosites that facilitate substrate recognition and allosteric regulation. Notably, IDE exhibits negative cooperativity and can adopt both open and closed conformations, which influence substrate access and catalytic efficiency. These structural features suggest that effective inhibition may be achieved not only through active-site blockade but also via allosteric modulation or stabilization of inactive conformations.

Recent advances in chemical biology and library screening technologies have opened new avenues for discovering non-classical inhibitors of IDE. In particular, DNA-templated synthesis has enabled the construction of macrocyclic compound libraries with unprecedented structural diversity and complexity, allowing for the identification of high-affinity ligands that might be inaccessible through conventional combinatorial chemistry. Macrocyclic compounds are especially attractive as drug candidates due to their ability to combine the target specificity of biologics with the oral bioavailability and metabolic stability of small molecules. Their constrained three-dimensional structures often enable high-affinity interactions with protein surfaces, including those outside traditional active sites, thereby facilitating the discovery of allosteric or non-competitive inhibitors.

Prior to the present invention, no macrocyclic small molecule had been demonstrated to inhibit IDE with high potency, selectivity, and in vivo efficacy. Furthermore, the binding site(s) and mechanism(s) of action of such macrocycles were unknown, limiting rational optimization and therapeutic application. There was thus a critical need for novel IDE inhibitors that overcome the limitations of existing compounds—specifically, molecules that are potent, stable, bioavailable, non-competitive with endogenous substrates, and capable of modulating IDE activity in a physiologically relevant context without inducing toxicity or off-target effects.

The present invention addresses this unmet need by providing a class of macrocyclic compounds that selectively and potently inhibit IDE through a novel mechanism involving binding to an allosteric site distinct from the canonical peptide substrate-binding pocket. These compounds, exemplified by the lead structure 6b and its lysine-conjugated derivative 6bK, demonstrate robust inhibition of both human and mouse IDE in biochemical assays, effectively stabilize endogenous peptide hormones such as insulin, glucagon, and amylin in plasma, and produce measurable metabolic effects in vivo, including enhanced insulin sensitivity and altered glucose tolerance. Critically, these macrocyclic inhibitors do not cross the blood-brain barrier in detectable amounts under the tested conditions, suggesting a peripheral mechanism of action that may mitigate potential central nervous system side effects while still offering therapeutic benefits for metabolic disorders.

Moreover, the invention provides methods for identifying, characterizing, and optimizing such macrocyclic IDE inhibitors, including in vitro selection from DNA-templated libraries, structure-activity relationship studies, mutant complementation assays to map binding epitopes, and in vivo pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profiling. The invention also encompasses diagnostic and therapeutic applications, including the use of labeled IDE inhibitor probes for imaging IDE activity in biological samples, kits for detecting IDE levels or activity, and pharmaceutical formulations for treating diseases associated with dysregulated IDE activity.

In summary, the background art lacks a safe, effective, and mechanistically well-defined small-molecule inhibitor of IDE suitable for clinical development. The present invention fills this gap by introducing a new class of macrocyclic compounds that inhibit IDE with high potency and selectivity through a non-competitive, allosteric mechanism, supported by comprehensive biochemical, structural, and in vivo evidence. This represents a significant advancement in the field of metabolic and neurodegenerative disease therapeutics.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel macrocyclic compounds that function as potent, selective, and non-competitive inhibitors of insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE). These compounds are characterized by a constrained cyclic structure comprising a scaffold linked to at least three variable building blocks, which together form a three-dimensional conformation capable of high-affinity binding to an allosteric site on IDE. In exemplary embodiments, the macrocyclic inhibitors include compounds designated herein as 1b through 6b, with 6b and its lysine-conjugated derivative 6bK exhibiting superior inhibitory potency, metabolic stability, and in vivo efficacy. The invention further encompasses methods for identifying such IDE-binding macrocycles through in vitro selection from DNA-templated libraries, as well as methods for detecting IDE activity or expression in biological samples using labeled inhibitor probes.

A key aspect of the invention is the discovery that the macrocyclic inhibitors bind to a site on IDE that is distinct from the conventional peptide substrate-binding pocket. This conclusion is supported by Yonetani-Theorell double-inhibitor plots demonstrating non-competitive kinetics with respect to known substrate mimetics such as Ii1 and bacitracin, as well as by mutagenesis studies showing that point mutations in IDE (e.g., A479L and G362Q) dramatically reduce inhibitor potency unless compensated by corresponding structural modifications in the macrocycle. These findings indicate that the inhibitors engage specific residues located outside the catalytic chamber, likely stabilizing a conformation of IDE that is less competent for substrate binding or catalysis.

The invention also provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the macrocyclic IDE inhibitors, optionally in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or other active agents. These compositions are useful for the treatment or prevention of diseases and conditions associated with aberrant IDE activity, including but not limited to type 2 diabetes, obesity, metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance, impaired glucose tolerance, and disorders of glucagon or amylin signaling. In particular, administration of the inhibitors in vivo results in prolonged half-life of endogenous insulin and other IDE substrates, leading to enhanced insulin sensitivity and improved glucose disposal, as demonstrated in both lean and diet-induced obese (DIO) mouse models.

Additionally, the invention includes diagnostic and research tools based on the macrocyclic inhibitors. For example, labeled versions of the inhibitors (e.g., isotopically labeled 6bK containing 13C6,15N2-lysine) enable sensitive quantification of inhibitor distribution in tissues and plasma via isotope dilution mass spectrometry. Fluorescently or radioactively tagged derivatives can be used as probes to detect IDE expression or activity in cells, tissues, or biological fluids, facilitating drug screening, patient stratification, or disease monitoring. The invention further provides kits containing one or more macrocyclic IDE inhibitors, detection reagents, and instructions for use in research, diagnostic, or therapeutic applications.

In certain embodiments, the macrocyclic inhibitors are administered peripherally (e.g., intraperitoneally or orally) and do not significantly penetrate the blood-brain barrier, as evidenced by the absence of detectable 6bK in brain tissue even when using highly sensitive LC-MS methods. This peripheral restriction may offer a safety advantage by avoiding potential modulation of central Aβ metabolism, which could otherwise complicate the therapeutic profile in patients with comorbid neurodegenerative conditions. Nevertheless, the inhibitors effectively modulate systemic glucose homeostasis through actions on peripheral tissues, including the liver, pancreas, and gastrointestinal tract, as demonstrated by effects on insulin tolerance, gastric emptying, and hepatic gene expression.

The invention is further supported by in vivo studies showing that acute administration of 6bK enhances the hypoglycemic response to exogenous insulin, improves glucose tolerance in lean mice, and produces dose-dependent effects in DIO mice, including transient hyperglycemia followed by improved glucose clearance—a phenomenon potentially linked to amplified glucagon and amylin signaling. These metabolic effects are absent in control animals treated with an inactive diastereomer (bisepi-6bK), confirming the on-target nature of the observed phenotypes. Moreover, experiments in Gcgr−/− mice demonstrate that the glucagon receptor is required for the full manifestation of the inhibitor’s effects during oral glucose challenges, underscoring the integrated role of IDE in regulating multiple hormonal axes.

In summary, the present invention provides a comprehensive platform for the discovery, development, and application of macrocyclic IDE inhibitors with novel mechanisms of action, favorable pharmacological properties, and significant therapeutic potential for metabolic diseases. The invention encompasses the compounds themselves, methods of making and using them, diagnostic and research tools, and pharmaceutical formulations, all of which represent a substantial advance over the prior art.

## DEFINITIONS

### Chemical Definitions

As used herein, the term “macrocyclic compound” refers to a cyclic organic molecule containing a ring of at least twelve atoms, wherein the ring includes carbon atoms and may further incorporate heteroatoms such as nitrogen, oxygen, or sulfur. The macrocyclic compounds of the invention are synthetically derived and typically comprise a central scaffold covalently linked to at least three variable building blocks, which are arranged in a spatially defined conformation that enables high-affinity binding to insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE). The ring structure confers conformational rigidity, which enhances binding affinity and selectivity relative to linear analogs.

The term “building block” denotes a molecular subunit incorporated into the macrocyclic framework during synthesis. In exemplary embodiments, the building blocks are selected from a diverse set of amino acids, aryl groups, alkyl chains, and functionalized heterocycles. Specific examples include benzophenone-containing moieties, cyclohexylalanine derivatives, and aromatic or aliphatic carboxylic acids. The identity and stereochemistry of the building blocks critically influence the inhibitory potency and selectivity of the resulting macrocycle.

The term “scaffold” refers to the core structural element that links the building blocks and forms the backbone of the macrocyclic ring. In the compounds of the invention, the scaffold is typically derived from a tri-functional linker such as tris(2-aminoethyl)amine or a related polyamine, which enables the cyclization of three distinct building blocks into a single macrocyclic entity. The scaffold may be modified to alter ring size, polarity, or hydrogen-bonding capacity, thereby tuning the pharmacological properties of the inhibitor.

The term “IDE inhibitor” or “insulin-degrading enzyme inhibitor” means a compound that reduces the enzymatic activity of IDE, as measured by a decrease in the rate of cleavage of a standard substrate such as the fluorogenic peptide Mca-RPPGFSAFK(Dnp)-OH, insulin, glucagon, or amyloid-beta. Inhibition may be competitive, non-competitive, uncompetitive, or mixed, and is quantified by the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) or the inhibition constant (Ki). The macrocyclic inhibitors of the invention are predominantly non-competitive with respect to peptide substrates.

The term “6b” refers to a specific macrocyclic IDE inhibitor identified through DNA-templated library screening, having a defined structure comprising a triazine-based scaffold linked to a benzophenone-containing building block, a cyclohexylalanine-derived moiety, and a third aromatic or heteroaromatic group. The “trans” isomer of this compound, designated 6b, exhibits superior inhibitory activity compared to its cis counterpart (6a). The term “6bK” denotes a lysine-conjugated derivative of 6b, synthesized by appending a lysine residue to enhance solubility and facilitate isotopic labeling for pharmacokinetic studies.

The term “isotopically labeled” refers to a compound in which one or more atoms have been replaced by a stable isotope, such as 13C, 15N, or 2H, for the purpose of tracking the compound in biological systems. For example, 6bK synthesized with 13C6,15N2-lysine is used as an internal standard in isotope dilution mass spectrometry to quantify the concentration of unlabeled 6bK in plasma and tissues.

The term “diastereomer” refers to a stereoisomer that is not a mirror image of another stereoisomer. In the context of the invention, “bisepi-6bK” is an inactive diastereomer of 6bK in which the stereochemistry at two or more chiral centers has been inverted, resulting in loss of IDE inhibitory activity despite identical chemical composition.

### Other Definitions

As used herein, the term “insulin-degrading enzyme” or “IDE” refers to the zinc metalloprotease encoded by the IDE gene in humans (GenBank accession NP_004960) and its orthologs in other species, including mouse (95% sequence identity to human IDE). IDE is a homodimeric enzyme that cleaves a variety of peptide hormones, including insulin, glucagon, amylin, and amyloid-beta, and is involved in the regulation of glucose homeostasis and neurodegeneration.

The term “allosteric site” means a binding site on an enzyme that is topographically distinct from the active site and whose occupancy by a ligand modulates enzyme activity through conformational changes. The macrocyclic inhibitors of the invention bind to an allosteric site on IDE, as evidenced by non-competitive inhibition kinetics and mutagenesis data.

The term “in vitro selection” refers to a method of screening large libraries of compounds for binding to a target protein, wherein the library is displayed on or linked to DNA tags that encode the chemical structure of each member. After incubation with the target, bound compounds are isolated, and their DNA barcodes are amplified and sequenced to identify enriched structures. In the present invention, this technique was applied to a 13,824-membered DNA-templated macrocycle library to discover IDE-binding compounds.

The term “diet-induced obese (DIO) mice” refers to C57BL/6J mice fed a high-fat diet for at least 20 weeks to induce obesity, insulin resistance, and metabolic dysfunction, serving as a model for human type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome.

The term “intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection” means administration of a substance into the peritoneal cavity of an animal, a common route for delivering drugs in preclinical studies.

The term “pharmaceutically acceptable carrier” includes any material that is not biologically or otherwise undesirable in the context of pharmaceutical administration, such as water, saline, ethanol, lipids, cellulose derivatives, and other excipients commonly used in drug formulation.

The term “kit” refers to a packaged set of components, which may include one or more macrocyclic IDE inhibitors, buffers, detection reagents, and instructions for use in research, diagnostic, or therapeutic applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

### Methods for Identifying IDE-Binding Compounds

The present invention provides robust methods for identifying macrocyclic compounds that bind to and inhibit insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE). Central to this approach is the use of DNA-templated synthesis to generate structurally diverse macrocyclic libraries, followed by in vitro selection against immobilized IDE protein. In a representative protocol, a DNA-templated library containing 13,824 unique macrocyclic compounds was synthesized using a split-and-pool strategy, wherein each compound is covalently linked to a unique DNA barcode that encodes its chemical structure. His6-tagged mouse IDE was immobilized on cobalt magnetic beads, and the library was incubated with the enzyme under physiological buffer conditions. After extensive washing to remove non-binders, the DNA barcodes of bound compounds were PCR-amplified and subjected to high-throughput sequencing. Enrichment analysis identified compounds that were consistently enriched across multiple independent selections, with a twofold enrichment threshold applied to minimize false positives.

This method enables the rapid identification of high-affinity IDE ligands without prior knowledge of the binding site or mechanism. The decoded structures revealed a series of macrocycles (designated 1–6) featuring a triazine-based scaffold linked to three variable building blocks, including aromatic ketones, aliphatic amino acids, and heterocyclic groups. Both cis and trans isomers of each compound were synthesized separately via Fmoc-based solid-phase synthesis and purified by reverse-phase HPLC to ensure stereochemical homogeneity. Subsequent biochemical screening confirmed that the trans isomers (e.g., 1b–6b) exhibited significantly higher IDE inhibitory potency than their cis counterparts, with 6b emerging as the lead compound.

Structure-activity relationship (SAR) studies were conducted by synthesizing 30 analogues of 6b in which the scaffold, linker length, or individual building blocks were systematically varied. These studies revealed that the benzophenone and cyclohexylalanine moieties are critical for activity, as their substitution with smaller or less hydrophobic groups led to dramatic losses in potency. This SAR guided the design of second-generation inhibitors, including 6bK, which incorporates a lysine residue to improve aqueous solubility and enable isotopic labeling.

### Methods for Detecting IDE

The invention further provides methods for detecting IDE expression or activity in biological samples, leveraging the high affinity and selectivity of the macrocyclic inhibitors. In one embodiment, a time-resolved fluorescence resonance energy transfer (TR-FRET) assay is employed, wherein anti-insulin antibodies labeled with donor and acceptor fluorophores are used to monitor insulin degradation by IDE in real time. In the presence of an IDE inhibitor, insulin degradation is suppressed, leading to increased FRET signal. This homogeneous assay is suitable for high-throughput screening and kinetic characterization.

Alternatively, a liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) assay can be used to quantify cleavage fragments of IDE substrates such as calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) in mouse plasma. By spiking plasma with CGRP and incubating ex vivo in the presence or absence of inhibitor, the extent of IDE-mediated proteolysis can be directly measured. This method provides high specificity and sensitivity, particularly when combined with stable isotope-labeled internal standards.

For cellular or tissue-based detection, immunohistochemistry or Western blotting using anti-IDE antibodies remains a standard approach. However, the invention enhances these methods by providing labeled macrocyclic probes that bind specifically to active IDE, thereby distinguishing functional enzyme from inactive or denatured forms. For example, a biotinylated derivative of 6b can be used to pull down IDE from cell lysates, followed by streptavidin-based detection.

### Labeled IDE Inhibitor Probes

Labeled derivatives of the macrocyclic inhibitors serve as powerful tools for studying IDE biology and pharmacology. In a preferred embodiment, 6bK is synthesized with 13C6,15N2-lysine to produce a heavy isotope-labeled internal standard for quantitative LC-MS analysis. This enables precise measurement of 6bK concentrations in plasma, liver, kidney, and other tissues following in vivo administration, using isotope dilution mass spectrometry (IDMS). Such pharmacokinetic profiling revealed that 6bK achieves micromolar concentrations in plasma within 15 minutes of intraperitoneal injection and exhibits a half-life of approximately 2 hours, with preferential distribution to metabolically active tissues and negligible brain penetration.

Fluorescently labeled probes, such as 6b conjugated to Alexa Fluor 488, can be used for live-cell imaging or flow cytometry to visualize IDE localization and dynamics. Radioactive labeling (e.g., with 3H or 125I) enables autoradiography or receptor binding assays with high sensitivity. These probes are invaluable for target engagement studies, biodistribution analysis, and validation of IDE as a therapeutic target in disease models.

### Clinical Applications of Macrocyclic IDE Inhibitors

The macrocyclic IDE inhibitors of the invention have significant therapeutic potential for the treatment of metabolic disorders. In lean and diet-induced obese (DIO) mouse models, acute administration of 6bK (80 mg/kg, i.p.) enhanced insulin sensitivity, as demonstrated by an exaggerated hypoglycemic response to exogenous insulin. During intraperitoneal glucose tolerance tests (IPGTT), 6bK-treated mice exhibited improved glucose clearance, although high doses in DIO mice sometimes produced a transient hyperglycemic rebound, likely due to amplified glucagon signaling.

Notably, 6bK did not alter basal glucose or hormone levels in the absence of a nutrient challenge, indicating that its effects are context-dependent and unlikely to cause hypoglycemia under fasting conditions. Furthermore, 6bK slowed gastric emptying in an amylin-dependent manner, as co-administration of the amylin receptor antagonist AC187 abolished this effect. This suggests that IDE inhibition potentiates endogenous amylin activity, which may contribute to satiety and postprandial glucose control.

In Gcgr−/− mice, the glucose-lowering effect of 6bK was blunted during oral glucose challenges but preserved during IPGTT, highlighting the role of glucagon signaling in mediating the inhibitor’s effects. These findings support the development of IDE inhibitors as adjunctive therapy for type 2 diabetes, particularly in patients with residual beta-cell function and intact incretin responses.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In this example, a DNA-templated macrocycle library containing 13,824 members was screened against His6-tagged mouse IDE immobilized on cobalt magnetic beads. Two independent selections were performed, and sequencing of enriched DNA barcodes identified six macrocyclic compounds (1–6) consistently enriched at least twofold. The trans isomers (1b–6b) were synthesized by Fmoc solid-phase synthesis and purified by HPLC. IDE inhibition was assayed using the fluorogenic substrate Mca-RPPGFSAFK(Dnp)-OH, revealing IC50 values ranging from 0.5 to 5 µM, with 6b being the most potent (IC50 = 0.5 µM). SAR studies with 30 analogues confirmed the importance of the benzophenone and cyclohexylalanine groups. Mutagenesis of IDE (A479L, G362Q) reduced 6b potency by >600-fold and 77-fold, respectively, but activity was restored using analogues with smaller substituents, confirming the binding epitope. In vivo, 6bK (80 mg/kg, i.p.) enhanced insulin sensitivity and improved glucose tolerance in mice without detectable brain exposure.

### Example 2

In this example, the metabolic effects of 6bK were evaluated in lean and DIO C57BL/6J mice. Mice were fasted overnight and injected i.p. with 6bK (80 mg/kg) or vehicle. Thirty minutes later, an insulin tolerance test (5-h fast) showed significantly greater glucose lowering in 6bK-treated mice. During IPGTT, 6bK improved glucose clearance in lean mice but caused a biphasic response in DIO mice (initial hyperglycemia followed by improved disposal). Plasma hormone analysis revealed elevated insulin, glucagon, and amylin levels post-glucose, consistent with IDE inhibition. Gastric emptying was slowed by 6bK, an effect reversed by the amylin antagonist AC187. In Gcgr−/− mice, 6bK failed to improve oral glucose tolerance, confirming glucagon dependence. Biodistribution studies using 13C6,15N2-6bK showed high plasma and liver concentrations but no brain accumulation. No changes in brain Aβ(40/42) were observed, supporting peripheral selectivity.
