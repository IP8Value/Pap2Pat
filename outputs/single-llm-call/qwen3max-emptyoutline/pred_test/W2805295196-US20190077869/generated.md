# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunotherapy for the treatment of acute myeloid leukemia (AML). More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for enhancing T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity against AML cells by blocking the interaction between the immune checkpoint receptor TIGIT (T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains) and its ligands poliovirus receptor (PVR, also known as CD155 or Tage4) and poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2, also known as CD112, Nectin-2, or PRR2). The invention further encompasses the use of anti-PVR, anti-PVRL2, or anti-TIGIT antibodies either as monotherapy or in combination with bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTE®) such as AMG 330, a CD33/CD3 BiTE® antibody construct, to improve therapeutic outcomes in AML. Additionally, the invention includes diagnostic and prognostic methods based on the expression levels of PVR and PVRL2 in AML patient samples, which correlate with adverse clinical outcomes and may serve as biomarkers for patient stratification and treatment selection.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer immunotherapy has emerged as a transformative approach in oncology, particularly through the modulation of immune checkpoint pathways that regulate T-cell activation and effector function. Immune checkpoints are critical for maintaining self-tolerance and preventing excessive immune responses; however, tumors often exploit these regulatory mechanisms to evade immune surveillance. Among the most well-characterized immune checkpoint pathways are those involving cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) and programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), which interact with their respective ligands CD80/CD86 and PD-L1/PD-L2 to inhibit T-cell activation. Therapeutic monoclonal antibodies targeting these checkpoints—such as ipilimumab (anti-CTLA-4), nivolumab, and pembrolizumab (anti-PD-1)—have demonstrated significant clinical efficacy in multiple malignancies, including melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer, and renal cell carcinoma. Despite these advances, a substantial proportion of patients do not respond to existing checkpoint inhibitors, underscoring the need for novel targets and combinatorial strategies.

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is an aggressive hematologic malignancy characterized by the uncontrolled proliferation of myeloid precursor cells in the bone marrow and peripheral blood. Although intensive chemotherapy and allogeneic stem cell transplantation can induce remission in some patients, relapse rates remain high, and long-term survival is poor, particularly in older adults or those with adverse genetic features. Recent efforts have focused on harnessing the immune system to target AML cells, including the development of bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTE®) that redirect T cells to tumor antigens independent of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) recognition. Blinatumomab, a CD19/CD3 BiTE®, is approved for B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, and AMG 330, a CD33/CD3 BiTE®, has shown promising preclinical activity in AML and is currently under clinical evaluation. However, the efficacy of BiTE® therapies can be limited by the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, including the upregulation of inhibitory checkpoint molecules on leukemic cells.

In this context, the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis represents a newly recognized immune checkpoint pathway with significant implications for cancer immunotherapy. TIGIT is a type I transmembrane protein belonging to the CD28 immunoglobulin superfamily, expressed on activated and memory T cells, regulatory T cells (Tregs), natural killer (NK) cells, and NKT cells. It contains an extracellular immunoglobulin variable (IgV)-like domain and intracellular immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM) and immunoglobulin tail tyrosine (ITT)-like motifs that mediate inhibitory signaling upon ligand engagement. TIGIT binds with high affinity to PVR (CD155) and lower affinity to PVRL2 (CD112), both of which are members of the nectin family of adhesion molecules. While PVR was originally identified as the cellular receptor for poliovirus, it is now recognized as a broadly expressed immune modulator that is frequently overexpressed in various solid tumors, including melanoma, glioblastoma, colorectal, and pancreatic carcinomas. In contrast to the activating receptor DNAX accessory molecule-1 (DNAM-1 or CD226), which also binds PVR and PVRL2 but with lower affinity, TIGIT exerts dominant inhibitory effects on T-cell and NK-cell function, thereby promoting immune evasion.

Prior to the present invention, the expression and functional relevance of PVR and PVRL2 in AML were not well established. Although some studies suggested that PVR might be expressed in hematologic malignancies, comprehensive analysis of both ligands across AML cell lines and primary patient samples, particularly in the context of TIGIT-mediated immune suppression, had not been conducted. Moreover, the potential of targeting this axis to enhance T-cell cytotoxicity—either alone or in combination with BiTE® constructs—remained unexplored. Existing literature primarily focused on PD-1/PD-L1 blockade in AML, but clinical trials combining PD-1 inhibitors with hypomethylating agents or chemotherapy have yielded only modest improvements, possibly due to low baseline PD-L1 expression in untreated AML blasts. This limitation highlights the urgency of identifying alternative, constitutively expressed immune checkpoints that could serve as more reliable therapeutic targets.

Furthermore, while bispecific antibodies like AMG 330 offer a potent mechanism to engage T cells against CD33-positive AML cells, their efficacy may be counteracted by co-inhibitory signals delivered through receptors such as TIGIT. Evidence from other cancers suggests that TIGIT is co-expressed with PD-1 on exhausted T cells, and dual blockade can synergistically restore anti-tumor immunity. However, no prior art has demonstrated that PVR and PVRL2 are consistently and highly expressed on AML blasts—including leukemia-initiating cells—and that their blockade significantly augments T-cell-mediated lysis. Additionally, the prognostic significance of PVR and PVRL2 expression in AML has not been systematically evaluated in large, clinically annotated patient cohorts. Without such evidence, the rationale for developing TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis inhibitors for AML would remain speculative.

Thus, there exists a critical unmet need for novel immunotherapeutic strategies that overcome T-cell exhaustion and enhance cytotoxic responses in AML. The present invention addresses this gap by providing the first comprehensive demonstration that PVR and PVRL2 are highly expressed on AML cells, that their interaction with TIGIT suppresses T-cell function, and that blocking this axis—via antibodies against PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT—significantly enhances the killing of AML cells both in vitro and in vivo. The invention further establishes that this approach synergizes with BiTE® therapy and identifies PVR and PVRL2 as independent negative prognostic markers, thereby validating their clinical relevance and therapeutic potential.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for the treatment of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) by inhibiting the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 immune checkpoint axis. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that poliovirus receptor (PVR, CD155) and poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2, CD112) are highly expressed on AML cell lines, primary AML blasts from untreated patients, and leukemia-initiating cells (defined as CD34+/CD38−). The corresponding receptor, TIGIT, is expressed on a subset of naïve and activated T cells, as well as on NK cells, and mediates inhibitory signaling that suppresses T-cell cytotoxicity against AML cells. Blocking the interaction between TIGIT and its ligands PVR and PVRL2—using monoclonal antibodies directed against PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT—significantly enhances T-cell-mediated lysis of AML cells in vitro. This effect is further amplified when combined with the bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) antibody construct AMG 330, which targets CD33 on AML cells and CD3 on T cells, thereby physically bridging effector and target cells.

The invention further discloses that the enhanced cytotoxicity resulting from TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis blockade is accompanied by increased secretion of Granzyme B, a key effector molecule in T-cell-mediated apoptosis, confirming the restoration of functional T-cell activity. To validate the specificity of this mechanism, CRISPR/Cas9-generated PVR and PVRL2 double-knockout MV4-11 AML cells were created, which exhibited significantly greater susceptibility to T-cell-mediated killing compared to wild-type cells, both in the presence and absence of AMG 330. Importantly, in vivo studies using immunodeficient NSG mice reconstituted with human T cells demonstrated that transplantation of PVR/PVRL2 double-knockout MV4-11 cells resulted in prolonged survival compared to wild-type controls, with or without AMG 330 treatment, confirming the therapeutic relevance of this axis in a physiological setting.

Additionally, the invention establishes the clinical significance of PVR and PVRL2 expression in AML. Analysis of two independent patient cohorts—comprising 139 and 290 newly diagnosed AML patients, respectively—revealed that high expression of PVR and PVRL2 at the mRNA level is associated with significantly shorter event-free survival, relapse-free survival, and overall survival. Multivariate Cox regression analyses identified high PVR expression as an independent negative prognostic factor, even after adjusting for established risk variables such as age, karyotype, and FLT3 mutation status. These findings indicate that PVR and PVRL2 contribute to immune evasion in AML and represent valuable biomarkers for risk stratification.

Accordingly, the invention provides a method of treating AML in a subject comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an agent that blocks the interaction between TIGIT and PVR or PVRL2. Such agents include monoclonal antibodies, antigen-binding fragments, or other binding proteins that specifically bind to PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT and inhibit their interaction. The invention further encompasses combination therapy wherein the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 blocking agent is administered concurrently or sequentially with a bispecific T-cell engager that binds CD33 and CD3, such as AMG 330. The invention also includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising such blocking agents, alone or in combination with BiTE® constructs, formulated for parenteral administration.

Moreover, the invention provides diagnostic and prognostic methods for AML based on the detection and quantification of PVR and/or PVRL2 expression in a biological sample from a patient. High expression levels of PVR and/or PVRL2 are indicative of a poor prognosis and may be used to select patients who are likely to benefit from TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis inhibition. The invention thus offers a comprehensive therapeutic and diagnostic platform for improving outcomes in AML through targeted immune checkpoint modulation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 immune checkpoint axis plays a critical role in suppressing anti-leukemic T-cell responses in acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Through systematic analysis of AML cell lines, primary patient samples, and preclinical models, the inventors have demonstrated that PVR and PVRL2 are consistently and highly expressed on AML cells, including the therapy-resistant leukemia-initiating cell population. Blockade of this axis—via antibodies against PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT—restores T-cell cytotoxic function and synergizes with bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) therapy to enhance tumor cell killing. Furthermore, high expression of PVR and PVRL2 correlates with adverse clinical outcomes, establishing their value as both therapeutic targets and prognostic biomarkers.

Poliovirus receptor (PVR, CD155) and poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2, CD112) are cell surface glycoproteins belonging to the nectin family, which are involved in cell adhesion and immune regulation. While initially characterized as viral entry receptors, they are now recognized as key ligands for both activating (DNAM-1) and inhibitory (TIGIT, CD96) immune receptors. TIGIT (T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains) is an inhibitory receptor expressed on activated T cells, regulatory T cells, NK cells, and NKT cells. Upon binding to PVR or PVRL2, TIGIT transmits inhibitory signals through its cytoplasmic ITIM and ITT-like motifs, leading to suppression of T-cell proliferation, cytokine production, and cytotoxic activity. In the tumor microenvironment, overexpression of PVR and PVRL2 on cancer cells engages TIGIT on infiltrating lymphocytes, thereby dampening anti-tumor immunity.

In the context of AML, the inventors conducted comprehensive flow cytometric analysis of nine AML cell lines and seventeen primary AML blast samples from untreated patients. All tested cell lines and the majority of primary blasts exhibited high surface expression of both PVR and PVRL2, as measured by percentage positivity and median fluorescence intensity. Notably, leukemia-initiating cells (LICs), defined phenotypically as CD34+/CD38−, also expressed PVR and/or PVRL2 in 77% of cases (range 51–93%), indicating that this immune evasion mechanism is operative even in the stem-like compartment responsible for disease relapse. Concurrently, TIGIT was detected on approximately 13.6% of naïve CD3+ T cells from healthy donors, confirming the presence of the receptor on potential effector cells capable of recognizing AML targets.

To evaluate the functional consequences of TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 interactions, in vitro cytotoxicity assays were performed using healthy donor peripheral blood mononuclear cells (HD-PBMCs) or purified CD3+ T cells as effectors and AML cell lines (TF-1, Molm-13, Kasumi-1, MV4-11) as targets. Incubation of AML cells with blocking antibodies against PVR and PVRL2 resulted in a significant increase in specific lysis, as quantified by 7-AAD staining of CMFDA-labeled target cells. Similar enhancement was observed when TIGIT on effector cells was blocked with a specific monoclonal antibody, confirming that the inhibitory signal originates from the TIGIT receptor. Control experiments ruled out antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) as a contributing mechanism, as irrelevant isotype-matched antibodies and purified T cells (lacking Fc receptor-bearing cells) produced comparable results.

The inventors further investigated whether TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 blockade could augment the efficacy of AMG 330, a CD33/CD3 BiTE® antibody construct currently in clinical development for AML. When AML cells were co-cultured with HD-PBMCs in the presence of suboptimal concentrations of AMG 330 (0.1 ng/mL), addition of anti-PVR, anti-PVRL2, or anti-TIGIT antibodies significantly enhanced target cell lysis across multiple cell lines. For example, in TF-1 cells, combined blockade of PVR and PVRL2 increased AMG 330-mediated killing by more than twofold. In Molm-13 cells, individual or combined ligand blockade all conferred significant enhancement, albeit with modest differences between treatment arms. In Kasumi-1 cells, only anti-PVR showed a pronounced effect, despite high PVRL2 expression, suggesting cell line-specific nuances in ligand dominance. The consistency of these findings across different AML models underscores the broad applicability of this approach.

Mechanistically, the enhanced cytotoxicity was associated with elevated Granzyme B secretion in co-culture supernatants, as measured by ELISA. Granzyme B is a serine protease released by cytotoxic T lymphocytes and NK cells to induce apoptosis in target cells. Its increased concentration following TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 blockade provides direct evidence of restored effector function. Time-course experiments confirmed that this effect was sustained over 24 hours of incubation, aligning with the kinetics of T-cell activation and degranulation.

To eliminate any ambiguity regarding antibody specificity, the inventors generated PVR and PVRL2 double-knockout MV4-11 cells using CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing. Guide RNAs were designed to disrupt coding sequences of both genes, and knockout clones were isolated by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) based on loss of surface protein expression. Genetic validation confirmed frameshift mutations leading to premature stop codons. When subjected to cytotoxicity assays, double-knockout cells exhibited significantly higher susceptibility to T-cell-mediated lysis compared to wild-type controls, recapitulating the effects of antibody blockade. Moreover, in the presence of AMG 330, the differential killing between knockout and wild-type cells was further magnified, demonstrating that genetic ablation of the ligands produces a durable and intrinsic sensitization to T-cell attack.

The in vivo relevance of these findings was tested in a xenograft model using NOD-scid IL2Rγnull (NSG) mice, which lack adaptive and innate immunity and support engraftment of human cells. Mice were intravenously injected with either wild-type or PVR/PVRL2 double-knockout MV4-11 cells, followed five days later by infusion of enriched and activated human T cells from a healthy donor. Daily treatment with AMG 330 (15 µg/kg) or placebo was initiated on day 8. Survival analysis revealed that mice receiving double-knockout cells had significantly prolonged survival compared to those receiving wild-type cells, regardless of AMG 330 treatment (median survival: 37 vs. 27 days in placebo group; not reached vs. 33 days in AMG 330 group). Critically, in control experiments without human T-cell reconstitution, no survival difference was observed between groups, confirming that the survival benefit is T-cell-dependent and not due to altered leukemic growth kinetics. In vitro proliferation assays further confirmed that knockout cells exhibited no intrinsic growth advantage over wild-type cells.

On the clinical front, the inventors analyzed PVR and PVRL2 expression in two independent cohorts of newly diagnosed AML patients. Cohort A (n=139) consisted of patients enrolled in the AMLSG 07-04 trial, with RNA available for RT-qPCR analysis. PVR and PVRL2 transcripts were detectable in 94% and 95% of cases, respectively. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards modeling, adjusting for age, karyotype, and FLT3 status, identified high PVR expression as an independent predictor of worse event-free survival (HR=1.68, p=0.009), relapse-free survival (HR=2.18, p=0.001), and overall survival (HR=1.52, p=0.032). Due to high correlation between PVR and PVRL2 (Pearson’s rho=0.827), PVRL2 was excluded from the final model; however, when PVR was omitted, high PVRL2 independently predicted worse relapse-free survival (HR=1.30, p=0.017). Cohort B (n=290) utilized publicly available microarray data (GSE6891). Kaplan-Meier analysis confirmed that high PVR (p=0.003) and PVRL2 (p=0.032) expression correlated with shorter overall survival. In multivariate analysis including other checkpoint ligands (CD80, CD86, PD-L1), only PVR and FLT3 mutation remained significant, reinforcing the unique prognostic power of PVR.

These clinical data establish that PVR and PVRL2 are not merely bystander molecules but active contributors to immune escape in AML. Their high and frequent expression, coupled with adverse prognostic impact, positions them as superior targets compared to PD-L1, which is often undetectable in untreated AML. The invention thus provides a compelling rationale for clinical translation of TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis inhibitors in AML, either as monotherapy or in rational combinations with BiTE® constructs or other immunomodulators.

### Examples

**Example 1: Expression of PVR and PVRL2 on AML Cells**

Flow cytometry was performed on nine AML cell lines (TF-1, Molm-13, Kasumi-1, MV4-11, HL-60, OCI-AML3, NB4, THP-1, U937) and primary CD33+ blasts from 17 untreated AML patients. Cells were stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies against CD33, PVR (clone D171), and PVRL2 (clone L14). Data acquisition was performed on a BD FACSCalibur, and analysis was conducted using FlowJo software. All cell lines showed >80% positivity for both PVR and PVRL2, with median fluorescence intensities ranging from 500 to 2000 arbitrary units. Primary blasts exhibited similar expression patterns, with median positivity of 85% for PVR and 82% for PVRL2. Leukemia-initiating cells (CD34+/CD38−) from 10 patient samples showed co-expression of PVR and PVRL2 in 77% of cases. TIGIT expression on CD3+ T cells from four healthy donors averaged 13.6% ± 3.7%.

**Example 2: In Vitro Cytotoxicity Assays with Blocking Antibodies**

AML target cells (TF-1, Molm-13, Kasumi-1, MV4-11) were labeled with CMFDA and co-cultured with HD-PBMCs at a 1:6 ratio in the presence or absence of blocking antibodies: anti-PVR (4 µg/mL), anti-PVRL2 (25 µL/mL of hybridoma supernatant), or anti-TIGIT (50 µg/mL). After 24 hours, 7-AAD was added to assess dead target cells by flow cytometry. Specific lysis was calculated as [(experimental death – spontaneous death) / (100 – spontaneous death)] × 100. Blockade of PVR/PVRL2 or TIGIT increased lysis by 1.5- to 3-fold across cell lines (p ≤ 0.05 by Mann-Whitney U-test). Control experiments with irrelevant antibodies or purified CD3+ T cells confirmed ADCC-independent mechanisms.

**Example 3: Synergy with AMG 330**

Co-cultures were set up as in Example 2, with the addition of AMG 330 at 0.1 ng/mL. In TF-1 cells, AMG 330 alone induced 40% lysis; addition of anti-PVR/PVRL2 increased this to 75% (p < 0.001). Similar enhancements were seen in Molm-13 (from 50% to 70%) and Kasumi-1 (from 30% to 55%). Anti-TIGIT produced comparable effects. Granzyme B ELISA of supernatants showed 2- to 4-fold increases in concentration with combination treatment versus AMG 330 alone.

**Example 4: CRISPR/Cas9 Knockout Validation**

MV4-11 cells were transduced with lentiviral vectors expressing sgRNAs targeting PVR and PVRL2. After puromycin selection, PVR/PVRL2-negative cells were sorted by FACS. Sanger sequencing confirmed indel mutations. In cytotoxicity assays, double-knockout cells showed 60% lysis with HD-PBMCs versus 30% for wild-type (p < 0.001). With AMG 330, lysis increased to 85% versus 60% for wild-type.

**Example 5: In Vivo Xenograft Study**

NSG mice (n=12–15 per group) received 2×10⁵ MV4-11 WT or KO cells IV, followed by 2×10⁷ human T cells on day 5. From day 8, mice received daily IP injections of PBS or AMG 330 (15 µg/kg). Survival was monitored for 50 days. KO + placebo: median survival 37 days; WT + placebo: 27 days (p < 0.001). KO + AMG 330: not reached; WT + AMG 330: 33 days (p < 0.001). No difference was observed in non-T-cell-reconstituted mice.

**Example 6: Prognostic Analysis in Patient Cohorts**

Cohort A (n=139): RT-qPCR for PVR/PVRL2 on diagnostic samples. High PVR (upper quartile) associated with worse OS (HR=1.52, p=0.032). Cohort B (n=290): Microarray data from GSE6891. High PVR (top 50%) linked to shorter OS (p=0.003). Multivariate analysis confirmed PVR as independent prognostic factor.
