# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

The global polymers industry is currently dominated by synthetic materials derived from petroleum-based feedstocks, which contribute significantly to the world’s carbon footprint and energy consumption. Plastics alone account for more than 7% of annual global fossil fuel usage, a figure that is projected to increase as industrialization and consumer demand rise in emerging economies. In response to mounting environmental concerns and regulatory pressures, there has been a growing impetus to transition toward renewable, bio-based alternatives that can mitigate the ecological impact of polymer production without compromising performance or cost-efficiency. Despite this urgent need, renewable polymers currently represent less than 5% of the global market, largely due to perceived limitations in mechanical properties, thermal stability, processability, and economic viability compared to conventional petrochemical counterparts. A critical challenge lies in developing new monomers that are not only derived from sustainable sources but also compatible with existing industrial polymerization infrastructure—ideally enabling “drop-in” replacement without requiring costly retooling or process redesign.

Terpenes, a class of naturally occurring hydrocarbons abundant in plant essential oils, have emerged as promising candidates for renewable monomer development. These compounds—such as α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene, and carvone—are commercially available on multi-tonne scales as byproducts of the citrus and pulp industries, making them economically attractive and environmentally benign starting materials. Structurally, terpenes contain one or more alkene functionalities, which theoretically lend themselves to polymerization via free radical, cationic, or coordination mechanisms. However, decades of research have demonstrated that most terpenes resist homopolymerization under standard conditions. Even when used as co-monomers with styrene or acrylates, their incorporation into polymer chains is typically low, and the resulting materials exhibit poor molecular weights and inadequate mechanical properties. While specialized techniques such as cationic polymerization at cryogenic temperatures (e.g., −80 °C) or ruthenium-catalyzed ring-opening metathesis have achieved limited success with specific terpenes like β-pinene or sesquiterpenes (e.g., caryophyllene), these methods are neither scalable nor compatible with mainstream industrial processes.

Alternative strategies have focused on chemical modification of terpenes to introduce polymerizable functional groups. For instance, oxidation of limonene to epoxides followed by copolymerization with CO₂ using zinc catalysts has yielded polycarbonates with high glass transition temperatures (T<sub>g</sub> ≈ 100 °C). Similarly, Baeyer-Villiger oxidation of carvone or menthone has produced lactone monomers suitable for ring-opening polymerization. Other approaches involve esterification of terpenoid carboxylic acids (e.g., abietic acid from rosin) with acryloyl chlorides to generate acrylate-functionalized monomers for photoresist applications. However, these methodologies are inherently limited to terpenes possessing reactive functional groups such as –OH or –COOH, thereby excluding the vast majority of commercially available terpene structures that lack such handles. Moreover, many of these synthetic routes rely on hazardous reagents (e.g., acryloyl chloride), non-renewable solvents, or stringent reaction conditions (e.g., anhydrous, anaerobic, or cryogenic environments), which undermine their sustainability and scalability.

A further limitation in prior art is the inability to control polymer architecture, molecular weight, and crosslinking behavior in terpene-derived polymers. For example, attempts to polymerize unmodified carvone often result in premature gelation due to unintended participation of multiple double bonds in chain propagation, leading to insoluble networks rather than processable linear polymers. This lack of control severely restricts the applicability of such materials in coatings, adhesives, or thermoplastics, where precise tuning of rheological and thermal properties is essential. Additionally, while some terpene-based polymers exhibit moderate T<sub>g</sub> values, few approach the performance benchmarks of engineering thermoplastics like polycarbonates or polyesters, limiting their use in high-value applications.

In light of these challenges, there remains a significant unmet need for a robust, scalable, and environmentally responsible method to convert abundant terpenes into well-defined, high-performance acrylic and methacrylic monomers that can be polymerized using standard industrial techniques—including free radical, controlled radical, or melt-processing methods—to yield materials with tunable thermal, mechanical, and chemical properties. Such a platform would not only advance the field of sustainable polymer science but also enable the direct integration of bio-based content into existing product lines across coatings, automotive, packaging, and construction industries. The present invention addresses this gap by introducing novel synthetic pathways that transform readily available terpenes into functionalized acrylate and methacrylate monomers, which can be polymerized into linear, high-molecular-weight polymers with predictable architectures and enhanced performance characteristics, including unexpectedly high glass transition temperatures and utility in advanced coating systems.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and industrially viable method for the synthesis and polymerization of renewable monomers derived from naturally occurring terpenes, specifically through the introduction of acrylate or methacrylate functional groups onto terpene scaffolds. These modified monomers are designed to overcome the historical limitations of terpene-based polymerization by enabling facile, controlled, and high-yield polymerization under standard industrial conditions, yielding polymers with tunable molecular weights, thermal properties, and mechanical performance. The invention encompasses both a two-step synthetic route involving hydroboration-oxidation or ketone reduction followed by esterification, and a more sustainable, catalytic one-pot method based on palladium-catalyzed allylic C–H oxidation, which directly couples terpenes with acrylic or methacrylic acid to form polymerizable monomers in high regioselectivity and yield.

Central to the invention is the discovery that certain terpene-derived acrylate and methacrylate monomers exhibit distinct polymerization behaviors dictated by the electronic and steric nature of the introduced vinyl group. In particular, methacrylate-functionalized terpenes demonstrate superior control over polymer architecture, avoiding unwanted crosslinking even when residual olefinic moieties are present, whereas analogous acrylates may undergo gelation due to dual reactivity of internal and terminal double bonds. This differential reactivity is leveraged to design linear, high-T<sub>g</sub> polymers suitable for demanding applications such as powder coatings, where thermal stability, hardness, and adhesion are critical. Notably, the invention discloses that polymers derived from α-pinene methacrylate exhibit glass transition temperatures as high as 142 °C—unprecedented for acrylic polymers and comparable to those of polycarbonates—thereby opening new avenues for bio-based engineering thermoplastics.

The invention further includes the formulation and application of these terpene-based polymers in industrial coating systems. Specifically, a carvone-derived methacrylate polymer is combined with a commercial thiol-based crosslinker and processed via standard powder coating protocols to produce transparent, glossy, and chemically resistant films that meet industry benchmarks for pencil hardness, König hardness, and adhesion. The retained exocyclic double bond in each monomer unit serves as a latent site for post-polymerization crosslinking, enabling UV- or thermal-cure mechanisms that enhance coating durability without compromising processability during extrusion or electrostatic spraying.

Additionally, the invention introduces a green chemistry advancement by replacing traditional, hazardous acylating agents (e.g., acryloyl chloride) with renewable acrylic and methacrylic acids, coupled with the use of propyl phosphonic anhydride (T3P®) as a coupling reagent that generates water-soluble, non-toxic byproducts. This modification significantly improves the environmental profile of the synthesis while maintaining high yields and purity. The catalytic one-pot method, utilizing only 2 mol% palladium acetate, benzoquinone as a multifunctional additive (oxidant, ligand, and polymerization inhibitor), and air as the terminal oxidant, represents a scalable and atom-economical route to terpene acrylates, particularly for β-pinene, achieving up to 82% yield with excellent regioselectivity (10:1 ratio of two isomers).

In summary, the invention provides a comprehensive platform for the sustainable production of high-performance polymers from renewable terpenes, combining innovative synthetic chemistry, controlled polymerization, and practical application in industrial formulations. The resulting materials offer a compelling combination of bio-based content, process compatibility, and enhanced physical properties, fulfilling the long-standing need for drop-in renewable alternatives that do not sacrifice performance or cost-efficiency.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Terpinolene and Gamma-Terpinene as Chain Transfer Agents

While the primary focus of the present invention lies in the synthesis and polymerization of terpene-derived acrylate and methacrylate monomers, it has been unexpectedly discovered that certain terpenes—specifically terpinolene and gamma-terpinene—exhibit significant efficacy as chain transfer agents (CTAs) in free radical polymerization processes. This finding represents a novel and advantageous application of these naturally occurring compounds, which are typically regarded as inert diluents or fragrance components rather than functional additives in polymer chemistry. Chain transfer agents are critical in controlling molecular weight, narrowing polydispersity, and modulating end-group functionality in free radical polymerizations, and their selection profoundly influences the final properties of the polymer. Conventional CTAs such as dodecanethiol (DDM) are effective but suffer from drawbacks including odor, toxicity, and the introduction of sulfur-containing end groups that may compromise thermal stability or color. In contrast, terpinolene and gamma-terpinene are renewable, low-odor, and sulfur-free, offering a sustainable alternative with additional benefits stemming from their unique molecular structure.

Terpinolene (1-methyl-4-(1-methylethenyl)-1,3-cyclohexadiene) and gamma-terpinene (1-methyl-4-(1-methylethyl)-1,3-cyclohexadiene) are monoterpene hydrocarbons containing conjugated diene systems and allylic methyl groups that are highly susceptible to hydrogen abstraction by propagating polymer radicals. During free radical polymerization, the growing polymer chain radical can abstract an allylic hydrogen from terpinolene or gamma-terpinene, thereby terminating the original chain and generating a resonance-stabilized terpene radical. This terpene radical, while less reactive than typical carbon-centered radicals, can reinitiate polymerization, albeit at a slower rate, leading to a net reduction in molecular weight without complete inhibition of the reaction. The efficiency of this chain transfer process is quantified by the chain transfer constant (C<sub>tr</sub>), which for terpinolene and gamma-terpinene has been found to be comparable to or greater than that of conventional alkyl thiols under similar conditions.

Experimental studies conducted as part of this invention demonstrate that the inclusion of terpinolene or gamma-terpinene at concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 10 wt% relative to monomer results in a predictable and dose-dependent decrease in the number-average molecular weight (M<sub>n</sub>) of the resulting polymer, consistent with classical chain transfer behavior. For example, in the polymerization of methyl methacrylate (MMA) at 65 °C using AIBN as initiator, the addition of 1 wt% terpinolene reduced M<sub>n</sub> from approximately 120,000 g/mol (control) to 45,000 g/mol, while maintaining high conversion (>90%) and narrow polydispersity (Đ < 1.5). Similar effects were observed with gamma-terpinene, though terpinolene exhibited slightly higher chain transfer activity due to its more accessible allylic hydrogens and extended conjugation.

Importantly, the use of these terpenes as CTAs does not introduce undesirable side reactions such as branching or crosslinking, as their radical intermediates are stabilized and do not readily participate in addition to double bonds under standard conditions. Furthermore, the terpene-derived end groups impart subtle modifications to polymer properties, including enhanced hydrophobicity, altered surface energy, and potential for post-functionalization via the residual double bonds. In the context of the terpene-based monomers disclosed herein—such as α-pinene methacrylate or carvone methacrylate—the concurrent use of terpinolene or gamma-terpinene as CTAs enables fine-tuning of molecular weight without interfering with the main-chain architecture or the integrity of pendant functional groups.

This dual functionality—as both monomer precursors and polymerization modifiers—underscores the versatility of terpenes in sustainable polymer design. The ability to use terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as green chain transfer agents aligns with the broader objectives of the invention to maximize the utility of renewable feedstocks while minimizing reliance on petrochemical additives. Their incorporation into industrial polymerization recipes offers a straightforward path to reducing molecular weight in acrylic systems, improving melt flow for extrusion or injection molding, and tailoring mechanical properties for specific applications, all while maintaining the environmental and performance advantages of bio-based materials.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The synthesis and polymerization of α-pinene-derived methacrylate monomer (1c) and its subsequent evaluation as a high-T<sub>g</sub> polymer are described herein as a representative embodiment of the invention. (+)-α-Pinene was first converted to the corresponding alcohol (1a) via a hydroboration-oxidation sequence. Specifically, (+)-α-pinene (50.0 g, 367 mmol) was dissolved in dry tetrahydrofuran (THF, 500 mL) under nitrogen atmosphere and cooled to 0 °C. A solution of borane-dimethyl sulfide complex (10.0 M in THF, 36.7 mL, 367 mmol) was added dropwise over 15 minutes, and the reaction mixture was allowed to warm to room temperature and stirred for 12 hours. The reaction was then quenched by cautious addition of 3 M aqueous sodium hydroxide (100 mL), followed by the slow addition of 30% hydrogen peroxide (50 mL). After stirring for an additional 2 hours, the mixture was extracted with diethyl ether (3 × 100 mL), and the combined organic layers were dried over anhydrous magnesium sulfate, filtered, and concentrated under reduced pressure to yield alcohol 1a as a colorless oil (52.1 g, 92% yield). The structure was confirmed by <sup>1</sup>H NMR and <sup>13</sup>C NMR spectroscopy, showing characteristic signals for the tertiary alcohol and pinane skeleton.

In the second step, alcohol 1a was esterified with methacrylic acid using propyl phosphonic anhydride (T3P®, 50% in ethyl acetate) as the coupling reagent. Alcohol 1a (20.0 g, 130 mmol) and methacrylic acid (13.5 g, 156 mmol) were dissolved in dichloromethane (200 mL), and triethylamine (18.2 mL, 130 mmol) was added, followed by T3P® (50.0 g, 156 mmol). The reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature for 24 hours under nitrogen. The solvent was then removed under reduced pressure, and the crude product was purified by flash chromatography on silica gel (eluent: hexane/ethyl acetate 9:1) to afford methacrylate monomer 1c as a clear, colorless liquid (24.3 g, 88% yield). Spectroscopic data confirmed the presence of the methacrylate vinyl protons (δ 5.6 and 6.1 ppm) and the absence of hydroxyl signals.

Monomer 1c was then subjected to free radical polymerization. In a typical procedure, 1c (5.0 g, 23.6 mmol), AIBN (0.12 g, 0.73 mmol, 3 mol%), and dodecanethiol (DDM, 0.05 g, 0.24 mmol, 1 wt%) were dissolved in toluene (10 mL) in a sealed ampoule. The solution was degassed by three freeze-pump-thaw cycles and heated at 65 °C for 24 hours. The resulting polymer was precipitated into cold methanol, filtered, and dried under vacuum to yield a white solid (4.7 g, 94% yield). Gel permeation chromatography (GPC) analysis (vs. polystyrene standards) showed M<sub>n</sub> = 23,600 g/mol, Đ = 1.42. Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) revealed a glass transition temperature (T<sub>g</sub>) of 142 °C, which is remarkably high for an acrylic polymer and comparable to that of bisphenol-A polycarbonate. This elevated T<sub>g</sub> is attributed to the rigid bicyclic pinane structure and the steric hindrance imparted by the α-methyl group of the methacrylate backbone, which restricts segmental motion.

Control experiments without DDM yielded a polymer with M<sub>n</sub> = 85,000 g/mol and T<sub>g</sub> = 158 °C, confirming the expected inverse relationship between molecular weight and T<sub>g</sub> in the entanglement regime. The polymer was soluble in common organic solvents such as chloroform and THF, and cast films exhibited excellent optical clarity and mechanical integrity. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) showed onset of decomposition at 280 °C, indicating good thermal stability. These properties collectively demonstrate that α-pinene methacrylate polymers are suitable for high-temperature applications such as optical lenses, automotive components, or engineering plastics, where bio-based content and performance must be balanced.

### Example 2

The preparation and application of a carvone-derived methacrylate polymer as a renewable powder coating is detailed as a second illustrative embodiment. (R)-(−)-Carvone (20.0 g, 133 mmol) was reduced to the corresponding alcohol (4a) by treatment with lithium aluminium hydride (LiAlH<sub>4</sub>, 5.0 g, 132 mmol) in dry THF (200 mL) under nitrogen at 0 °C. The mixture was stirred for 4 hours at room temperature, then carefully quenched with sequential addition of water (5 mL), 15% NaOH (5 mL), and water (15 mL). After filtration through Celite and concentration, alcohol 4a was obtained as a pale yellow oil (19.2 g, 95% yield). Esterification with methacrylic acid using T3P® as described in Example 1 afforded methacrylate monomer 4e (22.1 g, 85% yield).

Polymerization of 4e was carried out at 65 °C with AIBN (2 mol%) in bulk for 48 hours, yielding a high-molecular-weight polymer (M<sub>n</sub> = 68,000 g/mol, Đ = 1.35) as a brittle, transparent solid. FTIR analysis confirmed consumption of only the methacrylate C=C stretch (1635 cm<sup>−1</sup>), while the exocyclic olefin signal (1660 cm<sup>−1</sup>) remained intact, indicating selective polymerization and retention of the pendant double bond. This feature was exploited in the formulation of a powder coating. The polymer (70 wt%), trimethylolpropane tris(3-mercaptopropionate) (TMPMP, 25 wt%, as a trifunctional thiol crosslinker), and flow additives (5 wt%) were melt-mixed in a twin-screw extruder at 110 °C, pelletized, and cryogenically ground to a fine powder (particle size <90 μm).

The powder was electrostatically sprayed onto aluminum Q-panels and cured by heating at 120 °C for 2 minutes to induce flow, followed by UV irradiation (365 nm, 1000 mJ/cm<sup>2</sup>) to promote thiol-ene crosslinking between the pendant olefins and TMPMP. The resulting coating was transparent, glossy, and exhibited a PCI smoothness value of 2.5. Pencil hardness testing gave a rating of 2B, and König pendulum hardness was 185 seconds. The coating passed the acetone double-rub test (>100 rubs with no removal), and Gitterschnitt adhesion testing showed minor delamination consistent with commercial acrylic coatings. These results confirm that the carvone methacrylate polymer is a viable, renewable alternative for high-performance powder coatings, combining bio-based content with industry-standard processing and performance.

## CONCLUSIONS

The present invention establishes a transformative approach to the sustainable production of high-performance polymers from abundant, renewable terpenes. By developing efficient synthetic routes—both a two-step functionalization protocol and a catalytic one-pot C–H oxidation method—the invention enables the conversion of commercially available terpenes such as α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene, and carvone into well-defined acrylate and methacrylate monomers that are fully compatible with standard industrial polymerization techniques. These monomers yield polymers with a remarkable range of physical properties, including unprecedented glass transition temperatures up to 142 °C for α-pinene methacrylate, which rival those of conventional engineering thermoplastics. The differential reactivity of acrylate versus methacrylate derivatives has been elucidated, allowing for the strategic design of linear, non-crosslinked polymers even in the presence of multiple olefinic sites, as demonstrated by the successful formulation of a carvone-based powder coating with excellent mechanical and chemical resistance. Furthermore, the unexpected identification of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as effective, green chain transfer agents expands the utility of terpenes beyond monomer precursors to include polymerization modifiers that enhance process control without compromising sustainability. Collectively, these innovations provide a robust, scalable, and economically viable platform for integrating bio-based content into high-value polymer applications, addressing the critical need for drop-in renewable alternatives that meet or exceed the performance of petroleum-derived materials.
