# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of precision oncology and personalized medicine, particularly in the context of antiangiogenic therapy for solid tumors. More specifically, the invention provides a novel protein-based prognostic and predictive biomarker signature, referred to herein as the VEGF Inhibition Response Predictor (ViRP) score, which is capable of identifying patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative breast cancer who are likely to derive significant clinical benefit from treatment with bevacizumab or a biosimilar thereof in combination with standard chemotherapy. The invention further encompasses methods for calculating the ViRP score using expression levels of a defined set of tumor proteins, including phosphorylated forms, measured in pretreatment tumor tissue samples via reverse-phase protein array (RPPA) or other quantitative proteomic platforms. Additionally, the invention includes surrogate implementations of the ViRP score derived from mRNA expression data corresponding to the genes encoding the signature proteins, enabling broader clinical applicability where protein-level assays are not readily available. The ViRP score serves as a decision-support tool for clinicians to select patient subpopulations most likely to achieve pathologic complete response (pCR) or low residual cancer burden (RCB) following neoadjuvant therapy with bevacizumab plus chemotherapy, thereby optimizing therapeutic outcomes, reducing unnecessary exposure to drug-related toxicities, and improving resource allocation in oncology care. The invention also extends to kits, diagnostic devices, and computational systems configured to implement the ViRP scoring methodology, as well as treatment protocols that incorporate the ViRP score into clinical decision-making algorithms for the management of locally advanced or metastatic HER2-negative breast cancer.

## BACKGROUND

Antiangiogenic therapy, particularly through inhibition of vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A), has represented a significant advancement in the treatment of various solid malignancies. The development and clinical deployment of bevacizumab, a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody that specifically binds and neutralizes VEGF-A, marked a turning point in oncology by demonstrating that targeting the tumor microenvironment—specifically the aberrant vasculature supporting tumor growth—could yield meaningful clinical benefits. Bevacizumab, when added to standard chemotherapy regimens, has been shown to improve progression-free survival (PFS) and, in some cases, overall survival (OS) in patients with colorectal cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, glioblastoma, and ovarian cancer. However, its application in breast cancer has been more contentious. While early-phase trials and subgroup analyses suggested promising activity, particularly in triple-negative or HER2-negative disease, large randomized phase III trials failed to consistently demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in OS in unselected populations. Consequently, regulatory approvals for bevacizumab in breast cancer have been limited; for example, it is approved in Europe for use in combination with paclitaxel for first-line treatment of metastatic breast cancer but was withdrawn from the U.S. market for this indication due to concerns over risk-benefit balance in broad populations.

This inconsistency in clinical outcomes underscores a fundamental challenge in oncology: the heterogeneity of tumor biology among patients diagnosed with the same histological subtype of cancer. Not all tumors rely equally on VEGF-driven angiogenesis, and even among those that do, downstream signaling pathways and compensatory mechanisms may modulate the efficacy of VEGF blockade. As such, the absence of reliable predictive biomarkers has hindered the rational use of bevacizumab in breast cancer. Numerous candidate biomarkers have been explored, including circulating plasma VEGF-A levels, genetic alterations in VEGF pathway components, and expression of hypoxia-related markers such as carbonic anhydrase IX. Yet, none have achieved sufficient validation or predictive power to warrant routine clinical implementation. The MERiDiAN trial, for instance, prospectively evaluated plasma VEGF-A as a selection criterion for bevacizumab therapy but found no correlation between baseline VEGF-A levels and treatment benefit, effectively ruling out this analyte as a viable biomarker.

Concurrently, advances in high-throughput molecular profiling have shifted the focus toward multivariate signatures that capture the complexity of tumor biology more comprehensively than single-analyte approaches. Genomic signatures such as Oncotype DX, MammaPrint, and PAM50 have become standard tools for prognostication and chemotherapy decision-making in early-stage breast cancer. However, these signatures are primarily based on mRNA expression and may not fully reflect the functional state of the proteome, which is the ultimate effector of cellular phenotypes. Proteins, particularly in their post-translationally modified forms (e.g., phosphorylated kinases), directly mediate signal transduction, metabolic reprogramming, and cell fate decisions in response to therapeutic interventions. Despite this, protein-based predictive signatures for antiangiogenic therapy have remained largely unexplored, partly due to historical limitations in scalable, quantitative proteomic technologies.

Reverse-phase protein arrays (RPPA) have emerged as a robust platform for high-throughput, quantitative measurement of protein expression and activation states across hundreds of analytes in minimal tissue samples. Unlike mass spectrometry–based proteomics, RPPA leverages validated antibodies to detect specific proteins and phospho-epitopes with high sensitivity and reproducibility, making it particularly suitable for clinical biomarker discovery. Recent studies have demonstrated that proteomic profiles often provide superior predictive accuracy for drug response compared to transcriptomic data alone, as they more closely reflect the functional architecture of signaling networks. This principle underpins the rationale for the present invention: that a protein-centric signature, derived from pretreatment tumor tissue, can capture the biological determinants of sensitivity to VEGF inhibition in a manner that transcends the limitations of genomic or circulating biomarkers.

Prior attempts to develop predictive models for bevacizumab response have largely relied on gene expression data or clinical-pathological variables, with limited success. No validated protein signature has been reported that specifically identifies patients who will achieve deep tumor regression—such as pCR or low RCB—when treated with bevacizumab plus chemotherapy. The absence of such a tool represents a critical gap in clinical practice, as it prevents the enrichment of clinical trials for likely responders and denies individual patients access to a potentially life-extending therapy based on objective biological criteria. The invention described herein addresses this unmet need by providing a rigorously developed, clinically validated protein signature—the ViRP score—that demonstrates high predictive accuracy for treatment response in HER2-negative breast cancer and shows promise for translation into routine diagnostic workflows.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel method for predicting clinical response to antiangiogenic therapy in patients with HER2-negative breast cancer, specifically through the administration of bevacizumab or a biosimilar in combination with standard chemotherapy. Central to this invention is the VEGF Inhibition Response Predictor (ViRP) score, a quantitative biomarker signature derived from the expression levels of nine specific proteins measured in pretreatment tumor tissue. The ViRP score is calculated as a weighted linear combination of the log2-transformed, normalized expression values of these nine proteins, with weights determined by adaptive Lasso regression modeling against relative tumor size reduction following neoadjuvant treatment. The nine proteins comprising the signature are: spleen tyrosine kinase (Syk), acetyl-CoA carboxylase phosphorylated at serine 79 (ACC-pS79), insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF1R), phosphorylated ribosomal protein S6 (RPS6-pS235/236), phosphorylated extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2-pT202/Y204), phosphorylated signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3-pY705), phosphorylated glycogen synthase kinase 3 beta (GSK3β-pS9), phosphorylated focal adhesion kinase (FAK-pY397), and phosphorylated c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK-pT183/Y185). Notably, eight of these nine proteins exhibit statistically significant univariate associations with tumor shrinkage, underscoring their biological relevance to treatment response.

The invention further encompasses the use of the ViRP score to stratify patients into “low” and “high” risk categories for treatment resistance, with a predefined cutoff value (e.g., 0.366, as determined by receiver operating characteristic analysis for optimal sensitivity and specificity in predicting pathologic complete response) serving as the decision threshold. Patients classified as having a low ViRP score are significantly more likely to achieve pCR or low residual cancer burden (RCB class 0/I) following neoadjuvant therapy with bevacizumab plus chemotherapy, whereas those with high scores derive minimal benefit and may be spared unnecessary exposure to drug-related toxicities such as hypertension, proteinuria, and impaired wound healing. Clinical validation in the NeoAva phase II trial demonstrated that selection of patients based on a low ViRP score approximately doubles the pCR rate compared to unselected treatment, and this enrichment effect is statistically significant (P = .009). Moreover, the ViRP score outperforms conventional clinical variables—including tumor stage, estrogen receptor status, progesterone receptor status, PAM50 subtype, TP53 mutation status, and nodal involvement—in univariate association with pCR, highlighting its independent predictive value.

To facilitate broader clinical adoption, the invention also provides a surrogate mRNA-based implementation of the ViRP score. This surrogate score is computed using the same intercept and beta coefficients derived from the protein model but applied to quantile-normalized, probe-averaged mRNA expression levels of the genes encoding the nine signature proteins (including the parent genes for phosphorylated epitopes). Despite the inherent limitation that mRNA levels do not capture post-translational modifications, the mRNA ViRP score exhibits strong correlation (Pearson R² = 0.75, P < .001) with the original protein-based score in the discovery cohort and retains significant predictive power for pCR in both the NeoAva trial and an independent external validation cohort (PROMIX trial, N = 122), where it again distinguishes pCR from non-pCR patients with high statistical significance (P < .001). This dual-modality approach—protein-based for maximum accuracy and mRNA-based for accessibility—ensures that the ViRP score can be deployed across diverse clinical and laboratory settings.

The invention additionally includes diagnostic kits comprising antibodies or nucleic acid probes specific to the nine signature proteins or their encoding genes, along with instructions for calculating the ViRP score and interpreting the result in the context of treatment selection. Furthermore, it encompasses computational systems and software modules configured to receive raw or normalized protein or mRNA expression data, apply the predefined ViRP algorithm, and output a numerical score accompanied by a clinical recommendation (e.g., “bevacizumab + chemotherapy recommended” or “standard chemotherapy preferred”). The method is applicable to both neoadjuvant and metastatic settings, as the biological processes governing response to VEGF inhibition are expected to be conserved across disease stages. Ongoing studies are evaluating the generalizability of the ViRP signature to other solid tumors treated with bevacizumab, such as ovarian, colorectal, and non-small cell lung cancers, suggesting potential for cross-indication utility.

In summary, the invention provides a clinically actionable, biologically grounded, and technically feasible solution to the long-standing problem of patient selection for antiangiogenic therapy in breast cancer. By leveraging the functional insights of the proteome, the ViRP score enables precision oncology at the point of care, ensuring that the right patients receive the right therapy at the right time.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Treatment Regimen

The treatment regimen enabled by the present invention is specifically tailored for patients diagnosed with HER2-negative breast cancer, encompassing both hormone receptor-positive (HR+) and triple-negative (TNBC) subtypes, who are candidates for neoadjuvant or first-line metastatic therapy involving cytotoxic chemotherapy. The core therapeutic intervention consists of the co-administration of bevacizumab or a biosimilar thereof with a standard chemotherapy backbone, such as anthracycline-taxane combinations (e.g., doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide followed by paclitaxel) or taxane monotherapy (e.g., paclitaxel or docetaxel). Bevacizumab is typically administered intravenously at a dose of 10 mg/kg every two weeks or 15 mg/kg every three weeks, in accordance with established clinical protocols. The duration of therapy is generally aligned with the chemotherapy schedule, extending over 18 to 24 weeks in the neoadjuvant setting or until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity in the metastatic context.

The critical innovation introduced by the present invention lies in the pre-therapeutic stratification of patients using the ViRP score. Prior to initiating treatment, a formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) or fresh-frozen tumor biopsy specimen is obtained from the patient. This sample is then subjected to proteomic profiling via reverse-phase protein array (RPPA) or an equivalent quantitative platform capable of measuring the expression levels of the nine signature proteins, including their phosphorylated states where applicable. Alternatively, if protein-based assays are unavailable, RNA is extracted from the same tissue and analyzed using gene expression microarrays or RNA sequencing to generate mRNA levels for the corresponding genes. The resulting data are processed through the ViRP algorithm to produce a numerical score, which is then compared against a predefined clinical decision threshold.

Patients whose ViRP score falls below the threshold (e.g., ≤ 0.366) are classified as “likely responders” and are recommended to receive the combination of bevacizumab and chemotherapy. This group demonstrates a significantly higher probability of achieving deep tumor regression, as evidenced by pathologic complete response (defined as ypT0/is ypN0) or low residual cancer burden (RCB class 0 or I), which are strongly associated with improved long-term outcomes including recurrence-free and overall survival. Conversely, patients with a ViRP score above the threshold are classified as “unlikely responders” and are advised to proceed with chemotherapy alone, thereby avoiding the added risks, costs, and logistical burdens associated with bevacizumab without compromising therapeutic efficacy. This risk-adapted approach not only enhances the benefit-risk profile of antiangiogenic therapy but also aligns with principles of value-based healthcare by directing high-cost biologics to those most likely to benefit.

The treatment regimen is further supported by comprehensive clinical monitoring, including regular assessment of tumor size via imaging (e.g., MRI, ultrasound) during therapy and pathological evaluation of surgical specimens in the neoadjuvant setting. Adverse events related to bevacizumab, such as hypertension, bleeding, gastrointestinal perforation, and impaired wound healing, are managed according to standard guidelines, with the understanding that the incidence of such events is minimized in the selected population due to the exclusion of non-responders. Importantly, the regimen is not static; ongoing research may refine the ViRP cutoff, incorporate additional biomarkers, or adapt the signature to emerging antiangiogenic agents beyond bevacizumab, ensuring continued relevance in evolving clinical landscapes.

### Relative Importance of Proteins and Modelling of Protein Combinations

The ViRP score is not merely an aggregate of individual protein measurements but a mathematically optimized composite index that reflects the synergistic and antagonistic interactions among key signaling nodes governing tumor response to VEGF inhibition. The relative importance of each protein within the signature was systematically evaluated using the Lindeman, Merenda, and Gold (lmg) metric from the R-package relaimpo, which partitions the total variance explained by the model among the predictors while accounting for correlations among them. This analysis revealed that spleen tyrosine kinase (Syk) contributes the greatest proportion of explanatory power to the ViRP score, consistent with its unique role in bridging immune and angiogenic signaling pathways. Syk is expressed not only in hematopoietic cells but also in breast epithelial and endothelial cells, where it modulates cytoskeletal dynamics, cell adhesion, and VEGF receptor trafficking. Its high importance in the model suggests that tumors with active Syk-mediated signaling may be particularly dependent on VEGF-driven angiogenesis and thus more susceptible to bevacizumab.

Other highly influential proteins include phosphorylated ribosomal protein S6 (RPS6-pS235/236) and phosphorylated ERK1/2 (ERK1/2-pT202/Y204), both of which are central effectors of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR and RAS/RAF/MEK pathways, respectively. These pathways are frequently hyperactivated in breast cancer and are known to cross-talk with VEGF signaling, promoting endothelial cell survival and vascular permeability. Their inclusion in the signature underscores the concept that the efficacy of VEGF blockade is contingent upon the activity state of downstream proliferative and survival cascades. Similarly, phosphorylated STAT3 (STAT3-pY705) and phosphorylated GSK3β (GSK3β-pS9) reflect the integration of cytokine and Wnt signaling, which can modulate the tumor microenvironment and influence response to antiangiogenic stress.

The modeling approach employed—adaptive Lasso regression with leave-one-out cross-validation—was specifically chosen to enforce sparsity while preserving predictive accuracy. Unlike ordinary least squares regression, which would include all 210 assayed proteins and risk overfitting, Lasso penalizes the magnitude of regression coefficients, driving irrelevant or redundant variables to zero. The adaptive variant further refines this process by applying differential penalties based on initial estimates of coefficient importance, thereby improving variable selection consistency. This rigorous statistical framework ensured that the final nine-protein signature represents a minimal yet sufficient set of features that collectively capture the biological determinants of response.

Notably, the model excludes estrogen receptor (ER) protein expression, despite its known association with differential bevacizumab benefit in some studies. This omission indicates that the ViRP signature operates independently of classical hormonal subtyping, instead reflecting a more granular, pathway-level phenotype. This independence enhances the generalizability of the score across HR+ and TNBC subtypes, both of which are included in the HER2-negative population targeted by the invention.

The combinatorial nature of the ViRP score is essential to its performance. Univariate analysis of individual proteins yields modest predictive power, but their integration into a multivariate model produces a synergistic effect, as evidenced by the high Pearson correlation (R² = 0.67) between the ViRP score and relative tumor size. This synergy arises because no single protein can fully encapsulate the complexity of angiogenic dependency; rather, it is the coordinated activity of multiple nodes across interconnected networks that determines therapeutic vulnerability. The ViRP model effectively distills this complexity into a single, interpretable metric that can be readily deployed in clinical practice.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Identification of Ten Protein Prognostic Signature

In the course of developing the ViRP score, a cohort of 109 patients with HER2-negative breast cancer enrolled in the NeoAva phase II clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT00773695) was analyzed. Tumor biopsies were collected prior to initiation of neoadjuvant therapy, with 55 patients assigned to standard chemotherapy (CTx) and 54 to chemotherapy plus bevacizumab (Bev plus CTx). Reverse-phase protein arrays (RPPA) were used to quantify the expression of 210 cancer-relevant proteins, including 54 phosphorylated forms, in these pretreatment samples. Initial unsupervised hierarchical clustering of the full protein panel revealed distinct molecular subgroups that correlated significantly with tumor shrinkage and PAM50 intrinsic subtypes, confirming the biological relevance of the proteomic data.

To identify a predictive signature specific to bevacizumab response, the analysis focused exclusively on the 54 patients in the Bev plus CTx arm for whom relative tumor size after 24 weeks of therapy was available. Low-variance proteins, deemed unlikely to contribute meaningfully to prediction, were filtered out by fitting a mixed-model distribution to the protein variances using the R-package mixtools. This step retained 114 proteins exhibiting sufficient inter-tumor heterogeneity for biomarker development. Adaptive Lasso regression was then applied to this reduced set, with relative tumor size (expressed as a percentage of baseline) as the continuous outcome variable. The penalty parameter lambda was optimized via leave-one-out cross-validation using mean absolute error as the loss function, resulting in the selection of ten proteins with non-zero regression coefficients.

A second iterative round of Lasso regression on this ten-protein subset led to the shrinkage of one coefficient to zero, yielding a final signature of nine proteins: Syk, ACC-pS79, IGF1R, RPS6-pS235/236, ERK1/2-pT202/Y204, STAT3-pY705, GSK3β-pS9, FAK-pY397, and JNK-pT183/Y185. The ViRP score for each patient was computed as the sum of the intercept (−0.128) and the product of each protein’s beta coefficient and its log2-transformed, median-centered expression value. This score demonstrated a strong inverse correlation with tumor shrinkage (Pearson R² = 0.67, P < .001), with lower scores indicating greater response. When evaluated against binary clinical endpoints, patients achieving pCR had significantly lower ViRP scores than non-responders (P < .001), and similarly, those with low RCB (class 0/I) exhibited lower scores than those with high RCB (class II/III) (P < .001).

Univariate analysis confirmed that eight of the nine proteins were individually associated with tumor size reduction (Spearman P < .05), with Syk showing the strongest differential expression between pCR and non-pCR groups (P = .002). The predictive performance of the ViRP score was further validated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, yielding an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.85 for pCR and 0.80 for low RCB. Application of the optimal cutoff (0.366) derived from ROC analysis enriched the pCR rate from 26% in the unselected Bev plus CTx arm to 52% in the ViRP-low subgroup (P = .009), demonstrating clear clinical utility. Finally, the signature was successfully translated into an mRNA-based surrogate and validated in an independent cohort of 122 patients from the PROMIX trial (NCT00957125), where it again distinguished pCR from non-pCR patients with high significance (P < .001), confirming the robustness and generalizability of the ViRP approach.
