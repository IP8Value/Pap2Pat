# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates generally to the field of oncology and, more specifically, to novel therapeutic methods for the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma, a rare and aggressive malignancy arising from the epithelial cells of the intrahepatic or extrahepatic biliary tract. The invention provides a synergistic combination therapy involving cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) and a modified FOLFIRINOX chemotherapeutic regimen, which together significantly enhance anti-tumor efficacy while potentially reducing the required dosage of cytotoxic agents. This approach is particularly suited for adjuvant applications following surgical resection or in cases of recurrent or metastatic disease where conventional chemotherapy alone has demonstrated limited clinical success due to inherent chemoresistance, systemic toxicity, and high recurrence rates. The invention further encompasses the use of a specific CAP-generating medical device—the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System (CHCPS)—in conjunction with standardized FOLFIRINOX dosing protocols to achieve selective and potent cytotoxic effects against cholangiocarcinoma cells without causing significant damage to surrounding normal tissue. The methodology described herein represents a paradigm shift in the management of biliary tract cancers by integrating physical plasma-based tumor ablation with molecularly targeted chemotherapy, thereby offering a dual-mechanistic strategy that addresses both the proliferative and survival mechanisms of cholangiocarcinoma.

### Brief Description of the Related Art

Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) remains one of the most challenging malignancies in clinical oncology due to its late diagnosis, aggressive biological behavior, and poor response to conventional therapeutic modalities. Despite advances in imaging and surgical techniques, the five-year survival rate for patients diagnosed with CCA remains below 20%, primarily because the disease is often detected at an advanced stage when curative resection is no longer feasible. Even in cases where surgical intervention is possible, the recurrence rate exceeds 50% within two years, underscoring the inadequacy of current adjuvant strategies. Chemotherapy, particularly regimens based on gemcitabine and fluorouracil (5-FU), has been the mainstay of systemic treatment for unresectable or metastatic CCA; however, intrinsic and acquired resistance to these agents frequently limits their long-term efficacy. The FOLFIRINOX regimen—comprising oxaliplatin, irinotecan, leucovorin, and 5-fluorouracil—has emerged as a more potent alternative in gastrointestinal cancers, notably pancreatic adenocarcinoma, where it demonstrated superior overall survival compared to gemcitabine monotherapy in phase II–III clinical trials. Nevertheless, its application in CCA has been hindered by significant hematological and gastrointestinal toxicities, including neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and febrile neutropenia, which restrict its use to patients with excellent performance status and limit dose intensity in real-world settings.

In parallel, cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) has gained increasing attention as a novel, non-thermal, and minimally invasive anti-cancer modality. CAP is a partially ionized gas generated at or near room temperature that contains a complex mixture of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (RONS), charged particles, photons, and electric fields. Preclinical studies have consistently demonstrated that CAP can selectively induce apoptosis, DNA damage, and cell cycle arrest in a wide range of cancer cell types—including melanoma, glioblastoma, breast, and pancreatic carcinomas—while sparing adjacent non-malignant cells. This selectivity is attributed to the differential redox balance between cancer and normal cells, with malignant cells exhibiting higher basal oxidative stress and thus greater vulnerability to additional RONS-mediated insult. The Canady Helios Cold Plasma System (CHCPS), a CE-marked and FDA-investigational device, delivers CAP through a handheld scalpel-like applicator under precise control of power, gas flow (typically helium), and exposure duration. Clinical investigations, including a Phase I FDA Investigational Device Exemption (IDE) trial (G190165/R001), are currently evaluating the safety and feasibility of applying CHCPS directly to the tumor bed immediately after surgical resection to eliminate microscopic residual disease. Early data from in vitro and in vivo models indicate that CHCPS reduces tumor cell viability by up to 99% within 48 hours without causing thermal injury to surrounding tissues, owing to its operating temperature range of 26–31°C.

Recent efforts have explored the potential of combining CAP with conventional or targeted anti-neoplastic agents to exploit synergistic interactions and overcome therapeutic resistance. For instance, CAP has been shown to enhance the cytotoxicity of gemcitabine in murine pancreatic cancer models, potentiate the effects of temozolomide in glioblastoma, and augment nanoparticle-mediated drug delivery in breast cancer. These combinatorial approaches aim to lower the effective dose of chemotherapy, thereby mitigating systemic side effects while maintaining or even improving tumor control. However, despite the growing body of evidence supporting CAP’s role as a chemosensitizing agent, no prior study has investigated its interaction with the FOLFIRINOX regimen, particularly in the context of cholangiocarcinoma—a disease with urgent unmet therapeutic needs. Existing literature lacks any experimental or clinical data demonstrating the feasibility, efficacy, or mechanistic basis for combining CAP with multi-agent chemotherapy protocols such as FOLFIRINOX in biliary tract malignancies. Consequently, there exists a critical gap in knowledge regarding optimal dosing sequences, exposure parameters, and biological outcomes of such a combination, which the present invention seeks to address through rigorous in vitro validation using a well-characterized human intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICCA) cell line, KKU-055.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and unexpectedly synergistic method for the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma by combining a defined regimen of cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) generated by the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System (CHCPS) with a modified FOLFIRINOX chemotherapeutic protocol. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that sequential administration of a low-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen—comprising 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin at concentrations corresponding to 6.7 µM 5-FU, 1.7 µM leucovorin, 0.6 µM irinotecan, and 0.5 µM oxaliplatin—followed by CAP exposure for 1 to 5 minutes at a power setting of 120p (28.7 W) with a helium flow rate of 3 L/min and a fixed distance of 1.5 cm from the cell culture surface, results in significantly greater reduction in cholangiocarcinoma cell viability than either modality administered alone. This synergistic effect is evidenced by a 91% reduction in cell viability when the aforementioned FOLFIRINOX dose is combined with 5 minutes of CAP treatment, compared to only modest reductions achieved by either treatment individually at equivalent doses. The invention further discloses that this combination induces profound cell cycle arrest, suppresses cellular proliferation as measured by Ki-67 expression, and accelerates apoptotic cell death, as confirmed by live-cell imaging and confocal microscopy. Critically, the synergy is most pronounced at submaximal doses of FOLFIRINOX—doses that alone do not significantly impair cell viability—thereby suggesting that CAP can potentiate the anti-tumor activity of chemotherapy without requiring full-strength, highly toxic regimens. The method is particularly advantageous for adjuvant therapy following surgical resection, where CAP can be applied directly to the tumor bed to eradicate residual microscopic disease while simultaneously sensitizing any remaining cancer cells to systemic or locoregional chemotherapy. The invention thus offers a clinically translatable strategy to improve outcomes in cholangiocarcinoma by enhancing therapeutic efficacy, reducing chemotherapeutic burden, and minimizing systemic toxicity, all while leveraging an already-investigated medical device platform currently under FDA evaluation. This represents the first demonstration of synergistic interaction between CAP and FOLFIRINOX in any cancer type, and specifically in cholangiocarcinoma, establishing a new standard for combinatorial plasma-oncology therapeutics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Experiments

The experimental framework underlying the present invention was designed to systematically evaluate the individual and combined effects of cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) and the FOLFIRINOX chemotherapeutic regimen on the human intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICCA) cell line KKU-055, a well-established model of poorly differentiated biliary tract cancer. All experiments were conducted under controlled laboratory conditions at the Jerome Canady Research Institution for Advanced Biological and Technological Sciences (JCRI-ABTS) in Takoma Park, Maryland, USA, in accordance with institutional biosafety and ethical guidelines. The KKU-055 cell line was obtained from Sekisui XenoTech, LLC and maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Pen Strep) at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. For all assays, cells were seeded into 12-well plates at densities of either 50,000 or 100,000 cells per well in 1 mL of complete media and allowed to adhere for 24 hours prior to initiation of treatment protocols. This pre-incubation period ensured consistent baseline confluence and metabolic activity across experimental groups.

FOLFIRINOX treatment was prepared by first dissolving each of the four constituent drugs—oxaliplatin, leucovorin, irinotecan, and 5-fluorouracil—in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) to create stock solutions aligned with clinical dosing ratios: 85 mg/m² oxaliplatin, 400 mg/m² leucovorin, 180 mg/m² irinotecan, and 400 mg/m² 5-FU. Notably, the 5-FU component was administered as a single bolus dose rather than a prolonged infusion, consistent with in vitro modeling constraints. These stock solutions were then combined in DMSO and serially diluted in complete DMEM to generate six distinct concentration levels, with drug concentrations referenced by their corresponding 5-FU concentration ranging from 0.8 µM to 53.8 µM (Table 1). Final working solutions were prepared by a 1:1000 dilution of the combined stock into culture media, ensuring that the final DMSO concentration did not exceed 0.1%, a level previously confirmed to be non-toxic to KKU-055 cells. Cells designated for FOLFIRINOX treatment received 24 hours of continuous drug exposure before subsequent interventions.

CAP treatment was delivered using the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System (CHCPS), comprising a US Medical Innovations LLC 22–601 MCa high-frequency electrosurgical generator paired with the Canady Helios Cold Plasma Scalpel. The system was operated with a constant helium carrier gas flow rate of 3 liters per minute and a fixed power setting of 120p, which corresponds to an output of 28.7 watts. The plasma-emitting tip of the scalpel was positioned precisely 1.5 centimeters above the surface of the culture medium in each well, and exposure durations were varied from 1 to 7 minutes to assess dose dependency. Importantly, CAP treatment was administered 48 hours after initial cell seeding, meaning that in combination experiments, cells first underwent 24 hours of FOLFIRINOX incubation, followed by a 24-hour recovery period, and then received CAP. Control groups included untreated cells, DMSO vehicle controls, FOLFIRINOX-only, and CAP-only conditions. Immediately after CAP exposure, all plates were returned to the incubator for continued culture up to 72 hours post-treatment, depending on the assay endpoint.

To quantify cellular responses, three primary analytical methodologies were employed: (1) a Thiazolyl Blue Tetrazolium Bromide (MTT) assay to measure metabolic viability 48 hours after CAP treatment; (2) confocal immunofluorescence microscopy with Ki-67/DAPI co-staining to assess proliferative activity at 6, 24, and 48 hours post-treatment; and (3) live-cell imaging using the IncuCyte Live-Cell Analysis System to monitor real-time cell cycle progression and confluence over 72 hours. For the MTT assay, cells were incubated with MTT reagent for 3 hours, after which formazan crystals were solubilized and absorbance measured at 570 nm using a BioTek Synergy HTX microplate reader. Viability was normalized to untreated control wells and expressed as a percentage. Confocal analysis required seeding cells on fibronectin- and collagen II-coated platinum-lined cover glasses placed within 12-well plates. After treatment, cells were fixed in ice-cold methanol, stained with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated anti-Ki-67 antibody or isotype control, and mounted with DAPI-containing antifade reagent. Images were acquired using a Zeiss Confocal 510 LSM and analyzed with Zeiss ZenLite software to determine Ki-67 positivity based on mean fluorescence intensity thresholds. For cell cycle analysis, a stable KKU-055 cell line expressing IncuCyte Red/Green Lentivirus reporters was established, wherein red fluorescence indicated G1 phase and green indicated S/G2/M phases. Time-lapse imaging at 1-hour intervals enabled quantification of cell numbers in each phase and assessment of morphological changes indicative of apoptosis or mitotic arrest. All experiments were performed in triplicate with at least two intra-experimental replicates per condition, yielding a minimum of six independent data points per group. Statistical analyses were conducted using Microsoft Excel 2019, with significance determined by unpaired Student’s t-tests, two-way ANOVA, and post hoc Bonferroni-corrected comparisons, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

### Results

The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that the combination of cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) and FOLFIRINOX exerts a synergistic anti-proliferative effect on human intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (KKU-055) cells, surpassing the efficacy of either treatment administered in isolation. Initial dose-response characterization revealed that FOLFIRINOX, when applied as a 24-hour pretreatment, reduced cell viability in a concentration-dependent manner. At the lowest tested dose (0.8 µM 5-FU), viability remained at 94% ± 3%, indistinguishable from DMSO vehicle controls (103% ± 3%), confirming the absence of solvent-related cytotoxicity. However, significant reductions in viability were observed at doses of 3.4 µM 5-FU and above, culminating in a 19% ± 1.9% viability at the highest dose (53.8 µM 5-FU). Similarly, CAP treatment alone induced a time-dependent decrease in viability, with 1-minute exposure reducing viability to approximately 70% and 7-minute exposure achieving near-complete ablation (3% viability, p < 0.005). These findings established the individual therapeutic windows for both modalities.

When FOLFIRINOX and CAP were combined, a striking synergy emerged, particularly at intermediate drug concentrations. The most compelling result was observed with the 6.7 µM 5-FU FOLFIRINOX dose (corresponding to 1.7 µM leucovorin, 0.6 µM irinotecan, and 0.5 µM oxaliplatin) paired with 5 minutes of CAP, which reduced cell viability to 9%—a 91% reduction compared to untreated controls. This combination was significantly more effective than either the drug alone (p < 0.001) or CAP alone (p < 1 × 10⁻⁵). Two-way ANOVA confirmed significant main effects for both FOLFIRINOX dose (p < 0.05) and CAP duration (p < 0.05), as well as a significant interaction term (p < 0.05), indicating that the effect of one modality depended on the level of the other. Post hoc pairwise comparisons (Table 2) further validated that multiple combination regimens—particularly those involving 3 or 5 minutes of CAP with FOLFIRINOX doses ≥6.7 µM 5-FU—achieved statistically superior cytotoxicity relative to monotherapies.

Proliferation assays reinforced these findings. Confocal microscopy of Ki-67-stained cells showed a marked reduction in total cell counts and Ki-67-positive nuclei in combination-treated groups as early as 6 hours post-CAP. At the 3-minute CAP + 6.7 µM 5-FU condition, cell numbers were significantly lower than in untreated controls (p < 0.05), with representative images revealing sparse, fragmented cellular morphology and diminished nuclear Ki-67 signal. By 48 hours, combination-treated wells exhibited extensive cellular debris and minimal intact nuclei, consistent with widespread apoptosis.

Live-cell imaging provided dynamic insights into the mechanism of action. In untreated or FOLFIRINOX-only wells, cells progressed normally through the cell cycle, with increasing numbers in S/G2/M phases and high confluence by 48 hours. In contrast, combination treatment—especially with 3 or 5 minutes of CAP—halted cell cycle progression. The M-G1 transition population (indicative of mitotic exit or death) declined precipitously, while G1 and S/G2/M populations failed to expand. Morphologically, combination-treated wells displayed large aggregates of non-fluorescent cellular debris by 24–48 hours, confirming rapid induction of cell death. Notably, the 5-minute CAP + FOLFIRINOX combination resulted in complete cell death within 24 hours, precluding meaningful cell cycle analysis beyond 6 hours but underscoring its extreme potency.

Collectively, these results establish that sequential FOLFIRINOX pretreatment followed by CAP exposure synergistically enhances cholangiocarcinoma cell killing through mechanisms involving suppressed proliferation, cell cycle arrest, and accelerated apoptosis.

### Discussion

The findings presented herein represent a significant advancement in the therapeutic management of cholangiocarcinoma, a malignancy historically refractory to conventional treatment paradigms. The demonstrated synergy between cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) and the FOLFIRINOX regimen addresses several critical limitations in current oncology practice: the high toxicity of intensive chemotherapy, the pervasive issue of chemoresistance, and the unacceptably high recurrence rates following surgical resection. By showing that a subtherapeutic dose of FOLFIRINOX—insufficient to significantly impair cell viability on its own—can be dramatically potentiated by subsequent CAP exposure, the invention offers a viable path to reduce systemic drug burden while maintaining or even enhancing anti-tumor efficacy. This is particularly relevant for elderly or frail patients with cholangiocarcinoma who are often excluded from aggressive FOLFIRINOX protocols due to comorbidities or poor performance status.

The mechanistic basis for this synergy, while not fully elucidated, likely involves the convergence of two distinct yet complementary cytotoxic pathways. FOLFIRINOX exerts its effects through multiple mechanisms: 5-FU and leucovorin inhibit thymidylate synthase, disrupting DNA synthesis; irinotecan stabilizes topoisomerase I-DNA complexes, causing replication fork collapse; and oxaliplatin forms DNA adducts that trigger apoptosis. CAP, meanwhile, delivers a burst of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (RONS) that overwhelm the already stressed redox homeostasis of cancer cells, leading to oxidative damage to lipids, proteins, and DNA, mitochondrial dysfunction, and activation of intrinsic apoptotic pathways. The sequential administration—chemotherapy first, followed by CAP—may prime cancer cells by inducing DNA damage and cell cycle checkpoint activation, rendering them more susceptible to the oxidative onslaught of plasma. This temporal sequence is critical; prior studies in melanoma showed that oxaliplatin pretreatment enhanced CAP efficacy, suggesting that chemotherapy-induced genomic instability creates a permissive environment for plasma-mediated cytotoxicity.

Importantly, the selectivity of CAP for malignant cells, well-documented in prior literature, implies that this combination would spare normal biliary or hepatic tissue during adjuvant application. In the ongoing Phase I FDA IDE trial of the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System, CAP is applied directly to the surgical tumor bed immediately after resection, a scenario perfectly aligned with the proposed method. Residual microscopic cholangiocarcinoma cells, which are the primary source of local recurrence, would be exposed to both the direct ablative effects of CAP and the systemic (or potentially intraperitoneal) effects of a reduced-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen, thereby creating a dual barrier against regrowth.

While the current data are derived from an in vitro model, the consistency of results across multiple endpoints—viability, proliferation, and cell cycle dynamics—strengthens the translational relevance. Future studies should validate these findings in patient-derived xenografts or orthotopic models that better recapitulate the tumor microenvironment, including stromal interactions and immune components. Additionally, investigations into the molecular signaling pathways—such as p53, ATM/ATR, caspase activation, and NRF2-mediated antioxidant responses—will be essential to refine dosing and predict patient responsiveness. Nevertheless, the present invention provides a robust, clinically actionable foundation for integrating physical plasma technology with modern chemotherapy, heralding a new era of multimodal, precision oncology for cholangiocarcinoma.

### Conclusion

In conclusion, the present invention establishes a novel, synergistic therapeutic strategy for cholangiocarcinoma by combining a modified FOLFIRINOX chemotherapeutic regimen with cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) generated by the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System. The experimental data conclusively demonstrate that sequential administration of a low-dose FOLFIRINOX protocol—specifically 6.7 µM 5-FU, 1.7 µM leucovorin, 0.6 µM irinotecan, and 0.5 µM oxaliplatin—followed by CAP exposure for 1 to 5 minutes results in significantly greater reduction in cholangiocarcinoma cell viability, suppression of proliferation, and induction of cell cycle arrest compared to either treatment alone. This synergy enables the use of reduced chemotherapy doses, thereby mitigating systemic toxicity while maintaining potent anti-tumor efficacy. The method is particularly suited for adjuvant application in the post-resection setting, where CAP can be directly applied to the tumor bed to eliminate residual disease and sensitize remaining cells to concurrent chemotherapy. As the first demonstration of CAP-FOLFIRINOX synergy in any cancer type, and specifically in cholangiocarcinoma, this invention fills a critical therapeutic gap and provides a clinically translatable framework for improving outcomes in this aggressive malignancy. Future clinical trials building upon this foundational work are expected to validate the safety and efficacy of this combination in human patients, potentially establishing a new standard of care for cholangiocarcinoma treatment.
