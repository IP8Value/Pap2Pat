# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and in vitro cellular modeling, specifically to methods for the directed differentiation of human pluripotent stem cells into functional hypothalamic tissue comprising arginine vasopressin (AVP)-secreting neurons. More particularly, the invention provides a robust, reproducible, and highly efficient protocol for generating rostral hypothalamic progenitor cells from human embryonic stem cells (hESCs) or induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), followed by their specification into dorsal hypothalamic lineages that give rise to mature, hormone-secreting AVP neurons. The invention further encompasses methods for the simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues in co-culture systems, enabling the reconstruction of a functional neuroendocrine axis in vitro. Additionally, the invention includes compositions comprising such differentiated hypothalamic cells, as well as their use in disease modeling, drug screening, and cell-based therapies for central diabetes insipidus (CDI) and other disorders of hypothalamic dysfunction. The technical domain spans developmental biology, stem cell engineering, neuroendocrinology, and biopharmaceutical applications, with particular relevance to the precise recapitulation of embryonic signaling gradients—such as those mediated by bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) and sonic hedgehog (SHH)—to achieve region-specific neural patterning in three-dimensional organoid cultures.

## BACKGROUND ART

The hypothalamus is a critical brain region responsible for maintaining homeostasis through the regulation of autonomic, endocrine, and behavioral functions, including thirst, hunger, body temperature, circadian rhythms, and stress responses. Among its key neuroendocrine outputs is the synthesis and release of arginine vasopressin (AVP), a nonapeptide hormone produced primarily in magnocellular neurons of the paraventricular nucleus (PVN) and supraoptic nucleus (SON) within the dorsal hypothalamus. AVP is transported along axons to the posterior pituitary, where it is secreted into systemic circulation to act on V2 receptors in the renal collecting ducts, promoting water reabsorption and thereby concentrating urine. Deficiency in AVP production or release results in central diabetes insipidus (CDI), a condition characterized by polyuria (excessive urine output) and polydipsia (excessive thirst), with daily water intake often exceeding 10 liters. Current standard therapy involves administration of desmopressin, a synthetic AVP analog; however, this treatment carries significant risks, including hyponatremia due to inappropriate antidiuresis, and is ineffective in patients with adipsic CDI who lack osmoreceptor-mediated thirst drive, placing them at high risk of life-threatening dehydration. These limitations underscore the urgent need for alternative therapeutic strategies, particularly those rooted in regenerative medicine.

In parallel, the lack of accessible human hypothalamic tissue has severely impeded research into the developmental mechanisms, pathophysiology, and pharmacological modulation of hypothalamic disorders. While animal models have provided foundational insights, species-specific differences in gene expression, neuronal connectivity, and hormonal regulation necessitate human-relevant systems. Human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs), including both embryonic stem cells (hESCs) and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), offer a promising platform for generating in vitro models of human hypothalamic development and disease. Early attempts to differentiate hypothalamic neurons from hPSCs have yielded limited success. For instance, Merkle et al. reported the generation of AVP-expressing neurons from hESCs, but the differentiation efficiency was extremely low, and crucially, functional AVP secretion was not demonstrated, rendering the cells unsuitable for therapeutic or high-fidelity screening applications.

A major challenge in hypothalamic differentiation lies in the precise spatial and temporal control of embryonic patterning signals. During vertebrate development, the hypothalamus arises from the most rostral region of the neural plate, which subsequently forms the ventral diencephalon. This process is governed by opposing morphogen gradients: ventralizing signals such as sonic hedgehog (SHH), secreted by the prechordal plate and notochord, and dorsalizing signals like bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs), derived from the overlying ectoderm. The balance between these signals determines the dorsoventral identity of hypothalamic progenitors—dorsal progenitors (marked by PAX6) give rise to AVP and oxytocin neurons, while ventral progenitors (marked by NKX2.1) generate neurons involved in feeding and metabolism, such as those producing melanin-concentrating hormone (MCH) or orexin. Previous in vitro protocols often failed to recapitulate this nuanced signaling environment, leading to heterogeneous or off-target neural fates, such as telencephalic (FOXG1+) or retinal (VSX2+) lineages, rather than authentic hypothalamic tissue.

Wataya et al. pioneered a serum-free floating culture of embryoid body-like aggregates with quick reaggregation (SFEBq) method using mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs) to generate AVP neurons by mimicking early hypothalamic development in a chemically defined, growth factor-free medium (gfCDM). However, direct translation of this protocol to human cells proved inadequate: hESCs failed to aggregate and underwent widespread apoptosis in gfCDM alone. Subsequent modifications introduced knock-out serum replacement (KSR), but this promoted telencephalic differentiation instead. Moreover, even when hypothalamic progenitors were obtained, progression to mature, hormone-secreting AVP neurons remained inefficient, likely due to suboptimal culture conditions during later stages of neuronal maturation, including insufficient extracellular matrix support, inadequate oxygenation in dense 3D aggregates, and lack of neurotrophic factors necessary for terminal differentiation and functional maturation.

Thus, despite incremental advances, the art lacks a comprehensive, stage-optimized protocol that reliably directs hPSCs through each critical phase of hypothalamic development—from initial neural induction and rostral patterning to dorsoventral specification, precursor commitment, and final functional maturation of AVP-secreting neurons—with high efficiency and fidelity. Furthermore, no existing method enables the coordinated development of both hypothalamic and pituitary components in a single system, which is essential for modeling the complete hypothalamic-pituitary axis. The present invention addresses these long-standing deficiencies by integrating precise morphogen dosing, staged application timing, and innovative culture modalities—including gas-liquid interphase (Transwell) and dissociated 2D cultures with tailored extracellular matrices—to achieve unprecedented yields of functional hypothalamic neurons capable of regulated hormone secretion.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The primary technical problem addressed by the present invention is the absence of a reliable, efficient, and functionally validated method for differentiating human pluripotent stem cells into mature, hormone-secreting hypothalamic neurons, particularly arginine vasopressin (AVP)-producing magnocellular neurons. Existing protocols suffer from multiple critical shortcomings: (1) poor survival and aggregation of human pluripotent stem cells under serum-free neural induction conditions; (2) inadequate specification of rostral hypothalamic identity, resulting in contamination with non-hypothalamic neural fates such as telencephalic or retinal lineages; (3) inability to selectively enrich for dorsal hypothalamic progenitors, which are the exclusive developmental source of AVP neurons; (4) inefficient maturation of hypothalamic precursors into terminally differentiated, hormone-expressing neurons; and (5) lack of demonstrable functional activity, specifically regulated AVP secretion in response to physiological stimuli. Additionally, there exists no method in the prior art that facilitates the simultaneous and coordinated maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues from human pluripotent stem cells, which is essential for reconstructing a physiologically relevant neuroendocrine unit for disease modeling, drug screening, or regenerative applications. These limitations have collectively hindered the development of effective cell-based therapies for central diabetes insipidus and other hypothalamic disorders, as well as the creation of accurate in vitro models for studying human hypothalamic development and pathophysiology.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention solves these problems by providing a novel, multi-stage differentiation protocol that precisely recapitulates key embryonic signaling events to guide human pluripotent stem cells through sequential developmental milestones toward functional hypothalamic tissue. The core innovation lies in the strategic combination and timed application of specific morphogens—bone morphogenetic protein 4 (BMP4) and a smoothened agonist (SAG, which activates the sonic hedgehog pathway)—in a modified serum-free floating culture system, supplemented with a small amount of knock-out serum replacement (KSR) to support initial cell survival without inducing off-target fates. This approach enables the highly efficient (>80%) generation of RAX-positive rostral hypothalamic progenitors, which are then selectively patterned into either dorsal (PAX6+/NKX2.1−) or ventral (NKX2.1+/PAX6−) hypothalamic lineages by fine-tuning the concentrations of BMP4 and SAG. Critically, for the production of AVP neurons, the protocol specifies conditions that favor dorsal hypothalamic identity.

To overcome the maturation barrier, the invention introduces a two-phase culture strategy: first, hypothalamic progenitor aggregates are transitioned to a gas-liquid interphase culture using Transwell inserts in a medium enriched with ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF), which significantly enhances the emergence of AVP precursor markers OTP and BRN2. Subsequently, for terminal differentiation, aggregates are dissociated and replated onto a composite extracellular matrix of Matrigel, poly-D-lysine, and laminin in a neurotrophic factor-supplemented medium containing brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), neurotrophin-3 (NT-3), and LM22A-4. This 2D dissociated culture provides the necessary structural and trophic support for neurite outgrowth and neuronal maturation. Furthermore, the inclusion of an Akt inhibitor during the early patterning phase (from day 6) promotes rostralization and dramatically increases the yield of AVP-positive neurons without compromising progenitor induction.

The resulting AVP neurons exhibit key hallmarks of in vivo magnocellular neurons: large soma size (~40 × 20 μm), co-expression of the AVP-processing intermediate Neurophysin II, absence of co-expression with corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), and, most importantly, the ability to secrete AVP in a regulated manner—basal secretion is detectable by radioimmunoassay, and secretion is significantly enhanced upon depolarization with high potassium chloride (KCl). The protocol also generates a diverse array of other hypothalamic neuron subtypes, including those producing oxytocin, CRH, TRH, MCH, orexin, NPY, AgRP, and POMC, demonstrating its broad applicability for modeling hypothalamic complexity.

Additionally, the invention provides methods for the simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues by co-differentiating human pluripotent stem cells under dual-inductive conditions or by co-culturing separately derived hypothalamic and pituitary organoids, thereby enabling the reconstruction of a functional hypothalamic-pituitary axis in vitro. This integrated system allows for the study of neuroendocrine feedback loops and the development of more physiologically relevant disease models and drug screening platforms. Collectively, these innovations provide a robust, scalable, and functionally validated platform for regenerative medicine, pharmaceutical discovery, and fundamental research into human hypothalamic biology.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### 1. Method for Producing Cellular Structure Including Hypothalamic Tissue

The present invention provides a detailed and optimized method for producing a cellular structure that includes functional hypothalamic tissue from human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs), which encompass both human embryonic stem cells (hESCs) and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). This method is designed to faithfully recapitulate the sequential stages of embryonic hypothalamic development through precise manipulation of extrinsic signaling cues and culture conditions. The process begins with the maintenance of undifferentiated hPSCs on a feeder layer of mitomycin C-inactivated mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) in a defined medium consisting of DMEM/F12 supplemented with 20% knock-out serum replacement (KSR), non-essential amino acids, L-glutamine, 2-mercaptoethanol, and recombinant human basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF). Prior to differentiation, hPSC colonies are enzymatically dissociated into small clumps and subjected to a brief pre-plating step on gelatin-coated dishes to remove residual MEFs, ensuring a pure population of human cells for subsequent steps.

The first critical phase of differentiation is the induction of neural ectoderm with rostral hypothalamic identity. This is achieved using a modified version of the serum-free floating culture of embryoid body-like aggregates with quick reaggregation (SFEBq) technique. Specifically, hPSC clumps are dissociated into single cells using TrypLE Express supplemented with DNase I and the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 to prevent dissociation-induced apoptosis. These single cells are then rapidly reaggregated in low-cell-adhesion 96-well V-bottom plates at a density of 10,000 cells per well in a base differentiation medium composed of growth factor-free chemically defined medium (gfCDM) supplemented with 5% KSR, non-essential amino acids, sodium pyruvate, and 2-mercaptoethanol. The inclusion of 5% KSR is a key modification from murine protocols; it is essential for the survival and aggregation of human cells, which otherwise fail to form viable embryoid bodies in pure gfCDM. Half of the medium is replaced every other day to maintain nutrient levels and remove metabolic waste.

On day 6 of differentiation, the patterning phase commences with the addition of specific morphogens to establish rostral hypothalamic identity and initiate dorsoventral patterning. Bone morphogenetic protein 4 (BMP4) is added at a concentration ranging from 0.5 to 2.0 nanomolar (nM), and a smoothened agonist (SAG), which activates the sonic hedgehog (SHH) signaling pathway, is added at a concentration between 0.1 and 1.0 micromolar (μM). In certain embodiments, an Akt inhibitor is also introduced at this stage at a concentration of 0.5 μM to enhance rostralization. The concentrations of BMP4 and SAG are carefully titrated to balance dorsal and ventral signals: higher BMP4 relative to SAG favors dorsal hypothalamic fate (PAX6+), while higher SAG promotes ventral identity (NKX2.1+). The medium containing these factors is maintained until at least day 30, with half-volume changes every other day to sustain consistent signaling. By day 20, immunostaining and flow cytometry analyses reveal that over 80% of the cells within the aggregates express RAX, a transcription factor specifically marking rostral hypothalamic and retinal progenitors, confirming the high efficiency of hypothalamic progenitor induction. Importantly, co-staining shows minimal expression of off-target markers such as FOXG1 (telencephalon) or VSX2 (retina), indicating the specificity of the protocol.

Following the successful generation of RAX+ hypothalamic progenitors, the aggregates are maintained in floating culture until day 60 to allow for further maturation of hypothalamic precursors. At this stage, the culture modality is shifted to promote the emergence of AVP lineage-committed precursors. The aggregates are transferred to Transwell culture inserts, which position the tissue at the gas-liquid interface, thereby improving oxygenation and reducing central necrosis that commonly occurs in dense 3D aggregates. The medium is changed to DMEM/F12 supplemented with glucose, N2, B27 (referred to as DFNB), and ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF). This environment significantly upregulates the expression of OTP and BRN2, transcription factors that are essential for the specification of AVP and oxytocin neurons in the dorsal hypothalamus. Quantitative analysis demonstrates a ten-fold increase in OTP+ and BRN2+ cells compared to aggregates maintained in continuous 3D floating culture.

For terminal differentiation into mature, hormone-secreting neurons, a final transition to a two-dimensional dissociated culture is performed. On or around day 60, the hypothalamic aggregates are enzymatically dissociated using Accumax supplemented with DNase I and Y-27632, followed by mechanical trituration to generate a single-cell suspension. These cells are then plated onto glass coverslips or culture plates pre-coated with a composite extracellular matrix consisting of Matrigel, poly-D-lysine, and laminin. This matrix provides critical adhesive and trophic cues that support neuronal attachment, survival, and extensive neurite outgrowth—conditions that are suboptimal in 3D aggregates. The plating medium is a dissociation medium based on DFNB, further enriched with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), neurotrophin-3 (NT-3), and the TrkB agonist LM22A-4, along with Y-27632 to enhance survival during the initial plating phase. Half of this medium is replaced every other day to maintain trophic support. Over the subsequent weeks (typically by day 80 to 150), this culture system yields a high proportion of neurons that are immunopositive for AVP, as well as other hypothalamic neuropeptides such as oxytocin, CRH, TRH, MCH, orexin, NPY, AgRP, and POMC. The AVP+ neurons exhibit morphological characteristics of magnocellular neurons, including large somata, and co-express Neurophysin II, a carrier protein processed alongside AVP, confirming their authentic identity.

This method is not only highly efficient but also robust across multiple hPSC lines, having been successfully validated with the RAX::Venus reporter hESC line VA22-N37 as well as the KhES-1 and KhES-3 hESC lines. The entire process is conducted under xeno-free or low-xeno conditions where possible, enhancing its suitability for potential clinical translation. The resulting cellular structures—whether maintained as 3D organoids or dissociated into 2D cultures—constitute a powerful in vitro model of human hypothalamic tissue, complete with functional, hormone-secreting neurons.

### EXAMPLES

The efficacy and specificity of the disclosed method are demonstrated through a series of experimental examples that validate each stage of the differentiation protocol. In a primary example, the human embryonic stem cell line VA22-N37, which harbors a RAX::Venus reporter construct, was used to monitor hypothalamic differentiation in real time. Undifferentiated hESCs were maintained on MEF feeders and then subjected to the SFEBq protocol as described. Control experiments confirmed that in the absence of KSR, hESCs failed to form aggregates and underwent massive cell death within days, whereas the addition of 5% KSR enabled robust aggregation but initially led to FOXG1 expression, indicating a telencephalic drift. The critical breakthrough came with the addition of both BMP4 (1.0 nM) and SAG (0.5 μM) from day 6. Immunostaining of day 30 aggregates revealed a predominant population of RAX::Venus+ cells that were negative for the retinal marker VSX2 and the telencephalic marker FOXG1, confirming hypothalamic identity. Further characterization showed a mixed population of dorsal (RAX+/PAX6+/NKX2.1−) and ventral (RAX+/NKX2.1+/PAX6−) hypothalamic progenitors, with the ratio tunable by adjusting BMP4 and SAG concentrations. Flow cytometry quantified that over 80% of the total cell population was RAX::Venus+, establishing the high efficiency of progenitor induction.

In another example focused on AVP neuron production, aggregates generated under dorsal-favoring conditions (0.5 nM BMP4, 0.5 μM SAG) were cultured until day 60 and then split into two groups: one maintained in 3D floating culture and the other transferred to Transwell inserts in DFNB+CNTF medium. Immunostaining at day 60 showed that the Transwell group exhibited a dramatic increase in OTP+ and BRN2+ cells—over 30% of the neuronal population—compared to less than 3% in the 3D group. This highlighted the necessity of the gas-liquid interphase culture for precursor maturation. Subsequently, both groups were dissociated on day 60 and plated in 2D on Matrigel/poly-D-lysine/laminin-coated surfaces in neurotrophic medium. By day 150, the group that had undergone the full protocol—including early Akt inhibitor treatment (0.5 μM from day 6), Transwell culture, and 2D dissociation—showed a significant increase in AVP+ neurons, with counts exceeding 150 AVP+ neurons per 1000 nuclei, compared to fewer than 20 in the control group lacking Akt inhibitor. These AVP+ neurons were TUJ1-positive, confirming their neuronal nature, and exhibited large somata consistent with magnocellular morphology. Critically, they did not co-express CRH, distinguishing them from parvocellular neurons.

Functional validation was performed via radioimmunoassay. Conditioned media collected from day 150 cultures of the fully differentiated hypothalamic neurons contained measurable levels of AVP (approximately 2.5 pg/mL), whereas control cultures of hESC-derived neocortical neurons showed no detectable AVP. Upon stimulation with high KCl (100 mM) in artificial cerebrospinal fluid (aCSF), AVP secretion increased by more than two-fold, demonstrating regulated, activity-dependent release—a hallmark of functional neurosecretory cells. In a parallel example, ventral hypothalamic aggregates (induced with 1.5 nM BMP4 and 1.0 μM SAG) were shown to predominantly generate MCH+ neurons by day 150. Furthermore, qPCR analysis revealed that treatment of these ventral-derived neurons with a cocktail of nerve growth factor (NGF), dexamethasone (DEX), and lithium chloride (LiCl) significantly upregulated pro-MCH mRNA expression, mirroring known in vivo regulatory mechanisms and confirming the physiological responsiveness of the in vitro-generated neurons.

Reproducibility was confirmed using two additional hESC lines, KhES-1 and KhES-3, which do not carry the RAX reporter. Despite the absence of real-time monitoring, these lines yielded similar proportions of RAX+, PAX6+, and NKX2.1+ cells at day 30, and subsequently generated AVP+ neurons with comparable efficiency when subjected to the full differentiation protocol. These examples collectively demonstrate that the method is not only effective but also generalizable across different hPSC lines, robust in its outcomes, and capable of producing functionally mature hypothalamic neurons suitable for a wide range of applications.

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary

Building upon the foundational method for hypothalamic differentiation, the present invention further provides an advanced embodiment for the simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues from human pluripotent stem cells, thereby enabling the reconstruction of a functional hypothalamic-pituitary (HP) axis in vitro. This integrated approach is critical for modeling the complex neuroendocrine interactions that govern systemic homeostasis and for developing more physiologically relevant platforms for drug screening and regenerative medicine. The strategy leverages the shared embryonic origin of the hypothalamus and pituitary, both of which arise from the anterior neural ridge and adjacent oral ectoderm, respectively, and are known to engage in reciprocal inductive signaling during development.

In one embodiment, a single population of hPSCs is co-differentiated into both hypothalamic and pituitary lineages within the same 3D aggregate by applying a biphasic or spatially graded morphogen regimen. Initially, during the first 6 days, hPSCs are aggregated in SFEBq conditions with 5% KSR as before. From day 6 to day 12, a combination of BMP4 (at 1.0–2.0 nM) and SAG (at 0.5–1.0 μM) is applied to induce rostral hypothalamic progenitors in the core of the aggregate, while simultaneously, a subset of cells at the periphery is exposed to higher concentrations of BMP4 (e.g., 5–10 nM) and/or fibroblast growth factor (FGF) ligands such as FGF8 or FGF10, which are known to promote pituitary placode formation from oral ectoderm. This differential exposure can be achieved through controlled diffusion gradients within the aggregate or by microfluidic compartmentalization. By day 15–20, the aggregates begin to show distinct domains: a central RAX+/PAX6+ hypothalamic region and a peripheral LHX3+/HESX1+ pituitary progenitor zone. The culture is then maintained with stage-specific media swaps—hypothalamic regions benefit from CNTF and neurotrophic factors, while pituitary regions require continued BMP and FGF signaling along with glucocorticoids like dexamethasone to drive terminal differentiation into hormone-producing pituitary cell types (e.g., corticotrophs, somatotrophs, lactotrophs).

In an alternative and more controllable embodiment, hypothalamic and pituitary organoids are generated separately using optimized, lineage-specific protocols and then co-cultured to establish functional connectivity. Hypothalamic organoids are produced as described in the primary method, yielding AVP-secreting neurons by day 150. Concurrently, pituitary organoids are differentiated from hPSCs using established protocols involving dual-SMAD inhibition followed by sequential activation of BMP, FGF, and WNT pathways to generate Rathke’s pouch-like structures that mature into hormone-secreting pituitary cells. At a defined maturation stage (e.g., day 60 for both), the hypothalamic and pituitary organoids are placed in close proximity within a shared culture dish or connected via microfluidic channels that allow for the exchange of secreted factors while maintaining physical separation. Over time, axons from hypothalamic AVP neurons extend toward the pituitary organoids, mimicking the natural hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract. Functional integration is assessed by measuring the release of pituitary hormones (e.g., adrenocorticotropic hormone, ACTH) in response to hypothalamic stimuli. For instance, CRH-secreting hypothalamic neurons should stimulate ACTH release from corticotrophs in the pituitary organoid, and this response can be blocked by CRH receptor antagonists, confirming specificity.

This simultaneous maturation system offers several advantages. First, it allows for the study of HP axis development and the molecular crosstalk that coordinates the differentiation of both tissues. Second, it provides a more accurate disease model for disorders affecting the entire axis, such as panhypopituitarism or combined hypothalamic-pituitary insufficiencies. Third, it serves as a superior platform for drug screening, where compounds can be tested for their ability to modulate not just isolated hypothalamic or pituitary cells, but the integrated feedback loops that characterize in vivo physiology. For example, a candidate drug for CDI could be evaluated not only for its effect on AVP neuron survival but also for its impact on downstream water balance regulation via the pituitary-kidney axis, using co-cultures with kidney organoids. The invention thus extends the utility of hypothalamic differentiation beyond isolated tissue models to encompass the dynamic, interactive nature of the neuroendocrine system.

### 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, the embodiments described herein represent a significant advancement in the field of stem cell-derived neural tissue engineering, specifically for the hypothalamus. The method for producing hypothalamic tissue is distinguished by its high efficiency, stage-specific optimization, and functional validation, overcoming the major limitations of prior art protocols. The strategic use of BMP4 and SAG at precisely titrated concentrations and timings ensures the robust generation of rostral hypothalamic progenitors with minimal off-target differentiation. The introduction of the Akt inhibitor as a rostralizing agent further refines the protocol to favor the dorsal hypothalamic lineage, which is essential for AVP neuron production. The sequential culture strategy—transitioning from 3D SFEBq aggregates to Transwell gas-liquid interphase culture and finally to 2D dissociated cultures on a tailored extracellular matrix—addresses the distinct microenvironmental requirements of each developmental stage, culminating in the generation of mature, hormone-secreting neurons that exhibit regulated secretory activity. The examples provided offer compelling empirical evidence of the protocol’s efficacy, reproducibility, and functional relevance across multiple hPSC lines. Furthermore, the extension of this technology to enable the simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues establishes a new paradigm for modeling the integrated neuroendocrine axis in vitro. Together, these innovations provide a comprehensive and versatile platform that bridges the gap between basic developmental biology and translational applications in regenerative medicine, disease modeling, and pharmaceutical discovery.

### 1. Appearance of Hypothalamic AVP Neuron

The appearance and maturation of hypothalamic arginine vasopressin (AVP) neurons through the disclosed method is a multi-stage process that mirrors key aspects of in vivo development, resulting in cells that are not only phenotypically authentic but also functionally competent. The journey begins with the specification of rostral hypothalamic progenitors, marked by the expression of the transcription factor RAX, which is induced with high efficiency (>80%) by the synergistic action of BMP4 and SAG during the early patterning phase (days 6–30). These RAX+ progenitors are further regionalized into dorsal hypothalamic identity, characterized by the co-expression of PAX6 and the absence of the ventral marker NKX2.1, under conditions of relatively higher BMP4 to SAG ratio (e.g., 0.5–1.0 nM BMP4 and 0.5 μM SAG). This dorsal hypothalamic territory is the exclusive developmental source of AVP neurons in the mammalian brain.

The commitment to the AVP lineage is marked by the emergence of intermediate precursor markers, specifically orthopedia (OTP) and BRN2 (POU3F2), which are transcription factors essential for the differentiation and survival of AVP and oxytocin neurons. The transition from RAX+/PAX6+ progenitors to OTP+/BRN2+ precursors is critically dependent on the shift to Transwell gas-liquid interphase culture at around day 60. This culture modality, which improves oxygen and nutrient diffusion, creates a microenvironment conducive to the upregulation of these key transcription factors, increasing their prevalence from negligible levels in 3D aggregates to over 30% of the neuronal population. Without this step, precursor induction remains inefficient, highlighting the importance of culture architecture in neuronal subtype specification.

Terminal differentiation into mature AVP neurons occurs during the extended 2D dissociated culture phase (days 60–150). Plating the dissociated hypothalamic cells onto a composite matrix of Matrigel, poly-D-lysine, and laminin provides the necessary adhesive and trophic support for neuronal maturation. In this environment, supplemented with BDNF, NT-3, and LM22A-4, OTP+/BRN2+ precursors progressively acquire the full molecular and morphological characteristics of magnocellular AVP neurons. Immunocytochemical analysis reveals that these neurons express AVP peptide robustly in their cytoplasm and project extensive neurites. They co-express Neurophysin II, the carrier protein that is co-synthesized and co-packaged with AVP into neurosecretory vesicles, confirming proper processing of the AVP precursor protein. Morphometric analysis shows that the soma size of these in vitro-generated AVP neurons averages approximately 40 × 20 micrometers, which closely matches the dimensions of native magnocellular neurons in the human PVN and SON. Importantly, these AVP+ neurons are negative for CRH, a marker of parvocellular neurons in the PVN, thereby confirming their correct magnocellular identity and excluding contamination from other hypothalamic lineages.

The ultimate hallmark of their authenticity is functional: these neurons are capable of synthesizing and secreting AVP in a regulated manner. Basal levels of AVP are detectable in the conditioned culture medium by sensitive radioimmunoassay, and this secretion is significantly potentiated—by more than two-fold—upon depolarization with high extracellular potassium (100 mM KCl). This activity-dependent release demonstrates that the neurons possess the necessary machinery for calcium-triggered exocytosis of neurosecretory vesicles, a defining feature of functional neuroendocrine cells. The appearance of these AVP neurons is thus not merely a matter of marker expression but represents the culmination of a developmentally informed, multi-step protocol that yields cells with genuine physiological relevance.

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary

The simultaneous maturation of hypothalamus and pituitary tissues, as detailed in the preceding embodiment, represents a sophisticated extension of the core differentiation technology, enabling the recreation of a pivotal neuroendocrine circuit in a dish. This achievement is grounded in a deep understanding of the embryological interdependence of these two structures: during development, the hypothalamus and pituitary engage in a tightly choreographed dialogue of reciprocal induction, where signals from the ventral diencephalon (future hypothalamus) instruct the overlying oral ectoderm to invaginate and form Rathke’s pouch (future anterior pituitary), while the pituitary, in turn, provides feedback that refines hypothalamic patterning. The present invention harnesses this natural synergy by either co-differentiating both tissues from a common hPSC pool under spatially or temporally controlled morphogen gradients, or by co-culturing pre-formed hypothalamic and pituitary organoids to encourage functional integration.

In practice, the co-differentiation approach exploits the inherent self-organizing capacity of hPSC aggregates. By applying a core-periphery morphogen strategy—where lower BMP4/SAG concentrations in the aggregate center promote RAX+/PAX6+ hypothalamic fate, while higher BMP4 (and potentially FGF) at the periphery drives LHX3+/HESX1+ pituitary placode formation—the system spontaneously segregates into distinct but adjacent tissue domains. Over time, these domains mature in parallel: the hypothalamic core gives rise to AVP, CRH, and TRH neurons, while the pituitary periphery differentiates into hormone-secreting cell types such as ACTH-producing corticotrophs, growth hormone-secreting somatotrophs, and prolactin-secreting lactotrophs. The close physical proximity facilitates the establishment of axonal projections from hypothalamic neurons into the pituitary tissue, mimicking the hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal system.

In the co-culture approach, the independent generation of each organoid type allows for greater control over their individual maturation states before integration. Hypothalamic organoids, rich in AVP and CRH neurons, are juxtaposed with pituitary organoids containing responsive endocrine cells. Within days to weeks of co-culture, functional coupling is evident: for example, CRH released from hypothalamic neurons binds to CRH receptors on corticotrophs, triggering intracellular cAMP signaling and the subsequent release of ACTH into the medium. This response is dose-dependent and can be pharmacologically modulated, providing a robust assay for HP axis functionality. Similarly, AVP neurons can influence water balance pathways if co-cultured with kidney organoids, creating a multi-organ system.

This simultaneous maturation capability transforms the utility of the invention. It moves beyond static, single-tissue models to dynamic, interactive systems that can recapitulate the feedback loops, hormone cascades, and regulatory mechanisms of the intact organism. For diseases like CDI, where the defect may lie not only in AVP production but also in the pituitary's ability to store and release it, such a model is indispensable. It also opens avenues for personalized medicine, where patient-derived iPSCs can be used to generate HP axis models that reflect individual genetic backgrounds, enabling tailored drug testing and therapy development. Thus, the simultaneous maturation of hypothalamus and pituitary is not merely an incremental improvement but a foundational advance that unlocks the full potential of stem cell-based neuroendocrine modeling.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention possesses broad and significant industrial applicability across multiple sectors, including regenerative medicine, pharmaceutical research and development, toxicology screening, and basic biomedical science. In the realm of regenerative medicine, the highly efficient and reproducible method for generating functional AVP-secreting hypothalamic neurons provides a direct cell source for transplantation therapies aimed at treating central diabetes insipidus (CDI). Unlike current pharmacological interventions that carry risks of hyponatremia and are ineffective in adipsic CDI, a cell-based therapy could restore endogenous, physiologically regulated AVP production, offering a potential cure. The protocol’s compatibility with human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) further enables the development of autologous grafts, minimizing immune rejection and ethical concerns associated with embryonic sources.

In the pharmaceutical industry, the invention offers a transformative platform for drug discovery and safety assessment. The in vitro-generated hypothalamic tissue, particularly when integrated with pituitary co-cultures to form a functional hypothalamic-pituitary axis, serves as a human-relevant model for screening compounds that modulate neuroendocrine function. This is invaluable for identifying novel therapeutics for a wide array of hypothalamic disorders, including obesity, eating disorders, sleep dysregulation, and stress-related pathologies, as well as for evaluating the off-target effects of drugs on water and electrolyte balance. The ability to measure regulated AVP secretion in response to stimuli provides a quantitative, high-throughput-compatible endpoint for assessing drug efficacy and toxicity, surpassing the limitations of animal models or non-functional cell lines.

Moreover, the technology is highly applicable to disease modeling and mechanistic studies. Patient-specific iPSCs derived from individuals with genetic forms of CDI or other hypothalamic syndromes can be differentiated using this protocol to create “disease-in-a-dish” models. The simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues further enables the modeling of complex, multi-glandular endocrine disorders that cannot be adequately studied in isolation.
