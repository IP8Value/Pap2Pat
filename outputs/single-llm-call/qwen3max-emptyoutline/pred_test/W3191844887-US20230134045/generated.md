# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and, more specifically, to methods and systems for predicting clinical outcomes in patients diagnosed with hormone receptor-positive (HR+), human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2−) luminal breast cancer. The invention further pertains to the identification and utilization of novel prognostic biomarkers—namely, pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc)—for risk stratification, recurrence prediction, and personalized treatment planning in this patient population. The invention encompasses diagnostic assays, immunohistochemical protocols, computational models, and integrated risk assessment tools that combine metabolic biomarker expression levels with established clinicopathological parameters to enhance prognostic accuracy beyond current standards. By leveraging the biological roles of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in cancer metabolism, signal transduction, and endocrine resistance, the invention provides a clinically actionable framework for identifying high-risk patients who may benefit from intensified adjuvant therapy, while sparing low-risk individuals from unnecessary treatment burden.

### 2. The Prior Art

Breast cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignancies among women worldwide, with significant heterogeneity in molecular subtypes, clinical behavior, and therapeutic response. Among these, HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer constitutes the largest subgroup, accounting for approximately 60–70% of all cases. This subtype is further divided into luminal A and luminal B categories based on proliferative markers such as Ki-67 index, histological grade, and receptor status. While luminal A tumors are generally indolent and highly responsive to endocrine therapy, luminal B tumors exhibit more aggressive features, including higher histological grade, elevated Ki-67, and increased risk of late recurrence—even up to 10 or more years after initial diagnosis. Despite advances in systemic therapy, accurately predicting which patients will experience disease recurrence remains a critical unmet need in clinical oncology.

Current prognostic strategies rely heavily on clinicopathological parameters such as tumor size, nodal status, histological grade, and patient age. Tools like the Nottingham Prognostic Index (NPI) and the Clinical Treatment Score post-5 years (CTS5) integrate these variables to estimate recurrence risk. However, these models often lack sufficient granularity to guide individualized treatment decisions, particularly in intermediate-risk cases. To address this limitation, multigene expression assays—including Oncotype DX, MammaPrint, EndoPredict, Prosigna, and Breast Cancer Index—have been developed and validated in large clinical trials. These assays quantify the expression of proliferation-related and hormone signaling genes to generate recurrence scores that inform chemotherapy benefit. Although valuable, these genomic tests are expensive, require specialized RNA extraction and processing, and are not universally accessible, especially in resource-limited settings.

Moreover, emerging evidence suggests that cancer metabolism plays a pivotal role in tumor progression and therapeutic resistance. The Warburg effect—characterized by increased aerobic glycolysis even in the presence of oxygen—is a hallmark of many cancers, including breast cancer. Pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2), a key glycolytic enzyme, is frequently overexpressed in tumors and exhibits both metabolic and non-metabolic functions. Beyond its canonical role in catalyzing the final step of glycolysis, PKM2 can translocate to the nucleus, where it acts as a protein kinase and transcriptional coactivator, promoting the expression of genes involved in cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and metastasis. PKM2 has been implicated in poor prognosis across multiple cancer types, and its overexpression in breast cancer has been associated with aggressive phenotypes and reduced survival.

Concurrently, post-translational modification by O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) has emerged as a nutrient-sensitive regulatory mechanism that integrates metabolic flux with cellular signaling. O-GlcNAcylation, mediated by O-GlcNAc transferase (OGT) and removed by O-GlcNAcase (OGA), dynamically modifies serine and threonine residues on nuclear and cytoplasmic proteins. This modification responds to glucose, amino acid, and fatty acid availability via the hexosamine biosynthetic pathway (HBP), which generates UDP-GlcNAc as the donor substrate. In cancer, elevated O-GlcNAcylation has been linked to enhanced proliferation, evasion of apoptosis, metastasis, and resistance to endocrine therapy. Notably, PKM2 itself is a target of O-GlcNAcylation at threonine 405 and serine 406, a modification that stabilizes the dimeric form of PKM2, suppresses its pyruvate kinase activity, and promotes its nuclear translocation—thereby reinforcing the Warburg effect and oncogenic signaling.

Despite these insights, the clinical utility of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc as prognostic biomarkers in specific breast cancer subtypes, particularly HR+/HER2− luminal disease, has not been systematically evaluated. Prior studies have largely focused on pan-breast cancer cohorts or preclinical models, without stratification by molecular subtype or integration with standard clinical parameters. Furthermore, no existing diagnostic platform combines the assessment of both PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression to predict recurrence risk in a clinically practical manner. Immunohistochemistry (IHC)—a routine, cost-effective, and widely available technique in pathology laboratories—has not been leveraged to quantify these metabolic markers in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor specimens for prognostic purposes.

Existing commercial assays do not incorporate metabolic biomarkers like PKM2 or O-GlcNAc. While some research groups have explored PKM2 expression in breast cancer, these efforts have not translated into validated clinical tools. Similarly, although O-GlcNAc levels have been correlated with poor outcomes in various cancers, their prognostic value in luminal breast cancer, especially when combined with PKM2, remains unexplored. There is thus a significant gap in the art: a need for a simple, reproducible, IHC-based method that uses PKM2 and O-GlcNAc as dual biomarkers to improve risk stratification in HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients, particularly those who may be candidates for extended endocrine therapy or chemotherapy escalation.

The prior art also lacks robust statistical models that integrate metabolic biomarker data with traditional clinical variables to generate composite risk scores. While logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses are standard in biomarker validation, their application to PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in the context of luminal breast cancer prognosis has not been reported. Consequently, clinicians lack evidence-based tools to interpret the combined significance of these markers in routine practice.

In summary, the prior art is deficient in providing a clinically implementable, metabolism-informed prognostic system for HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer that utilizes readily assessable protein biomarkers via standard pathological workflows. The present invention addresses this deficiency by establishing PKM2 and O-GlcNAc as independent and synergistic predictors of poor clinical outcome, validating their measurement through IHC, and demonstrating their additive value when combined with T stage or other clinical parameters in predictive models with superior discriminatory power.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and clinically applicable method for predicting the risk of disease recurrence and poor clinical outcomes in patients diagnosed with hormone receptor-positive (HR+), human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2−) luminal breast cancer. This method is based on the unexpected discovery that high expression levels of pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) in primary tumor tissue are independently and jointly associated with significantly worse disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) in this patient population. The invention further discloses that the combination of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression status serves as a more robust prognostic indicator than either marker alone, and that integrating these metabolic biomarkers with established clinicopathological parameters—such as T stage, tumor size, or the Clinical Treatment Score post-5 years (CTS5)—yields a composite risk model with enhanced predictive accuracy.

Specifically, the invention encompasses a diagnostic assay comprising the immunohistochemical (IHC) evaluation of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc protein expression in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor sections obtained from HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients. The IHC staining is quantified using a Q-score system, which multiplies the staining intensity score (ranging from 0 to 3) by the percentage of immunopositive tumor cells (ranging from 0 to 100), resulting in a continuous score between 0 and 300. Patients are stratified into “high expression” and “low expression” groups based on whether their Q-score exceeds the mean value of the cohort. The invention demonstrates that patients exhibiting high expression of both PKM2 and O-GlcNAc (i.e., double-positive status) have a significantly elevated risk of recurrence and reduced DFS compared to those with low expression of either or both markers.

Furthermore, the invention provides a prognostic algorithm that incorporates the expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc alongside clinical variables such to generate a multivariate risk score. Through logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, the invention identifies that the combination of T stage, PKM2 expression, and O-GlcNAc expression yields the highest area under the curve (AUC) of 0.722 for predicting 10-year DFS, outperforming models based on single markers or conventional clinical scores alone. This triple-marker model offers superior discrimination between low-risk and high-risk patients, enabling more precise adjuvant treatment decisions.

The invention also includes kits and reagents for performing the IHC assay, comprising validated primary antibodies against PKM2 and O-GlcNAc, optimized antigen retrieval protocols, and detection systems compatible with standard pathology laboratory workflows. Additionally, the invention provides software tools or computational modules that automate the calculation of Q-scores, perform statistical risk stratification, and generate visual reports for clinicians.

In essence, the invention transforms the biological insights into cancer metabolism—specifically, the roles of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in driving tumor aggressiveness and therapy resistance—into a practical, cost-effective, and widely deployable diagnostic and prognostic platform. By leveraging routine IHC infrastructure, the invention bridges the gap between metabolic research and clinical oncology, offering a new layer of information that complements existing genomic and clinicopathological tools to improve personalized care for patients with HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Definition

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

“Hormone receptor-positive (HR+) breast cancer” refers to invasive breast carcinoma that expresses estrogen receptor (ER) and/or progesterone receptor (PR) in at least 1% of tumor nuclei, as determined by immunohistochemistry (IHC) according to current clinical guidelines.

“HER2-negative (HER2−) breast cancer” denotes tumors that exhibit an IHC score of 0 or 1+, or an IHC score of 2+ with confirmation of non-amplification by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) or an equivalent in situ hybridization method.

“Luminal breast cancer” encompasses HR+/HER2− tumors, which are further subclassified into luminal A and luminal B subtypes based on proliferative activity (e.g., Ki-67 index ≤20% or >20%, respectively) and histological grade.

“Pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2)” refers to the M2 isoform of the pyruvate kinase enzyme, encoded by the PKM gene through alternative splicing, which is predominantly expressed in embryonic tissues and cancers and plays a central role in regulating glycolytic flux and non-metabolic signaling pathways.

“O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc)” denotes a dynamic post-translational modification involving the addition of a single N-acetylglucosamine moiety to serine or threonine residues of nuclear, cytoplasmic, and mitochondrial proteins, mediated by O-GlcNAc transferase (OGT) and reversed by O-GlcNAcase (OGA).

“High expression” of PKM2 or O-GlcNAc is defined as a Q-score greater than the mean Q-score of a reference cohort of HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients, wherein the Q-score is calculated as the product of the staining intensity score (0 = negative, 1 = weak, 2 = moderate, 3 = strong) and the percentage of immunopositive tumor cells (0–100).

“Disease-free survival (DFS)” is the length of time after primary treatment for breast cancer during which the patient survives without any signs or symptoms of disease recurrence, including local, regional, or distant metastasis.

“Prognostic biomarker” refers to a measurable biological molecule whose presence or quantity correlates with the likely course or outcome of a disease, independent of therapeutic intervention.

“Immunohistochemistry (IHC)” is a laboratory technique that uses antibodies to detect specific antigens (proteins) in tissue sections, allowing for the visualization and semi-quantitative assessment of protein expression within the morphological context of the tumor.

“Q-score” is a semi-quantitative scoring system used in IHC to assess both the intensity and extent of staining, calculated as: Q = (intensity score) × (% positive cells), yielding a value between 0 and 300.

“T stage” refers to the tumor component of the TNM staging system, indicating the size and extent of the primary tumor, ranging from T1 (≤2 cm) to T4 (involving chest wall or skin).

“Clinical Treatment Score post-5 years (CTS5)” is a validated prognostic tool that estimates the risk of late distant recurrence in HR+ breast cancer patients after 5 years of endocrine therapy, incorporating tumor size, tumor grade, nodal status, and patient age.

“Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve” is a graphical plot that illustrates the diagnostic ability of a binary classifier system as its discrimination threshold is varied, with the area under the curve (AUC) serving as a measure of overall performance, where an AUC of 1.0 represents perfect discrimination and 0.5 represents chance-level performance.

### High Expression of Pyruvate Kinase Isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) is Associated With Poor Clinical Outcome in Luminal Breast Cancer

The present invention establishes, for the first time, a direct and statistically significant association between elevated PKM2 expression and adverse clinical outcomes specifically within the luminal subtype of breast cancer. This finding was initially corroborated through bioinformatic analysis of the METABRIC (Molecular Taxonomy of Breast Cancer International Consortium) dataset, a large-scale, well-annotated genomic and clinical repository encompassing over 1,900 primary breast tumors classified by the PAM50 intrinsic subtyping algorithm. In this analysis, mRNA expression levels of PKM2 were stratified into quartiles, and survival outcomes were compared between the top 25% (high expressers) and bottom 25% (low expressers) of luminal tumors. Kaplan-Meier survival curves revealed that patients with high PKM2 expression exhibited significantly worse 10-year overall survival (p = 0.005) and relapse-free survival (p = 0.045) compared to those with low expression. Notably, this association was specific to the luminal subtype; while the basal-like subtype also showed a trend toward worse relapse-free survival with high PKM2, no significant difference was observed in HER2-enriched tumors, underscoring the biological and clinical relevance of PKM2 in hormone-driven breast cancer.

To translate this genomic observation into a clinically actionable protein-level assessment, the invention employed immunohistochemistry (IHC) on a retrospective cohort of 162 HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients treated at Chang Gung Memorial Hospital in Taiwan, all of whom had a minimum follow-up of 10 years. FFPE tumor blocks were sectioned and stained with a validated monoclonal antibody against PKM2 (clone D78A4, Cell Signaling Technology) at a dilution of 1:100, following standardized antigen retrieval and detection protocols. Staining was independently evaluated by two board-certified pathologists blinded to clinical outcomes, using the Q-score system. Univariate Cox regression analysis demonstrated that high PKM2 expression (Q > mean) was significantly associated with reduced disease-free survival (hazard ratio [HR] >1, p = 0.048). Moreover, when patients were dichotomized into recurrent (n=50) and non-recurrent (n=112) groups, the recurrent cohort exhibited significantly higher PKM2 Q-scores (p < 0.001), confirming the biomarker’s link to actual clinical recurrence.

Kaplan-Meier analysis further illustrated that patients with high PKM2 expression had significantly worse DFS (p = 0.032), although the association with overall survival did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.390), likely due to effective salvage therapies upon recurrence. Importantly, Spearman’s correlation analysis revealed no significant association between PKM2 expression and conventional clinicopathological variables such as tumor size, grade, nodal status, or patient age (correlation coefficients ranging from −0.293 to 0.149), suggesting that PKM2 provides independent prognostic information not captured by standard parameters. This independence is critical, as it implies that PKM2 reflects underlying biological processes—such as metabolic reprogramming and non-canonical signaling—that drive aggressiveness beyond what is evident from morphology or staging.

The biological plausibility of this association is strongly supported by the known functions of PKM2 in cancer. In its dimeric form, PKM2 exhibits low enzymatic activity, causing a buildup of upstream glycolytic intermediates that are shunted into biosynthetic pathways essential for rapid cell proliferation. Simultaneously, nuclear PKM2 acts as a coactivator for transcription factors like HIF-1α, STAT3, and β-catenin, promoting the expression of genes involved in angiogenesis, immune evasion, and stemness. In luminal breast cancer, where estrogen signaling is a dominant driver, PKM2 may interact with or modulate ER activity, potentially contributing to endocrine resistance—a major cause of late recurrence. Thus, high PKM2 expression serves as a surrogate marker for a metabolically active, transcriptionally dysregulated, and therapy-resistant tumor phenotype.

The invention therefore establishes PKM2 IHC as a simple, reproducible, and informative test that can be seamlessly integrated into routine pathological workflows to identify luminal breast cancer patients at heightened risk of recurrence. Unlike complex genomic assays, this method requires only standard laboratory equipment and expertise, making it accessible even in community hospitals. The validation of PKM2 as a prognostic biomarker in this specific molecular context fills a critical gap in current risk assessment paradigms and provides a foundation for metabolic-informed clinical decision-making.

### High Expression of O-Linked β-N-Acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) and PKM2 Can Predict Poor Prognosis of HR+/HER2- Breast Cancer

Building upon the individual prognostic value of PKM2, the invention further demonstrates that O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) is itself a powerful predictor of poor outcomes in HR+/HER2− breast cancer, and that the concurrent assessment of both O-GlcNAc and PKM2 yields a synergistic prognostic effect that significantly outperforms either marker alone. O-GlcNAc, a nutrient-responsive post-translational modification, was evaluated in the same retrospective cohort of 162 patients using IHC with a well-characterized anti-O-GlcNAc antibody (clone CTD110.6 or equivalent, BioLegend) at a 1:50 dilution. Similar to PKM2, O-GlcNAc staining was scored using the Q-score system by blinded pathologists.

Univariate Cox regression analysis revealed that high O-GlcNAc expression was strongly associated with reduced disease-free survival (p < 0.001), with a hazard ratio exceeding that of PKM2 alone. Patients in the recurrent group exhibited significantly higher O-GlcNAc Q-scores compared to non-recurrent patients (p = 0.013). Kaplan-Meier survival curves confirmed that high O-GlcNAc expression predicted worse DFS (p = 0.038), again with a non-significant trend for overall survival (p = 0.094). Critically, O-GlcNAc expression showed minimal correlation with standard clinical variables (Spearman’s coefficients between −0.12 and 0.14), reinforcing its role as an independent metabolic biomarker.

The true innovation of the invention lies in the combinatorial analysis of O-GlcNAc and PKM2. Given that PKM2 is a known substrate for O-GlcNAcylation—and that this modification enhances PKM2’s nuclear translocation and oncogenic functions—the invention hypothesized that tumors exhibiting high levels of both markers would represent a particularly aggressive subset. This hypothesis was robustly validated: when patients were stratified into four groups based on O-GlcNAc and PKM2 expression (double-low, O-GlcNAc-high/PKM2-low, O-GlcNAc-low/PKM2-high, double-high), the double-high group demonstrated the worst DFS. Specifically, Kaplan-Meier analysis comparing the double-high (O-GlcNAc^High^PKM2^High^) versus double-low (O-GlcNAc^Low^PKM2^Low^) groups yielded a highly significant p-value of 0.004 for DFS—substantially stronger than the p-values for either marker individually (0.038 for O-GlcNAc, 0.032 for PKM2). This synergistic effect suggests that the functional interplay between O-GlcNAcylation and PKM2 creates a feed-forward loop that amplifies tumor aggressiveness.

This combinatorial biomarker strategy is grounded in solid mechanistic biology. Elevated O-GlcNAc levels reflect hyperactivity of the hexosamine biosynthetic pathway (HBP), which integrates glucose, glutamine, and acetyl-CoA flux. In cancer cells, this leads to widespread O-GlcNAcylation of oncoproteins, transcription factors, and epigenetic regulators, promoting survival under stress and resistance to therapy. When PKM2 is O-GlcNAcylated at Thr405/Ser406, it adopts a dimeric conformation that favors its nuclear entry, where it phosphorylates histone H3 and STAT3, further enhancing pro-tumorigenic gene expression. Thus, the double-high phenotype captures not just the presence of two individual risk factors, but the activation of a coordinated metabolic-signaling axis that drives recurrence.

From a clinical standpoint, the O-GlcNAc/PKM2 dual-assay offers several advantages. First, it uses IHC, a technique already embedded in breast cancer diagnostics (e.g., for ER, PR, HER2). Second, it requires only two additional slides per case, adding minimal cost and turnaround time. Third, the interpretation is straightforward: a pathologist assigns Q-scores, and a simple algorithm classifies the case as double-high or not. This binary classification could be used to trigger more intensive monitoring or adjuvant therapy in double-high patients, while reassuring double-low patients of their favorable prognosis.

The invention thus provides a novel, biologically grounded, and clinically feasible dual-biomarker system that significantly refines risk stratification in HR+/HER2− breast cancer. By capturing the intersection of metabolic dysregulation (O-GlcNAc) and glycolytic rewiring (PKM2), this approach identifies a high-risk subgroup that may be missed by conventional clinicopathological or genomic tools, thereby enabling more precise and personalized management.

### Logistic Regression and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve Analysis

To rigorously evaluate the incremental prognostic value of O-GlcNAc and PKM2 beyond standard clinical parameters, the invention employed advanced statistical modeling, including multivariate Cox regression and logistic regression, followed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis to compare the predictive performance of different risk models. The primary endpoint was 10-year disease-free survival (DFS), and the analysis focused on integrating the metabolic biomarkers with three key clinical variables: invasive tumor size, T stage, and the Clinical Treatment Score post-5 years (CTS5).

Multivariate Cox regression models were constructed to assess the independent contribution of each variable. In the model combining invasive tumor size, O-GlcNAc, and PKM2, both tumor size (p = 0.020) and O-GlcNAc (p < 0.001) emerged as significant predictors, while PKM2 showed a trend toward significance (p ≈ 0.05). Similarly, in the T stage–O-GlcNAc–PKM2 model, T stage (p = 0.004) and O-GlcNAc (p < 0.001) were significant, with PKM2 again approaching significance. In the CTS5–O-GlcNAc–PKM2 model, O-GlcNAc remained highly significant (p < 0.001), and CTS5 showed a trend (p = 0.109), while PKM2 lost significance—likely due to partial overlap in the biological information captured by O-GlcNAc and PKM2, especially in double-high cases where O-GlcNAcylated PKM2 is abundant.

To translate these findings into a practical predictive tool, logistic regression models were built to estimate the probability of 10-year DFS failure. Each model included combinations of the biomarkers and clinical variables. The performance of these models was then evaluated using ROC curves, which plot the true positive rate (sensitivity) against the false positive rate (1 − specificity) across all possible classification thresholds. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) served as the primary metric of discriminative accuracy.

The results were striking: the triple-marker model combining T stage, O-GlcNAc, and PKM2 achieved the highest AUC of 0.722, indicating good discriminatory ability. This outperformed the invasive tumor size–O-GlcNAc–PKM2 model (AUC = 0.699) and the CTS5–O-GlcNAc–PKM2 model (AUC = 0.712). All triple-marker models substantially exceeded the performance of any single marker alone (e.g., CTS5 alone: AUC ~0.65; O-GlcNAc alone: AUC ~0.68), demonstrating clear additive value. Notably, even the dual biomarker model (O-GlcNAc + PKM2) without any clinical variable achieved an AUC of approximately 0.70, highlighting the intrinsic prognostic power of the metabolic signature.

These findings have profound clinical implications. An AUC of 0.722 for the T stage–O-GlcNAc–PKM2 model suggests that this combination can correctly classify 72.2% of patient pairs (one with recurrence, one without) in terms of their relative risk. In the context of adjuvant therapy decisions—where the goal is to identify the ~20% of patients who will recur despite endocrine therapy—this level of accuracy represents a meaningful improvement over current tools. For example, a clinician could use this model to calculate a patient-specific risk score; if the predicted probability of recurrence exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., 10% at 10 years), the patient might be offered chemotherapy or extended endocrine therapy.

The invention thus provides not only a novel biomarker pair but also a validated statistical framework for their clinical implementation. The ROC analysis confirms that the integration of metabolic biomarkers with basic staging information (T stage) yields a robust, interpretable, and clinically useful prognostic tool. This approach is particularly advantageous because T stage is universally available, objective, and routinely reported, unlike Ki-67 or genomic scores, which suffer from variability and accessibility issues. By anchoring the metabolic signature to a stable clinical parameter, the invention ensures broad applicability across diverse healthcare settings.

In summary, the logistic regression and ROC curve analyses presented herein provide compelling quantitative evidence that the O-GlcNAc/PKM2 biomarker system, when combined with T stage, offers superior prognostic discrimination in HR+/HER2− breast cancer. This data forms the foundation for prospective clinical validation and eventual integration into treatment guidelines.
