# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Autoimmune diseases represent a significant and growing public health burden, affecting an estimated 15 to 24 million individuals in the United States alone. These conditions arise from a breakdown in immune tolerance, leading the immune system to mount pathological responses against self-antigens present in specific organs or tissues. Among the most debilitating of these disorders is relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS), a chronic autoimmune condition characterized by episodic inflammation, demyelination, and neurodegeneration within the central nervous system (CNS). Despite advances in therapeutic interventions that can modulate disease activity, current clinical management remains fundamentally reactive rather than proactive. This paradigm stems from critical limitations in existing diagnostic and monitoring technologies, which fail to provide timely, specific, and actionable insights into the immunological status of affected tissues before irreversible neurological damage occurs.

The cornerstone of current RRMS monitoring is magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), which visualizes areas of demyelination and blood-brain barrier disruption as gadolinium-enhancing lesions or T2 hyperintensities. While MRI provides invaluable anatomical information about disease burden and lesion distribution, it offers no direct insight into the underlying immunopathological processes driving tissue injury. Moreover, MRI findings often correlate poorly with clinical symptoms—a phenomenon known as the clinico-radiological paradox—and cannot distinguish between different immunological endotypes that may respond differently to available therapies. Peripheral blood analysis, another commonly employed monitoring strategy, suffers from similar limitations. Although blood contains a rich repertoire of immune cells, the vast majority are not directly involved in CNS-directed autoimmunity, and their transcriptional profiles often fail to reflect the inflammatory milieu within the target organ. Consequently, blood-based biomarkers for MS have demonstrated modest sensitivity and specificity, with many identified gene expression changes lacking clear mechanistic relevance to disease pathogenesis.

A fundamental challenge in autoimmune disease management lies in the inability to detect subclinical immune activation before the onset of overt symptoms or radiological evidence of tissue damage. In RRMS, this window of opportunity—during which therapeutic intervention could potentially abort an impending relapse or prevent cumulative disability—is currently invisible to clinicians. The development of strategies that enable early detection of immunological dysregulation within disease-relevant contexts would represent a transformative advance, facilitating precision medicine approaches that match patients to optimal therapies based on real-time immunological profiling rather than trial-and-error prescribing. Such an approach would be particularly valuable given the substantial inter-individual variability in treatment response observed with current disease-modifying therapies, many of which carry significant risks of adverse effects requiring long-term administration.

Tissue engineering has emerged as a powerful platform for creating artificial microenvironments that recapitulate key aspects of native tissue biology. Porous biomaterial scaffolds implanted subcutaneously have been shown to induce robust cellular infiltration, vascularization, and formation of organized tissue structures that persist over extended periods. These engineered niches serve as dynamic interfaces between the host immune system and the implanted material, with their cellular composition and functional state influenced by systemic physiological and pathological conditions. Previous studies have demonstrated that the inflammatory response surrounding such implants can be modulated by diverse disease states including diabetes, obesity, cancer, and aging, suggesting their potential utility as biosensors of systemic immune status. However, the application of engineered immunological niches specifically for monitoring organ-specific autoimmune diseases such as MS has not been previously explored.

The experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE) model in mice has served as a critical preclinical tool for investigating MS pathogenesis and testing therapeutic interventions. Both active immunization with myelin antigens and adoptive transfer of encephalitogenic T cells can induce EAE, with each approach modeling distinct phases of human disease. Active immunization recapitulates both the induction and effector phases of autoimmunity, involving antigen presentation, T cell priming, and subsequent migration to the CNS. In contrast, adoptive transfer models primarily the effector phase, wherein pre-activated autoreactive T cells directly infiltrate the CNS and initiate inflammatory cascades. These complementary models provide robust platforms for evaluating novel monitoring strategies across different stages of autoimmune pathogenesis.

Recent advances in high-throughput molecular profiling and machine learning algorithms have enabled the development of sophisticated diagnostic signatures capable of distinguishing disease states with high accuracy. Elastic net regularization, singular value decomposition (SVD), and ensemble machine learning methods such as bootstrap aggregated decision trees (Bagged Trees) offer powerful approaches for identifying minimal gene sets that capture essential biological differences between conditions while minimizing overfitting. When applied to complex biological datasets, these computational techniques can extract meaningful patterns from high-dimensional data, enabling the creation of robust classification systems that translate molecular measurements into clinically actionable insights.

The convergence of tissue engineering, immunology, and computational biology presents a unique opportunity to address the unmet need for real-time monitoring of organ-specific autoimmune activity. By creating an accessible, subcutaneous immunological niche that reflects the immunological status of distant target tissues such as the CNS, it becomes possible to obtain longitudinal molecular snapshots of disease activity without invasive procedures. Such a system would overcome the limitations of current monitoring approaches by providing direct access to disease-relevant immune cells within an inflammatory microenvironment that mirrors key aspects of the pathological tissue context. Furthermore, the ability to repeatedly biopsy these engineered niches would enable dynamic assessment of treatment response, facilitating personalized therapeutic adjustments based on individual immunological trajectories rather than delayed clinical or radiological outcomes.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides an implantable immunological niche (IN) system for monitoring immune dysregulation associated with organ-specific autoimmune diseases, particularly relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS). The system comprises a biocompatible, porous scaffold implanted subcutaneously that induces formation of a vascularized tissue compartment containing innate and adaptive immune cells whose phenotypic and transcriptional characteristics reflect the immunological status of distant target organs such as the central nervous system (CNS). Through comprehensive molecular profiling and computational analysis, the invention identifies specific gene expression signatures within these engineered niches that reliably distinguish between healthy and diseased states, detect subclinical disease activity before symptom onset, monitor disease dynamics during remission and relapse, and assess therapeutic response to both standard-of-care and investigational treatments.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for monitoring autoimmune disease activity comprising implanting a porous biocompatible scaffold subcutaneously in a subject, allowing formation of an immunological niche through cellular infiltration and vascularization, obtaining a biopsy sample from the niche at a selected time point, analyzing gene expression of a defined signature panel in the biopsy sample, and determining disease status based on the expression profile. The gene signature panels are derived from high-throughput transcriptomic analysis comparing IN samples from healthy controls versus subjects with induced autoimmune disease, followed by computational refinement using elastic net regularization and statistical filtering to identify minimal gene sets with maximal discriminatory power.

In another aspect, the invention provides specific gene expression signatures for detecting and monitoring experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE), a murine model of multiple sclerosis. For the adoptive transfer model of EAE, a 21-gene signature has been identified that includes TREM1, CD163, Il1b, Il1f9, S100a9, and other inflammatory mediators that collectively distinguish diseased from healthy states with high accuracy. For the active immunization model of EAE, a 25-gene signature has been developed that shares four overlapping genes with the adoptive transfer signature (CD163, Il1b, Il1f9, and S100a9) while containing additional model-specific markers reflecting differences in disease induction mechanisms. These signatures demonstrate remarkable sensitivity, detecting immunological abnormalities in the IN before clinical symptoms manifest and accurately tracking disease progression through symptomatic phases, remission, and relapse.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides computational scoring systems that translate complex gene expression data into simple numerical metrics for clinical decision-making. Two complementary approaches are employed: an unsupervised dimensionality reduction method using singular value decomposition (SVD) and a supervised machine learning algorithm based on bootstrap aggregated decision trees (Bagged Trees). Both methods generate continuous scores that effectively separate healthy from diseased samples, with combined metrics achieving area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) values approaching 1.0 in training datasets and maintaining strong diagnostic performance (AUC 0.81–0.90) in distinguishing remission from relapse states.

The invention further provides methods for guiding therapeutic intervention based on IN-derived molecular signatures. By detecting subclinical immune dysregulation before symptom onset, the system enables preemptive administration of therapeutics that can prevent or significantly attenuate disease development. Demonstrated examples include systemic glucocorticoid treatment and antigen-specific tolerogenic nanoparticle therapy, both of which show enhanced efficacy when administered based on IN-predicted disease activity rather than after clinical manifestation. Additionally, the IN system can monitor therapeutic response within days of treatment initiation, distinguishing effective from ineffective interventions and potentially enabling rapid therapeutic adjustments before clinical deterioration occurs.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a platform for precision medicine applications in autoimmune disease management. By capturing the molecular heterogeneity of individual immune responses within a disease-relevant tissue context, the IN system enables patient stratification based on immunological endotypes rather than clinical phenotypes alone. This capability addresses a critical unmet need in multiple sclerosis, where pathological heterogeneity likely contributes to variable treatment responses but cannot be assessed in living patients using current methodologies. The accessible nature of the subcutaneous IN allows for repeated sampling over time, facilitating longitudinal monitoring of individual disease trajectories and therapeutic responses in a manner not possible with conventional approaches.

The invention also encompasses methods for investigating disease mechanisms and therapeutic mechanisms of action through detailed characterization of immune cell populations within the IN. Flow cytometric analysis reveals that the IN contains substantial populations of both innate and adaptive immune cells, including dendritic cells, inflammatory monocytes, and T cell subsets that mirror those found in target organs such as the spinal cord during EAE. Notably, the IN faithfully recapitulates disease-associated changes in Th1 cell frequencies that are not apparent in peripheral lymphoid organs, highlighting its unique ability to capture tissue-specific immunological events. This feature makes the IN particularly valuable for studying the roles of specific immune cell subsets in disease pathogenesis and for evaluating how therapeutic interventions modulate these populations in vivo.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to an implantable immunological niche system for monitoring immune dysregulation in organ-specific autoimmune diseases, with particular application to relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS). The system leverages tissue engineering principles to create a subcutaneous biosensor that dynamically reflects the immunological status of distant target tissues through molecular and cellular profiling of engineered tissue compartments. Central to the invention is the discovery that porous biocompatible scaffolds implanted subcutaneously induce formation of vascularized tissue structures containing immune cell populations whose phenotypic and transcriptional characteristics correlate strongly with disease activity in target organs such as the central nervous system (CNS).

The immunological niche (IN) is formed through a well-characterized process of foreign body response to implanted biomaterials. When a porous scaffold is introduced into the subcutaneous space, it triggers a cascade of biological events including protein adsorption, inflammatory cell recruitment, fibroblast infiltration, and neovascularization. Over a period of approximately two weeks, this process results in the establishment of a stable, vascularized tissue compartment that persists for extended durations. Critically, the cellular composition and functional state of this engineered tissue are not static but rather dynamically responsive to systemic immunological conditions. During autoimmune disease states such as experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE), the IN undergoes specific molecular and phenotypic changes that mirror those occurring in the actual target organ, despite being physically separated from the site of primary pathology.

The scaffold material used to create the IN is selected based on biocompatibility, porosity, mechanical properties, and degradation characteristics. In the preferred embodiment, microporous poly(ε-caprolactone) (PCL) scaffolds are employed due to their excellent biocompatibility, controlled degradation profile, and ability to support robust cellular infiltration and vascularization. The fabrication process involves mixing PCL polymer with a salt porogen, pressing the mixture into molds, sintering the polymer to fuse particles while maintaining porosity, and subsequently dissolving the salt porogen to create an interconnected pore network. The resulting scaffolds typically exhibit pore sizes ranging from 100 to 300 micrometers, which optimally supports cellular migration, nutrient diffusion, and vascular ingrowth while preventing excessive fibrous encapsulation that might isolate the implant from systemic influences.

Alternative scaffold materials may include other biodegradable polymers such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA), polylactic acid (PLA), or natural polymers like collagen, hyaluronic acid, or chitosan. The choice of material can be tailored to specific applications based on desired degradation kinetics, mechanical properties, and potential for functionalization with bioactive molecules. For instance, scaffolds incorporating specific cytokines, chemokines, or extracellular matrix components could be engineered to preferentially recruit particular immune cell subsets or modulate their functional states, thereby enhancing the sensitivity or specificity of the monitoring system for particular disease contexts.

The implantation procedure is straightforward and minimally invasive, typically performed under brief anesthesia with appropriate analgesia. In murine models, scaffolds are implanted subcutaneously in the dorsal or flank region using standard surgical techniques. The subcutaneous location is deliberately chosen for its accessibility, minimal risk of complications, and established use in various biomedical applications including drug delivery devices and glucose sensors. Following implantation, a maturation period of approximately 14 days is allowed before the IN is considered ready for monitoring applications, ensuring complete vascularization and stabilization of the cellular infiltrate.

Gene expression analysis within the IN is performed using high-throughput quantitative PCR platforms such as OpenArray technology, which enables simultaneous measurement of hundreds of target genes with high sensitivity and reproducibility. RNA is extracted from IN biopsy samples using standard purification protocols, converted to cDNA, and analyzed using pre-configured panels focused on inflammatory pathways or custom-designed arrays targeting specific gene sets of interest. Quality control measures including RNA integrity number (RIN) assessment ensure reliable data generation, with samples meeting minimum quality thresholds (typically RIN > 8) being included in downstream analyses.

Computational analysis of gene expression data employs a multi-step approach to identify robust disease signatures. Initial screening identifies differentially expressed genes between healthy and diseased conditions using statistical criteria such as p-values < 0.05 and fold changes > 1.5. Elastic net regularization is then applied to select a minimal subset of genes that maximizes predictive accuracy while minimizing overfitting. This machine learning technique combines L1 and L2 penalties to perform both variable selection and regularization, effectively identifying the most informative features from high-dimensional data. Additional filtering ensures inclusion of both upregulated and downregulated genes to enhance model robustness and facilitate eventual sensor development.

Two complementary scoring systems are developed from the refined gene signatures: an unsupervised approach using singular value decomposition (SVD) and a supervised machine learning method employing bootstrap aggregated decision trees (Bagged Trees). SVD reduces the dimensionality of the gene expression matrix to capture the dominant patterns of variation across samples, with the first principal component serving as a continuous disease score. Bagged Trees, implemented using ensemble learning algorithms, train multiple decision tree classifiers on bootstrapped samples of the training data and aggregate their predictions to improve accuracy and reduce variance. Both methods generate numerical scores that effectively discriminate between healthy and diseased states, with combined metrics providing enhanced diagnostic performance.

The IN system demonstrates remarkable sensitivity in detecting subclinical disease activity. In EAE models, molecular abnormalities are detectable in the IN several days before the onset of clinical symptoms, providing a critical window for preemptive therapeutic intervention. This early detection capability stems from the IN's ability to capture subtle changes in immune cell phenotypes within inflammatory microenvironments, rather than merely reflecting gross alterations in cell trafficking patterns. Flow cytometric characterization confirms that while overall immune cell populations in the IN remain relatively stable between healthy and diseased states, specific functional subsets such as Th1 cells show disease-associated changes that parallel those observed in target organs like the spinal cord.

The dynamic nature of the IN is further evidenced by its ability to track disease progression through different phases including remission and relapse. During remission periods, IN gene expression profiles revert toward healthy baselines, while relapse induction triggers re-emergence of disease-associated signatures. This temporal fidelity enables accurate monitoring of disease activity over time and provides objective molecular correlates for clinical decision-making. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis demonstrates excellent diagnostic performance, with area under the curve (AUC) values approaching 1.0 for distinguishing healthy from diseased states and maintaining strong performance (AUC 0.81–0.90) in differentiating remission from relapse phases.

Therapeutic applications of the IN system include both preemptive intervention and treatment monitoring. When used to guide timing of therapeutic administration, the IN enables interventions that prevent or significantly attenuate disease development. Demonstrated examples include systemic glucocorticoid treatment and antigen-specific tolerogenic nanoparticle therapy, both showing enhanced efficacy when administered based on IN-predicted disease activity rather than after clinical manifestation. The single-dose nanoparticle therapy, in particular, demonstrates sustained disease prevention when administered preemptively, highlighting the potential for reducing treatment burden while improving outcomes.

For treatment monitoring, the IN provides rapid assessment of therapeutic efficacy within days of intervention, distinguishing effective from ineffective treatments before clinical deterioration occurs. This capability addresses a critical limitation of current monitoring approaches, which often require weeks or months to detect treatment failure through clinical or radiological measures. The ability to quickly identify non-responders enables timely therapeutic adjustments, potentially preventing unnecessary exposure to ineffective treatments and their associated side effects.

The IN system also serves as a platform for precision medicine applications by capturing individual variations in immune responses. Unlike blood-based biomarkers that average signals across diverse cell populations, the IN provides access to disease-relevant immune cells within an inflammatory tissue context that mirrors key aspects of the pathological microenvironment. This feature enables patient stratification based on immunological endotypes rather than clinical phenotypes alone, potentially explaining variable treatment responses and guiding personalized therapeutic selection.

Mechanistically, the IN appears to function as an ectopic tertiary lymphoid organ (TLO)-like structure, containing organized populations of both innate and adaptive immune cells within a stromal and vascular framework. The presence of large numbers of innate immune cells, particularly dendritic cells and inflammatory monocytes, makes the IN particularly valuable for studying the roles of these cell subsets in disease pathogenesis. Many of the genes comprising the EAE signatures reflect changing phenotypes of these innate immune populations, consistent with their established roles in MS and EAE pathophysiology. The inclusion of adaptive immune cells, including T cell subsets that mirror those found in target organs, further enhances the system's relevance for studying antigen-specific immune responses.

While the current implementation focuses on EAE as a model for MS, the fundamental principles of the IN system are broadly applicable to other organ-specific autoimmune diseases. By selecting appropriate disease models and performing similar transcriptomic analyses, gene signatures could be developed for conditions such as type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, or inflammatory bowel disease. The modular nature of the system—combining a universal scaffold platform with disease-specific molecular signatures—enables adaptation to diverse clinical contexts while maintaining the core advantages of accessibility, repeatability, and tissue-relevant immunological profiling.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Microporous poly(ε-caprolactone) (PCL) scaffolds were fabricated according to established protocols involving salt leaching and polymer sintering techniques. Female SJL/J mice aged six weeks were obtained from commercial suppliers and housed under specific pathogen-free conditions with controlled light-dark cycles. Mice were anesthetized using isoflurane inhalation, and PCL scaffolds measuring approximately 5 mm in diameter and 2 mm in thickness were implanted subcutaneously in the dorsal region using standard aseptic surgical techniques. Post-operative analgesia was provided with carprofen administered subcutaneously at 5 mg/kg immediately before surgery and again 24 hours post-operatively. Animals were allowed to recover for 14 days to permit complete vascularization and maturation of the immunological niche before experimental manipulation.

Experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE) was induced using the adoptive transfer model, wherein donor SJL/J mice were first immunized with proteolipid protein peptide PLP139–151 emulsified in complete Freund's adjuvant containing heat-killed Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Ten days post-immunization, donor mice were euthanized, and spleens along with inguinal, axillary, and brachial lymph nodes were harvested to prepare single-cell suspensions. Cells were cultured in RPMI medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, antibiotics, and 20 µg/mL PLP139–151 peptide for three days to expand encephalitogenic T cells. Non-adherent cells were collected, counted, and 30 million cells per recipient mouse were injected intraperitoneally to induce EAE. Control mice received equivalent numbers of T cells reactive to ovalbumin peptide OVA323–339 prepared using identical protocols.

Clinical disease severity was monitored daily using a standardized 0–5 scoring system: 0 = no disease; 1 = limp tail or hindlimb weakness; 2 = limp tail and hindlimb weakness; 3 = partial hindlimb paralysis; 4 = complete hindlimb paralysis; 5 = moribund requiring euthanasia. Immunological niches were harvested at predetermined time points corresponding to presymptomatic (day 7 post-transfer) and symptomatic (day 9 post-transfer) phases of disease. Biopsy procedures involved brief isoflurane anesthesia, small incision over the implant site, careful excision of the scaffold with adherent tissue, and closure with sutures. Harvested tissues were immediately processed for either RNA extraction (flash-frozen in isopentane on dry ice) or flow cytometry (placed in cold PBS).

High-throughput gene expression analysis was performed using OpenArray technology with a mouse inflammation-focused panel containing 632 validated genes plus 16 endogenous controls. RNA integrity was confirmed with RIN values exceeding 8.0 for all samples. cDNA synthesis was performed using standardized protocols, and OpenArray analysis was conducted by a core facility using robotic liquid handling and QuantStudio 12k Flex RT-PCR systems. Data analysis revealed 130 differentially expressed genes between control and EAE mice when pooling both time points (n=8 per group, p<0.05). Application of elastic net regularization combined with fold change and expression stability criteria identified a refined 21-gene signature that effectively discriminated diseased from healthy states. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering demonstrated clear separation between control and EAE samples, while radar plots showed similar expression patterns in both presymptomatic and symptomatic EAE mice that differed markedly from controls.

### Example 2

The 21-gene signature identified in Example 1 was validated using independent cohorts of mice and alternative analytical platforms. Replication studies employed custom 384-well plate quantitative PCR assays using the same TaqMan probes utilized in the original OpenArray analysis. Samples were processed in randomized order with alternating placement of healthy and diseased specimens to minimize batch effects. Each 384-well plate included reference samples from the original OpenArray dataset to enable cross-plate normalization and correction for technical variability. RNA concentrations were standardized to 200 ng/µL for cDNA synthesis, with blood samples requiring additional concentration steps using RNA clean-up kits to achieve sufficient material.

Computational scoring systems were developed using both unsupervised and supervised approaches. Singular value decomposition (SVD) was applied to the normalized gene expression matrix to extract the dominant pattern of variation across samples, with the first principal component serving as the SVD score. Bootstrap aggregated decision trees (Bagged Trees) were trained using MATLAB's fitcensemble function with the Bag method, employing leave-one-out cross-validation to optimize parameters and prevent overfitting. Both scoring methods demonstrated excellent discrimination between healthy and diseased samples, with clear separation evident when plotting SVD scores against Bagged Tree scores. Notably, presymptomatic EAE mice (day 7) exhibited high signature scores despite absence of clinical symptoms, confirming the system's ability to detect subclinical disease activity.

Additional validation included comparison with completely naive mice that received neither adoptive transfer nor any immunological manipulation. These naive controls clustered tightly with OVA-control mice in both hierarchical clustering and scoring analyses, confirming that the observed gene expression changes were specifically associated with autoimmune disease rather than general inflammatory responses to the adoptive transfer procedure itself. Statistical analysis using two-way ANOVA confirmed significant differences between pooled control and EAE groups regardless of time point (p<0.001 for both SVD and Bagged Tree scores), with post-hoc testing revealing significant differences at both day 7 and day 9 time points individually.

### Example 3

The active immunization model of EAE was employed to demonstrate broader applicability of the immunological niche approach across different disease induction paradigms. Female SJL/J mice with mature immunological niches (14 days post-implantation) were immunized subcutaneously at three sites with 100 µL total volume of complete Freund's adjuvant emulsion containing 2 mg/mL PLP139–151 peptide. Control mice received identical immunizations lacking the PLP peptide. Disease monitoring began immediately post-immunization, with clinical scoring performed daily using the same 0–5 scale as in Example 1. Due to the slightly slower disease course in this model compared to adoptive transfer, time points for IN harvesting were adjusted accordingly: asymptomatic samples were collected at day 7 post-immunization, while symptomatic samples were obtained at day 13 to ensure robust disease development.

OpenArray analysis of IN samples from actively immunized mice revealed 222 differentially expressed genes between control and EAE groups (n=8 control, n=16 EAE across both time points). Application of the same gene selection criteria used in Example 1 (elastic net regularization, fold change >1.5, p<0.05) yielded a 25-gene signature specific to the active immunization model. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering demonstrated clear separation between healthy and diseased samples, with four genes overlapping between the active immunization and adoptive transfer signatures: CD163, Il1b, Il1f9, and S100a9. These shared genes likely represent core inflammatory pathways common to both disease induction methods, while the model-specific genes reflect differences in immunological mechanisms between active priming versus passive transfer of effector cells.

Computational scoring systems analogous to those developed in Example 2 were created for the active immunization signature, demonstrating significant discrimination between control and EAE mice regardless of time point (p<0.001 by two-way ANOVA). The presence of overlapping genes between the two models suggests potential for developing a universal EAE signature that captures fundamental disease mechanisms while accommodating model-specific variations. This finding has important implications for translational applications, as human MS likely involves elements of both active immune priming and effector cell trafficking, making a composite signature potentially more robust than model-specific versions.

### Example 4

Comprehensive immunophenotyping of cellular populations within immunological niches was performed using multicolor flow cytometry to characterize the immune cell composition and identify potential cellular sources of the observed gene expression changes. IN samples from adoptively transferred mice were harvested at days 7, 9, and 21 post-transfer and processed for flow cytometric analysis using established protocols involving mechanical mincing and enzymatic digestion with Liberase TL. Single-cell suspensions were stained with fluorescently conjugated antibodies against a panel of surface markers to identify major immune cell subsets: CD45+ (pan-leukocyte), CD11b+ F4/80+ (tissue-resident macrophages), CD11b+ Ly6C+ F4/80− (inflammatory monocytes), CD11c+ (dendritic cells), CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, CD19+ B cells, and CD49b+ NK cells.

Analysis revealed that the majority of CD45+ cells within INs were innate immune cells, consistent with the inflammatory nature of the engineered tissue compartment. Surprisingly, comparison between healthy (OVA-control) and diseased (PLP-EAE) mice showed only minor differences in overall immune cell proportions, with statistically significant changes limited to slight increases in CD4+ T cells at days 7 and 21 and CD8+ T cells at day 9. These findings suggested that the dramatic gene expression changes observed in Example 1 were not primarily driven by alterations in cell trafficking or population frequencies, but rather reflected functional phenotypic changes within existing cell populations in response to systemic disease activity.

Parallel analysis of blood and spleen samples from the same mice revealed more pronounced differences between healthy and diseased states, particularly increased frequencies of Ly6C+ F4/80− inflammatory monocytes in blood and spleen at day 13 post-immunization in the active disease model. However, these circulating changes were not mirrored in the IN, further supporting the hypothesis that the IN captures tissue-specific immunological events rather than simply reflecting systemic immune activation. The predominance of innate immune cells within the IN, particularly dendritic cells and macrophages known to play critical roles in EAE pathogenesis, positions the niche as an ideal platform for studying the functional states of these disease-relevant cell populations in vivo.

### Example 5

Detailed comparison of T cell subset distributions was performed across multiple tissue compartments to evaluate the IN's ability to recapitulate immunological changes occurring in target organs versus peripheral lymphoid tissues. At day 9 post-adoptive transfer, samples were simultaneously harvested from INs, spinal cords (target organ), inguinal lymph nodes, and spleens for comprehensive T cell phenotyping. Five functionally distinct CD4+ T cell subsets were identified using intracellular cytokine staining and transcription factor analysis: regulatory T cells (Treg: CD25+ FoxP3+ CD127−), Th2 cells (IL-4+), Th1 cells (IFNγ+ IL-17−), Th1/17 cells (IFNγ+ IL-17+), and Th17 cells (IFNγ− IL-17+).

Quantitative analysis revealed that INs and spinal cords contained similar absolute numbers of T cells, both being approximately one order of magnitude lower than lymph nodes and spleens, consistent with their non-lymphoid nature. Critically, both INs and spinal cords from EAE mice showed significantly elevated frequencies of Th1 cells compared to OVA-controls, while lymph nodes and spleens showed no significant differences between groups. This finding demonstrates that the IN faithfully mirrors disease-associated changes in T cell polarization occurring within the actual target organ, whereas conventional peripheral immune monitoring sites fail to capture these tissue-specific immunological events.

The ability of the IN to reflect target organ immunology has profound implications for clinical monitoring applications. Current practice relies heavily on blood and sometimes cerebrospinal fluid analysis, both of which may miss critical immunological changes occurring within affected tissues. The IN provides direct access to disease-relevant immune cells within an inflammatory microenvironment that recapitulates key aspects of the pathological tissue context, offering a more accurate window into the immunological processes driving tissue damage. This feature makes the IN particularly valuable for studying the roles of specific T cell subsets in disease pathogenesis and for evaluating how therapeutic interventions modulate these populations in vivo.

### Example 6

Direct comparison between immunological niche and peripheral blood gene expression profiles was conducted to evaluate the relative merits of each monitoring approach. Blood samples were collected via intracardiac puncture from symptomatic EAE mice (day 10 post-transfer) and OVA-controls, with red blood cells lysed using ACK solution before RNA extraction. The same 21-gene signature panel identified in Example 1 was analyzed in both blood and IN samples using identical qPCR protocols and normalization procedures.

Results demonstrated stark differences between the two compartments. While IN samples showed clear separation between healthy and diseased states with consistent directional changes across all signature genes, blood samples exhibited minimal differential expression with poor reproducibility. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of blood gene expression data failed to stratify diseased from healthy samples, with approximately 18% of samples yielding invalid readings for key signature genes. Several genes, including Ereg, were undetectable in most blood samples, highlighting the technical limitations of blood-based monitoring for low-abundance transcripts.

Radar plots comparing log2 fold changes between compartments revealed that blood gene expression patterns bore little resemblance to those observed in INs, confirming that these monitoring sites provide fundamentally different information. The superior performance of the IN likely stems from its enrichment for disease-relevant immune cells within an inflammatory tissue context, whereas blood contains a heterogeneous mixture of cells most of which are not directly involved in CNS-directed autoimmunity. These findings validate the IN as a superior monitoring platform that captures tissue-specific immunological events invisible to conventional blood-based approaches.

### Example 7

The dynamic responsiveness of immunological niches to changing disease states was evaluated through a relapse-remitting EAE model designed to mimic the clinical course of human multiple sclerosis. Mice with mature INs received adoptive transfer of PLP139–151-reactive T cells to induce initial disease episode, followed by natural progression toward symptomatic remission. IN biopsies were obtained at day 21 post-transfer during the remission phase, after which mice received a second adoptive transfer of T cells reactive to a different immunodominant epitope (PLP178–191) to simulate epitope spreading and induce clinical relapse. A second IN biopsy was obtained at day 28 post-initial transfer during the relapse phase.

Gene expression analysis of the 21-gene signature revealed remarkable temporal dynamics corresponding to disease phases. During remission (day 21), IN gene expression profiles closely resembled those of healthy controls, with radar plots showing minimal deviation from baseline. In contrast, during relapse (day 28), gene expression patterns reverted to those observed during initial disease onset, with similar directional changes across all signature genes. Computational scoring using both SVD and Bagged Tree methods confirmed these observations, with remission samples clustering with healthy controls and relapse samples grouping with initial disease onset specimens.

Statistical analysis using one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc testing demonstrated that signature scores during remission were significantly lower than during peak disease (day 9), while relapse scores were comparable to initial disease onset. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis for distinguishing remission from relapse samples yielded area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.81–0.90 depending on the scoring metric used, confirming the system's diagnostic efficacy for monitoring disease dynamics. These findings establish the IN as a sensitive and reliable tool for tracking disease activity through different clinical phases, providing objective molecular correlates for treatment decisions.

### Example 8

Preemptive therapeutic intervention guided by immunological niche molecular signatures was evaluated using two distinct treatment modalities: systemic glucocorticoid therapy representing standard-of-care treatment and antigen-specific tolerogenic nanoparticle therapy representing an emerging investigational approach. Mice with mature INs received adoptive transfer of PLP139–151-reactive T cells, followed by IN biopsy at day 7 post-transfer (presymptomatic phase) for gene expression analysis. Based on elevated signature scores indicating subclinical disease activity, mice were randomly assigned to receive either daily intraperitoneal injections of 5 mg/kg dexamethasone from days 7–11 or a single intravenous injection of 2.5 mg PLP139–151-encapsulating poly(lactide-co-glycolide) (PLG) nanoparticles.

Clinical monitoring revealed that both preemptive treatments significantly reduced disease severity compared to untreated controls (two-way ANOVA, p=0.0034). Dexamethasone treatment prevented disease onset through day 14, after which clinical scores began to rise but remained significantly lower than controls through day 18. Nanoparticle treatment demonstrated even more sustained protection, with complete prevention of clinical disease through day 18 following just a single administration. These results demonstrate that early intervention based on IN-predicted disease activity can substantially modify disease course, with the investigational nanoparticle therapy showing particular promise for long-term disease prevention with minimal treatment burden.

The success of preemptive intervention highlights the transformative potential of the IN system for clinical practice. Current MS management is fundamentally reactive, initiating treatment only after clinical relapses occur or new MRI lesions appear—by which time irreversible neurological damage may have already occurred. The IN enables a proactive approach wherein treatment is administered during the subclinical phase when immunological dysregulation is detectable but before tissue damage manifests clinically. This paradigm shift could dramatically improve long-term outcomes by preventing cumulative disability rather than merely slowing its progression.

### Example 9

Therapeutic response monitoring using immunological niches was evaluated by comparing effective versus ineffective treatment scenarios in the adoptive transfer EAE model. Mice with mature INs received adoptive transfer of PLP139–151-reactive T cells, followed by intravenous administration of 2.5 mg nanoparticles at day 2 post-transfer. Three experimental groups were established: (1) control group receiving OVA-reactive T cells plus PLP-loaded nanoparticles; (2) effective treatment group receiving PLP-reactive T cells plus PLP-loaded nanoparticles; and (3) ineffective treatment group receiving PLP-reactive T cells plus OVA-loaded nanoparticles (antigen-mismatched control).

IN biopsies were obtained at day 9 post-transfer (disease onset phase) for gene expression analysis of the 21-gene signature. Results demonstrated that the effective treatment group exhibited signature scores and clinical scores comparable to healthy controls, while the ineffective treatment group showed significantly elevated scores similar to untreated diseased mice. Radar plots revealed that gene expression patterns in the effective treatment group closely matched those of controls, whereas the ineffective treatment group displayed the characteristic disease-associated signature.

Computational scoring using both SVD and Bagged Tree methods confirmed these findings, with clear separation between responders (effective treatment) and non-responders (ineffective treatment) when plotted against historical confidence intervals derived from disease onset data. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis for treatment monitoring yielded exceptional diagnostic performance, with AUC values of 0.97–1.00 depending on the scoring metric used. These results establish the IN as a highly sensitive and specific tool for rapidly assessing therapeutic efficacy, potentially enabling treatment adjustments within days rather than weeks or months required by conventional monitoring approaches.

The ability to distinguish effective from ineffective treatments has profound implications for clinical practice, where current disease-modifying therapies for MS show substantial inter-individual variability in response rates. The IN could serve as a companion diagnostic to guide personalized treatment selection, identifying non-responders early in the treatment course before unnecessary exposure to ineffective therapies and their associated side effects. Furthermore, the system could accelerate drug development by providing sensitive pharmacodynamic biomarkers for evaluating novel therapeutic candidates in preclinical and early-phase clinical trials.
