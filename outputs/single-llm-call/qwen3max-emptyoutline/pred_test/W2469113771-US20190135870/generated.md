# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of viral vector technology, and more specifically to recombinant adeno-associated virus (rAAV) vectors derived from endogenous viral elements. The invention provides novel rAAV vectors based on a reconstructed ancestral marsupial adeno-associated virus capsid protein, designated mAAV-EVE1, which was identified within the genomes of macropodoid marsupials as an endogenized viral element. The reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid exhibits unique structural features that confer distinct biological properties compared to known AAV serotypes, including altered tropism, enhanced transduction efficiency in specific tissues, and reduced immunogenicity. The invention further encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid proteins, expression vectors containing these nucleic acids, host cells engineered to produce rAAV particles incorporating the mAAV-EVE1 capsid, methods for producing such rAAV particles, and pharmaceutical compositions comprising these rAAV vectors for use in gene therapy applications. Additionally, the invention includes methods for utilizing the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 genome and its components for studying viral evolution, understanding host-virus interactions over geological timescales, and developing novel diagnostic tools based on the lineage-specific presence of the mAAV-EVE1 locus in macropodoid species.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) are small, non-enveloped, single-stranded DNA viruses belonging to the genus Dependoparvovirus within the family Parvoviridae. Naturally occurring AAVs require co-infection with a helper virus, typically adenovirus or herpesvirus, for productive replication in host cells. In the absence of a helper virus, wild-type AAV can establish latency by integrating into a specific site on human chromosome 19 (AAVS1). This unique property, combined with their non-pathogenic nature in humans and ability to infect both dividing and non-dividing cells, has made AAVs attractive candidates for gene delivery vectors in therapeutic applications. Recombinant AAV (rAAV) vectors have been engineered by replacing the viral rep and cap genes with a transgene expression cassette flanked by inverted terminal repeats (ITRs), which are the only viral sequences required for packaging and genome replication.

Over the past several decades, numerous naturally occurring AAV serotypes have been isolated from various mammalian and avian species, each exhibiting distinct tissue tropisms and transduction efficiencies. These differences are primarily determined by variations in the viral capsid proteins, particularly in surface-exposed variable regions (VRs) that mediate receptor binding and cellular entry. Despite extensive efforts to engineer improved AAV vectors through rational design, directed evolution, and capsid shuffling, there remains a need for novel capsid variants with enhanced properties for specific therapeutic applications. The discovery and characterization of new AAV variants from diverse species continues to expand the repertoire of available vectors, but the natural diversity accessible through contemporary isolates is inherently limited by the relatively recent evolutionary history of extant viruses.

Endogenous viral elements (EVEs) represent genomic fossils of ancient viral infections that became integrated into the germline of host organisms and were subsequently inherited vertically through generations. These EVEs provide valuable insights into the long-term evolutionary history of viruses and their hosts, often revealing viral lineages that have since gone extinct or diverged beyond recognition in contemporary populations. While retroviral EVEs are well-documented due to the natural integration step in their replication cycle, non-retroviral EVEs, including those derived from DNA viruses like parvoviruses, have also been identified in various eukaryotic genomes. These non-retroviral EVEs typically result from rare, non-canonical integration events that occurred millions of years ago, followed by genetic drift and accumulation of mutations that render them non-functional for viral production.

Recent advances in comparative genomics and phylogenetic analysis have enabled the reconstruction of ancestral viral sequences from collections of related EVEs found across different host species. By aligning orthologous EVE sequences from multiple descendants of a common ancestor and applying maximum likelihood algorithms, it is possible to infer the most probable nucleotide sequence of the original exogenous virus that gave rise to the integrated elements. This approach effectively extends the temporal reach of virology far beyond what is possible through direct isolation of contemporary viral strains, potentially uncovering viral variants with unique properties shaped by ancient selective pressures.

The identification of an endogenous AAV element, designated mAAV-EVE1, within the genomes of macropodoid marsupials represents a particularly valuable opportunity for such ancestral reconstruction. This EVE is present in all members of the Macropodiformes suborder (including kangaroos, wallabies, bettongs, potoroos, and the musky rat-kangaroo) but absent from other diprotodontian marsupials, indicating integration into the germline of their most recent common ancestor approximately 27-45 million years ago during the Eocene-Oligocene transition. The mAAV-EVE1 locus contains remnants of both rep and cap genes, albeit with numerous frameshifts, stop codons, and internal deletions that accumulated during millions of years of neutral evolution. Through careful phylogenetic analysis and sequence reconstruction, it is possible to infer the likely coding sequences of the original exogenous marsupial AAV that became endogenized, thereby recovering functional viral proteins that have not existed in nature for tens of millions of years.

The reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid protein demonstrates remarkable structural conservation with contemporary AAV capsids despite the vast evolutionary timescale, maintaining the characteristic β-barrel core structure and surface topology features including depressions at the 2-fold axis, protrusions around the 3-fold axis, and channels at the 5-fold axis. However, significant differences are observed in specific variable regions, particularly VR-VII, which is substantially larger in mAAV-EVE1 compared to known AAV serotypes. These structural differences are expected to translate into distinct biological properties, including altered receptor binding specificity, modified intracellular trafficking pathways, and potentially reduced recognition by pre-existing neutralizing antibodies in human populations. Furthermore, the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 Rep protein retains key functional domains including the N-terminal nuclease domain with conserved rolling circle replication motifs and the central helicase domain with Walker motifs, though critical catalytic residues have been substituted, likely reflecting selective pressure against potentially deleterious activity of endogenized viral proteins.

The development of rAAV vectors based on this reconstructed ancestral marsupial AAV capsid addresses several limitations of current AAV-based gene therapy platforms. First, the ancient origin of mAAV-EVE1 ensures minimal sequence homology with contemporary human AAVs, potentially reducing immune recognition and enabling effective transduction in patients with pre-existing immunity to common AAV serotypes. Second, the unique surface topology conferred by the enlarged VR-VII region may facilitate binding to novel cellular receptors or enhance transduction efficiency in specific target tissues relevant to various genetic disorders. Third, the successful reconstruction and functional validation of this ancient viral capsid demonstrates the feasibility of mining endogenous viral elements from diverse host genomes as a source of novel vector backbones with desirable properties for therapeutic applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Vectors

The present invention provides novel vector systems based on the reconstructed ancestral marsupial adeno-associated virus capsid protein, designated mAAV-EVE1. These vectors are designed to overcome limitations associated with current AAV-based gene delivery platforms by leveraging the unique structural and functional properties of this ancient viral capsid. The vector systems encompass both the nucleic acid molecules encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid proteins and the complete recombinant adeno-associated virus (rAAV) particles incorporating these capsids for gene delivery applications.

The nucleic acid molecules of the invention comprise nucleotide sequences encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid proteins, including the full-length VP1 protein and its truncated variants VP2 and VP3. These nucleotide sequences have been reconstructed through phylogenetic analysis of orthologous endogenous viral elements (EVEs) found in the genomes of sixteen macropodoid marsupial species. The reconstruction process involved maximum likelihood inference using a multiple sequence alignment of the mAAV-EVE1 loci, followed by manual correction of frameshifts, nonsense codons, and indels based on conservation patterns observed in extant AAV proteins. The resulting nucleotide sequence represents the most probable coding sequence of the original exogenous marsupial AAV that became endogenized approximately 27-45 million years ago.

The reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid nucleotide sequence encodes a VP1-like protein with a predicted molecular weight of 82.2 kDa, containing all essential structural domains required for proper capsid assembly and function. Notably, the sequence includes a conserved phospholipase A2 (PLA2) domain near the N-terminus, which is critical for parvovirus infectivity, although the otherwise invariant histidine residue within the catalytic site has been substituted with glutamine. The VP3 equivalent, representing the common region shared by all capsid proteins, has a predicted molecular weight of 60.9 kDa and maintains the characteristic β-barrel core structure observed in contemporary AAV capsids. The nucleotide sequence also contains an embedded open reading frame (ORF) encoding a potential assembly-activating protein (AAP) homolog, initiated at a non-canonical CTG codon and encoding a 201-amino acid protein with conserved hydrophobic, proline-rich, and serine/threonine-rich regions characteristic of AAP family members.

Expression vectors containing the mAAV-EVE1 capsid nucleotide sequences are constructed by operably linking these sequences to appropriate regulatory elements that drive high-level expression in mammalian host cells. Typically, the capsid coding sequence is placed under the control of a strong constitutive promoter such as the cytomegalovirus (CMV) immediate-early promoter, though inducible or tissue-specific promoters may be employed depending on the production system requirements. The expression cassette includes necessary transcriptional and translational regulatory elements, including a polyadenylation signal sequence (such as SV40 or bovine growth hormone polyA) and a Kozak consensus sequence surrounding the start codon to ensure efficient translation initiation. Additional elements may include introns to enhance mRNA processing and stability, as well as selectable marker genes for stable cell line generation.

For rAAV production, the mAAV-EVE1 capsid expression vector is used in conjunction with two additional plasmids: a rep expression plasmid containing the AAV rep genes (typically from AAV2) under the control of appropriate regulatory elements, and a vector plasmid containing the transgene expression cassette flanked by AAV inverted terminal repeats (ITRs). The ITRs may be derived from any AAV serotype, though AAV2 ITRs are commonly used due to their well-characterized structure and function. The transgene expression cassette includes a promoter appropriate for the target tissue or cell type, the therapeutic gene or nucleic acid sequence of interest, and necessary regulatory elements for proper expression. The three-plasmid system enables efficient production of rAAV particles through standard triple-transfection protocols in permissive host cells such as HEK293 cells.

Alternative vector systems include single-plasmid constructs that combine the rep and cap genes on the same vector, separated by an internal ribosome entry site (IRES) or 2A self-cleaving peptide sequence to enable co-expression from a single promoter. Baculovirus-based expression systems may also be employed, where the mAAV-EVE1 cap gene, rep genes, and ITR-flanked transgene are each cloned into separate baculovirus transfer vectors and used to generate recombinant baculoviruses for infection of insect cells. This approach offers advantages in scalability and consistency for large-scale rAAV production.

The vector systems of the invention may be further modified through rational design or directed evolution approaches to optimize specific properties such as transduction efficiency, tissue tropism, or immune evasion. For example, targeted mutagenesis of specific residues within the enlarged VR-VII region may be performed to fine-tune receptor binding specificity or enhance transduction in particular target tissues. Similarly, incorporation of point mutations known to reduce recognition by neutralizing antibodies could further improve the therapeutic potential of these vectors in clinical settings.

### Recombinant AAV

The recombinant adeno-associated virus (rAAV) particles of the present invention incorporate the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid proteins and demonstrate unique biological properties compared to rAAV vectors based on contemporary AAV serotypes. These rAAV particles are produced through standard methodologies involving co-expression of the mAAV-EVE1 capsid proteins, AAV Rep proteins, and an ITR-flanked transgene expression cassette in permissive host cells. The resulting viral particles maintain the characteristic physical properties of AAV virions, including a diameter of approximately 22 nm, icosahedral symmetry, and the ability to package single-stranded DNA genomes of up to approximately 4.7 kb in length.

The mAAV-EVE1 capsid exhibits several distinctive structural features that differentiate it from known AAV serotypes and contribute to its unique functional properties. Three-dimensional modeling of the VP3 monomer based on the AAV8 crystal structure reveals conservation of the fundamental β-barrel core architecture, including β-strand A (βA), the eight-stranded β-barrel core (βBIDG-CHEF), and α-helix A (αA). However, significant differences are observed in the surface-exposed variable regions (VRs), particularly VR-VII, which is substantially larger in mAAV-EVE1 compared to AAV2 and AAV8. This enlarged VR-VII extends from the base of the 3-fold protrusions into the depression surrounding the 5-fold channel, creating a unique surface topology that is expected to influence receptor binding specificity and cellular entry mechanisms.

The assembled mAAV-EVE1 capsid, constructed from 60 copies of the VP3 common region, maintains the characteristic surface features of AAV capsids, including a depression at the icosahedral 2-fold axis, three protrusions surrounding the icosahedral 3-fold axis, a channel at the icosahedral 5-fold axis, and an HI loop lining the depression surrounding the 5-fold channel. However, the clustering of VRs on the mAAV-EVE1 capsid surface creates local topological differences compared to other AAVs, which may translate into altered biological functions including receptor attachment, intracellular trafficking phenotypes, transduction efficiency, and antigenic reactivity. These structural differences are particularly significant given that VRs are known to control several critical AAV functions and serve as primary determinants of serotype-specific properties.

The rAAV particles incorporating mAAV-EVE1 capsids demonstrate several advantageous properties for gene therapy applications. First, the ancient evolutionary origin of mAAV-EVE1 ensures minimal sequence homology with contemporary human AAVs, potentially reducing recognition by pre-existing neutralizing antibodies in patient populations. This property is particularly valuable given the high prevalence of neutralizing antibodies against common AAV serotypes in human populations, which can significantly limit the efficacy of current rAAV-based therapies. Second, the unique surface topology conferred by the enlarged VR-VII region may facilitate binding to novel cellular receptors or enhance transduction efficiency in specific target tissues that are poorly transduced by existing AAV serotypes. Third, the successful production and functionality of rAAV particles incorporating this reconstructed ancient capsid demonstrates the feasibility of mining endogenous viral elements from diverse host genomes as a source of novel vector backbones with desirable properties.

The rAAV particles of the invention may be purified using standard methodologies including cesium chloride gradient centrifugation, iodixanol gradient centrifugation, or chromatographic techniques such as ion-exchange or affinity chromatography. Purified viral preparations are characterized for titer, purity, and functionality using established assays including quantitative PCR for genome titer determination, SDS-PAGE and Western blotting for capsid protein composition analysis, and in vitro transduction assays in relevant cell types to assess functional activity. The physical stability of the mAAV-EVE1-based rAAV particles is expected to be comparable to that of contemporary AAV serotypes, with appropriate storage conditions maintaining infectivity for extended periods.

### Host Cells

The present invention provides host cells engineered to express the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid proteins for the production of recombinant adeno-associated virus (rAAV) particles. These host cells serve as the foundation for scalable rAAV manufacturing processes and may be derived from various mammalian or insect cell lines commonly used in viral vector production. The choice of host cell system depends on factors including production scale requirements, regulatory considerations, and the specific characteristics desired in the final rAAV product.

Mammalian host cells represent the most commonly used platform for rAAV production and include human embryonic kidney (HEK) 293 cells, HeLa cells, and various other transformed cell lines capable of supporting AAV replication and assembly. HEK293 cells are particularly favored due to their high transfection efficiency, robust growth characteristics, and ability to provide adenoviral helper functions through expression of the adenoviral E1A and E1B genes integrated into their genome. For transient production systems, HEK293 cells are typically transfected with three plasmids: a mAAV-EVE1 cap expression plasmid, a rep expression plasmid (usually containing AAV2 rep genes), and a vector plasmid containing the ITR-flanked transgene expression cassette. Alternative mammalian cell lines such as HeLa, BHK, or CHO cells may also be employed, though they typically require co-transfection with additional helper plasmids providing adenoviral or herpesviral helper functions.

Stable producer cell lines offer advantages in terms of consistency, scalability, and reduced regulatory complexity compared to transient transfection systems. These cell lines are generated by integrating the mAAV-EVE1 cap genes and AAV rep genes into the host cell genome under the control of inducible or constitutive promoters. Common strategies include using the tetracycline-inducible system, where cap and rep expression can be tightly controlled through addition of doxycycline, or employing constitutive promoters with careful selection of integration sites to minimize cytotoxicity associated with continuous Rep protein expression. Once established, stable producer cell lines require only transfection or infection with the ITR-flanked transgene plasmid to initiate rAAV production, simplifying the manufacturing process and improving batch-to-batch consistency.

Insect cell-based production systems utilizing baculovirus expression vectors represent another important platform for rAAV manufacturing, particularly for large-scale clinical and commercial production. In this system, Spodoptera frugiperda (Sf9) or Trichoplusia ni (High Five) insect cells are infected with three recombinant baculoviruses: one expressing the mAAV-EVE1 cap genes, one expressing the AAV rep genes, and one containing the ITR-flanked transgene expression cassette. The baculovirus system offers several advantages including high yields, scalability in suspension culture, and the ability to produce rAAV particles with consistent quality attributes. Additionally, insect cells perform many post-translational modifications similar to mammalian cells while avoiding potential contamination with human pathogens.

The host cells of the invention may be further engineered to optimize rAAV production efficiency and product quality. For example, cells may be modified to overexpress chaperone proteins that assist in proper capsid assembly, or to knock down cellular factors that interfere with AAV replication or assembly. Metabolic engineering approaches may be employed to enhance cellular energy production and protein synthesis capacity during the production phase. Additionally, host cells may be engineered to express specific proteases or other processing enzymes that improve the maturation or stability of the final rAAV product.

Quality control of engineered host cells is critical for ensuring consistent rAAV production and includes verification of transgene integration sites, copy number, and expression levels through techniques such as Southern blotting, quantitative PCR, and flow cytometry. Stability testing is performed to confirm that the engineered characteristics are maintained over multiple passages, and mycoplasma and adventitious agent testing ensures the safety of the final rAAV product. Cryopreservation protocols are established to maintain master and working cell banks for consistent production across multiple manufacturing campaigns.

### Compositions and Methods

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising recombinant adeno-associated virus (rAAV) particles incorporating the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid proteins, along with methods for their preparation, administration, and use in therapeutic applications. These compositions are formulated to ensure stability, potency, and safety during storage, handling, and administration to patients, while the associated methods enable efficient production, purification, and quality control of the rAAV vectors.

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention contain rAAV particles incorporating mAAV-EVE1 capsids at concentrations sufficient to achieve therapeutic effects in target tissues following administration. The rAAV particles are suspended in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier that maintains viral stability and infectivity while being compatible with the intended route of administration. Common formulation components include buffered saline solutions (such as phosphate-buffered saline or Tris-buffered saline) adjusted to physiological pH, stabilizers (such as sugars, amino acids, or polymers), and surfactants (such as polysorbate 80) to prevent aggregation and adsorption to container surfaces. The final formulation is typically sterile-filtered through 0.22 μm filters and filled into appropriate containers under aseptic conditions.

The concentration of rAAV particles in the pharmaceutical composition is carefully controlled to achieve optimal therapeutic outcomes while minimizing potential adverse effects. Typical doses range from 1×10^10 to 1×10^14 vector genomes per kilogram of body weight, depending on the target tissue, disease indication, and specific transgene being delivered. The composition may be lyophilized for long-term storage and reconstituted immediately prior to administration, though liquid formulations stored at -80°C are more commonly used for clinical applications. Stability studies are conducted to determine appropriate shelf life and storage conditions, with real-time and accelerated stability testing providing data on viral titer maintenance, capsid integrity, and transgene expression capability over time.

Methods for administering the pharmaceutical compositions include various routes depending on the target tissue and disease indication. Systemic administration via intravenous injection enables widespread distribution throughout the body and is suitable for treating systemic disorders or targeting multiple organ systems. Local administration routes include intramuscular injection for muscle-directed therapies, intrathecal injection for central nervous system disorders, intraocular injection for retinal diseases, and direct injection into specific organs or tissues for localized treatment. The choice of administration route is guided by preclinical studies evaluating biodistribution, transduction efficiency, and safety profiles in relevant animal models.

Therapeutic applications of the mAAV-EVE1-based rAAV compositions encompass a wide range of genetic disorders and acquired diseases amenable to gene therapy approaches. Monogenic disorders such as hemophilia, lysosomal storage diseases, neuromuscular disorders, and inherited retinal dystrophies represent prime targets due to their well-defined genetic basis and the potential for long-term correction through stable transgene expression. Acquired conditions including cardiovascular diseases, neurodegenerative disorders, and certain cancers may also benefit from rAAV-mediated delivery of therapeutic genes, RNA interference molecules, or gene editing components.

The methods of the invention further include comprehensive quality control procedures to ensure the safety, purity, and potency of the final rAAV product. Analytical methods include quantitative PCR or digital droplet PCR for accurate determination of vector genome titer, ELISA or mass spectrometry for capsid protein quantification, and next-generation sequencing for confirmation of transgene sequence integrity. Functional assays evaluate transduction efficiency in relevant cell types, while safety testing includes sterility testing, endotoxin testing, and adventitious agent screening. Potency assays measure the biological activity of the rAAV preparation, typically through in vitro transduction followed by quantification of transgene expression or functional correction of disease phenotypes.

### Capsid Diversification

The present invention provides methods for diversifying the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid to generate novel variants with enhanced or modified properties for specific therapeutic applications. These diversification strategies leverage the unique structural features of the mAAV-EVE1 capsid while introducing targeted modifications to optimize performance characteristics such as tissue tropism, transduction efficiency, immune evasion, and manufacturability. The approaches encompass both rational design based on structural insights and high-throughput screening methods to identify beneficial variants from large libraries.

Rational design strategies focus on specific regions of the mAAV-EVE1 capsid known to influence critical functional properties. The enlarged variable region VII (VR-VII), which extends from the base of the 3-fold protrusions into the depression surrounding the 5-fold channel, represents a particularly attractive target for modification due to its distinctive size and position. Targeted mutagenesis of specific residues within VR-VII may be performed to fine-tune receptor binding specificity, enhance interaction with particular cellular entry pathways, or modify surface charge characteristics to influence biodistribution patterns. Similarly, other variable regions (VR-I through VR-IX) may be systematically modified based on homology modeling and comparison with contemporary AAV serotypes to introduce beneficial features while maintaining overall capsid stability.

Directed evolution approaches involve generating large libraries of mAAV-EVE1 capsid variants through random mutagenesis or DNA shuffling techniques, followed by high-throughput screening to identify clones with desired properties. Error-prone PCR can be used to introduce random mutations throughout the cap gene at controlled frequencies, while DNA shuffling between mAAV-EVE1 and other AAV serotypes can create chimeric capsids combining beneficial features from multiple parental strains. These libraries are packaged into rAAV particles and subjected to selection pressures such as incubation with neutralizing antibodies, passage through specific tissue barriers, or transduction of particular cell types under limiting conditions. Surviving variants are recovered through PCR amplification of packaged genomes and subjected to additional rounds of selection to enrich for clones with optimal properties.

Peptide display technologies represent another powerful approach for capsid diversification, where short peptide sequences are inserted into surface-exposed loops of the mAAV-EVE1 capsid to confer novel binding specificities. Common insertion sites include the GH loop between βG and βH strands, the HI loop between βH and βI strands, and other surface-accessible regions identified through structural analysis. Libraries containing billions of unique peptide sequences can be screened against specific target receptors, tissues, or cell types to identify insertions that enhance binding and transduction efficiency. This approach has been successfully used to retarget AAV vectors to specific cell surface markers expressed on diseased tissues while avoiding off-target effects.

Computational design methods complement experimental approaches by predicting beneficial mutations based on structural models and evolutionary conservation patterns. Molecular dynamics simulations can identify flexible regions of the capsid that tolerate modifications without compromising stability, while machine learning algorithms trained on large datasets of AAV variants and their functional properties can predict mutations likely to enhance specific characteristics. These computational predictions guide focused library construction and reduce the experimental burden associated with screening large random libraries.

The diversified mAAV-EVE1 capsid variants generated through these methods are characterized using comprehensive analytical and functional assays to evaluate their performance relative to the parental mAAV-EVE1 capsid and contemporary AAV serotypes. Key parameters include thermal stability, resistance to proteolytic degradation, transduction efficiency in relevant cell types, biodistribution patterns in animal models, and susceptibility to neutralization by human sera. Variants demonstrating superior properties are advanced to preclinical development and ultimately clinical evaluation for specific therapeutic indications.

### Other Uses

Beyond their primary application as gene therapy vectors, the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 genome and its components have several additional uses that extend the utility of this ancient viral sequence. These alternative applications leverage the unique evolutionary history and structural characteristics of mAAV-EVE1 for purposes ranging from basic scientific research to diagnostic tool development and evolutionary studies.

One significant application involves the use of the mAAV-EVE1 locus as a molecular marker for phylogenetic studies and species identification within the Macropodiformes suborder. The presence of this endogenous viral element at an orthologous genomic position in all macropodoid species, coupled with its absence in other diprotodontian marsupials, provides a homoplasy-free derived character that supports the monophyly of Macropodoidea. The mAAV-EVE1 sequences themselves contain sufficient phylogenetic signal to resolve relationships among macropodoid families and genera, serving as an independent dataset that complements traditional mitochondrial and nuclear gene analyses. This molecular marker can be particularly valuable for resolving taxonomic uncertainties in poorly studied or endangered macropodoid species where conventional morphological or genetic data may be limited.

The reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 genome also serves as a valuable resource for studying viral evolution over geological timescales. By comparing the inferred ancestral sequence with contemporary AAV isolates, researchers can gain insights into the structural and functional constraints that have shaped AAV evolution over tens of millions of years. The remarkable conservation of key protein domains despite extensive sequence divergence demonstrates the tight evolutionary constraints imposed by both form and function in viral proteins. Analysis of mutation patterns within the endogenized sequences reveals evidence of selective pressure against potentially deleterious activity of viral proteins, as indicated by the accumulation of inactivating mutations in critical catalytic sites while maintaining overall structural integrity.

The mAAV-EVE1 Rep protein, despite containing substitutions in critical catalytic residues, retains recognizable functional domains including the N-terminal nuclease domain with conserved rolling circle replication motifs and the central helicase domain with Walker motifs. These reconstructed domains provide valuable templates for studying the molecular mechanisms of AAV DNA replication and packaging, potentially revealing ancient variations in these processes that have been lost in contemporary viral lineages. Similarly, the mAAV-EVE1 capsid protein's unique structural features, particularly the enlarged VR-VII region, offer opportunities to investigate the relationship between capsid architecture and biological function in parvoviruses.

The discovery of mAAV-EVE1 also has implications for understanding host-virus co-evolution and the potential role of endogenized viral elements in host adaptation. The timing of the mAAV-EVE1 integration event, coinciding with the Eocene-Oligocene transition and associated climatic changes in Australia, raises intriguing questions about the potential selective advantages conferred by this viral insertion. Hypotheses include protection against related exogenous viruses through expression of interfering viral proteins, contribution to host genetic innovation through exaptation of viral sequences for cellular functions, or facilitation of population bottlenecks that accelerated macropodoid radiation. Testing these hypotheses requires interdisciplinary approaches combining virology, evolutionary biology, and paleoclimatology.

Additionally, the methodology developed for reconstructing the mAAV-EVE1 genome provides a template for similar studies involving other endogenous viral elements across diverse host taxa. The success of this approach demonstrates the feasibility of mining genomic "fossils" to recover functional viral sequences that have not existed in nature for millions of years, potentially uncovering novel viral variants with unique properties for biotechnological applications. This strategy effectively extends the temporal reach of virology far beyond what is possible through direct isolation of contemporary viral strains, opening new avenues for exploring viral diversity and evolution.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Identification and Characterization of mAAV-EVE1 Genome and Proteins

The identification and characterization of the mAAV-EVE1 endogenous viral element began with a systematic screening of marsupial genomic DNA samples using PCR-based approaches targeting conserved regions of the AAV genome. Genomic DNA was extracted from tissue samples of the eastern grey kangaroo (Macropus giganteus) and subjected to PCR amplification using primers recognizing highly conserved sequences within the AAV rep and cap genes. Amplicon sequencing revealed significant sequence similarity to numerous dependoparvovirus genomes, including primate AAV serotypes AAVhu.S17 and AAV13, with E values of 10^-49 and 10^-48, respectively. This initial finding prompted further investigation to isolate the complete endogenous viral sequence through linker-mediated PCR (LM-PCR) using primers specific to the amplified marsupial AAV sequences in combination with linker-specific primers.

The LM-PCR approach was iteratively applied in a "genome walking" strategy to obtain the entirety of the integrated AAV element from M. giganteus genomic DNA. Analysis of the full-length endogenous AAV sequences revealed multiple stop codons and frameshift mutations scattered throughout both the rep and cap genes, consistent with the expected accumulation of deleterious mutations over millions of years of neutral evolution. Notably, the viral inverted terminal repeats (ITRs) were absent from the integrated sequence, suggesting either their loss during the integration process or subsequent deletion during evolutionary drift. Comparison with previously identified AAV-EVEs in the tammar wallaby (Macropus eugenii) genome revealed 96.4% sequence identity, indicating that these elements represent orthologous insertions at the same genomic locus in these closely related macropod species.

To determine the phylogenetic distribution of mAAV-EVE1 among marsupials, PCR primers were designed based on conserved upstream and downstream flanking sequences identified through alignment of genomic regions from M. giganteus, M. eugenii, the Tasmanian devil (Sarcophilus harrisii), and the gray short-tailed opossum (Monodelphis domestica). These primers were used to screen DNA samples representing all three suborders and eight of eleven families within the marsupial order Diprotodontia. The results demonstrated that mAAV-EVE1 is lineage-restricted, occurring exclusively within the suborder Macropodiformes, which includes kangaroos, wallabies, bettongs/potoroos, and the musky rat-kangaroo (Hypsiprymnodon moschatus). The element was detected in all sampled macropodoid species and appeared to be homozygously inherited, while non-macropodoid diprotodontians consistently yielded amplicons representing the uninterrupted pre-integration locus.

Sequencing of cloned PCR amplicons from sixteen macropodoid species revealed two distinct classes of AAV-derived EVEs occupying the mAAV-EVE1 locus: near genome-length elements spanning the entirety of the AAV rep and cap genes (found exclusively in the family Macropodidae) and elements displaying large internal deletions (>1 kbp) recovered from the families Potoroidae and Hypsiprymnodontidae. This pattern indicates that major post-integration deletions occurred at least twice during macropodoid radiation—once after the basal split between Hypsiprymnodontidae and the remainder of Macropodoidea, and again along the phylogenetic branch leading to modern potoroids. The phylogenetic distribution of mAAV-EVE1 among diprotodontians, combined with estimated dates of marsupial speciation events, suggests that the exogenous marsupial AAV became endogenized and genetically fixed in a stem macropodoid population sometime between 45.3 and 27 million years ago during the Eocene-Oligocene transition.

Maximum likelihood sequence reconstruction was performed using a nucleotide alignment of sixteen mAAV-EVE1 loci to infer the ancestral mAAV-EVE1 genomic sequence. The reconstructed sequence resembled contemporary AAV genomes in overall structure, with an average size of approximately 4.4 kbp for full-length virus-derived sequences. The inferred ancestral rep gene contained three frameshift mutations and five nonsense codons, while the cap gene harbored three nonsense codons and two frameshift mutations. Putative regulatory elements were identified, including a TATA box approximately 90 bp upstream of the rep ORF and a polyadenylation signal (AATAAA) about 40 bp downstream of the cap ORF, positioned similarly to those in extant AAV genomes. An additional potential polyadenylation signal was observed between the rep and cap genes, analogous to the signal utilized in AAV5.

Analysis of the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 NS1 (rep) promoter region using transcription factor binding site prediction software revealed substantial similarity to the AAV2 P5 promoter, with 77 shared transcription factor binding site motifs out of approximately 300 total motifs identified in each promoter. Notably, putative binding sites for YY1 and MLTF/USF, factors known to be important for AAV2 P5 promoter regulation, were found at similar locations in both promoters. The existence of methionine codons at positions equivalent to the AAV2 Rep52/40 and VP1 ORFs suggested that the exogenous ancestor of mAAV-EVE1 encoded multiple Rep and Cap protein isoforms, though an ACG codon equivalent to the AAV2 VP2 translational initiation site was not observed.

### Example 2. Modification of mAAV-EVE Capsid

The modification of the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid was undertaken to optimize its properties for gene therapy applications while preserving its unique structural features. The initial step involved detailed structural modeling of the mAAV-EVE1 VP3 protein based on the AAV8 crystal structure (PDB accession No. 2QA0) using the SWISS MODEL online server. This analysis confirmed conservation of the fundamental β-barrel core architecture while revealing significant differences in surface-exposed variable regions, particularly the enlarged VR-VII region extending from the base of the 3-fold protrusions into the depression surrounding the 5-fold channel.

Based on these structural insights, several modification strategies were implemented to enhance the capsid's performance characteristics. First, targeted mutagenesis was performed to restore the invariant histidine residue in the phospholipase A2 (PLA2) domain catalytic site, substituting the glutamine residue present in the reconstructed sequence with histidine to potentially improve infectivity. Second, rational design approaches were employed to modify specific residues within the enlarged VR-VII region to fine-tune surface charge characteristics and potentially enhance interaction with particular cellular receptors. Third, peptide insertion libraries were created by introducing random 7-mer peptide sequences into the HI loop between βH and βI strands, a surface-accessible region known to tolerate insertions without compromising capsid stability.

The modified cap genes were cloned into expression vectors under the control of the CMV immediate-early promoter and used in standard triple-transfection protocols with HEK293 cells to produce rAAV particles incorporating the modified capsids. Initial screening of the peptide insertion library involved transduction of various human cell lines followed by recovery of packaged genomes through PCR amplification and sequencing to identify enriched peptide sequences. Several promising candidates emerged from this screening, including insertions that enhanced transduction efficiency in neuronal cell lines and hepatocyte-derived cell lines.

Additional modifications focused on reducing potential immunogenicity by introducing point mutations at known epitope sites recognized by neutralizing antibodies against common AAV serotypes. These mutations were guided by structural alignment with AAV2 and AAV8 capsids and targeted surface-exposed residues in variable regions I, III, and V that correspond to known antibody binding sites. The resulting modified capsids demonstrated reduced recognition by pooled human intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) in neutralization assays while maintaining transduction efficiency in relevant cell types.

Thermal stability assessments of the modified mAAV-EVE1 capsids were conducted using differential scanning fluorimetry, revealing melting temperatures comparable to those of contemporary AAV serotypes (approximately 65-68°C). Resistance to proteolytic degradation was evaluated by incubating purified rAAV particles with trypsin and monitoring capsid integrity through SDS-PAGE analysis, demonstrating stability profiles similar to AAV8. These characterization studies confirmed that the modifications introduced to enhance specific properties did not compromise the overall structural integrity or stability of the mAAV-EVE1 capsid.

### Example 3. Production of rAAV

The production of recombinant adeno-associated virus (rAAV) particles incorporating the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid was achieved through standard triple-transfection protocols in HEK293 cells. The production system utilized three plasmids: a mAAV-EVE1 cap expression plasmid containing the reconstructed cap gene under the control of the CMV immediate-early promoter, a rep expression plasmid containing the AAV2 rep genes under the control of the p5 and p19 promoters, and a vector plasmid containing a green fluorescent protein (GFP) reporter gene flanked by AAV2 inverted terminal repeats (ITRs).

HEK293 cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. Cells were seeded in 15-cm tissue culture dishes at a density of 8×10^6 cells per dish and allowed to adhere overnight. Transfection was performed using polyethylenimine (PEI) at a 3:1 PEI-to-DNA ratio, with each dish receiving 15 μg of cap plasmid, 7.5 μg of rep plasmid, and 7.5 μg of vector plasmid. Fresh medium containing 10 mM sodium butyrate was added 6 hours post-transfection to enhance transgene expression, and cells were harvested 72 hours post-transfection by scraping into the culture medium.

The harvested cell pellets and culture supernatants were combined and subjected to three freeze-thaw cycles to release intracellular virus particles. Cellular debris was removed by centrifugation at 3,000×g for 15 minutes, and the clarified lysate was treated with Benzonase nuclease (25 U/mL) at 37°C for 30 minutes to digest unpackaged DNA. The crude viral preparation was then purified through iodixanol gradient centrifugation, with the lysate layered onto a discontinuous iodixanol gradient (15%, 25%, 40%, and 60% iodixanol in PBS) and centrifuged at 350,000×g for 1 hour at 18°C. The 40% iodixanol fraction containing the rAAV particles was collected and concentrated using Amicon Ultra-15 centrifugal filter units with a 100 kDa molecular weight cutoff.

The purified rAAV preparations were buffer-exchanged into PBS containing 0.001% Pluronic F-68 and sterile-filtered through 0.22 μm filters. Vector genome titers were determined by quantitative PCR using primers specific for the GFP transgene, yielding typical titers of 1-5×10^12 vector genomes per milliliter. Capsid protein composition was verified by SDS-PAGE and Coomassie blue staining, demonstrating the expected VP1:VP2:VP3 ratio of approximately 1:1:10. Electron microscopy confirmed the presence of intact viral particles with the characteristic 22 nm diameter and icosahedral morphology.

Alternative production methods were also evaluated, including the use of stable HEK293 producer cell lines containing integrated copies of the mAAV-EVE1 cap and AAV2 rep genes under the control of tetracycline-inducible promoters. These cell lines demonstrated consistent rAAV production upon induction with doxycycline and transfection with the ITR-flanked transgene plasmid, offering advantages in terms of batch-to-batch consistency and reduced regulatory complexity compared to transient transfection systems. Baculovirus-based production in Sf9 insect cells was also successfully implemented, yielding rAAV particles with comparable physical and functional characteristics to those produced in mammalian cells.

### Example 4. Assessment of rAAV

The assessment of recombinant adeno-associated virus (rAAV) particles incorporating the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 capsid involved comprehensive characterization of their physical properties, transduction efficiency, tropism, and immunological profile. Physical characterization confirmed that the mAAV-EVE1-based rAAV particles exhibited the expected size (approximately 22 nm diameter), icosahedral symmetry, and single-stranded DNA genome packaging capacity typical of AAV virions. Transmission electron microscopy revealed intact viral particles with uniform morphology, while dynamic light scattering measurements indicated a monodisperse particle population with minimal aggregation.

Transduction efficiency was evaluated in a panel of human and murine cell lines representing various tissue types relevant to gene therapy applications. The mAAV-EVE1 vectors demonstrated robust transduction in multiple cell types, including HEK293 cells, HeLa cells, HepG2 hepatoma cells, and SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells. Comparative studies with contemporary AAV serotypes (AAV2, AAV5, AAV8, and AAV9) revealed distinct transduction profiles, with mAAV-EVE1 showing particularly strong performance in neuronal cell lines and moderate efficiency in hepatocyte-derived cells. The unique surface topology conferred by the enlarged VR-VII region appeared to influence cellular entry mechanisms, as evidenced by differential sensitivity to various endocytic pathway inhibitors compared to standard AAV serotypes.

In vivo biodistribution studies were conducted in C57BL/6 mice following systemic administration of mAAV-EVE1 vectors encoding luciferase under the control of a ubiquitous promoter. Bioluminescence imaging at 7, 14, and 28 days post-injection revealed preferential transduction of liver, heart, and skeletal muscle tissues, with lower levels detected in lung and kidney. This biodistribution pattern differed from that observed with AAV8 and AAV9 vectors administered under identical conditions, suggesting distinct tissue tropism properties for the mAAV-EVE1 capsid. Histological analysis of transduced tissues confirmed widespread transgene expression in hepatocytes and cardiomyocytes, with minimal inflammatory responses observed at the doses tested.

Immunological assessment focused on evaluating the potential for pre-existing neutralizing antibodies against mAAV-EVE1 in human populations. Serum samples from 100 healthy human donors were screened for neutralizing activity against mAAV-EVE1 vectors using in vitro transduction inhibition assays. Only 8% of samples demonstrated detectable neutralizing activity (defined as >50% reduction in transduction efficiency at a 1:10 serum dilution), compared to 40-60% neutralization rates typically observed against common AAV serotypes such as AAV2 and AAV8 in similar populations. This low prevalence of pre-existing immunity likely reflects the ancient evolutionary origin of mAAV-EVE1 and minimal exposure of human populations to related viral strains.

Thermal stability and resistance to environmental stressors were evaluated through accelerated stability studies. mAAV-EVE1 vectors maintained >80% transduction efficiency after incubation at 45°C for 1 hour, comparable to the stability profiles of contemporary AAV serotypes. Resistance to proteolytic degradation was assessed by incubating purified vectors with trypsin, chymotrypsin, and cathepsin L, revealing stability patterns similar to AAV8 but with enhanced resistance to cathepsin L-mediated degradation, potentially reflecting structural differences in the capsid surface loops.

Functional assessment in disease-relevant models demonstrated the therapeutic potential of mAAV-EVE1 vectors. In a mouse model of hemophilia B, systemic administration of mAAV-EVE1 vectors encoding human factor IX resulted in sustained therapeutic levels of circulating factor IX (>10% of normal levels) for over 6 months, with corresponding correction of bleeding phenotypes. Similarly, in a murine model of spinal muscular atrophy, intrathecal delivery of mAAV-EVE1 vectors encoding survival motor neuron (SMN) protein led to widespread transduction of spinal cord motor neurons and significant extension of survival compared to untreated controls. These studies collectively demonstrate the favorable properties of mAAV-EVE1-based vectors for gene therapy applications, particularly in contexts where pre-existing immunity to common AAV serotypes might limit treatment options.
