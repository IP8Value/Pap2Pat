# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and epigenetics, specifically to methods and compositions for the detection, quantification, and sequencing of modified cytosine bases in genomic DNA. More particularly, the invention provides novel, bisulfite-free, enzymatic and chemical-based sequencing methodologies that enable base-resolution, quantitative, and direct profiling of 5-methylcytosine (5mC), 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC), 5-formylcytosine (5fC), and 5-carboxylcytosine (5caC) across the entire genome. These methods are designed to overcome the significant limitations of traditional bisulfite sequencing and its derivatives, including severe DNA degradation, reduced sequence complexity, indirect detection mechanisms, and the necessity for computationally intensive subtraction-based inference of individual modifications. The invention encompasses a suite of complementary techniques—TAPSβ (TET-assisted pyridine borane sequencing with β-glucosyltransferase blocking), CAPS (chemical-assisted pyridine borane sequencing), PS (pyridine borane sequencing), and PS-c (pyridine borane sequencing for carboxylcytosine)—which collectively offer a comprehensive, high-fidelity, and non-destructive platform for epigenomic analysis. This platform is particularly valuable for studying the dynamic interplay between different cytosine modifications in biological processes such as gene regulation, cellular differentiation, development, and disease pathogenesis.

## BACKGROUND

The genetic information of all known life forms is encoded in the linear sequence of four canonical nucleobases: guanine (G), cytosine (C), adenine (A), and thymine (T). However, this primary sequence is overlaid with a complex layer of chemical modifications that constitute the epigenome, which regulates gene expression without altering the underlying DNA sequence. Among these, cytosine modifications are the most extensively studied and functionally significant in mammals. The first discovered and most abundant modified cytosine is 5-methylcytosine (5mC), often referred to as the “fifth base.” It plays a critical role in numerous biological processes, including transcriptional silencing, X-chromosome inactivation, genomic imprinting, and the suppression of transposable elements. An aberrant 5mC landscape is a hallmark of many diseases, notably cancer.

The discovery of the ten-eleven translocation (TET) family of dioxygenases revealed that 5mC is not a terminal epigenetic mark but can be actively oxidized. TET enzymes successively convert 5mC to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC, the “sixth base”), then to 5-formylcytosine (5fC), and finally to 5-carboxylcytosine (5caC). While 5fC and 5caC were initially considered transient intermediates in the active DNA demethylation pathway—where they are excised by thymine DNA glycosylase (TDG) and replaced with an unmodified cytosine via the base excision repair (BER) machinery—emerging evidence suggests they may also serve as stable epigenetic marks with distinct regulatory functions. Notably, 5hmC is highly enriched in neuronal tissues and embryonic stem cells, where it is generally associated with active or poised gene expression, in stark contrast to the repressive nature of 5mC. This functional antagonism underscores the critical need for technologies that can accurately and independently map the genomic distribution of 5mC and 5hmC.

For decades, bisulfite sequencing (BS-seq) has been the gold standard for detecting 5mC at single-base resolution. In this method, genomic DNA is treated with sodium bisulfite, which deaminates unmodified cytosines to uracils (which are subsequently read as thymines during PCR amplification and sequencing), while 5mC and 5hmC are resistant to conversion and remain as cytosines. However, BS-seq suffers from several major drawbacks. First, the harsh chemical conditions cause extensive DNA degradation, with up to 99% of the input material being lost, which is particularly problematic for low-input or precious clinical samples. Second, the conversion of ~95% of all cytosines to thymines drastically reduces the sequence complexity of the library, leading to poor mapping efficiency, ambiguous alignments, and uneven coverage, especially in CpG-rich regions like promoters and CpG islands. Third, and most critically for the study of oxidized derivatives, standard BS-seq cannot distinguish between 5mC and 5hmC, as both are read as “C.”

To address this limitation, derivative methods were developed. Oxidative bisulfite sequencing (oxBS-seq) uses potassium perruthenate (KRuO₄) to selectively oxidize 5hmC to 5fC, which is then converted to uracil by bisulfite treatment. By comparing a standard BS-seq profile (5mC + 5hmC) with an oxBS-seq profile (5mC only), one can infer the 5hmC level by subtraction. Conversely, TET-assisted bisulfite sequencing (TAB-seq) uses recombinant TET enzyme to convert 5mC to 5caC, and then β-glucosyltransferase (βGT) to add a glucose moiety to 5hmC, protecting it from TET oxidation. Subsequent bisulfite treatment converts all unmodified C and 5caC to T, leaving only the glucosylated 5hmC as C. Again, this requires a subtraction step if one wishes to obtain a pure 5mC map. These subtraction-based approaches are inherently noisy; random sampling errors and systematic biases from two separate experiments are compounded, often leading to negative or inflated modification levels and necessitating very high sequencing depth for statistical confidence. Furthermore, they still rely on the destructive bisulfite chemistry.

In response to these challenges, bisulfite-free methods have recently emerged. APOBEC-coupled epigenetic sequencing (ACE-seq) replaces bisulfite with the APOBEC3A enzyme to deaminate unmodified C, while Enzymatic Methyl-seq (EM-seq) uses a combination of TET2 and APOBEC3A. While these methods mitigate DNA damage, they retain the core logic of BS-seq: converting unmodified C to T to indirectly infer modification status. They still suffer from reduced sequence complexity and the indirect detection paradigm.

A significant breakthrough was the development of TET-assisted pyridine borane sequencing (TAPS). TAPS leverages a fundamentally different chemical principle: instead of converting the unmodified base, it directly converts the modified bases into a detectable form. In TAPS, 5mC and 5hmC are first oxidized by TET enzyme to 5caC. The 5caC is then subjected to a mild, reductive decarboxylation and deamination reaction using pyridine borane, which converts it directly to dihydrouracil (DHU). DHU is a natural intermediate that is faithfully amplified by DNA polymerases as thymine (T) during PCR. Thus, in the final sequencing data, a T at a cytosine position indicates the presence of either 5mC or 5hmC in the original sample, while a C indicates an unmodified cytosine. This direct conversion strategy preserves DNA integrity, maintains high sequence complexity, and yields superior sequencing metrics. However, the original TAPS method, like standard BS-seq, reports the combined signal of 5mC and 5hmC and does not provide their individual distributions.

Therefore, a critical unmet need in the field remains: a set of truly bisulfite-free, direct, quantitative, and subtraction-free methods that can independently and simultaneously profile all four major cytosine modifications (5mC, 5hmC, 5fC, 5caC) with high fidelity and minimal DNA damage. The present invention directly addresses this need by providing a complete and versatile toolkit based on the core chemistry of borane-mediated reduction.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive suite of novel, bisulfite-free methods for the direct, quantitative, and base-resolution sequencing of all four major cytosine modifications in genomic DNA: 5-methylcytosine (5mC), 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC), 5-formylcytosine (5fC), and 5-carboxylcytosine (5caC). These methods are built upon a foundational chemistry involving the reductive conversion of modified cytosines to dihydrouracil (DHU), which is subsequently read as thymine (T) during standard PCR amplification and next-generation sequencing. This direct conversion strategy eliminates the need for harsh bisulfite treatment, thereby preserving DNA integrity and maintaining high sequence complexity, which results in superior sequencing quality, higher mapping rates, and more uniform coverage compared to all existing methods.

In a first aspect, the invention provides a method for 5mC-specific sequencing, termed TAPSβ (TET-assisted pyridine borane sequencing with β-glucosyltransferase blocking). This method comprises the steps of: (a) treating a double-stranded DNA sample with β-glucosyltransferase (βGT) and a UDP-glucose donor to selectively add a glucose moiety to 5hmC residues, thereby blocking them from subsequent enzymatic oxidation; (b) oxidizing the DNA sample with a TET-family dioxygenase under conditions that convert 5mC to 5caC, while the glucosylated 5hmC remains unoxidized; and (c) treating the oxidized DNA with pyridine borane under reducing conditions to convert 5caC to DHU, which is read as T during sequencing. In the resulting sequencing data, a T at a cytosine position specifically indicates the presence of 5mC in the original sample, while a C indicates either an unmodified cytosine or 5hmC. This method is the first and only bisulfite-free technique capable of providing a direct, quantitative, and base-resolution map of 5mC without requiring any computational subtraction.

In a second aspect, the invention provides a method for 5hmC-specific sequencing, termed CAPS (chemical-assisted pyridine borane sequencing). This method comprises the steps of: (a) denaturing a DNA sample to generate single-stranded DNA; (b) chemically oxidizing the single-stranded DNA with potassium ruthenate (K₂RuO₄) under optimized conditions to selectively convert 5hmC to 5fC; (c) treating the chemically oxidized DNA with 2-methylpyridine borane (pic-borane) under reducing conditions to convert 5fC to DHU, which is read as T during sequencing. To protect the DNA from oxidative damage during the chemical oxidation step, the method further comprises preparing the sequencing library using a loop-structured adaptor containing a uracil base, which is subsequently cleaved with a USER enzyme mixture to generate protected DNA ends. In the resulting sequencing data, a T at a cytosine position specifically indicates the presence of 5hmC in the original sample, while a C indicates an unmodified cytosine, 5mC, or other modifications. This subtraction-free approach provides a direct and robust readout of 5hmC.

In a third aspect, the invention provides a method for the combined sequencing of 5fC and 5caC, termed PS (pyridine borane sequencing). This method is remarkably simple and comprises the direct treatment of a double-stranded DNA sample with pyridine borane under reducing conditions, which converts both 5fC and 5caC directly to DHU, read as T during sequencing. This allows for the direct detection of these rare demethylation intermediates without any prior enzymatic or chemical pre-treatment.

In a fourth aspect, the invention provides a method for 5caC-specific sequencing, termed PS-c (pyridine borane sequencing for carboxylcytosine). This method comprises the steps of: (a) treating a double-stranded DNA sample with O-ethylhydroxylamine to selectively react with and block 5fC residues, forming a stable oxime adduct; and (b) treating the blocked DNA with pyridine borane under reducing conditions to convert 5caC to DHU, which is read as T during sequencing. The blocked 5fC is inert to the borane reduction and remains as C. Thus, in the sequencing data, a T specifically indicates 5caC, while a C can represent an unmodified cytosine, 5mC, 5hmC, or 5fC.

Collectively, these four methods—TAPSβ, CAPS, PS, and PS-c—constitute a unified and powerful platform for comprehensive epigenomic analysis. They enable researchers to independently and accurately map the genomic landscapes of all major cytosine modifications from the same biological sample, using a consistent chemical framework that minimizes technical bias. This platform overcomes the fundamental limitations of all previous technologies, offering unprecedented accuracy, sensitivity, and data quality for studying the roles of DNA modifications in health and disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a family of novel sequencing methods that utilize a core borane-mediated reduction chemistry to directly convert specific modified cytosines into dihydrouracil (DHU), a base that is universally recognized as thymine (T) by DNA polymerases during PCR amplification and next-generation sequencing. This direct-to-T conversion strategy represents a paradigm shift from the indirect, unmodified-C-to-T conversion logic of bisulfite sequencing and its derivatives. By preserving the vast majority of the original cytosines in the genome, these methods maintain high sequence complexity, which translates into dramatically improved bioinformatic performance, including higher mapping rates, better base quality scores, and more uniform genomic coverage. Furthermore, the mild reaction conditions are non-destructive to DNA, allowing for the use of low-input and degraded samples that are incompatible with bisulfite treatment.

The foundation of this invention is the discovery and optimization of specific chemical and enzymatic protocols that allow for the selective targeting of individual cytosine modifications within this borane reduction framework. The key to achieving specificity lies in the strategic use of blocking, oxidation, and protection steps that precede the final borane reduction.

For 5mC-specific sequencing (TAPSβ), the method exploits the well-established biochemical property of β-glucosyltransferase (βGT). βGT is an enzyme that transfers a glucose moiety from UDP-glucose specifically to the hydroxyl group of 5hmC, creating β-glucosyl-5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5gmC). This bulky glucose adduct serves as a steric block that prevents the TET-family dioxygenase from accessing and oxidizing the 5hmC residue. Therefore, in a genomic DNA sample containing a mixture of unmodified C, 5mC, and 5hmC, the initial βGT treatment effectively “masks” all 5hmC. The subsequent TET oxidation step then acts exclusively on the unmasked 5mC, converting it through the intermediates 5hmC and 5fC to the final product, 5caC. The now fully oxidized DNA contains 5caC (from original 5mC), 5gmC (from original 5hmC), and unmodified C. The final pyridine borane reduction step is highly specific for 5caC, converting it efficiently to DHU (read as T). The 5gmC and unmodified C are completely inert to this reaction and remain as C in the final sequence. Thus, the T signal is a direct and exclusive reporter of the original 5mC state. The protocol is performed on double-stranded DNA throughout, which is crucial for maintaining DNA integrity and compatibility with standard library preparation workflows. The use of a double TET oxidation step ensures complete conversion of 5mC to 5caC, which is essential for quantitative accuracy.

For 5hmC-specific sequencing (CAPS), a different strategy is required because there is no known enzyme that can selectively block 5mC while leaving 5hmC accessible. Instead, the invention utilizes a selective chemical oxidation. Potassium ruthenate (K₂RuO₄) is employed as a mild and efficient oxidant that can convert 5hmC directly to 5fC in single-stranded DNA. A critical innovation in the CAPS protocol is the library preparation step. Standard double-stranded DNA library construction is used, but with a specialized loop-structured adaptor that contains a uracil base. After ligation, the USER enzyme (a mixture of Uracil DNA Glycosylase and Endonuclease VIII) is used to cleave the loop at the uracil site, generating a linear DNA fragment with a 3' phosphate and a 5' phosphate at the adaptor junctions. These phosphorylated ends are resistant to the oxidative damage that K₂RuO₄ can inflict on free DNA termini, thereby protecting the library from degradation during the harsh chemical oxidation step. The DNA is then denatured to single strands, and a double oxidation with K₂RuO₄ is performed to ensure near-complete conversion of 5hmC to 5fC. The choice of the reducing agent is also critical; while pyridine borane works well on double-stranded DNA, its efficiency drops significantly on single-stranded templates. The inventors discovered that 2-methylpyridine borane (pic-borane) is far more effective at converting 5fC to DHU in single-stranded DNA, achieving high conversion rates with a low false-positive rate. Consequently, in the final CAPS data, the T signal is a direct and specific readout of the original 5hmC.

For the detection of the rarer oxidation products, 5fC and 5caC, the invention provides two elegant and simple methods. The basic PS method requires no pre-treatment; pyridine borane alone is sufficient to reduce both 5fC and 5caC to DHU. This provides a straightforward way to map the combined landscape of these active demethylation intermediates. For situations where it is necessary to distinguish between 5fC and 5caC, the PS-c method is used. This method takes advantage of the unique aldehyde functional group on 5fC, which can react with O-ethylhydroxylamine to form a stable oxime. This chemical blocking step renders 5fC unreactive towards the subsequent pyridine borane reduction. Therefore, only 5caC is converted to DHU (T), while the blocked 5fC, along with all other cytosine species, remains as C. This two-step chemical protocol provides a clean and specific route to a 5caC-only map.

All methods of the invention are designed to be quantitative, meaning that the ratio of C-to-T at any given genomic cytosine position directly reflects the fractional abundance of the target modification in the original cell population. This is validated through the use of spike-in controls—synthetic DNA fragments with known, defined modification states—that are processed alongside the experimental samples. The high conversion rates (>97% for target modifications) and extremely low false-positive rates (<0.3% for unmodified C) demonstrate the quantitative fidelity of these methods. The resulting sequencing libraries are fully compatible with standard Illumina sequencing platforms and can be analyzed using conventional bioinformatic pipelines with minor adjustments for the C-to-T conversion logic. The collective power of this invention lies in its ability to generate a complete, unbiased, and high-quality picture of the cytosine epigenome from a single biological source, enabling new insights into the complex dynamics of DNA methylation and demethylation.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the practice of the invention and demonstrate its superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. All experiments were performed using genomic DNA (gDNA) extracted from mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs), a well-established model system for epigenetic studies.

**Example 1: Validation and Application of TAPSβ for 5mC-Specific Sequencing**

Genomic DNA from mESCs was subjected to the TAPSβ protocol. The DNA was first treated with β-glucosyltransferase (βGT) to block all 5hmC residues. It was then oxidized with the catalytic domain of mouse TET1 (mTet1CD) in a double-oxidation reaction to ensure complete conversion of 5mC to 5caC. Finally, the DNA was treated with pyridine borane to reduce 5caC to DHU. The resulting library was sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. To assess performance, the library was spiked with control DNA fragments: CpG-methylated lambda phage DNA (for 5mC), synthetic oligonucleotides containing 5hmC, and an unmodified 2-kb plasmid fragment.

The results were exceptional. The conversion rate for 5mC in the lambda DNA spike-in was 97.6%, demonstrating near-complete and quantitative conversion. The false-positive rate, measured as the conversion of unmodified C in the 2-kb spike-in, was a remarkably low 0.24%. Crucially, the conversion rate for 5hmC was only 1.9%, confirming the high efficiency of the βGT blocking step, which prevented >98% of 5hmC from being oxidized and converted. The sequencing data showed excellent base quality scores across all positions, and the overall mapping rate to the mouse reference genome was 90.7%, which is substantially higher than the 66-68% for reduced representation oxBS-seq (RRoxBS-seq) and the mere 21-26% for whole-genome oxBS-seq reported in the literature. When the TAPSβ 5mC profile was compared to published oxBS-seq datasets from mESCs, strong positive correlations were observed (Pearson’s r = 0.77 with RRoxBS-seq and r = 0.72 with whole-genome oxBS-seq), validating its biological accuracy. This example establishes TAPSβ as the first and only bisulfite-free, direct, and quantitative method for base-resolution 5mC mapping.

**Example 2: Development and Benchmarking of CAPS for 5hmC-Specific Sequencing**

To develop a 5hmC-specific method, mESC gDNA was processed using the CAPS protocol. A sequencing library was first constructed using a loop-structured NEBNext Adaptor containing a uracil base. The loop was opened with USER enzyme to create protected ends. The library was then denatured, and the single-stranded DNA was subjected to a double oxidation with potassium ruthenate (K₂RuO₄) to convert 5hmC to 5fC. The oxidized DNA was then treated with 2-methylpyridine borane (pic-borane) to reduce 5fC to DHU. The final library was sequenced and analyzed alongside spike-in controls.

The optimization of the reducing agent was critical. Pyridine borane showed poor performance on single-stranded DNA (65.8% conversion), but pic-borane achieved a high 5hmC-to-T conversion rate of 83.1% with a low false-positive rate of 0.72% on unmodified C. The CAPS library yielded a total of 1,762,287 high-confidence 5hmC sites in the mESC genome. Its performance was benchmarked against the two leading whole-genome 5hmC sequencing methods: TAB-seq (bisulfite-based) and ACE-seq (enzymatic, bisulfite-free). CAPS demonstrated a significantly higher mapping rate than both TAB-seq and ACE-seq. It also showed strong correlation with their published mESC datasets (Pearson’s r = 0.79 with TAB-seq and r = 0.67 with ACE-seq). In stark contrast, a 5hmC profile generated by computationally subtracting the TAPSβ (5mC) data from the original TAPS (5mC+5hmC) data showed much weaker correlation (r = 0.54 with TAB-seq and r = 0.40 with ACE-seq) and an abnormal distribution of modification levels, highlighting the noise and inaccuracy inherent in subtraction-based approaches. Genome browser views confirmed that CAPS detected 5hmC peaks at known regulatory elements, such as enhancers and CTCF-binding sites, in agreement with TAB-seq and ACE-seq. This example demonstrates that CAPS is a superior, subtraction-free method for direct 5hmC profiling.

**Example 3: Profiling of 5fC and 5caC using PS and PS-c**

To map the rare demethylation intermediates, mESC gDNA was split and processed in parallel with the PS and PS-c protocols. For PS, the library was directly treated with pyridine borane. For PS-c, the library was first blocked with O-ethylhydroxylamine before the borane treatment. Both libraries were sequenced.

The PS method showed a high conversion rate for a 5caC spike-in (93.8%) and a good rate for a 5fC spike-in (76.8%), with a very low false-positive rate of 0.27%. The PS-c method successfully blocked 5fC, reducing its conversion rate to just 15.2%, while maintaining a high 5caC conversion rate (95.3%) and a low false-positive rate (0.22%). Given the extremely low genomic abundance of 5fC/5caC (<0.002% of total cytosines), whole-genome calling of individual sites is challenging. Therefore, the analysis was focused on aggregate signals over regulatory regions. Both PS and PS-c data revealed a clear enrichment of 5fC/5caC signals at active histone marks H3K4me1 (enhancers) and H3K4me3 (promoters), consistent with their role in active demethylation at regulatory elements. A specific example was the detection of 5fC/5caC modification on the promoter of the *Nanog* gene, a key pluripotency regulator, which had been previously suggested by enrichment-based methods. This example validates the sensitivity and biological relevance of the PS and PS-c methods for studying active DNA demethylation dynamics.

In summary, these examples collectively demonstrate that the suite of methods described in the present invention—TAPSβ, CAPS, PS, and PS-c—provides a complete, high-performance, and bisulfite-free solution for the comprehensive analysis of the cytosine epigenome.
