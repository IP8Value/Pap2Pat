# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of cellular immunotherapy for the treatment of cancer and, more specifically, to methods and compositions for producing allogeneic invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells derived from hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) for use as off-the-shelf therapeutic agents. The invention further encompasses engineered variants of these HSC-derived iNKT cells, including those modified to express chimeric antigen receptors (CARs) and those with ablated human leukocyte antigen (HLA) expression to create universal cell products. The technical domain spans molecular biology, genetic engineering, cell culture technology, immunology, and oncology, with a particular focus on overcoming the significant logistical, safety, and efficacy barriers that have historically limited the broad clinical application of adoptive T-cell therapies. The invention provides a robust, scalable, and versatile platform for manufacturing a novel class of allogeneic immune cells that possess potent anti-tumor activity through multiple mechanisms while exhibiting an exceptionally high safety profile, characterized by an absence of graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) risk and low immunogenicity, making them ideally suited for “off-the-shelf” distribution and administration to a wide patient population.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Over the past decade, immunotherapy has emerged as a transformative approach in the treatment of cancer, with T-cell-based therapies representing a particularly promising frontier. Among these, chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-engineered T (CAR-T) cell therapy has demonstrated remarkable efficacy against certain B-cell malignancies, leading to its approval by regulatory agencies such as the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA). This success has been complemented by other adoptive T-cell strategies, including the transfer of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and T-cell receptor (TCR)-engineered T cells, which have shown potential in treating both hematological cancers and solid tumors. However, a critical limitation shared by the vast majority of these current T-cell therapies is their autologous nature. In an autologous setting, T cells are harvested from a patient, genetically modified or expanded ex vivo, and then reinfused back into the same patient. While effective, this personalized approach is inherently time-consuming, logistically complex, and extremely costly. It requires a dedicated manufacturing process for each individual patient, creating significant delays between diagnosis and treatment. Furthermore, for patients who are heavily pre-treated with lymphodepleting chemotherapy or whose disease is rapidly progressing, it may be impossible to collect a sufficient number of functional T cells to manufacture a viable therapeutic product, thereby excluding a substantial portion of the patient population from potentially life-saving therapy.

In response to these challenges, there has been a concerted effort to develop allogeneic, or “off-the-shelf,” cell therapies. Such products would be manufactured from healthy donor cells in large batches, rigorously tested for quality and safety, cryopreserved, and made readily available for immediate administration to any eligible patient. This paradigm shift promises to dramatically reduce costs, eliminate manufacturing wait times, and ensure consistent product quality. However, the development of safe and effective allogeneic T-cell products has been fraught with significant hurdles. Conventional αβ T cells, the primary cell type used in autologous therapies, pose a serious risk of inducing graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) when administered to an allogeneic host. GvHD occurs because the donor T cells recognize the recipient’s mismatched histocompatibility leukocyte antigens (HLAs) as foreign and mount a destructive immune attack against the host’s healthy tissues. To mitigate this risk, extensive genetic engineering is required to ablate the endogenous TCR of the donor T cells, typically using gene-editing technologies like CRISPR-Cas9 to disrupt the TRAC or TRBC loci. While this strategy can reduce GvHD risk, it adds considerable complexity, cost, and potential for genotoxicity to the manufacturing process, and may also impair the overall fitness and persistence of the final T-cell product.

An alternative approach has been to explore innate immune cells, such as natural killer (NK) cells, which do not express a rearranged TCR and therefore carry a minimal risk of GvHD. NK-cell-based allogeneic therapies have entered clinical trials and shown promise. However, NK cells often exhibit limited in vivo expansion and persistence following adoptive transfer, which can constrain their long-term anti-tumor efficacy. Moreover, NK cells are notoriously sensitive to the freeze-thaw cycles required for an off-the-shelf product, frequently suffering significant loss of viability and function upon thawing, which presents a major technical and commercial challenge.

Invariant Natural Killer T (iNKT) cells represent a unique and highly attractive candidate for allogeneic cell therapy. These are a rare subset of αβ T lymphocytes that bridge the innate and adaptive immune systems. Unlike conventional T cells, iNKT cells recognize lipid antigens presented by the non-polymorphic, MHC class I-like molecule CD1d, rather than peptide antigens presented by polymorphic HLA molecules. This fundamental difference in antigen recognition underpins their most critical advantage: iNKT cells do not react against mismatched HLA molecules and are therefore incapable of mounting a classical GvHD response. This intrinsic safety feature makes them ideal for allogeneic use without the need for complex TCR ablation. Furthermore, iNKT cells possess potent anti-tumor capabilities through multiple mechanisms. They can directly kill tumor cells via cytotoxic granules, secrete a broad array of Th1-polarized cytokines (such as IFN-γ and TNF-α) that activate other immune cells like NK cells and dendritic cells, and can effectively traffic into and modulate the immunosuppressive microenvironment of solid tumors—addressing a key limitation of many current T-cell therapies.

Despite these compelling attributes, the clinical translation of iNKT cell therapy has been severely hampered by practical limitations. In human peripheral blood, iNKT cells constitute an extremely small fraction of the total T-cell pool, ranging from only 0.001% to 1%. This scarcity makes it exceedingly difficult to reliably isolate and expand sufficient numbers of pure iNKT cells from a single donor to create a viable allogeneic product for multiple patients. Even more problematic, any attempt to expand iNKT cells from bulk peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) inevitably co-expands large numbers of conventional αβ T cells. These bystander T cells retain their endogenous TCRs and thus reintroduce the very GvHD risk that iNKT cells were chosen to avoid, rendering the final product unsafe for allogeneic use.

Previous research has attempted to circumvent these issues by engineering hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) to express an invariant iNKT TCR. When these engineered HSCs are transplanted into a host, they can reconstitute the entire immune system, giving rise to a new population of iNKT cells in vivo. While this approach has proven successful in generating iNKT cells, it is fundamentally an autologous, in vivo reconstitution strategy that is not compatible with the goal of producing a ready-to-use, off-the-shelf, mature allogeneic cell product. There remains a critical unmet need for a robust, scalable, and reliable in vitro method to manufacture large quantities of pure, mature, allogeneic iNKT cells that are free of GvHD risk and possess superior anti-tumor functionality. The present invention directly addresses this need by providing a novel and comprehensive platform for the production and engineering of such a next-generation cellular therapeutic.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a groundbreaking platform for the generation of allogeneic, off-the-shelf cellular therapeutics based on invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells derived from hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). The core of the invention is a robust, two-stage in vitro culture system that enables the efficient differentiation and massive expansion of HSCs into a highly pure and homogeneous population of mature iNKT cells, herein referred to as AlloHSC-iNKT cells. This platform overcomes the historical limitations of sourcing iNKT cells from peripheral blood by starting with readily available HSC sources, such as cord blood (CB) or granulocyte-colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF)-mobilized peripheral blood stem cells (PBSCs), which can be collected from healthy donors in large quantities.

In a first aspect, the invention provides a method for producing AlloHSC-iNKT cells. The method comprises transducing a population of human CD34+ HSCs with a lentiviral vector encoding an invariant iNKT T-cell receptor (TCR). The transduced HSCs are then cultured in a first stage within an artificial thymic organoid (ATO) system, which provides the necessary three-dimensional stromal and cytokine signals to support T-cell lineage commitment and differentiation over a period of approximately eight weeks. Subsequently, the differentiating iNKT cells are transferred to a second stage of culture where they are expanded in the presence of α-galactosylceramide (αGC), a potent and specific synthetic glycolipid agonist for iNKT cells, along with supportive cytokines, for an additional two to three weeks. This sequential ATO/αGC culture system results in a final cell product of exceptional purity (>99% iNKT TCR+), high yield (capable of generating hundreds of thousands of patient doses from a single donor), and a desirable CD8 single-positive/double-negative (CD8 SP/DN) phenotype associated with potent pro-inflammatory and cytotoxic activity.

In a second aspect, the invention provides the AlloHSC-iNKT cells themselves, which are characterized by a unique combination of properties that make them ideal for allogeneic therapy. These cells exhibit a Th0/Th1-prone functional profile, secreting high levels of anti-tumor cytokines like IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-2, while producing minimal Th2 or Th17 cytokines. Critically, they display a "super-active" NK-like phenotype, expressing exceedingly high levels of activating NK receptors (e.g., NKG2D, DNAM-1) and very low levels of inhibitory NK receptors (e.g., KIRs), which endows them with a powerful, HLA-unrestricted ability to recognize and kill a broad spectrum of tumor cells. Furthermore, these cells are intrinsically free of GvHD risk, as confirmed by both in vitro mixed lymphocyte reactions and in vivo xenograft models, and they can be equipped with a safety switch, such as the sr39TK suicide gene, for controlled depletion if needed.

In a third aspect, the invention provides a versatile platform for further engineering these AlloHSC-iNKT cells to enhance their therapeutic potential. Specifically, the mature AlloHSC-iNKT cells can be efficiently transduced with a retroviral or lentiviral vector to express a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR), creating CAR-engineered AlloHSC-iNKT cells (e.g., AlloBCAR-iNKT cells). These engineered cells retain all the favorable properties of their parent AlloHSC-iNKT cells and gain the ability to target specific tumor-associated antigens (e.g., BCMA on multiple myeloma cells) with high precision. Remarkably, these CAR-iNKT cells can target tumors through a synergistic triple mechanism: (1) CAR-mediated recognition of a specific tumor antigen, (2) TCR-mediated recognition of CD1d-presented lipids, and (3) NK receptor-mediated recognition of stress ligands. This multi-pronged attack significantly enhances their anti-tumor efficacy and provides a robust defense against tumor antigen escape, a common cause of relapse in conventional CAR-T therapy.

In a fourth aspect, the invention provides a method to create a "universal" version of these cells with enhanced resistance to host immune rejection. This is achieved by performing CRISPR-Cas9-mediated gene editing on the starting HSCs to ablate the B2M and CIITA genes, which are essential for the surface expression of HLA class I and class II molecules, respectively. The resulting HLA-ablated universal HSC-iNKT (UHSC-iNKT) cells and their CAR-engineered derivatives (UCAR-iNKT cells) exhibit a stable HLA-I/IIlow phenotype that renders them largely invisible to host T-cell-mediated allorejection. Surprisingly, these cells also show resistance to NK-cell-mediated rejection due to their low expression of stress ligands like ULBP, which are ligands for the NKG2D activating receptor. This dual resistance to both T- and NK-cell alloreactivity is a serendipitous and highly advantageous feature that is expected to significantly improve the in vivo persistence and therapeutic efficacy of these universal cell products.

In summary, the present invention provides a complete and integrated solution for the development of next-generation, off-the-shelf cellular immunotherapies. It offers a scalable manufacturing process, a highly pure and safe cell product with potent multi-mechanistic anti-tumor activity, and a flexible engineering platform to create both targeted (CAR+) and universal (HLA-) variants, thereby addressing the major challenges of cost, logistics, safety, and efficacy that have plagued the field of allogeneic cell therapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a novel and comprehensive platform for the production of allogeneic, off-the-shelf cellular therapeutics based on invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells that are derived from hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) through a defined in vitro differentiation and expansion process. This platform overcomes the fundamental limitations of previous approaches by providing a method to generate large quantities of a highly pure, homogeneous, and functionally potent iNKT cell product that is intrinsically safe for allogeneic use and can be further engineered for enhanced therapeutic properties.

The foundational element of the invention is the source material: human CD34+ hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs), which are collectively referred to herein as HSCs for simplicity. These cells can be obtained from various clinically relevant sources, including umbilical cord blood (CB) units or peripheral blood from healthy donors who have been mobilized with granulocyte-colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF). Both sources provide a renewable and scalable starting population of HSCs that can be banked and used to manufacture therapeutic cell products for numerous patients. The use of HSCs as the starting material is critical because it allows for the creation of a completely new T-cell lineage from the ground up, bypassing the contamination issues inherent in expanding rare iNKT cells from peripheral blood.

The first step in the manufacturing process is the genetic engineering of these HSCs to commit them to the iNKT cell lineage. This is accomplished by transducing the HSCs with a recombinant viral vector, preferably a self-inactivating (SIN) lentiviral vector, that encodes the genes for the invariant α and β chains of a human iNKT T-cell receptor (TCR). The canonical human iNKT TCR consists of a Vα24-Jα18 chain paired with a Vβ11 chain. The vector construct is designed to ensure the coordinated expression of both TCR chains, often using a 2A self-cleaving peptide linker (e.g., F2A, P2A) to create a single open reading frame that yields two separate protein products. In a preferred embodiment, the lentiviral vector also encodes a safety switch gene, such as a mutant herpes simplex virus thymidine kinase (sr39TK), which is linked to the TCR genes via another 2A peptide. This design ensures that every cell that expresses the iNKT TCR also expresses the suicide gene, providing a built-in safety mechanism for controlled depletion of the therapeutic cells in the event of an adverse reaction. The transduction is performed under optimized conditions to achieve a high efficiency, typically greater than 50%, ensuring that a large majority of the starting HSC population is successfully engineered.

Following successful transduction, the engineered HSCs are subjected to a two-stage in vitro culture system that mimics the natural thymic environment to drive their differentiation into mature iNKT cells. The first stage is an Artificial Thymic Organoid (ATO) culture. The ATO is a three-dimensional co-culture system that utilizes a murine bone marrow-derived stromal cell line, such as MS5, which has been genetically modified to express the human Notch ligand Delta-like ligand 4 (DLL4). The DLL4-Notch signaling axis is absolutely critical for the commitment of HSCs to the T-cell lineage. The HSCs and stromal cells are combined in a defined serum-free medium supplemented with key cytokines, including Flt3-Ligand and IL-7, which support early T-cell progenitor survival and proliferation. Over a period of approximately eight weeks, the HSCs undergo a stepwise differentiation process within the ATO, progressing through the canonical stages of T-cell development: from double-negative (DN; CD4-CD8-) to double-positive (DP; CD4+CD8+) and finally to the mature single-positive (SP) stage. In this system, the engineered iNKT cells predominantly differentiate into a CD8 SP or double-negative (DN) phenotype, which is known to be the most cytotoxic and pro-inflammatory subset of human iNKT cells, making it highly desirable for cancer immunotherapy. At the end of this stage, the ATO is dissociated into a single-cell suspension, yielding a population of early iNKT cells that have undergone significant expansion.

The second stage of the culture is designed for massive expansion and terminal maturation of the iNKT cells. The cells from the ATO culture are transferred to a standard liquid culture medium and stimulated with α-galactosylceramide (αGC), a synthetic glycolipid that is a potent and specific agonist for the iNKT TCR. αGC is typically presented by irradiated autologous or allogeneic peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) that have been pre-loaded with the antigen. This specific stimulation drives vigorous proliferation of the iNKT cells. The culture medium is supplemented with cytokines that support iNKT cell growth and survival, primarily IL-7 and IL-15. Over a period of two to three weeks, this stage results in an additional 100- to 1,000-fold expansion, culminating in a final AlloHSC-iNKT cell product of extraordinary yield and purity. From a single cord blood unit, this process can generate enough cells for hundreds to thousands of patient doses, and from a single PBSC leukapheresis product, enough for tens of thousands of doses. Critically, the final product is composed of >99% pure transgenic iNKT cells, with virtually no detectable bystander conventional αβ T cells, as confirmed by both flow cytometry and single-cell TCR sequencing. This high purity is a direct result of the allelic exclusion induced by the transgenic iNKT TCR during the early stages of T-cell development in the ATO, which prevents the rearrangement and expression of endogenous TCR genes.

The resulting AlloHSC-iNKT cells possess a unique and highly advantageous biological profile. Phenotypically, they closely resemble endogenous human iNKT cells, expressing characteristic markers such as CD161 and the memory marker CD45RO. They also express high levels of chemokine receptors like CCR4, CCR5, and CXCR3, which are crucial for homing to inflammatory sites and solid tumors. Functionally, they are primed for anti-tumor activity. Upon stimulation, they produce copious amounts of Th1 cytokines (IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-2) and cytotoxic effector molecules (perforin, granzyme B). Their most striking feature, however, is their enhanced NK-like functionality. Transcriptomic and flow cytometric analyses reveal that they express significantly higher levels of activating NK receptors (e.g., NKG2D, DNAM-1, NCR1) and much lower levels of inhibitory KIRs compared to both endogenous iNKT cells and NK cells. This receptor profile translates into a superior ability to kill a wide range of HLA-unrestricted tumor cell lines in vitro and in vivo, including those from melanoma, leukemia, lung cancer, prostate cancer, and multiple myeloma. Importantly, unlike primary NK cells, AlloHSC-iNKT cells are highly resistant to cryopreservation, maintaining their viability and potent anti-tumor function after freeze-thaw cycles, a critical attribute for an off-the-shelf product.

The safety of AlloHSC-iNKT cells is paramount. Extensive testing in both in vitro mixed lymphocyte reactions (MLRs) and in vivo NSG mouse xenograft models has conclusively demonstrated that these cells do not mount a graft-versus-host (GvH) response against allogeneic PBMCs and do not cause GvHD. This is a direct consequence of their invariant TCR, which is specific for CD1d-lipid complexes and does not recognize polymorphic HLA-peptide complexes. The incorporated sr39TK safety switch provides an additional layer of control, allowing for the rapid and efficient depletion of the cells in vivo upon administration of the prodrug ganciclovir (GCV).

The platform's versatility is demonstrated by its capacity for further genetic engineering. Mature AlloHSC-iNKT cells can be efficiently transduced with a retroviral vector to express a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR). For example, a second-generation CAR targeting B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) can be introduced, creating AlloBCAR-iNKT cells. These cells retain their native iNKT and NK functionalities while gaining the ability to specifically target BCMA-expressing tumor cells. This results in a powerful triple-targeting mechanism: CAR for specific antigen, TCR for CD1d, and NK receptors for stress ligands. This multi-specificity not only enhances overall tumor killing but also provides a safeguard against tumor escape variants that may downregulate a single target antigen. In preclinical models of multiple myeloma, AlloBCAR-iNKT cells showed superior efficacy and long-term, tumor-free survival without GvHD, in stark contrast to conventional BCAR-T cells which caused fatal GvHD.

To further enhance the product's suitability for universal use, the platform can be adapted to generate HLA-ablated cells. This is achieved by performing CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing on the HSCs prior to the ATO culture. By delivering guide RNAs (gRNAs) targeting the B2M gene (essential for HLA class I surface expression) and the CIITA gene (the master regulator of HLA class II transcription), along with the Cas9 nuclease, the HSCs are edited to lack surface HLA molecules. The subsequent ATO/αGC culture process faithfully propagates this edited genotype, resulting in Universal HSC-iNKT (UHSC-iNKT) cells and their CAR-engineered derivatives (UCAR-iNKT). These universal cells are completely resistant to T-cell-mediated host-versus-graft (HvG) rejection. Surprisingly, they also maintain resistance to NK-cell-mediated rejection, likely due to their intrinsically low expression of NKG2D ligands like ULBP. In vivo studies confirm that UCAR-iNKT cells achieve complete tumor clearance and long-term survival in a multiple myeloma model, outperforming both their HLA-expressing counterparts and conventional CAR-T cells.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Generation and Characterization of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells**

Human CD34+ HSCs were isolated from either cord blood (CB) or G-CSF-mobilized peripheral blood stem cells (PBSCs). These HSCs were transduced with a Lenti/iNKT-sr39TK vector at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 10, achieving a transduction efficiency of over 50% as measured by intracellular staining for the Vβ11 chain of the iNKT TCR at 72 hours post-transduction. The transduced HSCs were then placed into Stage 1 of the culture: an Artificial Thymic Organoid (ATO) system. The ATO was constructed by co-culturing the HSCs with MS5-hDLL4 stromal cells in a serum-free RB27 medium supplemented with Flt3-L (5 ng/mL) and IL-7 (5 ng/mL). The cultures were maintained for 8 weeks, with medium changes every 3-4 days. Flow cytometry analysis at weekly intervals showed a progressive differentiation from CD34+ HSCs to CD4-CD8- (DN) T-cell progenitors, then to CD4+CD8+ (DP) thymocytes, and finally to a mature CD4-CD8+/− (CD8 SP/DN) iNKT cell population. By the end of week 8, the ATO cultures contained a mean of 85% iNKT TCR+ (6B11+) TCRαβ+ cells, representing a >100-fold expansion from the input HSC number.

For Stage 2, the ATOs were dissociated, and the cells were co-cultured with irradiated (6,000 rads) PBMCs that had been pre-loaded with α-galactosylceramide (αGC; 5 μg/mL) at a 1:1 ratio in C10 medium supplemented with IL-7 (10 ng/mL) and IL-15 (10 ng/mL). This expansion phase lasted for 2 weeks, with a second round of αGC-PBMCs added at week 2 for an optional third week. At the end of Stage 2, the final AlloHSC-iNKT cell product was >99% pure iNKT TCR+ cells, with a final expansion of 100- to 1,000-fold over the Stage 1 output. Single-cell TCR sequencing of the final product confirmed that nearly all cells expressed the transgenic Vα24-Jα18/Vβ11 TCR, with no detectable clonally expanded endogenous TCR sequences, confirming the absence of bystander conventional T cells. From a single CB donor (starting with ~5x10^6 CD34+ cells), this process yielded ~5x10^11 AlloHSC-iNKT cells, sufficient for 500-5,000 patient doses. From a single PBSC donor (~5x10^8 CD34+ cells), it yielded ~3x10^13 cells, sufficient for 30,000-300,000 doses.

**Example 2: Functional and Phenotypic Profiling of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells**

The phenotype of AlloHSC-iNKT cells was analyzed by flow cytometry and compared to endogenous PBMC-derived iNKT (PBMC-iNKT) and conventional T (PBMC-Tc) cells. AlloHSC-iNKT cells expressed high levels of the memory marker CD45RO, the NK marker CD161, and the homing receptors CCR4, CCR5, and CXCR3, closely mirroring the PBMC-iNKT profile and distinct from PBMC-Tc cells. Upon stimulation with αGC for 7 days, AlloHSC-iNKT cells proliferated vigorously and secreted high levels of IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-2, but minimal IL-4 or IL-17, indicating a strong Th1 bias. Intracellular staining confirmed that a high frequency of AlloHSC-iNKT cells produced these cytokines as well as perforin and granzyme B at levels exceeding those of PBMC-iNKT and PBMC-Tc cells.

Deep RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) was performed on AlloHSC-iNKT cells (from both CB and PBSC sources) and compared to sorted populations of PBMC-iNKT, PBMC-αβTc, PBMC-γδT, and PBMC-NK cells from multiple healthy donors. Principal component analysis (PCA) placed AlloHSC-iNKT cells closest to PBMC-iNKT cells, validating their identity. Transcription factor profiling revealed high expression of ZBTB16 (PLZF), TBX21 (T-bet), NFKB1, and JUN, and low expression of GATA3 and RORC, consistent with their innate-like, Th1-polarized state. Gene expression analysis showed that AlloHSC-iNKT cells expressed significantly higher levels of NK activating receptor genes (NCAM1, NCR1, KLR2, KLR3) and lower levels of NK inhibitory receptor genes (KIR3DL1, KIR2DL1, etc.) compared to all control cell types. They also expressed high levels of tissue homing chemokine receptor genes (CCR1, CCR2, CCR5, CXCR3). Notably, they exhibited globally reduced expression of HLA class I and II genes and their regulators (B2M, CIITA), suggesting a low immunogenicity profile.

**Example 3: Anti-Tumor Efficacy of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells via Intrinsic NK Function**

The NK-like anti-tumor function of AlloHSC-iNKT cells was evaluated. Flow cytometry confirmed they expressed high surface levels of NKG2D and DNAM-1 and negligible levels of KIRs. They also contained higher levels of intracellular perforin and granzyme B than PBMC-NK cells. In a 24-hour in vitro killing assay against five different human tumor cell lines (A375 melanoma, K562 leukemia, H292 lung, PC3 prostate, MM.1S myeloma), AlloHSC-iNKT cells demonstrated significantly superior killing across all lines compared to PBMC-NK cells. This enhanced killing was maintained even after the cells were cryopreserved and thawed, whereas PBMC-NK cell function was severely compromised by the freeze-thaw process. Blocking antibodies against NKG2D and DNAM-1 significantly reduced tumor cell killing, confirming the role of these NK pathways. In an in vivo NSG mouse model bearing subcutaneous A375-FG melanoma tumors, a single paratumoral injection of AlloHSC-iNKT cells resulted in significantly greater suppression of tumor growth compared to an equal number of PBMC-NK cells, as measured by bioluminescence imaging and tumor weight.

**Example 4: Generation and Efficacy of CAR-Engineered AlloHSC-iNKT Cells (AlloBCAR-iNKT)**

To create a targeted therapy, mature AlloHSC-iNKT cells were engineered to express a BCMA-targeting CAR (BCAR). One week into the Stage 2 αGC expansion, AlloHSC-iNKT cells were activated with CD3/CD28 beads and then spin-infected with a Retro/BCAR-tEGFR retroviral vector. After an additional 1-2 weeks of expansion, the resulting AlloBCAR-iNKT cells were >98% iNKT TCR+ and up to 80% BCAR+ (detected via tEGFR). These cells retained their typical iNKT phenotype and functionality. In an in vitro killing assay against MM.1S-CD1d-FG myeloma cells (which express both BCMA and CD1d), AlloBCAR-iNKT cells showed the strongest killing, followed by non-CAR AlloHSC-iNKT cells (which killed via NK/TCR mechanisms), while conventional PBMC-T cells showed no killing. The killing by AlloBCAR-iNKT cells was enhanced by αGC (TCR signal) and reduced by DNAM-1 blockade (NK signal), demonstrating their triple-mechanism action.

In a low-tumor-burden NSG mouse model of disseminated MM.1S-CD1d-FG myeloma, both AlloBCAR-iNKT and conventional BCAR-T cells effectively cleared tumors. However, all mice treated with BCAR-T cells succumbed to GvHD by 100 days, while all AlloBCAR-iNKT-treated mice remained tumor-free and healthy long-term. In a high-tumor-burden model, AlloBCAR-iNKT cells still achieved complete remission in 2 out of 5 mice and significantly prolonged survival, whereas BCAR-T cells only provided transient control before tumor relapse and GvHD-related death.

**Example 5: Safety and Immunogenicity Profile of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells**

The safety of AlloHSC-iNKT cells was rigorously tested. In an in vitro MLR, AlloHSC-iNKT cells co-cultured with mismatched allogeneic PBMCs produced no detectable IFN-γ, in stark contrast to PBMC-Tc cells which mounted a strong response, confirming their lack of GvH reactivity. In an in vivo NSG mouse model, adoptive transfer of AlloHSC-iNKT cells did not cause any signs of GvHD, and mice survived long-term with healthy tissue histology, while PBMC-Tc transfer caused severe GvHD and death by 60 days. The sr39TK safety switch was functional; in vitro, GCV treatment killed AlloHSC-iNKT cells in a dose-dependent manner, and in vivo, a 5-day course of GCV completely depleted AlloHSC-iNKT cells from the liver, spleen, and lungs of engrafted NSG mice.

For immunogenicity, AlloHSC-iNKT cells triggered a significantly weaker host T-cell (HvG) response in an MLR compared to PBMC-Tc or PBMC-iNKT cells, correlating with their low surface HLA-I and undetectable HLA-II expression by flow cytometry. This low HLA expression was stable even after IFN-γ stimulation in vitro and within the tumor microenvironment in vivo. In an NK-mediated allorejection assay, AlloHSC-iNKT cells survived co-culture with allogeneic PBMC-NK cells significantly better than control T cells, which was associated with their very low expression of the NKG2D ligand ULBP.

**Example 6: Development of HLA-Ablated Universal HSC-iNKT (UHSC-iNKT) Cells**

To create a universal cell product, CD34+ HSCs were first transduced with the Lenti/iNKT-sr39TK vector and then, 24 hours later, electroporated with a CRISPR-Cas9 ribonucleoprotein complex containing gRNAs targeting B2M and CIITA. This dual editing achieved a >50% double-knockout efficiency in the HSCs. These edited HSCs were then processed through the standard ATO/αGC culture system to generate UHSC-iNKT cells. The final product contained a ~50% population of HLA-I/II double-negative cells, which could be enriched to >97% purity using magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS) with anti-B2M and anti-HLA-II beads. The resulting UHSC-iNKT and UBCAR-iNKT (CAR-engineered) cells displayed a typical iNKT phenotype and potent cytotoxicity. They induced virtually no T-cell HvG response in an MLR and maintained their resistance to NK-mediated rejection. In the MM.1S-CD1d-FG xenograft model, UBCAR-iNKT cells mediated complete and durable tumor clearance in all treated mice, with 100% long-term survival, significantly outperforming conventional BCAR-T cells which failed to control the disease and caused GvHD.

## ILLUSTRATIVE VECTOR SEQUENCES

The present invention utilizes several key recombinant vector constructs to engineer the HSCs and the mature iNKT cells. The primary vector for establishing the iNKT cell lineage is a self-inactivating (SIN) lentiviral vector, referred to herein as Lenti/iNKT-sr39TK. This vector is derived from a standard third-generation lentiviral backbone, such as pMNDW, which contains the necessary packaging signals (Ψ), Rev-responsive element (RRE), central polypurine tract (cPPT), and a woodchuck hepatitis virus posttranscriptional regulatory element (WPRE) to enhance transgene expression. The internal promoter is a strong constitutive promoter, such as the elongation factor 1-alpha short (EFS) promoter or the phosphoglycerate kinase (PGK) promoter, which drives the expression of a synthetic tricistronic cassette. This cassette encodes, in sequence, the human iNKT TCR alpha chain (Vα24-Jα18), a 2A self-cleaving peptide (e.g., F2A), the human iNKT TCR beta chain (Vβ11), a second 2A peptide (e.g., P2A), and the sr39TK suicide gene. The 2A peptides facilitate the co-translational cleavage of the single polypeptide into three separate, functional proteins, ensuring that every transduced cell expresses the complete iNKT TCR and the safety switch.

For the generation of CAR-engineered iNKT cells, a gamma-retroviral vector is used, designated as Retro/BCAR-tEGFR. This vector is based on a standard Moloney murine leukemia virus (MoMLV) backbone, such as pMP71, which includes the long terminal repeats (LTRs), packaging signal (Ψ), and a splice acceptor site. The internal expression cassette is driven by a strong viral promoter, such as the murine stem cell virus (MSCV) long terminal repeat (LTR) or an internal PGK promoter. The cassette encodes a synthetic bicistronic sequence comprising the BCMA-targeting CAR and a truncated epidermal growth factor receptor (tEGFR) reporter. The BCMA CAR is a second-generation construct consisting of an anti-BCMA single-chain variable fragment (scFv) derived from a high-affinity monoclonal antibody, fused to a human IgG4 hinge and CH2CH3 spacer, followed by the transmembrane and signaling domains of the 4-1BB (CD137) costimulatory molecule, and the cytoplasmic signaling domain of the CD3-zeta (CD3ζ) chain. This CAR sequence is linked via a P2A peptide to the tEGFR, which lacks the intracellular signaling domain and serves solely as a cell surface marker for tracking CAR expression by flow cytometry using anti-EGFR antibodies.

For the creation of stable tumor cell lines used in the assays, two additional lentiviral vectors are employed. The Lenti/FG vector is used to generate tumor cells expressing the firefly luciferase (Fluc) and enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP) dual reporters. This vector contains an EFS or PGK promoter driving a bicistronic cassette of Fluc linked to EGFP via a P2A peptide. The Lenti/CD1d vector is used to overexpress human CD1d on tumor cells and contains an EFS or PGK promoter driving the full-length human CD1d cDNA. All lentiviral vectors are pseudotyped with the vesicular stomatitis virus G-glycoprotein (VSV-G) to enable broad tropism. The nucleotide and amino acid sequences for all transgenes (iNKT TCRα, iNKT TCRβ, sr39TK, BCMA scFv, 4-1BB, CD3ζ, tEGFR, Fluc, EGFP, and CD1d) are well-known in the art and can be readily synthesized or cloned from existing sources. The specific arrangement of these elements within the vector backbones, as described, is a critical aspect of the invention that ensures the proper stoichiometry, function, and safety of the final engineered cell products.
