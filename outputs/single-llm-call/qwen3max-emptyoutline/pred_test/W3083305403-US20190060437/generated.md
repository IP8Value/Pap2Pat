# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of synthetic carbohydrate-based vaccines, particularly to stabilized glycomimetic antigens for use in conjugate vaccines against bacterial pathogens. More specifically, the invention provides novel synthetic oligomeric glycomimetics of the capsular polysaccharide (CPS) of *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup A (MenA), wherein the natural pyranose ring oxygen is replaced by a methylene group, thereby conferring enhanced hydrolytic stability while preserving or even improving immunogenicity and functional antibody induction. The invention further encompasses methods for the chemical synthesis of such glycomimetics, their conjugation to carrier proteins, and their use in stable, liquid-formulated glycoconjugate vaccines that are effective in eliciting protective immune responses against MenA infection. The technical field thus spans medicinal chemistry, vaccinology, immunology, and pharmaceutical formulation science, with particular relevance to the development of next-generation subunit vaccines that overcome the limitations of natural polysaccharide-based antigens, including instability, poor immunogenicity in vulnerable populations, and dependence on cold-chain storage.

## BACKGROUND ART

Vaccination remains one of the most effective public health interventions for the prevention of infectious diseases, with carbohydrate-based vaccines playing a critical role in combating bacterial infections caused by encapsulated pathogens such as *Haemophilus influenzae* type b, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, and *Neisseria meningitidis*. Among these, *N. meningitidis* serogroup A (MenA) is a leading cause of epidemic bacterial meningitis, particularly in the African meningitis belt, where it poses a significant threat to over 250 million people. Traditional vaccines based on purified capsular polysaccharides (CPS) have been developed and deployed successfully; however, these polysaccharide vaccines suffer from well-documented limitations. They are T cell-independent antigens, which results in poor immunogenicity in infants and young children, lack of immunological memory, and an inability to elicit booster responses upon repeated immunization. To overcome these drawbacks, glycoconjugate vaccines were developed by covalently linking the CPS to a carrier protein, such as tetanus toxoid (TT), diphtheria toxoid (DT), or the non-toxic mutant of diphtheria toxin known as Cross-Reacting Material 197 (CRM197). This conjugation converts the immune response to a T cell-dependent one, enabling robust B cell activation, class switching, affinity maturation, and the generation of long-lasting immunological memory, thereby providing durable protection even in early childhood.

Despite their clinical success, current MenA glycoconjugate vaccines face significant challenges related to the inherent chemical instability of the MenA CPS. The MenA CPS is composed of repeating units of (1→6)-linked 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-α-D-mannopyranosyl phosphate. This structure contains phosphodiester linkages that are highly susceptible to hydrolysis, particularly under physiological conditions. The instability is exacerbated by the axial orientation of the C2 acetamido group, which facilitates the cleavage of the anomeric phosphodiester bond. Consequently, the MenA CPS degrades rapidly in aqueous solution, especially at elevated temperatures or non-optimal pH, which compromises vaccine efficacy and necessitates strict cold-chain storage (2–8°C) or lyophilization to maintain stability. While conjugation to a carrier protein has been shown to modestly improve stability, the fundamental lability of the polysaccharide backbone remains a critical issue, particularly for deployment in resource-limited settings where cold-chain infrastructure is unreliable. Moreover, the natural MenA CPS exhibits variable O-acetylation, primarily at the C3 and C4 hydroxyl positions, which is crucial for its immunogenicity and the induction of functional, bactericidal antibodies. The heterogeneity and potential loss of these acetyl groups during manufacturing or storage further complicate the production of consistent and potent vaccines.

In parallel, the field of synthetic vaccinology has emerged as a promising alternative to traditional polysaccharide extraction. Synthetic carbohydrate vaccines offer the advantages of precise chemical definition, minimal batch-to-batch variation, and controlled conjugation chemistry. Efforts have focused on chemically synthesizing fragments of the natural MenA CPS to serve as haptens in conjugate vaccines. However, these synthetic “natural” fragments still inherit the same hydrolytic instability as the native polysaccharide, limiting their utility in stable liquid formulations. To address this, researchers have explored the design of glycomimetics—structurally modified analogs of natural glycans that retain the essential epitopes for immune recognition while incorporating stabilizing features. One such strategy involves replacing the ring oxygen atom in the pyranose sugar with a methylene group, creating a carbasugar analog. This modification eliminates the endocyclic oxygen, which is implicated in the hydrolytic mechanism of phosphodiester cleavage, thereby rendering the interglycosidic linkage non-hydrolyzable. Previous work by the inventors and others has demonstrated the synthesis of short carbaMenA oligomers (up to trimer, DP3) and shown that their conjugates can induce antibodies that cross-react with the native MenA CPS. However, these short oligomers exhibited poor antigenic mimicry, as evidenced by their weak ability to inhibit the binding of anti-MenA CPS antibodies to the native capsule. It was hypothesized that longer oligomeric structures would be required to effectively present the conformational epitope necessary for high-affinity antibody recognition and the induction of functional, bactericidal immune responses. Furthermore, the critical role of O-acetylation in MenA immunogenicity suggested that any successful glycomimetic must also incorporate this key structural feature. Thus, there existed a clear need in the art for a stable, synthetic MenA glycomimetic antigen of sufficient length and appropriate acetylation pattern that could serve as the basis for a next-generation, shelf-stable, liquid-formulated glycoconjugate vaccine with efficacy equal to or better than current benchmarks.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel class of stabilized glycomimetic antigens for use in conjugate vaccines against *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup A (MenA). The invention is based on the discovery that a synthetic oligomer of a carba-analog of the MenA capsular polysaccharide (CPS), specifically an octamer (DP8) with a degree of polymerization of eight repeating units, wherein the ring oxygen of each mannosamine residue is replaced by a methylene group, serves as a highly effective and stable mimic of the native MenA CPS. This carbaMenA DP8 oligomer is remarkably resistant to hydrolytic degradation, maintaining its structural integrity under conditions that cause rapid depolymerization of the natural polysaccharide. Crucially, the invention further discloses that the immunogenicity and functional efficacy of this carbaMenA DP8 antigen are critically dependent on the presence of O-acetyl esters at the C3 and/or C4 hydroxyl positions of the carba-sugar residues. The inventors have developed a method to introduce a controlled level of random O-acetylation onto the carbaMenA DP8 scaffold, resulting in an Ac-carbaMenA DP8 glycomimetic that closely resembles the natural, variably O-acetylated MenA CPS.

The invention demonstrates that a glycoconjugate vaccine comprising this Ac-carbaMenA DP8 antigen covalently linked to a carrier protein, such as CRM197, elicits a robust immune response in a murine model. The antibodies induced by this synthetic conjugate are not only capable of binding to the native MenA CPS but also exhibit high levels of functional, complement-mediated bactericidal activity against MenA bacteria. Significantly, the bactericidal titers achieved with the Ac-carbaMenA DP8-CRM197 conjugate are statistically non-inferior to those induced by a benchmark vaccine based on the natural MenA CPS of average degree of polymerization ~15. This finding represents a major breakthrough, as it provides the first proof-of-principle that a fully synthetic, stabilized glycomimetic can match the protective efficacy of a natural polysaccharide-based conjugate vaccine. The invention therefore solves the long-standing problem of MenA CPS instability by providing a chemically defined, hydrolytically stable antigen that does not require cold-chain storage and can be formulated in a liquid state, while simultaneously delivering a potent and protective immune response. The core of the invention lies in the synergistic combination of three key elements: the carba-sugar backbone for stability, the octameric length for optimal epitope presentation, and the strategic O-acetylation for authentic mimicry of the natural pathogen-associated molecular pattern.

### Particular Embodiments of the Invention

In a particular embodiment, the invention provides a glycomimetic compound for use as a vaccine antigen, the compound having the formula (I):
[Formula I: A linear oligomer consisting of eight repeating units. Each repeating unit is a 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-α-D-mannopyranosyl analog where the ring oxygen is replaced by a -CH2- group, linked via (1→6) phosphodiester bonds. The compound is optionally substituted with acetyl groups on the hydroxyls at the C3 and/or C4 positions of the carba-sugar rings, with an average degree of acetylation of between 50% and 90%. The oligomer is terminated at the reducing end with a linker moiety, such as a 6-aminohexanol spacer, for conjugation to a carrier protein.]

In another particular embodiment, the invention provides a glycoconjugate vaccine composition comprising the glycomimetic compound of formula (I) covalently conjugated to a carrier protein. The carrier protein is selected from the group consisting of CRM197, tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, and outer membrane protein complex. The molar ratio of saccharide to protein in the conjugate is between 5:1 and 25:1.

In a further particular embodiment, the invention provides a method for the synthesis of the glycomimetic compound of formula (I). The method comprises the iterative assembly of a carba-mannose phosphoramidite building block on a solid support or in solution phase, using a cycle of phosphoramidite coupling, oxidation, and detritylation to build the oligomer chain up to a degree of polymerization of eight. The method further includes the steps of global deprotection to remove all protecting groups and, subsequently, the introduction of O-acetyl groups by treatment with acetic anhydride in the presence of a base catalyst, to achieve a desired level of acetylation.

In yet another particular embodiment, the invention provides a stable, liquid pharmaceutical formulation for parenteral administration, comprising the glycoconjugate vaccine composition described above, a physiologically acceptable buffer, and optionally an adjuvant such as aluminum phosphate. The formulation is stable for at least 12 months when stored at 25°C.

In a final particular embodiment, the invention provides a method for inducing an immune response against *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup A in a subject, the method comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of the glycoconjugate vaccine composition. The immune response is characterized by the production of IgG antibodies that are bactericidal against MenA bacteria in a serum bactericidal assay (SBA).

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The invention will now be described in detail by way of example, with reference to specific embodiments and experimental procedures. It is to be understood that these examples are provided for illustrative purposes and are not intended to limit the scope of the invention in any way.

The synthesis of the nonacetylated carbaMenA oligomers of varying lengths (DP1 to DP8) was carried out using a solution-phase phosphoramidite chemistry approach. The key building block, a carba-mannose phosphoramidite, was prepared from commercially available 3,4,6-tri-O-acetyl-D-glucal through a multi-step sequence that established the carbasugar core and installed the necessary protecting groups. Specifically, the primary silyl ether and acetyl esters were removed from a known intermediate to yield a diol, which was then regioselectively protected with a dimethoxytrityl (DMTr) group on the primary alcohol. This DMTr-protected alcohol was subsequently converted into the phosphoramidite elongation block by reaction with 2-cyanoethyl-N,N-diisopropylchlorophosphoramidite. The oligomer assembly was initiated by coupling this phosphoramidite to a phosphoramidite reagent containing a 6-aminohexanol spacer, which would later serve as the conjugation handle. The elongation cycle for each subsequent monomer addition consisted of three main steps: (1) coupling of the carba-mannose phosphoramidite to the growing chain’s terminal primary alcohol using dicyanoimidazole (DCI) as an activator; (2) oxidation of the resulting phosphite triester intermediate to the more stable phosphate triester using (1S)-(+)-(10-camphorsulfonyl)-oxaziridine (CSO); and (3) removal of the DMTr protecting group from the newly added monomer using a solution of trichloroacetic acid (TCA) in dichloromethane, thereby exposing the primary alcohol for the next coupling cycle. This iterative process was repeated to build oligomers up to the octamer (DP8). After the final elongation, the crude oligomers were purified by size-exclusion chromatography. The final deprotection to yield the free oligomeric acids was achieved in a two-step sequence: first, the 2-cyanoethyl phosphate protecting groups were removed by treatment with aqueous ammonia, and second, the benzyl ether and carboxybenzyl carbamate protecting groups were cleaved by catalytic hydrogenation over palladium black. This methodology proved to be highly efficient, with good to excellent yields for each elongation cycle, culminating in the successful preparation of the target carbaMenA DP8.

To confirm the enhanced stability conferred by the carba-sugar modification, an accelerated stability study was performed. The carbaMenA DP8 was compared to a natural MenA CPS fragment of average degree of polymerization ~15 (avDP~15), both in its native acetylated form and in a de-O-acetylated form. The samples were incubated in 5 mM sodium acetate buffer at pH 7 and 37°C, and their integrity was monitored over time by 1H and 31P NMR spectroscopy. The degree of polymerization was calculated from the 31P NMR spectra by comparing the signal intensity of internal phosphodiester groups to that of terminal phosphomonoester groups. The results were unequivocal: the carbaMenA DP8 showed no detectable degradation even after 56 days of incubation, as evidenced by the absence of any new phosphomonoester peaks in the 31P NMR spectrum. In stark contrast, the de-O-acetylated natural MenA fragment underwent rapid and severe depolymerization, dropping from avDP~15 to avDP7 within just 28 days. The acetylated natural MenA fragment was more stable but still showed a significant reduction in size, from avDP18 to avDP12.8 over the same period. This experiment conclusively demonstrated that the replacement of the ring oxygen with a methylene group in the carbaMenA structure provides exceptional resistance to hydrolytic cleavage, a critical property for a stable vaccine antigen.

The antigenic potential of the synthesized carbaMenA oligomers (DP1-DP8) was then evaluated in vitro. A competitive ELISA was used to assess their ability to inhibit the binding of a functional, bactericidal monoclonal antibody (mAb JW-A1) to the native MenA CPS. The results revealed a clear length dependence: oligomers shorter than DP8 (i.e., DP1-DP7) showed no significant inhibition, while the carbaMenA DP8 was able to compete for binding, albeit with a much lower affinity (IC50 four orders of magnitude higher) than the native polysaccharide. This indicated that a minimum of eight repeating units is required for the carbaMenA structure to effectively present the conformational epitope recognized by this protective mAb. Similar results were obtained when testing against a polyclonal anti-MenA serum, where again only the DP8 showed notable inhibitory activity. Based on these findings, the carbaMenA DP6 and DP8 were selected for conjugation to the carrier protein CRM197 to evaluate their immunogenicity in vivo. The conjugation was performed using a two-step procedure involving activation of the oligomer’s terminal amine with di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate (SIDEA) to form an active NHS ester, followed by reaction with lysine residues on the CRM197 protein. The resulting neoglycoconjugates were purified by dialysis and characterized by MALDI-TOF MS, which confirmed saccharide-to-protein molar ratios of 16.9 for the DP6 conjugate and 10.4 for the DP8 conjugate.

An immunogenicity study was conducted in BALB/c mice, where groups were immunized with the carbaMenA-CRM197 conjugates, alongside control groups receiving conjugates made from natural MenA CPS of avDP8.5 and avDP~15. After three immunizations, the elicited antisera were analyzed by ELISA. The results showed that both carbaMenA conjugates induced a strong anti-hapten response. However, when tested for cross-reactivity with the native, acetylated MenA CPS, the antibody titers were significantly lower than those from the natural polysaccharide controls. The sera from the carbaMenA DP8 group did show some capacity to recognize the de-O-acetylated CPS backbone, confirming that the carba-sugar structure was being presented as an antigen. A serum bactericidal assay (SBA), the gold standard for predicting vaccine efficacy against meningococcal disease, was then performed. The pooled sera from the carbaMenA DP8 group showed low but detectable bactericidal activity with human complement (titer of 128), while the DP6 conjugate was essentially inactive. Although this was a promising sign of functionality for the DP8 conjugate, its performance was still markedly inferior to the benchmark natural conjugate.

To bridge this efficacy gap, the inventors hypothesized that the lack of O-acetyl groups on the carbaMenA DP8 was the primary reason for its suboptimal performance, given the known importance of acetylation for MenA immunogenicity. Therefore, the carbaMenA DP8 lead candidate was optimized by introducing O-acetyl esters. A temporary Boc protecting group was first installed on the terminal amine of the linker to prevent its acetylation. The free hydroxyl groups on the carba-sugar rings were then acetylated by treatment with acetic anhydride and imidazole, a condition carefully controlled to achieve a level of acetylation (~75%) comparable to that found on the natural MenA CPS. Subsequent Boc deprotection yielded the conjugation-ready Ac-carbaMenA DP8. NMR analysis confirmed that the acetyl groups were distributed across the C3 and C4 positions, including a significant proportion of residues that were di-acetylated at both positions, closely mimicking the natural acetylation pattern.

The Ac-carbaMenA DP8 was then subjected to a more sensitive competitive Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR) assay with the anti-MenA mAb. The results were dramatic: the acetylated mimic showed a four-log improvement in its inhibitory capacity compared to the nonacetylated DP8, and its binding affinity was nearly on par with that of the natural MenA oligomers (avDP8 and avDP~15). This compelling in vitro data prompted the conjugation of Ac-carbaMenA DP8 to CRM197 using the same protocol as before. A new immunogenicity study in mice was conducted, directly comparing the Ac-carbaMenA DP8-CRM197 conjugate to the natural avDP~15-CRM197 benchmark. The results were groundbreaking. The synthetic conjugate induced anti-MenA CPS IgG antibody titers that were not only comparable but actually slightly higher than the benchmark. Most importantly, the SBA titers, measured using both rabbit and human complement on individual mouse sera, were statistically non-inferior to those of the natural conjugate. This demonstrated that the Ac-carbaMenA DP8 glycomimetic, when presented as a glycoconjugate, is a true functional mimic of the native MenA CPS, capable of eliciting a protective immune response of equivalent potency. This final set of experiments constitutes the definitive mode for carrying out the invention, providing a clear and reproducible path to a stable, synthetic, and highly efficacious MenA vaccine.
