## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to quantum sensing and, more particularly, to a method and apparatus for enhancing the sensitivity of alternating current (AC) magnetic field detection using nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond through quantum logic enhanced readout protocols. The invention further pertains to the integration of hybrid electronic-nuclear spin systems as two-qubit quantum sensors within solid-state platforms, enabling high-fidelity state transfer, repetitive non-destructive readout, and substantial improvements in signal-to-noise ratio and overall magnetometric sensitivity. The techniques disclosed herein are applicable to a broad class of quantum metrology applications, including but not limited to nanoscale nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), precision electrometry, thermometry, strain sensing, and fundamental physics experiments such as dark matter detection.

## BACKGROUND

Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond have emerged as leading candidates for solid-state quantum sensors due to their long-lived spin coherence at room temperature, optical addressability, and sensitivity to a variety of physical fields, including magnetic fields, electric fields, temperature, and mechanical stress. An NV center consists of a substitutional nitrogen atom adjacent to a lattice vacancy in the diamond crystal structure. The negatively charged state (NV⁻) possesses an electronic spin triplet ground state (S = 1) that can be initialized into the mₛ = 0 sublevel via green laser illumination and read out through spin-dependent fluorescence. This optical interface enables high-fidelity initialization and measurement, forming the basis for many quantum sensing protocols.

A key limitation in NV-based sensing, particularly when using ensembles of NV centers, is the relatively low signal contrast during optical readout—typically on the order of a few percent—which fundamentally limits the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and thus the sensitivity of the sensor. While ensemble averaging improves statistical precision, the shot-noise-limited readout fidelity remains constrained by the intrinsic inefficiency of converting spin state information into detectable photons. Moreover, the electronic spin coherence time (T₂) of NV centers in ensemble samples is often limited by dipolar interactions between neighboring NV centers or paramagnetic impurities, restricting the maximum useful sensing duration in dynamical decoupling sequences.

To overcome these limitations, researchers have explored coupling the NV electronic spin to nearby nuclear spins, such as the intrinsic ¹⁵N or ¹³C nuclei, to create hybrid two-qubit systems. Nuclear spins offer exceptionally long coherence times (T₁ and T₂ on the order of milliseconds to seconds) due to their weak coupling to the environment, making them ideal quantum memory elements. Quantum logic operations, such as controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates and SWAP gates, can be implemented between the electronic and nuclear spins using resonant microwave and radiofrequency (RF) pulses, enabling coherent transfer of quantum information from the fast but noisy electronic sensor to the stable nuclear memory.

Recent advances have demonstrated the use of such hybrid systems for improved quantum memory and error correction. However, the application of these techniques to enhance the sensitivity of AC magnetometry—particularly in ensemble settings where global control is used—has remained largely unexplored. Conventional AC magnetometry with NV centers relies on dynamical decoupling sequences (e.g., XY8, CPMG) to filter out noise and selectively respond to oscillating magnetic fields at specific frequencies. In correlation spectroscopy, a variant of this technique, two decoupling sequences are separated by a variable delay (T_corr), and the resulting signal reveals the spectral content of the target field. Despite its utility, the sensitivity of such methods is ultimately capped by the readout fidelity of the electronic spin.

One approach to circumvent this bottleneck is to encode the sensor state into a long-lived nuclear memory and then perform repeated readouts by mapping the nuclear state back onto the electronic spin multiple times. This "quantum logic enhanced" (QLE) readout protocol leverages the extended lifetime of the nuclear spin to accumulate multiple measurements before the stored information decays, thereby boosting the effective SNR. However, practical implementation of this scheme requires precise control over spin-spin interactions, suppression of relaxation channels, and optimization of pulse sequences to minimize overhead while maximizing information extraction.

Prior art has not adequately addressed the integration of repetitive quantum logic readout into standard AC magnetometry protocols, nor has it quantified the resulting sensitivity enhancements under realistic experimental conditions. Furthermore, existing methods fail to account for the trade-offs between sensing duration, readout overhead, and nuclear spin lifetime, which are critical for determining the optimal operating regime of QLE sensing. There remains a need for a robust, scalable, and broadly applicable framework that combines quantum memory, repetitive readout, and dynamical decoupling to achieve order-of-magnitude improvements in quantum sensing performance.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method and system for quantum logic enhanced (QLE) AC magnetic field sensing using an ensemble of nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond, each coupled to a proximal nuclear spin (e.g., ¹⁵N) to form a hybrid two-qubit sensor. The invention overcomes the fundamental limitations of conventional NV magnetometry by transferring the quantum state of the electronic sensor spin to a long-lived nuclear memory spin via a high-fidelity SWAP operation, resetting the electronic spin, and then repeatedly mapping the nuclear memory state back onto the electronic spin for optical readout. This repetitive readout protocol, performed within the nuclear spin lifetime T₁, significantly enhances the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and, under appropriate conditions, the overall magnetic field sensitivity.

In one embodiment, the method comprises the following steps: (a) applying a dynamical decoupling sequence (e.g., XY8, DROID-60) to the NV electronic spins to sense an AC magnetic field, thereby encoding the field-induced phase into the electronic spin population; (b) executing a SWAP gate between the electronic spin and the coupled nuclear spin to transfer the encoded state to the nuclear memory; (c) optically reinitializing the electronic spin to the mₛ = 0 state; (d) applying a series of N controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates, each conditioned on the nuclear spin state and followed by an optical readout pulse, to repetitively map the nuclear memory state onto the electronic spin and measure it optically; and (e) combining the N readout signals using optimal weighting to maximize the SNR.

The invention further includes the use of a bias magnetic field to suppress flip-flop relaxation channels between electronic and nuclear spins, thereby extending the nuclear T₁ lifetime. Measurements demonstrate that T₁ increases with magnetic field strength, following a power law with an exponent near 2, consistent with theoretical predictions. At a bias field of 3700 Gauss, the ¹⁵N nuclear T₁ reaches 3.44 milliseconds, enabling thousands of readout cycles.

The QLE protocol is integrated with both standard dynamical decoupling and correlation spectroscopy techniques. In correlation spectroscopy, two decoupling blocks are separated by a variable correlation time T_corr, and the QLE readout is applied after the second block. The invention accounts for the variable sensing duration T_sense (which depends on T_corr) when evaluating sensitivity enhancement, ensuring fair comparison with conventional methods by equalizing total acquisition time.

Experimental results show a 33.3-fold improvement in SNR with N = 2000 readout cycles. More importantly, when accounting for readout overhead (SWAP duration T_SWAP = 16.5 µs and per-readout time T_QLR = 3 µs), the sensitivity enhancement η_QLE reaches up to 2.4 for XY8 sequences, 5.6 for DROID-60 sequences, and 11.3 for correlation spectroscopy under optimal conditions. These enhancements occur when the sensing duration T_sense exceeds the SWAP overhead, a condition readily achievable with advanced decoupling sequences or low-nitrogen-doped diamond samples.

The invention is not limited to magnetometry. It is broadly applicable to any NV-based sensing modality that benefits from extended coherence or repetitive readout, including electric field sensing, temperature mapping, strain detection, and searches for exotic physics. The method is compatible with ensemble measurements using global control, making it scalable and practical for real-world applications. Additionally, the approach can be extended to systems with multiple nuclear spins or other solid-state spin defects, paving the way for advanced quantum error correction and multi-qubit sensing architectures.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a quantum logic enhanced (QLE) sensing protocol that dramatically improves the sensitivity of AC magnetic field detection using ensembles of nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond. The core innovation lies in the strategic use of the intrinsic ¹⁵N nuclear spin as a quantum memory to store the sensor state, followed by repetitive, non-destructive readout via controlled quantum logic operations. This approach decouples the sensing duration from the readout fidelity constraint, enabling orders-of-magnitude gains in performance under favorable conditions.

The system employs a high-purity diamond chip containing a nitrogen-doped layer with a concentration of approximately 14 parts per million (ppm) of total nitrogen, resulting in an NV⁻ density of about 2.3 ppm after irradiation and annealing. The diamond is cut with its [100] axis perpendicular to the top surface and polished to expose four [110] edges, with the NV symmetry axis ([111]) aligned parallel to an externally applied bias magnetic field. This orientation maximizes the Zeeman splitting and minimizes transverse field components that could induce decoherence. A feedback-stabilized electromagnet generates a tunable bias field ranging from 0 to 4000 Gauss, which is critical for suppressing flip-flop relaxation between electronic and nuclear spins—a dominant T₁ decay channel at low fields.

Optical control is achieved using a 532 nm solid-state laser (130 mW power) focused to a 15 µm spot and gated by an acousto-optic modulator (AOM). Each optical pulse lasts 3 µs, comprising 1 µs for fluorescence readout and 2 µs for reinitialization of the NV electronic spin into the mₛ = 0 state. Fluorescence is collected via a liquid light guide and detected by a photodiode. Microwave (MW) and radiofrequency (RF) pulses are generated by an arbitrary waveform generator and amplified before being delivered to the sample through a single-loop coil. MW pulses (12 GS/s resolution) drive transitions between electronic spin states, while RF pulses (1–3 MHz) manipulate the ¹⁵N nuclear spin, conditioned on the electronic state, to implement quantum logic gates.

The QLE protocol begins with a standard AC magnetometry sequence. For instance, an XY8-6 or DROID-60 dynamical decoupling sequence is applied to the NV ensemble to sense an external AC magnetic field at a target frequency (e.g., 1 MHz). During this sensing interval T_sense, the NV spins accumulate a phase proportional to the field amplitude, which is mapped onto the population difference between mₛ = 0 and mₛ = ±1 states. Immediately after sensing, a SWAP gate is executed between the electronic spin and the ¹⁵N nuclear spin. This gate, implemented via a carefully calibrated sequence of MW and RF pulses, coherently exchanges the quantum states of the two spins with an estimated fidelity of 93%. As a result, the sensor state—now encoded in the nuclear spin—is preserved, while the electronic spin is left in a known state.

The electronic spin is then optically reset to mₛ = 0, preparing it for readout. A series of N quantum logic readout (QLR) cycles follows. Each cycle consists of a CNOT_e|n gate—where the electronic spin is the control and the nuclear spin is the target—followed by a 3 µs optical pulse. The CNOT gate maps the nuclear memory state onto the electronic spin: if the nuclear spin is |↑⟩, the electronic spin flips; if |↓⟩, it remains unchanged. The subsequent optical readout measures the electronic spin state, indirectly revealing the nuclear state. Because the nuclear spin is not directly disturbed by the optical pulse (due to its weak coupling to light), this process can be repeated many times before the nuclear state decays via T₁ relaxation.

The fluorescence signal from each of the N readouts is recorded, yielding a time series {S₁, S₂, ..., S_N}. Due to T₁ decay, the signal amplitude A_n decreases with cycle index n, following a stretched exponential. To maximize the final SNR, the signals are combined using optimal weighting: w_n = A_n / σ_n², where σ_n is the noise standard deviation at cycle n. This weighting ensures that early, high-amplitude readouts contribute more to the final estimate than later, noisier ones. The resulting SNR scales as √(∑ w_n² A_n² / ∑ w_n² σ_n²), which, under photon-shot-noise dominance, leads to significant gains over single-shot readout.

The nuclear T₁ lifetime is a critical parameter governing the number of useful readouts. Flip-flop transitions between the NV electron and ¹⁵N nucleus are the primary T₁ mechanism, and these are suppressed by increasing the bias magnetic field, which detunes the electron and nuclear Larmor frequencies. Experiments show that T₁ increases from ~0.5 ms at 200 G to 3.44 ms at 3700 G, following a power law T₁ ∝ B^1.8, consistent with a quadratic dependence predicted by theory. All QLE measurements are conducted at 3700 G to maximize T₁. Laser power also affects T₁, as optical excitation can induce ionization or phonon-mediated relaxation; thus, laser intensity is fixed at 130 mW, a value that balances rapid reinitialization (75% polarization in 3 µs) with acceptable T₁ degradation.

The QLE protocol is demonstrated in two sensing modalities: standard dynamical decoupling and correlation spectroscopy. In the former, a single decoupling block of duration T_sense is used. Sensitivity η is defined as η = σ_1s / |dS/dB_AC|, where σ_1s is the contrast uncertainty for 1-second averaging and |dS/dB_AC| is the signal slope at zero field. Using XY8-6 (T_sense = 24 µs), QLE achieves η_QLE = 2.4 at N ≈ 150. With DROID-60-6 (T_sense = 144 µs)—a sequence that suppresses NV-NV dipolar interactions and extends T₂—the enhancement rises to η_QLE = 5.6 at N ≈ 400. This occurs because the longer T_sense amortizes the fixed SWAP overhead (16.5 µs), making the QLE investment worthwhile.

In correlation spectroscopy, two identical decoupling blocks are separated by a variable delay T_corr. The total sensing duration T_sense = 2τ + T_corr (where τ is the interpulse spacing) varies with T_corr. To fairly compare QLE with conventional sensing, the number of conventional repetitions M is chosen such that M × (T_sense + T_QLR) = T_sense + T_SWAP + N × T_QLR. Over T_corr = 0–1.5 ms, QLE yields up to η_QLE = 11.3 at N ≈ 1000. This dramatic gain arises because T_sense becomes comparable to T₁ (~3.4 ms), allowing hundreds of readouts without significant decay.

The invention’s versatility extends beyond magnetometry. Any NV-based sensor that encodes information into the electronic spin state can benefit from QLE readout. For example, dynamical decoupling sequences sensitive to temperature or strain typically operate with T_sense > 10 µs—sufficient to exceed T_SWAP and realize η_QLE > 1. Moreover, the protocol is compatible with hyperpolarization techniques for NV-NMR, where enhanced sensitivity could enable single-molecule detection at micron scales.

Future improvements include using diamond with lower nitrogen concentration ([N] ≈ 0.8 ppm), which would extend T₂ and thus T_sense by ~18×, potentially pushing η_QLE beyond 50. Systems with multiple nuclear spins (e.g., ¹³C) could enable error-corrected readout or parallel sensing channels. The core principle—decoupling sensing from readout via quantum memory—is general and could be adapted to other solid-state qubits, such as silicon vacancies or rare-earth ions.

In summary, the invention provides a practical, high-gain enhancement to quantum sensing by merging quantum memory, repetitive readout, and optimized control. It transforms the NV ensemble from a shot-noise-limited detector into a high-SNR quantum instrument, opening new frontiers in nanoscale metrology and fundamental science.
