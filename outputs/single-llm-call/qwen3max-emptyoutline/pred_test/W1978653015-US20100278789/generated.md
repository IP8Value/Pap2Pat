# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD AND BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of regenerative medicine and cellular therapy, and more specifically to methods and compositions for the redifferentiation of expanded human pancreatic β-cells into functional insulin-producing cells suitable for therapeutic use in the treatment of diabetes mellitus. Diabetes is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both. Type 1 diabetes arises from autoimmune destruction of pancreatic β-cells, whereas type 2 diabetes involves progressive β-cell dysfunction and loss in the context of insulin resistance. In both forms, the ultimate pathophysiological consequence is an absolute or relative deficiency of biologically active insulin, leading to impaired glucose homeostasis and long-term complications affecting multiple organ systems.

Cell replacement therapy using functional insulin-producing cells represents a promising curative strategy for diabetes, particularly for patients with type 1 disease who lack endogenous β-cell mass. However, the clinical translation of such therapies has been severely limited by the scarcity of donor islets and the inability to generate sufficient quantities of functional β-cells in vitro. Primary human islets, when isolated and placed in culture, rapidly lose their differentiated phenotype—a process known as dedifferentiation—characterized by downregulation of key β-cell transcription factors (e.g., PDX1, MAFA, NKX6.1), loss of insulin expression, acquisition of mesenchymal markers, nuclear enlargement, and increased proliferative capacity. While this dedifferentiated state permits substantial in vitro expansion of islet-derived cells, the resulting population consists largely of non-insulin-producing progenitor-like or transdifferentiated cells that are functionally inadequate for transplantation.

Efforts to reverse this dedifferentiation and restore β-cell identity have included the use of serum-free media, high glucose, nicotinamide, exendin-4, activin A, and other soluble factors, which collectively can induce modest levels of insulin re-expression in a subset of expanded islet cells. However, these approaches typically yield low efficiency of redifferentiation (often less than 25% of the original β-cell fraction) and fail to fully recapitulate the molecular and functional characteristics of mature human β-cells. Moreover, the heterogeneous nature of expanded islet cultures—comprising dedifferentiated β-cells, α-cells, δ-cells, and possibly ductal or mesenchymal derivatives—complicates the interpretation of redifferentiation outcomes and raises concerns about the origin of newly insulin-positive cells.

A critical barrier to efficient redifferentiation lies in the persistent activity of developmental signaling pathways that maintain cells in a progenitor or undifferentiated state. Among these, the NOTCH signaling pathway plays a pivotal role in pancreatic development by inhibiting endocrine differentiation through the transcriptional repressor HES1 (Hairy and Enhancer of Split-1). In embryonic pancreas, HES1 suppresses the expression of pro-endocrine genes such as NEUROG3, thereby preventing premature differentiation of multipotent progenitor cells. In adult β-cells, NOTCH signaling is largely quiescent; however, upon dissociation and culture, expanded islet cells reactivate HES1 expression, which contributes to the maintenance of a dedifferentiated, proliferative state and actively represses the re-expression of β-cell-specific genes.

Prior art has demonstrated that inhibition of NOTCH signaling can promote endocrine differentiation in stem cell models, but its role in the redifferentiation of *expanded human adult β-cells*—a therapeutically relevant cell source—has remained unexplored. Furthermore, while RNA interference (RNAi) has been used to knock down HES1 in various contexts, no method has been established that combines HES1 inhibition with optimized redifferentiation conditions to achieve high-efficiency, lineage-specific restoration of functional β-cell identity in vitro and in vivo.

The background art thus reveals a significant unmet need: a robust, scalable, and clinically translatable method to convert expanded, dedifferentiated human islet cells—particularly those of β-cell origin—back into glucose-responsive, insulin-secreting cells capable of reversing diabetes in vivo. Existing protocols suffer from low yield, incomplete maturation, ambiguous cellular origins, and reliance on undefined or suboptimal culture conditions. There is therefore a pressing demand for an improved redifferentiation strategy that addresses these limitations by targeting key molecular brakes on β-cell identity, such as HES1, while synergizing with microenvironmental cues that support functional maturation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for the efficient redifferentiation of expanded human pancreatic β-cells into functional insulin-producing cells through the targeted inhibition of HES1, a downstream effector of the NOTCH signaling pathway. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that HES1 acts as a master repressor of β-cell redifferentiation in cultured, expanded human islet cells and that its inhibition—alone or in combination with defined redifferentiation media—induces robust re-expression of insulin and key β-cell transcription factors, reduces nuclear size, promotes cell clustering, induces growth arrest, and restores lineage-specific identity exclusively in cells of β-cell origin.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for redifferentiating expanded human islet cells, comprising the steps of: (a) providing a population of human islet cells that have been expanded in vitro for at least five passages under conditions that induce dedifferentiation; and (b) inhibiting HES1 expression or activity in said cells, thereby inducing redifferentiation into insulin-producing cells. The inhibition of HES1 may be achieved through genetic means, such as RNA interference using short hairpin RNA (shRNA) delivered via lentiviral vectors, or through pharmacological agents that suppress NOTCH signaling upstream of HES1, although the latter are not exemplified herein and are contemplated as future embodiments.

In another aspect, the invention provides an enhanced redifferentiation method wherein HES1 inhibition is combined with culture in a serum-free medium (SFM) or a specialized redifferentiation medium (RM). The RM comprises a basal medium (e.g., CMRL 1066) supplemented with high glucose (25 mM), nicotinamide (10 mM), exendin-4 (8 nM), activin A (8 nM), insulin/transferrin/selenium (ITS), and bovine serum albumin (1% BSA). Cells are preferably seeded in ultra-low attachment plates to promote three-dimensional cluster formation, which mimics native islet architecture and enhances redifferentiation efficiency.

A key feature of the invention is its lineage specificity. Using a dual-lentiviral lineage tracing system—wherein β-cells are permanently labeled with enhanced green fluorescent protein (eGFP) under the control of the insulin promoter via tamoxifen-inducible Cre recombinase—the inventors demonstrate that redifferentiation occurs predominantly, if not exclusively, in cells originally derived from β-cells (eGFP⁺ cells). This finding confirms that the observed insulin re-expression represents true *redifferentiation* rather than transdifferentiation of non-β endocrine cells or de novo differentiation from progenitors.

The redifferentiated cells exhibit multiple hallmarks of functional β-cells: they express insulin, C-peptide, islet amyloid polypeptide (IAPP), and key transcription factors including PDX1, NKX2.2, NKX6.1, MAFA, and NEUROD1; they show mutually exclusive hormone expression (no co-expression of insulin with glucagon or somatostatin); they undergo growth arrest as evidenced by reduced BrdU incorporation and upregulation of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors p57 (CDKN1C) and p27 (CDKN1B); and they display reduced nuclear area, consistent with reversal of culture-induced dedifferentiation.

Critically, the invention demonstrates therapeutic efficacy in vivo. Expanded human islet cells treated with HES1 shRNA and transplanted under the kidney capsule of streptozotocin-induced diabetic NOD-SCID mice are capable of further maturing in the host environment, leading to significant upregulation of insulin transcripts (over 400-fold compared to control cultured cells), secretion of human C-peptide into the bloodstream, and correction of hyperglycemia in a subset of recipient animals. This establishes that HES1 inhibition serves as a “priming” step that renders expanded β-cells competent for functional maturation in vivo.

The invention further discloses that overexpression of p57 alone is insufficient to induce redifferentiation, indicating that HES1 inhibition exerts its effects through mechanisms beyond mere cell cycle arrest. Instead, HES1 repression appears to coordinately derepress a network of β-cell identity genes while simultaneously enabling morphological and microenvironmental changes conducive to redifferentiation, such as cell aggregation.

In summary, the present invention overcomes longstanding limitations in β-cell expansion and redifferentiation by identifying HES1 as a central molecular barrier and providing a practical method to overcome it. The resulting protocol achieves significantly higher redifferentiation efficiency—approximately 50% of the original β-cell population—compared to prior art methods (~20–25%), and produces cells with advanced differentiation status suitable for therapeutic application. The invention thus enables the generation of clinically relevant quantities of functional human β-cells from a single donor islet preparation, addressing a major bottleneck in diabetes cell therapy.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to methods for the redifferentiation of expanded human pancreatic β-cells through the inhibition of HES1, a transcriptional repressor acting downstream of the NOTCH signaling pathway. The following detailed description sets forth preferred embodiments of the invention, including materials, methods, and experimental conditions that enable the practice of the claimed subject matter. It will be understood by those skilled in the art that the invention is not limited to the specific details described herein but encompasses variations and modifications within the scope of the appended claims.

Human islet cells used in the practice of the invention are obtained from cadaveric donors and isolated using standard collagenase digestion and purification techniques known in the art. Upon receipt, islets are allowed to recover for 2–3 days in culture before dissociation. Dissociation into single cells is performed using enzymatic (e.g., trypsin or Accutase) or mechanical methods, followed by plating in a growth medium that supports proliferation and expansion. The preferred growth medium is CMRL 1066 supplemented with 5.6 mM D-glucose, 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 units/mL penicillin, 100 µg/mL streptomycin, 100 µg/mL gentamycin, and 5 µg/mL amphotericin B. Cells are maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂ and are refed twice weekly, with passaging at a 1:2 ratio once per week. Under these conditions, human islet cells undergo significant expansion over 5–7 passages (approximately 5–7 weeks), during which they progressively lose β-cell markers and acquire a dedifferentiated, proliferative phenotype.

To track the lineage of β-cells during expansion and redifferentiation, a dual-lentiviral lineage tracing system is employed. This system utilizes two lentiviral vectors: (1) RIP-Cre/ER, which expresses a tamoxifen-inducible Cre recombinase under the control of the rat insulin promoter (RIP), and (2) pTrip-loxP-NEO-STOP-loxP-eGFP, a reporter vector in which eGFP expression is blocked by a loxP-flanked STOP cassette. Primary islet cells are co-infected with both vectors within the first 24 hours of culture, when residual insulin promoter activity is still present. Subsequent addition of 4-hydroxytamoxifen (1 µM final concentration) induces nuclear translocation of Cre-ER, resulting in excision of the STOP cassette and permanent eGFP expression exclusively in cells that were β-cells at the time of labeling. This labeling is irreversible and heritable through cell divisions, allowing unambiguous identification of β-cell-derived (BCD) cells throughout subsequent expansion and redifferentiation experiments. Labeled cells may be purified by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) using a FACS Aria sorter or similar instrument, yielding populations enriched for eGFP⁺ (BCD) or eGFP⁻ (non-BCD) cells, typically with 80 ± 10% purity.

HES1 inhibition is achieved through RNA interference using a lentiviral vector encoding a short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting human HES1 (GenBank accession number HC916590). A non-targeting shRNA cloned in the same pLKO.1 backbone, which also confers puromycin resistance, serves as a negative control. Lentiviral particles are produced in 293T cells using standard calcium phosphate or PEI-based transfection protocols. Expanded islet cells at passages 5–7 are infected at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 2.5:1 in the presence of 8 µg/mL Polybrene to enhance transduction efficiency. Infection efficiency exceeds 90%, as confirmed by puromycin selection and qPCR. Following overnight incubation, the virus-containing medium is replaced with fresh growth medium, and cells are allowed to recover for 2–4 days before initiation of redifferentiation protocols.

Redifferentiation is induced by transferring HES1-inhibited cells into one of three culture conditions: (1) standard growth medium (control), (2) serum-free medium (SFM), or (3) redifferentiation medium (RM). SFM consists of CMRL 1066 with 5.6 mM glucose, supplemented with 1% BSA (fraction V), ITS (insulin/transferrin/selenium), 50 units/mL penicillin, and 50 µg/mL streptomycin. RM is identical to SFM but further supplemented with 25 mM D-glucose, 10 mM nicotinamide, 8 nM exendin-4, and 8 nM activin A. Cells are seeded at a density of 3.2 × 10⁴ cells/cm² in ultra-low attachment tissue culture plates (e.g., Corning Costar) to promote spontaneous aggregation into islet-like clusters. Medium is replaced every 48 hours, and analyses are performed after 4–11 days of culture.

In alternative embodiments, HES1 inhibition is combined with overexpression of cell cycle regulators to dissect the contribution of growth arrest to redifferentiation. For example, an adenoviral vector expressing human p57 (CDKN1C) under the CMV promoter (Ad-p57) is used to infect expanded islet cells at MOI 100:1 in the presence of Polybrene. However, as demonstrated herein, p57 overexpression alone fails to induce insulin re-expression, indicating that HES1’s role extends beyond cell cycle control.

Phenotypic and molecular analyses are conducted to assess redifferentiation. Nuclear size is measured by staining cells with DAPI and imaging via fluorescence microscopy (e.g., Nikon TE-200), followed by quantitative analysis using NIS-Elements software. Proliferation is assessed by BrdU incorporation: cells are pulsed with 10 µM BrdU for 28 hours, fixed, denatured with HCl, and stained with anti-BrdU antibodies. Apoptosis is quantified using TUNEL assay (ApoTag kit) according to manufacturer instructions. Immunofluorescence is performed on cytospin preparations: cells are fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized, blocked in 1% BSA/5% fetal goat serum/0.2% saponin, and incubated with primary antibodies against eGFP, C-peptide, insulin, glucagon, somatostatin, PDX1, NKX2.2, or BrdU, followed by Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibodies. Confocal imaging is carried out using a Leica SP5 microscope.

Gene expression is analyzed by quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR). Total RNA is extracted using RNeasy Plus Micro Kit or TRIzol, treated with DNase, and reverse-transcribed using a high-capacity cDNA RT kit. qPCR is performed in triplicate on an Applied Biosystems 7300 system using TaqMan or Universal Probe Library assays. Transcript levels are normalized to housekeeping genes TBP and/or RPLPO, with undetectable transcripts assigned a cycle threshold (Ct) of 40. Primer sequences for target genes—including INS, PDX1, NEUROD1, MAFA, NKX6.1, GCK, SUR1, KIR6.2, IAPP, GCG, SST, PPY, MAFB, CDKN1C (p57), and CDKN1B (p27)—are provided in Table 2 of the research paper and are incorporated herein by reference.

Protein expression is evaluated by immunoblotting. Cells are lysed in 1% Nonidet P-40 buffer containing protease inhibitors, and 20 µg of protein is resolved by SDS-PAGE, transferred to Immobilon-P membranes, and probed with anti-HES1 (1:1000) or anti-p57 (1:500) antibodies. Detection is performed using HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies and chemiluminescent substrate, with quantification by TINA software.

For in vivo validation, expanded human islet cells at passage 5 are infected with HES1 shRNA or control virus, and 2 × 10⁶ cells are transplanted under the kidney capsule of streptozotocin-induced diabetic NOD-SCID mice (blood glucose >300 mg/dL). Mice are monitored weekly for glycemia (Accutrend strips) and human C-peptide (Millipore ELISA). At 56 days post-transplant, grafts are harvested for histology and qPCR. Paraffin sections are stained for human C-peptide, insulin, or IAPP using DAB chromogen and hematoxylin counterstain. Graft RNA is analyzed by preamplification followed by qPCR for human-specific transcripts, normalized to GAPDH.

Statistical significance is determined by Student’s t-test, with logarithmic transformation of qPCR data to approximate normality and Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons.

### Examples

**Example 1: HES1 Inhibition Induces Redifferentiation of Expanded Human Islet Cells**

Expanded human islet cells (passages 5–7) were infected with HES1 shRNA or non-target shRNA lentivirus. Nine days post-infection, HES1 protein levels were reduced by 40%, while p57 increased by 50%. Cells formed clusters even in serum-containing medium, and nuclear area decreased from 319 ± 185 µm² (control) to 201 ± 151 µm² (HES1 shRNA; p = 1.85 × 10⁻⁴⁵). qPCR revealed 6.7-fold increase in INS, 4.5-fold in PDX1, and 4.7-fold in NEUROD1 transcripts. Other β-cell genes (MAFA, NKX6.1, GCK, SUR1, KIR6.2, IAPP) were also upregulated. Notably, NGN3 remained undetectable, confirming absence of progenitor reactivation. Non-β endocrine genes (GCG, SST, PPY, MAFB) were induced, likely reflecting redifferentiation of non-BCD cells. Cell cycle inhibitors p57 and p27 were upregulated 5.8- and 3.2-fold, respectively, and BrdU incorporation dropped from 15.8% to 0.7%, confirming growth arrest.

**Example 2: Lineage Tracing Confirms β-Cell-Specific Redifferentiation**

Using the RIP-Cre/ER/eGFP lineage system, eGFP⁺ (BCD) cells at passage 4 were infected with HES1 shRNA. After 9 days, C-peptide staining was detected almost exclusively in eGFP⁺ cells, with no signal in controls. Nuclear size in eGFP⁺ cells decreased from 387 ± 284 µm² to 220 ± 166 µm² (p = 0.007). This demonstrates that HES1 inhibition specifically redifferentiates cells of β-cell origin.

**Example 3: p57 Overexpression Fails to Mimic HES1 Inhibition**

Expanded islet cells were infected with HES1 shRNA, non-target virus, or Ad-p57. Despite 12,000-fold overexpression of CDKN1C by adenovirus (vs. 4-fold by HES1 shRNA), only HES1 shRNA induced INS upregulation (5.3-fold). This proves that redifferentiation requires more than p57-mediated growth arrest.

**Example 4: Synergy Between HES1 Inhibition and Serum-Free Medium**

Sorted eGFP⁺ and eGFP⁻ cells (passage 5) were treated with HES1 shRNA and/or SFM. Combined treatment induced islet-like clustering preferentially in eGFP⁺ cells. qPCR showed additive effects: INS increased 2.3-fold with HES1 shRNA alone, 2.5-fold with SFM alone, and 5.8-fold with combination in eGFP⁺ cells. GCG induction occurred equally in both populations, suggesting α-cell origin.

**Example 5: Enhanced Redifferentiation with Soluble Factors**

eGFP⁺ cells (passages 5–6) were treated with HES1 shRNA and/or RM. RM + HES1 shRNA increased INS transcripts 144-fold vs. 23.5-fold with RM alone (6.1-fold enhancement; p = 0.04). PDX1 increased 5.5-fold over RM alone. Immunostaining showed 15% C-peptide⁺/eGFP⁺ cells with combination vs. 6.8% with RM alone. In unlabeled cells, 19.9% were C-peptide⁺ with HES1 shRNA/RM vs. 7.7% with RM alone—representing ~50% redifferentiation of BCD cells. All C-peptide⁺ cells expressed PDX1 and NKX2.2, and no hormone co-expression was observed, indicating advanced maturation.

**Example 6: In Vivo Functionality**

HES1-inhibited cells (passage 5) were transplanted into diabetic mice. Three of six recipients survived 56 days with normalized glycemia and serum human C-peptide (0.4 ± 0.2 ng/mL). Grafts showed 433-fold increase in INS mRNA vs. control cultured cells, plus expression of insulin, C-peptide, and IAPP by immunohistochemistry. This demonstrates that HES1 inhibition primes cells for functional maturation in vivo.

These examples collectively establish that HES1 inhibition is a potent, lineage-specific driver of human β-cell redifferentiation, especially when combined with optimized culture conditions, and yields cells capable of reversing diabetes in vivo.
