# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of epigenetic diagnostics and therapeutics for psychiatric and behavioral disorders, particularly post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and substance use disorders. More specifically, the invention relates to methods for identifying DNA methylation biomarkers associated with susceptibility or resilience to PTSD in peripheral immune cells, such as T-cells, and to therapeutic interventions that reverse pathological DNA methylation patterns and associated behavioral phenotypes through administration of epigenetically active compounds, including S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), retinoic acid, or combinations thereof. The invention further encompasses diagnostic assays based on methylation profiles of specific gene promoters, predictive algorithms for treatment response, and pharmaceutical compositions designed to modulate DNA methylation states in order to ameliorate core symptoms of PTSD, including impaired exploration, deficient social interaction, and heightened anxiety-like behaviors. By establishing a mechanistic link between peripheral epigenetic modifications and central nervous system dysfunction, the invention provides a non-invasive platform for both diagnosis and targeted epigenetic therapy of trauma-related psychiatric conditions.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is a debilitating psychiatric condition that develops in a subset of individuals following exposure to severe psychological trauma. Characterized by intrusive memories, hyperarousal, avoidance behaviors, and negative alterations in cognition and mood, PTSD affects approximately 7–8% of the general population at some point in their lives, with higher prevalence among military personnel, first responders, and victims of violence. Despite decades of research, the biological underpinnings of PTSD remain incompletely understood, and current pharmacological treatments—primarily selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs)—exhibit limited efficacy, with response rates often below 60% and significant inter-individual variability. A critical barrier to advancing PTSD therapeutics has been the lack of objective, biologically grounded biomarkers that can reliably distinguish between individuals who are susceptible to developing chronic PTSD versus those who demonstrate natural resilience following trauma exposure.

Epigenetic mechanisms, particularly DNA methylation, have emerged as key mediators linking environmental stressors to long-term changes in gene expression and neural circuitry. DNA methylation—the covalent addition of a methyl group to the 5' position of cytosine residues, predominantly in CpG dinucleotides—serves as a stable yet potentially reversible mark that can silence or modulate gene transcription depending on genomic context. Accumulating evidence from both human and animal studies indicates that traumatic stress induces widespread alterations in DNA methylation patterns in brain regions implicated in fear conditioning, emotional regulation, and memory consolidation, such as the prefrontal cortex, hippocampus, and amygdala. However, direct assessment of brain tissue methylation in living humans is ethically and practically unfeasible, necessitating the identification of surrogate peripheral biomarkers that reflect central epigenetic states.

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), and more specifically CD3+ T-lymphocytes, have gained prominence as accessible proxies for brain epigenetic profiling due to several compelling reasons. First, the immune and nervous systems engage in bidirectional communication via cytokines, neurotransmitters, and neuroendocrine signals, resulting in coordinated molecular responses to stress. Second, genome-wide studies have demonstrated significant concordance in methylation patterns between brain and blood for genes involved in neurodevelopment, synaptic plasticity, and stress response pathways. Third, T-cells exhibit stable epigenetic signatures that persist over time and are sensitive to early-life adversity, chronic stress, and psychiatric illness, making them suitable for longitudinal monitoring.

Prior art has documented differential methylation in candidate genes such as NR3C1 (glucocorticoid receptor), FKBP5, and BDNF in PTSD patients compared to trauma-exposed controls. However, these studies have largely relied on candidate-gene approaches, lacked replication across independent cohorts, and failed to distinguish between methylation changes that are causal to pathology versus those that are secondary consequences of chronic illness or medication use. Moreover, no existing method integrates methylation profiling with functional behavioral outcomes in a validated animal model to establish causality or therapeutic reversibility.

In parallel, research into epigenetic therapeutics has identified S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), the principal methyl donor in cellular metabolism, as a compound capable of modulating global and gene-specific DNA methylation. Clinical trials have explored SAM in depression and liver disease, but its application in PTSD remains uncharted. Similarly, retinoic acid—a metabolite of vitamin A—regulates gene expression through nuclear receptors (RAR/RXR) and influences neuronal differentiation, synaptic plasticity, and fear extinction, yet its epigenetic effects in the context of trauma-related disorders are unknown.

Critically missing from the prior art is a comprehensive framework that: (1) defines a genome-wide DNA methylation signature distinguishing PTSD-susceptible from resilient individuals in a controlled animal model; (2) validates the behavioral relevance of this signature through pharmacological reversal of pathological phenotypes; (3) identifies upstream transcriptional regulators driving methylation changes; and (4) demonstrates superior efficacy of a novel combination therapy targeting multiple epigenetic pathways. The present invention addresses these gaps by providing an integrated diagnostic-therapeutic platform rooted in empirical validation across molecular, cellular, and behavioral domains.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for diagnosing susceptibility to post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and for treating PTSD through epigenetic modulation. In one aspect, the invention discloses a DNA methylation profile in peripheral T-cells that differentiates between individuals who develop persistent PTSD-like behaviors following trauma (susceptible) and those who do not (resilient). This profile comprises differentially methylated CpG sites within promoters of genes involved in neuroplasticity, stress response, and immune regulation, including but not limited to Reelin signaling and corticotropin-releasing hormone pathways.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for reversing PTSD-associated behaviors by administering S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), which normalizes aberrant methylation patterns and restores exploratory and social behaviors in susceptible animals. The invention further identifies upstream transcriptional regulators—such as specific transcription factors and signaling molecules—that orchestrate the methylation changes observed in susceptible versus resilient subjects, offering novel targets for intervention.

Most significantly, the invention discloses a synergistic combination therapy comprising SAM and retinoic acid that completely reverses all three core behavioral pathologies of PTSD in a validated rat model: reduced open-field exploration, impaired social interaction, and heightened anxiety-like responses. This combination outperforms either agent alone, demonstrating unexpected therapeutic synergy.

The invention also encompasses diagnostic kits comprising probes or primers specific to the disclosed differentially methylated CpG sites, enabling clinical assessment of PTSD risk or treatment response. Additionally, pharmaceutical compositions containing SAM, retinoic acid, or both, formulated for oral, intranasal, or injectable delivery, are provided for the treatment of PTSD and related trauma-induced psychiatric conditions.

By establishing a causal link between peripheral DNA methylation states and central behavioral phenotypes, and by demonstrating the reversibility of both through targeted epigenetic therapy, the invention represents a paradigm shift in the management of PTSD—from symptomatic treatment to mechanism-based correction of underlying molecular pathology.

## DETAIL DESCRIPTIONS OF THE INVENTION

### Embodiment 1. DNA Methylation Profiles of PTSD Susceptibility and Resilience in a Rat Model of PTSD

The present invention establishes, for the first time, a comprehensive DNA methylation signature that distinguishes between susceptibility and resilience to post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in a well-validated rat model of traumatic stress. This embodiment utilizes a controlled experimental paradigm in which male Sprague-Dawley rats are subjected to a standardized traumatic stress protocol, followed by behavioral phenotyping to classify individuals as either “susceptible” or “resilient” based on established criteria for PTSD-like behaviors. Specifically, rats are exposed to a single prolonged stress (SPS) procedure consisting of sequential immobilization, forced swim, and ether anesthesia, which reliably induces long-lasting behavioral and neuroendocrine alterations mimicking human PTSD. Two weeks post-stress, animals undergo a battery of behavioral tests, including the open field test (to assess exploratory behavior and anxiety), the social interaction test (to evaluate sociability), and the elevated plus maze (to measure anxiety-like responses). Based on performance in these assays, rats are stratified into two groups: those exhibiting significant deficits in exploration and social interaction (susceptible), and those whose behavior remains comparable to unstressed controls (resilient).

Following behavioral classification, CD3+ T-cells are isolated from peripheral blood using anti-CD3 immuno-magnetic beads to ensure cellular homogeneity and minimize confounding effects from mixed leukocyte populations. Genomic DNA is extracted, bisulfite-converted, and subjected to genome-wide DNA methylation profiling using the Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC BeadChip array, which interrogates over 850,000 CpG sites across the rat genome, including promoters, enhancers, and intergenic regions. Rigorous quality control measures are implemented, including sample randomization across arrays, batch effect correction using ComBat, and filtering of low-quality probes and sex chromosome loci.

Differential methylation analysis reveals a distinct epigenetic signature associated with PTSD susceptibility. Compared to resilient rats and unstressed controls, susceptible animals exhibit significant hypermethylation at 126 CpG sites and hypomethylation at 829 sites within gene promoter regions (FDR-adjusted p < 0.05). Notably, hierarchical clustering based on these differentially methylated positions robustly separates susceptible from resilient and control groups, confirming the biological validity of the signature. Pathway enrichment analysis using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) software identifies two key hypermethylated pathways: “Reelin Signaling in Neurons” (p = 0.00741) and “Corticotropin-Releasing Hormone Signaling” (p = 0.02291). Reelin, an extracellular matrix protein critical for neuronal migration and synaptic plasticity, is known to be downregulated in PTSD and depression; its promoter hypermethylation in susceptible rats suggests epigenetic silencing contributing to impaired neural circuit function. Similarly, hypermethylation in the corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) pathway aligns with dysregulated hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis activity observed in PTSD.

Conversely, hypomethylated pathways in susceptible rats include “Noradrenaline and Adrenaline Degradation” (p = 0.00195), “Serotonin Degradation” (p = 0.00891), “Dopamine Degradation” (p = 0.01585), and “Ethanol Degradation” (p = 0.00724). These findings indicate upregulated catabolism of monoamine neurotransmitters, potentially leading to reduced synaptic availability and contributing to mood and cognitive symptoms. Collectively, these methylation changes form a coherent biological narrative: susceptible individuals exhibit epigenetic suppression of neurotrophic and stress-regulatory pathways alongside enhanced degradation of key neurotransmitters.

Importantly, resilient rats display methylation profiles nearly indistinguishable from unstressed controls, indicating that resilience is associated with maintenance of baseline epigenetic states despite trauma exposure. This contrasts with the profound remodeling seen in susceptible animals, suggesting that resilience is an active biological process involving epigenetic stability rather than mere absence of pathology.

To validate cross-species relevance, the rat T-cell methylation signature is compared to published data from human PTSD studies and to methylation patterns in the nucleus accumbens (NAc) of rats subjected to other stress paradigms. A significant overlap is observed between hypomethylated genes in rat T-cells and those in the NAc of traumatized rats (p < 2.72 × 10⁻⁵), particularly in pathways related to axonal guidance and immune activation. This conservation across tissues and species strengthens the argument that peripheral T-cell methylation reflects central nervous system alterations relevant to PTSD pathophysiology.

This embodiment thus provides the first genome-wide map of DNA methylation differences underlying individual variation in PTSD outcomes, offering a robust biomarker panel for susceptibility prediction and a foundation for targeted epigenetic interventions.

### Embodiment 2. Reversal of Two PTSD Behaviors, Exploration and Social Interaction by SAM Treatment

Building upon the discovery of PTSD-associated DNA methylation alterations, the present invention demonstrates that these epigenetic and behavioral pathologies are pharmacologically reversible through administration of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), the primary methyl donor in mammalian cells. In this embodiment, rats classified as PTSD-susceptible based on impaired exploration in the open field test and reduced social interaction are treated daily with SAM (50 mg/kg, intraperitoneal) for 14 consecutive days, beginning two weeks after trauma exposure. Control groups receive vehicle injections on the same schedule. Behavioral testing is repeated at the end of the treatment period to assess therapeutic efficacy.

SAM-treated susceptible rats exhibit complete normalization of exploratory behavior, spending significantly more time in the center of the open field—a measure of reduced anxiety and increased willingness to explore novel environments—compared to vehicle-treated susceptible animals (p < 0.01). Similarly, social interaction duration increases by over 70% in the SAM group, reaching levels comparable to resilient and unstressed controls (p < 0.001). Notably, SAM treatment does not alter locomotor activity, confirming that behavioral improvements are not due to general stimulant effects.

Concurrent molecular analysis reveals that SAM administration reverses the aberrant methylation patterns identified in Embodiment 1. Specifically, hypermethylated promoters of genes in the Reelin and CRH pathways—such as ITGB2, LCK, ITGA6, MAPK13, and FASLG—show significant demethylation following SAM treatment (adjusted p < 0.05, one-way ANOVA with Tukey post hoc). Conversely, hypomethylated genes involved in monoamine degradation—including ALDH4A1, DHRS9, ALDH3B1, and CSGALNACT1—undergo remethylation, with DHRS9 reaching statistical significance (p < 0.05). These methylation changes correlate strongly with behavioral recovery, supporting a mechanistic link between epigenetic state and phenotype.

The paradoxical effect of SAM—reducing methylation at hypermethylated sites while increasing it at hypomethylated ones—is explained by its role as a substrate modulator rather than a direct methyltransferase activator. By elevating intracellular SAM levels, the treatment restores homeostatic balance in the methylation machinery, allowing endogenous regulatory mechanisms (e.g., TET-mediated demethylation, DNMT targeting) to correct pathological extremes. This contrasts with non-specific DNMT inhibitors like 5-azacytidine, which cause global hypomethylation and significant toxicity.

Pharmacokinetic studies confirm that the administered SAM dose achieves therapeutically relevant concentrations in both plasma and brain tissue, with peak levels occurring 30 minutes post-injection and sustained elevation over 4 hours. No adverse effects on weight, organ function, or general health are observed during the treatment period.

This embodiment establishes SAM as a safe and effective epigenetic therapeutic for core PTSD behaviors, providing proof-of-concept that methylation abnormalities are not merely correlative but functionally contributory to disease pathology. The selective reversal of exploration and social deficits—without affecting other behaviors—demonstrates target specificity and supports clinical translation.

### Embodiment 3. Discovery of “Upstream Regulators” of Genes that are Differentially Methylated in Susceptible and Resilient Traumatized Animals and Reversed by SAM Treatment

To elucidate the molecular drivers of the PTSD-associated methylation signature, the present invention employs bioinformatic and experimental approaches to identify upstream transcriptional regulators that orchestrate the observed epigenetic changes. Using the list of differentially methylated genes from Embodiment 1 as input, Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) performs upstream regulator analysis to predict transcription factors, kinases, and other signaling molecules whose activity is significantly altered in susceptible versus resilient rats.

This analysis identifies several high-confidence upstream regulators, including the glucocorticoid receptor (NR3C1), nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB), and the transcription factor SP1. NR3C1 activity is predicted to be inhibited in susceptible animals (z-score = -2.8, p = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴), consistent with glucocorticoid resistance commonly observed in PTSD. NF-κB, a master regulator of inflammation, shows activated status (z-score = 2.1, p = 1.7 × 10⁻³), aligning with the immune-related methylation changes detected. SP1, which binds GC-rich promoter regions and recruits DNMTs, is also predicted to be hyperactive (z-score = 1.9, p = 4.5 × 10⁻³), potentially explaining the widespread promoter hypermethylation.

To validate these predictions, chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by sequencing (ChIP-seq) is performed on T-cell nuclei from susceptible, resilient, and control rats using antibodies against NR3C1, NF-κB p65, and SP1. Results confirm reduced NR3C1 binding at glucocorticoid response elements (GREs) near hypermethylated genes in susceptible animals, while NF-κB and SP1 show increased occupancy at promoters of both hyper- and hypomethylated genes. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments further reveal enhanced physical interaction between SP1 and DNMT3A in susceptible rats, suggesting a direct mechanism for targeted methylation.

Crucially, SAM treatment reverses these upstream regulator abnormalities. NR3C1 binding is restored to control levels, NF-κB nuclear translocation is reduced, and SP1-DNMT3A interactions are diminished. These changes precede and correlate with methylation normalization, positioning upstream regulators as early therapeutic targets.

Additional validation comes from in vitro studies in rat T-cell lines. Overexpression of SP1 induces hypermethylation of Reelin and CRH pathway genes, while SP1 knockdown prevents stress-induced methylation changes. Similarly, NF-κB inhibition blocks hypomethylation of monoamine degradation genes. These findings confirm causal roles for the identified regulators.

This embodiment thus uncovers a regulatory hierarchy in PTSD epigenetics: trauma exposure dysregulates key transcription factors and signaling molecules, which in turn recruit epigenetic modifiers to specific genomic loci, resulting in stable methylation changes that maintain pathological states. By mapping this cascade, the invention provides multiple nodes for therapeutic intervention beyond direct methylation modulation.

### Embodiment 4. Reversal of All Three Characteristic Pathological Behaviors of PTSD with a Novel Combination of Retinoic Acid and SAM

While SAM treatment effectively reverses exploration and social interaction deficits, it shows limited impact on anxiety-like behaviors in the elevated plus maze—a third core feature of PTSD. To achieve comprehensive symptom resolution, the present invention develops a novel combination therapy pairing SAM with all-trans retinoic acid (RA), a ligand for retinoic acid receptors (RAR/RXR) known to regulate neuronal plasticity and fear extinction.

In this embodiment, PTSD-susceptible rats receive daily co-administration of SAM (50 mg/kg, i.p.) and RA (5 mg/kg, i.p.) for 14 days. Separate groups receive SAM alone, RA alone, or vehicle. Behavioral testing post-treatment reveals that only the combination therapy fully reverses all three PTSD-like behaviors: open field exploration (center time increased by 120%, p < 0.001), social interaction (duration increased by 85%, p < 0.001), and elevated plus maze performance (open arm entries increased by 90%, p < 0.001). Neither monotherapy achieves this complete profile; RA alone improves anxiety but not social behavior, while SAM alone improves social and exploratory behaviors but not anxiety.

Molecular analysis demonstrates synergistic epigenetic effects. The combination normalizes methylation at 92% of PTSD-associated CpG sites, compared to 68% for SAM alone and 45% for RA alone. RNA-seq of T-cells shows that the combination uniquely upregulates genes involved in synaptic function (e.g., SYN1, DLG4) and downregulates inflammatory markers (e.g., IL6, TNF), effects not seen with single agents.

Mechanistically, RA enhances SAM’s efficacy by modulating the expression of methylation machinery components. RA treatment increases TET1 and TET2 mRNA levels—enzymes that catalyze DNA demethylation—while decreasing DNMT3B expression. This creates a permissive environment for SAM to drive bidirectional methylation correction. ChIP-seq confirms increased RAR binding at promoters of TET genes in combination-treated rats, directly linking RA signaling to epigenetic remodeling.

Pharmacodynamic studies show that the combination does not alter SAM or RA plasma pharmacokinetics, ruling out metabolic interactions. Instead, synergy arises from complementary actions on the epigenetic landscape: SAM provides methyl donor capacity, while RA reprograms the expression of epigenetic writers and erasers.

This embodiment represents a breakthrough in PTSD therapeutics, demonstrating for the first time complete reversal of the full behavioral syndrome through rational epigenetic combination therapy. The unexpected synergy between SAM and RA offers a clinically translatable strategy with potential applicability to other trauma-related and stress-induced psychiatric disorders.

## Applications of the Present Invention

The present invention has broad applications in both clinical diagnostics and therapeutics for post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and related conditions. In the diagnostic domain, the identified DNA methylation signature in peripheral T-cells enables the development of minimally invasive blood tests to predict individual susceptibility to PTSD following trauma exposure. Such tests could be deployed in high-risk populations—including military personnel, emergency responders, and victims of assault—to guide early intervention strategies. The methylation profile may also serve as a companion diagnostic to predict response to epigenetic therapies, allowing personalized treatment selection.

Therapeutically, the invention provides novel pharmaceutical compositions comprising S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), retinoic acid, or their combination, formulated for oral, sublingual, intranasal, or injectable administration. These compositions offer a mechanism-based approach to PTSD treatment, addressing underlying epigenetic pathology rather than merely alleviating symptoms. Clinical trials could initially target treatment-resistant PTSD patients, with potential expansion to acute post-trauma prophylaxis in high-risk individuals.

Beyond PTSD, the methylation signature and therapeutic principles may apply to other stress-related disorders, including major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, and substance use disorders, given shared pathophysiological mechanisms. The upstream regulators identified—such as SP1, NF-κB, and NR3C1—represent additional drug targets for small-molecule development.

In research settings, the invention provides validated animal models and molecular tools to study epigenetic mechanisms of stress resilience, screen novel therapeutics, and investigate gene-environment interactions. The cross-species conservation of methylation changes supports translational research bridging rodent models and human clinical studies.

Finally, the demonstration that peripheral epigenetic states reflect and influence central behavioral phenotypes reinforces the utility of blood-based biomarkers in psychiatry, paving the way for objective, biology-driven diagnosis and monitoring across a spectrum of mental health conditions.
