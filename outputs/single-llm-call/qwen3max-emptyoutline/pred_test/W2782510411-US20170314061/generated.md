# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of bioluminescence imaging and, more particularly, to novel near-infrared bioluminescent systems comprising engineered click beetle luciferase variants and naphthyl-based luciferin analogs for use in deep tissue imaging applications. The invention provides improved bioluminescent reporter systems that enable enhanced sensitivity and spatial resolution for non-invasive in vivo imaging, particularly in challenging biological contexts such as brain tissue and pigmented animal models. Specifically, the invention encompasses a mutant click beetle red luciferase (CBR2) encoded by a codon-optimized gene sequence (CBR2opt), along with two novel naphthyl-based luciferin substrates—amino-naphthyl naphtho[2,1]thiazole luciferin (NH2-NpLH2) and hydroxy-naphtha[2,1]thiazole luciferin (OH-NpLH2)—which together produce bioluminescent signals with peak emission wavelengths in the near-infrared range (650–900 nm). These systems overcome significant limitations of conventional firefly luciferase/D-luciferin (Luc2/D-LH2) imaging, including poor tissue penetration due to absorption by hemoglobin and melanin, insufficient substrate biodistribution in deep tissues, and limited signal intensity at longer wavelengths. The CBR2 variant maintains compatibility with the standard D-luciferin substrate while exhibiting superior thermal stability and cellular accumulation compared to Luc2, thereby providing enhanced signal output even with conventional substrates. When paired with the novel NH2-NpLH2 substrate, the CBR2 system generates highly red-shifted bioluminescence (730 nm peak emission) that enables precise three-dimensional diffuse tomographic reconstruction of cellular locations in deep tissues, such as the brain. The invention further provides methods for using these bioluminescent systems in various imaging applications, including but not limited to preclinical evaluation of therapeutic interventions, tracking of cell migration, gene expression analysis, and multispectral bioluminescence tomography. Additionally, the invention encompasses kits containing the engineered luciferase, novel substrates, and associated reagents for convenient implementation of these advanced imaging techniques in research and clinical settings.

## BACKGROUND

Bioluminescence imaging (BLI) has emerged as a powerful non-invasive technique for monitoring biological processes in living organisms, particularly in preclinical mouse models. The most widely adopted BLI system utilizes firefly luciferase (typically Luc2) and its natural substrate D-luciferin (D-LH2), which produces light with a peak emission near 600 nm at physiological temperatures. This system has been extensively employed for applications ranging from gene expression analysis to evaluation of therapeutic efficacy in oncology, immunology, and neuroscience. However, the utility of the Luc2/D-LH2 system is fundamentally constrained by the optical properties of mammalian tissues, which strongly absorb and scatter light in the visible spectrum. Hemoglobin, melanin, lipids, and water exhibit significant absorption coefficients below 650 nm, severely limiting the penetration depth and detection sensitivity of bioluminescent signals generated by conventional systems. Consequently, imaging of deep tissues such as the brain, lungs, and bone marrow remains challenging, often requiring high cell numbers or prolonged exposure times that may compromise experimental validity.

Efforts to address these limitations have focused on shifting the emission wavelength of bioluminescent systems into the near-infrared (NIR) window (650–900 nm), where tissue absorption is minimized and light penetration is maximized. Several strategies have been pursued, including mutagenesis of luciferase enzymes to alter their spectral properties and development of synthetic luciferin analogs with extended π-conjugation systems designed to produce lower-energy photons. While some success has been achieved in red-shifting emission spectra, these approaches have typically resulted in substantial trade-offs between wavelength and signal intensity. Mutant luciferases often exhibit reduced catalytic efficiency or stability, while synthetic substrates frequently demonstrate poor cellular uptake, rapid metabolism, or limited compatibility with existing luciferase enzymes. For instance, aminoluciferin derivatives with extended conjugation have been shown to produce NIR emission when paired with firefly luciferase, but the resulting signals are typically orders of magnitude weaker than those obtained with D-LH2. Similarly, energy transfer-based systems utilizing luciferase-dye conjugates have demonstrated NIR emission, but these constructs introduce additional complexity and potential artifacts related to dye photophysics and conjugation chemistry.

A recent advancement in this field involves the development of AkaLumine-HCl (Aka-HCl), a luciferin analog with extended conjugation that produces NIR bioluminescence (677 nm peak) when used with firefly luciferase. While this represents a significant step forward, the utility of Aka-HCl remains limited by its relatively low solubility and the fact that signal enhancement over conventional systems is only observed at specific substrate concentrations. Moreover, the fundamental photon-emitting species in firefly luciferase systems—oxyluciferin—is inherently constrained in its ability to achieve substantial red-shifts due to its molecular structure and the electronic transitions involved in light emission. This suggests that truly transformative improvements in NIR bioluminescence may require both novel substrate design and complementary engineering of the luciferase enzyme to optimize the interaction between the two components.

Click beetle luciferases represent an attractive alternative platform for engineering improved bioluminescent systems, as they naturally exhibit a broader range of spectral properties compared to firefly luciferases and have demonstrated greater tolerance for structural modifications in both the enzyme and substrate. Previous work has characterized various click beetle luciferase variants, including green (CBG99) and red (CBR) forms, which display different emission maxima and substrate specificities. However, these native enzymes still suffer from limitations in signal intensity and thermal stability when used with conventional or modified substrates. The development of a synergistic system combining engineered click beetle luciferase variants with specifically designed naphthyl-based luciferin analogs addresses these challenges by simultaneously optimizing both the enzyme and substrate components for enhanced NIR performance.

The present invention builds upon these foundational concepts by introducing a rationally designed mutant click beetle red luciferase (CBR2) that exhibits superior thermal stability and cellular accumulation compared to conventional luciferases, along with two novel naphthyl-based luciferin analogs (NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2) that produce significantly red-shifted emission when paired with CBR2. This integrated approach overcomes the historical trade-off between wavelength and intensity, enabling both sensitive detection and high-resolution imaging in deep tissues. The CBR2 variant maintains compatibility with standard D-luciferin while providing enhanced signal output due to its improved stability, and when paired with NH2-NpLH2, it generates bioluminescence with a peak emission of 730 nm—representing one of the most red-shifted bioluminescent systems reported to date without requiring energy transfer mechanisms. This combination enables unprecedented capabilities in bioluminescence tomography, allowing for precise three-dimensional localization of cells in complex anatomical structures such as the brain.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel near-infrared bioluminescent systems comprising engineered click beetle luciferase variants and naphthyl-based luciferin analogs for enhanced deep tissue imaging applications. In one aspect, the invention features a mutant click beetle red luciferase (CBR2) comprising amino acid substitutions at positions corresponding to R334S and G351R relative to the wild-type click beetle red luciferase sequence. This CBR2 variant demonstrates superior thermal stability and cellular accumulation compared to conventional firefly luciferase (Luc2), resulting in enhanced bioluminescent signal output when used with standard D-luciferin substrate. In another aspect, the invention provides a codon-optimized nucleic acid sequence (CBR2opt) encoding the CBR2 protein, which has been optimized for expression in mammalian cells through adaptation to match the codon usage frequency of murine genes.

The invention further encompasses two novel naphthyl-based luciferin analogs: amino-naphthyl naphtho[2,1]thiazole luciferin (NH2-NpLH2) and hydroxy-naphtha[2,1]thiazole luciferin (OH-NpLH2). These substrates feature extended π-conjugation systems achieved through fusion of an additional naphthyl ring to the benzothiazole fragment of conventional luciferin, resulting in reduced HOMO-LUMO energy gaps and consequently red-shifted emission spectra when oxidized by compatible luciferases. When paired with the CBR2 variant, NH2-NpLH2 produces bioluminescence with a peak emission wavelength of approximately 730 nm, while OH-NpLH2 yields emission peaking at approximately 743 nm. These wavelengths represent significant advancements in the field, providing emission well within the near-infrared window where tissue absorption is minimized.

In a particularly advantageous embodiment, the combination of CBR2opt and NH2-NpLH2 enables high-resolution bioluminescence tomography in deep tissues, such as the brain, facilitating precise three-dimensional localization of cellular sources. The system demonstrates superior performance compared to conventional Luc2/D-LH2 in both superficial and deep tissue imaging contexts, including challenging models such as black-furred mice where melanin absorption significantly attenuates visible light signals. The CBR2 variant maintains compatibility with standard D-luciferin, providing researchers with flexibility to choose between enhanced conventional imaging (using D-LH2) or specialized NIR applications (using NH2-NpLH2) depending on their experimental requirements.

The invention also provides methods for using these bioluminescent systems in various imaging applications, including but not limited to in vivo bioluminescence imaging, bioluminescence tomography, cell tracking, gene expression analysis, and preclinical evaluation of therapeutic interventions. Additionally, the invention encompasses kits containing the engineered luciferase (either as purified protein or encoded in expression vectors), the novel naphthyl-based substrates, and associated reagents necessary for implementing these advanced imaging techniques. The monopotassium salt forms of the novel substrates exhibit excellent aqueous solubility (46 mM for NH2-NpLH2 and 50 mM for OH-NpLH2) and stability in physiological buffers, making them suitable for both in vitro and in vivo applications without demonstrating cytotoxicity or acute toxicity in tested models.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### 1. DEFINITIONS

As used herein, the term "bioluminescence" refers to the production and emission of light by a living organism or biological system as the result of a biochemical reaction involving a luciferase enzyme and its corresponding luciferin substrate. The term "near-infrared" or "NIR" refers to the region of the electromagnetic spectrum spanning wavelengths from approximately 650 to 900 nanometers, which corresponds to the optical window where mammalian tissues exhibit minimal absorption and scattering of light. "Click beetle red luciferase" or "CBR" refers to the naturally occurring red-emitting luciferase enzyme derived from the click beetle Pyrophorus plagiophthalamus, which typically exhibits peak emission around 614 nm when used with D-luciferin substrate. The term "CBR2" specifically denotes the mutant form of click beetle red luciferase comprising amino acid substitutions at positions corresponding to R334S and G351R relative to the wild-type CBR sequence. "CBR2opt" refers to the codon-optimized nucleic acid sequence encoding the CBR2 protein, which has been adapted to match the codon usage frequency of murine genes to enhance expression in mammalian cells.

The terms "NH2-NpLH2" and "OH-NpLH2" refer respectively to amino-naphthyl naphtho[2,1]thiazole luciferin and hydroxy-naphtha[2,1]thiazole luciferin, which are novel luciferin analogs featuring extended π-conjugation systems achieved through fusion of an additional naphthyl ring to the benzothiazole fragment of conventional luciferin. "D-luciferin" or "D-LH2" refers to the natural substrate of firefly luciferase, (S)-2-(6′-hydroxy-2′-benzothiazolyl)-2-thiazoline-4-carboxylic acid. The term "bioluminescence tomography" or "BLT" refers to a computational imaging technique that reconstructs the three-dimensional distribution of bioluminescent sources within a living organism based on measurements of emitted light at the surface, typically using multiple wavelength filters and sophisticated algorithms to account for light propagation through heterogeneous tissues. "Thermal stability" as used herein refers to the resistance of a luciferase enzyme to denaturation and loss of activity when exposed to elevated temperatures, particularly at physiological temperature (37°C). The term "cellular accumulation" refers to the steady-state level of luciferase protein achieved in living cells, which depends on both the rate of protein synthesis and degradation.

### 2. CBR VARIANTS

The present invention provides novel variants of click beetle red luciferase (CBR) that have been engineered to enhance performance in bioluminescence imaging applications, particularly when used with novel naphthyl-based luciferin substrates. The wild-type CBR enzyme, while naturally exhibiting red-shifted emission compared to firefly luciferase, still suffers from limitations in thermal stability and catalytic efficiency with modified substrates. Through rational design based on homology modeling and structural analysis, specific amino acid residues in the active site of CBR were identified as targets for mutagenesis to improve substrate binding and catalytic turnover.

The CBR2 variant of the present invention comprises two specific amino acid substitutions: arginine at position 334 replaced with serine (R334S) and glycine at position 351 replaced with arginine (G351R). These positions were selected based on their proximity to the luciferin binding pocket and their potential role in modulating the hydrogen-bonding network that stabilizes the transition state during the bioluminescent reaction. Position 334 corresponds to a conserved arginine residue that has been implicated in modulating light output from 6′-substituted aminoluciferins and participates in a stabilizing hydrogen-bonding network that helps shield luciferin from solvent. Position 351 is located directly opposite position 334 in the active site and was hypothesized to influence substrate orientation and binding affinity.

The R334S substitution introduces a hydrogen bond donor/acceptor pair that can better accommodate the extended π-conjugation system of the novel naphthyl-based substrates, while the G351R substitution provides additional positive charge and potential hydrogen bonding interactions that stabilize the transition state. Together, these mutations create a modified active site environment that enhances both the binding affinity and catalytic efficiency for the novel substrates while maintaining reasonable activity with conventional D-luciferin. Biochemical characterization demonstrates that CBR2 exhibits approximately 44-fold higher affinity for NH2-NpLH2 compared to D-LH2, with a dissociation constant (Km) of 0.5 μM for NH2-NpLH2 versus 220 μM for D-LH2.

Importantly, the CBR2 variant demonstrates significantly improved thermal stability compared to conventional firefly luciferase (Luc2). When expressed in HEK-293 cells and incubated at 37°C, CBR2 exhibits a half-life greater than 10 hours, whereas Luc2 shows a half-life of less than 30 minutes under identical conditions. This enhanced stability translates directly into higher cellular accumulation of the CBR2 protein, resulting in substantially increased bioluminescent signal output even when using conventional D-luciferin substrate. In stable cell lines expressing equivalent amounts of luciferase protein, CBR2opt/D-LH2 produces approximately four times higher signal than Luc2/D-LH2, primarily due to this stability advantage rather than enhanced catalytic efficiency.

The codon-optimized version of the CBR2 gene, designated CBR2opt, has been specifically engineered to match the codon usage frequency of murine genes, thereby enhancing expression levels in mammalian cells. The CBR2opt sequence shares 79% identity with the original CBR2 coding sequence but incorporates synonymous codon changes that correspond to the most frequently used codons in mouse genes. This optimization strategy significantly increases protein expression without altering the amino acid sequence of the encoded enzyme, ensuring that the beneficial properties of the CBR2 variant are fully realized in mammalian expression systems.

### 3. NEAR-INFRARED BIOLUMINESCENCE SYSTEMS

The present invention provides complete near-infrared bioluminescence systems comprising the engineered CBR2 luciferase variant and novel naphthyl-based luciferin substrates that together produce bioluminescent signals with peak emission wavelengths well within the NIR window. The two primary substrate components of these systems are amino-naphthyl naphtho[2,1]thiazole luciferin (NH2-NpLH2) and hydroxy-naphtha[2,1]thiazole luciferin (OH-NpLH2), both of which feature extended π-conjugation systems achieved through fusion of an additional naphthyl ring to the benzothiazole fragment of conventional luciferin.

The design rationale for these novel substrates stems from the fundamental principle that extending the π-conjugation system of a chromophore reduces the energy gap between its highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO), thereby shifting its emission to longer wavelengths. However, previous attempts to achieve this through incorporation of additional alkene units between the aryl and thiazoline moieties (as in Aka-HCl) have resulted in compounds prone to chemical and photo-instability. The naphthothiazole-based approach of the present invention provides a more rigid and stable extended conjugation system that maintains chemical integrity while achieving significant red-shifts.

NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2 were synthesized using conventional luciferin chemistry with high enantiopurity, ensuring that the biologically active (S)-enantiomer predominates. Both compounds are formulated as monopotassium salts, which exhibit excellent aqueous solubility (46 mM for NH2-NpLH2 and 50 mM for OH-NpLH2) and stability in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for at least 24 hours at ambient temperature. Neither compound demonstrates cytotoxicity in standard cell lines (HEK-293, HeLa) or acute toxicity in mice when administered intraperitoneally at high concentrations, making them suitable for both in vitro and in vivo applications.

When paired with the CBR2 variant, these novel substrates produce bioluminescence with dramatically red-shifted emission spectra. Specifically, CBR2/NH2-NpLH2 produces light with a peak emission wavelength of 730 nm, while CBR2/OH-NpLH2 yields emission peaking at 743 nm. These wavelengths represent some of the most red-shifted bioluminescent systems reported to date that do not rely on energy transfer mechanisms. The 730 nm emission of CBR2/NH2-NpLH2 is particularly significant, as it falls well within the NIR window where tissue absorption is minimized, enabling enhanced penetration through deep tissues and pigmented structures.

The complete bioluminescence systems of the present invention demonstrate superior performance across multiple metrics compared to conventional and alternative NIR systems. In black-furred C57BL/6 mice, CBR2opt/D-LH2 produces nearly 8-fold higher signal than Luc2/D-LH2, primarily due to the combination of enhanced enzyme stability and a greater proportion of light being generated above 650 nm. When comparing NIR systems in deep brain imaging, CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 demonstrates superior spatial resolution compared to CBR2opt/D-LH2, enabling precise three-dimensional localization of cellular sources through bioluminescence tomography. This enhanced resolution is attributed to reduced signal scattering associated with the longer wavelength photons produced by the NH2-NpLH2 substrate.

### 4. METHODS OF USING THE CBR VARIANTS AND/OR BIOLUMINESCENT SYSTEMS

The present invention provides comprehensive methods for utilizing the engineered CBR variants and novel bioluminescent systems in various imaging applications. These methods encompass both in vitro and in vivo applications, with particular emphasis on deep tissue imaging scenarios where conventional bioluminescence systems have proven inadequate. The primary method involves introducing a nucleic acid sequence encoding the CBR2 variant (preferably the codon-optimized CBR2opt sequence) into target cells or organisms, followed by administration of either conventional D-luciferin or the novel naphthyl-based substrates to generate detectable bioluminescent signals.

For in vitro applications, cells can be transfected or transduced with expression vectors containing the CBR2opt sequence under the control of appropriate promoters (such as EF1α for constitutive expression). Stable cell lines can be generated through antibiotic selection or fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) when the expression construct includes a fluorescent reporter such as copGFP linked via a T2A peptide sequence. Once established, these cells can be used for various assays including drug screening, gene expression analysis, and cell migration studies. The choice of substrate depends on the specific application requirements: D-luciferin provides maximum signal intensity for general applications, while NH2-NpLH2 enables high-resolution imaging and multiplexing capabilities due to its NIR emission.

In vivo applications involve either direct injection of CBR2opt-expressing cells into target tissues or generation of transgenic animals expressing the CBR2 variant in specific cell types or organs. For cell transplantation studies, cells are typically injected at defined coordinates using stereotactic devices for brain applications or simple subcutaneous injection for superficial models. Substrate administration can be performed via intraperitoneal, intravenous, or subcutaneous routes, with dosing optimized based on the specific substrate and application. For D-luciferin, standard doses of 150 mg/kg are typically sufficient, while NH2-NpLH2 requires higher doses (220 mg/kg) due to its lower solubility limit in PBS.

Bioluminescence tomography represents a particularly valuable application of the present invention, enabling three-dimensional reconstruction of cellular locations within living organisms. This method involves acquiring bioluminescent images through multiple bandpass filters centered on the emission peak of the chosen substrate (e.g., 700, 720, and 740 nm for NH2-NpLH2; 580, 600, and 620 nm for D-LH2). The resulting multispectral data is then processed using specialized algorithms (such as those implemented in Living Image software) to reconstruct the three-dimensional distribution of light sources. Co-registration with computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) data provides anatomical context for the reconstructed bioluminescent signals.

The methods of the present invention are particularly advantageous for applications requiring high spatial resolution in deep tissues, such as tracking neural stem cell migration in the brain or monitoring tumor metastasis in internal organs. The CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 combination has demonstrated unprecedented capability in resolving multiple adjacent cellular sources in the brain, enabling visualization of cell migration patterns that were previously undetectable with conventional systems. Additionally, the orthogonal substrate specificity of the novel naphthyl-based luciferins (which are inefficient substrates for Luc2) enables multiplexed imaging applications when combined with other luciferase systems.

### 5. SAMPLE

The present invention contemplates various sample types that can be analyzed using the disclosed bioluminescent systems and methods. Primary sample types include living cells, tissues, and whole organisms that have been engineered to express the CBR2 variant luciferase. In cell-based applications, samples typically consist of cultured mammalian cells (such as HEK-293, HeLa, or MCF-7) that have been transiently transfected or stably transduced with expression vectors containing the CBR2opt sequence. These cells can be maintained in standard culture conditions and analyzed either in monolayer format or as three-dimensional spheroids/organoids to better model in vivo conditions.

Tissue samples for ex vivo analysis can be prepared from animals that have received CBR2opt-expressing cell transplants or that have been genetically engineered to express the CBR2 variant in specific tissues. Following euthanasia and perfusion, tissues can be sectioned and analyzed using standard histological techniques combined with bioluminescence detection. The superior stability of the CBR2 variant ensures that bioluminescent signals remain detectable even after tissue processing, facilitating correlation between bioluminescent imaging data and traditional histological markers.

Whole organism samples represent the most comprehensive application of the present invention, particularly in preclinical mouse models. The invention has been specifically validated in several challenging mouse models, including C57BL/6 black-furred mice (where melanin absorption significantly attenuates visible light signals) and nude mice with intracranial cell transplants (representing deep tissue imaging challenges). Samples can be imaged repeatedly over time to monitor dynamic biological processes such as cell proliferation, migration, or response to therapeutic interventions. The sustained signal kinetics of the CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 system, particularly following intraperitoneal administration, enables longitudinal studies with consistent signal quality across multiple imaging sessions.

Additional sample types include bodily fluids and excretions from animals expressing the CBR2 variant, which can be analyzed for bioluminescent signals as indicators of systemic processes or disease progression. The high sensitivity of the CBR2opt/D-LH2 system makes it particularly suitable for detecting low-abundance cellular populations in circulation or disseminated throughout the body. Furthermore, the invention enables analysis of samples from genetically diverse backgrounds, as the codon optimization of CBR2opt ensures robust expression across different mammalian species and strains.

### 7. EXAMPLES

The following examples demonstrate the practical implementation and superior performance of the bioluminescent systems disclosed in the present invention. These examples illustrate key aspects of substrate synthesis, enzyme engineering, biochemical characterization, cellular validation, and in vivo imaging applications.

**Example 1: Synthesis and Characterization of Novel Naphthyl-Luciferin Substrates**

NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2 were synthesized using conventional luciferin chemistry with high enantiopurity, as confirmed by chiral high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). The monopotassium salt forms were prepared and characterized for solubility and stability. Both compounds demonstrated excellent aqueous solubility (46 mM for NH2-NpLH2 and 50 mM for OH-NpLH2) and remained stable in PBS for at least 24 hours at ambient temperature. Cytotoxicity assays in HEK-293 and HeLa cells showed no adverse effects at concentrations up to 1 mM, and acute toxicity studies in mice confirmed safety at intraperitoneal doses of 220 mg/kg.

**Example 2: Engineering and Characterization of CBR2 Variant**

A homology model of CBR was constructed based on the X-ray structure of a related firefly luciferase (PDB code 2D1S). Analysis of the active site identified residues R334 and G351 as targets for mutagenesis to improve compatibility with the novel naphthyl-based substrates. A library of mutational combinations was created and screened in bacterial lysates, leading to the identification of the R334S+G351R double mutant (CBR2) as the variant with optimal performance. The CBR2opt gene sequence was subsequently synthesized with codon optimization for murine expression (79% identity to original CBR2 sequence).

**Example 3: Biochemical Characterization of CBR2 with Novel Substrates**

Purified CBR2 enzyme demonstrated dramatically red-shifted emission spectra when paired with the novel substrates: 730 nm for NH2-NpLH2 and 743 nm for OH-NpLH2, compared to 614 nm for D-LH2. Kinetic analysis revealed 44-fold higher affinity for NH2-NpLH2 (Km = 0.5 μM) compared to D-LH2 (Km = 220 μM). Thermal stability assays showed CBR2 half-life >10 hours at 37°C, compared to <30 minutes for Luc2 under identical conditions.

**Example 4: Cellular Validation of CBR2opt Expression Systems**

Stable HEK-293 cell lines expressing CBR2opt or Luc2 were generated using lentiviral transduction with T2A-copGFP reporters. In-cell Western analysis and fluorescence microscopy confirmed equivalent expression levels between the two cell lines. Bioluminescence assays demonstrated that CBR2opt/D-LH2 produced approximately four times higher signal than Luc2/D-LH2, primarily due to enhanced enzyme stability rather than catalytic efficiency. Cell membrane permeability assays showed NH2-NpLH2 permeated HEK-293 cells more efficiently than D-LH2 (intact/lysed ratio >10-fold higher).

**Example 5: In Vivo Imaging in Black-Furred Mouse Model**

CBR2opt or Luc2-expressing HEK-293 cells (1×10^6) were implanted subcutaneously in C57BL/6 black-furred mice. Following intraperitoneal administration of 150 mg/kg substrate, CBR2opt/D-LH2 produced nearly 8-fold higher signal than Luc2/D-LH2. The CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 combination produced signals only ~2-fold lower than Luc2/D-LH2, demonstrating the advantage of NIR emission in overcoming melanin absorption.

**Example 6: Deep Brain Imaging and Bioluminescence Tomography**

CBR2opt or Luc2-expressing cells (1×10^5) were implanted at 3 mm depth in mouse brains. Intravenous administration of 300 mg/kg D-LH2 or 220 mg/kg NH2-NpLH2 showed CBR2opt/D-LH2 produced 2-fold higher signal than Luc2/D-LH2. Bioluminescence tomography using multispectral acquisition (700, 720, 740 nm filters for NH2-NpLH2; 580, 600, 620 nm for D-LH2) enabled precise 3D reconstruction of cellular locations. At day 5 post-transplantation, CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 clearly resolved two distinct cellular populations in ventricular regions, while CBR2opt/D-LH2 showed only a single larger spot. Histological analysis confirmed the accuracy of the NH2-NpLH2-based reconstruction.

These examples collectively demonstrate the superior performance of the CBR2opt/naphthyl-luciferin systems across multiple imaging modalities and biological contexts, establishing their value as advanced tools for deep tissue bioluminescence imaging applications.
