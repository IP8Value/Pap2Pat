# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The maintenance of genome integrity is a fundamental biological imperative for all living organisms, particularly in the face of constant environmental and endogenous threats to DNA stability. Over evolutionary time, eukaryotic cells have developed sophisticated mechanisms to detect, signal, and repair DNA damage, with double-strand breaks (DSBs) representing among the most deleterious forms of genomic insult. Unrepaired or improperly repaired DSBs can lead to chromosomal rearrangements, loss of genetic information, cellular senescence, oncogenic transformation, or programmed cell death. In mammalian systems, two primary pathways govern the repair of DSBs: non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) and homology-directed repair (HDR). These pathways operate with distinct molecular mechanisms, fidelity outcomes, and cell cycle dependencies, often functioning in a competitive or antagonistic manner depending on cellular context.

NHEJ is the predominant DSB repair mechanism in mammalian cells and operates throughout the cell cycle, though it is most active during the G1 phase when a sister chromatid template is unavailable. This pathway directly ligates the broken DNA ends without requiring a homologous template, rendering it fast but error-prone. The core NHEJ machinery includes the Ku70/Ku80 heterodimer, which rapidly binds to DSB ends and recruits DNA-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs), followed by processing enzymes such as Artemis and the final ligation complex composed of XRCC4, XLF, and DNA Ligase IV (LIG4). Due to the frequent involvement of nucleolytic processing prior to ligation, NHEJ commonly results in small insertions or deletions (indels) at the break site, which can disrupt coding sequences or regulatory elements, leading to loss-of-function mutations or aberrant gene expression.

In contrast, HDR is a high-fidelity repair pathway that utilizes an undamaged homologous DNA sequence—typically the sister chromatid during S and G2 phases of the cell cycle—as a template to accurately restore the original sequence at the break site. This pathway is initiated by end resection, wherein the 5' ends of the DSB are nucleolytically processed to generate 3' single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) overhangs. Key proteins involved in this step include the MRN complex (MRE11-RAD50-NBS1), CtIP, and CDK1-mediated phosphorylation events that promote resection competence. The ssDNA is then coated by replication protein A (RPA), which is subsequently displaced by RAD51 with the assistance of BRCA2 and other mediators to form a nucleoprotein filament capable of homology search and strand invasion. Successful HDR results in precise restoration of the original sequence or, in the context of genome editing, the incorporation of exogenous donor DNA sequences with high accuracy.

The advent of programmable nucleases, particularly the CRISPR-Cas9 system derived from bacterial adaptive immunity, has revolutionized the field of genome engineering by enabling targeted induction of DSBs at virtually any genomic locus specified by a single guide RNA (sgRNA). However, the therapeutic and research utility of CRISPR-Cas9 is significantly constrained by the inherent inefficiency of HDR relative to NHEJ in most mammalian cell types. While NHEJ occurs robustly across all cell cycle stages and dominates repair outcomes, HDR is restricted primarily to S and G2 phases and typically accounts for only a small fraction of total repair events—often less than 10% even under optimized conditions. This imbalance poses a major challenge for applications requiring precise gene correction, knock-in, or insertion of functional cassettes, such as in gene therapy, disease modeling, or functional genomics.

Numerous strategies have been explored to enhance HDR efficiency, including chemical inhibition of NHEJ components (e.g., SCR7 targeting LIG4), synchronization of cells in HDR-permissive cell cycle phases using pharmacological agents (e.g., aphidicolin or nocodazole), fusion of Cas9 to HDR-promoting domains (e.g., RAD51 or CtIP), or modulation of key cell cycle regulators. However, these approaches suffer from significant limitations: small molecule inhibitors often exhibit cytotoxicity, poor bioavailability, inconsistent efficacy across cell types, and off-target effects; cell cycle synchronization is impractical for in vivo applications and may perturb normal cellular physiology; and protein fusions can reduce Cas9 activity or introduce steric hindrance. Moreover, many of these methods lack temporal control, potentially leading to prolonged dysregulation of DNA repair pathways with unintended consequences for genomic stability.

An alternative and increasingly attractive strategy involves the transcriptional modulation of endogenous DNA repair genes to shift the balance toward HDR. Recent advances in CRISPR-based gene regulation have enabled the development of catalytically dead Cas9 (dCas9) fused to transcriptional activators (CRISPRa) or repressors (CRISPRi) to upregulate or downregulate target genes without altering the DNA sequence. However, dCas9 lacks nuclease activity and therefore cannot simultaneously perform genome editing and gene regulation. This necessitates the co-expression of both active Cas9 and dCas9, increasing vector complexity, delivery burden, and potential for immune responses or off-target effects in therapeutic contexts.

A critical breakthrough emerged with the discovery that truncated guide RNAs (dgRNAs), typically 14–15 nucleotides in length, retain the ability to bind target DNA sequences with high specificity but fail to induce cleavage when complexed with wild-type Cas9. This property allows a single active Cas9 protein to be simultaneously directed toward different genomic functions: full-length sgRNAs mediate DSB formation for genome editing, while dgRNAs serve as scaffolds for recruiting transcriptional effector domains to modulate gene expression. By engineering dgRNAs with RNA aptamer loops (e.g., MS2 or Com), these molecules can recruit fusion proteins such as MCP-P65-HSF1 (for activation) or COM-KRAB (for repression) to specific promoters, thereby enabling CRISPRa and CRISPRi without requiring dCas9.

Despite these conceptual advances, the application of dgRNA-mediated CRISPRa/i to modulate DNA repair pathways for the purpose of enhancing HDR has not been systematically demonstrated. Prior art lacks a comprehensive, genetically encoded system that leverages a single Cas9 transgene to concurrently execute precise genome editing and reprogram the cellular DNA repair landscape by activating HDR-promoting genes and/or repressing NHEJ factors. Such a system would offer significant advantages in terms of simplicity, compatibility with viral delivery, and potential for inducible or tissue-specific control, addressing key limitations of current HDR enhancement strategies. The present invention fulfills this unmet need by providing a novel and effective platform for boosting HDR efficiency through coordinated transcriptional regulation of key DNA repair genes using dgRNA-guided CRISPRa and CRISPRi, all orchestrated by a single active Cas9 nuclease.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a genetically encoded system and method for enhancing homology-directed repair (HDR) efficiency in mammalian cells during CRISPR-Cas9-mediated genome editing by simultaneously modulating the expression of key DNA repair pathway components using a single active Cas9 nuclease in combination with catalytically inactive guide RNAs (dgRNAs). Specifically, the invention employs dgRNAs engineered with RNA aptamer loops to recruit transcriptional activator or repressor complexes to the promoters of HDR-promoting or NHEJ-related genes, respectively, thereby shifting the cellular repair balance toward high-fidelity HDR without the need for exogenous chemical compounds or multiple Cas9 variants.

In one aspect, the invention comprises a first expression vector encoding a dgRNA scaffold containing one or more MS2 RNA aptamer loops, wherein the dgRNA is designed to target the promoter region of an HDR-enhancing gene selected from the group consisting of CDK1, CtIP, BRCA1, BRCA2, RAD51, and combinations thereof. This dgRNA, when co-expressed with a second expression vector encoding a fusion protein comprising MCP (MS2 coat protein) linked to transcriptional activators P65 and HSF1 (MPH), directs the MPH complex to the target promoter, resulting in transcriptional activation of the HDR gene. In parallel, a third expression vector encodes a dgRNA scaffold containing one or more Com RNA aptamer loops, designed to target the promoter of an NHEJ-suppressing gene selected from KU70, KU80, LIG4, DNA-PKcs, and combinations thereof. This dgRNA, when co-expressed with a fourth expression vector encoding a fusion protein comprising COM (Com protein) linked to the KRAB transcriptional repressor domain (CK), recruits the CK complex to the NHEJ gene promoter, leading to transcriptional repression.

Critically, all these regulatory components function in concert with a single active Cas9 nuclease, which is also expressed in the same cell, to perform site-specific double-strand breaks (DSBs) at a target genomic locus specified by a conventional sgRNA. Thus, the same Cas9 protein mediates both genome editing (via sgRNA) and gene regulation (via dgRNA-aptamer/effector complexes), enabling simultaneous DSB induction and DNA repair pathway reprogramming. This integrated approach significantly enhances HDR efficiency—by up to 8-fold in multiple human cell lines and at multiple endogenous loci—compared to control conditions, without increasing mutagenic NHEJ events or causing significant cytotoxicity.

In another aspect, the invention provides inducible versions of the CRISPRa and CRISPRi effector constructs, wherein the expression of the MPH and CK fusion proteins is controlled by a tetracycline-responsive element (TRE) promoter and regulated by doxycycline (Dox)-dependent rtTA transactivator. This allows temporal control over HDR enhancement, minimizing potential long-term consequences of constitutive CDK1 activation or KU80 repression, and enabling precise timing of repair pathway modulation relative to DSB induction.

In yet another aspect, the invention encompasses viral delivery systems, particularly lentiviral vectors, for stable integration of the dgRNA-effector cassettes into target cells, facilitating long-term or heritable HDR enhancement and broad applicability across diverse cell types, including those relevant for therapeutic applications such as hematopoietic stem cells or induced pluripotent stem cells.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the aforementioned vectors or viral particles, suitable for in vitro or in vivo administration, as well as methods of use for precise genome editing in research, biotechnology, and gene therapy contexts. By leveraging endogenous transcriptional machinery and avoiding chemical modulators, the system offers improved safety, specificity, and adaptability compared to existing HDR enhancement strategies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

As used herein, the term “homology-directed repair” or “HDR” refers to a high-fidelity DNA double-strand break repair pathway that utilizes a homologous DNA template—such as a sister chromatid or an exogenously supplied donor DNA molecule—to accurately restore or modify the sequence at the break site. HDR is cell cycle-dependent, occurring predominantly during the S and G2 phases, and involves key steps including end resection, strand invasion, DNA synthesis, and resolution.

The term “non-homologous end joining” or “NHEJ” refers to an error-prone DNA repair pathway that directly ligates broken DNA ends without requiring a homologous template. NHEJ is active throughout the cell cycle but dominant in G1, and frequently results in small insertions or deletions (indels) at the repair junction due to end processing prior to ligation.

A “catalytically inactive guide RNA” or “dgRNA” denotes a truncated single guide RNA, typically 14 to 15 nucleotides in length at its spacer region, which retains the ability to direct Cas9 to a complementary DNA target sequence but fails to support double-strand break formation due to insufficient binding energy or improper conformational activation of the Cas9 nuclease domains.

“CRISPR activation” or “CRISPRa” refers to a technique for upregulating gene expression by recruiting transcriptional activator domains to a target promoter via a DNA-binding protein (e.g., dCas9 or Cas9-dgRNA complex) fused or tethered to activator moieties. In the context of this invention, CRISPRa is achieved through dgRNA scaffolds bearing MS2 aptamers that recruit MCP-P65-HSF1 (MPH) fusion proteins.

“CRISPR interference” or “CRISPRi” refers to a technique for repressing gene expression by recruiting transcriptional repressor domains to a target promoter. Herein, CRISPRi is implemented using dgRNA scaffolds with Com aptamers that recruit COM-KRAB (CK) fusion proteins.

The term “single active Cas9” means a wild-type or standard nuclease-competent Cas9 protein (e.g., from *Streptococcus pyogenes*) that retains its ability to cleave double-stranded DNA when guided by a full-length sgRNA, but can also bind DNA without cleavage when complexed with a dgRNA.

An “HDR-enhancing gene” is a gene whose increased expression promotes the efficiency or fidelity of homology-directed repair. Examples include CDK1, which phosphorylates resection nucleases to facilitate end processing; CtIP, which initiates DNA end resection; and RAD51, which mediates strand invasion.

An “NHEJ-suppressing gene” is a gene whose reduced expression diminishes the activity of the non-homologous end joining pathway. Examples include KU70, KU80, and LIG4, which are essential structural and enzymatic components of the canonical NHEJ machinery.

A “donor template” or “HDR donor” refers to an exogenous DNA molecule provided to the cell that contains homology arms flanking a desired sequence modification, enabling precise integration via HDR at a Cas9-induced break site.

The term “inducible system” refers to a genetic regulatory circuit wherein the expression of a transgene is controlled by an external stimulus, such as the presence of doxycycline in a Tet-On system, allowing temporal regulation of gene expression.

### Description

The present invention is based on the insight that the efficiency of CRISPR-Cas9-mediated homology-directed repair can be substantially enhanced by simultaneously reprogramming the cellular DNA repair machinery through transcriptional activation of HDR-promoting genes and repression of NHEJ-related genes, all orchestrated by a single active Cas9 nuclease in conjunction with specialized catalytically inactive guide RNAs (dgRNAs). This dual-functionality arises from the unique property of dgRNAs—shortened guide RNAs that bind target DNA with high specificity but fail to activate Cas9’s nuclease activity—thereby converting Cas9 into a programmable DNA-binding platform for recruiting transcriptional effectors without compromising its ability to cleave DNA when paired with conventional sgRNAs.

The core innovation lies in the combinatorial use of three RNA species within the same cellular environment: (1) a standard sgRNA that directs Cas9 to induce a double-strand break at a genomic target locus for editing; (2) a dgRNA-MS2 that targets the promoter of an HDR-enhancing gene and recruits an MCP-P65-HSF1 (MPH) transcriptional activator complex; and (3) a dgRNA-Com that targets the promoter of an NHEJ-suppressing gene and recruits a COM-KRAB (CK) transcriptional repressor complex. Because all three RNAs utilize the same Cas9 protein, the system requires only a single Cas9 transgene, simplifying vector design, reducing immunogenic risk, and improving compatibility with viral delivery platforms such as lentivirus or adeno-associated virus (AAV).

The dgRNA scaffolds are engineered to include RNA aptamer loops—specifically, MS2 stem-loops for activation and Com stem-loops for repression—within their tetraloop or stem-loop 2 regions, as previously established in CRISPRa/i literature. These aptamers serve as docking sites for cognate RNA-binding proteins (MCP or COM) fused to potent transcriptional modulators. The MPH fusion combines the strong activation domains of NF-κB p65 and heat shock factor 1 (HSF1) to drive robust gene upregulation, while the CK fusion leverages the KRAB domain, a well-characterized transcriptional silencer that induces heterochromatin formation.

Target selection for modulation is informed by the molecular biology of DNA repair. For HDR enhancement, CDK1 and CtIP are prioritized because they act early in the HDR cascade: CDK1 phosphorylates CtIP and other resection factors to license end processing, a prerequisite for RAD51 loading and strand invasion. Upregulation of these genes increases the pool of resection-competent cells, even outside optimal cell cycle windows. For NHEJ suppression, KU70, KU80, and LIG4 are targeted because they form the core ligation complex; their downregulation reduces the kinetic advantage of NHEJ, giving HDR more opportunity to engage the break. Notably, repression of KU70/KU80 does not increase mutagenic repair (as measured by frameshift reporters), suggesting a selective shift toward HDR rather than alternative error-prone pathways like MMEJ.

The system has been validated using both synthetic reporter assays (e.g., Traffic Light Reporter, TLR) and endogenous genomic loci (e.g., AAVS1, ACTB) across multiple human cell lines (HEK293, HEK293T, HeLa). In all contexts, combinatorial activation of CDK1 and repression of KU80 yields the greatest HDR enhancement—up to 8-fold over baseline—without elevating NHEJ-mediated indels or causing significant cell death. Sequencing of edited alleles confirms precise donor integration in HDR-positive events and heterogeneous indels in NHEJ-positive events, validating pathway specificity.

Furthermore, the invention incorporates modular design features for enhanced control and delivery. Inducible variants place MPH and CK expression under a TRE3G promoter responsive to rtTA/doxycycline, enabling transient, dose-dependent modulation. Lentiviral packaging allows stable integration of dgRNA-effector cassettes, facilitating long-term HDR enhancement in hard-to-transfect or therapeutically relevant cells. These adaptations underscore the system’s versatility for both research and clinical applications.

### Compositions

The invention provides several key compositions that enable the described HDR enhancement platform. A primary composition is a nucleic acid construct encoding a dgRNA scaffold modified to include at least one MS2 RNA aptamer loop, wherein the spacer sequence of the dgRNA is 14–15 nucleotides in length and is complementary to a sequence within 200 base pairs upstream of the transcription start site (TSS) of an HDR-enhancing gene such as CDK1 or CtIP. This construct is operably linked to a U6 or H1 RNA polymerase III promoter to ensure high-level, constitutive expression in mammalian cells.

Another composition is a nucleic acid construct encoding a fusion protein comprising MCP (MS2 coat protein) fused to the transcriptional activation domains of p65 and HSF1 (MPH), operably linked to a constitutive promoter such as CMV or EF1α, or an inducible promoter such as TRE3G in the presence of rtTA. Similarly, a third composition encodes a dgRNA scaffold with at least one Com RNA aptamer loop, with a 14–15 nt spacer targeting the promoter of an NHEJ gene like KU80, driven by a Pol III promoter. A fourth composition encodes the COM-KRAB (CK) repressor fusion under a Pol II promoter.

In preferred embodiments, these components are co-delivered via plasmid vectors or viral particles. For example, a dual-vector system may include: (1) a first vector carrying U6-dgCDK1-MS2 and CMV-MPH; and (2) a second vector carrying U6-dgKU80-Com and CMV-CK. Alternatively, all elements may be combined into a single vector using orthogonal promoters or self-cleaving peptides (e.g., P2A) to express multiple effectors from one transcript.

Lentiviral compositions are particularly advantageous for stable expression. These include transfer plasmids with dgRNA-MS2 or dgRNA-Com cassettes flanked by LTRs, along with MPH or CK coding sequences, packaged with standard lentiviral packaging plasmids (psPAX2, pMD2.G). Transduced cells exhibit persistent HDR enhancement upon subsequent Cas9/sgRNA/donor delivery.

Inducible compositions feature TRE-MPH and TRE-CK vectors, each containing a CMV-rtTA cassette and a TRE3G-driven MPH or CK gene. In the absence of doxycycline, rtTA does not bind TRE3G, and effector expression is minimal. Upon doxycycline addition, rtTA activates transcription, enabling precise temporal control—critical for minimizing potential genotoxic stress from chronic DNA repair modulation.

All compositions are designed for compatibility with standard CRISPR workflows: they co-express with Cas9 and conventional sgRNAs, require no special culture conditions beyond standard transfection or transduction protocols, and function across diverse human cell types.

### Methods

The invention encompasses methods for enhancing HDR efficiency in a eukaryotic cell, particularly mammalian cells, comprising the following steps: (a) introducing into the cell a nucleic acid encoding an active Cas9 nuclease; (b) introducing a first dgRNA comprising a 14–15 nt spacer targeting the promoter of an HDR-enhancing gene and bearing at least one MS2 aptamer; (c) introducing a second dgRNA comprising a 14–15 nt spacer targeting the promoter of an NHEJ-suppressing gene and bearing at least one Com aptamer; (d) introducing expression constructs for MPH and CK effector proteins; (e) introducing a conventional sgRNA targeting a genomic locus of interest; and (f) providing a donor DNA template with homology arms flanking the desired edit.

In practice, steps (a)–(f) may be performed via transient transfection of plasmids, electroporation of ribonucleoproteins (RNPs), or viral transduction. For example, HEK293T cells stably expressing Cas9 are transfected with dgCDK1-MS2 and dgKU80-Com plasmids along with MPH and CK expression vectors. After 24 hours, cells are co-transfected with sgAAVS1 and an SA-T2A-EGFP donor plasmid. After 48–72 hours, HDR efficiency is quantified by flow cytometry for EGFP expression within mCherry-positive (edited) cells.

The method may be adapted for inducible control by using TRE-MPH and TRE-CK cell lines. In this case, doxycycline is added 24 hours before sgRNA/donor transfection to induce effector expression, and removed afterward to limit duration of modulation.

The method is applicable to any genomic locus amenable to CRISPR targeting and has been demonstrated at AAVS1, ACTB, and synthetic TLR loci. It is effective in multiple cell lines, suggesting broad utility. Importantly, the method does not require cell cycle synchronization or toxic chemicals, making it suitable for sensitive primary cells.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the aforementioned nucleic acid constructs or viral vectors, formulated for administration to a subject in need of precise genome editing. Such compositions may include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, buffers, stabilizers, and delivery enhancers (e.g., lipids for nanoparticle formulation). For in vivo use, lentiviral or AAV vectors are preferred due to their tropism and persistence.

A typical composition for ex vivo gene therapy might involve: (1) harvesting patient-derived cells (e.g., T cells or hematopoietic stem cells); (2) transducing them with lentivirus encoding Cas9, dgCDK1-MS2, dgKU80-Com, MPH, and CK; (3) expanding the cells; (4) electroporating with sgRNA and donor RNP; and (5) reinfusing the edited cells. The composition ensures high HDR rates for therapeutic knock-ins (e.g., CAR expression) while minimizing indels that could cause oncogenesis.

For in vivo delivery, dual AAV systems may be employed: one AAV carries Cas9 and sgRNA, while another carries the dgRNA-effector cassettes. Doxycycline co-administration could provide temporal control if inducible systems are used.

These compositions represent a significant advance over small-molecule-based HDR enhancers, offering genetic precision, reduced toxicity, and compatibility with clinical manufacturing.

## Experimental Examples

### Example 1

The efficacy of the dgRNA-mediated CRISPRa/i system for enhancing HDR was rigorously evaluated using a multi-tiered experimental approach encompassing synthetic reporter assays, endogenous locus editing, inducible control, and viral delivery. Initially, a Traffic Light Reporter (TLR) system was established in HEK293 cells stably expressing Cas9 (HEK293-Cas9-TLR). This reporter contains a disrupted bf-Venus (a variant of EGFP) followed by a T2A self-cleaving peptide and a frameshifted mCherry (fs-mCherry). A Cas9-induced DSB within bf-Venus can be repaired by NHEJ, which in approximately one-third of cases restores the mCherry reading frame (yielding mCherry+ cells), or by HDR using an exogenous EGFP donor template, which precisely corrects bf-Venus to yield Venus+/mCherry− cells. This design enables simultaneous quantification of HDR and NHEJ outcomes by flow cytometry.

Plasmids encoding dgRNAs targeting CDK1, CtIP (HDR genes), or KU70, KU80, LIG4 (NHEJ genes) were constructed with MS2 or Com aptamers, respectively, and co-transfected with MPH or CK effector plasmids into HEK293-Cas9-TLR cells. After 24 hours, cells were transfected with sgVenus and an EGFP HDR donor. Flow cytometry analysis at 48 hours post-second transfection revealed that individual modulation—either CDK1 activation (dgCDK1-2 + MPH) or KU80 repression (dgKU80-1 + CK)—increased HDR efficiency from a baseline of 2.42% to 5.8% and 6.1%, respectively. Strikingly, combinatorial treatment (dgCDK1-2 + dgKU80-1 + MPH + CK) yielded 15.4% HDR, representing a 6.4-fold enhancement. Crucially, mCherry+ (NHEJ) frequencies remained stable or decreased slightly, confirming that HDR enhancement was not accompanied by increased mutagenesis.

To validate these findings at endogenous loci, the system was tested for knock-in of an SA-T2A-EGFP cassette into the AAVS1 safe harbor locus. Using sgAAVS1 and a donor with 489-bp and 855-bp homology arms, baseline HDR in HEK293-Cas9 cells was 2.09%. With dgCDK1-2 + MPH, HDR rose to 7.58%; with dgKU80-1 + CK, to 6.64%; and with dual modulation, to 15.3%—a 7.3-fold increase. Similar enhancements (4–5 fold) were observed in HEK293T and HeLa cells, demonstrating cell line generality. Sequencing of PCR-amplified AAVS1 loci confirmed precise EGFP integration without indels in HDR+ clones.

Further validation at the ACTB locus using a different sgRNA again showed 4–5-fold HDR enhancement with CDK1 activation and KU80 repression. An alternative AAVS1 sgRNA also yielded significant improvement, indicating robustness across target sites.

To address safety and controllability, an inducible system was developed using TRE-MPH and TRE-CK vectors. Stable HEK293-TRE-MPH-CK cell lines were generated, and doxycycline treatment induced CDK1 upregulation and KU80 downregulation. Upon doxycycline induction followed by AAVS1 editing, HDR efficiency increased four-fold compared to uninduced controls, with no cytotoxicity observed by CCK-8 assay. Cell cycle analysis confirmed no major perturbations, alleviating concerns about CDK1 overexpression.

Finally, lentiviral delivery was demonstrated: HEK293FT-Cas9 cells were transduced with dgCDK1-MS2-MPH lentivirus, selected with hygromycin, and then transfected with sgAAVS1 and donor. FACS analysis showed significant HDR enhancement, proving compatibility with viral platforms essential for therapeutic translation.

Collectively, these experiments establish that dgRNA-guided CRISPRa/i of DNA repair genes, mediated by a single Cas9, is a potent, specific, and adaptable strategy for HDR enhancement.

### Other Embodiments

The invention is not limited to the specific genes, cell lines, or delivery methods exemplified above. Alternative HDR-enhancing genes such as BRCA1, BRCA2, RAD51, or EXO1 may be activated using analogous dgRNA-MS2:MPH constructs. Similarly, other NHEJ components like XRCC4, XLF, or DNA-PKcs may be repressed via dgRNA-Com:CK. The system may be adapted to other Cas nucleases (e.g., Cas12a) if compatible dgRNA designs are developed.

The RNA aptamer system is extensible: alternative aptamers (e.g., PP7, BoxB) and effectors (e.g., VP64, SunTag) may be substituted to fine-tune activation/repression strength. Multiplexed dgRNAs targeting multiple repair genes simultaneously could yield synergistic effects.

The platform is applicable to non-human mammalian cells (e.g., mouse, primate) given the conservation of DNA repair pathways. It may also be combined with base editors or prime editors where HDR-like precision is desired without DSBs, though this would require adaptation.

For in vivo use, tissue-specific promoters (e.g., albumin for liver, synapsin for neurons) may drive Cas9 or effectors to restrict HDR enhancement to target organs. Dual AAV systems with split Cas9 or compact effectors (e.g., mini-KRAB) can overcome packaging constraints.

These embodiments underscore the invention’s broad utility in research, biomanufacturing, and next-generation gene therapies requiring high-fidelity genome editing.
