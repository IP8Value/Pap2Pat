# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Reinforcement learning (RL) is a computational framework wherein an agent learns to interact with an environment by selecting actions that maximize cumulative reward over time. In many real-world scenarios, however, the extrinsic reward signal provided by the environment is sparse, delayed, or entirely absent during initial phases of interaction. Under such conditions, conventional RL algorithms often fail to discover meaningful behaviors because random exploration strategies are unlikely to stumble upon rewarding states within feasible training horizons. This limitation has motivated extensive research into exploration mechanisms that can guide agents toward informative regions of the state space even in the absence of dense external feedback.

One prominent class of exploration strategies is curiosity-driven exploration, which posits that an agent should be intrinsically motivated to visit states where its internal model of the environment—commonly referred to as a world model—is inaccurate or uncertain. The core idea is that prediction errors between the world model’s forecasts and actual observations serve as a proxy for novelty or surprise, thereby incentivizing the agent to seek out situations that challenge its current understanding. Over time, as the agent gathers new data from these exploratory excursions, it refines its world model, leading to a virtuous cycle of improved representation learning and more effective exploration.

Historically, curiosity-driven methods have employed various forms of predictive modeling. Early approaches used pixel-level reconstruction losses, predicting raw sensory inputs directly; however, such methods suffer from high-dimensional output spaces and sensitivity to irrelevant visual details like background noise or lighting variations. To mitigate these issues, subsequent works introduced latent-space prediction models, where the agent learns compact representations of observations and predicts future latent states instead of raw pixels. Notable examples include the Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM), which combines forward dynamics prediction with inverse dynamics modeling to filter out unpredictable environmental factors, and Random Network Distillation (RND), which trains a predictor network to match the outputs of a fixed, randomly initialized target network, using the prediction error as an intrinsic reward.

Despite their successes, existing curiosity-driven methods often treat world model learning and policy optimization as separate objectives, requiring distinct loss functions and architectural components. This decoupling can lead to suboptimal alignment between the learned representations and the exploration goals, limiting the efficiency and robustness of the resulting policies. Moreover, many prior approaches rely on hand-crafted augmentations, fixed random projections, or auxiliary tasks that may not generalize well across diverse environments or task structures.

Recent advances in self-supervised representation learning, particularly in computer vision, have demonstrated the power of bootstrapping mechanisms—where a model learns to predict representations generated by a slowly updated copy of itself—to produce rich, general-purpose features without explicit supervision. Inspired by these developments, researchers have begun adapting such techniques to RL settings, primarily for representation learning purposes. However, the potential of these methods to drive exploration through intrinsic motivation remains largely unexplored.

The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing a unified framework that leverages a single self-supervised learning objective both to construct a powerful world model and to generate intrinsic rewards for exploration. By tightly coupling representation learning and exploration policy training within a joint optimization process, the invention enables agents to develop highly adaptive and task-relevant internal models while simultaneously guiding their behavior toward the most informative parts of the environment. This approach not only simplifies system design but also enhances performance across a wide range of challenging domains, including complex 3D navigation tasks and hard-exploration Atari games.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel reinforcement learning agent architecture and training methodology that integrates self-supervised representation learning with curiosity-driven exploration through a unified predictive modeling framework. Specifically, the invention introduces a latent-predictive world model based on a bootstrapped self-supervised learning mechanism, wherein an online neural network is trained to predict future latent representations generated by a target network whose parameters are maintained as an exponential moving average of the online network's weights. This world model operates in latent space and utilizes a recurrent neural network to maintain a history-dependent internal state, enabling multi-step predictions of future observations conditioned on past interactions.

In accordance with the invention, the same prediction loss used to train the world model serves dual purposes: first, as a self-supervised objective for learning meaningful latent representations of the environment; and second, as a source of intrinsic rewards that quantify the uncertainty or unpredictability of observed transitions. These intrinsic rewards are then combined with extrinsic rewards (when available) to form a composite reward signal that guides the agent’s policy toward states where the world model exhibits high prediction error, thereby promoting efficient exploration of novel or complex environmental dynamics.

To enhance stability and effectiveness, the invention further incorporates a reward normalization scheme that dynamically scales intrinsic rewards based on their empirical standard deviation estimated via an exponential moving average. Additionally, a reward prioritization mechanism may be optionally applied to focus learning on the most uncertain transitions by clipping low-magnitude rewards below a running threshold derived from the normalized reward distribution. This selective emphasis allows the agent to concentrate its exploratory efforts on the most informative aspects of the environment at each stage of training.

The policy component of the agent may be implemented using any standard reinforcement learning algorithm, such as Variational Model-based Policy Optimization (VMPO), Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C), or Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Critically, the invention supports optional weight sharing between the world model and the policy network, allowing the encoder and recurrent state modules of the world model to be jointly optimized through both the self-supervised prediction loss and the reinforcement learning objective. This shared representation paradigm ensures that the features learned for exploration are directly aligned with those used for decision-making, improving sample efficiency and overall performance.

The invention demonstrates exceptional generality and scalability, having been successfully evaluated on two distinct benchmark suites: the ten hardest exploration Atari games and the DM-HARD-8 suite of complex 3D manipulation and navigation tasks. In both domains, the invention achieves state-of-the-art results, surpassing previous curiosity-driven methods such as RND and ICM, and reaching human-level or superhuman performance without reliance on human demonstrations, reward shaping, or domain-specific engineering. Notably, the agent employs a single set of parameters trained concurrently across all tasks in a multi-task setting, underscoring its capacity for transfer and robustness.

By unifying representation learning and exploration under a single self-supervised objective, the invention offers a conceptually simple yet highly effective solution to the hard exploration problem in reinforcement learning. It eliminates the need for auxiliary networks, fixed random projections, or hand-designed augmentation schemes, while delivering superior performance across diverse environments characterized by partial observability, procedural generation, continuous control, and sparse rewards.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a reinforcement learning agent system and method that implements a curiosity-driven exploration strategy grounded in a self-supervised, latent-predictive world model. The system comprises three principal components: (1) a BYOL-Explore world model configured to learn predictive representations of the environment through a bootstrapped self-supervised mechanism; (2) a reward processing module that normalizes and optionally prioritizes intrinsic rewards derived from the world model’s prediction errors; and (3) a generic reinforcement learning policy optimizer that uses the composite reward signal—comprising both intrinsic and extrinsic rewards—to update the agent’s behavioral policy. These components operate in concert to enable efficient exploration and robust policy learning in environments with sparse or delayed reward signals.

The BYOL-Explore world model is constructed as a multi-step predictive model operating in latent space. It consists of an online network and a target network, both of which include an observation encoder and a recurrent neural network (RNN) for maintaining a history-dependent internal state. The observation encoder, denoted as \( f_\theta \), maps raw observations \( o_t \in \mathcal{O} \) (e.g., RGB images, sensor readings) into a fixed-dimensional latent representation \( f_\theta(o_t) \in \mathbb{R}^N \), where \( N \) is the embedding dimension. This encoder may be implemented using deep convolutional architectures such as ResNets for visual inputs, or fully connected networks for lower-dimensional observations.

The encoded observation is then fed into a closed-loop RNN cell \( h^c_\theta \), which also receives the previous action \( a_{t-1} \) and other auxiliary observation signals (e.g., grip force, held-object status, distance metrics, textual instructions) as input. The RNN updates its hidden state \( b_t \in \mathbb{R}^M \) to represent the agent’s belief about the current state of the environment based on the entire history of past observations and actions up to time \( t \). This recurrent state serves as the basis for making open-loop predictions of future latent representations.

For multi-step prediction, an open-loop RNN cell \( h^o_\theta \) is employed to unroll the future trajectory in latent space without receiving new observations. Starting from the current belief state \( b_t \), the open-loop RNN iteratively applies the transition dynamics using the sequence of actions \( a_t, a_{t+1}, \dots, a_{t+k-1} \) to produce a predicted latent state \( b_{t,k} \) corresponding to time \( t + k \). A predictor head \( g_\theta \) then maps this predicted state to a forecasted observation representation \( g_\theta(b_{t,k}) \in \mathbb{R}^N \).

The target for this prediction is obtained from the target network, which includes a target encoder \( f_\phi \) whose parameters \( \phi \) are maintained as an exponential moving average (EMA) of the online encoder’s parameters \( \theta \):  
\[
\phi \leftarrow \alpha \phi + (1 - \alpha)\theta,
\]  
where \( \alpha \in (0,1) \) is a decay coefficient (e.g., 0.99). The target representation \( f_\phi(o_{t+k}) \) is computed by applying the target encoder to the actual observation \( o_{t+k} \) collected during interaction. Importantly, no data augmentation is applied to the observations, distinguishing this approach from image-based BYOL variants in computer vision.

The online network is trained to minimize the cosine distance between its open-loop predictions and the target representations:
\[
L_{\text{BYOL-Explore}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{j=1}^{B} \frac{1}{T - 1} \sum_{t=0}^{T-2} \frac{1}{K(t)} \sum_{k=1}^{K(t)} \left(1 - \frac{g_\theta(b^j_{t,k})^\top \cdot \text{sg}(f_\phi(o^j_{t+k}))}{\|g_\theta(b^j_{t,k})\| \cdot \|\text{sg}(f_\phi(o^j_{t+k}))\|} \right),
\]  
where \( B \) is the batch size, \( T \) is the trajectory length, \( K(t) = \min(K, T - 1 - t) \) is the valid prediction horizon at time \( t \), and \( \text{sg}(\cdot) \) denotes the stop-gradient operator that prevents backpropagation into the target network. This loss function encourages the online network to learn representations that are predictive of future latent states as defined by the slowly evolving target network.

The intrinsic reward associated with the transition \( (o_t, a_t, o_{t+1}) \) is defined as the sum of all prediction losses that involve \( o_{t+1} \) as a target:
\[
r_i(o_t, a_t, o_{t+1}) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbb{I}[t + k \leq T - 1] \cdot \left(1 - \frac{g_\theta(b_{t-k+1,k})^\top \cdot \text{sg}(f_\phi(o_{t+1}))}{\|g_\theta(b_{t-k+1,k})\| \cdot \|\text{sg}(f_\phi(o_{t+1}))\|} \right).
\]  
This formulation accumulates uncertainty from multiple temporal perspectives, ensuring that observations that are consistently difficult to predict—whether due to novelty, complexity, or stochasticity—receive higher intrinsic rewards.

To address the non-stationarity of intrinsic rewards during training, the invention employs a dynamic normalization scheme. Let \( \{r_i^{(j,t)}\} \) denote the raw intrinsic rewards across a batch of trajectories. An exponential moving average of the reward mean \( \mu_r \) and second moment \( \mu_{r^2} \) is maintained:
\[
\mu_r \leftarrow \beta \mu_r + (1 - \beta) \cdot \frac{1}{BT} \sum_{j,t} r_i^{(j,t)}, \\
\mu_{r^2} \leftarrow \beta \mu_{r^2} + (1 - \beta) \cdot \frac{1}{BT} \sum_{j,t} (r_i^{(j,t)})^2,
\]  
with \( \beta = 0.99 \). The standard deviation is estimated as \( \sigma_r = \sqrt{\max(\mu_{r^2} - \mu_r^2, 0) + \epsilon} \), where \( \epsilon = 10^{-8} \) ensures numerical stability. The normalized intrinsic reward is then \( \tilde{r}_i^{(j,t)} = r_i^{(j,t)} / \sigma_r \).

Optionally, a reward prioritization mechanism clips low-magnitude rewards to zero:
\[
\hat{r}_i^{(j,t)} = \max\left( \tilde{r}_i^{(j,t)} - \mu_{\tilde{r}}, 0 \right),
\]  
where \( \mu_{\tilde{r}} \) is the EMA mean of the normalized rewards. This focuses the agent’s attention on the most uncertain transitions, accelerating learning in complex environments.

The composite reward used for policy optimization is a linear combination of the normalized extrinsic reward \( r_e \) and the processed intrinsic reward:
\[
r_t = r_e,t + \lambda \hat{r}_i,t,
\]  
where \( \lambda > 0 \) is a hyperparameter balancing exploration and exploitation.

The policy network may be implemented independently or share parameters with the world model. In the shared configuration, the encoder \( f_\theta \) and closed-loop RNN \( h^c_\theta \) are reused as the perception and memory modules of the policy. The policy head \( \pi_\psi \) and value head \( v_\psi \) are trained using any on-policy or off-policy RL algorithm (e.g., VMPO, PPO, A2C) with the composite reward. Joint training via both the BYOL-Explore loss and the RL loss ensures that the learned representations are optimized for both prediction accuracy and decision-making efficacy.

Experimental validation was conducted on two benchmark suites. On the ten hardest exploration Atari games—including Montezuma’s Revenge, Pitfall, and Gravitar—the invention achieved superhuman performance using a single agent trained across all games, outperforming RND, ICM, and pure RL baselines. Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of learned (vs. fixed) targets and the benefits of multi-step prediction in partially observable settings. On the DM-HARD-8 suite—a collection of procedurally generated 3D tasks requiring precise object interaction—the invention solved five of eight tasks without human demonstrations, surpassing prior methods that required expert trajectories. Notably, it achieved human-level performance on Navigate Cubes and superhuman scores on Throw-across, Baseball, and Wall Sensors.

The architecture is highly modular and scalable. For Atari, the encoder comprised four convolutional layers followed by fully connected layers; for DM-HARD-8, a deeper ResNet stack was used, augmented with embeddings for proprioceptive and linguistic inputs. The RNN was implemented as an LSTM with hidden size 512. Training utilized distributed infrastructure with hundreds of actors and multiple TPUs, demonstrating practical feasibility.

In summary, the invention provides a unified, self-supervised framework for curiosity-driven exploration that eliminates the need for auxiliary objectives, fixed targets, or manual reward engineering. By leveraging the same predictive loss for representation learning and intrinsic motivation, it achieves state-of-the-art results across diverse and challenging environments, marking a significant advance in autonomous exploration for reinforcement learning.
