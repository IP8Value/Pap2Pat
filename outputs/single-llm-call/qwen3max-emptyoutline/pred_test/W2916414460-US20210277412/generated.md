# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) is a small, non-enveloped, single-stranded DNA virus belonging to the genus Circovirus within the family Circoviridae. It possesses a circular genome of approximately 2 kilobases in length and is encapsidated by an icosahedral capsid approximately 17 nanometers in diameter, composed solely of a single coat protein (CP). First isolated in 1991 from Canadian piglets exhibiting postweaning multisystemic wasting syndrome (PMWS), PCV-2 has since been recognized as a major pathogen responsible for significant economic losses in the global swine industry. PMWS was the initial disease associated with PCV-2 infection; however, subsequent research has identified additional syndromes linked to this virus, including porcine dermatitis and nephropathy syndrome (PDNS), reproductive failure, and enteric and respiratory disorders. Transmission occurs primarily via the oral-nasal route, although all excretions and secretions may serve as vectors for viral spread. More recently, a novel strain designated PCV-3 has emerged, associated with similar clinical manifestations, though its full pathological profile remains under investigation.

Control of PCV-2-associated diseases has been largely achieved through widespread vaccination programs. Currently, five commercial vaccines are available: two based on whole-inactivated virus and three utilizing recombinant subunit technology. All these vaccines target the ORF2-encoded CP, which harbors the primary immunogenic epitopes capable of eliciting robust and durable humoral immune responses. Notably, when expressed heterologously—particularly in insect cells—the CP demonstrates an intrinsic capacity to self-assemble into virus-like particles (VLPs) that are morphologically indistinguishable from native virions. VLP-based vaccines offer a compelling alternative to conventional platforms: they mimic the structural conformation of authentic viruses, thereby enhancing immunogenicity, while eliminating the biosafety risks inherent in handling live or inactivated pathogens. Unlike live attenuated vaccines, which carry the potential for reversion to virulence, and inactivated vaccines, which often suffer from suboptimal efficacy due to denaturation of critical epitopes, VLPs combine safety with high immunological fidelity. Despite the commercial success of human VLP vaccines against hepatitis B virus and human papillomavirus, no veterinary VLP-based products have yet reached the market, highlighting an unmet need in animal health.

The production of recombinant proteins for vaccine development traditionally relies on microbial (e.g., *Escherichia coli*), yeast, insect, or mammalian cell culture systems. While effective, these platforms entail significant costs related to complex growth media, stringent sterility requirements, and expensive bioreactor infrastructure. Moreover, eukaryotic expression systems pose a non-negligible risk of contamination with adventitious animal pathogens, raising regulatory and safety concerns. In response, plant-based transient expression systems have emerged as a promising alternative. Plants such as *Nicotiana benthamiana* can be rapidly engineered via *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*-mediated transformation (agroinfiltration) to produce high yields of recombinant proteins within days. This system operates under Biosafety Level 1 (BSL-1) conditions and requires only standard greenhouse facilities, making it accessible and scalable. Crucially, plant cells perform essential post-translational modifications—including proper folding, disulfide bond formation, and limited glycosylation—while being inherently free of human and animal pathogens, thus ensuring product safety.

Downstream processing represents a major cost driver in recombinant protein manufacturing. Conventional purification strategies for PCV-2 CP and VLPs often involve affinity chromatography using engineered tags (e.g., His-tag), followed by size-exclusion or ion-exchange chromatography. Although effective, these methods increase production complexity and expense, particularly when tag removal is required to avoid immunological interference. Alternative approaches, such as density gradient centrifugation using sucrose or cesium chloride (CsCl), have proven successful in isolating intact VLPs from bacterial, yeast, and insect expression systems without the need for affinity tags. These techniques exploit the physical properties of VLPs—such as buoyant density and sedimentation coefficient—to achieve high-purity preparations in fewer steps. However, the feasibility of applying such purification strategies to plant-derived PCV-2 CP had not been previously demonstrated.

Despite advances in recombinant protein expression, the development of a cost-effective, scalable, and safe PCV-2 vaccine remains a priority, especially for resource-limited settings where swine production is economically vital. The absence of a plant-produced VLP vaccine for any veterinary application underscores both a technological gap and a commercial opportunity. Prior to this invention, there was no evidence that PCV-2 CP could be functionally expressed in plants, assemble into authentic VLPs, and retain immunogenicity comparable to licensed vaccines. Existing literature provided no guidance on optimal agroinfiltration parameters, purification workflows compatible with plant biomass, or the immunological profile of plant-derived VLPs in mammalian models. Thus, a critical need existed for a novel platform that leverages the advantages of plant molecular farming—low cost, scalability, safety, and rapid production—to deliver a next-generation PCV-2 vaccine candidate. The present invention addresses this need by establishing, for the first time, a complete pipeline for the transient expression, one-step purification, and immunological validation of PCV-2 VLPs in *N. benthamiana*, thereby enabling a new paradigm in veterinary vaccinology.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for producing immunogenic virus-like particles (VLPs) of porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) in plants, specifically using transient expression in *Nicotiana benthamiana* leaves mediated by *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*. The invention further encompasses the purified PCV-2 VLPs themselves, compositions comprising said VLPs, and their use as vaccines or diagnostic reagents. Central to the invention is the demonstration that the PCV-2 capsid protein (CP), encoded by the viral ORF2 gene, can be efficiently expressed in plant cells following codon optimization and agroinfiltration, and that the expressed CP spontaneously self-assembles into VLPs that are morphologically and immunologically comparable to native virions.

In one aspect, the invention provides a recombinant expression vector comprising a plant-optimized nucleotide sequence encoding the PCV-2 CP operably linked to regulatory elements functional in plant cells, wherein the nucleotide sequence is derived from a PCV-2 isolate and has been modified to enhance expression in *N. benthamiana* by increasing GC content, removing cryptic splice sites and destabilizing motifs, and eliminating restriction enzyme sites incompatible with cloning. In a preferred embodiment, the vector is a pEAQ-HT derivative, which enables high-level transient expression without genomic integration.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for producing PCV-2 VLPs in plants, comprising: (a) introducing the recombinant expression vector into *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*; (b) infiltrating *N. benthamiana* plants with the recombinant *Agrobacterium* at an optical density (OD600) of 1.0; (c) harvesting plant leaves at 4 days post-infiltration (dpi); (d) homogenizing the leaves in an extraction buffer comprising Tris-HCl, NaCl, CaCl2, Triton X-100, L-arginine, glycerol, and protease inhibitors; (e) clarifying the homogenate by centrifugation; and (f) purifying the PCV-2 VLPs from the clarified extract by a single-step sucrose density gradient centrifugation, wherein the VLPs pellet through the gradient and are recovered as a concentrated fraction. Optionally, the pellet may be further purified by cesium chloride (CsCl) density gradient centrifugation to achieve higher purity, though the sucrose step alone yields immunologically active material.

In a further aspect, the invention provides purified PCV-2 VLPs produced by the aforementioned method, characterized by a particle diameter of 12–24 nanometers as determined by transmission electron microscopy (TEM), a buoyant density of approximately 1.564 g/cm³ in CsCl, and the ability to elicit PCV-2-specific neutralizing antibodies in a mammalian host. The VLPs consist predominantly of the 27 kDa CP monomer and a stable 54 kDa dimer, both confirmed by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS).

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a vaccine composition comprising the plant-produced PCV-2 VLPs in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable adjuvant, such as mineral oil-based MONTANIDE™ ISA 50V2. The composition is suitable for parenteral administration to swine and is capable of inducing protective humoral immunity against PCV-2 infection. Immunogenicity studies in mice demonstrate that the plant-derived VLPs elicit antibody titers comparable to those induced by a commercially available subunit vaccine (Ingelvac CircoFLEX®), confirming their functional equivalence.

Additionally, the invention includes the use of the plant-produced PCV-2 VLPs as antigens in serological diagnostic assays for the detection of anti-PCV-2 antibodies in serum samples from swine. The high purity and structural authenticity of the VLPs make them ideal reagents for enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) and other immunoassays.

The invention overcomes several limitations of prior art systems. Unlike bacterial or yeast platforms, the plant-based method avoids endotoxin contamination and does not require complex fermentation infrastructure. Compared to insect cell systems, it eliminates the risk of baculovirus contamination and reduces production costs. The purification strategy eschews affinity tags and multi-step chromatography, relying instead on a scalable, low-cost centrifugation protocol that preserves VLP integrity. Most significantly, this is the first demonstration that PCV-2 CP can be produced in plants, assemble into VLPs, and retain full immunogenicity—thereby validating plant molecular farming as a viable platform for veterinary VLP vaccines.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to the production of porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) virus-like particles (VLPs) in plants, specifically *Nicotiana benthamiana*, using transient expression mediated by *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*. The method involves the design of a codon-optimized expression construct, optimization of agroinfiltration parameters, and development of a streamlined purification protocol yielding immunogenic VLPs. The following detailed description sets forth specific embodiments of the invention but is not intended to limit the scope thereof, which is defined by the appended claims.

### Example 1

The invention was implemented through a series of experiments designed to express, purify, and validate the immunogenicity of PCV-2 capsid protein (CP) produced in *N. benthamiana*. A synthetic gene encoding the PCV-2 CP (ORF2) was derived from a Lithuanian isolate (GenBank accession KJ128269) and subjected to plant-specific codon optimization. This process involved increasing the GC content to enhance mRNA stability, removing sequences resembling plant cis-acting regulatory elements that could interfere with transcription or translation, and eliminating internal restriction sites incompatible with the chosen cloning strategy. The optimized gene was flanked by AgeI and XhoI restriction sites and cloned into the pEAQ-HT transient expression vector, which contains a duplicated enhancer of the Cauliflower Mosaic Virus 35S promoter and a heat shock protein terminator to drive high-level expression in plant cells. The resulting construct, designated PCV-2_pEAQ, was verified by sequencing and transformed into *E. coli* for amplification before being introduced into *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* strains LBA4404 and EHA105 via electroporation.

To determine optimal expression conditions, *N. benthamiana* plants were vacuum-infiltrated with *Agrobacterium* cultures carrying the PCV-2_pEAQ construct at varying optical densities (OD600 of 0.25, 0.5, and 1.0) and harvested at 1, 3, 5, and 7 days post-infiltration (dpi). Leaf disc extracts were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and immunoblotting using anti-PCV-2 serum. Expression was undetectable at OD600 0.25. At OD600 0.5, peak expression occurred at 5 dpi with strain EHA105, whereas strain LBA4404 achieved higher expression levels earlier—at 3 dpi and beyond—when infiltrated at OD600 1.0. Based on these results, subsequent large-scale experiments employed LBA4404 at OD600 1.0 with harvest at 4 dpi, a time point balancing high yield with minimal leaf pathology.

For purification, 15 grams of fresh leaf tissue per batch were homogenized in DB150 extraction buffer (150 mM NaCl, 1 mM CaCl2, 0.001% Triton X-100, 0.25 mM L-arginine, 10% glycerol, 10 mM Tris/HCl pH 6.5) supplemented with protease inhibitors. The homogenate was clarified by centrifugation at 8,000 × g for 20 minutes, and the supernatant was filtered through Miracloth. A discontinuous sucrose gradient was prepared by layering 6 mL of 45% sucrose and 2 mL of 65% sucrose in ultracentrifuge tubes, onto which 30 mL of clarified extract was loaded. Centrifugation at 120,000 × g for 4 hours at 4°C resulted in the pelleting of PCV-2 CP-containing material. SDS-PAGE analysis of the resuspended pellet revealed a dominant band at 27 kDa corresponding to the CP monomer and a secondary band at 54 kDa, identified by LC-MS as a CP dimer. No such bands were observed in extracts from plants infiltrated with empty vector control. Quantification against a bovine serum albumin (BSA) standard curve indicated yields of 80–110 μg of purified CP per 15 g leaf tissue, equivalent to 6.5 mg per kg of fresh leaf weight.

To assess VLP formation, the sucrose-purified pellet and a CsCl-gradient-purified fraction were examined by transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Both preparations contained spherical particles ranging from 12 to 24 nm in diameter, consistent with reported PCV-2 virion dimensions. Particle counting software applied to TEM images yielded a mean diameter of 16 nm, with higher particle density in the CsCl-fractionated sample. Control extracts showed no particles.

Immunogenicity was evaluated in a murine model. Fifteen BALB/c mice were divided into three groups (n=5): Group 1 received sucrose-purified plant VLPs (10–20 μg) emulsified in MONTANIDE™ ISA 50V2 adjuvant; Group 2 received similarly processed empty vector extract; Group 3 received Ingelvac CircoFLEX® (10–20 μg CP). Animals were immunized subcutaneously on days 0, 14, and 28, and serum was collected on day 42. ELISA using plant VLPs or commercial vaccine as antigen demonstrated that Groups 1 and 3 developed high titers of PCV-2-specific IgG, significantly above pre-immune levels and the control group (P < 0.01). No seroconversion occurred in Group 2. These results confirm that plant-produced PCV-2 VLPs are immunologically functional and comparable to a licensed vaccine.

### Discussion

The successful implementation of this invention demonstrates that *N. benthamiana* is a viable platform for the production of PCV-2 VLPs. The codon-optimized CP gene was efficiently expressed using the pEAQ-HT vector and *Agrobacterium* strain LBA4404, with optimal yield achieved at 4 dpi and OD600 1.0. The purification strategy—relying solely on sucrose density gradient centrifugation—proved remarkably effective, yielding highly pure CP without the need for affinity tags or chromatography. The presence of both monomeric and dimeric CP forms aligns with prior observations in *E. coli* and suggests conserved folding and oligomerization mechanisms across expression systems. The dimer likely arises from interactions within the amino acid domain 51–103, known to mediate CP-CP contacts.

The buoyant density of plant-derived VLPs in CsCl (1.564 g/cm³) was higher than previously reported values (~1.37 g/cm³), possibly due to co-purification of plant nucleic acids or partial denaturation during processing. Nevertheless, TEM confirmed the assembly of correctly sized VLPs, albeit with some heterogeneity in shape—likely attributable to the absence of viral genomic DNA, which in native virions stabilizes the capsid structure. This phenomenon has been observed with other circoviruses, such as beak and feather disease virus, where ssDNA promotes formation of complete 12-pentamer particles versus smaller 10-pentamer assemblies in its absence.

Critically, the immunogenicity data validate the functional quality of the plant-made VLPs. The robust antibody response in mice, matching that of a commercial subunit vaccine, confirms that the plant-produced particles present conformational epitopes in an authentic manner, enabling effective B-cell recognition. This is particularly significant given the presence of immunodominant decoy epitopes on unassembled CP that can divert immune responses; VLP formation masks these decoys, focusing immunity on protective epitopes.

The yield of 6.5 mg/kg leaf tissue is competitive with bacterial systems (e.g., 3 mg/L in *E. coli*) and amenable to scale-up. Future optimizations could include subcellular targeting (e.g., to chloroplasts or ER) to enhance accumulation, or use of magnICON or other advanced vectors for even higher expression. The simplicity of the purification process—requiring only centrifugation equipment—makes it suitable for deployment in low-resource settings, aligning with the goal of affordable veterinary vaccines.

In conclusion, this invention establishes a complete, scalable, and cost-effective pipeline for PCV-2 VLP production in plants, offering a novel solution to a persistent challenge in swine health. The platform is readily adaptable to other circoviruses or emerging strains, positioning plant molecular farming as a cornerstone of next-generation veterinary vaccinology.
