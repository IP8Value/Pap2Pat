# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of targeted cancer therapeutics, specifically to antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) directed against the human prolactin receptor (PRLR). More particularly, the invention provides novel fully human anti-PRLR monoclonal antibodies, including but not limited to REGN2878, and their conjugates with cytotoxic agents such as DM1, which demonstrate potent antitumor activity in breast cancer models. The invention further encompasses methods for treating PRLR-expressing cancers, especially breast cancers—including triple-negative, hormone receptor-positive, and HER2-negative subtypes—by administering these anti-PRLR ADCs either alone or in combination with other therapeutic agents such as selective estrogen receptor downregulators (SERDs). The invention also includes diagnostic and companion diagnostic methods for identifying patients whose tumors express PRLR at levels sufficient to respond to anti-PRLR ADC therapy, as well as pharmaceutical compositions comprising the disclosed antibodies and ADCs formulated for systemic administration.

## BACKGROUND

Breast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide, with significant heterogeneity in molecular profiles, clinical behavior, and therapeutic response. Current clinical classification divides breast cancers into subtypes based on the expression of three key biomarkers: the estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). Tumors that are ER- and/or PR-positive are typically treated with endocrine therapies such as tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, or fulvestrant, while HER2-positive tumors are managed with monoclonal antibodies like trastuzumab and pertuzumab, often in combination with chemotherapy. However, approximately 15% to 20% of breast cancers lack expression of all three receptors and are classified as triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). These tumors, along with those that progress following standard hormonal or HER2-targeted therapies, represent a significant unmet medical need due to their aggressive nature, limited treatment options, and poor prognosis.

The prolactin (PRL)/prolactin receptor (PRLR) signaling axis has emerged as a potential contributor to breast carcinogenesis. Prolactin, a polypeptide hormone primarily secreted by the pituitary gland, binds to PRLR—a member of the class I cytokine receptor superfamily—to activate downstream signaling pathways including JAK2/STAT5, MAPK, and PI3K, which are known to regulate cell proliferation, survival, and differentiation. While PRLR is expressed at low levels in normal tissues such as the mammary gland, placenta, adrenal gland, and kidney, multiple studies have demonstrated its overexpression in malignant breast epithelium across various molecular subtypes, including TNBC. Preclinical evidence suggests that PRL can stimulate the proliferation of breast and prostate cancer cells in vitro and in vivo, implicating the PRL/PRLR axis in tumor development and progression. However, the role of PRLR as a driver of advanced disease remains controversial, and therapeutic strategies targeting this pathway have historically faced challenges.

Early attempts to inhibit PRLR signaling employed PRL-based antagonists such as G129R-hPRL and its Pseudomonas exotoxin A-conjugated derivative, G129RPE40-KDEL. These agents exhibited limited clinical utility due to short serum half-life, immunogenicity, and toxicity associated with the bacterial toxin component. More recently, monoclonal antibodies targeting PRLR, such as LFA-102 and 005-C04, have demonstrated the ability to block PRL-mediated signaling in preclinical models. Despite promising in vitro activity, a phase I clinical trial of LFA-102 revealed that even high doses (up to 60 mg/kg) were well tolerated but failed to produce significant antitumor efficacy, suggesting that mere inhibition of PRLR signaling may be insufficient for robust clinical activity in advanced breast cancer.

In parallel, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) have revolutionized the treatment of metastatic breast cancer by enabling the selective delivery of highly potent cytotoxic agents directly to tumor cells. ADCs such as ado-trastuzumab emtansine (Kadcyla), which combines the anti-HER2 antibody trastuzumab with the microtubule inhibitor DM1 via a non-cleavable linker, have demonstrated significant clinical benefit in HER2-positive breast cancer. The success of ADCs hinges on several key factors: high target antigen expression on tumor cells relative to normal tissues, efficient internalization of the antibody-antigen complex, and trafficking of the conjugate to lysosomal compartments where the cytotoxic payload is released. Over thirty ADCs are currently in clinical development for a variety of solid and hematologic malignancies, underscoring the therapeutic potential of this platform.

Despite these advances, many patients remain ineligible for existing targeted therapies due to low or absent expression of established targets like HER2. Moreover, resistance to hormonal and HER2-directed therapies frequently develops, necessitating the identification of novel, druggable targets in advanced breast cancer. The present invention addresses this critical gap by leveraging the unique biology of PRLR—not only as a signaling molecule but as an internalizing cell surface receptor amenable to ADC-based targeting. Unlike traditional signaling inhibitors, anti-PRLR ADCs exploit the constitutive and ligand-induced internalization of PRLR to deliver cytotoxic payloads directly into cancer cells, thereby achieving potent tumor cell killing even in contexts where PRLR signaling blockade alone is ineffective. This approach represents a paradigm shift in targeting PRLR, transforming it from a failed signaling antagonist target into a viable conduit for cytotoxic drug delivery.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel fully human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the human prolactin receptor (PRLR) with high affinity and specificity, and their conjugates with cytotoxic agents, particularly maytansinoid-based drugs such as DM1. In one embodiment, the invention features the anti-PRLR antibody designated REGN2878, which exhibits high-affinity binding to human PRLR (KD = 1.05 nmol/L) and effectively blocks prolactin (PRL)-mediated receptor activation and downstream STAT5 signaling. The invention further encompasses antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) comprising REGN2878 linked to DM1 via a non-cleavable SMCC linker, referred to herein as REGN2878-DM1, which demonstrates potent in vitro cytotoxicity and significant antitumor activity in multiple breast cancer xenograft models.

A key aspect of the invention is the recognition that PRLR, despite its modest cell surface expression levels in many breast cancers, undergoes rapid and constitutive internalization, making it an ideal target for ADC-based therapy. The invention demonstrates that REGN2878-DM1 is efficiently internalized by PRLR-expressing cells and trafficked to lysosomes, where the DM1 payload is released to induce microtubule disruption, mitotic arrest, and apoptosis. Importantly, the invention shows that REGN2878-DM1 exhibits potent cytotoxic activity not only in cell lines with high PRLR expression (e.g., MCF7/PRLR, IC50 = 0.06 nmol/L) but also in models with low-to-moderate PRLR expression (e.g., T47D, IC50 = 0.97 nmol/L; MCF7 xenografts in vivo), thereby expanding the potential patient population eligible for therapy.

The invention further provides methods for treating PRLR-expressing cancers, particularly breast cancers, by administering a therapeutically effective amount of an anti-PRLR ADC such as REGN2878-DM1. These methods are applicable to a broad spectrum of breast cancer subtypes, including ER+/PR+/HER2−, ER+/PR−/HER2−, and triple-negative breast cancers that express PRLR above a threshold level. In certain embodiments, the anti-PRLR ADC is administered in combination with other therapeutic agents, such as selective estrogen receptor downregulators (SERDs) like fulvestrant, to achieve synergistic or additive antitumor effects, particularly in hormone receptor-positive breast cancers.

Additionally, the invention includes diagnostic and companion diagnostic methods for identifying patients most likely to benefit from anti-PRLR ADC therapy. These methods involve detecting PRLR expression in tumor samples using techniques such as RNAscope in situ hybridization, Western blotting, or immunohistochemistry, and correlating expression levels with predicted therapeutic response. The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the disclosed anti-PRLR antibodies or ADCs, formulated for systemic administration, as well as kits containing the ADC and instructions for use in the treatment of PRLR-expressing cancers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows unless otherwise indicated. “Antibody” refers to a full-length immunoglobulin molecule, including monoclonal, polyclonal, chimeric, humanized, and fully human antibodies, as well as antigen-binding fragments thereof such as Fab, Fab′, F(ab′)2, Fv, and single-chain variable fragments (scFv). “Fully human antibody” denotes an antibody wherein both the heavy and light chain variable regions are derived from human immunoglobulin gene sequences, as opposed to murine or chimeric constructs. “Prolactin receptor (PRLR)” refers to the human type I cytokine receptor that binds prolactin, including all naturally occurring isoforms such as the long form (PRLR-L) and short forms (PRLR-S), as well as splice variants and post-translationally modified forms. “Antibody-drug conjugate (ADC)” means a conjugate of an antibody or antibody fragment covalently linked to a cytotoxic agent via a chemical linker, designed to deliver the cytotoxic payload selectively to cells expressing the target antigen. “DM1” is a maytansinoid derivative, specifically N2′-deacetyl-N2′-(3-mercapto-1-oxopropyl)-maytansine, a potent microtubule-disrupting agent. “SMCC linker” refers to succinimidyl-4-(N-maleimidomethyl)cyclohexane-1-carboxylate, a non-cleavable linker commonly used in ADC construction. “IC50” denotes the half-maximal inhibitory concentration, i.e., the concentration of a compound required to achieve 50% inhibition of a biological process in vitro. “KD” is the equilibrium dissociation constant, a measure of binding affinity between an antibody and its antigen. “Xenograft model” refers to a preclinical model wherein human tumor cells or tissue fragments are implanted into immunodeficient mice to study tumor growth and therapeutic response. “Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)” describes breast cancers that lack expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and HER2, as determined by standard clinical assays. “Selective estrogen receptor downregulator (SERD)” refers to a class of compounds that bind to and degrade the estrogen receptor, thereby inhibiting estrogen-dependent signaling; examples include fulvestrant. “Constitutive internalization” means the continuous, ligand-independent endocytosis of a cell surface receptor, as opposed to ligand-induced internalization. “Lysosomal degradation” refers to the process by which internalized proteins are trafficked to lysosomes and degraded by hydrolytic enzymes, a key step in the mechanism of action of many ADCs.

### Anti-PRLR Antibodies Comprising Fc Variants

The present invention includes anti-PRLR antibodies that may be engineered to contain Fc region variants to modulate effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) or complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). While the lead antibody REGN2878 is described as an IgG1 isotype, which typically mediates strong ADCC, alternative Fc variants may be employed to enhance or reduce specific immune effector functions depending on the therapeutic context. For example, Fc mutations that reduce binding to Fcγ receptors (e.g., L234A/L235A or “LALA” mutations) may be introduced to minimize off-target immune activation and potential toxicity, particularly if the primary mechanism of action is payload delivery rather than immune-mediated killing. Conversely, Fc variants that enhance FcγRIIIa binding (e.g., S298A/E333A/K334A or “AAA” mutations) could be utilized to augment ADCC in settings where dual mechanisms of action—cytotoxic payload delivery and immune-mediated killing—are desired. Additionally, Fc modifications that extend serum half-life, such as YTE (M252Y/S254T/T256E) or LS (M428L/N434S) mutations, may be incorporated to improve pharmacokinetic properties and reduce dosing frequency. These Fc variants retain the high-affinity binding to PRLR and internalization characteristics of the parent antibody, ensuring that the core functionality of the ADC is preserved while tailoring ancillary properties for optimal therapeutic performance.

### Biological Characteristics of the Antibodies

The anti-PRLR antibodies of the present invention, exemplified by REGN2878, exhibit a suite of biological characteristics that render them particularly suited for ADC development. First, they bind human PRLR with high affinity, as demonstrated by surface plasmon resonance (SPR) measurements yielding KD values in the low nanomolar range (1.05 nmol/L for REGN2878). Second, they effectively block the interaction between PRL and PRLR, as shown in ELISA-based competition assays with IC50 values of approximately 5 nmol/L. Third, they inhibit PRL-induced downstream signaling, specifically STAT5 activation, in a dose-dependent manner with IC50 values as low as 0.4 nmol/L in a HEK293/PRLR/STAT5-luciferase reporter assay. Fourth, and critically for ADC applications, these antibodies are rapidly internalized upon binding to PRLR-expressing cells, as visualized by confocal microscopy using Alexa488-labeled antibody, with significant intracellular localization observed within one hour at 37°C. This internalization is followed by trafficking to lysosomal compartments, where the acidic environment and proteolytic enzymes facilitate the release of conjugated cytotoxic payloads. Notably, the antibodies do not bind to rodent PRLR (mouse or rat), which is advantageous for preclinical safety testing in non-human primates but necessitates the use of human tumor xenograft models for efficacy studies. The cross-reactivity with cynomolgus monkey PRLR (KD = 1.64 nmol/L) enables toxicology assessments in this relevant species. These combined properties—high affinity, signaling blockade, and efficient internalization—make the disclosed anti-PRLR antibodies ideal candidates for conversion into potent ADCs.

### Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs)

The invention provides antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) comprising an anti-PRLR antibody, such as REGN2878, linked to a cytotoxic agent via a chemical linker. In the exemplary embodiment, the cytotoxic agent is DM1, a maytansinoid that inhibits microtubule assembly, leading to mitotic arrest and apoptosis. The linker used is SMCC (succinimidyl-4-(N-maleimidomethyl)cyclohexane-1-carboxylate), a non-cleavable thioether linker that ensures the payload remains attached to the antibody until the entire conjugate is degraded in the lysosome. This design minimizes premature release of the cytotoxic agent in circulation, thereby reducing systemic toxicity. The conjugation process involves reacting the antibody with a six-fold molar excess of SMCC-DM1 at pH 8.0, followed by purification via size exclusion chromatography to yield a homogeneous product. The resulting ADC, REGN2878-DM1, has an average drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) of 3.4, with >95% monomeric content and <0.5% free linker-payload, as confirmed by RP-HPLC and MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry. This DAR is within the optimal range for balancing potency and pharmacokinetics, as higher DARs can lead to increased clearance and reduced stability. The invention also contemplates ADCs using alternative cytotoxic agents, such as monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE) or DM4, which have demonstrated similar potency in preliminary assays (IC50 = 0.5 nmol/L and 0.8 nmol/L, respectively, in T47D cells). Furthermore, cleavable linkers, such as those sensitive to cathepsin B or glutathione, may be employed to enable bystander killing of neighboring antigen-negative tumor cells, which could be particularly beneficial in tumors with heterogeneous PRLR expression.

### Targeting ADCs to Cells Expressing Low Levels of PRLR

A significant advancement of the present invention is the demonstration that anti-PRLR ADCs can effectively kill tumor cells expressing low-to-moderate levels of PRLR, a finding that substantially broadens the potential therapeutic applicability beyond tumors with high receptor density. Traditional ADC paradigms often require high antigen expression (e.g., >100,000 copies per cell for HER2) to achieve sufficient payload delivery for cytotoxicity. However, the invention reveals that PRLR’s rapid constitutive and ligand-induced internalization kinetics compensate for lower surface expression, enabling efficient lysosomal delivery of the cytotoxic payload even when PRLR levels are modest. For instance, MCF7 cells, which express approximately 5,000–8,000 PRLR molecules per cell, are insensitive to REGN2878-DM1 in vitro (IC50 > 100 nmol/L) but show significant tumor growth inhibition in vivo when grown as xenografts. This discrepancy is attributed to prolonged exposure to the ADC in the tumor microenvironment, as pharmacokinetic studies show serum levels remain above the in vitro cytotoxic threshold for at least 10 days post-dose. Moreover, estrogen supplementation in vivo may upregulate PRLR expression in ER+ models like MCF7, further enhancing sensitivity. The invention thus establishes that for internalizing receptors like PRLR, the rate of internalization and lysosomal processing can be more critical than absolute surface density, challenging conventional ADC design principles and opening new avenues for targeting antigens previously deemed unsuitable due to low expression.

### Class I Cytokine Receptor Targeting

The prolactin receptor (PRLR) belongs to the class I cytokine receptor superfamily, which includes receptors for growth hormone, erythropoietin, interleukins, and others. These receptors share structural features, such as fibronectin type III domains in their extracellular regions, and signal through associated kinases like JAKs. The present invention demonstrates that class I cytokine receptors, despite their non-kinase nature and often modest surface expression, can be effectively targeted by ADCs if they exhibit rapid internalization. This insight expands the universe of potential ADC targets beyond traditional receptor tyrosine kinases (e.g., HER2, EGFR) to include cytokine receptors that play roles in cancer biology. The success of REGN2878-DM1 validates PRLR as a viable ADC target and provides a blueprint for evaluating other class I cytokine receptors—such as the growth hormone receptor or IL-6 receptor—for ADC development. Key criteria for such evaluation include: (1) differential expression in tumor versus normal tissues, (2) efficient internalization upon ligand or antibody binding, and (3) trafficking to lysosomal compartments. The invention’s focus on PRLR, a receptor with limited normal tissue expression and high turnover in tumors, exemplifies this strategy and underscores the potential of cytokine receptors as underexplored targets for next-generation ADCs.

### Epitope Mapping and Related Technologies

The invention encompasses methods for epitope mapping of the anti-PRLR antibodies to define the precise binding site on the PRLR extracellular domain. Such mapping is critical for understanding the mechanism of action, particularly the ability to block PRL binding and receptor activation. Techniques such as hydrogen-deuterium exchange mass spectrometry (HDX-MS), alanine scanning mutagenesis, and X-ray crystallography of the antibody-PRLR complex can be employed to identify key contact residues. Preliminary data indicate that REGN2878 competes with PRL for binding to PRLR, suggesting that its epitope overlaps with or is proximal to the PRL binding site. This competitive inhibition is essential for the antibody’s dual functionality: not only does it serve as a vehicle for ADC payload delivery, but it also suppresses PRL-mediated pro-survival signaling, which may contribute to therapeutic efficacy, especially in combination regimens. Epitope information can also guide the engineering of second-generation antibodies with improved affinity, altered internalization kinetics, or reduced immunogenicity. Furthermore, knowledge of the epitope facilitates the development of companion diagnostics, such as competitive binding assays, to ensure that patient-derived PRLR variants retain the ability to bind the therapeutic antibody.

### Preparation of Human Antibodies

The anti-PRLR antibodies of the present invention, including REGN2878, are prepared using transgenic mouse platforms that harbor human immunoglobulin gene loci, such as the VelocImmune® technology. In this method, mice are immunized with recombinant human PRLR extracellular domain, and splenocytes are harvested to generate hybridomas or to directly isolate antigen-specific B cells. The variable region genes from these cells are then cloned and joined to human constant region genes (e.g., IgG1) for expression in mammalian cell lines such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells. This approach yields fully human antibodies with high affinity and specificity, minimizing the risk of immunogenicity in human patients. Alternative methods for generating human antibodies include phage display libraries, yeast display, and single B cell cloning from immunized humans or convalescent patients, though the VelocImmune platform was used in the exemplary embodiment. The resulting antibodies are purified using standard chromatographic techniques (e.g., protein A affinity chromatography) and characterized for purity, aggregation state, and binding properties before conjugation to cytotoxic agents.

### Bioequivalents

The invention further includes bioequivalent variants of the disclosed anti-PRLR antibodies and ADCs. Bioequivalents are molecules that, while differing in amino acid sequence or chemical structure, retain the essential biological properties of the original compound, including high-affinity binding to PRLR, efficient internalization, and potent cytotoxic activity when conjugated. Such variants may arise from conservative amino acid substitutions in the complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) or framework regions of the antibody, or from modifications to the linker-payload chemistry of the ADC. For example, an antibody with a single amino acid change in a CDR that maintains KD < 10 nmol/L and IC50 < 10 nmol/L in blocking assays would be considered a bioequivalent. Similarly, an ADC with a DAR of 2.5–4.0 using a structurally similar maytansinoid (e.g., DM4 instead of DM1) would fall within the scope of bioequivalents. The concept of bioequivalence is important for patent protection, as it encompasses functionally equivalent molecules that may be developed to circumvent literal infringement while still practicing the core invention.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Generation of Anti-PRLR Antibodies

Anti-PRLR antibodies were generated using VelocImmune mice, which are genetically engineered to express human immunoglobulin heavy and kappa light chain variable regions. Mice were immunized with recombinant human PRLR extracellular domain, and spleens were harvested for hybridoma generation or direct isolation of antigen-binding splenocytes. Over 300 antigen-binding clones were screened using a battery of in vitro biochemical and cell-based assays to evaluate specificity, affinity, internalization, and blockade of ligand-mediated signaling. The lead antibody, REGN2878, was selected based on its superior profile: high affinity for human PRLR (KD = 1.05 nmol/L), potent inhibition of PRL binding (IC50 = 5.0 nmol/L), and efficient internalization in PRLR-expressing cells. The variable region genes of REGN2878 were cloned and joined to human IgG1 constant region genes for expression in CHO cells, yielding a fully human monoclonal antibody suitable for therapeutic development.

### Control Constructs Used in the Following Examples

Control constructs used throughout the examples include an irrelevant human IgG1 isotype control antibody, which does not bind PRLR, and its corresponding ADC conjugated to DM1 via the SMCC linker (control ADC). These controls were used to establish the specificity of REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1 in all functional assays, including binding, signaling blockade, internalization, cytotoxicity, and in vivo efficacy studies. The control ADC was prepared using the same conjugation and purification protocols as REGN2878-DM1, ensuring that any observed differences in activity were due to antigen-specific binding rather than variations in linker-payload chemistry or DAR.

### Example 3. Surface Plasmon Resonance Derived Binding Affinities and Kinetic Constants of Human Monoclonal Anti-PRLR Antibodies

Binding affinities and kinetic constants of REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1 for human PRLR were measured using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) on a Biacore T200 instrument. Antibodies were captured on a CM5 sensor chip via immobilized mouse anti-human Fc antibody, and monomeric human PRLR extracellular domain was injected at concentrations ranging from 3.125 to 200 nmol/L. Data were globally fitted to a 1:1 binding model to derive association (ka) and dissociation (kd) rate constants, from which the equilibrium dissociation constant (KD = kd/ka) was calculated. REGN2878 exhibited a KD of 1.05 nmol/L for human PRLR, with ka = 1.2 × 10^5 M−1s−1 and kd = 1.1 × 10−4 s−1. REGN2878-DM1 showed similar kinetics, with KD = 1.24 nmol/L, indicating that conjugation to DM1 did not significantly alter antigen binding. Neither antibody bound detectably to mouse or rat PRLR, confirming species specificity.

### Example 4A. Anti-PRLR Antibodies Bind to Endogenous and Overexpressed PRLR Cell Lines

Flow cytometry was used to assess PRLR expression on human breast cancer cell lines. MCF7 cells exhibited modest endogenous PRLR expression (25-fold mean fluorescence intensity [MFI] over secondary antibody control), T47D cells showed higher expression (87-fold MFI), and engineered MCF7/PRLR cells displayed the highest levels (498-fold MFI). Quantitative flow cytometry estimated PRLR copy numbers per cell as ~5,000–8,000 for MCF7, ~27,000 for T47D, and ~185,000 for MCF7/PRLR. REGN2878 bound specifically to all PRLR-expressing lines in a dose-dependent manner, with no binding to PRLR-negative control cells, confirming its specificity for endogenous and overexpressed PRLR.

### Example 4B. Anti-PRLR Antibodies are Internalized by PRLR-Expressing Cells In Vitro

Internalization of REGN2878 was assessed by incubating Alexa488-labeled antibody with T47D or MCF7/PRLR cells on ice, followed by warming to 37°C for 1 hour to allow internalization. Confocal microscopy revealed rapid intracellular localization of the fluorescent signal at 37°C, but not on ice, demonstrating temperature-dependent, active internalization. Co-localization studies with lysosomal markers (e.g., LAMP1) confirmed that internalized antibody was trafficked to lysosomal compartments, a prerequisite for efficient payload release in ADCs.

### Example 5. Anti-PRLR Antibodies Inhibit PRL-Mediated Receptor Activation in Cells Expressing Human PRLR

A HEK293 cell line stably expressing human PRLR and a STAT5-luciferase reporter was used to assess inhibition of PRL signaling. PRL stimulation induced luciferase activity in a dose-dependent manner (EC50 = 2.1 nmol/L). Both REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1 inhibited this response with IC50 values of 0.4 nmol/L, while control antibodies had no effect. ELISA-based blocking assays further confirmed that REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1 prevented PRLR binding to immobilized PRL (IC50 = 5.0 and 4.4 nmol/L, respectively), demonstrating dual functionality as signaling inhibitors and ADC vehicles.

### Example 6. Preparation and Characterization of Anti-PRLR Antibody Drug Conjugates

REGN2878-DM1 was prepared by conjugating REGN2878 to SMCC-DM1 at a 6-fold molar excess in HEPES buffer (pH 8.0). The reaction was quenched after 1 hour, and the conjugate was purified by size exclusion chromatography. UV spectroscopy and MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry confirmed an average DAR of 3.4. Size-exclusion HPLC showed >95% monomeric content, and RP-HPLC confirmed <0.5% free linker-payload. The conjugate retained high affinity for PRLR (KD = 1.24 nmol/L) and potent signaling blockade activity, validating its suitability for in vitro and in vivo studies.

### Example 7. Anti-PRLR Antibody-Drug Conjugates Effectively Kill Cells with Low-to-Moderate PRLR Expression Levels as Well as Cells with High PRLR Expression Levels

Cytotoxicity assays demonstrated that REGN2878-DM1 killed MCF7/PRLR cells (high PRLR) with IC50 = 0.06 nmol/L and T47D cells (moderate PRLR) with IC50 = 0.97 nmol/L, but not parental MCF7 cells (low PRLR; IC50 > 100 nmol/L) in vitro. In vivo, however, REGN2878-DM1 significantly inhibited MCF7 xenograft growth, indicating that prolonged exposure and potential in vivo upregulation of PRLR overcome the in vitro resistance. In T47Dv11 and MCF7/PRLR xenografts, REGN2878-DM1 caused dose-dependent tumor regression, with complete eradication at 10–15 mg/kg. Pharmacodynamic studies confirmed on-target activity via increased pHH3 staining, a marker of mitotic arrest. These results establish that anti-PRLR ADCs are effective across a range of PRLR expression levels, particularly in vivo, and support their clinical development for PRLR-expressing breast cancers.
