# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular diagnostics and targeted therapeutics in oncology, with specific focus on a novel protein biomarker—Odontogenic Ameloblast-Associated Protein (ODAM)—and its utility in the detection, diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment of epithelial malignancies. More particularly, the invention provides methods and compositions for detecting ODAM expression in human tissues and biological fluids, including serum, as well as reagents such as monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies specifically reactive with distinct epitopes of ODAM. The invention further encompasses diagnostic assays based on the detection of ODAM protein or anti-ODAM autoantibodies in patient samples, which may serve as sensitive and specific indicators of the presence, progression, or recurrence of cancers of epithelial origin, including but not limited to breast, gastric, and lung carcinomas. Additionally, the invention includes therapeutic strategies that exploit ODAM as a molecular target, such as antibody-based therapies, vaccine development, or small-molecule inhibitors designed to modulate ODAM-mediated signaling pathways implicated in tumorigenesis. Given the dual expression of ODAM in both normal odontogenic tissues and a spectrum of epithelial neoplasms, the invention bridges developmental biology and oncology, offering a unique platform for precision medicine approaches that leverage tissue-specific protein expression patterns for clinical benefit.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Historically, the identification of reliable biomarkers for early cancer detection and therapeutic targeting has remained a central challenge in oncology. While numerous proteins have been proposed as potential markers, few demonstrate sufficient specificity, sensitivity, and clinical correlation to warrant routine use. The Odontogenic Ameloblast-Associated Protein (ODAM), initially characterized in the context of tooth development, has recently emerged as a molecule of significant interest due to its unexpected expression in a variety of epithelial cancers. ODAM is encoded by a gene located within the secretory calcium-binding phosphoprotein (SCPP) gene cluster on human chromosome 4q13—a genomic region known to harbor genes critical for biomineralization processes in teeth and bone, including amelogenin, ameloblastin, and enamelin. Early studies identified ODAM transcripts in mature ameloblasts and junctional epithelial cells, suggesting a role in enamel formation and periodontal integrity. However, subsequent investigations revealed that ODAM is not restricted to dental tissues; rather, it is aberrantly upregulated in several human carcinomas, including cervical and gastric cancers, as evidenced by DNA microarray analyses.

The molecular characterization of amyloid deposits in calcifying epithelial odontogenic tumors (CEOT)—a rare benign odontogenic neoplasm—provided the first definitive evidence of ODAM protein expression in human pathology. Chemical and mass spectrometric analyses demonstrated that these amyloid fibrils consist of N-terminal fragments derived from a 153-residue protein product of the FLJ20513 cDNA, which corresponds to exons 5 through 10 of the full-length ODAM gene. Subsequent studies identified a longer isoform of ODAM comprising 279 amino acids, encoded by all ten coding exons, thereby confirming the existence of alternative splicing or differential promoter usage leading to multiple protein variants. This discovery raised critical questions regarding the physiological and pathological roles of ODAM beyond odontogenesis.

Despite these advances, the lack of high-affinity, specific immunological reagents hindered comprehensive analysis of ODAM expression across normal and neoplastic tissues. Prior to the present invention, no validated antibodies existed that could reliably detect endogenous ODAM in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) clinical specimens or in liquid biopsies such as serum. Consequently, the true prevalence, subcellular localization, and clinical relevance of ODAM in human disease remained poorly understood. Moreover, while genomic data suggested ODAM overexpression in certain cancers, the absence of protein-level validation left open the possibility that transcriptional upregulation did not necessarily translate into functional protein expression.

Compounding this knowledge gap was the observation that adjacent genes within the 4q13 SCPP cluster—including FDC-SP (follicular dendritic cell secretory protein) and epidermal growth factor family members such as amphiregulin, epigen, and epiregulin—are also implicated in both dental development and carcinogenesis. These genes are co-oriented on the same DNA strand and share regulatory elements, raising the possibility of coordinated dysregulation in malignancy. Notably, FDC-SP has been included in gene signatures predictive of breast cancer invasiveness, further implicating this chromosomal locus in oncogenic processes. Additionally, ODAM has been shown to interact with parvalbumin, a calcium-binding protein, and to modulate the expression of matrix metalloproteinase-20 (MMP-20), an enzyme associated with tumor invasion and metastasis. These findings suggest that ODAM may participate in signaling networks that influence cancer cell behavior.

Another critical aspect of the background lies in the emerging field of autoantibody-based cancer diagnostics. It is well established that patients with various malignancies produce autoantibodies against tumor-associated antigens, often before clinical symptoms manifest. These immune responses can serve as highly specific serological markers, as the immune system effectively amplifies low-abundance tumor antigens into detectable antibody signals. Examples include autoantibodies against p53, NY-ESO-1, and HER2/neu. However, the identification of novel autoantigens with broad applicability across multiple cancer types remains an active area of research. The potential for ODAM to elicit such an autoimmune response had not been previously explored.

Thus, there existed a clear and unmet need for: (1) validated, high-specificity antibodies capable of detecting both short and long isoforms of ODAM in diverse tissue and fluid matrices; (2) robust immunoassays for quantifying ODAM protein or anti-ODAM antibodies in clinical samples; (3) comprehensive mapping of ODAM expression across normal human tissues and a wide array of epithelial tumors; and (4) demonstration of the clinical utility of ODAM as a diagnostic, prognostic, or therapeutic target. The present invention addresses each of these needs through the development of novel immunoreagents, detailed molecular and histopathological characterization of ODAM expression, and the establishment of serological assays that reveal a significant association between anti-ODAM autoantibodies and epithelial malignancies. By bridging fundamental discoveries in developmental biology with translational applications in oncology, the invention provides a foundation for next-generation cancer diagnostics and targeted interventions.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses a comprehensive platform centered on Odontogenic Ameloblast-Associated Protein (ODAM) as a novel biomarker and therapeutic target in epithelial cancers. Central to the invention is the development and validation of highly specific monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies directed against defined epitopes of ODAM, enabling precise detection of both the full-length (279-amino acid) and truncated (153-amino acid, corresponding to FLJ20513) isoforms of the protein. These antibodies were generated using synthetic peptides and recombinant protein immunogens corresponding to distinct regions of the ODAM sequence, ensuring coverage of both N-terminal (exon 2–4 encoded) and C-terminal (exon 5–10 encoded) domains. Specifically, a monoclonal antibody designated 5A-1 was produced against a 45-amino acid peptide spanning residues 52–90 encoded by exon 4, thereby recognizing only the long isoform of ODAM. In contrast, monoclonal antibody 8B-4 was raised against the recombinant short isoform (residues 127–279, corresponding to exons 5–10) and thus detects both isoforms, with epitope mapping localizing its binding site to residues 178–216 within the C-terminal domain. A complementary polyclonal antiserum was also developed against a 12-mer peptide (residues 67–78) to provide additional validation of immunohistochemical findings.

Using these reagents, the invention establishes that ODAM is expressed not only in expected odontogenic contexts—such as ameloblasts in developing murine teeth, human unerupted tooth follicles, ameloblastomas, and calcifying epithelial odontogenic tumors—but also in a range of non-dental normal epithelial tissues, including trachea, salivary gland, bronchus, ovary, bladder, ureter, endometrium, and skin. Critically, the invention demonstrates for the first time that ODAM is significantly upregulated at the protein level in malignant epithelial tissues, particularly in adenocarcinomas of the breast, stomach, and lung. Immunohistochemical analysis of tissue microarrays containing 60 breast and 60 gastric cancer specimens revealed moderate to intense nuclear and/or cytoplasmic staining in 46/60 (77%) and 48/60 (80%) of cases, respectively, with similar reactivity observed in three of six lung adenocarcinoma samples. This expression was confirmed at the mRNA level via in situ hybridization using biotinylated antisense oligonucleotide probes specific for exons 2–10, with signal specificity verified through competitive inhibition with unlabeled homologous probe but not with non-corresponding sequences.

Furthermore, the invention introduces a novel serological diagnostic method based on the detection of autoantibodies against ODAM in patient serum. Using a solid-phase ELISA in which full-length recombinant ODAM (exons 2–10) is immobilized as capture antigen, the inventors discovered that 35% (25/72) of female patients with breast cancer exhibited IgG autoantibody titers exceeding two standard deviations above the mean of healthy controls (n=48). This difference was statistically significant (p < 0.001) by both standard and asymmetric t-tests. Parallel analyses of sera from patients with lung (n=63) and gastric (n=7) cancers revealed similar elevations in anti-ODAM IgG in approximately 30% of cases compared to normal subjects (n=27). Importantly, these autoantibodies were functionally validated by their ability to immunoprecipitate recombinant ODAM from radiolabeled lysates, confirming antigen-specific reactivity. Notably, direct detection of circulating ODAM protein in serum using antibody-based capture assays yielded negative results in both healthy individuals and cancer patients, suggesting that ODAM is not secreted in soluble form under the conditions tested, and that the immune response is likely triggered by intracellular or membrane-associated antigen exposure during tumorigenesis.

The invention also provides detailed molecular tools and protocols for ODAM detection and manipulation. Recombinant ODAM polypeptides representing both isoforms were successfully expressed in prokaryotic systems (E. coli BL21(DE3)) using codon-optimized constructs incorporating a C-terminal FLAG tag for purification and detection. These proteins were purified via FLAG-affinity chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography and validated by Western blotting, immunoprecipitation, and mass spectrometry. RT-PCR methodologies using isoform-specific primer sets (e.g., exon 2 sense + exon 10 antisense for full-length; exon 5 sense + exon 10 antisense for short form) enable precise transcript-level discrimination. In situ hybridization protocols using biotinylated or radioactively labeled probes allow spatial resolution of ODAM mRNA in FFPE tissues, while standardized immunohistochemical procedures using antigen retrieval and polymer-based detection systems ensure reproducible protein localization.

Therapeutically, the invention contemplates multiple strategies leveraging ODAM as a target. These include: (1) monoclonal antibody-based therapies (e.g., naked antibodies, antibody-drug conjugates, or bispecific engagers) directed against extracellular or surface-exposed epitopes of ODAM in tumor cells; (2) ODAM-derived peptide vaccines to boost anti-tumor immunity in patients who do not naturally mount an autoantibody response; (3) small-molecule inhibitors designed to disrupt ODAM interactions with binding partners such as parvalbumin or MMP-20; and (4) gene silencing approaches (e.g., siRNA, antisense oligonucleotides) to downregulate ODAM expression in ODAM-positive tumors. Given the co-expression of ODAM and Gli-1—a downstream effector of the Sonic Hedgehog (Shh) pathway—in multiple carcinomas, the invention further suggests that ODAM may be transcriptionally regulated by Shh signaling, opening avenues for combination therapies involving Shh pathway inhibitors (e.g., vismodegib) in ODAM-positive malignancies.

In summary, the invention provides a complete toolkit for the clinical exploitation of ODAM, encompassing diagnostic reagents, validated assays, expression databases, and therapeutic concepts. By establishing ODAM as a bona fide tumor-associated antigen with detectable humoral immune responses, the invention enables non-invasive cancer screening, risk stratification, and monitoring of treatment response through simple blood tests. Simultaneously, the tissue-specific expression pattern—absent in most normal adult tissues but prominent in select epithelial cancers—offers a favorable therapeutic window for targeted interventions with minimal off-target toxicity.

### EXAMPLE 1

In a first exemplary embodiment, the invention provides a method for diagnosing or monitoring epithelial cancer in a human subject comprising the steps of: (a) obtaining a serum sample from the subject; (b) contacting the serum sample with a solid support coated with full-length recombinant ODAM protein comprising amino acid residues 25–279 encoded by exons 2 through 10 of the human ODAM gene; (c) detecting the presence or quantity of immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies in the serum that specifically bind to the immobilized ODAM protein; and (d) comparing the detected level of anti-ODAM IgG antibodies to a predetermined threshold derived from a reference population of healthy individuals, wherein a level exceeding the threshold is indicative of the presence of epithelial cancer. In this embodiment, the recombinant ODAM protein is produced in Escherichia coli by cloning a PCR-amplified cDNA fragment spanning exons 2–10 into an expression vector containing a ribosomal binding site, a start codon corresponding to methionine at position 25, and a C-terminal FLAG epitope tag followed by a stop codon. Protein expression is induced with isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG), and the recombinant protein is purified using anti-FLAG affinity chromatography and size-exclusion chromatography to ensure homogeneity. The solid support is a standard 96-well microtiter plate, and the detection step involves sequential incubation with biotinylated goat anti-human IgG secondary antibody and horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated streptavidin, followed by colorimetric development using ABTS/H₂O₂ substrate and measurement of absorbance at 405 nm. The predetermined threshold is established as the mean optical density value plus two standard deviations obtained from testing serum samples from at least 40 healthy adult volunteers. When applied to a cohort of 72 female patients with histologically confirmed breast adenocarcinoma, this method identified 25 patients (35%) with anti-ODAM IgG levels above the threshold, demonstrating a statistically significant association (p < 0.001) between seropositivity and disease status. Similarly, when tested on sera from 63 lung cancer and 7 gastric cancer patients, approximately 30% exhibited elevated anti-ODAM IgG compared to 27 healthy controls. This serological assay offers a minimally invasive, cost-effective approach for early cancer detection, particularly in high-risk populations, and may also be used to monitor disease recurrence or therapeutic response by tracking changes in autoantibody titers over time.

### EXAMPLE 2

In a second exemplary embodiment, the invention provides a kit for immunohistochemical detection of ODAM protein in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections, comprising: (a) a monoclonal antibody selected from the group consisting of 5A-1 and 8B-4; (b) a secondary detection reagent comprising a polymer-conjugated horseradish peroxidase (HRP) complex specific for mouse immunoglobulins; (c) an antigen retrieval solution comprising Glyca pH 4.0 buffer; and (d) instructions for use. Monoclonal antibody 5A-1 is specific for an epitope within amino acid residues 52–90 of ODAM, encoded by exon 4, and therefore selectively detects the full-length 279-amino acid isoform. Monoclonal antibody 8B-4 recognizes an epitope between residues 178–216, present in both the full-length and truncated (153-amino acid) isoforms. The kit is used by deparaffinizing 4-µm-thick tissue sections mounted on poly-L-lysine-coated slides, performing antigen retrieval by microwave heating in Glyca pH 4.0 solution, blocking endogenous peroxidase activity, incubating with the primary anti-ODAM monoclonal antibody (e.g., 8B-4 at 1:100 dilution) for 1 hour at room temperature, applying the HRP-polymer secondary reagent, and developing the signal with 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) chromogen. Using this kit, pathologists can assess ODAM expression in tumor biopsies to aid in diagnosis, prognosis, or selection for ODAM-targeted therapies. Validation studies demonstrated strong nuclear and cytoplasmic staining in 77% of breast adenocarcinomas (46/60) and 80% of gastric adenocarcinomas (48/60) on commercial tissue arrays, with minimal background in adjacent normal epithelium. Staining intensity correlated with tumor grade in preliminary analyses, suggesting potential prognostic value. The kit may also be used in research settings to map ODAM expression across normal human tissues, revealing constitutive expression in tracheal and salivary gland epithelia, with weaker signals in bronchus, ovary, bladder, ureter, endometrium, and skin. Specificity is confirmed by pre-absorption of the primary antibody with excess immunizing peptide, which abolishes staining.

### EXAMPLE 3

In a third exemplary embodiment, the invention provides a method for producing recombinant ODAM polypeptides for use as immunogens, diagnostic standards, or therapeutic agents, comprising the steps of: (a) designing PCR primers that amplify cDNA sequences encoding either the full-length ODAM isoform (residues 25–279, exons 2–10) or the truncated isoform (residues 127–279, exons 5–10); (b) cloning the amplified fragments into a prokaryotic expression vector containing a T7 promoter, ribosomal binding site, and C-terminal FLAG tag; (c) transforming the construct into E. coli BL21(DE3) cells; (d) inducing protein expression with 0.1 M IPTG at 37°C for 3 hours; (e) lysing the bacterial cells and purifying the recombinant protein using anti-FLAG affinity chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography; and (f) verifying protein identity by Western blotting with anti-FLAG antibody and mass spectrometry. The forward primer for the full-length construct includes the start codon corresponding to methionine at position 25 (5′-ATGTCTGCCAGCAATAGCAAT), while the reverse primer encodes the last seven amino acids of ODAM plus a stop codon (5′-TGGTTCCCTTAGGCTGTCAGT). For the truncated construct, the forward primer begins at the methionine corresponding to residue 127 (5′-ATGCCCTATGTATTCTCC). The purified recombinant proteins exhibit apparent molecular weights of ~38 kDa and ~21 kDa on SDS-PAGE under reducing conditions, consistent with predicted masses of 29,298 Da and 18,424 Da, respectively, with minor discrepancies attributed to anomalous migration due to high proline/glutamine content. These proteins are used to immunize mice or rabbits to generate monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies, serve as coating antigens in ELISA, or act as positive controls in Western blotting and immunoprecipitation assays. The method ensures high yield and purity, enabling scalable production for clinical and research applications.

### EXAMPLE 4

In a fourth exemplary embodiment, the invention provides a method for in situ detection of ODAM mRNA in tissue sections comprising: (a) deparaffinizing and rehydrating FFPE tissue sections; (b) performing heat-induced epitope retrieval in 0.5× SSC at 90°C for 20 minutes; (c) pre-hybridizing the sections in a hybridization buffer containing 50% formamide, 5× SSC, 0.5% SDS, 10% dextran sulfate, yeast tRNA, and denatured herring sperm DNA; (d) hybridizing overnight at 42°C with a biotinylated antisense oligonucleotide probe specific for ODAM exons 2–10 (e.g., 5′-CAGAGAAAGGTGGAATCCATGAATTAAGTG); (e) washing the sections under stringent conditions (0.2× SSC with pyrophosphate); (f) detecting bound probe using HRP-conjugated antibiotin antibody and tyramide signal amplification; and (g) visualizing the signal with DAB chromogen. Specificity is confirmed by competition with a 100-fold molar excess of unlabeled homologous probe, which abolishes signal, whereas a non-homologous ODAM probe (e.g., targeting exon 5 only) does not. This method was applied to breast cancer tissue arrays and revealed strong hybridization signals in tumor cells of adenocarcinoma specimens, with negligible signal in adjacent normal breast ductal epithelium. The technique provides spatial resolution of ODAM transcriptional activity at the single-cell level and complements protein-based detection methods by confirming that observed immunoreactivity corresponds to active gene expression rather than protein uptake or cross-reactivity. It is particularly useful in distinguishing tumors with true ODAM upregulation from those with passive accumulation or artifactual staining.

### EXAMPLE 5

In a fifth exemplary embodiment, the invention provides a therapeutic composition for treating ODAM-positive epithelial cancer, comprising a monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to an extracellular or surface-accessible epitope of ODAM, optionally conjugated to a cytotoxic agent. Although ODAM is primarily described as an intracellular protein, the consistent detection of autoantibodies in patient sera suggests that ODAM or fragments thereof may be presented on the cell surface via MHC class I or other mechanisms, or released during cell death and subsequently processed by antigen-presenting cells. Based on epitope mapping, monoclonal antibody 8B-4, which recognizes residues 178–216, is selected for therapeutic development due to its reactivity with both ODAM isoforms expressed in tumors. The antibody is humanized to reduce immunogenicity and may be administered intravenously at doses ranging from 1 to 10 mg/kg weekly. In an alternative formulation, the antibody is conjugated to a potent cytotoxic payload such as monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE) via a protease-cleavable linker, creating an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) that delivers the toxin selectively to ODAM-expressing tumor cells. Preclinical testing in xenograft models using Kato III gastric carcinoma cells—which endogenously express ODAM—demonstrates significant tumor growth inhibition compared to control IgG. Additionally, the invention contemplates combination therapy with inhibitors of the Sonic Hedgehog pathway (e.g., Gli-1 antagonists), given the observed co-expression of ODAM and Gli-1 in multiple carcinomas, suggesting a potential synergistic effect. This therapeutic strategy exploits the tumor-restricted expression of ODAM to achieve selective targeting, minimizing damage to normal tissues where ODAM expression is limited to specific epithelial sites such as salivary gland and trachea, which are less susceptible to systemic toxicity.
