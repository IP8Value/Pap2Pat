# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapeutics and diagnostics, particularly to novel monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to mesothelin. More specifically, the invention provides fully human single-domain antibodies and their Fc fusion constructs that recognize a previously untargeted C-terminal epitope of mature mesothelin expressed on the surface of cancer cells. The invention further encompasses immunoconjugates, pharmaceutical compositions, and methods of using these antibodies for the treatment and diagnosis of mesothelin-expressing cancers, including malignant mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and breast cancer. The antibodies of the invention are distinguished by their ability to elicit complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) in addition to antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC), thereby providing a dual mechanism of action that significantly enhances their antitumor efficacy compared to existing anti-mesothelin therapeutics.

## BACKGROUND

Mesothelin is a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored cell surface glycoprotein that is highly overexpressed in a variety of human malignancies while exhibiting limited expression in normal tissues. The mesothelin (MSLN) gene encodes a precursor protein of approximately 71 kDa that undergoes proteolytic cleavage to yield a 31 kDa N-terminal fragment known as megakaryocyte-potentiating factor (MPF) and a 40 kDa C-terminal membrane-bound mature mesothelin. This restricted expression pattern—high in tumors such as malignant mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and breast cancer, but low or absent in most healthy adult tissues—has made mesothelin an attractive target for cancer immunotherapy.

Several anti-mesothelin monoclonal antibodies have been developed and evaluated in clinical and preclinical settings. Notably, SS1P is an immunotoxin composed of a murine anti-mesothelin Fv fragment fused to a truncated Pseudomonas exotoxin, which mediates direct tumor cell killing through inhibition of protein synthesis. MORAb-009 (amatuximab) is a chimeric (mouse/human) IgG1 antibody derived from the same murine SS1 Fv, engineered to elicit antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) against mesothelin-positive tumor cells. Additionally, fully human monoclonal antibodies such as HN1 and m912 have been generated, with HN1 recognizing an epitope overlapping the SS1 binding site in the N-terminal region of mesothelin.

Structural studies have delineated three distinct domains within the mature, cell surface-expressed mesothelin: Region I (residues 296–390), Region II (residues 391–486), and Region III (residues 487–598). Critically, all previously characterized therapeutic anti-mesothelin antibodies—including SS1P, MORAb-009, HN1, and m912—bind exclusively to Region I, the N-terminal portion of the mature protein, which is presumed to be distal from the plasma membrane due to the extended conformation of the GPI-anchored protein. While these agents have demonstrated antitumor activity primarily through ADCC or direct toxin-mediated cytotoxicity, none have exhibited complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), a potent immune effector mechanism that can significantly enhance therapeutic efficacy.

Complement-dependent cytotoxicity is a well-established mechanism by which therapeutic antibodies eliminate target cells. Upon binding to cell surface antigens, certain IgG subclasses (notably IgG1 and IgG3) can activate the classical complement pathway by recruiting the C1q complex, leading to the formation of the membrane attack complex (MAC) and subsequent osmotic lysis of the target cell. The first FDA-approved monoclonal antibody for cancer therapy, rituximab (anti-CD20), derives part of its clinical efficacy from CDC. Moreover, ofatumumab, a second-generation anti-CD20 antibody that binds an epitope closer to the cell membrane than rituximab, demonstrates markedly enhanced CDC activity, underscoring the importance of epitope proximity to the plasma membrane in enabling effective complement activation.

Despite the therapeutic potential of CDC, standard hybridoma technologies—both murine and rabbit—have consistently failed to generate high-affinity antibodies targeting the C-terminal Region III of mesothelin. In large-scale screening efforts involving thousands of hybridoma clones immunized with full-length mesothelin, over 95% of positive clones recognized Region I, with only a handful binding Region III and none targeting the extreme C-terminus near the GPI anchor. This poor immunogenicity of the membrane-proximal domain is likely due to steric occlusion, conformational constraints, or immune tolerance mechanisms that limit B-cell responses to this region.

Consequently, there remains a critical unmet need for anti-mesothelin antibodies capable of engaging multiple immune effector functions, particularly CDC, to improve clinical outcomes in patients with mesothelin-expressing cancers. The development of such antibodies requires innovative approaches to overcome the limitations of conventional immunization and hybridoma strategies, particularly for accessing cryptic, membrane-proximal epitopes that are poorly immunogenic yet therapeutically advantageous.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel fully human single-domain antibodies that specifically bind to a previously untargeted C-terminal epitope of mesothelin, located within Region III (residues 487–598) and proximal to the plasma membrane. In particular, the invention discloses an antibody designated SD1, which was isolated using phage display technology from a naïve human VH domain library panned against a synthetic peptide corresponding to residues 539–588 of human mesothelin. Unlike all previously described anti-mesothelin antibodies that bind Region I, SD1 recognizes a conformational epitope near the C-terminus of mature mesothelin, approximately 10 amino acids upstream of the predicted glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) cleavage site at Ser598.

The invention further provides a recombinant fusion protein, SD1-hFc, comprising the SD1 VH domain fused to the Fc region of human IgG1. This construct forms a dimeric, IgG-like molecule that retains high-affinity binding to native mesothelin on cancer cells and exhibits both complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) against mesothelin-expressing tumor cells in vitro. Importantly, SD1-hFc does not compete with existing anti-mesothelin antibodies such as HN1 or SS1P for binding, confirming that its epitope is non-overlapping and distinct from the N-terminal Region I.

In functional assays, SD1-hFc demonstrated potent CDC activity, inducing significant lysis of mesothelin-positive tumor cells (A431/H9 and NCI-H226) in the presence of human complement, while control antibodies (including HN1 and irrelevant single-domain antibodies) showed no such activity. Flow cytometry confirmed that SD1-hFc, but not HN1, recruits C1q to the surface of tumor cells in a dose-dependent manner, directly linking its membrane-proximal binding to complement activation. Additionally, SD1-hFc mediated robust ADCC using human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and purified natural killer (NK) cells as effectors.

In vivo, SD1-hFc significantly inhibited the growth of A431/H9 xenograft tumors in nude mice when administered as a single agent, achieving approximately 70% tumor growth inhibition compared to control groups. This antitumor efficacy was associated with both CDC and ADCC activity, as demonstrated by the ability of SD1-hFc to engage mouse complement and NK cells, albeit with reduced potency relative to human effectors.

The invention thus provides a new class of anti-mesothelin therapeutics with a unique mechanism of action, combining dual immune effector functions (CDC and ADCC) through targeting of a therapeutically advantageous, membrane-proximal epitope. The SD1 antibody and its derivatives are fully human, minimizing immunogenicity risks, and represent a significant advance over existing chimeric or murine-derived anti-mesothelin agents. The invention further encompasses diagnostic applications, including the use of SD1-based reagents for detecting mesothelin expression in biological samples to confirm cancer diagnosis or monitor disease progression.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### I. Abbreviations

The following abbreviations are used throughout this application: ADCC (antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity); CDC (complement-dependent cytotoxicity); CDR (complementarity-determining region); ELISA (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay); Fc (fragment crystallizable); Fv (fragment variable); GPI (glycosylphosphatidylinositol); IgG (immunoglobulin G); LDH (lactate dehydrogenase); mAb (monoclonal antibody); MSLN (mesothelin); NK (natural killer); PBMC (peripheral blood mononuclear cell); scFv (single-chain variable fragment); VH (variable heavy chain domain); hFc (human Fc region); rFc (rabbit Fc region); RIPA (radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer); FITC (fluorescein isothiocyanate); PE (phycoerythrin); E:T (effector-to-target ratio); MAC (membrane attack complex).

### II. Terms and Methods

As used herein, the term “mesothelin” refers to the mature, C-terminal 40 kDa fragment of the MSLN gene product that is anchored to the cell surface via a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) linkage. It includes wild-type human mesothelin and naturally occurring variants thereof, but excludes the N-terminal MPF fragment. The term “Region III” denotes residues 487–598 of human mesothelin, as defined by structural and functional studies. The “C-terminal epitope” or “membrane-proximal epitope” refers to the antigenic determinant recognized by the SD1 antibody, located within residues 539–588 of mesothelin, approximately 10 amino acids from the GPI anchor site.

A “single-domain antibody” or “VH domain” refers to a functional antigen-binding unit derived solely from the variable heavy chain domain of a human immunoglobulin, lacking light chain association. The term “SD1” designates the specific human VH domain disclosed herein, isolated from a phage display library and characterized by its unique binding to the C-terminal epitope of mesothelin. “SD1-hFc” refers to a recombinant fusion protein comprising the SD1 VH domain linked to the CH2 and CH3 domains of human IgG1, forming a dimeric molecule with Fc-mediated effector functions.

“Complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC)” is defined as the lysis of target cells mediated by the classical complement pathway following antibody binding and C1q recruitment. “Antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC)” refers to the destruction of target cells by immune effector cells (e.g., NK cells, macrophages) through recognition of the antibody Fc region bound to the target antigen.

Methods for generating and characterizing antibodies include phage display panning, ELISA (direct and competition), flow cytometry for cell surface binding and C1q recruitment, Western blotting and immunoprecipitation for protein detection, LDH release assays for cytotoxicity quantification, and xenograft tumor models in immunodeficient mice for in vivo efficacy evaluation. All cell lines used are authenticated and mycoplasma-free, and animal studies are conducted in accordance with institutional ethical guidelines.

### III. Introduction

The development of targeted cancer therapeutics has been revolutionized by the advent of monoclonal antibodies that selectively recognize tumor-associated antigens. Mesothelin stands out as a compelling target due to its high expression across multiple aggressive carcinomas and minimal presence in vital normal tissues. Despite promising preclinical data, clinical outcomes with existing anti-mesothelin agents have been modest, partly due to their reliance on a single mechanism of action—primarily ADCC or toxin-mediated killing—and their inability to engage the complement system. The spatial orientation of antibody binding relative to the cell membrane has emerged as a critical determinant of CDC efficacy, with membrane-proximal epitopes enabling optimal C1q engagement and complement activation. However, the C-terminal domain of mesothelin has proven refractory to conventional antibody discovery platforms, necessitating alternative strategies to access this therapeutically valuable region. The present invention addresses this gap by leveraging phage display technology to isolate a fully human single-domain antibody, SD1, that targets a novel, membrane-proximal epitope and confers dual CDC/ADCC functionality, thereby offering a superior therapeutic profile for the treatment of mesothelin-positive cancers.

### IV. Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies

The invention provides novel monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to mesothelin with high affinity and selectivity. These antibodies are characterized by their recognition of a C-terminal epitope within Region III (residues 487–598) of mature mesothelin, distinct from the N-terminal Region I targeted by all previously known anti-mesothelin therapeutics. The lead antibody, designated SD1, was isolated from a naïve human VH domain phage display library through panning against a synthetic peptide encompassing residues 539–588 of human mesothelin. This epitope lies approximately 10 amino acids upstream of the predicted GPI anchor cleavage site at Ser598, positioning it in close proximity to the plasma membrane—a feature critical for enabling complement-dependent cytotoxicity.

SD1 binds native, conformationally intact mesothelin on the surface of cancer cells with high specificity, as demonstrated by flow cytometry on a panel of mesothelin-expressing tumor lines (including A431/H9, NCI-H226, OVCAR-8, KMBC) and lack of binding to mesothelin-negative controls (e.g., parental A431, HuCCT1). The binding affinity of SD1, as measured by equilibrium dissociation constant (KD), is 13.59 nM for full-length human mesothelin and 16.08 nM for the C-terminal peptide, indicating strong and specific interaction. Importantly, SD1 does not cross-react with mouse mesothelin or unrelated proteins, ensuring species specificity and reducing off-target risks.

Unlike HN1 and SS1P, which bind Region I and block the mesothelin-MUC16 interaction, SD1 does not interfere with this ligand-receptor binding, confirming its non-overlapping epitope. Competition ELISAs further validate that the C-terminal peptide specifically inhibits SD1 binding to full-length mesothelin, whereas it has no effect on HN1 or SS1P binding. This unique epitope specificity enables SD1 to function independently of existing anti-mesothelin agents and opens the possibility for combination therapies without competitive interference.

### V. Antibodies and Antibody Fragments

The invention encompasses the SD1 VH domain in various formats, including as a standalone single-domain antibody and as part of engineered constructs. The isolated SD1 sequence comprises the variable heavy chain framework and complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) necessary for mesothelin recognition. To enhance pharmacokinetics and enable Fc-mediated effector functions, SD1 was fused to the Fc region of human IgG1, generating the dimeric SD1-hFc fusion protein. This construct retains the antigen-binding specificity of SD1 while acquiring the ability to recruit immune effector mechanisms such as CDC and ADCC through its human Fc domain.

SD1-hFc is produced in mammalian HEK-293F cells, yielding high expression levels (>70 mg/L) and >95% purity. The protein migrates as a dimer on SDS-PAGE, consistent with IgG-like architecture. Alternative formats contemplated include scFv (single-chain variable fragment), Fab fragments, and bispecific antibodies incorporating SD1 with other targeting moieties. The invention also covers humanized or affinity-matured variants of SD1 that maintain binding to the C-terminal mesothelin epitope, provided they retain the core functional characteristics disclosed herein.

Notably, attempts to generate functional single-domain antibodies from the VH regions of SS1 or HN1 were unsuccessful, as these domains failed to bind mesothelin when expressed in isolation. This underscores the unique structural stability and antigen-binding capability of the SD1 VH domain, which is amenable to single-domain engineering—a property not shared by conventional anti-mesothelin Fvs.

### VI. Immunoconjugates and Fusion Proteins

Beyond the SD1-hFc fusion protein, the invention includes a range of immunoconjugates and fusion constructs designed to expand therapeutic utility. SD1 can be conjugated to cytotoxic payloads such as Pseudomonas exotoxin fragments, auristatins, or maytansinoids to create targeted immunotoxins or antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) that deliver potent cell-killing agents directly to mesothelin-positive tumor cells. Preliminary studies have demonstrated that SD1-based immunotoxins inhibit proliferation of mesothelin-expressing cancer cells in a dose-dependent manner, validating this approach.

Additionally, SD1 can be fused to cytokines (e.g., IL-2, IL-12), immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., PD-1, CTLA-4 blocking domains), or radionuclides for multimodal therapy. The small size of the SD1 single-domain format may facilitate better tumor penetration compared to full IgG molecules, making it particularly suitable for solid tumor targeting. Fusion proteins may also incorporate tags (e.g., FLAG, His) for purification and detection, as exemplified in the production of SD1-hFc.

The invention further contemplates bispecific antibodies wherein one arm comprises SD1 and the other targets a complementary tumor antigen or immune cell receptor, thereby enhancing tumor selectivity or engaging additional immune effector pathways. Such constructs could simultaneously block mesothelin signaling and recruit T cells or macrophages to the tumor microenvironment.

### VII. Compositions and Methods of Use

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the SD1 antibody or its derivatives in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, formulated for intravenous, subcutaneous, or intraperitoneal administration. Dosage regimens may range from 1 to 100 mg/kg, with exemplary doses of 50 mg/kg administered on a schedule such as days 7, 9, 11, 14, 17, and 20 post-tumor inoculation, based on preclinical efficacy data. Compositions may be combined with chemotherapy, radiation, or other immunotherapies to enhance antitumor responses.

Methods of treatment involve administering the SD1-based composition to a subject diagnosed with a mesothelin-expressing cancer, including but not limited to malignant mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian carcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer, and triple-negative breast cancer. The dual CDC/ADCC mechanism of SD1-hFc offers superior tumor cell killing compared to agents relying solely on ADCC, potentially overcoming resistance mechanisms observed with current therapies.

Diagnostic methods are also encompassed, wherein SD1 or labeled derivatives (e.g., fluorescent, radiolabeled) are used to detect mesothelin expression in tissue biopsies, blood samples, or imaging modalities (e.g., PET, SPECT) to confirm cancer diagnosis, assess tumor burden, or monitor treatment response. The high specificity of SD1 for the C-terminal epitope ensures accurate detection without cross-reactivity to non-target tissues.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Discovery and production of the SD1 human antibody. An engineered human VH domain phage display library (m8l, diversity ~2.5 × 10^10) was subjected to four rounds of panning against a synthetic C-terminal mesothelin peptide (residues 539–588). Phage clones were screened by ELISA for binding to both the peptide and full-length human mesothelin. Clone SD1 was selected based on its dual reactivity. The SD1 VH sequence was PCR-amplified and cloned into vector pVRC8400 to generate plasmid pMH148, encoding SD1 fused to human IgG1 Fc and FLAG/His tags. Stable HEK-293F transfectants produced SD1-hFc at >70 mg/L, purified to >95% homogeneity by protein A chromatography. Control panning on full-length mesothelin yielded clone SD2, which bound only the full-length protein and not the peptide, and was excluded from further study due to its non-C-terminal specificity.

### Example 2

Binding characterization and functional validation. SD1-hFc binding to native mesothelin was confirmed by pull-down assays from cancer cell lysates (A431/H9, NCI-H226, KMBC), detecting the ~40 kDa mature mesothelin band by Western blot. Flow cytometry demonstrated specific binding to mesothelin-positive cells (A431/H9, OVCAR-8, NCI-H226, KMBC) but not mesothelin-negative controls (A431, HuCCT1). Competition ELISA showed that the C-terminal peptide blocked SD1-hFc binding to mesothelin, whereas HN1 and SS1P binding was unaffected. Kinetic analysis revealed KD values of 13.59 nM (full-length mesothelin) and 16.08 nM (peptide). SD1 did not inhibit mesothelin-MUC16 binding, unlike SS1P, confirming non-overlapping epitopes. C1q recruitment assays by flow cytometry demonstrated dose-dependent C1q binding to tumor cells only in the presence of SD1-hFc, not HN1 or control antibodies.

### Example 3

Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies for Detecting Cancer in a Subject or Confirming the Diagnosis of Cancer in a Subject

The SD1 antibody and its derivatives are useful as diagnostic reagents for the detection of mesothelin-expressing cancers in biological samples. In one embodiment, SD1-hFc is conjugated to a detectable label such as a fluorophore (e.g., FITC, Alexa Fluor), enzyme (e.g., horseradish peroxidase), or radionuclide (e.g., ⁸⁹Zr, ¹²⁴I). The labeled SD1 is then applied to formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor sections or fresh frozen tissues, followed by standard immunohistochemistry (IHC) or immunofluorescence protocols. Specific staining of tumor cells confirms mesothelin expression and supports diagnosis of mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, or other mesothelin-positive malignancies.

In another embodiment, SD1 is used in liquid biopsy applications. Serum or plasma samples from subjects are incubated with SD1-coated magnetic beads to capture soluble mesothelin-related peptides (SMRPs). Captured SMRPs are detected by ELISA using a secondary anti-mesothelin antibody, providing a quantitative measure of tumor burden. This method offers a non-invasive alternative to tissue biopsy for monitoring disease progression or treatment response.

For in vivo imaging, SD1 is radiolabeled with positron-emitting isotopes and administered intravenously to subjects. Whole-body PET/CT scans are performed 24–72 hours post-injection to visualize mesothelin-positive tumor deposits. The high specificity and rapid clearance of the SD1 single-domain format may provide superior tumor-to-background ratios compared to full IgG imaging agents.

### Example 4

Antitumor efficacy and mechanism of action. In vitro CDC assays using human serum as a complement source showed that SD1-hFc induced 40% lysis of A431/H9 and >30% lysis of NCI-H226 cells at 100 µg/mL, while HN1 and control antibodies showed no activity. ADCC assays with human PBMCs or purified NK cells demonstrated >40% killing of A431/H9 and >30% of NCI-H226 cells. In vivo, athymic nude mice bearing A431/H9 xenografts treated with 50 mg/kg SD1-hFc (intravenous, six doses) exhibited average tumor volumes of 300 mm³ versus 1,000 mm³ in PBS controls at day 20, representing 70% growth inhibition. Mouse serum supported modest CDC (11% lysis), and purified mouse NK cells mediated ADCC, confirming that both mechanisms contribute to in vivo efficacy. Histological analysis of excised tumors revealed increased immune cell infiltration and complement deposition in SD1-treated groups.

## DISCUSSION

The present invention represents a paradigm shift in anti-mesothelin antibody therapy by successfully targeting a therapeutically superior, membrane-proximal epitope that enables complement-dependent cytotoxicity—a mechanism previously unattainable with existing agents. The failure of conventional hybridoma approaches to access this region underscores the necessity of advanced display technologies like phage selection on defined peptide antigens. SD1’s unique epitope specificity not only confers CDC activity but also avoids interference with mesothelin’s physiological interactions, potentially reducing unintended biological consequences. The fully human nature of SD1 minimizes immunogenicity, a critical advantage over chimeric antibodies like MORAb-009. The robust in vivo efficacy as a single agent, coupled with the versatility of the single-domain platform for engineering immunoconjugates and bispecifics, positions SD1 as a foundational molecule for next-generation mesothelin-targeted therapeutics. Future work will focus on clinical translation, combination strategies, and exploration of SD1’s potential in overcoming resistance to current anti-mesothelin regimens.
