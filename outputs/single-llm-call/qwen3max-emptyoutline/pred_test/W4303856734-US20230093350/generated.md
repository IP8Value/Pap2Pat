# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to noninvasive medical diagnostic and monitoring systems, and more specifically to a portable, super-continuum infrared spectroscopy system for the simultaneous measurement of cerebral or tissue hemodynamics and metabolism. The invention provides a novel apparatus and method for monitoring the redox state of cytochrome c oxidase (CCO) in conjunction with oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO) and deoxygenated hemoglobin (HbR) concentrations in biological tissues using a high-brightness, all-fiber integrated super-continuum laser source. This system enables real-time, continuous assessment of both oxygen delivery and cellular metabolic activity in the brain and other organs, offering significant clinical utility in emergency medicine, critical care, neurology, and trauma surgery. The invention is particularly suited for applications involving traumatic brain injury, hemorrhagic shock, stroke, concussion screening, and intraoperative monitoring where rapid assessment of tissue viability and metabolic status is essential for guiding therapeutic interventions. By integrating advanced optical technologies with a ruggedized, portable platform, the present invention overcomes longstanding limitations of conventional near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) systems that measure only hemodynamic parameters without direct assessment of mitochondrial function and cellular energy metabolism.

## BACKGROUND

The clinical need for noninvasive, continuous monitoring of tissue metabolism and hemodynamics has long been recognized across multiple medical specialties, yet existing technologies have failed to provide a practical solution that combines both capabilities in a single, portable instrument. Current approaches to monitoring cerebral and tissue oxygenation primarily rely on functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) systems that measure changes in oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin concentrations. While these systems provide valuable information about blood flow and oxygen delivery, they fundamentally lack the ability to directly assess cellular metabolic activity, which is critical for understanding tissue viability and predicting patient outcomes in conditions such as traumatic brain injury, hemorrhagic shock, and stroke. The limitation of hemodynamic-only monitoring becomes particularly problematic in clinical scenarios where brain tissue dysfunction may occur without corresponding alterations in regional blood flow, leading to delayed recognition of metabolic crisis and missed opportunities for timely intervention.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and functional MRI (fMRI) represent the gold standard for noninvasive assessment of brain structure and function, but these modalities suffer from significant practical limitations including high cost, poor portability, inability to provide continuous monitoring, and incompatibility with many critically ill patients who require life-support equipment. Computed tomography (CT) scans similarly provide only static snapshots of anatomical structure without functional metabolic information and involve ionizing radiation exposure. Invasive monitoring techniques such as brain tissue oxygen tension probes and cerebral microdialysis can provide direct metabolic measurements but require surgical placement of probes into brain tissue, limiting their use to specialized neurocritical care settings and carrying inherent risks of infection, hemorrhage, and additional tissue damage.

The scientific foundation for measuring cellular metabolism noninvasively lies in the optical properties of cytochrome c oxidase (CCO), the terminal enzyme in the mitochondrial electron transport chain responsible for more than 95% of oxygen metabolism in the body. CCO exists in two redox states—oxidized and reduced—and the ratio between these states reflects the balance between oxygen availability and cellular energy demand. Changes in the CCO redox state can indicate whether mitochondria are able to compensate for changes in oxygen delivery at the cellular level, providing a direct measure of metabolic function that complements hemodynamic measurements. However, despite the theoretical attractiveness of CCO as a metabolic marker, practical implementation has been hindered by significant technical challenges. The in vivo concentration of CCO is only 5-10% that of hemoglobin, and its spectral signature is broad and featureless compared to the more distinct absorption spectra of hemoglobin species. Furthermore, the CCO signal is less than 10% of the hemoglobin signal amplitude, making it extremely difficult to detect reliably above background noise and interference from other chromophores.

Previous attempts to measure CCO noninvasively have employed conventional light sources such as tungsten-halogen lamps or light-emitting diodes (LEDs), but these approaches have consistently suffered from inadequate signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) due to insufficient brightness and inefficient coupling into delivery fibers. The low SNR has resulted in inconsistent, inconclusive measurements that lack the reliability required for clinical decision-making. Additionally, conventional NIRS systems typically employ only a few discrete wavelengths, limiting their ability to resolve the overlapping spectra of multiple chromophores simultaneously. The development of broadband light sources capable of covering the full near-infrared spectrum from approximately 700-900 nm, where CCO exhibits its characteristic absorption features, has been identified as a key enabling technology for reliable CCO measurement.

Recent advances in laser technology have produced super-continuum laser (SCL) sources that generate broadband output spanning the visible to short-wave infrared spectrum, but these systems have historically been based on complex, mode-locked laser architectures that are expensive, bulky, and unsuitable for clinical environments. Furthermore, previous SCL implementations for biomedical applications have not been optimized for the specific requirements of CCO measurement, including appropriate spectral coverage, sufficient brightness within safety limits, and robustness for portable operation. The integration of such light sources into complete, self-contained monitoring systems that can be deployed in diverse clinical settings—from emergency departments to operating rooms to battlefield environments—has remained an unmet need.

The clinical context for this technological gap is particularly acute in trauma care, where hemorrhagic shock and traumatic brain injury represent the leading causes of preventable death in both military and civilian settings. The "golden hour" following injury represents a critical window during which timely intervention can significantly improve survival outcomes, yet current monitoring capabilities are insufficient to guide optimal resuscitation strategies. Emerging interventions such as resuscitative endovascular balloon occlusion of the aorta (REBOA) for controlling noncompressible torso hemorrhage create new challenges in monitoring distal tissue perfusion and metabolic status, as complete aortic occlusion can lead to ischemic injury in organs below the balloon while partial occlusion requires precise titration based on real-time metabolic feedback. Similarly, pharmacological adjuncts such as valproic acid (VPA) that enhance ischemia tolerance require monitoring systems capable of assessing both hemodynamic and metabolic responses to optimize dosing and timing.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges by providing a novel super-continuum infrared spectroscopy system specifically designed and optimized for simultaneous measurement of CCO, HbO, and HbR with unprecedented signal quality and reliability. By combining an innovative, all-fiber integrated super-continuum laser source with advanced optical detection schemes and robust mechanical packaging, the invention overcomes the fundamental limitations that have prevented previous CCO measurement attempts from achieving clinical utility. The resulting system provides a practical, portable solution for continuous monitoring of both oxygen delivery and cellular metabolic activity, enabling new approaches to diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic guidance in critical care medicine.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a noninvasive super-continuum infrared spectroscopy system for cytochrome c oxidase (SCISCCO) that enables simultaneous, continuous monitoring of tissue hemodynamics and metabolism through the concurrent measurement of oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO), deoxygenated hemoglobin (HbR), and the redox state of cytochrome c oxidase (CCO). The system comprises a novel all-fiber integrated super-continuum laser (SCL) source that generates high-brightness, broadband output spanning from 670 nm to 2500 nm, with particular optimization for the 750-900 nm wavelength range where CCO exhibits its characteristic absorption features. This SCL source provides nearly an order of magnitude higher spectral density compared to conventional lamp-based systems, enabling high signal-to-noise ratio measurements of the weak CCO signal that has previously been obscured by noise in conventional near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) systems.

The SCISCCO system employs a two-arm differential optical configuration that includes a reference arm and a sample arm, with polarization filtering and lock-in amplification to further enhance signal quality and reject environmental interference. The system utilizes a modified Beer-Lambert law-based algorithm to simultaneously resolve the concentrations of HbO, HbR, and CCO from the measured wavelength-dependent optical intensity changes across the 750-900 nm spectral range. The invention is implemented in a self-contained, portable, cart-based prototype that integrates the SCL source, optical components, electronics, and processing computer into a single ruggedized unit suitable for deployment in diverse clinical environments including emergency departments, operating rooms, intensive care units, and field settings.

The key innovation of the present invention lies in the combination of the high-brightness, all-fiber integrated SCL source with the optimized optical detection scheme and robust mechanical packaging, which together overcome the fundamental limitations that have prevented reliable CCO measurement in previous systems. The SCL source is based on a modulational instability-initiated super-continuum generation architecture that uses picosecond or nanosecond pump pulses from a distributed feedback semiconductor seed laser, amplified through ytterbium-doped fiber amplifiers in a master oscillator power amplifier (MOPA) configuration. This design provides scalable time-averaged output power from 0.3 W to 11 W with excellent power stability (±0.21%) and maintains a consistent output spectrum across the entire operating range. The system's diffraction-limited beam quality enables efficient coupling into monochromators and delivery fibers, maximizing the spectral density delivered to the tissue sample while remaining within established maximum permissible exposure (MPE) safety limits.

The SCISCCO system has been validated through comprehensive laboratory, human, and animal studies demonstrating its ability to reliably measure CCO redox state changes in response to physiological and cognitive challenges. In pilot human studies involving 25 healthy participants performing cognitive attention tasks, the system demonstrated that HbO concentration increases while the CCO redox state decreases during periods of increased neural activity, consistent with established physiological principles of neurovascular coupling and metabolic demand. The system's measurements showed good correlation with commercial fNIRS systems for hemodynamic parameters while providing the additional metabolic dimension through CCO measurement that was previously unavailable. In swine animal models of hemorrhagic shock treated with partial resuscitative endovascular balloon occlusion of the aorta (pREBOA) with and without valproic acid (VPA) supplementation, the SCISCCO system successfully tracked expected changes in HbO, HbR, and CCO concentrations across different clinical phases, with measurements correlating well with independent physiological monitors such as arterial blood gas analyzers and mean arterial pressure measurements.

The invention provides significant clinical advantages over existing technologies by enabling simultaneous assessment of both oxygen delivery (through HbO and HbR measurements) and cellular metabolic activity (through CCO redox state measurement). This dual capability allows clinicians to distinguish between situations where decreased oxygen utilization reflects true cellular dysfunction versus decreased oxygen demand (as in deep sedation), providing critical information for guiding therapeutic interventions. The system's portability and ruggedized design enable deployment in diverse clinical settings where continuous monitoring of tissue viability is essential, including trauma resuscitation, neurocritical care, cardiac surgery, and organ transplantation. The invention represents a fundamental advance in noninvasive monitoring technology by providing the first practical, reliable means to assess mitochondrial function and cellular energy metabolism in real-time, opening new possibilities for early detection of metabolic crisis, optimization of resuscitation strategies, and improved prediction of patient outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive solution for noninvasive monitoring of tissue hemodynamics and metabolism through the development of a super-continuum infrared spectroscopy system for cytochrome c oxidase (SCISCCO). The system architecture integrates multiple innovative components and methodologies to overcome the longstanding challenges associated with reliable CCO measurement, resulting in a practical, portable instrument suitable for clinical deployment. The core of the invention is the all-fiber integrated super-continuum laser (SCL) source, which represents a significant advancement over conventional light sources used in previous NIRS systems. This SCL source is based on a modulational instability-initiated super-continuum generation mechanism that provides high-brightness, broadband output with exceptional spectral flatness and power stability.

The SCL source operates through a master oscillator power amplifier (MOPA) configuration comprising a distributed feedback semiconductor seed laser that outputs approximately 1-nanosecond pulses at adjustable repetition rates ranging from 100 kHz to 4 MHz. These pulses are amplified through a two-stage ytterbium-doped fiber amplifier (YDFA) system, where the preamplifier stage is optimized for noise performance and the power amplifier stage employs cladding-pumped fiber technology to minimize nonlinear distortions at high power levels. Band-pass filters and isolators between amplifier stages prevent amplified spontaneous emission and spurious reflections that could degrade system performance. The amplified pulses are then coupled into a cascade of specialty fibers for super-continuum generation, beginning with a few meters of standard single-mode fiber (SMF) where modulational instability converts the nanosecond pulses into a series of shorter, high-peak-power pulses through soliton formation. These shorter pulses are subsequently coupled into several meters of nonlinear super-continuum fiber where spectral broadening occurs through multiple physical processes including four-wave mixing, self-phase modulation, and the optical Raman effect.

The resulting SCL output provides time-averaged power scalable from 0.3 W to 11 W depending on the pulse repetition rate, with a consistent output spectrum spanning from 670 nm to 2500 nm and power stability of ±0.21%. The system achieves a wall-plug efficiency of approximately 11% and produces a spatially coherent, near-diffraction-limited output beam across the entire spectral range. All fiber components are fusion-spliced to create a monolithic, alignment-free optical path with no moving parts, contributing to the system's robustness and reliability. The entire SCL source occupies a compact footprint of 10 × 14 × 2.5 inches and weighs approximately 11 pounds, making it suitable for integration into portable monitoring systems.

The optical detection scheme of the SCISCCO system employs a two-arm differential configuration that significantly enhances signal quality and rejects common-mode noise. The SCL output is coupled into a tunable grating spectrometer (such as the SpectraPro 2150 with 600 g/mm grating) that selects specific wavelengths for interrogation. A polarizer ensures single polarization state to avoid noise from fluctuating polarization states, and a broadband beam splitter (typically 50:50 for laboratory configurations or 99:1 for human studies) divides the light into reference and sample arms. The reference arm contains a matched cuvette or serves as a direct reference path, while the sample arm delivers light to the tissue through a fiber-optic probe and collects the diffusely reflected light. Matched silicon photodetectors in both arms feed into lock-in amplifiers synchronized to an optical chopper operating at 271 Hz, providing exceptional rejection of ambient light and other spurious signals outside the detection bandwidth.

The fiber-optic probe design is optimized for different measurement scenarios, with adjustable source-detector separation distances ranging from 2 cm to 3 cm to achieve appropriate penetration depths for various tissue types. For forehead measurements targeting the frontal lobe, a 3 cm separation provides adequate penetration through the scalp and skull to reach cortical tissue, while 2 cm separation is sufficient for peripheral tissue measurements such as forearm muscle. The probe construction uses 2-mm diameter borosilicate fiber bundles with SMA connectors mounted on a solid base to ensure stable positioning and minimize motion artifacts. In comparative studies with commercial fNIRS systems, the probe design was modified to interleave SCISCCO and fNIRS fibers in a rectangular configuration to ensure measurement of the same tissue volume.

Signal processing in the SCISCCO system employs a modified Beer-Lambert law-based algorithm that simultaneously resolves the concentrations of HbO, HbR, and CCO from the measured wavelength-dependent optical intensity changes. The algorithm uses known extinction coefficients for each chromophore across the 750-900 nm wavelength range, along with estimated differential pathlength factors (DPF) and source-detector separation distances to calculate absolute concentration changes. For adult forehead measurements, a DPF of 6.26 is typically used, while the wavelength range is sampled at 3-nm intervals across 50 data points to provide sufficient spectral resolution for distinguishing the overlapping absorption spectra of the three chromophores. The system's high spectral density enables measurements within established maximum permissible exposure (MPE) limits while maintaining superior signal-to-noise ratio compared to conventional systems.

The complete SCISCCO system is implemented in a cart-based prototype that integrates all components into a single, portable unit suitable for clinical deployment. The rolling cart system has three levels: the top level houses the SCL source and processing laptop, the middle level contains the optical system hardware including the spectrometer, beam steering optics, and fiber launchers, and the bottom level accommodates the electronic components including lock-in amplifiers and power supplies. All components are mechanically secured with vibration-isolation mounting to ensure stability during transport and operation. The system includes a user-friendly graphical interface that allows operators to set measurement parameters, initiate data collection, and view real-time plots of HbO, HbR, and CCO concentrations as functions of time.

The cart-based prototype has been extensively tested for mechanical stability and transportability, demonstrating robust performance when rolled across brick hallways and transported in vehicles over both paved and dirt roads. Spectral and power output measurements before and after transport show minimal degradation, with the ratio of sample to reference arm signals (after cancellation) remaining close to unity, indicating excellent system stability. The prototype has been successfully deployed in remote locations, including transport from the University of Michigan in Ann Arbor to Northwestern University Medical Center in Chicago for animal studies, demonstrating its suitability for multi-site clinical research and eventual widespread clinical adoption.

The system's performance characteristics have been validated through comprehensive testing across multiple domains. Laboratory measurements of purified bovine heart CCO solutions in both oxidized and reduced states confirm the system's ability to accurately capture the characteristic CCO absorption spectrum with a broad peak near 825 nm spanning 750-900 nm. Human validation studies include blood pressure cuff occlusion tests on the forearm, breath-holding challenges on the forehead, and cognitive attention tasks, all demonstrating expected physiological responses consistent with established literature. The system shows good correlation with commercial fNIRS systems for hemodynamic measurements while providing the additional metabolic dimension through CCO measurement. Animal studies in swine models of hemorrhagic shock treated with partial REBOA with and without VPA supplementation demonstrate the system's ability to track expected changes in hemodynamic and metabolic parameters across different clinical phases, with measurements correlating well with independent physiological monitors.

The SCISCCO system represents a significant advancement in noninvasive monitoring technology by providing the first practical, reliable means to simultaneously assess both oxygen delivery and cellular metabolic activity in real-time. The combination of high-brightness SCL source, optimized optical detection scheme, and robust mechanical packaging overcomes the fundamental limitations that have prevented previous CCO measurement attempts from achieving clinical utility. The system's portability and ease of use make it suitable for deployment in diverse clinical settings where continuous monitoring of tissue viability is essential for guiding therapeutic interventions and improving patient outcomes.

### Empirical Examples

The empirical validation of the SCISCCO system encompasses comprehensive testing across laboratory, human, and animal domains, demonstrating the system's reliability, accuracy, and clinical relevance. Laboratory validation began with in vitro measurements of purified bovine heart cytochrome c oxidase (CCO) solutions prepared in 25 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.8) with 5 mM EDTA and 39 mM n-dodecyl β-D-maltoside at a concentration of 5 mg/mL (approximately 16 µM). The CCO solution was first measured in its natural oxidized state, then converted to the fully reduced state by addition of 80 mM dithionite solution and allowed to equilibrate for 8 minutes. Using the laboratory configuration with a 50:50 beam splitter and matched water reference cuvette, the system successfully captured the characteristic CCO absorption spectrum showing a broad, featureless peak near 825 nm spanning the 750-900 nm wavelength range. The measured spectrum showed excellent agreement with literature values, confirming the system's ability to accurately resolve CCO spectral features. Repeated measurements demonstrated the system's stability and reproducibility, with five measurements of each redox state yielding consistent results.

Human validation studies employed three distinct experimental paradigms to test the system's performance under different physiological and cognitive conditions. The first validation study involved blood pressure cuff occlusion tests on the forearm of healthy human participants. A blood pressure cuff was inflated to above 100 mmHg for 4 minutes while the SCISCCO probe was applied to the ipsilateral forearm. The system successfully captured the expected hemodynamic response: HbR concentration increased during cuff occlusion due to venous pooling and reduced arterial inflow, while HbO concentration initially increased slightly then decreased as arterial inflow became restricted. Critically, CCO concentration remained relatively unchanged throughout the occlusion period, consistent with the low metabolic demand of resting forearm muscle and the absence of significant metabolic stress during brief occlusion. These results matched published literature findings from similar studies using different super-continuum sources, validating the SCISCCO system's accuracy for hemodynamic measurements while demonstrating its ability to detect the absence of metabolic changes when none are expected.

The second human validation study employed breath-holding challenges to test the system's response to transient hypoxia and hypercapnia. Healthy participants performed repeated 30-second breath-holding episodes interspersed with 5-minute normal breathing periods while the SCISCCO probe was applied to the forehead. Simultaneous measurements with a commercial fNIRS system (CW6, TechEn Inc.) allowed direct comparison of hemodynamic responses. The SCISCCO system demonstrated good correlation with the fNIRS system for HbO measurements, with both systems showing the expected increase in HbO concentration during breath-holding due to cerebral vasodilation in response to rising CO2 levels. The correlation for HbR measurements was less robust, consistent with the known challenges of HbR measurement due to lower signal-to-noise ratio at shorter wavelengths. Importantly, the SCISCCO system detected a consistent decrease in CCO redox state during breath-holding periods across multiple participants, indicating increased metabolic demand as the brain responds to the physiological stress of breath-holding. This finding demonstrates the system's ability to detect metabolic changes that would be invisible to conventional hemodynamic-only monitoring systems.

The third and most significant human validation study involved a pilot cognitive attention task with 25 healthy participants. The study employed a modified probe design that interleaved SCISCCO and fNIRS fibers to ensure measurement of the same frontal lobe tissue volume. Participants performed a letter discrimination task requiring sustained attention while the system recorded continuous measurements of HbO, HbR, and CCO concentrations. Due to the system's current 17-second scan time limitation, rigorous data screening protocols were implemented to exclude measurements corrupted by motion artifacts or other interference. After removing eight outlier participants due to technical errors, poor signal quality, or unexpected hemodynamic responses, the remaining 17 participants' data were analyzed using two complementary screening methods: a "best of six" approach selecting the six blocks per participant with the largest HbO increases, and an "HbO increasing" approach selecting only blocks where HbO concentration exceeded baseline during the attention task.

Both analysis methods yielded consistent results: during attention task periods, HbO concentration increased significantly while CCO redox state decreased significantly, with the latter change being more pronounced in the "HbO increasing" analysis. Specifically, the "best of six" method showed CCO decreasing in 68 out of 102 analyzed blocks (67%), while the "HbO increasing" method showed CCO decreasing in 57 out of 72 analyzed blocks (79%). These findings are physiologically consistent with established models of neurovascular coupling and metabolic demand: increased neural activity in the frontal lobe during attention tasks requires additional ATP production, leading to increased reduction of CCO as electrons flow through the mitochondrial electron transport chain, while compensatory vasodilation increases oxygen delivery reflected in rising HbO concentration. The system's ability to detect this expected dissociation between hemodynamic and metabolic responses—where HbO increases while CCO decreases—demonstrates its unique capability to provide insights beyond conventional hemodynamic monitoring.

Animal validation studies employed Yorkshire swine (40-60 kg) subjected to controlled hemorrhagic shock followed by treatment with partial resuscitative endovascular balloon occlusion of the aorta (pREBOA) with and without valproic acid (VPA) supplementation. The cart-based SCISCCO prototype was transported from the University of Michigan to Northwestern University Medical Center and deployed in the operating room setting. Probes were secured to the swine forehead with surgical tape throughout the 8-hour experimental protocol, which included 40% total blood volume hemorrhage, 1 hour of non-resuscitated shock (MAP 30-35 mmHg), 3 hours of pREBOA with or without VPA infusion, and whole blood transfusion with balloon deflation.

The SCISCCO system successfully tracked expected changes in hemodynamic and metabolic parameters across all experimental phases. During initial hemorrhage, the system detected increased HbO concentration reflecting compensatory tachycardia and vasoconstriction, followed by sharp increases in HbR and decreases in HbO as hemorrhage outpaced compensatory mechanisms ("stage 4 shock"). During pREBOA application, the system captured the expected shunting of oxygenated blood to the brain, with HbO concentration initially increasing then showing oscillatory behavior correlating with mean arterial pressure fluctuations. Throughout the pREBOA period, CCO redox state gradually decreased, reflecting the anesthetized animal's stable metabolic demand against gradually deteriorating oxygen supply. Upon balloon deflation and blood transfusion, the system detected the expected reperfusion injury pattern with initial surge of deoxygenated blood followed by restoration of oxygenated blood as transfusion progressed.

Critically, the SCISCCO measurements showed good correlation with independent physiological monitors. Comparison with arterial blood gas (ABG) measurements demonstrated reasonable agreement in oxygenation trends, with the SCISCCO system providing superior temporal resolution. Correlation with mean arterial pressure measurements during oscillatory periods confirmed the system's ability to detect hemodynamic instability. The preliminary data from pREBOA plus VPA experiments suggested potential differences in hemodynamic and metabolic responses compared to pREBOA alone, though larger studies are needed to confirm these findings. Overall, the animal studies demonstrated the SCISCCO system's feasibility for continuous monitoring in complex surgical and critical care scenarios, with measurements consistent with established physiological principles and independent monitoring modalities.

These empirical examples collectively demonstrate the SCISCCO system's reliability, accuracy, and clinical relevance across diverse measurement scenarios. The system's ability to simultaneously resolve hemodynamic and metabolic parameters with high signal-to-noise ratio represents a significant advancement over existing technologies, providing new insights into tissue physiology and opening possibilities for improved clinical decision-making in critical care settings. The successful validation across laboratory, human, and animal domains confirms the system's readiness for broader clinical evaluation and eventual deployment in diverse medical environments.
