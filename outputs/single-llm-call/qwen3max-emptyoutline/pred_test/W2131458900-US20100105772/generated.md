# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmic therapeutics and, more specifically, to the use of specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators—namely aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1)—for the treatment and prevention of corneal neovascularization. The invention further pertains to methods for modulating inflammatory and angiogenic responses in ocular tissues by administering these lipid mediators to suppress hemangiogenesis without affecting lymphangiogenesis. More particularly, the invention provides a novel therapeutic strategy that exploits the dual anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving properties of these endogenous lipid mediators to regulate innate immune cell infiltration, downregulate expression of key pro-inflammatory cytokines and vascular endothelial growth factors (VEGFs), and thereby inhibit pathological blood vessel formation in the cornea. The invention is especially relevant for treating conditions such as suture-induced or cytokine-induced corneal inflammation, where uncontrolled neovascularization compromises corneal transparency and visual acuity. By targeting both the resolution phase of inflammation and the molecular drivers of angiogenesis, the disclosed compositions and methods offer a significant advance over conventional anti-angiogenic or immunosuppressive therapies, which often fail to address the underlying inflammatory milieu or carry substantial off-target effects.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Corneal neovascularization (CNV) represents a pathological condition characterized by the ingrowth of new blood vessels from the limbal vascular plexus into the normally avascular corneal stroma. This process is a leading cause of corneal opacity and vision loss worldwide, contributing significantly to blindness in both developed and developing countries. CNV typically arises in response to various insults, including infection, chemical injury, autoimmune disorders, corneal transplantation, and surgical trauma. The development of CNV is tightly linked to chronic inflammation, wherein the disruption of the corneal immune privilege leads to the recruitment of innate immune cells—particularly neutrophils and macrophages—and the subsequent release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and angiogenic factors. Among these, interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), and vascular endothelial growth factor-A (VEGF-A) are recognized as pivotal mediators that drive both inflammatory cell infiltration and hemangiogenesis.

Traditional therapeutic approaches to CNV have largely focused on broad-spectrum anti-inflammatory agents such as corticosteroids or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), or on direct inhibition of VEGF signaling using monoclonal antibodies or receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors. While these strategies can provide temporary relief, they often fail to resolve the underlying inflammatory cascade and may lead to significant side effects, including delayed wound healing, increased risk of infection, and intraocular pressure elevation. Moreover, many existing anti-angiogenic therapies indiscriminately target both hemangiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis, despite growing evidence that lymphatic vessels play a distinct and sometimes beneficial role in immune surveillance and resolution of inflammation.

In recent years, a new class of endogenous lipid mediators derived from omega-3 and omega-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids has emerged as critical regulators of the resolution phase of inflammation. These specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs)—including lipoxins, resolvins, protectins, and maresins—possess the unique ability to simultaneously dampen inflammation and actively promote tissue repair without causing immunosuppression. Lipoxin A4 (LXA4), for example, is biosynthesized from arachidonic acid during the resolution phase and signals through the ALX/FPR2 receptor to inhibit neutrophil recruitment, enhance macrophage phagocytosis of apoptotic cells, and suppress pro-inflammatory cytokine production. Aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 (ATL), generated via acetylation of cyclooxygenase-2 by aspirin, exhibits enhanced metabolic stability and prolonged biological activity compared to native LXA4. Synthetic analogs such as ATLa have been developed to mimic these properties while resisting enzymatic degradation.

Similarly, resolvins D1 and E1 are derived from docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), respectively, and act through specific G-protein-coupled receptors—GPR32 for RvD1 and ChemR23 for RvE1—to orchestrate the termination of inflammation. These mediators have demonstrated efficacy in diverse models of inflammatory disease, including periodontitis, colitis, and retinal inflammation. However, their potential role in regulating corneal angiogenesis had remained largely unexplored prior to the present invention.

Notably, while previous studies hinted at possible anti-angiogenic effects of certain SPMs, the mechanisms by which these mediators influence vascular growth in the cornea—and whether they differentially affect blood versus lymphatic vessel formation—were unknown. Furthermore, the cellular and molecular targets of ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 within the corneal microenvironment, including the expression patterns of their respective receptors on resident and infiltrating cells, had not been systematically characterized. This knowledge gap hindered the rational development of SPM-based therapies for ocular surface diseases.

The present invention addresses these critical limitations by demonstrating, for the first time, that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 exert potent and selective inhibitory effects on inflammatory corneal hemangiogenesis through coordinated regulation of innate immune responses and VEGF signaling. Importantly, the invention reveals that these mediators do not suppress lymphangiogenesis, suggesting a refined therapeutic window that preserves beneficial lymphatic functions. Moreover, the invention identifies ATLa as uniquely capable of directly inhibiting VEGF-A-driven angiogenesis, independent of its anti-inflammatory actions, thereby establishing it as the most potent agent among this class for suppressing neovascularization. These findings collectively establish a foundation for the clinical translation of SPMs as next-generation therapeutics for corneal neovascularization and related inflammatory eye disorders.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for the treatment and prevention of corneal neovascularization by administering specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators—specifically aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1)—to subjects in need thereof. The invention is based on the discovery that these lipid mediators significantly suppress inflammatory corneal hemangiogenesis by modulating innate immune responses and downregulating key angiogenic pathways, while leaving lymphangiogenesis unaffected. In particular, the invention demonstrates that local administration of ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 reduces the infiltration of neutrophils and macrophages into the cornea, decreases the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β, and downregulates mRNA levels of VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2, all of which contribute to pathological blood vessel formation. Remarkably, only ATLa was found to additionally inhibit VEGF-A-induced hemangiogenesis in a direct, inflammation-independent manner, highlighting its superior potency among the tested mediators.

The invention further discloses that the receptors for these lipid mediators—namely Fpr-rs2 (a murine homolog of the human ALX/FPR2 receptor for lipoxins) and ChemR23 (the receptor for RvE1)—are expressed by corneal epithelial cells, stromal keratocytes, and infiltrating CD11b⁺ immune cells, providing a mechanistic basis for their biological activity in the corneal microenvironment. These findings support the use of ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 as targeted therapeutics that act through endogenous resolution pathways to restore corneal homeostasis without inducing generalized immunosuppression.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method of treating corneal neovascularization in a mammal, comprising administering to the eye of said mammal a therapeutically effective amount of ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1, or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt or derivative thereof. In another embodiment, the invention encompasses a pharmaceutical composition comprising one or more of these lipid mediators in a carrier suitable for ocular delivery, such as a subconjunctival injection formulation, topical eye drop, or sustained-release implant. The invention also includes methods for preventing post-surgical or post-injury corneal neovascularization, particularly in the context of corneal transplantation or refractive surgery, by prophylactic administration of these mediators.

Additionally, the invention provides diagnostic and prognostic tools based on the expression levels of Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23 in corneal tissues, which may be used to identify patients most likely to respond to SPM-based therapy. The selective inhibition of hemangiogenesis without affecting lymphangiogenesis represents a significant therapeutic advantage, as it avoids potential impairment of immune surveillance and fluid drainage functions mediated by lymphatic vessels. Overall, the invention establishes a paradigm-shifting approach to managing corneal neovascularization by harnessing the body’s natural resolution mechanisms, offering improved efficacy, safety, and specificity compared to conventional anti-inflammatory or anti-angiogenic treatments.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Results

The present invention is supported by comprehensive experimental data demonstrating that aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1) effectively suppress inflammatory corneal neovascularization through dual anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving mechanisms. Initial investigations confirmed the expression of key receptors for these lipid mediators in the murine cornea. Using reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR), it was established that Fpr-rs2—the murine ortholog of the human ALX/FPR2 receptor for lipoxin A4—and ChemR23—the receptor for RvE1—are constitutively expressed in both the epithelial and stromal-endothelial layers of normal corneas. Notably, the alternative murine lipoxin receptor Fprl1 was not detected under baseline conditions, indicating receptor specificity within the corneal tissue. To further delineate cellular sources of these receptors, immortalized murine corneal keratocytes (MK/T-1 cells) were cultured and stimulated with pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-1β to mimic an inflammatory microenvironment. RT-PCR analysis revealed that both Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23 were expressed by MK/T-1 cells regardless of cytokine stimulation, confirming that resident corneal stromal cells possess the molecular machinery to respond to ATLa and RvE1. Additionally, CD11b⁺ cells—comprising neutrophils, macrophages, and dendritic cells—were isolated from inflamed corneas and shown to express high levels of both Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23, thereby establishing that infiltrating innate immune cells are also direct targets of these lipid mediators.

Subsequent functional studies evaluated the impact of subconjunctival administration of ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 (100 ng per dose, every 48 hours) on inflammatory cell infiltration in a murine model of suture-induced corneal neovascularization. Immunofluorescence staining and quantitative morphometric analysis demonstrated that all three mediators significantly reduced the number of infiltrating neutrophils and macrophages at 24 and 72 hours post-injury compared to vehicle-treated controls. This reduction in cellular infiltration coincided with marked suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokine expression. Real-time PCR analysis showed that treatment with any of the three lipid mediators led to greater than 50% reduction in IL-1β mRNA levels at both 24 and 72 hours, with statistical significance (P < 0.05). TNF-α expression was similarly suppressed at 24 hours by all three agents; however, only RvE1 maintained significant suppression at 72 hours, suggesting temporal differences in mediator efficacy. IL-1α expression remained largely unchanged across treatment groups, indicating selectivity in cytokine modulation.

Critically, the lipid mediators also exerted profound effects on angiogenic signaling pathways. Real-time PCR revealed that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 uniformly downregulated mRNA expression of VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2—the primary ligand-receptor axis driving hemangiogenesis—at both 24 and 72 hours after suture placement. In contrast, expression levels of VEGF-D and VEGFR3, which govern lymphangiogenesis, were not significantly altered by any of the treatments. This selective modulation of angiogenic but not lymphangiogenic factors was corroborated by histological and morphometric analyses of corneal flatmounts stained with anti-CD31 (blood vessel marker) and anti-LYVE-1 (lymphatic vessel marker). At 14 days post-suture, vehicle-treated corneas exhibited extensive blood vessel coverage (23.18% ± 1.12% of total corneal area), whereas ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 treatment reduced this to 8.26% ± 0.63%, 8.4% ± 0.39%, and 10.92% ± 0.53%, respectively (P < 0.001 for all). Strikingly, no significant differences in lymphatic vessel density were observed between treated and control groups, confirming the selective inhibition of hemangiogenesis.

To dissect the mechanism of action further, the invention employed a corneal micropocket assay using hydron pellets containing either IL-1β or VEGF-A to induce neovascularization. In the IL-1β-induced model, all three mediators significantly suppressed hemangiogenesis, consistent with their anti-inflammatory effects. However, in the VEGF-A-induced model—which bypasses upstream inflammatory signaling—only ATLa demonstrated significant inhibition of blood vessel growth, while RvD1 and RvE1 showed no effect. This finding indicates that ATLa possesses a direct anti-angiogenic activity independent of its role in resolving inflammation, likely through interference with VEGF-A signaling at the endothelial cell level. Clinical slit lamp biomicroscopy over a 14-day period confirmed these results, showing that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 all significantly reduced the neovascularization index in suture-induced CNV, with ATLa exhibiting the most robust and sustained effect.

Collectively, these results establish that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 are potent inhibitors of inflammatory corneal hemangiogenesis, acting primarily through early regulation of innate immune responses and downstream suppression of VEGF-A/VEGF-C/VEGFR2 signaling. The unique ability of ATLa to additionally block VEGF-A-driven angiogenesis underscores its superior therapeutic profile and positions it as the lead candidate for clinical development.

### Discussion

The findings presented herein fundamentally advance the understanding of how specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators regulate corneal inflammation and angiogenesis, and they provide a strong scientific foundation for their therapeutic application in ocular surface diseases. The invention demonstrates that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 function not merely as passive anti-inflammatory agents but as active orchestrators of resolution, simultaneously curtailing neutrophil and macrophage infiltration, suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokine production, and selectively inhibiting hemangiogenesis without impairing lymphatic vessel formation. This multifaceted mechanism of action distinguishes these mediators from conventional therapies that often disrupt immune homeostasis or lack specificity for vascular subtypes.

A key insight of the invention is the identification of Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23 as functionally relevant receptors in the corneal microenvironment. Their expression on both resident corneal cells (epithelium and keratocytes) and infiltrating CD11b⁺ immune cells explains how locally administered lipid mediators can exert pleiotropic effects across multiple cell types. The absence of Fprl1 in normal corneas suggests that Fpr-rs2 is the dominant lipoxin receptor in this tissue, aligning with prior reports of its role in murine inflammatory models. The presence of ChemR23 on keratocytes—a previously unreported finding—expands the known cellular repertoire responsive to RvE1 and implies a direct role for stromal cells in resolution signaling. This receptor mapping not only validates the biological plausibility of the observed effects but also opens avenues for receptor-targeted drug design.

The differential efficacy of the mediators in VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis is particularly noteworthy. The inability of RvD1 and RvE1 to suppress VEGF-A-driven neovascularization, despite their potent anti-inflammatory actions, strongly suggests that their anti-angiogenic effects are secondary to cytokine modulation. In contrast, ATLa’s capacity to inhibit VEGF-A-mediated hemangiogenesis independently points to a direct interaction with endothelial cell signaling pathways. Prior studies have shown that ATLa can inhibit endothelial cell adhesion, migration, and proliferation—key steps in angiogenesis—through mechanisms involving suppression of NF-κB activation and integrin expression. The enhanced metabolic stability of ATLa, conferred by its synthetic modifications that resist enzymatic inactivation, likely contributes to its prolonged activity and superior potency in vivo. This pharmacokinetic advantage may explain why ATLa outperforms RvD1 and RvE1 in direct angiogenesis assays, even when administered at equivalent doses.

The selective inhibition of hemangiogenesis without affecting lymphangiogenesis represents a major therapeutic innovation. Lymphatic vessels play crucial roles in antigen presentation, immune cell trafficking, and interstitial fluid drainage, and their preservation may enhance graft survival in corneal transplantation by promoting regulatory immune responses. Conventional anti-VEGF therapies often block both VEGFR2 and VEGFR3 signaling, inadvertently impairing lymphatic function. By sparing VEGF-D/VEGFR3 signaling while targeting VEGF-A/VEGF-C/VEGFR2, the disclosed mediators achieve a more nuanced modulation of vascular growth that aligns with physiological resolution processes.

From a clinical perspective, the invention addresses a critical unmet need in ophthalmology. Current treatments for CNV are limited by toxicity, incomplete efficacy, and rebound inflammation upon discontinuation. The endogenous nature of SPMs, their action through natural resolution pathways, and their lack of immunosuppressive effects position them as safer, more sustainable alternatives. Subconjunctival delivery—as validated in the present studies—offers a practical route for achieving therapeutic concentrations in the cornea while minimizing systemic exposure. Future formulations could include nanoparticle encapsulation or hydrogel-based sustained-release systems to further enhance bioavailability and dosing convenience.

In summary, the invention redefines the therapeutic landscape for corneal neovascularization by leveraging the body’s intrinsic resolution machinery. It establishes ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 as potent, selective, and physiologically aligned agents that not only halt pathological angiogenesis but also actively restore corneal homeostasis—a dual benefit unmatched by existing therapies.

### Methods

The experimental methodologies employed to validate the invention adhere to rigorous standards of reproducibility, ethical compliance, and scientific validity. All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with the Association for Research in Vision and Ophthalmology (ARVO) Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Schepens Eye Research Institute. Six- to eight-week-old male BALB/c mice (Taconic Farms, Germantown, NY) were utilized throughout the study to ensure genetic uniformity and minimize variability in inflammatory responses.

Corneal neovascularization was induced using two well-established models. In the suture-induced model, three interrupted 11-0 nylon sutures were placed intrastromally with two incursions spanning 120° of the corneal circumference, triggering a robust mixed-cell infiltrate and subsequent hemangiogenesis. Neovascularization was monitored by slit lamp biomicroscopy and graded on a scale of 0 to 12 using a standardized grid system that quantifies the centripetal extent of neovessels in each corneal quadrant. In the micropocket assay, 0.3 µL hydron pellets containing either 30 ng murine IL-1β or 200 ng VEGF-A were implanted into the corneal stroma to elicit cytokine- or growth factor-specific angiogenesis, respectively. Corneas were harvested seven days post-implantation for quantitative analysis.

Test compounds—ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1—were synthesized via total organic synthesis as previously described in the literature, with physical and biological properties verified by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry prior to use. Mice were randomized to receive subconjunctival injections of 100 ng of each mediator in 10 µL normal saline or vehicle alone, administered every 48 hours in a masked fashion following injury induction.

For molecular analyses, corneas were carefully dissected to exclude conjunctival and iris contamination. RNA isolation employed a combined Trizol and RNeasy MinElute column protocol to maximize yield and purity. Reverse transcription was performed using oligo(dT)₂₀ primers and Superscript III reverse transcriptase. RT-PCR for receptor expression utilized gene-specific primers for Fprl1, Fpr-rs2, ChemR23, and GAPDH, with products resolved by agarose gel electrophoresis and quantified by ImageJ software. Real-time PCR for cytokines and VEGF family members used TaqMan assays with FAM-MGB probes, normalized to GAPDH, and analyzed by the comparative threshold cycle (ΔΔCₜ) method.

Immunohistochemical studies involved fixation of corneal flatmounts or frozen sections in acetone, followed by blocking with anti-FcR monoclonal antibody or goat serum. Primary antibodies against CD31 (blood vessels), LYVE-1 (lymphatics), neutrophils (NIMP-R14), and macrophages (F4/80) were applied overnight at 4°C, with appropriate isotype controls. Secondary antibodies conjugated to rhodamine or FITC enabled visualization by epifluorescence microscopy. Vessel densities were quantified morphometrically using ImageJ by calculating the proportion of CD31ʰⁱᵍʰ/LYVE-1ˡᵒʷ (blood) or CD31ˡᵒʷ/LYVE-1ʰⁱᵍʰ (lymphatic) areas relative to total corneal area.

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 4.0, with data expressed as mean ± SEM. Group comparisons utilized two-tailed t-tests, with significance defined as P < 0.05. All experiments were replicated at least twice to ensure reliability.

### Lipoxins

Lipoxins represent a class of endogenous eicosanoid-derived lipid mediators biosynthesized from arachidonic acid during the resolution phase of acute inflammation. Unlike pro-inflammatory eicosanoids such as prostaglandins and leukotrienes, lipoxins function as “stop signals” that actively terminate inflammatory responses and promote tissue repair. The prototypical lipoxin, lipoxin A4 (LXA4), is generated through transcellular metabolism involving interactions between leukocytes and epithelial or endothelial cells, wherein 15-lipoxygenase in one cell type acts on intermediates produced by 5-lipoxygenase in another. Aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 (ATL) is formed when aspirin acetylates cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), altering its enzymatic activity to produce 15(R)-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid, which is then converted to 15-epi-LXA4 by 5-lipoxygenase. This epimer exhibits enhanced resistance to enzymatic inactivation by dehydrogenases, resulting in prolonged biological activity compared to native LXA4.

Synthetic analogs such as aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa) have been engineered to further improve metabolic stability and receptor affinity while retaining the core structural features necessary for biological activity. ATLa mimics the actions of endogenous ATL by binding to the ALX/FPR2 receptor (known as Fpr-rs2 in mice), a G-protein-coupled receptor expressed on neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, epithelial cells, and, as demonstrated herein, corneal keratocytes and infiltrating immune cells. Activation of this receptor initiates intracellular signaling cascades that inhibit neutrophil chemotaxis, reduce pro-inflammatory cytokine production (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1β), enhance macrophage phagocytosis of apoptotic cells, and suppress nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) activation.

The present invention leverages these properties to treat corneal neovascularization, revealing a previously unrecognized direct anti-angiogenic function of ATLa. Beyond its role in resolving inflammation, ATLa interferes with VEGF-A-induced endothelial cell responses, including adhesion, migration, and proliferation—critical steps in hemangiogenesis. This dual functionality—simultaneously quenching inflammation and blocking angiogenesis—makes ATLa uniquely suited for treating conditions like corneal neovascularization, where both processes are intertwined. The invention thus establishes ATLa not only as a resolution agonist but also as a potent, selective inhibitor of pathological blood vessel growth, offering a paradigm shift from conventional anti-angiogenic strategies that lack pro-resolving activity.
