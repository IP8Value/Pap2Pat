# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The global burden of infectious diseases caused by pathogenic microorganisms—including bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites—continues to pose significant challenges to public health, economic stability, and societal well-being. Among these, viral respiratory infections have demonstrated particularly devastating potential due to their high transmissibility, rapid mutation rates, and capacity to trigger widespread pandemics. Historical outbreaks such as the 1918 Spanish influenza, the 2009 H1N1 swine flu, the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) pandemic, the Ebola virus disease (EVD), the Zika virus epidemic, and the severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS) and Middle East respiratory syndrome (MERS) coronavirus outbreaks have each underscored the critical need for rapid, accurate, and accessible diagnostic tools capable of detecting viral pathogens at the earliest stages of infection. Most recently, the ongoing global pandemic of Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19), caused by the novel severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), has further emphasized the urgent demand for point-of-care diagnostics that can be deployed in resource-limited settings without reliance on sophisticated laboratory infrastructure.

Traditional diagnostic approaches for viral infections fall into three broad categories: antigen-based detection, serological assays, and nucleic acid amplification tests. Antigen tests, while rapid and inexpensive, often suffer from limited sensitivity and require prior development of specific antibodies against the target pathogen, rendering them less adaptable to emerging viral variants. Serological methods, which detect host immune responses such as immunoglobulin M (IgM) or immunoglobulin G (IgG), are valuable for determining past exposure but cannot reliably distinguish active from resolved infections, thereby limiting their utility in acute clinical decision-making. In contrast, nucleic acid-based diagnostics—particularly those employing polymerase chain reaction (PCR)—offer high sensitivity, specificity, and quantitative capability. However, conventional PCR and its real-time variant (qRT-PCR) necessitate thermal cycling equipment, trained personnel, and controlled laboratory environments, which restricts their deployment in low-resource or field settings.

In response to these limitations, the advent of CRISPR-Cas (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats and CRISPR-associated proteins) technologies has revolutionized molecular diagnostics by enabling isothermal, enzyme-driven detection of nucleic acids with high fidelity. CRISPR systems, originally discovered as adaptive immune mechanisms in prokaryotes, have been repurposed for programmable nucleic acid recognition. Among the most widely adopted for diagnostics are Class 2 systems such as Cas12 (targeting DNA) and Cas13 (targeting RNA), both of which exhibit collateral cleavage activity upon target binding—non-specific degradation of reporter nucleic acids that generates a measurable signal. These systems have been successfully integrated into platforms like SHERLOCK (Specific High-sensitivity Enzymatic Reporter unLOCKing) and DETECTR (DNA Endonuclease Targeted CRISPR Trans Reporter), offering rapid, instrument-free detection with attomolar sensitivity when coupled with pre-amplification steps such as recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA) or loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP).

Despite these advances, existing CRISPR-based diagnostics face several constraints. Cas12 requires a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) sequence adjacent to the target DNA, limiting the number of suitable genomic sites for probe design. Cas13, while more flexible in RNA targeting, still depends on precise guide RNA engineering and may exhibit off-target effects under certain conditions. Moreover, both systems rely on a single enzymatic activity per detection event, which, although sufficient for many applications, does not fully exploit the potential for signal amplification or multiplexed readouts. Additionally, the requirement for pre-amplification in most clinical scenarios—due to the low abundance of viral nucleic acids in patient samples—introduces complexity, cost, and potential for contamination.

A promising alternative lies in the underutilized Class 1 Type III-A CRISPR-Cas systems, which operate through multi-subunit effector complexes (e.g., Csm in Type III-A) and exhibit unique biochemical properties distinct from Class 2 enzymes. Unlike Cas12 or Cas13, Type III-A systems are activated not by direct target binding alone, but by the transcription of target DNA into RNA or by the presence of complementary RNA transcripts. Upon activation, the Csm complex executes multiple enzymatic functions simultaneously: (1) sequence-specific cleavage of the target RNA by the Csm3 subunit, (2) non-specific single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) degradation via the HD nuclease domain of Csm1, (3) synthesis of cyclic oligoadenylate (cOA) second messengers by the GGDD motif of Csm1, and (4) cOA-dependent activation of ancillary ribonucleases such as Csm6, which then cleave ssRNA indiscriminately. This multi-pronged response—combining direct DNase activity with a potent, amplified RNase cascade—offers a natural signal amplification mechanism that could dramatically enhance detection sensitivity without requiring external amplification in certain contexts.

However, the practical implementation of Type III-A systems for diagnostics has been hindered by the complexity of reconstituting functional multi-subunit complexes in vitro. Unlike single-polypeptide Cas12 or Cas13, the Csm complex comprises five to six protein subunits and a CRISPR RNA (crRNA), making co-expression, purification, and stabilization challenging. Recent advances in synthetic biology have addressed this bottleneck through the development of all-in-one expression plasmids that enable high-yield production of intact Csm ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complexes in bacterial hosts, followed by streamlined purification protocols. This breakthrough paves the way for the development of robust, scalable diagnostic platforms based on Type III-A CRISPR-Cas biochemistry.

Accordingly, there remains a compelling need for a novel, versatile, and highly sensitive diagnostic system that leverages the intrinsic signal amplification and dual-reporter capabilities of Type III-A CRISPR-Cas systems, operates under isothermal conditions, supports both amplification-free and amplification-coupled modalities, and is adaptable to a wide range of viral pathogens—regardless of whether their genomes are composed of DNA or RNA. Such a system would represent a significant advancement over existing CRISPR-based diagnostics by offering enhanced sensitivity, reduced false-negative rates, and greater flexibility in assay design and deployment.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel, highly sensitive, and versatile diagnostic platform for the detection of viral pathogens, termed MORIARTY (Multipronged, One-pot, target RNA-Induced, Augmentable, Rapid, Test sYstem), which is based on the Type III-A CRISPR-Cas system derived from *Lactococcus lactis* (LlCsm). The invention exploits the unique multi-enzymatic activities of the LlCsm effector complex—specifically, its ability to simultaneously execute target RNA-guided ssDNA cleavage (DNase activity), synthesize cyclic oligoadenylate (cOA) second messengers, and activate an ancillary Csm6 ribonuclease (RNase activity) in a cOA-dependent manner—thereby generating cumulative, amplifiable signals that significantly enhance detection sensitivity and reliability.

In one embodiment, the invention provides an amplification-free diagnostic method comprising a one-pot reaction mixture containing: (i) a purified LlCsm ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complex programmed with a crRNA complementary to a target region of a viral RNA genome; (ii) an ancillary LlCsm6 protein; (iii) adenosine triphosphate (ATP); (iv) a divalent metal ion cofactor selected from Mg²⁺, Mn²⁺, or a combination thereof; (v) a first fluorescent reporter probe comprising a single-stranded DNA oligonucleotide labeled with a fluorophore-quencher pair, wherein cleavage by the LlCsm DNase activity results in a detectable increase in fluorescence; and (vi) a second fluorescent reporter probe comprising a single-stranded RNA oligonucleotide labeled with a fluorophore-quencher pair, wherein cleavage by the cOA-activated LlCsm6 RNase activity results in a second detectable increase in fluorescence. The reaction is conducted under isothermal conditions (e.g., 37°C) and yields a cumulative fluorescent signal proportional to the presence and concentration of the target viral RNA.

In another embodiment, the invention provides an amplification-coupled diagnostic method, referred to as T7-MORIARTY, which integrates reverse transcription-recombinase polymerase amplification (RT-RPA) with in situ T7 RNA polymerase-driven transcription to convert viral RNA into double-stranded DNA templates containing a T7 promoter, followed by transcription into RNA that activates the LlCsm complex. This approach enables attomolar-level detection of viral RNA and is particularly suited for clinical samples with low viral titers.

The invention further encompasses optimized reaction conditions that modulate the relative contributions of DNase and RNase activities by varying the concentrations of ATP, divalent ions (e.g., 10 mM MgCl₂ for RNase-dominant signaling; 10 mM MnCl₂ with low ATP for dual DNase/RNase signaling), and reporter probes. Additionally, the invention includes a multiplexing strategy wherein multiple LlCsm RNP complexes, each programmed to target distinct regions of the same viral genome (e.g., the Spike gene of SARS-CoV-2 at nucleotides 22280–22308, 24702–24730, and 25061–25089), are combined in a single reaction to synergistically enhance sensitivity and reduce the likelihood of false negatives due to viral mutations.

The MORIARTY platform is broadly applicable to any RNA or DNA virus, as Type III-A systems respond to either viral RNA directly or to RNA transcribed from viral DNA. The system operates isothermally, eliminating the need for thermocyclers, and can be adapted to lateral flow, fluorescence plate readers, or smartphone-based detection devices, making it ideal for point-of-care use in clinics, airports, or remote regions. The invention thus represents a significant advancement in molecular diagnostics by combining intrinsic signal amplification, dual-reporter redundancy, flexible target selection, and compatibility with both amplification-free and pre-amplified workflows.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention discloses a comprehensive diagnostic platform—MORIARTY—that harnesses the multi-functional enzymatic machinery of the Type III-A CRISPR-Cas system for the sensitive, specific, and rapid detection of viral nucleic acids. Central to this platform is the *Lactococcus lactis* Csm (LlCsm) effector complex, a multi-subunit ribonucleoprotein that, upon binding to a cognate target RNA, initiates a cascade of enzymatic activities resulting in amplified, detectable signals. Unlike Class 2 CRISPR systems (e.g., Cas12, Cas13), which rely on a single collateral nuclease activity, the LlCsm complex integrates four distinct biochemical functions: (1) sequence-specific cleavage of target RNA by Csm3, (2) non-specific ssDNA degradation by the HD domain of Csm1, (3) synthesis of cyclic oligoadenylate (cOA6) second messengers by the GGDD motif of Csm1, and (4) cOA6-dependent activation of the ancillary ribonuclease LlCsm6, which then cleaves ssRNA reporters indiscriminately. This multipronged response enables cumulative signal generation from a single target recognition event, dramatically improving the signal-to-noise ratio and detection limit.

The LlCsm complex used in the invention is produced via an all-in-one expression plasmid encoding all Csm subunits (Csm1–Csm6) along with a CRISPR array containing a single repeat-spacer unit, ensuring co-expression and proper assembly of the RNP complex in *Escherichia coli*. The complex is purified using Ni-NTA affinity chromatography, leveraging an N-terminal His₆-tag on Csm2, followed by size-exclusion chromatography to yield homogeneous, active complexes at high concentrations (~55 µM). The ancillary LlCsm6 protein is expressed separately from a dedicated plasmid and purified using identical chromatographic methods. Both components are stable when stored at −80°C in appropriate buffers containing reducing agents (e.g., 14 mM 2-mercaptoethanol) and salts (e.g., 200–300 mM NaCl).

For diagnostic application, the crRNA spacer sequence within the LlCsm complex is reprogrammed to be complementary to a conserved region of the target viral genome. In the case of SARS-CoV-2, spacers targeting the Spike (S) gene at positions 22280–22308 (S0), 24702–24730 (S7), and 25061–25089 (S8) of the NCBI reference sequence MT801051.1 have been validated. Critically, the 3′ protospacer flanking sequence (3′ PFS) of the viral RNA must be non-complementary to the 5′ tag of the crRNA to ensure activation, as complementarity in this region leads to self/non-self discrimination and inhibition of the complex. This requirement is readily satisfied in SARS-CoV-2, where numerous target sites meet the criterion.

The core MORIARTY reaction is conducted in a one-pot format at 37°C in a buffer containing Tris acetate (33 mM, pH 7.6), potassium acetate (66 mM), and optimized concentrations of divalent ions and ATP. Two fluorescent reporter probes are employed: (i) DNA-Alexa, a ssDNA oligo labeled with Alexa Fluor 594 (excitation/emission: 570/630 nm) and Iowa Black RQ quencher, which reports LlCsm1 DNase activity; and (ii) RNA-FAM, a ssRNA oligo labeled with 6-FAM (480/530 nm) and Iowa Black FQ quencher, which reports LlCsm6 RNase activity. Cleavage of either probe separates fluorophore from quencher, yielding a time-dependent increase in fluorescence measurable by standard plate readers.

Key to the invention is the tunable nature of the system. In Mg²⁺-only conditions (e.g., 10 mM MgCl₂), DNase activity is suppressed, and detection relies solely on the cOA6-LlCsm6 RNase axis, which is highly sensitive due to signal amplification (one cOA6 molecule can activate multiple LlCsm6 turnovers). Under these conditions, high ATP (1.5 mM) maximizes cOA6 synthesis, enabling detection of SARS-CoV-2 RNA at 5 fM (~3,000 copies/µL). In contrast, Mn²⁺ (e.g., 10 mM MnCl₂) activates both DNase and cOA synthesis, allowing dual-channel detection; however, ATP must be kept low (0.05 mM) to avoid inhibition of DNase. For clinical samples with very low viral loads, a hybrid condition (10 mM MgCl₂ + 0.5 mM MnCl₂) is optimal in RT-RPA-coupled assays, as it supports both T7 transcription (Mg²⁺-dependent) and weak DNase activity (Mn²⁺-enhanced), yielding additive signals from both reporters.

To further enhance sensitivity, the invention incorporates a multiplexing strategy wherein three distinct LlCsm complexes (S0, S7, S8), each targeting non-overlapping regions of the S gene, are combined in equimolar amounts (250 nM each). This approach increases the probability of target engagement and aggregates signals from multiple activation events, lowering the limit of detection to ~2,000 copies/µL in amplification-free mode—sufficient to identify patients with high viral titers (Ct < 25 by qRT-PCR).

For samples with lower viral loads, the invention provides T7-MORIARTY, an integrated workflow combining RT-RPA with in situ transcription and detection. Viral RNA is first reverse-transcribed and amplified using RPA primers, one of which contains a T7 promoter. The resulting dsDNA is then transcribed by T7 RNA polymerase (included in the detection mix) into RNA, which activates LlCsm. This one-pot, two-enzyme (RPA + T7) system operates entirely at 37–42°C and achieves attomolar sensitivity (≤62 copies/µL), with excellent concordance to qRT-PCR in clinical validation using 14 patient nasopharyngeal swabs.

The invention also includes engineered variants to improve performance. For instance, the Csm3 D30A mutation abolishes target RNA cleavage while preserving DNase and cOA synthesis activities, prolonging the activation state of the complex and enhancing signal duration. Similarly, mutations in the HD domain (H13A/D14N) or HEPN domain of Csm6 (R355A) serve as negative controls to validate signal origin.

Overall, MORIARTY represents a paradigm shift in CRISPR diagnostics by exploiting the natural complexity of Type III systems to achieve unprecedented sensitivity, flexibility, and robustness in viral detection.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Amplification-Free Detection of SARS-CoV-2 RNA Using Multiplexed MORIARTY**

A one-pot MORIARTY reaction was assembled containing 250 nM each of LlCsm_S0_D30A, LlCsm_S7_D30A, and LlCsm_S8_D30A (targeting three sites in the SARS-CoV-2 S gene), 250 nM LlCsm6, 2 µM RNA-FAM, 10 mM MgCl₂, 1.5 mM ATP, in 33 mM Tris acetate (pH 7.6), 66 mM potassium acetate, at 37°C. Serial dilutions of in vitro transcribed S mRNA (0.5–50 fM) were added, and fluorescence (480/530 nm) was monitored for 30 minutes. A statistically significant signal (p < 0.0001) was observed at 5 fM, demonstrating high sensitivity. When applied to heat-inactivated SARS-CoV-2 control RNA (BEI NR-52347), the assay detected down to 2,000 copies/µL. Testing of six patient RNA extracts showed positive identification of all qRT-PCR-positive samples with Ct < 25.

**Example 2: RT-RPA-Coupled T7-MORIARTY for Attomolar Detection**

Patient RNA samples were subjected to RT-RPA using primers with a T7 promoter on the forward strand. The RPA product (12.5 µL) was added to a T7-MORIARTY mix containing 250 nM LlCsm_S0_D30A, 250 nM LlCsm6, 2 µM DNA-FAM, 2 µM RNA-FAM, 17 mM MgCl₂, 0.5 mM MnCl₂, 0.5 mM rNTPs, 60 µg/mL T7 RNA polymerase, in K-HEPES buffer (pH 7.6), at 37°C. Fluorescence was monitored for 40 minutes. The assay detected SARS-CoV-2 RNA down to 62 copies/µL in control samples. In 14 clinical samples, MORIARTY showed 93% agreement with qRT-PCR (10/11 correct calls), with strong correlation to E gene Ct values.

**Example 3: Metal Ion and ATP Optimization**

Reactions with 5 fM S_IVT_RNA were tested under varying conditions: (i) 10 mM MgCl₂ + 1.5 mM ATP → strong RNA-FAM signal; (ii) 10 mM MnCl₂ + 0.05 mM ATP → dual DNA-Alexa and RNA-FAM signals; (iii) no ATP or no Mn²⁺ → no signal. Mutant controls (Csm1 HD⁻ or Csm6 HEPN⁻) abolished respective signals, confirming specificity.

### Material and Methods

**Plasmid Construction**: The pACYC-based all-in-one plasmid encoding LlCsm subunits and crRNA was modified via Q5 site-directed mutagenesis or BbsI cloning to incorporate SARS-CoV-2-targeting spacers (S0, S7, S8) and mutations (Csm3 D30A, Csm1 H13A/D14N). The LlCsm6 expression plasmid was constructed separately.

**Protein Purification**: *E. coli* NiCo21(DE3) cells transformed with LlCsm plasmids were induced with 0.3 mM IPTG at OD₆₀₀ ~0.6. Cells were lysed, and LlCsm RNP was purified via Ni-NTA chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography in 20 mM HEPES (pH 7.5), 200 mM NaCl, 5 mM MgCl₂, 14 mM 2-mercaptoethanol. LlCsm6 was purified similarly but with 300 mM NaCl. Proteins were concentrated to ~55 µM and stored at −80°C.

**In Vitro Transcription**: The SARS-CoV-2 S gene plasmid (pGBW-m4046887) was linearized with BamHI, and 1 µg DNA was transcribed using T7 RNA polymerase (480 µg/mL) in 500 mM Tris (pH 8.0), 100 mM DTT, 200 mM MgCl₂, 5 mM NTPs, at 37°C for 3 h. RNA was DNase-treated and purified using Monarch RNA Cleanup Kit.

**Amplification-Free MORIARTY**: Reactions (25 µL) contained 0.1× TAPA buffer, 250 nM LlCsm, 1–250 nM LlCsm6, 0.5–2 µM reporters, 0–10 mM MgCl₂/MnCl₂, 0–1.5 mM ATP, and target RNA. Fluorescence was read every minute (480/530 and 570/630 nm) on SpectraMax iD5.

**T7-MORIARTY**: Reactions included 1× TAPA + 1× transcription buffer, 2 µM DNA-FAM/RNA-FAM, 250 nM LlCsm, 250 nM LlCsm6, 17 mM MgCl₂, 0.5 mM MnCl₂, 0.5 mM rNTPs, 60 µg/mL T7 RNA polymerase, and target DNA or RT-RPA product. Incubation at 37°C with fluorescence reads every 5 minutes.

**RT-RPA**: Performed using TwistAmp Basic kit with 0.5 µM primers (forward with T7 promoter), 100 U Protoscript RT, 14 mM MgOAc, at 42°C for 30 min.

**qRT-PCR**: Conducted per FDA EUA protocol using CDC/WHO primer-probe sets for N1, N2, E, and RNase P on Analytik Jena qTower3. Ct < 35 deemed positive.
