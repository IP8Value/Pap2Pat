# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and genetic engineering, specifically to novel thermostable RNA-guided endonucleases derived from thermophilic microorganisms. More particularly, the invention provides a Cas9 endonuclease from *Geobacillus thermodenitrificans* T12, herein referred to as ThermoCas9 or gtCas9, which exhibits robust enzymatic activity over a broad temperature range from 20°C to 70°C. The invention further encompasses compositions comprising this thermostable Cas9 protein in complex with guide RNAs, expression vectors encoding the protein, host cells engineered to express the system, and methods for using the system for targeted genome editing, gene silencing, and nucleic acid manipulation in both thermophilic and mesophilic organisms. The invention is particularly advantageous for applications requiring high-temperature stability, such as genome engineering in obligate thermophiles, industrial bioprocesses conducted at elevated temperatures, and molecular biology techniques that benefit from enhanced protein robustness.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR) and their associated (Cas) proteins constitute an adaptive immune system in prokaryotes that provides defense against invading genetic elements such as bacteriophages and plasmids. This system has been repurposed as a powerful tool for genome engineering across a wide range of organisms. CRISPR-Cas systems are broadly classified into two classes and six types based on their structural complexity and signature proteins. Class 2 systems, which include type II (Cas9) and type V (Cas12a), are particularly attractive for genome engineering applications because they utilize a single effector protein guided by RNA to introduce targeted double-stranded DNA breaks (DSBs).

The most widely used Cas9 nuclease is derived from *Streptococcus pyogenes* (SpCas9), which has been extensively characterized and adapted for various genome editing applications. However, SpCas9 is a mesophilic enzyme with optimal activity between 25°C and 44°C, and its activity is significantly diminished or completely lost at temperatures above 42°C. This thermal limitation has precluded the direct application of SpCas9-based tools in obligate thermophilic microorganisms, which typically grow at temperatures between 50°C and 80°C. While some genetic tools have been developed for thermophiles, their efficiency remains low, hindering the full exploration and exploitation of these industrially important organisms for biotechnological applications such as biofuel production, bioremediation, and synthesis of high-value chemicals.

Previous attempts to develop CRISPR-Cas9 systems for thermophiles have relied on indirect approaches, such as combining homologous recombination at elevated temperatures with SpCas9-based counter-selection at moderate temperatures. However, these strategies are cumbersome and not directly applicable to obligate thermophiles that cannot survive at lower temperatures. Moreover, it was previously believed that type II CRISPR-Cas systems were absent in thermophilic bacteria, which further limited the search for naturally thermostable Cas9 orthologues. The lack of a thermostable Cas9 system has thus represented a significant bottleneck in the genetic manipulation of thermophilic microorganisms and in applications requiring enzymatic activity at elevated temperatures.

While some Class 1 CRISPR-Cas systems have been identified in thermophilic archaea and bacteria, these systems are more complex, requiring multiple Cas proteins for activity, and have not been as readily adapted for genome engineering purposes as the simpler Class 2 systems. The development of a thermostable, single-protein Cas9 system would therefore represent a significant advancement in the field, enabling direct genome editing in thermophiles and expanding the temperature range of CRISPR-Cas applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel thermostable Cas9 endonuclease, designated ThermoCas9 or gtCas9, isolated from the thermophilic bacterium *Geobacillus thermodenitrificans* T12. This enzyme exhibits remarkable thermal stability and maintains robust nuclease activity over an unprecedented temperature range from 20°C to 70°C, making it uniquely suited for applications in both thermophilic and mesophilic organisms. The invention further provides comprehensive characterization of the molecular requirements for ThermoCas9 activity, including its protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) specificity, guide RNA structural requirements, metal ion dependency, and mismatch tolerance.

In one aspect, the invention provides an isolated ThermoCas9 protein comprising the amino acid sequence of SEQ ID NO:1 or a functional variant thereof that maintains thermostable nuclease activity. The ThermoCas9 protein is characterized by its small size (1082 amino acids) compared to other Cas9 orthologues, primarily due to a truncated REC lobe, while maintaining all essential catalytic domains including RuvC and HNH nuclease domains.

In another aspect, the invention provides nucleic acid molecules encoding the ThermoCas9 protein, including codon-optimized sequences for expression in various host organisms. The invention also provides expression vectors comprising these nucleic acid molecules under the control of appropriate promoters for constitutive or inducible expression in bacterial, archaeal, or eukaryotic host cells.

In a further aspect, the invention provides guide RNA molecules specifically designed for use with ThermoCas9, including single guide RNA (sgRNA) chimeras that combine the crRNA and tracrRNA components into a single transcript. These guide RNAs are optimized for thermostability and efficient complex formation with ThermoCas9 at elevated temperatures.

The invention also provides methods for targeted genome editing in thermophilic microorganisms, particularly in the industrially important species *Bacillus smithii* ET138, using the ThermoCas9 system. These methods enable efficient gene deletion, insertion, and modification at growth temperatures of 55°C or higher, which was previously impossible with mesophilic Cas9 systems.

Additionally, the invention provides methods for targeted genome editing in mesophilic organisms, demonstrating the broad applicability of the ThermoCas9 system. Successful genome editing has been demonstrated in *Pseudomonas putida* KT2440 at 37°C, expanding the utility of the system beyond thermophiles.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a catalytically inactive variant of ThermoCas9 (ThermodCas9) containing mutations in the RuvC (D8A) and HNH (H582A) catalytic domains. This dead Cas9 variant is useful for transcriptional regulation applications, including gene silencing (CRISPRi) in thermophilic organisms, where it can be used to reversibly repress gene expression without introducing DNA breaks.

The invention further provides detailed characterization of the PAM requirements for ThermoCas9, which recognizes an 8-nucleotide PAM sequence with the consensus 5′-NNNNCNRA-3′, where N represents any nucleotide, R represents a purine (A or G), and there is a preference for cytosine at positions 1, 3, 4, and 6, and adenine at position 8. This extended PAM recognition provides additional targeting specificity compared to many other Cas9 orthologues.

The invention also encompasses methods for using the ThermoCas9 system in various molecular biology applications that benefit from thermostable enzymatic activity, including but not limited to in vitro DNA cleavage, diagnostic applications, and synthetic biology circuits operating at elevated temperatures.

## RNA Guides and Target Sequences

### Binding, Cleavage, Marking and Modifying Temperatures

The ThermoCas9 system of the present invention demonstrates exceptional thermal adaptability, functioning effectively across a temperature range from 20°C to 70°C. This broad operational window represents a significant advancement over existing Cas9 systems, which are typically restricted to mesophilic temperature ranges. At the lower end of this spectrum, around 20°C, ThermoCas9 exhibits selective activity, cleaving only DNA substrates that possess the most preferred PAM sequences, specifically those with 5′-CVAA-3′ and 5′-CCCA-3′ motifs in the fifth to eighth PAM positions (where V represents A, C, or G). This temperature-dependent PAM stringency provides a natural mechanism for enhancing target specificity at lower temperatures, potentially reducing off-target effects in applications where precision is paramount.

As the temperature increases to 30°C, ThermoCas9's activity expands to include substrates with slightly less optimal PAM sequences, particularly those containing 5′-CNAA-3′ and 5′-CMCA-3′ motifs (where M represents A or C). This intermediate temperature range maintains a balance between activity and specificity, making it suitable for applications requiring moderate stringency. At temperatures between 37°C and 60°C, which encompasses the optimal growth temperatures of many industrially relevant thermophiles, ThermoCas9 demonstrates robust cleavage activity across a broader range of PAM sequences, including those with 5′-CAKA-3′ motifs (where K represents G or T), though with varying efficiencies.

The upper limit of ThermoCas9 activity extends to 70°C, which is remarkable for a CRISPR-Cas system. Pre-assembled ThermoCas9:sgRNA ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complexes retain detectable cleavage activity even after pre-incubation at 70°C for 5 minutes, followed by incubation with target DNA at the same temperature for 1 hour. This extreme thermostability is particularly valuable for applications in hyperthermophilic organisms or industrial processes conducted at elevated temperatures. The thermal stability of the ThermoCas9 protein itself is significantly enhanced by complex formation with sgRNA, as apo-ThermoCas9 denatures at 65°C, whereas the ThermoCas9-sgRNA complex remains stable above 65°C. This RNA-dependent stabilization suggests that guide RNA binding induces a conformational change that protects the protein from thermal denaturation, a characteristic that can be exploited to maintain enzyme activity during prolonged high-temperature incubations.

The temperature-dependent activity profile of ThermoCas9 also influences its mismatch tolerance. At lower temperatures (37°C), the system exhibits greater sensitivity to spacer-protospacer mismatches, with cleavage activity being abolished for most single-mismatch targets on linear DNA substrates. However, at higher temperatures (55°C), the system shows increased tolerance for certain mismatches, particularly at PAM-distal positions, while maintaining strict discrimination against mismatches at PAM-proximal positions 4, 5, and 10. This temperature-modulated specificity provides additional control over targeting fidelity, allowing users to select operating conditions that balance activity and precision according to their specific application requirements.

### Functional Moieties

The ThermoCas9 protein of the present invention comprises several essential functional moieties that contribute to its unique thermostable properties and RNA-guided DNA cleavage activity. The primary functional domains include the RuvC and HNH nuclease domains, which are responsible for cleaving the non-target and target DNA strands, respectively, to generate double-stranded breaks. The catalytic residues within these domains have been identified as D8 in the RuvC domain and H582 in the HNH domain, and mutation of these residues to alanine (D8A and H582A) results in a catalytically inactive variant (ThermodCas9) that retains DNA binding capability without cleavage activity.

The protein also contains a truncated REC (recognition) lobe, which contributes to its relatively compact size of 1082 amino acids compared to other Cas9 orthologues such as SpCas9 (1368 amino acids). Despite this truncation, the REC lobe maintains sufficient structural integrity to support guide RNA binding and target recognition, suggesting that the essential recognition functions have been preserved through evolutionary optimization in the thermophilic environment. The structural organization of ThermoCas9 includes distinct domains that can be visualized through homology modeling, revealing a compact architecture that likely contributes to its thermal stability.

The guide RNA component of the ThermoCas9 system comprises both crRNA and tracrRNA elements, which can be combined into a single chimeric sgRNA molecule. The tracrRNA scaffold contains three predicted hairpin structures that are critical for maintaining thermostability and cleavage efficiency at elevated temperatures. Removal of the 3′ hairpin results in decreased cleavage efficiency across all tested temperatures, while removal of both the middle and 3′ hairpins leads to a drastic reduction in activity. The full-length tracrRNA scaffold is therefore essential for optimal ThermoCas9 function, particularly at temperatures approaching 65°C.

The spacer sequence within the guide RNA, which determines target specificity through complementary base pairing with the protospacer sequence in the target DNA, has an optimal length of 19-23 nucleotides. Spacer lengths shorter than 19 nucleotides, particularly 18 nucleotides, result in significantly reduced cleavage efficiency, indicating that sufficient complementarity is required to maintain stable RNP complex formation and efficient target recognition at elevated temperatures. The repeat-anti-repeat region connecting the crRNA and tracrRNA components can tolerate a 41-nucleotide truncation at the spacer-distal end without significantly affecting cleavage efficiency, suggesting flexibility in this region while maintaining core functionality.

### Nuclease Activity

The nuclease activity of ThermoCas9 is characterized by its ability to introduce double-stranded DNA breaks at specific locations determined by the guide RNA sequence and the presence of an appropriate PAM sequence. The cleavage pattern of ThermoCas9 is consistent with other Cas9 orthologues, generating breaks predominantly between the third and fourth nucleotides proximal to the PAM sequence on both DNA strands. This results in blunt-ended or nearly blunt-ended double-stranded breaks that can be repaired through homologous recombination or non-homologous end joining pathways, depending on the host organism's DNA repair machinery.

ThermoCas9 demonstrates strict dependence on divalent metal cations for its nuclease activity, with magnesium (Mg²⁺) and manganese (Mn²⁺) supporting the highest levels of DNA cleavage. Other divalent cations including iron (Fe²⁺), cobalt (Co²⁺), nickel (Ni²⁺), and zinc (Zn²⁺) also mediate cleavage activity, albeit at reduced efficiencies compared to Mg²⁺ and Mn²⁺. Calcium (Ca²⁺) supports only nicking activity, suggesting that it permits function of only one of the two nuclease domains (either RuvC or HNH), resulting in single-stranded breaks rather than double-stranded breaks. This metal ion dependency profile is consistent with other characterized Cas9 systems and provides additional control over enzymatic activity through buffer composition.

The nuclease activity of ThermoCas9 is highly sensitive to spacer-protospacer mismatches, particularly on linear DNA substrates. At 55°C, single mismatches at PAM-proximal positions 4, 5, and 10 severely impair cleavage activity, while mismatches at positions 1, 2, and 20 (PAM-distal) are more tolerated. Multiple mismatches, particularly four or more at the PAM-distal end, completely abolish cleavage activity at both 37°C and 55°C. This high mismatch sensitivity contributes to the system's targeting specificity and reduces the likelihood of off-target effects. Interestingly, supercoiled plasmid DNA substrates show higher tolerance for mismatches compared to linear DNA, possibly due to the enhanced accessibility of negatively supercoiled DNA or differences in local DNA structure that facilitate RNP complex formation.

ThermoCas9 exhibits exclusive double-stranded DNA nuclease activity and does not cleave single-stranded DNA substrates, distinguishing it from some other CRISPR-Cas systems that have demonstrated ssDNA cleavage capabilities. This dsDNA specificity ensures that the system targets genomic DNA and double-stranded plasmid DNA without unintended cleavage of single-stranded intermediates or RNA molecules.

The catalytically inactive ThermodCas9 variant (D8A, H582A) retains the ability to bind target DNA sequences in a guide RNA-dependent manner but does not introduce DNA breaks. This property makes it suitable for transcriptional regulation applications, where it can be used to block transcription initiation or elongation by sterically hindering RNA polymerase progression along the DNA template.

## Expression Vectors

The present invention provides a comprehensive suite of expression vectors designed for optimal expression and function of the ThermoCas9 system in various host organisms. These vectors are engineered to accommodate the specific requirements of thermophilic and mesophilic hosts, incorporating appropriate promoters, selection markers, and regulatory elements to ensure efficient expression and controlled activity of the ThermoCas9 components.

For applications in thermophilic bacteria, particularly *Bacillus smithii* ET138, the invention provides pNW33n-based vectors that contain the *thermocas9* gene under the control of the native *xylL* promoter (PxylL) from *B. smithii*. This promoter provides constitutive expression suitable for thermophilic growth conditions. The vectors also include a sgRNA expression module driven by the constitutive *pta* promoter (Ppta) from *Bacillus coagulans*, which has been shown to function effectively in *B. smithii* at 55°C. Additionally, these vectors incorporate homologous recombination flanks (typically 1 kb upstream and downstream of the target gene) to facilitate precise genome editing through homology-directed repair following ThermoCas9-induced double-stranded breaks.

For applications in mesophilic bacteria, particularly *Pseudomonas putida* KT2440, the invention provides pEMG-based suicide vectors that contain the *thermocas9* gene under the control of the 3-methylbenzoate-inducible Pm promoter. This inducible system allows for tight temporal control of ThermoCas9 expression, which is crucial for efficient genome editing in organisms with limited homologous recombination capacity. The sgRNA expression module in these vectors is driven by the constitutive P3 promoter, which provides consistent guide RNA expression throughout the editing process. The vectors also include homologous recombination flanks (typically 0.5 kb upstream and downstream of the target gene) appropriate for the *P. putida* genome.

The expression vectors are designed with modular architectures that facilitate easy exchange of promoter elements, selection markers, and homologous recombination flanks to adapt the system for different host organisms. The vectors incorporate standard multiple cloning sites and restriction enzyme recognition sequences to enable straightforward insertion of custom sgRNA sequences targeting specific genomic loci. Antibiotic resistance markers appropriate for each host organism are included for selection of transformants, with chloramphenicol resistance for *B. smithii* and kanamycin resistance for *P. putida*.

For applications requiring catalytically inactive ThermoCas9 (ThermodCas9), the invention provides specialized vectors containing the D8A and H582A mutations in the *thermocas9* gene. These vectors maintain the same promoter and regulatory elements as their active counterparts but are designed specifically for transcriptional regulation applications rather than genome editing.

The expression vectors are also compatible with standard molecular cloning techniques, including Gibson assembly, Golden Gate assembly, and traditional restriction enzyme-based cloning, facilitating rapid construction of custom editing constructs. The modular design allows for easy adaptation of the system to new host organisms by simply exchanging the origin of replication, selection marker, and promoter elements while maintaining the core ThermoCas9 expression cassette.

## Host Cells

The present invention encompasses a variety of host cells engineered to express the ThermoCas9 system for genome editing and transcriptional regulation applications. These host cells include both thermophilic and mesophilic microorganisms that have been transformed with the expression vectors described herein, as well as specialized host strains optimized for efficient genetic manipulation.

For thermophilic applications, the invention provides *Bacillus smithii* ET138 ΔsigF ΔhsdR host cells that have been engineered to express the ThermoCas9 system. This strain has been modified to improve transformation efficiency and reduce restriction-modification barriers that might interfere with plasmid maintenance. The ΔsigF mutation affects sporulation regulation, while the ΔhsdR mutation inactivates a restriction endonuclease component, both of which enhance the ability to introduce and maintain foreign DNA. These host cells are cultured at 55°C in LB2 medium supplemented with appropriate antibiotics, and they serve as an ideal platform for demonstrating the thermophilic capabilities of the ThermoCas9 system.

For mesophilic applications, the invention provides *Pseudomonas putida* KT2440 host cells that have been transformed with ThermoCas9 expression vectors. This strain is particularly valuable because it represents the first successful application of CRISPR-Cas9 technology in *P. putida*, which previously lacked efficient genome editing tools. The host cells are cultured at 37°C in LB medium with appropriate selection, and ThermoCas9 expression is induced with 3-methylbenzoate when using the Pm promoter system. This demonstrates the broad temperature compatibility of the ThermoCas9 system and its applicability across diverse bacterial lineages.

The invention also encompasses *Escherichia coli* host strains used for heterologous expression and purification of ThermoCas9 protein. Specifically, *E. coli* Rosetta (DE3) strains are employed for protein production, as they provide tRNAs for rare codons that may be present in the *thermocas9* gene sequence. These host cells are grown at 37°C to mid-log phase before induction with IPTG at 16°C to promote proper protein folding and solubility. The purified protein can then be used for in vitro applications or structural studies.

For cloning and plasmid construction purposes, the invention utilizes standard *E. coli* laboratory strains including DH5α, DH10B, and DH5α λpir. These strains are chosen based on their specific characteristics: DH5α for general cloning and plasmid propagation, DH10B for electrotransformation of large plasmid libraries, and DH5α λpir for maintenance of plasmids containing the R6K origin of replication (such as the pEMG vector used for *P. putida* applications).

The host cells of the present invention are not limited to the specific strains mentioned but can be extended to any microorganism capable of expressing the ThermoCas9 system components. The broad temperature range of ThermoCas9 activity (20-70°C) suggests that the system could be adapted for use in a wide variety of thermophilic and mesophilic bacteria, archaea, and potentially eukaryotic organisms that can tolerate the expression conditions. The modular nature of the expression vectors facilitates adaptation to new host systems by simply exchanging the appropriate regulatory elements and selection markers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1: Isolation of Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

*Geobacillus thermodenitrificans* strain T12 was isolated from compost material collected from a commercial composting facility. The isolation procedure involved enrichment culture at 65°C in nutrient-rich medium followed by serial dilution and plating on solid agar plates. Individual colonies were purified through repeated streaking and characterized by 16S rRNA gene sequencing, which confirmed their identity as *Geobacillus thermodenitrificans*. The strain was maintained on LB2 agar plates at 65°C and stored as glycerol stocks at -80°C for long-term preservation. Genomic DNA was extracted from overnight cultures using standard protocols for Gram-positive bacteria, and the complete genome was sequenced using Illumina technology. Analysis of the genome sequence revealed the presence of a type-IIC CRISPR-Cas system, which was unexpected given previous assumptions about the absence of type II systems in thermophilic bacteria. The CRISPR-Cas locus was located on the chromosome and contained the *cas9* gene along with associated *cas1*, *cas2*, and *csn2* genes, as well as a CRISPR array consisting of 10 spacers separated by 36-nucleotide repeats. This discovery provided the foundation for the subsequent characterization and development of the ThermoCas9 system described herein.

### Example 2: Defining the Essential Consensus Sequences for Cas9 in Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

Bioinformatic analysis of the *Geobacillus thermodenitrificans* T12 genome identified the CRISPR-Cas type-IIC locus encoding ThermoCas9. The *cas9* gene was found to encode a protein of 1082 amino acids, which is smaller than the canonical SpCas9 (1368 amino acids) due to a truncated REC lobe. Sequence alignment with other Cas9 orthologues revealed conservation of the essential catalytic residues in the RuvC and HNH nuclease domains, specifically D8 in the RuvC domain and H582 in the HNH domain. The CRISPR array adjacent to the *cas* genes contained 10 spacers, each 30 nucleotides in length, separated by 36-nucleotide repeats. The tracrRNA sequence was predicted based on sequence complementarity to the repeat regions and the presence of conserved secondary structure elements. The predicted tracrRNA consisted of an anti-repeat region followed by three stem-loop structures, which were later confirmed to be essential for thermostability and activity. The essential consensus sequences for ThermoCas9 function were defined through comparative analysis with other Cas9 orthologues and experimental validation of catalytic residue importance through site-directed mutagenesis.

### Example 3: Identifying Core Amino Acid Motifs which are Essential for the Function of CAS9 and Those which Confer Thermostability in Thermophilic Cas9 Nucleases

Comparative sequence analysis of ThermoCas9 with other Cas9 orthologues revealed several conserved amino acid motifs essential for catalytic function and structural integrity. The RuvC domain contained the conserved D8 residue, which corresponds to D10 in SpCas9, while the HNH domain contained H582, corresponding to H840 in SpCas9. These catalytic residues were confirmed as essential through mutagenesis studies, where substitution with alanine completely abolished nuclease activity while maintaining DNA binding capability. Additional conserved motifs included the PI domain responsible for PAM recognition and the bridge helix involved in DNA unwinding and R-loop formation. Thermostability-conferring motifs were identified through comparison with mesophilic Cas9 orthologues and included increased numbers of charged residues, particularly arginine and glutamate, which can form salt bridges that stabilize the protein structure at elevated temperatures. The truncated REC lobe in ThermoCas9 also contributed to thermostability by reducing the overall protein size and potentially minimizing flexible regions susceptible to thermal denaturation. Secondary structure prediction revealed an increased proportion of alpha-helices compared to beta-sheets, which is consistent with thermophilic protein adaptation.

### Example 4: Determination of the PAM Sequence of G. thermodenitrificans gtCas9

The PAM sequence requirement for ThermoCas9 was determined through a combination of in silico prediction and experimental validation. Initial in silico analysis using the CRISPRtarget algorithm with the 10 spacers from the *G. thermodenitrificans* T12 CRISPR array identified only two potential protospacer matches in phage genomes, both containing cytosine at the fifth PAM position and adenine at the eighth position. This limited dataset prompted the development of an in vitro PAM determination approach using a randomized PAM library. A 7-nucleotide degenerate PAM library was constructed by annealing complementary oligonucleotides containing the protospacer sequence followed by NNNNNNN (where N represents any nucleotide) and cloning into a plasmid vector. In vitro cleavage assays were performed at 55°C with ThermoCas9 and sgRNA, followed by deep sequencing of cleaved versus uncleaved products. Analysis revealed a consensus PAM sequence of 5′-NNNNCNR-3′, where R represents a purine (A or G), with preferences for cytosine at positions 1, 3, 4, and 6. Subsequent experiments extending the analysis to the eighth PAM position confirmed that adenine at position 8 significantly enhanced cleavage efficiency, establishing the complete 8-nucleotide PAM requirement as 5′-NNNNCNRA-3′.

### Example 5: Target Generation with Randomized PAM

To systematically determine the PAM requirements for ThermoCas9, a randomized PAM library was generated using synthetic DNA oligonucleotides. A 122-bp DNA fragment was constructed containing a fixed 20-nucleotide protospacer sequence followed by a 7-nucleotide degenerate sequence (NNNNNNN) at the 3′ end. This fragment was generated by annealing complementary oligonucleotides and extending with Klenow fragment (exo-) to create double-stranded DNA. The PAM library fragment was then digested with BspHI and BamHI restriction enzymes and ligated into the pNW33n vector backbone that had been similarly digested. The ligation mixture was transformed into electrocompetent *E. coli* DH10B cells, and plasmid DNA was isolated from pooled transformants to create a diverse library representing all possible 7-nucleotide PAM combinations. For cleavage assays, the plasmid library was linearized with SapI to create defined DNA ends, and individual reactions were set up with purified ThermoCas9 protein, in vitro transcribed sgRNA, and the linearized PAM library. After incubation at 55°C, cleaved products were separated from uncleaved DNA by gel electrophoresis, and both fractions were subjected to deep sequencing to identify enriched PAM sequences in the cleaved fraction compared to the control.

### Example 6: In Vitro Determination of PAM Sequences for gtCas9

In vitro cleavage assays were performed to experimentally determine the PAM sequence preferences for ThermoCas9. Purified ThermoCas9 protein (160 nM), in vitro transcribed sgRNA (150 nM), and DNA substrate (4 nM) were incubated separately at the assay temperature for 10 minutes before combining in cleavage buffer (100 mM sodium phosphate pH 7, 500 mM NaCl, 25 mM MgCl₂, 25% glycerol, 5 mM DTT) and incubating for 1 hour at the specified temperature. The DNA substrates consisted of either the randomized PAM library or specific plasmid constructs containing defined PAM sequences. Reactions were stopped by adding 6X loading dye and analyzed by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis. For PAM library analysis, cleaved DNA fragments were excised from gels, purified, and prepared for Illumina HiSeq 2500 sequencing by ligating sequencing adapters. Both cleaved and uncleaved (control) samples were sequenced to the same depth, and bioinformatic analysis identified PAM sequences that were significantly enriched in the cleaved fraction. The analysis revealed that ThermoCas9 recognizes a 7-nucleotide PAM with the consensus 5′-NNNNCNR-3′, with strong preferences for cytosine at positions 1, 3, 4, and 6, and a purine (A or G) at position 7.

### Example 7: In Silico PAM Prediction for gtCas9

In silico prediction of the ThermoCas9 PAM sequence was performed using the CRISPRtarget algorithm, which searches for potential protospacer matches in viral and plasmid databases based on the spacer sequences from the organism's CRISPR array. The 10 spacer sequences from the *G. thermodenitrificans* T12 CRISPR array were used as queries against the NCBI viral and plasmid databases. Only two significant hits were identified, both located in phage genomes. Analysis of the sequences adjacent to these protospacers revealed a conserved pattern with cytosine at the fifth position and adenine at the eighth position relative to the protospacer. Although the limited number of hits prevented comprehensive PAM determination, the identified pattern provided initial insights that were later confirmed through experimental approaches. The in silico prediction suggested that ThermoCas9 might recognize an extended PAM sequence beyond the typical 3-6 nucleotides observed for many Cas9 orthologues, which motivated the subsequent experimental investigation of 8-nucleotide PAM requirements.

### Example 8: Determination of 8 Nucleotide Long PAM Sequences for gtCas9

Based on the initial identification of adenine at the eighth PAM position in the in silico analysis, additional experiments were conducted to confirm and characterize the extended 8-nucleotide PAM requirement for ThermoCas9. Four linearized plasmid targets were constructed, each containing a distinct 5′-CCCCCCAN-3′ PAM sequence where N represented A, C, G, or T at the eighth position. In vitro cleavage assays were performed at 55°C with ThermoCas9 and sgRNA, and the reactions were analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis. The results demonstrated that adenine at the eighth position (5′-CCCCCCAA-3′) supported the highest cleavage efficiency, while other nucleotides at this position resulted in reduced activity. To further refine the PAM requirements, a series of 16 different target DNA fragments were generated containing all possible combinations of nucleotides at the sixth and seventh PAM positions within the context of 5′-CCCCCNNA-3′. Cleavage assays performed across a temperature range from 20°C to 60°C revealed that optimal targeting efficiency occurred with PAM sequences containing 5′-CNAA-3′ and 5′-CMCA-3′ motifs in the fifth to eighth positions, while 5′-CAKA-3′ sequences showed the lowest efficiency. These results established the complete 8-nucleotide PAM consensus as 5′-NNNNCNRA-3′, where N represents any nucleotide, R represents a purine (A or G), with preferences for cytosine at positions 1, 3, 4, and 6, and adenine at position 8.

### Example 9: In Vivo Genome Editing of Bacillus smithii ET138 with gtCas9 and 8 Nucleotide Length PAM Sequences

The ThermoCas9 system was successfully applied for in vivo genome editing of the thermophilic bacterium *Bacillus smithii* ET138 at 55°C. A single-plasmid approach was employed using pNW33n-based vectors containing the *thermocas9* gene under the control of the native *xylL* promoter, a sgRNA expression module driven by the constitutive *pta* promoter, and homologous recombination flanks for the target gene. The *pyrF* gene was selected as the initial target for deletion, and two different sgRNAs were designed to target distinct sites within the gene, both containing PAM sequences matching the 8-nucleotide consensus 5′-NNNNCNRA-3′. Transformation of *B. smithii* ET138 competent cells with the editing plasmids resulted in significantly fewer colonies compared to control plasmids containing non-targeting sgRNAs, demonstrating the lethal effect of ThermoCas9-induced double-stranded breaks in the absence of successful homologous recombination. PCR analysis of transformants revealed that 10% of colonies contained the desired Δ*pyrF* genotype, with one colony representing a clean deletion mutant lacking the wild-type allele. The low efficiency was attributed to the constitutive expression of ThermoCas9 from the *xylL* promoter, which did not allow sufficient time for homologous recombination to occur before Cas9-mediated counter-selection. Despite the modest efficiency, this experiment demonstrated the first successful application of a Cas9-based genome editing tool in an obligate thermophile, validating the thermostable nature of the ThermoCas9 system and its compatibility with high-temperature growth conditions.
