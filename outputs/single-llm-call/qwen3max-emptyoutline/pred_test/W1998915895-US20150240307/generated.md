# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, specifically to methods and reagents for the diagnosis and pathological assessment of rheumatoid arthritis (RA). More particularly, the invention provides a novel diagnostic approach based on the quantitative detection of specific microRNAs (miRNAs) in plasma or serum samples derived from human subjects. The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits, reagents, and algorithms that utilize the expression levels of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and optionally miR-30a-5p to determine the presence, likelihood, or pathological status of rheumatoid arthritis with high specificity and sensitivity. This technology enables early and accurate differentiation of RA from other arthritic conditions such as osteoarthritis (OA) and systemic autoimmune diseases like systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), thereby facilitating timely therapeutic intervention and personalized treatment strategies.

## BACKGROUND ART

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic, systemic autoimmune disorder characterized by persistent synovial inflammation, progressive joint destruction, and functional disability. Early diagnosis and aggressive treatment are critical to preventing irreversible joint damage and improving long-term clinical outcomes. Current diagnostic criteria, such as those established by the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) and the European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR) in 2010, rely on a combination of clinical symptoms, serological markers—including rheumatoid factor (RF) and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA)—and imaging findings. However, these criteria have limitations, particularly in patients who are seronegative for both RF and ACPA. In such cases, a definitive diagnosis often requires the involvement of more than ten joints, delaying the initiation of disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) or biologic therapies during a crucial early window when intervention is most effective.

Moreover, existing serological biomarkers lack sufficient specificity to distinguish RA from other inflammatory or degenerative joint diseases. For instance, RF can be elevated in other autoimmune conditions, chronic infections, and even in healthy elderly individuals, while ACPA, although highly specific for RA, is absent in approximately 30% of RA patients. Consequently, there remains an unmet clinical need for novel, non-invasive, and highly specific biomarkers that can facilitate early diagnosis, particularly in seronegative populations, and support differential diagnosis from conditions such as osteoarthritis—a non-inflammatory degenerative joint disease—and systemic lupus erythematosus, which shares some immunological features but requires distinct therapeutic management.

In recent years, microRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as promising candidates for liquid biopsy-based diagnostics. These small, non-coding RNA molecules, typically 18–25 nucleotides in length, regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally by targeting messenger RNAs (mRNAs) for degradation or translational repression. Dysregulation of specific miRNAs has been implicated in numerous pathological processes, including autoimmunity, inflammation, and tissue remodeling. Importantly, miRNAs have been found to be remarkably stable in extracellular biological fluids such as plasma, serum, and synovial fluid, where they are protected from ribonuclease degradation through association with proteins (e.g., Argonaute 2), encapsulation in exosomes or microvesicles, or binding to high-density lipoproteins (HDL). This stability, combined with their disease-associated expression patterns, renders circulating miRNAs attractive as minimally invasive biomarkers.

Previous studies have identified alterations in miRNA profiles in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, T lymphocytes, synovial fibroblasts, and osteoclasts of RA patients, linking specific miRNAs to inflammatory pathways and joint destruction mechanisms. Additionally, certain plasma miRNAs, such as miR-16, miR-132, and miR-223, have been proposed as potential RA biomarkers. However, these candidates have demonstrated insufficient specificity, as they fail to reliably differentiate RA from osteoarthritis or other inflammatory conditions. Furthermore, the lack of standardized normalization strategies for circulating miRNA quantification has hindered reproducibility and clinical translation. Commonly used endogenous controls, such as miR-16 or U6 snRNA, exhibit variable expression across disease states, while spike-in controls like cel-miR-39—derived from *Caenorhabditis elegans* and absent in humans—offer a more consistent reference but require careful validation.

Despite advances in miRNA profiling technologies, including quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) arrays and next-generation sequencing, no plasma miRNA-based diagnostic test for RA has achieved widespread clinical adoption. Existing approaches often suffer from limited cohort sizes, inadequate validation against relevant differential diagnoses, or reliance on single-miRNA signatures that lack robustness due to biological heterogeneity among patients. There is therefore a pressing need for a rigorously validated, multi-miRNA signature that demonstrates high diagnostic accuracy across diverse patient populations, including those with seronegative RA, and that can be implemented using standardized, clinically feasible methodologies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The present invention addresses several critical limitations in the current diagnostic landscape for rheumatoid arthritis. First, it solves the problem of delayed diagnosis in seronegative RA patients—those lacking detectable RF and ACPA—who do not meet conventional classification criteria until significant joint involvement has occurred. Second, it overcomes the insufficient specificity of existing biomarkers, which often cannot distinguish RA from osteoarthritis or other systemic autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus. Third, the invention resolves the issue of biological and technical variability in circulating miRNA measurements by establishing a robust normalization strategy and identifying a minimal set of miRNAs whose combined expression yields superior diagnostic performance compared to individual markers. Finally, the invention provides a quantitative, objective, and reproducible method that can be integrated into routine clinical workflows, enabling earlier therapeutic decisions and improved patient stratification.

### Means of Solving the Problems

The invention achieves these objectives through the identification and validation of a specific panel of plasma microRNAs—namely miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and optionally miR-30a-5p—as highly specific biomarkers for rheumatoid arthritis. The method involves obtaining a plasma or serum sample from a human subject, isolating total RNA, and quantifying the absolute concentrations of these miRNAs using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) with appropriate normalization, preferably using a synthetic spike-in control such as cel-miR-39. The diagnostic decision is made either by evaluating the concentration of each miRNA against predetermined cutoff values or, more advantageously, by computing a composite score termed ePRAM (estimated Probability of RA by plasma MiRNAs), which integrates the expression levels of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p through a multivariate logistic regression model. This algorithmic approach enhances diagnostic sensitivity and specificity by accounting for inter-individual variability and mitigating the influence of confounding factors such as age or general inflammation. The invention further provides diagnostic reagents, including primer sets, probes, and calibration standards, optimized for the reliable detection of these miRNAs in clinical specimens.

### Effect of the Invention

The invention delivers significant clinical and diagnostic benefits. It enables the early and accurate identification of rheumatoid arthritis, even in seronegative patients, thereby facilitating timely initiation of appropriate therapy and potentially altering disease trajectory. The high specificity of the miRNA panel minimizes false-positive diagnoses, reducing unnecessary treatments and healthcare costs. The ePRAM score offers a continuous, probabilistic assessment of RA likelihood, supporting nuanced clinical judgment rather than binary classification. Moreover, because the biomarkers are derived from a simple blood draw, the method is minimally invasive, cost-effective, and amenable to automation in standard clinical laboratories. The invention thus represents a substantial advancement over existing diagnostic tools, with the potential to improve patient outcomes, enhance clinical trial enrollment through better phenotyping, and guide personalized treatment selection based on molecular pathology.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### 1. Test Method of a Sample Derived From the Blood of a Test Subject for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis

The present invention provides a method for determining the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis in a human subject by analyzing a blood-derived sample, such as plasma or serum. The method comprises the steps of: (a) collecting a blood sample from the subject into an anticoagulant-containing tube, preferably EDTA; (b) processing the blood sample to obtain plasma or serum through centrifugation; (c) isolating total RNA from the plasma or serum, wherein the isolation protocol includes the addition of a known quantity of a synthetic non-human miRNA, such as cel-miR-39, as an exogenous spike-in control to normalize for variations in RNA extraction efficiency and sample handling; (d) performing quantitative reverse transcription followed by real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) to measure the absolute concentrations of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and optionally miR-30a-5p in the isolated RNA; and (e) interpreting the results to assess the likelihood or presence of rheumatoid arthritis.

In step (d), the qRT-PCR is conducted using sequence-specific primers and probes designed to selectively amplify mature forms of miR-24 (specifically miR-24-3p, as referenced by mature sequence MIMAT0000083 in miRBase), miR-125a-5p (MIMAT0000443), and miR-30a-5p (MIMAT0000087). The amplification is carried out under stringent conditions to ensure specificity, and standard curves generated from synthetic miRNA oligonucleotides or plasmid constructs containing the target sequences are used to convert cycle threshold (Ct) values into absolute concentrations, typically expressed in picomolar (pM) units. Normalization is performed relative to the spiked-in cel-miR-39, whose recovery is monitored to validate the integrity of the RNA isolation process.

In step (e), interpretation may be performed in two modes. In a first mode, the absolute concentration of each miRNA is compared against predefined diagnostic cutoffs: for miR-24, a concentration of 1.46 pM or higher; for miR-26a, 3.09 pM or higher; and for miR-125a-5p, 0.0817 pM or higher. A positive result for any one of these markers, particularly in the context of clinical suspicion, supports a diagnosis of RA. However, the preferred mode employs the ePRAM algorithm, which calculates a probability score using the formula:

ePRAM = exp(−x) / (1 + exp(−x)),

where x is a linear combination of the normalized concentrations of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p, with coefficients derived from multivariate logistic regression analysis of a large validation cohort. Specifically, x = β₀ + β₁·[miR-24] + β₂·[miR-125a-5p] − β₃·[miR-30a-5p], where β₀, β₁, β₂, and β₃ are empirically determined constants that reflect the relative contribution of each miRNA to the diagnostic model. The subtraction of miR-30a-5p accounts for its role as a stable reference that reduces measurement noise. A subject is classified as likely having RA if the ePRAM score exceeds a threshold value of 0.50, which corresponds to a sensitivity of 78.4% and a specificity of 92.3% in the validated population.

The method is applicable to subjects presenting with arthralgia, joint swelling, or other signs suggestive of inflammatory arthritis, especially when conventional serological tests (RF and ACPA) are negative. It is also useful for monitoring disease activity, as miR-24 and ePRAM show correlation with clinical disease activity indices such as DAS28. Importantly, the method has been validated against key differential diagnoses: plasma levels of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and ePRAM are significantly lower in patients with osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus, confirming disease specificity. The test is unaffected by common RA treatments, including conventional DMARDs and biologic agents, ensuring reliability across diverse therapeutic regimens.

### 2. Diagnostic Reagent for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis

The invention further provides a diagnostic reagent kit for implementing the aforementioned method. The kit comprises: (i) a lysis buffer suitable for plasma or serum RNA extraction, optionally containing guanidinium thiocyanate or phenol-based components; (ii) a solid-phase RNA purification column or magnetic beads for selective binding of small RNAs; (iii) a vial containing a lyophilized or liquid formulation of synthetic cel-miR-39 at a defined concentration (e.g., 25 femtomoles per assay); (iv) reverse transcription reagents, including a miRNA-specific stem-loop RT primer mix or polyadenylation-based cDNA synthesis enzymes; (v) qPCR master mix containing DNA polymerase, dNTPs, and fluorescent dye or probe system; (vi) sequence-specific forward primers and TaqMan probes or SYBR Green-compatible primers for miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p; (vii) standard templates for generating calibration curves, which may include synthetic miRNA oligonucleotides or plasmids encoding the mature miRNA sequences; and (viii) detailed instructions for use, including protocols for RNA isolation, qRT-PCR setup, data analysis, and interpretation guidelines.

The primers and probes are designed to ensure high specificity for the mature, functional isoforms of the target miRNAs, avoiding cross-reactivity with precursor forms or closely related family members. For example, the forward primer for miR-24-3p may have the sequence 5′-TGGCTCAGTTCAGCAGGAACAG-3′, while that for miR-125a-5p may be 5′-TCCCTGAGACCCTTTAACGTG-3′. The kit may be configured for use on common real-time PCR platforms such as Applied Biosystems 7500 or 7900 systems, and may include pre-aliquoted reagents to minimize pipetting errors and enhance reproducibility.

Optionally, the kit may include software or a digital calculator for computing the ePRAM score from raw concentration data, automating the diagnostic interpretation. The reagents are manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) conditions and are stable for at least 12 months when stored at −20°C. The kit enables clinical laboratories to perform the test with minimal specialized training, promoting widespread adoption.

## EXAMPLES

### Method

Plasma samples were collected from 102 patients diagnosed with rheumatoid arthritis according to ACR/EULAR 2010 criteria, 104 healthy controls, 24 patients with osteoarthritis, and 11 patients with systemic lupus erythematosus. All participants provided informed consent, and the study was approved by the ethics committee of Kyoto University. Blood was drawn into EDTA-2K tubes and centrifuged at 1400 g for 7 minutes to separate plasma, which was stored at −80°C. Total RNA was extracted from 150 µL of plasma using TriPure Isolation Reagent and RNeasy Mini Spin Columns, with 25 fmol of cel-miR-39 added as a spike-in control prior to extraction. Reverse transcription was performed using the NCode VILO miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit, and qRT-PCR was conducted on an Applied Biosystems 7500 system using EXPRESS SYBR GreenER qPCR SuperMix. Absolute concentrations of miR-24, miR-26a, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p were calculated from standard curves generated with synthetic miRNA standards. Statistical analyses, including ROC curve construction, logistic regression, and correlation testing, were performed using JMP 8 software.

### Results

Plasma concentrations of miR-24, miR-26a, and miR-125a-5p were significantly elevated in RA patients compared to healthy controls (p < 0.001). ROC analysis yielded area-under-the-curve (AUC) values of 0.83 for miR-24, 0.81 for miR-26a, and 0.85 for miR-125a-5p. At optimal cutoffs, miR-24 demonstrated 63.7% sensitivity and 89.5% specificity; miR-26a, 53.9% and 94.3%; and miR-125a-5p, 64.7% and 89.5%. The ePRAM score, combining miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p, achieved an AUC of 0.89, with 78.4% sensitivity and 92.3% specificity at a cutoff of 0.50. In ACPA-negative RA patients (n=15), ePRAM was positive in 73%, compared to 67% for miR-125a-5p alone. Levels of all three markers were significantly lower in OA and SLE patients than in RA (p < 0.05), confirming specificity. No significant correlations were observed between miRNA levels and treatment modalities, and miR-125a-5p showed only weak negative correlation with age (r = −0.25, p < 0.05), which was compensated for in the ePRAM model.

## COMPARATIVE EXAMPLE

For comparison, plasma levels of previously reported RA-associated miRNAs—miR-16, miR-132, and miR-223—were measured in the same cohort. While these miRNAs were elevated in RA versus healthy controls, they failed to distinguish RA from osteoarthritis, with overlapping concentration ranges and AUC values below 0.65 in ROC analysis for OA differentiation. In contrast, miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and ePRAM maintained high specificity against OA, demonstrating the superior diagnostic utility of the newly identified markers.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The invention is highly applicable in clinical diagnostics, pharmaceutical development, and personalized medicine. Diagnostic laboratories can implement the method using standard qRT-PCR infrastructure, enabling widespread screening for early RA. Pharmaceutical companies can use the biomarkers to enrich clinical trial populations with confirmed RA pathology, improving trial power and reducing attrition. The test also supports therapeutic decision-making by confirming RA diagnosis in ambiguous cases, thereby guiding the appropriate use of biologics. Given its robustness, specificity, and compatibility with routine blood sampling, the invention is well-positioned for commercialization and integration into global rheumatology practice guidelines.
