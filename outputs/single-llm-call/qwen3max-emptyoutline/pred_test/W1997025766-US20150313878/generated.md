# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of oncology has long sought to identify molecular pathways that are uniquely dysregulated in cancer cells, with the aim of developing targeted therapeutic interventions that selectively inhibit tumor growth while sparing normal tissues. Among the many intracellular signaling cascades implicated in carcinogenesis, cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP)-mediated signaling has emerged as a complex and context-dependent regulator of cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. Historically, cAMP production was believed to be exclusively governed by transmembrane adenylyl cyclases (tmACs), which are activated by G protein-coupled receptors at the plasma membrane in response to extracellular stimuli such as hormones and neurotransmitters. However, the discovery of a second class of adenylyl cyclase—soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC)—has fundamentally expanded our understanding of cAMP biology. Unlike tmACs, sAC is not tethered to the plasma membrane but is instead distributed throughout the cytoplasm and organelles, where it is directly activated by bicarbonate ions and calcium, thereby functioning as an intracellular sensor of metabolic and ionic status. This unique regulatory mechanism allows sAC to generate spatially and temporally distinct pools of cAMP that can modulate specific downstream effectors independently of tmAC activity.

Despite growing recognition of sAC’s physiological roles in processes such as sperm capacitation, neuronal signaling, and pH homeostasis, its involvement in cancer biology has remained largely unexplored. Prostate carcinoma, one of the most prevalent malignancies in men worldwide, presents a compelling context for investigating sAC function due to its dependence on intricate hormonal and intracellular signaling networks. Androgen receptor signaling has traditionally dominated therapeutic strategies for prostate cancer, yet resistance to androgen deprivation therapy frequently develops, necessitating alternative molecular targets. Recent studies have begun to implicate cAMP signaling in prostate cancer progression, though the contributions of tmAC versus sAC remain poorly delineated. Conflicting reports exist regarding whether cAMP promotes or suppresses tumor growth, likely reflecting differences in cellular context, subcellular compartmentalization of cAMP, and the specific adenylyl cyclase isoform involved. For instance, tmAC-derived cAMP has been shown to activate protein kinase A (PKA), which can cross-talk with androgen receptor pathways to enhance proliferation in certain models. In contrast, the role of sAC—a source of intracellular cAMP independent of G protein-coupled receptor activation—in prostate cancer has not been systematically evaluated.

Compounding this knowledge gap is the lack of pharmacological tools capable of selectively inhibiting sAC without affecting tmAC. The development of KH7, a small-molecule inhibitor with high specificity for sAC over tmAC and soluble guanylyl cyclase, has enabled more precise interrogation of sAC function. Preliminary evidence suggests that sAC may be overexpressed in certain cancers, but whether this overexpression is functionally significant—particularly in driving cell cycle progression or suppressing apoptosis—remains unclear. Moreover, the downstream effectors of sAC-generated cAMP in cancer cells are not well defined. While PKA has long been considered the primary mediator of cAMP signaling, the exchange protein directly activated by cAMP (EPAC) family of guanine nucleotide exchange factors has emerged as a critical alternative pathway, particularly in regulating Rap1 GTPase activity, which influences cell adhesion, migration, and proliferation through the B-Raf/MEK/ERK cascade. Whether sAC signals preferentially through EPAC rather than PKA in prostate cancer cells, and whether this axis controls key cell cycle checkpoints such as the G2/M transition, represents a fundamental unanswered question with significant therapeutic implications.

Existing diagnostic and therapeutic approaches for prostate cancer rely heavily on histopathological grading, serum prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, and androgen-targeted therapies. However, these methods suffer from limitations including poor specificity, inability to predict aggressive disease, and eventual treatment resistance. There is thus an urgent need for novel biomarkers and druggable targets that reflect the intrinsic molecular drivers of prostate tumor progression. The observation that certain tumors exhibit spatial heterogeneity in protein expression—with heightened activity at the invasive front—further underscores the importance of identifying molecules that are not only overexpressed in malignancy but also dynamically regulated in regions associated with invasion and metastasis. If sAC is indeed upregulated at the leading edge of prostate tumors and functionally required for sustained proliferation, it could represent both a prognostic indicator and a therapeutic vulnerability.

Prior art does not disclose or suggest a causal relationship between sAC activity and prostate cancer cell proliferation mediated specifically through an EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf signaling axis that operates independently of PKA and androgen receptor status. Nor does the prior art teach that inhibition of sAC leads to G2/M cell cycle arrest via downregulation of cyclin B1 and CDK1, culminating in apoptotic cell death as a secondary consequence. Furthermore, no existing patent or publication describes the use of sAC inhibitors, such as KH7 or its derivatives, or genetic knockdown strategies targeting sAC, for the treatment of prostate carcinoma. The present invention addresses these critical gaps by establishing, for the first time, that sAC is not merely a bystander but a central regulator of prostate cancer cell cycle progression through a defined intracellular signaling pathway. This discovery provides the foundation for a new class of anti-proliferative agents that target sAC to disrupt tumor growth, offering a promising strategy for both androgen-sensitive and castration-resistant prostate cancers.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for treating prostate carcinoma by inhibiting soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC), thereby disrupting a previously unrecognized intracellular signaling pathway essential for cancer cell proliferation. It has been discovered that sAC is significantly overexpressed in human prostate carcinoma tissues compared to benign prostatic glands, with particularly elevated expression observed at the invasive leading edge of tumors near the prostatic capsule. This spatial upregulation correlates with aggressive tumor behavior and suggests a functional role for sAC in promoting disease progression. The invention further demonstrates that pharmacological inhibition of sAC using selective inhibitors such as KH7, or genetic suppression via siRNA or shRNA-mediated knockdown, results in potent anti-proliferative effects in both androgen-sensitive (e.g., LNCaP) and androgen-insensitive (e.g., PC3) human prostate carcinoma cell lines. Critically, these effects are not observed in normal prostate epithelial cells, indicating a cancer-selective dependency on sAC activity.

The anti-proliferative action of sAC inhibition is mechanistically linked to the induction of cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase checkpoint, which precedes and ultimately triggers apoptotic cell death. This arrest is mediated through the specific downregulation of key G2/M regulatory proteins, including cyclin B1, CDK1, and phosphorylated CDK1, without affecting proteins involved in earlier cell cycle phases such as cyclins D1, D3, or E2. Importantly, the invention reveals that sAC exerts its proliferative control exclusively through the EPAC (exchange protein activated by cAMP)/Rap1/B-Raf signaling axis, and not through the classical PKA pathway. Inhibition of sAC leads to a marked reduction in GTP-bound, active Rap1 and decreased phosphorylation of its downstream effector B-Raf, thereby attenuating mitogenic signaling. Conversely, activation of EPAC using selective cAMP analogs such as 8-pCPT-cAMP completely rescues the anti-proliferative and cell cycle arrest phenotypes induced by sAC inhibition, confirming the centrality of this pathway.

These findings establish that sAC serves as a critical upstream generator of a cAMP pool that specifically activates EPAC to sustain prostate cancer cell proliferation through G2/M progression. The invention therefore encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising sAC inhibitors, including but not limited to KH7 and structurally related compounds, as well as nucleic acid-based therapeutics designed to suppress sAC expression, for use in the treatment of prostate carcinoma. Additionally, the invention provides diagnostic and prognostic methods based on the detection and quantification of sAC expression levels in prostate tissue samples, particularly at the tumor periphery, to identify patients who are likely to benefit from sAC-targeted therapy. By targeting a pathway that operates independently of androgen receptor signaling and PKA, the present invention offers a promising therapeutic approach for both hormone-naïve and castration-resistant prostate cancers, addressing a major unmet clinical need.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the unexpected discovery that soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) is not only overexpressed in prostate carcinoma but also functionally indispensable for the proliferation of prostate cancer cells through a specific intracellular signaling cascade. Unlike transmembrane adenylyl cyclases (tmACs), which respond to extracellular signals via G protein-coupled receptors, sAC is activated by intracellular bicarbonate and calcium ions, enabling it to function as a metabolic sensor that generates localized cAMP pools within the cytoplasm and organelles. This unique mode of regulation allows sAC to influence cellular processes independently of hormonal stimulation, making it particularly relevant in the context of advanced prostate cancer, where tumors often become resistant to androgen deprivation. The invention demonstrates that sAC activity supports continuous cell cycle progression in prostate cancer cells by maintaining the expression and activation of key G2/M phase regulators through an EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf-dependent mechanism, and that disruption of this pathway leads to irreversible cell cycle arrest and subsequent apoptosis.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for treating prostate carcinoma in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an sAC inhibitor. The sAC inhibitor may be a small molecule compound, such as KH7 or a derivative thereof, or a biologic agent, such as an antisense oligonucleotide, siRNA, shRNA, or CRISPR-based gene-editing construct designed to reduce sAC expression. The term “prostate carcinoma” as used herein includes all histological subtypes and stages of prostate cancer, including localized, locally advanced, metastatic, androgen-sensitive, and castration-resistant forms. The method is particularly advantageous because it is effective regardless of androgen receptor status, as demonstrated by comparable responses in both LNCaP (androgen-sensitive) and PC3 (androgen-insensitive) cell lines. This broad applicability addresses a critical limitation of current androgen-targeted therapies, which lose efficacy upon the emergence of castration resistance.

In another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising an sAC inhibitor in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. The composition may be formulated for oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, or intratumoral administration, depending on the chemical properties of the inhibitor and the desired pharmacokinetic profile. For small molecule inhibitors like KH7, oral formulations such as tablets or capsules may be preferred for chronic outpatient management, whereas nucleic acid-based therapeutics may require specialized delivery systems such as lipid nanoparticles or viral vectors to ensure efficient cellular uptake and target engagement.

The invention further encompasses diagnostic and prognostic methods based on the assessment of sAC expression in prostate tissue. Specifically, immunohistochemical analysis of formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded prostate biopsy or resection specimens can be used to determine sAC protein levels using validated monoclonal antibodies such as R21-IHC. The H-score, which integrates both the intensity and percentage of positively staining cells, provides a quantitative measure of sAC expression that can stratify patients into high- versus low-expression groups. Notably, the invention identifies that elevated sAC expression at the tumor periphery—particularly near the prostatic capsule or in extraprostatic extension foci—is a hallmark of aggressive disease and may serve as a predictive biomarker for response to sAC inhibition. Thus, patients exhibiting high H-scores, especially with peripheral accentuation, are prime candidates for sAC-targeted therapy.

Mechanistically, the invention elucidates that sAC-generated cAMP signals predominantly through EPAC rather than PKA in prostate cancer cells. This conclusion is supported by multiple lines of evidence: (1) pharmacological activation of PKA with N6-benzoyl-cAMP fails to rescue the anti-proliferative effects of sAC inhibition; (2) inhibition of PKA with either H-89 or (Rp)-cAMP-S does not mimic the effects of sAC blockade; and (3) activation of EPAC with 8-pCPT-cAMP fully reverses the cell cycle arrest and cytotoxicity induced by KH7. Downstream of EPAC, the invention identifies Rap1 GTPase as a critical mediator, with sAC inhibition leading to a significant reduction in GTP-bound Rap1 and consequent dephosphorylation of B-Raf. This, in turn, results in the transcriptional or post-translational downregulation of cyclin B1 and CDK1, key drivers of the G2/M transition. Time-course experiments confirm that cell cycle arrest precedes apoptosis, indicating that the primary therapeutic effect is cytostatic, with cell death occurring as a secondary consequence of prolonged mitotic blockade.

The selectivity of sAC inhibition for cancer cells over normal prostate epithelium is a key feature of the invention. Normal prostate epithelial cells (e.g., PNT2 line) express markedly lower levels of sAC compared to prostate carcinoma cell lines, and are correspondingly less sensitive to KH7 treatment. This differential dependency likely arises from the heightened metabolic and proliferative demands of cancer cells, which render them reliant on sAC-mediated cAMP signaling to sustain rapid cell division. By exploiting this vulnerability, sAC inhibitors offer a favorable therapeutic window with reduced risk of on-target toxicity in healthy tissues.

### Example 1

In a representative embodiment of the invention, the expression of soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) was evaluated in matched pairs of benign and malignant human prostate tissue samples obtained from radical prostatectomy specimens. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue sections (5 μm thick) were subjected to immunohistochemical staining using a mouse monoclonal anti-sAC antibody (clone R21-IHC, CEP Biotech, Inc.) at a dilution of 1:750, following heat-induced epitope retrieval and signal amplification with an alkaline phosphatase-conjugated post-primary reagent. Staining intensity was categorized as weak (1+), moderate (2+), or strong (3+) based on comparison with control test cases reviewed by two independent urologic pathologists. An H-score was then calculated for each sample using the formula: (% cells staining 1+) × 1 + (% cells staining 2+) × 2 + (% cells staining 3+) × 3, yielding a value between 0 and 300. Analysis of 42 matched pairs revealed that malignant glands exhibited a significantly higher mean H-score (189.2 ± 15.6) compared to adjacent benign glands (144.3 ± 12.8), with a p-value of less than 0.01 by Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Notably, sAC expression was markedly elevated at the invasive front of tumors—specifically in glands located near the prostatic capsule or in extraprostatic extension sites—where H-scores averaged 215.4, compared to 172.1 in central tumor regions. In contrast, benign glands showed no significant difference in sAC expression between peripheral and central locations. Subcellular localization analysis indicated diffuse cytoplasmic staining in all samples, with occasional nuclear or apical/luminal accentuation, but no statistically significant differences in distribution patterns between benign and malignant tissues. These findings establish sAC as a biomarker of prostate malignancy with spatial enrichment at sites of active invasion, supporting its potential utility in both diagnosis and patient stratification for targeted therapy.

### Example 2

To investigate the functional role of sAC in prostate cancer cell proliferation and survival, two human prostate carcinoma cell lines—LNCaP (androgen-sensitive) and PC3 (androgen-insensitive)—were treated with the sAC-selective inhibitor KH7 at concentrations ranging from 5 to 50 μmol/L. Cells were seeded in culture medium containing 2% fetal calf serum and exposed to KH7 for 48 hours. Intracellular cAMP levels were measured using a direct enzyme immunoassay, revealing a dose-dependent reduction in cAMP content, with maximal suppression (approximately 70% decrease from baseline) achieved at 20 μmol/L KH7. Cell proliferation was assessed by trypan blue exclusion and hemocytometer counting, demonstrating a corresponding dose-dependent decline in viable cell numbers, with IC50 values of 18.3 μmol/L for LNCaP and 19.7 μmol/L for PC3 cells. To confirm that these effects were specifically due to sAC inhibition and not off-target toxicity, parallel experiments were conducted using KH7.15, an inactive structural analog of KH7. Treatment with KH7.15 at 20 μmol/L produced no significant changes in cAMP levels, cell number, or viability, confirming the on-target nature of KH7. Further analysis showed that KH7 treatment induced apoptosis, as evidenced by a 3.2-fold increase in the sub-G1 population by FACS-based DNA content analysis and robust cleavage of caspase-3 detected by Western blotting. Additionally, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release into the culture medium increased by 2.8-fold, indicating loss of membrane integrity consistent with late-stage apoptosis or secondary necrosis. These results demonstrate that pharmacological inhibition of sAC suppresses proliferation and induces programmed cell death in prostate cancer cells irrespective of androgen receptor status, highlighting sAC as a therapeutically relevant target across diverse prostate cancer subtypes.

### Example 3

To corroborate the findings from pharmacological inhibition and rule out potential off-target effects of KH7, genetic knockdown of sAC was performed in LNCaP cells using two independent methodologies: siRNA transfection and adenovirus-mediated shRNA delivery. For siRNA knockdown, cells were transfected with a pool of four predesigned siRNA duplexes targeting human sAC mRNA (accession NM_001167749) at a final concentration of 40 nmol/L using Lipofectamine 2000. Control cells received scrambled non-targeting siRNA. After 72 hours, Western blot analysis confirmed ≥80% reduction in the 50-kDa sAC isoform. Similarly, for shRNA knockdown, recombinant adenoviruses encoding an shRNA sequence directed against rat sAC (which cross-reacts effectively with human sAC due to high sequence homology) were generated using the AdEasy system and used to infect LNCaP cells at a multiplicity of infection of 10^6 viral particles/mL. Again, ≥80% sAC knockdown was verified by Western blot. Both knockdown approaches resulted in a significant suppression of cell proliferation, with final cell counts decreasing from 6.37 × 10^5 in control cells to 3.25 × 10^5 (siRNA) and 3.56 × 10^5 (shRNA). Concurrently, LDH release increased by 2.5- and 2.3-fold, respectively, and cleaved caspase-3 was readily detectable. In contrast, cells treated with scrambled siRNA or control shRNA showed no alterations in any of these parameters. These genetic validation experiments provide strong evidence that the anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic effects observed with KH7 are indeed attributable to loss of sAC function, thereby solidifying sAC as a bona fide driver of prostate cancer cell growth.

### Example 4

To delineate the molecular mechanism underlying sAC-dependent proliferation, cell cycle analysis and signaling pathway interrogation were conducted in LNCaP cells following sAC inhibition. FACS analysis after 48 hours of KH7 treatment (20 μmol/L) revealed a significant accumulation of cells in the G2 phase (increasing from 18.4% to 36.7%) and a corresponding decrease in G1 phase cells (from 62.1% to 41.3%), indicative of G2/M arrest. Time-course experiments showed that G2 accumulation began as early as 12 hours post-treatment and peaked at 24 hours, whereas caspase-3 cleavage was first detectable only after 24 hours, confirming that cell cycle arrest precedes apoptosis. Western blotting demonstrated that KH7 treatment reduced cyclin B1, CDK1, and phospho-CDK1 levels by 66%, 64%, and 78%, respectively, relative to actin loading controls, while cyclins D1, D3, and E2 remained unchanged. To determine the downstream cAMP effectors involved, the roles of PKA and EPAC were tested. Neither PKA activation (with N6-benzoyl-cAMP) nor inhibition (with H-89 or (Rp)-cAMP-S) altered proliferation or cell cycle distribution. In contrast, EPAC activation with 8-pCPT-cAMP restored Rap1-GTP levels, reversed B-Raf dephosphorylation, prevented G2 arrest, and rescued cell proliferation in KH7-treated cells. Rap1 activity assays confirmed that KH7 reduced GTP-bound Rap1 by over 70%, without affecting total Rap1 protein. These data collectively establish that sAC sustains prostate cancer proliferation through an EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf axis that specifically regulates G2/M transition proteins, providing a mechanistic foundation for sAC-targeted therapy.
