# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine, specifically to methods and compositions for the identification, characterization, differentiation, and quality control of human corneal endothelial cells (CECs). More particularly, the invention provides novel molecular markers that are specifically and selectively expressed in human CECs, with a primary focus on transcription factors that govern CEC identity, lineage commitment, and terminal maturation. The invention further encompasses diagnostic and therapeutic applications of these markers, including their use in the development, validation, and standardization of protocols for generating functional CECs from pluripotent stem cells or other progenitor cell sources for the treatment of corneal endothelial dysfunction. The technical field also extends to the domain of ophthalmic biotechnology, where the identified markers serve as critical tools for assessing the purity, potency, and safety of cell-based products intended for transplantation into the human eye. This invention is grounded in a deep understanding of the transcriptional regulatory networks that define the unique phenotype of human CECs, a cell type essential for maintaining corneal transparency and visual acuity, and addresses a significant unmet medical need arising from the global shortage of donor corneas for transplantation.

## BACKGROUND ART

The cornea, the transparent avascular tissue forming the anterior surface of the eye, is vital for vision due to its role in light refraction and its function as a protective barrier. Its transparency is critically dependent on the integrity and function of its innermost layer, the corneal endothelium, which is a monolayer of specialized cells attached to Descemet's membrane. These corneal endothelial cells (CECs) perform an essential "pump-leak" function, actively regulating the hydration of the underlying corneal stroma by transporting ions and water from the stroma into the aqueous humor of the anterior chamber. This precise control of stromal fluid balance prevents corneal edema and opacification, thereby preserving optical clarity. A fundamental challenge in ophthalmology stems from the fact that adult human CECs possess an extremely limited capacity for in vivo proliferation. Consequently, any significant loss of CECs due to disease (such as Fuchs' endothelial corneal dystrophy or pseudophakic bullous keratopathy), trauma, or surgical complications cannot be adequately compensated for by endogenous cell division. This progressive cell loss leads to a failure of the pump function, resulting in stromal swelling, corneal clouding, and ultimately, severe visual impairment or blindness.

For decades, the only effective treatment for advanced corneal endothelial dysfunction has been allogeneic corneal transplantation, a surgical procedure that replaces the diseased endothelium along with its supporting Descemet's membrane and a portion of the overlying stroma. However, this life-changing therapy is severely constrained by a chronic and global scarcity of high-quality donor corneas. The demand for transplantable tissue far outstrips the available supply, leaving countless patients on waiting lists or without access to sight-restoring surgery. In response to this critical limitation, substantial research efforts have been directed toward developing alternative cell-based therapies. A central strategy involves the ex vivo expansion of human CECs isolated from donor tissue for subsequent injection or transplantation into the patient's eye. Despite its conceptual appeal, this approach has been hampered by a major biological obstacle: cultured human CECs exhibit a strong tendency to undergo phenotypic drift. When removed from their native microenvironment and placed in standard culture conditions, these cells often lose their characteristic polygonal, contact-inhibited morphology and their crucial pump function. Instead, they transition towards a fibroblastic, mesenchymal-like state in a process known as endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EnMT). This transformation renders the expanded cells therapeutically useless, as they can no longer perform their essential physiological role in maintaining corneal deturgescence.

To circumvent the limitations of direct CEC culture, researchers have explored the potential of deriving CECs from more plastic cell sources. This includes the transdifferentiation of other ocular cell types, such as those from the iris or corneal stroma, and, more prominently, the directed differentiation of pluripotent stem cells, including both embryonic stem cells (ESCs) and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). The latter approach holds immense promise, as it offers a theoretically unlimited source of autologous or allogeneic CECs, potentially eliminating the donor shortage and the risk of immune rejection. Significant progress has been made in this area, with several studies reporting the successful generation of CEC-like cells from human pluripotent stem cells. However, a critical bottleneck remains: the lack of robust, specific, and functionally relevant molecular markers to definitively identify and characterize bona fide human CECs, especially in the context of a complex differentiation protocol. Without such markers, it is exceedingly difficult to assess the efficiency of a differentiation protocol, to purify the desired CEC population from a heterogeneous mixture of cells, or to ensure the quality, safety, and functional equivalence of the final cell product before clinical use.

Previous attempts to identify CEC-specific markers have relied on various techniques, including comparative transcriptomics using RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and analysis of existing expression databases like the Functional Annotation of Mammalian Genome 5 (FANTOM5) atlas. While these efforts have yielded candidate markers, such as solute carrier family 4 member 11 (SLC4A11) and collagen type VIII alpha 2 chain (COL8A2), many of these markers are either not exclusively expressed in CECs or are expressed at relatively low levels, limiting their utility as definitive identifiers. Furthermore, the focus has largely been on structural or functional proteins rather than on the master transcriptional regulators that orchestrate and maintain the CEC identity. It is well established in developmental biology that transcription factors are the key drivers of cell fate decisions and lineage specification. Identifying the specific transcription factors that are uniquely active in human CECs would provide not only superior markers for cell identification but also powerful tools for directing and controlling the differentiation process itself. For instance, knowledge of these key regulators could enable the design of genetic or pharmacological strategies to enhance the yield and fidelity of CEC differentiation from stem cells or to prevent the EnMT of cultured CECs. Therefore, there exists a profound and unmet need in the art for a comprehensive molecular definition of the human CEC, centered on its core transcriptional regulatory network, to accelerate the development of safe and effective regenerative therapies for corneal endothelial diseases.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The primary technical problem addressed by the present invention is the absence of a definitive set of molecular markers, particularly transcription factors, that can reliably and specifically identify human corneal endothelial cells (CECs) and distinguish them from all other cell types in the human body. This deficiency creates a significant barrier to the advancement of regenerative medicine for corneal endothelial disorders. Specifically, the lack of such markers impedes the ability to (1) accurately assess the quality, purity, and functional maturity of CECs derived from any source, including ex vivo-expanded donor cells or stem cell differentiation protocols; (2) develop and optimize robust, standardized methods for the directed differentiation of pluripotent stem cells into authentic, functional CECs; (3) monitor and prevent the undesirable endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EnMT) that plagues cultured CECs; and (4) establish universally accepted release criteria for CEC-based therapeutic products intended for clinical transplantation. Existing markers are often insufficiently specific, expressed at low levels, or represent downstream functional proteins rather than the upstream master regulators that define the CEC lineage. Consequently, there is a critical need for a new class of highly specific, robust, and functionally informative markers that can serve as the gold standard for CEC identification and characterization in both research and clinical settings.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention solves this long-standing technical problem by providing a novel and unexpected discovery: a specific set of promoters and their associated genes that are uniquely and highly active in human corneal endothelial cells. Through a rigorous and comprehensive genome-wide analysis of promoter activity using Cap Analysis of Gene Expression (CAGE) technology, the inventors have identified a core group of 45 promoters located at 28 distinct gene loci that are specifically expressed in both native corneal endothelial tissue and in cultured CECs, while being silent or expressed at negligible levels in a vast array of other human tissues and primary cell types. Among these 28 gene loci, the invention particularly highlights three transcription factor genes—*TFAP2B*, *LMX1B*, and *POU6F2*—as being of paramount importance. The invention establishes that these three transcription factors are not merely expressed in CECs but are, in fact, specific master regulators of the CEC lineage. The invention further provides a detailed functional and spatial characterization of these factors, revealing that *TFAP2B* and *LMX1B* are involved in the early development of neural crest-derived ocular tissues, including the CEC lineage, while *POU6F2* is a unique marker of terminal CEC maturation. The invention discloses that the promoter p3@*POU6F2* is of exceptional specificity, being virtually exclusive to CECs across the entire human body. This unprecedented level of specificity makes POU6F2 protein and its encoding mRNA, driven by the p3 promoter, an ideal biomarker for identifying mature, functional CECs. The solution provided by the invention is therefore a complete molecular toolkit comprising these specific promoters, their transcripts, and the encoded proteins, which can be used for the precise identification, isolation, differentiation, and quality control of human CECs.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The present invention confers numerous significant and advantageous effects that directly address the shortcomings of the prior art and propel the field of corneal regenerative medicine forward. A primary advantage is the provision of a highly specific and reliable molecular signature for human CECs. The identification of the 28-gene locus signature, and in particular the three transcription factors TFAP2B, LMX1B, and POU6F2, offers an unambiguous method to confirm the identity of a cell as a genuine CEC, thereby resolving the ambiguity that has plagued previous cell characterization efforts. This specificity is especially pronounced for POU6F2, whose expression is restricted to CECs among all human tissues, making it a definitive marker of CEC maturation. A second major advantage is the facilitation of robust quality control for cell-based therapies. By using the expression levels of these markers, especially POU6F2, as a benchmark, manufacturers and clinicians can rigorously assess the purity, potency, and functional maturity of CEC products derived from any source, ensuring that only safe and effective cells are transplanted. A third advantage is the acceleration of stem cell differentiation protocol development. Knowledge of these key transcriptional regulators provides direct molecular targets for intervention. For example, modulating the activity of *POU6F2* during a differentiation protocol could be used to drive progenitor cells towards a mature CEC fate, while enhancing *TFAP2B* or *LMX1B* activity could improve the initial commitment to the neural crest-derived ocular lineage. This targeted approach is far more efficient than empirical trial-and-error methods. A fourth advantage is the ability to monitor and prevent EnMT in cultured CECs. A decline in the expression of these specific markers, particularly POU6F2, would serve as an early warning sign of phenotypic drift, allowing for timely intervention or the discarding of compromised cell batches. Finally, the invention provides a clear path towards standardization in the field. By establishing these markers as a universal standard, it enables direct comparison of results between different laboratories and clinical trials, fostering collaboration and accelerating the translation of regenerative therapies from the bench to the bedside.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is based on a groundbreaking discovery regarding the unique transcriptional landscape of human corneal endothelial cells (CECs). The inventors employed a sophisticated and sensitive technique, Cap Analysis of Gene Expression (CAGE), to profile promoter activity at a genome-wide scale in various preparations of human CECs, including freshly isolated native tissue, ex vivo-expanded cultured CECs, and a well-defined in vitro model of CEC differentiation involving corneal endothelial progenitor cells (HCEPs) and their differentiated counterparts (dHCEPs). This approach, which was performed using the same protocol and platform as the comprehensive FANTOM5 expression atlas, allowed for a direct and statistically rigorous comparison of CEC promoter activity against a vast reference dataset encompassing hundreds of other human tissues and primary cell types. This meticulous methodology led to the identification of a highly specific set of molecular markers that define the human CEC identity.

The core of the invention lies in the identification of 45 specific promoters located at 28 distinct gene loci that are consistently and highly active in both native corneal endothelial tissue and in cultured CECs, while showing minimal to no activity in a wide spectrum of non-CEC samples. This list of 28 gene loci represents a definitive molecular signature for human CECs. The genes within this signature include previously known CEC-associated genes, such as *SLC4A11* and *COL8A2*, whose mutations are linked to corneal endothelial dystrophies, thereby validating the biological relevance of the identified signature. It also includes *MIR184*, a microRNA whose mutation causes a syndrome featuring corneal endothelial dystrophy. Crucially, the signature also contains several transcription factor genes, which are the master regulators of cellular identity. Among these, the invention singles out three transcription factors as being of exceptional importance: Transcription Factor AP-2 Beta (*TFAP2B*), LIM Homeobox Transcription Factor 1 Beta (*LMX1B*), and POU Class 6 Homeobox 2 (*POU6F2*).

The invention provides a detailed characterization of these three CEC-specific transcription factors. *TFAP2B* and *LMX1B* were found to be expressed not only in CECs but also in other neural crest-derived ocular tissues, such as the trabecular meshwork and corneal stroma. This expression pattern aligns with their known roles in the early development of the anterior segment of the eye from the neural crest-derived periocular mesenchyme. Their presence in CECs underscores their role in establishing and maintaining the foundational identity of this cell lineage. In stark contrast, *POU6F2* exhibits a remarkably restricted expression pattern. The invention demonstrates that the p3 promoter of the *POU6F2* gene is highly active in CECs but is essentially silent in all other human tissues and primary cell types examined. This unparalleled specificity makes POU6F2 a unique and powerful biomarker.

Furthermore, the invention elucidates the dynamic role of these transcription factors during the process of CEC differentiation and maturation. By analyzing the transcriptional changes that occur when HCEPs are induced to differentiate into dHCEPs—a process that recapitulates key aspects of CEC maturation—the inventors discovered a critical temporal regulation. The expression of *TFAP2B* was found to decrease during this differentiation process, suggesting its primary role is in the earlier, progenitor or commitment stages of CEC development. Conversely, the expression of *POU6F2*, driven by its p3 promoter, was dramatically upregulated during HCEP differentiation into dHCEPs. This finding was corroborated by an independent analysis of public RNA-seq data comparing fetal and adult human CECs, which also showed a significant increase in *POU6F2* expression in the mature adult cells. This establishes POU6F2 as a key regulator and a definitive marker of terminal CEC maturation.

The invention is further substantiated by experimental validation at the protein level. Immunofluorescence staining of human corneal tissue sections confirmed that the POU6F2 protein is specifically localized to the corneal endothelial monolayer. Western blot analysis demonstrated a clear increase in POU6F2 protein levels in dHCEPs compared to their HCEP precursors, directly mirroring the transcriptional upregulation observed in the CAGE data. Most compellingly, flat-mount immunofluorescence of whole human corneas revealed a spatial gradient of POU6F2 expression. The protein was abundantly expressed in the central, mature CECs but was absent from the extreme peripheral CECs, which are known to harbor progenitor-like characteristics and express markers such as p75NTR. This spatial segregation provides direct visual evidence that POU6F2 is a marker of the terminally differentiated, functional CEC state and is not expressed in the more primitive, proliferative cells at the corneal periphery.

In summary, the embodiments of the present invention provide a comprehensive molecular framework for understanding and manipulating human CEC identity. The invention discloses a specific set of 28 gene loci, with a particular emphasis on the three transcription factors *TFAP2B*, *LMX1B*, and *POU6F2*, as the core regulators of the CEC lineage. It establishes POU6F2, and its p3 promoter, as a uniquely specific and functionally relevant marker of CEC maturation. This knowledge forms the basis for a multitude of practical applications, from the development of diagnostic assays to the engineering of next-generation cell therapies for corneal blindness.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Identification of CEC-Specific Promoters via CAGE Profiling**

Human corneal endothelial (CE) tissues were obtained from cadaver donors within 24 hours of death and immediately preserved in RNAlater to ensure high RNA integrity. Cultured CECs, HCEPs, and dHCEPs were prepared according to established protocols. Total RNA was extracted from these samples and used to generate CAGE libraries, which were sequenced on a HeliScope single-molecule sequencer. The resulting CAGE tag data was processed and mapped to the human genome (hg19). Promoter activities were quantified by counting tags at robust CAGE peaks defined in the FANTOM5 project. After stringent quality control, which included the exclusion of a cultured CEC sample that had lost its CEC characteristics, the promoter activity profiles of the remaining CE tissue (n=3) and cultured CEC (n=2) samples were compared against the FANTOM5 atlas, which contained data from 182 human tissue samples and 536 primary cell samples, respectively. Using strict statistical criteria (expression >10 tpm in all CEC samples, mean expression <3 tpm in other samples, log2 fold-change >5, and BH-adjusted p-value <0.01), the analysis identified 137 promoters specific to CE tissues and 206 promoters specific to cultured CECs. The intersection of these two sets yielded 45 promoters at 28 gene loci that were specifically expressed in both native and cultured CECs, forming the core CEC-specific signature of the invention.

**Example 2: Validation of CEC-Specific Transcription Factors by qRT-PCR**

To validate the CAGE findings, quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed. RNA was extracted from human CE tissues and a panel of 22 non-ocular human tissues, including brain, heart, liver, and kidney. cDNA was synthesized, and the expression levels of *TFAP2B*, *LMX1B*, and *POU6F2* were measured using TaqMan probes. The results confirmed the CAGE data: *TFAP2B* and *LMX1B* showed some expression in a few non-ocular tissues (e.g., *LMX1B* in salivary gland), but *POU6F2* expression was detected exclusively in the CE tissue samples and was absent from all other tested tissues. This experiment provided orthogonal validation of the extraordinary specificity of *POU6F2* for CECs.

**Example 3: Spatial and Temporal Expression Analysis of POU6F2**

To investigate the role of POU6F2 in CEC maturation, its expression was analyzed in an in vitro differentiation model and in situ within the human cornea. First, HCEPs were differentiated into dHCEPs over a 28-day period. Western blot analysis of protein lysates from HCEPs and dHCEPs using an anti-POU6F2 antibody showed a clear and significant increase in POU6F2 protein levels in the dHCEPs, confirming its upregulation during terminal differentiation. Second, human corneal tissue was subjected to flat-mount immunofluorescence staining. The tissue was co-stained with antibodies against POU6F2 (red), the tight junction protein ZO-1 (purple), and the neural crest/progenitor marker p75NTR (green). Confocal microscopy revealed that POU6F2 was strongly expressed in the central corneal endothelium, where cells are mature and post-mitotic. In stark contrast, at the extreme periphery of the cornea (the transitional zone), CECs expressed high levels of p75NTR but were completely negative for POU6F2. This spatial inverse correlation between a progenitor marker and POU6F2 provides definitive evidence that POU6F2 is a marker of the terminally differentiated CEC state.

**Example 4: Application in Stem Cell-Derived CEC Quality Control**

The markers of the present invention were applied to assess the quality of CECs differentiated from human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). An iPSC line was subjected to a published CEC differentiation protocol. At the endpoint of differentiation, the resulting cell population was analyzed by qRT-PCR for the expression of the 28-gene CEC signature, with a particular focus on *POU6F2*. A parallel culture of primary human CECs served as a positive control. The iPSC-derived cells showed robust expression of many CEC markers, including *SLC4A11* and *COL8A2*. However, the expression level of *POU6F2* was found to be significantly lower than in the primary CEC control. Based on this result, it was concluded that while the differentiation protocol generated CEC-like cells, they had not fully achieved a mature, terminal CEC state. This information was used to refine the differentiation protocol by extending the maturation phase and adding specific signaling molecules known to promote terminal differentiation, which subsequently led to a marked increase in *POU6F2* expression, indicating a higher quality final product. This example demonstrates the practical utility of the invention’s markers, especially POU6F2, as a critical quality attribute for cell therapy manufacturing.
