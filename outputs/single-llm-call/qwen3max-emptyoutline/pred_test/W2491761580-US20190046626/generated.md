# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of recombinant vaccine development, specifically to novel methods for the expression, purification, and use of recombinant Chlamydia major outer membrane protein (MOMP) as a subunit vaccine antigen. More particularly, the invention provides codon-harmonized nucleotide sequences encoding MOMP from Chlamydia trachomatis or Chlamydia muridarum, optimized expression systems in Escherichia coli that enable high-level surface localization of correctly folded recombinant MOMP in the bacterial outer membrane, and pharmaceutical compositions comprising such recombinant MOMP for the prevention of Chlamydia infection. The invention further encompasses methods of immunizing a subject against Chlamydia trachomatis using these recombinant MOMP antigens, which elicit robust humoral immune responses and confer protective efficacy comparable to native MOMP purified from elementary bodies. This technology addresses longstanding challenges in the production of conformationally authentic recombinant MOMP, thereby enabling scalable, cost-effective manufacturing of a promising vaccine candidate against one of the most prevalent sexually transmitted bacterial pathogens worldwide.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Chlamydia trachomatis is an obligate intracellular Gram-negative bacterium that constitutes a major global public health burden due to its role as the causative agent of the most common bacterial sexually transmitted infection. The pathogen exists in two primary developmental forms: the infectious, metabolically inert elementary body (EB), and the replicative reticulate body. The EB is characterized by a rigid outer membrane rich in the major outer membrane protein (MOMP), which accounts for approximately sixty percent of the total outer membrane protein mass. MOMP is not only structurally critical for maintaining the integrity of the EB but also serves as a dominant target of both humoral and cellular immune responses during natural infection. Serological classification of C. trachomatis into distinct serovars—A, B, Ba, and C associated with ocular trachoma; L1–L3 causing lymphogranuloma venereum; and D–K responsible for urogenital infections—is based primarily on antigenic variation within four hypervariable domains of MOMP. These urogenital serovars are linked to a spectrum of clinical manifestations including urethritis, cervicitis, pelvic inflammatory disease, tubal factor infertility, ectopic pregnancy, and neonatal complications such as conjunctivitis and pneumonia, underscoring the urgent need for an effective prophylactic vaccine.

Historically, early vaccine efforts employed formalin-inactivated whole Chlamydia organisms, which conferred only transient and incomplete protection, and in some cases exacerbated immunopathology upon subsequent natural infection. Consequently, subunit vaccine strategies focusing on defined immunogenic components have gained prominence. Among potential candidates, MOMP has emerged as a leading target due to its surface exposure, abundance, conservation across strains, and demonstrated capacity to induce neutralizing antibodies that block EB infectivity as well as T-cell epitopes capable of mediating cellular immunity. Indeed, native MOMP (nMOMP) purified directly from EBs has been shown to protect mice against genital challenge in preclinical models, validating its potential as a vaccine antigen. However, reliance on nMOMP presents significant practical limitations for commercial vaccine development, as large-scale production necessitates cultivation of Chlamydia in mammalian cell cultures—a process that is labor-intensive, low-yielding, costly, and poses biosafety and regulatory complexities.

Recombinant DNA technology offers a compelling alternative by enabling heterologous expression of MOMP in microbial hosts such as Escherichia coli, which supports high-density fermentation and established downstream processing. Nevertheless, recombinant MOMP (rMOMP) expression has proven exceptionally challenging due to the protein’s intrinsic properties: it is a cysteine-rich, 16-stranded β-barrel integral outer membrane protein belonging to the OmpA family, requiring proper folding, disulfide bond formation, and insertion into the outer membrane via the β-barrel assembly machinery (BAM) complex. When expressed in the E. coli cytoplasm without a secretion signal, rMOMP typically aggregates into insoluble inclusion bodies. Although refolding protocols can recover soluble protein, the resulting rMOMP often lacks native conformation, leading to diminished immunogenicity and reduced protective efficacy in animal models compared to nMOMP. Attempts to direct rMOMP to the outer membrane using its native leader sequence or heterologous signal peptides have yielded inconsistent results, frequently accompanied by host cell toxicity, poor surface expression, or misfolding.

Previous studies have identified several factors influencing successful outer membrane expression of complex β-barrel proteins in E. coli. These include transcriptional and translational regulation—where excessive expression rates overwhelm chaperone and BAM capacity—codon usage bias between the native and heterologous host affecting translation kinetics and co-translational folding, the choice of signal peptide governing efficient translocation across the inner membrane, and culture conditions modulating cellular stress responses. Codon optimization, traditionally used to replace rare codons in the native gene with those frequently used in E. coli, has sometimes improved yield but may disrupt natural translational pausing essential for correct folding. In contrast, codon harmonization—a more nuanced approach that preserves the relative codon usage pattern of the native organism while adapting it to the tRNA pool of the expression host—has shown promise in enhancing solubility and functionality of difficult-to-express proteins. Additionally, the selection of expression vectors with moderate promoter strength and low copy number, rather than strong inducible systems like T7, appears beneficial for membrane proteins by reducing metabolic burden and allowing time for proper biogenesis.

Despite these insights, no prior art has successfully integrated these strategies into a robust platform for producing conformationally authentic, immunogenic rMOMP suitable for vaccine development. Earlier reports of outer membrane-localized rMOMP either lacked immunogenicity data or failed to demonstrate equivalence to nMOMP in protective efficacy. There remains an unmet need for a scalable, reproducible method to produce rMOMP that recapitulates the structural and immunological features of the native antigen. The present invention addresses this gap by systematically optimizing multiple parameters—codon usage, vector design, leader sequence selection, and fermentation conditions—to achieve high-level, non-toxic expression of rMOMP in the E. coli outer membrane, followed by a tailored purification process that maintains protein integrity. The resulting rMOMP exhibits β-barrel secondary structure, reacts with conformation-sensitive antibodies, and induces protective immunity in mice equivalent to nMOMP, thereby providing a viable path toward a commercially feasible Chlamydia subunit vaccine.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive solution to the long-standing problem of producing immunologically relevant recombinant Chlamydia major outer membrane protein (MOMP) for use in prophylactic vaccines. At its core, the invention discloses a method for the high-yield, conformationally faithful expression of full-length MOMP from Chlamydia trachomatis or Chlamydia muridarum in the outer membrane of Escherichia coli, achieved through a synergistic combination of genetic, molecular, and bioprocess engineering strategies. Specifically, the invention employs codon harmonization of the MOMP-encoding nucleotide sequence to match the translational kinetics of the native Chlamydia host while ensuring compatibility with the E. coli expression system, thereby promoting co-translational folding and reducing aggregation. This harmonized sequence is operably linked to a heterologous secretion leader sequence—preferably derived from E. coli outer membrane proteins such as OmpA or OmpP, or from bacterial virulence factors like PelB—that efficiently directs translocation of the nascent polypeptide across the inner membrane into the periplasm, where it can engage the β-barrel assembly machinery for correct insertion into the outer membrane.

The recombinant construct is cloned into a low-copy-number expression vector under the control of a moderately strong, inducible promoter, such as the λPL promoter, which avoids the cytotoxic effects and misfolding associated with high-level, rapid expression driven by strong promoters like T7. Expression is conducted in a specialized E. coli host strain under tightly controlled fermentation conditions: induction occurs at mid-log phase (OD590 ≈ 0.5) using a defined concentration of inducer (e.g., 0.4 mM IPTG), at a reduced temperature (30°C), and in a chemically optimized growth medium (e.g., Cinnabar medium) that maximizes both cell viability and surface display of rMOMP. These conditions collectively mitigate host stress responses, prevent inclusion body formation, and ensure that the rate of rMOMP synthesis is balanced with the capacity of the cellular machinery for folding and membrane insertion.

Following expression, the invention provides a multi-step purification protocol designed to isolate rMOMP from the E. coli outer membrane while preserving its native-like conformation. This process involves selective detergent extraction—using sarkosyl in the presence of a reducing agent such as DTT—to solubilize rMOMP while minimizing co-purification of contaminating host proteins, followed by sequential chromatographic steps including size exclusion and anion exchange to achieve high purity. The resulting recombinant protein retains key structural features of native MOMP, as evidenced by circular dichroism spectroscopy confirming predominant β-sheet secondary structure, migration patterns on SDS-PAGE consistent with native oligomerization states, and reactivity with conformation-dependent antibodies raised against Chlamydia elementary bodies.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the purified rMOMP, formulated with appropriate adjuvants such as CpG oligonucleotides and Montanide ISA 720 VG, are disclosed for use in immunization. The invention further provides methods of inducing a protective immune response against Chlamydia trachomatis in a subject, comprising administering an effective amount of the rMOMP-based vaccine composition. Preclinical data demonstrate that immunization with rMOMP elicits serum IgG titers and subclass profiles (IgG1/IgG2c ratios) comparable to those induced by native MOMP, and confers significant protection against intravaginal challenge with C. trachomatis serovar D in a mouse model, as measured by reduced bacterial shedding over time. Critically, the level of protection afforded by rMOMP is statistically indistinguishable from that provided by nMOMP, establishing the functional equivalence of the recombinant antigen.

In summary, the invention overcomes the historical limitations of rMOMP-based vaccines by providing a scalable, reproducible platform for producing a conformationally authentic, highly immunogenic MOMP antigen. This platform not only enables the development of a much-needed Chlamydia vaccine but also serves as a generalizable methodology for the recombinant production of other complex bacterial outer membrane proteins for therapeutic, diagnostic, or research applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising recombinant Chlamydia major outer membrane protein (rMOMP) as the active immunogenic ingredient, formulated in a manner suitable for administration to a human or animal subject to elicit a protective immune response against Chlamydia trachomatis infection. The rMOMP utilized in these compositions is produced according to the optimized expression and purification methods detailed herein, ensuring that the antigen maintains structural features critical for immunogenicity, including β-barrel conformation and surface-exposed epitopes recognized by neutralizing antibodies. The pharmaceutical composition comprises an immunologically effective amount of rMOMP, which is defined as the quantity sufficient to induce a measurable humoral and/or cellular immune response that reduces the risk, severity, or duration of Chlamydia infection upon subsequent exposure. In exemplary embodiments, the dose of rMOMP per administration ranges from 1 microgram to 100 micrograms, with a preferred dose of 10 micrograms per subject per immunization, as validated in murine challenge models.

The rMOMP is combined with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, which includes any diluent, excipient, or vehicle that does not interfere with the stability, delivery, or immunogenicity of the antigen and is compatible with the intended route of administration. Suitable carriers include aqueous buffers such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), Tris-buffered saline, or histidine buffer, optionally containing stabilizers like sucrose, trehalose, or human serum albumin to prevent aggregation during storage. The composition is preferably formulated with one or more adjuvants to enhance and modulate the immune response. Adjuvants useful in the present invention include, but are not limited to, Toll-like receptor agonists such as CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (e.g., CpG 7909 or similar Class B CpG), oil-in-water or water-in-oil emulsions (e.g., Montanide ISA 720 VG, MF59, or AS03), aluminum salts (e.g., aluminum hydroxide or aluminum phosphate), saponin-based adjuvants (e.g., QS-21), or combinations thereof. In a particularly preferred embodiment, the adjuvant system comprises CpG (at a dose of approximately 50 micrograms per immunization) in combination with Montanide ISA 720 VG (at 70% w/v), which has been shown to promote a balanced Th1/Th2 response characterized by robust IgG2c production indicative of cellular immunity alongside strong antibody titers.

The pharmaceutical composition may be presented in unit dosage form, such as vials or pre-filled syringes, and is typically stored at 2–8°C or frozen at −20°C or lower to maintain antigen integrity. Prior to administration, lyophilized formulations may be reconstituted with sterile water or buffer. The composition is substantially free of contaminants that could cause adverse reactions, including endotoxins; in preferred embodiments, the endotoxin level is below 10 endotoxin units (EU) per milligram of protein, as measured by the Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) assay, and more preferably undetectable by sensitive immunoassays such as anti-LPS western blotting. The purity of rMOMP in the final composition is at least 50% by mass, more preferably at least 70%, as determined by densitometry of Coomassie-stained SDS-PAGE gels or mass spectrometry, with the balance consisting primarily of residual host cell proteins that do not detract from immunogenicity.

Routes of administration contemplated by the invention include parenteral routes such as subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intradermal injection, with subcutaneous administration being particularly effective as demonstrated in murine studies. The vaccination regimen typically involves multiple doses administered at intervals of 2–4 weeks to prime and boost the immune response; for example, a three-dose schedule on days 0, 20, and 30 has proven efficacious in preclinical models. The composition may be co-administered with other Chlamydia antigens or vaccines targeting different pathogens, provided there is no immunological interference. Importantly, the pharmaceutical compositions of the invention are designed to be stable, scalable, and compliant with current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP), facilitating their transition from laboratory-scale production to commercial vaccine manufacturing.

### Methods of Use

The present invention provides methods for preventing or reducing the severity of Chlamydia trachomatis infection in a subject by administering a pharmaceutical composition comprising recombinant major outer membrane protein (rMOMP) as described herein. The method comprises the step of delivering an immunologically effective amount of the rMOMP-based vaccine composition to a subject in need thereof, thereby inducing an adaptive immune response that confers protection against subsequent exposure to Chlamydia trachomatis. The term “subject” as used herein refers to any mammal susceptible to Chlamydia infection, including humans, non-human primates, and laboratory animals such as mice, with human subjects being the primary target for prophylactic vaccination. The method is particularly indicated for individuals at elevated risk of Chlamydia infection, such as sexually active adolescents and young adults, individuals with multiple sexual partners, or those residing in regions with high prevalence of the disease.

Administration is performed via a parenteral route, preferably subcutaneous injection, although intramuscular or intradermal routes are also encompassed. The dosing regimen involves an initial priming dose followed by one or more booster doses to achieve sustained high-titer antibody responses and immunological memory. Based on preclinical data, an optimal regimen includes three doses administered at intervals of approximately three weeks (e.g., on days 0, 20, and 30), though alternative schedules with two or more doses spaced 2–8 weeks apart may be employed depending on the desired immune profile and practical considerations. Each dose contains from 1 to 100 micrograms of rMOMP, with 10 micrograms being a preferred dose that balances efficacy with antigen-sparing considerations for large-scale deployment.

The induced immune response includes the production of serum immunoglobulins, particularly IgG subclasses that correlate with protection. In murine models, rMOMP immunization elicits total IgG titers against Chlamydia elementary bodies that are statistically equivalent to those induced by native MOMP, with a favorable IgG1/IgG2c ratio indicating a mixed Th1/Th2 response conducive to both antibody-mediated neutralization and cellular clearance mechanisms. The antibodies generated recognize conformational epitopes on the native MOMP present on infectious EBs, as demonstrated by ELISA reactivity with whole EBs and flow cytometry binding to surface-expressed rMOMP. This conformational fidelity is critical for functional activity, as antibodies against linear epitopes may lack neutralizing capacity.

Protection conferred by the method is evidenced by a significant reduction in bacterial burden following challenge with C. trachomatis, as quantified by real-time PCR measurement of Chlamydial genomic copies in genital tract swabs over time. In vaccinated mice, rMOMP immunization results in accelerated clearance of the pathogen and lower peak shedding compared to adjuvant-only controls, with efficacy comparable to that of native MOMP. The method thus provides a means to interrupt the cycle of infection, transmission, and sequelae such as pelvic inflammatory disease and infertility. Additionally, the vaccine may reduce asymptomatic carriage, thereby contributing to herd immunity. The method is safe and well-tolerated, with no evidence of vaccine-enhanced disease or significant local or systemic reactogenicity in preclinical studies. By enabling scalable production of a conformationally authentic MOMP antigen, the invention facilitates the implementation of this method on a population scale to address a major unmet medical need in global sexual health.

### Codon-Harmonized MOMP Nucleotide Sequences

Central to the present invention is the design and use of codon-harmonized nucleotide sequences encoding Chlamydia major outer membrane protein (MOMP), which enable high-level, conformationally accurate expression in Escherichia coli. Unlike conventional codon optimization—which replaces all rare codons in the native gene with the most frequently used synonymous codons in the expression host—codon harmonization preserves the relative codon usage pattern of the native organism while adapting it to the tRNA availability of the heterologous host. This approach maintains natural translational pauses that are critical for co-translational folding of complex multidomain proteins, particularly those with extensive secondary structure like the 16-stranded β-barrel of MOMP.

The codon-harmonized sequences of the invention encode full-length MOMP polypeptides from Chlamydia trachomatis (e.g., serovars D or E) or Chlamydia muridarum (strain MoPn), including an N-terminal secretion leader sequence and the mature outer membrane domain. The amino acid sequences of these MOMPs are well-established in the art and correspond to GenBank entries such as P17451 (Ct serovar E), NP_220200.1 (Ct serovar D), or P75024.1 (Cm MoPn). To generate the harmonized nucleotide sequence, codon usage frequencies for Chlamydia (as represented by the strain with the most comprehensive codon data, e.g., C. trachomatis D/UW-3/CX) and E. coli (e.g., strain K-12 MG1655) are obtained from databases such as the Codon Usage Database (http://www.kazusa.or.jp/codon/). For each amino acid position in the MOMP sequence, if the native Chlamydia gene uses a codon that is rare in Chlamydia (defined as having a relative synonymous codon usage, RSCU, below a threshold such as 0.5), the corresponding rare codon in E. coli is selected to preserve the translational pause. For positions where the native codon is common in Chlamydia, the E. coli codon with the closest frequency match is chosen, ensuring that the overall codon adaptation mirrors the native kinetics.

The resulting harmonized nucleotide sequences are devoid of restriction enzyme sites that would interfere with cloning (e.g., NdeI and XhoI sites are avoided except at the termini for directional cloning), and they exhibit a calculated Codon Adaptation Index (CAI) that reflects balanced expression rather than maximal speed. Exemplary harmonized sequences include those encoding C. trachomatis serovar E MOMP (CtE-MOMP) with a PelB leader, C. trachomatis serovar D MOMP (CtD-MOMP), and C. muridarum MOMP (Cm-MOMP), all of which have been demonstrated to support superior outer membrane expression compared to standard codon-optimized versions. These sequences are typically synthesized de novo and cloned into expression vectors such as pAVE029, which features flanking NdeI and XhoI sites for seamless insertion.

The codon-harmonized MOMP genes of the invention are functionally defined by their ability to direct the production of rMOMP that localizes to the E. coli outer membrane with high efficiency, as measured by whole-cell flow cytometry using anti-Chlamydia EB sera, and that retains β-sheet secondary structure and immunogenicity equivalent to native MOMP. They represent a non-obvious improvement over prior art sequences, as the specific preservation of rare codons at strategic positions—rather than their elimination—proves essential for achieving the correct folding trajectory in the heterologous host. These sequences are thus foundational to the scalable production platform disclosed herein and are claimed as isolated nucleic acids, expression constructs, and recombinant host cells transformed therewith.

## EXAMPLE 1

### Expression of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP.

The successful expression of recombinant Chlamydia major outer membrane protein (rMOMP) in Escherichia coli required systematic optimization of multiple genetic and process parameters to overcome the inherent challenges of producing a complex, cysteine-rich β-barrel membrane protein. Initial efforts focused on comparing codon harmonization versus standard codon optimization for the Chlamydia muridarum MOMP (Cm-MOMP) gene. A codon-optimized version replaced all Chlamydia-preferred codons with the most frequent E. coli synonyms, while the harmonized version preserved the relative codon usage pattern of the native organism. Both constructs included the native Cm-MOMP leader sequence and were cloned into a pET-based expression vector under T7 promoter control. When expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) and analyzed by whole-cell flow cytometry using polyclonal mouse sera against Chlamydia EBs, the harmonized construct yielded approximately two-fold higher geometric mean fluorescence intensity (GFI) than the optimized version, demonstrating that translational kinetics mimicking the native host significantly enhance surface expression.

Subsequent optimization targeted the expression vector system. A panel of vectors differing in promoter strength and copy number was evaluated using C. trachomatis serovar E MOMP (CtE-MOMP) fused to the PelB leader sequence. High-copy-number vectors with strong T7 promoters (e.g., pET22b) resulted in minimal surface expression and severe growth inhibition, likely due to overwhelming the BAM complex and inducing envelope stress. In contrast, the low-copy-number vector pAVE029, featuring a moderately strong λPL promoter inducible by IPTG, supported robust rMOMP display with GFI values around 300–400. Vectors with titratable promoters (e.g., arabinose- or rhamnose-inducible) in low-copy backbones showed intermediate or negligible expression, underscoring the importance of promoter strength modulation. The pACYDuet vector, when paired with a host strain allowing RNA polymerase titration, also achieved moderate expression, confirming that transcriptional rate must be carefully balanced.

Leader sequence selection proved equally critical. Native Chlamydia MOMP leaders failed to direct CtD- or CtE-MOMP to the outer membrane, whereas heterologous leaders from E. coli (OmpA, OmpP), Shigella (SopA), Salmonella (PgtE), Yersinia (Pla), and Erwinia (PelB) all enabled detectable surface expression. PelB consistently yielded the highest levels, likely due to its efficient recognition by E. coli signal peptidase and compatibility with the Sec translocon. Notably, attempts to engineer the C-terminus of CtD-MOMP to match E. coli BAM recognition motifs did not improve expression, suggesting that N-terminal translocation is the primary bottleneck.

Finally, culture conditions were fine-tuned using the pAVE029-PelB-CtE-MOMP construct. Induction at mid-log phase (OD590 ≈ 0.5) with 0.4 mM IPTG for 4 hours at 30°C in Cinnabar medium maximized surface expression while maintaining cell viability. Longer induction times (16 h) or higher temperatures (37°C) caused cell lysis and reduced yields, while induction at high cell density (OD590 > 1.2) or in standard media like LB severely diminished expression. This optimized protocol was successfully applied to Cm-, CtD-, and CtE-MOMP, yielding transformants with clear surface display confirmed by flow cytometry histograms showing strong antibody binding compared to negative controls. Thus, Example 1 establishes that codon harmonization, coupled with judicious vector, leader, and process choices, enables reliable outer membrane expression of diverse Chlamydia MOMPs in E. coli.

## EXAMPLE 2

### Purification of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP.

Following successful outer membrane expression of recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP), a tailored purification strategy was developed to isolate the antigen while preserving its conformational integrity. E. coli cells expressing rCtE-MOMP under optimized conditions (pAVE029 vector, PelB leader, Cinnabar medium, 30°C induction) were harvested by centrifugation and resuspended in Tris-Cl buffer containing protease inhibitors. Cell disruption was achieved via microfluidization, and the crude lysate was clarified by low-speed centrifugation to remove unbroken cells. The membrane fraction, enriched in rMOMP, was then pelleted by ultracentrifugation at 23,800 × g for 90 minutes.

To enhance purity, the membrane pellet underwent sequential washes designed to remove specific contaminants. A high-salt wash (1 M NaCl, 0.05% Tween-20) eliminated residual soluble proteins, while a 1% Triton X-100 treatment selectively solubilized the inner membrane, leaving the outer membrane intact. Subsequently, a 3% β-octyl-glucoside wash removed non-target outer membrane proteins, exploiting differences in detergent sensitivity. For rMOMP extraction, various detergents were screened: sarkosyl (anionic), foscholine-14 (zwitterionic), and zwittergent 3–12. Sarkosyl proved most effective, particularly when supplemented with 5 mM DTT to reduce disulfide bonds critical for MOMP stability. The extraction was performed at room temperature for 2 hours, followed by ultracentrifugation to separate solubilized rMOMP from insoluble debris.

The sarkosyl extract was then subjected to size exclusion chromatography (SEC) on a Sephacryl S300 column equilibrated in HEPES-buffered saline with 0.1% zwittergent 3–14, which exchanged the detergent and separated rMOMP from aggregates and smaller contaminants. SEC fractions containing rMOMP were pooled and further purified by anion exchange chromatography (Hitrap Q FF), leveraging the protein’s net negative charge at pH 8.0. Elution with a NaCl gradient yielded highly enriched rMOMP, which was concentrated and stored at 4°C. The final yield averaged 6 mg per liter of culture across three preparations.

Analytical characterization confirmed the quality of the purified rMOMP. SDS-PAGE revealed a major band at ~40 kDa corresponding to monomeric MOMP, with minor dimeric and oligomeric species resistant to SDS and reducing agents—patterns nearly identical to native MOMP (nMOMP) purified from Chlamydia EBs. Western blotting with anti-EB sera verified antigenicity, while probing with anti-E. coli outer membrane vesicle sera indicated ~70% purity, with residual host proteins not removable without affinity tags. Mass spectrometry confirmed complete PelB leader cleavage, and anti-LPS western blots showed undetectable endotoxin. Circular dichroism spectroscopy demonstrated a pronounced minimum at 215 nm, characteristic of β-sheet structure, with rMOMP exhibiting even higher ellipticity than nMOMP—possibly due to differences in purification or co-purified chaperones. Thus, Example 2 details a robust, scalable purification process yielding conformationally authentic rMOMP suitable for vaccine formulation.

## EXAMPLE 3

### Mouse Immunogenicity and Challenge Study

The immunogenicity and protective efficacy of purified recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) were evaluated in a murine model of genital tract infection. Female C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks old) were divided into four groups (n=8–10 per group): (1) rCtE-MOMP (10 µg/dose) + adjuvant (CpG + Montanide ISA 720 VG); (2) native CtE-MOMP (nMOMP, 10 µg/dose) + same adjuvant; (3) live C. trachomatis serovar D EBs (1×10⁶ IFU/mouse) via intraperitoneal route; and (4) adjuvant alone as negative control. Mice received three subcutaneous immunizations on days 0, 20, and 30. Serum samples collected pre-immunization and two weeks post-final boost were analyzed by ELISA using C. trachomatis serovar D EBs as coating antigen.

Results showed that rMOMP induced total IgG titers against EBs that were statistically equivalent to those elicited by nMOMP (geometric mean titers ~10⁴–10⁵), with no significant difference by one-way ANOVA (p>0.05). Subclass analysis revealed similar IgG1/IgG2c ratios (~0.5–1.0), indicating a balanced Th1/Th2 response in both groups, unlike the Th2-skewed profile (high IgG1) seen with alum-adjuvanted vaccines. The live EB group generated the highest titers but with greater variability. These data confirm that OM-expressed rMOMP presents conformational epitopes recognized by functional antibodies, mirroring the immunogenicity of native antigen.

Two weeks after the final immunization, mice were prepared for intravaginal challenge by subcutaneous progesterone administration (2.5 mg) on days −10 and −3 to synchronize estrus. On day 0 of challenge, mice were inoculated with 1×10⁵ C. trachomatis serovar D EBs in 10 µL SPG buffer. Vaginal swabs were collected on days 7, 11, 14, 18, and 21 post-challenge and analyzed by quantitative real-time PCR targeting the Chlamydial 16S rRNA gene to quantify bacterial load.

Both rMOMP and nMOMP groups exhibited significantly reduced bacterial shedding compared to the adjuvant control (p<0.01 by Wilcoxon rank sum test on area-under-curve values), with near-complete clearance by day 21. The kinetics and magnitude of protection were indistinguishable between rMOMP and nMOMP groups, demonstrating that the recombinant antigen confers equivalent efficacy. The live EB group showed the fastest clearance but is not a practical vaccine strategy due to safety concerns. No signs of enhanced pathology were observed in any group. Thus, Example 3 validates that rMOMP produced via the disclosed platform is a potent vaccine candidate, inducing protective immunity comparable to the gold-standard native antigen in a relevant preclinical model.

### EXAMPLE 4

### Codon Harmonization Enhances rMOMP Solubility and Surface Expression

To rigorously evaluate the impact of codon harmonization on recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) expression, parallel constructs encoding Chlamydia muridarum MOMP (Cm-MOMP) were generated: one codon-harmonized according to the method described herein, and one conventionally codon-optimized for E. coli. Both constructs included the native Cm-MOMP leader sequence and were cloned into the pET22b(+) vector under control of the T7 promoter. These plasmids were transformed into E. coli BL21(DE3) cells, and expression was induced with 1 mM IPTG at 30°C for 4 hours. Whole-cell lysates were analyzed by SDS-PAGE under reducing conditions, revealing that the codon-optimized construct predominantly formed insoluble inclusion bodies, with minimal protein detected in the soluble fraction. In stark contrast, the codon-harmonized construct showed a significant proportion of rMOMP in the soluble membrane-associated fraction, with markedly reduced aggregation.

Surface expression was quantified using whole-cell flow cytometry. Cells were incubated with polyclonal mouse antisera raised against Chlamydia muridarum elementary bodies (diluted 1:250), followed by Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated anti-mouse IgG. Geometric mean fluorescence intensity (GFI) measurements from three independent experiments demonstrated that the codon-harmonized rMOMP yielded a GFI of approximately 180 ± 25, whereas the codon-optimized version produced a GFI of only 90 ± 15—a statistically significant two-fold enhancement (p < 0.01 by Student’s t-test). Western blot analysis of outer membrane fractions confirmed higher levels of full-length, properly processed rMOMP in the harmonized sample. These results establish that codon harmonization, by preserving native translational kinetics, facilitates correct co-translational folding and membrane insertion, thereby increasing both the solubility and surface display of rMOMP compared to traditional codon optimization approaches that accelerate translation indiscriminately.

### EXAMPLE 5

### Evaluation of Secretion Leader Sequences for rMOMP Outer Membrane Targeting

The efficiency of recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) localization to the E. coli outer membrane is critically dependent on the choice of N-terminal secretion leader sequence. To identify optimal leaders, a panel of eight sequences was tested: the native leaders from C. muridarum MOMP (Cm-MOMP), C. trachomatis serovar D MOMP (CtD-MOMP), and C. trachomatis serovar E MOMP (CtE-MOMP); E. coli-derived leaders from OmpA and OmpP; pathogen-derived leaders from Shigella flexneri SopA, Salmonella enterica PgtE, and Yersinia pestis Pla; and the commonly used PelB leader from Erwinia carotovora. Each leader was fused to the mature domain of Cm-MOMP, CtD-MOMP, or CtE-MOMP and cloned into the pAVE029 expression vector. Constructs were transformed into E. coli K12 W25113, and expression was induced under standardized conditions (0.4 mM IPTG, 30°C, 4 h in Cinnabar medium).

Surface expression was assessed by whole-cell flow cytometry using anti-Chlamydia EB mouse sera. Results revealed stark differences in leader performance. The native Chlamydia leaders were functional only for their cognate MOMP: the Cm-MOMP native leader directed robust surface expression of Cm-MOMP (GFI ~250) but failed completely for CtD- and CtE-MOMP (GFI < 50). Conversely, heterologous leaders showed broader compatibility. The PelB leader consistently yielded the highest expression across all three MOMP types (GFI 300–400), followed closely by E. coli OmpA and OmpP (GFI 250–350). Leaders from bacterial omptins (SopA, PgtE, Pla) supported moderate expression (GFI 150–250). Control constructs without any leader sequence resulted in cytoplasmic inclusion bodies and no surface signal.

Western blot analysis of whole-cell lysates confirmed that high-GFI leaders correlated with efficient cleavage of the signal peptide and accumulation of mature-sized rMOMP. Mass spectrometry of purified rCtE-MOMP with PelB leader verified complete removal of the leader sequence, yielding an N-terminus identical to native MOMP. These findings demonstrate that heterologous leaders, particularly PelB and E. coli Omp-family sequences, are superior to native Chlamydia leaders for directing rMOMP to the outer membrane in E. coli, likely due to better recognition by the host Sec translocon and signal peptidase machinery. This insight was pivotal for achieving high-yield, conformationally authentic rMOMP production.

### EXAMPLE 6

### Optimization of Expression Vector Parameters for rMOMP Production

The selection of an appropriate expression vector is paramount for achieving high-level, non-toxic expression of recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) in E. coli. A comparative study evaluated ten different vectors spanning a range of promoter strengths (strong T7, moderate λPL, titratable araBAD/rhaBAD) and copy numbers (high: pUC/pBR origin; medium: pMB1; low: p15A/pSC101). All constructs encoded C. trachomatis serovar E MOMP (CtE-MOMP) fused to the PelB leader sequence. Vectors were transformed into suitable E. coli hosts (e.g., BL21(DE3) for T7 systems, W25113 for λPL), and expression was induced under standardized conditions.

Surface expression was quantified by whole-cell flow cytometry. Vectors with strong T7 promoters and high copy numbers (e.g., pET22b, pJ831) resulted in negligible surface expression (GFI < 30) and severe growth defects, with cultures failing to reach OD600 > 0.3 post-induction. Medium-copy T7 vectors (pJ841) showed slightly improved viability but still minimal rMOMP display. Low-copy T7 vectors (pACYDuet, pJ851) permitted cell growth but yielded only intermediate expression (GFI ~120), likely because basal T7 RNA polymerase activity remained too high. In contrast, the pAVE029 vector—featuring a moderate-strength λPL promoter and low-copy pAT153 origin—supported robust surface expression (GFI ~300–400) with healthy cell growth (final OD600 > 1.0). A pACYDuet derivative used in a strain with titratable T7 RNA polymerase also achieved good expression (GFI ~200), confirming that transcriptional rate must be restrained.

SDS-PAGE analysis of membrane fractions corroborated these findings: pAVE029 cultures contained abundant full-length rMOMP, while T7-driven systems showed mostly degradation products or aggregates. These results underscore that for complex outer membrane proteins like MOMP, slower transcription and translation rates afforded by moderate promoters and low-copy vectors are essential to avoid overwhelming the BAM complex and inducing envelope stress. The pAVE029 system emerged as optimal, balancing expression level with host fitness to maximize functional rMOMP yield.

### EXAMPLE 7

### Impact of Culture Medium on rMOMP Outer Membrane Expression

The composition of the growth medium profoundly influences the efficiency of recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) expression in E. coli. Seven commercially available media were evaluated: Luria-Bertani (LB) broth, 2xYT, Cinnabar medium, Progro medium, Azura chemically defined medium, and two auto-induction formulations (0.2% lactose-based and Overnight Express). E. coli K12 W25113 harboring the pAVE029-PelB-rCtE-MOMP construct was grown in each medium at 37°C to mid-log phase (OD590 ≈ 0.5), induced with 0.4 mM IPTG, and incubated at 30°C for 4 hours. Surface expression was measured by whole-cell flow cytometry.

Cinnabar medium yielded the highest rMOMP surface expression, with geometric mean fluorescence intensity (GFI) values of 350–400 across replicates. LB, 2xYT with 1% glucose, Azura, and the lactose auto-induction medium supported only low-level expression (GFI 50–150). Progro medium was anomalous: it produced very high levels of rMOMP in the periplasm (detected by osmotic shock and western blot) but virtually no surface expression (GFI < 50), suggesting a defect in outer membrane insertion or stability in this medium. The Overnight Express auto-induction medium showed moderate expression but high batch-to-batch variability.

Further analysis revealed that Cinnabar medium’s superiority likely stems from its balanced nutrient profile, which supports robust growth without excessive metabolic burden, and possibly from components that stabilize membrane integrity or modulate chaperone expression. Cell viability post-induction was highest in Cinnabar, with minimal lysis observed. In contrast, LB-grown cultures exhibited significant fragility at 30°C post-induction. These findings highlight that medium selection is not merely a matter of biomass yield but critically affects the fidelity of membrane protein biogenesis. Cinnabar medium was therefore adopted as the standard for all subsequent rMOMP production runs.

### EXAMPLE 8

### Temperature and Induction Duration Effects on rMOMP Expression and Host Viability

The temperature and duration of induction are critical process parameters for recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) production, as they directly impact protein folding kinetics and host cell physiology. Using the pAVE029-PelB-rCtE-MOMP system in Cinnabar medium, induction was performed at four temperatures (16°C, 25°C, 30°C, 37°C) for three durations (4 h, 6 h, 16 h). Surface expression was measured by flow cytometry, and cell viability was monitored by optical density (OD590).

At 37°C, 4-hour induction yielded high rMOMP expression (GFI ~350) but caused significant cell lysis, as evidenced by a 30% drop in OD590 post-induction. Extending induction to 6 h exacerbated lysis with no gain in expression, while 16 h resulted in complete culture collapse and no detectable rMOMP. At 30°C, 4- and 6-hour inductions gave comparable high expression (GFI 300–350) with stable OD590, indicating optimal balance between synthesis and host tolerance. At 25°C, expression was markedly reduced (GFI ~150), and at 16°C, no surface rMOMP was detected despite cell growth, likely because low temperature impaired membrane fluidity and BAM complex activity.

These data demonstrate that 30°C is the ideal induction temperature for rMOMP: it supports efficient folding and insertion while avoiding the proteotoxic stress and membrane damage seen at 37°C. The 4-hour induction window is sufficient for maximal expression, with longer durations offering no benefit and increasing the risk of degradation. This precise thermal control is essential for reproducible, high-yield rMOMP production without compromising cell integrity.

### EXAMPLE 9

### Cell Density at Induction Dictates rMOMP Expression Efficiency

The optical density (OD) of the E. coli culture at the time of induction is a decisive factor in recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) yield. Cultures of E. coli K12 W25113 harboring pAVE029-PelB-rCtE-MOMP were grown in Cinnabar medium to varying densities (OD590 = 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1.2, 1.5) before induction with 0.4 mM IPTG at 30°C for 4 hours. Both final cell density (OD590 at harvest) and rMOMP surface expression (GFI by flow cytometry) were recorded.

Peak rMOMP expression occurred when induction was initiated at OD590 ≈ 0.5, yielding GFI values of 350–400 and final OD590 > 1.0. Induction at lower density (OD590 = 0.3) resulted in slightly reduced expression (GFI ~300), possibly due to insufficient biomass. Induction at OD590 = 0.8 caused a sharp decline in expression (GFI ~200) and reduced final biomass, indicating metabolic stress. At high densities (OD590 ≥ 1.2), surface expression plummeted to background levels (GFI < 50), and cultures showed signs of stagnation or lysis.

This inverse relationship between induction density and rMOMP yield likely reflects the depletion of critical resources (e.g., chaperones, BAM components, ATP) in late-log-phase cells, which cannot cope with the sudden demand for membrane protein biogenesis. Mid-log-phase cells (OD590 ≈ 0.5) possess optimal metabolic vigor and folding capacity. Thus, tight control of induction timing is essential for maximizing functional rMOMP production, and automated bioreactor systems that trigger induction at precise OD setpoints are recommended for scale-up.

### EXAMPLE 10

### Detergent Screening for Selective rMOMP Extraction from Outer Membranes

The choice of detergent is crucial for solubilizing recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) from E. coli outer membranes while preserving its native conformation and minimizing co-extraction of contaminants. Membrane pellets from rCtE-MOMP-expressing cells were treated with various detergents at concentrations known to selectively solubilize outer membrane proteins: 1% sarkosyl (anionic), 1% foscholine-14 (zwitterionic), 1% zwittergent 3–12 (zwitterionic), 1% Triton X-100 (non-ionic), and 2% CHAPS (zwitterionic). Extractions were performed in the presence or absence of 5 mM DTT, and solubilized fractions were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and western blot.

Sarkosyl proved uniquely effective, extracting >90% of rMOMP into the supernatant only when DTT was included, highlighting the necessity of reducing disulfide bonds for MOMP solubilization. Foscholine-14 and zwittergent 3–12 showed moderate extraction efficiency (~60–70%) with DTT, while Triton X-100 and CHAPS extracted minimal rMOMP (<20%). Without DTT, all detergents failed to solubilize rMOMP, confirming its dependence on cysteine reduction. Sarkosyl extracts also contained fewer contaminating E. coli proteins compared to other detergents, as judged by Coomassie staining.

Circular dichroism of sarkosyl-extracted rMOMP confirmed retention of β-sheet structure, whereas other detergents sometimes induced aggregation or denaturation. Thus, sarkosyl with DTT was selected as the optimal extraction system, enabling high-yield recovery of conformationally intact rMOMP for downstream purification.

### EXAMPLE 11

### Chromatographic Purification of rMOMP to Homogeneity

Following sarkosyl extraction, recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) was purified to high homogeneity using a two-step chromatographic process. The sarkosyl extract was first applied to a Sephacryl S300 size exclusion chromatography (SEC) column equilibrated in 10 mM HEPES pH 7.3, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% zwittergent 3–14. This step served dual purposes: exchanging sarkosyl for the milder zwittergent 3–14, and separating rMOMP (eluting at ~12 mL, corresponding to ~150 kDa, consistent with trimeric assembly) from high-molecular-weight aggregates and low-molecular-weight contaminants. SEC fractions containing rMOMP were pooled based on UV absorbance at 280 nm and SDS-PAGE analysis.

The pooled SEC fractions were then loaded onto a Hitrap Q FF anion exchange column equilibrated in 20 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0. rMOMP bound strongly to the column and was eluted with a linear NaCl gradient (0–1 M). Peak fractions (eluting at ~300 mM NaCl) contained >90% pure rMOMP by densitometry, with residual contaminants identified by mass spectrometry as E. coli outer membrane proteins (e.g., OmpA, OmpC). The final yield was 6.2 ± 0.8 mg per liter of culture (mean ± SD, n=3). The purified rMOMP was stable for >6 months at 4°C in HEPES-buffered saline with 0.05% zwittergent 3–14, with no loss of antigenicity or structural integrity by CD spectroscopy. This robust, scalable purification process ensures consistent production of clinical-grade rMOMP for vaccine formulation.

### EXAMPLE 12

### Structural Characterization of rMOMP by Circular Dichroism Spectroscopy

The secondary structure of purified recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) was analyzed by circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy to confirm its conformational authenticity relative to native MOMP (nMOMP). Samples of rCtE-MOMP and nCtE-MOMP (both in 10 mM HEPES pH 7.3, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% zwittergent 3–14) were analyzed on a Chirascan spectrometer using a 0.5 mm pathlength quartz cuvette at 20°C. Spectra were recorded from 200–280 nm, with buffer baselines subtracted and data smoothed using the Savitzky-Golay algorithm.

Both rMOMP and nMOMP exhibited a pronounced negative ellipticity minimum at ~215 nm, the hallmark signature of β-sheet secondary structure, consistent with MOMP’s predicted 16-stranded β-barrel fold. Quantitatively, rMOMP displayed a mean molar ellipticity of −12,500 deg·cm²·dmol⁻¹ at 215 nm, compared to −9,800 for nMOMP—a ~28% higher signal intensity. This difference may arise from subtle variations in tertiary packing, the presence of co-purified E. coli chaperones in the rMOMP preparation, or differences in detergent micelle interactions. Crucially, the spectral shape and peak position were nearly identical, confirming that rMOMP adopts the correct β-barrel architecture. Thermal denaturation studies (not shown) revealed similar melting temperatures (Tm ~65°C) for both proteins, indicating comparable stability. These data provide biophysical validation that OM-expressed rMOMP is structurally analogous to its native counterpart, a prerequisite for functional immunogenicity.

### EXAMPLE 13

### Comparative Immunogenicity of rMOMP and nMOMP in a Murine Challenge Model

To definitively establish the protective equivalence of recombinant and native Chlamydia MOMP, a head-to-head immunogenicity and challenge study was conducted in C57BL/6 mice. Groups (n=10) received three subcutaneous immunizations (days 0, 20, 30) with: (1) 10 µg rCtE-MOMP + CpG/Montanide; (2) 10 µg nCtE-MOMP + CpG/Montanide; (3) 1×10⁶ C. trachomatis D EBs i.p.; or (4) adjuvant alone. Pre- and post-immune sera were tested by ELISA against CtD EBs.

Post-boost, rMOMP and nMOMP groups showed identical geometric mean IgG titers (1:25,600) and IgG1/IgG2c ratios (0.75), confirming equivalent humoral responses. Two weeks post-boost, mice were challenged intravaginally with 1×10⁵ CtD EBs. Vaginal swabs collected on days 7–21 were analyzed by qPCR for Chlamydial 16S rDNA.

Both rMOMP and nMOMP groups exhibited >99% reduction in median bacterial load on day 7 versus adjuvant control (p<0.001), with 80% of mice clearing infection by day 14. Area-under-curve (AUC) analysis of shedding kinetics showed no significant difference between rMOMP and nMOMP (p=0.85 by Wilcoxon test), while both were superior to adjuvant control (p<0.01). Histopathology of genital tracts revealed minimal inflammation in vaccinated groups versus severe pathology in controls. These results conclusively demonstrate that rMOMP produced by the disclosed platform is immunologically and functionally indistinguishable from nMOMP, validating its use as a vaccine antigen.
