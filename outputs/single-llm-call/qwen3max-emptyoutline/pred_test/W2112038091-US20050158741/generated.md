# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The controlled regulation of gene expression in mammalian systems has long represented a cornerstone of both basic biological research and therapeutic development. Historically, such control has been achieved primarily through transcriptional regulation, employing inducible promoters responsive to exogenous ligands such as tetracycline, ecdysone, or rapamycin analogs. These systems, while powerful, are inherently limited by the kinetics of transcriptional initiation, mRNA processing, and protein turnover, often resulting in delayed onset and offset of gene expression. Moreover, transcriptional regulators frequently suffer from leaky basal expression, incomplete induction, or immunogenicity when applied in vivo, thereby constraining their utility in precise temporal and spatial control of gene function. In response to these limitations, alternative strategies have emerged that operate at the post-transcriptional level, including RNA interference (RNAi) and antisense oligonucleotide technologies. However, these approaches typically function by degrading or blocking existing mRNA transcripts, rather than enabling reversible, on-demand activation of gene expression from a silent state.

A particularly promising yet underexplored avenue for gene regulation involves the exploitation of naturally occurring catalytic RNA molecules known as ribozymes. Ribozymes are RNA sequences capable of self-cleavage at specific sites without the need for protein enzymes, thereby destabilizing the mRNA in which they are embedded and preventing translation of the downstream coding sequence. Among the most well-characterized ribozymes is the hammerhead ribozyme, originally identified in plant viroids and subsequently engineered for function in mammalian cells. The hammerhead ribozyme folds into a conserved tertiary structure that positions a catalytic core to cleave a specific phosphodiester bond, typically yielding two non-functional RNA fragments that are rapidly degraded by cellular nucleases. When inserted into the 5′ or 3′ untranslated region (UTR) of an mRNA transcript, a functional hammerhead ribozyme can effectively silence gene expression by ensuring that the full-length mRNA never accumulates to levels sufficient for protein production.

The inverse of this silencing mechanism—namely, the conditional inhibition of ribozyme self-cleavage to restore gene expression—offers a novel paradigm for gene regulation. In such a system, a gene of interest is placed under the control of a constitutively active promoter, but its mRNA is rendered non-functional by the inclusion of one or more self-cleaving ribozyme motifs. In the absence of an inhibitor, the mRNA is cleaved co-transcriptionally or immediately post-transcriptionally, leading to negligible protein output. Upon administration of a small molecule that specifically inhibits ribozyme activity, the mRNA remains intact, is exported to the cytoplasm, and is translated into functional protein. This “RNA-only” regulatory system bypasses the need for transcription factors, recombinases, or exogenous RNA-binding proteins, thereby reducing genetic complexity and potential immunogenicity. Critically, because the regulatory event occurs at the RNA level, induction can be rapid, reversible, and titratable, depending on the pharmacokinetics of the inhibitor.

Despite the theoretical elegance of this approach, its practical implementation has been hindered by two major challenges. First, many naturally occurring ribozymes exhibit poor catalytic efficiency when embedded in mammalian mRNA contexts, due to suboptimal folding, interference from flanking sequences, or incompatibility with the intracellular ionic environment. Second, and perhaps more significantly, there has been a profound lack of small molecules capable of potently and selectively inhibiting ribozyme self-cleavage in living cells. While certain aminoglycoside antibiotics have been shown to bind structured RNA motifs and inhibit hammerhead ribozyme activity in vitro, these compounds generally fail to function in cellular environments, likely due to poor cell permeability, sequestration by ribosomal RNA, or insufficient affinity for the ribozyme in the context of a full-length mRNA transcript associated with RNA-binding proteins.

Prior to the present invention, no robust, high-throughput method existed to identify small molecules capable of modulating ribozyme activity in mammalian cells. Most screening efforts focused on in vitro biochemical assays, which do not account for critical cellular variables such as compound uptake, metabolism, subcellular localization, or competition with endogenous RNA structures. Consequently, the discovery of effective ribozyme inhibitors remained largely serendipitous. The few reports of cellular ribozyme inhibition relied on antisense oligonucleotides or morpholino-based blockers, which, while effective, are expensive, difficult to deliver systemically, and not amenable to oral administration or chronic dosing.

The present invention addresses these longstanding deficiencies by providing a genetically encoded, self-cleaving RNA regulatory system that is responsive to small-molecule inhibitors identified through a purpose-built, cell-based high-throughput screening platform. Central to this innovation is the development of a highly efficient mutant hammerhead ribozyme, designated N79, which exhibits robust self-cleavage activity across diverse mammalian cell types and in vivo. Coupled with this ribozyme is a reporter cell line engineered to produce minimal basal expression of a luciferase gene unless ribozyme activity is inhibited. This system enabled the first large-scale, phenotypic screen for small molecules that restore gene expression by blocking RNA self-cleavage in living cells. Remarkably, this screen identified not only known nucleoside analogs with previously unrecognized ribozyme-inhibitory properties, but also revealed a novel mechanism of action: inhibition via covalent incorporation of modified nucleosides into the ribozyme-containing mRNA during transcription, thereby disrupting the structural integrity required for catalysis.

This discovery fundamentally shifts the paradigm for RNA-targeted small molecule design, moving away from traditional RNA-binding inhibitors toward metabolic precursors that are hijacked by the cell’s own transcription machinery to generate dysfunctional RNA. Furthermore, the identification of synergistic combinations of adenosine and uridine analogs enables potent, tunable gene induction with reduced cytotoxicity—a critical advance for potential therapeutic applications. The invention thus provides not only a new class of gene regulation tools for research and medicine but also establishes a generalizable framework for discovering small molecules that modulate RNA function through incorporation rather than direct binding.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel system for the conditional regulation of gene expression in mammalian cells and organisms through the use of self-cleaving RNA motifs, specifically engineered hammerhead ribozymes, whose activity can be reversibly inhibited by small-molecule compounds. At the core of this invention is a modified hammerhead ribozyme, termed N79, which demonstrates exceptionally high self-cleavage efficiency when embedded within the untranslated regions of mRNA transcripts in a wide range of mammalian cell types, including primary cells and in vivo models. In the absence of an inhibitor, the N79 ribozyme mediates rapid and near-complete degradation of the host mRNA, resulting in negligible expression of the downstream coding sequence. However, upon administration of specific small molecules that interfere with the ribozyme’s catalytic function, the mRNA remains intact and is translated into functional protein, thereby enabling inducible gene expression.

A key aspect of the invention is the identification of a new class of ribozyme inhibitors that function not through direct binding to the RNA structure, as previously hypothesized, but through covalent incorporation into the nascent RNA transcript during transcription. These inhibitors are primarily modified nucleoside analogs, including adenosine derivatives such as toyocamycin, tubercidin, and sangivamycin, as well as uridine derivatives such as 5-fluorouridine (FUR) and 5-bromouridine. Critically, these compounds require a 2′-hydroxyl group on the ribose sugar for activity, indicating that they must be recognized by RNA polymerase II and incorporated into RNA as triphosphate precursors. Once incorporated at specific positions within the conserved catalytic core or structural loops of the N79 ribozyme, these modified nucleotides disrupt essential base-pairing interactions or tertiary contacts, thereby abolishing self-cleavage activity. This mechanism was confirmed by in vitro transcription experiments demonstrating that ribozymes synthesized in the presence of tubercidin-triphosphate completely lose catalytic function, whereas pre-formed ribozymes are unaffected by the addition of the same compound in solution.

The invention further discloses a synergistic combination of adenosine and uridine analogs—specifically, toyocamycin and FUR—that together produce significantly higher levels of gene induction than either compound alone, while simultaneously reducing overall cytotoxicity. This two-component cocktail allows for robust, dose-dependent control of gene expression over a broad dynamic range, making it suitable for both in vitro and in vivo applications. The efficacy of this system has been demonstrated in stable reporter cell lines as well as in live animal models using adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors encoding luciferase under the control of dual N79 ribozymes, where systemic or local administration of the inhibitor cocktail results in rapid, localized, and quantifiable induction of gene expression.

Additionally, the invention encompasses expression vectors and host cell lines engineered to carry transgenes regulated by one or more copies of the N79 ribozyme, positioned in the 5′ or 3′ UTR of the mRNA. These vectors can be used to conditionally express any protein of interest, including therapeutic proteins, reporters, or genome-editing enzymes, in response to small-molecule administration. The invention also includes methods for delivering these nucleic acid constructs to target cells or tissues using viral vectors (e.g., AAV, lentivirus) or non-viral methods (e.g., lipid nanoparticles, electroporation).

Moreover, the invention provides a high-throughput screening platform for identifying new ribozyme inhibitors, based on a stable mammalian cell line expressing a luciferase reporter flanked by N79 ribozymes. This assay enables the direct identification of compounds that function in a cellular context, accounting for bioavailability, metabolism, and off-target effects. Using this platform, the inventors screened over 58,000 compounds and identified 15 distinct inhibitors, none of which were aminoglycosides, thereby challenging prior assumptions about RNA-targeted small molecules.

The invention further extends to pharmaceutical compositions comprising the identified ribozyme inhibitors, alone or in combination, formulated for systemic or local administration. These compositions may be used in research settings to control transgene expression or in therapeutic contexts—for example, to regulate the expression of a therapeutic protein in a patient in response to disease state or to minimize side effects through dose titration. Notably, the discovery that the chemotherapeutic agent 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) functions as a ribozyme inhibitor opens the door to using ribozyme-regulated reporter systems as biosensors for drug distribution and metabolism in vivo.

In summary, the present invention provides a complete, modular, and scalable platform for RNA-based gene regulation, comprising: (1) a highly efficient self-cleaving ribozyme motif (N79); (2) small-molecule inhibitors that act via RNA incorporation; (3) synergistic inhibitor combinations with improved safety profiles; (4) engineered expression vectors and cell lines; (5) delivery methods; (6) a high-throughput screening assay for novel inhibitors; and (7) pharmaceutical formulations for in vivo use. This system represents a significant advance over existing gene regulation technologies by offering rapid, reversible, and titratable control without the need for exogenous protein regulators.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Self-Cleaving RNA Mutant Motifs

The present invention centers on a novel class of engineered self-cleaving RNA motifs derived from the hammerhead ribozyme, specifically optimized for high-efficiency catalysis in mammalian cellular environments. Natural hammerhead ribozymes, originally discovered in plant satellite RNAs, possess a conserved catalytic core flanked by three helical stems (I, II, and III) that facilitate proper folding and cleavage at a specific NUH triplet (where N is any nucleotide and H is A, C, or U). However, when embedded in the context of mammalian mRNA transcripts, wild-type hammerhead ribozymes often exhibit suboptimal activity due to misfolding, steric hindrance from flanking sequences, or incompatibility with the intracellular magnesium concentration and RNA-binding proteins. To overcome these limitations, the inventors developed a series of mutant hammerhead ribozymes through iterative screening and rational design, culminating in the identification of a highly active variant designated N79.

The N79 ribozyme is a modified version of the Schistosoma mansoni-derived Sm1 hammerhead ribozyme, which was selected for its inherent stability and catalytic potential. Through systematic mutagenesis of loop regions and stem sequences, the N79 variant was engineered to enhance intramolecular interactions between loop I and loop II, a critical tertiary contact required for catalytic activation in vivo. Specifically, N79 contains substitutions that increase the A/U content in these loops, promoting dynamic flexibility and facilitating the formation of the active conformation under physiological conditions. Additionally, the cleavage site was optimized to a GUC sequence, which is efficiently recognized by the catalytic core. When tested in a variety of mammalian cell lines—including HEK293, HeLa, and primary neurons—as well as in vivo in mouse models, N79 demonstrated near-complete self-cleavage (>95% efficiency) within minutes of transcription, leading to undetectable levels of full-length mRNA and consequently, no protein expression from downstream coding sequences.

The high activity of N79 is maintained whether the ribozyme is placed in the 5′ untranslated region (5′ UTR), 3′ untranslated region (3′ UTR), or even within an intron, although optimal silencing is achieved when two copies are inserted in tandem in the 3′ UTR. The use of dual ribozymes ensures redundancy and minimizes the chance of escape mutants arising from partial cleavage. Importantly, N79 retains its function across different promoter contexts (e.g., CMV, EF1α, CAG), making it compatible with a wide range of expression vectors. Furthermore, the ribozyme is insensitive to common cellular stressors such as oxidative stress, heat shock, or serum starvation, ensuring reliable baseline silencing under diverse experimental conditions.

A critical feature of N79 is its responsiveness to small-molecule inhibitors that disrupt its catalytic function. Unlike natural ribozymes that may be refractory to modulation, N79’s engineered structure contains specific nucleotide positions that, when substituted with modified bases, lead to graded loss of activity. In particular, adenosine residues at positions 7, 9, and 13 in the catalytic core, as well as uridine residues at positions 4 and 7, are highly sensitive to modification. The incorporation of 7-deaza-adenosine analogs (e.g., tubercidin) or 5-halo-uridines (e.g., FUR) at these sites perturbs essential non-Watson-Crick base pairs, such as the A9-G12 and A13-G8 interactions that stabilize the active conformation. This structural vulnerability is precisely what enables the invention’s unique mode of regulation: inhibition via metabolic incorporation of modified nucleotides.

The N79 ribozyme sequence, as used in the invention, is provided herein as SEQ ID NO:1. Variants of N79 that retain at least 80% self-cleavage efficiency in mammalian cells and are responsive to the described inhibitors are also encompassed by the invention. These include conservative substitutions in the stem regions that maintain base-pairing, as well as loop extensions that do not interfere with the loop-loop interaction. The invention further includes inactive control versions of N79, such as those with point mutations in the catalytic core (e.g., G12A), which are used to validate the specificity of inhibitor action.

### Expression Vectors and Cell Lines

The invention provides a suite of expression vectors designed to place any gene of interest under the conditional control of the N79 ribozyme. These vectors are constructed with a strong constitutive or tissue-specific promoter (e.g., CMV, CAG, SYN1) driving a transcription unit that includes the coding sequence of the transgene, flanked by one or more copies of the N79 ribozyme in the 5′ or 3′ UTR. In a preferred embodiment, two copies of N79 are inserted in the 3′ UTR in a head-to-tail orientation to maximize mRNA destabilization. The vectors may also include selectable markers such as puromycin resistance, neomycin resistance, or fluorescent proteins (e.g., GFP) for easy identification of transduced cells.

Stable cell lines have been generated using these vectors by co-transfecting mammalian cells (e.g., HEK293) with the reporter construct and a selection plasmid, followed by antibiotic selection. One such cell line, designated HEK79, carries a luciferase reporter under dual N79 control and exhibits extremely low basal luminescence (<1% of maximal induction) but can be induced up to 365-fold by toyocamycin. This cell line serves as a validated platform for both mechanistic studies and high-throughput screening. Similar cell lines can be created for any transgene, including therapeutic proteins such as erythropoietin, insulin, or monoclonal antibodies, enabling conditional expression in response to small-molecule administration.

The vectors are compatible with a range of delivery systems, including plasmid transfection, lentiviral transduction, and adeno-associated virus (AAV) packaging. For in vivo applications, AAV serotypes with tropism for specific tissues (e.g., AAV9 for CNS, AAV8 for liver) can be used to deliver the N79-regulated transgene to target organs. Once integrated or episomally maintained, the transgene remains silent until the inhibitor is administered, at which point protein expression is rapidly induced. The reversibility of this system is demonstrated by the return to baseline expression upon withdrawal of the inhibitor, as the newly synthesized mRNA again undergoes self-cleavage.

### Methods of Delivering Nucleic Acids

The nucleic acid constructs of the invention can be delivered to target cells or tissues using a variety of established methods, tailored to the experimental or therapeutic context. In vitro, standard transfection reagents such as Lipofectamine, Fugene, or calcium phosphate can be used to introduce plasmid DNA into cultured cells. For hard-to-transfect cells, electroporation or nucleofection may be employed. Viral delivery offers higher efficiency and stable expression: lentiviral vectors integrate into the host genome, providing long-term regulation, while AAV vectors remain episomal but can persist for months in post-mitotic cells.

For in vivo delivery, systemic administration of AAV vectors is the preferred method, with serotype selection dictating tissue specificity. Local delivery via direct injection (e.g., intramuscular, intracerebral, intraocular) is also feasible for targeted applications. Non-viral methods such as lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) or polymer-based carriers can be used for transient expression, particularly when combined with mRNA constructs that include the N79 ribozyme in the 3′ UTR. These mRNA-LNP formulations are especially relevant for vaccine or protein replacement therapies, where short-term, inducible expression is desired.

The choice of delivery method depends on the duration of expression needed, the target tissue, and safety considerations. All methods are compatible with the N79 regulatory system, as the ribozyme functions independently of the delivery vehicle once the mRNA is transcribed.

### Methods of Identifying Ribozyme Regulators

The invention includes a high-throughput screening (HTS) platform for identifying novel small molecules that inhibit ribozyme self-cleavage in mammalian cells. This platform is based on the HEK79 stable cell line, which expresses luciferase only when N79 activity is suppressed. Cells are seeded in 384-well plates and exposed to compound libraries at a final concentration of 4 µg/mL for 48 hours. Luciferase activity is then measured using a plate reader, and hits are defined as compounds that induce luminescence in HEK79 cells but not in control cells carrying an inactive N79 mutant.

This cell-based assay accounts for critical factors such as cell permeability, metabolic stability, and off-target toxicity, which are absent in in vitro screens. The inventors used this platform to screen 58,076 compounds, including FDA-approved drugs, natural products, and synthetic diversity libraries, leading to the identification of 15 ribozyme inhibitors. The assay can be adapted to screen for inhibitors of other ribozymes or RNA motifs by generating analogous reporter cell lines.

Secondary validation includes dose-response analysis, counter-screening against inactive ribozyme controls, and orthogonal assays such as β-galactosidase reporters. Mechanistic studies involve in vitro transcription with modified nucleotides and RNA structure probing to confirm incorporation-dependent inhibition.

### Methods of Treatment and Administration

The ribozyme-regulated gene expression system of the invention can be used therapeutically to control the production of therapeutic proteins in patients. For example, a patient with a genetic deficiency could receive an AAV vector encoding the missing protein under N79 control. The protein would remain silent until the patient is administered a ribozyme inhibitor, allowing for on-demand dosing and minimizing immune responses or side effects from constitutive expression.

Inhibitors such as toyocamycin and FUR can be administered via oral, intravenous, subcutaneous, or local routes, depending on the target tissue. The synergistic cocktail of toyocamycin and FUR is preferred due to its lower effective doses and reduced toxicity. Dosing regimens can be titrated to achieve the desired level of protein expression, with monitoring via imaging (e.g., bioluminescence) or serum biomarkers.

The system is also applicable to cancer therapy, where a toxic protein (e.g., diphtheria toxin) is expressed only in tumor cells upon local inhibitor injection, sparing healthy tissue. Additionally, the discovery that 5-FU acts as a ribozyme inhibitor enables the use of N79-reporter systems to monitor chemotherapy distribution in real time.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise one or more ribozyme inhibitors, such as toyocamycin, FUR, or their analogs, formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents. The compositions may be in the form of tablets, capsules, solutions, suspensions, or injectables. For systemic delivery, inhibitors are dissolved in saline or buffered solutions with solubilizing agents (e.g., cyclodextrins). For local delivery, hydrogels or slow-release implants can be used.

The synergistic combination of toyocamycin and FUR is formulated at a molar ratio of approximately 1:6 (20 µM:120 µM) to maximize induction while minimizing toxicity. Stability studies ensure that the compounds do not degrade during storage, and sterility is maintained for injectable forms.

These compositions are used to induce gene expression in subjects who have received N79-regulated transgenes, enabling precise control over therapeutic protein levels.

### Exemplification

The invention is exemplified by the generation of the HEK79 cell line, which shows 20-fold induction with morpholino and up to 365-fold with toyocamycin. In vivo, AAV-N79-luc injected into mouse ears showed 13- to 37-fold induction after a single cocktail injection. The synergistic combination achieved 670-fold induction in cells, far exceeding single agents. These examples demonstrate the system’s robustness, scalability, and translational potential.

### Methods:

Standard molecular biology techniques were used, including PCR, cloning, cell culture, transfection, luciferase assays, and in vitro transcription. High-throughput screening was performed in 384-well plates with automated liquid handling. In vivo imaging used IVIS spectrum. Statistical analysis was performed with triplicate repeats.
