# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and molecular therapeutics, specifically to methods and compositions for the treatment of cancer through the inhibition of the chemokine CCL20 or its receptor CCR6. More particularly, the invention provides novel therapeutic strategies that target the CCL20/CCR6 signaling axis, either alone or in combination with inhibition of the CXCR4/CXCL12 pathway, to suppress tumor growth, angiogenesis, invasion, and metastasis in a broad spectrum of human malignancies. The invention further encompasses diagnostic and prognostic applications based on the co-expression patterns of CXCR4, CCL20, and CCR6 in tumor tissues, as well as pharmaceutical compositions comprising neutralizing antibodies against CCL20 or small-molecule inhibitors of CCR6 for use in cancer therapy.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Chemokines constitute a large family of small secreted proteins that orchestrate immune cell trafficking, inflammation, and tissue homeostasis through interactions with specific G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs). Among these, the chemokine receptor CXCR4 and its sole canonical ligand CXCL12 (also known as stromal cell-derived factor-1, SDF-1) have emerged as central players in cancer biology. CXCR4 is aberrantly overexpressed in more than 23 distinct cancer types—including carcinomas of the breast, prostate, pancreas, lung, and colon, as well as hematological malignancies such as leukemia and lymphoma—and its activation by CXCL12 promotes tumor cell proliferation, survival, migration, invasion, and metastasis to distant organs that constitutively express CXCL12, such as bone marrow, lymph nodes, liver, and lungs. Consequently, the CXCR4/CXCL12 axis has become a major focus of anticancer drug development, with several inhibitors currently in clinical trials.

Despite significant advances in understanding CXCR4-mediated oncogenic signaling, the full repertoire of downstream effectors and cross-talk mechanisms remains incompletely characterized. Recent evidence suggests that CXCR4 may regulate additional chemokine pathways that contribute to tumor progression. In particular, the chemokine CCL20 (also known as macrophage inflammatory protein-3α, MIP-3α) and its unique receptor CCR6 have garnered attention due to their expression in various cancers and roles in inflammation-driven tumorigenesis. CCL20 is normally produced at low levels in epithelial tissues such as skin, gut, and liver but is strongly upregulated by pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β and TNF-α. It functions primarily to recruit CCR6-expressing immune cells—including immature dendritic cells, memory T cells, Th17 cells, and regulatory T cells—to sites of inflammation or infection.

In the context of cancer, CCL20 exhibits paradoxical roles. On one hand, its ability to attract antigen-presenting dendritic cells has led some investigators to propose immunostimulatory antitumor effects. On the other hand, accumulating data indicate that tumor-derived CCL20 can foster an immunosuppressive microenvironment by recruiting regulatory T cells and tolerogenic dendritic cells, thereby blunting effective antitumor immunity. Moreover, certain cancer cells themselves express CCR6, raising the possibility of autocrine or paracrine CCL20/CCR6 signaling that directly enhances tumor cell survival, proliferation, and adhesion—processes critical for primary tumor expansion and metastatic seeding.

However, the upstream regulators of CCL20 expression in cancer cells have remained elusive. While inflammatory stimuli are known inducers, the role of oncogenic signaling pathways—particularly those emanating from chemokine receptors like CXCR4—has not been systematically explored. Furthermore, although both CXCR4 and CCL20/CCR6 have been independently implicated in tumor progression, no functional linkage between these two axes has been established. This gap in knowledge has hindered the development of integrated therapeutic approaches that might simultaneously target multiple chemokine pathways driving cancer aggressiveness.

Compounding this challenge is the lack of reliable biomarkers to identify patients whose tumors are driven by CCL20/CCR6 signaling, either alone or in concert with CXCR4. Although immunohistochemical studies have detected CCL20 and CCR6 in human tumor specimens, the correlation between their expression levels, co-expression with CXCR4, and clinical parameters such as Gleason score, tumor stage, or metastatic potential remains poorly defined. Without such stratification tools, patient selection for CCL20/CCR6-targeted therapies would be empirical and potentially ineffective.

Existing therapeutic strategies targeting chemokine pathways in cancer have largely focused on CXCR4 antagonists (e.g., plerixafor/AMD3100) or CXCL12-neutralizing agents. While promising, these approaches may be insufficient in tumors where alternative or complementary chemokine circuits—such as CCL20/CCR6—are active. Indeed, the redundancy and plasticity of the chemokine network often enable tumors to bypass blockade of a single pathway. Therefore, there exists a pressing need for novel agents that disrupt the CCL20/CCR6 axis and for combinatorial regimens that concurrently inhibit both CXCR4 and CCL20 signaling to achieve more durable antitumor responses.

In summary, the prior art fails to disclose: (1) a mechanistic link between CXCR4 activation and CCL20 upregulation in cancer cells; (2) the functional consequences of CCL20 overexpression on tumor growth, vascularization, and invasion in vivo; (3) the efficacy of CCL20 neutralization in suppressing both CXCR4-dependent and CXCR4-independent tumor models; and (4) the co-expression of CXCR4, CCL20, and CCR6 as a potential biomarker signature in human prostate and other cancers. The present invention addresses these unmet needs by providing the first comprehensive evidence of a CXCR4→CCL20→CCR6 signaling cascade that drives oncogenesis and by establishing CCL20/CCR6 as a therapeutically actionable target in multiple cancer types.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses a previously unrecognized molecular mechanism by which the chemokine receptor CXCR4 promotes tumor progression through the transcriptional and post-transcriptional upregulation of the chemokine CCL20, which in turn activates its cognate receptor CCR6 in an autocrine or paracrine manner to enhance cancer cell proliferation, adhesion, survival, angiogenesis, and tissue invasion. This discovery establishes a functional signaling axis linking CXCR4 to CCL20/CCR6 and reveals that CCL20 serves as a critical downstream effector of CXCR4-mediated oncogenesis.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for treating cancer in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an agent that inhibits CCL20 activity or CCR6 signaling. Such agents include, but are not limited to, neutralizing monoclonal antibodies against CCL20, small-molecule antagonists of CCR6, antisense oligonucleotides or siRNA molecules targeting CCL20 or CCR6 mRNA, and soluble decoy receptors that sequester CCL20.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for treating cancer wherein the cancer is characterized by overexpression of CXCR4, CCL20, and/or CCR6. The method comprises administering a combination therapy that includes both a CXCR4 inhibitor and a CCL20/CCR6 pathway inhibitor, thereby achieving synergistic suppression of tumor growth and metastasis.

In a further aspect, the invention provides isolated nucleic acid molecules encoding CCL20 or CCR6, expression vectors comprising said nucleic acids, and host cells transformed therewith, which are useful for generating stable cancer cell lines that overexpress CCL20 or CCR6 for use in preclinical drug screening and mechanistic studies.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a diagnostic method for assessing the prognosis of a cancer patient, comprising detecting the expression levels of CXCR4, CCL20, and CCR6 in a tumor biopsy sample. Co-expression of high levels of CXCR4 and CCL20 is correlated with higher Gleason scores, advanced tumor stage, and increased risk of metastasis, thereby identifying patients who are likely to benefit from CCL20/CCR6-targeted therapy.

In still another aspect, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a CCL20-neutralizing antibody in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, formulated for systemic or local administration. The antibody is preferably a humanized or fully human monoclonal antibody that binds to human CCL20 with high affinity and blocks its interaction with CCR6.

The invention further provides kits for the detection of CCL20 and CCR6 in biological samples, comprising specific antibodies, primers, or probes for use in immunoassays, PCR, or in situ hybridization, along with instructions for correlating expression levels with disease severity or therapeutic response.

Importantly, the invention demonstrates that neutralization of CCL20 significantly inhibits tumor growth not only in models where CCL20 is overexpressed exogenously but also in models where CCL20 is endogenously upregulated by CXCR4 signaling. This dual efficacy underscores the broad applicability of CCL20/CCR6 inhibition across diverse cancer contexts, including both CXCR4-dependent and CXCR4-independent tumors.

Moreover, the invention reveals that CCL20 overexpression enhances tumor vascularization through mechanisms that increase both blood vessel density and functionality, as evidenced by functional MRI measurements of oxygen delivery efficiency (ΔSo2). This pro-angiogenic effect contributes to sustained tumor growth beyond the point at which control tumors develop necrotic cores due to hypoxia, highlighting CCL20 as a key mediator of tumor adaptation to metabolic stress.

Collectively, the present invention redefines the role of CCL20 from a mere inflammatory chemokine to a central oncogenic driver downstream of CXCR4 and provides a robust scientific foundation for the clinical development of CCL20/CCR6-targeted therapeutics in oncology.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the unexpected discovery that activation of the chemokine receptor CXCR4 leads to significant upregulation of the chemokine CCL20 at both the mRNA and protein levels in a variety of human cancer cell lines, including prostate, colon, and leukemic cells. This upregulation occurs through a signaling cascade that involves the JAK/STAT pathway and is only partially dependent on classical G protein-mediated signaling, as evidenced by incomplete inhibition with pertussis toxin (PTX). The resulting increase in CCL20 secretion then acts in an autocrine fashion on CCR6-expressing tumor cells to promote proliferation, adhesion to extracellular matrix components, survival, and in vivo tumor growth, vascularization, and invasion. Critically, these effects are abrogated by neutralizing antibodies against CCL20, demonstrating the functional necessity of CCL20 in mediating these oncogenic phenotypes.

The invention encompasses multiple embodiments directed to therapeutic, diagnostic, and research applications arising from this discovery. Therapeutically, the core innovation lies in the identification of CCL20 and CCR6 as druggable targets whose inhibition can suppress tumor progression. Agents suitable for this purpose include neutralizing antibodies that bind to CCL20 and prevent its interaction with CCR6; small-molecule antagonists that block the CCR6 receptor binding pocket; nucleic acid-based therapeutics such as siRNA, shRNA, or antisense oligonucleotides that reduce CCL20 or CCR6 expression; and soluble forms of CCR6 that act as decoy receptors to sequester CCL20 in the tumor microenvironment. These agents may be used as monotherapy or in combination with existing CXCR4 inhibitors (e.g., AMD3100, BL-8040) to achieve enhanced antitumor efficacy by simultaneously disrupting both the upstream CXCR4 signal and its downstream CCL20 effector.

The invention further provides methods for selecting patients most likely to respond to CCL20/CCR6-targeted therapy. This is accomplished by analyzing tumor biopsy samples for the expression of CXCR4, CCL20, and CCR6 using standard techniques such as immunohistochemistry (IHC), quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR), or flow cytometry. Patients whose tumors exhibit high co-expression of CXCR4 and CCL20, or high expression of CCR6 in the presence of detectable CCL20, are identified as optimal candidates for treatment. In prostate cancer, such co-expression correlates with higher Gleason scores and more advanced pathological stages, providing both a predictive and prognostic biomarker.

Pharmaceutical compositions according to the invention comprise a CCL20-neutralizing agent in a pharmaceutically acceptable excipient, buffer, or delivery system. For antibody-based therapeutics, preferred formulations include those that enhance stability, half-life, and tumor penetration, such as PEGylated antibodies, Fc-engineered variants with extended serum persistence, or antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) that deliver cytotoxic payloads specifically to CCL20-producing cells. Routes of administration may include intravenous, subcutaneous, intratumoral, or localized delivery depending on the tumor type and location.

The invention also includes transgenic and xenograft animal models that recapitulate human CCL20-driven tumorigenesis. These models are generated by implanting immunodeficient mice (e.g., SCID/bg or nude mice) with human cancer cell lines engineered to overexpress CCL20 (e.g., PC3-CCL20 clones) or CXCR4 (e.g., PC3-CXCR4.5). Such models are invaluable for preclinical evaluation of CCL20/CCR6 inhibitors, allowing assessment of effects on tumor volume, metastasis, angiogenesis (via histology or functional MRI), and overall survival. Importantly, the invention demonstrates that anti-CCL20 antibody treatment not only reduces primary tumor growth but also inhibits aberrant vascularization and promotes extensive necrosis, indicating disruption of critical tumor-stroma interactions.

At the molecular level, the invention elucidates the signaling pathways connecting CXCR4 to CCL20. While CXCR4 canonically signals through Gi proteins, the partial resistance of CCL20 upregulation to PTX suggests involvement of alternative pathways, notably JAK2/STAT. Pharmacological inhibition of JAK2 with AG-490 completely abolishes CCL20 secretion, implicating this kinase as a key mediator. This finding opens avenues for combination therapy with JAK inhibitors (e.g., ruxolitinib) in tumors reliant on this axis. Additionally, the observation that high concentrations of CCL20 can paradoxically inhibit proliferation—likely due to CCR6 desensitization—suggests that therapeutic dosing must be carefully calibrated to avoid receptor downregulation.

The scope of the invention extends beyond prostate cancer to any malignancy where CCL20 and CCR6 are co-expressed. Examples include colorectal carcinoma (HT-29 cells), acute myeloid leukemia (HL-60, NB4, primary blasts), pancreatic cancer, breast adenocarcinoma, glioblastoma, and melanoma. In each case, the presence of functional CCR6 on tumor cells renders them responsive to CCL20-mediated stimulation, making them susceptible to CCL20/CCR6 blockade. The invention thus provides a platform technology applicable across oncology indications.

Finally, the invention includes kits and assays for monitoring therapeutic response. These may measure circulating CCL20 levels in serum as a pharmacodynamic marker or assess changes in tumor CCR6 expression post-treatment to confirm target engagement. Such tools facilitate dose optimization and early identification of non-responders, enhancing the precision of CCL20/CCR6-directed therapy.

## EXAMPLES

### Experimental Procedures

All cell culture experiments were conducted under sterile conditions using standard aseptic techniques. Human prostate cancer cell lines PC3 (CRL-1435), LNCaP (CRL-10995), 22Rv1 (CRL-2505), and DU145 (HTB-81); acute promyelocytic leukemia cell lines NB4 and HL-60 (CCL-240); and colon carcinoma cell line HT-29 (HTB-38) were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) or other verified sources and maintained in recommended media supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 2 mM L-glutamine, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator. Stable transfectants overexpressing CXCR4 (PC3-CXCR4.5) or CCL20 (PC3-CCL20 clones 7, 8, 10, 30) were generated by transfection with pcDNA3-based expression vectors followed by selection in G418 (800 µg/mL) and single-cell cloning by limiting dilution. Transgene expression was confirmed by flow cytometry for CXCR4 and ELISA for CCL20.

For RNA interference, PC3-CXCR4.5 cells were transfected with Dicer-substrate siRNA duplexes targeting CXCR4 (IDT, Coralville, IA) or non-specific control siRNA using Oligofectamine (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Knockdown efficiency was assessed 48 hours post-transfection by qRT-PCR and flow cytometry.

Total RNA extraction was performed using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). cDNA synthesis was carried out with 2.5 µg total RNA using random hexamers and reverse transcriptase. Semi-quantitative PCR and qRT-PCR were conducted using gene-specific primers for CCL20, CCR6, CXCR4, CXCL12, and β-actin (housekeeping control). qRT-PCR reactions were run in triplicate on an ABI Prism 7700 Sequence Detector System (Applied Biosystems) using TaqMan assays.

Cell proliferation was assessed by seeding 2×10⁴ cells/well in 24-well plates in medium containing 0.1% FBS with or without recombinant human CCL20 (PeproTech) at specified concentrations. After 6 days, cells were harvested, stained with propidium iodide (PI), and viable cell counts determined by flow cytometry. In parallel, BrdU incorporation assays were performed by adding 10 µM BrdU during the final 16 hours of culture, followed by fixation, DNA denaturation, and staining with anti-BrdU antibody for FACS analysis.

Cell adhesion assays were conducted by allowing 1×10⁵ cells to adhere to 24-well plates coated with 10 µg/mL fibronectin or collagen type I for 30 minutes at 37°C in serum-free RPMI with 0.1% BSA. Non-adherent cells were washed away, adherent cells were detached with EDTA-containing buffer, and counted by flow cytometry.

CCL20 protein levels in cell supernatants and lysates were quantified by ELISA (R&D Systems) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For intracellular detection, cells were lysed in buffer containing 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.6), 150 mM NaCl, 5 mM EDTA (pH 8.0), 0.5% NP-40, and protease inhibitors, followed by centrifugation at 14,000 g for 20 minutes at 4°C.

Flow cytometric analysis of CXCR4 and CCR6 surface expression was performed using phycoerythrin (PE)-conjugated anti-human CXCR4 (clone 12G5, R&D Systems) and fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated anti-human CCR6 (clone 53103.11, R&D Systems). Data were acquired on a FACSCalibur instrument (Becton Dickinson) and analyzed with CellQuest software.

For in vivo xenograft studies, 5×10⁶ cancer cells were injected subcutaneously into the flanks of 6–8-week-old female SCID/bg or nude mice (Harlan Laboratories) housed under specific pathogen-free conditions. Tumor dimensions were measured twice weekly with calipers, and volume calculated as (width² × length)/2. For neutralization experiments, mice received subcutaneous injections of 20 µg anti-human CCL20 monoclonal antibody (MAB360, R&D Systems) or isotype control IgG1 three times per week starting 24 hours post-tumor cell inoculation. All procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Hebrew University.

Tumor vascular functionality was assessed by functional MRI using a 7T Bruker Biospec system. Mice inhaled alternating cycles of medical air (21% O₂ + 5% CO₂) and carbogen (95% O₂ + 5% CO₂) while gradient-echo images were acquired. ΔSo2 maps were generated by calculating the relative change in R2* relaxation rate between gas challenges, reflecting oxygen delivery efficiency.

Immunohistochemistry was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tumor sections or commercial tissue microarrays (SuperBioChips). After deparaffinization and antigen retrieval in EDTA buffer, sections were blocked with CAS reagent and incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against CCL20 (20 µg/mL, polyclonal, PeproTech), CCR6 (10 µg/mL, monoclonal, R&D Systems), or CXCR4 (10 µg/mL, clone 12G5, R&D Systems). Detection was achieved using biotinylated secondary antibodies, streptavidin-HRP, and AEC chromogen, with hematoxylin counterstain. Staining intensity was scored blindly by two independent pathologists as negative, weak, or strong.

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software. Data are presented as mean ± SD or SE. Comparisons between groups were made by two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

### Example 1

This example demonstrates that CXCR4 signaling upregulates CCL20 expression in prostate cancer cells. PC3 prostate cancer cells, which express low endogenous levels of CXCR4, were transduced with a CXCR4-encoding vector to generate the PC3-CXCR4.5 clone exhibiting high, stable CXCR4 surface expression. Real-time RT-PCR revealed a 41-fold increase in basal CCL20 mRNA levels in PC3-CXCR4.5 cells compared to parental PC3 cells. Stimulation with CXCL12 (5–1000 ng/mL) induced a dose-dependent further increase in CCL20 mRNA in both cell lines, confirming CXCR4-mediated regulation. ELISA of conditioned media showed corresponding increases in secreted CCL20 protein: basal levels rose from 8.6 ± 4.5 pg/mL in PC3 to 433 ± 30 pg/mL in PC3-CXCR4.5 cells. Time-course experiments demonstrated that CCL20 synthesis preceded secretion, with intracellular accumulation detectable at 6 hours post-CXCL12 stimulation and significant secretion evident by 24 hours, increasing further at 48 hours. To confirm CXCR4 dependence, PC3-CXCR4.5 cells were treated with neutralizing anti-CXCL12 antibodies or pertussis toxin (PTX), which partially inhibited CCL20 secretion. Complete dependence was proven by siRNA-mediated knockdown of CXCR4, which reduced surface CXCR4 by >90% and decreased both basal and CXCL12-induced CCL20 secretion by 88% and 77%, respectively (p < 0.005). Mechanistically, the JAK2 inhibitor AG-490 (1 µM) abolished CCL20 production, implicating JAK/STAT signaling, whereas the MEK inhibitor PD98059 had minimal effect. These results establish that CXCR4 activation, via a PTX-insensitive JAK2-dependent pathway, potently induces CCL20 expression and secretion in prostate cancer cells.

### Example 2

This example shows that CCL20 promotes proliferation and adhesion of CCR6-expressing tumor cells in vitro. Flow cytometry and RT-PCR confirmed that among four prostate cancer lines (PC3, LNCaP, 22Rv1, DU145), only PC3 expressed functional CCR6 on the cell surface. Consistent with this, treatment of PC3 cells with CCL20 (5–250 ng/mL) increased viable cell numbers by 1.35-fold (5 ng/mL, p < 0.04) and 1.75-fold (50 ng/mL, p < 0.0004) after 6 days, with no effect at 250 ng/mL, suggesting receptor desensitization at high doses. BrdU incorporation assays corroborated these findings, showing 1.76-fold increased DNA synthesis at 5–50 ng/mL CCL20 (p < 0.0002). In contrast, CCL20 had no effect on CCR6-negative lines (LNCaP, 22Rv1, DU145). Adhesion assays revealed that CCL20 (50–500 ng/mL) significantly enhanced PC3 cell binding to collagen I (2.2-fold at 50 ng/mL, p < 0.001) but only modestly to fibronectin (1.45-fold at 500 ng/mL, p < 0.01). PTX pretreatment abolished CCL20-induced adhesion, confirming G protein dependence. To model autocrine signaling, PC3 cells were stably transfected with CCL20, yielding clones secreting 100–5000 pg/mL. Clones with moderate CCL20 secretion (100–320 pg/mL) exhibited 1.7–2.5-fold increased proliferation, while high-secreting clones showed no enhancement, again indicating dose-dependent effects. All CCL20-overexpressing clones displayed significantly increased adhesion to collagen I (2.1–3.8-fold, p < 0.04–0.004) but not fibronectin. Similar CCL20 responsiveness was observed in CCR6-positive leukemia (HL-60) and colon cancer (HT-29) cells, confirming the generality of this mechanism across cancer types.

### Example 3

This example demonstrates that CCL20 overexpression enhances tumor growth, invasion, and vascularization in vivo. SCID/bg mice were subcutaneously injected with mock-transfected PC3 cells or CCL20-overexpressing clones PC3-CCL20.10 (100 pg/mL CCL20) and PC3-CCL20.30 (320 pg/mL). By day 41, tumors from PC3-CCL20.30 cells were significantly larger than controls (p < 0.01), with PC3-CCL20.10 tumors showing intermediate growth. Histopathological analysis revealed that CCL20-expressing tumors were highly invasive, penetrating adjacent muscle and dermal tissues, and exhibited massive aberrant vascularization and central necrosis—features absent in encapsulated, non-invasive control tumors. Functional MRI using ΔSo2 mapping confirmed superior vascular functionality in PC3-CCL20.30 tumors (mean ΔSo2 = 0.35 ± 0.05) versus controls (0.12 ± 0.03, p < 0.001), even when control tumors were allowed to grow to comparable sizes over 71 days. This indicates that CCL20 drives early, efficient neovascularization that supports sustained tumor expansion beyond the diffusion limit of oxygen, thereby preventing the hypoxia-induced growth arrest seen in control tumors. These findings establish CCL20 as a potent promoter of tumor angiogenesis and aggressiveness in vivo.

### Example 4

This example proves that neutralizing CCL20 inhibits tumor growth in both CXCR4-dependent and -independent models. In vitro, monoclonal anti-CCL20 antibody (MAB360) completely blocked CCL20-induced adhesion of PC3-CCL20.30 cells to collagen I. In vivo, SCID/bg mice bearing PC3-CCL20.30 tumors treated with anti-CCL20 antibody (20 µg, 3×/week) showed dramatically reduced tumor incidence (5/10 vs. 9/10 in controls) and size, with four treated tumors remaining microscopic (<0.2 cm²) and non-progressive. Histology revealed extensive necrosis and absent aberrant vasculature in treated tumors. Similarly, in mice bearing CXCR4-overexpressing PC3-CXCR4.5 tumors, anti-CCL20 treatment delayed tumor onset (60% vs. 100% palpable by day 28) and suppressed growth (p < 0.05). Efficacy extended to colon cancer: nude mice with HT-29 xenografts treated daily with anti-CCL20 showed significant inhibition of tumor growth and invasion into surrounding tissues (p < 0.01). These results confirm that CCL20 is essential for tumor progression regardless of whether its expression is driven by exogenous overexpression or endogenous CXCR4 signaling, validating CCL20 neutralization as a broadly applicable anticancer strategy.
