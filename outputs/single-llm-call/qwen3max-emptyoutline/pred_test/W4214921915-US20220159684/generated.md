# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to wireless communication systems operating in the millimeter wave (mmWave) frequency band, and more particularly to methods and apparatus for power-efficient beamforming in user equipment (UE) devices such as fifth-generation (5G) mobile terminals. Specifically, the invention concerns techniques for designing sub-chain beam codebooks that enable dynamic adjustment of active antenna elements during uplink transmission while preserving downlink-uplink (DL-UL) beam correspondence, thereby reducing power consumption and thermal load without compromising communication performance or requiring frequent re-execution of beam management procedures. The invention is particularly applicable to mmWave 5G New Radio (NR) systems employing antenna arrays with dual-polarization patch elements, where high data rates are achieved through directional beamforming but at the cost of significant power dissipation and heat generation in compact mobile form factors.

## BACKGROUND

Wireless communication systems have evolved significantly over successive generations to meet increasing demands for higher data rates, lower latency, and greater spectral efficiency. The advent of fifth-generation (5G) cellular networks has introduced operation in the millimeter wave (mmWave) frequency bands—typically defined as frequencies between 24 GHz and 100 GHz—as a key enabler for multi-gigabit-per-second throughput. However, mmWave propagation is characterized by high path loss, severe atmospheric attenuation, and limited penetration through obstacles, necessitating the use of highly directional antenna arrays to achieve sufficient link budgets. Consequently, modern 5G user equipment (UE), such as smartphones and tablets, are commonly equipped with multiple antenna arrays, often arranged along the edges of the device housing, with each array comprising several dual-polarization patch antennas. Typical configurations include 2×1, 4×1, or 2×2 arrays per side, resulting in a total of up to 14 or more radiating elements when considering both left and right edges and vertical (V) and horizontal (H) polarizations.

To overcome the inherent path loss at mmWave frequencies, these antenna arrays employ analog beamforming, wherein phase shifters adjust the relative phases of signals fed to individual antenna elements to steer a high-gain beam toward the base station (BS). The beamforming weights are typically constrained by hardware limitations: the magnitude of each weight is binary (either zero or one, indicating whether an antenna element is activated or deactivated), and the phase is quantized to a finite number of discrete values determined by the resolution of the phase shifters (e.g., 5-bit phase shifters yield 32 possible phase states). This architecture enables the formation of narrow, high-gain beams that can deliver gigabits-per-second data rates, but it also introduces significant challenges related to power consumption and thermal management.

One of the most pressing issues in mmWave 5G UEs is excessive power draw and consequent heating during prolonged high-throughput operation. Unlike sub-6 GHz bands, where power amplifiers (PAs) operate relatively efficiently, mmWave PAs exhibit lower efficiency and generate substantial heat due to the high frequencies involved and the need for high output power to compensate for path loss. Each active PA consumes a fixed “base” power regardless of its output power level, meaning that even if transmission power is scaled down uniformly across all antennas, the total power consumption remains high because all PAs remain powered on. This thermal buildup can trigger thermal throttling mechanisms in the device, which may force a fallback to lower-frequency sub-6 GHz bands—a process known as LTE fallback. While this mitigates overheating, it comes at a steep cost: data rates plummet from gigabits per second to a few hundred megabits per second or less, and the frequent switching between mmWave and sub-6 GHz modes introduces additional latency, signaling overhead, and potential service disruption.

An alternative approach to thermal management is to dynamically deactivate a subset of antenna elements during uplink (UL) transmission, thereby reducing the number of active PAs and lowering both radiated power and base power consumption. Such beams, which utilize only a portion of the full antenna array, are referred to herein as “sub-chain beams,” in contrast to “full-chain beams” that activate the entire array. Importantly, because downlink (DL) reception typically consumes far less power than UL transmission and often has higher data rate requirements, it is advantageous to maintain full-array activation for DL while using sub-chain beams for UL. This asymmetric operation scheme offers a practical compromise between performance and power efficiency.

However, this strategy introduces a critical problem: it risks violating the fundamental assumption of DL-UL beam correspondence that underpins the 5G beam management (BM) framework. Beam correspondence posits that the optimal beam direction for DL reception is also optimal for UL transmission, owing to the reciprocity of the wireless channel in time-division duplex (TDD) systems. This principle allows the network to infer the best UL beam from DL measurements, avoiding the need for separate and resource-intensive UL beam sweeping procedures. When sub-chain beams are employed for UL while full-chain beams are used for DL, the radiation patterns of the corresponding beams may diverge significantly, breaking beam correspondence. As a result, the UE may be forced to perform additional UL-specific beam training whenever it switches between full-chain and sub-chain modes, negating much of the power-saving benefit and introducing undesirable latency.

Prior art has explored antenna deactivation primarily for the purpose of generating wider beams in hierarchical codebook designs, where reduced array apertures naturally produce broader main lobes useful for initial access or coverage extension. However, these approaches do not address the specific challenge of maintaining beam correspondence under dynamic power-saving operations. Moreover, existing codebook design methodologies—such as those based purely on spherical coverage maximization—optimize for omnidirectional performance without regard to alignment with full-chain beam patterns, rendering them unsuitable for seamless mode transitions.

There remains a need, therefore, for a novel beamforming strategy that enables power-efficient sub-chain operation in mmWave UEs while preserving DL-UL beam correspondence to the greatest extent possible. Such a solution must account for practical hardware constraints—including binary antenna activation, quantized phase control, and polarization balancing—and must be implementable within the existing 5G NR beam management framework without requiring protocol modifications or additional signaling overhead.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods, systems, and apparatus for designing sub-chain beam codebooks in millimeter wave (mmWave) user equipment (UE) that enable dynamic reduction of active antenna elements during uplink transmission to conserve power and mitigate thermal load, while substantially preserving downlink-uplink (DL-UL) beam correspondence to avoid unnecessary beam management overhead. The invention achieves this by introducing three distinct codebook design methodologies—Similarity Score Maximization (Sim-Max), Spherical Coverage Maximization (SC-Max), and Beam Correspondence Spherical Coverage Maximization (BC-SC-Max)—each optimized for different operational priorities, with BC-SC-Max offering a superior balance between beam correspondence preservation and spherical coverage performance.

In accordance with the invention, a UE is equipped with one or more antenna arrays, each comprising multiple dual-polarization patch antennas arranged along the device edges. The beamforming vector applied to the array is constrained such that each antenna element is either fully activated (weight magnitude = 1) or deactivated (weight magnitude = 0), and the phase of each active weight is selected from a discrete set determined by the phase shifter resolution (e.g., 2^b possible phases for b-bit shifters). Additionally, the invention may impose a constraint that the number of activated antennas in the vertical and horizontal polarizations be equal, though the specific indices of activated elements may differ between polarizations.

The core innovation lies in the structured design of sub-chain beam codebooks that are explicitly aligned with a pre-existing full-chain beam codebook. In the Sim-Max approach, each sub-chain beam is optimized to maximize the similarity of its radiation pattern to the corresponding full-chain beam across the entire unit sphere, using a normalized inner product metric evaluated over a dense set of angular sampling points. This ensures that the shape and orientation of the main lobe closely match, thereby preserving beam index correspondence.

In the SC-Max approach, sub-chain codebooks are designed independently to maximize average beam gain over the entire sphere, without regard to alignment with full-chain beams. While this yields excellent standalone coverage, it results in poor beam correspondence, necessitating full beam sweeping upon mode transitions.

The preferred embodiment employs the BC-SC-Max method, which partitions the unit sphere into disjoint coverage regions associated with each full-chain beam and then optimizes each sub-chain beam to maximize gain exclusively within its corresponding full-chain coverage region. This targeted optimization ensures that, for any given channel direction, if a particular full-chain beam is optimal for DL reception, the corresponding sub-chain beam is highly likely to be optimal for UL transmission, thus maintaining beam correspondence without sacrificing spherical coverage.

The invention further provides a quantitative metric—the best beam matching rate—to evaluate the degree of beam correspondence between any two codebooks. This metric is defined as the fraction of angular directions on the unit sphere where the index of the optimal beam is identical between the two codebooks, under the assumption of uniform channel direction distribution due to random UE orientation. Simulation results demonstrate that BC-SC-Max achieves matching rates exceeding 90% when switching among full-chain, 4-antenna, and 3-antenna sub-chain modes, and maintains over 60% correspondence even when transitioning to a 1-antenna configuration. In contrast, SC-Max exhibits matching rates below 25%, and Sim-Max performs poorly in extreme deactivation scenarios.

Importantly, the invention enables seamless transitions between full-chain and sub-chain operation without triggering additional beam management procedures, thereby reducing signaling overhead, latency, and power consumption associated with beam sweeping. By allowing the UE to dynamically scale its antenna usage based on thermal and power conditions while retaining beam correspondence, the invention enhances mmWave utilization, extends battery life, and improves user experience in 5G and beyond networks.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The following detailed description sets forth numerous specific details to provide a thorough understanding of the present invention. However, it will be apparent to those skilled in the art that the invention may be practiced without these specific details. In other instances, well-known structures, materials, and processes have not been described in detail so as not to obscure the invention.

The invention is implemented in a user equipment (UE) device configured for operation in millimeter wave (mmWave) bands of a 5G New Radio (NR) communication system. The UE includes at least one antenna array, and preferably two arrays positioned along the left and right edges of the device housing, as is common in contemporary smartphone designs. Each array comprises L dual-polarization patch antennas, yielding a total of 2L antenna elements per array when considering both vertical (V) and horizontal (H) polarizations. For example, with L = 5, each edge hosts five V-polarized and five H-polarized elements, labeled as 1V, 2V, …, LV and 1H, 2H, …, LH, respectively. The total number of radiating elements across both arrays is thus 4L, though the invention may be applied to single-array configurations as well.

Beamforming in the UE is performed using analog beamforming circuitry that includes phase shifters and power amplifiers (PAs) coupled to each antenna element. The beamforming vector **w** ∈ ℂ^(2L) (for a single array) is applied to modulate the signal prior to radiation. Due to hardware constraints, the magnitude of each component w_ℓ (1 ≤ ℓ ≤ 2L) is restricted to binary values: |w_ℓ| ∈ {0, 1}, where 0 indicates the antenna is deactivated (and its PA is turned off) and 1 indicates activation. The phase of each non-zero w_ℓ is constrained to a discrete set determined by the phase shifter resolution; specifically, if b-bit phase shifters are employed, then w_ℓ^(2^b) = 1 for all activated elements, meaning the phase can take one of 2^b equally spaced values in [0, 2π).

A key feature of the invention is the ability to operate in multiple beamforming modes: a full-chain mode, wherein all 2L antenna elements per array are activated, and one or more sub-chain modes, wherein only a subset of elements is activated. The number of activated antennas per polarization is denoted L_A, and the invention may enforce the constraint that the same number of V- and H-polarized elements are activated, i.e., ||[**w**]_(1:L)||₀ = ||[**w**]_(L+1:2L)||₀ = L_A, though the specific indices of activated elements may differ between polarizations. This constraint simplifies hardware implementation and maintains polarization balance, but the invention is extensible to other activation patterns.

The radiation pattern of a beam formed by weight vector **w** in direction (θ, φ) is computed as B(θ, φ) = ||**M**(θ, φ)**w**||², where **M**(θ, φ) ∈ ℂ^(2×2L) is a matrix containing the electric field (E-field) responses of each antenna element in that direction. The E-field data incorporates the effects of the terminal housing, mutual coupling, and other real-world impairments, and is obtained either through electromagnetic simulation or anechoic chamber measurement. Because **M**(θ, φ) is typically rank-2 (due to dual polarization), the resulting pattern captures both co-polar and cross-polar components.

The invention addresses the problem of designing sub-chain beam codebooks that maintain DL-UL beam correspondence when the UE switches from full-chain to sub-chain operation for uplink transmission. Beam correspondence is preserved if, for a given channel direction, the index of the optimal beam in the full-chain codebook (used for DL) matches the index of the optimal beam in the sub-chain codebook (used for UL). To enable this, the invention proposes three codebook design methodologies.

First, the Similarity Score Maximization (Sim-Max) method designs each sub-chain beam to closely resemble its full-chain counterpart. Given a full-chain codebook {**w**₁^full, …, **w**_K^full} of size K, the Sim-Max method generates a sub-chain codebook {**w**₁^sub, …, **w**_K^sub} by solving, for each k, the optimization problem:

maximize ∑ₙ₌₁^Nₚ G_k(θₙ, φₙ) B_k(θₙ, φₙ)

subject to |w_ℓ| ∈ {0, 1}, w_ℓ^(2^b) = 1 if |w_ℓ| = 1,

||[**w**]_(1:L)||₀ = ||[**w**]_(L+1:2L)||₀ = L_A,

where G_k(θ, φ) = ||**M**(θ, φ)**w**_k^full||² is the full-chain beam pattern, B_k(θ, φ) = ||**M**(θ, φ)**w**||² is the sub-chain pattern, and {(θₙ, φₙ)}ₙ₌₁^Nₚ is a set of N_p uniformly distributed points on the unit sphere (e.g., generated via Fibonacci lattice). The objective function is normalized by ∑ₙ G_k²(θₙ, φₙ) to yield a similarity score between 0 and 1. Due to the non-convex, combinatorial nature of the constraints, the problem is solved by exhaustive enumeration over all possible antenna activation patterns satisfying the L₀ norm constraints—there are (L choose L_A)² such patterns—and for each pattern, an iterative phase optimization algorithm (e.g., cyclic coordinate descent) is used to find a locally optimal phase configuration. Multiple random initializations ensure a high-quality local optimum is selected.

Second, the Spherical Coverage Maximization (SC-Max) method ignores beam correspondence and instead designs the sub-chain codebook to maximize average beam gain over the entire sphere. This is formulated as:

maximize (1/N_p) ∑ₖ₌₁^K ∑ₙ₌₁^Nₚ B_k(θₙ, φₙ)

subject to the same hardware constraints as above.

This problem is solved using a K-means clustering approach: directions are assigned to the beam providing highest gain, and beams are re-optimized to serve their assigned directions. Again, antenna activation is enumerated exhaustively, and phase optimization is performed iteratively per activation pattern. While SC-Max yields excellent standalone coverage, it results in poor alignment with the full-chain codebook, as evidenced by low best beam matching rates.

Third, and most advantageously, the Beam Correspondence Spherical Coverage Maximization (BC-SC-Max) method strikes an optimal balance. It begins by partitioning the unit sphere into K disjoint coverage regions {D₁, …, D_K}, where D_k = {(θ, φ) : G_k(θ, φ) ≥ G_j(θ, φ) ∀ j ≠ k} is the set of directions for which full-chain beam k is optimal. Then, for each k, the sub-chain beam **w**_k^sub is designed by solving:

maximize ∑_{(θₙ,φₙ) ∈ D_k} B_k(θₙ, φₙ)

subject to the same constraints.

This focuses optimization on the angular region where the corresponding full-chain beam is dominant, ensuring that the sub-chain beam is tailored to perform well precisely where it needs to for beam correspondence. The same iterative phase optimization is used, but summation is restricted to D_k and unweighted (unlike Sim-Max, which weights by G_k). This approach yields sub-chain beams that not only align well with their full-chain counterparts but also achieve spherical coverage nearly as good as SC-Max.

The performance of these methods is evaluated using two key metrics. The first is the best beam matching rate, defined for two codebooks C₁ (with L₁ antennas) and C₂ (with L₂ antennas) as:

η(C₁, C₂) = (1/N_p) ∑ₙ₌₁^Nₚ 1{argmax_i B_i^(C₁)(θₙ, φₙ) = argmax_j B_j^(C₂)(θₙ, φₙ)},

which measures the fraction of directions where the optimal beam index is identical. The second is the composite beam gain cumulative distribution function (CDF), which shows the probability that the maximum beam gain across the codebook exceeds a given threshold at a random direction.

Simulation results based on a realistic 5G phone model with two 1×5 arrays (L = 5) and 5-bit phase shifters confirm the superiority of BC-SC-Max. When switching among 5-Ant (full-chain), 4-Ant, and 3-Ant sub-chain modes, BC-SC-Max maintains η > 90%, enabling seamless mode transitions without beam sweeping. Even for 1-Ant operation, η remains above 60%. In contrast, SC-Max exhibits η ≤ 25% across all transitions, and Sim-Max degrades sharply for extreme deactivation (e.g., 1-Ant). Moreover, BC-SC-Max achieves spherical coverage comparable to SC-Max and significantly better than Sim-Max, especially in the low-percentile (cell-edge) regime.

The invention thus provides a practical, standards-compliant solution for power-efficient mmWave operation in 5G UEs. By embedding beam correspondence directly into the sub-chain codebook design, it eliminates the need for additional beam management procedures during thermal throttling events, thereby reducing latency, conserving battery, and enhancing user experience—all while maintaining high data rates through continued mmWave utilization.
