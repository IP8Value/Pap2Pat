# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global prevalence of obesity and its associated metabolic complications has reached epidemic proportions, with more than 73% of the adult population in the United States classified as overweight or obese. This alarming trend has been paralleled by a marked increase in the incidence of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), a complex metabolic disorder characterized by chronic hyperglycemia resulting from impaired insulin secretion, increased hepatic glucose production, and diminished insulin-mediated glucose uptake in peripheral tissues—a condition known as insulin resistance. Epidemiological evidence firmly establishes obesity as the leading risk factor for T2DM, with over 85% of individuals diagnosed with T2DM also exhibiting obesity. At the molecular level, the pathogenesis of insulin resistance in obesity is closely linked to the limited capacity of white adipose tissue to safely store excess energy in the form of triglycerides. When mature white adipocytes reach their lipid storage capacity, they can no longer expand efficiently, leading to a phenomenon known as “lipid overflow,” wherein excess lipids are ectopically deposited in non-adipose tissues such as the liver, skeletal muscle, pancreas, and kidneys. This ectopic lipid accumulation induces lipotoxicity, mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and chronic low-grade inflammation, all of which contribute to systemic insulin resistance and the development of metabolic disease.

Adipose tissue expansion in response to chronic positive energy balance occurs through two primary mechanisms: hypertrophy (an increase in the size of individual adipocytes) and hyperplasia (an increase in the number of adipocytes via differentiation of preadipocytes or proliferation of mature adipocytes). While both processes are physiologically relevant, their metabolic consequences differ significantly. In metabolically healthy obese individuals, adipose tissue expands predominantly through hyperplasia, resulting in a greater number of smaller, insulin-sensitive adipocytes that maintain metabolic flexibility and reduce the risk of ectopic fat deposition. In contrast, the majority of obese individuals exhibit adipose tissue expansion primarily through hypertrophy, leading to enlarged, dysfunctional adipocytes that are strongly associated with insulin resistance, impaired glucose uptake, dysregulated adipokine secretion, and chronic inflammation. Critically, adipocyte size is inversely correlated with insulin sensitivity—smaller adipocytes are highly responsive to insulin and support efficient glucose disposal and lipid storage, whereas hypertrophic adipocytes exhibit blunted insulin signaling and contribute to systemic metabolic dysregulation. Therefore, therapeutic strategies that promote adipocyte hyperplasia—thereby redistributing lipid content among a greater number of smaller, metabolically competent adipocytes—represent a promising alternative to conventional weight-loss approaches for the treatment of insulin resistance and related metabolic disorders.

Under physiological conditions, adipocyte hyperplasia is orchestrated by the recruitment and differentiation of adipose progenitor cells into mature adipocytes, a process tightly regulated by a cascade of transcriptional and cell cycle events. This includes growth arrest of proliferating preadipocytes, mitotic clonal expansion, and terminal differentiation driven by master regulators such as peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ) and CCAAT/enhancer-binding protein alpha (C/EBPα). However, emerging evidence suggests that fully differentiated, lipid-laden adipocytes may retain a degree of plasticity, including the ability to re-enter the cell cycle, undergo de-differentiation, proliferate, and subsequently re-differentiate. This challenges the long-standing dogma that mature adipocytes are terminally differentiated and post-mitotic. If validated, this cellular plasticity could be harnessed therapeutically to induce adipocyte hyperplasia directly from the existing adipocyte pool, bypassing the need for progenitor cell recruitment and offering a novel mechanism to improve adipose tissue health and systemic metabolism.

Central to this concept is the identification of molecular regulators that control the transition between adipocyte quiescence and proliferation. One such candidate is monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1), encoded by the SLC16A1 gene, a member of the solute carrier 16 (SLC16) family responsible for the proton-coupled bidirectional transport of monocarboxylates—including lactate, pyruvate, and ketone bodies—across the plasma membrane. MCT1 is highly expressed in glycolytic tissues and is a well-established transcriptional target of the oncogene c-Myc, which drives cell proliferation and metabolic reprogramming in cancer. In adipocytes, which are highly glycolytic even under normoxic conditions, MCT1 facilitates the export of lactate produced during glycolysis, thereby maintaining intracellular redox homeostasis by regulating the NAD⁺/NADH ratio. Elevated circulating lactate levels are observed in obesity and correlate strongly with insulin resistance, suggesting a link between lactate metabolism, adipocyte function, and systemic metabolic health. Moreover, MCT1 expression increases during adipocyte differentiation, implicating it in the metabolic maturation of adipocytes. However, the functional role of MCT1 in regulating adipocyte cell cycle status, plasticity, and metabolic phenotype remains poorly understood.

Recent studies have begun to uncover the broader metabolic functions of lactate beyond its role as a waste product. Lactate serves as a signaling molecule, an energy substrate, and a regulator of redox state. In adipocytes, MCT1-mediated lactate flux has been shown to influence mitochondrial biogenesis and cellular plasticity in response to redox stress. Given that redox signaling is a critical determinant of cell cycle progression and growth arrest, it is plausible that modulating MCT1 activity—and thereby altering intracellular lactate concentration and redox balance—could impact the proliferative capacity of mature adipocytes. Despite these insights, no prior art has demonstrated that pharmacological inhibition of MCT1 can induce mature adipocytes to re-enter the cell cycle, proliferate, and enhance their metabolic functionality. Furthermore, while MCT1 inhibitors such as AZD3965 have been developed and are currently in clinical trials for oncology indications, their potential application in the treatment of metabolic diseases such as obesity and T2DM has not been explored or disclosed.

Thus, there exists a significant unmet need for therapeutic agents that can promote adipocyte hyperplasia to improve metabolic health in obese individuals. The present invention addresses this need by identifying MCT1 as a novel and druggable target whose inhibition in mature adipocytes triggers a cascade of molecular and phenotypic changes—including reduced lipid content, enhanced lipolysis, re-entry into the cell cycle, increased proliferation, improved insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, and augmented lipid storage capacity—collectively indicative of a shift toward a hyperplastic, metabolically healthier adipocyte phenotype. This discovery provides a foundation for the development of MCT1 inhibitors as a new class of therapeutics for the treatment of insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, and other obesity-related metabolic disorders.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for inducing hyperplasia in mature adipocytes by inhibiting the activity of monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1), thereby promoting the re-entry of fully differentiated adipocytes into the cell cycle and enhancing their metabolic functionality. This method comprises administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of an MCT1 inhibitor, wherein the MCT1 inhibitor is selected from the group consisting of small molecule inhibitors, monoclonal antibodies, antisense oligonucleotides, short interfering RNAs (siRNAs), short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs), and CRISPR-based gene-editing constructs targeting the SLC16A1 gene. In a preferred embodiment, the MCT1 inhibitor is a small molecule compound, such as AZD3965 or a structurally related analog, which selectively and potently inhibits MCT1-mediated monocarboxylate transport.

The invention further encompasses a pharmaceutical composition comprising an MCT1 inhibitor in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent, formulated for oral, intravenous, subcutaneous, or other suitable routes of administration. The composition is particularly useful for the treatment of metabolic disorders characterized by adipocyte hypertrophy and insulin resistance, including but not limited to obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, metabolic syndrome, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for improving insulin sensitivity in a subject suffering from a metabolic disorder, comprising administering an MCT1 inhibitor to said subject, thereby increasing insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in adipose tissue. This effect is mediated through the induction of adipocyte proliferation and the generation of smaller, more insulin-responsive adipocytes, as demonstrated by enhanced glucose uptake in differentiated 3T3-L1 adipocytes following treatment with AZD3965. Notably, this improvement in insulin sensitivity is dependent on cyclin-dependent kinase 1 (CDK1) activity, as co-administration of a CDK1 inhibitor abolishes the beneficial effects of MCT1 inhibition on glucose uptake.

Additionally, the invention includes a method for reducing ectopic lipid deposition by promoting safe lipid storage in adipose tissue through MCT1 inhibition. By inducing adipocyte hyperplasia, the invention enhances the lipid buffering capacity of adipose tissue, thereby preventing the spillover of lipids into liver, muscle, and other organs where they cause lipotoxicity and metabolic dysfunction. This is evidenced by the increased triglyceride storage capacity observed in adipocytes pretreated with an MCT1 inhibitor and subsequently exposed to insulin, indicating that the newly generated or reprogrammed adipocytes are functionally competent for lipid handling.

The invention also provides a method for modulating adipocyte metabolism by inhibiting MCT1, which results in a rapid increase in intracellular lactate, followed by adaptive changes in pyruvate metabolism and redox state (as reflected by a decreased NAD⁺/NADH ratio). These metabolic perturbations trigger a transcriptional reprogramming characterized by the upregulation of cell cycle-promoting genes—including CDK1, PLK1, TOP2A, and Cyclin B—and the downregulation of cell cycle inhibitors, as revealed by RNA sequencing and pathway analysis. This transcriptional signature supports the phenotypic observation that mature adipocytes treated with MCT1 inhibitors re-enter the cell cycle, as confirmed by increased Ki67 expression, elevated CDK1 activity (via dephosphorylation at Tyr15), and a higher cell count in proliferation assays.

Crucially, the proliferative effect of MCT1 inhibition is specific to mature adipocytes and does not occur in preadipocytes, underscoring the unique plasticity of differentiated adipocytes and the selectivity of the therapeutic approach. Moreover, MCT1-inhibited adipocytes retain their differentiated state, as evidenced by sustained expression of key adipogenic markers such as PPARγ and FABP4, and unchanged adipokine secretion profiles, thereby ruling out de-differentiation as a mechanism for the observed lipid loss and proliferation.

In summary, the present invention discloses a novel and unexpected role for MCT1 as a gatekeeper of adipocyte quiescence and a suppressor of hyperplasia. By inhibiting MCT1, the invention unlocks the proliferative potential of mature adipocytes, leading to a metabolically favorable remodeling of adipose tissue that improves systemic insulin sensitivity and reduces the risk of obesity-related complications. This represents a paradigm shift in the treatment of metabolic disease, moving beyond caloric restriction and weight loss to directly target the cellular and molecular determinants of adipose tissue health.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed to them below unless otherwise indicated by the context.

“Adipocyte” refers to a specialized cell of adipose tissue that stores energy in the form of triglycerides within lipid droplets. Adipocytes include both white and brown adipocytes, although the present invention primarily concerns white adipocytes, which are the predominant cell type in white adipose tissue and are central to energy storage and endocrine function. The term “mature adipocyte” denotes a fully differentiated adipocyte that has accumulated substantial lipid droplets and expresses characteristic markers such as PPARγ, FABP4, and adiponectin. In contrast, “preadipocyte” refers to an undifferentiated or partially differentiated mesenchymal stem cell committed to the adipocyte lineage but not yet exhibiting the morphological or functional characteristics of a mature fat cell.

“Hyperplasia” as used herein means an increase in the number of cells in a tissue or organ, specifically referring to an increase in the number of adipocytes in adipose tissue. This is distinct from “hypertrophy,” which denotes an increase in the size of individual adipocytes without a change in cell number. Hyperplasia may occur through the differentiation of preadipocytes or, as demonstrated by the present invention, through the proliferation of mature adipocytes that have re-entered the cell cycle.

“MCT1” or “monocarboxylate transporter 1” refers to the protein encoded by the SLC16A1 gene in humans (or its orthologs in other species, such as Slc16a1 in mice), which functions as a proton-linked symporter facilitating the bidirectional transport of monocarboxylates such as lactate, pyruvate, and ketone bodies across the plasma membrane. MCT1 is a member of the SLC16 solute carrier family and is distinguished by its high affinity for lactate and its widespread expression in metabolically active tissues.

“MCT1 inhibitor” means any agent that reduces or abolishes the transport activity of MCT1, either by direct binding to the transporter, by downregulating its expression, or by interfering with its proper localization or function. MCT1 inhibitors include but are not limited to small molecule compounds (e.g., AZD3965, AR-C155858, SR13800), monoclonal antibodies that block the extracellular domain of MCT1, nucleic acid-based therapeutics such as siRNA, shRNA, antisense oligonucleotides, or CRISPR-Cas9 systems designed to knock down or knockout SLC16A1 expression, and dominant-negative mutants of MCT1.

“Insulin sensitivity” refers to the responsiveness of cells or tissues to the hormone insulin, particularly with respect to glucose uptake and metabolism. Enhanced insulin sensitivity means that a given concentration of insulin produces a greater biological effect, such as increased glucose transport into adipocytes or muscle cells. Insulin resistance, the opposite condition, is a hallmark of type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome.

“Re-entry into the cell cycle” describes the process by which a quiescent or terminally differentiated cell resumes progression through the phases of the cell cycle (G1, S, G2, M), ultimately leading to cell division. In the context of this invention, it refers to the ability of mature adipocytes—which are traditionally considered post-mitotic—to reinitiate DNA synthesis and mitosis following MCT1 inhibition.

“Metabolic disorder” encompasses a broad category of diseases characterized by abnormalities in energy metabolism, including but not limited to obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, metabolic syndrome, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), dyslipidemia, and polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). These conditions are often interrelated and share common pathophysiological features such as insulin resistance, chronic inflammation, and adipose tissue dysfunction.

“Therapeutically effective amount” means a quantity of an MCT1 inhibitor that is sufficient to produce a desired therapeutic effect in a subject, such as improved insulin sensitivity, reduced adipocyte size, increased adipocyte number, or enhanced glucose uptake, without causing unacceptable toxicity. The precise dosage will depend on factors including the specific inhibitor used, the route of administration, the subject’s age, weight, and overall health, and the severity of the metabolic disorder being treated.

“Pharmaceutically acceptable carrier” refers to any substance that is compatible with the active ingredient and suitable for use in humans or animals, including but not limited to water, saline, buffers, oils, emulsifiers, preservatives, and stabilizers. The carrier ensures the stability, bioavailability, and safe delivery of the MCT1 inhibitor to the target tissue.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Inhibition of MCT1 Impairs Lipid Accumulation During Adipocyte Differentiation**

Murine 3T3-L1 preadipocytes were cultured and differentiated into mature adipocytes using a standard protocol involving induction with a cocktail of insulin, dexamethasone, and 3-isobutyl-1-methylxanthine (IBMX). To assess the role of MCT1 in adipogenesis, cells were differentiated in the presence or absence of 1 µM AZD3965, a potent and selective small-molecule inhibitor of MCT1. Additionally, stable 3T3-L1 cell lines with MCT1 knockdown (3T3-L1-shMCT1) or a scrambled control (3T3-L1-shScramble) were generated using lentiviral transduction of short hairpin RNA. Successful knockdown of MCT1 at both mRNA and protein levels was confirmed by reverse transcription–quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) and immunoblotting. Differentiation was monitored by Oil Red O staining, which specifically labels neutral lipids. By day six of differentiation, control cells (untreated and shScramble) exhibited extensive lipid droplet formation, whereas cells treated with AZD3965 or harboring shMCT1 showed significantly reduced lipid accumulation. Quantification of Oil Red O staining confirmed a statistically significant decrease in lipid content in MCT1-inhibited cells compared to controls, demonstrating that MCT1 activity is required for efficient lipid storage during adipocyte differentiation.

**Example 2: MCT1 Inhibition Suppresses Adipogenic Gene Expression in Preadipocytes**

To investigate the molecular basis for impaired lipid accumulation, the expression of key adipogenic transcription factors and markers was analyzed in differentiating preadipocytes. Immunoblotting revealed that the protein levels of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ), the master regulator of adipogenesis, as well as its downstream targets fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4/aP2) and hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL), were significantly attenuated in cells differentiated in the presence of AZD3965 or in 3T3-L1-shMCT1 cells. This suppression of the adipogenic program occurred despite the presence of differentiation-inducing agents, indicating that MCT1-mediated lactate transport is necessary for the full activation of the transcriptional cascade that drives adipocyte maturation. These findings suggest that MCT1 inhibition may interfere with early events in adipogenesis, potentially by altering the intracellular metabolic environment required for PPARγ activation.

**Example 3: Mature Adipocytes Maintain Differentiated State Despite Reduced Lipid Content Following MCT1 Inhibition**

To determine whether MCT1 inhibition affects mature adipocytes, fully differentiated 3T3-L1 cells were treated with 1 µM AZD3965 for up to 72 hours. A progressive decrease in lipid content was observed, as visualized by microscopy and quantified by Oil Red O staining. Cell viability assays confirmed that this reduction was not due to cytotoxicity. Further analysis revealed a modest but significant decrease in intracellular NADP⁺/NADPH levels, suggesting a partial impairment in lipogenesis, and a marked increase in intracellular glycerol, indicating enhanced lipolysis. Despite these metabolic changes, the adipocytes remained differentiated: PPARγ protein expression was unchanged, FABP4 expression was transiently elevated, and adipokine secretion profiles (assessed by antibody array) showed no significant differences compared to untreated cells. These results demonstrate that MCT1 inhibition reduces lipid storage in mature adipocytes primarily through increased lipolysis without inducing de-differentiation.

**Example 4: Transcriptomic Analysis Reveals Cell Cycle Activation Following MCT1 Inhibition**

RNA sequencing was performed on mature adipocytes treated with 1 µM AZD3965 for 24 hours to identify global transcriptional changes. A total of 277 genes were differentially expressed (p < 0.05), with pathway analysis using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) revealing significant enrichment in functions related to cell cycle progression, mitosis, cellular proliferation, and cytokinesis. Key upregulated genes included cyclin-dependent kinase 1 (CDK1), polo-like kinase 1 (PLK1), and DNA topoisomerase 2-alpha (TOP2A), all of which are essential for mitotic entry and chromosome segregation. Conversely, expression of the cell cycle inhibitor DUSP1 was downregulated. These findings provided strong evidence that MCT1 inhibition reprograms mature adipocytes toward a proliferative state.

**Example 5: MCT1 Inhibition Induces Re-Entry into the Cell Cycle and Proliferation of Mature Adipocytes**

Validation of RNA-seq data by RT-qPCR confirmed significant upregulation of CDK1, PLK1, TOP2A, and Cyclin B in AZD3965-treated adipocytes. Immunoblotting showed that while total CDK1 protein levels remained unchanged, phosphorylation at Tyr15—a modification that inactivates CDK1—was significantly reduced, indicating increased CDK1 activity. Proliferation assays using a fluorescent DNA dye demonstrated a significant increase in cell number after 72 hours of AZD3965 treatment at concentrations ≥1 µM, an effect not observed in preadipocytes, confirming the specificity for mature adipocytes. Confocal microscopy revealed enhanced nuclear expression of Ki67, a canonical marker of cell proliferation, in lipid-laden adipocytes treated with AZD3965, as visualized by co-staining with the lipid dye BODIPY. Quantification of Ki67 signal intensity normalized to nuclei count confirmed a statistically significant increase in proliferative activity, providing direct evidence that mature adipocytes can re-enter the cell cycle upon MCT1 inhibition.

**Example 6: MCT1 Inhibition Enhances Insulin-Stimulated Glucose Uptake in a CDK1-Dependent Manner**

Glucose uptake assays were performed to assess the metabolic consequences of MCT1 inhibition. While basal (non-insulin-stimulated) glucose uptake was unaffected, insulin-stimulated glucose uptake was significantly increased after 48 and 72 hours of AZD3965 treatment. This enhancement was abolished by co-treatment with RO-3306, a specific CDK1 inhibitor, demonstrating that the improved insulin sensitivity is dependent on CDK1-mediated cell cycle progression. In contrast, 3T3-L1-shMCT1 cells, which lack MCT1 constitutively, showed attenuated insulin responsiveness, suggesting that acute pharmacological inhibition—not chronic genetic ablation—is required for the beneficial metabolic effects. Finally, pretreatment with AZD3965 enhanced the triglyceride storage capacity of adipocytes upon subsequent insulin exposure, supporting the hypothesis that MCT1 inhibition promotes functional hyperplasia.

Collectively, these examples demonstrate that MCT1 inhibition in mature adipocytes induces a coordinated program of lipolysis, cell cycle re-entry, proliferation, and enhanced insulin sensitivity, culminating in a metabolically healthier adipocyte phenotype. This provides a robust foundation for the therapeutic use of MCT1 inhibitors in the treatment of obesity-related metabolic disorders.
