# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The progressive biological process of aging is a complex and multifactorial phenomenon that affects all organ systems, leading to a gradual decline in physiological function, increased vulnerability to disease, and ultimately, death. In developed nations, the demographic shift toward an increasingly aged population has intensified the urgency for interventions capable of mitigating or reversing age-related deterioration. Among the most critical consequences of aging is immunosenescence—the functional decline of the immune system—which significantly contributes to morbidity and mortality in older adults. Central to this decline is thymic involution, a well-documented process wherein the thymus, the primary lymphoid organ responsible for T-cell maturation, undergoes progressive fatty atrophy beginning as early as the first year of life and continuing throughout adulthood. By middle age, the thymus is largely replaced by adipose tissue, resulting in a dramatic reduction in the production of naïve T cells and a consequent narrowing of the T-cell receptor (TCR) repertoire. This collapse in immune diversity, which typically becomes pronounced after the age of 63 in humans, is strongly associated with heightened susceptibility to infections, cancer, autoimmune disorders, chronic inflammation, and cardiovascular disease, as well as increased all-cause mortality.

Historically, thymic involution has been considered an irreversible hallmark of aging. However, emerging evidence from animal models suggests that certain pharmacological interventions may possess thymotrophic properties—capable of stimulating thymic regeneration and restoring immune function. Notably, growth hormone (GH) has demonstrated such effects in aged rodents and canines, where administration of recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH) has been shown to reverse thymic fatty infiltration, restore thymic epithelial architecture, and enhance T-cell output. In human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)-positive patients, who often experience accelerated thymic atrophy, rhGH therapy has similarly been associated with increased thymic density on imaging and improved immune reconstitution. Nevertheless, these findings have not been systematically evaluated in healthy, aging individuals without underlying immunosuppressive conditions, leaving a significant gap in knowledge regarding the potential for thymic regeneration in normal human aging.

A major barrier to the therapeutic application of rhGH in aging populations is its well-known diabetogenic effect, primarily mediated through the induction of insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) and subsequent hyperinsulinemia. Chronically elevated insulin levels are not only a risk factor for type 2 diabetes but may also counteract the beneficial effects of GH on thymic regeneration and immune function. To address this limitation, researchers have explored the co-administration of agents that mitigate insulin resistance and improve metabolic health. Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), a naturally occurring adrenal androgen precursor that declines with age, has been shown to exert anti-aging effects, including improvements in insulin sensitivity, body composition, and immune parameters. Similarly, metformin, a widely used antidiabetic drug, functions as a calorie restriction mimetic and has been proposed as a candidate geroprotector due to its ability to activate AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), reduce hepatic glucose production, and improve insulin sensitivity. Neither DHEA nor metformin has been independently reported to possess thymotrophic activity, but their combined use with rhGH offers a rational strategy to harness the regenerative potential of GH while minimizing its adverse metabolic consequences.

Concurrently, advances in epigenetics have provided powerful new tools to quantify biological aging. Epigenetic clocks—algorithms based on DNA methylation patterns at specific CpG sites across the genome—now offer highly accurate estimates of biological age that often diverge from chronological age. These clocks, including those developed by Horvath, Hannum, Levine (PhenoAge), and Lu (GrimAge), have been validated as predictors of age-related morbidity, mortality, and overall healthspan. Critically, GrimAge has been shown to be particularly robust in predicting life expectancy, incorporating not only methylation patterns but also plasma protein levels associated with aging. The ability to measure changes in epigenetic age provides a practical and objective biomarker for evaluating the efficacy of anti-aging interventions within feasible clinical trial durations, circumventing the need for decades-long studies of hard clinical endpoints.

Despite these advances, no prior clinical trial has successfully demonstrated the reversal of systemic aging in humans using a combination of readily available, repurposed pharmaceuticals. While individual components of such regimens have been studied in isolation, the synergistic potential of a multi-drug approach targeting both immune restoration and metabolic optimization remains unexplored. Furthermore, the relationship between thymic regeneration, immune cell subset dynamics, systemic inflammation, and epigenetic aging has not been comprehensively investigated in a controlled human setting. The absence of such data has left the scientific and medical communities without a clear pathway to translate promising preclinical findings into clinically viable anti-aging therapies.

This patent application addresses these critical gaps by disclosing a novel method for reversing multiple biomarkers of human aging, including epigenetic age, through the coordinated administration of rhGH, DHEA, and metformin. The invention is grounded in the unexpected discovery that this specific combination not only induces significant thymic regeneration and improves immune parameters in healthy middle-aged men but also leads to a statistically significant and sustained regression of epigenetic age across multiple independent clocks. Importantly, the treatment regimen is designed to minimize known risks, such as prostate cancer progression and hyperinsulinemia, while simultaneously enhancing markers of metabolic and renal health. The disclosed method represents a paradigm shift in the field of geroscience, offering a practical, pharmacologically-based strategy to ameliorate fundamental aspects of human aging and potentially extend healthspan.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for reversing biological aging in a human subject, comprising the concurrent administration of recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH), dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and metformin. This method is specifically designed to induce thymic regeneration, restore immune function, and reduce epigenetic age, thereby ameliorating multiple hallmarks of aging. The invention is predicated on the unexpected discovery that the synergistic combination of these three agents, when administered according to a defined dosing protocol, produces a profound and measurable regression of biological age that is not achievable with any of the components alone.

In one embodiment, the method is applied to a human subject who is between the ages of 50 and 70 years, a demographic that is post-pubertal and exhibits signs of early immunosenescence but is otherwise generally healthy. The rhGH is administered subcutaneously at a dose ranging from approximately 0.01 mg/kg to 0.03 mg/kg of body weight, typically three to four times per week. The DHEA is administered orally at a daily dose of about 25 mg to 100 mg, and metformin is administered orally at a daily dose of about 500 mg to 2000 mg, divided into two doses. The dosing regimen is initiated with a lead-in period where rhGH is administered alone for one week, followed by the addition of DHEA in the second week, and finally the inclusion of metformin in the third week. From the fourth week onward, the doses of all three agents are individually titrated based on periodic measurements of serum IGF-1, insulin, and DHEA sulfate (DHEAS) levels to maximize IGF-1 within a safe range while maintaining insulin and glucose levels within normal limits.

The primary therapeutic outcomes of the invention include the regeneration of functional thymic tissue, as evidenced by a significant increase in the thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF) measured via quantitative MRI, and the restoration of a more youthful immune profile. This immune restoration is characterized by an increase in the lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), a decrease in the percentage of CD38-positive monocytes, an increase in naïve CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, and an increase in CD4+ recent thymic emigrants (RTEs). Additionally, the method results in a significant reduction in epigenetic age, as measured by multiple independent epigenetic clocks, including the Horvath clock, Hannum clock, PhenoAge clock, and GrimAge clock. The regression of epigenetic age is observed to accelerate over time, with a mean rate of aging reversal increasing from approximately -1.56 years per year during the first nine months of treatment to approximately -6.48 years per year during the final three months of a twelve-month treatment period. Critically, a substantial portion of this epigenetic age reversal, particularly as measured by the GrimAge clock, persists for at least six months after discontinuation of the treatment regimen.

The invention further encompasses a method for improving metabolic and inflammatory health in an aging human subject. The co-administration of DHEA and metformin with rhGH effectively mitigates the diabetogenic side effects of GH, resulting in stable or improved insulin and glucose levels. The treatment also leads to a significant reduction in systemic inflammation, as indicated by a decrease in C-reactive protein (CRP) levels, and an improvement in renal function, as evidenced by an increase in the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Safety monitoring reveals no exacerbation of prostate cancer risk, as indicated by favorable changes in prostate-specific antigen (PSA) and percent free PSA levels.

In another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising rhGH, DHEA, and metformin in a single dosage form or in a kit containing separate dosage forms for each agent, along with instructions for their sequential and titrated administration according to the disclosed protocol. The invention also includes a method for identifying a human subject who is a candidate for this anti-aging therapy, based on criteria such as age, baseline thymic fat content, and epigenetic age. The disclosed method represents a comprehensive and practical approach to targeting the root causes of aging, with the potential to delay or prevent a wide spectrum of age-related diseases and extend the period of healthy life.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a method for reversing biological aging in a human subject through the synergistic administration of recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH), dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and metformin. This method is founded upon the novel and non-obvious finding that the specific combination and dosing protocol of these three agents produces a cascade of regenerative and restorative effects that collectively result in a measurable regression of biological age. The detailed description below outlines the components of the invention, the rationale for their selection, the precise administration protocol, the mechanisms of action, the expected physiological outcomes, and the methods for monitoring efficacy and safety.

The first active agent in the inventive combination is recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH). rhGH is a 191-amino acid, single-chain polypeptide hormone that is identical to the endogenous growth hormone produced by the pituitary gland. It is commercially available under various brand names, such as Omnitrope, Genotropin, and Humatrope, and is approved for a number of indications, including growth hormone deficiency in children and adults, Turner syndrome, and muscle wasting in HIV/AIDS. In the context of the present invention, rhGH is employed for its potent thymotrophic and hematopoietic properties. Growth hormone signaling, primarily through its downstream mediator IGF-1, is known to stimulate the proliferation of thymic epithelial cells, which are essential for the proper development and selection of T cells. In aged animal models, GH administration has been shown to reverse thymic atrophy, restore thymic architecture, and increase the output of naïve T cells. The inventors have now demonstrated that this effect is translatable to healthy, aging human males, where rhGH, when used in the inventive combination, leads to a highly significant increase in the thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF), a quantitative MRI-based measure of functional thymic mass. The rhGH is administered subcutaneously, typically at bedtime to mimic the natural circadian rhythm of GH secretion. The initial dose is set at approximately 0.015 mg/kg of body weight, administered three to four times per week. This dose is carefully chosen to be low enough to minimize side effects while being sufficient to initiate a biological response.

The second active agent is dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), a C19 steroid hormone produced primarily by the adrenal glands. DHEA serves as a precursor to both androgens and estrogens and is the most abundant circulating steroid in humans. Its levels peak in the mid-20s and then decline steadily with age, a phenomenon sometimes referred to as "adrenopause." DHEA has been associated with a wide array of anti-aging effects, including improvements in bone mineral density, skin health, cognitive function, mood, and immune response. In the present invention, DHEA is included primarily for its ability to counteract the insulin-desensitizing effects of rhGH. GH is known to induce insulin resistance, which can lead to compensatory hyperinsulinemia—a state that is not only undesirable from a metabolic health perspective but may also interfere with the thymotrophic actions of GH. DHEA has been shown to improve insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism in both men and women. In the inventive protocol, DHEA is administered orally in a micronized form to enhance bioavailability. The starting dose is 50 mg per day, which may be adjusted upward or downward based on serum DHEA sulfate (DHEAS) levels and clinical response. It is important to note that DHEA itself does not appear to have direct thymotrophic activity; its role is supportive, enabling the safe and effective use of rhGH.

The third active agent is metformin, a biguanide derivative that is the first-line oral medication for the treatment of type 2 diabetes. Metformin works primarily by decreasing hepatic glucose production and improving insulin sensitivity in peripheral tissues. Beyond its antidiabetic effects, metformin has garnered significant interest in the field of geroscience due to its ability to mimic some of the beneficial effects of calorie restriction, a well-established intervention for extending lifespan in model organisms. Metformin activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), a key cellular energy sensor that regulates metabolism, mitochondrial biogenesis, and autophagy. In the present invention, metformin serves a dual purpose: it provides a second line of defense against GH-induced hyperinsulinemia and may contribute directly to the anti-aging effects of the regimen through its pleiotropic actions on cellular metabolism and stress resistance. Metformin is administered orally, starting at a dose of 500 mg per day, which is gradually increased to a maintenance dose of 1000-2000 mg per day, divided into two doses to minimize gastrointestinal side effects. The dose is titrated based on fasting insulin and glucose levels.

The administration protocol is a critical feature of the invention. The treatment is initiated with a one-week lead-in period during which only rhGH is administered. This allows for the assessment of the subject's initial insulin response to GH. In the second week, DHEA is added to the regimen to evaluate its ability to suppress the insulin response. In the third week, metformin is introduced. From the fourth week onward, the doses of all three agents are individually titrated for each subject. Blood is drawn at regular intervals (e.g., at months 2, 3, 4, 6, and 9) to measure serum levels of IGF-1, insulin, glucose, and DHEAS. The goal of titration is to maintain IGF-1 levels in the upper half of the normal range for young adults (e.g., 200-300 ng/mL), while keeping fasting insulin below 10 µIU/mL and fasting glucose within the normal range (70-100 mg/dL). This personalized approach ensures that each subject receives the optimal dose to achieve the desired biological effects while minimizing the risk of adverse events.

The physiological outcomes of the inventive method are multifaceted and interconnected. The primary target is the thymus, and successful treatment is marked by a significant increase in TFFF, as quantified by a specialized MRI protocol that calculates the fat fraction in the thymic region. This regeneration of thymic tissue is accompanied by a rejuvenation of the peripheral immune system. There is a notable increase in the lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), a biomarker that is inversely correlated with mortality from cancer, cardiovascular disease, and other age-related conditions. This increase in LMR is driven primarily by a decrease in the absolute number and percentage of monocytes, particularly those expressing the ectoenzyme CD38. CD38 is a major NADase whose expression increases with age and is a primary driver of age-related NAD+ depletion, a key contributor to cellular senescence and mitochondrial dysfunction. The reduction in CD38+ monocytes suggests a potential mechanism for the observed epigenetic age reversal, as increased NAD+ availability can enhance the activity of sirtuins, a family of NAD+-dependent deacetylases that play a central role in regulating longevity pathways.

Concurrently, there is an increase in the proportion of naïve T cells (both CD4+ and CD8+) and in CD4+ recent thymic emigrants (RTEs), which are newly produced T cells that have recently exited the thymus. This indicates a restoration of thymic output and a broadening of the T-cell receptor (TCR) repertoire, which is essential for mounting effective immune responses against novel pathogens and for immune surveillance against cancer. The treatment also leads to a decrease in PD-1-positive CD8+ T cells, a population of "exhausted" T cells that are dysfunctional and unable to proliferate effectively. Reducing this exhausted pool is a significant immunological benefit.

Perhaps the most groundbreaking outcome of the invention is the regression of epigenetic age. Using state-of-the-art DNA methylation profiling on peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), the inventors have shown that the treatment leads to a significant decrease in epigenetic age as measured by four independent and well-validated epigenetic clocks. The mean reduction in the difference between epigenetic age and chronological age (EA-A) is approximately 2.5 years after 12 months of treatment. Remarkably, the rate of this reversal accelerates over time, suggesting a positive feedback loop or the engagement of deeper regenerative processes with prolonged treatment. Most importantly, the gains in epigenetic age, especially those measured by the GrimAge clock, which is the strongest predictor of mortality, are largely maintained for at least six months after the treatment is stopped.

Safety is rigorously monitored throughout the protocol. Prostate health is assessed via serial measurements of total PSA, percent free PSA, and the PSA/free PSA ratio, all of which show favorable trends, alleviating concerns about GH's potential mitogenic effects. Systemic inflammation is monitored via CRP, which shows a significant decline. Renal function, a concern with metformin use, is monitored via eGFR, which actually improves. Common side effects of rhGH, such as arthralgia, fluid retention, and carpal tunnel syndrome, are generally mild and manageable. The co-administration of DHEA and metformin is crucial for preventing the hyperinsulinemia that would otherwise be a major contraindication to long-term GH use in an aging population.

In summary, the detailed description of the invention reveals a carefully orchestrated, multi-targeted pharmacological intervention that safely and effectively reverses key biomarkers of human aging. By combining a thymotrophic agent (rhGH) with two metabolic modulators (DHEA and metformin), the method achieves a synergistic effect that is greater than the sum of its parts, offering a realistic and scalable approach to extending human healthspan.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A clinical trial was conducted to evaluate the safety and efficacy of a novel combination therapy for reversing biological aging in healthy, middle-aged men. The study, designated as the Thymus Regeneration, Immunorestoration, and Insulin Mitigation (TRIIM) trial, enrolled ten male volunteers aged between 51 and 65 years. All participants were deemed to be in good general health following a rigorous screening process that included a detailed medical history, physical examination, and comprehensive blood work. The primary objectives of the trial were to assess the ability of the treatment to regenerate thymic tissue, restore a more youthful immune profile, and reduce epigenetic age, while secondary objectives focused on monitoring safety, particularly with respect to prostate health, metabolic parameters, and systemic inflammation. The treatment regimen consisted of the concurrent administration of recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH), dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and metformin, following a specific titration protocol. The trial was conducted under an Investigational New Drug (IND) application with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and was approved by an Institutional Review Board. All participants provided written informed consent.

### Example 2

The treatment protocol was initiated with a one-week lead-in period during which participants self-administered rhGH (Omnitrope, Sandoz) subcutaneously at a dose of 0.015 mg/kg of body weight, three to four times per week, at bedtime. During the second week, oral DHEA (50 mg daily) was added to the regimen. In the third week, oral metformin (500 mg daily) was introduced. From the fourth week onward, the doses of all three agents were individually titrated for each participant based on their biochemical response. Blood samples were collected at baseline and at months 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, and 12 to measure serum levels of insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), insulin, glucose, and DHEA sulfate (DHEAS). The titration goal was to maintain IGF-1 levels in the upper half of the normal young adult range (approximately 200-300 ng/mL) while keeping fasting insulin levels below 10 µIU/mL and fasting glucose within the normal range (70-100 mg/dL). Participants were also instructed to take daily supplements of 3,000 IU of vitamin D3 and 50 mg of elemental zinc. Compliance was monitored through medication diaries and by verifying the expected pharmacodynamic responses in blood biomarkers.

### Example 3

Thymic regeneration was assessed using a quantitative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) protocol. All scans were performed on a 3T GE Premier MRI scanner at a single imaging center to ensure consistency. A computational methodology, previously validated for bone marrow fat quantification, was adapted to calculate the thymic fat fraction (TFF) in three replicate central thymic regions at each time point. The thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF) was then derived as 100% minus TFF. Baseline MRI scans revealed varying degrees of thymic fatty infiltration among the participants, which is typical for this age group. Following the initiation of treatment, a clear and consistent trend of decreasing thymic fat and increasing TFFF was observed. By the 12-month endpoint, the mean TFFF had increased significantly, with a p-value of 8.57 × 10−17 based on a linear mixed-model analysis. Seven out of the nine evaluable participants showed a statistically significant improvement in TFFF. The degree of improvement was found to be dependent on the baseline TFFF, with participants who had higher baseline fat content showing a more robust regenerative response.

### Example 4

To investigate whether the observed thymic changes were part of a generalized fat loss or a specific regenerative process, the same MRI methodology was applied to the sternal bone marrow to calculate the bone marrow fat-free fraction (BMFFF). While a small but statistically significant increase in BMFFF was observed (p < .001), the magnitude of the change was far less than that seen in the thymus. This differential effect strongly supports the conclusion that the treatment induced a specific reversal of thymic involution rather than a nonspecific reduction in body fat. The pattern of response in the bone marrow was also distinct from that in the thymus, further indicating a targeted biological effect on the thymic microenvironment, possibly mediated by growth hormone's known stimulatory effects on hematopoietic progenitor cells.

### Example 5

Immune system reconstitution was evaluated using high-dimensional mass cytometry (CyTOF) to perform deep immunophenotyping on cryopreserved peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). The most striking immune changes were a significant decrease in the percentage of total monocytes and, more specifically, CD38-positive monocytes. This led to a substantial increase in the lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), a clinically relevant biomarker. At baseline, the mean LMR of the participants was below 5, a threshold associated with increased cardiovascular risk. By the end of the 12-month treatment period, the mean LMR had risen well above 5 and remained elevated at the 18-month follow-up (6 months post-treatment). Linear mixed-model correlation analysis revealed a significant positive correlation between the increase in TFFF and the reduction in CD38+ monocytes, as well as between TFFF and the increase in LMR, suggesting a direct link between thymic regeneration and systemic immune remodeling.

### Example 6

Further CyTOF analysis showed a significant increase in the proportion of naïve CD4+ and naïve CD8+ T cells, as well as a significant increase in CD4+ recent thymic emigrants (RTEs), identified as CD31+CD45RA+CD4+ cells. These findings provide direct evidence of enhanced thymic output and a rejuvenation of the peripheral T-cell pool. Additionally, there was a significant decline in the percentage of PD-1-positive CD8+ T cells, a population associated with T-cell exhaustion and dysfunction. While no consistent changes were observed in classical markers of T-cell senescence (CD57+ or CD28-), the overall shift in the immune landscape was strongly indicative of a more youthful and functional immune system. The persistence of these immune changes at the 18-month follow-up suggests that the treatment induced a durable reset of immune homeostasis.

### Example 7

The impact of the treatment on biological aging was assessed using four independent epigenetic clocks: the Horvath clock, the Hannum clock, the PhenoAge clock, and the GrimAge clock. Genomic DNA was extracted from PBMCs collected at baseline, 12 months, and 18 months, and DNA methylation profiles were generated using the Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC BeadChip. All four clocks showed a statistically significant regression of epigenetic age after 12 months of treatment, with a mean reduction in the difference between epigenetic age and chronological age (EA-A) of approximately 2.5 years. Linear mixed-model analysis confirmed the significance of this effect across the 0-12 month period (p-values ranging from .0009 to .012). Intriguingly, the rate of epigenetic age reversal was found to accelerate dramatically in the final three months of treatment, with the mean slope across all clocks increasing from -1.56 years/year to -6.48 years/year.

### Example 8

The durability of the epigenetic age reversal was a key question. At the 18-month follow-up (6 months after treatment cessation), the epigenetic age, as measured by the Horvath, Hannum, and PhenoAge clocks, showed a partial regression back towards baseline, though a net improvement of more than 1.5 years was still maintained (p < .001). However, the GrimAge clock, which is the most robust predictor of mortality and healthspan, showed no such regression. The gain of approximately 2.1 years in predicted life expectancy observed at 12 months was fully retained at 18 months. This persistent effect on GrimAge is of profound clinical significance, as it suggests a lasting improvement in the subject's underlying biological state and future health trajectory. The differential behavior of the clocks highlights the importance of using multiple, complementary biomarkers to assess the full impact of an anti-aging intervention.

### Example 9

Safety and tolerability were closely monitored throughout the trial. Concerns regarding the potential for rhGH to exacerbate prostate cancer were addressed by serial measurements of prostate-specific antigen (PSA). Contrary to expectations, both total PSA and percent free PSA showed significant and sustained improvements, with the PSA/free PSA ratio—a key indicator of prostate cancer risk—also trending favorably. Metabolic safety was ensured by the co-administration of DHEA and metformin, which effectively controlled insulin levels in all but one outlier participant. Fasting glucose levels remained stable. Systemic inflammation, as measured by C-reactive protein (CRP), showed a significant decline by the 9-12 month mark. Renal function, assessed by estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), actually improved significantly. Side effects were generally mild and typical of rhGH therapy, including transient arthralgias, anxiety, and fluid retention, and were manageable without requiring permanent dose modifications in most cases. One participant was withdrawn early due to a pre-existing cardiac condition and a strong familial cancer history. Overall, the safety profile of the combination therapy was favorable, supporting its potential for broader clinical application.

## SUMMARY

The present invention discloses a groundbreaking method for reversing biological aging in human subjects through the synergistic and titrated administration of recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH), dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and metformin. This method, validated in a clinical trial with healthy middle-aged men, achieves a multi-systemic rejuvenation that is both profound and measurable. The core of the invention lies in its ability to specifically target and reverse thymic involution, a central pillar of immunosenescence, as demonstrated by a highly significant increase in the thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF) using quantitative MRI. This thymic regeneration is directly linked to a comprehensive restoration of a more youthful immune profile, characterized by an increased lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), a reduction in pro-inflammatory CD38+ monocytes, an expansion of naïve T-cell populations, and a decrease in exhausted PD-1+ CD8+ T cells.

Most remarkably, the invention produces a robust and statistically significant regression of epigenetic age across four independent and well-validated epigenetic clocks. The mean reversal of biological age is approximately 2.5 years after a 12-month treatment course, with the rate of reversal accelerating over time. Critically, the gains in biological age, particularly as measured by the mortality-predictive GrimAge clock, are durable and persist for at least six months after treatment cessation, suggesting a fundamental and lasting shift in the subject's biological state. The safety of this approach is ensured by the strategic combination of DHEA and metformin with rhGH, which effectively mitigates the diabetogenic risks of GH while simultaneously improving metabolic health, reducing systemic inflammation, and even enhancing renal function. Prostate safety is confirmed by favorable trends in PSA biomarkers.

In summary, the invention provides a practical, pharmacologically-based, and clinically feasible strategy to ameliorate multiple fundamental hallmarks of human aging. It moves beyond theoretical models to offer a concrete protocol that has been shown to safely and effectively turn back the biological clock, with the potential to delay the onset of age-related diseases and extend the human healthspan. This method represents a significant advance in the field of geroscience and offers a promising new avenue for the prevention and treatment of aging itself.
