# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of natural language processing, and more specifically to systems, methods, and computer-readable media for generating abstractive summaries of source documents that are faithful to the factual content contained therein. The invention addresses a critical problem in modern abstractive summarization: the generation of hallucinated or fabricated content that is not supported by the source document. In particular, the invention provides novel techniques for controlling the faithfulness of generated summaries through the use of entity control codes during both training and inference phases of sequence-to-sequence models. The invention further extends to zero-shot summarization scenarios across multiple domains by incorporating controllable intermediate fine-tuning with domain-specific control codes. The technical field encompasses artificial intelligence, machine learning, transformer-based architectures, named entity recognition, and controllable text generation, with specific applications in automated content summarization for news articles, scientific publications, dialogue transcripts, and other textual corpora where factual accuracy is paramount.

The invention operates within the broader context of pre-trained language models that have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in various natural language understanding and generation tasks. However, these models often suffer from generating content that appears fluent and coherent but contains factual errors or information not present in the source material. This problem is particularly acute in abstractive summarization, where the goal is to produce concise, readable summaries that capture the essence of longer documents while maintaining strict fidelity to the original content. The present invention provides a systematic approach to mitigate this issue by introducing explicit control mechanisms that guide the model's generation process toward higher levels of faithfulness without compromising the quality, fluency, or salience of the generated summaries. The technical solution involves quantifying entity-level faithfulness through entity coverage precision metrics, discretizing these metrics into control codes, and conditioning the model's generation on these codes during both training and inference. This approach represents a significant advancement over existing methods that either sacrifice training data through filtering approaches or introduce additional errors through post-processing correction techniques.

## BACKGROUND

Abstractive summarization has emerged as a fundamental capability in natural language processing, enabling the automatic generation of concise, fluent summaries that capture the most salient information from source documents. Unlike extractive summarization, which selects and concatenates existing sentences or phrases from the source, abstractive summarization generates entirely new text that may contain paraphrased content, restructured information, and novel linguistic formulations. This flexibility has made abstractive approaches particularly valuable for applications requiring human-like summarization capabilities, such as news aggregation, scientific literature review, legal document analysis, and customer service automation. Recent advances in pre-trained language models, including BART, T5, and other transformer-based architectures, have significantly improved the quality of generated summaries in terms of fluency, coherence, and informativeness.

However, a persistent and critical limitation of current abstractive summarization systems is their tendency to generate hallucinated or fabricated content that is not supported by the source document. This phenomenon, known as summary hallucination, can manifest in various forms, including the introduction of non-existent entities, the attribution of incorrect relationships between entities, the fabrication of events or circumstances, or the misrepresentation of quantitative information. Hallucinations can be categorized as either intrinsic (where the hallucinated content contradicts information explicitly stated in the source document) or extrinsic (where the hallucinated content introduces information not present in the source document but potentially verifiable through external knowledge). Both types of hallucinations pose significant challenges for practical deployment of summarization systems, particularly in domains where factual accuracy is essential, such as journalism, scientific communication, legal proceedings, and medical documentation.

Previous approaches to addressing hallucination in abstractive summarization have employed various strategies, each with notable limitations. Some methods have focused on post-processing correction techniques, where hallucinated entities in generated summaries are identified and replaced with appropriate entities from the source document. While these approaches can reduce certain types of hallucinations, they often introduce additional errors and may increase intrinsic hallucination rates by substituting entities without proper contextual consideration. Other approaches have attempted to address the problem through data filtering, removing training examples that exhibit low faithfulness from the training corpus, or through multi-task learning frameworks that incorporate auxiliary tasks such as summary-worthy named entity classification. However, these methods typically sacrifice valuable training data and may degrade overall summary quality by limiting the model's exposure to diverse linguistic patterns and content structures.

Alternative approaches have explored the integration of external knowledge sources, fact-checking mechanisms, or entailment-based verification systems to improve summary faithfulness. These methods often require additional computational resources, complex pipeline architectures, or access to external databases and knowledge graphs, making them less practical for real-world deployment scenarios. Furthermore, many existing approaches focus primarily on entity-level hallucination while neglecting other forms of factual inconsistency, or they address faithfulness at the expense of other important summary qualities such as fluency, coherence, and salience.

The need for a more effective, efficient, and comprehensive solution to the hallucination problem in abstractive summarization has become increasingly apparent as these systems are deployed in more critical applications. Users and stakeholders require summaries that not only read well and capture important information but also maintain strict fidelity to the source material. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel framework that leverages control codes to guide the model's learning process toward higher levels of faithfulness without compromising other desirable summary characteristics. This approach represents a significant departure from previous methods by directly conditioning the model's generation process on explicit faithfulness signals during both training and inference, rather than relying on post-hoc corrections or data manipulation strategies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Systems for Abstractive Summarization

The present invention provides a comprehensive system for generating faithful abstractive summaries through the use of entity control codes (ECC) and controllable intermediate fine-tuning. The system architecture comprises several key components that work in concert to produce summaries that maintain high levels of factual accuracy while preserving the quality, fluency, and salience expected from state-of-the-art abstractive summarization models. At the core of the system is a sequence-to-sequence transformer-based model, which serves as the foundation for summary generation. While the invention is described primarily in the context of BART-large as the backbone model, it is important to note that the disclosed techniques are generally applicable to any sequence-to-sequence architecture, including but not limited to T5, PEGASUS, and other transformer-based models.

The system begins with a preprocessing component that analyzes training document-summary pairs to compute entity coverage precision metrics. This component utilizes advanced named entity recognition (NER) systems to identify and extract named entities from both source documents and their corresponding reference summaries. For general domain applications, the system employs neural NER systems trained on comprehensive corpora such as OntoNotes, while for specialized domains such as biomedical or scientific literature, domain-specific NER tools like scispaCy are utilized to ensure accurate entity recognition. The entity coverage precision is calculated as the ratio of named entities in the reference summary that are also present in the source document, providing a quantitative measure of faithfulness at the entity level.

Based on these computed entity coverage precision values, the system implements a quantization mechanism that discretizes the continuous faithfulness spectrum into k distinct bins or categories. The bin boundaries are strategically selected to ensure balanced distribution of training examples across categories, thereby preventing data imbalance issues that could negatively impact model performance. Each bin is represented by a unique special token, referred to as an entity control code, which is added to the input vocabulary of the sequence-to-sequence model. In the preferred embodiment, k is set to 3, resulting in three discrete control codes: <FF-low>, <FF-mid>, and <FF-high>, representing low, medium, and high levels of entity faithfulness, respectively.

During the training phase, the system modifies the input representation by prepending the appropriate entity control code to each source document based on the computed entity coverage precision of its corresponding reference summary. This modification conditions the model's learning process on both the source document content and the explicit faithfulness signal provided by the control code. The model learns to associate different control codes with distinct generation patterns, effectively developing the ability to produce summaries with varying levels of faithfulness depending on the provided control signal. This training approach enables the model to internalize the relationship between faithfulness requirements and appropriate generation strategies without requiring modifications to the underlying architecture or loss functions.

For inference, the system consistently prepends the highest faithfulness control code (<FF-high>) to all input documents, thereby guiding the model to generate summaries that maximize entity coverage precision while maintaining other desirable summary qualities. The system's design ensures that this faithfulness guidance does not compromise the fluency, coherence, or salience of generated summaries, as demonstrated through extensive experimental validation. The control code mechanism operates seamlessly within the existing model architecture, requiring no additional computational overhead during inference beyond the inclusion of the control token in the input sequence.

The system further extends its capabilities to zero-shot summarization scenarios through a controllable intermediate fine-tuning component. This component addresses the challenge of applying summarization models to domains or datasets for which no labeled training data is available. The intermediate fine-tuning process begins with the generation of pseudo document-summary pairs from large-scale knowledge sources such as Wikipedia article dumps. These pseudo pairs are constructed to match the characteristics of target datasets in terms of summary length, document length, and abstractiveness levels, ensuring that the model learns appropriate generation patterns for the intended application domains.

Unlike previous approaches that require separate models for different target datasets, the present invention implements a unified model architecture that can generalize across multiple domains through the use of target-specific control codes. Each pseudo training subset corresponding to a particular target domain is associated with a unique special token, referred to as a target control code, which is added to the input vocabulary. During intermediate fine-tuning, the system prepends the appropriate target control code to each pseudo document, conditioning the model's learning on both the document content and the target domain characteristics. This approach enables the model to develop domain-specific generation capabilities while maintaining a single, unified architecture.

The system's control code mechanism is designed to be stackable, allowing for the combination of multiple control signals during both training and inference. For instance, during zero-shot summarization, the system can simultaneously prepend both a target control code (to specify the desired domain characteristics) and an entity control code (to ensure high faithfulness), resulting in summaries that are both domain-appropriate and factually accurate. This stacking capability provides unprecedented flexibility in controlling various aspects of summary generation through simple modifications to the input sequence.

The system architecture also includes evaluation and monitoring components that assess the faithfulness and quality of generated summaries using both automatic and human evaluation metrics. Automatic evaluation employs entity coverage precision and FEQA (Fact Extraction and Verification Question Answering) metrics to quantify faithfulness, while ROUGE scores measure fluency and salience. Human evaluation involves expert annotators who categorize summaries according to faithfulness levels (faithful, intrinsic hallucination, extrinsic hallucination) and quality ratings (low, medium, high). These evaluation components provide comprehensive feedback on system performance and enable continuous improvement through iterative refinement of control code parameters and training strategies.

The implementation of the system leverages established deep learning frameworks and libraries, such as Hugging Face Transformers, to ensure compatibility with existing infrastructure and facilitate deployment in production environments. The system is designed to operate efficiently on modern GPU hardware, with training and inference processes optimized for parallel processing and memory management. The modular architecture allows for easy integration with existing natural language processing pipelines and supports customization for specific application requirements through parameter tuning and control code configuration.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Experimental Methods and Results

The effectiveness of the present invention was rigorously evaluated through a series of comprehensive experiments conducted across multiple benchmark datasets and evaluation scenarios. The experimental setup was designed to assess the system's performance in both supervised and zero-shot summarization settings, with particular emphasis on measuring improvements in summary faithfulness while maintaining or improving other quality metrics. The experiments were conducted using BART-large as the backbone sequence-to-sequence model, with all implementations leveraging the Hugging Face Transformers library for consistency and reproducibility.

In the supervised setting, the system was evaluated on three benchmark datasets: XSum, SAMSum, and PubMed. These datasets represent diverse domains including news articles, dialogue transcripts, and scientific literature, respectively, providing a comprehensive test of the system's generalizability across different content types and writing styles. For each dataset, the entity coverage precision was computed for all document-summary pairs in the training set using appropriate named entity recognition tools. The PubMed dataset utilized the scispaCy biomedical NER toolkit due to its specialized domain characteristics, while the XSum and SAMSum datasets employed the Stanza NLP toolkit trained on the OntoNotes corpus.

The entity coverage precision values were quantized into three discrete bins, with bin boundaries selected to ensure balanced distribution of training examples. For the PubMed dataset, the bin boundaries were set at 0.36 and 0.5, while for SAMSum and XSum, the boundaries were established at 0.33 and 0.66. These thresholds resulted in three entity control codes: <FF-low>, <FF-mid>, and <FF-high>, representing increasing levels of entity faithfulness. During training, each source document was prepended with the appropriate control code based on its reference summary's entity coverage precision, and the model was fine-tuned using standard optimization procedures with a learning rate of 5e-5 and Adam optimizer with weight decay.

The experimental results demonstrated significant improvements in summary faithfulness without compromising quality metrics. On the XSum dataset, the system achieved a substantial increase in entity coverage precision compared to the baseline BART model, while maintaining comparable ROUGE scores. This finding is particularly noteworthy given the inherent trade-off between faithfulness and quality often observed in the XSum dataset, which is known to contain reference summaries with relatively low faithfulness levels. The system's ability to improve faithfulness without degrading quality represents a significant advancement over existing approaches.

When compared to state-of-the-art baselines, including post-processing correction methods and data filtering approaches, the present invention demonstrated superior performance. The post-processing correction method, which replaces hallucinated entities in generated summaries with appropriate entities from the source document, was found to introduce additional errors and increase intrinsic hallucination rates. The data filtering approach, which removes low-faithfulness training examples, resulted in degraded summary quality due to the reduced training data. In contrast, the entity control code approach achieved comparable or better faithfulness improvements while maintaining or improving summary quality metrics.

Human evaluation was conducted on a randomly sampled subset of 50 articles from the XSum dataset, following established protocols from prior research. Four expert annotators independently assessed each summary output according to faithfulness categories (faithful summary, intrinsic hallucination, extrinsic hallucination) and quality ratings (low, medium, high). The results confirmed the automatic evaluation findings, showing that the entity control code model significantly improved summary faithfulness without degrading quality. The inter-annotator agreement was calculated using additional annotations from two other experts, with confidence intervals estimated from the mean and standard deviation of ratings.

Analysis of the generated summaries revealed that the system did not achieve higher faithfulness by simply reducing the number of entities generated. The distribution of entity counts in summaries generated by the entity control code model was nearly identical to that of the baseline BART model, with similar mean values and variance. This finding indicates that the system maintains appropriate entity generation behavior while improving the accuracy of those entities, rather than adopting a conservative strategy of under-generating entities to avoid hallucination.

The effect of different control codes during inference was also investigated by prepending various entity control codes to the same input documents. The results showed that the model could generate reasonable summaries across all control code levels, with summaries generated using low faithfulness control codes achieving higher ROUGE scores but lower entity coverage precision. This demonstrates the system's ability to provide controllable trade-offs between faithfulness and other quality metrics based on user requirements or application constraints.

In the zero-shot summarization setting, the system was evaluated against the state-of-the-art WikiTransfer approach. The controllable intermediate fine-tuning process generated pseudo document-summary pairs from Wikipedia article dumps, matching the characteristics of target datasets in terms of length and abstractiveness. The unified model architecture, conditioned on target-specific control codes, demonstrated superior performance compared to WikiTransfer, achieving improvements in both entity coverage precision and summary quality metrics. Importantly, the system maintained cross-dataset generalizability, enabling the use of a single model for multiple downstream targets rather than requiring separate models for each dataset as in the WikiTransfer approach.

Representative examples from the XSum dataset illustrate the practical benefits of the entity control code approach. In one example, the baseline BART model generated a summary containing the hallucinated location "Los Angeles," while the reference summary itself was unfaithful by mentioning "Los Angeles" when the source document only discussed Mexico. The post-processing correction method incorrectly replaced "Los Angeles" with "Mexico," introducing a factual error. In contrast, the entity control code model generated a completely faithful summary that accurately reflected the source document's content about Mexican wrestling being released at E3, without introducing any hallucinated entities.

Another example demonstrated the controllability of the system through different control codes during inference. When decoding with a low faithfulness control code, the generated summary remained fluent and reasonable but contained fewer faithful entities compared to the same document decoded with a high faithfulness control code. This capability provides users with flexible control over the faithfulness-quality trade-off based on their specific needs and preferences.

The system's performance was also analyzed in cases involving factual statements that require external knowledge for verification. While the entity control code approach focuses primarily on entity-level hallucination, it correctly handles cases where reference summaries contain generalizations that may be factually accurate but not directly supported by the source document. For instance, when a reference summary used "most of Wales" to summarize a list of specific Welsh counties, the entity control code model appropriately generated a summary that accurately reflected the specific counties mentioned in the source document, avoiding potential overgeneralization while maintaining factual accuracy.

These experimental results collectively demonstrate that the present invention provides a robust, effective, and flexible solution to the hallucination problem in abstractive summarization. The system achieves significant improvements in summary faithfulness across multiple domains and evaluation scenarios while maintaining or improving other important quality metrics. The controllable nature of the approach, through both entity control codes and target-specific control codes, provides unprecedented flexibility in tailoring summary generation to specific application requirements and user preferences.
