# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of regenerative medicine and cellular therapeutics, and more specifically to methods and compositions for promoting the maturation of cardiomyocytes derived from human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). The invention provides a three-dimensional (3D) culture system comprising decellularized extracellular matrix (ECM) derived from adult cardiac tissue, which significantly enhances the structural, functional, and electrophysiological maturation of iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes compared to conventional two-dimensional (2D) culture systems or 3D systems utilizing non-cardiac or fetal-derived matrices. The matured cardiomyocytes produced by the disclosed methods exhibit improved calcium handling, enhanced contractile properties, appropriate electrophysiological responses, reduced automaticity, and heightened sensitivity to pharmacological agents—characteristics that are essential for their safe and effective use in cardiac cell therapy, disease modeling, drug screening, and cardiotoxicity assessment. The invention further encompasses the use of adult cardiac ECM hydrogels as biomimetic scaffolds that recapitulate the native myocardial microenvironment, thereby providing biomechanical and biochemical cues necessary for the terminal differentiation and functional maturation of human cardiomyocytes in vitro.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of mortality in the United States and across much of the developed world, with myocardial infarction representing its most common and devastating clinical manifestation. Myocardial infarction results in the irreversible loss of millions to billions of cardiomyocytes due to ischemic injury, and the adult human heart possesses an extremely limited capacity for endogenous regeneration owing to the negligible proliferative potential of terminally differentiated cardiomyocytes. Consequently, the damaged myocardium is replaced by fibrotic scar tissue, which not only compromises cardiac contractility but also disrupts electrical conduction pathways, thereby increasing the risk of life-threatening arrhythmias and progressive heart failure. Despite advances in medical and surgical interventions, no currently available pharmacological therapy can regenerate lost myocardium or restore functional cardiac tissue, creating a critical unmet need for novel therapeutic strategies capable of replacing dead cardiomyocytes with healthy, functional equivalents.

In recent years, human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) have emerged as a promising autologous cell source for cardiac regeneration, offering the potential to generate patient-specific cardiomyocytes for transplantation without the ethical concerns associated with embryonic stem cells or the risk of immune rejection. Protocols for differentiating iPSCs into cardiomyocytes have been well established, yielding populations that express canonical cardiac markers and exhibit spontaneous contractile activity. However, a significant limitation of these protocols is that the resulting cardiomyocytes remain functionally and structurally immature, closely resembling fetal rather than adult cardiomyocytes. These immature cells display disorganized sarcomeric architecture, weak contractile force, spontaneous automaticity, aberrant calcium handling, immature electrophysiological profiles, and altered pharmacological responses—all of which pose substantial barriers to their clinical translation. For instance, the spontaneous beating of immature cardiomyocytes increases the risk of arrhythmogenic complications upon transplantation, while their poor contractile strength limits their ability to contribute meaningfully to cardiac output. Moreover, their immature phenotype renders them suboptimal for accurate drug screening and cardiotoxicity testing, as their responses often fail to predict those of adult human cardiomyocytes.

Recognizing these limitations, considerable research efforts have been directed toward developing strategies to enhance the maturation of iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes. Various approaches have been explored, including long-term culture, application of mechanical or electrical stimulation, exposure to thyroid hormone (e.g., triiodothyronine, T3), co-culture with non-cardiomyocyte cell types, overexpression of specific ion channels, and modulation of culture media composition. Additionally, the role of the extracellular matrix (ECM)—the complex network of proteins and polysaccharides that provides structural and biochemical support to surrounding cells—has garnered increasing attention as a critical regulator of cardiomyocyte development and maturation. The cardiac ECM is not merely a passive scaffold; it dynamically influences cell behavior through biomechanical cues (e.g., stiffness, topography) and biochemical signals (e.g., integrin-mediated signaling, growth factor presentation). Studies have shown that decellularized cardiac ECM, when used as a substrate for cardiomyocyte culture, can promote aspects of maturation, particularly in rodent models. However, these studies have largely been confined to two-dimensional (2D) culture formats, which fail to recapitulate the three-dimensional architecture and mechanical complexity of the native myocardium.

Furthermore, the developmental origin of the ECM—whether derived from fetal or adult cardiac tissue—has been hypothesized to influence its biological activity, given that the composition and mechanical properties of the cardiac ECM undergo significant changes during postnatal development. Adult cardiac ECM is generally stiffer and more fibrous than fetal ECM, reflecting the increased collagen content and cross-linking that occur with maturation. While some studies have compared the effects of fetal versus adult ECM on neonatal rat cardiomyocytes, it remains unclear whether these differences translate to human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, which represent a distinct developmental context. Equally important is the question of dimensionality: does culturing cardiomyocytes within a three-dimensional (3D) hydrogel composed of native cardiac ECM confer advantages over traditional 2D monolayer cultures in terms of promoting structural and functional maturation? Prior attempts to address this have often employed simplified 3D matrices composed of single ECM proteins such as collagen I or fibrin, which lack the compositional complexity and tissue-specific signaling cues of native cardiac ECM.

Thus, there exists a pressing need for a culture system that more faithfully mimics the in vivo cardiac microenvironment to drive the robust maturation of iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes. Such a system would ideally incorporate both the biochemical specificity of native cardiac ECM and the three-dimensional architecture that enables proper cell-cell and cell-matrix interactions. The present invention addresses this need by demonstrating that 3D hydrogels formulated from decellularized adult cardiac ECM—particularly from bovine sources—provide a superior microenvironment for the maturation of human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes compared to both 2D cultures and 3D cultures using fetal cardiac ECM or non-native matrices. The invention further reveals that the mechanical properties of the ECM, notably its stiffness and fiber architecture, play a pivotal role in modulating cardiomyocyte gene expression, calcium handling, and drug responsiveness. By leveraging these insights, the disclosed methods enable the production of cardiomyocytes that more closely resemble their adult counterparts in key functional attributes, thereby enhancing their utility for therapeutic and pharmaceutical applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present invention provides a novel method for promoting the maturation of human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived cardiomyocytes by culturing said cardiomyocytes in a three-dimensional (3D) hydrogel scaffold composed of decellularized extracellular matrix (ECM) derived from adult cardiac tissue. The invention is based on the discovery that both the source (fetal versus adult) and the geometric configuration (two-dimensional versus three-dimensional) of the cardiac ECM profoundly influence the structural, functional, and electrophysiological maturation of iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes. Specifically, the inventors have found that 3D hydrogels prepared from decellularized adult bovine cardiac ECM induce significantly higher expression levels of key maturation-associated genes—including those involved in calcium handling (e.g., Junctin, SERCA2a, Triadin, CaV1.2), ion channel function (e.g., Kir2.1, HCN4), and structural organization (e.g., MYL2, connexin 43)—compared to 2D cultures on the same matrix or 3D cultures using fetal cardiac ECM. This enhanced gene expression profile is accompanied by corresponding increases in protein levels of critical cardiac markers, as confirmed by immunofluorescence and Western blot analyses.

Functionally, iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes cultured in 3D adult cardiac ECM hydrogels exhibit markedly improved calcium handling characteristics, including increased calcium transient amplitude, faster upstroke and decay kinetics, and reduced time to peak and decay—features that are hallmarks of mature cardiomyocytes. These improvements are particularly evident when cardiomyocytes are cultured as aggregates or spheroids within the 3D matrix, underscoring the importance of cell-cell coupling in achieving physiological calcium dynamics. In contrast, single cardiomyocytes embedded in 3D gels display attenuated calcium transients, highlighting that maturation is not solely dependent on matrix cues but also requires multicellular organization. Furthermore, cardiomyocytes matured in 3D adult cardiac ECM demonstrate enhanced responsiveness to pharmacological agents: they exhibit a robust increase in beat rate upon exposure to the β-adrenergic agonist isoproterenol, which is subsequently reversed by the antagonist propranolol, and show accelerated calcium release kinetics in response to caffeine—a ryanodine receptor sensitizer—consistent with elevated sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium stores and improved excitation-contraction coupling.

The invention also discloses that the superior maturation-promoting activity of adult cardiac ECM is attributable, at least in part, to its distinct biophysical properties. Comparative analysis revealed that decellularized adult cardiac ECM contains a higher density of organized collagen and elastin fibers and forms hydrogels that are approximately ten-fold stiffer (storage modulus ~67.5 Pa) than those derived from fetal cardiac ECM (~7.2 Pa). This increased stiffness mirrors the mechanical environment of the adult myocardium and is believed to provide critical biomechanical cues that drive cardiomyocyte maturation through mechanotransduction pathways, potentially involving integrin-mediated signaling. Although the protein composition of fetal and adult cardiac ECM is largely similar—with both containing fibrinogen, collagen, periostin, fibronectin, and other matrix components—the adult matrix uniquely lacks certain proteoglycans such as versican and mimecan, which may otherwise inhibit maturation. Thus, the invention establishes that the combination of adult-derived ECM composition and 3D architecture creates a biomimetic niche that recapitulates key aspects of the native cardiac microenvironment, thereby enabling more efficient and comprehensive maturation of iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for producing mature human cardiomyocytes, comprising: (a) differentiating human iPSCs into cardiomyocytes using standard cardiac induction protocols; and (b) culturing the resulting cardiomyocytes in a 3D hydrogel comprising decellularized extracellular matrix derived from adult mammalian cardiac tissue, wherein the hydrogel has a storage modulus of at least 50 Pa. In another aspect, the invention provides a composition comprising human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes embedded in a 3D hydrogel of decellularized adult cardiac ECM, wherein the cardiomyocytes exhibit increased expression of maturation markers selected from the group consisting of Junctin, SERCA2a, MYL2, connexin 43, and Kir2.1, as compared to cardiomyocytes cultured in 2D or in 3D fetal cardiac ECM. The invention further encompasses the use of such mature cardiomyocytes in cardiac cell therapy, drug screening platforms, disease modeling, and cardiotoxicity assays, where their enhanced physiological fidelity offers significant advantages over immature counterparts.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to methods and compositions for enhancing the maturation of human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived cardiomyocytes through the use of a three-dimensional (3D) culture system incorporating decellularized extracellular matrix (ECM) derived from adult cardiac tissue. The following detailed description sets forth specific embodiments of the invention, including materials, methods, and experimental results that support the claimed advantages of the disclosed approach. It will be understood by those skilled in the art that the invention is not limited to the particular embodiments described herein, and that various modifications and equivalents may be made without departing from the scope of the invention as defined by the appended claims.

The foundation of the invention lies in the recognition that the microenvironment in which cardiomyocytes are cultured exerts a profound influence on their phenotypic maturation. Traditional protocols for generating cardiomyocytes from iPSCs typically involve monolayer (2D) culture on substrates such as Matrigel or gelatin, followed by differentiation using small molecule modulators of the Wnt signaling pathway. While these methods efficiently produce cardiomyocytes that express early cardiac markers and exhibit spontaneous contractility, the resulting cells remain functionally immature, displaying characteristics reminiscent of fetal cardiomyocytes. This immaturity manifests in multiple domains: structurally, the sarcomeres are disorganized and lack the well-defined Z-discs and M-lines of adult cells; functionally, contractile force is weak and calcium handling is inefficient due to underdeveloped sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) and T-tubule systems; electrophysiologically, the cells exhibit automaticity and immature action potential profiles; and pharmacologically, their responses to cardioactive drugs often diverge from those observed in adult human myocardium.

To overcome these limitations, the inventors systematically investigated the role of the extracellular matrix in driving cardiomyocyte maturation, with a focus on two key variables: the developmental origin of the ECM (fetal versus adult) and the dimensionality of the culture system (2D versus 3D). Cardiac ECM was isolated from bovine hearts—both fetal and adult—through a detergent-based decellularization process designed to remove cellular components while preserving the native matrix architecture and composition. The protocol involved sequential treatment with sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) and Triton X-100, followed by extensive washing and lyophilization. The resulting decellularized tissue was then milled into a fine powder and digested with pepsin in acidic conditions to generate a soluble ECM prepolymer solution, which could be neutralized and allowed to self-assemble into hydrogels at physiological temperature.

Comprehensive characterization of the decellularized ECM revealed significant differences between fetal and adult sources. Histological analysis confirmed complete removal of cellular nuclei, as evidenced by the absence of hematoxylin staining in both fetal and adult samples. Proteomic profiling by nano-LC MS/MS identified a core set of matrix proteins common to both, including collagens, fibronectin, periostin, fibrillin, and fibulin, consistent with the known composition of cardiac ECM. However, two proteins—mimecan and versican—were uniquely detected in the fetal ECM, suggesting potential developmental regulation of matrix composition. More strikingly, multiphoton imaging demonstrated that the adult ECM exhibited a higher density of organized collagen and elastin fibers, with larger bundle sizes and greater signal intensity compared to the more diffuse and evenly distributed fibers in the fetal ECM. Rheological measurements further confirmed that hydrogels formed from adult cardiac ECM were substantially stiffer, with a storage modulus (G′) of approximately 67.5 ± 12.6 Pa, compared to 7.2 ± 3.2 Pa for fetal ECM hydrogels—a difference of nearly an order of magnitude that aligns with known mechanical distinctions between fetal and adult myocardium.

Human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes were generated using a well-established protocol involving sequential activation and inhibition of Wnt signaling. The WTC-11 GCaMP iPSC line, which carries a genetically encoded calcium indicator (GCaMP6f), was used to facilitate real-time monitoring of calcium dynamics. Following differentiation, cardiomyocytes were seeded either as single cells or as pre-formed aggregates onto 2D coatings of cardiac ECM or embedded within 3D hydrogels of the same material. After seven days of culture, gene expression analysis by quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) revealed that 3D culture, regardless of ECM source, upregulated numerous maturation-associated genes compared to 2D. These included calcium-handling genes such as Junctin (JCN), L-type calcium channel (CaV1.2), Triadin, and SERCA2a, as well as ion channel genes like KCNJ2 (Kir2.1) and HCN4. Notably, the magnitude of induction was significantly greater when adult ECM was used, particularly in the 3D configuration. For example, JCN expression was elevated over 10-fold in 3D adult ECM versus 2D, whereas the increase was only modest in 3D fetal ECM. Protein-level validation by immunostaining confirmed these findings, showing robust expression of CaV1.2, MYL2 (a marker of ventricular identity), and connexin 43 (a gap junction protein essential for electrical coupling) specifically in cardiomyocytes cultured in 3D adult ECM.

Functional assessment of calcium handling using the GCaMP6f reporter yielded nuanced insights. When single cardiomyocytes were embedded in 3D hydrogels, they exhibited slower calcium transients with reduced amplitude and prolonged decay times compared to their 2D counterparts—suggesting that isolation from neighboring cells impairs functional maturation despite the presence of a supportive matrix. However, when cardiomyocytes were cultured as aggregates within the 3D matrix, the opposite effect was observed: calcium transients were faster, larger in amplitude, and exhibited accelerated kinetics, closely mirroring the behavior of adult cardiomyocytes. This dichotomy underscores the critical interplay between matrix cues and cell-cell interactions in driving functional maturation. Additional experiments using cardiac spheroids embedded in various 3D matrices (adult cardiac ECM, fibrin, or collagen I) demonstrated that only the native cardiac ECM conferred a significant reduction in beat rate—a hallmark of maturation, as adult cardiomyocytes rely on pacemaker input rather than intrinsic automaticity.

Pharmacological testing further validated the enhanced maturity of cardiomyocytes cultured in 3D adult cardiac ECM. Upon exposure to isoproterenol, a β-adrenergic agonist, these cells exhibited a pronounced increase in beat rate, which was fully reversible upon addition of propranolol, a β-blocker. Similarly, caffeine—which sensitizes the ryanodine receptor to luminal calcium—induced a steeper calcium release slope and shorter time to peak in 3D-cultured cells, indicative of greater SR calcium stores and more efficient excitation-contraction coupling. These responses were significantly more robust than those observed in 2D cultures, confirming that the 3D adult ECM environment not only promotes structural and molecular maturation but also enhances functional drug responsiveness.

Collectively, these results establish that the combination of adult-derived cardiac ECM and 3D culture geometry provides a synergistic microenvironment that drives comprehensive maturation of iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes. The invention thus provides a robust platform for generating cardiomyocytes with enhanced physiological relevance, suitable for a wide range of applications including regenerative medicine, precision drug screening, and disease modeling.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Decellularization and Characterization of Bovine Cardiac ECM**

Adult and fetal bovine hearts were obtained from a commercial supplier and processed for decellularization. Excess fat was removed, and tissues were flash-frozen at -80°C. After thawing, tissues were minced into 3 mm³ pieces and subjected to sequential detergent treatment: 1% SDS for adult tissue (0.5% for fetal) for 72 hours, followed by 1% Triton X-100 for 90 minutes, with intermediate and final washes in deionized water and PBS. Decellularized tissues were lyophilized, milled into powder, and digested with 2 mg/mL pepsin in 0.01 M HCl for 48–72 hours. The resulting ECM solution was aliquoted and stored at -80°C. Histology (H&E staining) confirmed complete cell removal. Multiphoton imaging revealed that adult ECM had denser, more organized collagen and elastin fibers than fetal ECM. Rheology showed adult ECM hydrogels had a storage modulus of 67.5 ± 12.6 Pa, versus 7.2 ± 3.2 Pa for fetal ECM.

**Example 2: Differentiation of iPSCs into Cardiomyocytes and Culture in 2D vs. 3D ECM**

WTC-11 GCaMP iPSCs were maintained on Matrigel in mTeSR1 medium. Cardiac differentiation was initiated at 75–90% confluence by adding CHIR99021 (6 µM) in RPMI/B27 minus insulin. After 24 hours, medium was changed; on Day 3, IWP2 (5 µM) was added. Beating began by Day 12. On Day 7, cardiomyocytes were dissociated and seeded either as single cells or aggregates onto 2D ECM-coated plates or embedded in 3D ECM hydrogels (2 mg/mL final concentration). Cultures were maintained in RPMI/B27 plus insulin for 7 additional days before analysis.

**Example 3: Gene and Protein Expression Analysis**

qRT-PCR on Day 14 showed that 3D adult ECM cultures had significantly higher expression of JCN, CaV1.2, SERCA2a, MYL2, and Kir2.1 compared to 2D or 3D fetal ECM (p < 0.01). Immunostaining confirmed elevated protein levels of cTNT, CaV1.2, MYL2, and connexin 43 specifically in 3D adult ECM conditions, with organized sarcomeric structures.

**Example 4: Calcium Handling and Electrophysiological Assessment**

Using GCaMP6f fluorescence, calcium transients were recorded. Single cells in 3D showed reduced amplitude and slower kinetics versus 2D. In contrast, aggregates in 3D adult ECM exhibited increased amplitude, faster upstroke/downstroke, and shorter decay times (p < 0.001). Beat rate decreased in 3D adult ECM spheroids versus fibrin or collagen I controls.

**Example 5: Pharmacological Responsiveness**

Cells in 3D adult ECM responded to 1 µM isoproterenol with a 40% increase in beat rate, reversible by 10 µM propranolol. Caffeine (10 mM) induced a 25% steeper calcium release slope and 30% shorter time to peak in 3D versus 2D (p < 0.01), indicating enhanced SR function.

These examples demonstrate that 3D culture in adult cardiac ECM drives structural, functional, and pharmacological maturation of iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, providing a superior platform for cardiac applications.
