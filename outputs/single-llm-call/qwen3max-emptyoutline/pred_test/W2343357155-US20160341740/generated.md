# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Bipolar disorder (BD) is a chronic and debilitating psychiatric illness characterized by recurrent episodes of mania, hypomania, and depression, interspersed with periods of euthymia or relative clinical stability. The disorder typically manifests in late adolescence or early adulthood and often requires lifelong pharmacological and psychosocial management. BD imposes a substantial burden on individuals, healthcare systems, and society at large, with epidemiological studies estimating significant direct and indirect economic costs associated with its treatment, disability, and lost productivity. Despite decades of clinical and neurobiological research, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying BD remain incompletely understood, which has hindered the development of objective diagnostic tools and targeted therapeutics. Current diagnosis relies primarily on clinical interviews and symptom-based criteria outlined in diagnostic manuals such as the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM), which can lead to delays in diagnosis, misdiagnosis, and suboptimal treatment outcomes. There is therefore a critical unmet need for reliable, biologically grounded biomarkers that can facilitate early detection, differential diagnosis, and monitoring of disease progression or therapeutic response in BD.

A growing body of evidence implicates mitochondrial dysfunction as a central pathophysiological mechanism in BD. Mitochondria are essential organelles responsible for cellular energy production through oxidative phosphorylation, calcium buffering, regulation of reactive oxygen species, and modulation of apoptosis. Disruptions in mitochondrial bioenergetics have been consistently observed in post-mortem brain tissue, peripheral blood cells, and neuroimaging studies of BD patients. These disruptions include abnormalities in the electron transport chain, reduced activity of key metabolic enzymes, altered mitochondrial morphology, and impaired ATP synthesis. The citric acid cycle (also known as the Krebs cycle or tricarboxylic acid cycle) is a core mitochondrial pathway that generates reducing equivalents (NADH and FADH₂) for the electron transport chain and provides intermediates for biosynthetic processes. Dysregulation of this cycle may therefore represent a critical node in the metabolic pathology of BD.

Metabolomics—the comprehensive profiling of small-molecule metabolites in biological systems—offers a powerful approach to identify disease-associated metabolic signatures. Unlike genomics, transcriptomics, or proteomics, metabolomics captures the functional output of cellular processes and reflects the integrated influence of genetic, environmental, and pharmacological factors. Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) is a particularly valuable biofluid for studying central nervous system disorders because it bathes the brain and spinal cord, thereby providing a proximal readout of brain metabolism. While plasma and serum metabolomics have been explored in psychiatric disorders, CSF offers superior specificity for detecting brain-relevant metabolic alterations, as it is less influenced by systemic confounders such as diet, liver function, or gut microbiota. However, CSF metabolomic studies in BD have been scarce, largely due to the invasiveness of lumbar puncture and the logistical challenges of sample collection and storage.

Capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS) is a high-resolution analytical platform capable of simultaneously quantifying hundreds of charged low-molecular-weight metabolites, including amino acids, organic acids, nucleotides, and energy-related compounds. This technique has been successfully applied to identify metabolic perturbations in neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions such as autism spectrum disorder and schizophrenia. However, to date, no study has employed CE-TOFMS to conduct a comprehensive metabolomic analysis of CSF in well-characterized, mood-stabilized BD patients compared to healthy controls. Such an investigation could uncover novel metabolic biomarkers directly linked to the neurobiology of BD.

In parallel, gene and protein expression studies in post-mortem brain tissue provide complementary insights into the molecular underpinnings of psychiatric disorders. The dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), a region implicated in executive function, emotional regulation, and cognitive control—all domains frequently impaired in BD—has been a focus of neuropathological investigations. Enzymes involved in the citric acid cycle, such as aconitase (ACO) and isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH), are plausible candidates for dysregulation in BD given their pivotal roles in energy metabolism. IDH3, in particular, is the NAD⁺-dependent isoform of isocitrate dehydrogenase that catalyzes the rate-limiting conversion of isocitrate to α-ketoglutarate in the mitochondrial matrix, thereby generating NADH for oxidative phosphorylation. Reduced expression or activity of IDH3 could lead to accumulation of isocitrate, a phenomenon that might be detectable in CSF.

Furthermore, the potential confounding effects of psychotropic medications—particularly mood stabilizers such as lithium (Li) and valproic acid (VPA)—must be carefully evaluated when interpreting biomarker findings in BD. These agents are first-line treatments for BD and may themselves influence metabolic pathways. Preclinical studies using animal models are essential to disentangle disease-related metabolic changes from medication-induced effects. Chronic administration of Li or VPA in rodents followed by CSF metabolomic and brain gene expression analyses can clarify whether observed alterations in human BD are intrinsic to the disorder or secondary to pharmacotherapy.

Collectively, these considerations underscore the need for an integrated, multi-modal approach that combines CSF metabolomics, post-mortem brain molecular profiling, genetic association studies, and preclinical pharmacological experiments to elucidate the role of mitochondrial metabolism in BD. Such an approach not only advances our understanding of disease pathogenesis but also holds promise for the identification of clinically actionable biomarkers and novel therapeutic targets.

## Non-Patent Literature

The scientific literature provides a foundation for understanding the metabolic and mitochondrial abnormalities associated with bipolar disorder, though it lacks a cohesive framework linking specific citric acid cycle intermediates to diagnostic or therapeutic applications. Early studies using magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) reported altered levels of high-energy phosphates and lactate in the brains of BD patients, suggesting impaired energy metabolism. For instance, decreased phosphocreatine and creatine levels, as well as elevated lactate, have been observed in the prefrontal cortex and other brain regions of BD subjects, consistent with mitochondrial dysfunction. However, MRS lacks the molecular specificity to resolve individual metabolites within complex pathways such as the citric acid cycle, limiting its utility for biomarker discovery.

Post-mortem brain studies have further corroborated mitochondrial involvement in BD. Reduced expression of nuclear-encoded mitochondrial genes, particularly those encoding subunits of electron transport chain complexes I, III, IV, and V, has been documented in the DLPFC of BD patients. Additionally, decreased activity of complex I, a major site of reactive oxygen species production, has been reported, potentially contributing to oxidative stress—a hallmark of BD pathology. These findings align with the hypothesis that BD is a disorder of bioenergetic failure, wherein neurons are unable to meet the high energy demands required for synaptic plasticity, neurotransmission, and network stability.

Metabolomic analyses of peripheral tissues have yielded mixed results. Plasma metabolomics in BD has identified alterations in amino acids, lipids, and energy-related metabolites, but these findings are often inconsistent across studies and may reflect systemic rather than central nervous system pathology. Moreover, the blood-brain barrier restricts the passage of many metabolites, making plasma an imperfect proxy for brain metabolism. In contrast, CSF, which is in direct contact with the brain interstitial fluid, offers a more faithful representation of cerebral metabolic status. Despite this advantage, CSF metabolomic studies in BD are exceedingly rare. One prior study by Lan et al. analyzed post-mortem brain tissue from BD patients and reported increased levels of myo-inositol, creatine, glutamate, lactate, and phosphocholine, interpreted as evidence of mitochondrial impairment. However, as noted by the authors, post-mortem intervals significantly affect the stability of labile metabolites such as amino acids and organic acids, rendering such data unreliable for biomarker validation. Consequently, there remains a critical gap in the literature regarding in vivo CSF metabolomic profiles in living BD patients.

Genetic studies have explored the association between mitochondrial-related genes and BD susceptibility. While genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified numerous risk loci for BD, few implicate core metabolic enzymes. Candidate gene approaches have examined polymorphisms in genes involved in oxidative phosphorylation, antioxidant defense, and mitochondrial dynamics, but results have been inconclusive. Notably, no prior study has systematically investigated genetic variants in the ACO (aconitase) and IDH (isocitrate dehydrogenase) gene families in relation to BD, despite their central role in the citric acid cycle. Epistasis—gene-gene interactions—may also contribute to disease risk, yet such analyses are rarely performed in psychiatric genetics.

Preclinical research on the metabolic effects of mood stabilizers provides additional context. Lithium and valproic acid are known to modulate multiple signaling pathways, including those involved in neuroprotection, glycogen synthase kinase-3 inhibition, and histone deacetylase inhibition. However, their impact on mitochondrial metabolism, particularly on citric acid cycle flux, remains poorly characterized. Some studies suggest that lithium enhances mitochondrial biogenesis and respiratory function, while valproic acid may impair fatty acid oxidation and induce oxidative stress. Yet, whether these agents alter the levels of specific citric acid cycle intermediates such as isocitrate in the brain or CSF has not been rigorously tested.

In summary, while the non-patent literature supports a role for mitochondrial dysfunction in BD and hints at metabolic disturbances, it fails to establish a clear, reproducible, and clinically relevant biomarker based on CSF metabolite levels. The absence of integrated human and animal studies, combined with methodological limitations in prior metabolomic work, underscores the novelty and necessity of the present invention, which identifies isocitrate as a trait biomarker for BD and links it to reduced expression of IDH3 subunits in the brain.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for diagnosing bipolar disorder in a subject by measuring the concentration of isocitrate in a cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) sample obtained from the subject and comparing the measured concentration to a predetermined reference level derived from healthy control subjects. An elevated concentration of isocitrate in the CSF sample relative to the reference level is indicative of bipolar disorder. The invention further encompasses a method for identifying a therapeutic target for the treatment of bipolar disorder, comprising the steps of detecting increased isocitrate levels in the CSF of a bipolar disorder patient, analyzing the expression of isocitrate dehydrogenase 3 (IDH3) subunit genes—specifically IDH3A and IDH3B—in post-mortem brain tissue from bipolar disorder patients, and confirming reduced mRNA and protein expression of IDH3A and IDH3B compared to controls, thereby establishing IDH3 as a key enzyme whose dysfunction contributes to isocitrate accumulation and represents a novel target for pharmacological intervention.

Additionally, the invention includes a method for distinguishing bipolar disorder from other psychiatric conditions, such as major depressive disorder and schizophrenia, based on the specific pattern of isocitrate elevation in CSF and the selective reduction of IDH3A protein in the cerebellum, which is observed across multiple psychiatric disorders but in conjunction with CSF isocitrate levels provides a unique signature for bipolar disorder. The invention also provides a kit for diagnosing bipolar disorder, comprising reagents for quantifying isocitrate in a CSF sample, instructions for performing the assay, and a reference database containing normative isocitrate concentrations from healthy individuals. The diagnostic method is independent of the subject’s current medication regimen, as demonstrated by the absence of isocitrate level changes in rats chronically treated with lithium or valproic acid, indicating that the biomarker reflects a trait characteristic of the disorder rather than a state-dependent or drug-induced effect.

Moreover, the invention discloses a method for screening candidate compounds for the treatment of bipolar disorder, comprising the steps of administering the candidate compound to a cellular or animal model exhibiting reduced IDH3 activity or elevated isocitrate levels, and measuring the restoration of normal isocitrate concentrations or IDH3 expression as an indicator of therapeutic efficacy. The invention thus bridges the gap between metabolic biomarker discovery and targeted drug development, offering a precision medicine approach to bipolar disorder management based on mitochondrial metabolic profiling.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention is directed to methods and compositions for the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of bipolar disorder based on the discovery that isocitrate, a key intermediate in the mitochondrial citric acid cycle, is significantly elevated in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of individuals diagnosed with bipolar disorder compared to healthy controls. This elevation is not attributable to current pharmacological treatment, as evidenced by preclinical studies in rats chronically administered standard mood stabilizers such as lithium or valproic acid, which showed no alteration in CSF isocitrate levels. Furthermore, the invention establishes that this metabolic abnormality is mechanistically linked to reduced expression of the IDH3A and IDH3B subunits of the isocitrate dehydrogenase 3 (IDH3) enzyme complex in the brain, particularly in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and cerebellum of bipolar disorder patients. IDH3 catalyzes the NAD⁺-dependent conversion of isocitrate to α-ketoglutarate, a critical step in the citric acid cycle that generates NADH for oxidative phosphorylation. Diminished IDH3 activity, resulting from lower expression of its catalytic subunits, leads to the accumulation of isocitrate, which diffuses into the CSF and serves as a measurable biomarker of underlying mitochondrial dysfunction in bipolar disorder.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a diagnostic method comprising the steps of obtaining a CSF sample from a subject suspected of having bipolar disorder, quantifying the concentration of isocitrate in the sample using a high-resolution analytical technique such as capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS), and comparing the measured concentration to a reference value established from a cohort of age-matched, healthy control subjects. The reference value may be defined as the mean isocitrate concentration in controls plus two standard deviations, or alternatively, a cutoff value derived from receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis to maximize sensitivity and specificity. A CSF isocitrate concentration exceeding the reference threshold is indicative of bipolar disorder. The method is applicable to subjects in any phase of the illness, including euthymic, manic, or depressive states, as the biomarker reflects a trait characteristic rather than a state-dependent fluctuation.

In another embodiment, the invention includes a method for confirming the biological basis of the isocitrate elevation by assessing the expression of IDH3 subunit genes in post-mortem brain tissue. This involves extracting RNA from brain regions such as the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (Brodmann area 46) or cerebellum of deceased individuals with a confirmed diagnosis of bipolar disorder and comparing the mRNA levels of IDH3A and IDH3B to those from matched controls using quantitative real-time PCR or RNA sequencing. Concurrently, protein expression of IDH3A and IDH3B can be evaluated by Western blotting or immunohistochemistry. The finding of significantly reduced IDH3A and IDH3B mRNA and IDH3A protein levels in bipolar disorder patients validates the enzymatic deficit underlying isocitrate accumulation and reinforces the biomarker’s pathophysiological relevance.

The invention further encompasses a method for differentiating bipolar disorder from other psychiatric illnesses. Although reduced IDH3A protein was also observed in the cerebellum of patients with major depressive disorder and schizophrenia, the combination of elevated CSF isocitrate and regional IDH3 expression deficits provides a unique diagnostic signature for bipolar disorder. Thus, in a clinical setting, the CSF isocitrate assay can be used in conjunction with psychiatric evaluation to improve diagnostic accuracy, particularly in cases of diagnostic uncertainty such as bipolar depression versus unipolar depression.

Additionally, the invention provides a kit for performing the diagnostic assay. The kit includes a set of reagents for CSF sample preparation, internal standards for isocitrate quantification, calibration standards, and detailed protocols for CE-TOFMS analysis. It may also include software for data processing and comparison against a cloud-based reference database containing normative isocitrate values stratified by age, sex, and other relevant demographic variables. The kit is designed for use in specialized clinical laboratories equipped with mass spectrometry instrumentation.

For therapeutic development, the invention discloses a screening platform wherein candidate compounds are tested for their ability to upregulate IDH3 expression or enhance IDH3 enzymatic activity in neuronal cell cultures or animal models of mitochondrial dysfunction. Compounds that normalize isocitrate levels in CSF or brain tissue are prioritized for further development as potential treatments for bipolar disorder. This approach enables the rational design of therapies targeting the core metabolic pathology of the illness, moving beyond symptomatic management to disease modification.

The invention also contemplates the use of isocitrate levels as a pharmacodynamic biomarker in clinical trials. By monitoring CSF isocitrate before and after administration of a novel therapeutic agent, researchers can assess target engagement and biological efficacy, thereby accelerating drug development. Moreover, longitudinal measurement of isocitrate may help predict relapse or treatment response, facilitating personalized treatment adjustments.

In summary, the detailed description of the present invention encompasses a comprehensive suite of methods and tools centered on isocitrate as a CSF biomarker for bipolar disorder, supported by robust molecular evidence of IDH3 dysfunction, and extended to diagnostic, differential diagnostic, and therapeutic applications.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples were collected from 54 male patients diagnosed with bipolar disorder (BD) and 40 male healthy controls at the Northern Stockholm Psychiatric Clinic in Sweden. All BD patients were enrolled in the St Göran Bipolar Project and met DSM-IV criteria for bipolar spectrum disorder, including type I, type II, or not otherwise specified. Participants were in a euthymic state at the time of sampling, as confirmed by Clinical Global Impression (CGI) and Global Assessment of Functioning (GAF) scores, and had fasted overnight. CSF was obtained via lumbar puncture between 0900 and 1000 hours, with 12 mL of fluid collected and aliquoted into polypropylene tubes. Samples were stored at −80°C and shipped on dry ice to Chiba University, Japan, for metabolomic analysis. Healthy controls were recruited from the general population via random selection by Statistics Sweden, screened for psychiatric and neurological disorders, and matched for age and body mass index. Metabolomic profiling was performed using capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS) at Human Metabolome Technologies, Yamagata, Japan, targeting 116 major metabolic compounds across multiple pathways. Of these, 72 metabolites were detectable in CSF. Univariate statistical analysis using unpaired t-tests and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests identified 13 significantly altered metabolites in BD patients compared to controls, including uric acid, fructose 6-phosphate, ribose 5-phosphate, CoA, 2-oxoisovaleric acid, lactic acid, pyruvic acid, citric acid, isocitric acid, cis-aconitic acid, urea, alanine, and tryptophan. Among these, isocitrate showed the most robust association with BD in subsequent multivariate logistic regression analysis.

### Example 2

Multivariate logistic regression was employed to evaluate the independent association between the 13 significantly altered metabolites and the diagnosis of bipolar disorder. A stepwise selection-elimination method was used with a significance level of 5% for entry and removal of variables. The resulting model retained only isocitrate as a significant predictor of BD status. The odds ratio for isocitrate was 4.402 (95% confidence interval: 2.249–7.266), indicating that each unit increase in CSF isocitrate concentration was associated with a more than fourfold increase in the odds of having BD. The model’s goodness-of-fit was assessed using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, yielding a chi-square value of 12.145 with 8 degrees of freedom and a P-value of 0.145, confirming adequate model fit. Internal validity was further verified using the jack-knife (leave-one-out cross-validation) method, which demonstrated consistent predictive performance. To rule out confounding by clinical variables or medication, additional multiple regression analyses were conducted, incorporating covariates such as age at first symptoms, GAF score, total number of mood episodes, depression and mania rating scale scores, history of psychosis, substance use, and current medication (including lithium, valproic acid, lamotrigine, antidepressants, anxiolytics, and antipsychotics). None of these variables were selected in the final model, and no significant correlation was found between plasma lithium levels and CSF isocitrate, confirming that the isocitrate elevation is independent of pharmacological treatment and represents a trait marker of BD.

### Example 3

Post-mortem brain tissue from the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (Brodmann area 46) was obtained from the Stanley Medical Research Institute, comprising samples from 35 BD patients and 34 healthy controls. Total RNA was extracted, and mRNA expression levels of aconitase (ACO1, ACO2) and isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH1, IDH2, IDH3A, IDH3B, IDH3G) genes were quantified using quantitative real-time PCR. Statistical analysis using unpaired t-tests revealed no significant differences in ACO1 (P=0.178) or ACO2 (P=0.487) mRNA levels between groups. Similarly, IDH1 (P=0.755), IDH2 (P=0.279), and IDH3G (P=0.422) showed no group differences. However, IDH3A (P=0.007) and IDH3B (P=0.007) mRNA levels were significantly lower in BD patients compared to controls. Analysis of covariance, adjusting for potential confounders such as brain pH and post-mortem interval, confirmed these findings, reinforcing the specificity of IDH3A and IDH3B downregulation in the DLPFC of BD patients. This result provides a molecular explanation for the accumulation of isocitrate observed in CSF, as reduced expression of the catalytic subunits of IDH3 would impair the conversion of isocitrate to α-ketoglutarate in the citric acid cycle.

### Example 4

Protein expression of IDH3A and IDH3B was examined in post-mortem brain tissue from the cerebellum and parietal cortex (Brodmann area 7) of 15 BD patients, 15 major depressive disorder (MDD) patients, 15 schizophrenia (SCZ) patients, and 15 healthy controls, all obtained from the Stanley Medical Research Institute. Western blotting was performed using antibodies specific to IDH3A and IDH3B, with protein levels normalized to β-actin. In the cerebellum, IDH3A protein levels were significantly reduced in BD (P=0.017), MDD (P=0.006), and SCZ (P=0.001) compared to controls, as determined by one-way analysis of variance followed by post hoc testing. In contrast, IDH3B protein levels in the cerebellum did not differ significantly among the four groups. In the parietal cortex, neither IDH3A nor IDH3B protein levels showed significant differences across diagnostic categories. These findings indicate that IDH3A deficiency is a transdiagnostic feature of major psychiatric disorders in the cerebellum, but when combined with CSF isocitrate elevation—which is specific to BD—the biomarker profile achieves diagnostic specificity for bipolar disorder.

### Example 5

Genetic association analyses were conducted to investigate whether single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the ACO and IDH gene families contribute to BD susceptibility. Two independent Swedish cohorts were used: Sample Set 1 included 1,415 BD patients and 1,271 controls, and Sample Set 2 included 836 BD patients and 2,093 controls. Genotyping was performed using high-throughput platforms, with call rates exceeding 98% and no significant deviations from Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium. Initial single-marker analyses revealed nominal associations (P<0.05) for several SNPs in ACO1 and IDH2, but none survived correction for multiple testing using stringent thresholds (e.g., Bonferroni correction). Subsequent epistasis analyses, which test for gene-gene interactions, identified significant interactions between SNPs in ACO1 and IDH2 in both sample sets: in Sample Set 1, rs10970986 (ACO1) and rs2970357 (IDH2) interacted with P=0.007; in Sample Set 2, rs13302577 (ACO1) and rs2970359 (IDH2) interacted with P=0.003. These findings suggest that while common variants in ACO or IDH genes do not independently confer strong risk for BD, their interaction may modulate disease susceptibility, possibly by influencing the efficiency of the citric acid cycle.

### Example 6

To assess the potential impact of mood stabilizers on CSF metabolite levels, a preclinical study was conducted in male Sprague-Dawley rats. Animals were randomly assigned to three groups: control (saline), lithium (LiCl, 1.0 mmol/kg/day), or valproic acid (VPA, 200 mg/kg/day). Treatments were administered intraperitoneally for four weeks, a duration sufficient to achieve steady-state brain concentrations. At the end of the treatment period, CSF was collected via cisterna magna puncture under anesthesia. Metabolomic profiling using CE-TOFMS detected 64 metabolites in rat CSF. Chronic Li treatment significantly increased CSF levels of succinic acid and argininosuccinic acid, while VPA significantly altered threonine, glutamine, arginine, tryptophan, and argininosuccinic acid. Critically, CSF levels of isocitrate were unchanged in both Li- and VPA-treated groups compared to controls. This demonstrates that the isocitrate elevation observed in human BD patients is not an artifact of mood stabilizer pharmacotherapy, supporting its validity as a disease-specific biomarker.

### Example 7

In parallel with the CSF metabolomics, the effect of chronic Li and VPA treatment on brain gene expression was examined. After four weeks of treatment, rats were euthanized, and brain regions including the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus were dissected. Total RNA was extracted, and mRNA levels of Aco1, Aco2, Idh3a, and Idh3b were quantified by real-time PCR. No significant differences were found in the expression of any of these genes between drug-treated and control groups in either brain region. Specifically, Idh3a and Idh3b mRNA levels remained unchanged following Li or VPA administration, indicating that these mood stabilizers do not transcriptionally regulate the IDH3 complex. This finding further corroborates the conclusion that the reduced IDH3 expression observed in human BD post-mortem brains is a primary feature of the illness, not a secondary consequence of long-term medication exposure.

### Example 8

Based on the collective evidence from human CSF metabolomics, post-mortem brain molecular analyses, and preclinical pharmacological studies, a diagnostic and therapeutic framework for bipolar disorder was developed. The framework posits that mitochondrial dysfunction in BD, characterized by deficient IDH3 activity due to reduced expression of IDH3A and IDH3B subunits, leads to accumulation of isocitrate in the brain, which is reflected in elevated CSF levels. This metabolic signature is stable across mood states, unaffected by standard mood stabilizers, and distinct from other psychiatric disorders when considered in conjunction with regional brain protein expression patterns. Clinically, a CSF isocitrate assay can be implemented as an adjunctive diagnostic tool to improve the accuracy of BD diagnosis, particularly in complex cases. Therapeutically, compounds that enhance IDH3 expression or activity—such as small molecule activators, gene therapy vectors, or epigenetic modulators—represent a novel class of disease-modifying agents for BD. Screening assays using neuronal cell lines with CRISPR-mediated knockdown of IDH3A or IDH3B can be employed to identify such compounds, with normalization of isocitrate levels serving as a primary endpoint. This integrated approach exemplifies the translation of basic metabolic discovery into precision psychiatry applications.
