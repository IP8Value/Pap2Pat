# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The treatment of severe skin disorders, including genetic blistering diseases such as junctional epidermolysis bullosa and extensive burn injuries, has long relied on the transplantation of cultured epidermal autografts. A foundational method for expanding human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs) in vitro was established by Rheinwald and Green (R&G), which involves co-culturing primary keratinocytes with lethally irradiated murine 3T3-J2 fibroblasts as a feeder layer. This system has enabled the generation of sufficient numbers of keratinocytes for clinical transplantation and has been instrumental in life-saving interventions for patients with massive skin loss. However, despite its historical success, the R&G method incorporates several components that pose significant regulatory and safety concerns for broader therapeutic applications.

Specifically, the R&G culture system utilizes fetal bovine serum, cholera toxin, and murine 3T3 feeder cells—materials classified by the U.S. Pharmacopeia (USP <1043>) as Ancillary Materials (AMs) of Risk Tier 4, denoting high-risk biological substances due to their animal origin and undefined composition. These components introduce potential risks of zoonotic pathogen transmission, immunogenic reactions, and batch-to-batch variability, thereby limiting the regulatory approval of cultured epithelial autografts to only the most severe indications, such as burns covering more than 30% of the total body surface area or compassionate-use scenarios under strict oversight by agencies like the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA). Moreover, the removal or replacement of these high-risk materials, particularly cholera toxin, remains technically challenging within current good manufacturing practice (cGMP) frameworks, further hindering the scalability and standardization of keratinocyte-based therapies.

In response to these limitations, numerous efforts have been undertaken to develop xeno-free and chemically defined alternatives for HEK expansion. These attempts have included the use of human feeder cells, conditioned media from 3T3 fibroblasts, recombinant extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins such as fibronectin, collagens, and vitronectin, and various serum-free media formulations. Nevertheless, most reported “feeder-free” systems still depend on an initial expansion phase using the traditional 3T3 co-culture to generate a progenitor keratinocyte population before transitioning to alternative conditions. Additionally, many of these systems continue to rely on undefined supplements such as bovine pituitary extract to maintain proliferative capacity and delay senescence, thereby failing to achieve true chemical definition or eliminate xenogeneic exposure.

The native microenvironment of epidermal keratinocytes in human skin provides critical clues for designing a physiologically relevant and clinically compliant culture system. In vivo, basal keratinocytes reside on a specialized basement membrane (BM) composed of a precisely organized ECM that includes laminins, type IV and XVIII collagens, perlecan, agrin, and other proteoglycans. Among these, laminins are heterotrimeric glycoproteins consisting of α, β, and γ chains that play essential roles in cell adhesion, polarity, migration, and survival through interactions with integrin receptors. The sub-epidermal BM is enriched with specific laminin isoforms, notably LN-332 (α3β3γ2), LN-311 (α3β1γ1), LN-321 (α3β2γ1), LN-511 (α5β1γ1), and LN-521 (α5β2γ1). Of particular interest, LN-511 and LN-521 are not only present in the epidermal BM but are also ubiquitously expressed in the basement membranes of nearly all human tissues, where they serve as fundamental substrates for epithelial and endothelial cell anchorage.

Prior studies have demonstrated that recombinant human LN-511 and LN-521 can support the long-term expansion of human pluripotent stem cells in a fully defined, feeder-free manner, obviating the need for Rho kinase inhibitors or animal-derived matrices. This success suggests that analogous laminin-based strategies could be applied to primary somatic epithelial cells such as keratinocytes. However, the precise laminin repertoire required for optimal HEK adhesion, proliferation, and maintenance of progenitor identity in vitro has remained incompletely characterized. While some reports have indicated that LN-332 supports keratinocyte attachment, its utility for long-term expansion is limited, and purified recombinant forms may lack the structural complexity needed for sustained signaling. Furthermore, although murine 3T3-J2 fibroblasts express laminins such as LN-411 and LN-421 (containing the α4 chain), these isoforms are not naturally present in the human epidermal BM, raising questions about their physiological relevance and mechanism of action in supporting keratinocyte growth.

Thus, there exists a critical unmet need for a robust, reproducible, chemically defined, and entirely human-compatible culture platform that enables the expansion of functional human epidermal keratinocytes without reliance on xenogeneic or undefined components. Such a system would not only enhance the safety profile of keratinocyte-based therapies but also broaden their applicability to a wider range of dermatological, genetic, and regenerative medicine indications beyond emergency burn care.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a novel, chemically defined, and xeno-free method for the in vitro culture and expansion of human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs) without the use of feeder cells or animal-derived components. The method comprises culturing freshly isolated primary HEKs on a substrate coated with a recombinant human laminin selected from the group consisting of laminin-511 (LN-511), laminin-421 (LN-421), and combinations thereof, in a serum-free, chemically defined keratinocyte growth medium. The laminin-coated substrate serves as a synthetic mimic of the native epidermal basement membrane, providing the necessary biochemical and biophysical cues to support keratinocyte adhesion, proliferation, colony formation, and maintenance of progenitor identity over multiple passages.

In one embodiment, the laminin is provided as a purified, full-length, recombinant human protein produced in a human cell expression system, ensuring proper post-translational modifications and structural integrity. The culture medium is free of fetal bovine serum, bovine pituitary extract, cholera toxin, and any other animal-derived or undefined additives. The invention further encompasses keratinocyte populations expanded using this method, which exhibit genetic stability, normal karyotype, and the ability to form stratified, functional epidermis both in vitro (in organotypic culture) and in vivo (upon transplantation into immunodeficient animal models).

The invention also includes methods for producing epidermal equivalents or skin grafts using keratinocytes expanded on LN-511 or LN-421, as well as pharmaceutical compositions comprising such keratinocytes for use in the treatment of skin disorders, including genetic blistering diseases, chronic wounds, and large-area burns. By eliminating all xenogeneic and high-risk ancillary materials, the disclosed system meets stringent regulatory requirements for clinical-grade cell therapy products and enables broader therapeutic applications beyond the current limitations of the Rheinwald and Green method.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a fully defined, xeno-free, and feeder-free culture system for the expansion of human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs) that overcomes the limitations of conventional methods reliant on murine feeder layers and animal-derived supplements. Central to this invention is the discovery that specific recombinant human laminin isoforms—namely laminin-511 (LN-511) and laminin-421 (LN-421)—can function as standalone substrates to support the robust adhesion, proliferation, and long-term maintenance of HEK progenitor cells in a chemically defined environment. This system eliminates the need for 3T3-J2 fibroblast feeders, fetal bovine serum, cholera toxin, and other high-risk ancillary materials, thereby providing a clinically compliant platform for the production of therapeutic-grade keratinocytes.

Laminins are large, cross-shaped heterotrimeric glycoproteins composed of one α, one β, and one γ chain, which assemble into at least 16 distinct isoforms in humans. Each isoform exhibits unique tissue distribution and functional properties mediated through interactions with cell surface receptors, primarily integrins and dystroglycan. In the context of human skin, the epidermal basement membrane is enriched with laminins containing the α3 and α5 chains, including LN-332 (α3β3γ2), LN-311 (α3β1γ1), LN-321 (α3β2γ1), LN-511 (α5β1γ1), and LN-521 (α5β2γ1). These laminins engage keratinocyte-expressed integrins such as α3β1, α6β4, and α6β1 to mediate adhesion, signal transduction, and cytoskeletal organization.

Contrary to prior assumptions that only epidermal-specific laminins like LN-332 are suitable for keratinocyte culture, the inventors have discovered that LN-511 and LN-421—despite differing in chain composition—both effectively support HEK expansion. Notably, LN-511 is a naturally occurring component of the human epidermal basement membrane, whereas LN-421 (α4β2γ1) is not endogenously expressed in healthy human skin but is abundantly secreted by murine 3T3-J2 fibroblasts used in the traditional R&G system. This finding reveals that the supportive function of 3T3 feeders may be largely attributable to their deposition of LN-421 (and related α4-chain laminins) onto the culture surface, rather than solely to paracrine signaling or direct cell-cell contact.

The laminins used in the present invention are recombinant human proteins, preferably produced in human cell lines (e.g., HEK293 or HT-1080) to ensure authentic glycosylation, sulfation, and polymerization capabilities. The laminins are purified to homogeneity and formulated in aqueous buffer at concentrations suitable for coating standard tissue culture plasticware. In a preferred embodiment, culture surfaces are coated with LN-511 or LN-421 at a concentration of approximately 2.5 µg/cm², incubated overnight at 4°C, and rinsed prior to cell seeding. This coating density has been empirically optimized to maximize cell adhesion while minimizing non-specific binding or cost inefficiencies.

Freshly isolated primary HEKs—obtained from surgical waste skin samples following ethical approval and informed consent—are seeded directly onto the laminin-coated surfaces without any prior expansion on feeder layers. The cells are maintained in a chemically defined keratinocyte growth medium, such as Keratinocyte Growth Medium-Chemically Defined (KGM-CD), which contains no animal-derived components, serum, or undefined extracts. The medium includes essential nutrients, insulin, hydrocortisone, epidermal growth factor (EGF), and other defined supplements necessary for keratinocyte proliferation and survival. Critically, the medium does not contain cholera toxin, bovine pituitary extract, or fetal bovine serum, thereby eliminating major sources of regulatory concern.

Keratinocytes cultured on LN-511 or LN-421 exhibit robust adhesion within 24 hours of seeding and form confluent monolayers within 5–7 days. Cells can be passaged using gentle enzymatic dissociation (e.g., TrypLE Select) and reseeded at low densities (e.g., 1 × 10⁴ cells/cm²) to achieve high population doublings over multiple passages (≥10 passages demonstrated). Both LN-511 and LN-421 support colony-forming efficiency comparable to the 3T3 co-culture system, indicating preservation of clonogenic progenitor cells. Karyotype analysis confirms genetic stability, with no detectable chromosomal abnormalities after extended culture.

At the molecular level, keratinocytes expanded on LN-511 or LN-421 maintain high expression of basal progenitor markers, including keratin 5 (KRT5), keratin 14 (KRT14), and the transcription factor p63 (TP63), as confirmed by quantitative PCR, immunocytochemistry, and flow cytometry. Concurrently, expression of early differentiation markers such as keratin 1 (KRT1) and keratin 10 (KRT10) remains low during active proliferation but increases appropriately upon confluence or induction of differentiation, demonstrating regulated lineage progression. RNA sequencing reveals that the transcriptomic profiles of keratinocytes grown on LN-511 and LN-421 are highly similar (Spearman correlation ρ = 0.99) and distinct from those grown in the 3T3 co-culture system. Notably, laminin-cultured keratinocytes show significant downregulation of pro-inflammatory pathways (e.g., interferon response, TNFα/NFκB signaling) and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) genes, along with upregulation of MYC targets and cell cycle regulators, suggesting a more proliferative and less stressed cellular state.

Functionally, keratinocytes expanded using the laminin system retain full regenerative capacity. In organotypic culture, when seeded onto de-epidermalized dermis and raised to the air-liquid interface, they stratify into a multilayered epidermis with distinct stratum basale, spinosum, granulosum, and corneum, indistinguishable in thickness and marker expression from epidermis generated using the 3T3 method. In vivo, when transplanted onto nude mice via a flap grafting technique, these keratinocytes form a fully stratified, human-origin epidermis that expresses basal and differentiation markers and secretes a continuous basement membrane containing laminin γ2 chain—a hallmark of functional LN-332 deposition and epidermal maturation.

The invention further encompasses the production of epidermal equivalents or skin grafts using this platform. For transplantation, keratinocytes are expanded on laminin-coated fibrin matrices, which provide a biocompatible, resorbable scaffold. The resulting grafts are free of murine cells and animal proteins, reducing immunogenicity and enhancing safety. These grafts can be used to treat a wide range of conditions, including recessive dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa, junctional epidermolysis bullosa, chronic venous ulcers, diabetic foot ulcers, and large-area thermal burns.

The method is scalable, reproducible, and compatible with current good manufacturing practices (cGMP). It enables the production of clinical-grade keratinocytes for autologous or allogeneic cell therapy without the regulatory burdens associated with xenogeneic materials. Moreover, the defined nature of the system facilitates quality control, process validation, and regulatory submission, accelerating the translation of keratinocyte-based therapies to routine clinical use.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Isolation and Culture of Human Epidermal Keratinocytes on Recombinant Laminin Substrates**

Primary human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs) were isolated from discarded skin tissue obtained during cosmetic surgery procedures, with informed consent and institutional review board approval. Briefly, skin samples (up to 4 cm²) were washed in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and incubated overnight at 4°C in Dispase II (2.5 mg/mL in DMEM) to separate the epidermis from the dermis. The epidermal sheet was then treated with 0.05% trypsin-EDTA for 15 minutes at 37°C to dissociate keratinocytes, which were collected by centrifugation and resuspended in KGM-CD medium (Lonza).

Six-well tissue culture plates were coated overnight at 4°C with recombinant human laminins (BioLamina AB) at 2.5 µg/cm²: LN-111 (negative control), LN-332, LN-411, LN-421, LN-511, or LN-521. Non-coated plates and plates co-cultured with lethally irradiated (60 Gy) 3T3-J2 fibroblasts in FAD medium (DMEM/Ham’s F12, 3:1, supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, insulin, adenine, hydrocortisone, triiodothyronine, cholera toxin, EGF, and penicillin-streptomycin) served as additional controls. Freshly isolated HEKs were seeded at 9 × 10⁴ cells/cm² and cultured at 37°C, 10% CO₂.

After 7 days, cells cultured on LN-511 and LN-421 exhibited robust adhesion and spreading, forming confluent monolayers. In contrast, cells on non-coated plastic, LN-111, or LN-332 failed to adhere or survive. LN-411 and LN-521 supported initial adhesion but showed increased terminal differentiation and reduced long-term expansion compared to LN-511 and LN-421. No additive effects were observed when laminins were combined with other ECM proteins (e.g., fibronectin, collagen I, heparan sulfate). Thus, LN-511 and LN-421 were selected for further characterization.

**Example 2: Proliferation, Colony Formation, and Genetic Stability**

HEKs cultured on LN-511, LN-421, or 3T3 feeders were passaged every 5–7 days at 1 × 10⁴ cells/cm². Population doubling was calculated as PD = 3.32 × log(N/N₀), where N is the number of harvested cells and N₀ is the number of seeded cells. Over 10 passages, LN-511 and LN-421 supported cumulative population doublings comparable to the 3T3 system.

Colony-forming efficiency (CFE) was assessed by seeding 200 cells per 60-mm dish with irradiated 3T3 feeders in FAD medium (to standardize assay conditions). After 14 days, colonies were fixed and stained with Rhodamine B. CFE for LN-511 and LN-421 cultures was 8.5% and 8.2%, respectively, versus 8.7% for 3T3-expanded cells, confirming preservation of clonogenic potential.

Karyotype analysis was performed on metaphase spreads from passage 5 cells. G-banding revealed normal diploid karyotypes (46,XX or 46,XY) with no structural abnormalities in all laminin-cultured samples, matching the 3T3 control.

**Example 3: Molecular and Phenotypic Characterization**

Quantitative PCR on passages 1, 5, and 10 showed sustained expression of basal markers KRT5 and KRT14 and TP63 in LN-511 and LN-421 cultures, with gradual upregulation of KRT1 and KRT10 upon confluence—mirroring the 3T3 control. Flow cytometry confirmed >90% of cells expressed KRT14 and p63.

RNA sequencing of biological replicates (n=3–4 per condition) revealed high similarity between LN-511 and LN-421 (60 differentially expressed genes, FDR < 0.05), but substantial divergence from 3T3 cultures (7,587–7,694 DE genes). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) showed significant downregulation of inflammatory and EMT pathways and upregulation of MYC and E2F targets in laminin-cultured cells.

**Example 4: Functional Validation in Organotypic and In Vivo Models**

For organotypic culture, de-epidermalized dermis (DED) was seeded with human dermal fibroblasts, followed by HEKs (2 × 10⁵ cells) expanded on LN-511, LN-421, or 3T3. After 7 days submerged and 14 days at air-liquid interface, H&E staining showed fully stratified epidermis with normal architecture. Epidermal thickness was equivalent across groups (mean ~45 µm).

For in vivo testing, HEKs were seeded on laminin-coated fibrin mats and grafted onto nude mice using a flap technique. At 14 days post-transplantation, H&E and human Ku80 immunostaining confirmed human epidermal regeneration. Basal (KRT14) and suprabasal (KRT10) markers were appropriately localized, and a continuous laminin γ2-positive basement membrane was observed, indicating functional maturation.

These examples demonstrate that the laminin-based culture system supports the expansion of therapeutically competent HEKs without xenogeneic components, fulfilling the requirements for clinical translation.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a chemically defined, xeno-free, and feeder-free method for the expansion of human epidermal keratinocytes using recombinant human laminin-511 (LN-511) or laminin-421 (LN-421) as a culture substrate. This system eliminates the need for murine 3T3-J2 fibroblast feeders, fetal bovine serum, cholera toxin, and other high-risk ancillary materials, thereby addressing critical safety and regulatory barriers associated with conventional keratinocyte culture methods. Keratinocytes expanded using this platform exhibit robust proliferation, high colony-forming efficiency, genetic stability, and preserved progenitor identity, as evidenced by the sustained expression of basal markers such as KRT5, KRT14, and p63. Transcriptomic profiling reveals a less inflammatory and more proliferative cellular state compared to cells cultured in the traditional 3T3 co-culture system. Functionally, these keratinocytes are capable of generating fully stratified, functional epidermis both in vitro in organotypic culture and in vivo upon transplantation into immunodeficient mice, demonstrating intact regenerative capacity and basement membrane formation. The invention further encompasses epidermal equivalents, skin grafts, and pharmaceutical compositions derived from this culture system for the treatment of a broad spectrum of skin disorders, including genetic blistering diseases, chronic wounds, and extensive burns. By providing a clinically compliant, scalable, and reproducible platform, the disclosed technology enables the safe and effective application of keratinocyte-based therapies beyond emergency burn care to a wide range of regenerative medicine indications.
