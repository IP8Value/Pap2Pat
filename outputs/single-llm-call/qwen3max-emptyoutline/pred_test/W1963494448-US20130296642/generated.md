## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Incisional hernias represent a significant clinical burden in general surgical practice, with reported incidence rates ranging from two to eleven percent following abdominal surgery. Despite advances in surgical techniques, particularly the widespread adoption of prosthetic mesh reinforcement, recurrence rates remain alarmingly high, with estimates varying between ten and fifty percent across different patient populations and surgical settings. This persistent problem translates into substantial healthcare costs, with projections suggesting that approximately one hundred thousand incisional hernia repairs are performed annually in the United States alone, generating an estimated economic burden of $2.5 billion. The high recurrence rate indicates that current surgical approaches, while improved over historical methods, fail to address fundamental underlying biological factors that predispose certain individuals to hernia formation and recurrence.

Multiple risk factors have been associated with incisional hernia development, including wound infection, abdominal distension, pulmonary complications, male gender, advanced age, and obesity. However, the literature remains inconsistent regarding specific predictors of recurrent hernia formation, with conflicting evidence surrounding the roles of body mass index, presence of ascites, large hernia defects exceeding ten centimeters in dimension, continued tobacco use, occupational heavy lifting, and various wound-healing complications such as hematoma, seroma, or infection. This inconsistency suggests that conventional clinical risk factors may not fully capture the biological mechanisms driving hernia recurrence, pointing toward potential intrinsic molecular or genetic predispositions that remain poorly understood.

Current scientific understanding posits that incisional hernias commonly result from failure of early surgical wound healing processes. The structural integrity of healing tissue depends critically on the composition and organization of extracellular matrix components, particularly collagen fibers. Collagen type I provides the primary tensile strength to mature connective tissue, while collagen type III represents the immature, more pliable form predominant in early wound healing phases. Previous investigations have demonstrated that patients with both direct and indirect inguinal hernias exhibit a decreased collagen I-to-III ratio compared to control subjects, attributed primarily to relative increases in collagen III synthesis. This altered collagen ratio has also been observed in incisional hernias, supporting the hypothesis that a subset of patients may possess an inherent biological susceptibility to hernia formation due to aberrant wound healing responses.

Immunohistochemical studies have provided preliminary evidence for this hypothesis, with one investigation examining skin and fascial specimens from sixteen incisional hernia patients compared to normal foregut tissue from bariatric surgery patients. This study found a significantly decreased collagen I/III ratio in the skin of hernia patients but failed to detect differences in fascial tissue, highlighting the complexity of tissue-specific responses and the limitations of protein-level analysis alone. While patients with known connective tissue disorders such as Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, osteogenesis imperfecta, and Marfan’s syndrome demonstrate increased hernia prevalence, no systematic data exist regarding potential genetic or molecular predispositions in otherwise healthy individuals who develop recurrent incisional hernias.

The molecular biology underlying hernia repair and recurrence remains largely uncharacterized. Technical factors such as surgical approach, mesh type, and fixation methods have been extensively studied, yet these variables cannot fully explain the persistent recurrence rates observed in clinical practice. This gap in understanding suggests that intrinsic biological factors, potentially involving subtle alterations in gene expression profiles that affect wound healing pathways, may play a critical role in determining individual susceptibility to hernia formation and recurrence. However, prior to the present invention, no comprehensive genomic approach had been employed to systematically investigate gene expression differences between patients with recurrent incisional hernias and appropriate control populations.

Traditional approaches to understanding hernia pathogenesis have focused primarily on clinical risk factors, mechanical properties of repair materials, and gross histological examination of tissue specimens. These methods, while valuable, fail to capture the dynamic molecular processes that govern tissue repair and remodeling at the transcriptional level. The absence of genome-wide expression profiling in hernia research has left a critical void in our understanding of the biological mechanisms that differentiate patients who heal normally from those who develop recurrent hernias. This knowledge gap has prevented the development of predictive biomarkers that could identify high-risk patients preoperatively and has limited the potential for targeted therapeutic interventions designed to modulate specific molecular pathways involved in aberrant wound healing.

The present invention addresses this fundamental limitation by providing the first comprehensive genomic analysis of gene expression profiles in patients with recurrent incisional hernias compared to appropriate control subjects. Through the application of microarray technology, quantitative PCR validation, and advanced bioinformatic analysis, the invention identifies specific molecular signatures that distinguish recurrent hernia patients from controls, revealing novel insights into the biological mechanisms underlying hernia formation and recurrence. This approach represents a paradigm shift from purely clinical and mechanical considerations to a molecular understanding of hernia pathogenesis, opening new avenues for risk stratification, personalized treatment planning, and targeted therapeutic development.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for identifying patients at risk for recurrent incisional hernia formation through the detection of specific gene expression profiles in skin and fascial tissue. The invention is based on the discovery that patients with recurrent incisional hernias exhibit distinct molecular signatures characterized by differential expression of genes involved in wound healing, extracellular matrix remodeling, and developmental signaling pathways. Specifically, the invention identifies Gremlin 1 (GREM1) as a novel biomarker that is significantly downregulated in both skin and fascial tissue of recurrent hernia patients compared to normal controls. Additionally, the invention demonstrates that the ratio of collagen type I alpha 2 chain (COL1A2) to collagen type III alpha 1 chain (COL3A1) is significantly decreased in recurrent hernia patients, consistent with established literature on collagen composition in hernia tissue.

The invention provides methods for diagnosing or predicting susceptibility to recurrent incisional hernia by measuring the expression levels of GREM1, COL1A2, and COL3A1 in biological samples obtained from patients. In particular embodiments, the invention encompasses diagnostic methods that combine measurements of GREM1 and COL3A1 expression to achieve high accuracy in distinguishing between recurrent hernia patients and normal controls. The invention further provides kits containing reagents and instructions for performing these diagnostic assays, including primers, probes, antibodies, and standardized protocols for sample processing and analysis.

The methods of the invention involve obtaining skin or fascial tissue samples from patients, typically during surgical procedures, and analyzing the expression levels of specific biomarker genes using techniques such as quantitative real-time PCR, microarray analysis, or immunohistochemistry. The invention specifically contemplates the use of tissue samples obtained from sites remote from the hernia defect to avoid confounding effects of local inflammatory or healing processes. The diagnostic methods can be used preoperatively to stratify patients into high-risk and low-risk categories for hernia recurrence, enabling personalized surgical planning and postoperative management strategies.

In addition to diagnostic applications, the invention provides insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying recurrent incisional hernia formation. The identification of GREM1 as a key differentially expressed gene suggests a previously unrecognized role for bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) signaling pathways in hernia pathogenesis. GREM1 functions as a BMP antagonist, and its downregulation in recurrent hernia patients may lead to altered BMP signaling that affects tissue remodeling and wound healing processes. This discovery opens new avenues for therapeutic intervention targeting BMP signaling pathways to prevent or treat recurrent incisional hernias.

The invention also encompasses methods for monitoring treatment response and disease progression through serial measurement of biomarker expression levels. By establishing baseline expression profiles and tracking changes over time, clinicians can assess the effectiveness of therapeutic interventions and make informed decisions about treatment modifications. The invention further provides computer-implemented methods for analyzing gene expression data and generating diagnostic classifications using statistical algorithms such as quadratic discriminant analysis.

Overall, the invention represents a significant advancement in the understanding and management of recurrent incisional hernias by providing the first comprehensive molecular characterization of this condition and establishing novel biomarkers for diagnosis, risk stratification, and therapeutic targeting. The methods and compositions described herein enable a precision medicine approach to hernia management, moving beyond traditional clinical risk factors to incorporate molecular profiling for improved patient outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Abbreviations and Definitions

As used herein, the term “recurrent incisional hernia” (RH) refers to the protrusion of abdominal contents through a previous surgical incision site that has recurred following at least one prior surgical repair attempt. The term “normal control” (NC) refers to individuals who have no history of hernia formation and who undergo abdominal surgery for conditions unrelated to hernia, such as laparoscopic cholecystectomy. The phrase “skin and fascia” encompasses the epidermal, dermal, and subcutaneous layers of skin along with the underlying fascial tissue, specifically the anterior rectus sheath in the context of abdominal wall anatomy. The term “gene expression profile” refers to the complete set of RNA transcripts produced by the genome under specific circumstances or in a specific cell, which can be measured using techniques such as microarray analysis, quantitative PCR, or RNA sequencing.

The term “differentially expressed gene” refers to a gene whose expression level is statistically significantly different between two or more experimental conditions, such as between RH and NC patients. The phrase “fold change” describes the ratio of expression levels between two conditions, with values greater than 1 indicating upregulation and values less than 1 indicating downregulation in the experimental condition relative to the control. The term “collagen I/III ratio” refers to the relative abundance of collagen type I to collagen type III, either at the mRNA level (measured as COL1A1/COL3A1 or COL1A2/COL3A1 ratios) or at the protein level (measured by immunohistochemistry or other protein quantification methods).

The term “GREM1” refers to Gremlin 1, also known as cysteine knot superfamily 1, BMP antagonist 1 (CKTSF1B1), induced in high glucose 2 (IHG-2), or downregulated by v-mos (DRM), encoded by the GREM1 gene located on human chromosome 15q13.3. The terms “COL1A1” and “COL1A2” refer to the alpha 1 and alpha 2 chains of type I collagen, respectively, while “COL3A1” refers to the alpha 1 chain of type III collagen. The phrase “quantitative real-time PCR” (qPCR) refers to a laboratory technique used to amplify and simultaneously quantify a targeted DNA molecule, allowing for precise measurement of initial template amounts through fluorescence detection during amplification cycles.

The term “microarray analysis” refers to a high-throughput technique for measuring the expression levels of thousands of genes simultaneously by hybridizing labeled nucleic acid samples to arrays of known DNA sequences immobilized on a solid surface. The phrase “immunohistochemistry” (IHC) refers to a method that uses antibodies to detect specific antigens in tissue sections, allowing for visualization and localization of protein expression within cellular and tissue contexts. The term “quadratic discriminant analysis” (QDA) refers to a statistical classification method that assumes different covariance matrices for each class and finds the optimal decision boundary that maximizes separation between groups.

The phrase “leave-one-out cross-validation” refers to a model validation technique where each observation in the dataset is sequentially left out as a test case while the remaining observations are used to train the model, providing an unbiased estimate of model performance. The term “biological process” refers to a series of molecular events with a defined beginning and end, pertinent to the functioning of integrated living units such as cells, tissues, organs, and organisms. The phrase “molecular function” describes the biochemical activity of gene products at the molecular level, such as binding or catalysis. The term “cellular component” refers to the parts of a cell or its extracellular environment in which a gene product is active.

### Methods

The present invention provides methods for diagnosing or predicting susceptibility to recurrent incisional hernia formation through the analysis of gene expression profiles in skin and fascial tissue samples. The methods involve obtaining biological samples from patients, processing these samples to extract and analyze nucleic acids or proteins, and interpreting the results to determine the likelihood of recurrent hernia formation or to confirm a diagnosis of recurrent hernia susceptibility.

In practicing the methods of the invention, skin and fascial tissue samples are obtained from patients undergoing abdominal surgery. For diagnostic purposes, samples may be collected from patients presenting with recurrent incisional hernias (RH patients) or from patients with no history of hernia formation who are undergoing unrelated abdominal procedures (NC patients). The tissue samples are preferably obtained from sites remote from the hernia defect or previous surgical incisions to avoid confounding effects of local inflammatory processes, wound healing responses, or scar tissue formation. In laparoscopic procedures, samples may be collected from trocar placement sites at the beginning of the surgical procedure, prior to any manipulation that might induce local biological responses.

Following collection, tissue samples are immediately processed to preserve nucleic acid integrity. In preferred embodiments, samples are divided and placed in either 10% buffered formalin for subsequent immunohistochemical analysis or in RNA stabilization reagents such as RNALater™ for nucleic acid extraction. Samples stored in RNALater™ may be maintained at room temperature for up to 48 hours before further processing. For RNA isolation, approximately 100-150 mg of tissue is processed using commercially available RNA extraction kits, such as the RNeasy® Lipid Tissue Mini Kit, following manufacturer protocols that include mechanical homogenization and on-column DNase treatment to remove genomic DNA contamination.

Total RNA extracted from tissue samples may be subjected to amplification when sample quantities are limited, using systems such as the WT-Ovation™ Pico RNA Amplification System. Amplified RNA is then converted to cDNA for downstream analysis. Gene expression analysis may be performed using various techniques, including microarray analysis, quantitative real-time PCR, and PCR array analysis. For microarray analysis, biotin-labeled cDNA is hybridized to whole genome expression BeadChips, such as the Sentrix® Human-6 v.2 platform, and hybridization signals are detected and quantified using appropriate imaging systems.

Quantitative real-time PCR analysis is performed using standard protocols with appropriate reference genes for normalization. For analysis of specific genes of interest such as GREM1 and COL1A, the SuperScript™ III Platinum® Two-Step qPCR Kit with SYBR® Green detection may be employed, using GAPDH or other suitable reference genes. PCR array analysis focusing on wound healing and fibrosis-related genes may be performed using specialized arrays such as the Human Fibrosis RT2 Profiler™ PCR Array System, which interrogates the expression of 84 key genes involved in dysregulated tissue remodeling during wound healing.

Immunohistochemical analysis of protein expression is performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections. Sections are cut at 4 μm thickness and processed using automated immunohistochemistry systems such as the Autostainer/Envision Plus method with horseradish peroxidase detection. Primary antibodies specific for collagen type I, collagen type III, or other proteins of interest are applied according to manufacturer recommendations, and staining intensity is evaluated qualitatively or quantitatively using appropriate scoring systems.

Statistical analysis of gene expression data involves several steps to ensure robust identification of differentially expressed genes. Initially, genes with low detection rates across samples (e.g., detectable in less than 50% of samples) are excluded to reduce false positives. Nonspecific filtering removes genes with minimal variability across samples. Differential expression analysis is performed using moderated t-statistics applied to log2-transformed normalized intensity values, with adjustment for multiple testing using the false discovery rate method of Benjamini and Hochberg. Genes are considered differentially expressed if they meet statistical significance thresholds (e.g., q < 30%) and demonstrate biologically relevant fold changes (e.g., ≥ 1.5-fold).

Gene ontology analysis is performed on lists of differentially expressed genes to identify enriched biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components. This analysis helps interpret the biological significance of observed gene expression changes by identifying common functional themes among differentially expressed genes. Statistical validation of microarray findings is performed using independent techniques such as qPCR and PCR array analysis, with correlation between platforms assessed using Pearson correlation coefficients.

For diagnostic classification, the invention provides methods that combine measurements of multiple biomarkers to achieve optimal discrimination between RH and NC patients. Quadratic discriminant analysis is employed to determine the optimal decision boundary that separates patient groups based on biomarker expression levels. Leave-one-out cross-validation is used to estimate the accuracy of classification models, providing unbiased performance estimates that account for the limited sample sizes typical in clinical studies.

The methods of the invention may be implemented in clinical settings to provide preoperative risk assessment for patients scheduled for abdominal surgery. By identifying patients with molecular profiles characteristic of recurrent hernia susceptibility, surgeons can modify their surgical approach, select appropriate mesh types, or implement additional preventive measures to reduce recurrence risk. The methods may also be used postoperatively to monitor healing responses and identify early signs of abnormal wound healing that might predispose to hernia formation.

## EXAMPLES

### Patient Samples and Tissue Acquisition

The methods and compositions of the present invention were developed and validated through a comprehensive clinical study involving thirty-three human subjects who provided informed consent under Institutional Review Board approval. The study population consisted of two distinct groups: eighteen patients diagnosed with recurrent incisional hernias (RH group) and fifteen healthy control patients with no history of hernia formation (NC group). The RH patients were eligible for inclusion if they were eighteen years of age or older and scheduled to undergo laparoscopic repair of a recurrent ventral or incisional hernia. Control patients were selected from individuals undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy for benign gallbladder disease, representing a population with no known predisposition to hernia formation.

Exclusion criteria were carefully defined to minimize confounding variables that could affect wound healing and gene expression profiles. Patients were excluded if they were under eighteen years of age, had a history of chronic steroid use, suffered from severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), had known pulmonary or connective tissue disorders, or were incarcerated individuals. These exclusion criteria ensured that the study population represented otherwise healthy individuals whose gene expression profiles would reflect intrinsic biological differences related specifically to hernia susceptibility rather than secondary effects of systemic diseases or medications.

Tissue acquisition was performed using standardized protocols to ensure sample quality and minimize procedural variability. Approximately one square centimeter of full-thickness skin and underlying fascia was harvested from each patient at the beginning of the surgical procedure, prior to any surgical manipulation that might induce local biological responses. Critically, the tissue samples were collected from trocar placement sites that were anatomically remote from the hernia defect or any previous surgical incisions. This strategic sampling approach eliminated potential confounding effects from local inflammatory processes, ongoing wound healing responses, or scar tissue formation that might otherwise obscure the intrinsic molecular signatures associated with hernia susceptibility.

Immediately following collection, each tissue sample was divided into two portions for parallel processing. One portion was placed in 10% buffered formalin for subsequent immunohistochemical analysis, while the other portion was immersed in RNALater™ RNA Stabilization Reagent to preserve RNA integrity for molecular analysis. Samples stored in RNALater™ were maintained at room temperature for up to forty-eight hours before further processing, providing flexibility in sample handling while maintaining nucleic acid quality. For RNA isolation procedures, approximately 100-150 milligrams of tissue was allocated from each sample, ensuring sufficient material for comprehensive molecular analysis while minimizing the impact of tissue removal on the surgical procedure.

Demographic characteristics of the study population were carefully documented to assess potential confounding factors. The enrolled cohort consisted predominantly of female patients (26 out of 33 total participants), reflecting the demographic distribution typical of patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy and incisional hernia repair in the study institution. Age distribution ranged from 39 to 55 years between the NC and RH groups, with no statistically significant differences observed. Body mass index (BMI) showed a trend toward higher values in the RH group (mean BMI of 36.6 versus 30.5 in NC), though this difference did not reach statistical significance in the subset analyzed by microarray. Notably, the RH group demonstrated significantly higher prevalence of diabetes (7 out of 18 patients versus 0 out of 15 in NC, p=0.01) and previous abdominal surgeries (18 out of 18 versus 6 out of 15, p=0.01), which are clinically expected differences given the nature of recurrent hernia patients compared to elective cholecystectomy patients.

The tissue acquisition protocol was specifically designed to address limitations of previous studies that examined tissue directly adjacent to hernia defects. By sampling from anatomically normal sites distant from the hernia, the present invention captures the systemic or constitutional molecular characteristics that predispose individuals to hernia formation, rather than merely documenting the local consequences of hernia presence. This approach represents a fundamental methodological advance in hernia research, enabling the identification of true predisposing factors rather than secondary adaptive responses.

### RNA Isolation and RNA Amplification

RNA isolation and amplification procedures were performed using standardized protocols to ensure high-quality nucleic acid preparations suitable for sensitive downstream molecular analyses. Total RNA was extracted from skin and fascial tissue specimens using the RNeasy® Lipid Tissue Mini Kit according to manufacturer specifications, with modifications optimized for fibrous connective tissue samples. The extraction process began with mechanical homogenization of tissue samples using a rotor-stator homogenizer to disrupt the dense extracellular matrix characteristic of skin and fascia. This mechanical disruption was essential for efficient lysis and release of intracellular RNA from fibroblasts, keratinocytes, and other resident cell populations.

Following homogenization, samples underwent on-column DNase treatment during the purification process to eliminate genomic DNA contamination that could interfere with subsequent quantitative PCR analysis. This enzymatic digestion step was critical for ensuring that measured transcript levels accurately reflected RNA abundance rather than residual DNA amplification. RNA yield and purity were assessed using spectrophotometric analysis with the NanoDrop ND-2000 instrument, with acceptable samples demonstrating A260/A280 ratios between 1.8 and 2.0 and A260/A230 ratios greater than 1.8, indicating minimal protein or organic solvent contamination.

RNA integrity was rigorously evaluated using the Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 with Eukaryotic RNA Pico Series II reagents, generating RNA Integrity Number (RIN) values that ranged from 5 to 8 across the study samples. While RIN values below 7 are generally considered suboptimal for some applications, the moderate degradation observed in these surgical specimens was deemed acceptable given the challenging nature of RNA preservation in clinical tissue samples and the robustness of the downstream amplification and analysis methods employed.

For samples with limited RNA quantities, whole transcriptome amplification was performed using the WT-Ovation™ Pico RNA Amplification System, which employs a single-primer isothermal amplification approach to generate microgram quantities of amplified cDNA from nanogram inputs of total RNA. This linear amplification method preserves relative transcript abundance while providing sufficient material for multiple downstream analyses, including microarray hybridization and quantitative PCR validation. The amplification protocol followed manufacturer recommendations with minor modifications to optimize yield and fidelity for connective tissue-derived RNA.

Quality control measures were implemented throughout the RNA processing workflow to ensure consistency and reliability of results. Negative controls consisting of extraction reagents without tissue input were processed alongside experimental samples to monitor for contamination. Positive control RNA samples with known concentration and integrity were included in each batch to validate extraction efficiency and amplification performance. All RNA samples were aliquoted and stored at -80°C to prevent freeze-thaw degradation, with separate aliquots designated for different analytical platforms to minimize repeated thawing of precious clinical specimens.

The RNA isolation and amplification protocols described herein represent optimized methods specifically tailored for the challenging matrix of skin and fascial tissue, which contains abundant collagen and other structural proteins that can interfere with standard nucleic acid extraction procedures. These optimized protocols enabled successful molecular analysis of clinical specimens that might otherwise yield insufficient or degraded RNA for reliable gene expression profiling.

### Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical analysis was performed to validate molecular findings at the protein level and to provide spatial context for gene expression changes observed in RNA-based assays. Tissue specimens preserved in 10% buffered formalin were processed through standard histological protocols, including dehydration through graded alcohol series, clearing in xylene, and embedding in paraffin wax. Serial sections of 4 micrometers thickness were cut using a rotary microtome and mounted on charged glass slides to ensure optimal tissue adherence during subsequent staining procedures.

Antigen retrieval was performed to unmask epitopes that may have been obscured during formalin fixation and paraffin embedding. Heat-induced epitope retrieval was accomplished using citrate buffer (pH 6.0) in a pressure cooker or microwave system, followed by gradual cooling to room temperature. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked using 3% hydrogen peroxide in methanol for 15 minutes to prevent nonspecific background staining from endogenous enzyme activity.

Primary antibodies specific for collagen type I and collagen type III were applied to tissue sections using the automated Dakocytomation Autostainer/Envision Plus detection system, which employs a horseradish peroxidase-labeled polymer backbone conjugated to secondary antibodies. This amplification system provides enhanced sensitivity compared to traditional avidin-biotin complex methods while reducing background staining. Antibody concentrations and incubation times were optimized through titration experiments to achieve maximal specific signal with minimal nonspecific binding.

Detection was accomplished using 3,3'-diaminobenzidine (DAB) chromogen, which produces a brown precipitate at sites of antibody binding, followed by hematoxylin counterstaining to visualize tissue architecture. Slides were dehydrated through graded alcohols, cleared in xylene, and coverslipped using permanent mounting medium. Stained sections were evaluated by board-certified pathologists blinded to patient group assignment, with staining intensity scored on a semi-quantitative scale ranging from 0 (no staining) to 3+ (intense staining).

Immunohistochemical analysis confirmed the molecular findings regarding collagen composition in recurrent hernia patients. Examination of five representative patient samples demonstrated slightly greater staining intensity for collagen type III compared to collagen type I in both skin and fascial tissue from RH patients relative to NC controls. This protein-level observation corroborated the RNA expression data showing increased COL3A1 relative to COL1A1 and COL1A2 transcripts in RH patients, providing orthogonal validation of the molecular signature associated with recurrent hernia susceptibility.

The immunohistochemical protocol was specifically designed to enable direct comparison between RNA-based and protein-based measurements of collagen composition, addressing potential concerns about post-transcriptional regulation that might decouple mRNA abundance from protein levels. The concordance between molecular and immunohistochemical findings strengthens the biological relevance of the identified gene expression signatures and supports their utility as diagnostic biomarkers.

### Statistical Analysis of Microarray Data

Statistical analysis of microarray gene expression data was performed using rigorous bioinformatic approaches to ensure robust identification of differentially expressed genes while controlling for multiple testing artifacts. Raw intensity data from Illumina Sentrix® Human-6 v.2 Whole Genome Expression BeadChips were imported into the R open-source statistical computing environment for comprehensive analysis. Initial quality control assessments included evaluation of array-wide intensity distributions, spatial artifact detection, and inter-array correlation analysis to identify potential technical outliers.

Genes with poor detection characteristics were systematically excluded from further analysis to minimize false positive findings. Specifically, any gene annotated as “not detectable” (defined as Illumina software detection p-value > 0.01) across more than 50% of patient samples was removed from the dataset. This stringent filtering criterion ensured that only reliably measurable transcripts were considered in subsequent differential expression analysis. Additional nonspecific filtering was applied to remove genes exhibiting minimal variability across samples, as these transcripts are unlikely to contribute meaningfully to biological discrimination between patient groups.

Differential expression analysis was conducted using the limma package in R, which implements a moderated t-statistic approach that borrows information across genes to stabilize variance estimates, particularly important given the relatively small sample sizes typical in clinical studies. Log2-transformed normalized intensity values were used as input for statistical testing, with empirical Bayes moderation applied to improve power and reduce false discovery rates. Multiple testing correction was performed using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method, with a significance threshold of q < 0.30 established a priori based on the exploratory nature of the pilot study and the expectation of a relatively small number of truly differentially expressed genes.

Genes were declared differentially expressed if they met both statistical significance criteria (q < 0.30) and demonstrated biologically relevant magnitude of change (absolute fold change ≥ 1.5). This dual threshold approach balanced statistical rigor with biological relevance, ensuring that identified genes represented both statistically reliable and potentially functionally important differences between patient groups. The analysis revealed 167 differentially expressed features in skin tissue (142 complete genes and 25 expressed sequence tags) but only 7 differentially expressed features in fascial tissue (6 complete genes and 1 EST), highlighting the greater molecular heterogeneity and discriminatory power of skin tissue in this context.

Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed on the list of differentially expressed genes from skin tissue using the topGO package in R, implementing the weight01 algorithm to account for the hierarchical structure of GO terms. Enrichment was assessed for three independent ontologies: biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular component (CC). Statistical significance was determined using Fisher’s exact test, with p-values adjusted for multiple testing using the same FDR approach applied to gene-level analysis. Only GO categories containing at least 10 genes represented on the microarray platform were considered for reporting, ensuring statistical reliability of enrichment findings.

The statistical analysis framework described herein represents a comprehensive and rigorous approach to microarray data analysis that balances sensitivity with specificity in the identification of differentially expressed genes. The use of moderated statistics, appropriate multiple testing correction, and biologically relevant fold change thresholds ensures that the identified molecular signatures are both statistically robust and potentially functionally significant.

### Demographics

Demographic characteristics of the study population were carefully documented and analyzed to assess potential confounding factors that might influence gene expression profiles or clinical outcomes. The complete enrolled cohort consisted of 33 patients: 18 in the recurrent hernia (RH) group and 15 in the normal control (NC) group. The majority of participants were female (26 out of 33, or 79%), reflecting the demographic distribution typical of patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy and incisional hernia repair at the study institution. Gender distribution was comparable between groups, with 14 females in the RH group and 12 females in the NC group (p = 0.99).

Age distribution showed a trend toward older patients in the RH group (mean age 55 years) compared to the NC group (mean age 53.2 years), though this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.14). Similarly, body mass index (BMI) was higher in the RH group (mean BMI 36.6) compared to the NC group (mean BMI 30.5), with a p-value of 0.03 indicating statistical significance in the complete cohort. However, in the subset of 17 patients whose samples were analyzed by microarray (9 RH and 8 NC), the BMI difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.10), suggesting that the microarray subset was reasonably well-matched for this important clinical variable.

Tobacco use was more prevalent in the RH group (8 out of 18 patients) compared to the NC group (2 out of 15 patients), though this difference approached but did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.07). The microarray subset showed similar tobacco use patterns (4 out of 9 RH versus 2 out of 8 NC, p = 0.62), indicating adequate matching for this potential confounder in the molecular analysis cohort.

Two demographic variables showed statistically significant differences between groups in both the complete cohort and the microarray subset. Diabetes mellitus was present in 7 out of 18 RH patients but in none of the 15 NC patients (p = 0.01), with similar findings in the microarray subset (5 out of 9 RH versus 0 out of 8 NC, p = 0.03). Previous abdominal surgery was universal in the RH group (18 out of 18) but present in only 6 out of 15 NC patients (p = 0.01), with corresponding findings in the microarray subset (9 out of 9 RH versus 3 out of 8 NC, p = 0.01). These differences are clinically expected given the nature of the patient populations and do not represent methodological flaws but rather inherent characteristics of recurrent hernia patients compared to elective surgery controls.

The demographic analysis demonstrates that while some expected clinical differences exist between recurrent hernia patients and normal controls, the microarray analysis subset was reasonably well-matched for key variables such as age, BMI, and tobacco use that might otherwise confound molecular findings. The statistical significance of diabetes and previous surgery differences reflects the natural history of these patient populations rather than selection bias, and these variables were accounted for in the interpretation of molecular results.

### Gene Ontology Analysis of Differentially Expressed Genes

Gene ontology (GO) analysis of differentially expressed genes from skin tissue revealed extensive enrichment across multiple biological domains, providing critical insights into the functional implications of the observed molecular signatures. The analysis identified more than 53 enriched biological process (BP) terms, 18 enriched molecular function (MF) terms, and 10 enriched cellular component (CC) terms, demonstrating the broad biological relevance of the identified gene expression changes. The fascial tissue gene list was too sparse (only 7 differentially expressed features) to support meaningful GO analysis, further emphasizing the superior discriminatory power of skin tissue in this context.

Among the most significantly enriched biological processes were those directly relevant to wound healing and tissue remodeling. The term “response to wounding” (GO:0009611) showed strong enrichment (odds ratio 3.18, p = 0.001), with multiple differentially expressed genes contributing to this category including COL3A1, CFD, FABP4, FBN1, CFH, ANXA5, PROK2, VWF, CAV1, AOC3, and CD36. This finding directly supports the hypothesis that recurrent hernia patients exhibit altered molecular responses to tissue injury and repair processes.

Additional enriched biological processes included “regulation of immune response” (GO:0050776, odds ratio 4.89, p = 0.012), involving genes such as COL3A1, CFD, CFH, and THY1; “activation of plasma proteins involved in acute inflammatory response” (GO:0002541, odds ratio 7.01, p = 0.039), with CFD and CFH as contributing genes; and “cell-matrix adhesion” (GO:0007160, odds ratio 6.62, p = 0.001), involving COL3A1, ECM2, NID1, EPDR1, and THY1. These findings collectively suggest that recurrent hernia patients exhibit coordinated alterations in inflammatory, immune, and extracellular matrix interaction pathways that may collectively impair normal wound healing responses.

Molecular function analysis revealed enrichment for “enzyme inhibitor activity” and “receptor binding,” consistent with the involvement of regulatory proteins in modulating wound healing processes. Cellular component analysis showed predominant localization to the “extracellular region” and “plasma membrane,” reflecting the importance of secreted and membrane-associated proteins in mediating tissue repair and remodeling responses.

The GO analysis demonstrates that the differentially expressed genes do not represent random transcriptional noise but rather form coherent functional networks centered around wound healing, inflammation, and extracellular matrix biology. This functional coherence strengthens the biological plausibility of the identified molecular signatures and supports their potential utility as diagnostic biomarkers for recurrent hernia susceptibility.

### COL1/COL3 Ratio by Microarray, PCR Array, and Immunohistochemistry

Comprehensive analysis of collagen composition across multiple analytical platforms confirmed the established literature regarding altered collagen I/III ratios in recurrent incisional hernia patients while providing new insights into the specific molecular mechanisms involved. Microarray analysis revealed that the COL1A1/COL3A1 ratio in skin tissue was slightly lower in RH patients compared to NC patients (1.33 versus 1.46), though this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.65). However, the COL1A2/COL3A1 ratio showed a statistically significant decrease in RH patients (0.59 versus 0.79, p = 0.02), suggesting that the alpha 2 chain of type I collagen may be more sensitive to the pathological processes underlying hernia formation.

Neither collagen ratio showed statistically significant differences in fascial tissue by microarray analysis, consistent with the limited number of differentially expressed genes identified in this tissue compartment. Immunohistochemical analysis of five representative patient samples demonstrated slightly greater staining intensity for collagen type III compared to collagen type I in both skin and fascial tissue from RH patients relative to NC controls, providing protein-level confirmation of the molecular findings.

PCR array analysis, which interrogated a focused panel of 84 wound healing and fibrosis-related genes, provided the most robust evidence for altered collagen composition. The COL1A2/COL3A1 ratio was significantly decreased in RH patients compared to NC patients (1.51 versus 2.26, p = 0.058 by one-sided t-test), with COL3A1 showing a 4.54-fold increase in RH patients (p = 0.002) and COL1A2 showing a 2.92-fold increase (p = 0.035). The manufacturer’s selection of COL1A2 for the PCR array was based on its more frequent citation in fibrosis-related literature compared to COL1A1, and the results confirm its utility as a sensitive marker of altered collagen metabolism in recurrent hernia patients.

The concordance of findings across three independent analytical platforms—microarray, PCR array, and immunohistochemistry—provides strong validation for the altered collagen I/III ratio as a consistent molecular signature of recurrent incisional hernia susceptibility. This multi-platform confirmation addresses potential concerns about platform-specific artifacts and strengthens the biological relevance of the observed changes. The identification of COL1A2 as particularly sensitive to pathological alterations suggests that future diagnostic assays should prioritize this collagen chain for optimal discrimination between patient groups.

## Other Embodiments

The present invention encompasses numerous alternative embodiments and extensions beyond the specific examples described herein. In one embodiment, the diagnostic methods may be adapted for use with alternative tissue sources, including blood samples, saliva, or urine, through the identification of circulating biomarkers that correlate with tissue-specific expression profiles. Such non-invasive approaches would greatly enhance clinical utility by enabling preoperative risk assessment without requiring tissue biopsy.

In another embodiment, the invention encompasses the development of therapeutic interventions targeting the molecular pathways identified through gene expression profiling. Specifically, the discovery of GREM1 downregulation in recurrent hernia patients suggests that BMP signaling pathway modulation may represent a novel therapeutic approach. Recombinant GREM1 protein, small molecule BMP pathway modulators, or gene therapy approaches designed to restore normal GREM1 expression could be developed to prevent or treat recurrent incisional hernias.

The invention further encompasses the integration of molecular profiling with advanced imaging techniques to provide comprehensive preoperative assessment of hernia risk. Combining gene expression data with biomechanical properties measured by ultrasound elastography or magnetic resonance imaging could provide synergistic information for personalized surgical planning. Machine learning algorithms trained on combined molecular and imaging datasets could generate highly accurate predictive models for hernia recurrence risk.

Additional embodiments include the expansion of biomarker panels to include additional genes identified through the GO analysis, such as those involved in immune regulation (CFD, CFH), cell adhesion (THY1, MCAM), and extracellular matrix organization (FBN1, DCN). Multiplexed assays capable of simultaneously measuring dozens of biomarkers could provide even greater diagnostic accuracy and biological insight than the two-marker combinations described in the examples.

The invention also contemplates the development of point-of-care diagnostic devices that enable rapid biomarker measurement in clinical settings. Microfluidic platforms incorporating nucleic acid amplification and detection capabilities could provide same-day results for preoperative decision-making. Such devices would democratize access to molecular diagnostics and facilitate widespread implementation of precision medicine approaches to hernia management.

Finally, the invention encompasses longitudinal monitoring applications where serial biomarker measurements track healing responses following surgical repair. By establishing individual baseline profiles and monitoring changes over time, clinicians could identify early signs of abnormal healing before clinical manifestations of recurrence become apparent, enabling timely intervention to prevent hernia recurrence.
