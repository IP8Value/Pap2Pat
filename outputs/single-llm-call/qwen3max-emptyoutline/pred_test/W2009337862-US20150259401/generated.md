# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of antibody engineering and therapeutic protein development, and more specifically to novel chimeric antibodies comprising human variable regions and ferret constant regions that exhibit reduced immunogenicity and extended serum half-life in ferrets. The invention further encompasses methods for producing such chimeric antibodies, as well as engineered variants thereof containing specific amino acid substitutions in the Fc region that enhance binding affinity to the ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), thereby significantly prolonging their pharmacokinetic profile in ferret models. Additionally, the invention provides recombinant nucleic acid molecules encoding ferret immunoglobulin constant region sequences and ferret FcRn components, expression vectors containing these sequences, host cells transformed with such vectors, and methods for using these reagents to produce and characterize chimeric antibodies optimized for preclinical evaluation in ferret disease models. The invention is particularly relevant to the development of therapeutic antibodies for respiratory viral infections, including but not limited to influenza virus and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), where ferrets serve as a critical preclinical model due to their physiological and immunological similarities to humans in respiratory tract anatomy, viral susceptibility, and disease manifestation.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ferrets (Mustela putorius furo) have long been recognized as one of the most valuable animal models for studying human respiratory diseases, particularly influenza virus infection, due to their remarkable similarity to humans in terms of respiratory tract physiology, clinical symptoms following viral infection, and patterns of viral transmission. Unlike rodent models, ferrets develop fever, lethargy, sneezing, nasal discharge, and other clinical signs that closely mimic human influenza, making them indispensable for evaluating the efficacy of vaccines, antiviral therapeutics, and monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) against respiratory pathogens. However, despite their utility as a preclinical model, significant challenges have emerged when attempting to evaluate human therapeutic antibodies in ferrets, primarily related to their unexpectedly short serum half-life and the induction of anti-drug antibody (ADA) responses that can confound pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic studies.

Historically, when human monoclonal antibodies are administered to ferrets, they exhibit markedly accelerated clearance compared to their behavior in other non-human primate or rodent models. While human IgG antibodies typically demonstrate half-lives ranging from 7 to 21 days in cynomolgus monkeys and mice, respectively, studies have revealed that the same antibodies may persist in ferret circulation for only 14 to 31 hours. This rapid clearance presents substantial obstacles for conducting meaningful efficacy studies, as it necessitates frequent and high-dose administration to maintain therapeutic concentrations, which is both impractical and potentially confounding due to dose-related toxicity or saturation effects. Moreover, the foreign nature of human antibody constant regions in the ferret immune system frequently triggers adaptive immune responses, leading to the production of ferret anti-human antibodies that can accelerate clearance through immune complex formation, complement activation, or Fc receptor-mediated phagocytosis. These ADA responses not only compromise the integrity of pharmacokinetic data but can also mask or alter the true therapeutic effect of the antibody being evaluated.

To address the issue of immunogenicity in cross-species antibody studies, the scientific community has developed chimeric antibody technology, wherein the variable regions of an antibody of interest (typically human) are fused to the constant regions of the host species in which the antibody will be tested. This approach leverages the fact that approximately 70% of an antibody's sequence resides in the constant regions, and by replacing these with species-matched counterparts, the immunogenic potential is substantially reduced while preserving the antigen-binding specificity conferred by the variable domains. Such chimeric antibodies have been successfully employed in mouse, rat, and non-human primate models, demonstrating improved pharmacokinetics and reduced immunogenicity compared to their fully human counterparts. However, until the present invention, the necessary molecular tools and sequence information for generating human-ferret chimeric antibodies were unavailable, as the complete amino acid sequences of ferret immunoglobulin heavy and light chain constant regions had not been definitively characterized and cloned.

Concurrently with efforts to reduce immunogenicity, significant research has focused on extending the serum half-life of therapeutic antibodies through engineering of their Fc regions to enhance interaction with the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). The FcRn plays a crucial role in the homeostasis of IgG antibodies by rescuing them from lysosomal degradation through a pH-dependent recycling mechanism. In acidic endosomal compartments (pH ~6.0), FcRn binds to the Fc region of internalized IgG antibodies and redirects them back to the cell surface, where they are released into the extracellular space at neutral pH (~7.4). This recycling process is responsible for the characteristically long half-life of IgG antibodies compared to other immunoglobulin classes. Numerous studies have identified specific amino acid residues within the Fc region that modulate FcRn binding affinity, with particular attention paid to positions 252, 254, 256, 428, and 434 (using EU numbering convention). For instance, the M252Y/S254T/T256E (YTE) triple mutation and the M428L/N434S (LS) double mutation have been shown to enhance binding to human and cynomolgus monkey FcRn at pH 6.0, resulting in three- to four-fold increases in serum half-life in preclinical models. However, the applicability of these established mutations to ferret antibodies remained unknown due to the lack of structural and functional characterization of the ferret FcRn system and the absence of complete ferret immunoglobulin constant region sequences.

The present invention addresses these critical gaps in knowledge and technology by providing the first complete molecular characterization of ferret immunoglobulin constant regions and ferret FcRn components, enabling the rational design and production of human-ferret chimeric antibodies with optimized pharmacokinetic properties. Through systematic cloning, expression, and functional validation, the inventors have established that ferret antibodies naturally contain a serine residue at position 252 (corresponding to methionine in human IgG1), which contributes to their intrinsically shorter half-life compared to human antibodies. By introducing targeted amino acid substitutions at this and other key positions based on homology with human Fc engineering strategies, the inventors have successfully generated chimeric antibody variants that demonstrate dramatically enhanced binding to ferret FcRn and correspondingly extended serum half-lives in vivo, reaching up to 9 days compared to the typical 2-3 day half-life of wild-type chimeric antibodies. Furthermore, comprehensive immunogenicity assessment reveals that these human-ferret chimeric antibodies elicit minimal to no detectable ADA responses in ferrets, even upon repeated dosing, thereby providing a robust platform for reliable preclinical evaluation of therapeutic antibodies in this clinically relevant animal model.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### General

The present invention represents a significant advancement in the field of preclinical antibody development by providing the first comprehensive molecular toolkit for generating human-ferret chimeric antibodies with optimized pharmacokinetic and immunogenicity profiles. Prior to this work, researchers faced substantial limitations when attempting to evaluate human therapeutic antibodies in ferret models due to the dual challenges of rapid clearance and immune recognition of foreign constant regions. The invention overcomes these obstacles through the systematic identification, cloning, and functional characterization of ferret immunoglobulin constant region sequences and ferret FcRn components, enabling the rational design of chimeric antibodies that maintain human antigen-binding specificity while exhibiting ferret-compatible constant regions.

The core innovation lies in the discovery that ferret immunoglobulin heavy chains naturally contain a serine residue at position 252 (corresponding to methionine in human IgG1), which contributes to weaker binding to ferret FcRn and consequently shorter serum half-life compared to human antibodies. By leveraging established principles of Fc engineering while adapting them to the unique sequence context of ferret antibodies, the inventors have successfully generated chimeric antibody variants that demonstrate dramatically enhanced pharmacokinetic properties without compromising antigen-binding function or structural integrity. This approach represents a paradigm shift in preclinical antibody evaluation, as it enables reliable, long-term studies in ferrets without the confounding effects of immune-mediated clearance or the need for frequent high-dose administration.

The chimeric antibodies of the invention are constructed by splicing human variable region sequences, which confer specific antigen recognition, to ferret constant region sequences, which provide species compatibility. This modular design preserves the therapeutic intent of the original human antibody while optimizing its behavior in the ferret model system. The human variable regions can be derived from any monoclonal antibody of interest, including those specific for viral antigens, tumor-associated antigens, inflammatory mediators, or other therapeutic targets. The ferret constant regions, now fully characterized through the present invention, provide the necessary framework for proper antibody assembly, secretion, and Fc-mediated functions while minimizing recognition by the ferret immune system as foreign.

### DEFINITIONS

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms shall have the meanings indicated below unless otherwise specified:

"Chimeric antibody" refers to an antibody molecule comprising variable regions derived from one species (typically human) fused to constant regions derived from another species (in this case, ferret). The chimeric antibody retains the antigen-binding specificity of the donor variable regions while exhibiting the effector functions and pharmacokinetic properties associated with the recipient constant regions.

"Ferret immunoglobulin constant region" refers to the portion of ferret immunoglobulin heavy or light chains that does not participate directly in antigen binding but is responsible for effector functions, structural stability, and interaction with Fc receptors. The ferret heavy chain constant region includes the CH1, hinge, CH2, and CH3 domains, while the light chain constant region includes the CL domain.

"Neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn)" refers to the major histocompatibility complex class I-related receptor responsible for the extended serum half-life of IgG antibodies through pH-dependent recycling. FcRn is composed of an alpha chain (encoded by the FCGRT gene) and beta-2 microglobulin (β2m).

"EU numbering" refers to the standardized numbering system for immunoglobulin heavy chains based on the Eu myeloma protein sequence, which allows for consistent identification of equivalent positions across different antibody isotypes and species through homology alignment.

"Half-life extending substitutions" refers to specific amino acid modifications in the Fc region of antibodies that enhance binding affinity to FcRn at acidic pH (approximately 6.0) while maintaining appropriate release at neutral pH (approximately 7.4), thereby increasing serum persistence through enhanced recycling.

"Anti-drug antibodies (ADAs)" refers to antibodies produced by the host immune system in response to administration of a therapeutic protein, which can bind to the therapeutic agent and potentially neutralize its activity, accelerate its clearance, or cause adverse immune reactions.

"Recombinant expression" refers to the production of proteins through genetic engineering techniques involving the insertion of coding sequences into expression vectors, transformation of host cells, and subsequent protein synthesis and secretion by the engineered cells.

### Amino Acid Substitutions

The present invention encompasses specific amino acid substitutions in the ferret Fc region that enhance binding to ferret FcRn and extend serum half-life. These substitutions are identified based on homology with well-characterized human Fc engineering strategies, adapted to the unique sequence context of ferret immunoglobulins. The key substitution sites include positions 252, 254, 256, and 434 (using EU numbering based on homology alignment with human IgG1).

Position 252 in ferret IgG naturally contains a serine residue, whereas human IgG1 contains methionine at this position. The inventors discovered that this natural difference contributes to weaker binding of ferret antibodies to ferret FcRn compared to human antibodies binding to human FcRn. Substitution of serine 252 with methionine (S252M) restores the human-like residue and enhances FcRn binding, while substitution with tyrosine (S252Y) provides even greater enhancement, consistent with observations in human antibody engineering.

Additional substitutions that can be combined with S252Y include S254T and T256E, creating the YTE triple mutant variant. Although in human antibodies this combination provides synergistic enhancement of FcRn binding, the present invention reveals that in the ferret context, the S252Y substitution alone provides maximal benefit, with the additional mutations providing minimal incremental improvement.

Another substitution site identified is position 434, where asparagine in ferret IgG can be substituted with serine (N434S) to enhance FcRn binding, analogous to the N434S mutation in human antibodies. However, this substitution provides less dramatic enhancement compared to the position 252 modifications in the ferret system.

These substitutions can be introduced individually or in combination using standard molecular biology techniques such as site-directed mutagenesis, and the resulting variants can be screened for enhanced FcRn binding using competitive binding assays and evaluated for extended half-life in ferret pharmacokinetic studies.

### Antibodies of the Invention

The antibodies of the present invention are chimeric molecules comprising human variable regions fused to ferret constant regions. The human variable regions can be derived from any monoclonal antibody of therapeutic interest, including but not limited to antibodies specific for viral antigens (such as RSV F glycoprotein, influenza hemagglutinin, or SARS-CoV-2 spike protein), tumor-associated antigens (such as HER2, EGFR, or PD-L1), inflammatory cytokines (such as TNF-α, IL-6, or IL-17), or other disease-relevant targets.

The ferret constant regions used in the invention comprise the complete heavy chain constant region (including CH1, hinge, CH2, and CH3 domains) and kappa light chain constant region as characterized and cloned by the inventors. These sequences provide the necessary framework for proper antibody assembly, secretion, and Fc-mediated functions while minimizing immunogenicity in ferret models.

The chimeric antibodies retain full antigen-binding functionality as demonstrated by competitive binding assays showing equivalent or superior binding affinity compared to the original human antibodies. They also demonstrate proper structural integrity as evidenced by correct assembly into heterotetrameric structures (two heavy chains and two light chains) and monodisperse behavior in size exclusion chromatography.

Particularly preferred embodiments include chimeric antibodies specific for respiratory viral antigens, given the importance of ferrets as models for respiratory disease. However, the invention is broadly applicable to any therapeutic antibody program requiring evaluation in ferret models, regardless of the specific antigen target.

### Ferret Antibody Derivatives

The present invention also encompasses derivatives of the ferret antibodies described herein, including fragments, conjugates, and modified forms that retain the essential characteristics of reduced immunogenicity and extended half-life in ferrets. Antibody fragments such as Fab, Fab', F(ab')2, scFv, and single-domain antibodies can be generated using standard proteolytic cleavage or recombinant expression techniques, provided they incorporate the ferret constant region sequences or appropriate ferret-derived framework elements.

Conjugates of the chimeric antibodies with therapeutic agents such as cytotoxic drugs, radionuclides, or imaging agents are also within the scope of the invention, as the ferret constant regions would still provide the benefits of reduced immunogenicity in ferret models during preclinical evaluation.

Additionally, the invention includes bispecific antibodies comprising one arm with human variable regions specific for a therapeutic target fused to ferret constant regions, and another arm with specificity for a different antigen, which could be useful for redirecting immune effector functions or delivering payloads to specific cell populations.

### Immunoglobulin Constructs of the Invention

The immunoglobulin constructs of the invention include full-length chimeric antibodies as well as various engineered formats designed to optimize specific properties while maintaining the core benefits of ferret constant regions. These constructs can be expressed as complete IgG molecules with appropriate heavy and light chain pairing, or as alternative formats such as IgA, IgE, or IgM if desired for specific applications.

The constructs can also include additional modifications beyond the FcRn-enhancing substitutions, such as glycoengineering to modulate effector functions, introduction of cysteine residues for site-specific conjugation, or incorporation of stability-enhancing mutations to improve thermal or chemical resistance.

Multispecific constructs incorporating multiple different antigen-binding specificities while utilizing ferret constant regions for reduced immunogenicity in ferret models are also encompassed by the invention. These could include tandem scFv-Fc constructs, diabodies, or other engineered multispecific formats that leverage the ferret constant region sequences for optimal preclinical evaluation.

### Antibody Preparation

The preparation of chimeric antibodies according to the invention involves several key steps: (1) obtaining the human variable region sequences of interest; (2) cloning these sequences into expression vectors containing the ferret constant region sequences; (3) introducing desired FcRn-enhancing substitutions through site-directed mutagenesis; (4) co-transfecting mammalian host cells with heavy and light chain expression vectors; (5) culturing the transfected cells under conditions conducive to antibody expression and secretion; and (6) purifying the expressed antibodies using appropriate chromatographic techniques.

The human variable region sequences can be obtained from existing monoclonal antibodies through reverse transcription of mRNA from hybridoma cells or B cells, or they can be synthesized de novo based on known sequences. These sequences are then spliced to the ferret constant region sequences using standard molecular cloning techniques such as restriction enzyme digestion and ligation, Gibson assembly, or overlap extension PCR.

The resulting expression constructs are typically designed with strong promoters such as CMV or EF1α to drive high-level expression in mammalian cells. Both heavy and light chain constructs are co-transfected into host cells such as HEK293 or CHO cells using standard transfection methods including calcium phosphate precipitation, lipofection, or electroporation.

Following transfection, cells are cultured in appropriate media supplemented with necessary nutrients and selection agents if stable cell lines are desired. Transient expression systems are often preferred for rapid production and screening of multiple variants, while stable cell lines may be developed for large-scale production of lead candidates.

### Recombinant Production of Antibodies and Immunoglobulin Constructs

The recombinant production of antibodies and immunoglobulin constructs according to the invention is carried out using standard mammalian expression systems that ensure proper protein folding, assembly, and post-translational modifications. HEK293 cells, particularly the suspension-adapted HEK293F and HEK293E variants, are preferred for transient expression due to their high transfection efficiency and robust protein production capabilities. CHO cells may be used for stable cell line development when large-scale, consistent production is required.

Expression vectors containing the chimeric heavy and light chain sequences are co-transfected into the host cells using optimized protocols that maximize expression levels while maintaining cell viability. The transfected cells are then cultured in serum-free media under controlled conditions of temperature, pH, and dissolved oxygen to optimize antibody production and quality.

Following an appropriate culture period (typically 3-7 days for transient expression), the culture supernatant is harvested by centrifugation and filtration to remove cellular debris. The secreted antibodies are then purified using a combination of protein A and protein G chromatography, as the inventors discovered that ferret antibodies exhibit differential binding to these affinity resins compared to human antibodies. Specifically, initial attempts to purify ferret antibodies using protein G alone resulted in significant loss of product in the flow-through, necessitating the addition of protein A chromatography in series to achieve complete capture.

The purified antibodies are then subjected to quality control analyses including SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions to confirm proper assembly and purity, size exclusion chromatography to assess monodispersity and aggregation state, and functional assays to verify antigen binding and FcRn interaction.

### Chimeric Antibodies

The chimeric antibodies of the invention represent a novel class of therapeutic protein constructs specifically designed for optimal performance in ferret preclinical models. Unlike traditional human antibodies that exhibit rapid clearance and immunogenicity in ferrets, these chimeric molecules combine the antigen-binding specificity of human variable regions with the species-compatible constant regions of ferrets, resulting in dramatically improved pharmacokinetic and immunogenicity profiles.

The human variable regions maintain their original complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) responsible for antigen recognition, ensuring that the therapeutic intent of the original antibody is preserved. The ferret constant regions provide the necessary structural framework for proper antibody assembly and secretion while minimizing recognition by the ferret immune system as foreign.

Functional characterization demonstrates that these chimeric antibodies retain full antigen-binding capability, as evidenced by competitive binding assays showing equivalent or superior binding affinity compared to the parental human antibodies. Structural integrity is confirmed through proper assembly into heterotetrameric structures and monodisperse behavior in analytical chromatography.

Most importantly, pharmacokinetic studies in ferrets reveal that these chimeric antibodies exhibit significantly extended serum half-lives compared to their human counterparts, particularly when engineered with FcRn-enhancing substitutions. The S252Y and YTE variants demonstrate half-lives of approximately 9 days, representing a nearly 7-fold improvement over wild-type human antibodies in ferrets.

Immunogenicity assessment shows minimal to no detectable anti-drug antibody responses, even upon repeated dosing, which is a critical advantage for conducting long-term efficacy studies without the confounding effects of immune-mediated clearance.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Cloning of DNA Encoding Ferret Immunoglobulin Constant Sequences and FcRn

Total RNA was isolated from ferret kidney, lung, liver, and spleen tissues using TRIzol reagent followed by purification with the RNeasy kit according to manufacturer's instructions. The quality and quantity of isolated RNA were assessed using spectrophotometry and agarose gel electrophoresis to ensure integrity of ribosomal RNA bands. First-strand cDNA synthesis was performed using oligo(dT) primers and the Superscript III First Strand Synthesis System, generating templates for subsequent PCR amplification.

For amplification of ferret immunoglobulin heavy chain (HC) constant region sequences, degenerate primers were designed based on partial ferret sequences available in public databases (GenBank Accession Number EZ459103) and alignment with mink immunoglobulin sequences. The initial PCR used forward primer 5′-GGTCACCGTGTCCTCAGC-3′ and reverse primer 5′-GCGTGCGGCTCATTTACC-3′, followed by a second round of nested PCR with primers containing restriction sites: forward 5′-TTTCGTACGGCTTCCACCACGGCCCC-3′ and reverse 5′-AAATGATCATCATTTACCCGGAGACTGG-3′. Similarly, ferret kappa light chain (LC) constant region sequences were amplified using initial primers 5′-AAGGTGGAAATCAAACGG-3′ and 5′-ATAGGTGGTGGGTGCTGC-3′, followed by nested primers 5′-TTTCGTACGAATGATGCCCAGCCATCCG-3′ and 5′-AAATGATCACTAGGCCACTCATTGGCAC-3′.

For cloning of ferret FcRn components, the alpha chain was amplified using degenerate primers designed from alignment of known FcRn sequences across multiple species: initial forward primer 5′-ATGSGGVKYCCBCGGCCTC-3′ and reverse primer 5′-TTCCGATCACGGGCACGG-3′, followed by nested primers with restriction sites: forward 5′-TTTAAGCTTAGGTGCGTCCTTCGAGCCACCATGSGGVKYCCBCGGCCTC-3′ and reverse 5′-TTTTCTAGAGGAGGATCTGGCTGGTG-3′. Ferret β2-microglobulin (β2m) was amplified using primers 5′-CTACTCCGGTGGCGATGG-3′ and 5′-AAACCTCCATGATGCTGG-3′ for initial PCR, followed by nested primers 5′-TTTAAGCTTGCCACCATGGCGCTTCTCTGGACG-3′ and 5′-AAATCTAGATTAGTTGTCTCGCTCCC-3′.

All PCR products were cloned into mammalian expression vectors under control of the CMV promoter. The ferret FcRn alpha chain construct included a C-terminal 6× His tag to facilitate purification. Sequence analysis of multiple clones revealed consensus sequences for each component, with the ferret HC constant region showing 89% identity to mink sequences and three amino acid differences from the partial database sequence, all located in the hinge region. The ferret LC constant region was identical to the database sequence (GenBank Accession Number EZ457190) and showed 94% identity to mink sequences.

### Example 2

Preparation of Chimeric Antibodies and Variants

The variable regions from a humanized monoclonal antibody specific for respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) F glycoprotein were selected for incorporation into chimeric antibodies due to their lack of expected cross-reactivity with ferret tissues, which could confound pharmacokinetic studies. These variable regions were amplified by PCR and cloned into expression plasmids containing the newly characterized ferret IgG heavy chain and kappa light chain constant regions.

Site-directed mutagenesis was performed using the QuikChange Kit to introduce specific amino acid substitutions into the ferret Fc region aimed at enhancing binding to ferret FcRn. The following variants were generated: S252M (substituting serine 252 with methionine), S252Y (substituting serine 252 with tyrosine), YTE (combining S252Y with S254T and T256E substitutions), and N434S (substituting asparagine 434 with serine). Mutagenesis primers were designed according to standard protocols, with sequences verified by DNA sequencing following mutagenesis.

Chimeric antibodies were expressed by co-transfecting HEK293F cells with heavy and light chain expression plasmids using polyethylenimine (PEI) as the transfection reagent. Cells were cultured in FreeStyle 293 Expression Medium at 37°C with 8% CO2 for 5-7 days, after which culture supernatants were harvested by centrifugation at 3,000 × g for 20 minutes followed by 0.22 μm filtration.

Purification was performed using an AKTA purification system with HiTrap Protein G HP and HiTrap MabSelect Sure Protein A columns connected in series. This dual-column approach was necessary because initial attempts using Protein G alone resulted in significant antibody loss in the flow-through, indicating that ferret antibodies exhibit different binding characteristics to these affinity resins compared to human antibodies. Eluted antibodies were buffer-exchanged into PBS using Amicon Ultra centrifugal filters and concentrated to appropriate working concentrations.

Quality control analysis included SDS-PAGE under both reducing and non-reducing conditions using 4-12% Bis-Tris gels with MOPS running buffer, which confirmed proper assembly into heterotetrameric structures with expected molecular weights. Size exclusion high-performance liquid chromatography (SE-HPLC) using a TSKgel G3000SWx1 column demonstrated monodisperse peaks characteristic of properly folded, non-aggregated antibodies.

### Example 3

Preparation of Recombinant Soluble Ferret FcRn

Soluble ferret FcRn complex was produced by co-expressing the extracellular portion of the ferret FcRn alpha chain (with C-terminal 6× His tag) and ferret β2-microglobulin in HEK293E cells. Expression plasmids containing these sequences under CMV promoter control were co-transfected using PEI, and cells were cultured in FreeStyle 293 Expression Medium supplemented with 1% penicillin-streptomycin for 5-7 days.

Culture supernatants were harvested by centrifugation and filtration, then loaded onto a 5-ml HisTrap HP column equilibrated in binding buffer (20 mM sodium phosphate, 500 mM NaCl, 20 mM imidazole, pH 7.4). The column was washed extensively with binding buffer, and bound protein was eluted with elution buffer (20 mM sodium phosphate, 500 mM NaCl, 500 mM imidazole, pH 7.4). Eluted fractions containing the FcRn complex were pooled and further purified by size exclusion chromatography using a HiLoad 26/60 Superdex 200 column equilibrated in PBS.

Purity assessment by SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions revealed two bands migrating near the expected molecular weight of the alpha chain (30.1 kDa) and a single band near the expected molecular weight of β2m (11.5 kDa). The doublet observed for the alpha chain was attributed to glycosylation heterogeneity, as the ferret alpha chain contains two N-linked glycosylation motifs, consistent with observations in other species. SE-HPLC analysis confirmed the formation of a stable, monodisperse complex suitable for binding assays.

### Example 4

Ferret FcRn Binding Assays

Binding affinity of chimeric antibodies to ferret FcRn was evaluated using an AlphaScreen-based competitive binding assay. Biotinylated wild-type chimeric antibody (1-2 μg/ml final concentration) was mixed with titrated amounts of test antibodies, His-tagged ferret FcRn (4-8 μg/ml final concentration), nickel-coated AlphaScreen acceptor beads (1:50 dilution), and streptavidin-coated AlphaScreen donor beads (1:50 dilution) in white polystyrene half-area flat-bottom assay plates.

The assay mixture was incubated for 1 hour at room temperature in the dark to allow complex formation. During this incubation, test antibodies competed with biotinylated wild-type antibody for binding to ferret FcRn, thereby modulating the proximity between donor and acceptor beads. Following incubation, luminescence resulting from singlet oxygen transfer between proximal beads was measured using an EnVision plate reader.

Dose-response curves were generated by plotting luminescence signal against test antibody concentration, and IC50 values were calculated using nonlinear regression analysis. Results demonstrated that wild-type chimeric antibody exhibited approximately twofold stronger binding to ferret FcRn compared to human antibody (IC50 values of 78 μg/ml versus 152 μg/ml, respectively). All engineered variants showed enhanced binding compared to wild-type chimeric antibody, with S252M showing an IC50 of 13 μg/ml (6-fold improvement), S252Y showing an IC50 of 3.2 μg/ml (24-fold improvement), and YTE showing similar enhancement to S252Y alone. The N434S variant showed modest improvement with an IC50 of approximately 40 μg/ml.

### Example 5

Pharmacokinetic Studies in Ferrets

Pharmacokinetic evaluation was conducted in male ferrets (16 weeks old, 1-2 kg body weight) at Covance Laboratories, Inc. A vascular access port was surgically implanted into the jugular vein 5 days prior to dose administration to facilitate repeated blood sampling. Ferrets received a single intravenous dose of 2 mg/kg of test antibody via the vascular access port.

Blood samples were collected at predetermined time points (2, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, 168, 336, and 504 hours post-dose) into sodium heparin-containing tubes and kept on ice until plasma separation by centrifugation. Plasma samples were stored at -80°C until analysis.

Antibody concentrations in plasma were quantified using an electrochemiluminescent assay on Streptavidin Gold MultiArray plates. For human antibody detection, a biotinylated mouse anti-idiotype antibody specific for the test antibodies was captured on plates, followed by addition of plasma samples and detection with ruthenium-labeled F(ab')2 fragment of donkey anti-human IgG(H+L). For chimeric antibody detection, ruthenium-labeled anti-ferret IgG (cross-absorbed against human IgG) diluted in 20% mouse serum was used to minimize cross-reactivity.

Standard curves were generated using known concentrations of test antibodies, and sample concentrations were interpolated from these curves. The assay demonstrated a four-log linear range (0.1-100 ng/ml) with no interference from ferret plasma up to 20% concentration.

Terminal half-lives were calculated using two-phase decay nonlinear regression modeling in GraphPad Prism software. Outliers were identified using the Grubbs test with P > 0.05 significance threshold. Results showed that wild-type human IgG1 had an average half-life of 31 ± 10 hours, while the LS variant (M428L/N434S) extended this to 72 ± 21 hours. In contrast, wild-type chimeric antibody demonstrated a half-life of 65 ± 27 hours, with S252M extending to 127 ± 38 hours, S252Y to 206 ± 44 hours, and YTE to 213 ± 28 hours.

### Example 6

Measuring Ferret Immune Responses to Test Antibodies

Anti-drug antibody (ADA) responses were assessed using a bridging electrochemiluminescent assay. Biotinylated test antibody (1 μg/ml) was captured on blocked Streptavidin Gold MultiArray plates, followed by addition of diluted ferret plasma samples and incubation for 1 hour. Bound ferret antibodies were detected by adding ruthenium-labeled test antibody (1 μg/ml) and measuring electrochemiluminescent signal after 1 hour incubation.

Initial studies with human antibodies revealed ADA responses in 2 out of 8 ferrets, with one additional ferret showing precipitous drops in antibody concentration suggestive of immune-mediated clearance. In contrast, no ADA responses were detected in ferrets receiving chimeric antibodies, even after repeated dosing.

However, potential interference from residual test antibody in plasma samples was investigated by spiking known concentrations of anti-idiotype antibody (surrogate ADA) into 504-hour plasma samples. Detection of 1 μg/ml anti-idiotype was partially inhibited, while 0.1 μg/ml was completely inhibited in most samples, indicating that residual chimeric antibody could mask low-level ADA responses. Nevertheless, the consistent pharmacokinetic profiles observed across all chimeric antibody groups, without the abrupt concentration drops characteristic of ADA-mediated clearance, strongly suggest minimal immunogenicity of the chimeric constructs.

These findings demonstrate that human-ferret chimeric antibodies provide a superior platform for preclinical evaluation in ferret models, offering both extended half-life through FcRn engineering and reduced immunogenicity through species-matched constant regions, thereby enabling more reliable and interpretable efficacy studies.
