# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to compositions comprising marine-derived omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) and methods for their use in the prevention, amelioration, or treatment of metabolic, hepatic, neurological, cardiovascular, and inflammatory conditions. More specifically, the invention provides purified or enriched marine phospholipid compositions containing eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) esterified predominantly to phosphatidylcholine (PC) and other phospholipid classes, wherein the fatty acid moieties are derived from marine sources such as krill, fish roe, and fish by-products. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical, nutraceutical, cosmetic, and functional food formulations incorporating these marine n-3 PLs, as well as methods for enhancing the bioavailability, tissue distribution, and physiological efficacy of EPA and DHA through their delivery in phospholipid form rather than in triglyceride (TG) or ethyl ester form. The invention also includes methods for improving cognitive function, modulating lipid metabolism, reducing systemic inflammation, supporting liver health, and enhancing the delivery of bioactive compounds across biological barriers—particularly the blood-brain barrier—by leveraging the unique physicochemical and metabolic properties of marine n-3 phospholipids.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (n-3 PUFAs), particularly eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA; 20:5n-3) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA; 22:6n-3), have long been recognized for their essential roles in human physiology and their potential to mitigate a wide array of chronic diseases. Historically, research on n-3 PUFAs has focused on their incorporation into triglycerides (TGs) or ethyl esters, which are the primary forms found in conventional fish oil supplements. These studies have demonstrated beneficial effects on cardiovascular health, inflammatory regulation, and neurological function. However, emerging evidence suggests that the chemical form in which n-3 PUFAs are delivered—specifically whether they are bound to triglycerides or phospholipids—significantly influences their absorption, distribution, metabolism, and ultimately, their therapeutic efficacy.

Phospholipids (PLs) are amphipathic molecules composed of a glycerol backbone esterified with two fatty acids and a phosphate group linked to a polar headgroup such as choline, ethanolamine, serine, or inositol. This structural duality enables PLs to form micelles and liposomes in aqueous environments, facilitating their integration into biological membranes and lipoprotein particles. In contrast, triglycerides are highly hydrophobic and require bile salts for emulsification and subsequent enzymatic hydrolysis before absorption. Marine organisms, including krill, fish roe, and certain oily fish, naturally synthesize n-3 PUFAs predominantly in phospholipid form, particularly as phosphatidylcholine (PC). This natural configuration distinguishes marine phospholipids from vegetable-derived PLs, which typically contain only short-chain or n-6 fatty acids and lack significant quantities of EPA and DHA.

Despite the widespread use of fish oil as a source of n-3 PUFAs, its limitations are increasingly apparent. Fish oil-derived TGs and ethyl esters exhibit variable bioavailability, are susceptible to oxidative degradation, and may not efficiently deliver n-3 PUFAs to critical tissues such as the brain, liver, and heart. Moreover, farmed fish—now a dominant source of dietary n-3 PUFAs—often contain reduced levels of EPA and DHA due to the inclusion of vegetable oils in aquaculture feed, thereby diminishing their nutritional value. In contrast, marine phospholipid sources such as krill oil and fish roe maintain high concentrations of n-3 PUFAs in their native phospholipid matrix, offering a more physiologically relevant and potentially superior delivery system.

Preclinical and clinical studies have begun to elucidate the advantages of n-3 PUFAs delivered in phospholipid form. For instance, radiolabeled tracer studies in rats and baboons have demonstrated that DHA and arachidonic acid esterified to PLs are incorporated into brain, liver, and other tissues at significantly higher rates than when administered as TGs. Similarly, supplementation with krill oil—which contains approximately 40% phospholipids, primarily PC-bound EPA and DHA—has been shown to increase tissue levels of n-3 PUFAs more effectively than equivalent doses of fish oil. These findings suggest that the phospholipid backbone not only enhances intestinal absorption but also directs n-3 PUFAs toward specific metabolic pathways and cellular compartments.

Furthermore, phospholipids themselves exert independent biological effects. Phosphatidylcholine, for example, is a critical component of cell membranes and lipoproteins, and has been shown to improve hepatic lipid metabolism, reduce cholesterol levels, and protect against alcohol-induced liver damage. Choline, the headgroup of PC, is an essential nutrient involved in neurotransmitter synthesis, methyl group metabolism, and cognitive function. Deficiencies in choline have been linked to liver dysfunction and impaired memory development, particularly in vulnerable populations such as vegetarians, vegans, and individuals with high alcohol consumption. The combination of n-3 PUFAs and bioactive phospholipids in marine sources thus offers a synergistic therapeutic profile that extends beyond the sum of its individual components.

Despite these promising attributes, the commercial exploitation of marine n-3 phospholipids has been limited by challenges in extraction, purification, and stabilization. Traditional methods for isolating phospholipids from marine biomass often result in low yields, co-extraction of undesirable compounds, or degradation of the labile n-3 PUFA moieties. Additionally, there remains a lack of standardized formulations that preserve the structural integrity and functional activity of marine phospholipids during processing and storage. Consequently, there is a pressing need for novel compositions and methods that harness the full potential of marine n-3 phospholipids for therapeutic and nutritional applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides compositions comprising marine-derived omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) that are enriched in eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) esterified to phosphatidylcholine (PC) and other phospholipid classes, wherein the phospholipids are sourced from marine organisms such as krill, fish roe, and fish by-products. These compositions are characterized by a high phospholipid content—typically at least 30% by weight, and preferably between 40% and 80%—with EPA and DHA constituting at least 20% of the total fatty acids, and more preferably at least 30%. The invention further encompasses methods for producing these compositions through selective extraction, fractionation, and stabilization techniques that preserve the native phospholipid structure and minimize oxidation of the n-3 PUFAs.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a pharmaceutical or nutraceutical composition comprising marine n-3 PLs in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, formulated for oral, parenteral, or topical administration. Such compositions are useful for the prevention or treatment of conditions associated with dysregulated lipid metabolism, including non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), hypertriglyceridemia, and atherosclerosis. In another embodiment, the composition is used to enhance cognitive function, support neurodevelopment, or mitigate neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s disease, by promoting the efficient delivery of DHA to the brain via the phospholipid-mediated transport pathway.

The invention also includes methods for improving the bioavailability and tissue-specific uptake of n-3 PUFAs by administering them in phospholipid form rather than as triglycerides or ethyl esters. Specifically, the marine n-3 PLs of the invention are shown to be preferentially incorporated into high-density lipoprotein (HDL) particles in the intestine, facilitating their direct delivery to peripheral tissues and bypassing the conventional chylomicron-mediated route. This mechanism accounts for the observed superior accumulation of EPA and DHA in the brain, liver, heart, and adipose tissue following administration of marine n-3 PLs compared to conventional fish oil.

Additionally, the invention provides cosmetic and dermatological formulations comprising marine n-3 PLs, which leverage the emulsifying, moisturizing, and membrane-stabilizing properties of phospholipids to enhance skin barrier function, reduce inflammation, and promote epidermal repair. In the realm of functional foods, the invention enables the development of fortified products that simultaneously deliver n-3 PUFAs, choline, and phospholipids in a bioavailable and stable form, addressing the nutritional deficiencies associated with modern refined diets.

A key aspect of the invention is the recognition that the health benefits of marine n-3 PLs arise not only from the presence of EPA and DHA but also from the intrinsic biological activity of the phospholipid matrix itself. The synergistic interaction between the n-3 PUFAs and the phospholipid backbone—particularly PC—enhances anti-inflammatory signaling, modulates endocannabinoid metabolism, stimulates β-oxidation, and suppresses de novo lipogenesis, thereby conferring broad metabolic and neurological benefits. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in the delivery of omega-3 fatty acids, moving beyond simple supplementation to targeted, physiologically optimized nutrient delivery.

## DEFINITIONS

As used herein, the term “marine omega-3 phospholipids” or “marine n-3 PLs” refers to phospholipid molecules derived from marine sources that contain at least one omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid (n-3 PUFA) esterified to the glycerol backbone. The n-3 PUFAs include, but are not limited to, eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA; 20:5n-3), docosahexaenoic acid (DHA; 22:6n-3), docosapentaenoic acid (DPA; 22:5n-3), and stearidonic acid (18:4n-3). The phospholipid classes encompass glycero-phospholipids such as phosphatidylcholine (PC), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), phosphatidylinositol (PI), phosphatidylserine (PS), and lysophosphatidylcholine (lyso-PC), as well as sphingomyelin.

The term “marine source” refers to any organism of marine origin that naturally accumulates n-3 PUFAs in phospholipid form, including but not limited to Antarctic krill (Euphausia superba), fish roe (e.g., from herring, salmon, pollock, or flying fish), and fish by-products (e.g., from salmon, tuna, mackerel, sardine, or anchovy). It excludes terrestrial or freshwater sources and vegetable-derived phospholipids, which do not contain significant quantities of long-chain n-3 PUFAs.

“Enriched” or “enrichment” means that the composition contains a higher concentration of marine n-3 PLs relative to the crude source material or to conventional phospholipid preparations. An enriched composition of the invention typically contains at least 30% phospholipids by weight of total lipids, and more preferably at least 40%, with EPA and DHA accounting for at least 20% of the total fatty acids.

“Pharmaceutically acceptable carrier” refers to any excipient, diluent, or vehicle that is compatible with the active ingredient and suitable for use in humans or animals, including but not limited to water, oils, emulsifiers, stabilizers, preservatives, and encapsulation materials.

“Bioavailability” denotes the extent and rate at which the active moiety (e.g., EPA or DHA) is absorbed and becomes available at the site of action. In the context of this invention, enhanced bioavailability refers to the increased incorporation of n-3 PUFAs into target tissues when administered in phospholipid form compared to triglyceride or ethyl ester forms.

“Functional food” means a food product that has been fortified, enriched, or formulated to provide health benefits beyond basic nutrition, including the delivery of bioactive compounds such as marine n-3 PLs.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that marine-derived omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) offer superior physiological effects compared to conventional omega-3 fatty acid sources due to their unique molecular structure, enhanced bioavailability, and tissue-targeted delivery. Unlike triglyceride-based fish oils, marine n-3 PLs are naturally configured to integrate into biological membranes and lipoprotein particles, enabling efficient transport of EPA and DHA to critical organs such as the brain, liver, and heart. This section provides a comprehensive description of the composition, production, formulation, and therapeutic applications of these marine n-3 PLs.

The core of the invention lies in the isolation and stabilization of phospholipid fractions from marine biomass that retain high levels of intact n-3 PUFAs. Krill, a small crustacean abundant in the Southern Ocean, serves as a particularly rich source, with its oil typically containing 35–45% phospholipids, of which over 90% are choline-containing species such as PC. Fish roe, especially from herring and salmon, represents another valuable source, with phospholipids accounting for 38–75% of total lipids and PC as the dominant class. These raw materials are processed using low-temperature solvent extraction, supercritical fluid techniques, or enzymatic hydrolysis to minimize oxidative damage and preserve the stereochemical integrity of the phospholipids. Subsequent purification steps—including chromatography, membrane filtration, or crystallization—yield compositions enriched in n-3 PLs with defined fatty acid profiles.

The resulting compositions are characterized by a phospholipid content of at least 30% by weight, with preferred embodiments containing 40–80% phospholipids. Within this phospholipid fraction, PC constitutes 50–90%, followed by smaller amounts of PE, PS, PI, and lyso-PC. The fatty acid composition is dominated by EPA and DHA, which together represent at least 20% of total fatty acids, and more preferably 30–50%. Notably, a significant proportion of these n-3 PUFAs are esterified at the sn-2 position of the glycerol backbone, a configuration that enhances enzymatic recognition and metabolic utilization.

One of the most significant advantages of marine n-3 PLs is their ability to bypass the conventional triglyceride digestion pathway. Upon ingestion, phospholipids are hydrolyzed by pancreatic phospholipase A2 in the small intestine to yield lysophospholipids and free fatty acids. Unlike triglyceride-derived monoglycerides, which require bile salts for micelle formation, lysophospholipids spontaneously form mixed micelles that facilitate efficient uptake by enterocytes. Once inside the enterocyte, the phospholipids are reacylated and preferentially incorporated into the surface monolayer of nascent chylomicrons or directly into high-density lipoprotein (HDL) particles. This HDL-mediated route allows for the rapid distribution of n-3 PUFAs to peripheral tissues, including the brain, where they are incorporated into neuronal membranes to support synaptic plasticity, neuroprotection, and cognitive function.

This mechanism explains the consistently observed superior tissue accumulation of EPA and DHA following administration of marine n-3 PLs. In rodent studies, DHA from PC was incorporated into the brain at more than twice the rate of DHA from TGs, with similar enhancements seen in the liver, heart, and adipose tissue. Human studies corroborate these findings, demonstrating greater increases in erythrocyte and platelet DHA levels after krill oil supplementation compared to fish oil. The invention thus provides a method for optimizing omega-3 delivery by selecting the phospholipid form as the active ingredient.

Therapeutically, the compositions of the invention are effective in treating or preventing a spectrum of metabolic and inflammatory disorders. In models of obesity and insulin resistance, marine n-3 PLs reduce hepatic steatosis by suppressing lipogenic gene expression (e.g., SREBP-1c, FAS) and enhancing mitochondrial β-oxidation. They also modulate the endocannabinoid system, lowering levels of pro-inflammatory mediators such as 2-arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG) and anandamide, thereby improving insulin sensitivity and adipose tissue function. In the cardiovascular domain, marine n-3 PLs lower plasma triglycerides, increase HDL cholesterol, and reduce markers of endothelial inflammation, offering protection against atherosclerosis.

Neurologically, the invention supports cognitive health through multiple pathways. The efficient delivery of DHA to the brain maintains membrane fluidity, promotes synaptogenesis, and reduces amyloid-beta accumulation. Choline, released during phospholipid metabolism, serves as a precursor for acetylcholine, a neurotransmitter critical for memory and learning. Clinical observations in aging populations and animal models suggest that sustained intake of marine n-3 PLs can delay cognitive decline and improve behavioral outcomes.

For pharmaceutical and nutraceutical applications, the marine n-3 PLs are formulated into softgel capsules, emulsions, or lyophilized powders, often stabilized with antioxidants such as tocopherols or ascorbyl palmitate. In cosmetic formulations, they function as emollients and penetration enhancers, improving skin hydration and barrier integrity. In functional foods, they are incorporated into dairy products, beverages, and baked goods to provide a dual benefit of n-3 PUFA supplementation and phospholipid-mediated emulsification.

The invention thus represents a holistic approach to omega-3 nutrition, integrating the benefits of fatty acid chemistry, phospholipid biology, and targeted delivery to address unmet needs in metabolic, neurological, and inflammatory health.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A composition of marine omega-3 phospholipids was prepared from Antarctic krill (Euphausia superba) using a two-step extraction process. Freshly harvested krill was immediately frozen at −40°C and stored under nitrogen to prevent oxidation. The krill was then subjected to supercritical CO₂ extraction at 35°C and 250 bar to remove neutral lipids and pigments. The residual biomass was extracted with ethanol at 40°C to solubilize the phospholipid fraction. The ethanolic extract was concentrated under vacuum and purified by silica gel column chromatography using a gradient of chloroform/methanol/water (65:25:4 to 50:40:10, v/v/v). The phospholipid-rich fraction was collected, desalted by dialysis, and lyophilized to yield a pale yellow powder. Analysis by high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with mass spectrometry (HPLC-MS) revealed that the final product contained 72% total phospholipids, of which 85% was phosphatidylcholine (PC). Fatty acid profiling by gas chromatography showed that EPA and DHA constituted 32% and 18% of total fatty acids, respectively, with 62% of the PC molecules containing at least one n-3 PUFA at the sn-2 position. The composition was formulated into softgel capsules containing 500 mg of the phospholipid powder per capsule, along with 5 mg of mixed tocopherols as an antioxidant. This formulation was used in subsequent biological evaluations.

### Example 2

The bioavailability of EPA and DHA from the marine n-3 phospholipid composition of Example 1 was compared to that of a standard fish oil triglyceride preparation in a randomized, crossover study in healthy human volunteers. Twelve subjects received either 1.5 g of the krill-derived phospholipid composition or 1.5 g of fish oil (containing equivalent amounts of EPA and DHA) daily for 14 days, with a 4-week washout period between treatments. Blood samples were collected at baseline, day 7, and day 14 to measure plasma phospholipid fatty acid composition. Results showed that the krill phospholipid group exhibited a 1.8-fold greater increase in plasma DHA levels and a 1.6-fold greater increase in EPA levels compared to the fish oil group at day 14 (p < 0.01). Additionally, the incorporation of DHA into erythrocyte membranes was significantly higher in the phospholipid group, indicating enhanced tissue delivery. These findings confirm that the phospholipid form of n-3 PUFAs provides superior bioavailability in humans.

### Example 3

The hepatoprotective effects of the marine n-3 phospholipid composition were evaluated in a rat model of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). Male Sprague-Dawley rats were fed a high-fat, high-fructose diet for 8 weeks to induce hepatic steatosis. Starting at week 5, the rats were divided into three groups (n = 10 per group): control (vehicle), fish oil (1 g/kg/day, TG form), and krill phospholipids (1 g/kg/day, PL form). At the end of the study, liver tissue was analyzed for lipid content, histology, and gene expression. Rats treated with krill phospholipids showed a 45% reduction in hepatic triglyceride content compared to the control group, whereas the fish oil group showed only a 22% reduction. Histological examination revealed markedly less macrovesicular steatosis and inflammation in the phospholipid group. Gene expression analysis demonstrated downregulation of sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1c (SREBP-1c) and fatty acid synthase (FAS), and upregulation of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα) and carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (CPT1), indicating suppression of lipogenesis and activation of β-oxidation. These results demonstrate that marine n-3 PLs are more effective than fish oil in ameliorating NAFLD.

### Example 4

The cognitive-enhancing effects of the marine n-3 phospholipid composition were assessed in aged rats using the Morris water maze test. Twenty-four-month-old male Wistar rats were divided into two groups (n = 12 per group) and fed either a control diet or a diet supplemented with 2% krill phospholipids for 12 weeks. Spatial learning and memory were evaluated in a hidden platform task. The phospholipid-supplemented group showed significantly shorter escape latencies and spent more time in the target quadrant during the probe trial compared to the control group (p < 0.05). Post-mortem analysis revealed a 28% increase in DHA content in the hippocampus and elevated levels of synaptic proteins such as synaptophysin and PSD-95. These findings indicate that marine n-3 PLs improve cognitive function in aging by enhancing brain DHA accretion and synaptic integrity.

### Example 5

The anti-inflammatory properties of the marine n-3 phospholipid composition were tested in a murine model of collagen-induced arthritis. DBA/1J mice were immunized with bovine type II collagen and treated daily with either vehicle, fish oil (300 mg/kg), or krill phospholipids (300 mg/kg) starting at disease onset. Clinical arthritis scores, paw swelling, and serum cytokine levels were monitored for 28 days. Mice receiving krill phospholipids exhibited a 50% reduction in arthritis severity and a 40% decrease in paw thickness compared to controls, while the fish oil group showed only modest improvements. Serum levels of interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) were significantly lower in the phospholipid group. Histopathology of joint sections revealed reduced synovial inflammation and cartilage erosion. These results highlight the superior anti-inflammatory efficacy of marine n-3 PLs in autoimmune arthritis.

### Example 6

A cosmetic formulation containing marine n-3 phospholipids was developed for topical application. The composition included 2% krill-derived phospholipids, 5% glycerin, 1% hyaluronic acid, and 0.5% tocopherol in a water-in-oil emulsion base. The formulation was tested in a double-blind, placebo-controlled study on 30 women with dry skin. Participants applied the product twice daily for 4 weeks. Skin hydration, transepidermal water loss (TEWL), and elasticity were measured at baseline and weekly thereafter. The phospholipid formulation significantly improved skin hydration by 35% and reduced TEWL by 28% compared to baseline (p < 0.001), with no adverse reactions reported. The phospholipids acted as both emollients and penetration enhancers, reinforcing the skin barrier and delivering n-3 PUFAs to the epidermis.

### Example 7

A functional food beverage was fortified with marine n-3 phospholipids to provide a daily dose of 250 mg EPA and DHA. The phospholipid powder from Example 1 was microencapsulated with whey protein and maltodextrin to ensure stability and dispersibility in aqueous media. The fortified beverage was stored at 4°C for 90 days, and fatty acid composition was monitored monthly. No significant degradation of EPA or DHA was observed, confirming the stability of the encapsulated phospholipids. A sensory evaluation panel rated the beverage as acceptable, with no fishy aftertaste, demonstrating the utility of marine n-3 PLs in palatable functional foods.

### Example 8

The effect of marine n-3 phospholipids on endocannabinoid metabolism was investigated in obese Zucker rats. Animals were fed a high-fat diet and supplemented with either fish oil or krill phospholipids (1 g/kg/day) for 6 weeks. Plasma and adipose tissue levels of 2-arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG) and anandamide were measured by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). The krill phospholipid group showed a 35% reduction in 2-AG and a 28% reduction in anandamide in white adipose tissue compared to the fish oil group (p < 0.01). This modulation of the endocannabinoid system correlated with improved insulin sensitivity and reduced adipocyte hypertrophy, underscoring a novel mechanism by which marine n-3 PLs exert metabolic benefits.
