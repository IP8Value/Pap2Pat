# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biochemistry and molecular physiology, particularly to the discovery and application of a novel peptide-based mechanism for modulating skeletal muscle energy metabolism and contractile function. More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions involving the use of teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP), particularly TCAP-1, to enhance glucose uptake, mitochondrial activity, and contractile kinetics in skeletal muscle cells through an insulin-independent pathway. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising TCAP peptides for the treatment of conditions characterized by impaired skeletal muscle function, including but not limited to metabolic disorders such as type II diabetes, age-related sarcopenia, muscular dystrophies, and other conditions involving reduced skeletal muscle performance or energy metabolism. The invention also relates to the identification of the molecular components of the teneurin-latrophilin (LPHN) signaling system in skeletal muscle tissue, establishing that both teneurins and their putative receptors, the latrophilins, are expressed in skeletal muscle and form a functional signaling axis that can be targeted therapeutically using TCAP peptides to improve muscle physiology and metabolic health.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Skeletal muscle represents one of the most metabolically active tissues in the human body and serves as the primary site for glucose disposal under insulin-stimulated conditions, accounting for approximately 80% of postprandial glucose uptake. The proper functioning of skeletal muscle is essential not only for locomotion and physical activity but also for maintaining whole-body glucose homeostasis and metabolic health. Impaired skeletal muscle function is a hallmark of numerous pathological conditions, including type II diabetes mellitus, where patients exhibit reduced grip strength and overall muscle performance compared to non-diabetic individuals. Despite the critical importance of skeletal muscle in metabolic regulation, current therapeutic approaches for improving muscle function in metabolic disorders remain limited, with many relying on insulin sensitization or exogenous insulin administration, which carry significant limitations and side effects.

The teneurin family of proteins represents an evolutionarily ancient system that originated through lateral gene transfer from prokaryotes into early metazoan ancestors. These type-II transmembrane proteins are characterized by a unique β-barrel structure at their extracellular C-terminus, which is homologous to bacterial Tc-toxins. Associated with this domain is a conserved peptide sequence known as the teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP), which has been shown to possess independent biological activity distinct from the full-length teneurin protein. Previous research has established that TCAP peptides share structural similarities with members of the secretin superfamily of peptides, including vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) and growth hormone-releasing hormone, suggesting potential roles in cellular signaling and metabolism.

In the central nervous system (CNS), teneurins and their associated TCAP peptides have been extensively studied for their roles in neuronal development, synaptic plasticity, and behavioral regulation. Administration of TCAP-1 to rats has been shown to increase brain glucose uptake as measured by functional positron emission tomography (fPET), accompanied by decreased serum glucose and insulin levels. In vitro studies have demonstrated that TCAP-1 stimulates glucose transporter translocation to neuronal membranes, leading to increased glucose uptake and subsequent ATP production, thereby providing enhanced energy availability for neuronal function. However, despite the evolutionary conservation of the teneurin system and its fundamental role in cellular metabolism, the potential involvement of this signaling pathway in skeletal muscle physiology has remained largely unexplored until the present invention.

The latrophilins (LPHNs) represent a family of adhesion G protein-coupled receptors (aGPCRs) that have been identified as putative receptors for teneurins. Three paralogous forms of LPHNs (LPHN-1, LPHN-2, and LPHN-3) have been identified in vertebrates, and evidence suggests that these receptors mediate various cellular responses through interactions with teneurins. While the specific stoichiometry and binding preferences between different teneurin and LPHN paralogues remain incompletely understood, studies have indicated that the TCAP domain may play a crucial role in mediating these interactions. The complexity of this signaling system is further compounded by the presence of multiple functional domains within both teneurins and LPHNs, which can interact with various extracellular matrix components and intracellular signaling molecules.

Previous attempts to understand the role of the teneurin-LPHN system in peripheral tissues have been limited primarily to neuronal contexts, with little attention paid to potential functions in skeletal muscle. This oversight is particularly notable given the evolutionary origins of the teneurin system in primitive contractile cells and the critical importance of energy metabolism in skeletal muscle function. The absence of knowledge regarding teneurin and LPHN expression in skeletal muscle tissue, coupled with the lack of understanding about potential TCAP-mediated effects on muscle physiology, represents a significant gap in the current understanding of metabolic regulation and muscle function.

Furthermore, existing therapeutic approaches for improving skeletal muscle function in metabolic disorders often rely on insulin-dependent mechanisms, which can be problematic in conditions characterized by insulin resistance. The development of insulin-independent pathways for enhancing glucose uptake and energy metabolism in skeletal muscle would represent a significant advancement in the treatment of metabolic disorders and age-related muscle decline. The present invention addresses these critical gaps in knowledge by demonstrating for the first time that the teneurin-LPHN signaling system is functionally expressed in skeletal muscle and that TCAP peptides can modulate muscle energy metabolism and contractile function through an insulin-independent mechanism.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for enhancing energy availability to skeletal muscle cells through the administration of teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP), particularly TCAP-1. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that skeletal muscle tissue expresses functional components of the teneurin-latrophilin (LPHN) signaling system, including teneurins, TCAP peptides, and LPHN receptors, and that exogenous administration of TCAP-1 can modulate skeletal muscle physiology through an insulin-independent mechanism. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that TCAP-1 administration increases glucose uptake in skeletal muscle cells by stimulating the translocation of glucose transporter 4 (GLUT4) to the cell membrane, enhances mitochondrial activity through increased calcium flux and succinate dehydrogenase expression, and improves contractile kinetics during fatigue conditions.

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising TCAP peptides, particularly TCAP-1, for the treatment of conditions characterized by impaired skeletal muscle function or energy metabolism. These compositions can be administered via various routes, including subcutaneous injection, and have been shown to be effective in both short-term and long-term treatment regimens. The invention further encompasses methods for identifying compounds that modulate the teneurin-LPHN signaling pathway in skeletal muscle, as well as diagnostic methods for assessing skeletal muscle function based on the expression levels of teneurin and LPHN components.

A key aspect of the invention is the demonstration that TCAP-1 mediates its effects through interaction with LPHN-1 and LPHN-3 receptors, leading to activation of the phospholipase C (PLC)-inositol triphosphate (IP3)-diacylglycerol (DAG) signaling pathway. This activation results in increased intracellular calcium concentrations, which subsequently stimulate mitochondrial calcium uptake and enhance tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle activity, leading to increased ATP and NADH production. The invention also shows that TCAP-1 treatment promotes a shift toward more oxidative muscle fiber types, as evidenced by increased expression of myosin heavy chain I (MHCI) isoforms and enhanced NADH staining in treated muscle tissue.

The invention provides evidence that TCAP-1 enhances skeletal muscle contractile function without increasing muscle mass, indicating that the peptide improves the efficiency of existing muscle morphology rather than promoting hypertrophy. Specifically, TCAP-1 treatment maintains twitch maximum rate of force development (dx/dt) and half-relaxation time (1/2RT) during fatigue protocols, suggesting improved calcium handling and energy availability during prolonged muscle activity. Long-term administration of TCAP-1 results in slower contraction velocity and faster relaxation rates, consistent with a shift toward more oxidative, fatigue-resistant muscle fiber types.

The invention further demonstrates that the effects of TCAP-1 on skeletal muscle are dependent on LPHN receptor expression, as both siRNA knockdown and CRISPR knockout of LPHN-1 and LPHN-3 receptors significantly attenuate TCAP-1-mediated calcium flux and downstream metabolic effects. This establishes the teneurin-LPHN signaling system as a functional pathway in skeletal muscle and validates it as a therapeutic target for modulating muscle physiology and energy metabolism.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed to them below. The term "teneurin C-terminal associated peptide" or "TCAP" refers to the conserved peptide sequence located at the C-terminal end of teneurin proteins, which can be cleaved from the parent protein or expressed independently. TCAP peptides are characterized by their amphiphilic nature and structural similarity to members of the secretin superfamily of peptides. In the context of this invention, "TCAP-1" specifically refers to the peptide derived from the C-terminal region of teneurin-1, which has been shown to possess potent biological activity in modulating skeletal muscle energy metabolism and contractile function.

The term "latrophilin" or "LPHN" refers to a family of adhesion G protein-coupled receptors that serve as putative receptors for teneurins and TCAP peptides. The invention specifically encompasses LPHN-1 and LPHN-3, which have been demonstrated to mediate TCAP-1 effects in skeletal muscle cells. The term "skeletal muscle cells" includes both primary skeletal muscle cells and immortalized skeletal muscle cell lines, such as the C2C12 murine skeletal muscle cell line used in the experimental studies described herein.

"Glucose uptake" as used herein refers to the process by which glucose is transported from the extracellular environment into skeletal muscle cells, primarily through the action of glucose transporter proteins, particularly GLUT4. The term "insulin-independent" indicates that the mechanism of action does not require the presence of insulin or activation of the insulin signaling pathway, distinguishing the TCAP-mediated effects from traditional insulin-dependent glucose uptake mechanisms.

"Contractile kinetics" refers to the dynamic properties of muscle contraction and relaxation, including parameters such as peak twitch force, maximum rate of force development (dx/dt), half-relaxation time (1/2RT), and fatigue resistance. The term "mitochondrial activity" encompasses various aspects of mitochondrial function, including calcium uptake, membrane potential, succinate dehydrogenase expression, and ATP and NADH production.

"Pharmaceutical composition" as used herein refers to a formulation containing TCAP peptides in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents suitable for administration to a subject. The term "subject" includes both human and non-human mammals, particularly those suffering from conditions characterized by impaired skeletal muscle function or energy metabolism.

### Applications: The Use of TCAP to Increase Energy Available to Skeletal Muscle Cells

The present invention provides a novel approach for increasing energy availability to skeletal muscle cells through the administration of TCAP peptides, particularly TCAP-1. This approach is based on the discovery that skeletal muscle tissue expresses functional components of the teneurin-LPHN signaling system and that exogenous TCAP-1 can modulate muscle energy metabolism through an insulin-independent mechanism. The invention demonstrates that TCAP-1 administration enhances glucose uptake in skeletal muscle cells by stimulating GLUT4 translocation to the cell membrane, increases mitochondrial activity through enhanced calcium flux and TCA cycle enzyme expression, and improves contractile function during fatigue conditions.

The mechanism of action involves TCAP-1 binding to LPHN-1 and LPHN-3 receptors on skeletal muscle cells, leading to activation of the PLC-IP3-DAG signaling pathway. This activation results in increased intracellular calcium concentrations through IP3 receptor-mediated release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. The elevated cytosolic calcium levels subsequently stimulate mitochondrial calcium uptake, which enhances TCA cycle activity through activation of key enzymes such as pyruvate dehydrogenase phosphatase, isocitrate dehydrogenase, and oxoglutarate dehydrogenase. This leads to increased electron transport chain activity, resulting in enhanced ATP and NADH production.

The increased energy availability translates into improved muscle contractile function, particularly during fatigue conditions when energy demands are highest. TCAP-1 treatment maintains optimal contractile kinetics by ensuring adequate ATP supply for sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPase (SERCA) pump activity, which is essential for calcium reuptake and muscle relaxation. This prevents the accumulation of cytosolic calcium that typically occurs during fatigue and leads to impaired muscle function.

Additionally, TCAP-1 promotes a shift toward more oxidative muscle fiber types, as evidenced by increased expression of MHCI isoforms and enhanced NADH staining in treated muscle tissue. This fiber type transition contributes to improved fatigue resistance and sustained muscle performance during prolonged activity. The invention demonstrates that these effects occur without changes in muscle mass, indicating that TCAP-1 improves the metabolic efficiency of existing muscle fibers rather than promoting hypertrophy.

The insulin-independent nature of TCAP-1 action makes it particularly valuable for treating conditions characterized by insulin resistance, such as type II diabetes mellitus. Unlike traditional approaches that rely on enhancing insulin sensitivity or providing exogenous insulin, TCAP-1 provides an alternative pathway for glucose uptake and energy metabolism that remains functional even in the presence of insulin resistance. This represents a significant therapeutic advantage for patients with metabolic disorders who may not respond adequately to conventional insulin-based therapies.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Modes of Administration

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising TCAP peptides, particularly TCAP-1, formulated for administration to subjects requiring enhancement of skeletal muscle energy metabolism and contractile function. The TCAP peptides can be synthesized using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques and are preferably modified to enhance stability and biological activity. Specifically, the N-terminal glutamine residue is converted to pyroglutamic acid to inhibit N-terminal-directed peptidases, and the C-terminal residue is amidated to mimic the natural processing of the peptide from the parent teneurin protein.

Pharmaceutical compositions according to the invention contain TCAP peptides in concentrations ranging from 0.1 nM to 100 μM, with preferred concentrations between 1 nM and 10 μM for in vitro applications and 1-50 nmoles/kg body weight for in vivo administration. The peptides are formulated in pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, which may include saline solutions, buffered aqueous solutions, or other biocompatible vehicles suitable for the intended route of administration.

The preferred route of administration is subcutaneous injection, which has been demonstrated to be effective in both short-term and long-term treatment regimens. However, other routes of administration may also be employed, including intravenous, intramuscular, or transdermal delivery, depending on the specific clinical application and desired pharmacokinetic profile. For chronic conditions requiring long-term treatment, sustained-release formulations may be developed using biodegradable polymer matrices or other controlled-release technologies to maintain therapeutic peptide concentrations over extended periods.

The dosing regimen can be adjusted based on the specific condition being treated and the desired therapeutic outcome. For acute enhancement of muscle performance, single doses may be administered prior to anticipated physical activity. For chronic conditions such as type II diabetes or age-related sarcopenia, regular administration schedules ranging from daily to weekly injections may be employed, with treatment durations extending from several weeks to months or longer as needed.

The pharmaceutical compositions may also include additional therapeutic agents that complement the action of TCAP peptides. For example, combinations with traditional insulin-sensitizing agents may provide synergistic benefits in the treatment of type II diabetes, while combinations with anti-inflammatory agents may be beneficial in conditions involving muscle inflammation or degeneration. The compatibility of TCAP peptides with other therapeutic agents should be evaluated to ensure optimal efficacy and safety profiles.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Peptide Synthesis

TCAP-1 peptides were synthesized commercially by AmbioPharm, Inc. using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques. The synthetic rat/mouse TCAP-1 peptide was prepared as an acetylated salt with 95% purity and included specific modifications to enhance stability and biological activity. The N-terminal glutamine residue was converted to pyroglutamic acid (pE) to inhibit N-terminal-directed peptidases, while the C-terminal residue was amidated to mimic the natural processing of the peptide from the parent teneurin protein. A scrambled control peptide (sc-TCAP-1) was also synthesized, in which all amino acid residues except the initial pyroglutamyl residue were randomized in their placement within the peptide sequence. This scrambled peptide served as an additional control to ensure that observed effects were specific to the native TCAP-1 sequence rather than non-specific peptide actions. Both peptides were solubilized in 0.9% saline after alkalization and diluted to appropriate concentrations for in vitro or in vivo studies. The primary structure of rat and mouse TCAP-1 is identical, allowing the same peptide preparation to be used across both species in experimental studies.

### Example 2—In-Vivo Experiments

In vivo experiments were conducted using male adult Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats to evaluate the effects of TCAP-1 on skeletal muscle function. Short-term studies involved 16 male adult SD rats (250g) that were acclimated for one week on a 12:12 light-dark cycle and then treated daily for five days with either vehicle or TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) by subcutaneous injection in the intrascapular region. Muscle function was assessed by electrical muscle stimulation three days after the last treatment. Long-term studies utilized 20 adult male SD rats (350g) that were acclimated similarly and treated weekly for three months with either vehicle or TCAP-1 (25 nmoles/kg) via intrascapular subcutaneous injection. Muscle function testing was performed two weeks after the final treatment. Electrical muscle stimulation protocols were conducted using Dynamic Muscle Control software, with animals anesthetized and positioned in a testing apparatus. Electrodes were placed adjacent to the tibialis anterior (TA) muscle, and contractile parameters including peak tetanic tension, twitch force, and fatigue resistance were measured. Animals were immediately euthanized following completion of muscle function tests.

### Example 3—Effect of TCAP on Blood Glucose Levels

The effect of TCAP-1 on blood glucose levels was assessed using functional positron emission tomography (fPET) in male Wistar rats. Animals were treated with either vehicle or TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) by intrascapular subcutaneous injection, and three days later, 1 mCi of [18F]-2-deoxyglucose (18F-DG) radiotracer was administered intravenously under anesthesia. fPET scans were performed using a Siemens Inveon microPET small animal PET scanner with body temperature maintained using a thermostat-regulated recirculating water-heated pad. Static emission data was acquired for 20 minutes, and PET image analysis was conducted using Amira 5.5.0 analysis software. Whole-body regions of interest (ROIs) were delineated using a low threshold to identify areas of high 18F-DG uptake while eliminating background signal. Total PET counts were calculated from all voxels within segmented volumes of interest, and images were compiled into three-dimensional projections for accurate analysis of muscle tissue glucose uptake. Results demonstrated that TCAP-1 treatment significantly increased 18F-DG uptake in hind-limb skeletal muscle regions compared to vehicle-treated controls.

### Example 4—Glucose Insulin and Glucagon Changes in Blood

Blood samples were collected from TCAP-1-treated and vehicle-treated rats to assess changes in circulating glucose, insulin, and glucagon levels. Serum glucose concentrations were measured using standard enzymatic assays, while insulin and glucagon levels were determined using commercially available ELISA kits. Results showed that TCAP-1 administration led to decreased serum glucose levels accompanied by corresponding reductions in insulin concentrations, consistent with enhanced peripheral glucose uptake. Glucagon levels remained relatively stable, indicating that the observed metabolic effects were primarily mediated through peripheral tissue actions rather than hepatic glucose production regulation. These findings corroborated the fPET imaging results and provided additional evidence for TCAP-1-mediated enhancement of skeletal muscle glucose metabolism.

### Example 5—Liver and Muscle Glycogen Content in Extracted Liver of TCAP-1 Treated Wistar Rats

Glycogen content was measured in liver and skeletal muscle tissues extracted from TCAP-1-treated and vehicle-treated Wistar rats to assess the impact of TCAP-1 on glycogen storage. Tissues were homogenized and processed using standard biochemical techniques to isolate glycogen fractions. Glycogen content was quantified using enzymatic assays that measure glucose release following glycogen hydrolysis. Results demonstrated that TCAP-1 treatment led to increased glycogen storage in skeletal muscle tissue, consistent with enhanced glucose uptake and utilization. Liver glycogen content showed minimal changes, indicating that the primary metabolic effects of TCAP-1 were localized to peripheral tissues, particularly skeletal muscle, rather than hepatic glucose metabolism.

### Example 6—Haematology Assays

Comprehensive haematology assays were performed on blood samples collected from TCAP-1-treated and vehicle-treated rats to assess potential systemic effects of peptide administration. Complete blood counts including red blood cell count, white blood cell count, haemoglobin concentration, haematocrit, and platelet count were measured using automated haematology analysers. Additional parameters including mean corpuscular volume (MCV), mean corpuscular haemoglobin (MCH), and mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration (MCHC) were also evaluated. Results showed no significant differences between TCAP-1-treated and vehicle-treated groups across all measured parameters, indicating that TCAP-1 administration did not produce adverse haematological effects at the tested doses and treatment durations.

### Example 7—Localization of TCAP in Muscle Tissue

Immunohistochemical studies were conducted to localize TCAP expression in skeletal muscle tissue. Tibialis anterior (TA) muscles were excised from treated rats, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen-cooled isopentane, and cryo-sectioned at 10 μm thickness. Tissue sections were fixed using ice-cold methanol and blocked with 10% normal goat serum before incubation with primary antibodies specific for TCAP. Following overnight incubation at 4°C, sections were washed and incubated with fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies for one hour at room temperature in the dark. Confocal microscopy was performed using a Leica TCS-SP8 microscope at 400x magnification, and fluorescence intensity was quantified using Image J software to measure arbitrary fluorescent units (AFU). Results demonstrated specific punctate expression of TCAP in sarcolemmal regions of skeletal muscle fibers, with co-localization patterns consistent with expected receptor distribution.

### Example 8—Localization of TCAP-1 Relative to β-Dystroglycan in Skeletal Cells

Co-localization studies were performed to examine the spatial relationship between TCAP-1 and β-dystroglycan (DG) in skeletal muscle tissue. β-dystroglycan labeling was used to establish sarcolemmal boundaries, as previous studies had indicated a relationship between DG and TCAP signaling. Immunohistochemical staining was performed on TA muscle sections using primary antibodies against both TCAP-1 and β-dystroglycan, followed by species-specific fluorescent secondary antibodies. Confocal microscopy revealed that TCAP-1 immunoreactivity co-localized with β-dystroglycan along sarcolemmal regions, with specific nodes of aggregation observed at particular sites within the sarcolemma. This co-localization pattern suggested functional interactions between TCAP-1 and the dystrophin-glycoprotein complex in skeletal muscle cells.

### Example 9—Localization of TCAP-1 Relative to Rapsyn in Skeletal Cells

Additional co-localization studies were conducted to examine the relationship between TCAP-1 and rapsyn in skeletal muscle tissue. Rapsyn is a postsynaptic protein involved in acetylcholine receptor clustering at neuromuscular junctions, and its localization relative to TCAP-1 could provide insights into potential roles in neuromuscular transmission. Immunohistochemical staining was performed on TA muscle sections using primary antibodies against TCAP-1 and rapsyn, followed by appropriate fluorescent secondary antibodies. Confocal microscopy analysis revealed distinct but partially overlapping localization patterns, with TCAP-1 showing broader sarcolemmal distribution while rapsyn was concentrated at neuromuscular junction regions. This differential localization suggested that TCAP-1 functions extend beyond neuromuscular junction regulation to encompass general sarcolemmal signaling processes in skeletal muscle cells.
