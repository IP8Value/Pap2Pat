## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular biology and diagnostics, and more specifically to compositions and methods for the selective enrichment, detection, quantification, and functional modulation of activated nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) RelA protein complexes in biological samples. The invention provides a novel single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) aptamer that specifically binds to the transcriptionally active, inhibitor of kappa B alpha (IκBα)-free form of RelA with high affinity and selectivity. This aptamer enables the isolation and quantitative analysis of activated RelA from complex cellular environments using mass spectrometry-based techniques, particularly selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry (SRM-MS) coupled with stable isotope dilution (SID). The invention further encompasses diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic applications based on the ability of the aptamer to distinguish between inactive (IκBα-bound) and active (free) RelA conformations, thereby providing unprecedented insights into NF-κB signaling dynamics in health and disease.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) is a family of evolutionarily conserved transcription factors that play a central role in regulating immune responses, inflammation, cell survival, proliferation, and differentiation. The NF-κB pathway is tightly regulated through multiple mechanisms, with the canonical pathway being primarily controlled by the inhibitory protein IκBα. In unstimulated cells, NF-κB dimers, most commonly composed of RelA (p65) and p50 subunits, are sequestered in the cytoplasm through their association with IκBα. Upon stimulation by various extracellular signals such as tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFα), interleukin-1 (IL-1), or pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), the IκB kinase (IKK) complex is activated, leading to phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and subsequent proteasomal degradation of IκBα. This liberation allows NF-κB dimers to translocate into the nucleus, where they bind to specific κB DNA sequences in the promoters and enhancers of target genes, thereby initiating transcription of inflammatory mediators, anti-apoptotic proteins, and other regulatory molecules.

Despite extensive research over several decades, significant challenges remain in accurately measuring and characterizing the activated fraction of NF-κB RelA in biological systems. Traditional methods for studying NF-κB activation, including electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSAs), chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP), reporter gene assays, and Western blotting, provide valuable but often indirect or qualitative information about NF-κB activity. These techniques typically measure total RelA levels, nuclear translocation, DNA binding capacity, or transcriptional output, but fail to directly quantify the specific pool of transcriptionally competent, IκBα-free RelA complexes that are functionally relevant for gene regulation. Moreover, the dynamic nature of NF-κB signaling, characterized by rapid oscillations between cytoplasmic and nuclear compartments and transient interactions with chromatin, makes it particularly challenging to capture and analyze the activated state of RelA with sufficient temporal and spatial resolution.

Aptamers, which are short single-stranded oligonucleotides (DNA or RNA) that can fold into specific three-dimensional structures capable of binding target molecules with high affinity and specificity, have emerged as powerful tools for molecular recognition and detection. Unlike antibodies, aptamers can be generated through systematic evolution of ligands by exponential enrichment (SELEX) procedures against purified proteins or even whole cells, offering advantages in terms of stability, ease of modification, and lack of immunogenicity. While previous efforts have successfully isolated RNA aptamers and modified DNA aptamers that bind to NF-κB proteins, these reagents have exhibited limitations in binding affinity, stability, or specificity for the activated conformation of RelA. Furthermore, the application of aptamers for quantitative proteomic analysis of low-abundance transcription factors like RelA in complex biological matrices has remained largely unexplored.

Mass spectrometry-based proteomics, particularly targeted approaches such as SRM-MS, offers unparalleled sensitivity and specificity for protein quantification. When combined with stable isotope-labeled internal standards (SIS peptides), SRM-MS enables absolute quantification of proteins with high precision and accuracy. However, the direct application of SRM-MS to quantify low-abundance transcription factors like RelA in crude cellular extracts is often hampered by the overwhelming complexity of the sample matrix, which leads to poor signal-to-noise ratios and potential interference from co-eluting peptides with similar mass-to-charge ratios. Therefore, there exists a critical need for affinity reagents that can selectively enrich the target protein of interest while simultaneously discriminating between different functional states or post-translational modifications.

The present invention addresses these longstanding challenges in NF-κB research by providing a novel ssDNA aptamer that specifically recognizes the activated, IκBα-free conformation of RelA with nanomolar affinity. This aptamer enables the selective enrichment of transcriptionally competent RelA complexes from complex biological samples, thereby facilitating their accurate quantification using SID-SRM-MS. The invention represents a significant advancement over existing technologies by providing the first method capable of directly measuring the absolute number of activated RelA molecules in both cytoplasmic and nuclear compartments under basal and stimulated conditions. This capability has profound implications for understanding the quantitative dynamics of NF-κB signaling, developing more precise diagnostic biomarkers for inflammatory diseases, and designing targeted therapeutic interventions that specifically modulate the activated fraction of RelA without affecting its inactive pool.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) aptamer designated P028F4 that specifically binds to the activated, inhibitor of kappa B alpha (IκBα)-free conformation of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) RelA protein with high affinity and selectivity. The aptamer comprises a 30-nucleotide variable region containing the sequence GGGAC, which corresponds to half of the consensus NF-κB binding site, flanked by standard primer binding sequences. The invention further encompasses compositions comprising this aptamer conjugated to detectable labels, solid supports, or other functional moieties for various applications.

In one aspect, the invention provides methods for selectively enriching activated RelA protein complexes from biological samples comprising contacting the sample with the P028F4 aptamer under conditions that permit specific binding, followed by separation of the aptamer-RelA complexes from unbound material. This enrichment process dramatically reduces sample complexity while specifically isolating the transcriptionally competent fraction of RelA that is not associated with IκBα.

In another aspect, the invention provides quantitative methods for determining the absolute amount of activated RelA in biological samples by combining the selective enrichment capability of the P028F4 aptamer with stable isotope dilution-selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry (SID-SRM-MS). This integrated approach enables precise measurement of activated RelA molecules with unprecedented sensitivity and specificity, overcoming the limitations of traditional proteomic methods when applied to low-abundance transcription factors in complex matrices.

The invention also provides methods for detecting activated RelA in fixed cellular preparations using fluorescently labeled P028F4 aptamer, enabling visualization and colocalization studies with other nuclear markers through confocal microscopy. Additionally, the invention encompasses methods for inhibiting NF-κB-dependent gene expression by introducing the P028F4 aptamer into cells, where it competes with endogenous κB DNA binding sites for RelA binding, thereby preventing transcriptional activation of target genes.

Furthermore, the invention provides diagnostic and prognostic methods for assessing NF-κB pathway activation status in patient samples by quantifying the ratio of activated to total RelA using the disclosed aptamer-based enrichment and SID-SRM-MS methodology. This quantitative assessment provides more accurate information about the functional state of the NF-κB pathway compared to measurements of total RelA levels or nuclear translocation alone.

The invention additionally encompasses kits comprising the P028F4 aptamer, along with appropriate buffers, controls, and instructions for performing enrichment, detection, quantification, or inhibition assays. These kits facilitate standardized implementation of the disclosed methods across different laboratory settings and enable reproducible results for both research and clinical applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a novel single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) aptamer, designated P028F4, that specifically recognizes and binds to the activated, inhibitor of kappa B alpha (IκBα)-free conformation of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) RelA protein. This aptamer represents a significant advancement in the field of molecular recognition reagents for transcription factors, particularly because it discriminates between the inactive (IκBα-bound) and active (free) conformations of RelA, a capability that has not been previously achieved with existing affinity reagents. The P028F4 aptamer was isolated through a systematic selection process involving multiple rounds of in vitro binding and enrichment against recombinant glutathione-S-transferase (GST)-tagged RelA protein encompassing amino acids 1-313 of the human sequence.

The nucleotide sequence of the P028F4 aptamer comprises a 30-base variable region containing the core motif GGGAC, which corresponds to the 5' half of the consensus NF-κB binding site (GGGACTTTCC), flanked by standard primer binding sequences necessary for amplification during the selection process. Specifically, the complete 70-mer sequence includes the forward primer binding site (5'-GGTAACCTTGAGTCACGAATTCAA-3') at the 5' end, followed by the 30-base variable region, and concluding with the reverse primer binding site (5'-CAGAAGCTGTAAGTTGGGTACCTT-3') at the 3' end. The variable region sequence of P028F4 has been determined to be essential for RelA binding, as demonstrated by competition experiments showing that the 30-base variable region alone is sufficient for specific interaction with the target protein. Secondary structure analysis using computational prediction algorithms indicates that the P028F4 aptamer does not form significant secondary structures that would interfere with its binding capability, and importantly, lacks complementary sequences that would allow it to form double-stranded DNA regions.

The binding characteristics of the P028F4 aptamer have been extensively characterized using multiple biophysical and biochemical techniques. Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) measurements demonstrate that P028F4 binds to recombinant GST-RelA(1-313) with high affinity, exhibiting a dissociation constant (K_D) of 6.4 × 10^(-10) M, calculated from an association rate constant (k_a) of 1.8 × 10^6 M^(-1)s^(-1) and a dissociation rate constant (k_d) of 0.0018 s^(-1). This binding affinity represents approximately a 10-fold improvement over previously reported ssRNA aptamers targeting NF-κB proteins. The slow dissociation rate (long off-rate) of the P028F4-RelA interaction is particularly advantageous for applications requiring stable complex formation, such as affinity purification and enrichment procedures.

Critical to the utility of the P028F4 aptamer is its ability to specifically recognize only the activated, IκBα-free conformation of RelA. This selectivity has been rigorously demonstrated through multiple experimental approaches. Electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSAs) using purified recombinant proteins show that while P028F4 binds effectively to free GST-RelA(1-313), it fails to bind to RelA when complexed with full-length GST-IκBα. This discrimination is consistent with the known structural mechanism by which IκBα inhibits RelA function: the sixth ankyrin repeat domain of IκBα occludes the DNA-binding cleft of RelA, thereby preventing interaction with both natural κB DNA sequences and the P028F4 aptamer. Additional evidence for this conformational selectivity comes from cellular studies demonstrating that P028F4 colocalizes specifically with activated EGFP-RelA in the nuclei of TNFα-stimulated cells but shows no binding to RelA in unstimulated cells where it remains predominantly complexed with IκBα in the cytoplasm.

The invention provides comprehensive methods for utilizing the P028F4 aptamer in various applications, beginning with the selective enrichment of activated RelA complexes from biological samples. In a typical enrichment procedure, biological samples such as whole-cell extracts, cytoplasmic extracts, or nuclear extracts are prepared from cells under defined conditions (e.g., unstimulated versus TNFα-stimulated). The P028F4 aptamer, preferably conjugated to a biotin moiety at its 5' or 3' terminus, is added to the extract at a final concentration ranging from 10-50 nM and incubated for 1-3 hours at 4°C to allow specific binding to occur. Streptavidin-conjugated magnetic beads are then introduced to capture the biotinylated aptamer-RelA complexes, followed by extensive washing to remove non-specifically bound proteins. This enrichment process achieves remarkable specificity, with studies demonstrating 14-30 fold enrichment of activated RelA compared to control aptamers, while effectively excluding IκBα and other non-specific proteins from the final preparation.

The enriched activated RelA complexes can be utilized in multiple downstream applications. For qualitative analysis, the captured proteins can be eluted from the beads and analyzed by Western blotting using antibodies specific for RelA, phosphorylated RelA (e.g., Ser536-phosphorylated RelA), or associated co-activators such as p300. This approach has revealed that the P028F4-enriched fraction contains not only activated RelA but also post-translationally modified isoforms, including higher molecular weight species suggestive of ubiquitinated RelA complexes, as well as transcriptionally active complexes containing p300 histone acetyltransferase.

For quantitative analysis, the invention provides an integrated methodology combining P028F4-mediated enrichment with stable isotope dilution-selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry (SID-SRM-MS). This approach involves on-bead tryptic digestion of the enriched protein complexes, followed by spiking with known amounts of stable isotope-labeled (SIS) peptide standards corresponding to signature peptides derived from RelA and other proteins of interest. The resulting peptide mixtures are then analyzed by LC-SRM-MS using optimized collision energies and high-resolution acquisition parameters. The use of multiple signature peptides per protein, each monitored through multiple precursor-product ion transitions, ensures high specificity and accuracy in quantification. This methodology has been validated to provide linear responses over a 1000-fold concentration range with coefficients of variation below 20% at the lower limit of quantification (200 amol).

The quantitative capabilities of this integrated approach have enabled, for the first time, the determination of absolute numbers of activated RelA molecules in different cellular compartments under various conditions. Studies using this methodology have revealed that in unstimulated A549 pulmonary epithelial cells, approximately 5% of total cytoplasmic RelA exists in the activated (IκBα-free) state, while nuclear RelA levels are minimal. Following TNFα stimulation (30 ng/ml for 45 minutes), the activated RelA fraction in the cytoplasm increases approximately six-fold, and a significant portion translocates to the nucleus, where the amount of activated RelA equals the total nuclear RelA pool, indicating that virtually all nuclear RelA exists in the transcriptionally competent state.

The invention also encompasses methods for detecting activated RelA in fixed cellular preparations using fluorescently labeled P028F4 aptamer. In this application, cells are first stimulated under appropriate conditions, then fixed and permeabilized to allow aptamer access to intracellular targets. Fluorescently conjugated P028F4 (e.g., TEX615-labeled) is applied to the fixed cells and allowed to bind specifically to activated RelA. Confocal microscopy reveals strong nuclear staining with P028F4 only in TNFα-stimulated cells, with perfect colocalization observed between the fluorescent aptamer signal and EGFP-RelA fluorescence in transfected cells. Control experiments using a non-specific aptamer (P028A1) show no specific staining under identical conditions, confirming the specificity of the P028F4 interaction.

Additionally, the invention provides methods for functionally modulating NF-κB activity through the introduction of P028F4 aptamer into living cells. When transfected into cells at micromolar concentrations (typically 4 μM), P028F4 effectively competes with endogenous κB DNA binding sites for RelA binding, thereby inhibiting NF-κB-dependent gene expression. Quantitative reverse transcriptase PCR (Q-RT-PCR) analysis demonstrates that P028F4 transfection significantly reduces TNFα-induced expression of NF-κB target genes such as Groβ and TNFAIP3/A20, while having no effect on housekeeping genes like GAPDH. Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays further confirm that P028F4 treatment reduces RelA occupancy at endogenous promoter regions of target genes, providing direct evidence for its mechanism of action as a competitive inhibitor of RelA-DNA interactions.

The diagnostic and prognostic applications of the invention are particularly significant for inflammatory and autoimmune diseases where dysregulated NF-κB signaling plays a central role. By quantifying the ratio of activated to total RelA in patient-derived samples (e.g., peripheral blood mononuclear cells, tissue biopsies, or cultured primary cells), clinicians can obtain more accurate assessments of NF-κB pathway activation status compared to traditional methods that measure only total RelA levels or nuclear translocation. This quantitative information can guide therapeutic decisions, monitor treatment response, and potentially predict disease progression or relapse.

The invention further encompasses comprehensive kits designed to facilitate the implementation of these methodologies in research and clinical laboratories. Such kits typically include lyophilized or solution-form P028F4 aptamer (with or without biotin/fluorophore conjugation), appropriate buffers for binding and washing, streptavidin-magnetic beads, SIS peptide standards for key target proteins, detailed protocols for sample preparation and analysis, and quality control materials. These standardized reagents ensure reproducible results across different users and laboratory settings, enabling widespread adoption of the disclosed technologies.

The scope of the invention extends beyond the specific P028F4 sequence disclosed herein to include variants and derivatives that maintain the essential binding characteristics and conformational selectivity for activated RelA. Such variants may include truncated versions containing only the essential 30-base variable region, extended versions with additional stabilizing sequences, chemically modified versions with enhanced nuclease resistance (e.g., phosphorothioate linkages, 2'-O-methyl modifications), or conjugated versions with alternative functional groups (e.g., different fluorophores, affinity tags, or drug molecules). The invention also encompasses the use of P028F4 in combination with other molecular tools and technologies, such as proximity ligation assays, fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) sensors, or microfluidic platforms, to develop next-generation diagnostic and analytical devices.

In summary, the present invention provides a comprehensive platform for the selective recognition, enrichment, detection, quantification, and functional modulation of activated NF-κB RelA protein complexes. The P028F4 aptamer represents a breakthrough reagent that overcomes longstanding limitations in NF-κB research by enabling direct measurement of the transcriptionally competent fraction of RelA with unprecedented precision and specificity. This capability has far-reaching implications for basic research into NF-κB signaling dynamics, development of novel diagnostic biomarkers for inflammatory diseases, and design of targeted therapeutic strategies that specifically modulate the activated state of this critical transcription factor.
