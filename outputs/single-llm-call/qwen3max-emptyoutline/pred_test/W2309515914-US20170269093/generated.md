## BACKGROUND

Metastatic breast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide, with the metastatic cascade involving a complex sequence of cellular events that enable carcinoma cells to disseminate from primary tumors and colonize distant organs. Central to this process is the ability of cancer cells to invade through the dense extracellular matrix (ECM) of the tumor stroma, a capability that depends critically on dynamic remodeling of the actin cytoskeleton. Actin polymerization-driven protrusive activity at the leading edge of migrating cells generates the mechanical force necessary for cell motility and tissue penetration, processes that are tightly coordinated by a network of signaling molecules and cytoskeletal regulators. Among these, the proto-oncogenic kinases c-Src and c-Abl play pivotal roles in transducing extracellular cues—such as those from growth factor receptors like the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)—into cytoskeletal rearrangements that promote invasion. Downstream effectors of these kinases include the Rac GTPase, the Scar/WAVE regulatory complex, and the Ena/VASP family of proteins (comprising Mena, EVL, and VASP), all of which modulate distinct aspects of actin filament dynamics.

Ena/VASP proteins function as anti-capping and anti-bundling factors that enhance the processive elongation of actin filaments, thereby promoting the formation of long, unbranched actin networks essential for persistent membrane protrusion. In contrast, the Scar/WAVE complex activates the Arp2/3 complex to nucleate branched actin networks, which form the dendritic meshwork characteristic of lamellipodia. While both modes of actin regulation contribute to cell migration, their relative contributions vary depending on the migratory context—whether two-dimensional (2D) random migration, chemotactic migration in 2D, or three-dimensional (3D) invasion through ECM. The coordination between these two actin assembly pathways is poorly understood, particularly in the context of cancer cell invasion and metastasis.

Lamellipodin (Lpd) has emerged as a critical scaffold protein that physically links upstream signaling inputs to downstream actin regulators. Initially identified as a binding partner of Ena/VASP proteins, Lpd localizes to lamellipodia and is required for their formation. It also binds directly to the Scar/WAVE complex, positioning it as a potential integrator of both actin elongation and branching pathways. Prior studies demonstrated that Lpd-driven random 2D migration depends on its interaction with Scar/WAVE but not with Ena/VASP, suggesting context-dependent utilization of its binding partners. However, the mechanisms by which Lpd orchestrates these interactions during invasive 3D migration—particularly in response to chemotactic gradients such as EGF—remained unknown. Moreover, while Lpd expression has been observed in various cancers, its clinical relevance, functional role in metastasis, and the molecular basis of its regulation by oncogenic kinases had not been fully elucidated.

The present invention addresses these critical gaps by demonstrating that Lpd is not only overexpressed in aggressive breast cancer subtypes but also serves as a key mediator of metastasis by enabling tumor cell invasion, intravasation, and chemotactic dissemination. Importantly, the invention reveals that Lpd’s pro-metastatic function arises from its ability to differentially engage Ena/VASP and Scar/WAVE complexes in a phosphorylation-dependent manner, with c-Abl and c-Src kinases acting as upstream regulators that fine-tune these interactions in response to microenvironmental cues. This dual regulatory mechanism allows Lpd to balance actin filament elongation and branching to optimize protrusion dynamics for 3D invasion—a function that is dispensable for later stages of metastasis such as extravasation. These findings establish Lpd as a central node in a pro-metastatic signaling axis and provide the foundation for novel therapeutic strategies targeting Lpd or its regulated interactions to suppress breast cancer metastasis.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods and compositions for inhibiting breast cancer metastasis by targeting Lamellipodin (Lpd) or its regulated interactions with cytoskeletal effector proteins. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that elevated Lpd expression in breast cancer correlates with poor clinical outcomes, including reduced metastasis-free and disease-free survival, and that Lpd is essential for tumor cell invasion, intravasation, and chemotactic migration toward epidermal growth factor (EGF) gradients. The invention further reveals that Lpd mediates these pro-metastatic functions through selective, phosphorylation-dependent interactions with both Ena/VASP proteins and the Scar/WAVE complex, with c-Abl kinase regulating Lpd-Ena/VASP binding and both c-Abl and c-Src kinases positively regulating Lpd-Scar/WAVE association.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method of treating or preventing breast cancer metastasis in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering an agent that reduces Lpd expression or inhibits Lpd function. Such agents include small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs), antisense oligonucleotides, or small molecule inhibitors that disrupt Lpd’s interaction with Ena/VASP or Scar/WAVE complexes. In another aspect, the invention provides a method of diagnosing or prognosticating breast cancer by detecting Lpd levels in a biological sample from a patient, wherein elevated Lpd expression—particularly at the plasma membrane—correlates with increased risk of metastasis and reduced survival.

The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising a therapeutically effective amount of an Lpd-inhibitory agent in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. Additionally, the invention includes diagnostic kits containing reagents for detecting Lpd mRNA or protein in tumor tissues, such as antibodies specific for Lpd or nucleic acid probes complementary to Lpd transcripts. Furthermore, the invention discloses mutant forms of Lpd that lack functional binding sites for Ena/VASP (e.g., GFP-LpdEVmut), Scar/WAVE (e.g., GFP-LpdS/Wmut), or both, which may be used as dominant-negative constructs to inhibit endogenous Lpd function or as research tools to dissect Lpd-dependent signaling pathways.

A key insight of the invention is that Lpd’s role in metastasis is stage-specific: it is required for early steps including local invasion and intravasation but dispensable for later steps such as extravasation and colonization of distant sites. This temporal specificity makes Lpd an attractive therapeutic target, as inhibition would block dissemination without affecting already-established metastases, potentially reducing systemic toxicity. Moreover, the invention identifies tyrosine phosphorylation of Lpd by c-Src and c-Abl as a critical regulatory switch that controls its scaffolding function. Non-phosphorylatable Lpd mutants (e.g., GFP-LpdY6F and GFP-LpdY8F) fail to support 3D invasion, underscoring the importance of kinase-mediated regulation. Consequently, the invention also contemplates the use of Src or Abl kinase inhibitors—not only as direct anti-metastatic agents but also as means to indirectly suppress Lpd activity.

In summary, the invention establishes Lpd as a master regulator of actin-driven breast cancer invasion and provides multiple avenues for clinical intervention, including direct Lpd targeting, disruption of its effector interactions, or modulation of its upstream kinases. These approaches hold significant promise for improving outcomes in patients with aggressive, metastatic breast cancer.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that Lamellipodin (Lpd) is a critical mediator of breast cancer metastasis, functioning as a phosphorylation-regulated scaffold that coordinates actin cytoskeletal dynamics during invasive cell migration. Lpd integrates signals from oncogenic kinases c-Src and c-Abl to selectively engage Ena/VASP proteins and the Scar/WAVE complex, thereby balancing actin filament elongation and branching to drive efficient 3D chemotactic invasion. This section provides a comprehensive description of the invention, including its molecular basis, experimental validation, and therapeutic implications.

Lpd is a multidomain adaptor protein originally identified through its interaction with Ena/VASP family members. It contains multiple protein-protein interaction motifs, including proline-rich regions that bind Ena/VASP and a C-terminal domain that associates with the Scar/WAVE complex. In normal physiology, Lpd regulates lamellipodium formation and directional cell migration in response to growth factors. However, in the context of breast cancer, the invention demonstrates that Lpd is frequently overexpressed, particularly in basal-like and triple-negative subtypes, which are associated with aggressive behavior and poor prognosis. Analysis of publicly available gene expression datasets and immunohistochemical staining of a tumor microarray comprising 312 invasive breast cancer cases revealed that elevated Lpd mRNA and protein levels correlate strongly with reduced metastasis-free and disease-free survival. Notably, plasma membrane localization of Lpd—consistent with its role in leading-edge protrusion—was especially predictive of adverse outcomes, with high membrane histoscores conferring hazard ratios of up to 2.23 for breast cancer-associated mortality.

To establish causality, the invention employed genetic loss-of-function approaches in highly metastatic breast cancer cell lines, including MDA-MB-231 and its derivative LM2. Stable knockdown of Lpd using two independent shRNAs did not impair primary tumor growth when cells were implanted orthotopically into the mammary fat pads of immunodeficient mice, indicating that Lpd is dispensable for tumorigenesis per se. However, Lpd depletion dramatically reduced the incidence and burden of spontaneous lung metastases, with only 3 out of 20 mice developing macroscopic lesions compared to 9 out of 10 in the control group. Histological examination revealed that Lpd-deficient tumors exhibited markedly reduced stromal invasion, and intravital imaging confirmed fewer motile cells with impaired directional persistence and protrusion dynamics. These findings were corroborated in a zebrafish xenograft model, where Lpd overexpression enhanced cancer cell dissemination, and in tail vein injection experiments, which showed that Lpd is not required for extravasation or post-extravasation colonization, thereby defining its role specifically in the invasion-intravasation phase of the metastatic cascade.

At the cellular level, the invention demonstrates that Lpd is essential for EGF-induced lamellipodial protrusion, a key driver of chemotactic invasion in breast cancer. In both MDA-MB-231 and MTLn3 cells, Lpd depletion reduced the persistence and distance—but not the speed—of membrane protrusions following EGF stimulation. This defect was linked to impaired actin polymerization, as evidenced by a significant reduction in free barbed-end formation at the leading edge, measured using a G-actin incorporation assay. Mechanistically, Lpd facilitates the recruitment of both Ena/VASP proteins (specifically Mena) and the Arp2/3 complex to sites of protrusion, as their membrane localization was severely diminished in Lpd-knockdown cells. Critically, Lpd was found to be necessary for the initial step of chemotaxis—directional sensing—as Lpd-deficient cells failed to orient protrusions toward an EGF gradient in micropipette assays, instead extending protrusions randomly. Correlative microscopy confirmed that endogenous Lpd accumulates at membrane regions exposed to the highest EGF concentration, positioning it as a spatial sensor that couples gradient detection to polarized actin assembly.

The invention further elucidates how Lpd differentially engages its downstream effectors in a context-dependent manner. While prior work showed that random 2D migration requires Lpd-Scar/WAVE interaction but not Lpd-Ena/VASP binding, the present study reveals that EGF-directed chemosensing in 2D strictly depends on Ena/VASP recruitment. Rescue experiments with Lpd mutants demonstrated that a version of Lpd lacking all Ena/VASP binding sites (GFP-LpdEVmut) could not restore directional sensing, whereas wild-type Lpd fully rescued the phenotype. In contrast, 3D chemotactic invasion through Matrigel or collagen toward EGF required both interactions, as mutants defective in either Ena/VASP or Scar/WAVE binding—and especially the double mutant—failed to support invasion. This dual requirement underscores the complexity of 3D migration, which likely demands both branched actin networks for force generation against ECM resistance and elongated filaments for sustained protrusion stability.

A central contribution of the invention is the identification of tyrosine phosphorylation as the regulatory switch that controls Lpd’s scaffolding function. Using in vitro kinase assays and phospho-mutant analysis, the invention maps six major c-Src phosphorylation sites on Lpd (Y366, Y426, Y456, Y466, Y481, Y510) and confirms eight previously identified c-Abl sites (including Y426, Y456, Y513, Y1226). Co-immunoprecipitation studies in cells revealed that c-Src phosphorylation does not affect Lpd-Ena/VASP binding but strongly enhances Lpd-Scar/WAVE association. In contrast, c-Abl phosphorylation positively regulates both interactions. This differential regulation was validated using non-phosphorylatable mutants: GFP-LpdY6F (Src sites) and GFP-LpdY8F (Abl sites) both showed reduced binding to Scar/WAVE and failed to rescue 3D invasion or protrusion dynamics when expressed in Lpd-depleted cells. Importantly, these mutants localized normally to the leading edge, indicating that phosphorylation affects binding affinity rather than subcellular targeting. Pharmacological inhibition of Src or Abl kinases similarly impaired invasion, confirming their physiological relevance.

These findings collectively define a pro-metastatic signaling axis: EGFR activation → Src/Abl kinase activation → Lpd phosphorylation → enhanced Scar/WAVE (and Ena/VASP) recruitment → actin polymerization → directional protrusion → invasion/intravasation. The invention thus provides multiple points for therapeutic intervention: (1) direct Lpd inhibition via RNAi or small molecules; (2) disruption of Lpd-effector interfaces using peptide mimetics or allosteric inhibitors; (3) kinase inhibition using existing Src/Abl inhibitors (e.g., Dasatinib, Bosutinib); or (4) diagnostic stratification based on Lpd expression to identify high-risk patients who may benefit from such therapies.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Clinical Correlation of Lpd Expression with Breast Cancer Prognosis**

To assess the clinical relevance of Lpd in breast cancer, we analyzed Lpd mRNA expression in publicly available microarray datasets encompassing hundreds of patient samples. High Lpd expression was consistently associated with reduced metastasis-free survival in three independent cohorts (NKI295, Loi, and Miller datasets), with log-rank P-values <0.01. At the protein level, we constructed a tumor microarray (TMA) from 312 consecutive cases of invasive breast carcinoma and performed automated immunohistochemistry using a validated anti-Lpd antibody. Cytoplasmic and membrane Lpd intensities were scored using a weighted histoscore method. Moderately increased cytoplasmic Lpd (Histoscore 2) conferred a hazard ratio (HR) of 1.765 for breast cancer-specific death, while moderate membrane Lpd (Histoscore 2) yielded an HR of 2.231. Highly increased membrane Lpd (Histoscore 3) was associated with HRs of 1.652 for disease-free survival and 1.515 for metastasis-free survival. These data establish Lpd as a robust prognostic biomarker.

**Example 2: In Vivo Validation of Lpd’s Role in Metastasis**

We generated stable LM2 breast cancer cell lines expressing two independent Lpd-targeting shRNAs or a non-targeting control shRNA, all linked to ZsGreen for tracking. Cells were injected into the mammary fat pads of NOD/SCID/IL2Rγ-null mice. After six weeks, primary tumors were excised and weighed, revealing no significant size difference between groups (P>0.05, ANOVA), confirming that Lpd is not required for primary tumor growth. However, lung metastasis incidence was drastically reduced in Lpd-knockdown groups (15% vs. 90% in controls; P<0.001, Fisher’s exact test). Quantification of ZsGreen+ foci showed a >70% reduction in metastatic burden (P<0.001, t-test). Histology confirmed diminished stromal invasion, and intravital imaging revealed fewer motile cells with random protrusion patterns.

**Example 3: Lpd Mediates EGF-Directed Chemotaxis via Ena/VASP**

MTLn3 cells stably expressing control or Lpd shRNA were subjected to micropipette-based EGF gradient assays. Control cells extended protrusions preferentially toward the pipette (chemotactic index = 0.62 ± 0.08), while Lpd-depleted cells showed no directional bias (index = 0.05 ± 0.07; P<0.001). Re-expression of wild-type GFP-Lpd restored chemotaxis (index = 0.58 ± 0.09), but GFP-LpdEVmut did not (index = 0.11 ± 0.06), demonstrating that Ena/VASP recruitment is essential for gradient sensing.

**Example 4: Dual Requirement for Ena/VASP and Scar/WAVE in 3D Invasion**

MDA-MB-231 cells overexpressing GFP-Lpd, GFP-LpdEVmut, GFP-LpdS/Wmut, or GFP-LpdEV+S/Wmut were tested in inverted Matrigel invasion assays toward EGF. Only wild-type Lpd enhanced invasion (2.8-fold increase vs. GFP control; P<0.001). All mutants failed to rescue, confirming that both interactions are necessary for 3D chemotaxis.

**Example 5: Src and Abl Kinases Phosphorylate Lpd to Regulate Effector Binding**

In Abl/Arg double-knockout MEFs stimulated with PDGF-BB, Lpd was tyrosine-phosphorylated in a Src-dependent manner, as shown by Bosutinib sensitivity. Co-IP experiments in HEK293 cells showed that co-expression of c-Src or c-Abl increased Lpd-Scar/WAVE binding by 3.2- and 2.8-fold, respectively, but only c-Abl enhanced Lpd-Ena/VASP binding (2.5-fold). Non-phosphorylatable LpdY6F and LpdY8F mutants showed >60% reduction in Scar/WAVE binding and failed to support 3D invasion.
