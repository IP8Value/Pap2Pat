# DESCRIPTION

## INTRODUCTION

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a hematologic malignancy characterized by the uncontrolled proliferation of clonal plasma cells within the bone marrow microenvironment. This disease represents a terminal phase in a spectrum of plasma cell dyscrasias that begins with the asymptomatic premalignant condition known as monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS), which may progress through an intermediate smoldering multiple myeloma stage before culminating in overt, symptomatic MM. The clinical course of MM is highly heterogeneous, with patient outcomes ranging from indolent disease requiring minimal intervention to aggressive, rapidly progressive forms that are refractory to standard therapies. This variability in disease behavior is underpinned by a complex landscape of genomic alterations that accumulate during disease progression and contribute to therapeutic resistance, immune evasion, and proliferative advantage. Understanding the molecular architecture of MM is therefore essential for accurate risk stratification, treatment selection, and the development of novel therapeutic strategies.

The genomic landscape of MM is broadly categorized into two major subtypes that are largely mutually exclusive: hyperdiploid (HRD) and non-hyperdiploid disease. Hyperdiploid MM is characterized by trisomies affecting multiple odd-numbered chromosomes, including chromosomes 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 15, 19, and 21. In contrast, non-hyperdiploid MM is frequently associated with immunoglobulin heavy chain (IGH) translocations that juxtapose oncogenes with the powerful enhancer elements of the IGH locus on chromosome 14q32. These canonical translocations include t(4;14)(p16;q32), which dysregulates WHSC1 (also known as MMSET) and FGFR3; t(11;14)(q13;q32), which upregulates CCND1; t(6;14)(p21;q32), which affects CCND3; t(14;16)(q32;q23), which involves MAF; and t(14;20)(q32;q12), which dysregulates MAFB. These primary genetic events are typically present in the MGUS phase and are considered initiating lesions that drive early clonal expansion.

In addition to these primary events, secondary genetic alterations accumulate during disease progression and are associated with more aggressive clinical phenotypes. These secondary events include MYC translocations involving the IGH locus or other partner chromosomes, activating point mutations in KRAS and NRAS, inactivating mutations in tumor suppressor genes such as TP53 and RB1, and copy number alterations (CNVs) that result in amplification of chromosome 1q or deletion of chromosomal regions including 1p, 6q, 13q, 14q, and 17p. The prognostic significance of these alterations has been increasingly recognized, leading to the development of integrated staging systems such as the International Staging System incorporating molecular markers (ISS-MUT), which combines traditional clinical parameters with genomic information to improve risk stratification.

The detection of these diverse genomic alterations has traditionally required multiple complementary assays, including fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) for translocations and CNVs, Sanger sequencing for point mutations, and karyotyping for ploidy assessment. However, this multi-platform approach is resource-intensive, time-consuming, and often requires substantial amounts of patient material, which may be limiting in the context of bone marrow aspirates. Moreover, the integration of results from disparate platforms presents analytical challenges and may lead to incomplete genomic characterization. Recent advances in next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies have enabled the development of targeted sequencing approaches that can simultaneously detect multiple classes of genomic alterations from a single assay, offering a more comprehensive and efficient alternative to conventional testing methods.

Targeted sequencing panels designed for MM have demonstrated the ability to detect single-nucleotide variants (SNVs), small insertions and deletions (indels), and in some cases CNVs, but the reliable detection of structural variants such as translocations has remained challenging. The IGH locus presents particular difficulties due to its complex repetitive structure, extensive somatic hypermutation, and the diversity of potential translocation breakpoints across the variable (V), diversity (D), joining (J), and constant/switch regions. Furthermore, the detection of CNVs from targeted sequencing data requires specialized bioinformatic approaches that account for technical biases in capture efficiency and sequencing depth, while maintaining sufficient sensitivity to detect focal alterations against the background of genome-wide copy number changes characteristic of MM.

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a comprehensive, MM-specific targeted sequencing platform that enables the simultaneous detection of SNVs, CNVs, and translocations—including both canonical IGH translocations and novel structural variants—from a single assay. This platform incorporates a carefully designed probe set that covers 465 genes selected based on their established or putative roles in MM pathogenesis, as well as comprehensive coverage of the entire IGH locus and key oncogenes involved in translocations. The invention further encompasses novel computational methods for the accurate calling of CNVs and translocations from targeted sequencing data, which have been optimized and validated against orthogonal reference standards including exome sequencing and FISH. Through application of this integrated platform to a cohort of 95 primary MM samples, the inventors have not only confirmed the reliability of the approach for detecting known genomic alterations but have also discovered novel genetic events with potential clinical significance, including recurrent mutations in IGLL5 and previously uncharacterized IGH translocations. These findings demonstrate the utility of the invention for comprehensive genomic profiling of MM and highlight its potential for improving risk stratification, treatment selection, and the discovery of novel therapeutic targets.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a comprehensive, targeted sequencing platform specifically designed for the simultaneous detection of multiple classes of genomic alterations in multiple myeloma (MM), including single-nucleotide variants (SNVs), copy number variations (CNVs), and chromosomal translocations. This platform comprises a custom-designed oligonucleotide probe set that targets approximately 3.3 megabases of genomic space, encompassing 465 genes selected based on their established or putative roles in MM pathogenesis, as well as comprehensive coverage of the immunoglobulin heavy chain (IGH) locus and key oncogenes involved in translocations. The invention further includes novel computational methods for the accurate calling of CNVs and translocations from targeted sequencing data, which have been specifically optimized for the unique genomic characteristics of MM and validated against orthogonal reference standards.

The probe set of the invention targets exons, untranslated regions, and splice sites of 465 genes that meet one or more of the following criteria: (1) annotation as cancer genes in established databases such as COSMIC or identification as significantly mutated in published studies; (2) involvement in DNA repair pathways or B-cell biology; (3) mutation frequency exceeding 3% in published MM cohorts; or (4) presence of mutation hotspots. To enable comprehensive detection of IGH translocations, the probe set includes unbiased tiling across the entire IGH locus, covering the variable (IGHV), diversity (IGHD), joining (IGHJ), and constant/switch regions, as well as targeted coverage of the exonic regions of canonical IGH translocation partners including CCND1, CCND3, FGFR3, MAF, MAFB, WHSC1, and WWOX. Additionally, the probe set includes comprehensive coverage of the MYC locus, including both exonic and intronic regions, to facilitate detection of secondary MYC translocations.

The computational methods of the invention include a novel approach for CNV detection that utilizes per-probe ratios of tumor to normal sequencing depth analyzed using the CopyCAT2 algorithm, with an additional filtering step that removes calls with ratios below a noise threshold estimated from diploid genomic regions using a Gaussian mixture model. This approach enables reliable detection of chromosome-level, arm-level, and focal CNVs, including prognostically significant alterations such as amplification of 1q, deletion of 1p, 13q, and 17p, and genome-wide hyperdiploidy. For translocation detection, the invention employs the LUMPY algorithm combined with a machine learning-based filtering approach that has been specifically tuned to maximize precision relative to available fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) validation data. This method achieves 100% precision and 64% recall for IGH translocations and has been further adapted for the detection of MYC translocations through a specialized filtering strategy that accounts for the high false-positive rate observed in normal samples.

Application of this integrated platform to a cohort of 95 primary MM samples has demonstrated high concordance with orthogonal validation methods, including exome sequencing for SNVs and FISH for translocations. The platform successfully detected canonical IGH translocations at expected frequencies and identified the full spectrum of prognostically relevant CNVs. Importantly, the comprehensive nature of the platform enabled the discovery of novel genetic alterations with potential clinical significance, including recurrent mutations in IGLL5 and previously uncharacterized IGH translocations involving the IGLL5 locus. The IGLL5 mutations were found to be mutually exclusive with RAS mutations and associated with disease progression, suggesting a potential role in MM pathogenesis or as a biomarker of high-risk disease. Additionally, the platform facilitated integrative analysis across mutation types, revealing patterns of mutual exclusivity and co-occurrence that provide insights into the molecular architecture of MM.

The invention further demonstrates that sequencing depths of approximately 100× are sufficient to capture the majority of clinically relevant genomic alterations in MM, with diminishing returns observed beyond 300× coverage. This finding has important implications for clinical implementation, as it enables comprehensive genomic profiling at a reasonable cost and turnaround time. The platform's ability to simultaneously detect multiple classes of genomic alterations from a single assay represents a significant advance over conventional multi-platform approaches, offering improved efficiency, reduced sample requirements, and more comprehensive genomic characterization. The invention thus provides a powerful tool for risk stratification, treatment selection, and the discovery of novel therapeutic targets in MM, with potential applications extending to other B-cell malignancies and hematologic disorders.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive, targeted sequencing platform specifically designed for the simultaneous detection of multiple classes of genomic alterations in multiple myeloma (MM), including single-nucleotide variants (SNVs), copy number variations (CNVs), and chromosomal translocations. This platform represents a significant advancement over existing approaches by integrating the detection of all three major classes of genomic alterations into a single, efficient assay that can be readily implemented in clinical and research settings. The invention addresses several key limitations of conventional genomic profiling methods in MM, including the need for multiple complementary assays, the difficulty of detecting structural variants from targeted sequencing data, and the challenges associated with accurate CNV calling from hybrid capture-based sequencing.

At the core of the invention is a custom-designed oligonucleotide probe set that targets approximately 3.3 megabases of genomic space. This probe set was developed through careful consideration of the known genomic landscape of MM and incorporates several innovative design features that enable comprehensive detection of clinically relevant alterations. The probe set targets 465 genes that were selected based on multiple criteria designed to maximize clinical utility and biological relevance. Specifically, genes were included if they met one or more of the following criteria: (1) annotation as cancer genes in established databases such as the Catalogue of Somatic Mutations in Cancer (COSMIC) or identification as significantly mutated in published studies using algorithms such as MutSig; (2) known involvement in DNA repair pathways or B-cell biology, which are particularly relevant to MM pathogenesis; (3) reported mutation frequency exceeding 3% in published MM cohorts, ensuring coverage of commonly altered genes; or (4) presence of mutation hotspots, which may indicate functional importance even in genes with lower overall mutation frequencies.

The selection of these 465 genes represents a substantial expansion over previous targeted panels for MM, which typically covered fewer than 100 genes. This broader coverage enables more comprehensive detection of SNVs and indels across the MM genome, including both well-established driver mutations and potentially novel alterations in less-studied genes. The probe set targets exons, untranslated regions (UTRs), and splice sites of these genes, ensuring comprehensive coverage of functionally relevant regions while maintaining reasonable panel size and cost. This design allows for the detection of missense, nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site mutations that may have functional consequences for protein function or gene expression.

A particularly innovative aspect of the probe set design is its comprehensive coverage of the immunoglobulin heavy chain (IGH) locus on chromosome 14q32. The IGH locus is notoriously difficult to sequence due to its complex repetitive structure, extensive somatic hypermutation in B-cell malignancies, and the diversity of potential translocation breakpoints. Previous targeted approaches have often focused only on annotated segments within the variable (V), diversity (D), and joining (J) regions, potentially missing translocations that occur in inter-segment regions. The present invention addresses this limitation by employing unbiased tiling across the entire IGH locus, including all V, D, J, and constant/switch regions. This comprehensive coverage enables the detection of IGH translocations regardless of breakpoint location, significantly improving the sensitivity for detecting both canonical and novel translocations.

In addition to comprehensive IGH locus coverage, the probe set includes targeted coverage of the exonic regions of canonical IGH translocation partners, including CCND1 (11q13), CCND3 (6p21), FGFR3 and WHSC1 (4p16), MAF (16q23), MAFB (20q12), and WWOX (16q23). This dual approach—comprehensive IGH coverage combined with targeted partner gene coverage—maximizes the likelihood of detecting known IGH translocations while also enabling the discovery of novel translocation partners. The probe set also includes comprehensive coverage of the MYC locus on chromosome 8q24, including both exonic and intronic regions, to facilitate detection of secondary MYC translocations that are associated with disease progression in MM.

The invention further encompasses novel computational methods specifically designed for the accurate calling of CNVs and translocations from targeted sequencing data. For CNV detection, the invention employs the CopyCAT2 algorithm, which analyzes per-probe ratios of tumor to normal sequencing depth to identify copy number alterations. However, the inventors recognized that standard CNV calling approaches often produce false positives in targeted sequencing data due to technical biases in capture efficiency and sequencing depth. To address this issue, the invention includes a novel filtering approach that removes CNV calls with ratios below a noise threshold estimated from diploid genomic regions using a Gaussian mixture model. This approach significantly improves the specificity of CNV calling while maintaining high sensitivity for detecting clinically relevant alterations.

For translocation detection, the invention employs the LUMPY algorithm, which integrates multiple signals including split reads and discordant paired-end reads to identify structural variants. However, the inventors recognized that standard translocation calling approaches produce high false-positive rates, particularly in complex genomic regions like the IGH locus. To address this challenge, the invention includes a machine learning-based filtering approach that has been specifically tuned to maximize precision relative to available fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) validation data. This approach uses features such as the number of supporting split reads, the number of discordant paired-end reads, and various quality metrics to distinguish true translocations from technical artifacts. The resulting method achieves 100% precision and 64% recall for IGH translocations, representing a significant improvement over standard approaches.

The MYC translocation detection method presented additional challenges, as FISH validation data were not available for tuning the parameters. The inventors observed that MYC translocations were called at a high false-positive rate in both tumor and normal samples, suggesting that many calls represented technical artifacts rather than true somatic events. To address this issue, the invention includes a specialized filtering strategy that compares translocation calls between tumor and normal samples from the same patient, removing calls that are present in the normal sample. This approach effectively eliminates germline structural variants and technical artifacts, enabling reliable detection of somatic MYC translocations.

The invention has been extensively validated using a cohort of 95 primary MM samples, including 44 samples that were previously subjected to exome sequencing and 22 samples that were previously assayed by FISH for IGH translocations. This validation demonstrated high concordance between the targeted platform and orthogonal reference standards, confirming the reliability of the approach for detecting known genomic alterations. The platform successfully detected canonical IGH translocations at expected frequencies and identified the full spectrum of prognostically relevant CNVs, including amplification of 1q, deletion of 1p, 13q, and 17p, and genome-wide hyperdiploidy.

Importantly, the comprehensive nature of the platform enabled the discovery of novel genetic alterations with potential clinical significance. The inventors identified recurrent mutations in IGLL5, a gene located within the immunoglobulin lambda light chain locus, which were found to be mutually exclusive with RAS mutations and associated with disease progression. Additionally, the platform facilitated the discovery of novel IGH translocations involving the IGLL5 locus, which were validated by PCR and found to be associated with overexpression of nearby genes including DERL3. These findings demonstrate the utility of the invention for both comprehensive genomic profiling and novel discovery in MM.

The invention also provides important insights into optimal sequencing depth for MM genomic profiling. Through deep sequencing of a subset of 15 samples to mean depths exceeding 1,200×, the inventors demonstrated that sequencing depths of approximately 100× are sufficient to capture the majority of clinically relevant genomic alterations in MM, with diminishing returns observed beyond 300× coverage. This finding has important implications for clinical implementation, as it enables comprehensive genomic profiling at a reasonable cost and turnaround time while minimizing the detection of low-frequency subclonal variants that may not be clinically actionable.

The platform's ability to simultaneously detect multiple classes of genomic alterations from a single assay represents a significant advance over conventional multi-platform approaches. Traditional genomic profiling in MM typically requires separate assays for different classes of alterations: FISH for translocations and CNVs, Sanger sequencing or small-panel NGS for SNVs, and karyotyping for ploidy assessment. This multi-platform approach is resource-intensive, time-consuming, and often requires substantial amounts of patient material, which may be limiting in the context of bone marrow aspirates. Moreover, the integration of results from disparate platforms presents analytical challenges and may lead to incomplete genomic characterization. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a single, integrated platform that can comprehensively characterize the MM genome from limited sample material.

The invention thus provides a powerful tool for multiple applications in MM management and research. In the clinical setting, the platform can be used for comprehensive risk stratification by integrating information about SNVs, CNVs, and translocations into prognostic models such as the ISS-MUT system. This integrated genomic profiling can inform treatment selection by identifying patients who may benefit from specific targeted therapies or who are at high risk of early progression and may require more intensive treatment approaches. In the research setting, the platform enables comprehensive genomic characterization of MM cohorts for biomarker discovery, molecular subclassification, and the identification of novel therapeutic targets. The same probe set can also be adapted for targeted RNA sequencing, enabling integrated analysis of genomic alterations and gene expression from the same samples.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The development and validation of the MM-specific targeted sequencing platform began with the design of a custom oligonucleotide probe set targeting approximately 3.3 megabases of genomic space. This probe set was designed using NimbleGen technology (Roche) and incorporated several innovative features to enable comprehensive detection of clinically relevant genomic alterations in MM. The probe set targeted 465 genes that were selected based on multiple criteria designed to maximize clinical utility and biological relevance. Genes were included if they met one or more of the following criteria: (1) annotation as cancer genes in established databases such as COSMIC or identification as significantly mutated in published studies using algorithms such as MutSig; (2) known involvement in DNA repair pathways or B-cell biology; (3) reported mutation frequency exceeding 3% in published MM cohorts; or (4) presence of mutation hotspots.

To enable comprehensive detection of IGH translocations, the probe set included unbiased tiling across the entire IGH locus on chromosome 14q32, covering approximately 1.2 megabases including the variable (IGHV), diversity (IGHD), joining (IGHJ), and constant/switch regions. This comprehensive coverage was designed to detect translocations regardless of breakpoint location, addressing a key limitation of previous targeted approaches that focused only on annotated segments within these regions. Additionally, the probe set included targeted coverage of the exonic regions of canonical IGH translocation partners, including CCND1 (11q13), CCND3 (6p21), FGFR3 and WHSC1 (4p16), MAF (16q23), MAFB (20q12), and WWOX (16q23). The probe set also included comprehensive coverage of the MYC locus on chromosome 8q24, including both exonic and intronic regions spanning approximately 200 kilobases, to facilitate detection of secondary MYC translocations.

The probe set was used to prepare sequencing libraries from 95 tumor-normal pairs, where tumor samples consisted of CD138-purified plasma cells isolated from bone marrow aspirates and normal samples consisted of peripheral blood mononuclear cells from the same patients. Library preparation involved standard protocols including DNA fragmentation, end repair, adapter ligation, and PCR amplification, followed by hybridization to the custom probe set. Sequencing was performed on Illumina HiSeq2000 instruments using 2×100 base pair paired-end reads for the initial sequencing of all 95 pairs, achieving a mean sequencing depth of 104× across tumor samples (range: 33× to 140×) and 107× across normal samples (range: 43× to 168×).

Read alignment was performed against the human reference genome GRCh37-lite using the Burrows-Wheeler Aligner (BWA) with standard parameters. Quality control metrics were assessed using standard tools including FastQC and Picard, with all samples meeting minimum quality thresholds for downstream analysis. The resulting aligned sequencing data provided comprehensive coverage of the targeted regions, with greater than 95% of targeted bases covered at a minimum depth of 20× in both tumor and normal samples. This high-quality sequencing data formed the foundation for subsequent variant calling and validation experiments.

### Example 2

The detection of copy number variations (CNVs) from the targeted sequencing data was performed using a novel computational approach that combined the CopyCAT2 algorithm with a specialized filtering strategy to reduce false positives. CopyCAT2 analyzes per-probe ratios of tumor to normal sequencing depth to identify copy number alterations, but standard implementations often produce false positives in targeted sequencing data due to technical biases in capture efficiency and sequencing depth. To address this issue, the inventors developed a novel filtering approach that removes CNV calls with ratios below a noise threshold estimated from diploid genomic regions.

The noise threshold was estimated using a Gaussian mixture model applied to log2 ratios of tumor to normal sequencing depth across diploid regions of the genome. Diploid regions were identified as those with consistent log2 ratios near zero across multiple samples, representing regions without copy number alterations. The Gaussian mixture model was used to estimate the distribution of technical noise in these diploid regions, and a conservative threshold was set at three standard deviations from the mean to define the noise floor. CNV calls with log2 ratios below this threshold were filtered out as likely false positives.

This approach enabled reliable detection of chromosome-level, arm-level, and focal CNVs across the MM genome. Chromosome-level alterations included genome-wide hyperdiploidy, characterized by trisomies of multiple odd-numbered chromosomes including 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 15, 19, and 21. Arm-level alterations included prognostically significant events such as amplification of chromosome 1q (amp1q), deletion of chromosome 1p (del1p), deletion of chromosome 13q (del13q), and deletion of chromosome 17p (del17p). Focal alterations included homozygous deletions of tumor suppressor genes such as BRCA2 and smaller amplifications or deletions affecting individual genes or gene clusters.

The CNV calling approach was validated using the subset of 44 samples that had previously been subjected to exome sequencing. High concordance was observed between CNVs called from the targeted sequencing data and those called from exome sequencing data, with correlation coefficients exceeding 0.9 for log2 ratios across chromosome arms. Additionally, the approach successfully detected all known prognostically significant CNVs in the cohort, including amp1q in 42% of samples, del1p in 38% of samples, del13q in 52% of samples, and del17p in 12% of samples. These frequencies were consistent with those reported in large MM cohorts, confirming the accuracy and clinical relevance of the CNV calling approach.

### Example 3

The detection of IGH translocations from the targeted sequencing data was performed using the LUMPY algorithm combined with a machine learning-based filtering approach specifically tuned to maximize precision relative to available fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) validation data. LUMPY integrates multiple signals including split reads and discordant paired-end reads to identify structural variants, but standard implementations produce high false-positive rates in complex genomic regions like the IGH locus. To address this challenge, the inventors developed a specialized filtering strategy using a random forest classifier trained on features derived from the LUMPY output and validated against FISH results from 22 samples.

The random forest classifier was trained using features including the number of supporting split reads, the number of discordant paired-end reads, the mapping quality of supporting reads, the distance between breakpoints in paired-end reads, and various quality metrics derived from the alignment data. The training set consisted of translocation calls from the 22 samples with available FISH validation, where true positives were defined as calls confirmed by FISH and false positives were defined as calls not confirmed by FISH. The classifier was optimized to maximize precision while maintaining reasonable recall, resulting in a final model that achieved 100% precision and 64% recall for IGH translocations.

This approach successfully detected canonical IGH translocations at expected frequencies in the cohort of 95 samples. Specifically, t(11;14) translocations involving CCND1 were detected in 14% of samples, t(4;14) translocations involving WHSC1/FGFR3 were detected in 13% of samples, t(8;14) translocations involving MYC were detected in 7% of samples, t(6;14) translocations involving CCND3 were detected in 2% of samples, and t(14;20) translocations involving MAFB were detected in 2% of samples. These frequencies were consistent with those reported in large MM cohorts, confirming the accuracy of the translocation detection approach.

Breakpoint analysis revealed that canonical IGH translocations occurred predominantly within the IGH constant region, but also telomeric of the IGHM switch region and occasionally within the D and J regions. Notably, one of the t(11;14) translocations occurred within the constant region but outside all constant and switch segments, demonstrating the ability of the comprehensive probe design to detect translocations in regions that might be missed by more limited approaches. No translocations within the V region passed the filtering step, consistent with the biological expectation that V region translocations are rare in MM due to the timing of V(D)J recombination relative to malignant transformation.

### Example 4

The comprehensive nature of the targeted sequencing platform enabled the discovery of novel genetic alterations with potential clinical significance, including recurrent mutations in IGLL5 and previously uncharacterized IGH translocations involving the IGLL5 locus. IGLL5 (immunoglobulin lambda-like polypeptide 5) is located within the immunoglobulin lambda light chain locus on chromosome 22q11.2 and encodes a protein involved in pre-B cell receptor signaling. Analysis of the 95 MM samples revealed that IGLL5 was the third most frequently mutated gene in the cohort, with mutations detected in 18% of samples (17 of 95).

The IGLL5 mutations included both silent and non-synonymous variants, with 38 of the 40 detected SNVs occurring in amino acid positions 1 to 100, outside of any annotated protein domains. The remaining two mutations (K166N and K189R) occurred in the immunoglobulin C1-set domain. The mutation spectrum was enriched for a c-AID signature, characterized by C to T/G mutations at WRCY motifs, which is consistent with the known activity of activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) in the immunoglobulin lambda light chain locus. This enrichment was statistically significant (p = 2.7 × 10−6, binomial test), supporting the hypothesis that these mutations result from somatic hypermutation rather than replication errors or other mutational processes.

Analysis of variant allele frequencies (VAFs) revealed that IGLL5 mutations in diploid loci had a median VAF of 58% and a first quartile VAF of 39%, suggesting that the majority are likely clonal events present in the founding clone rather than subclonal alterations acquired during disease progression. This finding is consistent with the hypothesis that IGLL5 mutations may play a role in early disease pathogenesis rather than representing late events associated with treatment resistance or disease progression.

### Example 5

Integrative analysis across mutation types revealed important patterns of mutual exclusivity and co-occurrence that provide insights into the molecular architecture of MM. Statistical analysis of the 95 samples, excluding one apparent hypermutator sample to improve statistical power, revealed significant associations between different classes of genomic alterations. Within CNVs, significant co-occurrence was observed between del(6q) and del(16q) (p < 0.01), del(6q) and amp(1q) (p < 0.01), and del(13q) and amp(1q) (p < 0.01). Additionally, del(14q) was found to co-occur with t(4;14) translocations (p < 0.05), suggesting a potential synergistic relationship between these alterations.

As expected, mutual exclusivity was observed between hyperdiploidy and t(11;14) translocations (p < 0.001), confirming the well-established division of MM into these two major molecular subtypes. More importantly, cross-mutation type exclusivity was observed between CNVs and SNVs, with both RAS mutations (KRAS or NRAS) and FAM46C mutations found to be mutually exclusive with del(6q) (p < 0.05 for both associations). This finding suggests that these different classes of alterations may represent alternative pathways to similar biological endpoints or that they confer redundant selective advantages that make their co-occurrence unnecessary.

IGLL5 mutations were found to be mutually exclusive with RAS mutations (p = 0.006), with trends toward mutual exclusivity with KRAS (p = 0.054), NRAS (p = 0.111), and FAM46C (p = 0.113) independently. This mutual exclusivity pattern suggests that IGLL5 mutations and RAS pathway alterations may represent alternative mechanisms for activating similar downstream signaling pathways or that they confer similar selective advantages that make their co-occurrence evolutionarily disadvantageous. The biological basis for this mutual exclusivity remains to be determined but may provide important insights into MM pathogenesis and potential therapeutic vulnerabilities.

### Example 6

The discovery of IGH translocations involving the IGLL5 locus represented a significant finding enabled by the comprehensive nature of the targeted sequencing platform. Analysis of translocation calls revealed two samples with predicted t(14;22)(q32.33;q11.22) translocations involving the IGH and IGLL5 loci. The first translocation was highly supported by both split reads and discordant paired-end reads and was selected for experimental validation. PCR amplification using primers specific to the predicted breakpoints successfully amplified a product of the expected size from CD138+ tumor cells but not from peripheral blood mononuclear cell controls, confirming the tumor-specific nature of the translocation.

Re-sequencing and mapping of the tumor-specific PCR product confirmed the reciprocal translocation spanning chromosomes 14 and 22, with small regions deleted on both derivative chromosomes. Primers designed within these deleted regions were used to amplify the corresponding wild-type chromosomes, confirming the retention of one copy of each wild-type chromosome in the tumor sample. This validation confirmed the presence of a balanced reciprocal translocation between IGH and IGLL5 in this MM sample.

To search for additional IGLL5 translocations, the filtering constraints were relaxed, revealing a second sample predicted to harbor a t(14;22) translocation. Although DNA was not available for validation of this second translocation, RNA-seq expression data from a partially overlapping set of 84 MM patients provided supporting evidence. The validated t(14;22) translocation was predicted to juxtapose the IGH μ and 3' enhancers with DERL3, a gene located within 1 Mb of the IGLL5 breakpoint on chromosome 22. Analysis of RNA-seq data revealed outlying expression of DERL3 in six samples, including the second sample with the putative t(14;22) translocation, suggesting that the translocation may result in DERL3 overexpression.

### Example 7

The detection of MYC translocations presented unique challenges due to the high false-positive rate observed in both tumor and normal samples. Initial analysis using the standard LUMPY pipeline resulted in MYC translocation calls in every tumor and normal sample, suggesting that many calls represented technical artifacts rather than true somatic events. To address this issue, the inventors developed a specialized filtering strategy that compared translocation calls between tumor and normal samples from the same patient, removing calls that were present in the normal sample.

This approach effectively eliminated germline structural variants and technical artifacts, enabling reliable detection of somatic MYC translocations. Application of this method to the 95 tumor samples resulted in the identification of five intra-chromosomal and two non-IGH inter-chromosomal MYC translocations, with one sample having both an intra- and an inter-chromosomal translocation. This yielded a total of 6 samples with MYC translocations (6% of the cohort), which is consistent with frequencies reported in newly diagnosed MM patients using other methods.

The intra-chromosomal translocations involved neighboring genes PVT1 and POU5F1B, as previously reported in MM and other cancers. PVT1 is a long non-coding RNA that has been shown to stabilize MYC protein and enhance its oncogenic activity, while POU5F1B is a pseudogene of the pluripotency factor OCT4 that may contribute to stem-like properties in cancer cells. The identification of these known MYC translocation partners validates the accuracy of the MYC translocation detection approach and confirms its ability to detect biologically relevant alterations.

The frequency of MYC translocations detected in this study (6%) is at the lower end of the range reported in the literature, which may reflect differences in patient populations, disease stages, or detection methodologies. However, the consistency with other recent studies using similar NGS approaches suggests that the specialized filtering strategy successfully reduced false positives while maintaining sensitivity for true somatic events.

### Example 8

The relationship between IGLL5 mutations and clinical outcomes was investigated through survival analysis of the 95 MM patients. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that patients with non-synonymous IGLL5 mutations or indels had significantly worse progression-free survival compared to patients with wild-type IGLL5 (hazard ratio = 1.46, 95% confidence interval: 1.03–2.08, p = 0.03 by log-rank test). This association remained significant after adjusting for other known prognostic factors including age, ISS stage, and the presence of high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities.

The association between IGLL5 mutations and disease progression, combined with their mutual exclusivity with RAS mutations and their clonal nature (median VAF of 58%), suggests that IGLL5 mutations may contribute to MM pathogenesis rather than simply representing passenger events. The biological mechanism by which IGLL5 mutations might contribute to disease progression remains to be determined, but several possibilities exist. IGLL5 is involved in pre-B cell receptor signaling, and mutations might alter this signaling pathway in ways that promote plasma cell survival or proliferation. Alternatively, the mutations might represent biomarkers of underlying genomic instability or aberrant somatic hypermutation activity that drives disease progression through other mechanisms.

The fact that IGLL5 mutations do not recurrently affect specific residues in the protein supports a loss-of-function model rather than a gain-of-function mechanism. However, the concentration of mutations in the N-terminal region (amino acids 1–100) outside of annotated protein domains suggests that this region may have important functional properties that are disrupted by mutations. Future functional studies will be necessary to determine the precise role of IGLL5 mutations in MM pathogenesis and to evaluate their potential as therapeutic targets or biomarkers.

### Example 9

The optimal sequencing depth for MM genomic profiling was investigated through deep sequencing of a subset of 15 tumor-normal pairs to mean depths exceeding 1,200×. This deep sequencing was performed on Illumina HiSeq2500 instruments using 2×125 base pair paired-end reads, achieving a mean sequencing depth of 1,259× across tumor samples (range: 506× to 1,660×) and 1,326× across normal samples (range: 763× to 1,727×). Variant calling was performed using the same pipeline as the initial sequencing, and the results were compared to identify variants that were unique to either the shallow or deep sequencing datasets.

To focus on high-confidence events likely to be of biological relevance, variants were filtered to remove silent variants, those in intronic, intergenic, or flanking regions, those in IGH (likely arising from somatic hypermutation), and those flagged as likely germline variants by at least one caller. This filtering resulted in 57 variants in the original sequencing study (mean depth = 92×) and 67 variants in the subsequent deep sequencing study (mean depth = 1,169×). Variant allele frequencies (VAFs) of mutations shared across the two studies were highly correlated (R2 = 0.80, p < 2.2e-16), confirming the reproducibility of the variant calling pipeline.

The vast majority of variants unique to either study had low VAFs: one of the four variants unique to the original study had a VAF <10%, though all had an alternate allele count of three or fewer supporting reads, while 12 of the 14 variants unique to the subsequent study had a VAF <10%. Although relatively few new variants were discovered by the additional sequencing, these did include several annotated in COSMIC in genes KRAS, HECW1, and ZFHX4, suggesting that deep sequencing can identify additional clinically relevant variants in some cases.

To further explore the effects of sequencing depth, the sequencing reads from the 15 deeply-sequenced samples were downsampled to various depths, and variant calling was performed on the downsampled data. The number of variants following filtering was plotted against sequencing depth, revealing a strong correlation (R2 = 0.52, p = 10−3). However, beyond approximately 25% of the final sequencing depth (or a mean depth of approximately 300×), the increase in the number of discovered variants was marginal. Similar performance was observed at an even lower depth of 92× in the original capture results, suggesting that depths as low as 100× can capture the majority of variants of interest while coverage beyond 300× leads to sharply diminishing returns. This finding has important implications for clinical implementation, as it enables comprehensive genomic profiling at a reasonable cost and turnaround time.
