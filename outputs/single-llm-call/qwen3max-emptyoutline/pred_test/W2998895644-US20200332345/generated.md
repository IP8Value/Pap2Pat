## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular diagnostics and, more specifically, to rapid, sensitive, and highly specific nucleic acid-based detection methods for identifying and differentiating subspecies and subtypes of *Francisella tularensis*, a Tier 1 select agent bacterium responsible for the zoonotic disease tularemia. The invention provides novel quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assays—both singleplex and multiplex formats—that enable definitive discrimination among all four recognized subspecies of *F. tularensis* (*tularensis*, *holarctica*, *mediasiatica*, and *novicida*) and further distinguish between the two clinically and epidemiologically significant subtypes of *F. tularensis* subsp. *tularensis* (subtypes A.I and A.II). Additionally, the invention encompasses an integrated two-tiered diagnostic platform that combines these assays into streamlined workflows suitable for use in high-containment biosafety level 3 (BSL-3) laboratories, public health reference laboratories, and biodefense surveillance systems. The methods described herein are designed to overcome critical limitations of existing diagnostic platforms, including cross-reactivity with near-neighbor species such as *Francisella persica* and *Francisella philomiragia*, indeterminate results due to high genomic similarity among *Francisella* clades, and the inability to reliably differentiate select agent strains from non-select agent variants such as *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida*. The invention further includes compositions comprising optimized primer and hydrolysis probe sets targeting unique genomic loci, as well as protocols for direct application to cultured isolates or extracted DNA, thereby facilitating rapid clinical decision-making, outbreak response, and regulatory compliance with federal select agent reporting requirements.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

*Francisella tularensis* is a Gram-negative, facultative intracellular bacterium and the causative agent of tularemia, a potentially fatal zoonotic disease that can be transmitted via multiple routes, including inhalation, ingestion, arthropod bites, and direct contact with infected tissues or contaminated environmental sources. Due to its extremely low infectious dose—estimated at fewer than ten organisms when inhaled—and its potential for aerosol dissemination, *F. tularensis* is classified as a Tier 1 select agent by the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) under the Federal Select Agent Program (FSAP). This designation reflects the organism’s capacity to pose a severe threat to public health, national security, and critical infrastructure if deliberately misused. Among the four recognized subspecies—*F. tularensis* subsp. *tularensis* (Type A), *F. tularensis* subsp. *holarctica* (Type B), *F. tularensis* subsp. *mediasiatica*, and *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida*—the first three are regulated as select agents, whereas *novicida* is not, despite its close genetic relationship and occasional misidentification in diagnostic settings. Notably, Type A strains are further subdivided into subtypes A.I and A.II, with A.I exhibiting the highest virulence and mortality rates in human infections, historically reaching up to 35% in untreated cases.

Accurate and timely identification of *F. tularensis* at the subspecies and subtype level is essential for appropriate clinical management, public health intervention, and forensic attribution in the context of bioterrorism. However, conventional diagnostic approaches have proven inadequate for this purpose. Culture-based identification remains the gold standard but requires several days for colony formation and poses significant biosafety risks due to the organism’s high infectivity. Serological methods lack early sensitivity and cannot resolve subspecies distinctions. Molecular assays, particularly PCR-based techniques, offer speed and specificity but have been hampered by several key limitations. Early methods relied on gel electrophoresis of amplicons generated from insertion sequence (IS) element patterns, which, while informative, are labor-intensive, subjective, and incompatible with high-throughput or point-of-need testing. Subsequent development of quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) assays improved turnaround time but failed to achieve comprehensive differentiation across all *F. tularensis* clades. For instance, some qPCR platforms utilize complex scoring matrices based on multiple targets and require hierarchical interpretation, increasing the risk of operator error and inconsistent results. Others suffer from reduced sensitivity, sporadic amplification failure, or cross-reactivity due to unanticipated single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) within primer or probe binding sites—issues that compromise reliability in real-world diagnostic scenarios.

Moreover, the presence of *Francisella*-like endosymbionts in environmental vectors such as ticks—particularly *Dermacentor* species—further complicates diagnosis. These endosymbionts share substantial genomic homology with *F. tularensis*, leading to false-positive signals in assays targeting conserved regions. Similarly, non-pathogenic relatives like *F. persica* (formerly *Wolbachia persica*) and *F. philomiragia* can confound results if the assay lacks sufficient discriminatory power. The inability to definitively distinguish the hypervirulent A.I subtype from less virulent clades or from non-select agent *novicida* has significant implications: misclassification may lead to inappropriate antibiotic regimens, delayed public health alerts, or unnecessary activation of biodefense protocols. Current CDC-approved qPCR assays used in Laboratory Response Network (LRN) reference laboratories confirm *F. tularensis* at the species level but do not provide subspecies or subtype resolution, leaving a critical gap in diagnostic capability.

In silico genomic analyses have revealed that select agent *F. tularensis* subspecies possess a duplicated Francisella Pathogenicity Island (FPI) and numerous IS elements, whereas *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida* contains only a single FPI and fewer IS copies. These structural differences, along with single-nucleotide variations and unique genomic signatures, represent exploitable targets for molecular discrimination. However, translating these genomic insights into robust, field-deployable assays has remained challenging due to the need for high analytical sensitivity (to detect low pathogen loads), absolute specificity (to avoid cross-reactivity), and compatibility with multiplex formats that reduce reagent costs and sample consumption. Prior attempts to develop SNP-based allelic discrimination assays required stepwise testing and parameter optimization for individual reactions, rendering them impractical for routine use. Furthermore, none of the existing platforms integrate an internal bacterial control to verify sample integrity and rule out PCR inhibition—a critical feature when testing complex environmental or clinical matrices.

Thus, there exists a long-felt but unmet need for a rapid, accurate, and user-friendly molecular diagnostic system that can simultaneously: (1) confirm the presence of bacterial DNA; (2) detect all *F. tularensis* strains regardless of subspecies; (3) differentiate select agent strains from non-select agent *novicida*; (4) identify the hypervirulent A.I subtype; and (5) resolve the remaining select agent clades (A.II, *holarctica*, and *mediasiatica*) in a minimal number of tests without reliance on interpretive matrices or post-amplification processing. The present invention addresses these challenges through the rational design of novel primer-probe sets targeting previously unexploited, clade-specific genomic loci, combined with a strategically engineered two-tier multiplex qPCR architecture that delivers comprehensive genotyping in just two reactions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for the rapid, sensitive, and specific detection and differentiation of *Francisella tularensis* subspecies and subtypes using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR). The invention overcomes the limitations of prior art by introducing a suite of singleplex qPCR assays, each targeting a unique genomic locus that is exclusively present in one of the following *F. tularensis* clades: (i) all four subspecies (*tularensis*, *holarctica*, *mediasiatica*, and *novicida*); (ii) the three select agent subspecies (*tularensis*, *holarctica*, and *mediasiatica*), excluding *novicida*; (iii) subtype A.I of *F. tularensis* subsp. *tularensis*; (iv) subtype A.II of *F. tularensis* subsp. *tularensis*; (v) *F. tularensis* subsp. *holarctica* (Type B); (vi) *F. tularensis* subsp. *mediasiatica*; and (vii) *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida*. Each assay employs a pair of oligonucleotide primers and a hydrolysis probe labeled with a fluorophore and quencher, wherein the target sequences were identified through rigorous in silico screening of all publicly available *Francisella* genomes to ensure absolute clade specificity and absence of cross-reactivity with near-neighbor species or environmental endosymbionts.

In addition to these singleplex assays, the invention discloses a two-tier multiplex qPCR platform that consolidates diagnostic decision-making into two efficient reactions. The Tier 1 multiplex assay combines four targets in a single tube: (a) a universal bacterial 16S rRNA gene control (U16S) to confirm the presence of bacterial DNA and monitor for PCR inhibition; (b) a pan-*Francisella* target (4Pan1) that detects all four *F. tularensis* subspecies; (c) a select agent pan-target (3Pan) that detects only the three regulated subspecies (*tularensis*, *holarctica*, *mediasiatica*) and excludes *novicida*; and (d) an A.I-specific target (A1d) that identifies the hypervirulent subtype. Interpretation of the Tier 1 results enables immediate classification: a positive signal for all four targets indicates a hypervirulent A.I strain; positivity for U16S and 4Pan1 only (with negative 3Pan and A1d) identifies *novicida*; and positivity for U16S, 4Pan1, and 3Pan (with negative A1d) confirms a select agent strain that is not A.I or *novicida*. When the latter result is obtained, the Tier 2 multiplex assay is deployed, which includes U16S along with three clade-specific targets: A2c (for A.II), B2 (for *holarctica*), and M3 (for *mediasiatica*). This two-tier approach minimizes the number of tests required while eliminating the need for complex scoring algorithms or post-PCR analysis.

The primer and probe sequences, optimal concentrations, and cycling conditions for each assay have been empirically validated using a comprehensive inclusivity panel of 35 *F. tularensis* strains (including reference and wild-type isolates across all subspecies and subtypes) and an exclusivity panel comprising non-*tularensis* bacteria, *Francisella*-like endosymbionts from environmental ticks, and other potential interferents. All assays demonstrate exceptional analytical sensitivity, with limits of detection (LOD) ranging from 2 to 7 femtograms of genomic DNA (equivalent to 1–3 chromosomal copies) in singleplex format and 10–100 femtograms in multiplex format. Specificity is absolute: no cross-reactivity is observed with any exclusivity panel member, including *F. persica*, *F. philomiragia*, or tick lysates harboring *Francisella*-like organisms. The assays are compatible with multiple real-time PCR instruments, including the Applied Biosystems 7500 Fast Dx and the 3M Integrated Cycler, and can be performed directly on crude lysates of cultured colonies, eliminating the need for time-consuming DNA extraction in urgent scenarios.

Furthermore, the invention includes a sequencing-compatible extension wherein the Tier 1 primers are synthesized with barcoded adapters for amplicon sequencing on next-generation platforms such as the Ion Torrent PGM. This enables strain-level confirmation, detection of intra-clade SNPs, and phylogeographic tracking without compromising qPCR performance. The entire system is designed for deployment in LRN reference laboratories, CDC-affiliated facilities, military medical centers, and public health agencies, providing a turnkey solution for definitive *F. tularensis* characterization that supports clinical treatment, epidemiological investigation, and biodefense readiness.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to molecular diagnostic assays for the detection and differentiation of *Francisella tularensis* subspecies and subtypes. Central to the invention are novel oligonucleotide primer pairs and hydrolysis probes that selectively amplify and detect clade-specific genomic regions, enabling unambiguous identification of all major *F. tularensis* lineages. The assays are designed for use in quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) platforms and can be implemented as standalone singleplex reactions or as part of an integrated two-tier multiplex system. Below, the components, design rationale, validation data, and operational protocols are described in detail.

### Example 1

In a first embodiment, the invention provides seven singleplex qPCR assays, each optimized for the detection of a specific *F. tularensis* clade. These assays are designated as follows: U16S (universal bacterial control), 4Pan1 (pan-*F. tularensis*), 3Pan (select agent pan), A1d (subtype A.I), A2c (subtype A.II), B2 (*holarctica*), M3 (*mediasiatica*), and N1 (*novicida*). Each assay utilizes a forward primer, a reverse primer, and a hydrolysis probe labeled with a 5′ fluorophore (e.g., FAM) and a 3′ quencher (e.g., BHQ-1). The target loci were identified through exhaustive in silico analysis of all available *Francisella* genomic sequences in public databases, including complete genomes, draft assemblies, and short reads. Candidate regions were evaluated for uniqueness using BLAST and multiple sequence alignment tools (e.g., Clustal Omega), ensuring that amplification would occur only in the intended clade.

For instance, the 4Pan1 assay targets a 116-bp region within the *ostA* gene (locus tag FTT_0467 in SCHU S4), which encodes an organic solvent tolerance protein. This locus is conserved across all four *F. tularensis* subspecies but absent in non-*tularensis* bacteria. The 3Pan assay targets an 83-bp segment in a hypothetical protein gene (FTL_1858 in LVS) located within the CR16 genomic region; this target is present in the three select agent subspecies but deleted or divergent in *novicida*. The A1d assay exploits a unique genomic architecture in subtype A.I: although primer-binding sites exist in other clades, they are separated by over 300 kb, preventing amplicon formation under standard PCR conditions. The amplified 114-bp product thus serves as a definitive marker for A.I. Similarly, the A2c assay targets the *mviN* gene (FTW_1702), B2 targets a hypothetical protein (FTS_0806), M3 targets a major facilitator superfamily transporter (FTM_1104), and N1 targets a metabolite:H+ symporter (FTN_0003)—each locus exhibiting clade-restricted conservation.

Each singleplex assay was optimized for primer and probe concentrations using matrix titration. For example, the 4Pan1 assay achieves maximum sensitivity at 0.5 μM forward primer, 0.75 μM reverse primer, and 0.3 μM probe. Analytical validation demonstrated LODs of 2–7 fg (1–3 genome copies), with R² values ≥0.997 in standard curves, confirming excellent linearity and reproducibility. Specificity was confirmed against an exclusivity panel including *F. persica*, *F. philomiragia*, *Legionella pneumophila*, *Escherichia coli*, and DNA from 25 tick specimens known to harbor *Francisella*-like endosymbionts; all yielded negative results. The U16S assay, while exhibiting background signal due to residual bacterial DNA in commercial polymerases (CT >31), reliably detected bacterial DNA at CT <31, serving as a functional internal control.

These singleplex assays can be used individually for targeted screening or in combination for comprehensive profiling. They are compatible with DNA extracted via standard kits (e.g., Qiagen, Lucigen) or crude lysates prepared by boiling colonies in water, offering flexibility in resource-limited settings.

### Example 2

In a second embodiment, the invention implements a two-tier multiplex qPCR strategy to maximize efficiency and minimize testing burden. The Tier 1 quadruplex assay combines U16S, 4Pan1, 3Pan, and A1d in a single reaction. Using instrument-compatible fluorophores (e.g., FAM, VIC, ABY, JUN), each target is monitored in a distinct optical channel. Interpretation is binary:  
- **Pattern 1**: U16S(+), 4Pan1(+), 3Pan(+), A1d(+) → *F. tularensis* subtype A.I (hypervirulent select agent).  
- **Pattern 2**: U16S(+), 4Pan1(+), 3Pan(–), A1d(–) → *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida* (non-select agent).  
- **Pattern 3**: U16S(+), 4Pan1(+), 3Pan(+), A1d(–) → Select agent *F. tularensis* (not A.I or *novicida*).  

When Pattern 3 is observed, the Tier 2 quadruplex assay (U16S, A2c, B2, M3) is performed to resolve the specific clade: A2c(+) indicates A.II, B2(+) indicates *holarctica*, and M3(+) indicates *mediasiatica*. Both tiers were validated on multiple qPCR instruments with LODs of 10–100 fg and R² values ≥0.961, demonstrating robust performance despite multiplexing. Crucially, no cross-talk or inhibition was observed, even when spiked into complex tick lysates.

Additionally, the Tier 1 primers were adapted with Ion Torrent PGM-compatible barcodes for amplicon sequencing. Sequencing of barcoded amplicons confirmed target identity and revealed strain-specific SNPs, enabling high-resolution genotyping. CT values with barcoded primers were only marginally higher than unlabeled versions, confirming compatibility.

This two-tier system provides a complete diagnostic solution in two tests, replacing multi-step, matrix-dependent workflows. It is ideal for public health laboratories requiring rapid, unambiguous results for clinical management, outbreak response, and regulatory reporting.
