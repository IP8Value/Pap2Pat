# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of human activity monitoring has emerged as a critical technological frontier in the pursuit of enhancing quality of life, particularly for aging populations and individuals with physical or cognitive impairments. As demographic shifts worldwide continue to increase the proportion of elderly individuals, the demand for unobtrusive, reliable, and cost-effective monitoring systems has grown substantially. Traditional approaches to human activity detection have relied heavily on either camera-based visual surveillance systems or wearable sensor technologies, each of which presents significant limitations that hinder their widespread adoption in real-world domestic and clinical environments. Camera-based systems, while capable of capturing rich visual data, raise profound privacy concerns that are often unacceptable in private living spaces such as bedrooms or bathrooms. Furthermore, these systems typically require substantial computational resources for video processing and analysis, making them impractical for continuous, low-power ambient monitoring applications. On the other hand, wearable sensors—such as accelerometers, gyroscopes, and inertial measurement units—necessitate consistent user compliance, which is frequently compromised due to discomfort, forgetfulness, or social stigma associated with wearing conspicuous monitoring devices. These limitations render wearable solutions unreliable for long-term, passive monitoring scenarios where user adherence cannot be guaranteed.

Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology has emerged as a promising alternative for human activity monitoring due to its inherent advantages of low cost, passive operation, and minimal infrastructure requirements. RFID systems operate by interrogating passive tags using radio frequency signals, with the tags modulating and reflecting these signals back to a reader device. The received signal strength indicator (RSSI) and phase information from these backscattered signals can be analyzed to detect changes in the electromagnetic environment caused by human movement. Previous research has demonstrated the feasibility of using RFID for both on-body and off-body activity recognition, where tags are either attached to the human body or placed in fixed locations within the environment. However, conventional RFID-based activity monitoring systems have been constrained by fundamental limitations in spatial sensitivity and detection range. Most commercially available RFID tags employ linearly polarized antennas, while RFID readers typically utilize circularly polarized antennas to ensure reliable reading of tags regardless of orientation—a configuration that is optimal for inventory tracking applications but suboptimal for human activity sensing. In this conventional setup, the line-of-sight (LOS) signal between the reader and tag dominates the received signal, while the weaker multipath components reflected from human bodies are often buried in noise, limiting the system's ability to detect subtle movements or activities occurring outside the direct LOS path.

The spatial sensitivity limitation of traditional RFID systems has led researchers to explore various mitigation strategies, including the deployment of multiple tags throughout the monitoring environment to increase coverage area. While this approach can improve detection probability, it introduces significant practical challenges, including increased system complexity, higher costs, and the need for careful tag placement to avoid mutual coupling effects that can degrade performance. Moreover, the requirement for multiple tags undermines the fundamental advantage of RFID as a low-cost, simple monitoring solution. The core challenge in RFID-based human activity monitoring thus lies in enhancing the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the system, where the "signal" represents the multipath components carrying information about human movement, and the "noise" represents the dominant LOS component that provides no useful information about activity. Conventional approaches have attempted to address this challenge through signal processing techniques or machine learning algorithms, but these methods are fundamentally limited by the poor SNR of the underlying physical layer.

The polarization characteristics of electromagnetic waves offer a promising avenue for addressing this fundamental limitation. When circularly polarized electromagnetic waves reflect off conductive or dielectric surfaces—including human tissue—the handedness of the polarization is inverted. This physical phenomenon, well-established in electromagnetic theory, suggests that a carefully designed cross-polarized RFID system could selectively suppress the LOS signal while preserving the reflected signal components that carry information about human activity. Specifically, by employing a reader antenna with right-hand circular polarization (RHCP) and a tag antenna with left-hand circular polarization (LHCP), or vice versa, the direct LOS signal would experience significant polarization mismatch loss, thereby reducing its amplitude relative to the reflected signals. Meanwhile, signals reflected from the human body would undergo polarization inversion, effectively converting them to the co-polarized state relative to the receiving antenna, thus avoiding additional polarization mismatch loss. This hardware-level approach to SNR enhancement represents a paradigm shift from conventional signal processing methods, offering the potential for dramatic improvements in detection range, spatial sensitivity, and the ability to detect subtle physiological movements such as respiration.

Despite the theoretical promise of cross-polarized RFID systems for human activity monitoring, several practical challenges must be addressed to realize this potential. First, the majority of commercially available RFID tags employ linearly polarized antennas, necessitating the development of custom circularly polarized tag designs that maintain compatibility with existing RFID infrastructure and standards. Second, the design of such tags must balance competing requirements of circular polarization purity, bandwidth, gain, and physical size, all while maintaining the low-cost, passive nature that makes RFID attractive for widespread deployment. Third, the system must be robust to real-world environmental conditions, including multipath interference from walls, furniture, and other objects, which can significantly affect signal characteristics. Finally, the system must demonstrate practical utility across a range of human activities, from gross motor movements like walking and gesturing to subtle physiological processes like breathing and heart rate monitoring. Addressing these challenges requires a comprehensive approach that integrates electromagnetic theory, antenna design, system calibration, and experimental validation in realistic environments.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel radio frequency identification (RFID) system for non-contact human activity monitoring that achieves unprecedented spatial sensitivity and detection range through the strategic exploitation of cross circular polarization between the reader antenna and tag antenna. Unlike conventional RFID systems that employ linearly polarized tags with circularly polarized readers—a configuration optimized for inventory tracking but suboptimal for activity sensing—the present invention utilizes a circularly polarized RFID tag specifically designed to work in cross-polarization with the reader antenna. This configuration leverages fundamental electromagnetic principles to achieve significant improvements in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) by suppressing the dominant line-of-sight (LOS) signal while preserving and enhancing the multipath signal components that carry information about human movement.

The core innovation of the present invention lies in the recognition that circularly polarized electromagnetic waves undergo polarization inversion upon reflection from conductive or dielectric surfaces, including human tissue. By configuring the RFID reader antenna with one circular polarization handedness (e.g., right-hand circular polarization, RHCP) and the RFID tag antenna with the opposite handedness (e.g., left-hand circular polarization, LHCP), the direct LOS signal between reader and tag experiences substantial polarization mismatch loss, typically exceeding 30 dB. This effectively suppresses the "noise" component of the received signal. Meanwhile, signals that reflect from the human body undergo polarization inversion during the reflection process, converting them to the co-polarized state relative to the receiving antenna. This eliminates additional polarization mismatch loss for the "signal" component, thereby enhancing the overall SNR of the system.

The present invention includes a custom-designed circularly polarized RFID tag that maintains compatibility with standard RFID infrastructure while providing the necessary polarization characteristics for cross-polarized operation. In one embodiment, the tag employs an Archimedean spiral antenna geometry fabricated on low-cost FR4 substrate, which provides excellent circular polarization properties across the UHF RFID frequency band (902-928 MHz). The tag incorporates a T-shaped matching network to achieve conjugate impedance matching between the spiral antenna and the RFID integrated circuit, ensuring maximum power transfer efficiency. The resulting tag design provides a realized gain of approximately 3.3 dBi with an axial ratio bandwidth sufficient for UHF RFID operation.

The system further includes specialized data preprocessing techniques to address the unique challenges of RFID-based activity monitoring in real-world environments. These techniques include frequency channel calibration to compensate for the frequency hopping spread spectrum transmission employed by commercial RFID readers, which otherwise introduces significant variations in RSSI and phase measurements. The calibration process involves collecting baseline measurements across all frequency channels in a static environment, then referencing subsequent measurements to this baseline to isolate the effects of human movement from environmental multipath variations. This approach enables the system to detect subtle changes in the electromagnetic environment caused by human activity, even when these changes are small relative to the overall signal magnitude.

Experimental validation of the present invention demonstrates dramatic improvements in spatial sensitivity and detection range compared to conventional linearly polarized RFID systems. In controlled anechoic chamber testing, the cross-polarized system shows RSSI variations of 10-20 dB in response to human presence, compared to only 1-2 dB for linearly polarized systems under identical conditions. Real-world testing in typical indoor environments reveals a coverage area improvement of over 230% compared to linearly polarized systems, enabling reliable detection of subtle activities such as head nodding, hand waving, and even respiration from distances and positions where conventional systems fail completely. The enhanced SNR provided by the cross-polarized configuration enables detection of physiological signals such as breathing patterns, with clear frequency domain signatures corresponding to normal respiratory rates.

The present invention provides a low-cost, non-intrusive solution for ambient computing applications in residential, healthcare, and institutional settings. By eliminating the need for multiple tags or complex sensor networks, the system maintains the simplicity and affordability that make RFID attractive for widespread deployment while dramatically improving performance. The technology enables new applications in elder care, rehabilitation monitoring, sleep quality assessment, and general wellness tracking, all without requiring user compliance or compromising privacy. The system's compatibility with existing RFID infrastructure ensures straightforward integration into current monitoring frameworks, while its enhanced sensitivity opens new possibilities for detecting subtle behavioral and physiological indicators that were previously undetectable with conventional RFID systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive system for non-contact human activity monitoring using radio frequency identification (RFID) technology enhanced through cross circular polarization between the reader antenna and tag antenna. This detailed description outlines the complete system architecture, including the custom circularly polarized RFID tag design, system configuration, data preprocessing methodology, and operational procedures that enable unprecedented spatial sensitivity and detection range for human activity monitoring applications.

The foundation of the present invention lies in the strategic exploitation of electromagnetic polarization properties to enhance the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of RFID-based activity monitoring systems. In conventional RFID systems used for human activity monitoring, the reader antenna typically employs circular polarization while the tags use linear polarization. This configuration, while effective for inventory tracking applications where tag orientation is unpredictable, creates fundamental limitations for activity sensing. The dominant line-of-sight (LOS) signal between reader and tag overwhelms the weaker multipath components that carry information about human movement, resulting in poor SNR and limited detection range. The present invention addresses this limitation by implementing a cross circular polarization configuration, where the reader antenna and tag antenna have opposite circular polarization handedness—for example, a right-hand circularly polarized (RHCP) reader antenna paired with a left-hand circularly polarized (LHCP) tag antenna, or vice versa.

This cross-polarized configuration leverages the fundamental electromagnetic principle that circularly polarized waves undergo polarization inversion upon reflection from conductive or dielectric surfaces. Human tissue, being both conductive and having high relative permittivity, acts as an effective reflector that inverts the handedness of incident circularly polarized waves. Therefore, when an RHCP wave from the reader antenna reflects off a human body, it becomes LHCP, which is co-polarized with an LHCP tag antenna. This eliminates polarization mismatch loss for the reflected signal component, preserving its amplitude. Conversely, the direct LOS signal between the RHCP reader and LHCP tag experiences significant polarization mismatch loss, typically exceeding 30 dB, effectively suppressing this dominant but information-poor signal component. The result is a dramatic improvement in SNR, where the signal components carrying information about human movement are preserved while the dominant noise component is suppressed.

The implementation of this cross-polarized configuration requires a custom-designed circularly polarized RFID tag, as commercially available RFID tags predominantly employ linear polarization. The present invention includes a specific tag design that maintains compatibility with standard UHF RFID infrastructure while providing the necessary circular polarization characteristics. In one embodiment, the tag employs an Archimedean spiral antenna geometry, which is particularly well-suited for circular polarization applications due to its frequency-independent characteristics. The spiral antenna is fabricated on standard FR4 substrate with a thickness of 0.8 mm, maintaining low cost and lightweight properties essential for practical deployment. The antenna has a radius of 13 cm, providing sufficient aperture for effective operation in the UHF RFID frequency band (902-928 MHz).

The RFID integrated circuit used in the tag is the Impinj Monza R6 chip (Murata LXMS21ACMF-183), which presents a complex impedance of approximately 20-145j ohms at the operating frequency. To achieve maximum power transfer efficiency between the antenna and the integrated circuit, a T-shaped matching network is implemented. This matching network is designed to provide the complex conjugate impedance of the chip, effectively canceling the reactive component and presenting a purely resistive load to the antenna. The T-shaped feed structure is symmetrically integrated into the spiral antenna design, with both arms of the Archimedean spiral extending outward from the matching network. This design approach ensures optimal impedance matching while maintaining the circular polarization characteristics of the spiral antenna.

The resulting tag design demonstrates excellent performance characteristics, with a simulated realized gain of 3.3 dBi and an axial ratio bandwidth of 105 degrees, which is sufficient for UHF RFID operation. The axial ratio, which measures the quality of circular polarization, remains below 3 dB across the entire UHF RFID band, ensuring consistent polarization performance. In co-polarized testing configurations, the tag demonstrates approximately 13 dB better RSSI compared to conventional linearly polarized tags, confirming the expected performance gains from both the higher antenna gain and improved polarization matching. More importantly, in cross-polarized configurations, the tag demonstrates the expected 30+ dB polarization mismatch loss, validating the fundamental operating principle of the invention.

The complete system configuration includes a standard commercial RFID reader, specifically the Impinj R420 model, which provides the necessary functionality for tag interrogation and data collection. The reader operates at a maximum output power of 31.5 dBm and supports the MAXTHROUGHPUT mode, which provides approximately 250 tag reads per second (one read every 4 ms). This high read rate is essential for capturing the dynamic characteristics of human movement, including rapid gestures and subtle physiological motions. The reader is connected to a standard personal computer running custom software developed using the Impinj Octane SDK, which provides access to the raw tag data including individual product codes, RSSI values, phase measurements, and precise timestamps.

The reader antenna employed in the system is a commercially available circularly polarized antenna, specifically the S9028PC model with 9 dBi gain and RHCP polarization. The antenna is mounted at a height of 1.2 meters above the floor, which represents a typical installation height for indoor monitoring applications. The custom circularly polarized RFID tag is similarly mounted on a wall at the same height, positioned to provide optimal coverage of the monitoring area. The distance between reader and tag is typically set to 1.5 meters, though the system demonstrates effective operation across a range of distances and configurations.

A critical aspect of the present invention is the specialized data preprocessing methodology developed to address the unique challenges of RFID-based activity monitoring in real-world environments. Commercial RFID readers, including the Impinj R420, employ frequency hopping spread spectrum transmission techniques to reduce interference between multiple readers operating in the same area. Specifically, the reader hops through 50 discrete frequency channels within the 902-928 MHz band over a 10-second period. This frequency hopping introduces significant variations in RSSI and phase measurements that can mask the subtle changes caused by human activity, making direct analysis of raw data impractical.

The present invention addresses this challenge through a comprehensive calibration and preprocessing procedure. The first step involves collecting baseline calibration data in a static environment where no human activity is present. This calibration process requires a minimum 10-second measurement period to ensure that the reader cycles through all 50 frequency channels. During this period, the system records the RSSI and phase values for each frequency channel, creating a comprehensive baseline profile of the static environment. For RSSI calibration, the system calculates the mean RSSI value for each frequency channel and stores these values in a key-value data structure where the key is the frequency channel identifier and the value is the mean RSSI.

During actual monitoring operations, the system continuously collects RSSI and phase data from the tag. Each incoming measurement is referenced to the corresponding calibration value for that frequency channel through simple subtraction, yielding the difference between the current measurement and the baseline. This differential approach effectively removes the frequency-dependent variations introduced by the hopping mechanism, revealing the true changes caused by human activity. For phase measurements, a more sophisticated remapping technique is employed to address the periodic nature of phase data. The phase measurements from different frequency channels are remapped to a chosen reference frequency using the mathematical relationship described in Equation 1 of the research paper. The reference frequency is selected to have an initial phase closest to π radians, which provides the widest possible [0, 2π] range without phase wrapping issues. This preprocessing methodology results in clean, frequency-independent data streams that clearly reveal the effects of human movement on the electromagnetic environment.

The operational procedure for the system involves several key steps. First, the system is installed in the monitoring environment with the reader antenna and circularly polarized tag positioned to provide optimal coverage of the area of interest. The tag is mounted on a wall or other fixed surface at a height of approximately 1.2 meters, ensuring it remains unobtrusive while maintaining good visibility of the monitoring area. The reader is connected to the control computer and configured for MAXTHROUGHPUT operation mode.

Before beginning active monitoring, the system performs the calibration procedure described above. This calibration should be performed whenever the physical environment changes significantly, such as when furniture is moved or new objects are introduced into the monitoring area. Once calibration is complete, the system enters monitoring mode, continuously collecting and preprocessing RSSI and phase data from the tag. The preprocessed data is analyzed in real-time to detect deviations from the baseline that indicate human activity.

The system's enhanced sensitivity enables detection of a wide range of human activities, from gross motor movements to subtle physiological processes. For repetitive activities such as hand waving or breathing, the system can perform frequency domain analysis using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to identify characteristic frequency signatures. For example, normal respiratory rates of 12-20 breaths per minute produce clear peaks in the frequency domain around 0.2-0.33 Hz, which can be reliably detected and quantified. Similarly, repetitive gestures like hand waving at known frequencies produce corresponding peaks in the frequency domain, enabling both detection and characterization of the activity.

For non-repetitive activities such as head nodding, arm crossing, or walking, the system analyzes the time-domain characteristics of the RSSI and phase variations. Each activity produces a unique signature in terms of amplitude, duration, and temporal pattern of the signal variations. These signatures can be used for activity classification through pattern recognition algorithms or machine learning techniques. The system's high temporal resolution (4 ms per measurement) enables detailed analysis of activity dynamics, including speed, amplitude, and coordination of movements.

The present invention demonstrates exceptional performance in real-world testing environments. In a typical room setting with standard furniture, walls, and other common objects, the system achieves a coverage area improvement of over 230% compared to conventional linearly polarized RFID systems. Activities that are completely undetectable with linear tags, such as subtle head movements or respiration, produce clear and measurable signals with the cross-polarized system. The enhanced SNR enables reliable detection even at the edges of the monitoring area, where conventional systems fail entirely.

The system's performance is consistent across different users and activity styles, demonstrating robustness to individual variations in movement patterns. While different users may perform the same activity with slightly different speeds, amplitudes, or positions, the fundamental characteristics of the signal variations remain consistent enough for reliable detection and classification. This robustness is essential for practical applications where the system must work reliably across diverse populations and activity patterns.

The present invention represents a significant advancement in RFID-based human activity monitoring technology, providing a low-cost, non-intrusive solution that overcomes the fundamental limitations of conventional approaches. By leveraging electromagnetic polarization principles at the hardware level, the system achieves dramatic improvements in sensitivity and range without requiring complex signal processing or multiple sensor deployments. The technology is immediately applicable to a wide range of applications including elder care monitoring, rehabilitation tracking, sleep quality assessment, and general wellness monitoring, all while maintaining user privacy and comfort. The system's compatibility with existing RFID infrastructure ensures straightforward integration into current monitoring frameworks, while its enhanced capabilities open new possibilities for detecting subtle behavioral and physiological indicators that were previously beyond the reach of RFID technology.
