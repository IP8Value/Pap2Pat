# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The emergence and re-emergence of pathogenic microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, and plant-derived toxins, pose significant threats to global public health, national security, and economic stability. Among the most insidious mechanisms employed by these agents is their ability to hijack host cellular machinery—particularly proteins and signaling pathways—to facilitate entry into cells, disrupt essential physiological functions, and ultimately induce programmed cell death. Traditional therapeutic strategies have largely focused on targeting the pathogen itself, such as through antibiotics or antivirals that inhibit microbial replication or neutralize virulence factors. However, this pathogen-centric approach is inherently limited by the rapid evolution of resistance mechanisms, the narrow spectrum of activity against diverse pathogens, and the inability to address the downstream host-mediated damage that often drives disease pathology.

In recent years, a paradigm shift has emerged toward host-directed therapeutics—interventions that modulate host cellular components exploited by pathogens rather than directly targeting the pathogen. This strategy offers several compelling advantages: it may confer broad-spectrum efficacy against multiple unrelated pathogens that converge on common host pathways; it reduces the selective pressure for resistance development, as host targets are genetically stable; and it can mitigate the excessive inflammatory or apoptotic responses that contribute significantly to tissue injury and mortality in infectious diseases. Central to this approach is the identification of “hub” proteins—host factors that serve as critical nodes in cellular networks co-opted by diverse pathogens. These hubs represent high-value therapeutic targets because their modulation can simultaneously disrupt multiple pathogenic processes.

A growing body of evidence indicates that caspases—cysteine-aspartic proteases traditionally associated with apoptosis and inflammation—are frequently exploited by bacterial toxins, plant toxins, and viruses to execute cytotoxic effects. For instance, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* exotoxin A (PE), diphtheria toxin, anthrax lethal toxin, ricin, and botulinum neurotoxin have all been shown to activate specific subsets of caspases, leading to caspase-dependent cell death. Similarly, emerging viruses such as Zika virus (ZIKV) induce neuronal apoptosis through caspase-3 activation. Despite this convergence, no clinically approved drug currently exists that effectively inhibits multiple caspases in a manner suitable for acute anti-infective therapy. Existing caspase inhibitors are largely experimental, suffer from poor pharmacokinetic properties, lack selectivity, or have not been evaluated in the context of infectious disease.

Bithionol, a halogenated salicylanilide compound, was historically used as an anthelmintic agent and as a bacteriostatic preservative in cosmetics. Although its use declined due to photosensitization concerns and the advent of more effective antiparasitic drugs like praziquantel, bithionol remains approved for certain indications in parts of Europe and Asia and is included in formulations such as Fonergine® for oral mucosal disorders. Its mechanism of action in helminths is incompletely understood but is thought to involve disruption of oxidative phosphorylation. Importantly, bithionol exhibits favorable pharmacokinetic characteristics in humans: it is orally bioavailable, achieves high serum concentrations (225–480 μM after a 50 mg/kg dose), crosses the blood-brain barrier, and penetrates the placenta—properties that are highly desirable for a systemic anti-infective agent. However, prior to the present invention, bithionol had never been recognized as a caspase inhibitor or as a potential broad-spectrum countermeasure against pathogenic toxins and viruses.

There remains a critical unmet need for safe, orally available, host-directed therapeutics capable of neutralizing the cytotoxic effects of diverse biological threat agents, including Category A and B priority pathogens designated by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). The development of de novo drugs for such indications is prohibitively time-consuming and costly, especially in the context of rapidly emerging outbreaks or bioterrorism scenarios. Drug repurposing—the strategic redeployment of existing, clinically characterized compounds for new indications—offers a pragmatic solution to accelerate therapeutic availability. Yet, systematic approaches to identify repurposable drugs with broad anti-pathogenic activity through host-targeted mechanisms have been lacking.

Accordingly, there is a pressing need for a rational, multiplexed screening methodology capable of simultaneously identifying both therapeutic compounds and their host protein targets within interconnected pathogenicity networks. Such an approach would enable the rapid discovery of polypharmacological agents that inhibit key hub proteins, thereby conferring protection against multiple unrelated pathogens. The present invention addresses these needs by disclosing bithionol as a novel, potent, and selective inhibitor of a defined subset of human caspases—specifically caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9—and demonstrating its unexpected and broad-spectrum efficacy in neutralizing the cytotoxic and pathogenic effects of diverse bacterial toxins (including PE, diphtheria toxin, cholera toxin, anthrax toxin), plant toxins (ricin), neurotoxins (botulinum neurotoxin A), and viruses (Zika virus). Furthermore, the invention provides a robust, dual-multiplex screening platform—termed “Disparate Dual Multiplexing”—that integrates cellular phenotypic assays with biochemical target-based assays to accelerate the identification of host-directed, broad-spectrum anti-pathogenic agents from clinical compound libraries.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods and compositions for treating or preventing infections and intoxications caused by a wide range of pathogenic agents, including bacterial toxins, plant toxins, neurotoxins, and viruses, through the administration of bithionol or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, ester, or derivative thereof. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that bithionol functions as a direct and selective inhibitor of a specific subset of human caspases—namely caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9—which are commonly exploited by diverse pathogenic agents to induce host cell death and propagate infection. By inhibiting these caspase “hub” proteins, bithionol disrupts a convergent host pathway that is critical for the pathogenicity of multiple unrelated biological threat agents, thereby conferring broad-spectrum protection.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method of treating or preventing an infection or intoxication in a subject caused by a pathogenic agent that induces host cell death via activation of one or more caspases selected from the group consisting of caspase-1, caspase-3, caspase-6, caspase-7, and caspase-9. The method comprises administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of bithionol or a pharmaceutically acceptable form thereof. The pathogenic agent may be, for example, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* exotoxin A, diphtheria toxin, cholera toxin, anthrax toxin (comprising protective antigen and lethal factor), ricin, botulinum neurotoxin serotype A, or Zika virus.

In another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising bithionol or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, ester, or derivative thereof, in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. The composition may be formulated for oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, topical, or inhalation administration, and is particularly suited for oral delivery due to bithionol’s established gastrointestinal absorption profile.

In a further aspect, the invention encompasses the use of bithionol for the manufacture of a medicament for treating or preventing infections or intoxications caused by caspase-dependent pathogenic agents. This includes medicaments intended for emergency use in biodefense scenarios involving exposure to bacterial toxins such as anthrax or ricin, as well as for emerging viral infections such as Zika virus.

Additionally, the invention discloses a novel drug screening methodology—referred to herein as “Disparate Dual Multiplexing”—that enables the simultaneous identification of broad-spectrum anti-pathogenic compounds and their host protein targets. This methodology involves conducting parallel multiplexed cellular screens to assess compound efficacy against multiple pathogenic agents (e.g., PE, cholera toxin, diphtheria toxin) and multiplexed biochemical screens to evaluate inhibition of specific host protease activities (e.g., caspase-3, -6, -7). Compounds that yield positive hits in both screening modalities are prioritized as high-confidence candidates with defined mechanisms of action. The application of this methodology led to the identification of bithionol as a dual-validated hit, demonstrating its utility in accelerating drug repurposing efforts.

The invention further provides kits comprising bithionol in unit dosage form, optionally accompanied by instructions for use in the treatment of toxin-mediated or virus-mediated diseases. These kits may be deployed in clinical, military, or public health settings for rapid response to biological threats.

Collectively, the present invention represents a significant advance in host-directed anti-infective therapy by repurposing a known clinical compound—bithionol—as a first-in-class, orally bioavailable, broad-spectrum caspase inhibitor with demonstrated efficacy against a diverse array of high-consequence pathogens. The invention overcomes longstanding limitations in anti-toxin and antiviral therapy by targeting a conserved host vulnerability rather than the highly variable pathogen itself, thereby offering a resilient and adaptable therapeutic strategy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Combination Compositions and Treatments

The present invention provides novel therapeutic strategies centered on the administration of bithionol to counteract the deleterious effects of pathogenic agents that exploit host caspase pathways to induce cell death and propagate disease. Bithionol, chemically known as 2,2'-thiobis(4,6-dichlorophenol), is a small molecule with a molecular weight of 355.14 g/mol and a well-documented safety profile in humans from its historical use as an anthelmintic and topical antiseptic. The invention leverages the newly discovered property of bithionol as a selective inhibitor of caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9 to provide broad-spectrum protection against a range of biological threat agents.

In accordance with the invention, bithionol may be administered as a monotherapy or in combination with other therapeutic agents to enhance efficacy, reduce required dosages, or mitigate potential side effects. Combination therapies are particularly advantageous when addressing complex infections or intoxications where multiple pathogenic mechanisms are at play. For example, in the case of anthrax infection, bithionol may be co-administered with antibiotics such as ciprofloxacin or doxycycline to simultaneously target the bacterium (*Bacillus anthracis*) and neutralize the cytotoxic effects of anthrax toxin on host cells. Similarly, in the context of Zika virus infection, bithionol could be combined with antiviral agents that inhibit viral replication (e.g., nucleoside analogs) to provide a dual-pronged attack on both viral propagation and virus-induced neuronal apoptosis.

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise bithionol in an amount sufficient to achieve a therapeutically effective concentration in the relevant tissues. Based on the experimental data disclosed herein, effective concentrations of bithionol in vitro range from approximately 5 μM to 33 μM, with EC50 values for cytoprotection typically around 10 μM for bacterial and plant toxins and between 5.5 μM and 6.7 μM for Zika virus. Given that oral administration of bithionol in humans yields serum concentrations of 225–480 μM—well above these effective thresholds—the invention contemplates standard oral dosing regimens that ensure sustained therapeutic levels. Typical unit doses may range from 50 mg to 500 mg per administration, adjusted based on patient weight, age, and severity of condition.

The compositions may be formulated using conventional pharmaceutical techniques. For oral administration, bithionol may be incorporated into tablets, capsules, powders, or liquid suspensions using excipients such as lactose, starch, magnesium stearate, cellulose derivatives, and surfactants to enhance solubility and bioavailability. Enteric coatings may be applied to protect the compound from gastric degradation and promote intestinal absorption. For parenteral administration, sterile solutions or lyophilized powders for reconstitution may be prepared using isotonic vehicles such as saline or dextrose solutions. Topical formulations, including creams, ointments, or gels, may be developed for localized applications, such as in the treatment of mucosal infections or skin exposures to toxins.

The invention also encompasses prodrugs and derivatives of bithionol that retain the core caspase-inhibitory activity while improving pharmacokinetic properties such as solubility, metabolic stability, or tissue penetration. Such derivatives may include esterified forms (e.g., acetate or phosphate esters) that are hydrolyzed in vivo to release active bithionol, or structural analogs with modified halogen substitutions that enhance potency or selectivity for specific caspases.

Treatment regimens according to the invention may be prophylactic or therapeutic. Prophylactic administration is envisioned in scenarios of known or suspected exposure to biological threat agents, such as in military personnel deployed to endemic regions or laboratory workers handling hazardous pathogens. Therapeutic administration is indicated upon diagnosis of infection or intoxication, with early intervention being critical to prevent irreversible cellular damage. The duration of treatment may vary from a single dose in acute toxin exposure to multiple daily doses over several days in viral infections, guided by clinical response and pharmacokinetic monitoring.

Importantly, the broad-spectrum nature of bithionol’s action means that a single therapeutic agent can be stockpiled and deployed against multiple unrelated threats, simplifying logistics and enhancing preparedness. This is particularly valuable in resource-limited settings or during public health emergencies where rapid identification of the causative agent may be delayed.

### Administration of the Bithionol Composition

The administration of bithionol compositions in accordance with the present invention is tailored to achieve optimal therapeutic outcomes while minimizing adverse effects. Given bithionol’s established pharmacokinetic profile, oral administration is the preferred route due to its convenience, non-invasiveness, and proven ability to achieve systemic concentrations far exceeding those required for caspase inhibition and cytoprotection.

For oral delivery, bithionol is formulated into solid dosage forms such as tablets or capsules containing 50 mg, 100 mg, 250 mg, or 500 mg of active ingredient. These formulations include standard pharmaceutical excipients: fillers (e.g., microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders (e.g., povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants (e.g., croscarmellose sodium), lubricants (e.g., magnesium stearate), and glidants (e.g., colloidal silicon dioxide). To enhance dissolution and absorption, surfactants such as sodium lauryl sulfate or solubilizers like cyclodextrins may be incorporated. Enteric coatings composed of methacrylic acid copolymers or cellulose acetate phthalate may be applied to prevent degradation in the acidic gastric environment and target release in the small intestine, where absorption is most efficient.

In liquid formulations, bithionol may be suspended or dissolved in aqueous vehicles containing co-solvents (e.g., propylene glycol, ethanol), sweeteners (e.g., sucralose), flavoring agents, and preservatives (e.g., sodium benzoate). Such formulations are particularly useful for pediatric or geriatric patients who have difficulty swallowing solid dosage forms.

Parenteral administration is an alternative route for situations requiring rapid onset of action or when oral intake is contraindicated (e.g., severe gastrointestinal distress). Intravenous formulations consist of sterile, pyrogen-free solutions of bithionol in isotonic saline or 5% dextrose, with concentrations adjusted to deliver doses equivalent to the oral regimen. Lyophilized powders for reconstitution allow for extended shelf life and are reconstituted immediately prior to use. Subcutaneous or intramuscular injections may utilize oil-based or aqueous suspensions with sustained-release properties to prolong therapeutic effect.

Topical administration is contemplated for localized applications, such as in the prevention of dermal absorption of toxins or treatment of mucosal infections. Creams and ointments contain bithionol dispersed in emulsion bases (e.g., white petrolatum, lanolin) with permeation enhancers (e.g., oleic acid) to facilitate transdermal delivery. Oral rinses or throat sprays, similar to the commercial product Fonergine®, may be used to treat or prevent oral manifestations of systemic infections.

Dosing regimens are determined based on the indication, severity, and patient characteristics. For acute toxin exposure (e.g., anthrax, ricin), a loading dose of 250–500 mg may be administered orally, followed by maintenance doses of 100–250 mg every 6–12 hours for 3–7 days. In viral infections such as Zika, a daily dose of 200–400 mg divided into two administrations may be continued for 5–10 days. Pediatric dosing is adjusted proportionally based on body weight, typically ranging from 2–5 mg/kg per dose.

Therapeutic monitoring may include assessment of serum bithionol levels to ensure concentrations remain within the therapeutic window (≥10 μM) while avoiding excessive accumulation that could increase the risk of photosensitivity. Patients are advised to minimize sun exposure and use protective clothing or sunscreen during treatment.

The invention further contemplates the use of bithionol in emergency medical kits, field deployable units, and national stockpiles for rapid response to biological incidents. Pre-packaged blister cards or auto-injectors containing predetermined doses simplify administration in crisis scenarios.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Identification of Host Hub Proteins Exploited by Multiple Pathogenic Toxins

Pathogenic toxins, whether of bacterial, plant, or algal origin, have evolved sophisticated mechanisms to subvert host cellular processes for their entry, trafficking, and cytotoxic action. While each toxin may utilize distinct initial receptors or intracellular routes, their downstream effects often converge on common host signaling nodes—so-called “hub” proteins—that integrate multiple stress and death pathways. The identification of such hubs is critical for the development of broad-spectrum countermeasures that target host vulnerabilities rather than pathogen-specific features, which are prone to variation and resistance.

In this example, we sought to map the host-pathogen interface for *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* exotoxin A (PE), a potent ADP-ribosylating toxin that inhibits protein synthesis and induces apoptosis. PE binds to the α2-macroglobulin receptor/LRP1 on the cell surface, undergoes endocytosis, and is trafficked to acidified endosomes where its catalytic domain is cleaved and translocated into the cytosol. Once intracellular, PE triggers mitochondrial dysfunction and activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway, culminating in caspase activation and cell death. However, the full complement of host factors that determine cellular sensitivity to PE remained incompletely defined.

To address this, we leveraged the genetic diversity of human populations as captured in the International HapMap Project, which provides immortalized B-lymphoblastoid cell lines derived from individuals of geographically and ethnically distinct backgrounds. B-cells are natural targets of PE in vivo, making them physiologically relevant for studying toxin susceptibility. We exposed B-cell lines from 234 individuals—comprising 87 Yoruba in Ibadan, Nigeria (YRI); 60 Utah residents with northern and western European ancestry (CEU); 43 Japanese in Tokyo (JPT); and 44 Han Chinese in Beijing (CHB)—to a standardized dose of PE and measured cell viability after 48 hours using Alamar Blue fluorescence. Strikingly, we observed a 200-fold variation in the toxin dose required to kill 20% of cells (LD20), indicating profound inter-individual differences in PE sensitivity.

The distribution of log(1/LD20) values across all populations was unimodal and continuous, suggesting a polygenic basis for this trait. Analysis of parent-offspring trios confirmed that PE sensitivity is heritable (P < 0.0001), implying that genetic variants in host genes modulate toxin response. We then performed association studies between PE sensitivity and single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in genes encoding proteins known to be involved in PE pathogenesis or apoptosis. Notably, SNPs in *CASP7* (rs3814231 and rs2227309) showed significant association with PE sensitivity in the CEU population (P = 0.01 and P = 0.02, respectively), while SNPs in *CASP3* (rs4647601, rs4647693, rs1049216) were associated in East Asian populations (CHB+JPT, P = 0.04 for each). These findings directly implicate caspase-3 and caspase-7 as host determinants of PE cytotoxicity.

Given that multiple unrelated toxins—including anthrax lethal toxin, diphtheria toxin, ricin, and cholera toxin—also induce caspase-dependent cell death, we hypothesized that caspases represent a class of hub proteins exploited across pathogenic modalities. This convergence makes caspases ideal targets for broad-spectrum therapeutic intervention. The identification of caspase variants that modulate toxin sensitivity provided the genetic validation necessary to prioritize these proteases for subsequent drug screening efforts aimed at discovering host-directed anti-pathogenic agents.

### Example 2. The Effect of Caspase Mutations on the Sensitivity of Human B-Cells to P. aeruginosa Exotoxin A

Building on the genetic association data, this example further characterizes the functional role of caspases in mediating cellular sensitivity to *P. aeruginosa* exotoxin A (PE). Caspases are a family of cysteine proteases that function as central executioners of apoptosis (caspases-3, -6, -7) and mediators of inflammation (caspase-1, -4, -5). Upon activation by upstream signals, executioner caspases cleave hundreds of cellular substrates, leading to the stereotypical morphological and biochemical changes of programmed cell death.

Previous studies had shown that PE intoxication leads to the activation of caspases-3, -6, and -7 in various cell types, but the extent to which natural genetic variation in these enzymes influences cellular outcomes in primary human cells was unknown. Using the HapMap B-cell panel described in Example 1, we stratified cell lines based on their *CASP3* and *CASP7* genotypes and compared their survival following PE exposure. Cells homozygous for the minor alleles of the associated SNPs exhibited significantly reduced sensitivity to PE, consistent with a model in which these variants result in decreased caspase activity or expression.

To confirm the causal role of caspases, we treated sensitive B-cell lines with a pan-caspase inhibitor (Z-VAD-FMK) prior to PE challenge. As expected, caspase inhibition conferred near-complete protection against PE-induced cell death, validating caspases as essential mediators of toxicity. Moreover, immunoblot analysis of PE-treated B-cells revealed time-dependent cleavage of caspase-3 and its substrate PARP, confirming activation of the apoptotic cascade.

These results establish that natural polymorphisms in executioner caspases can modulate host cell susceptibility to bacterial toxins, providing a mechanistic explanation for the observed inter-individual variation in PE sensitivity. More importantly, they underscore the therapeutic potential of pharmacologically inhibiting these caspases to achieve a protective phenotype akin to that conferred by protective genetic variants. This rationale formed the foundation for the subsequent high-throughput screening campaigns designed to identify clinically approved compounds capable of inhibiting caspase activity and neutralizing toxin effects.

### Example 3. A Multiplexed Cellular Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Cytotoxic Activities of Bacterial Toxins Exploiting Unique but Interconnected Host Pathways

To discover repurposable drugs with broad-spectrum anti-toxin activity, we implemented a multiplexed cellular screening strategy using the Johns Hopkins Clinical Compound Library (CCL), which contains over 3,000 small molecules with established human safety profiles. The screen was designed to identify compounds that protect host cells from the cytotoxic effects of three mechanistically distinct bacterial toxins: *P. aeruginosa* exotoxin A (PE), cholera toxin (CT), and diphtheria toxin (DT).

PE and CT intoxicate mouse RAW264.7 macrophages, while DT requires human cells due to species-specific receptor requirements; thus, human C32 melanoma cells were used for DT challenges. Cells were pre-treated with individual CCL compounds at a concentration of 33 μM for 1 hour, followed by toxin addition at doses that induce 30–50% cell death within 12–24 hours. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay, which measures mitochondrial reductase activity as a proxy for live cells.

A stringent hit criterion was applied: compounds had to increase cell survival by at least 20 standard deviations above the toxin-only control (approximately 1% hit rate) and show no intrinsic cytotoxicity in the absence of toxin. Hits that protected against two or more toxins were classified as “multiplex hits,” reflecting potential activity against shared host pathways.

From this screen, two compounds emerged as multiplex hits capable of reducing cytotoxicity induced by all three toxins: bithionol and pyrogallol. Bithionol, in particular, demonstrated robust and reproducible protection across all toxin-cell combinations, prompting its selection for further mechanistic investigation. This cellular screen thus successfully identified bithionol as a candidate broad-spectrum anti-toxin agent, setting the stage for target deconvolution studies.

### Example 4. A Multiplex Protein Function-Based Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Proteolytic Activities of Host Caspase-3, -6, and -7

Parallel to the cellular screen, we conducted a biochemical screen to identify CCL compounds that directly inhibit the proteolytic activity of caspases-3, -6, and -7—the executioner caspases implicated in toxin-induced cell death. Caspase activity was induced in RAW264.7 cells by PE treatment, and cell lysates containing activated caspases were incubated with fluorogenic peptide substrates specific for each caspase: DNLD-AMC for caspase-3, VEID-AMC for caspase-6, and DQTD-AMC for caspases-3/7. Cleavage of these substrates releases the fluorescent AMC moiety, allowing real-time quantification of protease activity.

CCL compounds were tested at 33 μM, and those exhibiting >80% inhibition of substrate cleavage (approximately 1% hit rate) were considered hits. Multiplex hits inhibited at least two caspases. Bithionol was one of only three compounds that potently inhibited all three caspase activities, with near-complete suppression of fluorescence signal.

This biochemical screen independently validated bithionol as a caspase inhibitor, and the concordance between the cellular and biochemical screens—both identifying bithionol as a top multiplex hit—provided strong evidence that bithionol’s anti-toxin effects are mediated through direct caspase inhibition. This dual-validation approach, termed “Disparate Dual Multiplexing,” significantly increases confidence in the mechanism of action and reduces the likelihood of false positives arising from off-target or assay-specific artifacts.

### Example 5. Bithionol Reduces the Pathogenicity of a Range of Toxins by Inhibiting Host Caspases

Having identified bithionol as a dual-validated hit, we next characterized its potency and mechanism in detail. Dose-response experiments in RAW264.7 and C32 cells confirmed that bithionol protects against PE, CT, and DT with an EC50 of approximately 10 μM. Concurrently, in vitro caspase inhibition assays using cell lysates showed that bithionol suppresses caspase-3, -6, and -3/7 activities in a dose-dependent manner, with IC50 values of 21 μM, 13 μM, and 11 μM, respectively—values closely aligned with the cellular EC50, supporting a direct causal link.

To extend these findings to human genetic diversity, we tested bithionol in three randomly selected PE-sensitive HapMap B-cell lines. Pre-treatment with 33 μM bithionol rescued 80–90% of cells from an 80% lethal dose of PE, demonstrating efficacy across different genetic backgrounds.

Furthermore, we evaluated bithionol against the full panel of ten human caspases using purified recombinant proteins. Bithionol selectively inhibited caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9, with no effect on caspases-2, -4, -5, -8, or -10. This selectivity profile is therapeutically advantageous, as it spares caspases involved in immune surveillance (e.g., caspase-8 in extrinsic apoptosis) while blocking those central to toxin-induced death and inflammation (caspase-1 in pyroptosis).

These results establish bithionol as a first-in-class, selective, multi-caspase inhibitor with potent anti-toxin activity, acting through a host-directed mechanism that is conserved across diverse pathogenic insults.

### Example 6. Bithionol Inhibits Cytotoxic Activity of Anthrax Toxins

Anthrax lethal toxin (LeTx), composed of protective antigen (PA) and lethal factor (LF), is a major virulence determinant of *Bacillus anthracis*. LeTx enters cells via PA-mediated endocytosis, and LF—a zinc-dependent metalloprotease—cleaves mitogen-activated protein kinase kinases (MAPKKs), disrupting signaling and inducing caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis in macrophages.

We tested bithionol in RAW264.7 cells challenged with LeTx. At 33 μM, bithionol provided substantial protection, increasing survival from 20% to over 80%. FRET-based assays confirmed that bithionol completely blocked LeTx-induced caspase-1 activation. In contrast, bithionol did not inhibit LF’s proteolytic activity against MAPKK2 (as shown by immunoblot), nor did it affect furin- or cathepsin B-mediated processing of PA, indicating that its protective effect is specifically due to caspase-1 inhibition rather than interference with toxin entry or LF enzymatic function.

These findings demonstrate that bithionol is effective against anthrax toxin, a Category A bioterrorism agent, by targeting the host inflammatory caspase-1, thereby preventing pyroptotic cell death and potentially mitigating the cytokine storm associated with systemic anthrax.

### Example 7. Bithionol Inhibits Ricin and Botulinum Neurotoxin A-Induced Death In Vitro and In Vivo

Ricin, a plant toxin that depurinates ribosomal RNA, and botulinum neurotoxin A (BoNT/A), a zinc protease that cleaves SNAP-25, both induce caspase-dependent apoptosis. In K562 leukemia cells, bithionol reduced ricin cytotoxicity with an EC50 of 10 μM. For BoNT/A, we conducted in vivo studies in Swiss Webster mice challenged with a lethal oral dose (3 μg/mouse). All control mice succumbed within 3 days, exhibiting classic symptoms (wasp waist, paralysis). In contrast, 100% of mice treated with bithionol (6 mg/kg, oral) survived without symptoms.

Crucially, a FRET assay using BoNT/A and its SNAP-25 substrate showed that bithionol does not inhibit BoNT/A proteolytic activity, confirming that protection is mediated through host caspase inhibition (caspases-3/-7) rather than direct toxin neutralization. This in vivo efficacy, coupled with bithionol’s oral bioavailability, positions it as a promising post-exposure therapeutic for neurotoxin intoxication.

### Example 8. Bithionol Acts as a Zika Virus Inhibitor

Zika virus (ZIKV) causes neuronal apoptosis via caspase-3 activation. We tested bithionol against African (DAK AR D 41525) and Asian (PRVABC59) ZIKV strains in Vero E6 cells and human astrocytes. High-content imaging revealed that bithionol reduced viral abundance with EC50 values of 5.5–6.7 μM. Protection was observed in both lineages, indicating broad anti-ZIKV activity.

Given bithionol’s ability to cross the blood-brain barrier and its safety in pregnant women (from historical use), it represents a viable candidate for treating ZIKV infection, particularly to prevent congenital complications. This extends the invention’s applicability beyond bacterial toxins to emerging viral threats.
