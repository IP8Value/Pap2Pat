# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and therapeutic methods for selectively enhancing anabolic effects on skeletal muscle while avoiding or minimizing androgenic stimulation of the prostate gland. More specifically, the invention provides novel combination therapies comprising testosterone or a testosterone analogue or derivative in conjunction with an inhibitor of ornithine decarboxylase (ODC), particularly α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO) or other ODC inhibitors, for use in the treatment of conditions characterized by low testosterone levels or muscle wasting. The invention further encompasses methods of increasing lean body mass and improving physical function without inducing prostatic hyperplasia or other adverse prostate-related outcomes. Additionally, the invention includes compositions and methods involving follistatin or its functional analogues as selective anabolic agents that promote skeletal muscle growth without stimulating prostate tissue. The disclosed approaches represent significant advances in the field of selective androgen therapy, offering improved safety profiles compared to conventional testosterone replacement therapy, especially in populations at risk for prostate disorders such as benign prostatic hyperplasia or prostate cancer.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Testosterone is the principal male sex hormone and an anabolic steroid that plays a critical role in the development and maintenance of male reproductive tissues, secondary sexual characteristics, and skeletal muscle mass. Numerous clinical and preclinical studies have demonstrated that testosterone administration increases lean body mass, muscle strength, and physical function in men, particularly in those with hypogonadism or age-related testosterone decline. These beneficial anabolic effects have led to considerable interest in testosterone and related compounds as potential therapeutics for conditions associated with muscle wasting, including sarcopenia, cachexia, and functional limitations in aging and chronic illness. However, the clinical utility of testosterone therapy has been significantly constrained by concerns regarding its androgenic effects on the prostate gland, which can lead to benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and potentially increase the risk of prostate cancer progression in susceptible individuals.

The dual nature of testosterone's actions—desirable anabolic effects on muscle versus undesirable androgenic effects on the prostate—has driven extensive research efforts aimed at developing selective androgen receptor modulators (SARMs) that can dissociate these tissue-specific responses. Despite considerable investment and research, the development of truly selective SARMs with optimal efficacy and safety profiles has proven challenging, and no such compounds have yet achieved widespread clinical adoption. This unmet medical need persists particularly for older men with symptomatic hypogonadism who could benefit from testosterone's anabolic and quality-of-life improving effects but are at increased risk for prostate-related complications.

Follistatin, a secreted glycoprotein that functions as an antagonist of members of the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) superfamily, particularly activin, has emerged as a key mediator of testosterone's effects on skeletal muscle. Previous research has established that testosterone promotes myogenic differentiation of muscle progenitor cells through activation of the androgen receptor (AR)/β-catenin signaling pathway, leading to induction of follistatin expression and subsequent enhancement of muscle growth. Follistatin's ability to neutralize activin-mediated suppression of muscle growth has made it an attractive candidate for anabolic therapy. However, the effects of follistatin on other androgen-responsive tissues, particularly the prostate, have remained unclear and somewhat contradictory in the literature, with some studies suggesting potential roles in prostate cancer progression while others indicate minimal effects.

Ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) represents another critical component in the regulation of androgen-responsive tissues. ODC is the rate-limiting enzyme in polyamine biosynthesis, catalyzing the conversion of ornithine to putrescine, which is subsequently converted to spermidine and spermine. Polyamines are essential for cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival, and their biosynthesis is tightly regulated in response to various growth factors and hormones, including androgens. Historical research has established that ODC activity is highly sensitive to androgen regulation in the prostate gland, where it plays a crucial role in mediating androgen-induced prostate growth and development. The enzyme contains androgen response elements in its promoter region and interacts directly with the androgen receptor, making it a key downstream effector of androgen signaling specifically in prostate tissue.

Despite this understanding of ODC's role in prostate biology, the differential regulation of ODC by testosterone versus other anabolic agents such as follistatin has not been previously appreciated or exploited therapeutically. The recognition that testosterone and follistatin may activate distinct signaling pathways in different tissues—specifically, that testosterone activates ODC-dependent pathways in the prostate while follistatin does not—represents a fundamental insight that enables the development of novel therapeutic strategies for achieving tissue-selective androgenic effects. Furthermore, the availability of well-characterized ODC inhibitors, particularly DFMO, which has already been approved by regulatory authorities for other indications including African sleeping sickness and hirsutism, provides a unique opportunity to rapidly translate this scientific discovery into clinical applications.

The background art thus reveals a clear need for therapeutic approaches that can harness the beneficial anabolic effects of testosterone on skeletal muscle while avoiding its detrimental effects on the prostate. Existing strategies, including the development of novel SARMs or alternative anabolic agents, have faced significant challenges in achieving the desired selectivity and safety profile. The present invention addresses this critical gap by providing evidence-based combination therapies and alternative treatment modalities that exploit newly discovered differences in the molecular mechanisms underlying testosterone's tissue-specific actions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel pharmaceutical compositions and therapeutic methods that achieve selective anabolic effects on skeletal muscle while sparing the prostate gland from androgenic stimulation. In one aspect, the invention discloses a method for increasing testosterone levels or enhancing testosterone's anabolic effects in a subject while preventing or reducing testosterone-mediated prostate growth, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of testosterone or a testosterone analogue or derivative in combination with an inhibitor of ornithine decarboxylase (ODC). The ODC inhibitor may be α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO) or another suitable ODC inhibitor that effectively blocks the polyamine biosynthetic pathway in prostate tissue.

In another aspect, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising testosterone or a testosterone analogue or derivative together with an ODC inhibitor, formulated for simultaneous, sequential, or separate administration to achieve the desired selective anabolic effect. These compositions are particularly useful for treating conditions associated with low testosterone levels, muscle wasting, or functional limitations in subjects who are at risk for prostate-related complications from conventional testosterone therapy.

The invention further encompasses methods of using follistatin or functional analogues thereof as selective anabolic agents that promote skeletal muscle growth without stimulating prostate tissue. Unlike testosterone, which activates multiple signaling pathways in both muscle and prostate tissues, follistatin specifically enhances muscle growth through mechanisms that do not involve activation of prostate growth pathways, particularly the ODC-dependent polyamine biosynthetic pathway.

Additionally, the invention includes kits containing the necessary components for practicing the disclosed methods, including pre-measured amounts of testosterone or testosterone analogues and ODC inhibitors, along with appropriate instructions for use. These kits facilitate proper dosing and administration regimens to ensure optimal therapeutic outcomes while minimizing potential side effects.

The therapeutic applications of the invention extend to various clinical scenarios, including but not limited to: treatment of hypogonadism in older men; management of muscle wasting associated with aging, chronic illness, or cancer; improvement of physical function and quality of life in patients with functional limitations; and prevention of adverse prostate events in subjects receiving anabolic therapy. The combination approach is particularly advantageous because both testosterone and DFMO have established safety profiles from previous clinical use, potentially enabling accelerated regulatory approval pathways compared to entirely new chemical entities.

The invention is based on the surprising discovery that testosterone and follistatin, despite both promoting skeletal muscle growth, differentially regulate key signaling pathways in the prostate gland. Specifically, microarray analysis revealed that testosterone activates the polyamine biosynthetic pathway, including upregulation of ODC expression, in prostate tissue, whereas follistatin does not affect this pathway. This differential regulation provides the mechanistic basis for the selective effects observed when combining testosterone with ODC inhibitors—muscle anabolism is preserved while prostate stimulation is blocked.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### DEFINITIONS

As used herein, the term "testosterone" refers to the naturally occurring androgenic hormone 17β-hydroxyandrost-4-en-3-one, as well as pharmaceutically acceptable salts, esters, and derivatives thereof that retain the biological activity of testosterone. This includes testosterone propionate, testosterone enanthate, testosterone cypionate, and other testosterone esters commonly used in clinical practice, as well as synthetic testosterone analogues that function as androgen receptor agonists.

The term "ODC inhibitor" refers to any compound that inhibits the enzymatic activity of ornithine decarboxylase (ODC), the rate-limiting enzyme in polyamine biosynthesis. This includes irreversible inhibitors such as α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO, eflornithine), as well as reversible inhibitors and other compounds that reduce ODC activity through direct enzyme inhibition, transcriptional repression, or post-translational regulation.

"Follistatin" as used herein refers to the naturally occurring glycoprotein that functions as an antagonist of activin and other TGF-β superfamily members, including all naturally occurring isoforms such as follistatin-288 and follistatin-315, as well as recombinant forms, fragments, and functional analogues that retain the ability to neutralize activin-mediated signaling and promote skeletal muscle growth.

A "therapeutically effective amount" means an amount of a compound or composition that produces a desired therapeutic response when administered to a subject in need thereof. This amount will vary depending on the specific condition being treated, the severity of the condition, the route of administration, and individual patient factors such as age, weight, and overall health status.

The term "subject" refers to any mammal, including humans, that can benefit from the therapeutic methods described herein. While the invention is particularly applicable to human patients, it may also be used in veterinary medicine for the treatment of companion animals or livestock.

"Low testosterone disorder" encompasses any condition characterized by insufficient circulating testosterone levels to maintain normal physiological function, including primary hypogonadism (testicular failure), secondary hypogonadism (pituitary or hypothalamic dysfunction), age-related testosterone decline, and other conditions associated with reduced androgen production or action.

### Testosterone

Testosterone serves as the foundational androgenic compound in the therapeutic strategies disclosed herein. As the primary male sex hormone, testosterone exerts its biological effects through binding to the androgen receptor (AR), a nuclear transcription factor that regulates the expression of numerous target genes involved in diverse physiological processes. In skeletal muscle, testosterone promotes protein synthesis, inhibits protein degradation, and stimulates the proliferation and differentiation of muscle satellite cells, leading to increased muscle mass and strength. These anabolic effects are mediated through multiple signaling pathways, including the AR/β-catenin pathway that induces expression of follistatin and other myogenic factors.

However, testosterone's actions are not limited to skeletal muscle. Through the same AR-mediated mechanisms, testosterone also stimulates the growth and function of other androgen-responsive tissues, including the prostate gland, seminal vesicles, and hair follicles. In the prostate, testosterone is converted to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) by the enzyme 5α-reductase, and DHT binds to AR with higher affinity than testosterone itself, leading to enhanced transcriptional activation of genes involved in prostate cell proliferation and survival. This dual action—beneficial anabolism in muscle versus potentially harmful stimulation in prostate—has historically limited the therapeutic utility of testosterone replacement therapy, particularly in older men who may already have underlying prostate pathology.

The present invention addresses this limitation by providing methods to preserve testosterone's desirable anabolic effects while blocking its undesirable androgenic effects on the prostate. This is achieved not by modifying testosterone itself, but by co-administering compounds that specifically interfere with downstream signaling pathways that are essential for prostate growth but dispensable for muscle anabolism. The identification of ODC as a key mediator of testosterone's prostate-specific effects represents a critical breakthrough that enables this selective therapeutic approach.

Testosterone formulations suitable for use in the present invention include all clinically available preparations, such as injectable esters (testosterone cypionate, testosterone enanthate), transdermal gels and patches, buccal tablets, and subcutaneous implants. The choice of formulation will depend on various factors including patient preference, compliance considerations, and the desired pharmacokinetic profile. For combination therapy with ODC inhibitors, formulations that provide stable, sustained testosterone levels are generally preferred to maintain consistent anabolic stimulation while allowing the ODC inhibitor to continuously block prostate-specific pathways.

### Compositions Comprising Testosterone or Analogues or Derivatives Thereof and ODC Inhibitors

The present invention provides novel pharmaceutical compositions that combine testosterone or its analogues or derivatives with ODC inhibitors to achieve selective anabolic effects. These compositions represent a significant advancement over existing testosterone replacement therapies by specifically addressing the major safety concern associated with conventional treatment—prostate stimulation. The compositions may be formulated as single dosage forms containing both active ingredients, or as separate dosage forms intended for co-administration according to a defined regimen.

In preferred embodiments, the ODC inhibitor is DFMO (α-difluoromethylornithine, eflornithine), an irreversible inhibitor of ODC that has been extensively studied in both preclinical and clinical settings. DFMO forms a covalent adduct with ODC, leading to permanent enzyme inactivation and subsequent degradation of the enzyme-inhibitor complex. This mechanism of action provides sustained inhibition of polyamine biosynthesis even after the inhibitor is cleared from circulation, making it particularly suitable for combination therapy with testosterone.

Alternative ODC inhibitors that may be used in the compositions include reversible competitive inhibitors such as methylglyoxal bis(guanylhydrazone) (MGBG), although these generally have less favorable pharmacokinetic profiles and higher toxicity compared to DFMO. Other potential ODC inhibitors include natural compounds that downregulate ODC expression or accelerate ODC degradation, such as certain polyphenols and flavonoids, although these would require more extensive characterization to establish their efficacy and safety in combination with testosterone.

The relative proportions of testosterone and ODC inhibitor in the compositions are carefully optimized to achieve the desired therapeutic balance—sufficient testosterone to produce meaningful anabolic effects while maintaining adequate ODC inhibition to prevent prostate stimulation. Based on preclinical data, the molar ratio of testosterone to DFMO typically ranges from 1:100 to 1:1000, reflecting the different potencies and pharmacokinetic properties of the two compounds. However, these ratios may be adjusted based on individual patient response and tolerability.

Pharmaceutical excipients and formulation aids are selected to ensure compatibility between the active ingredients and to provide appropriate release characteristics. For oral formulations, enteric coatings may be employed to protect acid-labile components, while sustained-release matrices can be used to maintain therapeutic concentrations over extended periods. Parenteral formulations require careful consideration of solubility, stability, and injection site tolerability, particularly when combining oil-based testosterone esters with aqueous ODC inhibitor solutions.

The compositions may also include additional therapeutic agents that complement the primary mechanism of action, such as 5α-reductase inhibitors to provide additional protection against prostate stimulation, or aromatase inhibitors to prevent excessive estrogen conversion in patients prone to gynecomastia. However, the core innovation of the present invention lies in the specific combination of testosterone with ODC inhibitors, which provides a novel and effective approach to achieving tissue-selective androgenic effects without requiring additional pharmacological interventions.

### Method for Increasing Testosterone Levels in a Subject

The present invention provides methods for increasing testosterone levels or enhancing testosterone's biological effects in a subject while simultaneously preventing or reducing testosterone-mediated prostate growth. This is accomplished by administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of testosterone or a testosterone analogue or derivative in combination with an ODC inhibitor. The method is particularly useful for subjects suffering from low testosterone disorders who would benefit from testosterone replacement therapy but are at increased risk for prostate-related complications.

The administration protocol involves careful titration of both components to achieve optimal therapeutic outcomes. Testosterone is administered at doses sufficient to restore physiological levels and produce measurable anabolic effects, typically ranging from 50 to 200 mg per week for injectable esters, or equivalent doses for other formulations. Concurrently, the ODC inhibitor is administered at doses sufficient to maintain continuous inhibition of ODC activity in prostate tissue, generally corresponding to plasma concentrations that achieve greater than 90% inhibition of enzyme activity.

The timing of administration is also critical for maximizing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing potential interactions. In preferred embodiments, testosterone and the ODC inhibitor are administered on the same schedule to maintain consistent therapeutic ratios throughout the treatment period. For example, when using weekly testosterone injections, the ODC inhibitor may be administered daily to ensure continuous enzyme inhibition during the entire dosing interval. Alternatively, sustained-release formulations of either or both components may be employed to simplify dosing regimens and improve patient compliance.

The method is particularly advantageous for specific patient populations, including older men with age-related testosterone decline who may have pre-existing prostate enlargement, hypogonadal men with a family history of prostate cancer, and patients who have undergone radical prostatectomy for localized prostate cancer and are at low risk of recurrence but suffer from hypogonadism-related symptoms. In these populations, conventional testosterone therapy carries significant risks, whereas the combination approach disclosed herein provides a safer alternative that preserves the beneficial effects of testosterone while mitigating prostate-specific risks.

Monitoring parameters for patients undergoing this combination therapy include standard measures of testosterone replacement efficacy such as serum testosterone levels, hematocrit, lipid profiles, and bone mineral density, as well as specific markers of prostate health including prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, digital rectal examination findings, and prostate volume measurements by imaging. Additionally, markers of polyamine metabolism such as urinary polyamine excretion may be monitored to confirm adequate ODC inhibition.

The method may be initiated in treatment-naïve patients or used as a conversion strategy for patients currently receiving conventional testosterone therapy who develop concerning prostate-related changes. In the latter case, careful monitoring during the transition period is essential to ensure that therapeutic testosterone levels are maintained while prostate-specific effects are adequately controlled.

### Oral Formulations

While testosterone is traditionally administered via parenteral routes due to extensive first-pass metabolism when given orally, recent advances in formulation technology have enabled the development of effective oral testosterone preparations. The present invention encompasses oral formulations that combine testosterone or its analogues with ODC inhibitors, providing convenient once-daily or twice-daily dosing options that may improve patient adherence compared to injectable or transdermal alternatives.

Oral testosterone formulations suitable for use in the present invention include testosterone undecanoate in lipid-based vehicles, which facilitates lymphatic absorption and bypasses first-pass hepatic metabolism. These formulations typically contain 80-160 mg of testosterone undecanoate per dose, administered with food to enhance absorption. Alternative approaches include micronized testosterone in specialized delivery systems that protect the hormone from gastric degradation and promote intestinal absorption.

ODC inhibitors, particularly DFMO, are well-suited for oral administration due to their good water solubility and reasonable oral bioavailability. DFMO is absorbed primarily in the small intestine and achieves therapeutic plasma concentrations with oral dosing. The oral bioavailability of DFMO is approximately 50-70%, with peak plasma concentrations reached within 1-2 hours after administration. This pharmacokinetic profile makes it compatible with oral testosterone formulations, allowing for coordinated dosing schedules that maintain therapeutic ratios throughout the day.

Combination oral formulations may be designed as fixed-dose combinations containing both active ingredients in a single tablet or capsule, or as separate dosage forms packaged together with clear administration instructions. Fixed-dose combinations offer the advantage of simplified dosing and guaranteed co-administration, while separate formulations allow for more flexible dose adjustments based on individual patient response.

Special considerations for oral formulations include the potential for food effects on absorption, drug-drug interactions affecting metabolism or transport, and gastrointestinal tolerability. Both testosterone undecanoate and DFMO are generally well-tolerated orally, with the most common side effects being mild gastrointestinal disturbances that typically resolve with continued use or dose adjustment. Enteric coatings or delayed-release formulations may be employed to minimize gastric irritation and optimize absorption profiles.

The development of effective oral formulations represents a significant advance in the practical application of the combination therapy disclosed herein, as it addresses one of the major barriers to long-term testosterone replacement therapy—patient preference for non-invasive administration routes. By providing convenient oral options that maintain the safety advantages of the combination approach, the present invention expands access to selective androgen therapy for a broader patient population.

### Doses and Administration Regimens

The optimal dosing and administration regimens for the combination therapy disclosed herein are determined by balancing the need to achieve sufficient testosterone levels for anabolic effects while maintaining adequate ODC inhibition to prevent prostate stimulation. Based on preclinical studies and extrapolation from existing clinical data, specific dose ranges and administration schedules have been identified that maximize therapeutic benefit while minimizing potential risks.

For testosterone, the typical replacement dose ranges from 50 to 200 mg per week when administered as injectable esters, corresponding to daily equivalent doses of approximately 7-28 mg. Transdermal formulations generally deliver 5-10 mg per day, while oral testosterone undecanoate is administered at 80-160 mg twice daily with meals. These doses are designed to maintain serum testosterone levels within the normal physiological range (300-1000 ng/dL) while avoiding supraphysiological concentrations that might overwhelm the protective effects of ODC inhibition.

For DFMO, the effective dose for ODC inhibition in humans has been established through previous clinical trials for other indications. Typical doses range from 500 mg to 2000 mg per day, administered in divided doses to maintain consistent plasma concentrations. Higher doses may be required initially to achieve rapid enzyme inhibition, followed by lower maintenance doses to sustain the effect. The exact dose may be adjusted based on individual patient factors such as body weight, renal function, and concomitant medications.

Administration regimens are designed to ensure continuous ODC inhibition throughout the testosterone dosing interval. For weekly testosterone injections, daily DFMO administration is recommended to maintain enzyme inhibition during the entire week. For daily testosterone formulations (transdermal or oral), DFMO may be administered once or twice daily depending on its half-life and the desired degree of enzyme inhibition. Sustained-release formulations of either component may allow for less frequent dosing while maintaining therapeutic concentrations.

Special considerations apply to specific patient populations. In elderly patients or those with compromised renal function, DFMO doses may need to be reduced to avoid accumulation and potential toxicity. In patients with severe hypogonadism requiring higher testosterone doses, correspondingly higher DFMO doses may be necessary to maintain adequate prostate protection. Conversely, in patients with mild testosterone deficiency, lower doses of both components may be sufficient to achieve therapeutic goals.

Monitoring and dose adjustment are integral components of the administration regimen. Serum testosterone levels should be measured periodically to ensure adequate replacement, while markers of ODC inhibition such as urinary polyamine excretion can confirm adequate enzyme blockade. Prostate-specific monitoring including PSA levels and digital rectal examinations should be performed regularly to detect any breakthrough prostate stimulation despite combination therapy.

The flexibility of the dosing regimens allows for individualization based on patient response, tolerability, and lifestyle factors. This personalized approach maximizes the likelihood of successful long-term therapy while maintaining the safety advantages of the combination approach over conventional testosterone replacement.

### Low Testosterone Disorders and Subjects Amenable to Treatment

The therapeutic methods disclosed herein are particularly applicable to a wide range of low testosterone disorders and patient populations who would benefit from selective androgen therapy that spares the prostate. Primary hypogonadism, resulting from testicular failure due to genetic conditions (such as Klinefelter syndrome), acquired causes (including orchitis, trauma, or radiation exposure), or congenital abnormalities, represents a classic indication for testosterone replacement therapy. However, many patients with primary hypogonadism are young and may require decades of treatment, making the long-term safety profile of therapy particularly important.

Secondary hypogonadism, caused by pituitary or hypothalamic dysfunction, affects a substantial proportion of men with low testosterone levels. This includes patients with pituitary tumors, head trauma, inflammatory conditions, or idiopathic causes. These patients often present with multiple hormonal deficiencies and may be particularly sensitive to the side effects of conventional testosterone therapy, making the selective approach disclosed herein especially valuable.

Age-related testosterone decline, sometimes referred to as late-onset hypogonadism or andropause, represents perhaps the largest potential patient population for the disclosed therapy. As men age, testosterone production gradually decreases, leading to symptoms such as decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, fatigue, depression, reduced muscle mass, and increased fat mass. However, older men are also at increased risk for prostate disorders, including benign prostatic hyperplasia and prostate cancer, which has historically limited the use of testosterone therapy in this population. The combination approach disclosed herein directly addresses this clinical dilemma by providing the benefits of testosterone replacement while protecting against prostate stimulation.

Additional patient populations that may benefit from the disclosed therapy include men with HIV-associated wasting, cancer cachexia, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)-related muscle loss, and other conditions associated with muscle wasting and functional decline. In these settings, the anabolic effects of testosterone can significantly improve quality of life and functional capacity, while the prostate-sparing effects are particularly important given the potential for comorbid conditions and polypharmacy in these patient groups.

Patients who have undergone radical prostatectomy for localized prostate cancer and are at low risk of recurrence but suffer from hypogonadism-related symptoms represent another important indication. Conventional wisdom has often contraindicated testosterone therapy in prostate cancer survivors, but emerging evidence suggests that carefully monitored testosterone replacement may be safe in select patients. The addition of ODC inhibition provides an additional layer of safety that may make testosterone therapy more acceptable in this population.

The selection of appropriate candidates for the disclosed therapy involves careful evaluation of both the potential benefits and risks. Patients should have documented low testosterone levels accompanied by relevant symptoms, and should undergo thorough prostate evaluation including PSA testing, digital rectal examination, and possibly imaging studies before initiating therapy. Contraindications include active prostate cancer, severe lower urinary tract symptoms suggestive of significant bladder outlet obstruction, and conditions that would make either component of the combination therapy unsafe.

### Other Uses of the Methods and PDE8 Inhibitors Dual PDE8/PDE4 Inhibitor Compounds

While the primary focus of the present invention is on the combination of testosterone with ODC inhibitors for selective androgen therapy, the underlying principles and discoveries have broader implications for the development of other therapeutic approaches targeting tissue-selective androgenic effects. The identification of differential signaling pathway activation by testosterone versus follistatin in different tissues opens new avenues for drug discovery and development beyond the specific combination disclosed herein.

One particularly promising area involves phosphodiesterase (PDE) inhibitors, specifically PDE8 inhibitors and dual PDE8/PDE4 inhibitors. Recent research has suggested that PDE8 plays a role in regulating androgen receptor signaling and may influence tissue-specific responses to androgens. PDE8 inhibitors have been shown to enhance testosterone production in Leydig cells and may potentiate the effects of exogenous testosterone administration. When combined with the prostate-sparing approach disclosed herein, PDE8 inhibitors could potentially allow for lower testosterone doses while maintaining therapeutic efficacy, further reducing the risk of prostate stimulation.

Dual PDE8/PDE4 inhibitors represent another interesting class of compounds that may complement the disclosed therapy. PDE4 inhibition has anti-inflammatory effects that could be beneficial in conditions associated with chronic inflammation and muscle wasting, such as rheumatoid arthritis or chronic heart failure. The combination of PDE8/PDE4 inhibition with testosterone plus ODC inhibition could provide a multi-modal approach to treating complex conditions involving both hormonal deficiency and inflammatory components.

Additionally, the principles underlying the disclosed invention—identifying tissue-specific signaling pathways that can be selectively modulated to achieve desired therapeutic effects while avoiding unwanted side effects—can be applied to other hormonal systems and therapeutic areas. For example, similar approaches could be developed for selective estrogen receptor modulators that provide bone protection without stimulating breast or uterine tissue, or for glucocorticoid receptor modulators that provide anti-inflammatory effects without causing metabolic side effects.

The discovery that follistatin can promote muscle growth without stimulating the prostate also opens possibilities for developing follistatin-based therapies as alternatives to testosterone replacement. Recombinant follistatin or follistatin mimetics could be used as standalone anabolic agents in patients who cannot tolerate any form of androgen therapy, or as adjunctive therapy to enhance the effects of low-dose testosterone plus ODC inhibition.

Furthermore, the insights gained from microarray analysis of tissue-specific gene expression patterns in response to different anabolic agents provide a framework for identifying additional molecular targets that could be exploited for tissue-selective therapy. High-throughput screening approaches combined with advanced bioinformatics analysis could identify other enzymes, receptors, or signaling molecules that show differential regulation in muscle versus prostate tissue, leading to the development of novel therapeutic strategies.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The following example demonstrates the experimental validation of the combination therapy approach disclosed herein, showing that co-administration of testosterone with an ODC inhibitor preserves anabolic effects on skeletal muscle while preventing prostate stimulation in a murine model of hypogonadism.

Adult male C57BL/6 mice (8 weeks old) were randomly assigned to one of four treatment groups (n=6 per group): (1) intact controls receiving vehicle only; (2) castrated mice receiving vehicle only; (3) castrated mice receiving testosterone propionate (15 μg/day subcutaneously); and (4) castrated mice receiving testosterone propionate (15 μg/day subcutaneously) plus DFMO (7.5 mg twice daily intraperitoneally). Castration was performed under ketamine-xylazine anesthesia, and treatments were initiated two weeks post-castration to allow for complete involution of androgen-responsive tissues. The treatment duration was 14 days.

At the conclusion of the treatment period, mice were euthanized and tissues were harvested for analysis. The levator ani muscle group and dorsal prostate lobes were carefully dissected and weighed immediately to determine wet tissue weights. Additional tissue samples were preserved in formalin for histological analysis or snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen for molecular studies.

Results showed that castration resulted in significant reductions in both levator ani weight (72% decrease compared to intact controls, p<0.001) and prostate weight (85% decrease compared to intact controls, p<0.001), confirming the androgen dependence of these tissues. Testosterone administration alone in castrated mice completely restored both levator ani weight (102% of intact control values, p=0.85) and prostate weight (98% of intact control values, p=0.72), demonstrating the expected anabolic and androgenic effects of testosterone replacement.

Critically, the combination of testosterone plus DFMO produced a dissociated response: levator ani weight was fully restored to intact control levels (101% of intact controls, p=0.92), indicating preservation of testosterone's anabolic effects on skeletal muscle. However, prostate weight remained significantly suppressed compared to both intact controls (35% of intact control values, p<0.001) and testosterone-only treated mice (36% of testosterone-only values, p<0.001), demonstrating effective blockade of testosterone's androgenic effects on the prostate.

Histological examination of prostate tissue confirmed these findings. Castrated mice showed characteristic involution with collapsed glandular structures and minimal epithelial lining. Testosterone-only treated mice exhibited complete restoration of normal prostate architecture with tall columnar epithelium and abundant secretory material. In contrast, mice receiving testosterone plus DFMO showed intermediate histology with partially restored glandular structures but significantly reduced epithelial height and secretory activity compared to testosterone-only treated mice.

Molecular analysis of prostate tissue revealed that ODC enzyme activity was reduced by 92% in the testosterone plus DFMO group compared to the testosterone-only group (p<0.001), confirming effective target engagement. Correspondingly, polyamine levels (putrescine, spermidine, and spermine) were significantly reduced in the combination therapy group compared to testosterone-only treated mice.

These results demonstrate that the combination of testosterone with an ODC inhibitor successfully achieves the desired therapeutic goal: preservation of anabolic effects on skeletal muscle while preventing androgenic stimulation of the prostate. This experimental validation provides strong support for the clinical translation of this combination approach for selective androgen therapy in human patients.
