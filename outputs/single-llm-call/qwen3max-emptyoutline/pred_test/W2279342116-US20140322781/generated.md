# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of heterogeneous catalysis, and more specifically to a novel heterobimetallic zeolite catalyst composition and its application in the conversion of bio-derived ethanol into high-value hydrocarbon blend-stocks suitable for use as transportation fuels or chemical feedstocks. The invention provides a bimetallic indium-vanadium exchanged ZSM-5 zeolite (hereinafter referred to as “InV-ZSM-5”) that exhibits superior catalytic performance—characterized by enhanced selectivity toward C3+ hydrocarbons, reduced ethylene (C2) formation, improved durability against deactivation by coking, and tolerance to aqueous ethanol feeds containing typical fermentation impurities—compared to monometallic vanadium-exchanged ZSM-5 (V-ZSM-5), indium-exchanged ZSM-5 (In-ZSM-5), or the parent proton-form H-ZSM-5. The invention further encompasses methods for synthesizing the InV-ZSM-5 catalyst via sequential ion exchange, processes for converting ethanol—whether anhydrous or derived directly from fermentation broths—into a liquid hydrocarbon product stream rich in aromatics and iso-paraffins with high octane numbers, and systems for implementing such conversion under industrially relevant conditions. The resulting hydrocarbon blend-stock possesses favorable fuel properties, including a research octane number exceeding 105, low Reid vapor pressure, and a molecular weight distribution suitable for gasoline-range applications. This invention addresses critical challenges in biomass valorization, particularly the need for robust, selective, and water-tolerant catalysts that enable cost-effective upgrading of renewable ethanol without requiring energy-intensive dehydration steps, thereby facilitating the integration of bio-refineries with existing petroleum infrastructure.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global transition toward sustainable energy sources has intensified efforts to develop renewable alternatives to fossil-derived transportation fuels. Among potential bio-based platforms, ethanol produced via fermentation of lignocellulosic biomass or sugar crops represents one of the most mature and scalable options. However, the direct use of ethanol as a fuel additive is limited by its low energy density, high oxygen content, hygroscopic nature, and incompatibility with existing engine and distribution systems at high blending ratios. Consequently, significant research has focused on catalytic upgrading of ethanol to hydrocarbons that are chemically and physically compatible with conventional gasoline, diesel, or jet fuels. Zeolite-based catalysts, particularly those derived from the ZSM-5 framework, have emerged as leading candidates for this transformation due to their well-defined microporous structure, tunable acidity, and proven efficacy in hydrocarbon conversion processes such as methanol-to-gasoline (MTG) and fluid catalytic cracking (FCC).

Early studies on ethanol conversion over H-ZSM-5 revealed that the reaction proceeds primarily through acid-catalyzed dehydration to ethylene, followed by oligomerization, cyclization, and aromatization to form higher hydrocarbons. While effective in achieving full conversion, H-ZSM-5 suffers from several drawbacks: excessive production of light olefins (especially ethylene), rapid deactivation due to coke formation, and sensitivity to water—a major component of fermentation-derived ethanol streams—which competes for active sites and suppresses desired reactions. To mitigate these issues, researchers have explored metal-modified ZSM-5 catalysts. Vanadium-exchanged ZSM-5 (V-ZSM-5) was identified as a promising candidate because vanadium species can partially neutralize Brønsted acid sites responsible for ethylene formation while introducing redox functionality that promotes oxidative dehydrogenation pathways. Indeed, prior work demonstrated that V-ZSM-5 significantly reduces ethylene selectivity compared to H-ZSM-5 and enhances yields of C3+ hydrocarbons in the lower temperature range (250–350°C). However, V-ZSM-5 still exhibits limited durability, requiring frequent regeneration due to coking, and its performance degrades when exposed to wet ethanol feeds.

Separately, indium-exchanged ZSM-5 (In-ZSM-5) has been extensively studied for alkane aromatization, where indium species facilitate dehydrogenation and cyclization steps. When applied to ethanol conversion, In-ZSM-5 shows moderate improvements in aromatic selectivity but does not sufficiently suppress ethylene formation or extend catalyst lifetime. These limitations suggest that neither vanadium nor indium alone provides an optimal balance of acidity modulation, redox activity, and stability required for efficient ethanol-to-hydrocarbons conversion.

The concept of bimetallic modification—introducing two distinct metal species into a single zeolite framework—has gained traction as a strategy to create synergistic effects unattainable with monometallic analogues. For instance, CuFe-ZSM-5 was shown to outperform individual Cu-ZSM-5 or Fe-ZSM-5 in certain oxidation reactions, attributed to electronic or structural interactions between the two metals. However, the synthesis and characterization of true heterobimetallic zeolites remain challenging. Conventional analytical techniques such as X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and even X-ray absorption spectroscopy often fail to distinguish whether two metals are co-located within the same zeolite crystal or merely form a physical mixture of two monometallic phases. Moreover, the catalytic behavior of bimetallic systems is highly sensitive to the method of preparation, metal loading sequence, and thermal treatment conditions.

Despite these advances, no prior art discloses a heterobimetallic In-V co-exchanged ZSM-5 catalyst specifically designed and optimized for ethanol conversion to high-octane hydrocarbon blend-stocks. Existing literature either focuses on monometallic systems or employs mechanical mixtures that lack the intimate metal-metal interactions necessary for synergistic performance. Furthermore, previous studies have not adequately addressed the practical requirement of processing crude fermentation broths containing water and trace impurities without costly pre-purification steps. There remains a critical unmet need for a catalyst that simultaneously achieves: (1) high selectivity to valuable C3+ hydrocarbons (particularly C5+ liquid fraction with high aromatic content); (2) minimal ethylene byproduct formation; (3) extended operational lifetime before regeneration; (4) full tolerance to aqueous ethanol feeds; and (5) regenerability under mild conditions. The present invention fulfills this need by providing a uniquely structured InV-ZSM-5 catalyst whose properties arise not from the simple addition of In-ZSM-5 and V-ZSM-5 functionalities, but from a distinct heterobimetallic environment created through controlled sequential ion exchange, as evidenced by distinctive spectroscopic signatures and superior catalytic metrics.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel heterobimetallic zeolite catalyst comprising indium and vanadium cations ion-exchanged into a ZSM-5 zeolite framework, wherein the molar ratio of SiO₂ to Al₂O₃ in the ZSM-5 ranges from 15 to 50, the vanadium content is from 0.3 to 1.0 weight percent based on the total catalyst mass, and the indium content is from 1.0 to 2.0 weight percent based on the total catalyst mass. This catalyst, designated InV-ZSM-5, is distinguished from physical mixtures of monometallic In-ZSM-5 and V-ZSM-5 by the absence of a Raman band at approximately 402 cm⁻¹ and the presence of unique Raman peaks at 484 cm⁻¹ and 919 cm⁻¹, which are indicative of specific indium-vanadium interactions within the zeolite pores. The invention further provides a method for synthesizing the InV-ZSM-5 catalyst by first preparing a vanadium-exchanged ZSM-5 precursor via ion exchange of NH₄-ZSM-5 with an aqueous solution of V(III) chloride, followed by calcination, and then subjecting the resulting V-ZSM-5 to a second ion exchange with an aqueous solution of indium(III) nitrate, followed by drying and calcination at 500°C.

In another aspect, the invention provides a process for converting ethanol to hydrocarbons, comprising contacting a feed stream comprising ethanol—optionally in aqueous solution containing up to 90% water and up to 0.5% by weight of typical fermentation impurities such as acetaldehyde, methanol, diacetyl, ethyl acetate, and acetic acid—with the InV-ZSM-5 catalyst at a temperature between 250°C and 450°C and a weight hourly space velocity (WHSV) between 1.0 h⁻¹ and 10.0 h⁻¹. This process achieves 100% ethanol conversion to hydrocarbons with a selectivity to C3+ hydrocarbons exceeding 80% by carbon yield in the temperature range of 250–350°C, while maintaining ethylene selectivity below 15%. The resulting liquid hydrocarbon product stream comprises a C5+ fraction having an average molecular weight of 90–110 g/mol, a specific gravity of 0.78–0.85, a Reid vapor pressure of 4–7 psi, a research octane number of at least 100, and a motor octane number of at least 85, with aromatic content greater than 55% by volume.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for regenerating the spent InV-ZSM-5 catalyst by heating in air at 500°C for one hour, which fully restores catalytic activity after deactivation by coking. The regenerated catalyst demonstrates stable performance over multiple reaction-regeneration cycles without loss of structural integrity or metal leaching. The invention also encompasses a system for continuous ethanol upgrading comprising a fixed-bed reactor charged with InV-ZSM-5 catalyst, a feed delivery system for aqueous ethanol, a product separation unit for isolating hydrocarbons from water, and a regeneration loop for periodic catalyst reactivation.

The key advantages of the present invention include: (i) synergistic enhancement of C3+ selectivity and suppression of ethylene formation compared to monometallic V-ZSM-5 or In-ZSM-5; (ii) doubled operational lifetime before regeneration relative to V-ZSM-5 under identical conditions; (iii) complete insensitivity to water concentration in the ethanol feed, enabling direct use of fermentation broth without dewatering; (iv) tolerance to common fermentation impurities without catalyst poisoning; and (v) production of a high-octane, low-vapor-pressure gasoline blend-stock meeting ASTM specifications. These benefits stem from the unique heterobimetallic structure of InV-ZSM-5, which modulates acid site distribution, introduces complementary redox and dehydrogenation functions, and stabilizes reactive intermediates involved in the hydrocarbon pool mechanism, as supported by deuterium labeling experiments and in-situ DRIFTS studies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a heterobimetallic zeolite catalyst, methods of its preparation, and its use in the catalytic conversion of ethanol to hydrocarbons. The catalyst is based on the ZSM-5 zeolite framework, which possesses a three-dimensional pore system consisting of straight channels (5.3 × 5.6 Å) intersecting with sinusoidal channels (5.1 × 5.5 Å), providing shape-selective properties ideal for hydrocarbon transformations. The ZSM-5 starting material may be obtained commercially or synthesized hydrothermally, and is typically provided in the ammonium form (NH₄-ZSM-5) to facilitate subsequent ion exchange. The SiO₂/Al₂O₃ molar ratio of the ZSM-5 is a critical parameter influencing acidity and stability; ratios between 15 and 50 are preferred, with 20–30 being optimal for balancing active site density and hydrothermal stability during ethanol conversion.

The catalyst of the invention contains two active metal components: vanadium and indium, both incorporated via ion exchange into the zeolite channels and cages. Vanadium is introduced first by contacting NH₄-ZSM-5 with an aqueous solution of a vanadium(III) salt, preferably VCl₃, at a concentration sufficient to achieve a final vanadium loading of 0.3–1.0 wt%, more preferably 0.5–0.7 wt%. The exchange is conducted under reflux at 70–90°C for 12–24 hours to ensure diffusion of vanadium species into the micropores. Following filtration and drying, the material is calcined in air at 450–550°C for 2–6 hours to oxidize V³⁺ to V⁵⁺ and stabilize the exchanged species. Subsequently, indium is introduced by a second ion exchange step using an aqueous solution of indium(III) nitrate at a concentration yielding 1.0–2.0 wt% indium, preferably 1.5–1.7 wt%. This exchange is also performed under reflux at 70–90°C for 12–24 hours. After filtration, washing, and drying, a final calcination at 500°C for 4 hours completes the synthesis. This sequential exchange protocol is essential to achieve the desired heterobimetallic structure; simultaneous co-exchange or reverse-order exchange (In first, then V) results in inferior catalytic performance due to competitive adsorption and phase segregation.

The resulting InV-ZSM-5 catalyst is characterized by a unique combination of spectroscopic features that confirm the formation of a true bimetallic phase rather than a physical mixture. UV-Vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy reveals an absorption band near 300 nm attributable to both In³⁺ and V⁵⁺ species, but notably lacks the 402 nm band characteristic of isolated V⁵⁺ in octahedral coordination observed in monometallic V-ZSM-5. Raman spectroscopy further distinguishes InV-ZSM-5 by the presence of sharp peaks at 484 cm⁻¹ and 919 cm⁻¹, which do not correspond to any known bulk oxides of vanadium (e.g., V₂O₅), indium (e.g., In₂O₃), or mixed oxides (e.g., VInO₄), and persist after high-temperature pretreatment, ruling out surface-adsorbed contaminants. These spectral signatures indicate the formation of novel In–V–O clusters or complexes confined within the zeolite pores, which are responsible for the enhanced catalytic properties.

In operation, the InV-ZSM-5 catalyst is employed in a fixed-bed reactor configuration, though fluidized or moving-bed designs are also feasible. The ethanol feed may be pure or diluted with water (10–90 wt% ethanol), and may contain up to 0.5 wt% total impurities typical of fermentation broths, including aldehydes, ketones, esters, and organic acids. The feed is vaporized and contacted with the catalyst at temperatures ranging from 250°C to 450°C, with 300–400°C being optimal for maximizing C3+ yield while minimizing coking. The WHSV is maintained between 1.0 and 10.0 h⁻¹, with 2.0–5.0 h⁻¹ preferred for industrial throughput. Under these conditions, ethanol conversion is quantitative (>99.9%), and the product distribution is remarkably stable across varying water concentrations, demonstrating the catalyst’s immunity to steam-induced deactivation.

The product stream consists of gaseous C2–C4 hydrocarbons and a condensed C5+ liquid fraction. Critically, ethylene selectivity is suppressed to less than 15% in the 250–350°C range—significantly lower than H-ZSM-5 (>30%) or V-ZSM-5 (~20%)—while C3+ selectivity exceeds 85%. The C5+ fraction is a complex mixture of over 350 compounds, dominated by aromatics (60.2% by volume), followed by iso-paraffins (24.0%), paraffins (3.8%), olefins (6.5%), naphthenes (5.4%), and minor unidentified components. This composition translates to excellent fuel properties: research octane number (RON) of 105.7, motor octane number (MON) of 90.6, average molecular weight of 97.6 g/mol, specific gravity of 0.81, and Reid vapor pressure (RVP) of 5.14 psi—all within or exceeding specifications for premium gasoline blend-stocks.

Deactivation occurs gradually via coke deposition, with the C3+ yield declining to 80% of initial value after approximately 12 hours on-stream at 350°C and WHSV=4.0 h⁻¹—twice the lifetime of V-ZSM-5 under identical conditions. Regeneration is accomplished by burning off coke in air at 500°C for one hour, which fully restores activity; lower temperatures (e.g., 450°C) achieve only partial recovery. The enhanced durability is attributed to indium’s role in moderating acid strength and stabilizing reactive intermediates, thereby reducing polymerization pathways leading to coke.

Mechanistic studies using deuterated ethanol (C₂H₅OD) reveal that deuterium is incorporated into all hydrocarbon products except ethylene, supporting a hydrocarbon pool mechanism wherein ethanol adds directly to unsaturated species (olefins or aromatic side-chains) within the zeolite pores, followed by dehydration that can involve either H or D elimination. This contrasts with a simple dehydration-first pathway, which would produce non-deuterated ethylene and subsequent products. In-situ DRIFTS confirms the rapid formation of alkylated aromatics (1567 cm⁻¹ band) within minutes of ethanol exposure at 350°C, consistent with hydrocarbon pool propagation. The heterobimetallic In–V sites appear to facilitate this pathway by providing balanced acid-redox functionality that promotes C–C coupling while suppressing over-dehydration to ethylene.

### Example 1

A sample of InV-ZSM-5 catalyst was prepared as follows. Commercial NH₄-ZSM-5 (Zeolyst CBV2314, SiO₂/Al₂O₃ = 23) was first converted to H-ZSM-5 by calcination at 500°C for 4 hours. For V-ZSM-5 synthesis, 12.17 g of NH₄-ZSM-5 was added to 320 mL of a 0.05 M aqueous solution of VCl₃ and stirred under reflux at 80°C for 16 hours. The solid was filtered, washed with deionized water until chloride-free, dried at 105°C overnight, and calcined in air at 500°C for 4 hours, yielding a light yellow powder with 0.7 wt% vanadium as confirmed by elemental analysis. This V-ZSM-5 was then used as the precursor for InV-ZSM-5: 5.0 g of V-ZSM-5 was suspended in 200 mL of a 0.015 M aqueous solution of In(NO₃)₃·xH₂O and refluxed at 80°C for 16 hours. After filtration, washing, drying at 105°C, and calcination at 500°C (ramp rate 1°C/min, hold 4 hours), the final catalyst contained 0.6 wt% V and 1.6 wt% In. UV-Vis spectroscopy showed a broad absorption at ~300 nm and no band at 402 nm. Raman spectroscopy revealed peaks at 395, 484, and 919 cm⁻¹, distinct from monometallic controls.

Catalytic testing was performed in a quartz fixed-bed reactor (8 mm ID) loaded with 0.2 g of 250–500 μm catalyst particles. The catalyst was pretreated in helium (50 sccm) at 450°C for 30 minutes, then cooled to the reaction temperature (250–450°C). Ethanol was fed via syringe pump at a rate corresponding to WHSV = 4.0 h⁻¹. Products were analyzed by online GC/MS with HP-PLOT Q column. At 300°C, ethanol conversion was 100%, with C2 selectivity of 12.3% and C3+ selectivity of 87.7%. The C5+ liquid fraction, collected in a cold trap, was analyzed by SGS and found to contain 60.2% aromatics, 24.0% iso-paraffins, and had RON = 105.7, MON = 90.6, MW = 97.6, SG = 0.81, RVP = 5.14 psi. When tested with 40% aqueous ethanol containing 0.1% fermentation impurities, product selectivity remained unchanged. Durability testing at 350°C showed C3+ yield declined to 80% after 12 hours; regeneration at 500°C in air for 1 hour fully restored activity. Deuterium labeling with C₂H₅OD confirmed deuterium incorporation in C3+ hydrocarbons but not ethylene, supporting hydrocarbon pool mechanism.

### Example 2

Comparative catalysts were prepared and tested under identical conditions to demonstrate the superiority of InV-ZSM-5. H-ZSM-5 was made by calcining NH₄-ZSM-5 at 500°C for 4 hours. V-ZSM-5 (0.7 wt% V) and In-ZSM-5 (1.6 wt% In) were synthesized via single ion exchange as described in Example 1. A mechanical mixture was also prepared by physically blending V-ZSM-5 and In-ZSM-5 in a 1:2.3 mass ratio to match the V:In ratio of InV-ZSM-5.

All catalysts were tested at 300°C, WHSV = 4.0 h⁻¹ with pure ethanol feed. H-ZSM-5 gave 32.1% C2 and 67.9% C3+ selectivity. V-ZSM-5 yielded 19.8% C2 and 80.2% C3+. In-ZSM-5 produced 25.4% C2 and 74.6% C3+. The mechanical mixture showed 18.9% C2 and 81.1% C3+, similar to V-ZSM-5 alone. In stark contrast, InV-ZSM-5 achieved only 12.3% C2 and 87.7% C3+, confirming a synergistic effect beyond simple additive behavior. Raman spectra of the mechanical mixture matched V-ZSM-5 (no 484/919 cm⁻¹ peaks), while InV-ZSM-5 showed the unique bimetallic signature.

Durability tests at 350°C revealed V-ZSM-5 lost 20% C3+ yield in 6 hours, whereas InV-ZSM-5 required 12 hours for equivalent deactivation. Regeneration at 450°C recovered 94% activity for InV-ZSM-5 versus 98% for V-ZSM-5, but full recovery for both was achieved at 500°C. When challenged with 60% aqueous ethanol, all catalysts maintained conversion, but H-ZSM-5 showed a 15% drop in C3+ selectivity due to water competition, while InV-ZSM-5 performance was unaffected. These results unequivocally establish InV-ZSM-5 as a superior catalyst for selective, durable, and water-tolerant ethanol-to-hydrocarbons conversion.
