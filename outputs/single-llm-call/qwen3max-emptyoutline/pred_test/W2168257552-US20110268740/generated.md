# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunotherapeutics and, more specifically, to natural human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the extracellular domain of the matrix protein 2 (M2e) of Influenza A virus. These antibodies are capable of recognizing native M2 expressed on the surface of influenza-infected cells and mediate protective immune responses through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising such antibodies, methods for their production using mammalian cell display technology, and their use in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings for the prevention and treatment of Influenza A infection in humans. Given the high conservation of the M2e sequence across diverse Influenza A subtypes—including seasonal H1N1 and H3N2 strains as well as highly pathogenic avian influenza viruses such as H5N1—the antibodies described herein represent a broadly effective countermeasure against current and emerging influenza threats. The invention is particularly relevant to populations at high risk of severe influenza complications, including the elderly, young children, and immunocompromised individuals, who often exhibit suboptimal responses to conventional vaccines. Moreover, because the antibodies are derived from naturally occurring human B cells and have undergone physiological affinity maturation, they possess favorable pharmacological properties, including high expression yields in mammalian systems, low immunogenicity, and minimal off-target reactivity, thereby offering significant advantages over engineered or non-human-derived antibody formats.

## RELATED ART

Influenza A virus remains a persistent global public health challenge, causing an estimated three to five million cases of severe illness and between 250,000 and 500,000 deaths annually. Current strategies for controlling influenza rely heavily on vaccination, which primarily elicits neutralizing antibodies against the hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA) surface glycoproteins of the virus. However, the utility of these vaccines is fundamentally limited by the antigenic variability of HA and NA, which undergo continuous mutation—a process known as antigenic drift—and occasional major reassortment events leading to antigenic shift. These mechanisms necessitate annual reformulation of seasonal influenza vaccines and render them ineffective against novel pandemic strains that emerge through zoonotic transmission. Passive immunization with monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) targeting HA has demonstrated efficacy in preclinical models and early clinical trials, yet it suffers from the same vulnerability to viral escape due to antigenic variation. Consequently, there has been a long-standing interest in identifying conserved viral epitopes that could serve as targets for universal influenza vaccines or broadly protective therapeutics.

Among the candidate targets, the matrix protein 2 (M2) of Influenza A virus has attracted considerable attention due to the remarkable conservation of its 23-amino acid extracellular domain (M2e) across nearly all human influenza A isolates over the past century. This conservation is attributed, at least in part, to the genetic linkage of M2 with the matrix protein 1 (M1), as both are encoded by segment 7 of the viral genome through alternative splicing. Unlike HA and NA, which are abundantly displayed on the virion surface, M2 is present in very low copy numbers on infectious particles but is highly expressed on the plasma membrane of infected host cells. As a result, conventional influenza vaccines fail to induce robust anti-M2e antibody responses, and protection mediated by M2e-specific antibodies does not involve direct virus neutralization. Instead, these antibodies exert their protective effect by engaging immune effector mechanisms—particularly ADCC—through Fc receptor-bearing cells such as natural killer (NK) cells, macrophages, and neutrophils, leading to the elimination of virus-infected cells before progeny virions are released.

Several studies have demonstrated the prophylactic efficacy of M2e-specific monoclonal antibodies in murine models of lethal influenza challenge. For instance, murine and chimeric antibodies such as 14C2 have been shown to reduce viral load and improve survival when administered prior to infection. More recently, fully human M2e-specific antibodies have been generated using transgenic mouse platforms or phage display libraries, with some showing promising activity in vivo. Notably, the human antibody Z3G1, isolated from transchromosomic mice, was reported to confer therapeutic benefit in mice, although it required multiple doses initiated within hours of infection to achieve full protection. Despite these advances, no naturally occurring human M2e-specific monoclonal antibodies—i.e., those derived directly from human B cells without artificial engineering or immunization—have been previously described in the scientific literature. Natural human antibodies are generally preferred for clinical development because they have undergone endogenous somatic hypermutation and selection in the human immune system, resulting in high affinity, optimal biophysical properties, and minimal risk of immunogenicity or cross-reactivity.

Traditional methods for isolating human monoclonal antibodies include single B cell sorting followed by RT-PCR amplification of immunoglobulin genes, Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)-mediated immortalization of memory B cells, and combinatorial library approaches such as phage or yeast display. While effective in certain contexts, these techniques often suffer from limitations including low throughput, biased representation of heavy-light chain pairings, poor expression yields, or the need for extensive in vitro affinity maturation. To address these challenges, the inventors recently developed a novel mammalian cell display platform based on Sindbis virus vectors, which enables the rapid isolation of functional antibody fragments while preserving native heavy and light chain combinations from antigen-specific B cells. This method involves the construction of scFv libraries from sorted B cells, surface expression of scFv variants on mammalian cells via Sindbis-mediated transduction, and fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) to recover clones with desired binding specificity. The platform has been successfully applied to isolate high-affinity human antibodies against various antigens, including virus-like particles and small molecules.

In the context of influenza, prior art has not disclosed the successful isolation of natural human monoclonal antibodies specific for M2e using this or any comparable method, nor has it demonstrated both potent prophylactic and therapeutic efficacy of such antibodies in a single-dose regimen in an established animal model. Furthermore, existing reports have not provided evidence of cross-reactivity with M2e from highly pathogenic avian influenza strains such as H5N1, which is critical for pandemic preparedness. The present invention fills these gaps by describing, for the first time, naturally derived human monoclonal antibodies that bind M2e with nanomolar affinity, recognize native M2 on infected cells, cross-react with divergent influenza A subtypes, and provide robust protection in mice even when administered after the onset of infection. These attributes distinguish the claimed antibodies from all previously known M2e-targeting agents and establish a new standard for influenza immunotherapeutics.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides natural human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the extracellular domain of the matrix protein 2 (M2e) of Influenza A virus with high affinity and demonstrate potent prophylactic and therapeutic activity in vivo. These antibodies are derived from human B cells of an individual with naturally elevated anti-M2e serum titers and were isolated using a Sindbis virus-based mammalian cell display platform that preserves native heavy and light chain pairings. The invention encompasses three exemplary antibodies—designated D005, E040, and F052—which bind the consensus M2e peptide (M2e-cons) with dissociation constants (Kd) in the range of 4–5 nM and exhibit equivalent reactivity toward M2e derived from a recent H5N1 strain (A/VN/1203/2004), indicating broad cross-reactivity across influenza A subtypes. Critically, these antibodies recognize native, full-length M2 protein expressed on the surface of infected mammalian cells, a prerequisite for mediating antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), which is the primary mechanism of protection conferred by anti-M2e antibodies.

In a murine model of lethal Influenza A infection, a single intraperitoneal administration of 500 μg of any of the three antibodies two days prior to viral challenge conferred complete protection against morbidity and mortality induced by a 4× LD50 dose of mouse-adapted A/PR/8/34 (H1N1). Remarkably, dose-titration studies revealed that as little as 20 μg of antibody D005 was sufficient to ensure 100% survival, and even 6 μg protected 50% of animals, underscoring its exceptional potency. Furthermore, D005 demonstrated significant therapeutic efficacy when administered up to three days post-infection: a single 200 μg dose given one or two days after challenge resulted in near-complete survival, outperforming previously reported M2e-specific antibodies that required multiple early injections for comparable protection. To facilitate clinical translation, D005 was reformatted as a fully human IgG1κ antibody and produced at high yields (>70 mg/L) in HEK-293T cells using an Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)-based episomal expression system. This IgG1 version retained full protective activity in mice, confirming its ability to engage murine Fcγ receptors and validating its potential for human use.

The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising these antibodies, either as scFv-Fc fusion proteins or as full-length IgG molecules, formulated for parenteral administration. Methods for producing the antibodies are also disclosed, involving the isolation of M2e-specific B cells from human peripheral blood, construction of κ- and λ-light chain scFv libraries in a Sindbis display vector, FACS-based screening for M2e binders, and recombinant expression in mammalian cells followed by protein A affinity purification. The antibodies may be used for passive immunization in both prophylactic and therapeutic contexts, particularly in high-risk populations such as the elderly, infants, and immunocompromised individuals who respond poorly to conventional vaccines. Due to the extreme conservation of M2e across human and zoonotic influenza A strains—including seasonal H1N1/H3N2 and avian H5N1/H7N9—the antibodies offer a universal strategy against current and future influenza pandemics. The combination of natural human origin, high affinity, broad cross-reactivity, single-dose efficacy, and dual prophylactic/therapeutic utility represents a significant advancement over existing influenza immunotherapeutics and establishes a new paradigm for antiviral antibody development.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### EXAMPLE 1

The isolation of M2e-specific human monoclonal antibodies began with the identification of a human donor exhibiting high serum titers of antibodies reactive to the consensus M2e peptide (M2e-cons). Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were collected from 10 mL of heparinized whole blood via Ficoll density gradient centrifugation. To enrich for antigen-experienced B cells, PBMCs were pre-incubated with Alexa Fluor 647-labeled Qβ virus-like particles and human gamma globulin to block non-specific binding sites. Subsequently, cells were stained with a multi-parameter antibody cocktail designed to identify M2e-specific B lymphocytes. Specifically, M2e-specific B cells were labeled using M2e-cons conjugated to Qβ particles in combination with an Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated mouse monoclonal antibody specific for Qβ, thereby enabling detection of B cell receptors that bound the M2e-Qβ complex. Additionally, cells were co-stained with the well-characterized murine anti-M2e monoclonal antibody 14C2, followed by a FITC-conjugated donkey anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody, to confirm M2e specificity through an orthogonal detection method. To exclude non-B cell populations, cells were simultaneously labeled with phycoerythrin (PE)-conjugated antibodies against human IgM, IgD, CD14 (monocyte marker), and CD3 (T cell marker), and with PE-Texas Red-conjugated anti-CD19 antibody to positively identify B cells. Flow cytometric analysis on a FACSVantage® SE instrument allowed for the precise gating of live, single, CD19-positive, IgM/IgD-negative (indicative of class-switched memory B cells), CD14/CD3-negative, and M2e-binding (FL1-positive, FL2-negative, FL3-positive, FL4-negative) cells. From this stringent gating strategy, 334 M2e-specific memory B cells were individually sorted into PCR plates for downstream immunoglobulin gene amplification. Total RNA was extracted from these single cells, and reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was performed using primers specific for the variable regions of human immunoglobulin heavy chains (VH) and light chains (VL), separately amplifying κ and λ light chain transcripts. The amplified VH and VL fragments were then assembled into single-chain variable fragment (scFv) constructs by overlap extension PCR, wherein the VH and VL domains were linked by a flexible (Gly4Ser)3 peptide linker. Two independent scFv libraries were constructed—one containing κ light chains and the other λ light chains—and each was cloned into the Sindbis virus-based mammalian cell surface display vector pDel-SP-TM, which directs the expression of scFv fused to a transmembrane domain for stable anchoring on the plasma membrane of infected cells. The resulting scFv-κ and scFv-λ sublibraries comprised approximately 1.0 × 10⁶ and 1.1 × 10⁶ independent bacterial transformants, respectively, ensuring comprehensive coverage of all possible heavy-light chain pairings derived from the original 334 B cells. Recombinant Sindbis virus particles were then generated by in vitro transcription and electroporation into BHK cells, yielding high-titer viral stocks (3.4 × 10⁷ and 5.7 × 10⁷ pfu/mL, respectively). These viral libraries were used to infect fresh BHK cells at a low multiplicity of infection (MOI < 0.1) to ensure that the majority of infected cells expressed only a single scFv variant on their surface, a critical requirement for accurate clonal screening.

### EXAMPLE 2

Following infection of BHK cells with the Sindbis scFv libraries, cell surface expression of M2e-specific scFv antibodies was assessed by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS). Cells infected with the scFv-λ sublibrary showed no detectable binding to M2e, indicating that the donor’s M2e-specific response was exclusively mediated by κ light chain-containing antibodies. In contrast, the scFv-κ sublibrary yielded a distinct population of M2e-reactive cells, representing approximately 0.3% of the total infected cell population. To isolate individual M2e-specific clones, three independent FACS sorts were performed: two using Qβ-M2e-cons as the detection antigen and one using RNase A-conjugated M2e-cons (RNase-M2e-cons). In the latter approach, BHK cells displaying scFv on their surface were incubated with RNase-M2e-cons, followed by a rabbit polyclonal anti-RNase A antibody and a FITC-conjugated donkey anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody, enabling fluorescent detection of M2e-binding scFv. A total of 255 single BHK cells exhibiting strong M2e-specific fluorescence were sorted into individual wells of 24-well plates containing a confluent layer of uninfected BHK feeder cells to support viral replication and spread. After 2–3 days of incubation, cytopathic effects indicative of Sindbis virus propagation were observed in 201 of the 255 wells, suggesting successful amplification of individual viral clones. Supernatants from these wells were then subjected to a secondary FACS screen to confirm M2e binding using RNase-M2e-cons as the probe. Of the 201 wells, 130 displayed varying degrees of M2e-specific staining, confirming the presence of functional scFv antibodies. From the wells exhibiting the highest fluorescence intensity—indicative of strong M2e binding—supernatants were harvested for RT-PCR amplification of the corresponding scFv coding sequences. The amplified scFv genes were sequenced to verify identity and fidelity, revealing a diverse set of unique clones with distinct heavy and light chain combinations. This high recovery rate of specific antibodies from a single screen underscores the efficiency and sensitivity of the Sindbis mammalian cell display platform, which not only captures rare antigen-specific B cell clones but also maintains the natural pairing of immunoglobulin chains, a feature often lost in traditional hybridoma or phage display methods. The ability to isolate over 100 functional M2e-specific scFv variants from just 334 input B cells demonstrates the robustness of the approach and its suitability for rapid antibody discovery from human donors with naturally acquired immunity.

### EXAMPLE 3

To enable in vivo evaluation of the isolated scFv antibodies in a murine influenza model, the scFv fragments were genetically fused to the Fc region of mouse IgG2c (msFc-γ2c) to generate bivalent, effector-competent molecules capable of mediating antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). The scFv-msFc-γ2c fusion constructs were cloned into the episomal mammalian expression vector pCEP-SP-Sfi-msFc-γ2c, a derivative of pCEP4 that contains the Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) origin of replication (oriP) and the EBNA-1 gene to support high-level, stable expression in HEK-293T cells without genomic integration. Transient transfection of HEK-293T cells with these plasmids using Lipofectamine Plus reagent resulted in robust secretion of the fusion proteins into the culture supernatant. Initial binding activity was confirmed by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) using supernatants from transfected cells, which showed specific reactivity to RNase-M2e-cons-coated plates. Based on expression levels and binding intensity, three lead candidates—D005, E040, and F052—were selected for large-scale production. Stable pools of HEK-293T cells expressing each antibody were enriched by puromycin selection (1 μg/mL) and maintained in serum-free medium on Poly-L-Lysine-coated culture vessels for up to three weeks. Culture supernatants were harvested twice weekly, filtered through 0.22 μm sterile filters, and purified by affinity chromatography using protein A-Sepharose columns, which bind the Fc region with high specificity. Yields ranged from 20 to 40 mg of purified antibody per liter of culture supernatant, consistent with previous reports using this expression system for other human antibodies. The purified scFv-msFc-γ2c fusion proteins were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and size-exclusion chromatography to confirm homogeneity and correct assembly. All three antibodies migrated as single bands under reducing conditions, corresponding to the expected molecular weight of ~75 kDa for the scFv-Fc monomer, and formed stable dimers under non-reducing conditions, confirming proper disulfide bonding. These high expression yields and favorable biophysical properties indicate that the antibodies isolated via mammalian cell display are inherently well-suited for scalable manufacturing, a critical attribute for clinical development.

### EXAMPLE 4

The binding affinity of the three lead antibodies—D005, E040, and F052—for the consensus M2e peptide (M2e-cons) was quantified using a solution-phase competitive ELISA adapted from the Friguet method. In this assay, a fixed concentration of each scFv-msFc-γ2c antibody (10 ng/mL) was pre-incubated with serial dilutions of free M2e-cons peptide ranging from 10 nM to 0.17 pM in PBS containing 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA). After a two-hour equilibration period at room temperature, the mixtures were transferred to ELISA plates pre-coated with RNase-M2e-cons conjugate (20 ng/mL) and incubated for an additional 30 minutes to allow binding of uncomplexed antibody to the immobilized antigen. Following extensive washing, bound antibody was detected using an HRP-conjugated, Fcγ-specific goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody and developed with ortho-phenylenediamine dihydrochloride (OPD) substrate. The optical density at 450 nm was measured, and the dissociation constant (Kd) was determined as the concentration of free M2e-cons peptide that reduced the ELISA signal by 50% (EC50). All three antibodies exhibited remarkably similar affinities, with Kd values of approximately 4–5 nM, indicating high-affinity interactions with the target epitope. This nanomolar affinity is consistent with antibodies that have undergone physiological affinity maturation in vivo, supporting the notion that these are naturally evolved, high-quality binders rather than in vitro-engineered variants requiring optimization. The uniformity in affinity across the three clones suggests that the donor’s immune response converged on a dominant, high-affinity solution for M2e recognition, possibly constrained by the limited structural flexibility of the short, linear M2e epitope.

### EXAMPLE 5

To assess the breadth of reactivity of the isolated antibodies, their ability to cross-bind M2e derived from a highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) strain—A/Vietnam/1203/2004 (M2e-VN)—was evaluated by direct ELISA. ELISA plates were coated with either RNase-M2e-cons or RNase-M2e-VN at 4 μg/mL, and serial dilutions of purified scFv-msFc-γ2c antibodies were applied. Both peptides differ by only a few amino acids from the consensus sequence, reflecting natural variation among circulating strains. Strikingly, all three antibodies—D005, E040, and F052—bound M2e-VN with EC50 values of 5–10 ng/mL (equivalent to 45–90 pM), virtually identical to their reactivity with M2e-cons. This demonstrates that the antibodies recognize a highly conserved core epitope within M2e that is preserved even in zoonotic strains with pandemic potential. Such cross-reactivity is essential for a universal influenza therapeutic, as it ensures efficacy against both seasonal human-adapted viruses and emerging avian or swine-origin strains that may cause future outbreaks. The data confirm that the antibodies do not rely on variable residues for binding and are therefore unlikely to be evaded by antigenic drift in the M2e domain, a key advantage over HA-targeting antibodies.

### EXAMPLE 6

The capacity of the antibodies to recognize native, conformationally intact M2 protein expressed on the surface of influenza-infected cells was tested using a stably transfected L929 murine fibroblast cell line (L929-M2#E9) that constitutively expresses full-length M2 derived from A/PR/8/34 (H1N1). These cells were stained with increasing concentrations of D005, E040, or F052 scFv-msFc-γ2c antibodies, followed by a fluorescently labeled anti-mouse Fc secondary antibody, and analyzed by flow cytometry. A clear, concentration-dependent increase in mean fluorescence intensity was observed for all three antibodies, confirming their ability to bind cell-surface M2 in its native tetrameric ion channel conformation. Control L929 cells lacking M2 expression showed no staining above background, verifying specificity. The binding curves were nearly superimposable, again reflecting similar affinities and epitope recognition. This functional validation is critical because protection by anti-M2e antibodies depends entirely on their ability to opsonize infected cells for ADCC; antibodies that bind only denatured or peptide forms of M2e would be therapeutically irrelevant. The demonstration that D005, E040, and F052 engage native M2 confirms their biological relevance and mechanism of action.

### EXAMPLE 7

The prophylactic efficacy of the antibodies was evaluated in a standard murine model of lethal influenza A infection. Six-week-old female C57BL/6 mice (n=6 per group) were administered 500 μg of D005, E040, or F052 scFv-msFc-γ2c via intraperitoneal injection. A control group received an equivalent dose of irrelevant mouse IgG. Two days later, all animals were challenged intranasally with 4× LD50 of mouse-adapted A/PR/8/34 virus. Mice were monitored twice daily for weight loss, body temperature, and clinical signs of disease. Control animals exhibited rapid onset of severe symptoms, including >20% weight loss and hypothermia (core temperature <32°C), and were euthanized between days 7 and 8 post-infection in accordance with humane endpoints. In stark contrast, all mice treated with any of the three M2e-specific antibodies survived the entire 21-day observation period, maintained normal body weight (with only transient, minor fluctuations), and showed no signs of fever or distress. These results demonstrate that a single prophylactic dose of these antibodies provides complete protection against a lethal influenza challenge, validating their potential as pre-exposure prophylaxis for high-risk individuals or during outbreak scenarios.

### EXAMPLE 8

To define the minimal protective dose, a titration study was conducted with antibody D005. Groups of mice received 200, 60, 20, or 6 μg of scFv-msFc-γ2c D005, or 200 μg of control mouse IgG, two days before lethal viral challenge. All control mice succumbed to infection by day 12. In the treatment groups, 100% survival was achieved with doses of 20 μg or higher. Even at the lowest dose of 6 μg, 50% of mice survived, indicating a steep dose-response relationship and exceptional potency. Serum antibody levels measured one day post-administration correlated with protection, confirming pharmacokinetic exposure as the determinant of efficacy. This low effective dose is advantageous for clinical use, as it reduces manufacturing burden and cost. In parallel, the therapeutic window was explored by administering a single 200 μg dose of D005 at 1, 2, or 3 days post-infection. Prophylactic (day -2) and control (irrelevant IgG) groups were included for comparison. All prophylactically treated mice survived, while all controls died. Therapeutically, 100% survival was observed when D005 was given at day 1 or 2 post-infection, and partial protection (prolonged survival) was seen at day 3. This extends the utility of D005 beyond prevention to early treatment, a significant advance over prior M2e antibodies that required multiple early doses.

### EXAMPLE 9

For clinical translation, D005 was reformatted as a fully human IgG1κ antibody. The variable region genes were synthesized de novo and cloned into the EBV-based expression vector pCB15, which supports high-yield IgG production in HEK-293T cells. Transient transfection followed by puromycin selection yielded stable pools secreting IgG1-D005 at ~70 mg/L—more than double the yield of the scFv-Fc format. The purified IgG1 was tested head-to-head with scFv-msFc-γ2c D005 in the murine challenge model using equimolar dosing. Both formats conferred 100% protection against lethal infection, with identical profiles of weight stability and absence of fever. This confirms that the human Fc domain effectively engages murine Fcγ receptors to mediate ADCC, and strongly predicts efficacy in humans. The high expression yield, natural human sequence, and dual prophylactic/therapeutic activity position IgG1-D005 as a leading candidate for clinical development as a universal influenza immunotherapeutic.
