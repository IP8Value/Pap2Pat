## BACKGROUND

Underwater acoustic source localization and tracking are critical capabilities for a wide range of naval, environmental monitoring, and scientific applications. Passive sonar systems, which rely on arrays of hydrophones to detect and analyze acoustic signals without emitting energy themselves, offer a stealthy and effective means for monitoring underwater activity. However, the accurate localization and tracking of acoustic sources in underwater environments present significant technical challenges due to the complex physics of sound propagation in water, particularly in shallow-water scenarios where multipath effects create intricate interference patterns that can severely degrade localization performance.

Traditional approaches to underwater source localization have primarily relied on matched-field processing (MFP) techniques, which compare measured acoustic field data against a library of predicted acoustic field patterns (replicas) generated using an acoustic propagation model of the environment. These replicas are computed for a grid of potential source locations, and the measured data is correlated against each replica to produce an ambiguity surface that indicates the likelihood of a source being present at each grid point. While MFP has demonstrated success in controlled environments with accurate environmental models, its performance degrades significantly in the presence of environmental model mismatch, multiple simultaneous sources, or when the signal-to-noise ratio is low.

The extension of MFP to tracking applications, known as matched-field tracking (MFT), typically involves generating a sequence of ambiguity surfaces over time and then connecting peaks across consecutive surfaces to form source trajectories. This approach, however, suffers from several fundamental limitations. First, the computational complexity grows combinatorially with the number of detected peaks in each ambiguity surface, making real-time implementation impractical for scenarios with multiple sources or high peak densities. Second, MFT algorithms typically operate in batch mode, requiring the collection of multiple ambiguity surfaces before track formation can begin, which precludes true online tracking capabilities. Third, these methods generally do not incorporate prior information about source dynamics or previous localization estimates into the ambiguity surface generation process itself, treating each time instance independently and thus failing to leverage the temporal continuity inherent in most source trajectories.

More recent approaches have attempted to address these limitations through Bayesian frameworks that jointly estimate source parameters and track trajectories. However, these methods often rely on computationally intensive Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) techniques or maintain high-dimensional state spaces that become prohibitively expensive as the localization grid resolution increases. Sparsity-driven approaches have also been explored, recognizing that in a sufficiently fine localization grid, only a small number of grid points correspond to actual source locations, resulting in a sparse representation of the source distribution. While promising, direct application of these sparsity-based methods to underwater acoustic tracking has been limited by the high dimensionality of the problem and the computational burden of maintaining covariance matrices for large state vectors.

The fundamental challenge in underwater acoustic source tracking lies in developing a method that can effectively balance computational efficiency with robustness to environmental uncertainties while providing real-time tracking capabilities for multiple sources. Existing approaches either sacrifice computational tractability for accuracy (as in comprehensive MFT implementations) or compromise on tracking performance to achieve reasonable computational requirements (as in simplified peak-tracking methods). Furthermore, the broadband nature of many acoustic sources presents additional opportunities for improved localization performance through coherent processing across multiple frequency bands, but existing methods have not fully exploited this potential in a computationally efficient tracking framework.

What is needed is a novel approach to underwater acoustic source tracking that leverages the inherent sparsity of the source localization problem, incorporates temporal dependencies between consecutive time instances to enable true online tracking, provides robustness to environmental model mismatch, and maintains computational efficiency suitable for real-time implementation. The present invention addresses these needs through a sparsity-driven framework that constructs source localization maps (SLMs) with enforced coherence across frequency bands and temporal continuity between consecutive time instances, enabling accurate and efficient tracking of multiple acoustic sources in complex underwater environments.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF SOME EMBODIMENTS

The present invention provides a novel sparsity-driven framework for broadband source localization and tracking via passive sonar that overcomes the limitations of existing approaches by simultaneously addressing computational efficiency, robustness to model mismatch, and real-time tracking capabilities. The core innovation lies in the formulation of a regularized optimization problem that explicitly incorporates both the group sparsity structure inherent in broadband source localization and the temporal dependency between consecutive source localization maps (SLMs).

In accordance with the invention, the underwater acoustic tracking system comprises an array of N hydrophones deployed in the underwater environment to collect acoustic pressure measurements. The hydrophone array receives acoustic signals from K broadband acoustic sources, where K may be unknown a priori. The acoustic time series data collected by each hydrophone is transformed to the frequency domain using a discrete-time Fourier transform, resulting in frequency-domain measurements at F distinct frequencies {ω_f}_{f=1}^F. These frequency-domain measurements are organized into measurement vectors y_f(t) ∈ ℂ^N for each frequency f and time instance t, where the n-th component y_{n,f}(t) represents the Fourier coefficient estimate corresponding to frequency ω_f at time t obtained from the n-th hydrophone.

A critical component of the invention is the acoustic propagation model, which characterizes the underwater environment and enables the computation of model-predicted acoustic field patterns (replicas) for any potential source location. The propagation model takes into account environmental parameters such as water depth, sound speed profile, seabed properties, and other relevant characteristics of the underwater environment. Using this model, normalized replicas p_{g,f} ∈ ℂ^N are computed for each grid point r_g in a predefined localization grid G = {r_g}_{g=1}^G and each frequency ω_f, where the normalization ensures that ||p_{g,f}||_2 = 1. The localization grid G spans the region of interest in the underwater environment and typically includes dimensions for range, depth, and potentially azimuth, depending on the specific application requirements.

The invention formulates the source localization problem as a sparse recovery problem where the goal is to estimate a matrix S(t) ∈ ℂ^{G×F} whose entries s_{g,f}(t) represent the complex amplitude of a hypothetical source located at grid point r_g transmitting at frequency ω_f at time t. Due to the physical reality that only K << G grid points correspond to actual source locations, the matrix S(t) exhibits a sparse structure where most rows contain only zero entries. Specifically, only those rows corresponding to actual source locations contain non-zero values across the frequency dimension, while all other rows are identically zero. This row-wise sparsity structure is referred to as group sparsity, where each group corresponds to a row of S(t) representing a potential source location across all frequencies.

The key innovation of the present invention is the formulation of an optimization problem that simultaneously enforces this group sparsity structure and incorporates temporal continuity between consecutive time instances. At each time instance t, the estimate Ŝ(t) is obtained by solving the following regularized least squares regression problem:

min_S ∑_{f=1}^F ||y_f(t) - P_f s_f(t)||_2^2 + μ ∑_{g=1}^G ||ς_g(t)||_2 + (λ/2) ∑_{f=1}^F ||s_f(t) - ŝ_f(t-1)||_2^2

where P_f = [p_{1,f}, ..., p_{G,f}] ∈ ℂ^{N×G} is the matrix of replicas for frequency ω_f, s_f(t) ∈ ℂ^G is the f-th column of S(t) representing the source amplitudes at frequency ω_f, ς_g(t) ∈ ℂ^F is the g-th row of S(t) representing the source amplitudes at grid point r_g across all frequencies, μ > 0 is a regularization parameter controlling the degree of group sparsity, and λ > 0 is a regularization parameter controlling the emphasis placed on the previous estimate Ŝ(t-1) when computing the current estimate Ŝ(t).

The first term in the objective function represents the data fidelity term, ensuring that the estimated source amplitudes produce replicas that closely match the measured acoustic data. The second term, scaled by μ, is a group lasso penalty that encourages group sparsity by penalizing the l2-norm of each row ς_g(t). This penalty effectively promotes solutions where entire rows of S(t) are driven to zero, corresponding to the physical reality that most grid points do not contain actual sources. The third term, scaled by λ, is a temporal regularization term that encourages the current estimate to remain close to the previous estimate, thereby incorporating temporal continuity and enabling tracking of moving sources.

To facilitate efficient computation, the complex-valued optimization problem is converted to an equivalent real-valued convex optimization problem by representing all complex variables as the direct sum of their real and imaginary parts. Specifically, the measurement vectors are transformed as ỹ_f(t) = [Re{y_f(t)}^T, Im{y_f(t)}^T]^T, the source amplitude vectors as s̃_f = [Re(s_f)^T, Im(s_f)^T]^T, and the replica matrices as P̃_f = [Re(P_f), -Im(P_f); Im(P_f), Re(P_f)]. The resulting real-valued optimization problem maintains the same structural properties as the original complex-valued problem while enabling the application of standard convex optimization techniques.

The invention employs a proximal gradient (PG) algorithm to solve the resulting convex optimization problem efficiently. The PG algorithm is particularly well-suited for this problem because it can exploit the problem's structure to obtain closed-form updates while maintaining computational efficiency. The algorithm iteratively solves a sequence of simpler subproblems that approximate the original objective function using a quadratic majorizer, combined with the non-differentiable group sparsity penalty.

At each iteration i of the PG algorithm, the update for each row v_g(t) (which represents the concatenated real and imaginary parts of ς_g(t)) is computed as:

v_g^{[i]}(t) = (1 - (μ/L_h)/||w_g^{[i-1]}(t)||_2)_+ w_g^{[i-1]}(t)

where w_g^{[i-1]}(t) represents a gradient descent step for the g-th row, L_h is the Lipschitz constant of the gradient of the differentiable portion of the objective function, and (·)_+ = max{0, ·}. This closed-form update, known as the proximal operator of the group lasso penalty, effectively performs soft thresholding on the gradient descent step, driving small components to zero while preserving larger components.

The PG algorithm is implemented as follows: First, the algorithm initializes the estimate using the previous time instance's solution Ŝ(t-1). Then, for each iteration i, the algorithm computes the gradient of the differentiable portion of the objective function with respect to each row v_g(t). This gradient computation requires O(NGF) scalar operations per iteration, which is computationally feasible even for large grids due to the algorithm's parallel structure. The algorithm then applies the proximal operator to each row independently, which can be performed in parallel across all grid points, further enhancing computational efficiency.

The algorithm terminates when the relative change in the estimate between consecutive iterations falls below a predetermined threshold, typically on the order of 10^-5. While the theoretical worst-case convergence rate of the basic PG algorithm is O(1/i), empirical observations indicate that the support of the solution (i.e., the set of non-zero rows corresponding to source locations) can be correctly identified using relatively few iterations, typically around 50. Furthermore, accelerated variants of the PG algorithm with improved convergence rates of O(1/i^2) can be employed when higher accuracy is required.

An important aspect of the invention is its inherent robustness to environmental model mismatch. The temporal regularization term scaled by λ not only provides temporal continuity but also induces resilience to model mismatch through its mathematical structure. Specifically, the terms λ/2 ||s_f(t)||_2^2 in the regularizer are known to provide robustness to model mismatch by effectively accounting for random perturbations affecting each replica. From this perspective, the parameter λ corresponds to the variance of random perturbations affecting the replicas due to mismatch between the true propagation environment and the model used to generate them. As the model mismatch increases and the reliability of the replicas decreases, the optimization problem naturally steers its attention toward the prior SLM estimates captured by the previous solution Ŝ(t-1), thereby maintaining tracking performance even in the presence of significant environmental uncertainties.

The invention constructs broadband source localization maps (SLMs) by computing the l2-norm of each row ς_g(t) across all frequencies, resulting in a scalar value ||ς_g(t)||_2 for each grid point r_g. These values are then plotted as a function of the corresponding grid coordinates to produce the SLM, where peaks in the map correspond to likely source locations. Source location estimates are obtained by identifying the K largest peaks in the broadband SLM, where K may be determined adaptively based on the observed peak magnitudes or specified a priori based on operational requirements.

For tracking applications, the invention processes the acoustic data sequentially in real-time, updating the SLM at each time instance using the PG algorithm described above. The temporal regularization term ensures that the estimated source locations evolve smoothly over time, enabling the formation of continuous tracks without the need for post-processing peak association algorithms. This represents a significant advantage over traditional MFT approaches, which require separate track formation steps after ambiguity surface generation.

The invention has been validated using real-world data from the third Shallow-Water Evaluation Cell Experiment (SWellEX-3), which involved a towed broadband source transmitting at 10 frequencies and a vertical line array with 64 hydrophones. In experimental evaluations, the invention demonstrated superior performance compared to traditional MFT approaches, particularly in scenarios involving multiple sources where the combinatorial complexity of MFT becomes prohibitive. The invention successfully tracked a single towed source over extended time periods and demonstrated the ability to resolve two simulated sources by combining different segments of the SWellEX-3 dataset.

The computational efficiency of the invention makes it particularly suitable for real-time implementation on embedded systems or other resource-constrained platforms. The parallel structure of the PG algorithm enables efficient implementation on modern multi-core processors or graphics processing units (GPUs), while the relatively low computational complexity per iteration allows for high update rates suitable for tracking fast-moving sources. Additionally, the invention's robustness to model mismatch reduces the need for precise environmental characterization, making it more practical for operational deployment in uncertain or changing underwater environments.

Several implementation considerations enhance the practical utility of the invention. First, the regularization parameters μ and λ can be adapted dynamically based on the observed data characteristics or tracking performance metrics. For instance, μ can be adjusted to control the number of detected sources, while λ can be modified based on the estimated source velocity or environmental uncertainty. Second, the localization grid can be refined adaptively around detected source locations to improve localization accuracy without significantly increasing computational complexity. Third, the invention can be extended to incorporate additional physical constraints, such as maximum source velocity or depth constraints, by modifying the temporal regularization term or incorporating additional penalty terms in the optimization problem.

The invention represents a significant advancement in underwater acoustic source tracking technology by providing a unified framework that addresses the key challenges of computational efficiency, robustness to model mismatch, and real-time tracking capabilities. By leveraging the inherent sparsity of the source localization problem and incorporating temporal dependencies through a carefully designed regularization scheme, the invention enables accurate and efficient tracking of multiple acoustic sources in complex underwater environments, making it particularly valuable for naval surveillance, environmental monitoring, and scientific research applications.
