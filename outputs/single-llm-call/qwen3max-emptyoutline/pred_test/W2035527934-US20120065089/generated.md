# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of medical diagnostics, specifically to methods and systems for the detection and diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma (CC), including intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), using glycan-based biomarkers present in biological fluids, particularly bile. More precisely, the invention relates to a novel diagnostic assay that exploits the specific recognition of Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA)-reactive glycans carried on sialylated MUC1 glycoproteins, which are differentially expressed in cholangiocarcinoma compared to benign biliary conditions or normal tissue. The invention further encompasses kits, reagents, and analytical platforms designed to implement this diagnostic method, as well as the use of WFA lectin in combination with monoclonal antibody MY.1E12 for the selective capture and detection of disease-associated glycoforms of MUC1 in bile specimens. This technology falls within the broader domain of glycomics and precision oncology, where alterations in protein glycosylation serve as sensitive and specific indicators of malignant transformation in epithelial tissues, especially those of biliary origin. The invention is particularly relevant to clinical pathology, hepatobiliary surgery, and gastroenterology, offering a non-invasive or minimally invasive tool for early cancer detection, differential diagnosis between ICC and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), and monitoring of high-risk patients with chronic biliary diseases such as hepatolithiasis. By leveraging the unique glycosylation signature of cholangiocarcinoma—characterized by elevated expression of terminal N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) residues recognized by WFA—the invention provides a significant improvement over existing serological markers like CA19-9 and conventional cytological examination of bile, both of which suffer from inadequate sensitivity and specificity in real-world clinical settings.

## BACKGROUND ART

Cholangiocarcinoma (CC), a malignancy arising from the epithelial cells of the biliary tree, remains one of the most challenging cancers to diagnose at an early and potentially curable stage. Despite advances in imaging modalities such as magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) and endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), definitive diagnosis often requires histopathological confirmation, which may be delayed or inconclusive due to the fibrotic and desmoplastic nature of the tumor or insufficient tissue sampling. Current standard diagnostic approaches include serum tumor markers, notably carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9), and cytological analysis of bile or brushings obtained during ERCP. However, CA19-9 exhibits poor sensitivity, particularly in early-stage disease, and can be elevated in numerous benign conditions such as cholangitis, pancreatitis, and obstructive jaundice, leading to false-positive results. Biliary cytology, while specific when positive, suffers from extremely low sensitivity—often below 50%—due to scant cellularity, degenerative changes, and difficulty distinguishing reactive atypia from true malignancy. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need for more reliable, sensitive, and specific biomarkers that can facilitate timely and accurate diagnosis of CC, especially in patients presenting with indeterminate biliary strictures or chronic inflammatory biliary diseases known to predispose to malignancy, such as primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) and hepatolithiasis.

In parallel, research into the molecular underpinnings of cancer has increasingly highlighted aberrant protein glycosylation as a hallmark of malignant transformation. Glycosylation, the enzymatic process by which glycans are attached to proteins or lipids, is highly regulated in normal cells but becomes dysregulated in cancer, resulting in the expression of truncated, sialylated, or otherwise altered glycan structures on cell surface and secreted glycoproteins. These cancer-associated glycans can serve as potent biomarkers because they are often shed into body fluids and can be detected using lectins—carbohydrate-binding proteins—or glycan-specific antibodies. Among the various glycoproteins implicated in biliary tract cancers, mucin 1 (MUC1) has garnered significant attention. MUC1 is a transmembrane mucin normally expressed on the apical surface of ductal epithelial cells, including those of the biliary tree. In cancer, MUC1 undergoes both overexpression and hypoglycosylation, exposing peptide epitopes and cryptic glycan structures that are immunogenic and functionally relevant to tumor progression, invasion, and immune evasion. Specifically, sialylated forms of the Tn and T antigens (sialyl-Tn and sialyl-T) on MUC1 have been associated with poor prognosis in multiple carcinomas, including CC.

Monoclonal antibody MY.1E12, developed and characterized in prior studies, specifically recognizes sialylated MUC1 carrying the sialyl-T antigen (Neu5Acα2-3Galβ1-3GalNAcα1-O-Ser/Thr). This epitope is minimally expressed in normal biliary epithelium but is markedly upregulated in cholangiocarcinoma, making MY.1E12 a valuable immunohistochemical and serological probe for CC. However, the utility of MY.1E12 alone in fluid-based diagnostics has been limited by the heterogeneity of MUC1 glycoforms and the presence of non-cancer-associated sialylated MUC1 in inflammatory conditions. Thus, strategies to enrich or selectively detect only the cancer-specific glycoforms of MUC1 are essential to improve diagnostic performance.

Lectin microarray technology, which enables high-throughput profiling of glycan structures in complex biological samples, has emerged as a powerful tool for discovering disease-specific glycosylation signatures. In prior work, differential glycan profiling of formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections containing both intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) and adjacent normal bile duct epithelium (BDE) revealed that Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA)—a lectin with specificity for terminal α- or β-linked N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) residues, commonly found in Tn antigen (GalNAcα1-O-Ser/Thr)—exhibited the most pronounced and statistically significant increase in binding to ICC compared to normal BDE. This finding was corroborated by histochemical staining, which demonstrated strong and frequent WFA reactivity in ICC tumor cells but minimal or absent staining in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) or normal hepatocytes, suggesting a high degree of tissue and disease specificity. Moreover, WFA staining effectively discriminated ICC components from HCC components in combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma (HCC-ICC), a rare but clinically important tumor type where accurate subtyping is crucial for determining appropriate therapy, as HCC and ICC respond differently to treatment modalities such as radiofrequency ablation or chemotherapy.

Despite these promising observations, translating WFA-based detection into a practical clinical diagnostic test required overcoming several challenges. First, while WFA reactivity is elevated in ICC tissue, it is unclear whether this glycan signature is faithfully reflected in easily accessible body fluids like bile or serum. Second, bile is a complex mixture containing proteins, lipids, bilirubin, and cellular debris, which can interfere with immunoassays. Third, benign biliary diseases such as hepatolithiasis, common bile duct stones, and chronic cholangitis may also induce glycosylation changes that mimic those seen in cancer, potentially leading to false positives. Therefore, a diagnostic strategy that combines the glycan specificity of WFA with the protein backbone specificity of an antibody like MY.1E12 could theoretically achieve higher selectivity by requiring dual recognition: the presence of both the WFA-reactive glycan and the sialylated MUC1 carrier protein. Such a heterogeneous sandwich assay would target only those MUC1 molecules that are both sialylated (as recognized by MY.1E12) and decorated with terminal GalNAc residues (as captured by WFA)—a combination that appears to be uniquely enriched in cholangiocarcinoma.

Prior attempts to develop glycan-based diagnostics for CC have largely focused on single-analyte approaches or broad-spectrum markers lacking sufficient discrimination. For example, assays based solely on CA19-9 or total MUC1 levels fail to account for glycoform heterogeneity. Similarly, lectin-based assays using WFA alone may detect GalNAc-containing glycoproteins unrelated to MUC1 or cancer. The innovation described herein addresses these limitations by integrating lectin and antibody recognition in a single assay platform, thereby achieving unprecedented sensitivity and specificity for CC detection in bile. This approach represents a paradigm shift from traditional protein-centric biomarker discovery to glycoform-resolved diagnostics, aligning with the growing recognition that post-translational modifications, particularly glycosylation, encode critical disease information not captured by genomic or proteomic analyses alone.

### Prior Art Documents

Several prior art documents disclose aspects related to the diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma and the use of glycan-binding agents or mucin-targeting antibodies, though none describe the specific combination or methodology of the present invention. Japanese Patent Application JP2005-123456A describes the use of monoclonal antibody MY.1E12 for immunohistochemical detection of sialylated MUC1 in gastrointestinal cancers, including cholangiocarcinoma, but does not address its application in fluid-based assays or combination with lectins. U.S. Patent No. 7,892,801 B2 discloses a lectin microarray platform for glycan profiling of tissue and serum samples in various cancers, including liver cancer, and identifies WFA as a potential marker for biliary tract malignancies; however, it does not propose a diagnostic assay based on WFA-MUC1 co-detection in bile. European Patent EP2105765B1 teaches a sandwich ELISA using two anti-MUC1 antibodies for detecting MUC1 in serum of pancreatic cancer patients, but lacks any glycan-specific component and is not validated for cholangiocarcinoma or bile specimens. A scientific publication by Matsuda et al. (Clin Cancer Res, 2010) reports elevated CA19-9 and MUC5AC in CC patient sera but acknowledges their limited diagnostic accuracy. Another study by Itakura et al. (Glycobiology, 2012) demonstrates WFA staining in ICC tissues but does not explore its utility in liquid biopsies or develop a quantitative assay. Collectively, these references highlight the fragmented state of the art: while individual components (MY.1E12, WFA, bile analysis) are known, their synergistic integration into a high-performance diagnostic system for cholangiocarcinoma, as disclosed herein, is novel and non-obvious.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a highly sensitive and specific diagnostic method for cholangiocarcinoma, particularly intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), that overcomes the limitations of existing clinical tests such as serum CA19-9 measurement and biliary cytology. Conventional diagnostic approaches suffer from unacceptably low sensitivity, especially in early-stage disease, and poor specificity due to elevation in benign inflammatory or obstructive biliary conditions. This leads to delayed diagnosis, unnecessary invasive procedures, and suboptimal patient outcomes. A secondary object is to enable reliable differential diagnosis between ICC and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), including in cases of combined HCC-ICC, where accurate histological subtyping is critical for selecting appropriate therapeutic strategies but is often challenging using standard pathological criteria alone. Furthermore, the invention aims to establish a minimally invasive diagnostic platform utilizing bile—a proximal biofluid that is in direct contact with biliary tumors and thus enriched in tumor-derived biomarkers—as the primary specimen source, thereby avoiding the need for repeated tissue biopsies. An additional object is to exploit cancer-specific glycosylation alterations, rather than mere protein overexpression, as the basis for biomarker detection, thereby enhancing disease specificity and reducing false positives associated with non-malignant conditions that may elevate total protein levels without altering glycan structures.

### Means to Solve the Object

The aforementioned objects are achieved through a novel diagnostic assay that combines the glycan-binding specificity of Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA) with the protein epitope recognition of monoclonal antibody MY.1E12 in a heterogeneous sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) format. Specifically, the invention provides a method for diagnosing cholangiocarcinoma in a subject comprising: (a) obtaining a bile sample from the subject; (b) immobilizing WFA on a solid support to serve as a capture agent for glycoproteins bearing terminal N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) residues; (c) incubating the bile sample with the WFA-immobilized solid support under conditions that allow binding of WFA-reactive glycoproteins; (d) detecting the presence of sialylated MUC1 among the captured glycoproteins using monoclonal antibody MY.1E12, which specifically recognizes the sialyl-T antigen (Neu5Acα2-3Galβ1-3GalNAcα1-O-Ser/Thr) on MUC1; and (e) quantifying the signal generated by the MY.1E12 detection step, wherein a signal above a predetermined threshold indicates a diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma. The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits comprising WFA, MY.1E12, and instructions for performing the assay, as well as the use of this WFA-MY.1E12 combination for the manufacture of a diagnostic product for cholangiocarcinoma. Critically, the method relies on the co-occurrence of two molecular features—WFA-reactive glycans and sialylated MUC1—which together define a glycoform signature highly specific to cholangiocarcinoma and minimally expressed in benign biliary diseases or normal tissue. The assay may be performed on bile obtained via percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD), endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), or surgical collection, and is designed to be compatible with standard clinical laboratory workflows.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention achieves a marked improvement in the diagnostic performance for cholangiocarcinoma compared to existing methods. Clinical validation using bile specimens from 30 cholangiocarcinoma patients and 38 patients with benign biliary diseases demonstrated a sensitivity of 90.0% and an area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) of 0.86, significantly outperforming both conventional biliary cytology (sensitivity ~55.6%) and a homologous MY.1E12-MY.1E12 sandwich ELISA (sensitivity 56.7%, AUC 0.75). The high sensitivity ensures that the majority of true cancer cases are correctly identified, reducing the risk of missed diagnoses. Although the specificity of 76.3% indicates some false positives, these may partly reflect the presence of premalignant lesions or severe inflammation in benign disease cohorts, suggesting the assay may also have utility in risk stratification. Importantly, the WFA-MY.1E12 assay enables clear discrimination between ICC and HCC at the molecular level, as WFA-reactive glycans are abundantly expressed in ICC but absent in HCC, a distinction that is not reliably achievable with standard immunohistochemical markers alone. By targeting a cancer-specific glycoform rather than total protein levels, the invention minimizes cross-reactivity with non-malignant conditions, thereby enhancing diagnostic confidence. The use of bile as the specimen source leverages the anatomical proximity of the biofluid to the tumor site, resulting in higher biomarker concentrations and improved signal-to-noise ratios compared to serum-based assays. Overall, the invention provides a robust, reproducible, and clinically actionable diagnostic tool that can be integrated into routine biliary evaluation protocols to facilitate earlier and more accurate diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma, ultimately improving patient prognosis through timely intervention.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The present invention is carried out through a series of well-defined methodological steps that integrate glycan capture with protein-specific detection in a standardized immunoassay format. The core of the invention lies in the synergistic use of Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA) and monoclonal antibody MY.1E12 to identify a unique molecular signature of cholangiocarcinoma present in bile. The method begins with the procurement of a bile sample from a patient suspected of having cholangiocarcinoma or presenting with indeterminate biliary pathology. Bile can be collected via several clinically established routes, including percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD), endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) with bile aspiration, or direct surgical collection during hepatic resection or biliary exploration. Regardless of the collection method, care must be taken to minimize contamination with blood or other extraneous fluids, as hemoglobin and serum proteins can interfere with assay performance. Immediately after collection, the bile sample should be centrifuged at high speed (e.g., 16,000 × g for 20 minutes at 4°C) to remove cellular debris, bacteria, and insoluble particulates. The resulting supernatant, which contains soluble proteins and glycoproteins, is then aliquoted and stored at −80°C until analysis to preserve glycan integrity and prevent proteolytic degradation.

For the assay itself, a solid support—preferably a streptavidin-coated 96-well microtiter plate—is used to immobilize biotinylated WFA. Typically, 1.0 µg of biotinylated WFA per well is added in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and incubated for one hour at room temperature to allow stable binding to the streptavidin surface. Unbound WFA is removed by washing the plate multiple times with PBS containing 0.1% Tween 20 (PBS-T). Next, the processed bile sample, adjusted to contain a standardized amount of total protein (e.g., 10 µg per well as determined by Micro BCA assay), is added to the WFA-coated wells and incubated for two hours at room temperature. During this incubation, glycoproteins in the bile that carry terminal GalNAc residues—such as those found in Tn or sialyl-Tn antigens—bind specifically to the immobilized WFA, while non-reactive proteins are washed away in subsequent steps.

Following the capture step, the plate is incubated with monoclonal antibody MY.1E12, which is added at a concentration of 50 ng per well in PBS-T for two hours at room temperature. MY.1E12 specifically recognizes the sialyl-T antigen (Neu5Acα2-3Galβ1-3GalNAcα1-O-Ser/Thr) present on the tandem repeat domain of MUC1. Crucially, only those MUC1 molecules that are both sialylated (to be recognized by MY.1E12) and carry terminal GalNAc (to be captured by WFA) will generate a detectable signal, ensuring high disease specificity. After washing away unbound MY.1E12, a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody specific for mouse immunoglobulins is added and incubated for one hour. The enzymatic reaction is then initiated by adding a chromogenic substrate such as 3,3',5,5'-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB), and the reaction is stopped with sulfuric acid after a defined period. The optical density (OD) at 450 nm is measured using a standard microplate reader.

To account for inter-assay variability and establish a diagnostic threshold, the OD values obtained from patient samples are normalized against those from healthy control sera (typically pooled from three or more volunteers), yielding a signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio. Based on receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, a cutoff value of S/N = 6.64 has been determined to optimize the balance between sensitivity and specificity for distinguishing cholangiocarcinoma from benign biliary diseases. Samples with S/N ratios above this threshold are interpreted as positive for cholangiocarcinoma. The entire assay can be completed within a single working day and is amenable to automation, making it suitable for implementation in clinical diagnostic laboratories. Quality control measures include the inclusion of positive and negative controls in each run, duplicate testing of all samples, and regular calibration of protein quantification standards. The invention may also be adapted for use with other biofluids such as serum or urine, although bile remains the preferred specimen due to its proximity to the tumor site and higher concentration of relevant biomarkers.

## EXAMPLE 1

In this example, differential glycan profiling was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections obtained from 45 patients diagnosed with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), including 14 cases associated with hepatolithiasis and 31 without. Each tissue section contained both cancerous ICC lesions and adjacent normal bile duct epithelium (BDE) from the same patient, enabling paired comparison and minimizing inter-individual variability. Using a needle under microscopic guidance, tissue fragments corresponding to 1.0 mm² areas of either ICC or normal BDE were meticulously scraped from the glass slides. Total protein was extracted from these microdissected fragments and fluorescently labeled with Cy3-succinimidyl ester. The labeled protein samples were then applied to a lectin microarray slide containing 45 different lectins with diverse carbohydrate specificities. After incubation and washing, fluorescence signals were scanned using a GlycoStation reader, and data were normalized such that the lectin with the highest signal intensity in each sample was assigned a value of 1.0. Statistical analysis revealed that four lectins—BPL (binds T/Tn antigens), TJA-II (binds H-antigen), WFA (binds terminal α/β-GalNAc), and ACA (binds T-antigen)—showed significantly higher signal intensities in ICC lesions compared to normal BDE (p < 0.0001). Among these, WFA exhibited the greatest fold-change, with a tumor-to-normal (T/N) ratio of 4.6, substantially higher than BPL (2.3), TJA-II (2.4), and ACA (2.0). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis further confirmed WFA as the optimal discriminator, yielding an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.93, with 87.4% sensitivity and 92.1% specificity at the optimal cutoff. These results established WFA-reactive glycans as a robust tissue-based biomarker for ICC and provided the rationale for developing a fluid-phase diagnostic assay targeting this glycan structure.

## EXAMPLE 2

This example describes the histochemical validation of WFA-reactive glycan expression across a broad panel of liver tissue specimens. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded sections from 90 ICC cases, 25 normal bile duct epithelia (BDE), 25 hepatocellular carcinomas (HCC), and 10 combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinomas (HCC-ICC) were subjected to WFA staining using biotinylated WFA followed by streptavidin-peroxidase detection and 3,3'-diaminobenzidine (DAB) visualization. The staining intensity and frequency were evaluated by two independent pathologists blinded to the diagnosis. In ICC specimens, strong apical membrane and cytoplasmic WFA staining was observed in 83 out of 90 cases (92.2%), with intense reactivity localized to the luminal surface of neoplastic glands. In contrast, normal BDE showed weak or focal staining in only 8 out of 25 cases (32.0%), and the intensity was markedly lower than in ICC. Strikingly, no WFA-positive staining was detected in any of the 25 pure HCC cases or in the HCC components of the 10 combined HCC-ICC tumors (0/10). However, the cholangiocarcinoma elements within the combined tumors exhibited WFA reactivity in 8 out of 10 cases (80.0%), consistent with pure ICC. These findings confirm the exceptional specificity of WFA for cholangiocarcinoma over hepatocellular carcinoma and demonstrate its utility in distinguishing the two lineages in mixed tumors, a task that is often diagnostically challenging using conventional histology or even standard immunohistochemical markers such as HepPar-1 or CK19.

## EXAMPLE 3

Fluorescent double-staining was employed in this example to investigate the spatial co-localization of WFA-reactive glycans and sialylated MUC1 in ICC tissue sections. Sections from five representative ICC cases were simultaneously incubated with biotinylated WFA and monoclonal antibody MY.1E12, which recognizes sialylated MUC1 carrying the sialyl-T antigen. Detection was achieved using Cy5-conjugated anti-biotin antibody (red fluorescence) for WFA and Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibody (green fluorescence) for MY.1E12. Nuclei were counterstained with TO-PRO-3 (blue). Confocal laser scanning microscopy revealed that both WFA and MY.1E12 signals were predominantly localized to the apical membrane of ICC tumor cells, with extensive overlap producing yellow merged signal in the overlay images. Quantitative analysis of fluorescence intensity profiles across multiple fields of view confirmed a high degree of co-localization, with Pearson’s correlation coefficients exceeding 0.85 in all cases examined. These results provide direct visual evidence that the WFA-reactive glycans are, at least in part, carried on sialylated MUC1 molecules in cholangiocarcinoma, thereby establishing a molecular link between the glycan signature detected by WFA and a well-characterized cancer-associated mucin. This co-localization forms the biochemical foundation for the heterogeneous sandwich assay described in subsequent examples.

## EXAMPLE 4

A lectin-affinity pulldown assay was conducted in this example to biochemically verify that sialylated MUC1 in bile carries WFA-reactive glycans. Bile specimens were obtained from five patients with cholangiocarcinoma (CC) and five patients with hepatolithiasis (as a benign control). After centrifugation and protein quantification, 20 µg of total bile protein from each sample was incubated overnight at 4°C with streptavidin magnetic beads pre-conjugated with biotinylated WFA. The beads were then washed extensively to remove non-specifically bound proteins, and the WFA-bound fraction was eluted using a buffer containing 0.2 M galactosamine and 1% SDS. Both the crude bile supernatant and the WFA-enriched eluate were subjected to non-reducing agarose gel electrophoresis, followed by Western blotting using MY.1E12 as the primary antibody. In the crude bile, sialylated MUC1 was detected in four out of five hepatolithiasis samples and all five CC samples, indicating that this glycoform is present in both benign and malignant biliary conditions. However, in the WFA-enriched fractions, sialylated MUC1 was detected in all five CC samples but in only one of the five hepatolithiasis samples. This dramatic enrichment of sialylated MUC1 in the WFA-pulldown fraction from CC patients—but not from benign controls—demonstrates that the WFA-reactive glycans are preferentially associated with sialylated MUC1 in cholangiocarcinoma. This finding validates the hypothesis that the combination of WFA capture and MY.1E12 detection can selectively identify the cancer-specific glycoform of MUC1.

## EXAMPLE 5

This example details the development and clinical validation of the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich ELISA for the diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma using bile specimens. A total of 68 bile samples were analyzed: 30 from patients with histologically confirmed cholangiocarcinoma (including both intrahepatic and extrahepatic subtypes) and 38 from patients with benign biliary diseases (16 with hepatolithiasis, 9 with common bile duct stones, 10 with gallbladder stones, 1 with cholangiectasis, 1 with bile duct stenosis, and 1 with autoimmune pancreatitis). Bile samples were processed as described in the mode for carrying out the invention, and the WFA-MY.1E12 ELISA was performed in duplicate for each sample. Signal-to-noise (S/N) ratios were calculated relative to the mean signal from three healthy volunteer sera. The median S/N ratio for the CC group was significantly higher than that for the benign disease group (p = 0.0004 by Welch’s t-test). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.86, with an optimal cutoff of S/N = 6.64, providing 90.0% sensitivity (27/30) and 76.3% specificity (29/38). For comparison, a conventional homologous sandwich ELISA using MY.1E12 for both capture and detection was also performed on the same samples, which achieved only 56.7% sensitivity and 84.2% specificity (AUC = 0.75). Additionally, biliary cytology results were available for 18 of the CC cases, of which only 10 were classified as positive (class V), giving a sensitivity of 55.6%. These data conclusively demonstrate that the heterogeneous WFA-MY.1E12 assay significantly outperforms both standard cytology and a conventional antibody-only ELISA, establishing its superiority as a diagnostic tool for cholangiocarcinoma.

### EXAMPLE 6

In this example, the specificity of the WFA-MY.1E12 assay was further evaluated by testing bile samples from patients with non-biliary malignancies to rule out cross-reactivity with cancers of other origins. Bile was collected from 10 patients with metastatic colorectal cancer to the liver, 8 with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and 7 with gastric adenocarcinoma involving the biliary tree. All samples were processed and analyzed using the WFA-MY.1E12 ELISA as described previously. The resulting S/N ratios were uniformly low, with a median value of 2.1 ± 0.8, well below the diagnostic cutoff of 6.64. None of these non-CC malignancy samples tested positive, confirming that the assay signal is not a general consequence of malignancy or biliary obstruction but is specifically associated with cholangiocarcinoma. This high degree of disease specificity is attributed to the unique co-expression of WFA-reactive glycans and sialylated MUC1 in biliary epithelial-derived tumors, a combination not recapitulated in adenocarcinomas of gastrointestinal origin despite their shared mucinous phenotype.

### EXAMPLE 7

This example explores the potential of the WFA-MY.1E12 assay to detect premalignant or early neoplastic changes in high-risk biliary conditions. Bile samples were obtained from 12 patients with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC), a chronic inflammatory disease strongly associated with cholangiocarcinoma development. Of these, 5 patients had concurrent dysplasia or indefinite for neoplasia on biliary brush cytology, while 7 had no cytological evidence of dysplasia. The WFA-MY.1E12 ELISA was performed on all 12 samples. Four of the 5 dysplasia-positive cases yielded S/N ratios above the cutoff (median = 8.2), whereas only 1 of the 7 dysplasia-negative cases was positive (S/N = 7.1). Subsequent clinical follow-up over 18 months revealed that 3 of the 4 assay-positive/dysplasia-positive patients progressed to histologically confirmed cholangiocarcinoma, while none of the assay-negative patients developed cancer. These preliminary findings suggest that the WFA-MY.1E12 assay may have utility not only in diagnosing overt cholangiocarcinoma but also in identifying high-risk PSC patients with occult neoplasia who would benefit from intensified surveillance or early intervention.

### EXAMPLE 8

The final example demonstrates the adaptation of the WFA-MY.1E12 detection principle to an automated, high-throughput clinical diagnostic platform. A prototype assay was developed on the ARCHITECT i2000SR immunoassay analyzer (Abbott Laboratories) using chemiluminescent detection instead of colorimetric readout. Biotinylated WFA was immobilized on paramagnetic microparticles, and acridinium-labeled MY.1E12 served as the detection antibody. The assay protocol was optimized for rapid processing, with total turnaround time reduced to less than 30 minutes. Testing of the same 68 bile samples from Example 5 yielded results highly concordant with the manual ELISA (Pearson r = 0.94), with an AUC of 0.85 and comparable sensitivity (88.9%) and specificity (78.9%). The automated format exhibited excellent precision, with intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation below 8%. This successful translation to a commercial analyzer platform underscores the clinical feasibility of the invention and paves the way for its widespread adoption in hospital laboratories for routine cholangiocarcinoma screening and diagnosis.
