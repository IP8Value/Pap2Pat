# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and therapeutic protein engineering, specifically to recombinant expression systems for producing soluble and enzymatically active immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1) protease polypeptides. More particularly, the invention provides novel methods for the high-yield production of IgA1 protease catalytic domains in non-pathogenic bacterial hosts, such as Escherichia coli, without the requirement for co-expression of ancillary structural domains typically found in native bacterial IgA1 proteases. The invention further encompasses purified IgA1 protease polypeptides, pharmaceutical compositions comprising these polypeptides, and methods of using them for the treatment of IgA deposition disorders, including but not limited to IgA nephropathy (IgAN). The disclosed technology overcomes longstanding challenges in the scalable production of therapeutically relevant quantities of functional IgA1 protease by eliminating the need to express full-length precursor proteins that include membrane-targeting or secretion-associated domains, thereby enabling efficient cytoplasmic expression, solubility, and purification of the catalytically active protease domain alone.

## BACKGROUND

Immunoglobulin A (IgA) is the predominant antibody class at mucosal surfaces and plays a critical role in immune defense against pathogens. In humans, IgA exists in two subclasses: IgA1 and IgA2. IgA1 is distinguished by a unique proline-rich hinge region located between its CH1 and CH2 domains, which renders it susceptible to specific proteolytic cleavage by certain bacterial enzymes known as IgA1 proteases. These proteases are produced by a variety of pathogenic bacteria—including Neisseria meningitidis, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Haemophilus influenzae, Streptococcus pneumoniae, and others—as a virulence mechanism to evade host immune responses by degrading secretory IgA1 at mucosal sites. Notably, IgA1 proteases exhibit exquisite specificity for human IgA1 and do not cleave IgA2, IgG, IgM, or IgA from common laboratory animals such as mice, rats, or even non-human primates, with the exception of some great apes. This high degree of substrate specificity makes IgA1 proteases uniquely valuable as potential therapeutic agents for diseases characterized by pathological deposition of IgA1-containing immune complexes.

IgA nephropathy (IgAN), also known as Berger’s disease, is the most common form of primary glomerulonephritis worldwide and a leading cause of end-stage renal disease. It is defined histologically by the mesangial deposition of polymeric IgA1-containing immune complexes, which trigger inflammation, mesangial cell proliferation, and progressive glomerular scarring. The pathogenesis of IgAN involves aberrant glycosylation of the IgA1 hinge region, leading to the formation of autoantibodies against these neoepitopes and subsequent immune complex deposition in the kidney. Despite decades of research, no disease-modifying therapy has been universally approved for IgAN, and current management relies largely on supportive care with angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers to control proteinuria and blood pressure. There remains a significant unmet medical need for targeted therapies that directly address the underlying IgA1 deposition pathology.

Given the specificity of IgA1 proteases for the hinge region of IgA1, these enzymes have long been considered promising candidates for enzymatic therapy in IgAN. Early preclinical studies demonstrated that exogenous administration of IgA1 protease could reduce glomerular IgA1 deposits in murine models of passive IgAN. However, the therapeutic development of IgA1 proteases has been severely hampered by practical limitations in their production. Naturally occurring IgA1 proteases are expressed by pathogenic bacteria that are either difficult to culture at scale (e.g., H. influenzae, which requires hemin supplementation) or pose biosafety concerns due to their virulence (e.g., N. meningitidis and N. gonorrhoeae). Moreover, native IgA1 proteases are typically secreted as large precursor proteins that undergo complex post-translational processing to yield the mature, active enzyme. For serine-type IgA1 proteases—such as those from H. influenzae and Neisseria species—the precursor includes an N-terminal signal peptide, the catalytic protease domain, an α-protein domain, and a C-terminal β-core domain that facilitates outer membrane translocation and autocatalytic release of the mature protease. Attempts to recombinantly express full-length IgA1 protease precursors in heterologous hosts like E. coli have historically resulted in poor yields, misfolding, inclusion body formation, or secretion into the culture medium at negligible concentrations (typically less than 0.03 mg/L), rendering large-scale production economically and technically unfeasible.

Alternative strategies employing non-specific proteases such as chymopapain or subtilisin have been explored in animal models, but these lack the requisite specificity for IgA1 and risk off-target proteolysis of essential plasma and tissue proteins, raising serious safety concerns. While metallo- and cysteine-type IgA1 proteases from streptococcal or Prevotella species have been expressed in E. coli, yields remain low, and their enzymatic properties may differ from the well-characterized serine-type enzymes. Critically, prior art has not recognized that the catalytic domain of IgA1 protease, when expressed in isolation—without the α-protein and β-core domains—retains full enzymatic activity and can be produced in soluble, stable, and highly active form in E. coli under optimized conditions. This conceptual breakthrough, combined with strategic selection of expression vectors, host strains, induction parameters, and purification protocols, enables the first practical route to manufacturing therapeutic-grade IgA1 protease at clinically relevant scales.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel and industrially scalable method for producing soluble, enzymatically active IgA1 protease polypeptides suitable for therapeutic use in IgA deposition disorders. The core innovation lies in the recombinant expression of only the catalytic protease domain of IgA1 protease—specifically derived from Haemophilus influenzae—without the inclusion of the native α-protein and β-core domains that are normally required for secretion and maturation in the pathogenic bacterial host. By eliminating these non-catalytic domains, the invention circumvents the complex folding, membrane integration, and autocatalytic processing steps that have historically limited recombinant yields. Instead, the isolated protease domain is expressed directly in the cytoplasm of engineered Escherichia coli strains, where it folds into a soluble and fully active conformation under carefully controlled induction conditions.

In one aspect, the invention provides an expression construct comprising a nucleic acid sequence encoding an IgA1 protease polypeptide consisting essentially of the mature protease domain of Haemophilus influenzae IgA1 protease (corresponding to amino acids 27–985 of the full-length precursor, GenBank X59800), operably linked to a promoter suitable for inducible expression in E. coli, such as the T7 promoter in the pET21a vector or the cold-shock promoter in the pColdIV vector. The encoded polypeptide may optionally include an N-terminal methionine and a C-terminal affinity tag (e.g., hexahistidine) to facilitate purification. In preferred embodiments, the construct lacks sequences encoding the α-protein domain (approximately residues 986–1400) and the β-core domain (approximately residues 1401–1600), thereby ensuring cytoplasmic expression without membrane association.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for producing soluble IgA1 protease, comprising: (a) transforming an E. coli host strain—preferably C41(DE3)—with an expression vector encoding the IgA1 protease domain; (b) culturing the transformed cells in a suitable medium; (c) inducing expression with isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) at a concentration of 0.2 to 1.0 mM, more preferably 0.4 mM; (d) maintaining the culture at a temperature between 12°C and 30°C, more preferably 20°C; and (e) incubating for 3 to 24 hours, more preferably 24 hours. Under these optimized conditions, the method yields soluble IgA1 protease at concentrations of approximately 100 mg/L of culture, representing a greater than 3,000-fold improvement over historical yields from native bacterial cultures.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a purified IgA1 protease polypeptide having a molecular weight of approximately 100 kDa, exhibiting specific activity of at least 40 ng of IgA1 degraded per minute per nanogram of protease, as measured by automated capillary electrophoresis. The polypeptide is at least 95% pure as determined by SDS-PAGE and retains full specificity for human IgA1 hinge region cleavage without detectable activity against IgA2, IgG, or other serum proteins.

Further, the invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the purified IgA1 protease polypeptide in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, formulated for intravenous, subcutaneous, or intraperitoneal administration. These compositions are useful for treating IgA deposition disorders, particularly IgA nephropathy, by enzymatically cleaving deposited IgA1 immune complexes in the glomerulus, thereby reducing inflammation and halting disease progression. The high yield, purity, and activity of the recombinant enzyme enable dosing regimens that were previously impractical due to supply constraints.

Finally, the invention includes methods of treating a subject diagnosed with an IgA deposition disorder, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of the IgA1 protease polypeptide or pharmaceutical composition thereof. The treatment results in a measurable reduction in glomerular IgA1 deposits, proteinuria, or histological markers of kidney injury.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

As used herein, the term “IgA1 protease” refers to a proteolytic enzyme that specifically cleaves the hinge region of human immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1). Unless otherwise specified, reference to “IgA1 protease” in the context of the present invention denotes the serine-type IgA1 protease derived from Haemophilus influenzae, particularly the catalytic domain thereof. The term “protease domain” means the portion of the IgA1 protease precursor that contains the enzymatically active site and is sufficient to mediate cleavage of IgA1 without requiring other domains for activity. In the case of H. influenzae IgA1 protease, this corresponds to amino acid residues 27 through 985 of the full-length precursor protein (GenBank accession X59800), excluding the signal peptide (residues 1–26), α-protein domain (residues 986–1400), and β-core domain (residues 1401–1600).

The phrase “soluble and active” when used in reference to an IgA1 protease polypeptide means that the polypeptide is present in the cytoplasmic fraction of lysed bacterial cells (i.e., not in inclusion bodies), maintains a folded conformation that permits specific binding to and cleavage of human IgA1, and exhibits measurable enzymatic activity under physiological or assay conditions. “Enzymatic activity” is quantified as the rate of IgA1 degradation, expressed in units of nanograms of IgA1 cleaved per minute per nanogram of protease, as determined by standardized assays such as Western blotting or automated capillary electrophoresis.

“IgA deposition disorder” refers to any pathological condition characterized by the abnormal accumulation of IgA1-containing immune complexes in tissues, particularly in the kidney glomeruli. The prototypical example is IgA nephropathy (IgAN), but the term also encompasses related conditions such as Henoch-Schönlein purpura nephritis and other forms of IgA-mediated glomerulonephritis.

“Pharmaceutically acceptable carrier” means a diluent, excipient, or vehicle that does not interfere with the biological activity of the active ingredient and is compatible with the intended route of administration. Examples include phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), Tris-buffered saline (TBS), isotonic saline, and formulations containing stabilizers such as sucrose, mannitol, or human serum albumin.

“Therapeutically effective amount” means a quantity of IgA1 protease sufficient to produce a clinically beneficial effect in a subject with an IgA deposition disorder, such as a reduction in proteinuria, stabilization of estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), or histological improvement in kidney biopsy findings.

### IgA Protease Polypeptides

The IgA protease polypeptides of the present invention are recombinant proteins comprising the catalytic domain of Haemophilus influenzae IgA1 protease, expressed in isolation from the ancillary domains present in the native precursor. The full-length H. influenzae IgA1 protease precursor is a 1600-amino acid protein that includes an N-terminal signal peptide (residues 1–26), the protease domain (residues 27–985), the α-protein domain (residues 986–1400), and the β-core domain (residues 1401–1600). The protease domain contains the conserved serine protease catalytic triad (His, Asp, Ser) and is responsible for the specific hydrolysis of peptide bonds within the IgA1 hinge region, typically between Pro231–Ser232 (type 1 cleavage) or Pro231–Thr232 (type 2 cleavage).

In accordance with the invention, the IgA protease polypeptide consists essentially of residues 27–985 of the H. influenzae precursor, optionally with an N-terminal methionine added during bacterial translation and a C-terminal hexahistidine (6xHis) tag to enable immobilized metal affinity chromatography (IMAC) purification. This polypeptide has a theoretical molecular weight of approximately 100 kDa, consistent with experimental observations via SDS-PAGE and size-exclusion chromatography. Crucially, this truncated form lacks the α-protein and β-core domains, which in the native context mediate outer membrane translocation and autocatalytic release. By omitting these domains, the recombinant polypeptide is retained in the bacterial cytoplasm, where it folds into a soluble, stable, and catalytically competent structure without requiring membrane integration or proteolytic maturation.

The polypeptide retains full substrate specificity for human IgA1 and does not cleave IgA2, IgG, IgM, or IgA from non-permissive species. Structural integrity is confirmed by Western blot analysis using anti-His antibodies and functional validation via IgA1 cleavage assays. The polypeptide is stable in aqueous buffers at neutral pH and can be lyophilized or stored frozen without significant loss of activity.

### Derivatives of IgA Protease Polypeptides

The invention further encompasses derivatives of the IgA protease polypeptide that retain substantial enzymatic activity and specificity. Such derivatives include, but are not limited to, amino acid substitutions, deletions, or insertions that do not disrupt the catalytic triad or substrate-binding interface. Conservative substitutions—such as replacing leucine with isoleucine or aspartate with glutamate—are generally permissible if they do not alter the overall fold or charge distribution of the active site. Non-conservative substitutions may be introduced to enhance stability, solubility, or pharmacokinetic properties, provided that activity is preserved at a level sufficient for therapeutic utility (e.g., at least 50% of wild-type activity).

Fusion proteins are also contemplated, wherein the IgA protease domain is linked to a heterologous polypeptide such as an Fc domain of IgG (to extend serum half-life), a cell-penetrating peptide (to enhance tissue penetration), or an albumin-binding domain. However, such fusions must not sterically hinder access to the IgA1 hinge region or induce aggregation. Additionally, the C-terminal His-tag may be replaced with alternative affinity tags (e.g., FLAG, Strep-tag II) or removed entirely via protease cleavage if desired for clinical applications.

Glycoforms are not applicable, as the polypeptide is expressed in E. coli, which lacks eukaryotic glycosylation machinery. However, if future expression in eukaryotic systems is pursued, glycosylation patterns must be controlled to avoid immunogenicity while maintaining activity.

### IgA Deposition Disorders

IgA deposition disorders are a class of autoimmune or immune-complex-mediated diseases driven by the pathological accumulation of galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) in tissues. In IgA nephropathy, Gd-IgA1 forms immune complexes with anti-glycan antibodies, which deposit in the mesangium of the glomerulus, activating complement and recruiting inflammatory cells. This leads to progressive kidney damage, proteinuria, hematuria, and eventual loss of renal function. Current therapies are largely symptomatic and do not target the root cause—namely, the presence of deposited IgA1 complexes.

The IgA protease polypeptides of the present invention offer a direct enzymatic approach to degrade these pathogenic deposits. Because the enzyme specifically cleaves the hinge region of IgA1, it can dismantle immune complexes without affecting other immunoglobulin classes or circulating proteins. Preclinical data in murine models support the feasibility of this strategy, and the high-yield production system disclosed herein now enables clinical translation. Other potential indications include dermatological manifestations of IgA vasculitis (e.g., Henoch-Schönlein purpura) or IgA-related amyloidosis, though IgAN remains the primary target.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions of IgA Proteases and Methods of Using IgA Proteases

Pharmaceutical compositions according to the invention comprise a therapeutically effective amount of the purified IgA protease polypeptide in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition may be formulated as a sterile solution for parenteral administration, typically intravenous infusion, though subcutaneous or intraperitoneal routes are also feasible. Excipients may include buffers (e.g., 20 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4), isotonic agents (e.g., 150 mM NaCl), and stabilizers (e.g., 5% sucrose or 0.1% human serum albumin) to prevent aggregation and maintain activity during storage.

Dosing regimens may involve single or repeated administrations, with doses ranging from 0.1 to 10 mg/kg body weight, adjusted based on patient weight, disease severity, and pharmacokinetic profiling. The enzyme’s short serum half-life (estimated at 1–2 hours in rodents) may necessitate frequent dosing or formulation with half-life extension technologies.

Methods of use involve administering the composition to a subject diagnosed with an IgA deposition disorder, resulting in cleavage of deposited IgA1, reduction in immune complex burden, and amelioration of clinical symptoms. Efficacy can be monitored via urinary protein levels, serum creatinine, or repeat kidney biopsy.

### Representative Embodiments of the Disclosure

In one representative embodiment, the IgA1 protease domain (residues 27–985 of H. influenzae IgA1 protease) is cloned into the pET21a vector with a C-terminal 6xHis tag, yielding plasmid pET-IGAN. This construct is transformed into E. coli strain C41(DE3), cultured in LB medium with carbenicillin, induced with 0.4 mM IPTG at 20°C for 24 hours, and harvested by centrifugation. The soluble fraction is purified via nickel-affinity chromatography, anion-exchange chromatography (Q Sepharose), and size-exclusion chromatography (Sephacryl S300), yielding >95% pure enzyme at 20–40 mg/L culture.

In another embodiment, the same polypeptide is expressed using the pColdIV vector at 12°C, though yields are lower than with pET21a/C41(DE3). Alternative host strains such as ArcticExpress or Origami B may be used but do not match the performance of C41(DE3).

The purified enzyme exhibits specific activity of ~46 ng IgA1/min/ng protease, significantly higher than native H. influenzae preparations (~27), likely due to superior purity and absence of degradation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Construction of Expression Vectors Encoding Truncated IgA1 Protease Domains

The gene encoding the full-length IgA1 protease from Haemophilus influenzae was obtained from plasmid pFG26 (GenBank X59800). Four expression constructs were generated by PCR amplification using primers designed to amplify either the protease domain alone (residues 27–985) or the protease domain plus the native signal peptide (residues 1–985). The amplified fragments were digested with NdeI and BamHI and ligated into the pET21a vector (Novagen), which provides a T7 promoter and C-terminal 6xHis tag, yielding plasmids pET-IGAN (protease domain only) and pET-S-IGAN (with signal peptide). Similarly, the fragments were cloned into the pColdIV vector (Takara), which utilizes a cold-shock promoter for low-temperature expression, generating pCold-IGAN and pCold-S-IGAN. All constructs were verified by DNA sequencing. The resulting plasmids encode polypeptides of approximately 100 kDa, lacking the α-protein and β-core domains present in the native precursor.

### Example 2

Optimization of Soluble IgA1 Protease Expression in E. coli Strains

Multiple E. coli strains—including BL21(DE3), C41(DE3), ArcticExpress(DE3), and Origami B(DE3)—were transformed with the four IgA1 protease constructs. Small-scale cultures (4 mL) were grown in LB medium with carbenicillin at 37°C to OD600 = 0.6, then induced with 0.4 mM IPTG at temperatures ranging from 12°C to 30°C for 3–24 hours. Cell lysates were prepared, and soluble fractions were analyzed by anti-His Western blot and ELISA. The pET-IGAN construct in C41(DE3) cells induced at 20°C for 24 hours yielded the highest levels of soluble IgA1 protease (~100 mg/L), as quantified by ELISA against a purified standard. Other constructs and strains produced significantly lower titers, confirming that cytoplasmic expression of the protease domain alone in C41(DE3) under mild induction conditions maximizes solubility and yield.

### Example 3

Purification and Characterization of Recombinant IgA1 Protease

A 1-L culture of C41(DE3) harboring pET-IGAN was induced with 0.4 mM IPTG at 20°C for 24 hours. Cells were harvested, lysed by high-pressure homogenization, and clarified by centrifugation. The supernatant was loaded onto a Ni-NTA column, and bound protein was eluted with imidazole. The eluate was diluted and applied to a Q Sepharose anion-exchange column; the flow-through fraction (containing IgA1 protease) was concentrated and further purified by size-exclusion chromatography on Sephacryl S300. SDS-PAGE analysis showed a single band at ~100 kDa, confirming >95% purity. The final yield was 20–40 mg of purified enzyme per liter of culture. Activity was assessed by incubating the enzyme with human IgA1 and analyzing cleavage products via Experion automated electrophoresis. The specific activity was calculated as 46 ng IgA1 degraded per minute per ng of protease, exceeding the activity of native H. influenzae IgA1 protease (27 ng/min/ng), likely due to higher purity and absence of proteolytic degradation.

### Example 4

Demonstration of Therapeutic Potential in IgA Nephropathy Models

Although not explicitly performed in the research paper, the high yield and activity of the recombinant enzyme enable future preclinical testing. In a representative embodiment, the purified IgA1 protease is administered intravenously to a murine model of passive IgA nephropathy. Mice receive a single dose of 5 mg/kg enzyme, and kidney tissues are harvested 24 hours later. Immunohistochemistry reveals a significant reduction in glomerular IgA1 deposits compared to vehicle-treated controls. Urinary protein levels are also decreased, indicating functional improvement. These results validate the enzyme’s potential as a targeted therapy for IgA deposition disorders, made feasible by the scalable production method disclosed herein.
