# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of automated information extraction from structured and semi-structured documents, and more specifically to systems and methods for extracting field values from form-like documents such as invoices, purchase orders, tax forms, insurance quotes, and receipts using a representation learning approach. The invention provides a novel neural architecture that learns dense representations of extraction candidates and target fields, enabling accurate extraction of structured information from documents with diverse layouts and templates, including those previously unseen during training. The system leverages both textual and spatial features derived from optical character recognition (OCR) output to generate candidate representations that encode contextual neighborhood information, while simultaneously learning field-specific embeddings that capture indicative key phrases associated with each target field. By computing similarity scores between candidate and field representations, the invention achieves high-precision extraction performance across multiple document domains without requiring template-specific rules or extensive manual annotation.

## BACKGROUND

The processing of form-like documents represents a significant challenge in modern business operations, where organizations routinely handle thousands of documents such as invoices, purchase orders, tax forms, and insurance quotes on a daily basis. Traditional approaches to extracting structured information from these documents have relied heavily on either manual human effort or brittle heuristic-based systems that are highly susceptible to variations in document layout and formatting. Manual processing is not only time-consuming and expensive but also prone to human error, while rule-based systems require extensive customization for each document template and fail catastrophically when presented with documents that deviate from expected patterns. The proliferation of digital document formats, including both native PDFs and scanned images, has further complicated the extraction problem, as these formats often lack explicit markup or structural information that could facilitate automated processing.

Early attempts at automated information extraction focused primarily on natural language text organized in prose format, where techniques such as named entity recognition and relation extraction proved effective. However, form-like documents present fundamentally different challenges due to their sparse textual content, heavy reliance on spatial layout elements such as tables and grid formatting, and the absence of conventional sentence structure. Approaches developed for web page information extraction, including traditional wrapper induction methods, are largely inapplicable to form-like documents because they depend on HTML markup that is not available in PDF or scanned image formats. While some systems have attempted to address this problem by registering templates and matching new documents to existing templates, these approaches are limited in their ability to generalize to previously unseen document layouts and require significant upfront investment in template creation and maintenance.

Recent advances in deep learning have led to the development of various neural network architectures for document understanding, including graph-based approaches that model documents as networks of interconnected text elements and grid-based methods that represent documents as two-dimensional arrays of text tokens. While these approaches have shown promise in certain contexts, they often suffer from computational complexity, particularly when dealing with high-resolution document images, and may require sophisticated heuristics to construct appropriate multi-scale representations. Furthermore, many existing approaches focus on handling variations within known templates rather than addressing the more challenging problem of generalizing to completely unseen document layouts.

The fundamental challenge in form-like document information extraction lies in the fact that the same semantic information can be represented in vastly different ways across different document templates. For example, an invoice date might appear adjacent to the words "Date," "Invoice Date," or "Dated" in different positions relative to the actual date value, depending on the vendor's chosen layout. Similarly, the same vendor may consistently use the same layout across multiple documents, creating what can be described as a template, while different vendors may employ entirely different visual patterns for presenting the same types of information. This variability necessitates an approach that can learn to recognize the underlying semantic relationships between key phrases and their corresponding values regardless of their specific spatial arrangement or textual formulation.

Prior work in this area has not adequately addressed the need for a system that can effectively combine textual and spatial information to identify the correct field values while maintaining the ability to generalize across different document templates and domains. Existing approaches often treat text and layout as separate modalities or fail to properly encode the complex spatial relationships that exist between key phrases and their associated values. Additionally, many systems do not leverage the observation that fields in form-like documents typically correspond to well-understood data types such as dates, currency amounts, or addresses, which could significantly reduce the search space for potential extraction candidates. The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing a novel representation learning framework that explicitly models the relationship between extraction candidates and target fields through learned embeddings that capture both textual context and spatial positioning information.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a system and method for extracting structured information from form-like documents using a representation learning approach that overcomes the limitations of prior art systems. The invention operates by first generating extraction candidates for each target field based on the field's associated data type, then learning dense representations of these candidates and their corresponding fields, and finally scoring candidates based on the similarity between their representations and the field representations. This approach enables accurate extraction of field values from documents with diverse and previously unseen layouts while maintaining computational efficiency and interpretability.

The system begins by ingesting documents in various formats, including both native digital documents and scanned images, and converting them to a standardized representation through optical character recognition (OCR). The OCR output provides a hierarchical structure of text elements with associated bounding box coordinates that capture the spatial layout of the document. For each target field in the schema, the system generates extraction candidates by identifying text spans that match the field's data type using specialized candidate generators. These generators leverage libraries of detectors for common data types such as dates, currency amounts, integers, addresses, and email addresses, ensuring high recall in candidate generation.

The core innovation of the invention lies in its neural scoring model, which learns separate embeddings for extraction candidates and target fields. The candidate representation encodes information about the candidate's textual neighborhood and spatial position, while the field representation captures the key phrase variants typically associated with that field. The model computes a similarity score between these representations to determine the likelihood that a given candidate represents the true value for the corresponding field. This architecture enables the system to generalize across different document templates and domains because it learns to recognize the underlying semantic relationships between key phrases and their associated values rather than memorizing specific layout patterns.

The candidate representation is constructed through a multi-stage process that incorporates both textual and spatial features. The system defines a neighborhood zone around each candidate that extends to the left edge of the page and approximately 10% of the page height above the candidate. Text tokens whose bounding boxes overlap significantly with this neighborhood zone are considered neighbors of the candidate. Each neighbor is represented by its text content and relative position to the candidate, with positions normalized to account for variations in document resolution. The system employs separate embedding mechanisms for text tokens and positional information, using word embedding tables for text and nonlinear positional embeddings for spatial coordinates. These embeddings are then processed through a self-attention mechanism that allows each neighbor to influence the representation of other neighbors, capturing complex interactions between contextual elements.

The final candidate encoding is obtained by combining the processed neighborhood information with the candidate's absolute position, resulting in a representation that is independent of the specific field being extracted. This design choice ensures that the same candidate can be evaluated against multiple fields using the same underlying representation, improving computational efficiency and enabling cross-field generalization. The field representation is learned through a separate embedding table that maps each field to a dense vector in the same embedding space as the candidates.

During training, the model learns to position positive candidates (those that match the ground truth for a given field) close to their corresponding field embeddings while pushing negative candidates away from these embeddings. This metric learning approach results in meaningful internal representations that naturally cluster according to field type, as demonstrated through visualization techniques such as t-SNE. The system's performance is evaluated using both scorer-level metrics (ROC AUC) and end-to-end extraction metrics (maximum F1 score), demonstrating significant improvements over baseline approaches that either ignore spatial information or use simpler neural architectures.

The invention's architecture provides several key advantages over existing approaches. First, by explicitly modeling the relationship between candidates and fields through learned representations, the system can generalize to previously unseen document templates without requiring template-specific rules or extensive retraining. Second, the incorporation of both textual and spatial features enables the system to accurately distinguish between multiple instances of the same data type within a single document, such as differentiating between invoice date and due date when both are present as date values. Third, the self-attention mechanism allows the model to dynamically weight the importance of different contextual elements based on their relevance to the extraction task, improving robustness to variations in document layout and quality. Finally, the learned representations are interpretable, allowing developers to analyze failure cases and understand the factors contributing to extraction errors.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive system and method for extracting structured information from form-like documents through a novel representation learning approach. The detailed implementation encompasses several interconnected components that work together to achieve high-accuracy extraction across diverse document types and layouts. The system architecture is designed to be both computationally efficient and highly generalizable, enabling deployment in real-world business environments where document variety and volume present significant challenges.

The document ingestion process begins with the acceptance of input documents in various formats, including native PDF files, scanned images, and other digital document representations. Each document is rendered to a standardized image format to ensure consistent processing regardless of the original file type. Optical character recognition (OCR) is then applied to extract all textual content from the document, producing a hierarchical data structure that organizes text elements into characters, words, paragraphs, and blocks. Each element in this hierarchy is associated with a bounding box defined by its coordinates in the two-dimensional Cartesian plane of the document page. The hierarchical organization follows reading order conventions, with words arranged sequentially within paragraphs and paragraphs ordered appropriately within blocks. This structured OCR output serves as the foundation for all subsequent processing steps, providing both the textual content and spatial layout information necessary for accurate information extraction.

Following document ingestion, the system proceeds to candidate generation, which leverages the observation that fields in form-like documents typically correspond to well-understood data types. For each target field in the schema, the system identifies appropriate candidate generators based on the field's associated type. These generators utilize specialized detection algorithms optimized for specific data types such as dates, currency amounts, integers, addresses, and email addresses. The candidate generation process operates on the OCR-extracted text, scanning for spans that match the patterns characteristic of each data type. For example, all date instances detected in an invoice document become candidates for date-type fields such as invoice_date, due_date, and delivery_date. This approach dramatically reduces the search space compared to considering all possible text spans as potential candidates, while maintaining high recall through the use of robust detection algorithms.

The candidate generation phase is critical to the overall system performance, as the recall of the entire extraction pipeline cannot exceed that of the candidate generators. Therefore, the system prioritizes high recall in candidate generation, accepting that precision will be addressed in subsequent scoring and assignment stages. The candidate generators are implemented using a combination of regular expression matching, heuristic rules, and machine learning models trained on large corpora of web data, ensuring comprehensive coverage of various data type representations. For fields with particularly challenging detection requirements, multiple candidate generators may be employed in parallel to maximize recall.

The neural scoring model constitutes the core innovation of the present invention, implementing a representation learning approach that distinguishes it from prior art systems. The model architecture is designed to learn separate embeddings for extraction candidates and target fields, with the final extraction score determined by the similarity between these representations. This design enables the system to generalize across different document templates and domains by learning to recognize the underlying semantic relationships between key phrases and their associated values rather than memorizing specific layout patterns.

The candidate representation construction process begins with the definition of a neighborhood zone around each candidate. This zone extends horizontally to the left edge of the document page and vertically approximately 10% of the page height above the candidate's position. Any text tokens whose bounding boxes overlap by more than half with this neighborhood zone are considered neighbors of the candidate. This neighborhood definition is based on empirical observations of how key phrases typically appear in relation to their associated values in form-like documents, where key phrases often precede or appear above the actual field values.

Each neighbor is characterized by two primary features: its text content and its relative position to the candidate. The text content is represented using a vocabulary of the most frequent tokens observed in the training corpus, with special tokens reserved for numbers, out-of-vocabulary terms, and padding. The relative position is calculated as the difference between the normalized coordinates of the neighbor's centroid and the candidate's centroid, with normalization achieved by dividing by the corresponding page dimensions to ensure resolution independence. Additionally, the absolute position of the candidate itself is included as a feature, providing global context information that can be useful for distinguishing between similar patterns appearing in different regions of the document.

The feature embedding process employs separate mechanisms for different types of input features. Text tokens are embedded using a standard word embedding table that maps each vocabulary token to a dense vector representation. Relative position features are embedded through a nonlinear positional embedding consisting of two ReLU-activated layers with dropout regularization, allowing the model to learn fine-grained distinctions in spatial relationships. The candidate's absolute position is embedded using a simple linear layer, while the target field is represented through a dedicated field embedding table. All embeddings are configured to have the same dimensionality d, facilitating subsequent combination operations.

To capture interactions between neighboring elements, the system employs a self-attention mechanism inspired by transformer architectures. The initial neighbor embeddings, which are independent of each other, are transformed into query, key, and value representations through separate linear projection matrices. For each neighbor, attention weights are computed based on the compatibility between its query representation and the key representations of all neighbors, including itself. These attention weights are then used to compute a weighted sum of the value representations, producing self-attended neighbor encodings that incorporate contextual information from the entire neighborhood. The self-attention mechanism is particularly valuable for downweighting irrelevant neighbors that may be positioned between the candidate and truly indicative key phrases, or for emphasizing neighbors that provide complementary contextual information.

Following the self-attention processing, the neighbor encodings are projected through an additional feed-forward network consisting of a linear projection to a higher-dimensional space (4×2d) with ReLU activation, followed by a projection back to the original dimensionality (2d). This intermediate expansion allows the model to learn more complex feature interactions before reducing the dimensionality for final combination. The processed neighbor encodings are then combined into a single neighborhood representation through max-pooling, which has been empirically determined to outperform alternative aggregation strategies such as mean-pooling. Max-pooling ensures that the neighborhood representation is invariant to the arbitrary ordering of neighbors while preserving the most salient features from the neighborhood.

The final candidate encoding is obtained by concatenating the neighborhood representation (2d dimensions) with the candidate position embedding (d dimensions) and projecting the combined vector through a ReLU-activated linear layer back to d dimensions. This candidate encoding is designed to be field-independent, containing all relevant information about the candidate's context and position without incorporating the candidate's actual text content. This design choice prevents accidental overfitting to training data characteristics, such as temporal patterns in date values, and ensures that the same candidate representation can be evaluated against multiple fields efficiently.

The field representation is maintained in a separate embedding table that maps each target field to a dense vector in the same d-dimensional space as the candidate encodings. During training, the model learns to position field embeddings such that they are close to the encodings of their corresponding positive candidates while being distant from negative candidates and positive candidates of other fields. The scoring function computes the cosine similarity between the candidate encoding and the field embedding, followed by a linear rescaling operation to produce final scores in the range [0,1]. This metric learning approach results in meaningful internal representations that naturally cluster according to field type, as evidenced by visualization studies using techniques such as t-SNE.

The training process utilizes binary cross-entropy loss between the predicted scores and ground truth labels, where positive examples are candidates that match the annotated field values and negative examples are all other candidates for the same field. To address class imbalance issues that commonly arise in information extraction tasks, where negative examples typically far outnumber positive ones, the system implements strategic downsampling of negative examples during training. Specifically, the training set is constructed to maintain at most 40 negative examples for each positive example per field, while validation and test sets retain their original class distributions to provide realistic performance evaluation.

The assignment phase follows candidate scoring and involves selecting at most one candidate per field as the final extraction result. This selection is typically performed by choosing the highest-scoring candidate that exceeds a predetermined confidence threshold, though more sophisticated assignment strategies could be employed if needed. The end-to-end extraction performance is evaluated using maximum F1 score computed across all decision thresholds, providing a comprehensive measure of the system's precision-recall trade-off.

Experimental validation of the invention demonstrates significant performance improvements over baseline approaches. When evaluated on invoice and receipt datasets containing documents with diverse and previously unseen templates, the proposed architecture achieves substantial gains in both scorer-level metrics (ROC AUC) and end-to-end extraction metrics (maximum F1 score). The benefits of incorporating spatial information are clearly demonstrated by comparing against a bag-of-words baseline that ignores positional features, while the advantages of the proposed neural architecture over simpler multilayer perceptron (MLP) approaches highlight the importance of proper feature interaction modeling through mechanisms like self-attention.

The interpretability of the learned representations provides additional value beyond raw performance metrics. Visualization studies reveal that candidate encodings naturally cluster according to their ground truth field assignments, with field embeddings positioned near their corresponding positive candidate clusters. Analysis of misclassified examples provides insights into failure modes, including ground truth annotation errors, ambiguous contextual cues, and OCR quality issues. These interpretability characteristics enable systematic debugging and improvement of the system, making it particularly valuable for real-world deployment scenarios where understanding and addressing failure cases is crucial.

The invention's architecture is designed to be extensible and adaptable to new document domains and field types. The modular nature of the candidate generation and scoring components allows for easy integration of new data type detectors and field definitions without requiring complete system retraining. Additionally, the learned representations show promise for transfer learning applications, where knowledge gained from one document domain can be leveraged to improve performance in related domains with limited training data. Future extensions could include support for repeated fields, domain-specific candidate generators, and few-shot learning capabilities that would further enhance the system's practical utility in diverse business environments.
