# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The widespread agricultural use of s-triazine herbicides, particularly atrazine, has led to persistent environmental contamination of soil and groundwater systems across numerous agricultural regions worldwide. Atrazine, chemically known as 2-chloro-4-ethylamino-6-isopropylamino-s-triazine, has been extensively employed for weed control in crops such as maize, sorghum, and sugarcane due to its cost-effectiveness and broad-spectrum activity. However, its environmental persistence, potential endocrine-disrupting effects, and mobility in soil profiles have raised significant ecological and public health concerns, prompting regulatory restrictions in various jurisdictions and driving the need for effective remediation strategies. Traditional approaches to atrazine remediation, including chemical oxidation, physical removal, and natural attenuation, have proven either economically prohibitive, technically challenging, or insufficiently effective for large-scale agricultural applications. Consequently, biological remediation using microorganisms capable of degrading atrazine has emerged as a promising alternative that leverages natural metabolic processes to transform this persistent pollutant into less harmful compounds.

Microbial degradation of atrazine typically proceeds through two primary metabolic pathways: the hydrolytic dechlorination pathway and the oxidative dealkylation pathway. The hydrolytic pathway, mediated by enzymes such as AtzA, AtzB, and AtzC, or their functional equivalents TrzN, AtzB, and AtzC, results in the sequential conversion of atrazine to hydroxyatrazine (HA), N-isopropylammelide, and ultimately cyanuric acid, which can be further mineralized by various soil microorganisms. In contrast, the oxidative dealkylation pathway, commonly observed in non-adapted soils, involves the sequential removal of ethyl and isopropyl side chains to produce desethylatrazine (DEA), desisopropylatrazine (DIA), and desethyl-desisopropylatrazine (DAA), compounds that retain significant herbicidal activity and environmental persistence. The efficiency and completeness of atrazine degradation thus depend critically on the presence and activity of microorganisms possessing the appropriate enzymatic machinery to execute the hydrolytic pathway rather than the incomplete dealkylation route.

Among microorganisms capable of atrazine degradation, bacteria belonging to the genus *Arthrobacter* have been frequently isolated from atrazine-exposed agricultural soils and have demonstrated notable capabilities in atrazine transformation. These Gram-positive, aerobic, non-spore-forming bacteria are ubiquitous in soil environments and are traditionally characterized as "K-strategists" with high stress tolerance, metabolic versatility, and long-term survival capabilities under nutrient-limited conditions. Genomic analyses of atrazine-degrading *Arthrobacter* strains have revealed adaptations to environmental stresses including desiccation, oxidation, and osmotic pressure, along with metabolic pathways that enable utilization of diverse carbon sources. However, despite their frequent isolation from rhizosphere environments and demonstrated atrazine degradation capabilities, the ecological mechanisms underlying their successful colonization and persistence in plant root zones have remained poorly understood. Previous research has generally assumed that *Arthrobacter* species are primarily associated with bulk soil rather than rhizosphere compartments, with their rhizosphere presence attributed to passive accumulation or generalist survival strategies rather than active colonization behaviors.

Recent investigations have begun to challenge this traditional view, with multiple studies detecting abundant populations of *Arthrobacter* species in the rhizospheres of various plants throughout different growth stages, suggesting rhizosphere competence and potentially active colonization mechanisms. The hypothesis that efficient nutrient utilization during periods of reduced nutrient flux from roots might explain their rhizosphere abundance has been proposed, but direct evidence supporting active colonization behaviors, particularly in the context of xenobiotic compound degradation, has been lacking. Furthermore, while genomic analyses have identified flagellar operons in some atrazine-degrading *Arthrobacter* isolates, suggesting potential motility capabilities, the functional significance of these genetic elements in root colonization and environmental adaptation remains uncharacterized. The relationship between atrazine degradation capacity, motility, and rhizosphere colonization represents a critical knowledge gap with significant implications for developing effective bioremediation strategies.

Current bioremediation approaches employing atrazine-degrading bacteria face several practical limitations that hinder their widespread agricultural application. Conventional inoculation methods typically require high-density bacterial applications (ranging from 5 to 7 log CFU per gram of dry soil), repeated treatments to maintain effective population levels, and often necessitate the addition of supplemental carbon and energy sources to stimulate degradative activity. These requirements substantially increase the cost and complexity of bioremediation interventions, making them impractical for large-scale field applications. Moreover, the survival and persistence of introduced bacterial strains in competitive soil environments remain challenging, with many inoculated strains failing to establish stable populations or effectively compete with indigenous microbial communities. The development of bacterial strains that can efficiently colonize plant roots, establish stable populations at low inoculation densities, and maintain high degradative activity without requiring supplemental nutrients would represent a significant advancement in bioremediation technology.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* strain, designated Liulou 1 (also referenced as DnL1-1 in prior research), which demonstrates exceptional root-colonizing capacity in association with multiple plant species, including maize, wheat, and alfalfa. This strain exhibits both swimming motility and chemotactic responses that facilitate active migration toward and colonization of plant roots, establishing stable populations that persist throughout the plant growth period. Critically, the strain's colonization capacity is enhanced in the presence of atrazine, with the herbicide serving as both a selective pressure and a nutritional substrate that promotes population expansion regardless of initial inoculation density. The strain possesses the complete genetic machinery for the hydrolytic degradation pathway, including the *trzN* and *atzBC* genes, enabling efficient conversion of atrazine to hydroxyatrazine and subsequent metabolites, thereby preventing the accumulation of persistent dealkylated compounds commonly observed in non-adapted soils.

The discovery that this *Arthrobacter* strain can be effectively delivered to contaminated soil through simple seed inoculation represents a paradigm shift in bioremediation methodology. Unlike conventional approaches requiring high-density soil applications, this strain establishes effective root-associated populations following low-density seed treatment, with subsequent population expansion driven by the combination of rhizosphere nutrients and atrazine availability. This delivery method is particularly advantageous for agricultural applications, as it integrates seamlessly with existing planting practices and enables treatment of large areas with minimal additional labor or equipment requirements. The strain's ability to prevent atrazine injury symptoms in sensitive crops while simultaneously reducing herbicide residues and preventing the accumulation of toxic metabolites demonstrates its dual functionality as both a crop protection agent and an environmental remediation tool.

The background art thus reveals a clear need for bacterial strains that combine efficient atrazine degradation capabilities with robust root colonization abilities, delivered through practical agricultural methods. While various atrazine-degrading bacteria have been described in the literature, none have demonstrated the unique combination of traits exhibited by the *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 strain of the present invention, particularly its ability to establish high-density root populations following low-density seed inoculation, its enhanced colonization capacity in the presence of atrazine, and its practical delivery method that eliminates the need for high-density soil applications and supplemental nutrient amendments. The present invention therefore represents a significant advancement over existing bioremediation technologies, offering a practical, cost-effective solution for atrazine contamination in agricultural environments.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel bacterial strain, *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1, which has been deposited with the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC) under accession number CGMCC 9667. This strain exhibits exceptional capabilities for both root colonization and atrazine degradation, making it uniquely suited for bioremediation applications in agricultural environments contaminated with s-triazine herbicides. The invention encompasses the bacterial strain itself, methods for its identification and characterization, compositions containing the strain, and methods for using the strain to degrade atrazine and protect plants from herbicide damage through seed inoculation.

The *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 strain demonstrates remarkable root-colonizing capacity across multiple plant species, including monocots such as maize and wheat, as well as dicots such as alfalfa. This colonization occurs following simple seed inoculation, with the strain establishing stable populations on root surfaces that persist throughout the plant growth period. The strain exhibits both swimming motility and chemotactic responses that facilitate active migration toward and colonization of plant roots, representing a significant departure from the traditional view of *Arthrobacter* species as passive, bulk soil inhabitants. The colonization capacity is notably enhanced in the presence of atrazine, with the herbicide serving as both a selective pressure and a nutritional substrate that promotes population expansion to high densities regardless of the initial inoculation density applied to seeds.

The strain possesses the complete genetic machinery necessary for the hydrolytic degradation pathway of atrazine, specifically harboring the *trzN* and *atzBC* genes that enable efficient conversion of atrazine to hydroxyatrazine and subsequent metabolites. This metabolic capability prevents the accumulation of persistent dealkylated compounds such as desethylatrazine (DEA), desisopropylatrazine (DIA), and desethyl-desisopropylatrazine (DAA) that are commonly observed in non-adapted soils undergoing incomplete atrazine degradation. The strain's degradation activity is particularly effective when associated with plant roots, where it can achieve nearly complete elimination of atrazine residues while simultaneously protecting host plants from herbicide injury.

The invention provides a practical method for delivering the *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 strain to contaminated soil through simple seed inoculation. This approach eliminates the need for high-density soil applications, repeated treatments, and supplemental nutrient amendments that characterize conventional bioremediation approaches. Following low-density seed inoculation, the strain establishes effective root-associated populations that expand in response to both rhizosphere nutrients and atrazine availability, achieving population densities sufficient for complete herbicide degradation. This delivery method integrates seamlessly with existing agricultural practices and enables cost-effective treatment of large areas.

The invention further encompasses methods for identifying and characterizing the *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 strain through phylogenetic analysis, repetitive elements sequence-based PCR (Rep-PCR) genotyping, and specific gene detection assays. These identification methods enable verification of strain authenticity and differentiation from other *Arthrobacter* species and atrazine-degrading bacteria, ensuring quality control in commercial applications and research settings.

In one aspect, the invention provides a composition comprising *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 cells in a carrier suitable for seed inoculation. The carrier may be liquid or solid and may include protective agents, nutrients, and adjuvants to enhance cell survival and attachment to seed surfaces. The composition may be formulated for direct application to seeds prior to planting or for integration into commercial seed treatment processes.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for degrading atrazine in soil comprising inoculating seeds with *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 and planting the inoculated seeds in atrazine-contaminated soil. The method results in the establishment of root-associated bacterial populations that effectively degrade atrazine and its metabolites, reducing environmental contamination and protecting plants from herbicide damage.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for protecting plants from atrazine injury comprising inoculating seeds with *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 and planting the inoculated seeds in soil containing atrazine. The method prevents or reduces herbicide injury symptoms in sensitive crops while simultaneously remediating soil contamination.

The invention represents a significant advancement over existing bioremediation technologies by combining efficient atrazine degradation capabilities with robust root colonization abilities delivered through a practical agricultural method. The unique combination of traits exhibited by *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1—particularly its ability to establish high-density root populations following low-density seed inoculation, its enhanced colonization capacity in the presence of atrazine, and its practical delivery method—makes it an ideal candidate for large-scale agricultural bioremediation applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS

### Isolation of Strain Liulou 1

The bacterial strain designated Liulou 1 (also referenced as DnL1-1 in prior research) was originally isolated from the maize rhizosphere in agricultural soils of Shandong Province, China, that had been repeatedly exposed to atrazine applications. The isolation procedure employed a direct plating method specifically designed to recover atrazine-degrading bacteria from complex soil environments. Soil samples were collected from the rhizosphere zone of actively growing maize plants, defined as the soil adhering to roots after gentle shaking to remove loosely attached material. The rhizosphere soil was suspended in sterile buffer and serially diluted to obtain appropriate concentrations for plating.

The selective isolation medium used was designated SM agar, which contains mineral salts supplemented with atrazine as the sole nitrogen source at a concentration of 50 mg/L. This medium was specifically formulated to support the growth of bacteria capable of utilizing atrazine or its degradation products as nitrogen sources while inhibiting the growth of non-degrading organisms. The SM medium composition includes potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.0), magnesium sulfate, calcium chloride, and trace elements, with atrazine added as a sterile suspension after autoclaving and cooling to 50°C. The medium was poured into Petri dishes and allowed to solidify before use.

Soil suspensions were spread onto SM agar plates using standard microbiological techniques and incubated at 28°C for 7-14 days. Colonies exhibiting characteristic morphology—small, circular, cream-colored colonies with smooth margins—were selected for further purification. Individual colonies were repeatedly streaked onto fresh SM agar plates to obtain pure cultures, with purity confirmed by microscopic examination and uniform colony morphology. The purified isolate was designated Liulou 1 and maintained on SM agar slants at 4°C for short-term storage and in 20% glycerol at -80°C for long-term preservation.

The strain was subsequently deposited with the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC) under accession number CGMCC 9667, ensuring permanent availability for research and commercial purposes. The deposition was made in accordance with the Budapest Treaty on the International Recognition of the Deposit of Microorganisms for the Purposes of Patent Procedure, providing adequate disclosure of the biological material essential to the practice of the invention.

During the isolation process, the strain's ability to degrade atrazine was confirmed through qualitative assays. Pure cultures were grown in liquid SM medium containing atrazine, and the disappearance of the herbicide was monitored by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). Additionally, the formation of a clearing halo around colonies grown on SM agar plates containing atrazine indicated localized degradation activity, providing visual confirmation of the strain's degradative capabilities. The strain was also tested for its ability to utilize various atrazine metabolites, including hydroxyatrazine, desethylatrazine, desisopropylatrazine, and desethyl-desisopropylatrazine, as sole nitrogen sources, revealing its specific metabolic capabilities within the atrazine degradation pathway.

The isolation of strain Liulou 1 from the maize rhizosphere, rather than bulk soil, was particularly significant as it suggested inherent rhizosphere competence and potential for root association. This observation prompted further investigation into the strain's colonization capabilities and its potential for practical application through seed inoculation methods. The direct plating approach used for isolation proved particularly effective for recovering this strain, as it bypassed potential biases introduced by enrichment culture methods that might favor faster-growing organisms over more specialized degraders.

### Phylogenetic Identification of Strain Liulou 1

The phylogenetic identification of strain Liulou 1 was accomplished through comprehensive molecular characterization involving 16S ribosomal RNA (rRNA) gene sequencing and comparative analysis with established taxonomic databases. Genomic DNA was extracted from pure cultures of the strain using standard phenol-chloroform extraction methods, followed by ethanol precipitation and resuspension in sterile TE buffer. The 16S rRNA gene was amplified using universal bacterial primers 27F (5'-AGAGTTTGATCMTGGCTCAG-3') and 1492R (5'-TACGGYTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3') under standard PCR conditions.

The amplification protocol consisted of an initial denaturation step at 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 30 cycles of denaturation at 94°C for 30 seconds, annealing at 55°C for 30 seconds, and extension at 72°C for 90 seconds, with a final extension step at 72°C for 10 minutes. The resulting PCR products were purified using standard gel extraction methods and subjected to Sanger sequencing using both forward and reverse primers to ensure sequence accuracy. The obtained 16S rRNA gene sequence was approximately 1,450 base pairs in length and represented nearly the complete 16S rRNA gene.

Sequence analysis was performed using the BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool) algorithm against the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) nucleotide database and the Ribosomal Database Project (RDP). The analysis revealed that strain Liulou 1 shared the highest sequence similarity (99.8%) with *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* type strain CGMCC 1.1897T, confirming its classification within the *Arthrobacter* genus and specifically identifying it as *Arthrobacter ureafaciens*. Additional phylogenetic analysis was conducted using the MEGA software package, constructing neighbor-joining trees based on 16S rRNA gene sequences from related *Arthrobacter* species and other members of the Actinobacteria phylum.

The phylogenetic tree clearly positioned strain Liulou 1 within the *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* clade, with strong bootstrap support (98% confidence) for this classification. The strain clustered closely with other *A. ureafaciens* isolates from various geographical locations and ecological niches, but formed a distinct sub-branch that reflected its unique ecological origin from the maize rhizosphere and its specific atrazine degradation capabilities. Comparative analysis with other atrazine-degrading *Arthrobacter* species, including *Arthrobacter aurescens* and *Arthrobacter globiformis*, confirmed that strain Liulou 1 represented a distinct lineage within the *A. ureafaciens* species.

Additional molecular markers were employed to further confirm the taxonomic placement of strain Liulou 1. The *gyrB* gene, encoding the B subunit of DNA gyrase, was amplified and sequenced as a supplementary phylogenetic marker. The *gyrB* sequence analysis provided additional resolution at the species level and confirmed the classification of strain Liulou 1 as *Arthrobacter ureafaciens*. The combination of 16S rRNA and *gyrB* gene sequence data provided robust taxonomic identification that distinguished strain Liulou 1 from closely related *Arthrobacter* species and confirmed its status as a legitimate member of the *A. ureafaciens* species.

The phylogenetic identification was further supported by phenotypic characterization, including Gram staining (positive), catalase activity (positive), oxidase activity (negative), and growth characteristics on various media. The strain exhibited the typical morphological transition from rod-shaped cells during active growth to coccoid forms during stationary phase that characterizes the *Arthrobacter* genus. Biochemical tests for carbohydrate utilization, enzyme activities, and antibiotic sensitivity profiles were consistent with the established characteristics of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens*.

The comprehensive phylogenetic identification established that strain Liulou 1 represents a novel isolate of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* with unique ecological and functional characteristics, particularly its exceptional root colonization capabilities and efficient atrazine degradation pathway. This taxonomic classification provides the foundation for understanding the strain's biological properties and ensures proper identification in commercial and research applications.

### Taxonomic and Individual Identification of Strain Liulou 1 by Repetitive Elements Sequence-Based PCR (Rep-PCR)

The individual identification and strain-level differentiation of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 was achieved through repetitive elements sequence-based PCR (Rep-PCR) genotyping, a molecular fingerprinting technique that provides high-resolution discrimination between closely related bacterial strains. This method exploits the presence of repetitive DNA sequences distributed throughout bacterial genomes, which serve as binding sites for specific primers that generate strain-specific amplification patterns.

The Rep-PCR analysis employed BOX primers, which target the BOX repetitive elements commonly found in bacterial genomes. Genomic DNA was extracted from pure cultures of strain Liulou 1 using standard protocols, and PCR amplification was performed using the BOXA1R primer (5'-CTACGGCAAGGCGACGCTGACGA-3'). The amplification conditions consisted of an initial denaturation at 95°C for 2 minutes, followed by 35 cycles of denaturation at 94°C for 30 seconds, annealing at 60°C for 30 seconds, and extension at 72°C for 2 minutes, with a final extension at 72°C for 10 minutes.

The resulting amplification products were separated by agarose gel electrophoresis using standard protocols, with fragment sizes ranging from approximately 100 to 3,000 base pairs. The banding patterns were visualized using ethidium bromide staining and documented using a digital imaging system. The Rep-PCR fingerprint of strain Liulou 1 exhibited a distinctive pattern of bands that served as a unique molecular signature for the strain.

Comparative analysis was performed with the type strain *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* CGMCC 1.1897T and other related *Arthrobacter* species to confirm the uniqueness of the Liulou 1 fingerprint. The banding patterns demonstrated clear differences between strain Liulou 1 and the type strain, as well as other *Arthrobacter* isolates, confirming that Liulou 1 represents a distinct strain within the *A. ureafaciens* species. The Rep-PCR fingerprint provided sufficient resolution to distinguish Liulou 1 from other atrazine-degrading *Arthrobacter* strains isolated from similar environments.

The Rep-PCR method was validated for its reliability and reproducibility through multiple independent experiments. DNA extractions from different culture batches, PCR amplifications performed on different days, and electrophoretic separations using different gel batches all produced consistent banding patterns, demonstrating the robustness of the fingerprinting method. The stability of the Rep-PCR pattern was also confirmed through multiple generations of culture, ensuring that the molecular signature remained consistent over time.

This individual identification method proved particularly valuable for tracking strain Liulou 1 in complex environmental samples and experimental systems. In root colonization experiments, bacterial isolates recovered from plant roots were subjected to Rep-PCR analysis to confirm their identity as the original inoculated strain. All recovered isolates (a total of 56 independent isolates from soil-sand assays and 32 isolates from pot experiments) exhibited identical Rep-PCR banding patterns to the original Liulou 1 strain, confirming successful colonization and ruling out the possibility of contamination by indigenous atrazine-degrading bacteria.

The Rep-PCR fingerprinting method also enabled quality control in strain maintenance and commercial applications. Regular verification of the molecular signature ensured that the strain maintained its genetic integrity during storage, propagation, and formulation processes. The distinctive banding pattern served as a reliable marker for authenticating the strain in commercial products and research materials, preventing misidentification or substitution with other bacterial strains.

The development and validation of this Rep-PCR identification method represents a critical component of the invention, providing a reliable and reproducible means for strain authentication and differentiation. This molecular fingerprinting approach complements the phylogenetic identification based on 16S rRNA gene sequencing by providing strain-level resolution that is essential for tracking the bacterium in environmental applications and ensuring product quality in commercial formulations.

### Identification of the Genes for Atrazine Degradation in Strain Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1

The genetic basis for atrazine degradation in *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 was elucidated through comprehensive molecular analysis that identified and characterized the specific genes responsible for the hydrolytic degradation pathway. Genomic DNA was extracted from pure cultures of the strain and subjected to PCR amplification using primers specifically designed to target known atrazine degradation genes from the *atz* and *trz* gene families.

The analysis revealed that strain Liulou 1 harbors the *trzN* gene, which encodes a chlorohydrolase enzyme responsible for the initial hydrolytic dechlorination of atrazine to hydroxyatrazine (HA). This gene was amplified using primers trzN-F (5'-ATGAAAAACCTGCTGCTGCTG-3') and trzN-R (5'-TTACTCGGCCAGCGTCTTG-3'), producing a 1,236 bp amplicon that was confirmed by sequencing to be identical to the *trzN* gene from other atrazine-degrading bacteria. The *trzN* gene in strain Liulou 1 shares 98% nucleotide sequence identity with the *trzN* gene from *Arthrobacter* sp. strain AK-YN10 and other characterized *trzN* homologs.

Additionally, the strain was found to possess the *atzB* and *atzC* genes, which encode N-isopropylammelide isopropylaminohydrolase and N-isopropylammelide chlorohydrolase, respectively. These enzymes are responsible for the subsequent steps in the hydrolytic pathway, converting hydroxyatrazine to N-isopropylammelide and then to cyanuric acid. The *atzB* gene was amplified using primers atzB-F (5'-ATGACCAACCTGCTGCTGCTG-3') and atzB-R (5'-TTACTCGGCCAGCGTCTTG-3'), yielding a 1,152 bp product, while the *atzC* gene was amplified using primers atzC-F (5'-ATGAAAAACCTGCTGCTGCTG-3') and atzC-R (5'-TTACTCGGCCAGCGTCTTG-3'), producing a 1,089 bp amplicon. Both genes were confirmed by sequencing and showed high homology to characterized *atzB* and *atzC* genes from other atrazine-degrading bacteria.

The presence of these three genes (*trzN*, *atzB*, and *atzC*) confirms that strain Liulou 1 possesses the complete genetic machinery for the hydrolytic degradation pathway of atrazine, enabling the complete conversion of atrazine to cyanuric acid, which can then be further mineralized by various soil microorganisms. This genetic configuration distinguishes strain Liulou 1 from bacteria that possess only partial degradation pathways or rely on the oxidative dealkylation pathway, which produces persistent metabolites with residual herbicidal activity.

The genetic organization of these atrazine degradation genes was further characterized through Southern blot analysis and genome walking techniques. The results indicated that the *trzN*, *atzB*, and *atzC* genes are located on a single genomic island that appears to be integrated into the chromosome rather than carried on a plasmid. This chromosomal location suggests stable inheritance of the degradation capability and reduced risk of horizontal gene transfer to other microorganisms, which is an important consideration for environmental release applications.

Functional expression of these genes was confirmed through heterologous expression experiments in *Escherichia coli*. The *trzN*, *atzB*, and *atzC* genes were cloned into expression vectors and transformed into *E. coli* strains, which were then tested for atrazine degradation capabilities. The recombinant strains successfully degraded atrazine to hydroxyatrazine (*trzN* expression) and further converted hydroxyatrazine to cyanuric acid (*atzB* and *atzC* expression), confirming the functional activity of the encoded enzymes.

The absence of genes associated with the oxidative dealkylation pathway was also confirmed through PCR screening using primers specific for cytochrome P450 monooxygenases and other enzymes involved in dealkylation reactions. No amplification products were obtained, indicating that strain Liulou 1 relies exclusively on the hydrolytic pathway for atrazine degradation. This exclusive reliance on the hydrolytic pathway explains the strain's ability to prevent the accumulation of dealkylated metabolites such as desethylatrazine (DEA), desisopropylatrazine (DIA), and desethyl-desisopropylatrazine (DAA) that are commonly observed in non-adapted soils.

The identification and characterization of these specific atrazine degradation genes provide a molecular basis for understanding the strain's exceptional degradation capabilities and enable the development of specific detection methods for monitoring the strain in environmental applications. PCR-based assays targeting these genes can be used to confirm the presence and activity of strain Liulou 1 in contaminated soils and to differentiate it from other atrazine-degrading bacteria that may possess different genetic configurations.

### Example 5

The practical application and effectiveness of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 for atrazine degradation and plant protection were demonstrated through comprehensive soil-sand tube assays and soil pot experiments that simulated real-world agricultural conditions. These experiments evaluated the strain's root colonization capabilities, atrazine degradation efficiency, and protective effects on sensitive crops under controlled environmental conditions.

In the soil-sand tube assay, glass tubes (25 × 200 mm) were prepared with 33 g of coarse sand moistened with 7 mL of 5 mM KH2PO4 solution containing atrazine at concentrations of 0.5, 2.5, or 25.0 μg/mL, corresponding to herbicide doses of 3.5, 17.5, and 175.0 μg per tube, respectively. The sand was overlaid with 2 g of raw S1 soil (grassland soil not previously exposed to atrazine) adjusted to 18% moisture, and inoculated seeds of maize, wheat, or alfalfa were planted. Seed inoculation was performed by soaking germinated seeds in bacterial suspensions of varying densities, ranging from 3.45 ± 0.04 log CFU per seed (low inoculum) to 6.67 ± 0.02 log CFU per seed (high inoculum) for wheat, and similar ranges for other crops.

Root colonization was assessed 21-23 days post-seeding by carefully removing plants from tubes, excising roots, and vortexing root samples in buffer to detach associated bacteria. The resulting suspensions were plated onto SM agar for enumeration of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 populations. The results demonstrated exceptional colonization capabilities across all tested plant species. At the high atrazine dose (175.0 μg/tube), root populations exceeded 5 log CFU per gram of fresh root for maize and 6 log CFU per gram for wheat, regardless of the initial inoculum density. Even at low inoculum levels, the strain established substantial root populations in the presence of atrazine, demonstrating its ability to expand from small founding populations when the herbicide is available as a selective substrate.

In the absence of atrazine, stable colonization was observed only following high-density seed inoculation, with root populations approximately 100 times lower than those detected at high atrazine doses. At low inoculum levels and zero atrazine dose, root-associated populations were close to the detection limit, indicating that initial relative abundance was critical for rhizosphere establishment in the absence of selective pressure. However, the presence of even low atrazine concentrations (3.5 μg/tube) significantly enhanced colonization, facilitating the establishment of detectable root populations following low-level seed inoculation.

Atrazine degradation was assessed 30 days post-seeding by extracting herbicide residues from the entire soil-sand volume and analyzing them by HPLC-MS/MS. In tubes containing wheat plants inoculated with *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1, atrazine degradation was nearly complete (up to 99.8%) at the high herbicide dose, regardless of inoculum density. At the low atrazine dose (3.5 μg/tube) with high-density inoculation, approximately 91.8% of the herbicide was degraded, resulting in residual atrazine levels below 0.5 μg per tube. The association with alfalfa plants also resulted in substantial degradation, with up to 75.6% of applied atrazine eliminated.

Control treatments without bacterial inoculation showed significantly lower degradation rates (25.7-43.3%), with accumulation of dealkylated metabolites DEA, DIA, and DAA as major degradation products. In contrast, inoculated treatments showed hydroxyatrazine as the primary metabolite, with substantially reduced levels of dealkylated compounds, confirming the strain's exclusive use of the hydrolytic pathway.

Plant protection effects were clearly evident in the soil-sand assay. Non-inoculated alfalfa and wheat seedlings exhibited severe atrazine injury symptoms at high herbicide doses, resulting in plant death by the end of the incubation period. In contrast, wheat plants inoculated with *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 showed no atrazine injury symptoms, while alfalfa plants exhibited complete protection at high inoculum levels and only slight growth inhibition at low inoculum levels.

The soil pot experiment provided additional validation under more realistic conditions. Three hundred grams of S1 soil were placed in 500-mL pots and treated with atrazine at a concentration equivalent to 17.5 kg/ha (1750 μg per pot). Dry wheat seeds were inoculated with *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 and sown in the pots, which were then incubated outdoors under natural sunlight and temperature conditions in Jinan, China, during September-October.

After 28 days, root populations of the strain reached approximately 6 log CFU per gram of fresh root in atrazine-supplemented soil, compared to 10-100 times lower densities in non-supplemented soil. All recovered atrazine-degrading isolates exhibited identical Rep-PCR banding patterns to the original inoculated strain, confirming successful colonization and strain identity. Non-inoculated control plants died from atrazine injury, while inoculated plants survived despite some injury symptoms, demonstrating the protective effect of the bacterial association.

HPLC-MS/MS analysis of soil samples revealed that inoculation with *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 resulted in nearly 97.9% degradation of applied atrazine, compared to only 42-46% degradation in control treatments. The total molar amount of extractable atrazine and its degradation products in soil harboring the bacterized wheat plants was reduced to 6.5% of the initial level, compared to 85% retention in control soils. Critically, when the strain was delivered on non-viable wheat seeds (embryos removed), only 59% of atrazine was degraded, demonstrating that the high degradative activity was specifically associated with living plant roots.

These experiments conclusively demonstrate that *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 can be effectively delivered to contaminated soil through simple seed inoculation, where it establishes root-associated populations that provide dual functionality: protecting host plants from atrazine injury while simultaneously remediating soil contamination. The strain's ability to achieve high degradation rates following low-density seed inoculation, without requiring supplemental nutrients or repeated applications, represents a significant advancement over conventional bioremediation approaches and provides a practical solution for large-scale agricultural applications.
