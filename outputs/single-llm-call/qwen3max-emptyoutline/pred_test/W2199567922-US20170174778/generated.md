## BACKGROUND

The field of therapeutic protein engineering has witnessed significant advances through the strategic manipulation of pH-dependent binding interactions between proteins and their targets. Central to many of these innovations is the exploitation of histidine residues, which possess side-chain pKa values within the physiologically relevant range of approximately 5.5 to 6.5. This unique chemical property enables histidine to undergo reversible protonation and deprotonation in response to changes in environmental pH, thereby modulating protein conformation, stability, and binding affinity. In the context of intracellular trafficking pathways, such pH-sensitive behavior can be harnessed to control the timing and location of ligand-receptor dissociation, particularly within the endosomal compartment where the pH drops to between 5.0 and 6.0 following internalization via clathrin-mediated endocytosis.

One well-characterized example of a naturally occurring pH-dependent system is the interaction between transferrin (Tf) and its receptor (TfR). Transferrin circulates in the bloodstream either in an iron-free form (apoTf) or an iron-bound form (holoTf). At physiological blood pH (7.2–7.4), holoTf binds tightly to TfR on the cell surface, initiating receptor-mediated endocytosis. Once internalized into acidic endosomes, conformational changes triggered by protonation of key histidine residues in transferrin facilitate the release of iron, converting holoTf into apoTf. Interestingly, apoTf exhibits increased affinity for TfR at endosomal pH, allowing the apoTf-TfR complex to recycle back to the cell surface. Upon return to neutral extracellular pH, apoTf dissociates from TfR and re-enters circulation. This elegant cycle ensures efficient iron delivery while minimizing unnecessary degradation of the receptor or ligand.

Given the central role of TfR in cellular iron homeostasis and its overexpression on many cancer cells, TfR has emerged as a compelling target for therapeutic intervention. Strategies to exploit TfR include direct antagonism to inhibit iron uptake in rapidly dividing tumor cells, as well as indirect approaches that use TfR as a gateway for intracellular delivery of cytotoxic payloads. Antibody-drug conjugates and recombinant fusion proteins targeting TfR have been extensively studied for their potential to enhance drug accumulation within malignant cells. However, the efficacy of such therapeutics is often limited by inefficient intracellular release or premature degradation in lysosomes, highlighting the need for engineered molecules capable of modulating their binding behavior in response to endosomal acidification.

Recent efforts in antibody engineering have demonstrated that introducing histidine residues into complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) can confer pH-sensitive binding properties without compromising antigen recognition at neutral pH. For instance, the anti-interleukin-6 receptor antibody Tocilizumab and the anti-PCSK9 antibody RN316 were both modified to incorporate histidines in their CDR loops, enabling them to dissociate from their targets in acidic endosomes and thereby avoid target-mediated degradation. Similarly, mutations in the Fc region of monoclonal antibodies have been used to fine-tune interactions with the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), which regulates serum half-life through pH-dependent binding. These examples underscore the versatility of histidine-based engineering as a tool for optimizing pharmacokinetics, enhancing potency, and redirecting intracellular trafficking.

Despite these successes, there remains a critical unmet need for engineered anti-TfR antibodies that can actively promote enhanced intracellular accumulation by decoupling internalization from canonical recycling dynamics. While prior studies have focused on improving binding affinity or altering valency to influence cellular uptake, few have systematically addressed how pH-sensitive dissociation from TfR might reshape post-endocytic fate. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a novel single-chain variable fragment (scFv) antibody—designated M16—that maintains high-affinity binding to human TfR at physiological pH but rapidly dissociates upon exposure to endosomal acidity. This engineered dissociation profile leads to significantly greater intracellular accumulation compared to wild-type counterparts, offering new opportunities for targeted drug delivery and mechanistic studies of receptor trafficking.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a pH-sensitive single-chain variable fragment (scFv) antibody, designated M16, that specifically binds to the human transferrin receptor (TfR) with high affinity at neutral pH (approximately 7.4) and exhibits rapid dissociation from TfR under acidic conditions (pH 5.5), mimicking the environment of early endosomes. This engineered scFv was developed through histidine-saturation mutagenesis of the complementarity-determining region H1 (CDRH1) of a parental anti-TfR scFv, H7, followed by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS)-based screening in a yeast surface display system. The resulting M16 variant contains multiple histidine substitutions clustered in the central portion of CDRH1, along with adjacent proline mutations, which collectively confer pronounced pH-dependent binding behavior.

In contrast to the wild-type H7 scFv, which retains more than 50% of its TfR binding after a 10-minute incubation at pH 5.5, M16 demonstrates greater than 90% dissociation under identical conditions. Importantly, M16 preserves sub-nanomolar apparent equilibrium binding affinity at pH 7.4 (Kd ≈ 420 pM), ensuring robust target engagement at the cell surface. When applied to human breast cancer cells (SK-BR-3), M16 shows markedly increased total cellular association and intracellular accumulation compared to both H7 and a pH-insensitive mutant, N5. Quantitative flow cytometry reveals that M16 achieves 1.5-fold higher total cell-associated signal and 2.6-fold greater internalized fraction relative to H7. This enhanced internalization is strictly dependent on endosomal acidification, as treatment with bafilomycin A1—a potent inhibitor of vacuolar ATPase that blocks endosomal acidification—abolishes the differential accumulation phenotype, confirming that pH-driven dissociation is the mechanistic basis for improved intracellular retention.

Immunocytochemical analysis further demonstrates that M16 traffics to distinct intracellular compartments that do not strongly co-localize with canonical markers of early endosomes (EEA1) or late endosomes/lysosomes (LAMP1, LAMP2). Instead, M16 accumulates in large, punctate vesicular structures suggestive of altered trafficking routes, potentially involving non-degradative recycling pathways or specialized endosomal subdomains. This divergence from the canonical Tf-TfR recycling itinerary represents a significant functional advantage for therapeutic applications requiring sustained intracellular presence or avoidance of lysosomal degradation.

The invention also encompasses nucleic acid sequences encoding the M16 scFv, expression vectors containing these sequences, host cells engineered to produce M16, and pharmaceutical compositions comprising M16 alone or conjugated to cytotoxic agents, imaging probes, or other functional moieties. Additionally, the methods described herein for engineering pH-sensitive antibodies via histidine saturation mutagenesis and high-throughput screening are broadly applicable to other receptor-ligand systems where controlled dissociation in acidic compartments is desirable.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel engineered single-chain variable fragment (scFv) antibody, M16, that exhibits pH-dependent binding to the human transferrin receptor (TfR). This scFv was generated through a rational protein engineering approach involving histidine-saturation mutagenesis of the CDRH1 loop of a parental anti-TfR scFv, H7, followed by stringent selection using yeast surface display and fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS). The resulting M16 variant displays high-affinity binding to TfR at physiological pH (7.4) while undergoing rapid dissociation at endosomal pH (5.5), a property that translates into significantly enhanced intracellular accumulation in cultured human cancer cells.

The foundational concept underlying this invention is the exploitation of histidine’s unique protonation behavior to create molecular switches that respond to changes in environmental pH. Histidine residues, when positioned within antigen-binding interfaces, can act as pH sensors: at neutral pH, they remain largely unprotonated and contribute favorably to binding interactions; upon acidification, protonation introduces positive charges that may disrupt hydrogen bonding networks, electrostatic complementarity, or steric fit, thereby weakening or abolishing binding. By saturating a defined CDR loop with histidine codons, the inventors created a diverse library of variants wherein multiple histidines could be introduced in various combinations and positions, enabling the identification of synergistic mutational patterns that maximize pH sensitivity without compromising neutral-pH affinity.

The parental scFv, H7, was selected as the starting scaffold due to its well-characterized binding to human TfR and prior evidence suggesting involvement of CDRH1 in antigen contact. Initial characterization confirmed that H7 exhibits modest intrinsic pH sensitivity, retaining approximately 56% of bound TfR after a 10-minute incubation at pH 5.5. To enhance this property, a histidine-saturation library was constructed by replacing the native CDRH1 sequence (residues VH30–VH39) with a degenerate oligonucleotide pool designed to allow histidine incorporation at every position while preserving flanking homology for yeast homologous recombination. The resulting library, comprising approximately 3 × 10⁷ clones, was displayed on the surface of Saccharomyces cerevisiae using the pCT-ESO vector backbone.

Screening was performed in two phases. First, the library was enriched for functional TfR binders at pH 7.4 through two rounds of FACS sorting, isolating clones that maintained robust surface expression and antigen recognition despite CDRH1 mutations. This yielded a functional pool (“F”) enriched for variants with preserved neutral-pH activity. Subsequently, this pool was subjected to a pH-shift selection protocol: yeast-displayed scFvs were saturated with recombinant human TfR at pH 7.4, then exposed to pH 5.5 buffer for 10 minutes to induce dissociation. Clones exhibiting minimal residual TfR binding after acidification were isolated into a “pH-sensitive” pool (“M”), while those retaining strong binding formed a “pH-insensitive” pool (“N”). Parallel isolation from the unenriched library produced an additional pH-insensitive group (“5P”).

Sequence analysis of individual clones revealed a clear mutational signature associated with pH sensitivity. All M-series clones contained three or more histidines concentrated in the central residues VH31–VH34 of CDRH1, often accompanied by proline substitutions at flanking positions (VH30 or VH35). In contrast, N and 5P clones lacked this histidine core and instead featured conservative substitutions such as arginine or asparagine at VH32/VH33, with histidines appearing only at terminal positions. These findings support the hypothesis that clustered histidines act cooperatively to amplify pH responsiveness, likely through cumulative electrostatic repulsion or conformational strain upon protonation.

Among the pH-sensitive candidates, M16 emerged as a lead variant based on its exceptional dissociation profile (>90% loss of TfR binding at pH 5.5) and retained sub-nanomolar affinity at pH 7.4 (Kd = 420 ± 100 pM). Soluble M16 was successfully expressed and purified from yeast supernatants, confirming that the CDRH1 mutations did not impair protein folding or secretion. Functional validation using bead-based binding assays corroborated the pH-sensitive behavior observed in yeast display: M16 retained only 44% of TfR binding after acidification, compared to 74% for H7.

When tested on SK-BR-3 human breast cancer cells—which express high levels of TfR—M16 demonstrated superior cellular association. Total cell-associated fluorescence (measured by flow cytometry after non-enzymatic detachment) was 1.5-fold higher for M16 than H7. More strikingly, the internalized fraction (quantified after trypsinization to remove surface-bound antibody) was 2.6-fold greater for M16. This enhancement was entirely dependent on endosomal acidification, as co-treatment with bafilomycin A1 eliminated the difference between M16 and H7, confirming that pH-triggered dissociation from TfR is the key driver of increased intracellular retention.

Confocal microscopy revealed that M16 accumulates in large, discrete intracellular vesicles that do not strongly co-localize with EEA1 (early endosome marker), LAMP1, or LAMP2 (late endosome/lysosome markers). Quantitative Pearson correlation coefficients confirmed significantly reduced co-localization with all three markers compared to H7 and N5, suggesting that M16 follows a non-canonical trafficking route that avoids deep lysosomal penetration. This property is highly advantageous for therapeutic applications where payload stability or prolonged intracellular action is desired.

The invention thus provides not only a novel pH-sensitive anti-TfR scFv with enhanced intracellular accumulation but also a generalizable methodology for engineering pH-responsive antibodies. The M16 sequence, its encoding nucleic acid, and derivatives thereof are useful as standalone therapeutics, targeting moieties for drug conjugates, or tools for studying endocytic trafficking. Moreover, the histidine-saturation mutagenesis strategy described herein can be adapted to other antibody scaffolds or receptor systems to achieve controlled dissociation in acidic microenvironments such as tumors, inflamed tissues, or intracellular organelles.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Construction of the Histidine-Saturation Library Targeting CDRH1 of scFv H7**

The parental anti-human TfR scFv, H7, was subcloned into the yeast surface display vector pCT-ESO to generate plasmid pESO-H7. To facilitate homologous recombination-based library construction, a unique SpeI restriction site followed by dual stop codons was introduced into the middle of the CDRH1 coding sequence (spanning residues VH30–VH39) via site-directed mutagenesis using primers H7sdmF and H7sdmR. The resulting plasmid, pESO-H7sdm, served as the acceptor vector. A 90-base mixed oligonucleotide (IP90T) was synthesized with degenerate codons (using S = G/C, R = A/G, Y = C/T, K = G/T, M = A/C, W = A/T) to allow histidine (CAT/CAC) incorporation at every position within CDRH1, while maintaining 30-base homology arms matching the wild-type sequence upstream and downstream of the mutation zone. This oligonucleotide was converted to double-stranded DNA and extended to 100 bp via PCR using primer IPCDH1ampF. A second PCR with primers IPCDH1ampF and IPCDH1ampR generated a 120-bp mutagenic cassette containing the full histidine-saturated CDRH1 flanked by homology regions. This cassette was co-transformed with SpeI-linearized pESO-H7sdm into Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain EBY100, yielding a library of approximately 3 × 10⁷ transformants, which substantially exceeded the theoretical diversity of 1.8 × 10⁵. Sequencing of 12 random clones confirmed diverse histidine incorporation throughout CDRH1.

**Example 2: FACS-Based Screening for pH-Sensitive Binders**

Yeast displaying the CDRH1his library were induced for scFv surface expression and first sorted at pH 7.4 to enrich for functional TfR binders. Cells were incubated with 50 nM recombinant human TfR (rhTfR), washed, and labeled with anti-c-myc (for scFv detection) and anti-hTfR (for antigen binding) antibodies conjugated to Alexa488 and Alexa647, respectively. Two rounds of sorting isolated the “F” pool, which exhibited strong TfR binding at neutral pH. This pool was then subjected to a pH-shift protocol: after rhTfR binding at pH 7.4, cells were incubated in pH 5.5 buffer for 10 minutes, quenched with ice-cold PBS, and labeled as before. Clones showing minimal Alexa647 signal (indicating dissociation) were sorted into the “M” pool, while those with high signal formed the “N” pool. Control sorts using wild-type H7 and irrelevant scFv D1.3 established gating boundaries. After two rounds of pH-shift sorting, pools M and N were plated, and individual colonies were screened for pH-dependent binding by repeating the dissociation assay and quantifying mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) ratios (pH 5.5 / pH 7.4).

**Example 3: Characterization of Lead Clones M16 and N5**

From the M and N pools, clones M16 and N5 were selected for detailed analysis. Both were subcloned into the soluble expression vector pRS316-GAL-4420 and secreted from yeast strain YVH10. Purified proteins were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and Western blotting, confirming high purity and correct size. Equilibrium binding affinities at pH 7.4 were measured by titrating rhTfR against yeast-displayed scFvs and fitting binding curves to a bimolecular model: H7 Kd = 160 ± 30 pM, N5 Kd = 190 ± 40 pM, M16 Kd = 420 ± 100 pM. pH-dependent dissociation was assessed using magnetic beads coated with anti-c-myc antibody to capture scFvs, followed by rhTfR binding and acid challenge. M16 retained 44 ± 5% of TfR binding at pH 5.5 versus 74 ± 3% for H7 (p < 0.001), while N5 retained 80 ± 4% (p < 0.05 vs. H7).

**Example 4: Cellular Association and Internalization Assays**

SK-BR-3 cells were treated with artificially dimerized scFvs (200 nM) for 2 hours at 37°C. Total cell-associated antibody was quantified by non-enzymatic detachment and flow cytometry, revealing 1.5-fold higher signal for M16 versus H7 (p < 0.001). Internalized fraction was measured after trypsinization to cleave surface TfR-scFv complexes: M16 showed 2.6-fold greater internalization than H7 (p < 0.001), with 87% of total M16 being intracellular versus 52% for H7. Bafilomycin A1 (200 nM) pretreatment abolished the M16 advantage, confirming pH-dependence.

**Example 5: Immunocytochemical Analysis of Intracellular Trafficking**

Cells treated with scFv dimers were fixed, permeabilized, and stained for EEA1, LAMP1, and LAMP2. Confocal imaging showed M16 accumulating in large vesicles distinct from H7/N5 puncta. Pearson co-localization coefficients were significantly lower for M16 with all markers (LAMP1: M16 = 0.25 ± 0.03 vs. H7 = 0.42 ± 0.04, p < 0.01), indicating diversion from canonical endolysosomal pathways.

These examples collectively demonstrate the successful engineering of a pH-sensitive anti-TfR scFv with enhanced intracellular accumulation, validating the core principles of the invention.
