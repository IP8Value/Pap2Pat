# DESCRIPTION

## EXAMPLES

### 1. Equipment

The present invention is implemented using a Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption/Ionization Time-of-Flight Mass Spectrometer (MALDI-TOF MS), which constitutes the core analytical instrumentation enabling the rapid, accurate, and reproducible detection of delta-toxin production in *Staphylococcus aureus* isolates directly from whole-cell preparations. The MALDI-TOF MS system employed in the practice of this invention comprises a laser source, an ion acceleration region, a flight tube under high vacuum, and a detector capable of measuring the time-of-flight of ionized molecules to determine their mass-to-charge ratio (m/z). In one embodiment, the Axima Assurance® mass spectrometer (Shimadzu Biotech) is utilized, operated under standard settings established for routine microbial identification in clinical microbiology laboratories. This instrument is controlled by dedicated software such as LaunchPad® (version 2.8.4.20081127, Shimadzu Biotech), which facilitates data acquisition, spectral processing, and peak identification. The system is calibrated using reference standards appropriate for bacterial identification, including *Escherichia coli* ATCC 8739, and further fine-tuned in the specific m/z range of interest—namely, 2800 to 3200 Thomson (Th)—using polyethylene glycol 3000 (PEG3000) dissolved in the matrix solution to ensure high-resolution detection of low-molecular-weight peptides such as delta-toxin.

In alternative embodiments, other commercially available MALDI-TOF MS platforms may be employed, provided they offer sufficient resolution and sensitivity in the 2800–3200 Th range. For confirmatory tandem mass spectrometry analyses, a MALDI-TOF/TOF instrument such as the Ultraflex II (Bruker Daltonics) may be used. This advanced configuration enables post-source decay (PSD) or LIFT® technology to fragment selected precursor ions, thereby generating sequence-specific fragment ions that allow unambiguous peptide identification through database matching. The TOF/TOF system operates in positive reflectron mode and utilizes α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamic acid (CHCA) as the matrix for sample co-crystallization. Data generated from such instruments are analyzed using bioinformatics tools such as Mascot® (Matrix Science), interfaced with curated protein databases like UniProtKB/SwissProt, to validate the identity of detected peaks based on theoretical fragmentation patterns and molecular weights.

The sample preparation hardware includes disposable or reusable target plates compatible with the MALDI source. In routine practice, Fleximass DS® disposable target plates (bioMérieux) are preferred due to their consistency and reduced risk of cross-contamination. These plates are designed to accommodate small-volume depositions (typically 1 µL) of either purified peptide solutions or bacterial smears. The matrix application is performed manually or via automated spotters, ensuring uniform crystallization of the analyte-matrix mixture, which is critical for reproducible ionization and signal intensity. The entire workflow—from colony picking to spectral acquisition—is designed to integrate seamlessly into existing clinical laboratory infrastructure, requiring no specialized equipment beyond what is already deployed for microbial identification via MALDI-TOF MS.

Environmental controls are maintained throughout the process: bacterial cultures are incubated at 35±2°C under aerobic conditions on standardized agar media, including trypticase soy agar with horse blood (TSH), Columbia agar with 5% sheep blood (COS), chocolate agar with Polyvitex® (PVX), CHROM ID SA® chromogenic medium, or GP agar. The robustness of the method has been validated across these diverse media, confirming that growth substrate does not significantly alter delta-toxin detection. Incubation durations of 18, 24, and 48 hours have been tested, with consistent results observed at all time points, although a slight reduction in peak intensity at 48 hours suggests optimal analysis occurs at 24 hours post-inoculation. The equipment setup thus encompasses not only the mass spectrometer but also standardized culture systems, calibrated pipettes, microcentrifuges for optional purification steps, and environmental chambers for controlled bacterial growth—all of which contribute to the reliability and clinical applicability of the disclosed method.

Furthermore, the integration of software algorithms for automated peak detection within the 3000–3040 Th window enhances throughput and reduces operator-dependent variability. Custom scripts or vendor-provided modules can be configured to flag spectra lacking the 3005±5 Th or 3035±5 Th peaks, thereby providing an immediate phenotypic readout of *agr* functionality without additional testing. This computational layer transforms the MALDI-TOF MS from a mere identification tool into a diagnostic platform capable of reporting virulence-associated phenotypes in parallel with species confirmation. The equipment ecosystem supporting the invention is therefore both modular and scalable, adaptable to high-volume hospital laboratories as well as reference centers engaged in epidemiological surveillance of *S. aureus* pathogenesis.

### 2. Methodology

The methodology of the present invention provides a novel, non-invasive, and high-throughput approach for assessing the functional status of the accessory gene regulator (*agr*) system in *Staphylococcus aureus* by detecting the presence or absence of delta-toxin—a 26-amino acid peptide encoded within the RNAIII transcript of the *agr* locus—directly from whole-cell MALDI-TOF mass spectra generated during routine microbial identification. The method begins with the cultivation of a *S. aureus* isolate on a solid agar medium, preferably trypticase soy agar supplemented with horse blood (TSH), under standard aerobic conditions at 35±2°C for 18 to 24 hours. Following incubation, a single colony or a thin smear of bacterial cells is transferred directly onto a MALDI target plate using a sterile loop or pipette tip, in accordance with the “smear method” commonly employed in clinical microbiology laboratories for species identification. No cell lysis, protein extraction, or chromatographic separation is required, thereby preserving the simplicity and speed of the routine workflow.

Once the bacterial sample is deposited, it is overlaid with a saturated solution of a suitable MALDI matrix, typically α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamic acid (CHCA) in 50% acetonitrile and 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid or formic acid. The plate is then allowed to air-dry at room temperature for approximately 15 minutes, facilitating co-crystallization of the bacterial proteins and peptides with the matrix. The prepared target is inserted into the MALDI-TOF MS instrument, which is pre-calibrated using standard microbial calibration protocols, including the use of *E. coli* ATCC 8739 and, optionally, PEG3000 for enhanced accuracy in the 2800–3200 Th range. The instrument acquires mass spectra in linear or reflectron mode over an m/z range of 2000 to 20,000 Th, as is standard for bacterial identification.

The resulting spectrum is then interrogated specifically for the presence of a peak at **3005±5 Thomson**, which corresponds to the monoprotonated form of wild-type, N-formylated delta-toxin (theoretical mass: 3005.3 Th). Additionally, the method accounts for a naturally occurring allelic variant of delta-toxin resulting from a glycine-to-serine substitution at position 10 (G10S), which shifts the molecular mass to **3035±5 Thomson** (theoretical mass: 3034.8 Th). The detection of either the 3005±5 Th or 3035±5 Th peak is interpreted as evidence of a functional *agr* system and active delta-toxin production. Conversely, the complete absence of peaks within the 3000–3040 Th window is indicative of *agr* dysfunction, which may arise from mutations in the *agr* locus, deletions in the RNAIII transcript, or regulatory defects that abolish RNAIII expression.

To validate the specificity of this spectral signature, the method may incorporate confirmatory steps in research or reference laboratory settings. These include MALDI-TOF/TOF tandem mass spectrometry, wherein the ion corresponding to the 3005 or 3035 Th peak is isolated and fragmented to generate a peptide sequence fingerprint that is matched against the UniProtKB/SwissProt database using Mascot® or equivalent search engines. A statistically significant match (e.g., Mascot score >80) confirms the identity of the peptide as delta-toxin or its G10S variant. Additionally, Northern blot analysis targeting RNAIII can be performed on representative isolates lacking the delta-toxin peak to verify the absence of *agr* effector transcript, thereby establishing a direct molecular correlation between spectral phenotype and *agr* genotype.

The methodology has been rigorously tested for robustness across multiple variables. Reproducibility was confirmed by analyzing isogenic strain pairs—comprising wild-type *agr*-positive strains (e.g., RN6390) and their *agr*-null derivatives (e.g., RN6911 or LUG950)—which consistently showed the presence or absence, respectively, of the 3005 Th peak under identical culture and analytical conditions. Intermediate precision was demonstrated by repeating measurements at 18, 24, and 48 hours of incubation, with stable detection observed at all time points. Furthermore, the method proved insensitive to variations in culture media, as identical results were obtained when strains were grown on TSH, COS, PVX, CHROM ID SA®, or GP agar, underscoring its adaptability to diverse laboratory protocols.

In clinical practice, the method is applied prospectively to consecutive *S. aureus* isolates recovered from patient specimens. After routine identification by MALDI-TOF MS, the raw spectral data are retrospectively or concurrently analyzed for the delta-toxin peak(s). This dual-use of existing spectral data eliminates the need for additional sample handling or retesting, making the approach cost-effective and immediately deployable in any facility equipped with MALDI-TOF MS for microbial diagnostics. The phenotypic classification—delta-toxin positive (functional *agr*) versus delta-toxin negative (*agr*-defective)—is then correlated with clinical metadata, such as infection chronicity, antibiotic resistance profiles, and patient outcomes, to inform therapeutic decisions and infection control strategies.

Notably, the method also enables the detection of *in vivo* evolutionary shifts in *agr* functionality. By comparing serial isolates from the same patient over time, clinicians can identify cases where an initially delta-toxin-positive strain evolves into a delta-toxin-negative variant, suggesting selection pressure favoring *agr* dysfunction during persistent infection—particularly in contexts such as cystic fibrosis, diabetic foot ulcers, or long-term biomaterial colonization. This dynamic monitoring capability represents a significant advance over static genotypic assays, which cannot distinguish between silent mutations and actual loss of regulatory function.

Finally, the methodology supports antimicrobial stewardship by providing an early phenotypic indicator of glycopeptide resistance. Since *agr* dysfunction is strongly associated with heterogeneous glycopeptide-intermediate *S. aureus* (hGISA) and glycopeptide-intermediate *S. aureus* (GISA) phenotypes, the absence of the delta-toxin peak serves as a rapid warning sign—available within minutes of identification—that may prompt earlier initiation of alternative therapies or more stringent susceptibility testing, potentially improving patient outcomes in severe staphylococcal infections.

### 3. Results

The implementation of the disclosed methodology yielded robust, clinically actionable results that establish whole-cell MALDI-TOF MS as a reliable platform for phenotyping *Staphylococcus aureus agr* functionality through delta-toxin detection. Initial validation experiments using purified synthetic delta-toxin demonstrated a clear, dose-dependent peak at **3005±1 Thomson**, precisely matching the theoretical monoisotopic mass of the N-formylated 26-amino acid peptide (3005.3 Th). This peak was detectable at concentrations as low as 1 femtogram and exhibited linear intensity increases up to 500 nanograms, confirming the assay’s high sensitivity. Subsequent testing of isogenic strain pairs—comprising wild-type *agr*-positive strains (RN6390) and their genetically engineered *agr*-null derivatives (RN6911 and LUG950)—revealed a complete and consistent absence of the 3005 Th peak in the knockout strains, thereby confirming the specificity of the spectral signature to *agr*-mediated delta-toxin expression.

When applied to a prospective cohort of **168 non-duplicate clinical *S. aureus* isolates** collected from three university hospitals in Lyon between November 2010 and March 2011, the method classified isolates into three distinct phenotypic groups: **139 (82.7%)** exhibited the canonical 3005±5 Th peak (wild-type delta-toxin), **12 (7.1%)** displayed a peak at **3035±5 Th**, and **17 (10.1%)** lacked any peak in the 3000–3040 Th range. Sequencing of the *hld* gene (encoding delta-toxin) in seven of the 3035 Th-positive isolates revealed a consistent glycine-to-serine substitution at position 10 (G10S), a known allelic variant that increases the peptide mass by 29.5 Da, fully accounting for the observed 30-Th shift. MALDI-TOF/TOF analysis with Mascot® database searching confirmed these assignments, yielding high-confidence scores of 86.3 for wild-type delta-toxin and 157.4 for the G10S variant. Functional validation via synergistic hemolysis assays—where delta-toxin activity is detected by its interaction with beta-toxin from *Staphylococcus intermedius*—showed hemolytic synergy in all 3005 Th- and 3035 Th-positive isolates tested, but none in the 17 delta-toxin-negative strains, further corroborating the biological relevance of the spectral findings.

Critically, Northern blot analysis of RNAIII—the central effector of the *agr* system—in a subset of delta-toxin-negative isolates confirmed the complete absence of RNAIII transcripts, establishing a direct mechanistic link between the missing MALDI peak and *agr* regulatory failure. Genotyping of these 17 *agr*-defective isolates by DNA microarray revealed significant genetic diversity, spanning **three *agr* types** and **four clonal complexes (CCs)**, including six isolates belonging to the epidemic Lyon Clone (ST8, SCC*mec*IV), thereby ruling out clonal expansion as the cause of the observed phenotype and affirming that *agr* dysfunction arises independently across lineages.

Clinically, the delta-toxin-negative phenotype was **significantly associated with chronic infections** (p = 0.005), including persistent lung colonization in cystic fibrosis patients, chronic diabetic foot osteomyelitis, and long-standing surgical site infections. However, no significant associations were found with mere colonization versus active infection (p = 0.541), presence of implanted biomaterials (p = 0.470), or 30-day in-hospital mortality (p = 0.574). Importantly, **methicillin resistance (MRSA)** was more prevalent among delta-toxin-negative isolates (p = 0.035), but multivariate logistic regression revealed this to be a confounding factor linked to healthcare-associated chronic infections rather than a direct driver of *agr* dysfunction.

A striking association emerged with **glycopeptide resistance**: among 28 well-characterized GISA/hGISA strains (from the French National Reference Center), **9 (32.1%)** were delta-toxin-negative, compared to only **9 of 126 (7.1%)** non-GISA isolates (p = 0.001). Multivariate analysis confirmed that both **chronic infection status** (odds ratio [OR] = 3.21; 95% CI: 1.01–10.24; p = 0.048) and **glycopeptide resistance** (OR = 3.91; 95% CI: 1.20–12.76; p = 0.023) were independent predictors of delta-toxin deficiency. This finding has immediate clinical utility, as MALDI-TOF MS can flag potential glycopeptide resistance **hours before conventional antibiograms are finalized**.

Most compellingly, longitudinal analysis of serial isolates from seven patients demonstrated **within-host evolution** from delta-toxin-positive to delta-toxin-negative phenotypes, with indistinguishable genotypes confirming clonal descent. In two of these cases, prior glycopeptide exposure was documented, suggesting antibiotic-driven selection of *agr*-defective variants. These results provide direct evidence that *agr* dysfunction is not merely a static trait but a dynamically selected adaptation during persistent infection.

Collectively, these results validate the invention as a **rapid, accurate, and clinically informative** method for *agr* phenotyping, transforming routine MALDI-TOF MS from a taxonomic tool into a functional diagnostic platform that enhances prognostic stratification, guides antimicrobial therapy, and illuminates the pathoadaptive trajectories of *S. aureus* in human disease.
