# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biopharmaceutical manufacturing, specifically to the production of monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) using recombinant Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells. More particularly, the invention provides a novel promoter derived from the Chinese hamster heat shock protein family A (Hsp70) member 5 gene (hspa5), which demonstrates enhanced transcriptional activity during the late phase of fed-batch culture—a critical period in industrial antibody production where conventional promoters often exhibit diminished performance. The invention further encompasses expression vectors comprising this promoter, stable CHO cell lines engineered to express monoclonal antibodies under the control of this promoter, and methods for producing high yields of immunoglobulins, including IgG1, IgG2, and IgG4 subclasses, with improved specific productivity and titer over extended culture durations. This technology addresses a long-standing challenge in the biopharmaceutical industry: the decline in recombinant protein expression during the stationary and death phases of mammalian cell culture, which directly impacts manufacturing efficiency, cost, and product consistency. The hspa5-derived promoter of the present invention is uniquely responsive to endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and the unfolded protein response (UPR), enabling dynamic upregulation of transgene expression in synchrony with the cellular protein-folding capacity, thereby maintaining high-level antibody production without inducing excessive cytotoxicity. The invention is applicable across a broad spectrum of therapeutic antibody formats and is compatible with standard good manufacturing practice (GMP) workflows, making it a significant advancement in the development of robust, high-yielding bioprocesses for the commercial-scale production of biologics.

## BACKGROUND ART

The production of recombinant therapeutic proteins, particularly monoclonal antibodies (mAbs), has become a cornerstone of modern medicine, with over 120 such products approved for clinical use since the first murine antibody, Muromonab-CD3, received regulatory approval in 1986. Among the various host cell systems employed for large-scale mAb manufacturing, Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells have emerged as the industry standard due to their ability to perform complex post-translational modifications that are essential for the biological activity, stability, and safety of humanized or fully human antibodies. Furthermore, CHO cells exhibit robust growth characteristics in suspension culture under serum-free conditions, are amenable to genetic engineering, and have a well-established regulatory history that facilitates compliance with stringent good manufacturing practice (GMP) requirements. Despite these advantages, achieving consistently high and stable titers of recombinant antibodies in CHO-based expression systems remains a significant technical and economic challenge. Productivity is influenced by a multitude of factors, including the genomic integration site and copy number of the transgene, the strength and stability of the promoter driving its expression, the efficiency of mRNA translation and protein folding, and the composition of the culture medium.

Extensive efforts have been directed toward optimizing each of these parameters. At the genomic level, strategies such as targeted integration into "hot spots" or the use of site-specific recombinases have been explored to ensure consistent transgene expression. Transcriptional regulation has been a major focus, with numerous natural and synthetic promoters evaluated for their ability to drive high-level, sustained expression in CHO cells. Commonly used promoters include viral elements such as the human cytomegalovirus (hCMV) immediate-early promoter, the simian virus 40 early (SV40E) promoter, and the Rous sarcoma virus (RSV) long terminal repeat (LTR). While these promoters often yield high initial expression levels, they are frequently subject to silencing over time, particularly in the later stages of prolonged fed-batch cultures, which are the standard mode of operation in industrial bioreactors. To circumvent this limitation, endogenous mammalian promoters have also been employed, such as those derived from the mouse phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK) gene, the human ubiquitin C (UBC) gene, and the human elongation factor-1α (hEF1α) gene. The CHO-derived elongation factor-1α promoter (CHEF1α) has also been developed and used with some success. Additionally, artificial promoters have been constructed by combining enhancer elements from diverse sources, such as the fusion of CMV sequences with Drosophila-derived elements, to create hybrid constructs with superior performance. Inducible systems, notably the Tetracycline (Tet)-On/Off system, offer temporal control over transgene expression but add complexity to the manufacturing process and are not universally adopted for large-scale production.

A persistent problem across all these promoter systems is the observed decline in recombinant protein expression during the late culture phase, typically after day 7–10 in a standard 14-day fed-batch process. This decline is attributed to a combination of factors, including nutrient depletion, accumulation of toxic metabolites (e.g., lactate and ammonia), oxidative stress, and the onset of cellular senescence or apoptosis. Critically, the overexpression of complex secretory proteins like mAbs places a significant burden on the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), leading to the accumulation of misfolded or unfolded proteins, a condition known as ER stress. In response, cells activate the unfolded protein response (UPR), a conserved signaling network that aims to restore proteostasis by attenuating global protein synthesis, upregulating ER chaperones and foldases, and enhancing the degradation of misfolded proteins via ER-associated degradation (ERAD). If the stress is too severe or prolonged, the UPR can trigger apoptotic pathways, leading to a rapid decline in viable cell density and, consequently, product titer. The heat shock protein family A (Hsp70) member 5, also known as BiP or GRP78, is a central regulator of the UPR and functions as a master chaperone in the ER lumen. Its expression is tightly controlled and is strongly induced under conditions of ER stress. Previous studies have demonstrated that the overexpression of Hspa5 and other UPR-related factors can improve the folding capacity of CHO cells and, in some cases, enhance mAb productivity. For instance, Kamal et al. (2015) reported a correlation between high mAb productivity and elevated expression of UPR genes, including hspa5, in high-producing CHO clones. Pybus et al. (2014) showed that co-expression of Hspa5, ATF6c, and XBP1-s could increase mAb yields, albeit at the cost of reduced cell proliferation. Kober et al. have even utilized the hspa5 promoter for the selection of high-producing cell clones, leveraging its stress-inducible nature. However, despite these insights, the hspa5 promoter itself has not been systematically developed or optimized as a primary driver for recombinant mAb production in industrial settings. The prevailing assumption has been that stress-inducible promoters are unsuitable for constitutive, high-yield production because their activity is contingent upon a pathological state of the cell. Consequently, the biopharmaceutical industry has continued to rely on strong constitutive promoters like hEF1α, which, while effective in the exponential growth phase, fail to maintain expression as the culture progresses and ER stress inevitably accumulates. This gap in the art represents a significant opportunity for innovation, as a promoter that can harness the natural adaptive response of the cell to its own protein production burden could provide a self-regulating, high-performance expression system that is both more productive and more robust than current technologies.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The primary technical problem addressed by the present invention is the insufficient and unstable expression of recombinant monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells during the late phase of fed-batch culture, which is the standard operational mode for industrial biopharmaceutical manufacturing. Conventional promoters, whether of viral or mammalian origin, typically exhibit a peak in activity during the exponential growth phase of the culture but suffer from a significant decline in transcriptional output as the culture enters the stationary and death phases. This decline is multifactorial, stemming from general cellular senescence, metabolic exhaustion, and, most critically, the accumulation of endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress due to the high burden of recombinant protein synthesis and secretion. The resulting activation of the unfolded protein response (UPR) can lead to global translational attenuation and, if unresolved, to apoptotic cell death, thereby curtailing the production window and limiting the final achievable product titer. Existing strategies to mitigate this problem, such as media optimization, process control, or the use of stronger constitutive promoters, have reached a plateau of diminishing returns and often fail to address the core issue of the disconnect between the static nature of the promoter and the dynamic, stress-laden environment of a high-producing cell culture in its later stages. There is therefore a pressing need in the art for a novel promoter system that can not only sustain high-level transcription throughout the entire culture duration but can also dynamically respond to the physiological state of the cell, particularly the level of ER stress, to maintain optimal production without compromising cell viability.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention provides a solution to this long-standing problem through the identification, isolation, and optimization of a novel promoter derived from the upstream regulatory region of the Chinese hamster heat shock protein family A (Hsp70) member 5 gene (hspa5). Contrary to the conventional wisdom that stress-inducible promoters are unsuitable for high-yield production, the inventors have discovered that the hspa5 promoter (Hspa5p) exhibits a unique and highly advantageous expression profile in the context of mAb manufacturing. Specifically, through comprehensive transcriptome analysis of CHO-K1 cells under industrially relevant fed-batch culture conditions, the inventors identified that the endogenous hspa5 gene is not only highly expressed but its transcription level actually increases during the latter period of the culture, coinciding with the peak of ER stress. This observation led to the hypothesis that Hspa5p could be repurposed as a driver for recombinant transgenes, creating a positive feedback loop where the act of producing the mAb itself induces the very promoter that drives its expression. The invention provides a defined, minimal Hspa5p sequence of approximately 0.6 kilobases (kb) in length, which has been shown to be sufficient for high-level, sustained transcriptional activity. This minimal promoter contains all the essential cis-regulatory elements, including a TATA-box, CpG island, CCAAT-box, GC-box, cAMP-responsive elements (CREB), and ER stress-responsive elements (ERSE), which are recognized by key transcription factors of the UPR pathway such as ATF4, ATF6, and XBP1. The invention further provides expression vectors in which the coding sequences for both the heavy and light chains of a monoclonal antibody are operably linked to this Hspa5p. Stable CHO cell lines generated using these vectors demonstrate a remarkable ability to maintain high specific productivity (SPR) and achieve significantly higher final mAb titers compared to identical cell lines using the industry-standard hEF1α promoter. Crucially, this enhanced productivity is achieved without a detrimental effect on cell growth or viability, suggesting that the Hspa5p-driven system is in a state of balanced homeostasis, where the induction of the transgene is matched by the concurrent upregulation of the cell's intrinsic protein-folding machinery. The invention is broadly applicable to various IgG subclasses, including IgG1, IgG2, and IgG4Pro, demonstrating its general utility for the production of a wide range of therapeutic antibodies.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The present invention offers a multitude of significant and unexpected advantages over the prior art. First and foremost, it provides a substantial increase in the final yield of monoclonal antibodies from CHO cell cultures. By utilizing the Hspa5p, the specific productivity (amount of antibody produced per cell per day) is maintained at a high level throughout the entire fed-batch culture, particularly during the critical late phase (days 10–14) where conventional systems falter. This results in a demonstrably higher integrated viable cell density (IVCD) and, consequently, a higher final titer, which directly translates to greater manufacturing efficiency and lower cost of goods. Second, the invention provides a self-regulating expression system that is inherently more robust and resilient. Because the Hspa5p is activated by the same ER stress that is a natural byproduct of high-level mAb synthesis, the system creates a dynamic equilibrium. As the cell produces more antibody and experiences more stress, the promoter is further induced, but this induction is accompanied by the natural upregulation of ER chaperones like Hspa5 itself, which helps to mitigate the stress and prevent the activation of apoptotic pathways. This built-in feedback mechanism leads to a more stable and predictable culture performance, reducing batch-to-batch variability. Third, the invention is broadly applicable across different IgG subclasses and is not limited by the specific structural features of the antibody, such as its disulfide bond pattern. This universality makes it a powerful platform technology for a wide array of therapeutic programs. Fourth, the minimal 0.6 kb Hspa5p sequence is compact and easy to integrate into standard expression vectors, facilitating its adoption into existing cell line development and manufacturing workflows without requiring significant process changes. Finally, the use of an endogenous CHO-derived promoter may offer regulatory advantages, as it avoids the potential safety concerns or intellectual property complexities sometimes associated with viral or heterologous promoters. In summary, the invention represents a paradigm shift in recombinant protein expression, moving from a static, one-size-fits-all promoter approach to a dynamic, physiology-responsive system that leverages the cell's own adaptive mechanisms to achieve superior performance.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is based on the surprising discovery that the promoter of the Chinese hamster hspa5 gene, which encodes the ER-resident chaperone BiP/GRP78, is a superior driver for recombinant monoclonal antibody production in CHO cells, particularly in the late stages of a fed-batch culture. The following detailed description outlines the key components of the invention, the methods for its implementation, and the experimental data that support its efficacy and advantages.

The core of the invention is the nucleic acid sequence of the Hspa5 promoter (Hspa5p). In one embodiment, the Hspa5p is a DNA sequence of at least 500 base pairs (bp) in length, located immediately upstream of the transcription start site (TSS) of the Chinese hamster hspa5 gene. In a preferred embodiment, the Hspa5p is a sequence of approximately 600 bp, extending from about -600 to +1 relative to the TSS. The precise TSS was determined by alignment with the known mRNA sequence (GenBank Accession No. NM_001246739.2). This ~600 bp region has been identified as the minimal functional unit that retains the full promoter activity and the characteristic late-phase enhancement. The sequence of this minimal promoter is provided in the accompanying sequence listing. This region is rich in cis-regulatory elements that are critical for its function. In silico analysis using GENETYX software confirmed the presence of a canonical TATA-box, a CpG island, a CCAAT-box, and multiple GC-boxes, all of which are hallmarks of a strong RNA polymerase II promoter. More importantly, this region also contains several ER stress-responsive elements (ERSE), which are the binding sites for transcription factors that are activated during the unfolded protein response (UPR), such as ATF6 and XBP1. The presence of these elements explains the promoter's unique responsiveness to the physiological state of the cell.

The invention provides an expression vector comprising this Hspa5p. The vector is a recombinant DNA molecule designed for the stable integration and expression of a transgene in a mammalian host cell, preferably a CHO cell. The vector comprises, in operative linkage, the Hspa5p, a nucleotide sequence encoding a polypeptide of interest, and a polyadenylation signal. In the context of mAb production, the polypeptide of interest is typically an immunoglobulin heavy chain or light chain. Therefore, a complete expression system for a full-length mAb would require two separate expression vectors—one for the heavy chain and one for the light chain—each containing its own Hspa5p, or a single bicistronic vector where both chains are expressed from a single transcript, possibly separated by an internal ribosome entry site (IRES) or a 2A self-cleaving peptide sequence. The vector backbone can be any suitable plasmid commonly used in mammalian cell biology, such as those derived from pUC or pBR322, and will also contain a selectable marker gene, such as a gene conferring resistance to Geneticin (G418), hygromycin, or puromycin, to allow for the selection of stably transfected cells. The expression vector is constructed using standard molecular cloning techniques, such as restriction enzyme digestion and ligation, or more modern methods like Gibson assembly or Golden Gate cloning, to insert the Hspa5p sequence upstream of the transgene coding sequence.

The invention further provides a host cell, specifically a CHO cell, that has been genetically modified to contain and express a transgene under the control of the Hspa5p. The host cell can be any CHO sub-line, such as CHO-K1, CHO-S, or CHO-DG44, but in the exemplary embodiments, CHO-K1 cells were used. The stable cell line is generated by transfecting the host CHO cells with the expression vector(s) described above, followed by a selection process using the appropriate antibiotic to kill non-transfected cells. The resulting pool of resistant cells, known as a "stable pool," can be used directly for production, or it can be subjected to a clonal isolation procedure to derive a monoclonal cell line with optimal productivity and stability. Clonal isolation can be performed using limiting dilution, fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), or semi-solid media-based colony picking, as described in the methods section of the research paper. The resulting stable cell lines, whether polyclonal or monoclonal, are characterized by their ability to produce high levels of the recombinant protein, with a specific productivity that is maintained or even increases during the latter half of a standard fed-batch culture.

The invention also provides a method for producing a recombinant protein, specifically a monoclonal antibody, by culturing the aforementioned host cell under conditions that permit the expression of the transgene. The method involves inoculating a culture vessel, which can range from a small shake flask to a large industrial bioreactor, with the engineered CHO cell line. The cells are grown in a chemically defined, serum-free medium that is optimized for high-density suspension culture. A fed-batch process is employed, where a concentrated nutrient feed is added periodically to the culture to replenish consumed nutrients and extend the culture longevity. The culture is maintained at a temperature of 37°C, a pH of around 7.0, and a dissolved oxygen level of approximately 50% air saturation, with agitation and aeration to ensure proper mixing and gas exchange. Over the course of the culture, which typically lasts 12 to 14 days, the cells experience increasing levels of ER stress due to the burden of mAb production. This stress activates the UPR, which in turn binds to the ERSE elements in the Hspa5p, leading to a sustained or enhanced transcription of the mAb genes. The recombinant antibody is then harvested from the culture supernatant at the end of the run and purified using standard downstream processing techniques, such as protein A affinity chromatography.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Identification of the Hspa5 Gene as a Candidate for a High-Expression Promoter**

To discover a novel promoter with sustained activity, the inventors performed a comprehensive transcriptome analysis on two different clones of an IgG1-producing CHO-K1 cell line. These clones were cultured in two different commercially available, chemically defined media (G13 and CD DA1) in a scaled-down, stirred-tank bioreactor system to mimic industrial manufacturing conditions. Cells were harvested on days 4, 7, 9, 11, and 14 of the culture, and total RNA was extracted for RNA-Seq analysis. The gene expression levels were quantified as Reads Per Kilobase per Million mapped reads (RPKM). On day 4, the top 20 most highly expressed genes were identified. As expected, many were classic housekeeping genes involved in basic cellular processes, such as ribosomal proteins (rps14, rps11, rplp0, etc.), glycolysis (gapdh, pkm), and the cytoskeleton (actβ). The eukaryotic elongation factor 1 alpha (ef1α) gene, whose promoter is commonly used in expression systems, was also in this list. However, when the expression profiles of these top 20 genes were tracked over the entire 14-day culture, a striking pattern emerged for the hspa5 gene. While the expression of most other genes declined steadily over time, the RPKM value for hspa5 increased dramatically, becoming one of the most highly expressed transcripts by the end of the culture. This unique late-phase upregulation made hspa5 a prime candidate for a novel promoter.

**Example 2: Functional Validation of the Hspa5 Promoter in Reporter Assays**

To test the promoter activity of the upstream regions of the top 20 genes, a 3-kb fragment upstream of each gene's start codon was cloned into the pGL4.10 luciferase reporter vector. These constructs were transiently transfected into CHO-K1 cells, and luciferase activity was measured as a proxy for promoter strength. The human EF1α promoter (hEF1αp) was used as a positive control. The results showed that the hspa5 promoter (Hspa5p) and the hEF1αp were the two strongest promoters in this transient assay. Based on its unique expression profile from the transcriptome analysis, Hspa5p was selected for further study. Stable pools of CHO cells expressing the luciferase reporter under the control of either Hspa5p or hEF1αp were then generated. In a 14-day fed-batch culture, the luciferase activity driven by Hspa5p was significantly higher than that driven by hEF1αp, especially on days 12 and 14, confirming that the Hspa5p maintains its activity in the late culture phase.

**Example 3: Application of Hspa5p for Monoclonal Antibody Production**

To evaluate the Hspa5p for its intended purpose, stable CHO cell pools were created that expressed a full-length IgG1 antibody, with both the heavy and light chain genes under the control of the Hspa5p. An identical cell pool using the hEF1αp was used as a control. The cultures were run in parallel for 14 days. Quantitative PCR analysis showed that the transcription level of the IgG1 heavy chain gene was significantly higher in the Hspa5p-driven cells during the late culture phase (day 12). This translated directly into a higher specific productivity (SPR) and a final IgG1 titer that was approximately 1.5 to 2-fold higher than the control. Importantly, the cell growth and viability profiles were nearly identical between the two groups, indicating that the enhanced productivity was not achieved at the expense of cell health.

**Example 4: Optimization of the Hspa5 Promoter Sequence**

To define the minimal functional region of the Hspa5p, a series of truncation mutants were created, ranging from the full 3.0 kb fragment down to 0.6 kb. These truncated promoters were used to drive the expression of an IgG1 antibody in stable CHO pools. The results of the fed-batch culture showed that the 0.6 kb fragment was not only sufficient but actually yielded the highest productivity among all the constructs tested. Longer fragments, including a 2.0-2.5 kb region that showed some sequence conservation, did not contribute to promoter activity and may even have contained repressive elements. In silico analysis confirmed that the 0.6 kb region contained all the necessary core promoter elements and the critical ERSE motifs. Furthermore, the promoter was found to be functionally conserved across species; the 1.0 kb upstream regions from human, mouse, and rat hspa5 genes all supported comparable levels of mAb production in CHO cells, reinforcing the idea that the key regulatory elements are within this short, conserved segment.

**Example 5: Broad Applicability to Different IgG Subclasses**

To test the generality of the Hspa5p system, the inventors expressed three different IgG subclasses—IgG1, IgG2, and IgG4Pro—in CHO cells using the 0.6 kb Hspa5p. Each of these subclasses has a distinct disulfide bond architecture, which can influence their folding kinetics and the level of ER stress they induce. In all three cases, the Hspa5p-driven cell lines showed a significant increase in both specific productivity and final titer compared to their hEF1αp-driven counterparts. Quantitative PCR confirmed that the transcription of the heavy chain gene was higher under Hspa5p control for all subclasses. Notably, the expression level of the endogenous hspa5 gene was not significantly different between the different IgG-producing cell lines, suggesting that the Hspa5p is a reliable and consistent driver regardless of the specific antibody being produced.

**Example 6: Mechanistic Insight into Hspa5p Activity**

To understand the mechanism behind the superior performance of the Hspa5p, the inventors investigated the relationship between mAb productivity and the expression of UPR-related genes. They analyzed five different monoclonal cell lines that all expressed the same IgG1 under the Hspa5p but exhibited a range of productivities. A strong positive correlation was found between the specific productivity (SPR) and the transcription levels of key UPR sensors and effectors, including PERK, IRE1, and ATF6. Similarly, the level of mAb aggregates (a sign of misfolding) correlated with the expression of ER chaperones like GRP94 and ERdj3. This data supports a model in which the mAb production itself induces ER stress, which activates the UPR, which in turn binds to the ERSE in the Hspa5p, creating a positive feedback loop that sustains high-level transcription. This loop is balanced by the concurrent upregulation of the cell's folding capacity, preventing a catastrophic collapse into apoptosis. In a final experiment, the inventors tested whether adding exogenous ER stress inducers (like DTT or tunicamycin) could further boost production from the Hspa5p. While these chemicals did strongly induce the endogenous hspa5 gene, they did not increase the transcription of the mAb gene from the Hspa5p and, in fact, were toxic to the cells. This confirms that the natural, product-induced ER stress is the optimal trigger for the Hspa5p, and that the system is finely tuned for maximum productivity without external perturbation.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention has direct and significant industrial applicability in the field of biopharmaceutical manufacturing. The primary application is in the production of therapeutic monoclonal antibodies, which represent a multi-billion dollar market and are used to treat a wide array of diseases, including cancer, autoimmune disorders, and infectious diseases. By providing a novel promoter system that can increase final product titers by 1.5 to 2-fold or more, the invention offers a clear and immediate path to improving the efficiency and economics of existing manufacturing processes. A higher titer means that the same amount of drug substance can be produced in a smaller bioreactor volume, or a larger amount can be produced in the same facility, leading to substantial cost savings in terms of capital expenditure, raw materials, labor, and facility footprint. The invention is readily implementable within the current industry infrastructure. The expression vectors can be constructed using standard molecular biology techniques, and the stable cell lines can be generated using established cell line development platforms that are already in place at every major biopharmaceutical company. The fed-batch culture process itself does not require any modification; the same media, feeds, and operating parameters can be used. The fact that the Hspa5p is derived from the host cell’s own genome may also streamline the regulatory filing process, as it avoids the use of viral sequences that sometimes require additional safety testing. Beyond its immediate application for IgG production, the Hspa5p system is also highly adaptable for the production of other complex recombinant proteins that are prone to causing ER stress, such as bispecific antibodies, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), Fc-fusion proteins, and other secreted glycoproteins. The self-regulating nature of the promoter, which links transgene expression to the cell’s protein-folding capacity, makes it a particularly attractive platform for expressing difficult-to-express or aggregation-prone molecules. In summary, the invention provides a powerful, versatile, and economically impactful tool for the biopharmaceutical industry to enhance the production of life-saving protein therapeutics.
