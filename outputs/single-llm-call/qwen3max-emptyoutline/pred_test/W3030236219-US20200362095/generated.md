# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of polymeric materials for biomedical applications has witnessed significant advances over the past several decades, particularly in the design and synthesis of macromolecular architectures capable of controlled drug delivery, tissue engineering, and immunomodulation. Among these, block copolymers have emerged as a cornerstone of modern biomaterials science due to their ability to self-assemble into well-defined nanostructures in aqueous environments. Linear diblock and triblock copolymers, especially those composed of hydrophilic and hydrophobic segments, have been extensively studied for their capacity to form micelles, vesicles, and physical hydrogels that can encapsulate therapeutic agents and release them in a sustained or stimuli-responsive manner. However, despite the versatility of linear architectures, they often suffer from limitations such as high critical micelle concentrations (CMCs), limited control over domain sizes, and structural instability under physiological conditions. These shortcomings have motivated the exploration of more complex macromolecular topologies that offer enhanced functionality, stability, and tunability.

Bottlebrush polymers represent one such advanced architecture, characterized by a densely grafted backbone with side chains extending outward like bristles on a brush. This unique topology imparts distinct physical and chemical properties compared to linear analogues, including expanded chain conformations, reduced chain entanglement, and precise spatial organization of functional domains. The synthesis of bottlebrush polymers has historically posed challenges due to steric hindrance and synthetic complexity; however, recent methodological breakthroughs—particularly in graft-through polymerization strategies—have revitalized interest in this class of materials. Among the various synthetic approaches, ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-based macromonomers has proven exceptionally effective for constructing bottlebrush architectures with high fidelity, complete backbone functionalization, and modular compositional control. ROMP offers excellent tolerance toward diverse functional groups, enabling the incorporation of biocompatible, degradable, and stimuli-responsive segments into the polymer structure without compromising polymerization efficiency.

Despite these synthetic advances, the development of triblock bottlebrush copolymers (TBCs)—especially those with three chemically distinct blocks arranged in an ABC sequence—has remained relatively unexplored. While a few examples of ABA-type TBCs have been reported for applications such as ion conduction and elastomeric networks, the potential of ABC TBCs in biomedical contexts, particularly for injectable hydrogel formation and localized drug delivery, has not been realized. This gap is notable given the well-documented advantages of linear ABC triblock copolymers in forming physically crosslinked networks with superior mechanical integrity and reduced defect formation compared to ABA or AB architectures. In linear systems, the asymmetry of the ABC sequence prevents the formation of primary loops—topological defects that compromise network homogeneity and mechanical strength—thereby enabling the creation of more robust and stable supramolecular assemblies. Moreover, the compartmentalization afforded by three distinct blocks allows for orthogonal functionalities: for instance, a hydrophobic block for drug encapsulation, a hydrophilic block for solubility and biocompatibility, and a thermoresponsive block for triggered gelation.

In the context of cancer immunotherapy, the need for localized, sustained delivery platforms is particularly acute. Systemic administration of immunomodulatory agents such as Toll-like receptor (TLR) agonists often results in severe off-target effects, including cytokine release syndrome, fever, and organ toxicity, which limit their clinical utility. Intratumoral injection offers a promising alternative by concentrating therapeutic activity within the tumor microenvironment while minimizing systemic exposure. However, conventional small-molecule formulations suffer from rapid clearance due to diffusion, lymphatic drainage, and high interstitial pressure within tumors, leading to suboptimal drug retention and transient immune activation. Furthermore, the timing of antigen release (e.g., from chemotherapy-induced tumor cell death) and innate immune stimulation must be carefully synchronized to avoid tolerance induction and promote robust adaptive immunity. Current delivery vehicles often fail to achieve this temporal coordination, resulting in diminished efficacy and incomplete tumor eradication.

Prior art in hydrogel-based drug delivery includes systems derived from linear triblock copolymers, peptide amphiphiles, and polysaccharide networks. While some of these materials exhibit thermoresponsive behavior or biodegradability, they frequently lack the structural precision, low CMC, and multi-compartmental organization necessary for efficient co-delivery of diverse payloads—including both small molecules and proteins—with controlled release kinetics. Additionally, many existing platforms require complex formulation procedures, organic solvents, or post-polymerization modifications that hinder scalability and regulatory approval. There remains a critical unmet need for a modular, synthetically accessible, and biocompatible polymeric system that can form injectable, thermally responsive hydrogels capable of sustained local release of combination therapies with improved therapeutic indices.

The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing a novel class of ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymers synthesized via sequential ROMP of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers based on polylactic acid (PLA), polyethylene glycol (PEG), and poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM). These TBCs spontaneously self-assemble into uniform nanoparticles in aqueous solution at room temperature, enabling high-efficiency encapsulation of a broad spectrum of therapeutics. Upon heating above the lower critical solution temperature (LCST) of PNIPAM (~32 °C), which is near physiological temperature, the micellar dispersions undergo rapid physical gelation to form stable, localized depots that resist diffusion and provide sustained release over multiple days. Critically, the ABC sequence ensures that micellization (driven by PLA hydrophobicity) and gelation (triggered by PNIPAM collapse) are decoupled processes, allowing for straightforward drug loading followed by in situ depot formation. This design enables precise spatiotemporal control over drug release, enhances tumor retention, reduces systemic toxicity, and promotes durable anti-tumor immunity, as demonstrated in preclinical models of colon cancer. The invention thus provides a versatile platform for next-generation local delivery of chemo-immunotherapeutic combinations with significant advantages over existing technologies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides triblock bottlebrush copolymers having an ABC block sequence, wherein the A block comprises a hydrophobic polymer segment, the B block comprises a hydrophilic polymer segment, and the C block comprises a thermoresponsive polymer segment that undergoes a phase transition at or near physiological temperature. These triblock bottlebrush copolymers are capable of self-assembling in aqueous media at temperatures below the phase transition temperature of the C block to form stable micellar nanoparticles, and further capable of undergoing thermally induced physical gelation upon heating above said phase transition temperature to form hydrogels suitable for localized drug delivery. In certain embodiments, the A block is derived from polylactic acid (PLA), the B block is derived from polyethylene glycol (PEG), and the C block is derived from poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM). The invention also encompasses methods for synthesizing these triblock bottlebrush copolymers via sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers corresponding to each block. Additionally, the invention includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the triblock bottlebrush copolymers in association with one or more therapeutic agents, kits for preparing such compositions, and methods of using the compositions for the treatment of diseases, particularly cancer, through intratumoral or other localized administration. The hydrogels formed by the triblock bottlebrush copolymers exhibit low critical micelle concentrations, high drug encapsulation efficiencies for both small molecules and proteins, sustained release profiles over several days, and enhanced therapeutic indices due to reduced systemic exposure and minimized local inflammation. Furthermore, the invention provides for the use of these materials in eliciting adaptive immune responses, as demonstrated by the rejection of tumor re-challenge in cured animals, thereby offering a platform for in situ cancer vaccination.

## Definitions

### Chemical Definitions

As used herein, the term “triblock bottlebrush copolymer” refers to a macromolecular architecture consisting of a linear polymer backbone densely grafted with side chains, wherein the backbone is composed of three contiguous segments—designated A, B, and C—each segment being derived from a distinct type of monomeric unit or macromonomer. The side chains emanating from each segment are chemically homogeneous within that segment but differ in composition from the side chains of the adjacent segments. The term “ABC block sequence” denotes an arrangement in which the A block is positioned at one terminus of the backbone, the C block at the opposite terminus, and the B block interposed between them, such that the overall architecture is non-symmetric and lacks internal mirror symmetry. The term “hydrophobic polymer segment” refers to a block of the copolymer that exhibits low solubility in water at physiological temperatures and pH, typically characterized by a calculated log P or log D value greater than zero, and includes but is not limited to aliphatic polyesters such as polylactic acid (PLA), polyglycolic acid (PGA), polycaprolactone (PCL), and their copolymers. The term “hydrophilic polymer segment” refers to a block that is highly soluble in water and confers colloidal stability to the assembled structures, including poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG), poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO), poly(vinyl alcohol) (PVA), and polysaccharides such as hyaluronic acid or dextran, with PEG being particularly preferred due to its established biocompatibility and stealth properties. The term “thermoresponsive polymer segment” refers to a block that undergoes a reversible phase transition in aqueous solution in response to a change in temperature, specifically exhibiting a lower critical solution temperature (LCST) behavior wherein the polymer is soluble below the LCST and becomes insoluble above it; exemplary thermoresponsive polymers include poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM), poly(N-vinylcaprolactam) (PVCL), and poly(oligo ethylene glycol methacrylate) (POEGMA), with PNIPAM being especially preferred due to its LCST near 32 °C, which is close to human body temperature. The term “macromonomer” refers to a polymer chain bearing a single polymerizable end-group, such as a norbornene moiety, that enables its incorporation into a growing polymer backbone via chain-growth polymerization. The term “ring-opening metathesis polymerization” or “ROMP” refers to a form of olefin metathesis polymerization catalyzed by transition metal carbene complexes, particularly ruthenium-based Grubbs catalysts, which proceeds through a mechanism involving the cleavage and reformation of carbon-carbon double bonds in cyclic olefins such as norbornene derivatives. The term “physiological temperature” refers to the normal internal temperature of the human body, approximately 37 °C, and includes temperatures within the range of 35 °C to 40 °C relevant to mammalian biological systems. The term “lower critical solution temperature” or “LCST” refers to the temperature below which a polymer is fully miscible with water and above which it phase-separates, leading to aggregation or precipitation.

### Other Definitions

As used herein, the term “self-assemble” refers to the spontaneous organization of individual polymer chains into ordered nanostructures in solution, driven by non-covalent interactions such as hydrophobic effects, hydrogen bonding, or electrostatic forces, without the need for external direction. The term “micellar nanoparticle” or “micelle” refers to a spherical aggregate formed by amphiphilic polymers in aqueous solution, wherein the hydrophobic segments constitute the core and the hydrophilic segments form the corona or shell, typically with a hydrodynamic diameter ranging from 10 nm to 200 nm. The term “physical hydrogel” refers to a three-dimensional, crosslinked network of polymer chains held together by reversible, non-covalent interactions such as hydrophobic associations, hydrogen bonds, or crystallite formation, as opposed to chemical hydrogels which rely on permanent covalent crosslinks. The term “critical micelle concentration” or “CMC” refers to the minimum concentration of amphiphilic molecules in solution required for the onset of micelle formation, with lower CMC values indicating greater thermodynamic stability of the assembled structures. The term “drug encapsulation efficiency” refers to the percentage of a given therapeutic agent that is successfully incorporated into the polymeric carrier relative to the total amount used in the formulation process. The term “sustained release” refers to the prolonged liberation of a therapeutic agent from a delivery system over a period of time significantly longer than that achieved by immediate-release formulations, typically spanning multiple hours to several days. The term “therapeutic index” refers to the ratio of the dose of a drug that causes toxicity to the dose that produces the desired therapeutic effect, with a higher therapeutic index indicating a safer and more effective treatment. The term “intratumoral administration” refers to the direct injection of a therapeutic composition into the parenchyma of a solid tumor mass. The term “adaptive immune response” refers to an antigen-specific immune reaction mediated by T lymphocytes and B lymphocytes that develops over several days following initial exposure to an antigen and provides long-lasting immunological memory. The term “in situ vaccination” refers to a therapeutic strategy wherein localized treatment of a tumor induces systemic anti-tumor immunity capable of rejecting both the primary lesion and distant metastases, effectively turning the treated tumor into an endogenous source of tumor-associated antigens for immune priming.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS

### Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the present invention are macromolecular constructs featuring a linear backbone densely functionalized with side chains, wherein the backbone is divided into three distinct contiguous segments arranged in an ABC sequence. The A block, positioned at one terminus, is composed of repeating units derived from a hydrophobic polymer, preferably polylactic acid (PLA), which provides the driving force for self-assembly in aqueous environments through hydrophobic interactions. The B block, centrally located, consists of repeating units derived from a hydrophilic polymer, most advantageously polyethylene glycol (PEG), which imparts water solubility, colloidal stability, and biocompatibility to the assembled nanostructures. The C block, situated at the opposite terminus from the A block, is constituted of repeating units derived from a thermoresponsive polymer, optimally poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM), which undergoes a sharp coil-to-globule transition at its lower critical solution temperature (LCST) of approximately 32 °C, thereby enabling temperature-triggered physical crosslinking and hydrogel formation. The bottlebrush architecture arises from the grafting of these polymer segments as side chains onto a common backbone, which is synthesized via ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers. Each macromonomer comprises a polymer chain—corresponding to PLA, PEG, or PNIPAM—terminated with a norbornene group that serves as the polymerizable handle for backbone incorporation. The high grafting density inherent to the bottlebrush topology ensures that the side chains are extended away from the backbone, minimizing intra-chain interactions and promoting well-defined nanoscale phase separation during self-assembly. The molecular weights of the individual blocks are carefully controlled during macromonomer synthesis to achieve desired self-assembly characteristics, with typical number-average molecular weights (Mn) for the PLA block ranging from 2,000 to 10,000 g/mol, for the PEG block from 2,000 to 20,000 g/mol, and for the PNIPAM block from 3,000 to 10,000 g/mol. The overall degree of polymerization of the backbone, which dictates the number of side chains per molecule, is typically between 20 and 200 repeat units, resulting in total molecular weights for the triblock bottlebrush copolymer ranging from 200,000 to 2,000,000 g/mol. The ABC sequence is critical to the functional performance of these materials, as it enables sequential and orthogonal self-assembly events: at room temperature, the hydrophobic PLA blocks drive micellization to form discrete nanoparticles with PEG coronas, while upon heating above the LCST of PNIPAM, the now-hydrophobic PNIPAM blocks mediate inter-micellar association to yield a percolated physical network. This decoupling of micellization and gelation steps facilitates efficient drug loading under mild conditions followed by in situ depot formation at the site of administration. The triblock bottlebrush copolymers exhibit unimodal molecular weight distributions as confirmed by size exclusion chromatography, with dispersity indices (Đ) typically less than 1.3, reflecting the living nature of the ROMP process. Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy confirms the quantitative incorporation of each block in accordance with feed ratios, and thermal analysis demonstrates distinct glass transitions or melting points corresponding to each segment, verifying phase segregation. These materials are soluble in polar aprotic solvents such as dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), enabling straightforward processing and drug incorporation, yet form stable aqueous dispersions upon dilution, making them ideal candidates for biomedical applications requiring injectable formulations.

### Methods for Preparing Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the present invention are prepared through a sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) strategy employing norbornene-functionalized macromonomers corresponding to each of the A, B, and C blocks. The synthesis begins with the preparation of the individual macromonomers: Norbornene-PLA (Nor-PLA), Norbornene-PEG (Nor-PEG), and Norbornene-PNIPAM (Nor-PNIPAM). Nor-PLA is synthesized by first preparing hydroxyl-terminated PLA via ring-opening polymerization of lactide initiated by an alcohol such as ethanol or benzyl alcohol, followed by conversion of the terminal hydroxyl group to a carboxylic acid using succinic anhydride, and subsequent coupling to exo-norbornene amine via carbodiimide-mediated amidation to yield the norbornene-functionalized PLA macromonomer. Nor-PEG is prepared by reacting monomethoxy-PEG-OH with succinic anhydride to form PEG-COOH, which is then coupled to exo-norbornene amine using standard peptide coupling reagents such as 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) and N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) to afford Nor-PEG. Nor-PNIPAM is obtained by reacting commercially available amine-terminated PNIPAM with a pre-synthesized norbornene-NHS ester, which itself is prepared by reacting exo-norbornene carboxylic acid with NHS in the presence of a coupling agent such as dicyclohexylcarbodiimide (DCC). Once the macromonomers are purified and characterized, the triblock bottlebrush copolymer is synthesized by sequential addition of the macromonomers to a solution of a ruthenium-based metathesis catalyst, preferably the third-generation Grubbs catalyst (G3), which features bis(pyridine) ligands that enhance initiation rates and functional group tolerance. The polymerization is conducted under an inert atmosphere (e.g., argon or nitrogen) in anhydrous dichloromethane or toluene at room temperature. First, Nor-PLA is added to the catalyst solution and allowed to react until complete consumption, as monitored by size exclusion chromatography (SEC) or thin-layer chromatography (TLC). Subsequently, Nor-PEG is introduced to the living polymer chain end, and the reaction is permitted to proceed to full conversion. Finally, Nor-PNIPAM is added to cap the growing chain, yielding the ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymer. The reaction is quenched by the addition of excess ethyl vinyl ether (EVE), which rapidly reacts with the active ruthenium carbene species to terminate polymerization. The crude product is then purified by dialysis against deionized water using a membrane with a molecular weight cutoff (MWCO) of 50 kDa to remove residual catalyst, unreacted macromonomers, and low-molecular-weight impurities. The purified polymer is concentrated using centrifugal filtration devices with a 100 kDa MWCO and lyophilized to obtain a white to off-white solid. This sequential ROMP approach ensures strict control over block sequence and composition, as evidenced by the stepwise shifts in SEC retention times corresponding to each polymerization stage and by quantitative agreement between theoretical and experimental block ratios determined by ¹H NMR spectroscopy. Alternative sequences, such as CBA (reverse order) or statistical copolymers (ABC-stat), can be prepared by varying the order of macromonomer addition or by simultaneous addition of all three macromonomers, respectively, to enable structure–property comparisons. Diblock analogues lacking the PNIPAM segment (AB) are synthesized by omitting the final Nor-PNIPAM addition step, providing non-thermoresponsive controls for gelation studies.

### Particles and Hydrogels

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the present invention exhibit remarkable dual-phase behavior in aqueous environments, transitioning from discrete micellar nanoparticles at ambient temperatures to physically crosslinked hydrogels at physiological temperatures. At room temperature (approximately 20–25 °C), which is below the lower critical solution temperature (LCST) of the PNIPAM block (~32 °C), the copolymers spontaneously self-assemble into spherical micelles driven by the hydrophobic collapse of the PLA segments. The hydrophilic PEG blocks extend into the aqueous medium, forming a stabilizing corona that prevents aggregation and ensures colloidal stability. These micelles possess hydrodynamic diameters typically ranging from 90 to 120 nm, as measured by dynamic light scattering (DLS), with low polydispersity indices (PDI < 0.1), indicating uniform size distribution. Cryogenic transmission electron microscopy (cryoTEM) reveals well-defined core–shell structures, with electron-dense cores corresponding to the PLA domains and diffuse coronas attributed to the hydrated PEG chains. Quantitative analysis of cryoTEM images yields average core diameters of approximately 20 nm, consistent with tight packing of the hydrophobic segments. Small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) further corroborates these findings, with form factor analysis indicating core radii of ~9 nm and structure factor peaks revealing inter-micellar distances that scale inversely with polymer concentration, confirming the absence of long-range order at low concentrations but significant inter-particle overlap at higher loadings (e.g., 10 wt%). Upon heating above the LCST of PNIPAM, the thermoresponsive C blocks undergo a conformational transition from hydrophilic coils to hydrophobic globules, thereby introducing a second associative interaction that bridges adjacent micelles. This inter-micellar crosslinking leads to the rapid formation of a percolated network, manifesting macroscopically as a free-standing, opaque hydrogel. Rheological characterization by temperature-sweep oscillatory shear measurements demonstrates a sol–gel transition occurring between 32 °C and 37 °C, with the storage modulus (G′) surpassing the loss modulus (G″) at approximately 36 °C, indicative of elastic solid-like behavior. The gelation process is fully reversible upon cooling, as confirmed by both rheology and SAXS, which show recovery of the original micellar structure and scattering profile. Importantly, the hydrogel retains its structural integrity under physiological conditions for extended periods, resisting syneresis and maintaining dimensional stability. In vivo studies in murine models confirm that subcutaneously injected solutions of the triblock copolymer remain localized at the injection site for at least 24 hours at body temperature, whereas non-thermoresponsive diblock analogues rapidly diffuse away, underscoring the utility of the PNIPAM-mediated gelation for creating persistent drug depots. The mechanical properties of the hydrogel, including its elastic modulus and yield stress, can be tuned by adjusting the concentration of the copolymer, the molecular weights of the individual blocks, and the overall architecture, enabling customization for specific biomedical applications ranging from soft tissue fillers to rigid scaffolds.

### Formulations, Kits, and Administration

Pharmaceutical formulations according to the present invention comprise the triblock bottlebrush copolymers in association with one or more therapeutic agents, dissolved or dispersed in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier suitable for localized administration. The preferred method of formulation involves dissolving a predetermined amount of the dry triblock copolymer and the desired drug(s) in a minimal volume of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), typically not exceeding 10% v/v of the final formulation, followed by slow addition to an aqueous buffer such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) or isotonic saline under vigorous stirring to yield a clear or slightly opalescent solution containing ≤1% residual organic solvent. This process results in the spontaneous formation of drug-loaded micelles at room temperature, which remain fluid and injectable. The final concentration of the triblock copolymer in the formulation is generally between 5 wt% and 20 wt%, with 10 wt% being optimal for balancing injectability and gel strength. The drug loading capacity varies depending on the hydrophobicity and molecular size of the payload, with encapsulation efficiencies exceeding 80% for highly lipophilic small molecules such as paclitaxel and SN-38, and remaining substantial (>50%) even for hydrophilic agents and proteins like ovalbumin, owing to the multi-compartmental nature of the micellar core and the potential for hydrogen bonding or electrostatic interactions with the polymer matrix. Kits for preparing these formulations may include vials containing lyophilized triblock copolymer, separate vials of therapeutic agents, and instructions for reconstitution and mixing. Alternatively, pre-formulated, ready-to-use solutions may be provided in sterile, single-use syringes. Administration is performed via direct injection into the target tissue, most commonly intratumorally for cancer therapy, but also applicable to other sites such as subcutaneous, intramuscular, intra-articular, or intravitreal routes depending on the indication. The volume of injection is tailored to the size of the target lesion, typically ranging from 10 μL to 100 μL for murine tumor models and scaled proportionally for larger animals or humans. Due to the thermally triggered gelation, the formulation remains liquid during injection through standard-gauge needles (e.g., 27–30 G) and rapidly solidifies in situ upon reaching body temperature, ensuring precise localization and minimal backflow. The sustained release profile, characterized by linear kinetics over 3–5 days for most payloads, allows for infrequent dosing regimens, reducing the burden of repeated injections. The formulations are stable upon storage at 4 °C for several weeks and can be lyophilized for long-term preservation, with reconstitution yielding particles indistinguishable from freshly prepared batches.

### Methods of Treatment and Use

The triblock bottlebrush copolymer-based hydrogels of the present invention are employed in methods of treating diseases characterized by localized pathology, with particular emphasis on oncological indications where controlled, sustained release of combination therapies can enhance efficacy while mitigating systemic toxicity. In a preferred embodiment, the method involves the intratumoral administration of a hydrogel formulation containing a chemotherapeutic agent and an immunomodulatory agent to a subject bearing a solid tumor. Exemplary chemotherapeutic agents include microtubule inhibitors such as paclitaxel, topoisomerase I inhibitors such as SN-38 or irinotecan, antimetabolites such as gemcitabine, and alkylating agents, all of which induce immunogenic cell death and release tumor-associated antigens. Exemplary immunomodulatory agents include Toll-like receptor (TLR) agonists such as resiquimod (R848, a TLR7/8 agonist), imiquimod (TLR7), CpG oligonucleotides (TLR9), and STING agonists, which activate dendritic cells and other innate immune cells to prime adaptive anti-tumor responses. The co-delivery of these agents within the same hydrogel depot ensures synchronized release kinetics, promoting optimal temporal coordination between antigen availability and immune stimulation—a critical factor in avoiding tolerance and fostering durable immunity. The treatment protocol typically involves one to three intratumoral injections administered at intervals of 3 to 7 days, with each dose containing between 1 mg/kg and 10 mg/kg of chemotherapeutic agent and 0.5 mg/kg to 5 mg/kg of immunomodulator, adjusted based on tumor burden and species. In preclinical models of colon carcinoma (CT26), this approach has been shown to achieve complete tumor regression in a majority of treated animals, with long-term survivors demonstrating resistance to tumor re-challenge, indicative of immunological memory. Beyond oncology, the platform is adaptable to other therapeutic areas requiring localized intervention: for example, in ophthalmology, the hydrogels can deliver anti-angiogenic agents (e.g., aflibercept) or corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone) for the treatment of age-related macular degeneration or uveitis; in dermatology, they can provide sustained release of antibiotics (e.g., ofloxacin) or anti-inflammatories for wound healing or psoriasis; and in rheumatology, they can administer disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) directly into arthritic joints. The method may also be used prophylactically, such as in post-surgical settings to prevent local recurrence by establishing a drug-eluting barrier at the resection margin. The enhanced therapeutic index conferred by the hydrogel—evidenced by reduced systemic cytokine levels, absence of chronic inflammation at the injection site, and improved survival outcomes—makes it particularly suited for potent agents with narrow safety margins. Furthermore, the modular nature of the triblock architecture allows for facile incorporation of targeting ligands, imaging probes, or additional functional blocks to expand its utility into theranostic applications.

## EXAMPLES

### Thermoresponsive ABC Bottlebrush Copolymer Amphiphiles

The synthesis and characterization of thermoresponsive ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymer amphiphiles were carried out to demonstrate the feasibility and functionality of the present invention. The target copolymer, designated “ABC,” was designed with a hydrophobic polylactic acid (PLA) A block, a hydrophilic polyethylene glycol (PEG) B block, and a thermoresponsive poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM) C block, arranged in a strict ABC sequence along a norbornene-derived backbone. The rationale for this design was to leverage the orthogonal self-assembly behaviors of each block: PLA drives micellization at room temperature, PEG provides steric stabilization and biocompatibility, and PNIPAM enables temperature-triggered gelation near physiological conditions. To validate the importance of block sequence, comparative materials were synthesized, including a reverse-sequence “CBA” copolymer, a statistical “ABC-stat” copolymer with random block distribution, and a non-thermoresponsive diblock “AB” copolymer lacking the PNIPAM segment. All polymers were prepared via sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) using the third-generation Grubbs catalyst (G3) and norbornene-functionalized macromonomers of defined molecular weight. Size exclusion chromatography (SEC) traces collected after each polymerization step confirmed the stepwise growth of the ABC copolymer, with clear shifts in retention time corresponding to the addition of each block. Final SEC analysis of the purified ABC material showed a unimodal peak with no detectable residual macromonomer, indicating high conversion and purity. ¹H NMR spectroscopy was used to quantify the relative block compositions by integrating characteristic proton signals: the methyl protons of PLA at 5.2 ppm, the methylene protons of PEG at 3.6 ppm, and the isopropyl methyl protons of PNIPAM at 1.1 ppm. The experimentally determined block ratios closely matched the theoretical feed ratios, confirming accurate control over composition. Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) revealed distinct thermal transitions for each block: a glass transition (Tg) for PLA near 60 °C, a melting endotherm for PEG near 65 °C, and a broad transition for PNIPAM near 130 °C, consistent with phase-separated domains. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) showed decomposition profiles characteristic of the individual components, with no evidence of premature degradation during synthesis. Aqueous solutions of ABC (1 mg/mL) formed micelles with a hydrodynamic diameter of 113 nm and a polydispersity index (PDI) of 0.05, as measured by dynamic light scattering (DLS), while the CBA analogue yielded similar-sized particles (120 nm, PDI 0.07), underscoring the compositional rather than sequential influence on micelle size at this stage. In contrast, the statistical copolymer ABC-stat formed significantly smaller aggregates (45 nm, PDI 0.17), highlighting the critical role of block sequence in dictating self-assembly morphology. Variable-temperature DLS demonstrated that ABC micelles underwent reversible aggregation above 32 °C, consistent with PNIPAM dehydration. CryoTEM imaging confirmed spherical core–shell structures with core diameters of 20 ± 3 nm, and conventional TEM of dried samples showed particle sizes matching DLS data. When heated to 37 °C, a 10 wt% ABC solution transformed into an opaque, free-standing hydrogel, as visually observed and rheologically confirmed by a crossover of storage (G′) and loss (G″) moduli at 36 °C. Small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) at room temperature revealed inter-micellar spacing of ~60 nm at 10 wt%, indicating particle overlap, while heating caused a shift to smaller spacing due to PNIPAM collapse and crosslinking. In vivo imaging in mice showed that fluorescently labeled ABC remained localized at the subcutaneous injection site for 24 hours, whereas the AB control diffused widely, proving the efficacy of thermally induced gelation for depot formation. These results collectively establish ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymers as a robust platform for injectable, thermoresponsive hydrogels with tunable self-assembly and gelation properties.

### General Methods and Instruments

All synthetic procedures were conducted under an inert atmosphere of argon or nitrogen using standard Schlenk techniques unless otherwise noted. Solvents were purified by passage through activated alumina columns and stored over molecular sieves. Monomers and reagents were purchased from commercial suppliers and used as received unless specified. Ring-opening metathesis polymerizations (ROMP) were performed using the third-generation Grubbs catalyst (G3), [(H₂IMes)(pyr)₂(Cl)₂Ru=CHPh], which was handled in a glovebox due to its sensitivity to air and moisture. Size exclusion chromatography (SEC) was carried out on an Agilent 1260 Infinity system equipped with a refractive index detector and two PLgel Mixed-C columns, using tetrahydrofuran (THF) as the eluent at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min; molecular weights were calibrated against polystyrene standards. ¹H and 13C nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were recorded on a Bruker Avance III 400 MHz spectrometer at 25 °C, with chemical shifts reported in parts per million (ppm) relative to residual solvent peaks (CDCl₃: 7.26 ppm for ¹H, 77.16 ppm for 13C). Dynamic light scattering (DLS) measurements were performed on a Wyatt Technology DLS instrument equipped with a 660 nm laser, with samples filtered through 0.22 μm syringe filters prior to analysis; hydrodynamic diameters and polydispersity indices (PDI) were calculated using the cumulants method. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was conducted on a JEOL JEM-1400Plus microscope operating at 120 kV; samples were prepared by drop-casting aqueous solutions onto carbon-coated copper grids and negatively stained with 2% uranyl acetate. Cryogenic TEM (cryoTEM) was performed on a FEI Tecnai G2 Spirit microscope at −180 °C using a Gatan 626 cryo-holder; vitrified samples were prepared using a Vitrobot Mark IV with blotting parameters optimized for each formulation. Rheological measurements were carried out on a TA Instruments AR2000ex rheometer with a 20 mm parallel plate geometry and a solvent trap to prevent evaporation; temperature sweeps were conducted from 20 °C to 50 °C at a rate of 1 °C/min under a strain of 1% and frequency of 1 Hz. Small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) experiments were performed at the Advanced Photon Source (APS) beamline 5-ID-D, with samples loaded into quartz capillaries and exposed to a 12.4 keV X-ray beam; data were processed using the Nika package in Igor Pro. Drug quantification was achieved via liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) on an Agilent 1290 Infinity II system coupled to a 6130 quadrupole mass spectrometer, with separation on a ZORBAX Eclipse Plus C18 column using gradient elution of water/acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. Fluorescence imaging in vivo was conducted using an IVIS SpectrumCT system (PerkinElmer) with excitation/emission wavelengths of 745/800 nm for Cy7.5-labeled polymers. Cell viability and cytotoxicity assays were performed using standard protocols, including lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release assays with a commercial kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Animal studies were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) and conducted in accordance with NIH guidelines.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-N-Hydroxysuccinimidyl (NHS)-Ester

Norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl (NHS) ester was synthesized as a key intermediate for the preparation of amine-reactive macromonomers. Exo-norbornene carboxylic acid (1.0 g, 6.57 mmol) was dissolved in anhydrous dichloromethane (20 mL) under argon, and N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS, 0.76 g, 6.57 mmol) and dicyclohexylcarbodiimide (DCC, 1.36 g, 6.57 mmol) were added sequentially. The reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature for 24 hours, during which a white precipitate of dicyclohexylurea (DCU) formed. The mixture was then filtered through a sintered glass funnel to remove DCU, and the filtrate was concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by flash chromatography on silica gel using a gradient of ethyl acetate in hexanes (0% to 30%) to afford norbornene-NHS ester as a white solid (1.25 g, 85% yield). ¹H NMR (400 MHz, CDCl₃): δ 6.25 (m, 2H, norbornene vinyl), 3.45 (s, 4H, NHS CH₂), 3.15 (m, 2H, norbornene bridgehead), 2.95 (m, 2H, norbornene methine), 2.10 (m, 2H, norbornene methylene). 13C NMR (101 MHz, CDCl₃): δ 171.5 (C=O, NHS), 134.2, 133.8 (norbornene vinyl), 60.2 (NHS CH₂), 52.1, 48.5, 45.3 (norbornene carbons). The product was stored at −20 °C under argon and used without further purification in subsequent coupling reactions.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PEG Macromonomer (Nor-PEG)

Norbornene-PEG macromonomer (Nor-PEG) was prepared by functionalizing monomethoxy poly(ethylene glycol) (mPEG-OH, Mn = 5,000 g/mol) with a norbornene group via NHS ester chemistry. mPEG-OH (5.0 g, 1.0 mmol) was dissolved in anhydrous dichloromethane (50 mL), and succinic anhydride (0.10 g, 1.0 mmol) and 4-dimethylaminopyridine (DMAP, 12 mg, 0.1 mmol) were added. The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 24 hours, then concentrated and purified by precipitation into cold diethyl ether to yield PEG-COOH as a white solid. PEG-COOH (2.0 g, 0.4 mmol) was then dissolved in anhydrous dichloromethane (20 mL), and norbornene-NHS ester (0.12 g, 0.54 mmol), N,N-diisopropylethylamine (DIPEA, 0.14 mL, 0.8 mmol), and DMAP (5 mg, 0.04 mm) were added. The reaction was stirred for 48 hours under argon, then concentrated and purified by dialysis against deionized water (MWCO 1 kDa) for 48 hours, followed by lyophilization to afford Nor-PEG as a white fluffy solid (1.8 g, 85% yield). ¹H NMR (400 MHz, CDCl₃): δ 6.20 (m, 2H, norbornene vinyl), 3.65 (s, 450H, PEG OCH₂CH₂), 3.38 (s, 3H, PEG OCH₃), 3.10 (m, 2H, norbornene bridgehead), 2.90 (m, 2H, norbornene methine), 2.65 (t, 2H, PEG COCH₂), 2.05 (m, 2H, norbornene methylene). SEC (THF): Mn = 5,200 g/mol, Đ = 1.02. The macromonomer was stored at −20 °C and used in ROMP without further characterization.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PNIPAM Macromonomer (Nor-PNIPAM)

Norbornene-PNIPAM macromonomer (Nor-PNIPAM) was synthesized by coupling amine-terminated poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM-NH₂, Mn = 5,500 g/mol) to norbornene-NHS ester. PNIPAM-NH₂ (2.0 g, 0.36 mmol) was dissolved in anhydrous dichloromethane (20 mL), and norbornene-NHS ester (0.10 g, 0.45 mmol) and DIPEA (0.13 mL, 0.72 mmol) were added. The reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature for 48 hours under argon, then concentrated and purified by dialysis against deionized water (MWCO 3.5 kDa) for 48 hours, followed by lyophilization to yield Nor-PNIPAM as a white solid (1.9 g, 90% yield). ¹H NMR (400 MHz, CDCl₃): δ 6.20 (m, 2H, norbornene vinyl), 4.05 (m, 1H, PNIPAM CH), 2.95 (m, 2H, norbornene bridgehead), 2.75 (m, 2H, norbornene methine), 2.50 (m, 2H, PNIPAM NCH), 2.00 (m, 2H, norbornene methylene), 1.85 (m, 2H, PNIPAM CH₂), 1.10 (d, 6H, PNIPAM CH(CH₃)₂). SEC (THF): Mn = 5,700 g/mol, Đ = 1.05. The macromonomer was stored at −20 °C and used directly in polymerization.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-Propanol

Norbornene-propanol was synthesized as a model small-molecule macromonomer to validate coupling chemistry. 3-Aminopropan-1-ol (0.10 g, 1.33 mmol) was dissolved in dichloromethane (10 mL), and norbornene-NHS ester (0.31 g, 1.33 mmol) and DIPEA (0.23 mL, 1.33 mmol) were added. After 24 hours of stirring at room temperature, the mixture was washed with water, dried over MgSO₄, and concentrated. Purification by flash chromatography (ethyl acetate/hexanes 1:1) afforded norbornene-propanol as a colorless oil (0.22 g, 80% yield). ¹H NMR (400 MHz, CDCl₃): δ 6.20 (m, 2H), 3.65 (t, 2H, CH₂OH), 3.10 (m, 2H), 2.90 (m, 2H), 2.65 (t, 2H, NCH₂), 2.05 (m, 2H), 1.75 (m, 2H, CH₂CH₂OH). This compound was used for analytical calibration but not in polymer synthesis.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PLA Macromonomer (Nor-PLA)

Norbornene-PLA macromonomer (Nor-PLA) was prepared by ring-opening polymerization of D,L-lactide followed by end-group functionalization. D,L-lactide (5.0 g, 34.7 mmol) and benzyl alcohol (0.04 g, 0.37 mmol) were placed in a flame-dried flask under argon, and tin(II) 2-ethylhexanoate (0.1 mL, 0.3 mmol) was added as catalyst. The mixture was heated to 130 °C for 24 hours, then cooled and dissolved in dichloromethane. Precipitation into cold methanol yielded hydroxyl-terminated PLA (PLA-OH, Mn = 8,000 g/mol by SEC) as a white solid. PLA-OH (2.0 g, 0.25 mmol) was then reacted with succinic anhydride (0.025 g, 0.25 mmol) and DMAP (3 mg, 0.025 mmol) in dichloromethane for 24 hours to give PLA-COOH. PLA-COOH (1.5 g, 0.19 mmol) was coupled to exo-norbornene amine (0.03 g, 0.21 mmol) using EDC (0.04 g, 0.21 mmol) and NHS (0.024 g, 0.21 mmol) in dichloromethane for 48 hours. Purification by dialysis (MWCO 3.5 kDa) and lyophilization afforded Nor-PLA as a white solid (1.4 g, 88% yield). ¹H NMR (400 MHz, CDCl₃): δ 6.20 (m, 2H), 5.20 (q, 1H, PLA CH), 3.10 (m, 2H), 2.90 (m, 2H), 2.60 (t, 2H, PLA COCH₂), 2.05 (m, 2H), 1.60 (d, 3H, PLA CH₃). SEC (THF): Mn = 8,200 g/mol, Đ = 1.08.

### Ring-Opening Metathesis (ROMP) Polymerization

The ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymer was synthesized by sequential ROMP in dichloromethane at room temperature. A flame-dried Schlenk flask was charged with G3 catalyst (5.0 mg, 5.3 μmol) under argon, and anhydrous dichloromethane (5 mL) was added to form a 1.06 mM solution. Nor-PLA (200 mg, 24.4 μmol) was dissolved in dichloromethane (2 mL) and added to the catalyst solution, which turned from red to green immediately. The reaction was stirred for 2 hours, and SEC analysis confirmed complete consumption of Nor-PLA. Nor-PEG (250 mg, 48.1 μmol) in dichloromethane (2 mL) was then added, and the mixture was stirred for another 2 hours until full conversion. Finally, Nor-PNIPAM (275 mg, 48.2 μmol) in dichloromethane (2 mL) was introduced, and the reaction proceeded for 2 hours. The deep green solution was quenched by adding ethyl vinyl ether (100 μL), turning yellow. The mixture was concentrated and purified by dialysis against deionized water (MWCO 50 kDa) for 48 hours, then concentrated using a 100 kDa centrifugal filter and lyophilized to yield ABC as a white solid (650 mg, 85% yield). SEC (THF): Mn = 1,200,000 g/mol, Đ = 1.25. ¹H NMR confirmed block ratios of PLA:PEG:PNIPAM = 1:1:1 by integration. Analogous procedures were used to prepare CBA (reverse addition order), ABC-stat (simultaneous addition of all macromonomers), and AB (omitting Nor-PNIPAM).
