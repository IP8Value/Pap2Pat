# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and molecular therapeutics, specifically to novel peptidomimetic compounds that function as inhibitors of protein–protein interactions (PPIs) involving members of the human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER or ErbB) family. More particularly, the invention provides a symmetric cyclic peptidomimetic compound—designated herein as Compound 18—that selectively binds to domain IV of the HER2 extracellular domain (ECD), thereby inhibiting heterodimerization between HER2 and other HER family members, including EGFR (HER1) and HER3. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising Compound 18, methods for treating cancers characterized by overexpression or dysregulation of HER2 and/or EGFR—such as breast cancer, non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and ovarian cancer—and methods for synergistically enhancing the efficacy of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) in resistant tumor settings. The compound exhibits high specificity for HER2-overexpressing cells, demonstrates potent antiproliferative activity in the low nanomolar range, induces apoptosis, suppresses tumor growth in vivo, and displays exceptional stability against enzymatic degradation due to its cyclic architecture and strategic incorporation of D- and L-amino acid residues. The invention also includes diagnostic and research applications wherein Compound 18 serves as a molecular probe to elucidate mechanisms of HER2-mediated dimerization and downstream signaling.

## BACKGROUND ART

The human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER) family, also known as the ErbB receptor tyrosine kinase family, comprises four structurally related transmembrane glycoproteins: EGFR (HER1/ErbB1), HER2 (ErbB2/Neu), HER3 (ErbB3), and HER4 (ErbB4). These receptors play pivotal roles in regulating cellular processes such as proliferation, differentiation, migration, and survival through ligand-induced activation of intracellular signaling cascades, primarily the RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK and PI3K–AKT pathways. Under physiological conditions, receptor activation occurs upon binding of specific growth factor ligands—such as epidermal growth factor (EGF), transforming growth factor-alpha (TGF-α), or neuregulins—to the extracellular domains of EGFR, HER3, or HER4. Notably, HER2 lacks a known high-affinity ligand but functions as the preferred dimerization partner for other HER family members, forming either homodimers or, more commonly, heterodimers that exhibit enhanced signaling potency and duration compared to homodimers of other receptors.

Dysregulation of HER signaling is a hallmark of numerous epithelial malignancies. Overexpression of HER2, often due to gene amplification, is observed in approximately 15–20% of invasive breast cancers and is associated with aggressive disease, poor prognosis, and reduced overall survival. Similarly, activating mutations in EGFR—particularly exon 19 deletions and the L858R point mutation—are prevalent in NSCLC and confer sensitivity to first-generation TKIs like erlotinib and gefitinib. However, acquired resistance frequently emerges, most commonly through the T790M gatekeeper mutation in EGFR, which restores ATP affinity and diminishes drug binding. Moreover, compensatory upregulation of HER2 or HER3 can sustain downstream signaling despite EGFR inhibition, contributing to therapeutic failure.

Current therapeutic strategies targeting the HER axis include monoclonal antibodies and small-molecule TKIs. Trastuzumab and pertuzumab are FDA-approved humanized monoclonal antibodies directed against distinct epitopes on the HER2 extracellular domain: trastuzumab binds domain IV and mediates antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), while pertuzumab targets domain II and sterically blocks dimerization with other HER receptors. Although clinically effective, these biologics suffer from limitations including high production costs, limited tissue penetration, requirement for intravenous administration, potential immunogenicity, and development of resistance mechanisms such as masking of epitopes or activation of bypass signaling pathways. Small-molecule TKIs like lapatinib, afatinib, and osimertinib inhibit the intracellular kinase domains but are susceptible to resistance mutations and often lack selectivity, leading to off-target toxicities.

An alternative approach involves disrupting the initial step of receptor activation—namely, the protein–protein interaction (PPI) at the extracellular level that enables dimer formation. PPIs represent challenging yet promising therapeutic targets due to their large, shallow interfaces that are traditionally considered “undruggable” by conventional small molecules. Peptides derived from dimerization interfaces have shown promise as PPI inhibitors but are plagued by poor metabolic stability, rapid renal clearance, and limited oral bioavailability. To overcome these drawbacks, peptidomimetics—molecules that mimic the structural and functional features of bioactive peptides while incorporating non-natural elements to enhance stability and pharmacokinetics—have emerged as viable candidates. Cyclization, backbone modification, and strategic use of D-amino acids are established strategies to confer protease resistance and conformational rigidity, thereby improving target affinity and in vivo half-life.

Prior art includes linear peptidomimetics such as Compounds 5 and 9, developed by the inventors, which contain the sequence Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp (where Anapa denotes β-(2-naphthyl)-alanine) and demonstrate inhibitory activity against EGFR–HER2 and HER2–HER3 heterodimerization. However, these linear constructs remain vulnerable to exopeptidase-mediated degradation in biological fluids, limiting their therapeutic utility. Despite extensive research into HER-targeted agents, there remains an unmet need for stable, selective, and potent inhibitors that specifically disrupt HER2-containing heterodimers by binding to domain IV—a region less exploited than domain II—and that can be administered via non-parenteral routes or combined with existing TKIs to overcome resistance. No prior art discloses a symmetric cyclic peptidomimetic with dual chirality control (at both the β-amino acid and Pro-Pro turn motifs) that achieves nanomolar antiproliferative activity exclusively in HER2-overexpressing cells while exhibiting negligible toxicity to normal tissues. The present invention addresses this gap by providing Compound 18, a rationally designed cyclic peptidomimetic that fulfills these criteria and represents a significant advancement in targeted cancer therapy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel symmetric cyclic peptidomimetic compound, designated Compound 18, which functions as a highly specific inhibitor of protein–protein interactions involving the HER2 receptor. Compound 18 is characterized by a cyclic backbone formed through main-chain cyclization, incorporating two identical pharmacophore sequences—Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp—linked by a conformationally constrained L-Pro-D-Pro dipeptide motif that enforces a β-turn or β-hairpin secondary structure in solution. This design ensures optimal spatial orientation of key functional groups—including the guanidinium moiety of arginine, the hydrophobic β-naphthyl side chain of Anapa, and the aromatic phenyl ring of phenylalanine—for high-affinity binding to domain IV of the HER2 extracellular domain (ECD). The compound exhibits exceptional selectivity for HER2-overexpressing cancer cells, with IC₅₀ values in the low nanomolar range (e.g., 18 nM in Calu-3 lung cancer cells and 194 nM in SKBR-3 breast cancer cells), while showing minimal activity (>50 µM) in HER2-negative or normal cell lines, thereby minimizing off-target effects.

Compound 18 operates by competitively binding to domain IV of HER2, thereby preventing heterodimerization with EGFR (HER1) and HER3—two critical dimer pairs implicated in tumor progression and therapeutic resistance. This mechanism was confirmed through multiple orthogonal assays: surface plasmon resonance (SPR) demonstrated direct, concentration-dependent binding of Compound 18 to purified HER2 ECD and isolated domain IV with a dissociation constant (K<sub>d</sub>) of 0.125 µM, consistent with cellular IC₅₀ values; competitive binding studies using FITC-labeled Compound 5 showed displacement of the fluorescent probe from HER2-expressing BT-474 cells; proximity ligation assays (PLA) and enzyme fragment complementation (PathHunter®) assays revealed dose-dependent inhibition of both EGFR:HER2 and HER2:HER3 heterodimer formation in vitro; and Western blot analysis confirmed suppression of HER2 autophosphorylation, indicating abrogation of downstream kinase signaling.

In vivo, Compound 18 significantly delays tumor growth in a BT-474 xenograft model of HER2-positive breast cancer when administered intratumorally at 4 mg/kg twice weekly, with efficacy comparable to the TKI lapatinib and superior to its linear analog Compound 9. Histological analysis of excised tumors showed reduced HER2 phosphorylation and diminished HER2:HER3 dimer signals via PLA, confirming target engagement and mechanism of action in a physiological context. Importantly, Compound 18 exhibits remarkable stability in mouse serum, remaining intact for at least 48 hours—attributable to its cyclic structure and incorporation of D-proline—which contrasts sharply with the rapid degradation typical of linear peptides. Toxicity studies at a supratherapeutic dose (6 mg/kg) revealed no histopathological abnormalities in major organs (heart, liver, kidneys, lungs), supporting a favorable safety profile.

Furthermore, Compound 18 demonstrates synergistic antiproliferative effects when combined with the TKI erlotinib in EGFR-mutant NSCLC cell lines harboring the T790M resistance mutation (e.g., NCI-H1975), suggesting utility in overcoming TKI resistance through dual targeting of extracellular dimerization and intracellular kinase activity. Structure–activity relationship (SAR) studies revealed a critical "chiral switch" phenomenon: antiproliferative potency is exquisitely dependent on the stereochemistry of both the β-amino acid (Anapa) and the Pro-Pro turn. Specifically, the L-Pro-D-Pro configuration paired with (R)-Anapa yields maximal activity, whereas inversion to D-Pro-L-Pro requires (S)-Anapa for retained potency; all other stereoisomers show markedly reduced efficacy (>5 µM IC₅₀). Molecular modeling and NMR analyses indicate that active diastereomers adopt conformations wherein both β-naphthyl groups project toward a hydrophobic pocket on HER2 domain IV (comprising residues Pro543, Phe573, Pro584, Pro590), while inactive isomers either misorient these groups or bind outside the dimerization interface.

Thus, the invention encompasses: (1) Compound 18 and its pharmaceutically acceptable salts or solvates; (2) pharmaceutical compositions comprising Compound 18 in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier; (3) methods of treating HER2-overexpressing cancers—including breast, lung, and ovarian carcinomas—by administering an effective amount of Compound 18; (4) methods of enhancing the efficacy of EGFR TKIs in resistant tumors through co-administration with Compound 18; and (5) diagnostic kits utilizing Compound 18 as a probe to detect HER2 dimerization status in patient samples. The invention represents a paradigm shift from intracellular kinase inhibition to extracellular PPI blockade, offering a stable, selective, and mechanistically distinct therapeutic modality for HER2-driven malignancies.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### Definitions/General Discussion

As used herein, the term “peptidomimetic” refers to a compound that mimics the structure, function, or biological activity of a natural peptide but incorporates non-peptidic elements or modifications—such as cyclization, D-amino acids, β-amino acids, or unnatural side chains—to enhance metabolic stability, membrane permeability, or target affinity. The term “cyclic peptidomimetic” denotes a peptidomimetic in which the backbone or side chains are covalently linked to form a macrocyclic ring, thereby restricting conformational flexibility and conferring resistance to exo- and endoproteases. “Symmetric” indicates that the molecule possesses identical or near-identical pharmacophore units arranged in a mirror-image or repetitive fashion around a central structural motif, as exemplified by Compound 18’s duplicated Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp sequences.

“Protein–protein interaction (PPI) inhibitor” means a molecule that disrupts the physical association between two or more proteins, typically by binding to one or more interfaces involved in complex formation. In the context of the HER family, PPI inhibition specifically refers to the prevention of homo- or heterodimerization between receptor monomers via their extracellular domains. “Heterodimerization” denotes the association of two different HER family members, such as EGFR:HER2 or HER2:HER3, which are known to generate potent oncogenic signals. “Domain IV” refers to the fourth structural subdomain of the HER2 extracellular region (residues approximately 500–620 in mature HER2), which is distal to the membrane and distinct from the dimerization arm located in domain II.

“HER2-overexpressing cancer” describes a malignancy in which tumor cells exhibit elevated levels of HER2 protein on their surface, typically due to ERBB2 gene amplification, as detected by immunohistochemistry (IHC 3+) or fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). Examples include approximately 20% of breast cancers and subsets of gastric, ovarian, and non-small cell lung cancers. “Antiproliferative activity” is quantified by the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC₅₀), defined as the compound concentration required to reduce cell viability or proliferation by 50% relative to untreated controls, as measured by assays such as CellTiter-Glo®.

“Chiral switch” signifies a phenomenon wherein a change in the absolute stereochemistry of one or more chiral centers within a molecule leads to a significant alteration—typically a loss—in biological activity, underscoring the stereospecific nature of target engagement. In Compound 18, this applies to both the β-carbon of the Anapa residue and the proline residues in the turn-inducing dipeptide. “β-Amino acid” denotes an amino acid in which the amino group is attached to the β-carbon rather than the α-carbon relative to the carboxyl group; Anapa (β-(2-naphthyl)-alanine) is a non-natural β-amino acid employed herein for its enhanced hydrophobicity and conformational influence.

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention may be formulated for various routes of administration, including intravenous, intratumoral, subcutaneous, or oral delivery, though parenteral routes are preferred given the peptidic nature of the compound. Carriers may include saline, buffered solutions, liposomes, or polymeric nanoparticles to improve solubility and circulation time. Effective doses will vary based on tumor type, burden, and patient factors but are expected to range from 1–10 mg/kg in preclinical models, with human equivalents calculated using standard allometric scaling. Combination therapy with TKIs (e.g., erlotinib, osimertinib) or monoclonal antibodies (e.g., trastuzumab) is contemplated to achieve synergistic effects, particularly in resistant or heterogeneous tumors.

### Example 1. Compound 18: Synthesis and Characterization

Compound 18 was synthesized using standard Fmoc-based solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) on 2-chlorotrityl chloride resin, followed by on-resin cyclization and global deprotection. The synthesis began with the attachment of Fmoc-Pro-OH to the resin under basic conditions (DIEA in dichloromethane), yielding H-Pro-CTC resin with a substitution level of 0.55 mmol/g. After Fmoc deprotection with 20% piperidine/NMP, the linear precursor sequence—Fmoc-Asp(OtBu)-Phe-Anapa(R)-Arg(Pbf)-Pro-D-Pro-Pro—was assembled stepwise using an automated synthesizer. Each coupling employed 5 equivalents of Fmoc-amino acid and 5 equivalents of HCTU activator in the presence of 4-methylmorpholine, with double couplings performed for sterically hindered residues. Upon completion of chain elongation, the N-terminal Fmoc group was removed, and the peptide was cleaved from the resin using a mild acidolytic cocktail (2,2,2-trifluoroethanol:dichloromethane, 1:1) to preserve acid-sensitive side chains.

The linear seco-peptide was then subjected to solution-phase macrocyclization in THF/DMF (4:1) using PyAOP (4 eq) and DIEA (7 eq) as coupling reagents, affording the cyclic backbone after 2 hours of agitation at room temperature. Subsequent global deprotection was achieved by treatment with trifluoroacetic acid (TFA)/water/triisopropylsilane (95:2.5:2.5) for 3 hours, removing tert-butyl, trityl, and 2,2,4,6,7-pentamethyldihydrobenzofuran-5-sulfonyl (Pbf) protecting groups. The crude product was precipitated in cold diethyl ether, dissolved in 0.1% TFA/water, and lyophilized to yield a white powder. Purification by preparative reversed-phase HPLC (C18 column, acetonitrile/water gradient with 0.1% TFA modifier) afforded Compound 18 in >95% purity, as confirmed by analytical HPLC and electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (ESI-MS). The observed [M+H]⁺ ion at m/z 1425.699 matched the theoretical monoisotopic mass of 1425.5893 for the protonated molecule (C₇₂H₉₆N₁₆O₁₈).

Structural characterization included circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy, which displayed a minimum at ~200 nm and a weak positive band at ~225 nm, indicative of a type II β-turn stabilized by the L-Pro-D-Pro motif. Two-dimensional nuclear magnetic resonance (2D NMR) experiments—specifically TOCSY, NOESY, and ROESY—were conducted in DMSO-d₆ at 25°C to assign proton resonances and derive distance constraints. Key NOE cross-peaks between the α-protons of D-Pro and the δ-protons of L-Pro confirmed the cis-amide bond geometry typical of L-Pro-D-Pro turns. Molecular dynamics simulations, initialized from NMR-derived distance restraints, converged to a β-hairpin conformation wherein the two Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp arms extended outward from the Pro-Pro hinge, positioning the β-naphthyl and phenyl rings for simultaneous interaction with hydrophobic pockets on HER2 domain IV. This conformation aligns with the compound’s symmetric design principle and explains its bivalent binding mode.

### Example 2. Inhibition Testing

The antiproliferative activity of Compound 18 was evaluated across a panel of human cancer cell lines using the CellTiter-Glo® luminescent assay, which measures ATP content as a surrogate for viable cell number. HER2-overexpressing lines—including SKBR-3 (breast), BT-474 (breast), and Calu-3 (lung)—exhibited profound sensitivity to Compound 18, with IC₅₀ values of 194 nM, 150 nM, and 18 nM, respectively. In stark contrast, HER2-low MCF-7 breast cancer cells and non-transformed MCF-10A mammary epithelial cells showed minimal response, with IC₅₀ values exceeding 50 µM and 40 µM, respectively—representing selectivity indices of >250-fold and >2000-fold relative to Calu-3. This differential cytotoxicity confirms that Compound 18’s mechanism is contingent upon HER2 overexpression.

To assess functional consequences beyond growth inhibition, a TUNEL (terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling) assay was performed on SKBR-3 cells treated with 5 or 10 µM Compound 18 for 48 hours. A marked increase in DNA fragmentation—visualized as brown DAB-stained nuclei—was observed compared to vehicle controls, demonstrating induction of apoptotic cell death. Western blot analysis of lysates from BT-474 and Calu-3 cells treated with 1 µM Compound 18 for 36 hours revealed a dose-dependent reduction in phosphorylated HER2 (p-HER2) without affecting total HER2 levels, confirming on-target inhibition of receptor activation. Lapatinib, a dual EGFR/HER2 TKI, served as a positive control and produced comparable suppression of p-HER2.

Additional validation came from competitive binding studies: pre-incubation of BT-474 cells with increasing concentrations of Compound 18 (0.1–50 µM) displaced a fixed dose (50 µM) of FITC-labeled Compound 5—a known HER2 ECD binder—from the cell surface, as evidenced by decreased fluorescence intensity in a plate reader (excitation 485 nm, emission 528 nm). The IC₅₀ for displacement was ~0.2 µM, consistent with cellular potency. Control experiments with a structurally unrelated peptidomimetic showed no displacement, affirming specificity. Together, these data establish Compound 18 as a potent, selective, and mechanistically validated inhibitor of HER2-driven oncogenesis.

### Example 3. Competitive Binding Assays

Direct binding of Compound 18 to the HER2 extracellular domain was quantified using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) on a Biacore X100 instrument. Recombinant human HER2 ECD (comprising domains I–IV) and isolated domain IV were immobilized on separate flow cells of a CM5 sensor chip via amine coupling. Compound 18, dissolved in HBS-EP running buffer, was injected at concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 200 µM over the chip surface at a flow rate of 30 µL/min. Sensorgrams revealed rapid association and slow dissociation kinetics, fitting well to a 1:1 Langmuir binding model. Global fitting yielded an association rate constant (k<sub>on</sub>) of 4.76 × 10⁷ M⁻¹s⁻¹ and a dissociation rate constant (k<sub>off</sub>) of 5.97 s⁻¹, corresponding to an equilibrium dissociation constant (K<sub>d</sub>) of 0.125 µM. This K<sub>d</sub> aligns closely with the cellular IC₅₀ in SKBR-3 cells (0.194 µM), supporting a direct correlation between target binding and functional inhibition.

Specificity was confirmed by testing Compound 18 against immobilized EGFR, HER3, and HER4 ECDs under identical conditions; no significant SPR response was observed even at 200 µM, indicating exclusive HER2 binding. As a positive control, pertuzumab—a clinical anti-HER2 antibody known to bind domain II—showed robust binding to full-length HER2 ECD but negligible interaction with domain IV alone, thereby validating the domain-specificity of Compound 18. A negative control peptidomimetic (structurally similar but lacking key pharmacophores) produced flat sensorgrams across all HER proteins, ruling out non-specific adsorption. These results definitively localize Compound 18’s binding site to domain IV of HER2 and distinguish its mechanism from domain II-targeting antibodies like pertuzumab.

### Example 4. Testing Compound 18 Analogs for PPI Inhibition

A series of diastereomeric analogs of Compound 18 were synthesized to probe the impact of stereochemistry on PPI inhibition. These included: Compound 18-1 (L-Pro-D-Pro/(S)-Anapa), Compound 18-2 (L-Pro-D-Pro/(R,S)-Anapa mixture), Compound 18-3 (D-Pro-L-Pro/(R)-Anapa), Compound 18-4 (D-Pro-L-Pro/(S)-Anapa), and Compound 18-5 (D-Pro-L-Pro/(R,S)-Anapa). All analogs were tested in the PathHunter® enzyme fragment complementation assay, which quantifies HER2:HER3 heterodimerization in U2OS cells engineered to express β-galactosidase-tagged HER2 and HER3. Neuregulin-1 (NRG-1)-induced dimerization generates luminescence proportional to dimer abundance; inhibition reduces signal.

Compound 18 and Compound 18-4 (the latter bearing D-Pro-L-Pro/(S)-Anapa) both inhibited HER2:HER3 dimerization with IC₅₀ values of ~0.2 µM and ~0.3 µM, respectively. In contrast, Compounds 18-1, 18-2, and 18-5 showed weak or no inhibition even at 10 µM. Parallel PLA assays in SKBR-3 cells corroborated these findings: only Compound 18 and 18-4 significantly reduced red fluorescent puncta (indicative of HER2:HER3 dimers) at 1 µM. Molecular docking into HER2 domain IV (PDB 3N85) revealed that active isomers position both β-naphthyl groups into a hydrophobic cleft formed by Pro543, Phe573, Pro584, and Pro590, while inactive isomers either project one naphthyl away from the pocket or adopt conformations that clash with key residues. Notably, Compound 18-2, despite binding near the PPI interface (docking energy −5.0 kcal/mol), failed to block dimerization due to suboptimal side-chain orientation—highlighting that binding affinity alone is insufficient without correct pharmacophore alignment. These data confirm that the chiral switch governs not just binding but functional PPI blockade.

### Example 5. Dose-Response Curves for Heterodimerization

Dose-response relationships for heterodimer inhibition were established using both PLA and PathHunter® assays. In SKBR-3 cells, Compound 18 reduced HER2:HER3 PLA signals by 50% at 0.25 µM and by >80% at 1 µM, with similar potency against HER2:EGFR dimers. PathHunter® assays in U2OS-HER2/HER3 cells yielded an IC₅₀ of 0.18 µM for HER2:HER3 inhibition, with complete blockade at 5 µM. Time-course experiments showed maximal inhibition within 6 hours of treatment, persisting for 24 hours. Synergy with erlotinib was assessed in NCI-H1975 cells (EGFR L858R/T790M): isobologram analysis revealed combination indices <0.7 at multiple dose pairs, confirming strong synergy. In vivo, intratumoral Compound 18 (4 mg/kg, twice weekly) reduced BT-474 xenograft growth by 60% over 19 days versus vehicle (p<0.05, Mann-Whitney U test), with PLA on tumor sections showing >70% fewer HER2:HER3 dimers. Serum stability assays confirmed >95% intact Compound 18 after 48 hours in mouse serum, underpinning its sustained efficacy. These comprehensive dose-response profiles validate Compound 18 as a robust, durable, and synergistic PPI inhibitor suitable for clinical translation.
