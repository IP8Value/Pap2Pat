# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of video coding and, more specifically, to methods, systems, and apparatus for multiview video coding (MVC) that enable efficient compression, transmission, decoding, and rendering of three-dimensional (3D) video content. The invention provides a comprehensive framework for encoding, transporting, and decoding multiview video bitstreams that support multiple application scenarios—including free-viewpoint video, 3D television (3DTV), and immersive teleconferencing—while maintaining backward compatibility with existing monoview video coding standards such as H.264/AVC. The invention further addresses key technical challenges inherent in multiview systems, including view scalability, temporal scalability, random access across views, parallel decoding of multiple views, optimal decoded picture buffer (DPB) management, and robust adaptation of bitstreams to heterogeneous network and decoder capabilities. By introducing novel syntax elements, supplemental enhancement information (SEI) messages, and structural constraints within the bitstream, the invention enables flexible and efficient delivery of 3D video services over broadcast, multicast, and unicast networks, while minimizing decoder resource consumption and supporting real-time operation on consumer-grade hardware.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The evolution of video technology has progressed from standard two-dimensional (2D) representations to immersive three-dimensional (3D) experiences that convey depth, spatial relationships, and interactivity. With the maturation of acquisition technologies such as multi-camera arrays and advanced display technologies including autostereoscopic screens and head-mounted displays (HMDs), 3D video applications are increasingly feasible for consumer use. However, the transition from 2D to 3D video introduces significant technical complexities, particularly in the areas of data volume, compression efficiency, transmission bandwidth, and decoder complexity. A single 3D scene captured from multiple viewpoints generates a multiview video sequence comprising numerous correlated video streams, each corresponding to a distinct camera perspective. The sheer volume of this data necessitates highly efficient compression techniques to enable practical storage and transmission over existing communication infrastructures.

Multiview video coding (MVC) emerged as a natural extension of established monoview video coding standards, most notably H.264/Advanced Video Coding (AVC), to address these challenges. While traditional video codecs exploit temporal and spatial redundancies within a single video stream, MVC additionally leverages inter-view redundancies—i.e., similarities between simultaneously captured frames from different camera angles—to achieve higher compression efficiency. This is accomplished through inter-view prediction, wherein a picture in one view may be predicted using reconstructed pictures from other views captured at the same time instance. However, the introduction of inter-view dependencies creates new design challenges that are not present in monoview coding. For instance, the decoding process becomes inherently more complex due to the need to manage reference pictures across both temporal and view dimensions, which increases memory requirements and complicates buffer management. Moreover, the presence of inter-view dependencies can impede parallel processing, a critical requirement for real-time decoding of multiple views in applications such as 3DTV, where several views must be simultaneously rendered to support head-motion parallax.

Existing video coding standards prior to the present invention did not adequately address the unique requirements of multiview systems. Although H.264/AVC provided a robust foundation for monoview compression, it lacked mechanisms for signaling view dependencies, managing multi-dimensional reference picture buffers, or enabling scalable extraction of subsets of views. Similarly, the Scalable Video Coding (SVC) extension of H.264/AVC introduced temporal, spatial, and quality scalability but did not incorporate view scalability or inter-view prediction structures necessary for 3D video. Consequently, early implementations of multiview systems often relied on simulcast approaches—i.e., independently encoding each view using standard H.264/AVC—which resulted in suboptimal compression efficiency due to the failure to exploit inter-view correlations. Other ad hoc solutions attempted to modify the bitstream structure or introduce proprietary signaling, but these lacked standardization, interoperability, and backward compatibility.

Furthermore, the diverse application scenarios for 3D video impose conflicting requirements that complicate system design. In free-viewpoint video, users interactively navigate a 3D scene by selecting arbitrary viewpoints, necessitating efficient random access to any view at any time and the ability to synthesize virtual views from available reference views. This demands low-latency view switching and minimal decoding of non-target views. In contrast, 3DTV applications typically require the simultaneous decoding and display of multiple fixed views to create a stereoscopic or autostereoscopic effect, emphasizing parallel processing capabilities and consistent frame rates across views. Immersive teleconferencing combines aspects of both, requiring both interactivity and high-quality 3D rendering. Additionally, backward compatibility with existing 2D decoders remains a critical market requirement, as legacy set-top boxes and displays must be able to decode at least a base view from an MVC bitstream without modification.

The lack of standardized mechanisms for bitstream adaptation further exacerbated deployment challenges. Network conditions and decoder capabilities vary widely across devices and transmission channels, necessitating the ability to dynamically extract subsets of the full MVC bitstream—e.g., reducing the number of views or lowering the frame rate—without re-encoding. Prior to the present invention, such adaptation required deep parsing of the bitstream or was simply not supported, leading to inefficient use of bandwidth and limited flexibility in service delivery. Similarly, there was no standardized way to signal the dependencies between different operational points (i.e., combinations of views and temporal levels), making it difficult for media gateways or servers to determine which portions of the bitstream could be safely discarded during adaptation.

In summary, the state of the art prior to the present invention was characterized by a fragmented landscape of incompatible solutions, inefficient compression due to underutilized inter-view correlations, inadequate support for scalability and adaptation, poor decoder resource management, and insufficient mechanisms for enabling parallel processing and random access in multiview contexts. These limitations hindered the widespread adoption of 3D video services and underscored the need for a unified, standardized approach that could address the full spectrum of multiview application requirements while maintaining compatibility with existing infrastructure.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive and standardized solution for multiview video coding (MVC) that overcomes the limitations of the prior art by introducing a novel bitstream structure, syntax elements, and supplemental enhancement information (SEI) messages designed to support efficient compression, flexible adaptation, and low-complexity decoding of 3D video content. The invention achieves this through a series of interrelated innovations that collectively address the core challenges of multiview systems, including view and temporal scalability, backward compatibility, random access, parallel decoding, and optimal buffer management.

At the foundation of the invention is a hierarchical bitstream structure based on the Network Abstraction Layer (NAL) unit framework of H.264/AVC, extended to accommodate multiview semantics. The bitstream includes a base view that is fully compliant with H.264/AVC, ensuring that legacy decoders can extract and decode this view without modification. Non-base views are encoded using a new NAL unit type—coded slice of MVC extension—that contains inter-view prediction information and is ignored by H.264/AVC decoders, thereby preserving backward compatibility. To convey multiview-specific properties of the base view (such as temporal level or view dependency) that are not natively supported in H.264/AVC, the invention introduces prefix NAL units, which precede base view VCL NAL units and are similarly ignored by legacy decoders.

Critical to the invention’s adaptability is the inclusion of three new syntax elements in the NAL unit header: view_id, temporal_id, and priority_id. The view_id uniquely identifies the viewpoint associated with each NAL unit, enabling straightforward identification and extraction of specific views. The temporal_id indicates the temporal scalability layer, allowing truncation of higher temporal layers to reduce frame rate. The priority_id facilitates simple one-path adaptation by enabling media gateways to discard NAL units above a certain priority threshold, with the encoder responsible for assigning priority values that ensure any truncated subset remains a valid MVC bitstream.

To support intelligent and efficient bitstream adaptation, the invention introduces the View Scalability Information SEI message (VSSEI). This message enumerates all valid operation points (i.e., combinations of view_id and temporal_id values) supported by the bitstream and provides, for each operation point, essential metadata including profile and level requirements, bit rate estimates, and explicit dependency relationships between operation points. This dependency information goes beyond the static view dependency tree signaled in the Sequence Parameter Set (SPS) by accounting for dynamic inter-picture dependencies, enabling precise identification of non-required NAL units during adaptation and avoiding unnecessary transmission of redundant data.

For applications requiring interactive navigation, the invention enhances random access and view switching capabilities. It defines anchor pictures—marked by an anchor_pic_flag in the NAL header—as lightweight random access points that allow decoding to begin without requiring prior pictures, albeit with potential loss of a few subsequent frames. In addition to conventional IDR (Instantaneous Decoding Refresh) pictures, the invention supports view-IDR (V-IDR) pictures for non-base views, which reset inter-view dependencies within an access unit. All random access points also serve as view switching points, and the invention further enables non-random-access view switching through controlled inter-view prediction structures or SP/SI coding techniques.

A key innovation of the invention is the adoption of time-first coding order, wherein all pictures belonging to the same time instance (across all views) are contiguous in the bitstream. This contrasts with view-first coding, where all pictures of a single view are grouped together. Time-first coding resolves fundamental incompatibilities with media container formats like ISO Base Media File Format, eliminates excessive initial buffering delays, and enables efficient parallel decoding. It also simplifies decoded picture buffer (DPB) management by aligning the output timing of all views and allowing intra-view reference picture marking to operate independently per view, without requiring cross-view coordination.

The invention further optimizes DPB usage through implicit management of inter-view reference pictures. Rather than marking all inter-view reference pictures as “used for reference” (which would burden legacy decoders), the invention allows such pictures to be marked as non-reference when they are only used for inter-view prediction. Their retention in the DPB is then governed by the output timing and view dependency rules, with implicit removal occurring when a view is no longer required for output or as a dependency. An inter_view_flag in the NAL header signals whether a picture is actually used as an inter-view reference, enabling decoders to skip decoding of unused views during adaptation.

To enable real-time decoding of multiple views in 3DTV applications, the invention introduces the Parallel Decoding Information SEI message. This message indicates that the bitstream was encoded under systematic constraints that limit the spatial extent of inter-view prediction—for example, restricting macroblocks in one view to reference only a subset of macroblock rows in another view. These constraints create a pipeline-friendly decoding schedule where processors can work in parallel with a small delay, dramatically reducing the computational burden compared to sequential decoding. The coding efficiency penalty of these constraints is minimal (typically less than 0.1 dB), while the parallelism gain is substantial.

Additional SEI messages enhance functionality: the Active View Information SEI specifies which views are intended for output, allowing decoders to skip unnecessary views; the Multiview Scene and Acquisition Information SEIs provide camera parameters for view synthesis; and the Scalable Nesting SEI allows reuse of existing H.264/AVC SEI messages within specific view and temporal scopes. Together, these features create a robust, flexible, and efficient MVC framework that meets the diverse requirements of 3D video applications while maintaining interoperability and backward compatibility.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF VARIOUS EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a multiview video coding (MVC) system that provides a standardized, efficient, and flexible framework for encoding, transmitting, and decoding three-dimensional video content. The following detailed description sets forth numerous specific embodiments to provide a thorough understanding of the invention. However, those skilled in the art will appreciate that the invention may be practiced without these specific details, and that well-known structures, materials, and processes have not been described in exhaustive detail to avoid obscuring the novel aspects of the invention.

In accordance with the invention, an MVC bitstream is structured using the Network Abstraction Layer (NAL) unit framework inherited from H.264/AVC, with extensions to support multiview semantics. The bitstream comprises a base view and one or more non-base views. The base view is encoded using standard H.264/AVC syntax and NAL unit types, ensuring that it can be decoded by any compliant H.264/AVC decoder without modification. This satisfies the critical requirement for backward compatibility, allowing legacy set-top boxes and 2D displays to render a usable 2D representation from an MVC bitstream. Non-base views, which contain additional viewpoints for 3D rendering, are encoded using a new NAL unit type designated as “coded slice of MVC extension.” When an MVC bitstream is presented to an H.264/AVC decoder, the decoder ignores all NAL units of unrecognized types, including the MVC extension slices and other multiview-specific NAL units, thereby seamlessly decoding only the base view. This elegant design ensures interoperability while enabling enhanced 3D functionality for MVC-capable decoders.

To convey multiview-specific properties of the base view—such as its temporal scalability level or its role in the inter-view prediction hierarchy—the invention introduces a second new NAL unit type called the “prefix NAL unit.” Each prefix NAL unit immediately precedes a base view VCL (Video Coding Layer) NAL unit in the bitstream and contains essential metadata about that base view picture in the context of the multiview sequence. For example, the prefix NAL unit may indicate the temporal_id or view_id of the associated base view picture, information that is not natively signaled in H.264/AVC. Like the MVC extension slices, prefix NAL units are ignored by H.264/AVC decoders, preserving backward compatibility. This dual-layer approach—where base view data resides in standard NAL units and multiview metadata resides in prefix NAL units—provides a clean separation of concerns and minimizes the impact on legacy systems.

The NAL unit header in the MVC bitstream is extended to include several new syntax elements that are critical for adaptation, random access, and decoding control. These include view_id, temporal_id, priority_id, anchor_pic_flag, idr_flag, and inter_view_flag. The view_id is a 10-bit identifier that uniquely specifies the viewpoint associated with each NAL unit, enabling straightforward routing and extraction of specific views by media gateways or decoders. The temporal_id indicates the temporal scalability layer of the picture, with lower values corresponding to lower frame rates and higher values to higher frame rates. Pictures with a given temporal_id may depend on pictures with equal or lower temporal_id values but never on pictures with higher temporal_id values, ensuring that truncation of higher temporal layers yields a valid, decodable sub-bitstream. The priority_id is a non-normative field used primarily for simple one-path adaptation; media gateways can discard all NAL units with priority_id greater than a specified threshold, and the encoder is responsible for assigning priority_id values such that any resulting subset remains a conforming MVC bitstream. This allows for rapid bandwidth adaptation without deep bitstream parsing.

The anchor_pic_flag indicates whether a picture is an “anchor picture,” a lightweight random access point that allows decoding to begin without requiring prior pictures in the same view. Anchor pictures are particularly useful in open GOP (Group of Pictures) structures, where they provide random access capability with minimal compression efficiency loss compared to full IDR (Instantaneous Decoding Refresh) pictures. The idr_flag is set to 1 for IDR pictures in the base view and for view-IDR (V-IDR) pictures in non-base views; V-IDR pictures reset inter-view dependencies within their access unit. The inter_view_flag indicates whether a picture is actually used as an inter-view reference by other views, enabling decoders to skip decoding of views that are neither output nor required as references, thereby saving computational resources during adaptation.

A cornerstone of the invention’s adaptability is the View Scalability Information SEI message (VSSEI). This message is inserted into the bitstream to enumerate all valid operation points—i.e., combinations of view_id and temporal_id values—that the encoder supports. For each operation point, the VSSEI provides detailed metadata including the required decoder profile and level, estimated bit rate, and crucially, explicit dependency relationships with other operation points. These dependencies go beyond the static view dependency tree signaled in the Sequence Parameter Set (SPS) by accounting for dynamic inter-picture dependencies that may exist even within the same view. For example, in a two-view bitstream, the highest temporal layer pictures in view 1 might not actually depend on the highest temporal layer pictures in view 0, even though the SPS indicates a general view dependency. The VSSEI can signal this refined dependency, allowing a media gateway to discard the unused high-temporal-layer pictures in view 0 when adapting to an operation point that includes view 1, thereby reducing bandwidth without affecting decodability. This level of granularity is essential for efficient adaptation in heterogeneous networks and is a significant improvement over prior art.

Random access and view switching are enhanced through a combination of IDR/V-IDR pictures, anchor pictures, and controlled inter-view prediction structures. An IDR access unit is defined as an access unit where all views contain IDR pictures, providing a full reset of both temporal and inter-view dependencies. However, the invention also allows mixed access units containing both IDR and non-IDR pictures, offering flexibility. Anchor pictures, marked by anchor_pic_flag=1, serve as alternative random access points with slightly relaxed constraints—they allow a few subsequent pictures to be lost but enable higher compression efficiency. All random access points inherently serve as view switching points, as they provide a clean state from which new target views can be decoded. Additionally, the invention supports non-random-access view switching by designing inter-view prediction relationships such that, at certain pictures, switching from one view subset to another is possible without decoding intermediate views. This is particularly valuable in free-viewpoint video applications where users frequently change their viewing position.

The invention adopts a time-first coding order as a fundamental architectural choice to resolve numerous system-level challenges. In time-first coding, all pictures belonging to the same time instance (i.e., captured at the same moment) are contiguous in the bitstream, regardless of their view. This contrasts with view-first coding, where all pictures of a single view are grouped together. Time-first coding aligns naturally with the concept of an “access unit,” which in MVC is defined as the set of NAL units corresponding to a single time instance across all views. This structure is compatible with media container formats like ISO Base Media File Format, which require samples (i.e., access units) to be ordered by decoding time. View-first coding would cause excessive composition time offsets and initial buffering delays in such containers, whereas time-first coding avoids these issues. Moreover, time-first coding simplifies decoded picture buffer (DPB) management by ensuring that all views output their pictures simultaneously, allowing the DPB to be managed per view without complex cross-view coordination. It also enables efficient parallel decoding, as discussed below.

DPB management is optimized through a combination of time-first coding and implicit handling of inter-view reference pictures. Intra-view reference picture marking operates independently within each view, using standard H.264/AVC mechanisms like sliding window and memory management control operations (MMCO). Inter-view reference pictures—those used only for predicting other views and not for temporal prediction within their own view—are not required to be marked as “used for reference.” Instead, their retention in the DPB is governed by the output timing and the view dependency rules signaled in the SPS. If a view is not required for output and is not a dependency for any output view, its pictures can be implicitly removed from the DPB as soon as they are no longer needed for inter-view prediction. The inter_view_flag in the NAL header signals whether a picture is actually used as an inter-view reference, allowing decoders to make informed decisions about which views to decode during adaptation. This approach minimizes memory usage for both full MVC decoders and legacy H.264/AVC decoders, which would otherwise be burdened by unnecessary reference pictures.

Parallel decoding of multiple views is enabled by the Parallel Decoding Information SEI message. This message indicates that the bitstream was encoded under systematic spatial constraints that limit the extent of inter-view prediction. Specifically, the encoder restricts macroblocks in one view to reference only a predefined subset of macroblock rows in another view. For example, the first row of macroblocks in view 1 might be allowed to reference only the first two rows of view 0, the second row of view 1 might reference the first three rows of view 0, and so on. These constraints create a pipelined decoding schedule where multiple processors can work in parallel with a small, predictable delay. Processor P0 decodes view 0, and once it has decoded the first two rows, it notifies processor P1 that the first row of view 1 can be decoded. P1 then decodes the first row of view 1 while P0 proceeds to the third row of view 0, and the process continues. This parallelism is essential for real-time 3DTV applications that require simultaneous decoding of many views (e.g., up to 100 views for advanced autostereoscopic displays). The coding efficiency penalty of these constraints is negligible (typically less than 0.08 dB), while the computational savings are substantial, making real-time decoding feasible on consumer hardware.

Additional SEI messages enhance the functionality and usability of the MVC bitstream. The Active View Information SEI message specifies which views are intended for output, allowing decoders to skip decoding of non-output views and their non-required dependencies, thereby conserving resources. The Multiview Scene Information and Multiview Acquisition Information SEI messages provide camera calibration parameters (e.g., focal length, position, orientation) that are essential for view synthesis algorithms at the renderer, enabling the generation of virtual viewpoints not explicitly encoded in the bitstream. The Scalable Nesting SEI message allows existing H.264/AVC SEI messages (e.g., recovery point, buffering period) to be applied to specific subsets of views and temporal layers, ensuring that all legacy SEI functionality is preserved and extended to the multiview domain.

In operation, an MVC encoder generates a bitstream that includes the base view, non-base views, prefix NAL units, and the aforementioned SEI messages. The encoder assigns view_id, temporal_id, and priority_id values to each NAL unit and constructs the VSSEI to describe all valid operation points. During transmission, a media gateway can perform adaptation by extracting a sub-bitstream based on the client’s capabilities and network conditions. Using the VSSEI, the gateway identifies the appropriate operation point and discards NAL units outside the required view_id and temporal_id ranges, or above a certain priority_id threshold. The resulting sub-bitstream is guaranteed to be decodable by an MVC decoder. At the decoder, the time-first coding order ensures efficient DPB management, while the Parallel Decoding Information SEI enables parallel processing if multiple cores are available. The decoder uses the active view information to determine which views to output and skips unnecessary views, leveraging the inter_view_flag to avoid decoding unused inter-view references. Random access and view switching are handled by seeking to the nearest anchor or IDR picture and decoding the required views from that point.

The invention thus provides a complete, standardized solution for multiview video coding that addresses the full spectrum of 3D video application requirements—from free-viewpoint interactivity to 3DTV broadcasting—while maintaining backward compatibility, enabling efficient adaptation, and minimizing decoder resource consumption. The synergistic combination of time-first coding, intelligent SEI messages, optimized buffer management, and parallel decoding constraints represents a significant advancement over the prior art and enables the practical deployment of 3D video services in real-world networks and devices.
