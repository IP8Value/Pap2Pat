# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The mammalian heart possesses an extremely limited intrinsic capacity for regeneration following injury, particularly in the adult stage. Unlike lower vertebrates such as zebrafish or neonatal mammals, which can regenerate substantial portions of cardiac tissue after damage through robust cardiomyocyte proliferation, adult human and murine hearts respond to myocardial infarction (MI) primarily by forming non-contractile fibrotic scar tissue rather than replacing lost cardiomyocytes with functional myocardium. This fundamental biological limitation underlies the progressive deterioration of cardiac function observed in patients suffering from ischemic heart disease and represents a major unmet clinical need. Despite advances in reperfusion therapies, pharmacological management, and mechanical support devices, heart failure remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, largely because current treatments do not address the root problem of irreversible cardiomyocyte loss.

Extensive research over the past two decades has revealed that the proliferative potential of cardiomyocytes is not entirely extinguished in adulthood but is instead profoundly suppressed by multiple molecular and cellular mechanisms. Notably, studies involving apical resection in neonatal mice and pigs have demonstrated that the early postnatal mammalian heart retains a transient window of regenerative competence, during which complete structural and functional recovery can occur without significant scarring. This regenerative capacity is primarily mediated by the proliferation of preexisting cardiomyocytes rather than the recruitment of stem or progenitor cells, highlighting the therapeutic potential of reactivating endogenous cardiomyocyte cell cycle activity in the injured adult heart. However, this regenerative window closes rapidly within the first week after birth in rodents and likely even earlier in larger mammals, coinciding with cardiomyocyte binucleation, metabolic maturation, and the establishment of terminal cell cycle arrest.

Among the key signaling pathways implicated in the regulation of cardiomyocyte proliferation and survival is the Hippo signaling cascade, a highly conserved kinase network originally identified in Drosophila for its role in controlling organ size. In mammals, the Hippo pathway functions as a central regulator of cell proliferation, apoptosis, and tissue homeostasis across multiple organ systems, including the heart. The core components of the Hippo pathway include the serine/threonine kinases MST1/2 (mammalian Ste20-like kinases 1 and 2) and LATS1/2 (large tumor suppressor kinases 1 and 2), which phosphorylate and thereby inactivate the transcriptional coactivators Yes-associated protein (YAP) and transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ). When Hippo signaling is active, YAP and TAZ are phosphorylated, sequestered in the cytoplasm, and targeted for proteasomal degradation. Conversely, when Hippo signaling is inhibited—whether by developmental cues, mechanical stimuli, or pharmacological intervention—YAP and TAZ remain unphosphorylated, translocate into the nucleus, and form complexes with TEA domain (TEAD) family transcription factors to drive the expression of genes involved in cell cycle progression, anti-apoptosis, and cytoskeletal remodeling.

Genetic studies in mice have unequivocally established the critical role of YAP/TAZ in cardiac development and regeneration. Cardiomyocyte-specific deletion of YAP results in embryonic lethality due to severe cardiac hypoplasia, while conditional overexpression of a constitutively active form of YAP in postnatal cardiomyocytes enhances their proliferative capacity and confers resistance to ischemic injury. Similarly, pharmacological inhibition of upstream Hippo kinases or direct activation of YAP/TAZ has been shown to promote cardiomyocyte proliferation and improve cardiac function in various animal models of MI. These findings have spurred intense interest in developing small-molecule modulators of the Hippo-YAP/TAZ axis as potential therapeutics for heart disease.

One such compound is TT-10 (chemical formula C11H10FN3OS2), a fluorinated derivative of the previously identified TAZ-12 molecule. TT-10 was discovered through high-throughput screening as a potent inducer of cardiomyocyte proliferation and survival in vitro. Subsequent in vivo studies demonstrated that systemic administration of free (unencapsulated) TT-10 via intraperitoneal injection reduced infarct size in a mouse model of MI, consistent with its pro-proliferative and anti-apoptotic effects on cardiomyocytes. However, these benefits were transient; despite initial improvements, cardiac function in TT-10–treated animals progressively declined over time, ultimately converging with that of control groups. This limitation is likely attributable to the pharmacokinetic profile of free TT-10, which exhibits rapid systemic clearance, poor bioavailability at the site of injury, and insufficient duration of action to sustain meaningful regenerative responses.

To overcome these delivery challenges, nanotechnology-based strategies have emerged as promising platforms for enhancing the efficacy of cardiovascular therapeutics. Among the most extensively studied and clinically validated nanocarriers are poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticles (NPs). PLGA is a biodegradable and biocompatible copolymer approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for use in drug delivery, surgical sutures, and medical implants. PLGA NPs offer several advantages for cardiac drug delivery: they protect encapsulated payloads from premature degradation, enable controlled and sustained release over days to weeks, enhance local retention at the target site, and can be administered via minimally invasive routes such as catheter-based intramyocardial injection. Previous work has demonstrated that growth factors and small-molecule inhibitors encapsulated in PLGA NPs exhibit significantly prolonged biological activity compared to their free counterparts, resulting in improved therapeutic outcomes in models of myocardial injury.

Despite these advances, no prior art has successfully combined TT-10 with a sustained-release nanoparticle delivery system to achieve durable cardiac repair following MI. The present invention addresses this critical gap by providing a novel pharmaceutical composition comprising TT-10 encapsulated within PLGA nanoparticles, which enables localized, prolonged, and efficient delivery of TT-10 to the injured myocardium. This approach not only amplifies the intrinsic regenerative effects of TT-10 but also mitigates the need for high systemic doses, thereby reducing the risk of off-target toxicity and manufacturing burden. The invention further provides methods of using this composition to treat myocardial infarction and other forms of cardiac injury characterized by cardiomyocyte loss, offering a transformative strategy for heart regeneration that leverages endogenous repair mechanisms rather than exogenous cell transplantation or mechanical replacement.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising TT-10, a fluorine-containing small molecule with pro-regenerative activity, encapsulated within poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticles. This composition is designed for the treatment of myocardial infarction and other cardiac conditions involving loss of functional cardiomyocytes. The invention further encompasses methods of administering said composition to a subject in need thereof, wherein the PLGA nanoparticles provide sustained release of TT-10 over a period of up to four weeks, thereby enhancing the potency, durability, and therapeutic efficacy of TT-10 compared to conventional systemic or bolus delivery methods.

In one aspect, the invention relates to a nanoparticle formulation wherein TT-10 is encapsulated in PLGA nanoparticles having a mean diameter of approximately 100 to 200 nanometers, with a drug loading efficiency of at least 2.0 micrograms of TT-10 per milligram of PLGA. The nanoparticles are configured to release at least 40% of the encapsulated TT-10 within the first 24 hours and at least 85% by day 9 under physiological conditions, thereby maintaining effective local concentrations of TT-10 in the myocardium over an extended period. This controlled release profile is critical for sustaining activation of the Hippo/YAP signaling pathway in cardiomyocytes, which drives cell cycle re-entry, inhibits apoptosis, and promotes tissue repair.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating myocardial infarction in a mammalian subject, comprising administering to the peri-infarct or border zone region of the heart an effective amount of the TT-10–loaded PLGA nanoparticle composition. Administration may be performed via direct intramyocardial injection during open-chest surgery or, more preferably, through minimally invasive techniques such as echo-guided transthoracic or catheter-based delivery. The total dose of TT-10 administered is significantly lower—by several orders of magnitude—than doses required for equivalent efficacy with free TT-10, thereby improving the therapeutic index and reducing potential adverse effects.

The therapeutic benefits of the invention are multifaceted. First, TT-10–loaded nanoparticles induce robust proliferation of preexisting cardiomyocytes, as evidenced by increased expression of cell cycle markers including Ki67, phosphorylated histone H3 (PH3), Aurora B kinase, and bromodeoxyuridine (BrdU) incorporation. Second, the composition significantly reduces cardiomyocyte apoptosis in the early post-infarction period, a critical window during which oxidative stress and inflammatory mediators trigger widespread cell death. Third, TT-10–NP treatment enhances nuclear translocation of YAP, confirming on-target modulation of the Hippo signaling pathway, which is mechanistically linked to both proliferative and anti-apoptotic effects. Fourth, the invention promotes angiogenesis in the peri-infarct region, as demonstrated by increased density of endothelial and smooth muscle–lined vascular structures, thereby improving oxygen and nutrient supply to the recovering tissue. Collectively, these effects translate into measurable improvements in cardiac structure and function: treated subjects exhibit significantly smaller infarct sizes, reduced left ventricular dilation, and preserved ejection fraction and fractional shortening over at least four weeks post-injury—outcomes that are not achieved with equivalent doses of free TT-10 or empty nanoparticles.

Importantly, the proliferative response induced by TT-10–NPs is self-limiting and temporally restricted to the period of active drug release, which aligns with the natural kinetics of nanoparticle degradation. This feature is advantageous because persistent cardiomyocyte proliferation could lead to immature, arrhythmogenic tissue; the transient nature of the effect ensures that newly divided cardiomyocytes have the opportunity to mature and integrate functionally into the existing myocardial syncytium. Furthermore, the use of PLGA—a clinically approved, biodegradable polymer—ensures biocompatibility and eliminates concerns regarding long-term nanoparticle accumulation.

Thus, the invention represents a significant advancement in cardiac regenerative medicine by combining a mechanistically rational small-molecule activator of endogenous repair pathways with an optimized delivery platform that maximizes therapeutic impact while minimizing systemic exposure. The composition and methods described herein are applicable not only to acute myocardial infarction but also to chronic ischemic cardiomyopathy, heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, and other conditions where cardiomyocyte loss contributes to disease progression.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

As used herein, the term “TT-10” refers to the chemical compound having the molecular formula C11H10FN3OS2, specifically 2-(4-fluorophenyl)-5-(thiazol-2-yl)-1,3,4-thiadiazole, or any pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, or stereoisomer thereof. TT-10 is a fluorinated analog of TAZ-12 and functions as a modulator of the Hippo signaling pathway, promoting cardiomyocyte proliferation and survival.

The term “PLGA nanoparticles” or “PLGA NPs” denotes nanoparticles composed of poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid), a biodegradable copolymer synthesized from lactic acid and glycolic acid monomers. PLGA degrades in vivo via hydrolysis of its ester linkages into lactic and glycolic acids, which are normal metabolic intermediates readily cleared by the body. The lactic acid to glycolic acid ratio in the copolymer may vary (e.g., 50:50, 75:25, 85:15) to tune degradation kinetics and drug release profiles, though a 50:50 ratio is preferred in the present invention for balanced release characteristics.

“Encapsulation” or “loading” refers to the physical entrapment of TT-10 within the polymeric matrix of the PLGA nanoparticle during fabrication, as opposed to surface adsorption or simple mixing. Encapsulation efficiency is defined as the percentage of the initial TT-10 used in the formulation process that is successfully incorporated into the nanoparticles, and in the present invention, it is at least 80%, corresponding to a loading capacity of approximately 2.5 μg TT-10 per mg PLGA.

“Myocardial infarction” or “MI” denotes the necrosis of cardiac tissue resulting from prolonged ischemia due to obstruction of coronary blood flow, typically caused by thrombotic occlusion of a coronary artery. The “peri-infarct region” or “border zone” refers to the viable myocardial tissue immediately adjacent to the infarcted area, which is at high risk of secondary degeneration but also represents the primary site for regenerative interventions.

“Cardiomyocyte proliferation” encompasses the full spectrum of cell cycle re-entry and division by terminally differentiated cardiomyocytes, including DNA synthesis (S phase), chromatin condensation and mitotic entry (M phase), cytokinesis, and successful completion of cell division. This is distinct from hypertrophy, which involves cell enlargement without division.

“Hippo signaling pathway” refers to the evolutionarily conserved kinase cascade comprising MST1/2, LATS1/2, and their downstream effectors YAP and TAZ. “YAP signaling” specifically denotes the transcriptional activity mediated by nuclear YAP in complex with TEAD transcription factors, leading to expression of genes such as CTGF, CYR61, and AREG that regulate proliferation and survival.

“Sustained release” means the gradual liberation of TT-10 from the PLGA matrix over an extended period, typically ranging from several days to four weeks, as opposed to immediate or burst release. In the context of this invention, sustained release is characterized by an initial release of 40–50% of the payload within 24 hours, followed by continuous elution such that at least 85% is released by day 9 under physiological conditions (37°C, pH 7.4).

“Effective amount” refers to the quantity of TT-10–loaded PLGA nanoparticles sufficient to produce a statistically significant improvement in one or more parameters of cardiac structure or function, such as reduction in infarct size, preservation of ejection fraction, or increase in cardiomyocyte proliferation markers, without causing unacceptable toxicity.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The present invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising TT-10 encapsulated in PLGA nanoparticles. The nanoparticles are prepared using a single-emulsion solvent evaporation technique, wherein PLGA is dissolved in an organic solvent such as dichloromethane, and TT-10 is either co-dissolved or dispersed in this organic phase. This mixture is then emulsified into an aqueous phase containing a stabilizer such as polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) or dimethylamine borane, followed by ultrasonication to reduce droplet size. Evaporation of the organic solvent yields solid nanoparticles with TT-10 entrapped within the polymer matrix. The resulting nanoparticles exhibit a spherical morphology with a smooth surface, as confirmed by scanning electron microscopy, and a hydrodynamic diameter of 120 to 160 nanometers, as measured by nanoparticle tracking analysis.

The composition is formulated as a sterile, lyophilized powder or aqueous suspension suitable for parenteral administration. For intramyocardial delivery, the nanoparticles are resuspended in a physiologically compatible vehicle such as Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline (DPBS) at a concentration that allows precise dosing—typically 1 to 10 mg of nanoparticles per milliliter. The final injectable formulation contains between 2 and 3 micrograms of TT-10 per milligram of PLGA, ensuring that even low-volume injections (e.g., 8–24 μL total) deliver therapeutically relevant doses.

Stability studies demonstrate that the lyophilized nanoparticles retain structural integrity and drug content for at least six months when stored at –80°C, and the reconstituted suspension remains stable for up to 24 hours at 4°C, facilitating clinical use. The composition is free of endotoxins and meets sterility requirements for implantable biomaterials.

Optionally, the nanoparticles may be surface-functionalized with targeting ligands such as peptides or antibodies that bind to markers upregulated in injured myocardium (e.g., integrins, selectins), although in the preferred embodiment, passive targeting via direct intramyocardial injection obviates the need for active targeting moieties. The composition may also include imaging agents such as coumarin-6 for real-time tracking of nanoparticle biodistribution, though such agents are not required for therapeutic efficacy.

### EXAMPLES

**Example 1: Preparation and Characterization of TT-10–Loaded PLGA Nanoparticles**

PLGA nanoparticles were fabricated using a single-emulsion method. Briefly, 100 mg of PLGA (50:50 lactic:glycolic acid ratio, inherent viscosity 0.55–0.75 dL/g) was dissolved in 5 mL dichloromethane. Separately, TT-10 (2 mg/mL in dimethyl sulfoxide) was added to the PLGA solution at a volume sufficient to achieve a theoretical loading of 3%. The organic phase was probe-sonicated on ice (40% amplitude, 40 s on/20 s off, total 2 min) and then emulsified into 20 mL of 1% (w/v) dimethylamine borane aqueous solution under sonication (same parameters). The resulting oil-in-water emulsion was poured into 10 mL of 4% PVA solution diluted to 40 mL with Milli-Q water and stirred for 4 h to allow solvent evaporation. Nanoparticles were collected by centrifugation (45,000g, 20 min), washed twice, lyophilized, and stored at –80°C. Empty nanoparticles were prepared identically without TT-10.

Dynamic light scattering revealed mean diameters of 123.3 ± 1.7 nm for empty NPs and 155.4 ± 0.7 nm for TT-10–NPs. Scanning electron microscopy confirmed spherical morphology. Drug loading was quantified by dissolving nanoparticles in acetonitrile and measuring TT-10 absorbance at 200 nm via UV-Vis spectroscopy, yielding a loading efficiency of 2.50 ± 0.08 μg TT-10 per mg PLGA. In vitro release studies using dialysis bags (MWCO 20 kDa) in DPBS + 0.1% BSA at 37°C with shaking showed 47% release at 24 h and 90% by day 9.

**Example 2: In Vitro Effects of TT-10–NPs on Human iPSC-Derived Cardiomyocytes**

Human induced pluripotent stem cell–derived cardiomyocytes (hiPSC-CMs) were cultured and treated with free TT-10 (0–100 μM) or TT-10–NPs (equivalent to 10 μM TT-10) for 48 h. Immunofluorescence staining showed that 10 μM TT-10 maximally induced Ki67+, BrdU+, PH3+, and Aurora B+ cardiomyocytes, with declines at 100 μM, indicating a biphasic dose response. TT-10–NPs produced similar proliferative effects. TUNEL assays revealed a 60% reduction in apoptosis with TT-10 treatment. Western blotting showed decreased phospho-YAP (Ser127) without changes in total YAP, confirming pathway activation. Nuclear YAP immunofluorescence increased 3.5-fold. Coumarin-6–labeled NPs were internalized by hiPSC-CMs within 24 h.

**Example 3: In Vivo Efficacy in a Mouse Myocardial Infarction Model**

Myocardial infarction was induced in C57BL/6 mice by permanent LAD ligation. One hour post-MI, animals received intramyocardial injections (3 sites, 8 μL each) of DPBS, empty NPs, free TT-10 solution (67.2 ng total), or TT-10–NPs (67.2 ng TT-10). Sham-operated mice served as controls. Echocardiography at 1 and 4 weeks showed that only the TT-10–NP group maintained stable LVEF (~45%) and LVFS (~22%), while other MI groups deteriorated (LVEF ~30% at week 4). Infarct size at 4 weeks was 20% smaller in TT-10–NP mice versus controls. Heart weight/body weight ratios were normalized.

**Example 4: Mechanistic Studies in Treated Hearts**

At 1 week post-MI, TT-10–NP hearts exhibited 4–5-fold increases in Ki67+, PH3+, and nuclear YAP+ cardiomyocytes in the border zone versus all other groups. Apoptosis (TUNEL+) was reduced by 50% at 72 h. By 4 weeks, proliferative markers returned to baseline, indicating transient activation. Vascular density (isolectin B4+ and α-SMA+ structures) was 2-fold higher in TT-10–NP hearts at 4 weeks, demonstrating pro-angiogenic effects. Coumarin-6 tracking showed >70% NP degradation by week 1 and <1% remaining at week 4, correlating with the temporal window of biological activity.

These examples collectively demonstrate that PLGA nanoparticle–mediated delivery of TT-10 dramatically enhances its therapeutic efficacy in myocardial infarction by enabling sustained, localized action that activates endogenous regenerative pathways with high precision and safety.
