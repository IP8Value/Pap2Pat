# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

MLL3 and MLL4 are evolutionarily conserved members of the COMPASS family of histone H3 lysine 4 methyltransferases that play indispensable roles in the regulation of enhancer activity across mammalian genomes. These enzymes catalyze the mono-methylation of histone H3 at lysine 4, a modification tightly associated with active enhancers and the transcriptional activation of genes governing cell identity, differentiation, and tissue-specific functions. Beyond their catalytic function, MLL3 and MLL4 serve as critical scaffolding platforms that recruit additional chromatin-modifying complexes, including UTX and p300, to facilitate enhancer activation through coordinated histone acetylation and demethylation. Their activity is essential for the proper execution of developmental programs such as myogenesis, adipogenesis, cardiogenesis, and immune cell maturation, where precise spatiotemporal gene expression is required. Loss of either MLL3 or MLL4 leads to profound developmental defects, with MLL4 being critical for post-implantation embryonic development and MLL3 playing a more specialized role in late gestation and organ maturation. In somatic tissues, these proteins function as tumor suppressors, frequently inactivated by truncating or missense mutations in a wide array of human cancers. The Catalogue of Somatic Mutations in Cancer (COSMIC) has identified MLL3 and MLL4 as among the most commonly mutated chromatin regulators across tumor types, including colorectal, bladder, breast, and hematological malignancies. These mutations are not merely passenger events but are often clonally selected, suggesting they confer a selective advantage during tumorigenesis. Despite extensive genomic documentation of these mutations, the downstream molecular consequences that link MLL3/4 loss to cancer progression have remained poorly understood, leaving a critical gap in therapeutic strategy development for these genetically defined cancers.

## SUMMARY

The invention discloses a novel therapeutic approach for the treatment of cancers characterized by loss-of-function mutations in MLL3 or MLL4, which are components of the COMPASS histone H3 lysine 4 methyltransferase complex. This method exploits a synthetic lethal interaction wherein MLL3/4-deficient cancer cells become uniquely dependent on de novo nucleotide synthesis for survival, rendering them exquisitely sensitive to pharmacological inhibition of this pathway. The invention specifically encompasses the administration of nucleotide synthesis inhibitors to subjects harboring MLL3 or MLL4 loss-of-function mutations, thereby selectively targeting tumor cells while sparing normal cells with intact MLL3/4 function. The nucleotide synthesis inhibitors include, but are not limited to, agents that inhibit de novo purine synthesis such as lometrexol, pelitrexol, and methotrexate, as well as inhibitors of de novo pyrimidine synthesis such as brequinar, leflunomide, and lometrexol in combination with pyrimidine pathway inhibitors. The invention further specifies that the cancers amenable to this treatment include, but are not restricted to, colorectal cancer, bladder cancer, breast cancer, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, medulloblastoma, and prostate cancer, provided they harbor inactivating mutations in MLL3 or MLL4. The method further comprises obtaining a biological sample from a subject, testing the sample for the presence of MLL3 or MLL4 loss-of-function mutations as a biomarker, and administering an effective amount of a nucleotide synthesis inhibitor only if the biomarker is detected. This biomarker-guided strategy enables precise patient stratification, ensuring that therapy is directed exclusively toward those individuals most likely to benefit, thereby maximizing therapeutic efficacy and minimizing off-target toxicity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive disclosure relating to the identification and therapeutic exploitation of a metabolic vulnerability in cancers driven by loss-of-function mutations in MLL3 or MLL4. The invention is grounded in the discovery that the absence of functional MLL3 or MLL4 protein leads to a compensatory reprogramming of chromatin architecture and gene expression that results in a heightened dependency on de novo nucleotide synthesis pathways. This dependency arises not from a direct loss of catalytic activity alone, but from broader epigenetic and transcriptional rewiring, including the redistribution of COMPASS subunits and increased occupancy of MLL1 at purine biosynthesis gene promoters. The term “MLL3/4 COMPASS deficient cancer” refers to any malignant neoplasm in which the MLL3 or MLL4 gene is rendered nonfunctional through somatic mutation, including but not limited to nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, or missense mutations that disrupt catalytic or structural domains, leading to loss of protein expression or function. The method of treatment involves the administration of a nucleotide synthesis inhibitor to a subject diagnosed with such a cancer. The subject in need of treatment is defined as an individual whose tumor tissue or circulating tumor DNA has been confirmed to harbor a loss-of-function mutation in either MLL3 or MLL4, as determined by molecular diagnostic assays. MLL3 and MLL4 are large, multi-domain proteins that function as the principal H3K4 mono-methyltransferases at enhancer elements, where they facilitate chromatin accessibility and transcriptional activation through both enzymatic and non-enzymatic mechanisms. The COMPASS family includes multiple distinct complexes, with MLL3 and MLL4 forming a branch distinct from MLL1, MLL2, and SET1A/B, each with unique subunit compositions and genomic targeting properties. Loss-of-function mutations in MLL3 or MLL4 result in diminished H3K4me1 and H3K27ac at enhancers, leading to downregulation of genes involved in cell adhesion, cytoskeletal organization, and developmental signaling. In response, the cell compensates by increasing the chromatin occupancy of MLL1 and its associated WRAD complex, which in turn drives the aberrant upregulation of genes involved in de novo purine and pyrimidine synthesis. De novo nucleotide synthesis is the biochemical pathway by which cells generate purine and pyrimidine nucleotides from simple precursors such as amino acids, carbon dioxide, and formate, rather than recycling pre-existing nucleosides through salvage pathways. Nucleotide synthesis inhibitors are compounds that interfere with one or more enzymatic steps in this biosynthetic cascade. De novo purine synthesis inhibitors include lometrexol, which specifically targets glycinamide ribonucleotide formyltransferase (GARFT), as well as pelitrexol and methotrexate, which inhibit folate metabolism and thereby restrict the availability of one-carbon units essential for purine ring formation. Other purine synthesis inhibitors include agents targeting PPAT, PFAS, PAICS, ADSL, AdSS, and GMPS, each of which catalyzes a discrete step in the ten-step purine biosynthetic pathway. De novo pyrimidine synthesis inhibitors include compounds such as brequinar and leflunomide, which inhibit dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH), and lometrexol in combination with inhibitors of CAD, the multifunctional enzyme catalyzing the first three steps of pyrimidine synthesis. Lometrexol is a potent antifolate that competitively inhibits GARFT, thereby blocking the conversion of glycinamide ribonucleotide to formylglycinamide ribonucleotide, a critical step in purine biosynthesis. The method of treatment involves administering an effective amount of such an inhibitor to a subject diagnosed with MLL3/4 COMPASS deficient cancer. ### EXAMPLES

The invention is further illustrated by a series of experimental examples demonstrating the synthetic lethality between MLL3/4 deficiency and nucleotide synthesis inhibition. In one example, CRISPR-Cas9 was employed to generate double-knockout mouse embryonic stem cells lacking both Mll3 and Mll4, which were confirmed by Western blot analysis using antibodies specific to the N-terminal and C-terminal regions of each protein. Genome-wide CRISPR dropout screening revealed that genes encoding enzymes of de novo purine and pyrimidine synthesis were among the most significantly enriched for negative selection in Mll3/4 knockout cells compared to wild-type controls. Metabolomic profiling using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry demonstrated elevated steady-state levels of purine intermediates and increased flux through de novo purine synthesis upon 15N-glutamine tracing, while pyrimidine synthesis remained unchanged. Treatment with lometrexol resulted in selective cytotoxicity in Mll3/4-deficient cells, an effect that was fully rescued by supplementation with hypoxanthine, confirming the specificity of purine synthesis inhibition as the mechanism of cell death. In human colorectal cancer cell lines harboring endogenous MLL4 truncation mutations, similar sensitivity to lometrexol was observed, whereas MLL4 wild-type lines remained resistant. In vivo xenograft models using HCT116 cells with MLL4 mutations demonstrated significant tumor growth inhibition upon lometrexol administration, with no effect on tumors derived from MLL4 wild-type HT55 cells. Proteomic and transcriptomic analyses confirmed upregulation of purine metabolism genes and downregulation of mitotic cell cycle genes in response to lometrexol, with a defined signature of 217 genes associated with mitotic arrest selectively suppressed in MLL4-mutant cells. These findings were corroborated by chromatin conformation capture (Hi-C), which revealed that MLL3/4 loss induces a shift in higher-order chromatin structure toward more open A compartments, correlating with increased expression of purine synthesis genes. The invention further encompasses the use of this metabolic dependency as a predictive biomarker for patient selection, enabling the clinical application of nucleotide synthesis inhibitors in a precision oncology framework. The examples collectively establish that MLL3/4 deficiency creates a unique metabolic vulnerability that can be therapeutically targeted with existing or novel nucleotide synthesis inhibitors, providing a rational, biomarker-driven strategy for treating a broad spectrum of human cancers.
