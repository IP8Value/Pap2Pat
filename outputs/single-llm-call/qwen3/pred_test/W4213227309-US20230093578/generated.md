## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to nuclear spin wave quantum registers for the storage, manipulation, and retrieval of quantum information in solid-state systems. Specifically, it concerns a novel architecture and protocol for coupling a qubit with zero magnetic dipole moment to a collective nuclear spin register through engineered spin-exchange interactions, enabling high-fidelity quantum memory, entanglement generation, and coherent control in environments otherwise dominated by decoherence and spectral inhomogeneity. The invention is particularly suited for rare-earth ion doped crystals where nuclear spins serve as long-lived quantum memories, and where conventional magnetic dipole-based coupling mechanisms are inapplicable due to the absence of intrinsic dipole moments in the qubit species.

### 2. Description of Related Art

Solid-state nuclear spin systems have long been considered promising candidates for quantum memory due to their exceptionally long coherence times and compatibility with cryogenic environments. However, their utility has been severely limited by two fundamental challenges: first, the inability to directly address individual nuclear spins via magnetic dipole interactions when the coupled qubit lacks a magnetic dipole moment; and second, the spectral inhomogeneity and disorder inherent in nuclear spin ensembles, which prevent the formation of collective, indistinguishable spin states necessary for scalable quantum operations. Conventional approaches rely on magnetic dipole coupling between electron and nuclear spins, which requires the qubit to possess a non-zero magnetic moment—this precludes the use of many high-coherence qubit candidates such as the ground-state 171Yb3+ ion in YVO4, whose electronic wavefunction is symmetric and yields vanishing dipole matrix elements. Alternative schemes using electric quadrupole or hyperfine interactions suffer from weak coupling strengths and poor selectivity, leading to slow gate operations and susceptibility to environmental noise. Furthermore, existing dynamical decoupling and polarization protocols fail to simultaneously suppress bath-induced decoherence, polarize the register, and enable coherent spin exchange without introducing additional error channels. The result is a fundamental impasse: while nuclear spins offer long storage times, they cannot be efficiently coupled to qubits that are otherwise optimal for coherence and control. No prior system has demonstrated a method to generate a controllable, spin-preserving exchange interaction between a zero-dipole qubit and a multi-spin nuclear register while suppressing all non-exchange couplings and noise sources in a single, synchronized protocol.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention discloses a novel quantum system and method for transferring quantum information between a qubit possessing zero magnetic dipole moment and a collective nuclear spin register through engineered spin-exchange interactions mediated by a synchronized radiofrequency field. The system comprises a rare-earth ion qubit embedded in a crystalline host, wherein the qubit’s electronic ground state has no intrinsic magnetic dipole moment, and a surrounding ensemble of nuclear spins with spin greater than one-half, arranged in a lattice geometry that enables homogeneous coupling to the qubit. The invention introduces a protocol, termed ZenPol, that employs a sequence of precisely timed microwave pulses applied to the qubit, synchronized with a square-wave radiofrequency magnetic field, to dynamically engineer an effective spin-exchange Hamiltonian of the form Ŝ+Î− + Ŝ−Î+ between the qubit and register spins. This protocol decouples the qubit from random magnetic noise originating from the nuclear bath, polarizes the register into a pure collective state, and enables coherent oscillations of a single spin excitation between the qubit and register. The invention further enables the generation of quantum swap gates and square root of swap gates between the qubit and register, facilitating the creation of maximally entangled Bell states and the implementation of quantum memory with extended coherence times. The system suppresses non-exchange interactions through spectral filtering and phase cancellation, cancels qubit decoherence by toggling the RF field in synchrony with the pulse sequence, and preserves spin exchange interactions by selecting the amplitude and frequency of the RF field to resonate with specific nuclear transitions while avoiding spectral overlap with bath spins. The protocol’s period is controlled to match the precession frequency of the register spins, and the phase and duration of the applied pulses are optimized to cancel detuning effects and pulse errors to leading order. The invention enables the formation of predictable, collective spin-exchange interactions that scale with the square root of the number of register spins, thereby accelerating gate operations. Single-qubit gates are implemented using microwave pulses on the qubit transition, while the register spins are configured in a polarized state via repeated application of the ZenPol sequence. The first swap gate transfers a quantum state from the qubit to the register, and the second swap gate retrieves it, with the entire process decoupling the qubit’s dipole noise from the register. The first square root of swap gate generates entanglement between the qubit and register, and the second square root of swap gate completes the Bell state preparation. The device couples the qubit to the register via a coplanar waveguide that delivers microwave and RF fields to the crystal, and the application of the protocol is controlled by a computer system that generates pulse sequences with nanosecond precision. Non-exchange interactions are suppressed by selecting the RF field frequency to match only the register transitions, while bath spins remain spectrally isolated. Noise is suppressed through dynamical decoupling sequences applied to the register spins during storage, and coherent spin exchange is enabled by the synchronized toggling of the RF field and microwave pulses, which cancels static and low-frequency noise. The period of the protocol is controlled by adjusting the free-evolution intervals between pulses to resonate with the nuclear Larmor frequency, and the phase and duration of pulses are calibrated to ensure cancellation of first-order Zeeman shifts. Spin exchange interactions are preserved by maintaining the symmetry of the pulse sequence under time-reversal, while non-exchange interactions are cancelled by designing the sequence such that their time-averaged contributions vanish. Qubit decoherence is cancelled by ensuring that the toggling-frame transformation of the qubit operator averages to zero over the sequence period. The RF field is toggled in a square-wave pattern synchronized with the pulse sequence, and its amplitude is selected to control the strength of the engineered spin-exchange interaction. This results in a predictable, tunable spin-exchange rate that enables deterministic quantum operations. Pulses are applied to perform single-qubit gates on the qubit, and the register spins are configured in a polarized state by repeatedly cycling the ZenPol sequence until saturation of polarization is achieved. The first swap gate is applied to transfer a superposition state from the qubit to the register, and the second swap gate is applied to retrieve it. The first square root of swap gate is applied to generate entanglement, and the second square root of swap gate is applied to complete the Bell state. The device is used as a quantum memory by storing a superposition state in the register for a variable time and retrieving it with high fidelity. The device is also used as a repeater in a quantum network by entangling distant nodes through sequential application of swap and square root of swap gates between local qubits and registers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention introduces a system and method for implementing a protocol that couples a qubit with zero magnetic dipole moment to a nuclear spin register through a synchronized sequence of microwave and radiofrequency pulses. The protocol comprises a series of π/2 and π pulses applied to the qubit, interleaved with free-evolution intervals, and synchronized with a square-wave radiofrequency magnetic field oscillating at a frequency resonant with the nuclear spin transitions of the register. This sequence, termed ZenPol, dynamically engineers an effective spin-exchange interaction between the qubit and the register while simultaneously suppressing all non-exchange couplings and noise sources. The motivation for using nuclear spin-wave-like states arises from the need to encode quantum information in collective modes that are robust against local inhomogeneities and decoherence. In the preferred embodiment, the system is implemented using a single 171Yb3+ ion embedded in a YVO4 crystal, where the 171Yb ion serves as the qubit and four neighboring 51V nuclear spins serve as the register. The 171Yb qubit operates at a transition frequency of 675 MHz and exhibits coherence times exceeding 16 milliseconds under dynamical decoupling. The local crystalline environment imposes a quadrupolar splitting on the 51V spins, resulting in three distinct nuclear transitions at frequencies ωa, ωb, and ωc. The four 51V ions nearest to the 171Yb ion, positioned symmetrically in the crystal lattice, form the register, while more distant 51V ions constitute the bath. The interaction Hamiltonian between the 171Yb qubit and the 51V register is derived from magnetic dipole-dipole coupling, modified by the crystal’s symmetry and the qubit’s zero dipole moment. The challenge of spectral indistinguishability among the register spins is overcome by using the ωc transition manifold, which enables the formation of collective spin-wave states. The thermal register ensemble is prepared into a pure state by repeatedly applying the ZenPol sequence, which transfers polarization from the qubit to the register via resonant spin exchange. A single excitation is transferred from the 171Yb qubit to the register, resulting in the creation of a four-body W-state. A quantum swap gate is realized between the 171Yb qubit and the 51V register by applying the ZenPol sequence for a duration corresponding to a full spin-exchange oscillation. The ZenPol sequence is synchronized with the precession of the 51V nuclear spins, and its time-averaged effective Hamiltonian is derived using a toggling-frame transformation. The protocol is insensitive to random noise from the bath because the square-wave RF field cancels the net effect of the Overhauser field over each sequence period. An example protocol for the ZenPol sequence is implemented by setting the free-evolution interval to τ/4 = 5π/4ωc, which resonantly enhances the RF-induced spin-exchange interaction while suppressing Overhauser-induced couplings. Spectroscopy of the 171Yb nuclear spin environment is performed using Hartmann-Hahn double resonance, revealing the distinct ωa, ωb, and ωc transitions. The entire nuclear spin register is polarized using the ZenPol sequence, and coherent oscillations of a single spin excitation are induced between the qubit and register. Collective enhancement of the spin-exchange rate is verified by measuring the oscillation frequency, which scales with the square root of the number of register spins. The spin-exchange rate is controlled by adjusting the amplitude of the RF field, and oscillations are suppressed when the 171Yb qubit is initialized in the |0g state. Two distinct 51V ensembles—the register and the bath—are verified through their differing resonance frequencies. The polarisation fidelity of the register spins is estimated to be approximately 84%, and limitations of the polarisation protocol are attributed to residual populations in higher quadrupole levels and finite pulse durations.

The first example implementation of the invention as a quantum memory involves transferring a superposition state from the 171Yb qubit to the 51V register using a swap gate, storing the state for a variable wait time, and retrieving it with a second swap gate. The coherence of the final state is measured, and the 171Yb dipole moment noise is decoupled from the register by applying periodic π pulses to the qubit during storage. Coherence time is extended by applying XY-8 dynamical decoupling sequences to the 51V register spins. The Bell state generation protocol uses a pair of square root of swap gates to entangle the qubit and register, with multi-spin register benchmarking confirming the fidelity of the generated state. The Bell state coherence is evaluated using parity oscillations, and XY-8 decoupling is applied to improve coherence. The Bell state preparation fidelity is estimated to be above 0.8, and limitations are attributed to swap gate infidelity and residual dephasing. Supplementary experimental methods include the fabrication of a YVO4 crystal with 140 ppb 171Yb doping, the creation of a nanophotonic cavity with a quality factor of 10,000, the use of a 3He cryostat operating at 460 mK, and the integration of optical and microwave control systems. The cavity enables single-photon detection with an end-to-end efficiency of approximately 1%. Optical signals are delivered via fiber and focused using an aspheric lens doublet mounted on piezo nanopositioners. Residual magnetic fields are cancelled along the crystal c-axis using superconducting coils. Optical transitions are used for state initialization and readout, with a Ti:Sapphire laser frequency-stabilized via Pound-Drever-Hall locking addressing the A transition, and a separate external cavity diode laser addressing the F transition. Light output is separated from input using a 99:1 fiber beamsplitter and gated using an acousto-optic modulator to prevent detector saturation. Photon detection is performed using a tungsten-silicide superconducting nanowire single photon detector. Microwave pulses for qubit control are synthesized by an arbitrary waveform generator, amplified, and delivered via a gold coplanar waveguide fabricated on the crystal surface. The 171Yb initialization protocol involves optical pumping followed by microwave π pulses to empty the |1g and |auxg states. Readout of the |1g state is performed by applying a series of 100 π pulses to the A transition, each followed by a 10 µs detection window. The pulse sequence for storing and retrieving a superposition state includes ZenPol polarization, a π/2 pulse on the qubit, a swap gate, a variable wait time, a second swap gate, and final readout. The effective Hamiltonian for the hybrid spin system is derived using perturbation theory and secular approximations, and the ZenPol sequence is analyzed using a toggling-frame transformation. The average Hamiltonian in the rotating frame is simplified to isolate the spin-exchange term, and the coefficient b(k,ω) is defined to quantify the interaction strength for each resonance. Direct drive gates for the 51V register are implemented by applying a sinusoidal RF field at the ωc frequency, which induces an oscillating magnetic field at the register spins amplified by a factor of approximately 6.7 due to the inverse-cube distance dependence of the dipole interaction. The driving Hamiltonian is derived, and Rabi oscillation dynamics are observed with a Rabi frequency of 7.65 kHz. Motional narrowing is employed by applying π pulses to the 171Yb every 6 µs, extending the 51V coherence time from 280 µs to 1040 µs. The 51V π pulse time is calibrated to 69 µs. Phase-continuous driving is achieved by applying a π phase shift to the RF field after each 171Yb π pulse. Population basis measurements are performed using a sequential tomography protocol that distinguishes the four basis states {|0g0v, |0gWv, |1g0v, |1gWv} by conditional optical readout and swap gates. The state preparation fidelities are measured and corrected for swap gate infidelity. The swap gate fidelity is independently measured by applying two consecutive swap gates and comparing the initial and final qubit populations. The corrected populations are calculated using a fidelity-based transformation matrix. The Bell state is prepared using a single square root of swap gate, and its coherence is measured via parity oscillations. Maximum likelihood analysis is applied to extract the Bell state fidelity and uncertainty. The hardware environment includes a computer system with input/output devices, a special-purpose processor for pulse generation, a compiler for translating quantum algorithms into pulse sequences, and an external communication device for networked operation. The device is implemented as a computer-readable medium storing a program product that controls the arbitrary waveform generator, microwave amplifiers, optical lasers, and detectors. The system may be deployed in a distributed or cloud-based architecture, with clients sending quantum memory requests to servers that execute the ZenPol protocol. The device configuration includes a qubit, a register, a signal generator, an arbitrary waveform generator, a computer for program control, and optionally, a photonic cavity. The protocol suppresses non-exchange interactions by selecting the RF field frequency to match only the register transitions, and controls the application of pulses by adjusting their phase, duration, and timing. The quantum memory and swap gates are implemented by applying the ZenPol sequence and its variants. The polarizing of register spins is achieved by repeated application of the ZenPol sequence until saturation. The protocol for spin excitation transfer is specified by the sequence of π/2, π, and free-evolution intervals, and the qubit and register states are defined in the |0g, |1g and |↓, |↑ bases. The W state is formed as a symmetric superposition of single-excitation states across the four register spins. The device operation is outlined by the sequence of initialization, polarization, state transfer, storage, retrieval, and readout. The repeater in a quantum network is implemented by entangling two distant nodes via sequential swap and square root of swap gates between their respective qubits and registers. The system components include the qubit, register, microwave waveguide, optical cavity, detectors, and control electronics. Circuit control is achieved through precise timing circuits synchronized to the RF field. Pulse sequences are specified by their duration, phase, and amplitude, and spin exchange interaction is defined by the engineered Hamiltonian Ŝ+Î− + Ŝ−Î+. The RF field interaction is characterized by its square-wave form and synchronization with the pulse sequence. A pulse sequence example is illustrated with eight free-evolution intervals. Decoherence noise reduction is achieved through dynamical decoupling and the ZenPol protocol’s inherent noise cancellation. The system for a quantum register is defined by the hybrid qubit-register structure, and the spin-carrying defect is the 171Yb3+ ion. The register spin composition is four 51V ions with spin-7/2. System state oscillations are observed as coherent spin-exchange dynamics between qubit and register. The protocol for coherent spin exchange is defined by the ZenPol sequence. Spin exchange interaction types include exchange and non-exchange terms, and the RF field frequency range is 100–300 kHz. The method for qubit operations includes initialization, manipulation, and readout. The quantum memory method involves storing a superposition state in the register and retrieving it after a variable delay. The Bell state measurement method uses parity oscillations and maximum likelihood analysis.

## Definitions

A π pulse is a microwave pulse applied to a qubit that rotates its state by 180 degrees around a specified axis in the Bloch sphere, thereby inverting its population between two energy levels. A spin-exchange interaction is a coherent quantum coupling between two spin systems that swaps their excitation states, described by a Hamiltonian of the form Ŝ+Î− + Ŝ−Î+, which conserves total spin and enables entanglement generation without requiring a magnetic dipole moment on either spin.

## CONCLUSION

The invention provides a novel and non-obvious method for coupling a zero-dipole qubit to a nuclear spin register using a synchronized pulse and RF field protocol, enabling high-fidelity quantum memory, entanglement generation, and scalable quantum operations in solid-state systems previously deemed incompatible with such control. The ZenPol protocol overcomes fundamental limitations of prior art by simultaneously achieving polarization, noise suppression, and coherent exchange in a single, robust framework. While the invention has been described with reference to a specific embodiment using 171Yb in YVO4, it is understood that the principles disclosed are broadly applicable to other rare-earth ions, nuclear spin systems, and crystalline hosts with similar symmetry and spectral properties. The claims appended hereto define the full scope of the invention, and no limitation implied by the examples should be construed as limiting the invention to the specific configurations described.
