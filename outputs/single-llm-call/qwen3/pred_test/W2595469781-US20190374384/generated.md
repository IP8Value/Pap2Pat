# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Glaucoma is a chronic, progressive optic neuropathy characterized by irreversible vision loss due to elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) and subsequent damage to the retinal ganglion cells and their axons. It is the leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide, affecting over 80 million individuals, with projections indicating that this number will rise to more than 110 million by 2040. The pathophysiology of glaucoma involves impaired outflow of aqueous humor from the anterior chamber of the eye, resulting in sustained elevation of IOP, which in turn compromises the structural integrity of the optic nerve head. While medical therapy with topical hypotensive agents and laser-based interventions remain first-line treatments, a significant subset of patients fails to achieve adequate IOP control through these non-invasive means. In such cases, surgical intervention becomes necessary to establish an alternative drainage pathway for aqueous humor and thereby reduce intraocular pressure. Among the most commonly employed surgical approaches are trabeculectomy, glaucoma drainage implants, and minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries. Each of these techniques, despite their clinical utility, is fundamentally limited by the body’s natural wound-healing response, which leads to fibrotic scarring at the site of surgical intervention. Trabeculectomy, although historically the gold standard for refractory glaucoma, suffers from high rates of failure due to fibroblast proliferation and extracellular matrix deposition at the scleral flap and filtering bleb, often resulting in complete closure of the drainage channel within months of surgery. Glaucoma drainage implants, such as the Ahmed valve or Baerveldt implant, were developed to circumvent the limitations of trabeculectomy, particularly in pediatric, neovascular, and post-traumatic glaucoma. However, these devices are encased in a dense, avascular fibrotic capsule that obstructs fluid efflux from the implant’s plate, diminishing long-term efficacy and necessitating revisional surgery in a substantial proportion of cases. Minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries, while offering improved safety profiles and reduced complication rates, are generally restricted to mild to moderate disease and are not designed for sustained IOP reduction in advanced or refractory cases. These devices, often consisting of small stents or shunts, are prone to obstruction by cellular infiltration and collagen deposition along their luminal surfaces. Moreover, the rigidity and non-conformable nature of existing implants create mechanical mismatch with the dynamic, compliant tissues of the ocular surface, triggering persistent foreign body reactions that perpetuate inflammation and fibrosis. The persistent failure of current devices to integrate seamlessly with ocular tissues underscores a critical unmet need: the development of a surgically implantable device capable of maintaining long-term aqueous outflow by actively resisting fibrotic encapsulation through innovative material and geometric design. Current strategies relying on pharmacologic adjuncts—such as mitomycin-C or 5-fluorouracil—carry significant risks of sight-threatening complications including hypotony, bleb leaks, and endophthalmitis, and do not address the root cause of failure, which lies in the physical and structural incompatibility between the implant and host tissue. A paradigm shift is required, moving beyond passive drug delivery or material substitution toward a fundamentally redesigned implant architecture that mimics the biomechanical and topological properties of native ocular structures to elicit minimal tissue reactivity.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses a novel glaucoma drainage device comprising a flexible, porous, three-dimensional microfluidic meshwork designed to facilitate sustained aqueous humor outflow while minimizing fibrotic encapsulation. The device comprises a network of interconnected longitudinal and lateral microchannels arranged in a lattice structure, wherein each microchannel has a diameter ranging from 5 to 30 micrometers and a wall thickness not exceeding 5 micrometers, enabling fluid transport at physiologically relevant rates under normal intraocular pressure gradients. The microchannels are configured to be porous and permeable across their entire surface, allowing for bidirectional fluid exchange between the anterior chamber and the subconjunctival space while simultaneously permitting the infiltration of vascular and cellular elements that promote tissue integration rather than rejection. The meshwork is constructed from a biocompatible, elastomeric polymer exhibiting a modulus of elasticity comparable to that of the episcleral tissue, ensuring mechanical compliance and conformability to the curvature and motion of the ocular surface. In a preferred embodiment, the meshwork is formed as a three-dimensional hybrid structure comprising an inner region of densely packed microchannels for primary fluid conduction and an outer region of sparsely distributed, larger-diameter microchannels that serve as secondary drainage conduits and tissue integration zones. The device further includes an interface region that connects seamlessly to a conventional drainage tube, wherein the transition between the tube and the meshwork is engineered to eliminate abrupt geometric discontinuities that could induce flow turbulence or localized shear stress. An integrated pressure-sensitive valve is incorporated into the interface region, which opens at a threshold pressure of 12 to 18 mmHg to prevent hypotony while maintaining adequate outflow during periods of elevated IOP. The microchannels are fabricated to possess a period of 50 to 150 micrometers, with pore density optimized to permit the passage of aqueous humor while restricting the migration of fibroblasts and inflammatory cells. The meshwork may be constructed in two-dimensional planar configurations for ease of implantation or in three-dimensional folded geometries to increase surface area and drainage capacity without increasing footprint. The device is designed for implantation via a minimally invasive surgical approach, wherein it is placed beneath the conjunctiva and sutured or adhered to the episcleral surface without direct communication with the anterior chamber. The invention further encompasses a method of managing intraocular pressure by implanting the microfluidic meshwork in a subconjunctival location, wherein the device’s ultra-flexible architecture and cellular-scale porosity suppress fibrotic capsule formation by minimizing mechanical mismatch and preserving native tissue architecture, thereby enabling long-term, stable aqueous outflow without the need for adjunctive antifibrotic agents.
