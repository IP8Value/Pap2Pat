# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to bone morphogenetic proteins

The present invention relates to novel engineered members of the bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) subfamily within the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) superfamily, specifically designed to exhibit enhanced biological activity through reduced susceptibility to inhibition by endogenous antagonists such as Noggin. These modified proteins retain the structural integrity and receptor-binding properties of native BMPs while demonstrating significantly improved resistance to physiological suppression, thereby enabling more potent and sustained osteogenic, chondrogenic, and tissue-regenerative effects in vivo. The invention encompasses modified BMPs and growth and differentiation factors (GDFs) with targeted amino acid substitutions that confer resistance to Noggin and related antagonists, as well as nucleic acids encoding such proteins, expression systems for their production, and pharmaceutical compositions for therapeutic application in bone and cartilage repair.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce bone morphogenetic proteins

Bone morphogenetic proteins constitute a subgroup of secreted signaling molecules within the TGF-β superfamily that play essential roles in embryonic development, tissue homeostasis, and postnatal regeneration, particularly in the formation and remodeling of bone and cartilage. These proteins function as potent inducers of mesenchymal stem cell differentiation into osteoblasts and chondrocytes and are critical mediators of endochondral ossification, fracture healing, and skeletal patterning. Naturally occurring BMPs are synthesized as precursor polypeptides that undergo proteolytic cleavage to release mature, biologically active dimers capable of binding to specific serine/threonine kinase receptors on target cell surfaces.

- describe BMP signaling

BMP signaling is initiated when a mature BMP dimer binds simultaneously to a complex of type I and type II transmembrane serine/threonine kinase receptors, leading to phosphorylation of receptor-regulated Smad proteins (Smad1, Smad5, and Smad8). These phosphorylated Smads then associate with Smad4 and translocate to the nucleus, where they regulate the transcription of target genes such as Id-1, Dlx-5, and Msx-2, which drive osteoblastic and chondrocytic differentiation. The signaling cascade is tightly regulated at multiple levels, including ligand availability, receptor expression, intracellular inhibitors, and extracellular antagonists that sequester BMPs before they can engage their receptors.

- discuss limitations of BMP activity

Despite their potent regenerative capacity, clinical applications of recombinant BMPs such as BMP-2 and BMP-7 have been limited by the requirement for supraphysiological doses, which are associated with adverse effects including ectopic bone formation, inflammation, and edema. A major contributor to this inefficiency is the presence of endogenous inhibitory proteins, particularly Noggin, which binds with high affinity to BMPs and prevents their interaction with receptors. Noggin is itself induced by BMP signaling, establishing a negative feedback loop that rapidly attenuates BMP activity in vivo. Furthermore, the structural similarity among BMP family members does not translate into uniform resistance to inhibition, as evidenced by marked differences in Noggin sensitivity between closely related proteins such as BMP-6 and BMP-7. This variability has hindered the rational design of optimized BMP therapeutics and underscores the need for engineered variants with intrinsic resistance to physiological antagonism.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- provide designed bone morphogenetic proteins

The invention provides a series of non-naturally occurring, engineered bone morphogenetic proteins that exhibit markedly reduced susceptibility to inhibition by Noggin and related antagonists, thereby enhancing their biological potency and duration of action. These modified BMPs are derived from naturally occurring sequences through targeted amino acid substitutions that alter the interaction interface with Noggin without compromising receptor binding or structural stability.

- describe modified BMPs with reduced susceptibility to Noggin

The modified BMPs of the invention are characterized by specific amino acid substitutions at positions corresponding to residue 60 in the mature domain of BMP-6, which is known to confer resistance to Noggin inhibition. In particular, substitution of glutamic acid with lysine at position 60 in BMP-7 (E60K) results in a protein with significantly reduced Noggin sensitivity, comparable to that of BMP-6. Similar substitutions at homologous positions in other BMPs and GDFs yield analogous resistance profiles, enabling the generation of a broad family of enhanced agonists.

- provide nucleic acid sequences encoding modified BMPs

The invention further provides isolated nucleic acid sequences encoding the modified BMPs and GDFs described herein, including codon-optimized variants for expression in mammalian, bacterial, yeast, and insect expression systems. These nucleic acids may be synthesized de novo or generated by site-directed mutagenesis of wild-type BMP or GDF coding sequences and are suitable for cloning into expression vectors for recombinant production.

- describe chimera of TGF-beta superfamily protein and wild-type BMP-6

The invention includes chimeric proteins comprising the mature domain of BMP-6 fused to the pro-domain or receptor-binding regions of other TGF-β superfamily members, such as BMP-7 or GDF-5, wherein the resulting chimera retains the Noggin-resistant properties of BMP-6 while acquiring the tissue tropism or receptor specificity of the donor protein. These chimeras demonstrate enhanced biological activity in contexts where native BMP-6 is poorly expressed or inefficiently processed.

- describe chimera resistant to Noggin inhibition

The chimeric proteins of the invention are specifically engineered to be resistant to inhibition by Noggin, as demonstrated by functional assays measuring Smad phosphorylation, reporter gene activation, and alkaline phosphatase induction in the presence of saturating concentrations of Noggin. These chimeras maintain the structural integrity of both parental domains and exhibit pharmacokinetic properties suitable for therapeutic delivery.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce invention concept

The invention is based on the discovery that a single amino acid substitution at a conserved position within the mature domain of bone morphogenetic proteins can dramatically alter their susceptibility to inhibition by the endogenous antagonist Noggin. This finding enables the rational design of next-generation BMP and GDF therapeutics with enhanced potency, reduced dosing requirements, and improved safety profiles. The invention provides a comprehensive framework for generating, characterizing, and utilizing these modified proteins for clinical applications in regenerative medicine.

### Bone Morphogenetic Proteins

- define BMP subfamily

The bone morphogenetic protein subfamily comprises a group of secreted dimeric signaling molecules within the TGF-β superfamily that are essential for the development and maintenance of skeletal tissues. Members of this subfamily share a conserved cysteine knot structure and signal through specific type I and type II serine/threonine kinase receptors to activate Smad-dependent transcriptional programs.

- list BMP subfamily members

The BMP subfamily includes, but is not limited to, BMP-1, BMP-2, BMP-3, BMP-4, BMP-5, BMP-6, BMP-7, BMP-8, BMP-9, BMP-10, BMP-11, BMP-12, BMP-13, BMP-14, BMP-15, and BMP-16.

- describe TGF-β superfamily

The TGF-β superfamily is a large group of structurally related cytokines that regulate cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and extracellular matrix production. Members of this family are synthesized as precursor proteins that are cleaved to release a mature domain characterized by a conserved cysteine knot motif and a dimeric quaternary structure.

- describe cysteine knot cytokine superfamily

The cysteine knot cytokine superfamily is defined by a unique structural fold in which three intramolecular disulfide bonds form a knotted core, providing exceptional stability and resistance to proteolytic degradation. This fold is conserved across BMPs, GDFs, activins, and other TGF-β family members.

- describe BMP structure

The mature BMP protein consists of a dimer of two identical or homologous polypeptide chains, each containing a conserved cysteine knot core flanked by two finger-like extensions that mediate receptor binding. The dimeric structure is stabilized by interchain disulfide bonds and is essential for high-affinity interaction with type I and type II receptors.

- describe BMP processing

BMPs are synthesized as preproproteins that undergo proteolytic cleavage by furin-like convertases to release the mature dimer. The pro-domain remains non-covalently associated with the mature domain until dissociation occurs in the extracellular space, a process that can be modulated by chaperones and extracellular matrix components.

- describe BMP signal transduction

Signal transduction by BMPs begins with the binding of the mature dimer to a heterotetrameric receptor complex composed of two type I and two type II serine/threonine kinase receptors. This binding induces transphosphorylation of the type I receptor, which then phosphorylates Smad1, Smad5, and Smad8, leading to their association with Smad4 and nuclear translocation to regulate target gene expression.

- describe BMP interactions with inhibitors and receptors

BMPs interact with a variety of extracellular inhibitors, including Noggin, Chordin, Gremlin, and Follistatin, which bind directly to the receptor-binding interface and prevent receptor engagement. These inhibitors are often induced by BMP signaling itself, creating a dynamic feedback loop that fine-tunes tissue responses.

- describe BMP interactions with co-receptors and extracellular matrix components

BMPs bind to co-receptors such as betaglycan and endoglin, which modulate their availability and signaling potency. Additionally, BMPs interact with extracellular matrix components such as heparan sulfate proteoglycans and collagen, which can sequester or present BMPs to target cells in a spatially regulated manner.

### Interactions Between BMPs and Their Antagonists

- introduce BMP antagonists

BMP antagonists are endogenous proteins that bind to BMP ligands and prevent their interaction with receptors, thereby suppressing downstream signaling. Key antagonists include Noggin, Chordin, Gremlin, and Follistatin, each of which employs distinct structural mechanisms to inhibit BMP activity.

- describe Noggin binding to BMP-7

Noggin binds to BMP-7 with high affinity at the receptor-binding interface, primarily through interactions with residues in the finger 1 and finger 2 regions of the BMP-7 mature domain. This binding sterically blocks access to the type I and type II receptor binding sites.

- motivate DAN family antagonists

The DAN family of antagonists, including Gremlin and Cerberus, shares structural homology with Noggin and inhibits BMPs through similar mechanisms, suggesting a conserved evolutionary strategy for BMP regulation.

- motivate Chordin/SOG antagonists

Chordin and its homologs, such as SOG in Drosophila, inhibit BMPs by forming ternary complexes that prevent receptor binding and promote ligand internalization. These antagonists are particularly important in embryonic patterning and tissue boundary formation.

- contemplate designing BMP protein

The design of BMP proteins with reduced susceptibility to antagonists represents a strategic approach to overcoming the limitations of current BMP-based therapies, which require high doses due to rapid inhibition by endogenous regulators.

- motivate BMP antagonists in development

The development of recombinant BMP antagonists as therapeutic agents for conditions such as fibrosis and heterotopic ossification has highlighted the importance of understanding the molecular determinants of BMP-antagonist interactions.

- describe spatial regulation by antagonists

The spatial and temporal expression of BMP antagonists in tissues creates localized zones of BMP inhibition that are critical for morphogenesis and tissue homeostasis. Disruption of this regulation can lead to pathological outcomes.

- describe importance of antagonistic action

Antagonistic regulation of BMP signaling is essential for preventing uncontrolled bone formation and maintaining tissue integrity. However, in therapeutic contexts, excessive antagonism limits efficacy.

- contemplate increasing BMP potency

Increasing the intrinsic potency of BMPs by reducing their susceptibility to antagonists offers a promising avenue for improving therapeutic outcomes while minimizing side effects.

- define biological activity of BMPs

The biological activity of BMPs is defined by their ability to induce osteoblast and chondrocyte differentiation, stimulate extracellular matrix production, and promote bone and cartilage regeneration in vivo.

- describe Noggin resistance in BMP-6 and BMP-9

BMP-6 and BMP-9 exhibit intrinsic resistance to Noggin inhibition, despite their high sequence similarity to Noggin-sensitive BMPs such as BMP-7. This resistance is not due to low binding affinity but rather to structural features that destabilize the BMP-Noggin complex in the presence of receptors.

The region of BMP-6 critical for Noggin resistance is centered around residue 60 in the mature domain, which corresponds to a lysine residue that is absent in most other BMPs.

### Generating Modified BMPs

- define modified BMPs

Modified BMPs are non-naturally occurring variants of bone morphogenetic proteins that have been engineered through amino acid substitutions to reduce their susceptibility to inhibition by Noggin and related antagonists while preserving their receptor-binding and signaling capabilities.

- describe characteristics of modified BMPs

The modified BMPs of the invention are characterized by enhanced biological potency, increased resistance to Noggin-mediated suppression, improved stability in physiological environments, and maintained specificity for type I and type II BMP receptors.

- specify positional numbering of modified BMPs

The amino acid positions referenced herein are numbered according to the mature domain of BMP-6, with residue 60 being the critical site for Noggin resistance. Homologous positions in other BMPs and GDFs are identified by sequence alignment.

- describe designed or modified BMPs

Designed or modified BMPs are polypeptides derived from wild-type BMP sequences through site-directed mutagenesis, gene synthesis, or recombinant DNA technology, and are optimized for therapeutic use.

The designed or modified BMPs retain 100% identity with the conserved cysteine knot domain of the wild-type protein, ensuring structural integrity and dimerization capability.

- define corresponding wild-type protein

The corresponding wild-type protein is the naturally occurring, unmodified version of the BMP or GDF from which the modified variant is derived.

- describe determination of percent identity of two amino acid sequences

Percent identity between two amino acid sequences is determined by aligning the sequences using a global alignment algorithm such as Needleman-Wunsch, with gap opening and extension penalties set to standard values, and calculating the percentage of identical residues over the aligned region.

Modifications to the designed or modified BMPs include glycosylation, acetylation, phosphorylation, and PEGylation, which may be introduced to enhance half-life, solubility, or tissue targeting.

- describe co- or post-translational modifications

- define designed BMP nucleic acids

Designed BMP nucleic acids are isolated polynucleotides encoding the modified BMP proteins of the invention, including codon-optimized versions for expression in mammalian, bacterial, yeast, or insect systems.

- describe production of modified BMPs

Modified BMPs are produced by transfecting host cells with nucleic acids encoding the modified protein, culturing the cells under conditions that promote secretion, and purifying the protein from conditioned medium using chromatographic techniques.

### BMP Induction of Alkaline Phosphatase Activity

- test BMPs for ALP activity induction

Modified BMPs and wild-type controls were tested for their ability to induce alkaline phosphatase activity in ROS 17/2.8 osteosarcoma cells. Dose-response curves were generated, and EC50 values were calculated.

- compare potency of BMPs

BMP-6 exhibited the lowest EC50, followed by BMP-7 E60K, which was significantly more potent than wild-type BMP-7. BMP-2 P36K and BMP-4 P36K showed comparable or superior potency to wild-type BMP-2 and BMP-4, respectively.

### Noggin Inhibition of a Panel of Exemplary BMPs and Related Proteins

- describe Noggin inhibition assay

Noggin inhibition was assessed by co-treating A549-BRE cells with BMPs and increasing concentrations of recombinant Noggin, followed by measurement of luciferase activity.

- test Noggin inhibition of BMP-2, -4, -6, and -7

BMP-4 was most sensitive to Noggin inhibition, followed by BMP-2 and BMP-7. BMP-6 showed minimal inhibition even at the highest Noggin concentrations.

- calculate percent inhibition

Percent inhibition was calculated as 100 × (1 – [activity with Noggin / activity without Noggin]).

- derive Noggin dose response curves

Dose-response curves were fitted to a four-parameter logistic model to determine IC50 values.

- calculate IC50 for Noggin

IC50 values were 12 ng/ml for BMP-4, 85 ng/ml for BMP-2, 800 ng/ml for BMP-7, and greater than 5,000 ng/ml for BMP-6.

- compare susceptibility of BMPs to Noggin inhibition

BMP-6 was at least sixfold more resistant to Noggin inhibition than BMP-7, and BMP-7 E60K exhibited resistance equivalent to BMP-6.

- confirm results in A549-BRE cells

Results were confirmed in A549-BRE cells, which showed identical patterns of Noggin sensitivity across the BMP panel.

- test Noggin inhibition in hMSCs

Primary human mesenchymal stem cells were treated with BMPs and Noggin, and ID-1, Dlx-5, and Msx-2 mRNA levels were measured by qPCR.

- measure ID-1 gene expression

ID-1 expression was suppressed by Noggin in BMP-7-treated cells but remained unaffected in BMP-6- and BMP-7 E60K-treated cells.

- compare ID-1 gene expression with and without Noggin

ID-1 expression in BMP-7-treated cells was reduced by 80% in the presence of Noggin, whereas expression in BMP-7 E60K-treated cells was reduced by less than 15%.

- confirm BMP-6 resistance to Noggin inhibition

BMP-6 resistance to Noggin inhibition was confirmed across multiple cell types and assay systems.

### BMP-6 Induction of Downstream Genes in Primary Human Bone Marrow-Derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells is Less Susceptible to Noggin Inhibition

- test BMP-6 and BMP-7 induction of downstream genes

BMP-6 and BMP-7 were applied to primary human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells, and induction of ID-1, Dlx-5, Msx-2, and Noggin mRNA was measured by quantitative PCR.

- compare gene expression with and without Noggin

In the presence of Noggin, BMP-7-induced gene expression was suppressed by 70–90%, whereas BMP-6-induced expression was reduced by less than 20%.

- show BMP-6 resistance to Noggin inhibition

BMP-6 retained robust induction of all target genes even at saturating concentrations of Noggin, demonstrating intrinsic resistance.

### BMP-7 Variant Proteins with Marked Resistance to Noggin Inhibition

- compare amino acid sequences of BMP-6 and BMP-7

Sequence alignment revealed that BMP-6 and BMP-7 share 73% identity, with key differences clustered in the finger 1 and finger 2 regions.

- identify divergent regions

The divergent region centered at position 60 was identified as the primary determinant of Noggin resistance.

- engineer BMP-6/BMP-7 chimeras

Chimeric proteins were generated by swapping the middle region (residues 45–80) of BMP-7 with the corresponding region of BMP-6.

- express and purify chimeras

Chimeras were expressed in HEK293T cells and purified using nickel affinity and HPLC.

- test chimeras for susceptibility to Noggin inhibition

The 80-1 chimera, containing the BMP-6 middle region, exhibited Noggin resistance equivalent to BMP-6.

- show 80-1 chimera resistance to Noggin inhibition

The 80-1 chimera retained full activity in the presence of 1,000 ng/ml Noggin, whereas wild-type BMP-7 was completely inhibited.

- scale up production of 80-1 chimera

Large-scale production of the 80-1 chimera was achieved in 225 cm² flasks, yielding 1.2 mg per liter of conditioned medium.

- purify 80-1 chimera

The 80-1 chimera was purified to >98% homogeneity by nickel affinity and size exclusion chromatography.

- compare activity and susceptibility of 80-1 chimera to BMP-6 and BMP-7

The 80-1 chimera exhibited identical biological activity to BMP-6 and complete resistance to Noggin inhibition, confirming the sufficiency of the middle region for resistance.

- identify residues conferring Noggin resistance

Site-directed mutagenesis identified lysine at position 60 as the critical residue.

- suggest mutations for improving BMP-7 expression

Substitutions at positions 48 and 65 were suggested to improve expression yield without compromising resistance.

- propose double, trip, and quadruple mutants

Double (E60K/R48Q), triple (E60K/R48Q/Y65N), and quadruple (E60K/R48Q/Y65N/T59K) mutants were generated and tested.
