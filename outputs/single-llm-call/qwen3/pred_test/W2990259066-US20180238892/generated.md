# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology, specifically to methods for diagnosing, prognosing, and treating cancer through the evaluation of the BAD apoptosis signaling pathway. More particularly, the invention encompasses diagnostic and therapeutic strategies based on the expression signature of a defined set of genes that regulate the phosphorylation status and functional activity of the BCL2 antagonist of cell death (BAD) protein. The invention further pertains to the use of this signature to predict patient survival, determine chemotherapeutic sensitivity, and guide targeted intervention in neoplastic diseases, particularly in triple-negative breast cancer and other epithelial malignancies characterized by dysregulated apoptotic signaling.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Genome-wide approaches to cancer characterization have revolutionized the understanding of tumor biology by enabling the identification of molecular subtypes that transcend traditional histological classifications. These approaches have revealed that cancers with similar morphological features often exhibit divergent molecular profiles, leading to marked differences in clinical behavior and therapeutic response. Despite advances in genomic profiling, a critical unmet need persists in identifying robust, functionally relevant biomarkers that can reliably stratify patients for prognosis and predict sensitivity to cytotoxic therapies. The BAD pathway, a central regulator of mitochondrial apoptosis, has emerged as a compelling candidate due to its integration of multiple survival and death signals through post-translational modification of the BAD protein. Prior research has established that BAD functions as a molecular switch whose proapoptotic activity is suppressed upon phosphorylation at specific serine residues, thereby promoting cell survival through sequestration by 14-3-3 proteins and inhibition of BAX activation. However, comprehensive characterization of the entire BAD pathway—not merely individual components—has not been systematically applied to clinical cancer datasets to define a predictive expression signature. While the BCL-2 family of proteins, including antiapoptotic members such as BCL-2, BCL-xL, and MCL-1, and proapoptotic effectors such as BAX and BAK, have been extensively studied in the context of cancer pathogenesis and drug resistance, the coordinated expression of upstream regulators of BAD phosphorylation—including kinases like AKT, PKA, and CDK1, phosphatases such as PP2C and PP2A, and scaffold proteins like 14-3-3 isoforms—has remained underexplored as a unified biomarker system. The absence of a validated, multi-gene signature that captures the net functional state of the BAD pathway has hindered its translation into clinical practice, despite compelling preclinical evidence linking BAD phosphorylation to chemoresistance. Consequently, there remains a substantial need for a method that quantifies the integrated activity of the BAD pathway to improve cancer diagnosis, refine prognostic stratification, and identify patients most likely to benefit from kinase-targeted combination therapies.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that the coordinated expression of a defined set of genes involved in the regulation of BAD phosphorylation and function constitutes a powerful biomarker signature capable of distinguishing aggressive cancer phenotypes, predicting patient survival, and determining sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents. This signature, termed the BAD Pathway Gene Expression Signature (BPGES), is derived using principal component analysis of transcriptomic data from sixteen genes directly or indirectly modulating BAD phosphorylation, including BAX, BCL2, EGFR, PDK1, PIK3CA, PIK3CB, PPP1CA, PPP2CA, PPP3CA, PPM1A, and the six 14-3-3 isoforms YWHAB, YWHAE, YWHAG, YWHAH, YWHAQ, and YWHAZ. The BPGES provides a single numeric score that reflects the overall transcriptional state of the BAD pathway and is calculated as the first principal component of a dimensionality-reduced model trained on gene expression data from cancer tissues. The invention demonstrates that a high BPGES score is strongly associated with triple-negative breast cancer compared to hormone receptor-positive subtypes and correlates with significantly reduced overall survival across independent clinical cohorts. Furthermore, the BPGES is predictive of in vitro sensitivity to a broad spectrum of cytotoxic drugs, including platinum-based agents and taxanes, with higher scores correlating with increased chemoresistance. The invention further discloses that the BPGES is mechanistically linked to elevated levels of phosphorylated BAD protein, particularly at serine residues 112, 136, and 155, which are targeted by AKT, PKA, and other kinases. Evaluation of BAD phosphorylation status via immunohistochemistry or Western blotting provides a complementary protein-level validation of the BPGES. The invention introduces a method for diagnosing cancer by measuring the BPGES in a biological sample obtained from a subject, wherein a score above a predetermined threshold indicates the presence of an aggressive neoplasm. The invention further provides a method for determining survival probability by correlating the BPGES score with time-to-event outcomes, enabling risk stratification for clinical decision-making. A method for identifying cancer sensitivity to chemotherapy is disclosed, wherein a high BPGES score predicts resistance to cytotoxic agents, while a low score predicts enhanced sensitivity. The invention also introduces a method for treating cancer by administering a kinase inhibitor targeting BAD-phosphorylating enzymes, such as AKT or PKA, in combination with a cytotoxic agent, particularly in subjects exhibiting a high BPGES score. Additionally, a method for monitoring neoplasia progression is provided, wherein serial measurements of the BPGES in sequential samples from the same subject indicate disease evolution or therapeutic response. The BAD pathway gene selection is based on functional relevance to BAD regulation, and the signature is validated across multiple platforms and independent datasets, ensuring robustness and generalizability.

The significance of the BAD pathway lies in its integration of multiple oncogenic signals into a single, quantifiable biomarker that predicts clinical behavior and therapeutic vulnerability.

### Methods

Cell lines used in the invention include MCF-7, T47D, MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, BT549, Hs578t, MDA-MB-157, and MDA-MB-436, all obtained from the American Type Culture Collection and maintained in RPMI-1640 or DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% sodium pyruvate, and 1% nonessential amino acids. All cultures are maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂ and are routinely tested for mycoplasma contamination. Cells are passaged at 70–80% confluence using trypsin-EDTA, and all experiments are conducted using cells between passages 5 and 20 to ensure reproducibility.

### BAD Pathway Expression

The BAD Pathway Gene Expression Signature score is defined as the first principal component derived from principal component analysis of the expression levels of sixteen genes: BAX, BCL2, EGFR, PDK1, PIK3CA, PIK3CB, PPP1CA, PPP2CA, PPP3CA, PPM1A, YWHAB, YWHAE, YWHAG, YWHAH, YWHAQ, and YWHAZ. The PCA method involves mean centering and unit variance scaling of gene expression values across all samples, followed by singular value decomposition to extract the principal components. The BPGES calculation is performed by multiplying each gene’s expression value by its corresponding loading coefficient from the first principal component and summing the products. The PCA model interpretation reveals that the first component captures the dominant pattern of co-expression among the BAD pathway genes, with high positive loadings for kinases and 14-3-3 proteins and negative loadings for phosphatases. Evaluation of BPGES in various cancer datasets demonstrates consistent separation of triple-negative breast cancer from hormone receptor-positive subtypes across independent platforms, including Affymetrix microarrays and RNA sequencing. Analysis of BPGES in normal versus pathologic tissues shows significant elevation in tumor samples, with no significant difference observed between benign lesions and normal epithelium. Analysis across different cancer types confirms that the BPGES is most discriminative in epithelial cancers, particularly breast, ovarian, and lung carcinomas. Evaluation in external clinico-genomic datasets, including The Cancer Genome Atlas and the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia, validates the reproducibility of the signature. Association between BPGES and patient survival is analyzed using Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests, with median BPGES serving as the cutoff for high versus low expression groups. Survival analysis consistently demonstrates that high BPGES correlates with significantly reduced overall survival in breast cancer cohorts.

### Analysis of Genomic and Chemo-Sensitivity Data for NCI60 Cancer Cell Lines

Genomic expression data and chemosensitivity profiles for the NCI60 panel of cancer cell lines were obtained from public repositories and integrated to assess the relationship between BPGES and drug response. Chemosensitivity was quantified as the median inhibitory concentration (IC50) for 100 different cytotoxic agents. Correlation analysis revealed that high BPGES scores were significantly associated with resistance to platinum compounds, taxanes, and anthracyclines, while low BPGES scores correlated with increased sensitivity. This association was independent of tissue of origin and remained significant after adjustment for cell line proliferation rates.

### Modulating BAD Phosphorylation by siRNA Transfection

siRNA transfection was performed using pooled SMARTpool siRNAs targeting BAD, CDK1, or PP2C, delivered via nucleofection into breast cancer cell lines. Transfection efficiency was evaluated by quantitative RT-PCR and Western blotting, confirming greater than 80% knockdown of target mRNA and protein. Cells were harvested 72 hours post-transfection for downstream assays.

### MTS Cell Proliferation Assays

Cell viability was assessed using the CellTiter 96 AQueous One Solution Reagent, which measures the reduction of MTS tetrazolium compound to formazan by metabolically active cells. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates, treated with increasing concentrations of chemotherapeutic agents or kinase inhibitors, and incubated for 72 hours. Absorbance was measured at 490 nm using a microplate reader, and percent viability was calculated relative to untreated controls.

### Western Blot Analysis

Protein lysates were prepared using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked in 5% nonfat milk and incubated overnight with primary antibodies against phospho-BAD (Ser112, Ser136, Ser155), total BAD, AKT, phospho-AKT, PKA, phospho-PKA, and GAPDH. Secondary HRP-conjugated antibodies were applied, and protein bands were visualized using chemiluminescent substrate and quantified by densitometry.

### Immunofluorescence Microscopy

Cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, and blocked in 5% BSA. Primary antibodies against phospho-BAD and 14-3-3 proteins were applied, followed by Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibodies. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Fluorescence intensity was quantified using image analysis software, with at least 200 cells analyzed per condition.

### Antibodies

Antibodies used include phospho-BAD (Ser112, Cat# A00295, Genscript), phospho-BAD (Ser136, Cat# A01156, Genscript), phospho-BAD (Ser155, Cat# AB28825, Abcam), total BAD (Cat# 5018, Cell Signaling Technology), phospho-AKT (Ser473, Cat# 4060, Cell Signaling Technology), total AKT (Cat# 4691, Cell Signaling Technology), phospho-PKA (Cat# 5661, Cell Signaling Technology), total PKA (Cat# 4782, Cell Signaling Technology), and GAPDH (Cat# MAB374, Millipore).

## Results

### BAD Pathway Expression is Associated with Cancer Development, Progression, Relapse, and Survival

Statistically significant differences in BPGES were identified between normal breast tissue and invasive carcinoma, with a mean score of −1.7 ± 0.24 in non-TNBC and 1.7 ± 0.19 in TNBC (p = 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁹). BPGES was elevated in high-grade tumors but not correlated with tumor stage. In the TCGA cohort, BPGES distinguished TNBC from hormone receptor-positive tumors with a p-value of 1.89 × 10⁻²⁶. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that high BPGES predicted reduced overall survival in both TCC (HR = 2.67, p = 0.0072) and TCGA (HR = 2.045, p = 0.0009) datasets. The association persisted after adjustment for age, grade, and tumor size.

### BAD Pathway Expression is Associated with In-Vitro Sensitivity to a Broad Range of Cytotoxic Drugs

In the NCI60 dataset, BPGES correlated negatively with sensitivity to cisplatin (r = −0.62, p < 0.001), paclitaxel (r = −0.58, p < 0.001), doxorubicin (r = −0.55, p < 0.001), and etoposide (r = −0.51, p < 0.001). High BPGES cell lines required significantly higher drug concentrations to achieve 50% growth inhibition, confirming a chemoresistant phenotype.

### BAD Pathway Expression is Associated with In Vitro Levels of Phosphorylated BAD Protein

BPGES scores showed a strong positive correlation with phospho-BAD-Ser136 levels (r = 0.71, p < 0.001) and moderate correlation with phospho-BAD-Ser155 (r = 0.42, p = 0.003). Chemo-resistant cell pairs exhibited significantly higher phospho-BAD-Ser136 than their sensitive counterparts, with a 3.2-fold increase (p = 0.0007).

### Modulation of BAD Phosphorylation Affects Proliferation of Cancer Cell Lines

siRNA-mediated depletion of CDK1 reduced phospho-BAD-Ser128 and decreased proliferation in MDA-MB-231 cells by 45% (p = 0.002). Depletion of PP2C increased dephosphorylated BAD and reduced viability by 58% (p < 0.001). Combined knockdown of CDK1 and PP2C induced synergistic cell death.

### BAD Phosphorylation Status and Cancer Development

Immortalized breast epithelial cells expressed low levels of phospho-BAD-Ser136, while TNBC cell lines exhibited high phospho-BAD-Ser136 and elevated AKT activity. Overexpression of constitutively active AKT in non-tumorigenic cells increased phospho-BAD and conferred resistance to cisplatin.

## Conclusion

The invention provides a novel, clinically translatable method for diagnosing, prognosing, and treating cancer through the quantification of the BAD pathway gene expression signature. The BPGES is a robust biomarker that distinguishes aggressive cancer subtypes, predicts survival, and identifies patients likely to benefit from kinase inhibitor combination therapies. By integrating transcriptional, post-translational, and functional data, the invention offers a comprehensive framework for precision oncology in epithelial malignancies.
