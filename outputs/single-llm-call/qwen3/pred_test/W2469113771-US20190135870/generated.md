# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

- relate to polypeptides and nucleic acid molecules of adeno-associated virus

The present invention relates to novel polypeptides and nucleic acid molecules derived from endogenous adeno-associated virus elements, particularly those originating from the germline of marsupial species. These molecules encode capsid and non-structural proteins that exhibit structural and functional homology to contemporary adeno-associated viruses, despite having been preserved in the host genome for tens of millions of years. The invention encompasses isolated nucleic acid sequences encoding ancestral or reconstructed capsid polypeptides, as well as the corresponding polypeptides themselves, including fragments, variants, and chimeric forms thereof. These molecules are derived from a lineage-specific endogenous viral element, designated mAAV-EVE1, which was integrated into the genome of a common ancestor of the Macropodiformes suborder and has since been conserved across multiple extant marsupial species. The invention further includes recombinant vectors, host cells, and virions engineered to express these ancient viral proteins for use in gene delivery, immunological modulation, and therapeutic applications.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

- introduce gene therapy

Gene therapy has emerged as a transformative approach for the treatment of inherited and acquired diseases by introducing, replacing, or modulating genetic material within target cells. The successful delivery of therapeutic nucleic acids to specific tissues remains a central challenge, requiring vectors that are safe, efficient, and capable of evading immune detection. Adeno-associated viruses have become one of the most widely utilized platforms for in vivo gene delivery due to their non-pathogenic nature, ability to transduce both dividing and non-dividing cells, and long-term transgene expression in the absence of genomic integration. However, the clinical utility of existing adeno-associated virus vectors is constrained by pre-existing humoral immunity in human populations, limited tissue tropism, and suboptimal transduction efficiency in certain organs.

- describe adeno-associated virus

Adeno-associated viruses are small, non-enveloped, single-stranded DNA viruses belonging to the genus Dependoparvovirus. Their genome is approximately 4.7 kilobases in length and is flanked by inverted terminal repeats that are essential for replication and packaging. The viral genome encodes two major open reading frames: the rep gene, which produces non-structural proteins required for replication and genome packaging, and the cap gene, which encodes the structural capsid proteins VP1, VP2, and VP3. These capsid proteins assemble into a T=1 icosahedral particle that determines viral tropism, receptor binding, intracellular trafficking, and immunogenicity. Natural isolates of adeno-associated viruses have been identified in numerous mammalian and avian species, each exhibiting distinct capsid sequences that influence host range and transduction profiles.

- explain AAV genome and capsid proteins

The adeno-associated virus genome is organized such that the rep gene lies proximal to the left inverted terminal repeat and is expressed from three promoters, yielding four overlapping non-structural proteins: Rep78, Rep68, Rep52, and Rep40. These proteins mediate viral DNA replication, site-specific integration, and regulation of transcription. The cap gene, located adjacent to the right inverted terminal repeat, encodes three capsid proteins—VP1, VP2, and VP3—that are produced through alternative splicing and differential initiation of translation. VP3 constitutes the majority of the capsid structure, while VP1 and VP2 are incorporated at lower stoichiometries and contain unique N-terminal extensions critical for endosomal escape and phospholipase activity. The capsid surface is composed of variable regions that project from a conserved β-barrel core, enabling adaptation to host receptors while preserving structural integrity. These variable regions are the primary determinants of antigenic diversity and tissue specificity among serotypes.

- discuss limitations of recombinant AAV

Despite their widespread use, recombinant adeno-associated virus vectors derived from human isolates face significant limitations. Pre-existing neutralizing antibodies against common serotypes such as AAV2, AAV5, and AAV8 are prevalent in human populations, reducing transduction efficiency and limiting re-administration. Additionally, the tropism of naturally occurring serotypes is often insufficient for targeting specific tissues such as the central nervous system, skeletal muscle, or retinal pigment epithelium with high fidelity. Capsid engineering efforts have yielded improved variants, but these are typically derived from limited sequence diversity among contemporary isolates, constraining the scope of functional innovation. Furthermore, immune responses against capsid epitopes can trigger cytotoxic T-cell activation, leading to loss of transduced cells and diminished therapeutic durability.

- motivate need for alternative recombinant AAV

There is a compelling need for novel capsid proteins that possess unique antigenic and functional properties not found in naturally occurring or engineered adeno-associated viruses. The evolutionary conservation of endogenous viral elements provides a rich, unexplored reservoir of ancient viral sequences that have undergone natural selection over millions of years. These sequences, though fragmented and mutated in their host genomes, retain structural motifs and domain architectures that reflect functional constraints imposed by viral fitness. The discovery of a highly conserved endogenous adeno-associated virus element in marsupials, designated mAAV-EVE1, offers a unique opportunity to reconstruct a functional capsid protein from an ancestral lineage that diverged from modern AAVs prior to the radiation of primates and other placental mammals. This ancient capsid, reconstructed in silico and expressed in vitro, exhibits structural features distinct from all known AAV serotypes and possesses the potential to evade pre-existing human immunity while enabling novel tropisms.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

- identify adeno-associated virus-derived endogenous viral element

The present invention identifies a lineage-specific endogenous viral element derived from an ancient adeno-associated virus, designated mAAV-EVE1, which is stably integrated into the germline genome of all members of the Macropodiformes suborder of marsupials. This element is absent from the genomes of other marsupial lineages and is orthologously positioned across multiple species, indicating a single germline integration event that occurred approximately 45 to 27 million years ago in a common ancestor of kangaroos, wallabies, and related species. Despite the accumulation of frameshifts, nonsense mutations, and deletions, the mAAV-EVE1 locus retains recognizable open reading frames encoding homologs of the rep and cap genes, with conserved domains indicative of ancestral functionality.

- describe nucleic acid molecules encoding capsid polypeptides

The invention provides isolated nucleic acid molecules encoding capsid polypeptides derived from the reconstructed ancestral mAAV-EVE1 sequence. These nucleic acid molecules include sequences encoding the full-length VP1, VP2, and VP3 proteins, as well as fragments thereof, wherein the sequences have been optimized for expression in eukaryotic systems and are free of the inactivating mutations present in the native genomic context. The nucleic acid molecules are substantially identical to the inferred ancestral sequence, with modifications introduced to restore conserved functional motifs, including the phospholipase A2 domain in VP1 and the DNA-binding and helicase domains in the Rep proteins.

- introduce isolated capsid polypeptide

The invention further provides an isolated capsid polypeptide comprising an amino acid sequence derived from the mAAV-EVE1 cap gene, wherein the polypeptide retains the conserved β-barrel core structure of parvoviral capsids and exhibits variable regions that are distinct from all known adeno-associated virus serotypes. The polypeptide is capable of self-assembling into a T=1 icosahedral capsid and displays a unique surface topology characterized by an elongated variable region VII that projects into the depression surrounding the 5-fold axis, conferring novel antigenic and receptor-binding properties.

- define capsid polypeptide sequence identity

The capsid polypeptide of the invention exhibits at least 55% sequence identity to the VP3 protein of AAV2, AAV8, or AAV4, but less than 70% sequence identity to any naturally occurring AAV serotype currently known in the public domain. The polypeptide contains at least 15 amino acid residues within the variable regions that differ from all known AAV capsids, with at least five of these residues located at positions known to mediate receptor interaction or immune recognition.

- describe regions of capsid polypeptide

The capsid polypeptide comprises a conserved β-barrel core formed by eight β-strands arranged in a jelly-roll fold, flanked by surface-exposed loops designated variable regions I through IX. Variable region VII is substantially extended compared to all known AAV capsids, forming a protrusion that extends toward the 5-fold axis and alters the electrostatic potential of the capsid surface. Variable regions II, IV, and VI contain unique amino acid substitutions that are not found in any extant AAV serotype and are predicted to confer resistance to neutralization by human antibodies directed against common AAV serotypes.

- introduce chimeric capsid polypeptide

The invention further provides a chimeric capsid polypeptide comprising a first domain derived from the mAAV-EVE1 capsid sequence and a second domain derived from a non-mAAV-EVE1 capsid sequence, wherein the chimeric polypeptide retains the ability to form a functional capsid and exhibits altered tropism, reduced immunogenicity, or enhanced transduction efficiency relative to either parental sequence.

- define chimeric capsid polypeptide sequence identity

The chimeric capsid polypeptide of the invention contains at least 40% of its amino acid residues derived from the mAAV-EVE1 sequence, with the remaining residues derived from a heterologous capsid source. The chimeric polypeptide exhibits at least 60% overall sequence identity to the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 VP3 protein, but less than 75% identity to any single known AAV serotype, ensuring that it is distinct from naturally occurring variants.

- describe regions of chimeric capsid polypeptide

The chimeric capsid polypeptide is engineered such that the variable regions of the mAAV-EVE1 component are preserved in their native conformation, while the core structural elements are substituted with homologous regions from a different AAV serotype to enhance stability or packaging efficiency. The chimeric polypeptide retains at least three variable regions from the mAAV-EVE1 sequence, including variable region VII, which is critical for defining its unique surface topology.

- introduce recombinant AAV virions

The invention provides recombinant adeno-associated virus virions comprising a capsid composed of the mAAV-EVE1-derived capsid polypeptide or a chimeric variant thereof, encapsidating a heterologous nucleic acid sequence. These virions are devoid of wild-type AAV sequences and are produced in the absence of helper virus contamination, ensuring a pure and defined vector product.

- describe nucleic acid molecules encoding capsid polypeptides

The invention further includes nucleic acid molecules encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptide, wherein the nucleic acid sequence is codon-optimized for expression in mammalian cells and is operably linked to a promoter capable of driving high-level expression in packaging cell lines. The nucleic acid molecule may be integrated into a plasmid, viral vector, or artificial chromosome for stable maintenance and scalable production.

- introduce vectors comprising nucleic acid molecules

The invention provides vectors comprising the nucleic acid molecules encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptide, wherein the vector is selected from the group consisting of plasmids, cosmids, lentiviral vectors, adenoviral vectors, herpesviral vectors, and recombinant AAV vectors. The vector may be configured to express the capsid polypeptide in cis or in trans relative to the therapeutic transgene.

- describe host cells comprising rAAV and vectors

The invention further encompasses host cells comprising the recombinant AAV virions or the vectors encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptide. These host cells are selected from the group consisting of HEK293 cells, HeLa cells, Sf9 insect cells, and CHO cells, and are engineered to provide the necessary helper functions for AAV replication and packaging.

- introduce method for introducing heterologous sequence

The invention provides a method for introducing a heterologous nucleic acid sequence into a recombinant AAV virion by co-transfecting a packaging cell line with a vector encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptide, a vector encoding the AAV rep gene, a helper vector providing adenoviral or herpesviral functions, and a transfer vector containing the heterologous sequence flanked by AAV inverted terminal repeats.

- describe method for producing chimeric capsid gene

The invention further provides a method for producing a chimeric capsid gene by recombining fragments of the mAAV-EVE1 capsid sequence with fragments of a heterologous AAV capsid sequence using DNA shuffling, overlap extension PCR, or homologous recombination in yeast or bacterial systems.

- introduce chimeric capsid genes and libraries

The invention provides libraries of chimeric capsid genes generated by combinatorial recombination of mAAV-EVE1-derived sequences with sequences from other AAV serotypes, wherein each member of the library encodes a unique capsid variant with altered tropism or immune evasion properties.

- introduce method for producing rAAV virion

The invention provides a method for producing a recombinant AAV virion by transfecting a packaging cell line with a first vector encoding the mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptide, a second vector encoding the AAV rep gene, a third vector encoding adenoviral helper genes, and a fourth vector encoding a heterologous nucleic acid sequence flanked by AAV inverted terminal repeats, followed by harvesting, purification, and concentration of the resulting virions.

Ancestral sequences were inferred at each node using the maximum likelihood method, with the most probable sequence selected from node 39.

### Example 3. Production of rAAV

- clone mAAV-EVE1 cap gene

The mAAV-EVE1 cap gene was synthesized with codon optimization and cloned into a plasmid under control of the CMV promoter.

- co-transfect plasmids into HEK293T cells

HEK293T cells were co-transfected with the cap plasmid, rep plasmid, adenoviral helper plasmid, and ITR-flanked transfer vector.

- harvest cells and centrifuge

Cells were harvested 72 hours post-transfection and lysed by freeze-thaw cycles, followed by centrifugation at 10,000 × g.

- treat supernatant with benzonase

Supernatant was treated with benzonase to degrade unpackaged nucleic acids.

- add PEG and centrifuge

Polyethylene glycol was added to a final concentration of 10%, and the mixture was centrifuged at 10,000 × g to pellet virions.

- resuspend pellet and incubate

Pellet was resuspended in PBS and incubated at 4°C for 1 hour to facilitate capsid maturation.

- purify AAV using CsCl gradient

Purification was performed using a 40–70% CsCl gradient, with virions collected at a density of 1.41 g/mL.

- dialyze and filter rAAV

The purified virions were dialyzed against PBS and filtered through a 0.22-μm membrane.

### Example 4. Assessment of rAAV

- assess rAAV functionality

rAAV virions were assessed for transduction efficiency in human hepatocytes, neurons, and muscle cells.

- quantify vector genomes

Vector genomes were quantified by qPCR using primers specific to the transgene.

- measure transgene expression

Transgene expression was measured by luciferase assay and fluorescence microscopy.

- test resistance to neutralization

rAAV virions were pre-incubated with human sera from individuals with high titers of anti-AAV2, AAV5, and AAV8 antibodies, and transduction was measured.

- determine tropism

rAAV virions demonstrated enhanced transduction of retinal pigment epithelium and dorsal root ganglia compared to AAV9.

- confirm capsid integrity

Capsid integrity was confirmed by SDS-PAGE and Western blot using anti-VP3 antibodies.

- demonstrate in vivo delivery

rAAV virions were injected intravenously into mice, and transgene expression was detected in liver, heart, and brain after two weeks.
