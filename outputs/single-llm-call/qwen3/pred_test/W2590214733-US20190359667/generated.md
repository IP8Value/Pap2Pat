## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and therapeutic protein engineering, specifically to engineered variants of transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) that function as dominant negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling. The invention further pertains to the design, production, and therapeutic application of recombinant monomeric TGF-β proteins with modified structural features that abolish their ability to activate downstream signaling while retaining high-affinity binding to TGF-β type II receptors. These engineered proteins are useful for the inhibition of pathological TGF-β signaling in a variety of human diseases, including fibrosis, cancer progression, and immune dysregulation.

## BACKGROUND

Transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) is a multifunctional cytokine that plays a central role in the regulation of cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, and extracellular matrix deposition. It exists in multiple isoforms, including TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3, which signal through a conserved receptor complex composed of type I and type II serine/threonine kinase receptors. Upon ligand binding, the type II receptor recruits and phosphorylates the type I receptor, initiating a downstream signaling cascade primarily mediated by SMAD transcription factors. While TGF-β is essential for normal tissue homeostasis and wound healing, its aberrant or sustained activation is implicated in a broad spectrum of pathological conditions, including tumor metastasis, organ fibrosis, autoimmune disorders, and cachexia. In many cancers, TGF-β promotes epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, immune evasion, and angiogenesis, making it a compelling but challenging therapeutic target. Conventional approaches to inhibit TGF-β signaling have included neutralizing antibodies, small molecule kinase inhibitors, and decoy receptors; however, these strategies often suffer from poor specificity, limited tissue penetration, or off-target effects due to the structural conservation of TGF-β family members and their receptors.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel class of engineered TGF-β monomers that function as potent and selective dominant negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling. These monomers are derived from human TGF-β isoforms through targeted structural modifications that eliminate the α-helix 3 region responsible for non-covalent dimerization and type I receptor recruitment, while preserving the type II receptor binding interface. The engineered monomers are produced recombinantly in bacterial systems, refolded into stable, soluble conformations, and exhibit no intrinsic signaling activity despite high-affinity binding to TGF-β type II receptors. The invention further encompasses fusion proteins and pharmaceutical compositions comprising these monomers, which are formulated for systemic or localized administration to inhibit pathological TGF-β signaling in vivo. The method of inhibition involves competitive blockade of endogenous TGF-β ligands from engaging type II receptors, thereby preventing the assembly of functional receptor complexes and suppressing downstream SMAD-dependent transcriptional programs.

### III. Overview of Several Embodiments

The invention provides recombinant TGF-β monomers that lack the α-helix 3 region and the interchain cysteine residue at position 77, rendering them incapable of dimerization or type I receptor recruitment while preserving high-affinity binding to TβRII. These monomers are engineered to inhibit TGF-β signaling by acting as competitive antagonists that occupy TβRII without enabling the formation of a functional signaling complex. The invention specifically encompasses a monomer in which residues 52 through 71 of human TGF-β2 are deleted, and cysteine-77 is substituted with serine, thereby eliminating the structural motif responsible for non-covalent dimerization and TβRI binding. The deletion of this region results in the formation of a flexible loop connecting β-strands 4 and 5, which maintains the overall fold of the ligand while removing key interaction surfaces. At least one amino acid substitution is introduced to increase the net charge of the monomer at physiological pH, enhancing solubility and reducing aggregation. The monomer may be derived from human, mouse, rat, or other mammalian TGF-β isoforms, with human TGF-β2 being a preferred embodiment. To confer high-affinity binding to TβRII, at least one amino acid substitution is introduced within the TβRII-binding interface, including but not limited to leucine to arginine at residue 51, alanine to lysine at residue 73, lysine to arginine at residue 25, arginine to lysine at residue 26, leucine to valine at residue 89, isoleucine to valine at residue 92, asparagine to arginine at residue 94, threonine to lysine at residue 95, and isoleucine to valine at residue 98. A combination of these substitutions, particularly seven substitutions as described in SEQ ID NO: 8, yields a monomer designated mmTGF-β2-7M, which exhibits sub-nanomolar binding affinity for TβRII and potent inhibitory activity against all TGF-β isoforms. The resulting monomer is structurally homologous to wild-type TGF-β2 but lacks the ability to recruit TβRI, as confirmed by structural studies and binding assays. The engineered monomer is capable of inhibiting TGF-β signaling in cells and in vivo with an IC50 in the range of 20 to 70 nM, and is effective against TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3. The monomer may be further modified by PEGylation, fusion to albumin or Fc domains, or conjugation to targeting ligands to enhance pharmacokinetic properties.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: An Engineered TGF-β Monomer that Functions as a Dominant Negative to Block TGF-β Signaling

#### BACKGROUND

TGF-β1 and TGF-β3 monomers, generated by substitution of cysteine-77 with serine, retain the ability to non-covalently dimerize and activate signaling through recruitment of TβRI and TβRII. This dimerization is mediated by the α-helix 3 region, which forms a hydrophobic interface between monomers and contributes to TβRI binding. It was hypothesized that deletion of this helix would disrupt both dimer formation and receptor recruitment, while preserving TβRII binding. To test this, bacterial expression constructs were designed to delete residues 52–71 and substitute cysteine-77 with serine in TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3, generating mini-monomeric variants designated mmTGF-β1, mmTGF-β2, and mmTGF-β3. Additional substitutions were introduced to increase net charge at pH 7.0, improving solubility. The resulting proteins were expressed in Escherichia coli as inclusion bodies, refolded under denaturing conditions, and purified by cation exchange chromatography. mmTGF-β2 was selected for further development due to its superior folding efficiency and solubility. To enhance TβRII affinity, seven amino acid substitutions were introduced into mmTGF-β2, yielding mmTGF-β2-7M, which binds TβRII with affinity comparable to TGF-β3. Structural analysis by X-ray crystallography confirmed that mmTGF-β2-7M retains the canonical TGF-β fold, with the deleted helix replaced by a flexible loop. Surface plasmon resonance demonstrated high-affinity binding to TβRII, while analytical ultracentrifugation and TR-FRET assays showed that mmTGF-β2-7M does not dimerize or recruit TβRI. In cellular assays, mmTGF-β2-7M exhibited no signaling activity and potently inhibited TGF-β1-, TGF-β2-, and TGF-β3-induced transcription, with IC50 values below 100 nM. These results establish mmTGF-β2-7M as a dominant negative inhibitor capable of blocking endogenous TGF-β signaling with high specificity and potency.
