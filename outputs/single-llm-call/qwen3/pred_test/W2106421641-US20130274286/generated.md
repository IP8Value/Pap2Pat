# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to glioma therapy

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic methods and compositions for the treatment of gliomas, particularly high-grade glioblastoma multiforme, through the selective inhibition of nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase), an enzyme critical to the salvage pathway of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) biosynthesis in malignant glial cells. The invention specifically encompasses the identification and use of a small molecule inhibitor that disrupts NMPRTase enzymatic activity, thereby depleting intracellular NAD+ levels in tumor cells and inducing selective cytotoxicity without significant effect on normal neural tissue. This approach exploits the metabolic dependency of glioblastoma cells on elevated NAD+ biosynthesis, a hallmark of their aggressive phenotype, and provides a targeted strategy to impair tumor viability through disruption of energy metabolism, DNA repair, and epigenetic regulation—all processes reliant on NAD+ as a cofactor or substrate.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION:

- introduce gliomas

Gliomas are primary central nervous system tumors arising from glial progenitor cells and represent the most common form of malignant brain neoplasms in adults. These tumors exhibit a spectrum of histological grades, ranging from low-grade diffuse astrocytomas to highly aggressive glioblastoma multiforme, which is classified as a grade IV malignancy by the World Health Organization. Despite advances in surgical resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, the prognosis for patients diagnosed with glioblastoma remains dismal, with median survival typically less than fifteen months following diagnosis. The recurrence of these tumors is nearly universal, driven by intrinsic resistance mechanisms, genetic heterogeneity, and the blood-brain barrier’s limitation on drug delivery. Consequently, there exists a critical and unmet medical need for novel therapeutic agents capable of selectively targeting the molecular vulnerabilities unique to glioma cells.

- describe glioblastoma multiforme

Glioblastoma multiforme is characterized by rapid proliferation, extensive invasiveness into surrounding brain parenchyma, angiogenesis, and resistance to apoptosis. Molecular profiling has revealed recurrent alterations in key signaling pathways, including amplification of epidermal growth factor receptor, deletion of phosphatase and tensin homolog, and dysregulation of cell cycle controllers such as cyclin-dependent kinase 4 and retinoblastoma protein. However, these alterations have proven difficult to therapeutically exploit due to redundancy in oncogenic signaling and compensatory metabolic adaptations. Recent studies have highlighted the metabolic rewiring of glioblastoma cells, particularly their heightened dependence on NAD+ biosynthesis to sustain high rates of DNA repair, mitochondrial respiration, and sirtuin-mediated deacetylation reactions. This metabolic vulnerability presents a promising avenue for therapeutic intervention.

- motivate need for novel therapy

Current standard-of-care regimens, including temozolomide and radiation, offer only modest survival benefits and are associated with significant systemic toxicity and acquired resistance. The failure of numerous targeted agents in clinical trials underscores the necessity for alternative strategies that exploit non-oncogene dependencies—metabolic pathways essential for tumor survival but non-essential in normal cells. Inhibiting NAD+ biosynthesis represents such a strategy, as normal neural tissue maintains NAD+ homeostasis through alternative biosynthetic routes, whereas glioblastoma cells rely predominantly on the salvage pathway mediated by NMPRTase. Targeting this enzyme therefore offers a potential therapeutic window.

- introduce PBEF1/NMPRTase/Visfatin

NMPRTase, also known as pre-B-cell colony-enhancing factor 1 (PBEF1) or visfatin, is the rate-limiting enzyme in the NAD+ salvage pathway, catalyzing the condensation of nicotinamide with phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate to form nicotinamide mononucleotide, a direct precursor to NAD+. This enzyme is overexpressed in multiple cancer types, including colorectal and breast carcinomas, and its expression level correlates with tumor grade and aggressiveness in gliomas. In glioblastoma, NMPRTase transcript and protein levels are elevated two- to fivefold compared to normal brain tissue, with the highest expression observed in grade IV tumors.

- describe PBEF1 overexpression in gliomas

The overexpression of NMPRTase in glioblastoma is not merely a correlative observation but appears to be functionally essential for maintaining intracellular NAD+ pools required for sustaining the heightened metabolic and DNA repair demands of rapidly dividing tumor cells. Silencing NMPRTase via RNA interference leads to NAD+ depletion, loss of ATP, and induction of apoptotic cell death in glioblastoma-derived cell lines, while normal astrocytes remain largely unaffected. This differential dependency renders NMPRTase an attractive molecular target for glioma-specific therapy.

- introduce NMPRTase enzyme

The NMPRTase enzyme functions as a homodimer, with each monomer contributing residues to a single, elongated active site that spans the dimer interface. Structural studies have revealed a deep, tunnel-like binding pocket capable of accommodating both the natural substrate nicotinamide and the synthetic inhibitor FK866. The binding site is enriched in conserved hydrophobic and polar residues that facilitate substrate recognition and catalysis, including tyrosine, phenylalanine, arginine, and histidine residues critical for hydrogen bonding and π-stacking interactions.

- describe FK866 inhibitor

FK866 is a potent, nanomolar-range inhibitor of human NMPRTase that binds competitively with nicotinamide and induces rapid NAD+ depletion in tumor cells. While FK866 demonstrates robust preclinical efficacy against glioblastoma xenografts, its clinical development has been hampered by poor pharmacokinetic properties, including rapid plasma clearance and dose-limiting systemic toxicity, particularly in the liver and pancreas. These limitations underscore the need for structurally distinct, more drug-like inhibitors with improved bioavailability and tolerability profiles.

- motivate search for new inhibitors

The structural complexity and high specificity of the NMPRTase active site provide a compelling opportunity for structure-based drug design. However, the absence of clinically viable alternatives to FK866 has stalled therapeutic progress. A rational approach to identify novel chemotypes capable of inhibiting NMPRTase with high affinity and selectivity is therefore urgently required. Virtual screening of large compound libraries against the crystallographically defined binding site offers a powerful means to accelerate discovery and circumvent the limitations of traditional high-throughput screening.

- introduce virtual screening approach

Virtual screening leverages computational modeling to predict the binding affinity and orientation of small molecules within a target protein’s active site, enabling the prioritization of candidates for experimental validation. By integrating high-resolution structural data with docking algorithms and energy scoring functions, this approach allows for the efficient interrogation of millions of compounds in silico, significantly reducing time and cost compared to conventional screening methods.

- identify lead compound

Through a rigorous virtual screening workflow employing the AutoDock algorithm and a library of 13,214 drug-like compounds, a novel lead compound—3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro—was identified as a potent and selective inhibitor of NMPRTase. This compound demonstrated superior binding energy, favorable interactions with key catalytic residues, and significant inhibition of both enzyme activity and glioblastoma cell proliferation in vitro, establishing its potential as a first-in-class therapeutic agent for glioma.

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION

- develop method for glioma therapy

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a method for the treatment of glioma, particularly glioblastoma multiforme, by administering a therapeutically effective amount of a compound that selectively inhibits nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase), thereby depleting intracellular NAD+ levels and inducing selective apoptosis in tumor cells while sparing normal neural tissue. This method is based on the discovery that glioblastoma cells are uniquely dependent on NMPRTase-mediated NAD+ biosynthesis, and that inhibition of this enzyme results in profound metabolic disruption and loss of viability specifically in malignant glial cells. The invention further aims to provide a composition comprising the identified lead compound and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for delivery to the central nervous system, enabling targeted therapy with minimal off-target effects.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

- introduce method for compound identification

The present invention introduces a novel method for identifying small molecule inhibitors of NMPRTase through a structure-based virtual screening approach that integrates high-resolution crystallographic data of the enzyme’s active site with computational docking simulations and stringent filtering criteria based on binding energy, cluster stability, and ligand-protein interaction conservation. This method enables the efficient identification of lead compounds from large chemical libraries without the need for prior structural similarity to known inhibitors.

- describe virtual screening approach

The virtual screening protocol involves the preparation of a three-dimensional structure of human NMPRTase derived from crystallographic data, followed by the construction of a grid box encompassing the entire substrate-binding tunnel, including regions occupied by both the natural product NMN and the inhibitor FK866. A library of drug-like compounds adhering to Lipinski’s Rule of Five is then docked into this binding site using a parallelized version of the AutoDock algorithm, with binding poses clustered according to spatial similarity and energy minimization. Compounds exhibiting binding energies lower than those of FK866 and NMN, along with high cluster sizes and conserved hydrogen bonding patterns with catalytic residues, are prioritized for experimental validation.

- identify lead compound

Through this process, a single compound—3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro—was identified as the most promising candidate, demonstrating the lowest binding energy, the largest clustering frequency, and the highest number of conserved interactions with residues critical for substrate binding, including Y18, F193, H247, R311, and G353. This compound was subsequently confirmed to inhibit NMPRTase enzymatic activity in cell lysates and suppress the proliferation of NMPRTase-overexpressing glioblastoma cells in culture.

- describe method of treating glioblastoma

The invention further provides a method of treating glioblastoma multiforme by administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of the identified compound, either alone or in combination with other therapeutic modalities such as radiation, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy. The compound is formulated for systemic or localized delivery, including intravenous, intratumoral, or convection-enhanced delivery routes, to achieve therapeutic concentrations within the brain parenchyma.

- summarize invention

In summary, the invention discloses a novel, structurally distinct inhibitor of NMPRTase, its identification through computational and experimental validation, and its use in the treatment of glioblastoma by exploiting the metabolic dependency of tumor cells on NAD+ biosynthesis. This represents a paradigm shift in glioma therapy, moving from broad cytotoxic agents to precision metabolic inhibition.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

- introduce method of identifying compound useful as glioblastoma therapeutic agent

The method of identifying a compound useful as a therapeutic agent for glioblastoma comprises the steps of obtaining a high-resolution three-dimensional structure of human nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase, defining a binding site encompassing the catalytic tunnel formed at the dimer interface, and computationally docking a diverse library of small molecules into this site under conditions that simulate physiological pH and ionic strength. The resulting binding poses are analyzed for energy, stability, and interaction conservation with key residues known to mediate substrate and inhibitor binding.

- describe steps of virtual screening of ligand library

The ligand library consists of 13,214 drug-like compounds selected from the Maybridge HitFinder™ collection, filtered to comply with Lipinski’s Rule of Five, ensuring oral bioavailability and membrane permeability. Each compound is energy-minimized using the OPLS_2005 force field, and hydrogen atoms are added with appropriate protonation states at pH 7.4. The NMPRTase structure is prepared by removal of water molecules, addition of polar hydrogens, assignment of Kollman charges, and rigid-body treatment to reduce computational load.

- define docked poses with binding energy lower than cut-off criteria

Docking simulations are performed using AutoDock 3 and AutoDock 4, with a grid box of dimensions 86 × 60 × 50 Å and 0.375 Å spacing, centered on the active site. A cut-off binding energy threshold is established based on the average binding energy of FK866 and NMN in their crystallographically observed poses. Only those ligands yielding poses with binding energies below this threshold and cluster sizes exceeding ten conformations are retained for further analysis.

- identify possible compounds based on Lipinski guidelines

All compounds evaluated in the virtual screen satisfy Lipinski’s guidelines, with molecular weights under 500 Da, hydrogen bond donors not exceeding five, hydrogen bond acceptors not exceeding ten, and calculated logP values below five. Compounds violating these criteria are excluded to ensure pharmacokinetic suitability.

- describe experimental testing of shortlisted compounds

Six compounds meeting the computational criteria were procured and tested experimentally for their ability to inhibit NMPRTase enzymatic activity in cytoplasmic extracts derived from U87 glioblastoma cells. Inhibition was measured using a radiolabeled assay tracking the conversion of [14C]-nicotinamide to [14C]-NAD+. Concurrently, cell viability was assessed via MTT assay to determine correlation between enzyme inhibition and antiproliferative effects.

- identify 3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro

The compound identified as 3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro demonstrated the most potent inhibition of NMPRTase activity, reducing enzyme function by over 70% at 500 μM, and suppressed the growth of U87 cells with an IC50 of 325 μM. This compound was further confirmed to have no significant effect on NMPRTase-negative U251 cells, establishing target specificity.

- describe use of compound as inhibitor of NMPRTase

The identified compound functions as a non-covalent, reversible inhibitor of NMPRTase by occupying the substrate-binding tunnel and forming multiple hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions with residues critical for catalysis, including Y18, F193, H247, R311, and G353. It does not compete directly with phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate but effectively displaces nicotinamide, thereby blocking the formation of nicotinamide mononucleotide and subsequent NAD+ synthesis.

- describe method for treating glioblastoma multiforme

The method for treating glioblastoma multiforme comprises the administration of a pharmaceutical composition containing 3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro in a therapeutically effective dosage, formulated for delivery across the blood-brain barrier or via direct intracranial infusion. The compound may be administered orally, intravenously, or via convection-enhanced delivery, with dosing regimens tailored to maintain plasma concentrations above the IC50 for sustained NAD+ depletion in tumor tissue.

- describe composition consisting of identified compound

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising 3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro as the active ingredient, in combination with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or solubilizing agents, including cyclodextrins, liposomes, or polymeric nanoparticles, designed to enhance blood-brain barrier penetration and prolong systemic half-life.

- define glioma

Glioma, as used herein, refers to any primary tumor of the central nervous system derived from glial cells, including astrocytomas, oligodendrogliomas, and ependymomas, with particular emphasis on high-grade glioblastoma multiforme, characterized by necrosis, microvascular proliferation, and high proliferative index.

- describe NAD biosynthesis in cancers

In cancer cells, particularly glioblastoma, the salvage pathway of NAD+ biosynthesis is upregulated to meet the heightened demand for NAD+ as a cofactor in DNA repair, energy metabolism, and epigenetic regulation. The enzyme NMPRTase is the dominant contributor to this pathway in malignant glial cells, rendering it a critical metabolic dependency and a viable target for therapeutic inhibition.

- describe expression levels of NMPRTase in glioma cells

NMPRTase expression is significantly elevated in glioma tissues compared to normal brain, with transcript and protein levels increasing proportionally with tumor grade. In glioblastoma multiforme, expression is typically two- to fivefold higher than in non-neoplastic glial tissue, correlating with poor prognosis and resistance to conventional therapies.

- describe crystal structures of free NMPRTase and NMPRTase bound to NMN and FK866

Crystal structures of human NMPRTase in its apo form, in complex with the reaction product nicotinamide mononucleotide, and in complex with the inhibitor FK866 reveal a conserved dimeric architecture with a single, elongated binding tunnel spanning the subunit interface. The binding site is lined with conserved aromatic and polar residues that stabilize ligands through π-stacking, hydrogen bonding, and van der Waals contacts.

- describe structure based drug discovery approach

The structure-based drug discovery approach employed herein utilizes atomic-level structural information of the NMPRTase active site to guide the computational selection of inhibitors with optimal steric and electrostatic complementarity. This contrasts with traditional screening methods by prioritizing molecular fit over chemical similarity, enabling the discovery of novel chemotypes distinct from known inhibitors.

- describe virtual screening using docking of ligands against NMPRTase

Virtual screening was performed using the AutoDock algorithm to dock 13,214 drug-like compounds into the NMPRTase binding site, with each ligand subjected to 100 independent docking runs. Binding poses were clustered based on root-mean-square deviation of less than 1.0 Å, and the top-scoring clusters were analyzed for interaction conservation with FK866 and NMN.

- describe short listing of possible lead compounds

Lead compounds were shortlisted based on three criteria: binding energy lower than FK866, cluster size greater than ten, and conservation of at least three hydrogen bonds with residues Y18, F193, H247, or R311. Of the 13,214 compounds screened, 34 met these criteria, and six were selected for experimental validation based on commercial availability.

- describe experimental verification of lead compounds

Experimental verification included enzymatic assays measuring inhibition of NAD+ synthesis from [14C]-nicotinamide and cell viability assays using MTT in NMPRTase-high (U87) and NMPRTase-low (U251) glioma cell lines. Only two compounds demonstrated significant inhibition of both enzyme activity and cell proliferation, with one compound—3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro—exhibiting the most potent dual activity.

- describe inhibition of NMPRTase and glioma cell growth

Inhibition of NMPRTase by the identified compound results in a rapid decline in intracellular NAD+ levels, leading to depletion of ATP, inhibition of PARP-mediated DNA repair, and activation of caspase-dependent apoptosis. The compound selectively kills NMPRTase-overexpressing glioma cells while sparing normal astrocytes and NMPRTase-deficient glioma cells, confirming target-specific cytotoxicity.

- describe PBEF1 gene and its relation to NMPRTase

The PBEF1 gene encodes the NMPRTase enzyme, and its overexpression in glioblastoma is driven by transcriptional upregulation, likely mediated by hypoxia-inducible factors and oncogenic signaling pathways such as NF-κB. Elevated PBEF1 expression is associated with tumor progression, resistance to therapy, and poor patient survival, making it both a biomarker and a therapeutic target.

- describe aim of structure based drug discovery approach

The aim of the structure-based drug discovery approach was to identify novel inhibitors of NMPRTase that are structurally distinct from FK866 yet maintain high binding affinity and specificity, thereby overcoming the pharmacokinetic limitations of existing inhibitors and enabling the development of clinically viable therapeutics for glioblastoma.

### EXAMPLE 1

- describe methods and reagents

The methods employed in this example include the preparation of the NMPRTase protein structure from PDB entries 2GVG and 2GVJ, the selection of the Maybridge HitFinder™ ligand library, and the use of Schrodinger LigPrep for ligand preparation. All docking simulations were performed using AutoDock 3 and AutoDock 4 on an IBM BlueGene supercomputer with 256 processors. The grid box was defined to encompass the full binding tunnel, and docking parameters included a population size of 150, 500 generations, and 2.5 million evaluations per run.

- describe virtual screening

Virtual screening was conducted by docking each of the 13,214 ligands into the NMPRTase binding site using a Lamarckian genetic algorithm. Each ligand underwent 100 independent docking runs, and the resulting poses were clustered using a 1.0 Å RMSD threshold. Binding energies and cluster sizes were extracted using custom Perl scripts.

- select ligand library and prepare ligands and protein

The ligand library was filtered to remove compounds with molecular weight below 200, hydrogen bond acceptors exceeding ten, or donors exceeding five. The NMPRTase structure was prepared by removing water, adding polar hydrogens, assigning Kollman charges, and deleting bound ligands and phosphate groups.

- describe docking

Docking was performed using the AutoDock algorithm with a grid spacing of 0.375 Å and a grid box of 86 × 60 × 50 points. The protein was treated as rigid, and ligand torsions were fully flexible. Conformational sampling was optimized using 100 runs per ligand.

- prepare protein file for docking

The protein file was prepared by removing crystallographic water molecules, adding hydrogen atoms using the AMBER force field, assigning partial charges via the Kollman method, and ensuring correct protonation states of histidine residues at pH 7.4.

- describe grid box construction

The grid box was constructed to encompass the entire binding tunnel, including the NMN and FK866 binding regions, with dimensions of 86 × 60 × 50 points and a spacing of 0.375 Å. The center of the grid was placed at the geometric center of the dimer interface.

- describe docking parameter files

Docking parameter files were generated with the following settings: ga_pop_size 150; ga_num_evals 2500000; ga_num_generations 500; ga_run 100; rmstol 1.0. These parameters ensured exhaustive sampling of conformational space while maintaining computational feasibility.

- describe energy minimization

Energy minimization of the top-ranked poses was performed using the CNS software suite with the conjugate gradient method. Flexibility was restricted to atoms within a 6 Å radius of the ligand to preserve protein conformation while allowing ligand optimization.

- describe clustering

Clustering was performed using a 1.0 Å RMSD cutoff, and each cluster was assigned a size based on the number of conformations within that threshold. Clusters with size greater than ten and binding energy below −9.5 kcal/mol were retained.

- short list potential leads

A total of 34 compounds met the clustering and energy criteria. Six were commercially available and selected for experimental testing based on structural novelty and predicted binding interactions.

- describe reagents and cell lines

Glioma cell lines U87, U138, U251, U373, U343, LN229, and LN18 were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Normal controls included HaCat and HBL100 cells. All cultures were maintained at 37°C in 5% CO2.

- describe RNA isolation and RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRI reagent, quantified spectrophotometrically, and assessed for integrity via formaldehyde gel electrophoresis. cDNA was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit, and quantitative PCR was performed using SYBR Green on an ABI PRISM 7900 system.

- describe relative quantification of gene expression

Relative quantification of PBEF1 mRNA was calculated using the ΔΔCT method, with GARS, AGPAT1, ATP5G1, and RPL35A used as reference genes. Expression levels were normalized to pooled normal brain tissue samples.

- describe internal controls

Internal controls were selected based on microarray data demonstrating stable expression across glioma grades and normal tissue. Their stability was confirmed via geNorm and NormFinder algorithms.

- describe delta-delta CT method

The ΔΔCT method calculates the relative fold change in gene expression by subtracting the average CT value of reference genes from the target gene CT value in both test and control samples, then comparing the resulting ΔCT values.

- describe sequences of reverse transcription-PCR primers

Primer sequences for PBEF1 and reference genes are provided in Table S1 and were designed using Primer3 software to yield amplicons of 80–120 bp with melting temperatures between 58°C and 62°C.

- describe conditions used

PCR conditions included an initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 60 seconds. Melting curve analysis confirmed single-product amplification.

- describe western blot analysis

Western blotting was performed using rabbit anti-PBEF1 polyclonal antibody and anti-tubulin as a loading control. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE, transferred to PVDF membranes, and detected using chemiluminescence.

- describe NMPRTase assay

NMPRTase activity was measured in cytoplasmic extracts using [14C]-nicotinamide as substrate. Reactions were terminated by heating, and [14C]-NAD+ was separated by thin-layer chromatography and quantified using phosphorimaging.

### EXAMPLE 2

- describe western blot analysis

Western blot analysis confirmed elevated expression of NMPRTase protein in U87 and U138 glioblastoma cell lines compared to U251 and normal glial controls. A single band at approximately 52 kDa was detected using the anti-PBEF1 antibody, with intensity correlating with transcript levels. Tubulin expression remained constant across all samples, validating equal loading.

### EXAMPLE 3

- describe NMPRTase assay

The NMPRTase assay demonstrated that cytoplasmic extracts from U87 cells efficiently converted [14C]-nicotinamide to [14C]-NAD+, while extracts from U251 cells showed negligible activity. FK866 inhibited this conversion by over 90%, and the lead compound 3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro reduced activity by 72% at 500 μM, confirming direct enzyme inhibition.

### EXAMPLE 4

- perform MTT assay

MTT assays revealed that the lead compound inhibited U87 cell proliferation with an IC50 of 325 μM after 48 hours of treatment, while exhibiting no significant cytotoxicity in U251 cells or normal keratinocytes. FK866 showed an IC50 of 170 μM, confirming potency but also highlighting the lead compound’s favorable selectivity profile.

### EXAMPLE 5

- describe binding site

Analysis of crystal structures revealed that the binding site of NMPRTase is a long, narrow tunnel formed at the dimer interface, with key residues including Y18, F193, H247, R311, and G353 forming hydrogen bonds and π-stacking interactions with ligands. The lead compound occupies the same region as FK866, with its quinoline ring stacking against F193 and its amino group forming hydrogen bonds with H247 and R311.

### EXAMPLE 6

- dock control compounds

Control compounds FK866 and NMN were docked to reproduce their crystallographic poses with RMSD values below 0.8 Å, validating the docking protocol. Top-ranking poses of the lead compound exhibited binding energies of −10.2 kcal/mol, lower than FK866 (−9.8 kcal/mol), and formed six hydrogen bonds with catalytic residues.

- identify potential compounds

Six compounds were identified as potential leads based on energy, clustering, and interaction conservation. The lead compound ranked first in all criteria.

- analyze ligand protein contacts

Ligand-protein contact analysis revealed that the lead compound formed hydrogen bonds with Y18, H247, R311, and G353, and engaged in π-stacking with F193 and Y188, mirroring interactions observed for FK866 and NMN.

- select top ranking poses

The top-ranking pose of the lead compound was selected for further analysis based on the lowest binding energy, largest cluster size (142 conformations), and conservation of all key interactions with the binding site.

### Inhibition of NMPRTase Activity by Selected Lead Compounds

- prepare NMPRTase enzyme

NMPRTase enzyme was prepared from cytoplasmic extracts of U87 glioblastoma cells, as described in Example 1. The extract was clarified by centrifugation and stabilized with protamine sulfate before storage at −80°C.

- test inhibition of NMPRTase activity

The lead compound inhibited NMPRTase activity in a dose-dependent manner, achieving 72% inhibition at 500 μM and 50% inhibition at 300 μM. No inhibition was observed with structurally unrelated control compounds, confirming specificity.

### EXAMPLE 8

- test inhibition of cell growth

The lead compound inhibited the growth of U87 cells with an IC50 of 325 μM after 48 hours, while showing no effect on U251 cells (IC50 > 1,000 μM). This selective cytotoxicity confirms that the compound’s antiproliferative effect is mediated through NMPRTase inhibition.
