# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention

The present invention relates to the field of topical cosmetic and dermatological formulations designed for the protection, restoration, and rejuvenation of human skin subjected to oxidative stress. More specifically, the invention concerns a novel class of lipid-based microcapsules engineered to selectively interact with reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the skin’s outer layers, thereby mitigating oxidative damage while simultaneously enhancing hydration, reinforcing the epidermal barrier, and reducing visible signs of aging. The invention integrates a unique matrix composition that exhibits high affinity for ROS without acting as a conventional antioxidant, thereby avoiding pro-oxidative side effects commonly associated with free radical scavengers such as vitamins C and E. This technology is particularly suited for use in creams, serums, lotions, and gels intended for daily topical application to facial, cervical, and forearm skin regions affected by intrinsic and extrinsic aging processes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce skin care products

Skin care products have long been developed to address the aesthetic and physiological manifestations of skin aging, including fine lines, loss of elasticity, uneven pigmentation, dryness, and compromised barrier function. These products typically contain active ingredients formulated to penetrate the stratum corneum and modulate cellular processes within the epidermis and upper dermis. Over decades, formulations have evolved from simple emollients to complex delivery systems incorporating peptides, retinoids, growth factors, and antioxidants.

- define active ingredients

Active ingredients in these products are selected for their ability to neutralize free radicals, stimulate collagen synthesis, enhance moisture retention, or inhibit enzymatic degradation of the extracellular matrix. Commonly used agents include ascorbic acid (vitamin C), tocopherol (vitamin E), retinyl palmitate (vitamin A), niacinamide, and polyphenolic compounds derived from botanical sources. These substances are often incorporated into lipid-based carriers to improve their stability, bioavailability, and skin penetration.

- explain biological activity

The biological activity of these ingredients is primarily mediated through their interaction with cellular redox systems. Antioxidants function by donating electrons to unstable ROS molecules, thereby stabilizing them and preventing oxidative damage to lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. However, under certain conditions—particularly in the presence of transition metals or high concentrations—some antioxidants can paradoxically promote oxidation, leading to unintended cellular stress.

- describe effects of ROS

Reactive oxygen species, including superoxide anion, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radicals, are naturally generated during mitochondrial respiration and in response to environmental stressors such as ultraviolet radiation, pollution, and cigarette smoke. When produced in excess, these molecules overwhelm endogenous antioxidant defenses, triggering a cascade of molecular damage that accelerates skin aging. ROS directly oxidize membrane phospholipids, denature structural proteins such as collagen and elastin, and induce DNA strand breaks, particularly at telomeric regions, contributing to cellular senescence.

- explain ORAC

Oxygen Radical Absorbance Capacity (ORAC) is a standardized method used to quantify the total antioxidant capacity of a substance in vitro by measuring its ability to neutralize peroxyl radicals under controlled conditions. While ORAC values are frequently cited in marketing materials to suggest efficacy, they do not reliably predict in vivo biological activity due to differences in bioavailability, skin penetration, and metabolic stability.

- describe damage caused by ROS

The cumulative damage caused by ROS manifests clinically as increased transepidermal water loss, decreased skin hydration, roughened texture, deepening of wrinkles, and impaired barrier integrity. Oxidative stress also activates matrix metalloproteinases, which degrade collagen and hyaluronic acid, further exacerbating loss of skin firmness and volume.

- explain lipid peroxidation

Lipid peroxidation is a chain reaction initiated by ROS attacking polyunsaturated fatty acids within cellular membranes. This process generates lipid hydroperoxides and reactive aldehydes such as malondialdehyde and 4-hydroxynonenal, which act as secondary messengers of oxidative damage, propagating inflammation and triggering apoptosis in keratinocytes and fibroblasts.

- describe protein damage

Protein damage occurs through carbonylation of amino acid side chains, cross-linking of structural proteins, and disruption of enzyme function. Collagen fibers become brittle and fragmented, while elastin loses its recoil capacity, resulting in reduced skin resilience and increased sagging.

- describe DNA damage

DNA damage induced by ROS includes base modifications, single- and double-strand breaks, and telomere attrition. Persistent DNA lesions activate p53-mediated senescence pathways, leading to the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines known as the senescence-associated secretory phenotype, which further degrades the skin microenvironment.

- describe antioxidant products

Antioxidant-containing skin care products have been widely commercialized, often marketed as anti-aging solutions based on their ability to scavenge free radicals in test tube assays. These include serums with high concentrations of vitamin C, creams fortified with green tea extract, and lotions containing coenzyme Q10 or resveratrol.

- describe drawbacks of known products

Despite their popularity, many antioxidant formulations suffer from poor stability, limited skin penetration, and, critically, pro-oxidative behavior under physiological conditions. Free vitamin C, for example, oxidizes rapidly upon exposure to air and light, and at high concentrations can generate hydrogen peroxide in the presence of metal ions, thereby increasing rather than reducing oxidative burden. Encapsulation has improved stability but often fails to address the fundamental issue of selective ROS targeting. Moreover, many products lack measurable biological impact on key skin parameters such as TEWL, stratum corneum hydration, or wrinkle depth in controlled clinical studies.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- summarize invention

The invention discloses a novel lipid-based microcapsule system designed to mitigate oxidative damage in human skin by virtue of its intrinsic, non-scavenging affinity for reactive oxygen species. Unlike conventional antioxidants, the microcapsules do not chemically neutralize ROS but instead sequester and stabilize them within a lipid matrix that undergoes controlled, localized oxidation, thereby preventing ROS from interacting with cellular components. This mechanism preserves endogenous antioxidant systems and avoids the pro-oxidative side effects associated with traditional formulations.

- define capsule

The capsule is a solid lipid particle with a diameter ranging from 1 to 25 micrometers, comprising a lipophilic matrix embedded with unsaturated fatty acids and lipophilic active ingredients, surrounded by an aqueous phase stabilized by non-ionic surfactants. The capsule structure is formed through a low-temperature emulsification process that preserves the integrity of thermolabile components.

- describe matrix with high ROS affinity

The matrix is composed of a blend of natural waxes and polyunsaturated fatty acids selected for their high susceptibility to oxidation, creating a sacrificial lipid reservoir that preferentially reacts with ROS before they can reach cellular targets. This design ensures that oxidation occurs within the capsule rather than on the skin’s surface or within living cells.

- describe method for making capsules

The method involves melting a solid lipophilic component at a temperature below 60°C, dissolving lipophilic actives into the molten phase, combining it with an aqueous solution containing water-soluble antioxidants and surfactants, homogenizing the mixture under controlled shear, sieving to obtain uniform particle size, and cooling to induce solidification of the lipid core.

- describe skin care product

The skin care product is a topical formulation selected from the group consisting of creams, serums, lotions, and gels, incorporating the lipid microcapsules at concentrations ranging from 0.1% to 10% by weight. The product is stable under ambient storage conditions and exhibits no phase separation, discoloration, or odor change over a period of at least 12 months.

- describe advantages of invention

The invention offers superior protection against oxidative stress without triggering pro-oxidative responses, enhances skin hydration and barrier function, reduces wrinkle depth and roughness, and demonstrates greater clinical efficacy than formulations containing free or encapsulated vitamins. It is non-irritating, suitable for sensitive skin, and does not require refrigeration or airless packaging for stability.

- describe alternative to existing solutions

This system represents a paradigm shift from antioxidant-based strategies to ROS-targeted sequestration, providing a more physiologically aligned approach to combating skin aging.

- describe reliable and easy implementation

The manufacturing process is scalable, solvent-free, and compatible with existing cosmetic production lines, requiring no specialized equipment beyond standard homogenizers and sieving systems.

- describe competitive cost

The raw materials are naturally derived, widely available, and cost-effective compared to synthetic encapsulation technologies or stabilized vitamin derivatives.

- describe capsule composition

The capsule comprises a solid lipid core containing jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax, mixed with safflower oil-derived polyunsaturated fatty acids and retinyl palmitate, suspended in an aqueous phase containing decyl glucoside and sodium ascorbyl phosphate.

- describe process for preparing capsules

The process begins with melting the wax blend at 55°C, adding lipophilic actives under stirring, preparing an aqueous phase at 48°C, combining the two phases under high-shear homogenization, sieving through a 25 μm mesh, and cooling to 10°C to solidify the particles.

- describe method for topical treatment

The method involves applying the topical formulation once or twice daily to clean, dry skin of the face, neck, or forearms for a period of at least four weeks to achieve measurable improvements in hydration, barrier integrity, and wrinkle depth.

- describe benefits of invention

The invention provides sustained, targeted protection against oxidative damage, improves skin texture and appearance, restores barrier function, and delivers clinically significant results without the instability or paradoxical effects of conventional antioxidant systems.

## DESCRIPTION

- introduce skin care products and methods

Topical skin care products are formulated to deliver active ingredients to the stratum corneum and underlying epidermal layers with the goal of enhancing skin health, appearance, and resilience. Conventional methods rely on passive diffusion, chemical penetration enhancers, or encapsulation technologies such as liposomes, niosomes, or polymeric nanoparticles. However, these systems often fail to provide sustained protection against oxidative stress due to premature degradation, poor skin retention, or activation of counterproductive pathways.

- define skin care product

The skin care product of the invention is a semi-solid or liquid composition intended for dermal application, comprising a continuous aqueous phase and a dispersed phase of solid lipid microcapsules. The product may be formulated as a cream, gel, serum, or lotion and is packaged in non-reactive containers to maintain stability.

- describe capsule composition

Each microcapsule consists of a solid lipid matrix formed from a blend of jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax, with a melting point between 45°C and 55°C. The matrix is enriched with polyunsaturated fatty acids derived from safflower oil, constituting at least 15% by weight of the lipid phase, and further contains lipophilic active ingredients such as retinyl palmitate.

- define lipid particle

The lipid particle is a discrete, spherical, solid structure with a diameter not exceeding 25 micrometers, formed by the solidification of a molten lipid mixture following emulsification and cooling. The particle is impermeable to water but permits controlled diffusion of reactive oxygen species into its core.

- describe matrix composition

The matrix composition is engineered to have a high oxidative reactivity profile, such that reactive oxygen species preferentially oxidize the unsaturated fatty acids within the matrix rather than cellular lipids or proteins. This is achieved through the strategic selection of fatty acids with multiple double bonds and a low iodine value below 60 g I₂/100 g, ensuring controlled oxidation without rancidity.

- specify polyunsaturated fatty acid content

The polyunsaturated fatty acid content is at least 15% by weight of the lipid matrix and includes linoleic acid and linolenic acid in a ratio of approximately 3:1, mimicking the natural composition of the stratum corneum.

- describe lipophilic solid part

The lipophilic solid part is composed of natural waxes that provide structural integrity to the capsule, maintain shape upon cooling, and prevent aggregation during storage. These waxes are selected for their compatibility with human skin lipids and their ability to form a cohesive film upon application.

- describe lipophilic part of unsaturated fatty acids

The lipophilic portion of the unsaturated fatty acids is embedded within the wax matrix in a manner that exposes their double bonds to the external environment, creating a sacrificial oxidation target that intercepts ROS before they reach viable skin cells.

- describe surfactants and/or suspension agents and/or humectants

The aqueous phase contains non-ionic surfactants such as decyl glucoside to stabilize the emulsion, suspension agents to prevent sedimentation, and humectants such as glycerin or sodium hyaluronate to enhance moisture retention and improve spreadability.

- describe active ingredients

Active ingredients are incorporated both within the lipid matrix and the aqueous phase. Lipophilic actives include retinyl palmitate, while water-soluble actives include sodium ascorbyl phosphate, which remains protected from oxidation by the capsule structure.

- describe water-soluble active ingredients

Water-soluble active ingredients are dissolved in the aqueous phase and are not encapsulated within the lipid core, allowing for immediate release upon contact with the skin surface while the lipid matrix provides sustained, targeted ROS sequestration.

- describe antioxidant agents

Antioxidant agents are present in both phases but function not as direct scavengers, but as stabilizers of the capsule structure and enhancers of the overall oxidative resilience of the formulation.

- describe alternate embodiment of skin care product

An alternate embodiment comprises a lipid matrix with a higher melting point, achieved by increasing the proportion of mimosa wax, enabling the formulation to remain solid at ambient temperatures and be delivered as a balm or solid stick.

- describe matrix composition in alternate embodiment

In the alternate embodiment, the matrix includes a mixture of beeswax and carnauba wax in addition to the primary waxes, resulting in a higher melting temperature of 58°C to 62°C, which enhances stability in hot climates and allows for extended shelf life without refrigeration.

- describe lipid component in alternate embodiment

The lipid component in the alternate embodiment retains the same polyunsaturated fatty acid profile but is combined with saturated triglycerides to increase crystallinity and reduce permeability to moisture.

- describe advantage of melting temperature

The elevated melting temperature provides a more durable film on the skin surface, reduces migration of active ingredients, and enhances occlusive properties without compromising biocompatibility.

- describe lipid component mixture

The lipid component mixture is optimized to balance oxidative reactivity with physical stability, ensuring that the matrix remains solid during storage and application but allows sufficient ROS diffusion to initiate controlled oxidation.

- describe matrix composition with high ROS affinity

The matrix composition exhibits high ROS affinity by virtue of its high concentration of bis-allylic methylene groups in polyunsaturated fatty acids, which are energetically favorable sites for hydrogen abstraction by free radicals. This affinity is quantified by a measurable reduction in ROS concentration in vitro when the matrix is exposed to hydrogen peroxide or menadione-induced oxidative stress.

- define ROS affinity

ROS affinity is defined as the capacity of a lipid matrix to preferentially react with reactive oxygen species over cellular components, as demonstrated by a statistically significant reduction in intracellular ROS levels in human keratinocytes when exposed to the matrix compared to controls.

- describe oxidation stable matrix

The matrix is oxidation-stable during storage due to the controlled ratio of saturated to unsaturated lipids, the absence of pro-oxidative metals, and the use of natural antioxidants such as tocopherols derived from the same plant sources, which do not interfere with the sacrificial oxidation mechanism.

- describe matrix composition with unsaturated fatty acids

The matrix composition contains a minimum of 15% unsaturated fatty acids, with at least 10% linoleic acid and 5% linolenic acid, ensuring sufficient reactivity to intercept ROS while maintaining structural integrity.

- describe antioxidant agent in matrix

An antioxidant agent, such as mixed tocopherols, is incorporated into the lipid matrix at a concentration of 0.1% to 0.5% by weight to prevent premature autoxidation during storage, without interfering with the ROS-sequestering function during application.

- describe excipients in matrix

Excipients include natural emulsifiers such as lecithin, stabilizers such as xanthan gum, and preservatives such as potassium sorbate, all selected for compatibility with the lipid matrix and minimal skin irritation.

- describe active ingredients in matrix

Active ingredients embedded in the lipid matrix include retinyl palmitate, squalane, and phytosterols, which are released gradually upon contact with skin lipids and contribute to barrier repair and cellular renewal.

- describe method for making capsules

The method for making capsules involves heating the solid lipophilic components to a temperature of 55°C under magnetic stirring, adding lipophilic active ingredients until fully dissolved, preparing an aqueous solution containing surfactants and water-soluble actives at 48°C, combining the two phases under high-shear homogenization at 3500 rpm for 10 minutes, passing the resulting emulsion through a 25 μm mesh sieve, and cooling the sieved emulsion to 10°C over a period of 30 minutes to induce solidification of the lipid core.

- describe melting solid lipophilic part

The solid lipophilic part is melted at a temperature not exceeding 60°C to preserve the integrity of heat-sensitive actives and prevent degradation of the unsaturated fatty acids.

- describe solubilizing or dispersing active ingredients

Lipophilic active ingredients are solubilized in the molten lipid phase by continuous stirring for a minimum of 10 minutes until a homogeneous, translucent mixture is achieved.

- describe combining melted lipophilic part with aqueous solution

The melted lipid phase is slowly added to the aqueous solution under continuous agitation to form a water-in-oil emulsion, which is then inverted into an oil-in-water emulsion through high-shear mixing.

- describe screening emulsion through mesh

The emulsion is screened through a stainless steel mesh with a pore size of 25 micrometers to ensure uniform particle size distribution and eliminate aggregates larger than the target diameter.

- describe drying microemulsion

The microemulsion is dried under controlled conditions of temperature and humidity using spray drying or freeze-drying techniques to produce a powder that can be reconstituted into final formulations, or it is used directly in liquid formulations without drying.

- describe alternate method for making capsules

An alternate method involves high-pressure homogenization at 1500 bar for three cycles, followed by ultrasonication for 15 minutes, to produce smaller particles with a mean diameter of 5 to 10 micrometers.

- describe incorporating capsules into serum, cream, or lotion

The microcapsules are incorporated into a base formulation of serum, cream, or lotion at concentrations ranging from 0.1% to 10% by weight, with the final product exhibiting a smooth texture, no visible particles, and excellent spreadability.

- describe packaging skin care product

The skin care product is packaged in opaque, air-tight containers made of high-density polyethylene or aluminum tubes to prevent light exposure and oxidation of the lipid matrix during storage.

- describe ORAC value of capsules

The lipid microcapsules exhibit an ORAC value of at least 1200 μmol TE/g, measured using the standard fluorometric assay, indicating high radical scavenging potential in vitro, although the in vivo mechanism is distinct from conventional antioxidant activity.

- describe process for preparing capsules

The process for preparing capsules is a closed-system, solvent-free, scalable method that requires no organic solvents, high-pressure equipment, or cryogenic conditions, making it suitable for industrial production while maintaining product purity and efficacy.
