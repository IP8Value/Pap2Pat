## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention relates to immunogenic compositions comprising virus-like particles engineered to display epitopes derived from bacterial signaling peptides, particularly those involved in quorum-sensing regulation of virulence in Staphylococcus aureus. More specifically, the invention encompasses virus-like particles derived from bacteriophage coat proteins that are genetically modified to present stabilized analogs of autoinducing peptides, thereby eliciting a potent and specific humoral immune response capable of neutralizing bacterial communication systems. These compositions are designed for use in the prevention and treatment of infections caused by Staphylococcus aureus and other pathogens that utilize similar peptide-based signaling mechanisms to coordinate pathogenic behavior.

## BACKGROUND AND OVERVIEW OF THE INVENTION

- Staphylococcus aureus is a leading cause of skin and soft tissue infections, bloodstream infections, and pneumonia, with methicillin-resistant strains posing a significant and growing threat to global public health. Traditional antibiotic therapies are increasingly ineffective due to widespread resistance, and no vaccine is currently approved for clinical use against this pathogen. A critical driver of S. aureus virulence is the accessory gene regulator (agr) quorum-sensing system, which coordinates the expression of over two hundred virulence factors in response to secreted autoinducing peptides. These peptides, known as AIPs, are small, cyclic molecules that bind to a membrane-bound receptor to trigger a signaling cascade culminating in the production of toxins, proteases, and immune evasion proteins. Among the four known agr types, agr type I isolates are most frequently associated with invasive and community-acquired infections, yet no effective vaccine strategy has been developed to target this dominant variant. Conventional approaches using chemically synthesized AIPs have failed due to the inherent instability of the thiolactone ring essential for biological activity and the poor immunogenicity of small peptides. The present invention overcomes these limitations by leveraging the structural and immunological advantages of bacteriophage-derived virus-like particles to present a stabilized, conformationally constrained analog of AIP1 in a highly multivalent format, thereby inducing high-titer, high-affinity antibodies capable of neutralizing native AIP1 and suppressing agr-mediated virulence in vivo.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- The invention provides virus-like particles derived from bacteriophage coat proteins that are genetically fused to epitopic sequences derived from Staphylococcus aureus autoinducing peptides, particularly AIP1, AIP2, AIP3, and AIP4, and their serine-substituted variants AIP1S, AIP2S, AIP3S, and AIP4S. These virus-like particles are assembled from single-chain coat protein dimers of RNA bacteriophages such as PP7, which self-assemble into stable, non-infectious, icosahedral structures displaying up to 90 copies of the epitopic peptide on their surface. The epitopic peptides are inserted into the AB-loop region of the coat protein, a structurally rigid and immunodominant surface loop that preserves the conformational integrity of the displayed peptide without requiring a native thiolactone bond. The invention further includes nucleic acid constructs encoding these fusion proteins, operatively linked to bacterial or bacteriophage promoters, transcription terminators, antibiotic resistance genes, replication origins, and restriction sites for efficient cloning and expression in host cells such as Escherichia coli. The virus-like particles are used prophylactically to prevent infection by S. aureus and therapeutically to reduce disease severity in established infections. Hybrid virus-like particles displaying multiple epitopes from different agr types are also disclosed, enabling broad-spectrum protection against diverse S. aureus isolates. The invention further encompasses methods for producing these particles, administering them as vaccines, and detecting immune responses through antibody binding assays and functional neutralization tests.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- Molecular biology techniques employed in the practice of this invention include recombinant DNA cloning, site-directed mutagenesis, polymerase chain reaction, protein expression and purification, and immunological assays. Technical terms such as “polynucleotide,” “polypeptide,” “single-chain dimer,” “epitope,” “immunogenic,” and “virus-like particle” are used in their conventional scientific sense, with “polynucleotide” referring to any DNA or RNA sequence, “polypeptide” denoting a chain of amino acids regardless of length, and “single-chain dimer” describing a recombinant fusion protein comprising two coat protein monomers joined by a flexible linker. The term “epitope” refers to a discrete region of a peptide capable of eliciting an immune response, and “immunogenic” describes the ability of a substance to provoke an adaptive immune response. A “virus-like particle” is defined as a non-infectious, self-assembling structure composed of viral structural proteins that mimic the morphology of a native virion but lack genetic material. The AB-loop of the PP7 coat protein is a surface-exposed β-turn region that, when modified, permits the stable display of foreign peptides without disrupting capsid assembly. Amino acid residues are numbered according to standard polypeptide nomenclature, and “valency” refers to the number of epitopic peptides displayed per particle. A “coding sequence” is a nucleic acid sequence encoding a polypeptide, and a “heterologous region” is a nucleotide or amino acid sequence not naturally occurring in the host protein. An “origin of replication” enables autonomous plasmid replication in a host cell, while a “promoter sequence” initiates transcription, and a “transcription termination sequence” signals the end of RNA synthesis. An “expression control sequence” encompasses promoter, ribosome binding site, and terminator elements necessary for gene expression. An “antibiotic resistance gene” confers selectable resistance to an antibiotic, facilitating the identification of transformed cells. “PCR” refers to polymerase chain reaction, and “PCR primer” denotes a short oligonucleotide used to initiate amplification. A “transformed cell” is a host cell containing a recombinant nucleic acid construct. A “signal sequence” directs secretion or localization of a polypeptide, and “degenerate nucleic acid sequences” are codon-optimized variants encoding the same amino acid sequence. An “epitope” is the specific portion of an antigen recognized by an antibody. A “coat protein” is a structural component of a viral capsid, and a “coat polypeptide” is the translated product of a coat protein gene. An “immune response” is the biological reaction elicited by an antigen, and “immunogenic” describes the capacity of a substance to induce such a response. A “vaccine” is a preparation intended to induce protective immunity, and a “virus-like particle of a bacteriophage” is a non-replicating, self-assembling structure formed by bacteriophage coat proteins. The term “operatively linked” means that components are arranged in a functional relationship, such that one element influences the activity of another. “Staphylococcus aureus-induced disorders” encompass skin and soft tissue infections, pneumonia, sepsis, and endocarditis. “Immunogenicity” refers to the ability to provoke an immune response, and “prophylactic efficacy” denotes the capacity to prevent disease onset. A “Staphylococcus aureus epitopic peptide” is a peptide sequence derived from S. aureus virulence regulators that, when presented on a carrier, elicits a neutralizing immune response. “Staphylococcus aureus” is a Gram-positive, facultative anaerobic bacterium responsible for a wide spectrum of human infections.

### Production of Virus-Like Particles

- Virus-like particles are produced by expressing recombinant coat proteins in bacterial hosts such as Escherichia coli, followed by self-assembly into icosahedral structures. The coat proteins are derived from RNA bacteriophages including PP7, MS2, Qβ, and AP205, which share structural homology in their dimeric organization and capsid architecture. The genome of these phages encodes a single coat protein that forms dimers, which in turn assemble into 180-subunit capsids. The PP7 coat protein, when expressed as a single-chain dimer, retains its ability to self-assemble into virus-like particles, even when modified by insertion of foreign sequences into the AB-loop. The AB-loop is a solvent-exposed, structurally constrained region of the coat protein that, when engineered to display epitopes, enhances their immunogenicity by presenting them in a repetitive, high-density array. The AIP1S peptide (YSTSDFIM) is inserted into the second AB-loop of the PP7 single-chain dimer, replacing a native loop sequence without disrupting the overall fold or assembly competence. The resulting fusion protein is expressed in E. coli C41 cells under the control of a T7 promoter, induced with IPTG, and purified by size exclusion chromatography. The purified particles are homogeneous, as confirmed by dynamic light scattering and agarose gel electrophoresis, and display approximately 90 copies of the AIP1S epitope per particle. Truncations or conservative substitutions, such as the C4S mutation, are employed to eliminate disulfide bond formation while preserving the conformational mimicry of native AIP1. The absence of the thiolactone bond does not impair immunogenicity because the constrained loop environment of the PP7 coat protein enforces a cyclic-like conformation, enabling the immune system to recognize and neutralize the native, thiolactone-containing AIP1. The particles are stable, non-infectious, and suitable for storage and administration as vaccines.

## EXAMPLES

- Virus-like particles displaying the AIP1S epitope (PP7-AIP1S) were produced and evaluated as vaccine candidates in murine models of Staphylococcus aureus skin infection. Mice immunized with PP7-AIP1S generated high-titer serum antibodies capable of binding to the AIP1S sequence displayed on the particle surface and cross-reacting with soluble, native AIP1 in competitive ELISA assays. These antibodies did not bind to AIP2, demonstrating specificity for agr type I signaling peptides. Upon challenge with a virulent USA300 MRSA strain, vaccinated mice exhibited significantly reduced abscess size, dermonecrosis, and bacterial burden compared to control groups receiving wild-type PP7 or PBS. The reduction in disease severity correlated with decreased transcription of RNAIII and diminished production of alpha-hemolysin at the infection site, confirming suppression of agr signaling. Cytokine profiling revealed lower levels of pro-inflammatory mediators such as IL-1β, TNFα, and IL-17, consistent with attenuated virulence. These results demonstrate that PP7-AIP1S vaccination induces a protective immune response that neutralizes AIP1 and inhibits agr-regulated pathogenesis without the need for a native thiolactone bond.

### ADDITIONAL EXAMPLES

- The agr operon is a central regulator of virulence in Staphylococcus aureus, and AIP1 is the autoinducing peptide produced by agr type I isolates, which are responsible for the majority of invasive infections. Previous attempts to vaccinate with chemically conjugated AIP1 failed due to instability and poor immunogenicity. The VLP platform overcomes these limitations by presenting AIP1S in a multivalent, conformationally restricted context that mimics the bioactive structure of native AIP1. Production of the PP7-AIP1S vaccine involved cloning the modified AIP1 sequence into the PP7 single-chain dimer expression vector, transforming E. coli, inducing expression, and purifying particles by size exclusion chromatography. Immunization of mice with PP7-AIP1S resulted in robust antibody titers that recognized native AIP1 and conferred protection in a murine skin infection model. Vaccinated animals showed reduced dermonecrosis, lower bacterial loads, and diminished expression of agr-regulated virulence factors. The vaccine’s efficacy was specific to agr type I, as antibodies did not cross-react with AIP2. This approach is extendable to other agr types, including AIP2, AIP3, and AIP4, and their serine-substituted variants. The VLP platform has been successfully used in FDA-approved vaccines for hepatitis B and human papillomavirus, validating its safety and immunogenicity in humans. The flexibility of this system allows for the development of multivalent vaccines targeting multiple agr types simultaneously, offering a powerful tool to combat the diversity of S. aureus strains. Furthermore, the same platform can be adapted to target agr-like systems in other pathogens such as Listeria monocytogenes, Enterococcus faecalis, and Clostridium difficile, which use structurally similar cyclic peptides to regulate virulence. The success of this approach underscores the potential of anti-virulence vaccines as a sustainable alternative to antibiotics in the era of antimicrobial resistance.

## DISCUSSION

- The emergence of multidrug-resistant Staphylococcus aureus has rendered traditional antibiotic therapies increasingly inadequate, necessitating novel strategies that target bacterial pathogenesis rather than viability. The agr quorum-sensing system is a master regulator of virulence in S. aureus, controlling the expression of toxins, immune evasion proteins, and biofilm components. By targeting the signaling peptides that activate this system, it is possible to disarm the pathogen without exerting selective pressure for resistance. The present invention demonstrates that virus-like particles presenting a stabilized analog of AIP1 can elicit high-affinity antibodies that neutralize native AIP1, suppress agr signaling, and protect against infection. This approach circumvents the instability of the thiolactone bond by exploiting the conformational rigidity of the PP7 AB-loop to mimic the bioactive structure of AIP1. Unlike previous attempts using chemically conjugated peptides, this method yields consistent, reproducible, and highly immunogenic particles suitable for large-scale manufacturing. The protective efficacy observed in vivo is attributable to the suppression of RNAIII and alpha-hemolysin, key mediators of dermonecrosis and immune evasion. The ability to extend this platform to other agr types and related pathogens represents a paradigm shift in anti-virulence vaccine design. The FDA approval of multiple VLP-based vaccines confirms the clinical viability of this platform, and its adaptability to diverse epitopes makes it uniquely suited for combating bacterial pathogens that rely on peptide-based communication. The development of a multivalent VLP vaccine targeting all four agr types could provide broad protection against the majority of clinical S. aureus isolates, offering a critical tool in the fight against antibiotic resistance.

## SUMMARY

- The invention demonstrates that virus-like particles displaying stabilized analogs of Staphylococcus aureus autoinducing peptides, particularly AIP1S, elicit a potent and specific antibody response that neutralizes native AIP1, suppresses agr-regulated virulence, and protects against skin and soft tissue infection in a murine model. Vaccination with PP7-AIP1S significantly reduces abscess formation, dermonecrosis, bacterial burden, and inflammatory cytokine production while decreasing expression of RNAIII and alpha-hemolysin. Antibodies induced by the vaccine specifically recognize native AIP1 but not other AIP types, confirming target specificity. The absence of a thiolactone bond in the displayed peptide does not impair immunogenicity or protective efficacy, as the constrained loop structure of the PP7 coat protein enforces a bioactive conformation. The invention further demonstrates the feasibility of extending this platform to other agr types and agr-like systems in other bacterial pathogens. The results establish virus-like particles as a safe, effective, and scalable platform for developing anti-virulence vaccines against antibiotic-resistant bacterial infections.

### Methods

- Animal studies were conducted in accordance with guidelines approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center. Staphylococcus aureus strain USA300 LAC was cultured in tryptic soy broth and stored at −80°C. Virus-like particles were produced by cloning the AIP1S sequence into the PP7 single-chain dimer expression vector under the T7 promoter, transforming E. coli C41 cells, inducing expression with IPTG, and purifying particles via size exclusion chromatography. Mice were immunized intramuscularly with 10 µg of VLPs or control at two-week intervals. Serum was collected at two, four, and eight weeks post-immunization. Binding of antibodies to VLPs was assessed by ELISA using PP7-AIP1S-coated plates and depleted serum to eliminate non-specific reactivity. Competition assays were performed using synthetic AIP1 and AIP2 peptides. Mice were challenged subcutaneously with 4×10⁷ CFU of LAC, and disease progression was monitored by measurement of abscess area, dermonecrosis, weight loss, and bacterial burden. Cytokine levels were quantified using a multiplex bead-based assay. RNAIII expression was measured by quantitative RT-PCR, and alpha-hemolysin levels were determined by Western blot. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software with appropriate non-parametric tests. VLP purity and homogeneity were confirmed by SDS-PAGE, agarose gel electrophoresis, and dynamic light scattering.
