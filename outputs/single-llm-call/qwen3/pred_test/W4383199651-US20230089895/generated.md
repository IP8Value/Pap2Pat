## TECHNICAL FIELD

- define technical field of resource reservation

The present invention relates to the technical field of programmatic advertising systems, specifically to methods and apparatuses for real-time resource reservation in digital advertising auctions. More particularly, the invention concerns the dynamic allocation of advertising budget and auction participation rights within demand-side platforms operating in high-frequency, second-price auction environments. The invention addresses the challenge of optimizing bid submission decisions under conditions of uncertain user response predictions and evolving budget constraints, where the allocation of finite financial resources across a sequence of time-sensitive advertising opportunities must be governed by adaptive risk-sensitive decision policies. This domain encompasses the intersection of machine learning, stochastic optimization, and economic modeling in the context of online advertising ecosystems, wherein computational systems must simultaneously account for statistical uncertainty in predictive models, temporal budget depletion dynamics, and competitive market conditions to maximize long-term performance metrics such as total clicks or conversion volume.

## BACKGROUND

- motivate real-time resource allocation

Real-time resource allocation in digital advertising is driven by the necessity to efficiently deploy advertiser budgets across millions of daily user impressions, each representing a fleeting opportunity to deliver targeted advertisements. The economic value of each impression is inherently uncertain, as it depends on unpredictable user behavior such as clicks, conversions, or engagement events. Without precise and adaptive allocation strategies, advertisers risk either overspending on low-value impressions or underspending on high-potential opportunities, leading to suboptimal return on investment. The dynamic nature of online advertising markets demands that resource allocation decisions be made within milliseconds, requiring systems that can process vast streams of bid requests, evaluate the expected value of each impression, and determine optimal bid prices in real time. The efficiency and profitability of demand-side platforms are therefore critically dependent on their ability to allocate budget not merely based on point estimates of user response, but in a manner that anticipates future constraints and adapts to the stochastic evolution of market conditions.

- describe limitations of current bidding platforms

Current bidding platforms predominantly rely on deterministic models that output single-point estimates of user response metrics, such as click-through rate or conversion probability, without quantifying the inherent uncertainty in these predictions. These models treat each bid request as an independent event, ignoring the sequential and budget-constrained nature of advertising campaigns. As a result, they fail to account for the interdependence between past bidding decisions and future opportunities, leading to premature budget exhaustion or conservative underbidding in later stages of a campaign. Furthermore, existing systems do not incorporate the concept of risk tendency—the strategic inclination of a bidder to either conserve or expend remaining budget based on its current resource state—thereby treating all bidders as risk-neutral entities. This oversight results in suboptimal bid pricing that does not reflect the true economic trade-offs between immediate gains and long-term campaign sustainability. Consequently, these platforms are unable to adaptively balance the competing objectives of maximizing short-term returns while preserving budget for future high-value opportunities, a deficiency that significantly limits their performance in competitive and volatile advertising markets.

- identify need for improvement

There exists a critical need for an improved bidding framework that integrates predictive uncertainty with dynamic risk-sensitive decision-making to enable demand-side platforms to operate optimally under budget constraints. Such a framework must move beyond static, point-estimate-based bidding by explicitly modeling the variance of user response predictions and correlating this uncertainty with the bidder’s evolving resource state—namely, the remaining number of auctions and the remaining budget. It must further formalize a risk tendency function that captures the rational behavioral shift of a bidder from aggressive to conservative spending as its budget depletes relative to its remaining auction volume. The absence of such a unified model has resulted in persistent inefficiencies in ad spend utilization, reduced campaign performance, and an inability to generalize across diverse market conditions. A solution that simultaneously accounts for prediction uncertainty, budget dynamics, and risk preference is therefore essential to achieving optimal, adaptive, and economically rational bid optimization in real-time advertising ecosystems.

## SUMMARY

- summarize service-level agreement monitoring

The invention provides a system and method for monitoring and enforcing service-level agreements between advertisers and demand-side platforms by dynamically adjusting bid strategies to align with agreed-upon key performance indicators, such as total clicks or conversion volume, while respecting budgetary constraints. By incorporating predictive uncertainty and risk tendency into the bid determination process, the system ensures that the platform’s bidding behavior remains consistent with the advertiser’s performance expectations over the entire duration of a campaign, rather than optimizing for isolated impressions. This alignment is maintained through continuous state evaluation and bid adjustment, ensuring that the platform neither underdelivers due to excessive caution nor overspends due to overconfidence in point estimates, thereby upholding the integrity of the service-level agreement throughout the campaign lifecycle.

- describe method of operating demand side platform

The method of operating a demand-side platform involves continuously monitoring the platform’s current state, which is defined by the remaining number of auctions in the current campaign episode and the remaining budget allocated for that episode. Upon receiving a bid request from an ad exchange, the platform first calculates a predicted user response metric, such as a click-through rate, along with its associated uncertainty derived from a probabilistic model. The platform then determines a risk tendency value based on the current state, which reflects the platform’s strategic inclination to bid aggressively or conservatively. This risk tendency is combined with the predicted response and its uncertainty to compute an adjusted value for the ad impression. The adjusted value serves as the basis for determining an optimal bid price that maximizes the expected cumulative performance over the remaining auctions, subject to the budget constraint. The determined bid price is then transmitted to the ad exchange, and upon receipt of the auction result, the platform updates its current state to reflect the outcome, thereby enabling continuous, adaptive decision-making throughout the campaign.

- determine current state of DSP

The current state of the demand-side platform is determined by two critical parameters: the number of remaining auctions in the current campaign episode and the remaining budget available for allocation across those auctions. These parameters define the platform’s resource context at any given moment and serve as the foundation for all subsequent decision-making. The state is updated after each auction outcome, whether the platform wins or loses the bid, and is used to compute the risk tendency, adjust the perceived value of the upcoming impression, and determine the optimal bid price. The state is not influenced by the specific features of the individual bid request, as the risk profile of the platform is a function of its overall resource position rather than the characteristics of any single impression.

- receive bid request

The demand-side platform receives a bid request from an ad exchange, which contains metadata describing the user, the advertising inventory, and contextual information such as device type, location, time of day, and browsing behavior. This bid request initiates the platform’s decision-making pipeline and triggers the computation of the predicted user response metric and its associated uncertainty. The bid request is processed in real time, typically within a few milliseconds, to ensure compliance with the latency constraints of the real-time bidding ecosystem. Upon receipt, the platform immediately begins evaluating the impression’s potential value in the context of its current state and historical performance patterns.

- determine uncertainty of predicted user response

The uncertainty of the predicted user response is determined using a probabilistic modeling framework that estimates not only the expected value of the response metric—such as click-through rate—but also the variance or standard deviation of that prediction. This is achieved by treating model parameters as random variables with associated posterior distributions, allowing the system to quantify the confidence in its prediction based on the historical frequency and reliability of similar features. The resulting standard deviation reflects the degree of data sparsity, feature ambiguity, or model instability associated with the specific bid request, providing a measure of predictive uncertainty that is integrated into the subsequent bid calculation.

- determine risk tendency value

The risk tendency value is determined as a function of the current state of the demand-side platform, specifically the ratio of remaining budget to remaining auctions. This value encodes the platform’s strategic inclination toward risk-taking or risk-aversion, and is computed using a non-linear function that ensures the tendency is negative when the budget is insufficient relative to the number of remaining auctions, and positive when the budget is abundant. The function is designed to be monotonic with respect to both budget and auction count, and to saturate at extreme resource conditions, ensuring that the platform behaves rationally under all possible state configurations.

- determine adjusted value of advertisement impressions

The adjusted value of the advertisement impression is determined by combining the mean predicted user response with the product of the uncertainty and the risk tendency value. This linear combination effectively modifies the base value of the impression to reflect not only its expected performance but also the platform’s strategic posture and the reliability of the prediction. The adjusted value serves as a risk-aware proxy for the true economic value of the impression, enabling the platform to make bid decisions that are calibrated to both statistical confidence and resource constraints.

- determine bid price

The bid price is determined by applying a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that maps the adjusted value of the impression, along with the current state, to an optimal bid amount that maximizes the expected cumulative reward over the remaining auctions. The algorithm computes a cumulative reward function that accounts for the probability of winning future auctions, the cost of current bids, and the expected value of remaining impressions. The bid price is selected as the highest value that satisfies the condition that the marginal gain from increasing the bid does not exceed the expected loss in future opportunities, ensuring that the platform’s spending remains aligned with long-term campaign objectives.

- transmit bid price

The determined bid price is transmitted to the ad exchange within the required latency window, typically less than one hundred milliseconds, to participate in the real-time auction. The transmission is performed via a secure, standardized protocol that ensures the bid is received and processed by the auction mechanism without delay or corruption. The bid is submitted as a single numerical value, with no additional metadata, to maintain compatibility with existing auction infrastructure.

- receive auction result

The demand-side platform receives the auction result from the ad exchange, which indicates whether the platform won or lost the bid and, in the case of a win, the actual price paid, which is the second-highest bid in the auction. This result is used to update the platform’s state and to compute the immediate reward, which is the user response metric (e.g., click or conversion) if the impression was won, or zero otherwise. The auction result is processed in real time to ensure continuous state updates and to inform subsequent bidding decisions.

- update current state of DSP

The current state of the demand-side platform is updated by decrementing the remaining auction count by one and, if the platform won the bid, reducing the remaining budget by the actual price paid. This updated state is then stored in memory and used as the input for the next bid decision cycle. The state update is performed atomically and without delay to ensure consistency and to prevent any temporal mismatch between the platform’s internal representation and the actual market conditions.

- describe DSP embodiment

The demand-side platform embodiment comprises a high-performance computing system equipped with a main processor, memory, persistent storage, and network communication interfaces. The system is configured to execute software modules that implement the bid optimization algorithm, including modules for user response prediction, uncertainty estimation, risk tendency calculation, and bid price determination. The system operates in a distributed environment, receiving bid requests from multiple ad exchanges and transmitting bids to a centralized auction server. The platform is designed to handle thousands of bid requests per second with sub-millisecond latency, ensuring real-time responsiveness in high-volume advertising markets.

- describe non-transitory computer-readable medium

The invention further encompasses a non-transitory computer-readable medium storing executable instructions that, when executed by a processor, cause the processor to perform the method of operating a demand-side platform as described herein. The medium may include solid-state drives, hard disk drives, optical storage devices, or other persistent storage technologies capable of retaining data in the absence of power. The instructions are organized into modules corresponding to each step of the bid optimization process, and are structured to enable the processor to receive bid requests, compute adjusted impression values, determine optimal bid prices, and update platform state in real time, thereby enabling autonomous, risk-aware bidding without human intervention.
