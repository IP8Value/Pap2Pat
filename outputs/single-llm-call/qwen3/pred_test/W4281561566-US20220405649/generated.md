## BACKGROUND

- introduce decision support systems  
Decision support systems have become indispensable across a broad spectrum of industries, including finance, healthcare, telecommunications, and public safety, where the need to extract actionable insights from complex, high-dimensional data is paramount. These systems are typically grounded in analytical models trained on historical records to classify outcomes, predict behaviors, or estimate risks—such as credit default, patient readmission, fraudulent transactions, or customer attrition. The efficacy of such systems hinges on their capacity to discern intricate, non-linear relationships among input features, often requiring models capable of generalizing beyond the training data while maintaining robustness under distributional shifts. Conventional approaches rely heavily on classical machine learning techniques, including logistic regression, random forests, and gradient-boosted decision trees, which, despite their widespread adoption, are frequently constrained by the dimensionality and structure of the feature space they operate within. As datasets grow in complexity and volume, the limitations of classical models in capturing latent patterns become increasingly apparent, prompting the exploration of alternative computational paradigms that offer enhanced representational power.

- motivate quantum machine learning models  
Quantum machine learning represents a promising frontier in this pursuit, leveraging the principles of quantum mechanics—such as superposition, entanglement, and interference—to encode and manipulate data in high-dimensional Hilbert spaces that are intractable for classical computation. By mapping classical feature vectors into quantum states via parameterized unitary transformations known as feature maps, quantum support vector machines and related kernel-based methods can implicitly compute similarity measures over exponentially large feature spaces, potentially yielding decision boundaries that are unattainable through classical means. This theoretical advantage has motivated significant research into quantum algorithms for classification, regression, and clustering, particularly in the context of noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices. Unlike classical models that are limited by the explicit construction of feature expansions, quantum models exploit the intrinsic geometry of quantum state space to implicitly capture complex interactions, offering a pathway to improved generalization and reduced overfitting. Recent experimental implementations on superconducting and trapped-ion platforms have demonstrated the feasibility of executing quantum kernel functions, reinforcing the potential for quantum-enhanced decision support in real-world applications.

- limitations of quantum machine learning models  
Despite these theoretical advantages, practical implementations of quantum machine learning models have encountered substantial challenges that hinder their widespread adoption. A primary limitation lies in the sensitivity of quantum kernel performance to the choice of feature map, which remains largely heuristic and often derived from physical analogies such as Ising Hamiltonians, rather than being systematically optimized for the data at hand. Moreover, the computational cost of evaluating quantum kernels on current hardware is prohibitive, and noise-induced errors significantly degrade model fidelity. Additionally, most existing quantum machine learning frameworks lack automated procedures for model selection, hyperparameter tuning, or ensemble construction, forcing users to rely on domain expertise and manual trial-and-error. Furthermore, while some studies have demonstrated marginal performance gains over classical baselines, few have established consistent, statistically significant superiority across diverse datasets. The absence of a principled mechanism to iteratively refine and combine quantum learners—akin to boosting in classical machine learning—has left a critical gap in the ability to construct adaptive, high-performance quantum decision systems. Without such mechanisms, the promise of quantum advantage remains unrealized in practical, scalable applications.

## SUMMARY

- introduce ensemble generation  
The present invention introduces a novel method for generating an ensemble of quantum kernel-based learners through an automated, iterative boosting procedure that dynamically selects, weights, and combines distinct quantum classifiers to enhance predictive performance. Unlike conventional approaches that rely on fixed feature maps or weak classical learners, this system constructs an ensemble by iteratively identifying the most effective quantum kernel configuration from a broad parameterized space, excluding previously selected configurations to encourage exploration of diverse decision boundaries. Each iteration produces a quantum support vector machine trained on a modified distribution of training samples, with weights adjusted to emphasize misclassified instances. The resulting ensemble integrates these learners through a weighted voting mechanism, enabling the system to adaptively learn complex, non-linear decision surfaces that no single quantum model could capture alone.

- describe system embodiment  
The system embodiment comprises a computer-implemented architecture that integrates classical processing units with quantum computational resources, enabling seamless orchestration of data preprocessing, quantum kernel evaluation, performance assessment, and ensemble aggregation. The system includes a central control processor that coordinates the execution of a boosting algorithm across multiple quantum kernel-based learners, each instantiated using a distinct combination of feature map topology, Pauli rotation factors, and regularization parameters. The architecture is designed to interface with both classical data sources and quantum hardware platforms, allowing for the execution of quantum circuits on superconducting, trapped-ion, or photonic qubit systems while maintaining full control over sample weighting, kernel selection, and termination criteria. Memory components store training and validation datasets, kernel parameters, performance metrics, and learner weights, while a communications interface facilitates secure data transfer between classical and quantum subsystems.

- advantage of system embodiment  
The key advantage of this system embodiment lies in its ability to automate the discovery of high-performing quantum kernel architectures without requiring expert knowledge of quantum circuit design or physics-based heuristics. By systematically exploring a combinatorial space of feature maps and hyperparameters at each boosting iteration, the system avoids the pitfalls of static or hand-tuned models, instead evolving an ensemble that is empirically optimized for the dataset’s underlying structure. This results in significantly improved classification accuracy, particularly on datasets with complex, non-linear separability, while simultaneously reducing the risk of overfitting through iterative pruning and sample reweighting. The system’s modularity also enables compatibility with a wide range of quantum hardware, ensuring future-proof scalability as quantum technologies advance.

- describe computer-implemented method  
The computer-implemented method begins by initializing a training dataset with uniform sample weights and defining a parameterized grid of quantum feature maps, including variations in Pauli operator sequences, rotation angles, and regularization constants. The method then iteratively executes a boosting procedure: for each iteration, it performs a grid search over the available quantum kernel configurations using a validation subset, selects the learner with the lowest classification error, computes a weight for the learner based on its performance, updates the weights of misclassified training samples, excludes the selected feature map from future iterations to promote diversity, and repeats until a termination condition is met. Termination occurs upon achieving perfect classification, reaching a maximum number of learners, or when the best-performing learner exhibits performance no better than random guessing. The final model is formed by aggregating the selected learners via a weighted majority vote.

- advantage of computer-implemented method  
The advantage of this method resides in its capacity to transform a single, potentially underperforming quantum kernel into a robust, adaptive ensemble capable of outperforming both classical and single-quantum counterparts. By dynamically adjusting sample weights and constraining feature map reuse, the method ensures that each added learner contributes unique information to the ensemble, preventing redundancy and enhancing generalization. This structured exploration of the quantum feature space enables the discovery of novel kernel architectures that may not be intuitively obvious, thereby expanding the frontier of what quantum machine learning can achieve in practice.

- describe computer program product  
The computer program product comprises non-transitory machine-readable storage media encoded with instructions that, when executed by one or more processors, cause the system to perform the aforementioned computer-implemented method. The program product includes modules for data preprocessing, quantum circuit generation, kernel matrix computation, performance evaluation, sample reweighting, learner selection, and ensemble aggregation, all integrated into a unified software framework compatible with standard quantum computing libraries and classical machine learning toolkits.

- advantage of computer program product  
The advantage of this program product is its plug-and-play compatibility with existing quantum computing infrastructures, enabling researchers and practitioners to deploy quantum-enhanced decision support systems without requiring deep expertise in quantum hardware or algorithm design. The automation of model selection, hyperparameter tuning, and ensemble construction significantly reduces development time and increases reproducibility, making quantum machine learning accessible to a broader audience while ensuring optimal performance across diverse datasets.

- describe training and performance metric  
Training is conducted using a stratified split of the input dataset into training, validation, and testing subsets, with the validation set employed exclusively for selecting the optimal quantum kernel at each boosting iteration. The performance metric is defined as the classification accuracy on the validation set, with additional monitoring of error rates, learner weights, and ensemble diversity to detect overfitting or stagnation. The system computes a performance metric for each candidate quantum kernel, including both training and validation accuracy, and uses these metrics to guide the selection and weighting of learners.

- advantage of modified plurality of quantum kernels  
The advantage of the modified plurality of quantum kernels lies in its ability to enforce diversity across the ensemble by systematically excluding previously selected feature maps, thereby compelling the algorithm to explore new regions of the quantum Hilbert space. This prevents the ensemble from converging to a narrow set of similar decision boundaries and instead promotes the discovery of complementary classifiers that collectively capture a richer representation of the data. The resulting ensemble exhibits superior generalization, higher robustness to noise, and greater resilience to dataset-specific biases than any single kernel or unmodified ensemble.
