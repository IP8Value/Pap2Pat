# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- motivate vaccines  
Vaccines represent one of the most effective medical interventions for preventing infectious diseases and reducing global morbidity and mortality. By stimulating the body’s adaptive immune system to recognize and respond to specific pathogens, vaccines confer long-lasting protection without inducing disease. The success of vaccines against polio, measles, smallpox, and more recently influenza and SARS-CoV-2 underscores their critical role in public health. However, traditional parenteral vaccination strategies, while effective at inducing systemic immunity, often fail to generate robust mucosal immune responses at the primary sites of pathogen entry, such as the respiratory, gastrointestinal, and urogenital tracts. For pathogens that initiate infection via mucosal surfaces, including influenza, norovirus, rotavirus, and many bacterial agents, the absence of localized immunity can limit the ability of vaccines to prevent initial colonization and transmission. This limitation has driven the pursuit of alternative delivery platforms capable of engaging mucosal immune tissues directly, thereby eliciting both systemic and mucosal protective immunity in a single administration. Oral immunization, in particular, offers a non-invasive, cost-effective, and scalable approach to achieving this goal, especially in resource-limited settings where needle-based delivery is impractical or culturally unacceptable.

- limitations of current vaccine delivery methods  
Despite the theoretical advantages of oral vaccination, conventional oral vaccine formulations have faced significant challenges in clinical translation. Many live-attenuated or subunit vaccines are degraded by gastric acid, proteolytic enzymes, and bile salts before reaching the intestinal immune inductive sites. Even when formulations survive transit through the upper gastrointestinal tract, they often release their contents in the jejunum or duodenum, regions where the density of organized lymphoid tissue—such as Peyer’s patches—is relatively sparse compared to the ileum. Furthermore, the lack of precise spatial control over the site of antigen release results in inconsistent immune induction, variable responder rates, and diminished magnitude of mucosal antibody responses. Existing enteric coatings, while designed to resist gastric degradation, frequently disintegrate unpredictably due to inter-individual variations in gastric emptying, intestinal motility, and luminal pH. Additionally, many oral vaccine candidates require multiple doses to achieve adequate immunogenicity, increasing logistical complexity and reducing compliance. The absence of reliable methods to confirm delivery to the target intestinal segment further complicates dose optimization and clinical evaluation. As a result, few oral vaccines have achieved efficacy comparable to injectable counterparts, particularly for pathogens requiring strong secretory IgA responses. There remains a critical unmet need for a delivery system that ensures targeted, reproducible, and verifiable release of immunogenic agents within the ileum, where the highest concentration of gut-associated lymphoid tissue exists in humans.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce immunogenic compositions  
The present invention provides novel immunogenic compositions designed for targeted delivery to the human ileum, enabling the induction of potent systemic and mucosal immune responses following oral administration. These compositions comprise an immunogenic biological agent encapsulated within a controlled-release delivery system that ensures disintegration exclusively within the distal small intestine, thereby maximizing engagement with the dense network of Peyer’s patches and isolated lymphoid follicles that characterize the ileal mucosa. The resulting immune response is characterized by the generation of antigen-specific antibody-secreting cells, elevated serum neutralizing antibodies, and the expansion of mucosal-homing B cell populations expressing the α4β7 integrin and CCR9 chemokine receptor, which direct trafficking to intestinal and other mucosal effector sites.

- define immunogenic biological agent  
An immunogenic biological agent, as used herein, refers to any molecule or structure capable of eliciting an adaptive immune response in a subject when introduced into the gastrointestinal tract. Such agents include polypeptides, proteins, peptides, nucleic acids, carbohydrates, lipids, or combinations thereof, derived from pathogens, tumor cells, or synthetic sources. These agents may be administered in their native form, as recombinant constructs, or as components of viral vectors, and must be capable of being processed by antigen-presenting cells to initiate T and B cell activation.

- describe expression vector  
In certain embodiments, the immunogenic biological agent is encoded by a nucleic acid sequence delivered via an expression vector, such as a replication-incompetent adenoviral vector. The vector is engineered to express a heterologous antigen under the control of a transcriptional regulatory sequence that ensures high-level expression upon cellular uptake in the ileal epithelium or underlying lymphoid tissue. The adenoviral vector is selected for its ability to transduce dendritic cells and M cells within Peyer’s patches, thereby promoting efficient antigen presentation and T cell priming.

- specify promoters and dsRNA  
The expression vector contains a promoter operably linked to the nucleic acid encoding the immunogenic biological agent, wherein the promoter is selected from the group consisting of cytomegalovirus immediate early promoter, elongation factor-1α promoter, and simian virus 40 early promoter. In some embodiments, the vector further comprises double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) as an immunostimulatory adjuvant, which activates Toll-like receptor 3 and retinoic acid-inducible gene I (RIG-I)-like receptor pathways to enhance dendritic cell maturation and cytokine production.

- introduce adjuvants  
The immunogenic composition may further include one or more adjuvants selected from the group consisting of lipid-based adjuvants, Toll-like receptor agonists, cytokines, and synthetic analogs thereof. In preferred embodiments, the adjuvant is a TLR-3 agonist such as poly(I:C), or a lipid A derivative, which synergizes with the viral vector to amplify the magnitude and durability of the immune response.

- describe delivery to ileum  
The immunogenic composition is formulated for targeted delivery to the ileum, the terminal segment of the small intestine, where the highest density of gut-associated lymphoid tissue is found in humans. This targeted delivery ensures that the immunogenic agent is released in proximity to Peyer’s patches and mesenteric lymph nodes, maximizing the efficiency of antigen uptake and immune priming.

- specify enteric coating  
The composition is enclosed within an enteric coating that resists disintegration in the acidic environment of the stomach and the proximal small intestine but disintegrates upon exposure to the near-neutral pH of the ileum. The coating is composed of pH-sensitive polymers that remain intact until the luminal pH rises above 6.5, a condition typically achieved only in the distal ileum.

- describe pH-dependent disintegration  
The enteric coating is designed to disintegrate in a pH-dependent manner, with a disintegration time of less than 30 minutes following exposure to a pH of 6.8 to 7.4, and greater than 120 minutes at pH below 5.5. This ensures that release occurs only after the capsule has traversed the stomach and jejunum, minimizing premature degradation and maximizing ileal targeting.

- list enteric coating materials  
The enteric coating comprises one or more polymers selected from the group consisting of hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate, cellulose acetate phthalate, methacrylic acid–ethyl acrylate copolymer, Eudragit L100, Eudragit S100, and shellac. In preferred embodiments, the coating is a combination of two or more polymers with differing pH sensitivities to ensure robust and consistent disintegration across diverse physiological conditions.

- describe radio-controlled capsule  
In alternative embodiments, the immunogenic composition is contained within a radio-controlled capsule capable of receiving an external radiofrequency signal to trigger the release of its contents at a predetermined location within the gastrointestinal tract. The capsule includes a radio receiver, a microprocessor, and a pressure- or time-activated release mechanism, allowing precise spatial and temporal control over antigen delivery.

- specify enteric coating thickness  
The enteric coating has a thickness ranging from 50 to 500 micrometers, with a preferred range of 150 to 300 micrometers, to balance mechanical integrity during transit with timely disintegration upon reaching the target pH environment.

- describe tablet or capsule form  
The immunogenic composition is formulated as a solid dosage form, selected from the group consisting of tablets, capsules, or hard gelatin shells, each containing a single dose of the immunogenic biological agent. The dosage form is designed for oral ingestion and is stable under ambient storage conditions for at least 12 months.

- describe microparticles  
In certain embodiments, the immunogenic biological agent is encapsulated within microparticles composed of biodegradable polymers such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) or chitosan, which protect the agent from enzymatic degradation and facilitate uptake by M cells in the ileal epithelium.

- introduce method of delivery  
The method of delivery involves oral administration of the immunogenic composition to a human subject, followed by targeted release of the agent within the ileum, either through pH-dependent disintegration of an enteric coating or via external radiofrequency triggering of a radio-controlled capsule.

- specify immune response  
The method induces a systemic and mucosal immune response characterized by the presence of antigen-specific IgG and IgA antibody-secreting cells in peripheral blood, elevated serum neutralizing antibody titers, and the expansion of CD19+CD27+α4β7+CCR9+ B cells, indicative of mucosal homing potential.

- compare immune response to non-directed delivery  
Compared to non-targeted oral delivery, which results in inconsistent immune induction and lower responder rates, the targeted delivery to the ileum produces a significantly higher frequency of subjects exhibiting a two-fold or greater increase in neutralizing antibody titers and a greater magnitude of antigen-specific ASCs.

- describe increased immune response  
The targeted delivery to the ileum results in a greater than 50% increase in geometric mean titers of neutralizing antibodies and a greater than 70% increase in the number of antigen-specific IgA-secreting cells per million peripheral blood mononuclear cells, as compared to delivery to the jejunum or non-targeted formulations.

- specify immunization  
The method is used for immunization against infectious agents, including influenza virus, norovirus, rotavirus, respiratory syncytial virus, and bacterial pathogens such as Salmonella and Shigella, as well as for the induction of anti-tumor immunity in patients with epithelial cancers.
