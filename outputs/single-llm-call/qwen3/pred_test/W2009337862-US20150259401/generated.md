# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to antibodies with increased half-life

The present invention relates to novel immunoglobulin variants engineered for extended in vivo persistence in ferrets, particularly those comprising modified ferret immunoglobulin G (IgG) Fc regions that exhibit enhanced binding affinity for the ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). These variants are designed to overcome the historically short serum half-life observed when human or humanized antibodies are administered to ferrets, a critical limitation that has impeded their utility in preclinical studies of infectious diseases, immune modulation, and therapeutic antibody efficacy. The invention specifically encompasses isolated antibodies and immunoglobulin constructs in which the Fc region is derived from ferret IgG and contains one or more amino acid substitutions at position 252, or combinations thereof, that significantly enhance the interaction between the antibody and ferret FcRn under endosomal pH conditions. This modification results in a marked increase in the recycling efficiency of the antibody through the FcRn-mediated salvage pathway, thereby prolonging its circulation time in the ferret model system. The invention further includes chimeric, ferretized, and veneered antibodies in which the variable domains originate from non-ferret sources, such as human or humanized sequences, while the constant regions are derived from ferret IgG and contain the specified Fc modifications. These constructs are particularly useful for evaluating therapeutic antibodies targeting pathogens such as respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), influenza, or other human-relevant infectious agents, where prolonged exposure in ferrets is essential for assessing pharmacodynamic effects, dosing regimens, and protective efficacy without confounding immune responses or frequent dosing requirements.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce ferrets as laboratory model
- describe limitations of ferret model
- identify need for novel IgG variants

Ferrets have long served as a physiologically relevant animal model for studying human respiratory infections, including influenza and respiratory syncytial virus, due to their similar airway anatomy, receptor expression profiles, and clinical manifestations of disease. Their susceptibility to human-adapted strains of these pathogens, coupled with their ability to mount immune responses comparable to those observed in humans, has made them indispensable for evaluating vaccines, antiviral therapeutics, and monoclonal antibody interventions. However, a persistent and significant limitation in the use of ferrets for antibody-based studies has been the unusually rapid clearance of human or humanized immunoglobulins from their circulation. This phenomenon, often attributed to suboptimal binding between the human Fc region and the ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), results in terminal half-lives as short as 30 hours, necessitating frequent dosing regimens that complicate experimental design, increase variability, and elevate the risk of inducing anti-drug antibody responses. While chimeric antibodies composed of human variable regions and murine or cynomolgus monkey constant regions have been successfully employed in other species to mitigate immunogenicity and extend half-life, analogous constructs in ferrets have historically failed to achieve comparable persistence due to the absence of optimized Fc sequences tailored to the ferret FcRn interface. Furthermore, prior attempts to apply known human Fc engineering strategies—such as the YTE or LS mutations—to ferret models yielded inconsistent results, suggesting that the structural determinants governing FcRn binding are species-specific and cannot be reliably extrapolated from other mammals. Consequently, there exists a critical and unmet need for engineered ferret IgG Fc variants that are specifically optimized to bind ferret FcRn with high affinity at acidic pH while maintaining efficient release at neutral pH, thereby enabling prolonged serum exposure, reduced dosing frequency, and enhanced reliability in preclinical studies. The present invention fulfills this need by identifying and validating specific amino acid substitutions within the ferret Fc region that dramatically enhance its interaction with ferret FcRn, thereby transforming the utility of ferrets as a model for human antibody therapeutics.

The invention discloses a modified ferret immunoglobulin G Fc region that exhibits substantially increased binding affinity for the ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) at pH 6.0, resulting in a prolonged serum half-life when incorporated into therapeutic or diagnostic antibodies administered to ferrets. This modification is achieved through the introduction of specific amino acid substitutions at residue 252 of the ferret Fc region, which corresponds to the EU numbering system used in human immunoglobulins. In particular, substitution of the native serine at position 252 with either tyrosine (S252Y) or methionine (S252M) has been demonstrated to enhance FcRn binding by up to 24-fold compared to the wild-type ferret Fc, leading to terminal half-lives exceeding 200 hours in vivo. These substitutions are incorporated into isolated antibodies comprising a ferret-derived Fc region, wherein the variable domains may originate from human, humanized, or other non-ferret sources, thereby enabling the generation of chimeric, ferretized, or veneered antibody constructs that retain full antigen-binding functionality while achieving extended persistence in ferrets. The invention further encompasses isolated immunoglobulin constructs comprising an antibody fragment, such as Fab, scFv, or F(ab′)2, fused to the modified ferret Fc region, thereby conferring the half-life-extending properties of the engineered Fc to smaller, non-full-length antibody formats. In addition, the invention includes immunoglobulin fusion proteins in which the modified ferret Fc region is operably linked to a bioactive molecule, such as a cytokine, enzyme, toxin, or peptide, thereby extending the circulatory half-life of such molecules in ferret models. The YTE triple mutation (S254T/T256E), previously shown to enhance half-life in primates, was tested in combination with S252Y but did not confer additional benefit in ferrets, indicating that the S252Y substitution alone is sufficient to maximize FcRn engagement in this species. The invention further provides for antibodies that specifically bind to the F glycoprotein of respiratory syncytial virus, wherein the antibody comprises a human or humanized variable region and a modified ferret Fc region containing the S252Y or S252M substitution, enabling prolonged therapeutic exposure in ferret models of RSV infection. The resulting antibodies and immunoglobulin constructs demonstrate improved pharmacokinetic consistency, reduced inter-animal variability, and minimal induction of anti-drug antibodies, thereby facilitating more reliable, reproducible, and clinically predictive preclinical studies.

The term “corresponding” unmodified antibody refers to an antibody that is identical in all respects to a modified antibody except for the absence of the specified amino acid substitutions in the Fc region. An epitope is a discrete region of an antigen that is recognized and bound by an antibody, typically comprising a sequence of amino acids or a conformational structure. An antibody is a glycoprotein composed of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, connected by disulfide bonds, capable of binding specifically to a target antigen. The immunoglobulin or antibody molecule includes both full-length immunoglobulins and fragments thereof, and may be of any isotype, including IgG, IgM, IgA, IgE, or IgD, with IgG being preferred for its long half-life and effector functions. The constant domains refer to the regions of the heavy and light chains that are conserved within a species and are responsible for effector functions and receptor interactions. The Fc region is the C-terminal portion of the heavy chain, comprising the CH2 and CH3 domains and, in some cases, a portion of the hinge region, and is the site of interaction with FcRn and other Fc receptors. The CH2 and CH3 domains are structurally distinct immunoglobulin folds that mediate binding to FcRn and other immune effector molecules. The hinge region is the flexible segment connecting the Fab and Fc portions of the antibody, contributing to antigen-binding geometry and proteolytic stability. The Fab fragment is the antigen-binding portion of the antibody, comprising the variable domains of the heavy and light chains and the first constant domain of the heavy chain. The scFv is a single-chain variable fragment comprising the variable heavy and variable light domains linked by a peptide spacer. An isolated biomolecule is one that has been separated from its natural environment, including other cellular components, and is substantially free of contaminants. A substantially purified biomolecule is one that is at least 70% pure by weight, as determined by SDS-PAGE, HPLC, or other analytical methods. An immunoglobulin fusion protein is a chimeric molecule comprising an immunoglobulin domain fused to a non-immunoglobulin polypeptide, such as a bioactive molecule, enzyme, or reporter protein. In vivo half-life is the time required for the concentration of a substance in the bloodstream to decrease by one-half following administration. Increased half-life refers to a prolongation of at least two-fold compared to the corresponding unmodified antibody in the same species under identical conditions. Recombinant refers to a molecule or organism produced through genetic engineering techniques involving the manipulation of nucleic acids. Specifically binds indicates a binding interaction with a target molecule that exhibits a dissociation constant (KD) of less than 10⁻⁷ M, as measured by surface plasmon resonance, ELISA, or competitive binding assays. Cross-reactivity is the ability of an antibody to bind to an antigen other than its intended target, and is considered negligible if the binding affinity is less than 1% of that for the intended target. KD is the equilibrium dissociation constant, a quantitative measure of binding affinity, with lower values indicating higher affinity. Affinity refers to the strength of the interaction between a single binding site on an antibody and its epitope. An immunoglobulin construct is any molecule comprising one or more immunoglobulin domains, whether full-length, fragmented, or fused to other polypeptides. The constant region refers to the non-variable portion of an immunoglobulin chain, including the CH1, CH2, CH3, and CL domains. A constant fragment is a proteolytic or recombinant derivative of the constant region, such as Fc or Fc′. FcRn is the neonatal Fc receptor, a heterodimeric receptor composed of an alpha chain and β2 microglobulin, which binds IgG in acidic endosomes and mediates its recycling to the cell surface. β2 microglobulin is a non-covalently associated light chain required for FcRn stability and function. Numbering of residues follows the EU numbering system, which aligns immunoglobulin sequences based on conserved structural motifs and is used for consistent comparison across species.

Amino acid substitutions in the ferret Fc region may be introduced using a variety of molecular biology techniques, including site-directed mutagenesis, PCR-mediated mutagenesis, and oligonucleotide-directed mutagenesis using thermostable DNA polymerases and ligases. Site-directed mutagenesis is performed using complementary oligonucleotide primers containing the desired nucleotide changes, which are annealed to a plasmid template and extended by DNA polymerase, followed by digestion of the parental template and transformation into competent host cells. PCR-mediated mutagenesis employs overlapping primers to amplify and reassemble the target sequence with the desired substitution, allowing for the introduction of multiple mutations in a single step. The use of thermostable ligases, such as Taq or Pfu ligase, enhances the fidelity and efficiency of nick ligation during mutagenesis reactions. Mutagenic agents, including error-prone PCR, chemical mutagens, or transposon insertion, may be employed for random mutagenesis followed by high-throughput screening. Mutants are screened for enhanced FcRn binding using competitive binding assays at pH 6.0, such as AlphaScreen or surface plasmon resonance, and for in vivo half-life extension in ferrets via pharmacokinetic studies. Exemplary amino acid substitutions include S252Y and S252M, wherein the serine at position 252 is replaced by tyrosine or methionine, respectively, as determined by alignment with the EU numbering system. Other substitutions, such as S254T and T256E, were tested but did not provide additive benefit when combined with S252Y. The numbering system used herein is based on the EU system, which assigns positions according to conserved structural landmarks in the IgG Fc region, ensuring compatibility with published human Fc engineering data and enabling direct comparison across species.

### Antibodies of the Invention

- describe antibodies according to the invention
- describe characteristics of antibodies

The antibodies of the invention are characterized by the presence of a modified ferret IgG Fc region containing at least one substitution at residue 252, selected from the group consisting of S252Y and S252M. These antibodies exhibit a terminal serum half-life in ferrets of at least 120 hours, as measured following intravenous administration, and demonstrate a dissociation constant (KD) for ferret FcRn at pH 6.0 of less than 20 μg/ml, as determined by competitive binding assays. They retain full antigen-binding capacity of their variable domains, as confirmed by ELISA, surface plasmon resonance, or neutralization assays, and display no significant aggregation, fragmentation, or loss of structural integrity as assessed by size exclusion chromatography and SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions. The antibodies may be of any isotype, but are preferably IgG1, and may be fully human, humanized, chimeric, or ferretized, provided that the Fc region is derived from ferret IgG and contains the specified substitution. The antibodies are substantially free of anti-drug antibodies in ferrets following repeated dosing, as demonstrated by the absence of precipitous drops in plasma concentration and negative results in bridging assays for ADA detection. They are suitable for use in single-dose or multi-dose therapeutic regimens in ferret models of infectious disease, immune modulation, or antibody pharmacokinetic evaluation.

Ferret antibody derivatives encompass any variant of the disclosed antibodies that retains the essential pharmacokinetic properties conferred by the S252Y or S252M substitution, including those with additional conservative amino acid substitutions in the framework regions or CDR loops. A conservative substitution is defined as the replacement of an amino acid with another having similar physicochemical properties, such as substitution of a hydrophobic residue for another hydrophobic residue, or a polar residue for another polar residue. Mutations in framework regions may be introduced to improve stability, solubility, or expression yield, provided they do not disrupt the FcRn-binding interface. Mutations in CDR regions are permitted if they do not impair antigen binding or induce immunogenicity. Silent or neutral missense mutations are those that do not alter the functional activity of the antibody, whereas non-neutral missense mutations are those that enhance or diminish binding, stability, or half-life. Mutant molecules are designed using computational modeling of the Fc-FcRn interface, synthesized via recombinant expression, and tested for binding affinity, structural integrity, and pharmacokinetic performance in ferrets. Covalent attachment of molecules, such as polyethylene glycol, lipids, or carbohydrates, may be employed to further modulate pharmacokinetics or biodistribution. Peptides may be chemically synthesized and fused to the Fc region via solid-phase peptide synthesis and native chemical ligation. Immunoconjugates are formed by linking the antibody to cytotoxic agents, radionuclides, fluorescent dyes, or enzymes for diagnostic or therapeutic applications, with the modified Fc region ensuring prolonged circulation and enhanced tissue penetration.

Chimeric antibodies are recombinant molecules comprising variable regions derived from one species and constant regions derived from another. In the present invention, chimeric antibodies comprise human or humanized variable regions and ferret-derived constant regions containing the S252Y or S252M substitution. These are produced by cloning the variable domain genes from a human antibody into expression vectors containing the modified ferret Fc region, followed by co-transfection into mammalian host cells. Ferret antibodies are those in which both variable and constant regions are derived from ferret sources, while ferretized antibodies are non-ferret antibodies in which the constant regions have been replaced with ferret sequences to reduce immunogenicity. Ferretizing a non-ferret antibody involves replacing the Fc region, and optionally the CH1 and CL domains, with the corresponding ferret sequences using molecular cloning techniques such as overlap extension PCR or Gibson assembly. CDR-grafting involves transplanting the complementarity-determining regions of a non-ferret antibody onto a ferret framework, while veneering or resurfacing entails replacing surface-exposed residues of the ferret framework with those from human or other species to reduce immunogenicity. Chain shuffling involves random recombination of heavy and light chain pairs to optimize binding and stability. Ferret veneered antibodies are those in which the surface residues of the ferret Fc region have been modified to resemble those of human Fc, while preserving the S252Y or S252M substitution. Antibody binding is confirmed using ELISA, surface plasmon resonance, or flow cytometry. Immunoglobulin fusion proteins are produced by fusing the modified ferret Fc region to a bioactive molecule via a flexible linker, such as glycine-serine repeats, using recombinant DNA techniques. Polynucleotides encoding the modified antibodies are synthesized de novo or assembled from gene fragments and cloned into expression vectors. Half-life assays are performed by intravenous administration to ferrets, serial blood sampling, and quantification of antibody concentration using electrochemiluminescent or ELISA-based assays. The modified antibodies and immunoglobulin constructs are used for therapeutic, diagnostic, or prophylactic applications in ferret models of human disease.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- introduce pharmacokinetics study
- test human IgG antibody in ferrets
- analyze results

A pharmacokinetic study was conducted to evaluate the serum half-life of a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody specific for the RSV F glycoprotein in male ferrets. Eight ferrets received a single intravenous dose of 2 mg/kg of the antibody. Blood samples were collected at 2, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, 168, 336, and 504 hours post-dose. Plasma concentrations were quantified using an electrochemiluminescent assay with a ruthenium-labeled anti-human IgG detection reagent. The average terminal half-life of the human IgG was determined to be 31 ± 10 hours. Two ferrets exhibited a rapid decline in antibody concentration consistent with an anti-drug antibody response, as confirmed by a bridging assay detecting ferret anti-human IgG antibodies. These findings demonstrated that human IgG antibodies are rapidly cleared in ferrets and are prone to immunogenicity, necessitating the development of species-optimized alternatives.

### Example 2

- clone ferret IgG constant region sequences
- analyze cloned sequences

Total RNA was isolated from ferret kidney, lung, liver, and spleen tissues and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. Using primers designed based on partial ferret and mink sequences, the constant regions of the ferret IgG heavy chain and kappa light chain were amplified by PCR and cloned into plasmid vectors. The resulting sequences were sequenced and aligned with public databases. The ferret heavy chain constant region was found to be 89% identical to the mink sequence, with three amino acid differences localized to the hinge region. The light chain sequence was identical to the published database entry and shared 94% identity with mink. These sequences were used as templates for the construction of chimeric antibodies with human variable domains and ferret constant domains.

### Example 3

- prepare chimeric ferret/human antibodies
- introduce half-life extending substitutions
- express and purify chimeric mAbs
- analyze purified mAbs

Chimeric antibodies were generated by cloning the variable regions of a humanized anti-RSV antibody upstream of the cloned ferret IgG constant regions. Site-directed mutagenesis was performed to introduce the S252M, S252Y, and YTE substitutions into the ferret Fc region. The constructs were transiently expressed in HEK293E cells, and the antibodies were purified using tandem Protein A and Protein G affinity chromatography. SDS-PAGE and size exclusion HPLC confirmed the presence of intact, monomeric antibodies with no detectable aggregates or fragments. Antigen binding was preserved, with IC50 values for RSV F glycoprotein binding comparable to the parental human antibody, indicating that the ferret Fc region did not interfere with variable domain function.

### Example 4

- clone and express soluble ferret FcRn
- analyze expressed FcRn

The extracellular domains of the ferret FcRn alpha chain and β2-microglobulin were amplified from ferret cDNA and cloned into a mammalian expression vector with a C-terminal 6× His tag. The constructs were co-transfected into HEK293E cells, and the secreted complex was purified using Ni-NTA affinity chromatography followed by size exclusion chromatography. SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions revealed two bands corresponding to the alpha chain (∼30 kDa) and β2-microglobulin (∼11.5 kDa), with the alpha chain appearing as a doublet, likely due to glycosylation heterogeneity. The purified complex was confirmed to be >95% pure by HPLC and retained binding to human IgG, validating its structural and functional integrity.

### Example 5

- test chimeric mAb binding to ferret FcRn
- analyze binding results
- compare binding affinities

A competitive AlphaScreen assay was used to assess binding of chimeric antibodies to ferret FcRn at pH 6.0. The wild-type chimeric antibody exhibited an IC50 of 78 μg/ml, while the S252M variant showed an IC50 of 13 μg/ml and the S252Y variant showed an IC50 of 3.2 μg/ml, representing a 24-fold improvement in binding affinity. The YTE variant did not improve binding beyond S252Y. Binding affinity rankings were consistent across replicate experiments, confirming that the S252Y substitution confers the greatest enhancement in ferret FcRn interaction.

### Example 6

- evaluate pharmacokinetics of chimeric mAb variants
- inject ferrets with mAb variants
- collect and analyze blood samples
- determine half-lives of mAb variants
- compare half-lives of variants
- test for anti-drug antibodies
- analyze ADA detection results

Male ferrets received a single 2 mg/kg intravenous dose of either wild-type chimeric antibody or one of the S252M, S252Y, or YTE variants. Blood samples were collected at the same time points as in Example 1. The terminal half-lives were 65 ± 27 hours for the wild-type, 127 ± 38 hours for S252M, and 206 ± 44 hours and 213 ± 28 hours for S252Y and YTE, respectively. No precipitous drops in concentration were observed in any of the treated animals. Bridging assays for anti-drug antibodies were negative in all samples, even when spiked with a surrogate anti-idiotype antibody, suggesting that residual antibody did not interfere with detection. The consistent clearance profiles and absence of ADA indicate that the modified antibodies are non-immunogenic and suitable for repeated dosing in ferrets.
