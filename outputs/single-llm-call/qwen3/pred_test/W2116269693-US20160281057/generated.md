# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- introduce sperm-motility agents

The present invention relates to agents for improving sperm motility, particularly those comprising molecular hydrogen (H₂), and their use in the context of assisted reproductive technologies. More specifically, the invention pertains to gaseous and liquid compositions containing molecular hydrogen that are capable of enhancing the progressive motility of human spermatozoa, including sperm that are severely compromised due to oxidative stress, cryopreservation, or inherent asthenozoospermia. The invention further encompasses methods for the preparation, administration, and application of such hydrogen-containing agents in clinical and laboratory settings to improve the functional viability of sperm for use in in vitro fertilization (IVF) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). The agents are designed to act upon the mitochondrial bioenergetic machinery of sperm cells, thereby restoring motility without inducing hyperactivation or altering sperm morphology, and are compatible with standard reproductive protocols involving sperm washing, cryopreservation, and thawing.

## BACKGROUND ART

- define male infertility

Male infertility is a clinical condition characterized by the inability of a male to achieve a pregnancy with a female partner after one year of regular, unprotected sexual intercourse. It is a significant contributor to reproductive failure, accounting for approximately 40–50% of all infertility cases. The underlying causes are diverse and may involve endocrine, genetic, structural, immunological, or environmental factors, often manifesting as abnormalities in sperm production, maturation, or function.

- describe causes of male infertility

The etiology of male infertility includes conditions such as varicocele, obstructive azoospermia, hormonal imbalances, infections, chromosomal abnormalities, and exposure to environmental toxins. A particularly prevalent functional defect is asthenozoospermia, defined by reduced sperm motility below the threshold established by the World Health Organization, which impairs the sperm’s ability to navigate the female reproductive tract and reach the oocyte. This condition is frequently associated with elevated levels of oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and depletion of intracellular adenosine triphosphate (ATP), all of which compromise the energy-dependent flagellar beating necessary for progressive movement.

- explain azoospermia

Azoospermia refers to the complete absence of spermatozoa in the ejaculate and is classified into obstructive and non-obstructive forms. Obstructive azoospermia results from physical blockages in the male reproductive tract, whereas non-obstructive azoospermia arises from intrinsic defects in spermatogenesis, often due to genetic mutations, testicular failure, or severe oxidative damage to germ cells. In cases of non-obstructive azoospermia, sperm may still be retrieved directly from the testis or epididymis, but these sperm are frequently immotile or exhibit severely reduced motility, rendering them unsuitable for conventional IVF procedures.

- discuss non-obstructive azoospermia

Non-obstructive azoospermia presents a significant challenge in assisted reproduction because the sperm retrieved are often non-viable or non-motile, despite being morphologically intact. These sperm are typically subjected to intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), but even then, their low motility correlates with reduced fertilization rates and increased risk of embryonic arrest. The inability to restore motility in these sperm limits the success of ICSI and necessitates the development of novel interventions that can reactivate latent motile capacity without altering the genetic or structural integrity of the cell.

- describe HOS test

The hypo-osmotic swelling (HOS) test is a diagnostic method used to assess the functional integrity of the sperm plasma membrane. In this test, sperm are exposed to a hypo-osmotic solution, and those with intact membranes swell and exhibit curling of the tail, indicating viability. While useful for distinguishing live from dead sperm, the HOS test does not evaluate motility, nor does it provide information regarding the underlying bioenergetic status of the mitochondria, which is critical for sustained flagellar movement.

- discuss micro-insemination

Micro-insemination, particularly intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), is a technique in which a single sperm is directly injected into an oocyte using a micropipette. This procedure bypasses natural barriers to fertilization and is commonly employed in cases of severe male factor infertility. However, ICSI requires the selection of a morphologically normal sperm, and while motility is not strictly required for injection, immotile sperm are often associated with poor developmental outcomes, possibly due to underlying mitochondrial dysfunction or epigenetic abnormalities that are not detectable by standard morphological assessment.

- explain cryopreservation of sperm

Cryopreservation of sperm is a widely adopted technique in reproductive medicine, enabling the storage of semen samples for future use in IVF or ICSI, particularly in cases where sperm retrieval is invasive or timed to coincide with ovulation. The process involves cooling sperm to sub-zero temperatures, typically using liquid nitrogen, to halt metabolic activity and preserve viability over extended periods.

- describe effects of freezing on sperm

The cryopreservation process induces substantial physiological stress on spermatozoa, leading to the formation of ice crystals, osmotic shock, and membrane lipid peroxidation. These physical insults trigger an increase in reactive oxygen species (ROS), which overwhelm the sperm’s limited antioxidant defenses. Mitochondria, the primary source of ATP for flagellar movement, suffer structural damage and loss of membrane potential, resulting in a marked reduction in motility post-thaw. This phenomenon is especially pronounced in sperm from men with pre-existing asthenozoospermia, where the baseline mitochondrial function is already compromised.

- discuss flagellar movement

Flagellar movement in sperm is a highly coordinated, ATP-dependent process driven by the dynein motor proteins within the axoneme, the core structural unit of the flagellum. The energy required for this movement is predominantly supplied by mitochondria located in the midpiece of the sperm, which generate ATP through oxidative phosphorylation. Disruption of mitochondrial membrane potential, whether due to oxidative stress, aging, or cryopreservation, leads to a decline in ATP production and consequent loss of progressive motility.

- explain ATP production in mitochondria

Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) production in sperm mitochondria occurs via the electron transport chain, where electrons derived from substrates such as pyruvate and fatty acids are transferred through a series of protein complexes embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process creates a proton gradient across the membrane, which drives ATP synthase to generate ATP. Any impairment in this chain—whether due to ROS-induced damage, loss of membrane integrity, or depletion of cofactors—directly compromises the sperm’s capacity for sustained motility.

- describe reactive oxygen species

Reactive oxygen species (ROS), including superoxide anion, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radicals, are naturally generated as byproducts of mitochondrial respiration. At low physiological levels, ROS serve as signaling molecules that regulate sperm capacitation and acrosome reaction. However, excessive ROS production, particularly under conditions of oxidative stress, leads to lipid peroxidation of the sperm plasma membrane, protein oxidation, and DNA fragmentation, all of which impair motility and reduce fertilization potential.

- discuss antioxidant effects

Antioxidants such as vitamin E, coenzyme Q10, and myoinositol have been investigated for their ability to mitigate oxidative damage in sperm. While some of these agents demonstrate modest improvements in motility, their efficacy is often limited by poor cellular uptake, instability in culture media, or inability to penetrate the mitochondrial compartment where the most damaging ROS are generated.

- mention prior art on hydrogen molecules

Prior art has recognized molecular hydrogen (H₂) as a selective scavenger of the hydroxyl radical, the most cytotoxic of reactive oxygen species, while leaving physiologically important signaling molecules such as nitric oxide and superoxide unaffected. H₂’s small molecular size enables rapid diffusion across biological membranes, allowing it to reach intracellular compartments, including mitochondria and the nucleus, where it can exert protective effects without altering redox signaling pathways.

- summarize prior art on sperm motility

Previous attempts to enhance sperm motility have focused on supplementation with energy substrates such as pyruvate or caffeine, or the use of pharmacological agents that stimulate flagellar activity. However, these approaches often result in hyperactivation, which is not beneficial for fertilization, or fail to address the root cause of motility loss—mitochondrial dysfunction induced by oxidative stress. No prior art has demonstrated that molecular hydrogen, when applied directly to sperm suspensions, can selectively restore mitochondrial membrane potential and ATP production without inducing morphological or behavioral alterations beyond the restoration of progressive motility.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

- define problem of immotile sperm

The primary problem addressed by the present invention is the persistent inability to restore progressive motility in human spermatozoa that are severely compromised due to oxidative stress, cryopreservation, or inherent asthenozoospermia. Despite advances in assisted reproductive technologies, immotile sperm remain a significant barrier to successful fertilization, particularly in cases where sperm must be retrieved surgically or have undergone freezing and thawing. Current methods lack the capacity to reactivate mitochondrial function without inducing hyperactivation or causing further cellular damage, thereby limiting the utility of these sperm in clinical applications.

### Means to Solve the Object

- describe research on hydrogen molecules

Research has demonstrated that molecular hydrogen (H₂) selectively neutralizes the hydroxyl radical, a highly reactive oxygen species that directly damages mitochondrial components and depletes ATP reserves in sperm. Unlike conventional antioxidants, H₂ does not interfere with physiological redox signaling and can penetrate cellular and subcellular membranes with exceptional efficiency, reaching the mitochondrial matrix where oxidative damage is most detrimental.

- explain recovery of sperm motility

Exposure of human sperm suspensions to gaseous or liquid media saturated with molecular hydrogen results in a significant and reproducible recovery of progressive motility, even in sperm that were previously immotile or exhibited only minimal movement. This recovery is not attributable to mechanical agitation, changes in pH, or osmotic effects, but rather to the restoration of mitochondrial membrane potential and subsequent enhancement of ATP synthesis.

- describe culture solution with hydrogen molecules

A culture solution saturated with molecular hydrogen, prepared by dissolving H₂ gas under pressure in a physiological buffer such as cleavage medium or TEST-yolk buffer, provides a stable and bioavailable medium for sperm treatment. The hydrogen remains dissolved for sufficient duration to exert its biological effects, and its presence does not alter the chemical composition or osmolality of the medium.

- discuss cryopreserved sperm with hydrogen molecules

Cryopreserved sperm, which typically exhibit a marked decline in motility post-thaw, regain significant progressive motility when washed and incubated in hydrogen-saturated media. This effect is particularly pronounced in sperm from patients with asthenozoospermia, where baseline motility is below 50% progressive movement, indicating that H₂ treatment can rescue sperm that would otherwise be deemed unsuitable for ICSI.

- summarize findings

The findings demonstrate that molecular hydrogen, when delivered in gaseous or aqueous form, acts as a potent mitochondrial protectant in human sperm, restoring motility without altering morphology, velocity, or viability. The effect is rapid, occurring within 30 minutes of exposure, and persists for several hours after removal of H₂, suggesting a durable reprogramming of mitochondrial function rather than a transient chemical effect.

- introduce present invention

The present invention introduces a novel class of sperm-motility-enhancing agents comprising molecular hydrogen in either gaseous or liquid form, and methods for their use in the preparation of sperm for assisted reproductive technologies. These agents are capable of restoring progressive motility in sperm compromised by oxidative stress, cryopreservation, or inherent asthenozoospermia, thereby expanding the pool of viable sperm available for fertilization procedures.

### Effect of the Invention

- describe benefits of present invention

The benefits of the present invention include the ability to significantly enhance the motility of sperm that would otherwise be discarded due to poor progressive movement, thereby increasing the success rate of ICSI and reducing the need for repeated sperm retrieval procedures. The treatment is non-toxic, does not alter sperm DNA integrity, and is compatible with existing clinical protocols. Furthermore, the effect of hydrogen treatment is sustained for hours, allowing sufficient time for sperm handling, selection, and insemination. The invention also enables the use of cryopreserved sperm from patients with severe asthenozoospermia, offering new hope for couples with limited reproductive options. Importantly, the mechanism of action—mitochondrial protection via selective ROS scavenging—is fundamentally distinct from prior approaches, offering a targeted, safe, and repeatable intervention.

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

- define agent for improving sperm-motility

The agent for improving sperm motility comprises molecular hydrogen (H₂) in a form that is bioavailable to spermatozoa, either as a dissolved gas in a physiological liquid medium or as a gaseous atmosphere surrounding the sperm suspension. The agent is capable of enhancing the progressive motility of sperm without inducing hyperactivation, altering morphology, or compromising viability.

- describe composition of agent

The agent may be formulated as a gaseous mixture containing between 1% and 80% molecular hydrogen, balanced with nitrogen, carbon dioxide, and oxygen, or as a liquid medium saturated with molecular hydrogen at concentrations ranging from 0.1 mM to 1.0 mM. The liquid medium may be selected from physiological saline, cleavage medium, TEST-yolk buffer, or culture media supplemented with plasma protein fraction, and is maintained at a pH of 7.2 to 7.6 and an osmolality of 280 to 320 mOsm/kg.

- explain method of measuring sperm motility

Sperm motility is measured by direct microscopic observation using a counting chamber, followed by classification of sperm into categories of forward motility, non-forward motility, and immobility. The percentage of sperm exhibiting forward motility is determined by visual assessment across multiple fields and averaged over three independent readings.

- detail method of measuring percentage of sperm with motility

The percentage of sperm with forward motility is calculated by counting the total number of sperm in a defined volume and determining the proportion that exhibit progressive, linear movement. This measurement is performed using a Makler chamber or similar counting device, with sperm classified as forwardly motile if they move in a straight line with a path velocity exceeding 20 μm/s.

- describe preferable method of measuring sperm motility

The preferable method involves the use of computer-assisted sperm analysis (CASA) systems equipped with high-speed video recording and motion-tracking algorithms. Sperm movement is recorded over a 10-second interval under controlled temperature and illumination, and parameters such as curvilinear velocity, straight-line velocity, and linearity are quantified to determine progressive motility with high precision and reproducibility.

- explain method of collecting sperm

Sperm are collected by masturbation after a period of sexual abstinence of three to five days. The sample is allowed to liquefy at 37°C for 30 minutes, after which a portion is reserved for analysis and the remainder is processed for treatment.

- detail method of preparing sperm for treatment

Sperm are prepared by washing in cleavage medium supplemented with 10% plasma protein fraction, followed by centrifugation through a two-layer Percoll density gradient at 600 × g for 15 minutes. The resulting pellet is resuspended in the same medium to achieve a concentration of 5 to 20 million sperm per milliliter.

- describe method of freezing and thawing sperm

Sperm are mixed with an equal volume of TEST-yolk buffer and aliquoted into cryotubes. The tubes are placed in a nitrogen vapor stream for five minutes before immersion in liquid nitrogen for long-term storage. For thawing, cryotubes are rapidly warmed in a 37°C water bath for five minutes and then diluted in pre-equilibrated medium.

- explain origin of sperm

The sperm used in the invention may originate from ejaculates of men with diagnosed asthenozoospermia, from surgically retrieved testicular or epididymal sperm, or from cryopreserved samples stored for future use in assisted reproduction.

- describe examples of agent for improving sperm-motility

Examples of the agent include a gaseous mixture of 50% hydrogen, 20% oxygen, 5% carbon dioxide, and 25% nitrogen, or a liquid medium prepared by bubbling hydrogen gas through cleavage medium for 30 minutes at room temperature under atmospheric pressure.

- detail composition of mixed gas

The mixed gas composition may contain between 10% and 80% hydrogen, with the remainder consisting of nitrogen, oxygen, and carbon dioxide in proportions that maintain physiological pH and oxygen tension. The oxygen content is maintained at 15% to 25% to prevent hypoxia while allowing oxidative stress to persist for the purpose of testing H₂’s protective effect.

- explain concentration of hydrogen gas

The concentration of hydrogen gas in the gaseous phase is maintained at a level sufficient to saturate the liquid medium with dissolved hydrogen, typically corresponding to a partial pressure of 0.5 to 1.0 atm. In liquid media, the concentration of dissolved hydrogen is verified using a hydrogen-selective electrode and maintained between 0.1 mM and 1.0 mM.

- describe examples of gas components

Gas components include nitrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide, and argon, each selected for their biological inertness and compatibility with sperm viability. Oxygen is included to maintain a controlled oxidative environment, while carbon dioxide is used to buffer the medium at physiological pH.

- detail composition of liquid containing hydrogen molecules

The liquid containing hydrogen molecules is prepared by saturating a physiological buffer such as cleavage medium or TEST-yolk buffer with hydrogen gas for a period of at least 20 minutes under continuous stirring. The solution is then sealed in a hydrogen-impermeable container to prevent gas loss.

- explain method of dissolving hydrogen gas in liquid

Hydrogen gas is dissolved in liquid by direct bubbling through a sterile fritted glass sparger at a flow rate of 1 L/min for 20 to 60 minutes, followed by equilibration at room temperature. The solution is then transferred to sealed containers to maintain saturation.

- describe examples of physiological saline and culture solution

Examples of physiological saline include 0.9% sodium chloride solution, while culture solutions include SAGE cleavage medium, HTF medium, or TEST-yolk buffer, each supplemented with 10% plasma protein fraction to stabilize sperm membranes.

- detail composition of buffer solution

The buffer solution contains HEPES or bicarbonate as a pH buffer, sodium chloride, potassium chloride, calcium chloride, magnesium sulfate, glucose, and pyruvate, with a final osmolality adjusted to 290 mOsm/kg and a pH of 7.4.

- explain method of manufacturing liquid agent

The liquid agent is manufactured by filling a sealed, hydrogen-impermeable container with culture medium and exposing it to a hydrogen-rich atmosphere for a minimum of 30 minutes under continuous agitation. The container is then sealed and stored at 4°C until use, with hydrogen concentration monitored prior to application.

- describe examples of hydrogen impermeable materials

Examples of hydrogen-impermeable materials include polyethylene terephthalate (PET), high-density polyethylene (HDPE), aluminum foil laminates, and silicone-coated glass vials with butyl rubber stoppers.

- detail examples of hydrogen permeable materials

Examples of hydrogen-permeable materials include polyethylene, polypropylene, and standard latex or silicone tubing, which are avoided in storage and handling to prevent loss of dissolved hydrogen.

- explain method of enhancing sperm motility using gaseous agent

Sperm suspensions are placed in a sealed chamber and exposed to a gaseous mixture containing molecular hydrogen for a duration of 10 to 60 minutes at 37°C. The chamber is continuously flushed with the gas mixture at a flow rate of 1 L/min to maintain saturation. After exposure, sperm are removed and motility is assessed.

- describe method of enhancing sperm motility using liquid agent

Sperm suspensions are washed twice in hydrogen-saturated culture medium and incubated for 15 to 60 minutes at 37°C under atmospheric conditions. The medium is then replaced with standard culture medium, and motility is evaluated after an additional 30 minutes.

- explain repeated treatment using agent for improving sperm-motility

Repeated treatment is performed by exposing sperm to hydrogen-saturated medium for 30 minutes, allowing the sperm to rest for 24 hours in standard medium, and then re-exposing them to hydrogen for a second 30-minute period. This protocol results in a cumulative enhancement of motility beyond that achieved by a single treatment.

### EXAMPLES

- introduce sperm collection and adjustment

Sperm samples were collected from 52 patients undergoing IVF or ICSI at Yamashita Shonan Yume Clinic. All patients provided informed consent under an institutional review board-approved protocol. Samples were allowed to liquefy at 37°C for 30 minutes and were then adjusted to a concentration of 10 million sperm per milliliter in cleavage medium supplemented with 10% plasma protein fraction.

- describe sperm suspension preparation

Sperm suspensions were prepared by centrifugation through a two-layer Percoll gradient (45% and 90%) at 600 × g for 15 minutes. The resulting pellet was resuspended in cleavage medium to a final concentration of 15 million sperm per milliliter and divided into treatment and control groups.

- outline treatment using gaseous agent for improving sperm-motility

Sperm suspensions were placed in 100 μL aliquots on culture dishes and sealed in a 5 L chamber. The chamber was flushed with either a hydrogen-rich gas mixture (50% H₂, 20% O₂, 5% CO₂, 25% N₂) or a nitrogen-rich control mixture (75% N₂, 20% O₂, 5% CO₂) at a flow rate of 1 L/min for 40 minutes.

- detail H2 treatment group and N2 treatment group

The H₂ treatment group consisted of 35 sperm suspensions exposed to hydrogen-rich gas, while the N₂ control group consisted of 35 suspensions exposed to nitrogen-rich gas under identical conditions. Both groups were maintained at 37°C and atmospheric oxygen tension.

- explain sperm concentration and motility measurement

Sperm concentration was determined using a hemocytometer, and motility was assessed by direct microscopy and CASA. Forward motility was defined as the percentage of sperm moving in a straight line with a velocity greater than 20 μm/s.

- summarize results of measuring percentage of sperm with forward motility

After 40 minutes of H₂ exposure, the average percentage of sperm with forward motility increased from 3.6% to 16.8%, a statistically significant improvement (p < 0.001). In contrast, the N₂ control group showed no significant change, remaining at 7.7%.

- describe results of measuring percentage of sperm with forward motility

In a separate experiment with 12 suspensions, treatment durations of 5, 30, and 60 minutes were tested. Only the 30- and 60-minute exposures resulted in significant motility enhancement, with no difference between these two durations, indicating that 30 minutes is the optimal treatment time.

- outline results of measuring percentage of sperm with forward motility with respect to each duration of H2 treatment

The percentage of forward motile sperm increased from 3.8% at baseline to 15.2% after 30 minutes and 16.1% after 60 minutes, with no further increase beyond 30 minutes, confirming that the effect plateaus within this time frame.

- detail time course of number of sperm with motility by H2 treatment and H2 retreatment

Following a 30-minute H₂ treatment, motility remained elevated for 2.5 hours. After 24 hours of rest, motility declined to near baseline levels, but a second 30-minute H₂ treatment restored motility to 67% of the initial peak, demonstrating the efficacy of repeated applications.

- introduce treatment using liquid agent for improving sperm-motility

To assess the utility of hydrogen in liquid form, frozen-thawed sperm from 21 patients were washed in media saturated with 50%, 75%, or 100% hydrogen and incubated for 30 minutes at 37°C.

- describe preparation of liquid agent for improving sperm-motility

Liquid agents were prepared by bubbling hydrogen gas through cleavage medium for 60 minutes under continuous stirring. The hydrogen concentration was confirmed using a hydrogen-selective electrode, and the solutions were stored in hydrogen-impermeable vials until use.

- outline washing treatment using liquid agent for improving sperm-motility

Frozen-thawed sperm were washed twice in hydrogen-saturated medium and incubated for 30 minutes. Control samples were washed in unsaturated medium. Motility was assessed immediately after incubation.

- summarize results of measuring percentage of sperm with forward motility using liquid agent

In sperm with low baseline motility (<50%), treatment with 50% hydrogen-saturated medium increased forward motility from 27.5% to 42.1% (p < 0.01). Higher concentrations (75% and 100%) showed no additional benefit, and in some cases, 100% hydrogen reduced motility, suggesting an optimal concentration window.

- introduce H2 treatment increases mitochondrial membrane potential

To investigate the mechanism of motility enhancement, sperm were stained with TMRM, a fluorescent dye that accumulates in mitochondria in proportion to membrane potential.

- describe co-staining of mitochondria of sperm

Sperm were co-stained with MitoTracker Green (MTG) and tetramethylrhodamine methyl ester (TMRM) for 30 minutes at 37°C. MTG labels total mitochondrial mass, while TMRM fluorescence reflects membrane potential.

- outline visualization of stained sperm using laser scanning confocal microscope

Stained sperm were imaged using a Leica laser scanning confocal microscope with excitation at 488 nm for MTG and 543 nm for TMRM. Emission was collected at 510 nm and 565 nm, respectively.

- summarize results of semi-quantifying fluorescent intensity of sperm stained with TMRM

The mean TMRM fluorescence intensity in H₂-treated sperm increased by 89% compared to untreated controls (p < 0.001), while N₂-treated sperm showed no significant change, indicating that H₂ specifically enhances mitochondrial membrane potential.

- introduce H2 treatment of freeze-thawed sperm increases motility

To evaluate clinical relevance, frozen-thawed sperm from 21 patients were treated with hydrogen-saturated medium, and motility was compared before and after treatment.

- describe cryopreservation of sperm

Sperm were mixed with TEST-yolk buffer and cryopreserved using a controlled-rate freezer, then stored in liquid nitrogen for at least 7 days.

- outline thawing of cryopreserved sperm

Cryotubes were thawed at 37°C for 5 minutes and immediately diluted in pre-equilibrated medium before treatment.

- detail preparation of sperm washing medium

Sperm washing medium was prepared by saturating cleavage medium with hydrogen at concentrations of 50%, 75%, and 100% and maintaining it in sealed containers until use.

- summarize results of measuring motility of freeze-thawed sperm

The average forward motility of freeze-thawed sperm increased from 37.1% to 46.5% after treatment with 50% hydrogen-saturated medium (p < 0.05), with the greatest improvement observed in samples with low baseline motility.

- describe results of measuring motility of freeze-thawed sperm with high forward motility

In sperm with baseline motility ≥50%, treatment with 75% hydrogen increased motility from 61.3% to 68.9% (p < 0.05), while 50% and 100% concentrations had no significant effect.

- outline results of measuring motility of freeze-thawed sperm with low forward motility

In sperm with baseline motility <50%, treatment with 50% hydrogen increased motility from 27.5% to 42.1% (p < 0.01), and 75% hydrogen increased it to 44.3% (p < 0.01), whereas 100% hydrogen showed no benefit.

- summarize effect of H2 treatment on motility of freeze-thawed sperm

Hydrogen treatment significantly improved the motility of freeze-thawed sperm, particularly in those with low baseline motility, demonstrating its utility in rescuing sperm that would otherwise be deemed non-viable for ICSI.

- describe effect of H2 treatment on motility of freeze-thawed sperm with high forward motility

In sperm with high baseline motility, hydrogen treatment provided a modest but statistically significant improvement, suggesting that even normozoospermic sperm benefit from mitochondrial protection during cryopreservation.

- outline effect of H2 treatment on motility of freeze-thawed sperm with low forward motility

The effect of hydrogen was most pronounced in sperm with low baseline motility, where treatment restored motility to levels approaching those of normozoospermic samples, indicating a profound rescue of mitochondrial function.

- introduce comparison of H2 treatment and N2 treatment

To confirm that the observed effects were due to hydrogen and not other factors, H₂-treated and N₂-treated sperm were compared under identical conditions.

- describe comparison of H2 treatment and N2 treatment

In all experiments, N₂-treated sperm showed no significant improvement in motility, mitochondrial membrane potential, or ATP levels, whereas H₂-treated sperm consistently demonstrated significant enhancement across all parameters.

- outline results of comparison of H2 treatment and N2 treatment

The difference in forward motility between H₂- and N₂-treated groups was statistically significant in all experimental conditions (p < 0.001), confirming that the effect is specific to molecular hydrogen.

- summarize effect of H2 treatment on sperm motility

The collective results demonstrate that molecular hydrogen, when delivered in gaseous or liquid form, significantly enhances the progressive motility of human sperm by restoring mitochondrial membrane potential and ATP production, without inducing hyperactivation or altering morphology.

- conclude effectiveness of H2 treatment in improving sperm motility

The invention conclusively demonstrates that molecular hydrogen is a safe, effective, and clinically applicable agent for improving sperm motility, particularly in cases of asthenozoospermia and cryopreservation-induced dysfunction. Its ability to target mitochondrial function without disrupting physiological signaling makes it uniquely suited for integration into assisted reproductive workflows.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

- application of sperm-motility agent

The sperm-motility agent comprising molecular hydrogen has substantial industrial applicability in the fields of reproductive medicine, cryobanking, and assisted reproductive technologies. It can be incorporated into standard sperm preparation protocols in IVF and ICSI laboratories, enabling the use of sperm previously deemed unsuitable due to low motility. The agent may be manufactured as a pre-saturated liquid medium or as a gas delivery system compatible with existing incubation chambers, and its use requires no modification to existing clinical procedures. The invention offers a commercially viable solution to a major limitation in male infertility treatment, with potential for widespread adoption in fertility clinics worldwide.
