# DESCRIPTION

- claim benefit of provisional application

This invention claims the benefit of U.S. Provisional Patent Application No. 63/478,901, filed on June 12, 2023, entitled “System and Method for Digital-to-Analog Reconstruction of Electrocardiographic Signals for Multi-Manufacturer Diagnostic Interoperability,” the entire disclosure of which is hereby incorporated by reference in its entirety. The present invention builds upon the foundational disclosures contained therein, extending the scope of applicability, technical implementation, and clinical utility through refined hardware architectures, standardized digital formatting protocols, and validated reconstruction methodologies that enable consistent, high-fidelity analog signal reproduction across disparate electrocardiographic platforms. The provisional application established the conceptual basis for reconstructing original analog ECG waveforms from digitized data regardless of the source manufacturer, but the present invention materially advances this concept by introducing a universally compatible reference framework, optimized digital-to-analog conversion circuitry, and clinically validated calibration procedures that eliminate prior limitations related to electrode referencing, sampling rate discrepancies, and proprietary data encoding. The invention further expands upon the provisional disclosure by incorporating formal validation protocols using multiple ADC models, demonstrating clinical equivalence in automated diagnostic outputs, and defining a scalable software interface for integration into cloud-based diagnostic ecosystems. All claims herein are supported by the enabling disclosures of the provisional application, including but not limited to the algebraic transformations for electrode referencing, the structure of the optimized digital format, and the functional architecture of the DAC hardware system.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce field of invention

The field of this invention pertains to electrocardiography, specifically to systems and methods for the digital-to-analog conversion and reconstruction of twelve-lead electrocardiographic signals for the purpose of enabling interoperability between diagnostic platforms manufactured by disparate vendors. Electrocardiography remains one of the most widely used diagnostic tools in clinical medicine, providing critical insights into cardiac rhythm, conduction abnormalities, ischemia, and structural heart disease. Modern electrocardiogram machines digitize analog cardiac signals using internal analog-to-digital converters to facilitate storage, transmission, automated analysis, and graphical display. However, the resulting digital data are often encoded in proprietary formats that are incompatible across manufacturers, creating a significant barrier to the exchange of diagnostic information and limiting the ability of clinicians to obtain second opinions from alternative automated interpretation algorithms.

- describe current ECG machines

Current electrocardiogram machines are designed as integrated systems in which the analog signal acquisition, digitization, filtering, and interpretation functions are tightly coupled within a single proprietary hardware and software ecosystem. Each manufacturer employs unique sampling rates, reference electrode configurations, data encoding schemes, and signal processing pipelines that are not disclosed to third parties. As a result, digital ECG files generated by one manufacturer’s device cannot be directly interpreted by another manufacturer’s diagnostic software without prior conversion, which is typically not supported. Clinicians are therefore confined to the interpretive algorithms of the machine on which the ECG was originally recorded, even when those algorithms are known to have lower sensitivity or specificity for certain pathologies.

- describe limitations of current ECG machines

The primary limitation of current ECG machines is their inability to interoperate with systems from other manufacturers, which restricts diagnostic flexibility and undermines the potential for consensus-based interpretation. This limitation is particularly acute in complex or ambiguous cases where automated interpretations vary significantly between platforms. Additionally, the reliance on proprietary digital formats prevents the aggregation of ECG data across institutions for research or epidemiological studies unless each dataset is manually re-acquired using the same hardware. Furthermore, the high cost and bulk of clinical-grade ECG machines limit their deployment in remote, mobile, or resource-limited settings, where cheaper, lighter alternatives could be employed if their output could be reliably reconstructed on more sophisticated diagnostic platforms.

- describe need for compatibility between ECG manufacturers

There exists a critical and unmet need for a standardized, vendor-neutral method to reconstruct original analog ECG signals from any digital file, regardless of the source manufacturer, such that those signals may be re-digitized and interpreted by any other ECG machine. Such a capability would enable clinicians to obtain automated second opinions from multiple diagnostic algorithms, improve diagnostic accuracy through consensus, and facilitate the development of more robust and generalizable machine-learning models by providing a common input format. It would also allow healthcare institutions to standardize their diagnostic workflows despite using mixed ECG hardware, and enable the use of low-cost front-end acquisition devices in underserved areas while preserving access to high-fidelity interpretation systems.

- describe prior art patent 1

U.S. Patent No. 5,217,014 to Miyahara et al. discloses a system for regenerating analog ECG waveforms from digital data using magnetic tape playback and custom signal generators. This system, while conceptually similar, is obsolete due to its reliance on analog storage media, manual signal alignment, and lack of real-time processing capability. It also employs a dual-reference architecture that introduces redundant signal paths and is incompatible with modern digital acquisition standards.

- describe prior art patent 2

U.S. Patent No. 5,857,978 to Bailey et al. describes a method for converting ECG data into a standardized digital format for transmission over public networks. However, this system does not address the reconstruction of analog signals; it merely proposes a data exchange protocol without providing the hardware or software means to convert such data back into physiologically accurate waveforms suitable for re-digitization on commercial ECG machines.

- describe prior art patent 3

U.S. Patent No. 6,309,352 to Willems et al. outlines a digital ECG data compression and transmission protocol designed for interoperability among hospital systems. While this patent improves data portability, it does not enable the physical reconstruction of analog signals and therefore cannot be used to feed data into standard ECG machines for independent interpretation.

- describe prior art patent 4

U.S. Patent No. 7,184,825 to Johnson et al. relates to a remote ECG monitoring system that transmits digitized waveforms to a central server for analysis. The system does not include any mechanism for converting digital data back into analog form for re-digitization on local ECG machines, nor does it address the issue of reference electrode alignment across platforms.

- describe prior art patent 5

U.S. Patent No. 8,465,431 to Lee et al. describes a portable ECG device with cloud connectivity, but its data output is restricted to proprietary digital formats and cannot be reconstructed into analog signals for use on other manufacturers’ machines.

- describe prior art patent 6

U.S. Patent No. 9,125,577 to Chen et al. discloses a method for calibrating digital ECG signals using reference waveforms, but only within the context of a single manufacturer’s hardware ecosystem and without enabling cross-platform signal reconstruction.

- describe prior art patent 7

U.S. Patent No. 10,231,662 to Thompson et al. relates to a digital ECG simulator for training purposes, which generates synthetic waveforms rather than reconstructing authentic patient data from digital files. The system is not designed for clinical diagnostic use and lacks fidelity for real-world interpretation.

- describe prior art patent 8

U.S. Patent No. 10,874,111 to LifeSync, Inc. describes a commercial wearable ECG system that uses a digital-to-analog converter to enable patient mobility. However, the system is entirely closed, requiring the use of a proprietary analog-to-digital converter for data acquisition and only accepting digital input from its own hardware. It does not support reconstruction of data from third-party sources, nor does it enable transmission of reconstructed signals to other ECG machines for secondary interpretation.

- describe need for specialized DAC and reconstruction system

There is a clear and persistent need for a specialized digital-to-analog conversion and reconstruction system that can accept digital ECG data from any manufacturer, convert it into an optimized format, and then generate analog outputs that are electrically and physiologically indistinguishable from those produced by the original acquisition device. Such a system must account for variations in electrode referencing, sampling rates, and signal scaling, and must be capable of driving standard ECG machines without requiring modifications to their internal circuitry.

- describe benefits of present invention

The present invention provides a comprehensive solution to these longstanding limitations by enabling the faithful reconstruction of twelve-lead ECG signals from any digital source, regardless of manufacturer, and delivering those signals to any receiving ECG machine for re-digitization and independent interpretation. The system eliminates vendor lock-in, enhances diagnostic accuracy through algorithmic consensus, reduces costs by enabling the use of low-end acquisition hardware, and supports remote and mobile applications by decoupling data collection from interpretation. It further facilitates clinical research by allowing consistent analysis of heterogeneous ECG databases using a single diagnostic algorithm.

- describe commercial, military, and medical applications

Commercial applications include integration into hospital information systems, diagnostic cloud platforms, and telemedicine services. Military applications involve field-deployable cardiac monitoring in combat zones, submarines, and aircraft, where space, weight, and power constraints necessitate lightweight acquisition devices paired with centralized high-fidelity interpretation systems. Medical applications span emergency departments, rural clinics, space missions, and disaster response units, where access to advanced diagnostic tools is limited but the need for accurate cardiac assessment is paramount.

- describe value of present invention

The value of this invention lies in its ability to transform fragmented, proprietary ECG ecosystems into a unified diagnostic infrastructure. By enabling interoperability without requiring manufacturer cooperation, it empowers clinicians, accelerates algorithm development, reduces diagnostic errors, and lowers the cost of cardiac care. It represents a paradigm shift from hardware-dependent diagnostics to data-driven, platform-agnostic interpretation.

- conclude need for present invention

The present invention is therefore necessary to overcome the technical, commercial, and clinical barriers that have prevented true interoperability in electrocardiography for over four decades. It provides the missing link between digital data and analog physiology, enabling a new era of collaborative, accurate, and accessible cardiac diagnostics.
