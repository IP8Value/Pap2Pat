# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The present invention relates to the field of molecular oncology and targeted cancer therapeutics, specifically to the inhibition of oncogenic RAS signaling pathways through the modulation of plasma membrane-associated prohibitin complexes. The invention concerns novel flavagline derivatives that selectively interfere with the activation of KRAS, a frequently mutated proto-oncogene implicated in a broad spectrum of human malignancies including pancreatic, lung, and colorectal carcinomas. These compounds function by disrupting the spatial organization of KRAS at the plasma membrane, thereby preventing its GTP-loading and subsequent engagement with downstream effector proteins such as CRAF. This mechanism represents a distinct and previously unexplored approach to targeting RAS-driven cancers, circumventing the limitations of conventional inhibitors that focus on nucleotide-binding pockets or mutant-specific covalent modifications. The compounds of this invention are particularly effective against both wild-type and mutant forms of KRAS, offering a therapeutic strategy that mitigates the risk of feedback reactivation commonly observed with allele-specific inhibitors. The invention further encompasses the use of these derivatives in the prevention, suppression, or treatment of diseases characterized by aberrant RAS signaling, including but not limited to solid tumors, hematological malignancies, and precancerous lesions driven by RAS pathway hyperactivation.

## BACKGROUND ART

- RAS proteins are small GTPases that function as critical molecular switches in intracellular signal transduction, relaying extracellular stimuli from receptor tyrosine kinases to downstream effectors such as RAF, PI3K, and RALGDS, thereby regulating cell proliferation, survival, and differentiation. The RAS family comprises three major isoforms—HRAS, NRAS, and KRAS—each encoded by distinct genes but sharing high sequence homology, particularly within their G-domain. Despite this similarity, their C-terminal hypervariable regions confer unique posttranslational modification profiles and subcellular localization patterns that dictate isoform-specific signaling outputs. KRAS exists in two alternatively spliced isoforms, KRAS4A and KRAS4B, with KRAS4B being the predominant isoform expressed in human cancers. Oncogenic mutations in RAS, most frequently occurring at codons 12, 13, or 61, impair GTP hydrolysis by inhibiting the function of GTPase-activating proteins, resulting in constitutive GTP-bound activation and persistent downstream signaling. While direct inhibitors of the KRAS G12C mutant have recently entered clinical use, these agents are ineffective against other prevalent KRAS mutations such as G12D and G12V, and their utility is further limited by compensatory reactivation of wild-type RAS isoforms through feedback loops. Other therapeutic strategies have targeted upstream regulators or downstream effectors, yet these approaches often lack specificity and are hampered by toxicity or resistance. Palmitoylation of HRAS and KRAS4A, along with the polybasic domain of KRAS4B, governs their partitioning into distinct plasma membrane microdomains, where nanoscale clustering is essential for efficient effector recruitment. Prohibitin 1 (PHB1) and prohibitin 2 (PHB2) are evolutionarily conserved scaffold proteins that localize to both mitochondrial and plasma membranes, where they form oligomeric complexes implicated in membrane integrity, lipid organization, and signal transduction. PHB1 has been shown to physically interact with CRAF and facilitate its activation in a RAS-dependent manner. Natural compounds known as flavaglines, derived from plants of the genus Aglaia, have been reported to bind prohibitins and modulate their function, but prior studies have not established a direct link between flavagline activity and the inhibition of RAS GTP-loading or nanoclustering. Moreover, the structural determinants required for RAS inhibition among flavagline analogs remain undefined, and no prior work has demonstrated that specific chemical modifications to the flavagline scaffold can selectively uncouple prohibitin function from KRAS activation without affecting other known targets such as eIF4A.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

- The present invention discloses a class of novel flavagline derivatives that function as potent and selective inhibitors of KRAS activation by disrupting the prohibitin-dependent organization of phospholipids at the plasma membrane. These compounds are distinguished from previously known flavaglines by their ability to inhibit KRAS GTP-loading and nanoclustering at low nanomolar concentrations, irrespective of the mutational status of KRAS, while sparing the function of other RAS isoforms in certain contexts. Through systematic screening of a library of structurally diverse flavaglines, a subset of derivatives was identified that exhibit marked inhibition of KRAS-CRAF interaction and downstream MAPK signaling, whereas other structurally similar analogs showed no such activity. The most potent compounds possess specific substituents at key positions on the cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core, including R8 selected from the group consisting of —H, —OCH₃, —Br, —F, or —Cl, and R9 selected from the group consisting of —O—CH₃ or —O—(CH₂)₂—NH—CH₃. Among these, the compound bearing R8 as —H and R9 as —O—(CH₂)₂—NH—CH₃ demonstrated the highest potency, with an IC₅₀ of 25 nM for inhibition of KRAS activation in cellular assays. These derivatives do not directly bind to KRAS or interfere with nucleotide exchange but instead target the prohibitin complex at the plasma membrane, thereby preventing the segregation of phosphatidylserine and phosphatidic acid into lipid microdomains required for KRAS nanoclustering. As a result, KRAS fails to adopt its active conformation and cannot recruit downstream effectors such as CRAF, leading to suppression of the MAPK pathway. The invention further encompasses the use of these flavagline derivatives for the specific inhibition of proliferation in cell populations harboring oncogenic KRAS mutations, including but not limited to those with G12D, G12V, G12C, and Q61H substitutions. The compounds are suitable for formulation into pharmaceutical compositions for oral, intravenous, or intraperitoneal administration, and are particularly suited for the prevention or treatment of neoplastic diseases in which RAS signaling is pathologically hyperactivated, such as non-small cell lung carcinoma, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and colorectal cancer. Preferred embodiments include compositions containing the flavagline derivative with R8 as —H and R9 as —O—(CH₂)₂—NH—CH₃, optionally in combination with other targeted agents or chemotherapeutics, and formulations designed for sustained release or targeted delivery to tumor tissue.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

- The invention is demonstrated through a series of in vitro and in vivo experiments that establish the mechanism of action, efficacy, and specificity of the disclosed flavagline derivatives. In vitro studies using human cancer cell lines harboring KRAS mutations, including HCT-116, ASPC-1, and Calu-1, showed that treatment with the lead compound at concentrations as low as 25 nM significantly reduced GTP-loading of KRAS, as measured by active RAS pull-down assays and NanoBiT-based live-cell interaction assays. The compound inhibited the formation of KRAS nanoclusters, as confirmed by FLIM-FRET imaging and immunogold electron microscopy, without affecting the clustering of HRAS or NRAS. Soft agar colony formation assays revealed a dose-dependent suppression of anchorage-independent growth, with IC₅₀ values ranging from 6 to 20 nM, correlating precisely with the inhibition of KRAS activation. MTT assays confirmed a comparable reduction in cell viability across multiple KRAS-mutant lines, while non-mutant cell lines exhibited significantly less sensitivity. The compound did not alter the phosphorylation status of EGFR or other upstream receptors, confirming its action downstream of receptor activation. In vivo studies employed autochthonous mouse models of non-small cell lung cancer induced by doxycycline-driven expression of KRAS G12D. Administration of the flavagline derivative at 2.5 mg/kg body weight, intraperitoneally three times per week for six weeks, resulted in a statistically significant reduction in the number and size of lung nodules compared to vehicle-treated controls, without inducing measurable hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity as assessed by serum transaminase and creatinine levels. Cellular thermal shift assays confirmed direct binding of the compound to prohibitin 1, stabilizing the PHB1-KRAS complex at elevated temperatures, while siRNA-mediated knockdown of PHB1 phenocopied the inhibitory effects on RAS activation. The compound was shown to disrupt the interaction between PHB1 and phosphatidylserine and phosphatidic acid, as demonstrated by lipid-binding strip assays and biosensor-based FLIM-FRET analysis. These findings collectively establish that the flavagline derivatives of this invention act as potent, selective, and non-covalent inhibitors of KRAS activation by targeting the prohibitin-lipid scaffold essential for KRAS nanoclustering, thereby offering a novel therapeutic modality for RAS-driven cancers.
