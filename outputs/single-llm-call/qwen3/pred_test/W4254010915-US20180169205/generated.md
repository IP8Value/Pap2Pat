# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccine development, specifically to novel hybrid protein compositions designed to elicit T cell-independent humoral immune responses. The invention encompasses engineered flagellin-based filaments that present conformationally intact antigenic domains on their surface, enabling direct crosslinking of B cell receptors without the requirement for T helper cell involvement. These compositions are particularly useful for inducing protective antibody responses in immunocompromised individuals, including those with T cell deficiencies such as HIV/AIDS, DiGeorge syndrome, or patients undergoing immunosuppressive therapies.

### Related Art

The adaptive immune system relies heavily on T helper cell-mediated activation of B lymphocytes to generate high-affinity, class-switched antibody responses against pathogens. This T cell-dependent pathway is essential for long-lasting immunity but is severely impaired in individuals with compromised T cell function. In such settings, the immune system can still mount protective responses through alternative pathways involving innate-like B cells, particularly B1 and marginal zone B cells, which respond to repetitive, polymeric antigens in a T cell-independent manner. These B cells become activated when their surface B cell receptors undergo clustering, a process that requires antigens to present multiple copies of an epitope at precise spatial intervals, typically exceeding 500 nanometers in length to effectively bridge the spacing between receptors on resting B cells. While certain bacterial polysaccharides and viral capsids have been explored as T cell-independent antigens, their structural instability, low immunogenicity, and inability to induce class switching to IgG or IgA have limited their utility as vaccine platforms. Flagellin, the primary structural component of bacterial flagella, has been recognized for its potent adjuvant properties due to its ability to activate Toll-like receptor 5 and the NLRC4 inflammasome. However, prior studies have not demonstrated that flagellin filaments can function as a scaffold for T cell-independent antigen presentation, particularly when engineered to display foreign conformational epitopes. The D3 domain of flagellin, located at the surface of the filament and known to be dispensable for polymerization, has been identified as a potential site for antigen insertion, yet no prior work has successfully replaced this domain with a full-length, folded viral envelope protein while preserving both filament integrity and antigenicity. Furthermore, the ability of flagellin-based constructs to induce IgM, IgG3, and IgA responses — hallmarks of T cell-independent immunity — has not been systematically evaluated in T cell-restricted models, leaving a critical gap in the development of vaccines for immunodeficient populations.

## SUMMARY

The invention is motivated by the urgent need to develop vaccine platforms capable of inducing robust humoral immunity in the absence of functional T helper cells. This is achieved through the design of a hybrid flagellin protein composition in which the solvent-exposed D3 domain of flagellin is replaced with a structurally intact, conformationally dependent antigenic domain derived from a pathogenic microorganism. The resulting hybrid protein retains the ability to self-assemble into long, filamentous structures that display multiple copies of the inserted antigen at a spatial density sufficient to crosslink B cell receptors and trigger T cell-independent B cell activation. The invention encompasses not only the hybrid protein itself but also its polymeric form, methods for its production, and its use as a vaccine component capable of eliciting IgM, IgG3, and IgA responses even in T cell-deficient hosts. The hybrid flagellin composition is engineered to preserve the α-helical core structure of flagellin while incorporating a heterologous antigen that maintains its native folding and epitope presentation. The invention further includes nucleic acid sequences encoding the hybrid protein, expression systems for its recombinant production, and pharmaceutical compositions comprising the hybrid filament in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers. The method of inducing a T cell-independent immune response involves administering the polymeric hybrid flagellin composition to a subject, thereby stimulating B cell receptor clustering and subsequent plasma cell differentiation without the need for cognate T cell help. The resulting immune response is characterized by the production of antigen-specific antibodies of multiple isotypes, including IgM and IgG3, indicative of a T cell-independent mechanism, and in certain contexts, IgA, suggesting potential mucosal immunogenicity. The invention provides a novel vaccine scaffold that overcomes the limitations of conventional subunit vaccines and live attenuated formulations, particularly in populations with compromised adaptive immunity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The hybrid protein composition of the invention comprises a flagellin backbone derived from bacterial flagellin, wherein the D3 domain is entirely replaced by a heterologous antigenic polypeptide that retains its native three-dimensional conformation. The flagellin component is selected from the flagellin proteins of Gram-negative bacteria, including but not limited to Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium, and is engineered to preserve the D0, D1, and D2 domains responsible for polymerization and Toll-like receptor 5 binding. The heterologous antigen is selected from conformationally dependent epitopes of viral, bacterial, or parasitic pathogens, including but not limited to envelope glycoproteins of flaviviruses such as Dengue virus serotype 2, Zika virus, or West Nile virus; hemagglutinin of influenza virus; spike protein of coronaviruses; or surface antigens of malaria parasites. The replacement of the D3 domain is accomplished through genetic fusion using flexible glycine-serine linkers flanking both termini of the inserted antigen, ensuring structural independence and minimizing steric interference with flagellin polymerization. The hybrid protein may be further modified to include a C-terminal hexahistidine tag or other purification epitopes for recombinant expression and isolation. Multiple distinct hybrid flagellin proteins, each presenting a different antigenic domain, may be co-polymerized to generate multivalent filaments capable of simultaneously targeting multiple pathogens or serotypes. The hybrid protein is produced recombinantly using eukaryotic expression systems such as baculovirus-infected insect cells, which enable proper post-translational modification and secretion of the full-length fusion protein. Upon purification, the hybrid protein is induced to polymerize under physiological conditions using high concentrations of ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate, resulting in filamentous structures with diameters exceeding 30 nanometers and lengths extending to several micrometers. These filaments retain the antigenic integrity of the inserted domain, as confirmed by binding to conformation-specific monoclonal antibodies and immunogold labeling. When administered to a subject, the polymeric hybrid flagellin composition induces a humoral immune response characterized by the production of antigen-specific IgM and IgG3 antibodies, even in the absence of functional T helper cells, as demonstrated in T cell receptor transgenic mouse models. The vaccine composition may be formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers such as phosphate-buffered saline, and optionally combined with adjuvants that enhance innate immune recognition without requiring T cell involvement, such as CpG oligonucleotides or saponin derivatives. Administration may occur via intranasal, intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, or intramuscular routes, with intranasal delivery shown to preferentially induce mucosal IgA responses. The nucleic acid encoding the hybrid flagellin protein may be delivered in plasmid or viral vector form for in vivo expression, enabling sustained antigen presentation and prolonged immune stimulation. The filamentous nature of the composition is essential to its function, as monomeric forms fail to induce significant antibody titers or B cell receptor clustering in vitro or in vivo.

### Example 1

The hybrid flagellin protein was designed by replacing the D3 domain of Salmonella flagellin (residues 185–285) with the full-length envelope glycoprotein of Dengue virus serotype 2, flanked by two glycine-serine linkers (GGGSGGGS) at the N- and C-termini. The resulting gene construct was synthesized by overlapping PCR amplification of the N-terminal and C-terminal fragments of flagellin, followed by ligation with the DENV2 E gene sequence cloned into a pENTR vector. The construct was verified by DNA sequencing and subsequently recombined into a baculovirus transfer vector for expression in SF9 insect cells. Recombinant protein was secreted into the culture supernatant and purified via cobalt affinity chromatography, yielding a single band at approximately 85 kDa on SDS-PAGE under denaturing conditions, with minor degradation products observed. Circular dichroism spectroscopy confirmed that the hybrid protein retained a hybrid secondary structure profile, combining the α-helical signature of flagellin with the β-sheet content of DENV2 E. Polymerization of the purified protein was induced in 1.5 M ammonium sulfate, resulting in filamentous structures observable by negative stain transmission electron microscopy with a mean diameter of 35 nm, compared to 20 nm for native flagellin. Immunogold labeling using a DENV2-specific monoclonal IgG antibody confirmed the surface exposure of the DENV2 E domain, with gold particles localized exclusively to the outer periphery of the filament. Binding of a recombinant DENV2-specific IgA antibody to the hybrid filament was confirmed by ELISA, demonstrating antigenic competence. In vivo immunization of C57BL/6J mice via intranasal administration of polymeric hFliC induced robust serum IgA responses, while intraperitoneal delivery elicited strong IgG and IgM titers. B cell crosslinking was demonstrated using the 4G2 B cell hybridoma, where confocal microscopy revealed reorganization of surface Ig receptors into clustered patterns upon exposure to polymeric hFliC, but not monomeric controls. Statistical analysis confirmed significant differences in antibody titers between polymeric and monomeric groups, with p-values below 0.001 for IgM and IgG responses.

### Example 2

The D3 domain of native flagellin was replaced with the Dengue virus serotype 2 envelope glycoprotein to generate a hybrid flagellin protein designated hFliC. The construct was expressed in a baculovirus system, and protein purity was confirmed by Coomassie staining and Western blot using anti-His-tag antibodies. Circular dichroism analysis revealed a secondary structure profile consistent with the combined α-helical and β-sheet content of the parent proteins. Negative stain transmission electron microscopy confirmed the formation of filaments with an increased diameter of 35 nm relative to native flagellin, and molecular modeling supported the spatial arrangement of the DENV2 E domain on the filament surface. Immunogold labeling with DENV2-specific IgG demonstrated specific binding to the filament periphery, confirming epitope accessibility. ELISA using recombinant DENV2-specific IgA confirmed high-affinity binding to the hybrid filament. In wild-type C57BL/6J mice, polymeric hFliC administered intranasally induced strong serum IgA responses, while intraperitoneal delivery elicited high-titer IgG and IgM responses, with IgG1 and IgG2a/b subclasses detected. In T cell receptor-restricted 6.5-TCR mice, polymeric hFliC administered intraperitoneally induced a significant IgM response comparable to wild-type controls and a specific IgG3 isotype response, absent in monomeric controls. No IgA response was detected in the T cell-restricted model, consistent with the absence of T cell-dependent class switching. Polymerized hFliC, but not monomer, induced B cell receptor clustering in vitro, as visualized by confocal microscopy of 4G2 hybridoma cells. These results demonstrate that the polymeric hybrid flagellin composition can induce T cell-independent antibody responses, including IgM and IgG3, even in the absence of functional T helper cells, establishing its utility as a vaccine scaffold for immunocompromised hosts.

## CONCLUSIONS

The hybrid flagellin filament composition disclosed herein represents a novel vaccine scaffold capable of inducing potent T cell-independent humoral immune responses. By replacing the solvent-exposed D3 domain of flagellin with a conformationally intact antigenic domain, the invention enables the presentation of repetitive epitopes on a filamentous structure that meets the spatial requirements for B cell receptor crosslinking. The resulting polymeric assemblies elicit antigen-specific IgM, IgG3, and in certain contexts, IgA responses, even in T cell-deficient models, without the need for exogenous adjuvants or T cell help. This platform overcomes critical limitations of existing subunit vaccines and live attenuated formulations, particularly for populations with compromised adaptive immunity. The ability to engineer multiple antigenic domains into a single filament or to co-assemble distinct filaments enables the development of multivalent or universal vaccines against pathogens with multiple serotypes, such as Dengue virus. The invention thus provides a transformative approach to vaccine design for immunodeficiency, aging, and global health applications.
