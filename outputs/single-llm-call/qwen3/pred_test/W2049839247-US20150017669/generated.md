# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Glycan analysis has emerged as a powerful approach for the detection and characterization of disease states, particularly in oncology, where alterations in protein glycosylation patterns serve as early indicators of malignant transformation. The human serum glycome, composed primarily of N-linked glycans attached to circulating glycoproteins, reflects dynamic changes in cellular metabolism, immune activity, and tumor microenvironment signaling. These glycan structures, which include variations in branching, sialylation, fucosylation, and galactosylation, are increasingly recognized as functional biomarkers rather than mere structural appendages. In cancer, aberrant glycosylation is not a passive consequence but an active driver of metastasis, immune evasion, and angiogenesis, making the glycome a rich source of diagnostic information. Despite this biological significance, current clinical cancer screening methods remain largely reliant on single-protein biomarkers such as CA-125, PSA, or CEA, which suffer from low sensitivity in early-stage disease, high false-positive rates due to benign conditions, and limited specificity across tumor subtypes. These limitations stem from the inherent heterogeneity of cancer biology and the inability of single-molecule assays to capture the systemic complexity of disease progression. Mass spectrometry-based glycomic profiling offers a compelling alternative by enabling simultaneous, high-resolution detection of hundreds of glycan species in a single analytical run. Among mass spectrometric platforms, MALDI-TOF has gained prominence due to its speed, robustness, and compatibility with complex biological matrices such as serum. However, the widespread adoption of MALDI-TOF for clinical glycan analysis has been hindered by persistent issues of analytical reproducibility, particularly in sample preparation steps such as glycan extraction and matrix loading. Variability in these steps introduces significant technical noise, obscuring biologically meaningful differences between patient and control samples. While MALDI-FTICR provides superior mass accuracy and resolution, its high cost, long acquisition times, and stringent sample purity requirements render it impractical for routine clinical screening. In contrast, MALDI-TOF, despite its lower resolving power, offers sufficient precision for glycan biomarker discovery when technical variability is minimized. A critical bottleneck in achieving reproducibility lies in the inconsistent handling of glycan samples during extraction and deposition onto the target plate. Conventional methods rely on manual pipetting, uncontrolled drying conditions, and non-uniform matrix application, all of which contribute to high coefficients of variation in peak intensities. Furthermore, the enzymatic release of N-glycans from serum proteins using peptide-N-glycosidase F (PNGase F) is often performed under suboptimal conditions that yield incomplete digestion or protein carryover, leading to ion suppression and inaccurate quantification. The need for a standardized, automated, and highly reproducible workflow that integrates enzymatic digestion, glycan purification, and precise matrix-assisted deposition has become imperative for translating glycan biomarkers from research to clinical diagnostics.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel, highly reproducible analytical method for the profiling of glycans in human serum samples for the purpose of cancer screening and diagnosis. This method is specifically designed to minimize the coefficient of variation in glycan peak intensities across replicate analyses, thereby enhancing the reliability and clinical utility of mass spectrometry-based biomarker discovery. The invention comprises a two-plate system that separates the enzymatic digestion and glycan extraction process from the final mass spectrometry loading step, ensuring precise control over each stage of sample preparation. The first plate, designated as Plate #1, functions as a micro-reactor for the denaturation of serum proteins and the subsequent enzymatic release of N-linked glycans under controlled microwave irradiation. The second plate, designated as Plate #2, is a pre-coated, micro-focus self-condensing target plate that enables uniform, reproducible deposition of glycans with minimal manual intervention. The method begins with the injection of a defined volume of human serum into Plate #1, where proteins are denatured through a series of controlled thermal cycles involving alternating exposure to boiling water and ambient temperature water bath. Following denaturation, PNGase F enzyme is introduced and activated under microwave irradiation at 37°C for a precisely timed duration, ensuring complete and consistent glycan release without protein degradation. The reaction is terminated by rapid cooling using a dedicated water bath cooling device, followed by precipitation of proteins with ice-cold ethanol. The supernatant containing released glycans is then transferred to a solid-phase extraction cartridge for purification, and fractions are collected based on acetonitrile concentration gradients. The purified glycan fractions are reconstituted in a defined volume of purified water and loaded onto Plate #2, which is pre-spotted with a homogeneous matrix of 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid (DHB) and sodium chloride, applied via a controlled heating and spraying process that ensures uniform crystallization across all sample spots. The drying of Plate #2 occurs under reduced pressure at a precisely regulated temperature and duration to prevent analyte degradation and promote consistent crystal formation. MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry is then performed using a reflectron mode with a defined m/z range, multiple laser shots per spot, and internal calibration to ensure accurate and reproducible peak detection. Preferred embodiments of Plate #1 include a thermally conductive polymer substrate with integrated temperature sensors and microwave-transparent walls to enable uniform heating. Preferred embodiments of Plate #2 include a hydrophilic surface coating composed of silica-based nanomaterials that promote even matrix crystallization and reduce analyte diffusion. The method further includes the use of a pre-spotted micro-focus plate with 1700 μm spot diameter, allowing for high-density sample analysis and minimizing cross-contamination. The glycan-releasing enzyme is selected from PNGase F due to its specificity for N-linked glycans and compatibility with aqueous buffers. The matrix is optimized to contain 70% acetonitrile and 12 mM sodium chloride to enhance ionization efficiency and suppress background noise. The invention further encompasses the use of specific glycan fractions—namely, those eluted at 10% and 20% acetonitrile concentrations—as the primary sources of diagnostic biomarkers, excluding higher acetonitrile fractions that introduce non-glycan contaminants. The method achieves a coefficient of variation in peak intensities below 15% across replicate analyses, a level of reproducibility previously unattainable with conventional protocols. This enhanced precision enables the reliable identification and quantification of subtle glycan intensity differences between healthy and cancerous states, forming the foundation for multibiomarker panels capable of distinguishing ovarian cancer from benign conditions and healthy controls with superior sensitivity and specificity compared to existing clinical markers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is implemented through a precisely orchestrated sequence of steps that integrate enzymatic digestion, glycan purification, and matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) into a unified, reproducible workflow. The method begins with the injection of a 50 μL aliquot of human serum into each well of Plate #1, which serves as a micro-reactor designed to withstand thermal cycling and microwave irradiation. The serum is mixed with a denaturing buffer containing ammonium bicarbonate and dithiothreitol, and subjected to four cycles of alternating 15-second exposure to boiling water and 15-second immersion in a room-temperature water bath, resulting in complete protein unfolding without aggregation. Following denaturation, 1000 units of peptide-N-glycosidase F (PNGase F) are added to each well, and the plate is placed within a microwave irradiation system operating at 400 W power output for exactly 10 minutes at a constant temperature of 37°C. This microwave-mediated enzyme reaction ensures complete and uniform glycan release across all samples, eliminating the variability associated with conventional overnight incubations. Immediately following digestion, the plate is transferred to a dedicated cooling device that rapidly lowers the temperature to 4°C using a circulating ice-water bath, halting enzymatic activity and preventing glycan degradation. Ice-cold ethanol is then added to precipitate proteins, and the mixture is stored at −80°C for one hour to ensure complete protein aggregation. The supernatant, containing the released glycans, is collected by centrifugation and transferred to graphitized carbon cartridges for solid-phase extraction. Cartridges are first equilibrated with 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid in 80% acetonitrile/water, washed with nanopure water, and then sequentially eluted with 10%, 20%, and 40% acetonitrile solutions containing 0.05% TFA. The 10% and 20% acetonitrile fractions are retained for downstream analysis, as these fractions yield the most informative and reproducible glycan profiles. Each fraction is dried under reduced pressure using a vacuum concentrator at 30°C to avoid thermal degradation. The dried glycans are reconstituted in 15 μL of purified water and loaded onto Plate #2, a pre-spotted MALDI target plate coated with a homogeneous matrix of 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid and 12 mM sodium chloride. The matrix is applied via a controlled spraying system on a heated plate maintained at 40°C, ensuring uniform crystallization and minimal spot-to-spot variation. Each glycan solution (1 μL) is deposited onto a 1700 μm diameter spot and dried under reduced pressure for 15 minutes at 25°C. The plate is then analyzed using a 4800 Plus MALDI TOF/TOF analyzer in positive-ion reflectron mode, with an m/z range of 1000 to 3000, 25 laser shots per position, and data collected from 40 distinct positions per sample, yielding a total of 1000 laser shots per sample. Internal calibration is performed using twelve N-glycan standards with a tolerance of ±0.2 m/z. The resulting mass spectra are processed using an in-house software platform that extracts centroid m/z values and absolute peak intensities, normalizes them by total ion abundance, and calculates the coefficient of variation across replicates. The method achieves a mean relative standard deviation of less than 15% across all detected peaks, a level of reproducibility that enables the reliable detection of subtle intensity differences between patient and control groups. The invention further encompasses the application of this method to the screening of ovarian cancer, wherein biomarker peaks are selected based on statistical significance (P < 10−9), detection frequency (>95%), and area under the ROC curve (AUC > 0.72 for 10% fraction, AUC > 0.75 for 20% fraction). Cut-off values for each biomarker are determined from the training set using standard sensitivity thresholds, and a composite scoring algorithm integrates the weighted contributions of 15 biomarker peaks to classify samples as cancerous or non-cancerous. This system is validated in blind test sets, demonstrating sensitivity and specificity superior to CA-125, particularly in early-stage and recurrent ovarian cancer cases. The use of Plate #1 and Plate #2 in tandem eliminates manual pipetting variability, reduces contamination, and ensures batch-to-batch consistency, making the method suitable for high-throughput clinical laboratories. The invention further includes modifications for the analysis of other cancers, such as breast, prostate, and gastric malignancies, by adjusting the glycan fractionation parameters and biomarker selection criteria according to disease-specific glycan signatures.

### Glycan Analysis from Human Serum

Mass spectra of glycans derived from human serum exhibit a complex but reproducible pattern of ion signals corresponding to N-linked glycan structures ranging from 1000 to 3000 m/z. Experimental procedures begin with the precise volumetric injection of serum into Plate #1, followed by thermal denaturation and microwave-assisted enzymatic digestion as described. The resulting glycan profiles consistently reveal a core set of peaks associated with biantennary, triantennary, and tetraantennary structures bearing sialic acid, fucose, and galactose modifications. Peak intensities are normalized to total ion abundance, and the coefficient of variation across quadruplicate analyses is maintained below 15%, demonstrating exceptional technical reproducibility. Peaks are annotated using GlycoMod with a mass tolerance of ±10 ppm, and those with high detection frequency and significant intensity differences between patient and control groups are identified as candidate biomarkers. The method enables the detection of both glycan and non-glycan ion signals, with the latter contributing meaningfully to diagnostic classification when they exhibit consistent, disease-associated intensity shifts.

### Example 2

Mass spectra of glycans from ovarian tumor patients reveal distinct patterns of peak intensities compared to healthy controls, with specific m/z values showing statistically significant elevation or suppression in cancer samples. These include peaks at 1692.7, 1845.8, 1987.3, 2112.5, and 2301.9 m/z, which correspond to sialylated and fucosylated N-glycans. Molecular structures are inferred from accurate mass measurements and validated against public glycan databases. Limitations of individual biomarkers include partial overlap with benign conditions and variability across histological subtypes, necessitating the use of a multibiomarker panel to achieve high diagnostic accuracy.

### Example 3

Traditional methods of glycan analysis rely on manual glycan extraction and matrix spotting, resulting in coefficient of variation values exceeding 25% across replicate analyses. In contrast, the method of the present invention, utilizing Plate #1 for enzymatic digestion and Plate #2 for pre-spotted matrix loading, reduces the coefficient of variation to below 15%, significantly improving the reliability of quantitative comparisons between sample groups. This enhancement is attributable to the elimination of manual pipetting errors, uniform matrix crystallization, and controlled drying conditions.

### Example 4

Validation of reproducibility across 100 serum samples from patients aged 20 to 89 years demonstrates a mean relative standard deviation of 7.28% across all detected peaks, with no significant correlation between age and peak intensity variability. This indicates that the method is robust across diverse demographic groups and suitable for population-level screening applications.

### Example 5

Glycan profiling analysis of training and validation sets reveals that the 15-biomarker panel achieves an AUC of 0.89 in the training set and 0.84 in the blind test set, outperforming CA-125 (AUC 0.78). Cut-off values for each biomarker are established using 70% sensitivity thresholds, and cross-validation confirms consistent classification accuracy. The method enables high-throughput screening with minimal sample volume, and its commercial implementation is feasible using standardized reagent kits and automated liquid handling systems. Modifications for other cancers are readily adaptable by recalibrating biomarker selection criteria based on disease-specific glycan signatures.
