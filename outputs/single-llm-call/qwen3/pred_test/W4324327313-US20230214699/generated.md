## DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### 1. Technical Field

The present invention relates to quantum computing systems and methods for simulating stochastic processes with applications in financial modeling, statistical physics, and risk analysis. Specifically, the invention provides a novel quantum algorithmic framework for efficiently encoding, preparing, and manipulating analog representations of stochastic trajectories using quantum state preparation techniques, discrete cosine transforms, and recursive data loading protocols. The invention enables the coherent simulation of continuous-time stochastic processes such as Brownian motion and fractional Brownian motion on quantum hardware with logarithmic resource scaling in the number of time steps, thereby achieving a quantum advantage over classical Monte Carlo methods in estimating time-averaged functionals of these processes.

### 2. Description of Related Art

Stochastic processes are fundamental mathematical constructs used to model time-dependent phenomena across disciplines including finance, physics, biology, and engineering. Classical simulation of such processes typically relies on discrete-time sampling and Monte Carlo techniques, which require computational resources scaling polynomially with the desired precision and the number of time steps. While quantum computing offers theoretical promise for accelerating such simulations through amplitude estimation and superposition encoding, practical implementation has been hindered by the exponential cost of digitally compiling classical simulation algorithms into quantum circuits. Existing quantum approaches rely on state preparation methods that encode entire trajectories as bitstrings in computational basis states, resulting in circuit depths and qubit counts that scale linearly or worse with the number of time steps. These methods fail to exploit the spectral structure inherent in many stochastic processes, particularly those with stationary or self-similar properties such as Brownian motion. Furthermore, the requirement to repeat the simulation circuit O(1/ε) times to achieve quadratic speedup in mean estimation renders these approaches infeasible on near-term quantum hardware due to gate depth limitations and error accumulation. The absence of a gate-efficient, analog representation of stochastic trajectories that is compatible with quantum amplitude estimation has thus remained a critical barrier to realizing practical quantum speedups in Monte Carlo applications.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel quantum method for simulating stochastic processes by encoding their trajectories as analog quantum states, wherein the amplitude of each basis state corresponds to the value of the process at a given time index. This representation leverages the exponential state space of quantum systems to encode a T-step trajectory using only O(log T) qubits, enabling a dramatic reduction in resource requirements compared to classical or digital quantum simulations. The method employs a spectral decomposition of the stochastic process via the discrete cosine transform, which maps the trajectory into a superposition of frequency components with amplitudes drawn from a known probability distribution. By recursively applying a quantum data loader algorithm to prepare the Fourier coefficients and then applying the quantum Fourier transform, the invention constructs a coherent analog representation of the stochastic process that is directly compatible with quantum amplitude estimation routines. This approach achieves a quantum speedup in estimating time-averaged quantities such as realized variance or mean-squared displacement, with runtime scaling as O(polylog(T)/ε^c) for constants c < 2, outperforming classical Monte Carlo methods that scale as O(T/ε²). The invention further enables the preparation of mixed quantum states representing ensembles of trajectories through the use of controlled rotations and trace operations, allowing for the simulation of post-selected processes and statistical hypothesis testing without explicit sampling.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention provides a quantum method for simulating a stochastic process by representing its trajectories as quantum states in which the time index is encoded in the computational basis and the process value is encoded in the amplitude. The method begins by defining a quantum system composed of a time register and an amplitude register, where the time register consists of log T qubits to represent T discrete time points. The stochastic process is represented as a mixed quantum state σ, whose diagonal elements in the time basis correspond to the probability distribution over possible trajectories. To prepare this state, the invention first computes the discrete cosine transform (DCT) of the stochastic process, which decomposes the trajectory into a sum of cosine basis functions with stochastic amplitudes. The DCT is shown to be equivalent to the real part of the quantum Fourier transform, allowing its implementation via a logarithmic-depth quantum circuit composed of Hadamard and phase gates. The amplitudes of the DCT coefficients are drawn from a probability distribution determined by the spectral properties of the process—for example, for fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter H, the k-th coefficient has variance proportional to k^(-2H-1). The invention then introduces a quantum data loader algorithm that prepares a quantum state encoding these amplitudes by recursively applying beam splitter gates whose rotation angles are sampled from a known distribution derived from beta and gamma distributions. This data loader is applied twice in succession: first to generate a distribution over the angles used in the amplitude encoding, and then to apply those angles coherently to produce the desired quantum state. This “data loading the data loader” procedure ensures that the resulting state is a coherent superposition over trajectories, with the garbage register entangled with the randomness used to generate each trajectory. The final quantum state σ is obtained by applying the DCT circuit to the amplitude-encoded state and tracing out the angle registers. For Brownian motion, this procedure requires O(polylog(T) + poly(1/ε)) qubits and gate depth, achieving exponential savings over classical simulation. The linearity of the DCT ensures that the method generalizes to linear combinations of stochastic processes, including Lévy processes and Itô integrals, by composing the DCT with Toeplitz matrix multiplication implemented via circulant embedding and quantum Fourier transforms. The invention further describes a method for normalizing the resulting state by appending an ancilla qubit and applying a controlled rotation conditioned on the norm of the trajectory, followed by post-selection or amplitude amplification. The resulting state σ′ is then used as an oracle in a quantum amplitude estimation subroutine to estimate time-averaged functionals such as ∫ f(t)B(t)dt or the mean-squared displacement over a time window. ### Example Method

The method receives as input a description of a stochastic process defined over T discrete time steps, including its statistical properties such as drift, variance, and Hurst parameter. A first quantum circuit is determined to prepare a mixed quantum state ρ′ that encodes the discrete cosine transform of the process, where the DCT coefficients are sampled from a probability distribution derived from the spectral density of the process. The probability distribution over the DCT coefficients is computed analytically based on the known decay rate of the Fourier modes—for example, for fractional Brownian motion, the variance of the k-th coefficient is inversely proportional to k^(2H+1). The probability distribution of the data loader angles is then determined by mapping the DCT coefficient distribution through the recursive beam splitter structure of the data loader, yielding a set of independent angles whose distributions are expressed in closed form using beta and gamma functions. A first quantum circuit is executed to generate the mixed quantum state ρ′ by preparing the DCT coefficient amplitudes using the data loader with the computed angles and applying the discrete cosine transform circuit. The resulting state ρ′ is a coherent superposition over trajectories, with the garbage register encoding the randomness used to generate each trajectory. The method then applies quantum amplitude estimation to estimate a time-averaged functional of the process, achieving an additive error ε with a number of queries scaling as O(polylog(T)/ε^c) for c ∈ (3/2, 2), depending on the Hurst parameter.

### Description of a Computing System

The invention is implemented on a hybrid classical-quantum computing system comprising a classical computing system and a quantum computing system. The classical computing system includes a processor, chipset, memory, storage device, input devices, graphics adapter, network adapter, and display, and is configured to compute the probability distributions of the DCT coefficients and data loader angles, generate the quantum program, and interpret measurement outcomes. The quantum computing system includes a register of qubits, each controlled by a dedicated qubit controller that applies precise rotations, phase shifts, and entangling gates. The qubits are arranged in a linear or nearest-neighbor topology and are maintained at cryogenic temperatures to preserve coherence. The quantum routine is executed by transmitting a quantum program from the classical system to the quantum system, where the qubit controllers apply the sequence of gates corresponding to the data loader, DCT, and normalization modules. The resulting quantum state is measured in the computational basis, and the outcomes are recorded and processed by the classical system to compute estimates of the desired quantity. The architecture of the classical system includes a central processing unit executing software modules for quantum circuit compilation, error correction, and result analysis, while the quantum system comprises superconducting or trapped-ion qubits with individual microwave or laser control lines.
