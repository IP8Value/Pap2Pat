# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

- The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is a relativistic antisymmetric exchange interaction that arises in magnetic systems lacking inversion symmetry, enabling the stabilization of noncollinear spin textures such as chiral domain walls and magnetic skyrmions. This interaction originates from the coupling between spin-orbit coupling and broken spatial symmetry, typically at interfaces or surfaces where atomic layers terminate abruptly, inducing a preferred sense of rotation in the magnetic moments. The energy contribution of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is proportional to the scalar product of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya vector and the cross product of neighboring spin vectors, thereby dictating the handedness of spin spirals and the chirality of domain walls. Experimental efforts to characterize and exploit this interaction have primarily focused on interfaces between ferromagnetic layers and heavy transition metals such as tantalum, platinum, iridium, and tungsten, where strong spin-orbit coupling and orbital hybridization generate sizable Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interactions. These systems have enabled the development of spintronic devices based on current-driven domain wall motion and skyrmion manipulation. However, existing approaches are constrained by the reliance on rare, expensive, or toxic heavy elements, which limit scalability, cost-effectiveness, and integration with conventional semiconductor technologies. Furthermore, the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction in these systems is typically fixed by the atomic composition and layer thickness, rendering dynamic tuning of magnetic chirality impractical without external fields or electrical currents. A critical unmet need exists for a platform capable of reversibly and precisely modulating the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction at room temperature using chemically reversible, earth-abundant species, thereby enabling adaptive spin textures without requiring complex multilayer engineering or high-power stimuli.

- The mechanism underlying the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction at interfaces involves the asymmetric hybridization of electronic orbitals across a symmetry-broken boundary, combined with spin-orbit coupling that lifts the degeneracy between left- and right-handed spin configurations. In conventional systems, this asymmetry is engineered through the use of heavy elements with large atomic numbers, whose 5d or 6p orbitals strongly hybridize with the 3d orbitals of ferromagnets, generating a net spin-orbit torque that favors a specific rotational sense of magnetization. The magnitude and sign of the resulting Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya vector depend on the electronic structure of the interface, including orbital overlap, charge transfer, and the induced electric dipole moment perpendicular to the interface plane. Recent theoretical models have suggested that even light elements, when chemisorbed onto ferromagnetic surfaces, can induce substantial Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interactions if they exhibit a large electronegativity mismatch with the underlying metal, thereby creating a strong interfacial electric field that couples with spin-orbit coupling to break time-reversal symmetry in an asymmetric manner. Despite these predictions, experimental validation of such mechanisms has been lacking, particularly for non-metallic species that do not form stable oxides or compounds but instead remain as adsorbed atoms on the surface.

- Experimental efforts to quantify and manipulate the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction have relied heavily on spin-polarized low-energy electron microscopy, magneto-optical Kerr effect measurements, and current-driven domain wall velocity analysis to infer the sign and magnitude of the interaction. These techniques have successfully characterized Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interactions at metal-metal and metal-oxide interfaces but have not been applied to dynamically tunable systems where the interaction can be altered post-fabrication through surface chemistry. Most reported systems exhibit static Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interactions that are determined during deposition and remain invariant under ambient conditions, limiting their utility in reconfigurable devices. Attempts to electrically tune the interaction via gate voltages have been hindered by poor interfacial control, slow response times, and limited magnitude of modulation, often requiring cryogenic temperatures or high voltages incompatible with practical applications.

- Current approaches to controlling magnetic chirality are fundamentally limited by their inability to reversibly switch the sign of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction without altering the physical structure of the device. The reliance on fixed interfaces composed of heavy metals introduces material bottlenecks, increases fabrication complexity, and restricts device scalability. Moreover, the inability to modulate the interaction on demand prevents the realization of adaptive spintronic architectures capable of reconfiguring their magnetic topology in response to environmental stimuli. The absence of a chemically reversible, room-temperature, and scalable method for tuning the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction represents a significant barrier to the development of next-generation memory, logic, and neuromorphic devices that require dynamic control over magnetic textures.

- There is a pressing need for a novel class of magnetic heterostructures capable of generating, reversing, and precisely tuning the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction through the chemisorption of light, abundant, and reversible species such as oxygen and hydrogen. Such a system would eliminate dependence on rare heavy metals, enable real-time control of magnetic chirality without external currents or fields, and open pathways to magneto-ionic and gas-sensing applications where magnetic properties are directly coupled to chemical environments. The development of such a platform would represent a paradigm shift in spintronics by replacing static interface engineering with dynamic surface chemistry as the primary means of controlling magnetic order.

## SUMMARY

- Embodiments of the invention disclose a novel class of magnetic heterostructures in which the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction and perpendicular magnetic anisotropy are induced and reversibly controlled by the chemisorption of atomic species on ferromagnetic surfaces, enabling dynamic tuning of magnetic chirality and spin texture topology at room temperature. These systems exploit the electronegativity difference between chemisorbed atoms and ferromagnetic metals to generate a strong interfacial electric dipole moment that couples with spin-orbit coupling to produce a sizable and sign-tunable Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction. The invention enables the reversible switching of domain wall chirality and the creation or annihilation of magnetic skyrmions through controlled exposure to gaseous species, without requiring structural modifications or external magnetic fields.

- Oxygen-induced Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is generated upon chemisorption of atomic oxygen onto ferromagnetic surfaces such as nickel, cobalt, or their bilayers, where the large electronegativity difference between oxygen and the metal drives charge transfer and hybridization of oxygen p-orbitals with metal d-orbitals, resulting in a strong interfacial electric dipole perpendicular to the surface. This dipole couples with spin-orbit coupling to break inversion symmetry and induce a Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya vector whose magnitude is comparable to that observed at ferromagnet/heavy metal interfaces, despite oxygen’s low atomic number. The sign and strength of the interaction are directly dependent on the oxygen coverage, enabling continuous modulation of magnetic chirality from left-handed to right-handed configurations.

- The evolution of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is governed by the coverage of chemisorbed oxygen, with distinct chirality states observed at sub-monolayer coverages ranging from 0.05 to 0.30 monolayers. At low coverage, the interaction is weak and chirality remains unchanged; at intermediate coverage, the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya vector reverses sign, inducing a complete switching of domain wall handedness; at higher coverage, the interaction saturates and stabilizes a new chiral state. This coverage-dependent behavior allows precise, continuous control over the magnetic texture without altering the underlying layer structure.

- The magnitude of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction induced by chemisorbed oxygen exceeds 0.6 meV per atom, rivaling the strengths observed at interfaces with platinum and palladium, and is sufficient to stabilize magnetic skyrmions and chiral domain walls in ultrathin ferromagnetic films at room temperature. This magnitude is quantified through direct imaging of domain wall spin textures and validated by density functional theory calculations that confirm the origin of the interaction in charge transfer and orbital hybridization.

- The apparatus for generating the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction comprises a multilayer stack including a ferromagnetic layer, a non-magnetic spacer layer, and a substrate, wherein the ferromagnetic layer is exposed to a controlled atmosphere of oxygen or hydrogen gas to induce chemisorption. The system is maintained under ultra-high vacuum conditions during fabrication and operation to ensure surface purity and reproducible adsorption kinetics.

- The ferromagnet materials include nickel, cobalt, iron, or their alloys, deposited as single or bilayer films with thicknesses ranging from 0.5 to 5 monolayers, and are grown epitaxially on substrates such as tungsten or palladium to ensure structural order and uniform magnetic anisotropy.

- The substance chemisorbed on the surface is selected from atomic oxygen, atomic hydrogen, or other electronegative species with a Pauling electronegativity greater than 2.5, including nitrogen, fluorine, or selected organic molecules such as BCP, wherein the chemisorption induces a measurable change in the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction and perpendicular magnetic anisotropy.

- The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is controlled by varying the thickness of the palladium or tungsten spacer layer, which modulates the effective Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya coupling between the ferromagnetic layer and the substrate, enabling the system to be tuned from a W-like right-handed state to a Pd-like left-handed state, thereby providing a reference frame for quantifying the contribution of chemisorbed species.

- The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is further controlled by the identity of the chemisorbed substance, wherein oxygen induces a right-handed interaction on nickel and cobalt surfaces, while hydrogen induces a left-handed interaction on the same surfaces, enabling sign reversal through chemical substitution.

- The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is controlled by the thickness of the chemisorbed layer, wherein sub-monolayer coverages between 0.05 and 0.30 monolayers produce a continuous, reversible transition in domain wall chirality, with saturation occurring beyond 0.30 monolayers.

- A magnetic skyrmion is generated by increasing the oxygen coverage on a ferromagnetic layer initially exhibiting a left-handed domain wall chirality, thereby inducing a chirality transition that stabilizes a topologically nontrivial spin texture with a winding number of ±1.

- A specific apparatus embodiment comprises a vacuum chamber equipped with a gas dosing system, a spin-polarized low-energy electron microscope for real-time imaging of magnetic textures, and a substrate holder capable of precise temperature control, wherein the ferromagnetic multilayer is exposed to controlled doses of oxygen or hydrogen, and the resulting magnetic chirality is monitored in situ through SPLEEM imaging.

- The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is controlled by varying the thickness of a palladium or tungsten layer interposed between the ferromagnetic layer and the substrate, wherein a thickness range of 1.5 to 4.0 monolayers enables continuous tuning of the effective Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction from right-handed to left-handed dominance.

- Perpendicular magnetic anisotropy is introduced through the interfacial hybridization between the ferromagnetic layer and the chemisorbed species, wherein the induced charge redistribution enhances the spin-orbit coupling at the interface and increases the energy barrier for in-plane magnetization alignment.

- The perpendicular magnetic anisotropy is controlled by the identity of the chemisorbed substance, wherein oxygen enhances the anisotropy more strongly than hydrogen due to greater charge transfer and orbital hybridization.

- The perpendicular magnetic anisotropy is controlled by the thickness of the chemisorbed layer, wherein an optimal coverage of 0.15 to 0.25 monolayers maximizes the anisotropy constant, beyond which saturation or degradation occurs due to surface reconstruction.

- A method for generating perpendicular magnetic anisotropy involves exposing a ferromagnetic thin film to a controlled partial pressure of atomic oxygen or hydrogen, wherein the resulting chemisorption induces a surface dipole moment that modifies the orbital occupancy of the ferromagnetic layer and enhances the magnetic anisotropy energy by up to 50%.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction and perpendicular magnetic anisotropy are induced at the interface between a ferromagnetic layer and a chemisorbed atomic species, wherein the electronegativity mismatch between the species and the metal drives charge transfer and creates a strong interfacial electric dipole perpendicular to the surface. This dipole couples with spin-orbit coupling to break inversion symmetry, resulting in a finite Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya vector that stabilizes a preferred chirality in domain walls and skyrmions. The same charge redistribution enhances the spin-orbit coupling at the interface, leading to an increase in the perpendicular magnetic anisotropy energy, which suppresses in-plane magnetization and enables stable out-of-plane alignment of magnetic moments in ultrathin films.

- The evolution of domain wall chirality is directly dependent on the coverage of chemisorbed oxygen, wherein a transition from left-handed to right-handed Néel-type domain walls is observed as oxygen coverage increases from 0.05 to 0.25 monolayers. This transition is reversible and occurs without structural modification, as confirmed by in situ spin-polarized low-energy electron microscopy. The chirality switching is attributed to the sign reversal of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya vector, which is induced by the increasing strength of the oxygen-induced electric dipole moment that overcomes the intrinsic Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction of the underlying metal spacer.

- The effective Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction in the multilayer system is tuned by precisely controlling the thickness of the palladium spacer layer, which mediates the coupling between the ferromagnetic layer and the tungsten substrate. As the palladium thickness increases from 1.5 to 3.5 monolayers, the effective Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction transitions from a right-handed, tungsten-dominated state to a left-handed, palladium-dominated state, providing a tunable reference frame for quantifying the contribution of chemisorbed species. This tunability enables the unambiguous determination of the sign and magnitude of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction induced by oxygen.

- The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction induced by chemisorbed oxygen is characterized by a magnitude of approximately 0.63 ± 0.26 meV per atom, which is comparable to the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction at ferromagnet/platinum interfaces and significantly larger than that at graphene/ferromagnet interfaces. This magnitude is sufficient to stabilize chiral domain walls and skyrmions in ferromagnetic films as thin as one monolayer at room temperature, demonstrating that oxygen, despite its low atomic number, can serve as a potent source of Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction.

- The application of oxygen-induced Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction in spintronics enables the design of reconfigurable magnetic memory and logic devices that operate through chemical stimuli rather than electrical currents, reducing power consumption and eliminating the need for high-current write mechanisms. The ability to switch domain wall chirality via gas exposure opens new pathways for magneto-ionic devices, where magnetic states are directly coupled to environmental conditions such as oxygen partial pressure or humidity.

- An experimental approach to measure unknown Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya contributions involves using a multilayer stack with a known, tunable Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction as a reference, then introducing a chemisorbed species and monitoring the resulting change in domain wall chirality. The magnitude of the unknown Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is determined by comparing the coverage required to induce a chirality switch with the known Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya variation induced by spacer layer thickness modulation.

- The measurement of domain wall spin texture as a function of layer thickness is performed using spin-polarized low-energy electron microscopy, wherein the in-plane and out-of-plane magnetization components are resolved by acquiring image sets with different beam spin polarizations. - The description of hydrogen-based magneto-ionics demonstrates that hydrogen can be used as a reversible ion to switch magnetic chirality, with applications in hydrogen sensors and energy-efficient memory.

- The discussion of multilayer heterostructures emphasizes the compatibility of the invention with existing thin-film deposition techniques, enabling integration with standard spintronic platforms.

- The description of device embodiment includes a multilayer stack comprising a ferromagnetic layer, a spacer layer, and a substrate, with a gas chamber surrounding the device, wherein exposure to oxygen or hydrogen switches the magnetic state.

- The discussion of reservoir layer and ferromagnet layer describes a configuration wherein a hydrogen- or oxygen-storing reservoir is in direct contact with the ferromagnetic layer, enabling controlled release of adsorbates for magnetic switching.

- The description of ionization and chemisorption process involves the dissociation of gas molecules on the surface, followed by atomic adsorption and charge transfer, which induces the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction and perpendicular magnetic anisotropy.

- The discussion of switching of domain wall chirality confirms that the process is non-volatile, fast, and energy-efficient, requiring only millibar-level gas exposure for seconds.

- The description of artificial synapses involves the use of chemisorption-induced DMI to emulate synaptic weight changes, wherein the magnetic chirality represents the synaptic strength and gas exposure represents the presynaptic signal.

- The summary of DMI and PMA induced by chemisorbed species establishes that oxygen and hydrogen are potent, reversible, and tunable sources of both interactions, with magnitudes rivaling those of heavy metals.

- The description of sample preparation involves the deposition of ferromagnetic layers by molecular beam epitaxy under ultra-high vacuum, followed by exposure to controlled doses of oxygen or hydrogen gas, with surface cleanliness verified by LEED and XPS.

- The discussion of oxygen exposures details the use of leak valves to introduce oxygen at pressures between 10⁻⁹ and 10⁻⁷ torr, with exposure times ranging from 10 seconds to 10 minutes to achieve coverages from 0.05 to 0.30 monolayers.

- The discussion of hydrogen exposures follows the same protocol, with hydrogen introduced at pressures of 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁶ torr, and the system evacuated between cycles to ensure reversibility.

- The description of time-dependent work function measurement is performed using a Kelvin probe, which tracks the surface potential change during gas exposure, confirming the correlation between dipole moment and DMI.

- The description of time-dependent in-plane domain wall analysis involves acquiring SPLEEM images at regular intervals during gas exposure, enabling the reconstruction of chirality evolution as a function of time.

- The discussion of image denoising methods includes the use of wavelet transforms and Gaussian filtering to enhance contrast in SPLEEM images, enabling accurate extraction of domain wall orientations.

- The discussion of the scope of the invention encompasses all ferromagnetic materials, chemisorbed species with electronegativity greater than 2.5, and any substrate that supports epitaxial growth, including silicon, sapphire, and tungsten, and extends to three-dimensional nanostructures, flexible substrates, and integrated circuits.
