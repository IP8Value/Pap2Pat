# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

- introduce keratinocyte culturing  
Human epidermal keratinocytes are the principal cellular component of the epidermis and play a central role in maintaining skin integrity, barrier function, and wound repair. The ability to culture these cells in vitro has been foundational to the development of regenerative therapies for severe burns, genetic skin disorders, and other epithelial pathologies. Since the pioneering work of Rheinwald and Green in the 1970s, primary human keratinocytes have been routinely expanded using a co-culture system involving lethally irradiated murine 3T3 fibroblasts as a feeder layer. This method has enabled the generation of autologous epidermal grafts for clinical use, particularly in patients with extensive epidermal loss. However, the reliance on murine feeder cells introduces significant biological and regulatory challenges that impede the broad clinical translation of such therapies.

- motivate need for new methods  
The clinical application of keratinocyte-based therapies is increasingly constrained by the use of animal-derived components, which pose risks of immunogenicity, pathogen transmission, and batch-to-batch variability. Regulatory agencies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency classify products derived from xenogeneic materials as high-risk biologicals, limiting their approval to exceptional circumstances such as life-threatening burns exceeding 30% total body surface area. The persistent use of bovine serum, cholera toxin, and murine feeder cells in standard protocols renders these systems incompatible with current Good Manufacturing Practice standards for cell therapies intended for widespread clinical deployment. There is therefore an urgent and unmet need for a fully defined, xeno-free, and serum-free culture system that reliably supports the long-term proliferation and functional integrity of human keratinocytes without compromising safety or regulatory compliance.

- describe current gold standard protocol  
The current gold standard for the in vitro expansion of human epidermal keratinocytes involves seeding freshly isolated cells onto a monolayer of lethally irradiated mouse 3T3-J2 fibroblasts, maintained in a complex medium supplemented with fetal bovine serum, insulin, hydrocortisone, epidermal growth factor, triiodothyronine, adenine, and cholera toxin. This system, while effective in promoting keratinocyte proliferation and preventing terminal differentiation, is inherently undefined due to the variable composition of serum and the unknown secreted factors produced by the murine feeder cells. The requirement for continuous feeder cell maintenance, along with the necessity of removing residual feeder cells prior to transplantation, introduces procedural complexity, increases contamination risk, and complicates quality control.

- limitations of current protocol  
The principal limitations of this protocol stem from its reliance on xenogeneic components. Murine fibroblasts secrete a heterogeneous mixture of extracellular matrix proteins, growth factors, and signaling molecules that are not fully characterized and may vary between cell lines and passages. Bovine serum contains immunoglobulins, lipoproteins, and other proteins that can trigger immune responses in recipients and interfere with downstream therapeutic applications. Cholera toxin, a potent activator of adenylate cyclase, is used to sustain proliferation but is classified as a high-risk ancillary material under USP <1043> due to its neurotoxic potential and difficulty in complete removal. Furthermore, the co-culture system does not allow for precise control over the microenvironment, making it unsuitable for scalable, reproducible manufacturing required for commercial cell therapy production.

- need for xeno-free and serum-free methods  
The development of a xeno-free and serum-free culture system for human keratinocytes is not merely a technical improvement but a prerequisite for the safe, scalable, and globally accessible delivery of epidermal cell therapies. Such a system must eliminate all animal-derived components while preserving the proliferative capacity, genetic stability, and differentiation potential of keratinocytes. It must also be chemically defined, enabling consistent performance across laboratories and manufacturing sites. The absence of feeder cells and serum would simplify downstream processing, reduce regulatory burden, and facilitate integration into automated bioreactor systems. Achieving this goal requires a deep understanding of the native epidermal basement membrane and the identification of human-derived substrates capable of recapitulating the in vivo niche that supports keratinocyte stemness and self-renewal.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION

- introduce novel keratinocyte culturing method  
A novel method for the culture of human epidermal keratinocytes has been developed that eliminates the need for murine feeder cells, serum, and other undefined components by utilizing specific human laminin isoforms as a defined, xeno-free substrate. This method enables the robust, long-term expansion of primary human keratinocytes directly from tissue isolates without prior enrichment or adaptation on feeder layers. The system supports the maintenance of progenitor cell characteristics, genetic stability, and full differentiation potential, as demonstrated by in vitro and in vivo functional assays.

- describe xeno-free and serum-free system  
The culture system is entirely free of animal-derived materials, including serum, growth factors of non-human origin, and feeder cells. All components are recombinant, synthetic, or human-derived, ensuring compliance with regulatory guidelines for advanced therapy medicinal products. The system operates under serum-free and xeno-free conditions throughout the entire culture cycle, from initial seeding to final passaging, thereby minimizing immunogenic risk and enhancing reproducibility.

- specify laminin matrices used  
The keratinocytes are cultured on a substrate coated with one or more human laminin isoforms selected from the group consisting of Laminin-511, Laminin-421, or combinations thereof. These isoforms are purified from recombinant human sources and applied at a concentration of approximately 2.5 μg/cm². The laminin matrices are applied as a thin, uniform monolayer to tissue culture surfaces and allowed to adsorb overnight at 4°C prior to cell seeding.

- describe cell culture medium  
The cells are maintained in a chemically defined, serum-free, xeno-free medium designated Keratinocyte Growth Medium-Chemically Defined (KGM-CD), which contains no animal-derived components. This medium supports sustained proliferation, prevents spontaneous differentiation, and maintains the expression of basal keratinocyte markers over multiple passages.

- confirm keratinocyte markers  
Cells cultured under this system retain expression of canonical keratinocyte progenitor markers including KRT5, KRT14, and TP63, as confirmed by immunofluorescence, flow cytometry, and quantitative PCR. The cells also exhibit low expression of differentiation markers such as KRT1 and KRT10 during early passages, consistent with a progenitor phenotype.

- describe plating and culturing process  
Freshly isolated human epidermal keratinocytes are seeded directly onto laminin-coated surfaces at a density of approximately 9 × 10⁴ cells per cm². Cultures are maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 10% CO₂. Medium is replaced every 48 hours, and cells are passaged upon reaching 80–90% confluence using a non-enzymatic or gentle enzymatic dissociation method.

- specify cell culture medium ingredients  
The KGM-CD medium comprises a basal medium supplemented with human recombinant epidermal growth factor, insulin, hydrocortisone, triiodothyronine, adenine, and other essential nutrients, all of which are of synthetic or human recombinant origin. The medium is free of cholera toxin, fetal bovine serum, and any other animal-derived supplements.

- describe trypsinization process  
Upon reaching confluence, cells are detached using TrypLE Select, a recombinant enzyme formulation that effectively dissociates keratinocytes without inducing significant cellular stress or loss of viability. Incubation times are optimized between 8 and 16 minutes at 37°C, and enzymatic activity is neutralized by dilution with culture medium followed by centrifugation.

- describe system for maintaining keratinocytes  
The system enables the maintenance of keratinocytes for over 20 cumulative population doublings without signs of senescence, chromosomal aberration, or loss of colony-forming efficiency. The cells retain their ability to form a stratified epidermis in organotypic cultures and to regenerate functional human epidermis upon transplantation into immunodeficient mice.

- introduce kit for culturing keratinocytes  
A commercial kit is provided for the culture of human epidermal keratinocytes, comprising pre-coated tissue culture vessels containing Laminin-511 or Laminin-421, a lyophilized or liquid formulation of KGM-CD medium, and detailed instructions for use. The kit further includes a dissociation reagent, a cell counting solution, and positive control antibodies for marker verification.

- define keratinocyte  
A keratinocyte is a type of epithelial cell that constitutes the majority of the epidermis and is responsible for producing keratin, forming the physical barrier of the skin, and participating in immune surveillance and wound healing.

- describe function of keratinocytes  
Keratinocytes originate in the basal layer of the epidermis and undergo a program of terminal differentiation as they migrate toward the skin surface, ultimately forming the stratum corneum. They secrete structural proteins, lipids, and antimicrobial peptides that collectively maintain skin integrity and defend against environmental insults.

- discuss limitations of primary cell culture  
Primary keratinocytes are notoriously difficult to expand in vitro due to their propensity for senescence, spontaneous differentiation, and loss of proliferative capacity under suboptimal conditions. Traditional culture methods rely on undefined components that introduce variability and limit scalability.

- introduce basement membrane  
The basement membrane is a specialized extracellular matrix structure underlying epithelial tissues, composed of collagen IV, laminins, nidogens, and proteoglycans. It provides structural support and biochemical cues essential for epithelial cell adhesion, polarity, and survival.

- describe laminin isoforms  
Laminins are heterotrimeric glycoproteins composed of α, β, and γ chain subunits. Multiple isoforms exist based on the combination of these chains, each with distinct tissue distributions and biological functions.

- discuss importance of laminins  
Laminins are critical for epithelial cell adhesion through integrin binding and are indispensable for maintaining stem cell niches in various tissues, including the epidermis.

- describe laminin structure  
Each laminin molecule consists of one α chain, one β chain, and one γ chain, assembled into a cross-shaped structure with long arms and a short arm. The α chain determines the binding specificity to cell surface receptors.

- list laminin subunit chains  
The laminin subunit chains include α1–α5, β1–β3, and γ1–γ3. Specific isoforms are designated by the combination of these chains, such as Laminin-511 (α5β1γ1).

- discuss laminin types  
Laminin-332, Laminin-511, and Laminin-521 are naturally expressed in the human epidermal basement membrane, while Laminin-411 and Laminin-421 are typically associated with vascular and mesenchymal tissues.

- introduce methods of present disclosure  
The methods of the present disclosure involve the direct culture of human epidermal keratinocytes on recombinant human laminin-511 or laminin-421 substrates in a serum-free, xeno-free medium, without the use of feeder cells or animal-derived supplements.

- describe culturing keratinocytes on laminin matrices  
Keratinocytes are seeded directly onto laminin-coated surfaces and maintained in a defined medium that supports long-term proliferation and the retention of progenitor markers. The laminin matrices provide the necessary adhesion signals to replace the function of murine fibroblasts.

- discuss xeno-free culturing system  
The xeno-free nature of the system ensures that no non-human proteins are introduced during culture, thereby eliminating the risk of immune sensitization and pathogen transmission associated with traditional methods.

- describe cell culture substrate  
The substrate consists of a tissue culture surface coated with a purified human laminin isoform, applied at a concentration sufficient to promote cell attachment and spreading without inducing excessive differentiation.

- list effective laminins  
The laminin isoforms Laminin-511 and Laminin-421 are identified as the most effective substrates for the long-term culture of human epidermal keratinocytes.

- describe laminin combinations  
Combinations of Laminin-511 and Laminin-421 may be used, but no additive benefit is observed over the use of either isoform alone. Other laminins, including Laminin-332, Laminin-521, and Laminin-411, are less effective in sustaining long-term proliferation.

- define Laminin-521, Laminin-511, Laminin-421, and Laminin-411  
Laminin-521 is composed of α5, β2, and γ1 chains; Laminin-511 is composed of α5, β1, and γ1 chains; Laminin-421 is composed of α4, β2, and γ1 chains; and Laminin-411 is composed of α4, β1, and γ1 chains.

- describe cell culture medium  
The cell culture medium is a chemically defined formulation that supports keratinocyte proliferation without serum or undefined supplements. It contains recombinant human epidermal growth factor, insulin, hydrocortisone, triiodothyronine, adenine, and essential amino acids.

- list ingredients in cell culture medium  
The medium contains a basal medium such as DMEM/F12, supplemented with 5 ng/mL recombinant human EGF, 10 μg/mL insulin, 0.4 μg/mL hydrocortisone, 0.2 nM triiodothyronine, 0.18 mM adenine, and 100 IU/mL penicillin-streptomycin.

- discuss optional ingredients  
Optional ingredients may include B27 supplement, N-acetylcysteine, or ascorbic acid, provided they are of synthetic or recombinant origin and do not compromise the xeno-free status of the system.

- describe basal medium  
The basal medium is a balanced salt solution containing glucose, amino acids, vitamins, and inorganic salts, formulated to support mammalian cell growth without serum.

- summarize cell culture system  
The overall system comprises a laminin-coated substrate, a chemically defined, serum-free, xeno-free medium, and a gentle dissociation protocol, enabling the expansion of human keratinocytes with high fidelity to their in vivo phenotype.

### EXAMPLES

- isolate human epidermal keratinocytes  
Human epidermal keratinocytes were isolated from surgical waste obtained from healthy donors following informed consent and institutional review board approval. Skin samples were digested with dispase overnight at 4°C, and the epidermis was mechanically separated and treated with trypsin to release single cells.

- describe primary keratinocyte culture  
Isolated keratinocytes were seeded onto laminin-coated plates and cultured in KGM-CD medium. Cells were maintained at 37°C in 10% CO₂ and passaged upon reaching confluence.

- detail coating of tissue culture plates  
Tissue culture plates were coated with 2.5 μg/cm² of recombinant human laminin-511 or laminin-421 in PBS and incubated overnight at 4°C. Plates were rinsed with PBS prior to cell seeding.

- seed keratinocytes on pre-coated plates  
Keratinocytes were seeded at a density of 9 × 10⁴ cells/cm² and cultured in KGM-CD medium. Cell morphology, attachment, and proliferation were monitored daily.

- describe control plate culture  
Control cultures were maintained on non-coated plates or on a feeder layer of irradiated 3T3-J2 fibroblasts in complete FAD medium.

- calculate cumulative population doublings  
Cumulative population doublings were calculated using the formula PD = log(N/N₀)/log(2), where N is the number of harvested cells and N₀ is the number of seeded cells.

- perform quantitative PCR analysis  
Total RNA was extracted from cells at various passages, reverse transcribed into cDNA, and analyzed by quantitative PCR for expression of KRT5, KRT14, KRT1, KRT10, and TP63.

- synthesize cDNA  
cDNA was synthesized from 500 ng of total RNA using a reverse transcription kit with oligo(dT) primers.

- perform real-time quantitative RT-PCR  
Real-time PCR was performed using SYBR Green chemistry and gene-specific primers. GAPDH was used as a housekeeping control.

- analyze FACS data  
Cells were fixed, permeabilized, stained with fluorescently labeled antibodies against KRT5, KRT14, and p63, and analyzed by flow cytometry.

- fix and immunostain cells  
Cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, blocked with 5% goat serum, and incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C.

- detect with secondary antibodies  
Fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies were applied for one hour at room temperature, followed by nuclear counterstaining with DAPI.

- perform FACS analysis  
Flow cytometry was conducted using a MACSQuant VYB analyzer, and data were analyzed using MACSQuantify software.

- describe organotypic culture  
De-epidermalized dermis was seeded with human dermal fibroblasts, followed by keratinocytes. Cultures were raised to the air-liquid interface for 14 days to induce stratification.

- prepare de-epidermalized dermis  
Allogeneic skin was subjected to repeated freeze-thaw cycles to remove the epidermis, leaving a dermal scaffold.

- seed human dermal fibroblasts  
Fibroblasts were seeded onto the reticular side of the dermis and allowed to attach for 24 hours.

- seed HEK cells on dermis  
Keratinocytes were seeded on the dermal side and cultured for seven days before lifting to the air-liquid interface.

- lift cultures to air-liquid interface  
Cultures were raised to the air-liquid interface by transferring to porous inserts, allowing for stratification and terminal differentiation.

- process for cryosectioning  
Tissues were embedded in OCT compound, frozen in liquid nitrogen, and sectioned at 5 μm thickness using a cryostat.

- generate RNA-sequencing data  
RNA was extracted from cultured keratinocytes and whole skin, and RNA-seq libraries were prepared using the Illumina TruSeq Stranded Total RNA kit.

- isolate RNA from culture plates or whole skin  
RNA was purified using a microRNA purification kit, and quality was assessed using a Bioanalyzer.

- prepare RNAseq libraries  
Libraries were quantified, normalized, and pooled for sequencing on an Illumina HiSeq 2000 platform.

- perform sequencing  
Paired-end 100 bp sequencing was performed, yielding an average of 43 million reads per sample.

- analyze RNA-seq data  
Reads were aligned to the hg38 genome using STAR, and gene expression was quantified using RSEM.

- perform differential expression analysis  
Differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2, comparing keratinocytes cultured on laminin substrates to those grown on 3T3 feeders.

- perform functional enrichment analysis  
Gene Set Enrichment Analysis was performed using Hallmark gene sets to identify biological pathways enriched in laminin-cultured cells.

- visualize expression levels of selected genes  
TPM values were log-transformed and batch-corrected using limma, and expression of key genes was visualized as heatmaps.

- introduce cell culture method  
The method involves the direct culture of human epidermal keratinocytes on laminin-511 or laminin-421 substrates in a defined, serum-free medium.

- describe cell culture medium composition  
The medium contains recombinant human EGF, insulin, hydrocortisone, triiodothyronine, adenine, and a basal salt solution, with no serum or animal-derived components.

- explain cell passaging procedure  
Cells were passaged using TrypLE Select for 8–16 minutes at 37°C, followed by centrifugation and resuspension in fresh medium.

- calculate population doublings  
Cumulative population doublings were calculated for each passage to assess proliferative capacity.

Example 1 demonstrates the successful attachment and spreading of keratinocytes on laminin-511 and laminin-421 substrates.

- describe immunofluorescent staining  
Immunofluorescent staining confirmed the expression of KRT5, KRT14, and p63 in cells cultured on laminin substrates.

- summarize laminin isoform expression  
RNA-seq and immunohistochemistry confirmed the presence of laminin α5, β1, γ1, and α4 in human skin and 3T3 cells, respectively.

- introduce example 2  
Example 2 demonstrates the maintenance of keratinocyte progenitor markers over multiple passages.

- describe real-time PCR  
Real-time PCR showed stable expression of KRT5 and KRT14 and low expression of KRT1 and KRT10 up to passage 10.

- summarize mRNA expression levels  
mRNA levels of basal markers remained consistent across passages, while differentiation markers increased gradually.

- introduce example 3  
Example 3 demonstrates the ability of laminin substrates to support long-term proliferation.

- describe cell culture on laminin substrates  
Keratinocytes cultured on laminin-511 and laminin-421 exhibited comparable growth rates to those on 3T3 feeders.

- summarize cell growth and proliferation  
Cumulative population doublings exceeded 20 for cells on laminin substrates, with no signs of senescence.

- introduce example 4  
Example 4 demonstrates the genetic stability of keratinocytes cultured on laminin substrates.

- describe karyotyping  
Karyotype analysis revealed no chromosomal abnormalities in cells cultured on laminin-511 or laminin-421 after 20 passages.

- summarize genetic stability  
All cells maintained a normal diploid karyotype, indicating no genomic instability.

- introduce example 5  
Example 5 demonstrates the transcriptomic similarity of laminin-cultured keratinocytes to those grown on feeders.

- describe qPCR  
qPCR confirmed comparable expression of keratinocyte markers between laminin-cultured and 3T3-cultured cells.

- summarize expression profiles  
Expression profiles of basal and differentiation markers were nearly identical between the two systems.

- describe immunostaining  
Immunostaining confirmed the presence of KRT5, KRT14, and p63 in cells cultured on laminin substrates.

- introduce example 6  
Example 6 demonstrates the functional equivalence of laminin-cultured keratinocytes in organotypic and in vivo models.

- describe RNA-sequencing  
RNA-seq revealed a high degree of transcriptomic similarity between laminin-cultured and 3T3-cultured keratinocytes, with only minor differences in inflammatory and developmental pathways.

- summarize transcriptome analysis  
Keratinocytes cultured on laminin substrates exhibited downregulation of pro-inflammatory pathways and upregulation of cell cycle genes, suggesting a more favorable proliferative state.

## SUMMARY

- introduce keratinocyte culturing methods  
The present invention provides a novel method for the culture of human epidermal keratinocytes using defined, xeno-free, and serum-free conditions that eliminate the need for murine feeder cells and animal-derived supplements.

- limitations of current methods  
Current methods rely on undefined, xenogeneic components that introduce variability, immunogenic risk, and regulatory barriers, limiting their applicability in clinical settings.

- describe disclosed method advantages  
The disclosed method offers a fully defined, reproducible, and scalable system that supports long-term proliferation, maintains genetic stability, preserves progenitor phenotype, and enables the generation of functional epidermal grafts. The use of human laminin-511 or laminin-421 as a substrate provides a physiologically relevant niche that replaces the function of murine fibroblasts.

- application of cultured cell products  
The cultured keratinocytes are suitable for use in autologous skin grafts, treatment of genetic skin disorders, burn reconstruction, and as a platform for drug screening and disease modeling.

- scope of disclosure  
The scope of this disclosure encompasses the use of laminin-511, laminin-421, or combinations thereof as a substrate for keratinocyte culture, the defined medium composition, the passaging protocol, the kit for implementation, and any method of producing therapeutic-grade human epidermal keratinocytes without the use of xenogeneic materials.
