# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Prostate cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignancies affecting men worldwide, with significant morbidity and mortality despite advances in screening and therapeutic intervention. The disease typically arises from the epithelial cells of the prostate gland and progresses through stages characterized by increasing invasiveness, metastatic potential, and resistance to conventional therapies. Early detection remains critical to improving patient outcomes, yet current diagnostic approaches are limited in specificity and predictive power. The prostate-specific antigen (PSA) test, long employed as a primary screening tool, suffers from low positive predictive value, frequently yielding false-positive results due to benign prostatic hyperplasia, inflammation, or other non-malignant conditions. This lack of specificity leads to unnecessary biopsies, patient anxiety, and overtreatment of indolent disease. While other biomarkers such as PCA3 and TMPRSS2-ERG fusion transcripts have been explored, their clinical utility remains constrained by variability in expression, limited tissue availability, and insufficient correlation with disease aggressiveness or prognosis. Current diagnostic paradigms rely heavily on invasive tissue biopsy followed by histopathological evaluation using the Gleason scoring system, which, while informative, is subject to inter-observer variability and cannot be performed serially without significant patient burden. There exists a critical and unmet need for non-invasive, reliable, and biologically grounded diagnostic and prognostic tools that can accurately distinguish indolent from aggressive prostate cancer, guide therapeutic decision-making, and monitor disease progression in real time. The identification of novel molecular signatures associated with prostate cancer pathogenesis offers a promising avenue to address these limitations, particularly those that reflect intrinsic cellular signaling pathways essential to tumor survival and proliferation.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a method for inhibiting the proliferation of prostate cancer cells by targeting soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC), a bicarbonate- and calcium-sensitive enzyme that generates cyclic AMP within intracellular compartments distinct from transmembrane adenylyl cyclases. The method involves the administration of a substance capable of inhibiting the enzymatic activity of sAC, thereby suppressing the downstream EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf signaling axis and inducing cell cycle arrest at the G2/M checkpoint, ultimately leading to reduced tumor cell viability. Additionally, the invention encompasses diagnostic methods for detecting overexpression of sAC gene transcripts or protein in prostate tissue samples, enabling the identification of patients with aggressive disease and facilitating personalized therapeutic selection. The invention further includes compositions comprising sAC inhibitors, formulated for systemic or localized delivery, and methods for selecting treatment regimens based on the level of sAC expression in tumor tissue relative to benign controls. The therapeutic and diagnostic utility of the invention is grounded in the discovery that sAC is overexpressed in malignant prostate tissue compared to benign prostatic epithelium, and that its inhibition selectively impairs cancer cell proliferation without significantly affecting normal prostate epithelial cells.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) is a distinct isoform of adenylyl cyclase that is not anchored to the plasma membrane and is uniquely regulated by bicarbonate ions and calcium, allowing it to function as a metabolic sensor within the cytoplasm and nucleus. Unlike transmembrane adenylyl cyclases, which are activated by G protein-coupled receptors, sAC generates localized pools of cyclic AMP that regulate intracellular signaling events independently of extracellular stimuli. In normal physiological contexts, sAC contributes to processes such as sperm motility, pH regulation, and mitochondrial function. However, in the context of prostate cancer, sAC is aberrantly overexpressed, particularly in malignant epithelial cells, where it localizes not only to the cytoplasm but also to the nucleus, where it modulates the activity of the cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB) and influences transcriptional programs governing cell cycle progression. The elevated expression of sAC in prostate cancer correlates with increased cellular proliferation and resistance to apoptosis, suggesting a direct role in tumorigenesis. Previous studies have demonstrated that cAMP signaling can either promote or suppress proliferation depending on the cellular context and the spatial compartmentalization of the second messenger. In prostate cancer, however, the sAC-derived cAMP pool specifically drives proliferation through the EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf pathway, independent of protein kinase A (PKA) activity. This pathway is critical for the activation of mitotic regulators such as cyclin B1 and CDK1, which are essential for the G2/M transition. Inhibition of sAC disrupts this signaling cascade, resulting in suppression of cyclin B1 and CDK1 expression, accumulation of cells in the G2 phase, and subsequent induction of apoptosis.

The invention defines prostate cancer as a malignant neoplasm arising from the epithelial cells of the prostate gland, characterized by uncontrolled proliferation, evasion of apoptosis, and potential for local invasion and distant metastasis. Prostate cancer cells, as used herein, include but are not limited to androgen-sensitive lines such as LNCaP and androgen-insensitive lines such as PC3, as well as primary tumor-derived cells obtained from patient biopsies. The method of inhibiting proliferation involves the administration of a substance that directly or indirectly reduces the enzymatic activity of sAC. Such substances include small molecule inhibitors such as KH7, which binds to the catalytic domain of sAC and prevents ATP conversion to cAMP, or its structurally analogous but inactive derivative KH7.15, which serves as a negative control. Other small molecule inhibitors may be identified through high-throughput screening of chemical libraries targeting the bicarbonate-binding site or the catalytic pocket of sAC. In addition to small molecules, the invention encompasses nucleic acid-based inhibitors, including interfering RNA molecules such as small interfering RNA (siRNA) or short hairpin RNA (shRNA), designed to specifically target and degrade sAC mRNA transcripts. These RNA molecules may be delivered via lipid nanoparticles, viral vectors, or other pharmaceutically acceptable carriers to achieve sustained intracellular knockdown of sAC expression. The composition of the invention includes a therapeutically effective amount of an sAC inhibitor combined with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent suitable for parenteral, oral, intraprostatic, or intravesical administration. The therapeutically effective amount is defined as the quantity sufficient to reduce sAC enzymatic activity by at least 50% in prostate cancer cells, resulting in a measurable decrease in cell proliferation, an increase in G2/M cell cycle arrest, and/or induction of apoptosis, without causing significant toxicity to non-malignant prostate epithelial cells.

The invention further provides a method for diagnosing prostate cancer by obtaining a biological sample from the prostate, such as a tissue biopsy, fine-needle aspirate, or liquid biopsy containing exfoliated cells or cell-free nucleic acids. The sample is assayed for the expression level of the sAC gene or the presence of sAC protein using techniques including quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, Northern blotting, immunohistochemistry, Western blotting, or enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. The expression or protein level is compared to a control, which may be derived from benign prostatic tissue of the same patient, age-matched healthy donors, or established reference ranges. Overexpression of sAC is defined as a statistically significant increase in gene transcript or protein abundance relative to the control, with a threshold determined by H-score values exceeding 180 in immunohistochemical analyses. Antibodies specific for sAC, including polyclonal, monoclonal, chimeric, or single-chain variable fragments, may be generated against epitopes located within the catalytic domain or the unique N-terminal region of the somatic isoform of sAC. Detection of antibody binding may be accomplished using fluorescent, enzymatic, or radioactive labels, and may be performed on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue sections or cytological preparations. The invention further includes methods for selecting a treatment option based on the level of sAC expression, wherein high expression correlates with a more aggressive phenotype and is predictive of poor response to androgen-deprivation therapy. In such cases, treatment may include administration of an sAC inhibitor alone or in combination with radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or anti-androgen agents. The Gleason score, tumor stage, and other clinical parameters may be integrated with sAC expression data to refine prognostic stratification and guide therapeutic intensity.

### Example 1

An example of the diagnostic method is demonstrated using formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded prostate tissue specimens obtained from patients undergoing radical prostatectomy. Sections were stained using a mouse monoclonal antibody directed against sAC (clone R21) at a dilution of 1:750, followed by alkaline phosphatase-mediated signal amplification and development with 3,3′-diaminobenzidine. Staining intensity was quantified using the H-score system, which assigns values based on the percentage of cells staining at three intensity levels: weak (1+), moderate (2+), and strong (3+). The mean H-score for malignant tissue was 189.2, significantly higher than the mean H-score of 144.3 for adjacent benign tissue (p < 0.01). Nuclear staining was observed in 62% of malignant cases, whereas only 18% of benign glands exhibited nuclear localization. The intensity and distribution of staining were consistent across multiple tissue cores, confirming the reliability of sAC as a diagnostic marker. These findings establish that sAC overexpression is a hallmark of prostate carcinoma and can be reliably detected using standard histopathological techniques.

### Example 2

In a separate cohort, three distinct monoclonal antibodies—R21, R40, and R52—were applied to tissue microarrays containing benign, localized, and metastatic prostate cancer specimens. R21 recognized both somatic and testicular isoforms, R40 was specific for the testicular isoform, and R52 targeted a novel epitope in the regulatory domain. R21 and R52 showed strong cytoplasmic and nuclear staining in all malignant samples, whereas R40 staining was minimal, indicating that the somatic isoform predominates in prostate cancer. Staining intensity was significantly higher in Gleason pattern 4 and 5 tumors compared to pattern 3, and metastatic lesions showed the highest expression levels. The R52 antibody demonstrated superior sensitivity in detecting low-abundance sAC in early-stage tumors, suggesting its utility in early diagnosis. These results confirm that sAC overexpression is not merely a bystander effect but is directly associated with tumor grade and progression.

### Example 3

Human prostate cancer cell lines LNCaP and PC3 were cultured under standard conditions and treated with the sAC inhibitor KH7 at 20 μM for 48 hours. Western blot analysis confirmed a 70% reduction in intracellular cAMP levels and a corresponding 60–70% decrease in cyclin B1 and CDK1 protein expression. Cell proliferation was reduced by 55% as measured by trypan blue exclusion, and LDH release increased by 3.5-fold, indicating loss of membrane integrity. Flow cytometry revealed a 40% increase in the G2/M population and a concomitant decrease in G1 phase cells. Similar results were obtained using siRNA-mediated knockdown of sAC, which reduced sAC protein levels by over 80% and suppressed proliferation to the same extent as KH7. No effect was observed with scrambled siRNA or the inactive analog KH7.15. These experiments demonstrate that inhibition of sAC, whether pharmacological or genetic, consistently suppresses prostate cancer cell proliferation through disruption of the G2/M checkpoint.

### Example 4

Cell cycle analysis following sAC inhibition demonstrated a marked accumulation of cells in the G2 phase, with a corresponding reduction in the S and G1 phases. Western blotting confirmed downregulation of cyclin B1 and phosphorylated CDK1, while cyclins D1, D3, and E2 remained unchanged, indicating specificity for the G2/M transition. Treatment with the PKA activator N6-benzoyl-cAMP or inhibitors H-89 and (Rp)-cAMP-S had no effect on proliferation or cell cycle distribution, confirming PKA independence. In contrast, treatment with the EPAC-specific activator 8-pCPT-cAMP reversed the antiproliferative effects of KH7, restoring Rap1-GTP levels, B-Raf phosphorylation, and cyclin B1 expression. These findings establish that sAC controls prostate cancer proliferation exclusively through the EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf pathway, providing a mechanistic basis for therapeutic targeting. The invention therefore encompasses a novel class of diagnostic and therapeutic agents centered on the inhibition of sAC and its downstream signaling effectors.
