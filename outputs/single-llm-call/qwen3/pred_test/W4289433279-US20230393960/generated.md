# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The increasing reliance on machine learning systems for high-stakes decision-making in domains such as financial services, criminal justice, healthcare, and employment has intensified scrutiny over algorithmic bias and its societal consequences. Traditional approaches to mitigating bias in predictive models often rely on exclusion of protected attributes during training, which addresses disparate treatment but fails to account for disparate impact—where seemingly neutral policies produce systematically unequal outcomes across demographic groups. While fairness criteria such as demographic parity, equalized odds, and equal opportunity have been widely studied in academic literature, their practical deployment in real-world systems remains limited due to rigid constraints, computational inefficiency, and incompatibility with multi-attribute, high-arity sensitive variables. Many existing methods require retraining models from scratch, multiple sanitization passes per attribute value, or access to raw training features—all of which undermine operational scalability, data privacy, and model fidelity. Furthermore, these methods typically assume binary protected attributes, making them ill-suited for environments where sensitive characteristics such as country of origin, currency used, or zip code exhibit dozens or hundreds of distinct values. The inability to adapt fairness constraints dynamically to business-specific cost structures—such as prioritizing reduction of false positives over false negatives in fraud detection—further restricts their utility. Consequently, there exists a critical need for a post-hoc, model-agnostic, and computationally efficient framework that enables flexible, scalable, and privacy-preserving bias correction without compromising predictive accuracy or requiring access to internal model parameters.

## SUMMARY

The invention introduces a fairness deviation constraint that relaxes the strict equality requirements of traditional fairness metrics such as equalized odds by permitting performance rates to vary within a statistically defined tolerance around a mean value, rather than enforcing exact parity across all sub-populations. This constraint is applied through a decision matrix composed of attribute-specific decision thresholds that are dynamically calibrated to balance fairness and accuracy. The decision matrix is generated by analyzing classification probabilities across all values of one or more protected attributes, computing performance metrics such as true positive rate and false positive rate for each threshold candidate, and iteratively pruning thresholds that exceed the fairness deviation constraint. The system permits users to select whether to enforce fairness on false positive rates, true positive rates, or both, enabling alignment with domain-specific cost functions—for instance, prioritizing reduction of false positives in fraud detection where false acceptances carry higher financial risk. The fairness deviation constraint is determined by calculating the mean and standard deviation of performance rates across all attribute values, and defining permissible deviation bounds as a multiple of this standard deviation. This approach enables the system to accommodate natural variations in underlying data distributions while still ensuring meaningful fairness guarantees. The decision matrix is learned in a single pass without retraining the underlying machine learning model, preserving model fidelity and enabling real-time deployment. The framework supports multi-dimensional fairness by independently applying the constraint to each protected attribute or jointly to combinations of attributes, with pruning mechanisms that eliminate redundant or statistically dependent attributes to reduce computational complexity. The system further enhances flexibility by allowing users to define custom fairness deviation bounds, select alternative fairness models, and apply the constraint to sub-populations with sufficient sample size, ensuring statistical reliability. This architecture significantly improves upon conventional methods by eliminating the need for repeated model sanitizations, enabling differential privacy through aggregate-only access, and supporting arbitrary machine learning architectures without modification.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention comprises a bias correction system that operates as a post-processing layer on top of any pre-trained machine learning model, receiving classification probabilities as input and outputting sanitized predictions calibrated to meet user-defined fairness criteria. Conventional systems enforce fairness by either modifying training data, retraining models under fairness constraints, or applying uniform threshold adjustments across all groups—methods that are computationally expensive, inflexible, and often degrade model accuracy. These systems frequently suffer from type-1 errors, where legitimate cases are incorrectly rejected due to overly conservative thresholds, and type-2 errors, where harmful cases are incorrectly accepted due to insufficient discrimination. Equalized odds and equal opportunity, while theoretically sound, impose rigid equality conditions that are unattainable in practice when underlying base rates differ across groups, leading to suboptimal outcomes. Moreover, conventional systems require multiple sanitization cycles—one per attribute value—making them infeasible for high-arity attributes such as country or currency, which may have over fifty distinct values. These repeated sanitizations not only increase computational load but also expose sensitive data through repeated access to model outputs, undermining privacy, security, and data fidelity. The invention overcomes these limitations by introducing a relaxed fairness deviation constraint that defines acceptable variation in performance metrics as a function of their mean and standard deviation across attribute values. This constraint permits false positive rates and true positive rates to deviate from the group mean by no more than a configurable number of standard deviations, thereby preserving fairness without enforcing unrealistic parity. The system determines the mean performance rate across all values of a protected attribute and computes the standard deviation of the corresponding performance metric, such as false positive rate, for each candidate decision threshold. A fairness deviation constraint is then imposed, pruning thresholds whose performance metrics fall outside the allowable range. The remaining thresholds are evaluated using an accuracy model that selects the optimal threshold per attribute value based on a user-defined selection metric—such as F0.5, F1, or F2—that weights false positives and false negatives according to business priorities. The decision matrix, comprising one threshold per attribute value, is iteratively refined until all values satisfy the fairness deviation constraint. The system extracts features from input samples, generates classification probabilities using the underlying machine learning model, and applies the decision matrix to determine final classifications by comparing probabilities against the corresponding decision thresholds. This process is performed for multiple values of multiple data attributes, enabling simultaneous fairness enforcement across several protected dimensions. The system determines decision matrices for sub-populations by identifying statistically independent attributes and pruning those with high dependence, reducing redundancy. Multiple approaches are utilized to determine initial decision thresholds, including randomized sampling, grid-based enumeration, and adaptive search strategies. Computational complexity is reduced by pruning attribute spaces with insufficient sample sizes and by applying the fairness constraint independently per attribute rather than across all possible combinations. The system improves accuracy relative to conventional systems by preserving the original model’s discriminative power while selectively adjusting thresholds only where necessary. Flexibility is enhanced by allowing users to define custom fairness deviation bounds, select different fairness models, and apply the constraint to any combination of attributes. Efficiency is improved by requiring only a single pass over the model outputs, reducing time and computing resources by orders of magnitude compared to multi-pass sanitization techniques. Privacy, security, flexibility, and fidelity are preserved by operating solely on aggregated performance metrics and never requiring access to raw features or internal model weights. Decision thresholds are applied in a differentially private manner by adding noise to aggregate statistics, ensuring compliance with regulatory frameworks. The system processes model outputs regardless of their source, operating on predictions generated from client devices, cloud-based models, or batched datasets. The decision matrix is initialized with a set of candidate thresholds, and performance rates are computed for each. The system compares classifications against ground truth labels to determine performance metrics, calculates mean and deviation, and prunes thresholds violating the fairness deviation constraint. New thresholds are selected based on accuracy scores derived from the selection metric, and the process iterates until convergence. A user interface enables flexible selection of fairness deviation parameters, including the number of standard deviations allowed, the performance metric to optimize, and the protected attributes to include. The system operates with various machine learning model architectures—including logistic regression, gradient boosting, and neural networks—without modification.
