# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention

The present invention relates to the field of energy storage devices, specifically to stretchable, flexible, and mechanically robust lithium-ion batteries designed for integration into wearable electronic systems. The invention encompasses a novel class of battery architectures that utilize kirigami-inspired geometric patterning to achieve high levels of stretchability without compromising electrochemical performance, structural integrity, or cycle life. These batteries are engineered to conform to dynamic, non-planar surfaces such as human skin, clothing, or flexible substrates, enabling their deployment in next-generation wearable medical monitors, smart textiles, implantable devices, and consumer electronics where traditional rigid or even conventional flexible batteries are inadequate due to mechanical failure under strain. The invention further provides a scalable manufacturing methodology compatible with existing industrial processes for lithium-ion battery production, thereby facilitating commercial adoption across industries requiring adaptable power sources.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- motivate flexible batteries
- describe limitations of prior art
- summarize prior art approaches
- identify need for improvement

The increasing demand for wearable and implantable electronic devices has created a critical need for energy storage systems that can endure repeated mechanical deformation without degradation in performance or safety. Conventional lithium-ion batteries, while offering high energy density and reliable electrochemical characteristics, are inherently rigid and brittle, rendering them unsuitable for applications involving bending, twisting, or stretching. Prior attempts to address this limitation have focused on thin-film batteries, elastomeric composites, and serpentine interconnects; however, these approaches suffer from significant trade-offs between energy density, mechanical resilience, and manufacturability. Thin-film batteries, for instance, exhibit low areal capacity and require complex vacuum deposition techniques, while elastomeric composites often sacrifice energy density due to the incorporation of insulating polymers. Serpentine designs, though capable of limited stretchability, introduce high internal resistance and are prone to delamination under cyclic loading. Furthermore, many prior systems rely on rigid components that fracture or lose electrical contact under strain, leading to catastrophic failure. Existing approaches also lack a unified framework for simultaneously optimizing both electrochemical stability and mechanical adaptability, resulting in devices that perform well in static conditions but degrade rapidly under real-world dynamic loads. There remains a substantial unmet need for a battery architecture that combines high energy density, long cycle life, and exceptional stretchability—achieving these properties through geometric design rather than material substitution—thereby enabling seamless integration into conformable, motion-responsive wearable systems without compromising performance or safety.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- describe folding and cutting process
- explain stress release mechanism
- define stretchability
- describe battery assembly
- outline method of making battery

The present invention introduces a kirigami-based lithium-ion battery that achieves unprecedented levels of stretchability while maintaining high electrochemical efficiency and mechanical durability. The battery is fabricated by applying precise geometric cuts and folds to a multilayer stack comprising anode, cathode, separator, and packaging layers, all patterned identically to ensure structural coherence under deformation. The key innovation lies in the use of kirigami patterns—specifically zigzag-cut, cut-N-twist, and cut-N-shear configurations—that enable controlled in-plane rotation and shear at cut interfaces, thereby releasing mechanical stress without inducing fracture or delamination. Unlike conventional stretchable designs that rely on elastic deformation of materials, this invention exploits geometric reconfiguration to accommodate strain, allowing the battery to stretch beyond 100% of its original length while preserving electrical continuity. The stretchability is defined as the maximum elongation the battery can undergo without loss of electrochemical function or structural integrity, and in preferred embodiments, exceeds 150%. The battery assembly consists of a layered structure in which the anode current collector, cathode current collector, separator, and encapsulation film are all cut and folded using the same kirigami pattern, ensuring perfect alignment and uniform strain distribution. The method of manufacture involves cutting each layer with a precision punch or laser system according to a predefined geometric template, coating the cut foils with active electrode materials, laminating the layers in sequence, filling with electrolyte, and sealing under inert conditions. The resulting device exhibits exceptional mechanical resilience, electrochemical stability, and compatibility with standard industrial battery fabrication processes.

The invention employs three distinct kirigami patterns to achieve stretchability: the zigzag-cut pattern, the cut-N-twist pattern, and the cut-N-shear pattern. The zigzag-cut pattern is formed by making asymmetric cuts along the longitudinal axis of a folded stack of metal foil, resulting in a series of interconnected zigzag segments that undergo out-of-plane buckling during elongation. While effective, this pattern introduces localized bending stresses that may compromise long-term durability. The cut-N-twist pattern, by contrast, is produced by symmetrically cutting all creases in a folded stack, then twisting the ends in opposite directions to form a helical geometry analogous to a twisted telephone cord. This configuration eliminates out-of-plane deformation entirely, as stretching is accommodated solely through rotational motion at the cut interfaces, thereby minimizing mechanical fatigue. The packing density of this pattern is determined by the width of each face segment and can be optimized for specific form factors. The cut-N-shear pattern further enhances packing efficiency by folding the cut structure and then applying lateral shear, effectively doubling the areal energy density compared to the cut-N-twist design. In all embodiments, the stretching mechanism relies on the rotation of rigid segments around cut edges, preventing crack propagation and preserving electrical pathways. The lithium-ion battery cells are assembled using conventional materials, including graphite as the anode active material and lithium cobalt oxide as the cathode active material, with copper and aluminum serving as the anode and cathode current collectors, respectively. The separator is composed of polypropylene, and the packaging is formed from aluminized polyethylene to ensure hermetic sealing. Electrolyte is injected as a liquid solution of lithium hexafluorophosphate in ethylene carbonate, dimethyl carbonate, and diethyl carbonate. All layers are cut using the same pattern to ensure alignment, laminated in sequence, and sealed with an impulse sealer in an argon-filled glovebox. Electrochemical testing reveals stable cycling performance over 100 cycles with over 85% capacity retention and Coulombic efficiency exceeding 99.8%, even under repeated stretching. Rate performance remains consistent across C/3, C/2, and C discharge rates, with full recovery of capacity upon return to lower rates. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy shows no significant increase in internal resistance after mechanical deformation, confirming the stability of interfacial contacts. Scanning electron microscopy of both anode and cathode current collectors after 100 cycles of stretching reveals no cracks, fractures, or delamination, demonstrating the mechanical robustness of the design. Finite element simulations based on Griffith’s criterion and plastic rolling models reveal that the critical moment for crack growth is consistently higher than the critical moment for plastic rolling under operational strain conditions, ensuring that deformation occurs via controlled plastic rotation rather than fracture. The “safe zone” identified in simulations corresponds to the operational strain range of the battery, where plastic rolling dominates and fracture is suppressed. The battery was successfully integrated into a Samsung Gear 2 smart watch by sewing it between elastic bands worn around the wrist, where it experienced up to 45% strain during normal movement. The watch maintained full functionality during bending, stretching, and dynamic motion, including video playback and Bluetooth calling. Standby time was measured at 24.5 hours, and calling time at 90 minutes, matching quantitative discharge simulations under corresponding current loads. The battery was also tested under thermal conditions, showing no temperature rise during discharge due to its high surface-to-volume ratio, in contrast to conventional batteries that exhibited significant heating. The anode current collectors are fabricated from copper foils produced via electrolytic deposition and cold rolling, which enhances ductility and reduces internal stress. Cathode electrode layers are formed by coating aluminum foils with a slurry containing lithium cobalt oxide, conductive carbon black, and polyvinylidene fluoride binder, dried and calendared to achieve high density. Alternative cathode materials include manganese dioxide, fluorinated carbons, lithium sulfides, and lithium-containing composite oxides such as lithium nickel manganese cobalt oxide. Conductive agents such as carbon black or graphene enhance electron transport, while binders like carboxymethyl cellulose and styrene-butadiene rubber provide adhesion and flexibility. The separator is composed of microporous polypropylene or polyethylene, optionally coated with ceramic particles to improve thermal stability. Packaging is achieved using a multilayer laminated film consisting of a barrier layer of aluminum oxide, a resin layer of polyethylene terephthalate, and an outer sealant layer of polyethylene, with total thicknesses ranging from 50 to 150 micrometers to ensure flexibility and barrier properties. Gel polymer electrolytes may be substituted for liquid electrolytes to enhance safety and reduce leakage risk. The entire fabrication process is scalable and compatible with roll-to-roll manufacturing, enabling mass production of stretchable batteries for consumer, medical, and industrial applications.
