## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The present invention relates to the field of catalytic oxidation of methane, particularly in the context of exhaust gas treatment from lean-burn natural gas engines. More specifically, the invention pertains to a methane oxidation catalyst designed to operate efficiently under conditions of high humidity and the presence of sulfur-containing compounds, which are characteristic of real-world exhaust streams from natural gas vehicles. Such catalysts are critical for mitigating methane slip, a persistent environmental challenge associated with natural gas combustion, wherein unreacted methane—potent greenhouse gas—is emitted due to incomplete oxidation at low exhaust temperatures. The catalysts of this invention are engineered to maintain high activity and durability under prolonged exposure to water vapor and sulfur dioxide, conditions that typically cause rapid deactivation of conventional palladium-based oxidation catalysts. The invention is particularly suited for integration into aftertreatment systems for heavy-duty transportation, stationary power generation, and other applications utilizing lean-burn natural gas combustion technologies.

## BACKGROUND

- Natural gas has emerged as a preferred transitional fuel for reducing greenhouse gas emissions in heavy-duty and industrial applications due to its lower carbon intensity compared to diesel and gasoline. However, lean-burn natural gas engines, while efficient and clean in terms of nitrogen oxide and particulate emissions, consistently produce significant levels of unburned methane in their exhaust. This methane slip arises from incomplete combustion in cold zones of the combustion chamber and the inherent stability of the methane molecule, whose strong carbon-hydrogen bonds resist oxidation below 1000 °C. Conventional catalytic approaches for methane oxidation rely heavily on palladium-based systems, which demonstrate high intrinsic activity under ideal laboratory conditions. Yet, in practical exhaust environments, these catalysts suffer from severe deactivation due to the presence of water vapor and sulfur dioxide. Water vapor inhibits catalytic activity by forming stable hydroxyl species on palladium surfaces, blocking oxygen exchange pathways and reducing active site availability. Sulfur dioxide, even at low concentrations, irreversibly poisons palladium by forming inactive palladium sulfates or sulfides. Existing solutions, such as the use of alumina or zeolite supports, have shown limited success in improving hydrothermal stability, while the addition of platinum has been shown to mitigate sulfur poisoning to some extent. However, no prior system has successfully combined a high-surface-area mesoporous silica support with zirconium doping and a precisely controlled platinum-to-palladium ratio to simultaneously achieve high methane conversion, sulfur resistance, and long-term hydrothermal stability under realistic operating conditions.

## SUMMARY

- The present invention provides a methane oxidation catalyst comprising a mesoporous silica support doped with zirconium, impregnated with palladium and platinum in a specific weight ratio, and calcined under controlled conditions to achieve enhanced catalytic performance under sulfur-rich and humid exhaust streams. The catalyst exhibits a T50 value for methane conversion of less than 500 °C after aging under simulated lean-burn engine exhaust conditions containing water vapor and sulfur dioxide. The method for reducing methane emissions involves exposing a gas stream containing methane, oxygen, water vapor, and sulfur dioxide to the catalyst at temperatures between 250 °C and 600 °C, achieving methane conversion efficiencies exceeding 85% over extended operational periods. The catalyst composition includes a support material derived from SBA-15 mesoporous silica, doped with zirconium at a concentration between 5 and 15 weight percent, and loaded with palladium at 2 weight percent and platinum at 4 weight percent. The catalyst is prepared by sequential incipient wetness impregnation, first with a zirconium precursor, followed by palladium and then platinum precursors, with intermediate drying and calcination steps at 550 °C. The resulting catalyst exhibits a bimodal pore structure with pore diameters of approximately 3.8 nm and 5.6 nm, and the platinum exists predominantly in oxidized states (Pt²⁺ and Pt⁴⁺) stabilized by interaction with zirconium, which enhances methane activation. The catalyst demonstrates superior sulfur resistance by acting as a sacrificial sulfur trap, with zirconium preferentially binding sulfur dioxide and protecting palladium and platinum active sites. The catalyst also exhibits exceptional hydrothermal stability, maintaining structural integrity and catalytic activity after 40 hours of aging at 500 °C in the presence of 10 volume percent water vapor. The advantages of this catalyst include sustained high methane conversion under real-world exhaust conditions, extended service life without regeneration, and reduced dependency on precious metal loading compared to conventional systems requiring higher palladium content for comparable performance.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- The methane oxidation catalyst of the invention is defined as a heterogeneous catalytic material comprising a mesoporous silica support doped with zirconium and loaded with palladium and platinum as active components. The support material is derived from SBA-15, a thermally stable, ordered mesoporous silica with a hexagonal pore structure and high surface area, which provides a robust framework for metal dispersion. The zirconium is incorporated into the silica matrix at a concentration ranging from 5 to 15 weight percent, with optimal performance observed at 15 weight percent, where zirconium forms a tetragonal phase that interacts strongly with platinum and acts as a sulfur scavenger. The active phases consist of palladium primarily in the form of palladium oxide (PdO) and platinum existing in multiple oxidation states, including Pt⁰, Pt²⁺, and Pt⁴⁺, with the higher oxidation states being stabilized by the zirconium-doped support. The weight ratio of platinum to palladium is maintained at 2:1, corresponding to 4 weight percent platinum and 2 weight percent palladium, a ratio that balances catalytic activity with cost efficiency and prevents alloy formation between the two metals. The concentration of platinum and palladium is carefully controlled to ensure sufficient active site density without compromising pore accessibility or thermal stability. The T50 value—the temperature at which 50% methane conversion is achieved—is less than 500 °C after aging under simulated exhaust conditions, representing a significant improvement over prior art catalysts that exhibit T50 values exceeding 580 °C under identical conditions. The method of preparation involves incipient wetness impregnation, wherein the SBA-15 support is first treated with an aqueous solution of zirconium oxynitrate, dried at 120 °C, and calcined at 550 °C. Subsequently, palladium nitrate is impregnated, followed by drying and calcination, and finally tetraammineplatinum(II) nitrate is introduced using the same procedure. Sequential addition ensures distinct metal-support interactions and prevents premature alloying. Wet impregnation may be employed as an alternative method, though incipient wetness yields superior metal dispersion. Simultaneous addition of palladium and platinum precursors is not preferred, as it leads to reduced sulfur resistance and lower T50 performance. Calcination is performed at 550 °C for six hours in air to stabilize the oxide phases and remove organic residues. The gas stream to be treated contains between 0.5 and 2 volume percent methane, 5 to 15 volume percent oxygen, 5 to 15 volume percent water vapor, and 5 to 20 parts per million sulfur dioxide, with the remainder being nitrogen and carbon dioxide. Methane conversion is measured as the percentage reduction in methane concentration across the catalyst bed under steady-state or gradient temperature conditions. Sulfur resistance is demonstrated by the ability of the catalyst to maintain methane conversion above 60% after 40 hours of exposure to sulfur-containing exhaust, with minimal increase in outlet SO₂ concentration due to zirconium’s capacity to adsorb and retain sulfur species. Hydrothermal stability is confirmed by the absence of significant pore collapse or metal sintering following prolonged exposure to steam at elevated temperatures, as evidenced by retained surface area and unchanged T50 values.

## EXAMPLES

- Summary of catalysts prepared with varying zirconium content, metal loading sequences, and calcination conditions, demonstrating the reproducibility and scalability of the invention across multiple batches.

### Example 1: Catalysts with Pd and Pt on a Lanthanum Doped Alumina Exhibit Enhanced Methane Conversion after Aging in the Presence of Sulfur and Water

- The catalyst designated 2Pd/4Pt/15Zr/SBA-15 was prepared by incipient wetness impregnation of SBA-15 with zirconium oxynitrate to achieve 15 weight percent zirconium, followed by sequential impregnation with palladium and platinum precursors, with drying at 120 °C and calcination at 550 °C between each step. After aging for 40 hours at 500 °C in a gas stream containing 10 volume percent water vapor and 10 ppm sulfur dioxide, the catalyst exhibited a methane conversion of 60% at 500 °C, corresponding to a T50 value of 481 °C. Fresh catalyst performance showed a T50 of 360 °C, indicating a modest but acceptable loss of activity due to aging. The catalyst maintained structural integrity, with no detectable phase change in the zirconium or silica support, and the pore volume remained above 0.55 cm³/g.

### Example 2: A Catalyst with a Lanthanum Doped Alumina Support Exhibits Higher Activity in the Presence of Excess Water Vapour than a Catalyst with an Alumina Support not Doped with Lanthanum

- A comparative catalyst, 2Pd/4Pt/SBA-15 without zirconium doping, was prepared under identical conditions but with no zirconium addition. Under the same aging protocol and gas stream containing 10 volume percent water vapor, the undoped catalyst exhibited a T50 of 581 °C after aging, a 121 °C increase compared to its fresh state. In contrast, the 2Pd/4Pt/15Zr/SBA-15 catalyst showed only a 121 °C increase from 360 °C to 481 °C. The difference in performance was attributed to the absence of sulfur trapping capacity in the undoped catalyst, which allowed sulfur to directly poison palladium sites, whereas the zirconium-doped catalyst preserved active sites by preferentially adsorbing sulfur.

### Example 3: Sulfur Resistance and Hydrothermal Stability of Catalysts having an Alumina Support Doped with Lanthanum at Different Weight Ratios of Platinum and Palladium

- Three catalysts were prepared with 5, 10, and 15 weight percent zirconium, all loaded with 2 weight percent palladium and 4 weight percent platinum. After aging under identical sulfur and water conditions, the catalyst with 15 weight percent zirconium retained 60% methane conversion, while those with 10 and 5 weight percent zirconium retained 58% and 52% conversion, respectively. The catalyst with 5 weight percent zirconium showed a 19% increase in T50 from fresh to aged, while the 15 weight percent catalyst showed only a 121 °C increase. The outlet SO₂ concentration during gradient runs was lowest for the 15 weight percent zirconium catalyst, confirming its superior sulfur trapping capacity. Hydrothermal stability was further confirmed by nitrogen physisorption, which showed minimal reduction in surface area for all zirconium-doped catalysts after aging.

### Example 4: Effect of Calcination Temperature on Catalyst Activity

- Catalysts were prepared with 15 weight percent zirconium and calcined at 450 °C, 550 °C, and 650 °C. The catalyst calcined at 550 °C exhibited the lowest T50 both before and after aging, with a value of 481 °C. Catalysts calcined at 450 °C showed incomplete oxide formation and lower activity, while those calcined at 650 °C exhibited significant sintering of platinum particles, resulting in a T50 of 515 °C. The optimal calcination temperature was determined to be 550 °C, balancing phase stabilization with metal dispersion.

### Example 5: Effect of Method Preparation on Catalyst Activity

- Two catalysts were prepared using incipient wetness impregnation and wet impregnation methods, respectively, both with 15 weight percent zirconium and the same metal loadings. The incipient wetness catalyst achieved a T50 of 481 °C after aging, while the wet impregnation catalyst exhibited a T50 of 502 °C. The incipient wetness method yielded more uniform metal distribution and smaller particle sizes, as confirmed by XPS and TEM, resulting in higher active site density and improved sulfur resistance. Sequential addition of metals was critical; simultaneous impregnation led to reduced platinum oxidation states and lower methane conversion efficiency.
