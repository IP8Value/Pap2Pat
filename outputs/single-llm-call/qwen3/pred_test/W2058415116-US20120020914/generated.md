# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular oncology and virology, specifically to methods for the diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic management of epithelial cancers associated with persistent human papillomavirus infection. More particularly, the invention encompasses compositions and methods utilizing the detection and modulation of the oncoprotein ODAM, or antibodies directed against ODAM, for the identification of HPV-driven neoplastic progression and for the development of targeted interventions in cervical, anal, oropharyngeal, and other HPV-associated epithelial malignancies. The invention further pertains to diagnostic assays and therapeutic protocols that leverage the biological relationship between ODAM expression and the transcriptional activity of the HPV long control region, thereby enabling precision-based clinical decision-making in the context of precancerous and malignant lesions.

## BACKGROUND

Human papillomavirus infection is a necessary, though not sufficient, factor in the development of a significant proportion of human epithelial cancers. Despite the availability of prophylactic vaccines, a substantial global burden of persistent HPV infection remains, particularly in populations with limited access to screening and vaccination programs. The progression from asymptomatic viral colonization to high-grade dysplasia and invasive carcinoma is driven by the sustained expression of the viral E6 and E7 oncoproteins, which disrupt critical tumor suppressor pathways including p53 and retinoblastoma protein. Current diagnostic modalities, such as Pap smear cytology and HPV DNA testing, lack the specificity to distinguish transient infections from those with high malignant potential. Therapeutic options for high-grade lesions are largely surgical or ablative, with no approved pharmacologic agents capable of reversing the molecular phenotype of HPV-transformed cells. Chemotherapy and radiation remain palliative for metastatic disease, and no targeted biologic therapy has been successfully translated into routine clinical practice. There is a critical unmet need for non-invasive, mechanism-based interventions that can halt or reverse the oncogenic cascade driven by HPV without inducing systemic toxicity or compromising epithelial integrity.

## SUMMARY

The invention discloses a novel biological discovery that the odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein (ODAM) is aberrantly expressed in human epithelial cancers harboring high-risk human papillomavirus, and that the presence or absence of ODAM correlates with the transcriptional activity of the viral long control region and the clinical aggressiveness of the lesion. The invention further provides methods for diagnosing, stratifying, and managing HPV-associated epithelial cancers by detecting ODAM protein or anti-ODAM antibodies in clinical specimens, and for modulating disease progression through the administration of ODAM-targeting agents. In a first embodiment, the invention provides a method for diagnosing a HPV-associated epithelial cancer in a subject by detecting the presence of ODAM protein in a tissue sample obtained from the subject, wherein the presence of ODAM indicates a high-risk lesion requiring intervention. In a second embodiment, the invention provides a method for monitoring disease progression in a subject with a known HPV-associated epithelial lesion by serially measuring ODAM expression levels in serial biopsies or liquid biopsies over time, wherein an increase in ODAM correlates with progression toward malignancy. In a third embodiment, the invention provides a method for predicting response to therapy by determining baseline ODAM expression prior to treatment, wherein high ODAM expression predicts greater likelihood of response to ODAM-inhibiting agents. In a fourth embodiment, the invention provides a method for treating a HPV-associated epithelial cancer by administering to a subject an agent that reduces ODAM expression or activity, thereby inducing cell-cycle arrest or differentiation of transformed epithelial cells. In a fifth embodiment, the invention provides a method for identifying subjects at risk of developing HPV-associated cancer by detecting anti-ODAM antibodies in serum or plasma, wherein the presence of such antibodies indicates immune recognition of early neoplastic transformation. In a sixth embodiment, the invention provides a diagnostic kit comprising a solid support coated with an ODAM-specific binding agent, a detection reagent, and instructions for use in detecting ODAM in clinical samples. In a seventh embodiment, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising an ODAM-neutralizing antibody or ODAM-silencing oligonucleotide formulated for topical, intravaginal, or intratumoral delivery to HPV-infected epithelial tissues. In an eighth embodiment, the invention provides a method for validating the efficacy of a therapeutic intervention by measuring the reduction of ODAM expression following treatment, wherein a sustained decrease in ODAM levels correlates with durable clinical remission.

## DEFINITIONS

Conventional techniques in molecular biology, immunology, and oncology are employed in the practice of the invention and are defined herein with reference to standard authoritative texts including Molecular Cloning: A Laboratory Manual by Sambrook and Russell, Fundamental Immunology by Paul, and Cancer Biology by Weinberg. The term “ODAM” refers to the odontogenic ameloblast-associated protein, a secreted extracellular matrix protein encoded by the ODAM gene located on human chromosome 19q13.43, which is normally expressed in developing dental epithelium but is aberrantly re-expressed in HPV-transformed epithelial cells. The term “HPV-associated epithelial cancer” refers to any neoplasm arising from squamous or glandular epithelium in which high-risk human papillomavirus DNA is integrated and transcriptionally active, including but not limited to cervical, anal, vulvar, vaginal, penile, oropharyngeal, and laryngeal carcinomas. The term “anti-ODAM antibody” refers to any immunoglobulin molecule, whether polyclonal or monoclonal, that specifically binds to an epitope of the ODAM protein with an affinity sufficient to detect or neutralize its biological activity. The term “detection” includes qualitative or quantitative identification of ODAM or anti-ODAM antibodies by immunoassay, immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, mass spectrometry, or nucleic acid hybridization. The term “therapeutic agent” encompasses small molecules, peptides, antibodies, antisense oligonucleotides, siRNA, shRNA, CRISPR-based gene editors, or fusion proteins designed to inhibit ODAM expression or function. The term “clinical specimen” includes tissue biopsies, cytology smears, blood, serum, plasma, saliva, or urine samples obtained from a subject for diagnostic or monitoring purposes. The term “high-risk HPV type” refers to HPV genotypes classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer as having sufficient evidence of carcinogenicity in humans, including but not limited to HPV-16, HPV-18, HPV-31, HPV-33, HPV-35, HPV-45, HPV-52, and HPV-58.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Introduction to Papillomavirus (PV) and Its Relation to Cervical Cancer

Papillomaviruses are small, non-enveloped double-stranded DNA viruses that specifically infect stratified squamous epithelia and are associated with a spectrum of benign and malignant lesions. The oncogenic potential of certain HPV types is well established, with persistent infection by high-risk genotypes being the primary etiological factor in nearly all cases of cervical cancer. The viral life cycle is tightly linked to the differentiation state of the host epithelium, with viral genome replication and late gene expression occurring predominantly in the upper layers of the epithelium. In the context of malignant transformation, viral DNA frequently integrates into the host genome, leading to the dysregulated expression of the E6 and E7 oncoproteins, which inactivate tumor suppressor pathways and promote genomic instability. The long control region (LCR) of the HPV genome serves as the master regulatory element governing transcription of E6 and E7, and its activity is modulated by both viral and host cellular factors. The discovery that ODAM is expressed in HPV-transformed epithelial cells and that its expression correlates with LCR activity represents a novel biomarker and therapeutic target for HPV-associated neoplasia.

### Classification of Papillomaviruses

Papillomaviruses are classified into five genera—alpha, beta, gamma, mu, and nu—based on nucleotide sequence divergence of the L1 capsid gene, with sequence identity thresholds of less than 60% defining distinct species. The alpha-papillomaviruses, particularly HPV-16 and HPV-18, are the most clinically significant due to their strong association with anogenital and oropharyngeal cancers. The HPV genome is approximately 8 kilobases in length and encodes early (E1–E7) and late (L1–L2) proteins, with the E6 and E7 open reading frames being the primary drivers of cellular transformation. The LCR contains multiple transcription factor binding sites, including SP1, AP1, and nuclear factor I sites, as well as enhancer and promoter elements that respond to cellular signaling pathways. Phylogenetic analysis of PV types reveals distinct evolutionary lineages, with high-risk types clustering within the alpha genus and exhibiting conserved structural motifs in the LCR that facilitate interaction with host transcriptional regulators. The identification of ODAM as a host factor selectively expressed in cells harboring high-risk HPV types suggests a previously unrecognized host-virus interface that may be exploited for diagnostic and therapeutic purposes.

### Papillomaviruses and Cancer

Cervical cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related death among women in low-resource settings, despite the availability of screening programs in developed countries. The transition from low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion to high-grade dysplasia and invasive carcinoma is characterized by the accumulation of genetic and epigenetic alterations, with persistent E6 and E7 expression being the central oncogenic driver. E6 mediates the degradation of p53 through ubiquitin-dependent proteolysis, while E7 binds and inactivates the retinoblastoma protein, leading to unscheduled S-phase entry and genomic instability. Viral integration into the host genome often disrupts the E2 gene, which normally represses the LCR, resulting in constitutive overexpression of E6 and E7. The presence of ODAM in these transformed cells, but not in normal epithelium or low-grade lesions, indicates a specific molecular signature of advanced neoplastic progression. ODAM expression is not observed in HPV-negative epithelial cancers, nor in benign papillomas, suggesting its specificity for malignant transformation driven by high-risk HPV. This makes ODAM a unique biomarker for distinguishing indolent from aggressive HPV-associated lesions.

### Diagnosis and Treatment of HPV

Current diagnostic approaches for HPV infection include cytology, HPV DNA testing, and mRNA assays such as the Hybrid Capture II test. While these methods detect viral presence, they do not reliably predict which infections will progress to cancer. The detection of ODAM protein or anti-ODAM antibodies provides a functional readout of oncogenic activity, offering superior prognostic value. Therapeutic strategies for low-grade lesions often involve observation, while high-grade lesions are treated with excisional procedures such as loop electrosurgical excision or cold knife conization. These interventions are effective but carry risks of preterm labor and cervical insufficiency in reproductive-age women. The development of pharmacologic agents that target ODAM offers a non-surgical alternative capable of reversing the molecular phenotype of dysplastic cells. The inhibition of ODAM leads to downregulation of E6 and E7, restoration of p53 and pRb function, and induction of cellular differentiation, thereby halting progression without tissue destruction.

### Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) and its Utility in Treating HPV-Transformed Cells by Inhibiting LCR-Driven Transcription

Although LIF has been shown to inhibit HPV transcription and reduce proliferation of cervical cancer cells, the mechanism underlying this effect was previously unknown. The present invention reveals that LIF exerts its inhibitory effect on the HPV LCR indirectly through the suppression of ODAM expression. In HPV-transformed cells, ODAM acts as a transcriptional coactivator that stabilizes the binding of host transcription factors to the LCR. LIF signaling through the gp130/LIFR receptor complex leads to the downregulation of ODAM mRNA and protein, thereby disrupting the transcriptional machinery required for E6 and E7 expression. This finding establishes ODAM as the critical downstream mediator of LIF’s anti-viral activity and provides a direct mechanistic link between cytokine signaling and viral oncogene regulation. The therapeutic potential of targeting ODAM is enhanced by the fact that ODAM is not expressed in most adult tissues, minimizing the risk of systemic toxicity.

### Manipulating Proteins, DNA, and RNA

The detection and modulation of ODAM rely on established molecular and cellular techniques. ODAM protein can be detected in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue sections using immunohistochemistry with ODAM-specific monoclonal antibodies. Soluble ODAM can be quantified in serum or plasma using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays or multiplex immunoassays. ODAM mRNA levels can be measured by quantitative reverse transcription PCR using primers and probes specific to the ODAM transcript. For therapeutic purposes, ODAM expression can be silenced using antisense oligonucleotides, small interfering RNAs, or CRISPR interference systems delivered via lipid nanoparticles or viral vectors. Monoclonal antibodies against ODAM can be generated using hybridoma technology or phage display, and can be engineered for enhanced half-life, reduced immunogenicity, or conjugated to cytotoxic payloads for targeted delivery. The use of mass spectrometry enables the identification of post-translational modifications of ODAM that may influence its stability or function in transformed cells.

### Pharmaceutical Manufacturing

Pharmaceutical compositions for the treatment of HPV-associated epithelial cancers may be formulated as topical creams, vaginal suppositories, intravaginal gels, or injectable suspensions for direct delivery to the transformation zone. The active ingredient may be ODAM-neutralizing antibodies, ODAM-silencing RNA, or small-molecule inhibitors of ODAM expression. Excipients such as carbopol, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, and glycerin may be used to enhance mucosal adherence and prolong local exposure. The formulation may include penetration enhancers such as oleic acid or caprylocaproyl macrogol-8 glycerides to facilitate transmucosal delivery. The product may be manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practices, with quality control measures including sterility testing, potency assays, and stability studies under accelerated conditions. The formulation may be packaged in single-use applicators or pre-filled syringes for ease of administration in outpatient settings.

### Experimental Procedures

Experimental validation of the invention was performed using established cell culture models of HPV-positive cervical carcinoma, including SiHa and CaSki cell lines. ODAM expression was assessed by Western blot, immunofluorescence, and quantitative PCR following treatment with LIF or other cytokines. Luciferase reporter assays demonstrated that ODAM knockdown reduces LCR-driven transcription. Cell proliferation was measured by MTT assay, and cell cycle analysis was performed by flow cytometry. In vivo models of cervical dysplasia were established using xenografts of HPV-positive cells in immunodeficient mice, and treatment with ODAM-targeting agents resulted in significant reduction in lesion size and ODAM expression. Statistical analysis confirmed the significance of these findings with p-values less than 0.05.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

An HPV-16 reporter cell line was generated by transfecting SiHa cells with a plasmid containing the full-length HPV-16 LCR upstream of a secreted Gaussia luciferase gene. Upon treatment with recombinant human LIF, a dose-dependent reduction in luciferase activity was observed, with maximal inhibition at 10 ng/mL after 72 hours. Quantitative real-time PCR confirmed a corresponding decrease in E6 and E7 mRNA levels. Knockdown of ODAM using siRNA replicated the inhibitory effect of LIF, while overexpression of ODAM abolished LIF-mediated suppression of the LCR, demonstrating that ODAM is necessary and sufficient for LCR activity.

### Example 2

Phosphorylation of STAT3 was analyzed by intracellular flow cytometry in SiHa cells treated with LIF. A transient increase in phospho-STAT3 was observed within 15 minutes, followed by a decline by 24 hours. Concurrently, ODAM protein levels decreased over the same time course. Inhibition of STAT3 phosphorylation with a JAK inhibitor prevented the downregulation of ODAM, indicating that LIF suppresses ODAM expression via the JAK-STAT signaling pathway.

### Example 3

CaSki cells were treated with LIF, ODAM siRNA, or control oligonucleotides, and proliferation was measured over five days. Both LIF and ODAM knockdown resulted in a 50% reduction in cell number compared to controls. Cell cycle analysis revealed an accumulation of cells in G1 phase and a reduction in S-phase entry. No increase in apoptosis was observed, indicating that ODAM inhibition induces cell-cycle arrest rather than cell death. These data confirm that ODAM is a critical regulator of proliferation in HPV-transformed cells and that its targeting is sufficient to halt neoplastic progression.
