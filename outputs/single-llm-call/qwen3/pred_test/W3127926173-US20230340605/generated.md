# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to biomarkers for predicting clinical response to vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) inhibitory drugs in patients with solid malignant tumors. Specifically, the invention provides a method for identifying individuals who are likely to benefit from treatment with VEGF-A inhibitors, such as bevacizumab or biosimilar agents, by measuring the expression levels of a defined set of proteins or their corresponding mRNA transcripts in tumor tissue samples obtained prior to initiation of therapy. The invention further encompasses the use of a composite predictive score derived from these molecular measurements to guide therapeutic decision-making, enabling the selection of patients most likely to achieve pathologic complete response or low residual cancer burden following combination treatment with a VEGF-A inhibitory drug and chemotherapy. This approach addresses a critical unmet need in oncology by moving beyond population-level efficacy data to enable precision-based patient stratification for anti-angiogenic therapies.

## BACKGROUND

Anti-angiogenic therapies have emerged as a cornerstone in the treatment of various advanced solid tumors, particularly through the inhibition of vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A), a key signaling molecule that promotes the formation of new blood vessels essential for tumor growth and metastasis. The development of bevacizumab, a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody that binds and neutralizes VEGF-A, represented a major therapeutic advance, with clinical benefits demonstrated in multiple cancer types including colorectal, lung, and breast cancers. When combined with standard chemotherapy regimens, bevacizumab has been shown to extend progression-free survival and, in some cases, overall survival. However, these benefits are not uniformly observed across all treated patients, and in certain indications, such as breast cancer, regulatory approval remains restricted due to insufficient evidence of consistent survival advantage in unselected populations. 

Despite the biological plausibility of using plasma VEGF-A levels as a predictive biomarker, large-scale clinical trials, including the MERiDiAN trial, have failed to validate this approach, indicating that circulating levels of the target ligand do not reliably reflect tumor-specific pathway activity or therapeutic responsiveness. Other molecular biomarkers, such as BRCA1/2 mutations, DNA methylation signatures, and soluble carbonic anhydrase IX, have been explored but have not demonstrated sufficient predictive power or reproducibility across diverse patient cohorts. While gene expression profiles have been widely investigated, the functional state of proteins—particularly phosphorylated forms that reflect active signaling pathways—has been largely overlooked in the context of VEGF-A inhibitor response prediction. Protein-level analysis offers a more direct readout of cellular activity, as post-translational modifications and protein abundance are more closely linked to phenotypic outcomes than mRNA levels alone. Existing methods for identifying predictive signatures often rely on unsupervised clustering or single-marker associations, which lack the statistical rigor and biological coherence required for clinical translation. There remains a significant gap in the field for a validated, reproducible, and functionally grounded method capable of identifying patients who will derive meaningful clinical benefit from VEGF-A inhibitory therapy.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides an in vitro method for predicting the likelihood of clinical response to a VEGF-A inhibitory drug in a patient with a solid malignant tumor, wherein the method comprises obtaining a tumor tissue sample from the patient prior to administration of the drug, measuring the expression levels of a panel of nine specific proteins or their corresponding mRNA transcripts, calculating a VEGF inhibitory Response Predictor (ViRP) score based on a weighted combination of these measurements, and interpreting the score to determine whether the patient is likely to respond to treatment. The ViRP score is derived from a mathematical model incorporating an intercept and beta coefficients determined through adaptive Lasso regression applied to protein expression data from a discovery cohort of patients treated with chemotherapy in combination with bevacizumab. The invention further encompasses a second aspect directed to a method of treating a solid malignant tumor by administering a VEGF-A inhibitory drug in combination with chemotherapy to a patient identified as likely to respond based on the ViRP score. A third aspect of the invention provides a kit for performing the method, comprising reagents for quantifying the expression of the nine proteins or their corresponding genes, instructions for calculating the ViRP score, and a reference cutoff value for determining response likelihood. A fourth aspect introduces a method for identifying additional predictive molecular signatures by applying the same statistical framework to other drug-treatment combinations or cancer types. A fifth aspect describes a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a VEGF-A inhibitory drug, optionally in combination with a chemotherapeutic agent, for use in patients selected according to the ViRP score. Embodiments of the composition include bevacizumab, ranibizumab, aflibercept, or biosimilar variants thereof, formulated for intravenous administration.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention defines a method for predicting clinical response to a VEGF-A inhibitory drug in a patient diagnosed with a solid malignant tumor. For the purposes of this invention, a patient is considered “responsive to a VEGF-A inhibitory drug” if they achieve a pathologic complete response (pCR), defined as the absence of invasive cancer in the breast and lymph nodes following neoadjuvant therapy, or a low residual cancer burden (RCB), classified as RCB class 0 or I, as determined by standardized histopathological assessment. Response evaluation is conducted in accordance with the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) guidelines, with particular emphasis on tumor shrinkage and pathologic outcome metrics. The method begins with the acquisition of a tumor tissue sample from the patient, obtained prior to initiation of any treatment involving a VEGF-A inhibitory drug. The sample may be derived from a core needle biopsy, fine-needle aspiration, or surgical resection, and may be preserved in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue, fresh frozen tissue, or stabilized in RNA-protective or protein-stabilizing solutions. The invention is applicable to a broad range of solid malignant tumors, including but not limited to breast cancer, colorectal cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, ovarian cancer, renal cell carcinoma, and glioblastoma.

Following sample acquisition, gene-specific polynucleotides or proteins are collected and quantified using established molecular profiling techniques. For protein quantification, reverse-phase protein arrays (RPPA) are employed, wherein tumor lysates are serially diluted, immobilized on nitrocellulose membranes, and probed with validated antibodies targeting specific proteins, followed by chemiluminescent or colorimetric detection. For mRNA quantification, RNA is extracted and subjected to high-throughput sequencing (RNAseq) or quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR). Expression values are normalized to account for technical variability and biological heterogeneity. Normalization may be performed using endogenous control genes such as ACTB, GAPDH, HPRT1, or TBP, or through global normalization methods that adjust for total protein or RNA content across the sample set. In embodiments utilizing RPPA, protein signals are log2-transformed and median-centered across the cohort to ensure comparability. For mRNA data, quantile normalization and probe averaging are applied prior to score calculation.

The invention introduces the ViRP (VEGF inhibitory Response Predictor) signature, a composite molecular score derived from the expression levels of nine specific proteins: Syk, ACC-pS79, ERK1/2-pT202/Y204, STAT3-pY705, PLCG1-pY783, FAK-pY397, Src-pY416, PTEN-pS380, and p27-pT157. The ViRP score is calculated as the sum of the product of each protein’s expression level and its corresponding beta coefficient, plus a predefined intercept value. These coefficients were derived from adaptive Lasso regression modeling in a discovery cohort of patients with HER2-negative breast cancer treated with bevacizumab plus chemotherapy, using relative tumor size reduction as the continuous outcome variable. Phosphorylated protein measurements are included in the model to capture activated signaling states, and normalization of each molecule is performed relative to the median expression of the entire cohort. The ViRP score may be calculated using either protein or mRNA data, with mRNA surrogate scores computed using the same weights derived from the protein model. A cutoff value for dichotomizing patients into likely responders and non-responders is established using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, with the optimal threshold selected to maximize the sum of sensitivity and specificity for pathologic complete response. In one embodiment, the cutoff is set at 0.366, yielding a predictive accuracy with an area under the curve of 0.85 for pCR.

The method offers distinct advantages over existing approaches by integrating functional protein data, employing rigorous variable selection, and validating performance across independent cohorts. The predictive model is constructed using official gene and protein identifiers from standardized databases such as UniProt and HGNC to ensure reproducibility. The ViRP score is used to inform treatment strategies by identifying patients who are statistically more likely to benefit from the addition of a VEGF-A inhibitory drug to chemotherapy, thereby avoiding unnecessary toxicity and cost in non-responders. The method may be applied in both neoadjuvant and adjuvant treatment settings, and may be extended to metastatic disease. In the neoadjuvant setting, the ViRP score enables preoperative selection of patients for combination therapy, while in the adjuvant setting, it may guide decisions regarding post-surgical treatment intensification. Chemotherapy regimens may include taxanes, anthracyclines, platinum agents, or capecitabine, administered in combination with an anti-VEGF-A antibody such as bevacizumab. The relative importance of individual proteins within the signature is determined using metrics such as lmg, with Syk identified as the most influential component. Protein combinations are modeled exhaustively to assess all possible configurations, and the final signature is selected based on predictive performance, stability, and biological plausibility.

### Treatment Regimen

In the neoadjuvant treatment setting, the ViRP score is calculated from a pre-treatment tumor biopsy, and patients with a score below the established cutoff are selected for treatment with a VEGF-A inhibitory drug in combination with chemotherapy. This approach has been validated in the NeoAVA phase II clinical trial, wherein patients with low ViRP scores exhibited significantly higher rates of pathologic complete response and low residual cancer burden compared to those with high scores. In the adjuvant setting, the ViRP score may be used to determine whether patients with residual disease after primary surgery should receive additional systemic therapy that includes a VEGF-A inhibitor. The combination of bevacizumab with chemotherapy has been shown to improve progression-free survival in metastatic breast cancer, and the ViRP score provides a mechanism to identify which patients are most likely to benefit from this regimen. The NeoAVA and PROMIX trials serve as foundational datasets for the development and validation of the method, with consistent performance observed across both cohorts.

### Relative Importance of Proteins and Modelling of Protein Combinations

The relative importance of each protein in the ViRP signature is ranked using the lmg metric, which quantifies the proportion of variance in response explained by each variable. Syk, a tyrosine kinase involved in immune cell signaling and angiogenesis, is identified as the most important predictor, followed by ERK1/2-pT202/Y204 and STAT3-pY705, both of which are central to mitogenic and survival signaling pathways. All possible combinations of the nine proteins are evaluated using logistic regression models, and the final signature is selected based on its ability to maximize predictive accuracy while minimizing overfitting. The model demonstrates superior performance compared to single-protein biomarkers or gene expression signatures derived from unrelated biological processes. The inclusion of phosphorylated proteins enhances biological relevance, as these modifications reflect active signaling states induced by VEGF-A inhibition. The signature is not merely a statistical construct but reflects a coherent biological network involved in tumor-stroma interactions, endothelial cell activation, and immune modulation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Identification of Ten Protein Prognostic Signature

The invention is illustrated through the identification of a ten-protein prognostic signature in a cohort of 109 patients with HER2-negative breast cancer enrolled in the NeoAVA clinical trial. Patients were randomized to receive either chemotherapy alone or chemotherapy in combination with bevacizumab. Tumor biopsies collected prior to treatment were analyzed using reverse-phase protein arrays to quantify the expression of 210 cancer-relevant proteins, including 54 phosphorylated forms. Low-variance proteins were excluded using a mixed-model distribution analysis, leaving 114 proteins for further modeling. Adaptive Lasso regression was applied to identify proteins whose expression levels best predicted relative tumor size reduction after neoadjuvant treatment. The penalty parameter lambda was determined via leave-one-out cross-validation using mean absolute error as the loss function. A final signature of nine proteins was derived after iterative Lasso regression, with one protein shrunk to zero. The ViRP score was calculated for each patient and demonstrated a strong correlation with tumor shrinkage (Pearson R² = 0.67, P < .001). The score was significantly lower in patients achieving pCR or low RCB, with area under the ROC curve of 0.85 for pCR. Validation in the independent PROMIX cohort confirmed the predictive utility of the mRNA surrogate score, with a significant difference in ViRP scores between responders and non-responders (P < .001). The method enables the identification of patients with a greater than two-fold increase in response rate when selected by ViRP score, establishing its potential for clinical implementation.
