# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- The present invention relates to a novel peptide siderophore produced by Gram-positive bacteria, specifically strains of *Bacillus graminis*, and to the use of such peptide siderophores in enhancing iron acquisition in plants and soil systems. The peptide siderophore is characterized by a unique cyclic peptide backbone incorporating N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands that enable high-affinity chelation of ferric iron under physiological conditions. The bacterial cell producing this peptide siderophore is a non-pathogenic, rhizosphere-colonizing microorganism capable of synthesizing the compound under iron-limiting conditions through a nonribosomal peptide synthetase (NRPS) pathway. This bacterial strain exhibits a distinct metabolic profile that distinguishes it from known siderophore producers, as it simultaneously generates a bioactive nitrogen oxide moiety alongside the iron-chelating scaffold, thereby conferring dual functionality in both nutrient acquisition and signaling. The invention encompasses the isolated peptide siderophore, genetically modified variants thereof, and compositions comprising the peptide siderophore for agricultural and environmental applications.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- Iron is an essential micronutrient for all living organisms, playing a central role in electron transport, DNA synthesis, and enzymatic catalysis. In terrestrial ecosystems, particularly in alkaline or calcareous soils, iron exists predominantly in the insoluble ferric (Fe³⁺) oxidation state, rendering it biologically unavailable to most plants and microorganisms. Conventional iron transport mechanisms in plants rely on Strategy I and Strategy II systems, which involve proton extrusion and phytosiderophore secretion, respectively; however, these pathways are energetically costly and often inefficient under conditions of high pH or competing cations such as calcium and phosphate. Existing synthetic iron chelators, including EDTA and EDDHA, are environmentally persistent, exhibit poor biodegradability, and may leach into groundwater, posing ecological risks. Moreover, microbial siderophores produced by known bacterial genera such as *Pseudomonas* and *Bacillus* typically feature hydroxamate, catecholate, or carboxylate ligands, all of which are susceptible to hydrolysis or competitive displacement under fluctuating soil pH and redox conditions. There remains a critical unmet need for a novel class of iron-chelating agents that combine high stability, environmental compatibility, and additional physiological functions such as nitric oxide release, which can modulate plant defense responses and nutrient uptake efficiency. The present invention addresses this gap by introducing a peptide siderophore with unprecedented structural and functional properties.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of efficient, sustainable, and multifunctional iron delivery systems for use in agriculture, particularly under iron-deficient soil conditions where conventional chelators fail or cause environmental harm. The solution to this problem is the discovery of a novel peptide siderophore, herein named gramibactin, which contains N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands covalently integrated into a cyclic peptide scaffold, enabling exceptional affinity for ferric iron while simultaneously acting as a controlled-release donor of nitric oxide. Gramibactin is produced by *Bacillus graminis* and exhibits a dual function: it solubilizes and transports iron to plant roots, and it triggers localized nitric oxide signaling that enhances root architecture and iron transporter expression. PET-CT tracer studies using gallium-68-labeled gramibactin demonstrated rapid and selective uptake by plant root systems, with minimal translocation to non-target tissues, confirming its targeted delivery capability. In vivo fluorescence imaging confirmed the colocalization of iron and nitric oxide signals in root epidermal cells, indicating coordinated nutrient and signaling delivery. In vitro assays revealed that gramibactin forms a stable 1:1 complex with Fe³⁺ with a formation constant exceeding 10⁴⁵ M⁻¹, surpassing all known bacterial hydroxamate siderophores. Ecological and agricultural implications include the potential for reduced fertilizer inputs, enhanced crop resilience under stress, and the promotion of beneficial plant-microbe interactions. The chelating properties of N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands arise from their ability to form bidentate coordination with iron through oxygen atoms of the nitroso and hydroxylamine moieties, creating a rigid, low-spin complex resistant to ligand exchange. A preferred embodiment of the peptide siderophore comprises a cyclic hexapeptide with two side chains bearing N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands. Another embodiment features a hydroxy carboxylic acid ligand appended to the peptide backbone to enhance solubility. The peptide is defined by the sequence X1-X2-X3-Gra1-X4-Gra2, wherein Gra1 and Gra2 are non-proteinogenic residues containing the N-nitroso-hydroxylamine functionality. A particularly preferred sequence is Asp-Thr1-Thr2-Gra1-Gly-Gra2, wherein Thr1 and the C-terminus of Gra2 are modified to incorporate the chelating ligands. A cyclic variant of this peptide is formed via amide bond closure between the N-terminus of Asp and the C-terminus of Gra2. Another embodiment is the cyclic peptide of X2-X3-Gra1-X4-Gra2, wherein X2 and X3 are hydrophobic amino acids. The N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligand consists of a nitrogen atom bearing both a nitroso group and a hydroxylamine group, forming a unique N–N–O chelating unit. The invention further encompasses a cyclic peptide siderophore according to Formula I, wherein the ring is formed by a lactam linkage between the α-amino group of Asp and the carboxyl group of Gra2. Formula II describes a variant wherein the ring is stabilized by a thioether bridge between cysteine residues flanking Gra1 and Gra2. The bacterial cell producing the peptide siderophore is a strain of *Bacillus graminis* deposited under accession number DSM 12345, capable of constitutive or inducible expression of the NRPS gene cluster responsible for gramibactin biosynthesis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- Iron is indispensable for cellular respiration, photosynthesis, and nitrogen fixation in both prokaryotes and eukaryotes. Siderophores are low-molecular-weight iron-chelating compounds secreted by microorganisms to scavenge ferric iron from the environment. These molecules typically contain oxygen-rich ligands such as hydroxamates, catecholates, or carboxylates that form octahedral complexes with Fe³⁺. The binding of iron to these ligands is governed by hard acid-hard base interactions, with stability constants often exceeding 10³⁰ M⁻¹. The present invention introduces a new class of siderophores wherein the iron-binding moiety is an N-nitroso-hydroxylamine group, a previously unreported ligand in natural siderophores. This ligand combines the electron-donating capacity of hydroxylamine with the π-accepting character of the nitroso group, resulting in a synergistic stabilization of the Fe³⁺ center. Peptide siderophores are biosynthesized by large multimodular nonribosomal peptide synthetase (NRPS) complexes that assemble amino acid precursors in a template-driven manner. The production of such compounds can be achieved either through fermentation of producing strains or through chemical synthesis using solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques. The peptide siderophores of the present invention are distinguished by their cyclic architecture, which enhances metabolic stability and reduces proteolytic degradation in soil environments. The inclusion of non-proteinogenic amino acids bearing N-nitroso-hydroxylamine functionality enables a level of metal selectivity and binding kinetics not achievable with conventional siderophores.

SEQ ID NO: 2 is the mature cyclic peptide DTTZGZ, wherein Asp is the N-terminal residue, Thr1 and Thr2 are hydroxylated threonine residues modified at their β-carbons with N-nitroso-hydroxylamine groups, Gra1 and Gra2 are identical cyclic N-nitroso-hydroxylamine-containing residues, and Gly is the central spacer. SEQ ID NO: 3 is the truncated variant XXZXZ, wherein X2 and X3 are aliphatic residues, and Gra1 and Gra2 are as defined above. The nitroso group is defined as –N=O, the hydroxylamine group as –NH–OH, and the N-nitroso-hydroxylamine group as –N(–OH)=O, wherein the nitrogen atom is directly bonded to both the hydroxyl oxygen and the nitroso oxygen. The hydroxy carboxylic acid ligand is a substituent containing a terminal –OH and –COOH group separated by one or two methylene units. Hydroxamate refers to a functional group of the structure –C(O)–NH–OH, catecholate to a 1,2-dihydroxybenzene moiety, and salicylate to a 2-hydroxybenzoate group. Modifications to the peptide siderophore include N-terminal acetylation, C-terminal amidation, and side chain esterification. Conservative amino acid substitutions include replacement of leucine with isoleucine, aspartate with glutamate, or serine with threonine. Alkyl denotes a saturated hydrocarbon chain of 1 to 12 carbons, alkoxy denotes an alkyl group bonded via oxygen, carboxyl denotes –COOH, and hydroxyl denotes –OH. The peptide siderophore promotes plant growth by increasing iron bioavailability and inducing nitric oxide-mediated upregulation of ferric reductase and iron transporter genes. Compositions of the invention include aqueous suspensions, granular formulations, and seed coatings containing the peptide siderophore in concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 100 μg/mL. Formulations may include surfactants, stabilizers, and slow-release matrices for soil application.

## Methods of Administration

- The peptide siderophore of the invention may be applied to plants or plant parts via foliar spray, root drench, seed treatment, or soil incorporation. Treatment of plant roots is preferred, as it facilitates direct interaction with rhizosphere microorganisms and root epidermal cells. Soil treatment involves mixing the peptide siderophore with organic matter or clay carriers to enhance retention and slow release. Plant-bacterial interactions are enhanced when the peptide siderophore is co-applied with live cultures of *Bacillus graminis*, which continue to produce the compound in situ. The invention further encompasses the use of plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) engineered to overexpress the gramibactin biosynthetic gene cluster, thereby sustaining long-term iron delivery and nitric oxide signaling in the rhizosphere.

## EXAMPLES

- Rhizosphere bacteria were isolated from wheat fields in alkaline soils and screened for siderophore production. A strain designated *Bacillus graminis* DSM 12345 was identified as a potent producer. Siderophore biosynthesis was confirmed by detection of the NRPS gene cluster encoding three modules with adenylation domains specific for aspartate, threonine, and non-proteinogenic N-nitroso-hydroxylamine-containing residues. Structural prediction via bioinformatics aligned with mass spectrometry data. Siderophore production was detected using CAS agar, and gene cluster responsibility was confirmed via targeted knockout. Structure elucidation was performed by high-resolution NMR and tandem mass spectrometry. NMR spectra revealed characteristic shifts for N–N=O and N–OH protons. Absolute configuration was determined by Mosher ester analysis. N-nitroso-hydroxylamine functionality was confirmed by infrared spectroscopy and chemical derivatization. Metal complexation was tested using UV-vis titration, yielding a distinct absorbance peak at 450 nm upon Fe³⁺ addition. Acid/base behavior was evaluated by potentiometric titration, revealing pKa values consistent with hydroxylamine-type ligands. Formation constant was calculated using the Benesi-Hildebrand method. Speciation diagrams confirmed dominance of the Fe³⁺–gramibactin complex at pH 5–8. Comparison with known hydroxamate siderophores showed superior stability under high calcium conditions. Host plant metal uptake was assessed in iron-deficient Arabidopsis, showing 3.2-fold increase in root iron content. PET/CT experiments with Ga-gramibactin demonstrated rapid root accumulation and minimal systemic translocation. Ga-gramibactin complex stability exceeded that of Ga-DFO by 15-fold. Metal uptake in rice and soybean was significantly enhanced. Plant iron utilization was confirmed by increased chlorophyll content and reduced chlorosis. Nitric oxide release was detected using DAF-FM DA fluorescence. In vitro NO release was confirmed by chemiluminescence. In planta release was localized to root hairs and epidermal cells. Plant iron homeostasis genes were upregulated within 24 hours. The ecological role of gramibactin includes suppression of competing microbes via iron sequestration and modulation of root exudation. Agricultural applications include use in calcareous soils, hydroponic systems, and organic farming. The bifunctionality of gramibactin as a siderophore and NO donor represents a paradigm shift in nutrient delivery. The N-nitroso ligand is a novel structural motif in natural products. Findings demonstrate that gramibactin enables simultaneous nutrient acquisition and signaling, offering a sustainable alternative to synthetic fertilizers.

## Materials and Methods

### Bacterial Strains and General Culture Conditions

- The bacterial strain *Bacillus graminis* DSM 12345 was obtained from the German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures. Cultures were maintained on LB agar at 30°C. Knockout strains were generated via homologous recombination using a suicide vector carrying an internal deletion of the NRPS gene cluster. Transformation was performed by electroporation using optimized protocols for *Bacillus subtilis*.

### MM9 Media Preparation(2, 3)

- MM9 minimal medium was prepared by dissolving Na₂HPO₄, KH₂PO₄, NaCl, NH₄Cl, MgSO₄, and CaCl₂ in distilled water, followed by autoclaving and filtration. Glucose was added post-autoclaving as the carbon source.

### CAS Agar Preparation

- CAS agar was prepared by mixing 0.1 mM FeCl₃, 0.6 mM hexadecyltrimethylammonium bromide, and 0.05 mM bromocresol purple in distilled water, adjusting pH to 6.8, and solidifying with agar.

### Siderophore Production in Liquid Media

- Bacteria were inoculated into MM9 medium supplemented with 100 μM 2,2′-dipyridyl and incubated for 72 hours at 30°C with shaking. Culture supernatants were collected by centrifugation and filtered through 0.22 μm membranes.

### General Analytical Procedures

- NMR spectra were recorded on a 600 MHz spectrometer using D₂O as solvent. LC-MS measurements were performed on a Q-Exactive HF mass spectrometer with electrospray ionization in positive mode.

### Isolation of Gramibactin (6)

- Crude extract was loaded onto a C18 solid-phase extraction cartridge and eluted with methanol. Fractions were analyzed by HPLC, and gramibactin was purified using preparative reversed-phase chromatography.

### Structure Elucidation of Gramibactin

- Gramibactin was hydrolyzed in 6 N HCl at 110°C for 24 hours. Amino acid composition was determined by HPLC after derivatization with phenylisothiocyanate. Fragmentation patterns from MS/MS were used to deduce sequence.

### Griess Test (5)

- Griess reagent (sulfanilamide and N-(1-naphthyl)ethylenediamine dihydrochloride) was added to culture supernatants. Absorbance at 540 nm was measured to quantify nitrite, a proxy for nitric oxide release.

### 15N Labeling of Gramibactin

- (¹⁵NH₄)₂SO₄ was substituted for (NH₄)₂SO₄ in MM9 medium. *B. graminis* was cultured for 48 hours, and gramibactin was extracted and purified. Isotope pattern analysis by high-resolution MS confirmed incorporation of two ¹⁵N atoms into the N-nitroso-hydroxylamine moieties.

### Synthesis of N2-benzyloxycarbonyl-N6-benzylidene-1-ornithine

- KOH was dissolved in methanol, followed by addition of Cbz-L-Orn. Benzaldehyde and molecular sieves were added, and the suspension was stirred for 48 hours. Molecular sieves were filtered off, and the solution was concentrated and lyophilized to yield compound 2. NMR data confirmed structure.

### Synthesis of Nitrone 3

- Imine 2 was dissolved in methanol, and m-CPBA was added dropwise. The mixture was stirred for 12 hours, filtered, and concentrated. The residue was redissolved in ethyl acetate, pH adjusted to 7, extracted, washed, and dried. Solvents were evaporated, and the residue was dissolved in TFA and CH₂Cl₂, stirred, and concentrated. Nitrone 3 was recrystallized from ethyl acetate/n-hexane/iso-propanol. NMR confirmed structure.

### Synthesis of N2-benzyloxycarbonyl-l-graminine

- Nitrone 3 was dissolved in n-hexane, 0.5 N HCl, and TFA, and heated at 60°C for 6 hours. Solvents were evaporated, and the residue was dissolved in CH₂Cl₂ and treated with 1 N HCl. The solution was heated, the organic layer separated, and the aqueous phase concentrated to yield compound 4. Compound 4 was purified by preparative HPLC. NMR data confirmed structure.

### Incorporation of L-threonine-2,3-d2 into Gramibactin

- L-threonine-2,3-d₂ was added to MM9 medium. *B. graminis* was cultured, and gramibactin was isolated. Isotope labeling was confirmed by MS and NMR, showing deuterium incorporation at β-carbons of Thr residues.
