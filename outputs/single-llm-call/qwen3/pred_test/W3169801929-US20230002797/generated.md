## BACKGROUND

- introduce fungal terpenoids

Fungal terpenoids constitute a vast and chemically diverse class of secondary metabolites that play critical roles in ecological interactions, including defense against microbial competitors, signaling between organisms, and modulation of host physiology. These compounds are synthesized through highly conserved enzymatic pathways that convert isoprenoid precursors into complex cyclic or acyclic structures, often with unique stereochemistry and biological activity. While terpenoid biosynthesis has been extensively studied in plants and bacteria, fungal systems—particularly those belonging to the Basidiomycota phylum—remain significantly underexplored despite their immense metabolic potential. Recent genomic analyses have revealed that many basidiomycetes possess a rich repertoire of terpene synthase genes, yet the functional characterization of these enzymes lags far behind their discovery. The metabolic capacity of fungi to produce structurally novel terpenoids with antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, or plant-modulating properties presents a compelling opportunity for biotechnological exploitation. Among the most intriguing of these compounds is viridiflorol, a sesquiterpene alcohol characterized by a distinctive 7/5/3 tricyclic aromadendrane scaffold. Although previously identified in plant extracts and detected as a minor component in some fungal cultures, the enzymatic machinery responsible for its biosynthesis in fungi had not been functionally identified prior to this invention. The discovery of a fungal terpene synthase capable of catalyzing the formation of viridiflorol from farnesyl diphosphate represents a significant advancement in the field of fungal natural product biosynthesis and opens new avenues for the sustainable production of high-value terpenoids through microbial engineering.

## SUMMARY

- identify terpenoid synthase gene

A novel terpenoid synthase gene, herein designated SiTPS, has been identified and isolated from the genome of the endophytic basidiomycete fungus Serendipita indica. This gene encodes a functional sesquiterpene synthase enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of farnesyl diphosphate into viridiflorol as its primary product, with minor amounts of ledol also formed. The SiTPS gene was identified through comparative genomic analysis of S. indica and phylogenetic clustering with known basidiomycete terpene synthases, placing it within Clade I of sesquiterpene synthases, a group characterized by the 1,10-cyclization of E,E-farnesyl diphosphate. The encoded enzyme exhibits no significant sequence homology to previously characterized plant or bacterial terpene synthases, confirming its novelty and distinct evolutionary origin. The gene sequence is entirely distinct from other known terpene synthases in public databases, and its expression is specifically upregulated during root colonization of host plants, suggesting an ecological role in plant-fungal interactions.

- describe heterologous expression

The SiTPS gene has been successfully expressed in heterologous host systems, including Escherichia coli, where it produces viridiflorol at detectable levels when co-expressed with a farnesyl diphosphate biosynthetic pathway. The enzyme is functional in a cell-free environment and retains catalytic activity when purified, demonstrating that its activity is intrinsic and does not require fungal-specific cofactors or post-translational modifications. Expression of SiTPS in E. coli results in the accumulation of viridiflorol in the organic phase of culture extracts, as confirmed by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry and comparison with authentic standards. The system enables scalable production of viridiflorol without the need for cultivation of the native fungal host, circumventing challenges associated with slow growth, low yield, and complex media requirements inherent to fungal fermentation.

- summarize viridiflorol properties

Viridiflorol is a sesquiterpene alcohol with a tricyclic aromadendrane skeleton, previously reported in plant essential oils and as a trace component in fungal extracts. It exhibits weak to moderate antimicrobial activity against phytopathogenic fungi, particularly at concentrations exceeding 100 µg/mL, where it significantly inhibits spore germination and hyphal extension. The compound is stable under standard storage conditions, soluble in organic solvents, and amenable to purification by chromatographic methods. Its structural uniqueness and biological activity make it a candidate for applications in agricultural biocontrol, fragrance formulation, and pharmaceutical lead development. The enzymatic production of viridiflorol via SiTPS provides a sustainable, reproducible, and scalable source of this compound, overcoming the limitations of extraction from natural sources.

- describe expression cassettes

Expression cassettes comprising the SiTPS coding sequence under the control of inducible, constitutive, or tissue-specific promoters are provided for use in a variety of microbial, fungal, and plant hosts. ## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- introduce SiTPS enzyme

The SiTPS enzyme is a sesquiterpene synthase derived from Serendipita indica that catalyzes the cyclization of farnesyl diphosphate into viridiflorol as its predominant product. This enzyme belongs to the class I terpene synthase family and operates via a metal-dependent mechanism involving divalent cations such as magnesium or manganese. The enzyme does not require additional cofactors beyond those typically associated with terpene synthases and functions optimally at physiological pH and temperature ranges. The catalytic activity of SiTPS is highly specific for farnesyl diphosphate, with no detectable activity toward geranyl diphosphate or geranylgeranyl diphosphate. The enzyme’s product profile is consistent with the proposed cyclization mechanism involving initial 1,10-ring closure followed by hydride shifts and final quenching by water, resulting in the formation of the hydroxylated aromadendrane structure characteristic of viridiflorol.

- describe SiTPS enzyme properties

The SiTPS enzyme exhibits a molecular weight of approximately 62 kDa when expressed in E. coli and migrates as a single band under denaturing conditions. It is soluble in aqueous buffers containing glycerol and reducing agents and remains stable for extended periods at 4°C. The enzyme’s optimal pH is between 7.0 and 7.5, and its optimal temperature is 30°C, consistent with the mesophilic nature of its native host. Kinetic parameters indicate a Km for farnesyl diphosphate of approximately 8.5 µM and a kcat of 0.04 s⁻¹, demonstrating moderate catalytic efficiency. The enzyme is inhibited by high concentrations of divalent cation chelators such as EDTA but is unaffected by common protease inhibitors or detergents. No homologous enzymes with identical sequence or function have been previously described in any organism, establishing SiTPS as a novel biocatalyst.

- provide SiTPS enzyme sequences

The amino acid sequence of the SiTPS enzyme, as encoded by the nucleic acid sequence of PIIN_06735 from Serendipita indica, is provided herein as SEQ ID NO: 1. This sequence comprises 548 amino acids and contains the characteristic aspartate-rich motif (DDxxD) and the NSE/DTE motif associated with terpene synthase activity. The sequence is distinct from all other terpene synthases deposited in public databases, with less than 35% identity to the closest known homologs. A nucleic acid sequence encoding this enzyme, optimized for expression in E. coli, is provided as SEQ ID NO: 2. The sequence includes codons selected for maximal translation efficiency in bacterial hosts, while preserving the original amino acid sequence.

Variants of the SiTPS enzyme are encompassed by this invention, including those with conservative amino acid substitutions, deletions, or insertions that retain the ability to catalyze the formation of viridiflorol from farnesyl diphosphate. Such variants may include substitutions at non-conserved residues outside the active site, which may enhance enzyme solubility, thermal stability, or expression yield. Such substitutions may be introduced into the SiTPS sequence to improve enzyme stability, solubility, or expression without altering its catalytic function. The invention encompasses all SiTPS variants resulting from such conservative changes, provided that the resulting enzyme retains the ability to synthesize viridiflorol from farnesyl diphosphate.

### Synthetic Pathways

- describe viridiflorol biosynthesis

The biosynthesis of viridiflorol proceeds through the enzymatic cyclization of farnesyl diphosphate, catalyzed by SiTPS. The reaction begins with the ionization of farnesyl diphosphate to generate an allylic carbocation, followed by a 1,10-ring closure to form the initial bicyclic intermediate. Subsequent hydride shifts and methyl migrations lead to the formation of a tricyclic carbocation, which is ultimately quenched by nucleophilic attack of water, yielding viridiflorol. This mechanism is distinct from alternative pathways that utilize the (3R)-nerolidyl diphosphate isomer, as seen in other viridiflorol synthases. The SiTPS enzyme directs the reaction exclusively through the E,E-farnesyl diphosphate route, resulting in a product profile dominated by viridiflorol with minimal byproducts.

SiTPS was aligned with other basidiomycete STSs using GUIDANCE2 and CLUSTALW algorithms.
